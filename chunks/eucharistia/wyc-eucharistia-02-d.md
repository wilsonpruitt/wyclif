# *De eucharistia*, chapter 2, part 4: expounding the figures of scripture: God's repenting in Genesis 6

## ¶1 · 40.27–41.16

> [p. 40] Et quantum ad infinitatem membrorum, ut manuum, digitorum vel talium que preter personalitatem [p. 41] in Deo dicunt raciones exemplares, videtur quod non est numerus istorum, sicut non est finitus numerus omnium ydearum. Et istud considerarent canoniste qui in vulgari predicant laycis quod ille manus quibus formavit Deus celum et terram fuerant crucifixe; addiscerent, inquam, primo quot manus huiusmodi habet Deus et si manus sinistra Dei fuit in mundi inicio aliquid operata, ad excludendum Manicheos qui posuerunt Deum malum. Et infinite sunt conclusiones huiusmodi, quibus nimis oneraretur ecclesia a sophistis infidelibus, si omnia dicta figurativa scripture debent generaliter trahi in consequenciam, ut alii in toto libere loquantur conformiter; oportet ergo nos loqui plane interpretando figuras et reservando illis formas loquendi autenticas, ut docet Augustinus IVᵒ De Doctrina Christiana.

And as to the infinity of members, such as hands, fingers or such things, which besides [personality]{g:personalitas} [41] [signify]{g:dicere} [exemplary reasons]{g:raciones-exemplares} in God, it seems that there is no number of these, as there is no finite number of all the [ideas]{g:ydea}.[^1] And the [canonists]{g:canoniste} should consider this, who preach in the vernacular to lay people that those hands with which God formed heaven and earth were crucified; let them learn, I say,[^2] first how many hands of this kind God has, and whether the left hand of God did any work at the beginning of the world, so as to exclude the Manichees, who posited an evil God.[^3] And infinite are the [conclusions]{g:conclusio} of this kind with which the [church]{g:ecclesia} would be [exceedingly]{g:nimis} burdened by [unbelieving]{g:infidelis} [sophists]{g:sophista}, if all the [figurative]{g:figurative} sayings of scripture ought generally to be [drawn into a precedent]{g:trahere-in-consequenciam}, so that others should freely speak in conformity with them in everything. We must therefore speak plainly, interpreting the [figures]{g:figura} and reserving to them the [authentic]{g:autenticus} forms of speaking, as Augustine teaches in *On Christian Doctrine* IV.[^4]

## ¶2 · 41.17–42.7

> [p. 41] Sed tercio replicatur contra dicta per hoc quod non docetur que scriptura debet admitti et que abnui, nec in quo doctor est tenendus et in quo negandus. Ymmo videtur quod ecclesia tempore Beringarii fuit heretica vel aliter infidelis fuit ecclesia sequens illi contraria. Hic dico quod tota scriptura sacra debet admitti ut fides et detestari eius falsitas, cum ex integro sit vera et in nulla parte falsa, ut hic supponitur. Figurativas autem locuciones scripture debemus exponere et modo discipulorum dimissis figuris nostris auctoribus loqui plane. Unde solebam exponere predictam scripturam Genes. VIᵒ, ubi notato textu solebam dicere, quomodo minus bene intelligentibus textus iste videtur pretendere quod Deus prius ignorans peccatum hominum futurum, postquam in facto [p. 42] didicit hominem ex pronitate sic peccare, doluit et displicuit sibi vel quod unquam fecit hominem, vel quod fecit hominem tam fragilem; sed quod fecisset eum constantem et confirmatum ut angelum, quia tunc caruisset occupacione penali in castigando eum pro delicto; sed longe alium sensum pretendit bene intelligentibus.

But thirdly [it is rejoined]{g:replicatur} against what has been said, on the ground that it is not taught which scripture ought to be admitted and which refused, nor in what a doctor is to be held and in what denied. Indeed, it seems that the church in the time of Berengar was heretical, or else the church that followed, contrary to it, was unbelieving. Here I say that the whole of sacred scripture ought to be admitted as faith, and falsity in it detested, since it is entirely true and in no part false, as is supposed here.[^5] But the figurative locutions of scripture we ought to expound, and, after the manner of disciples, leaving the figures to our authors, speak plainly.[^6] Hence I used to expound the aforesaid scripture of Gen. 6, where, having marked the text, I used to say how to those who understand less well this text seems to put forward that God, previously ignorant of the future sin of men, after he [42] learned in fact that man sinned thus from proneness, grieved, and it displeased him either that he ever made man, or that he made man so frail; but [he wished] that he had made him steadfast and confirmed like an angel, because then he would have been without the [hard]{g:penalis} occupation of chastising him for his offence; but it puts forward a far other sense to those who understand well.[^7]

## ¶3 · 42.8–42.12

> [p. 42] Pro quo notandum quod Moyses alloquens populum valde rudem aptavit verba secundum eorum capacitatem, ut primo literaliter consencientes per fidem ascendant ad sensum alciorem, sicut utrobique facit scriptura.

For which it is to be noted that Moses, addressing a very rude people, fitted his words to their capacity, so that, first consenting literally through faith, they might ascend to a higher sense, as scripture does [on both sides]{g:utrobique}.[^8]

## ¶4 · 42.13–42.25

> [p. 42] Secundo secundum historiographos est notandum quod mortuo Adam Seth ex mandato patris separavit cognacionem suam a Kaym, rediens ad solum natale in quendam montem propinquum paradiso, sed post septimam generacionem fertur filios eius qui propter religiositatem vocantur filii Dei miscuisse cum filiabus Kaym que propter petulanciam vocantur filie hominum; et potuit cum hoc esse quod demones incubi qui numerantur inter filios Dei detulerunt semina hominum in matrices mulierum procreando gigantes ex industria fovendi fetus per virtutes mixtorum, et ista brutalitas cum aliis que narrat Methodius martyr fuit causa diluvii.

Secondly, it is to be noted, according to the historiographers, that when Adam was dead Seth, by his father's command, separated his [kindred]{g:cognacio} from Cain, returning to his native soil, onto a certain mountain near paradise; but after the seventh generation his sons, who on account of their [religiousness]{g:religiositas} are called sons of God, are said to have mingled with the daughters of Cain, who on account of their wantonness are called daughters of men; and it could be, along with this, that incubus demons, who are numbered among the sons of God, carried the seeds of men into the wombs of women, procreating giants by their skill in fostering the fetuses through the [powers]{g:virtus} of [mixed bodies]{g:mixtum}; and this brutishness, with other things that Methodius the martyr [relates]{g:narrare}, was the cause of the flood.[^9]

## ¶5 · 42.26–43.12

> [p. 42] Tercio notandum quod Deus apud quem omnia sunt presencia, loquitur in textu predicto tanquam pius pater consolans filios volentes per penitenciam converti ad Deum; quod patet ex tribus partibus huius textus. Nam primo dicit: Non permanebit spiritus [p. 43] meus in homine in eternum, quia caro est, eruntque dies eius centum viginti annorum, quasi diceret: Spiritus vivendi non permanebit eternaliter in toto genere peccatorum perpetuo dampnato, ut dampnatur genus demonum. Et racio secunda subditur, quia fragilis est ex carne et ut sic excusabilis. Tercio vero apponitur medium medendi, dum promittitur quod dies penitencie antequam inundet diluvium erunt CXX anni; hoc enim verbum Dei fuit viginti annis antequam Noe incepit procreare, et centum annorum fuit primogenitus suus tempore diluvii.

Thirdly, it is to be noted that God, with whom all things are present, speaks in the aforesaid text as a [loving father]{g:pius-pater} consoling sons who are willing through penance to be converted to God;[^10] which is clear from three parts of this text. For first he says: "My spirit shall not remain [43] in man for ever, because he is flesh, and his days shall be a hundred and twenty years",[^11] as if he said: The spirit of living shall not remain eternally in the whole race of sinners, perpetually damned, as the race of demons is damned. And the second reason is added: that he is frail from the flesh and as such excusable. And thirdly the means of healing is set down, when it is promised that the days of penance before the flood overflows will be 120 years; for this word of God was twenty years before Noah began to beget, and his firstborn was a hundred years old at the time of the flood.[^12]

## ¶6 · 43.13–43.29

> [p. 43] Secundo principaliter premittitur prima noticia malicie commisse, cum dicitur: Videns autem Deus etc. Secundo subditur pronitas ad recidivandum, dum adiungitur: Et cuncta cogitacio hominis etc. Et tercio ostenditur medicinalis satisfaccio, dum dicitur: Penituit eum quod hominem fecisset in terra. Ex faccione namque hominis terreni et peccato eius contra summum bonum oportuit Christum pati et intrare cum redemptis in gloriam suam; et hoc sonat penitere, id est, penam tenere. Nec moveat quod dicitur de presenti, quia Deus semper scit se pati. Tercio principaliter tangit in generali remedium ad abscindendum peccatum hominis in futurum, dum dicitur: Precavens in futurum, nam potestas gygancium qui fuerant super terram et sterilitas terre nascencium cum serenitate et salubritate aeris fuit illis occasio bestialiter lasciviendi.

Secondly, [as a main head]{g:principaliter}, there is set first the first knowledge of the malice committed, when it is said: "And God seeing" etc. Secondly is added the proneness to relapse, when there is joined: "And all the thought of man" etc. And thirdly is shown the medicinal [satisfaction]{g:satisfaccio}, when it is said: "It repented him that he had made man on the earth."[^13] For from the [making]{g:faccio} of earthly man and his sin against the highest good it behoved Christ to suffer and to enter with the redeemed into his glory; and this is what "repent" sounds: that is, *penam tenere*, "to hold the penalty". Nor let it trouble [anyone] that it is said of the present, since God always knows himself to suffer.[^14] Thirdly, as a main head, he touches in general on the remedy for cutting off the sin of man in the future, when it is said: "Taking precaution for the future";[^15] for the [power]{g:potestas} of the giants who had been upon the earth, and the barrenness of the earth's growths, with the serenity and healthiness of the air, was for them an occasion of wantoning like beasts.[^16]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 41.17: We are to accept all Scripture as true, and explain the figurative expressions.
- 41.27: Wyclif's exposition of 'it repented the Lord'.
- 42.8: Moses adapted his words to his hearers.
- 42.13: Cause of the deluge.
- 42.26: The text is an expression of God's mercy.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Dicunt* in its logical use, "signify" (GLOSSARY *dicere*): the members ascribed to God signify, apart from the personal properties, the exemplary reasons in the divine mind, which are without number.
[^2]: **T.** *Inquam* is the print; all the manuscripts read *inquit* ("Codd.: inquit"). The print is translated.
[^3]: **C.** *Ad excludendum Manicheos* is taken with *addiscerent*: the canonists would need to settle whether God's left hand worked at creation in order to keep out the Manichees, who posit an evil principle. DE read *posuerant*; ABCD lack *Et* before *infinite*.
[^4]: **C/S.** *Illis* is taken as the figures (or the scriptural sayings), to which the authentic forms of speaking are reserved; ABC read *reservandi*, D *reservanda formam*. Augustine, *De doctrina christiana* IV.8.22 (PL 34, 98–99). Expositors are not to copy the scriptural authors' useful obscurity, *tanquam se ipsi exponendos simili auctoritate proponant*, but are to speak so as to be understood. Loserth's reference is *Opp.* III.1, p. 70 (LEDGER K02d-1).
[^5]: **C.** *Detestari eius falsitas*: the passive infinitive, parallel to *admitti*, with *falsitas* its subject; taken as "falsity [imputed] in it", since the next clause says scripture has no part false. B reads *fuit infidelis*; ABC *replicatur tercio*.
[^6]: **C/T.** *Nostris auctoribus* is construed as dative with *dimissis figuris*, "leaving the figures to our authors" (the writers of scripture), as *reservando illis* at 41.14. Loserth's foot reads "Rectius: auditoribus", which would give "speak plainly to our hearers". ABC read *debent*; C corrected to *debemus*; C *autem* in the margin in another hand; C *predictam sentenciam*.
[^7]: **C.** *Sed quod fecisset eum*: the subjunctive clause depends on an unexpressed wish set against *displicuit*; "[he wished]" is supplied. The whole view is what the text "seems to put forward" to those who understand less well, not Wyclif's own. B lacks *vel quod — hominem*; B lacks *tam fragilem*; D *caruit*.
[^8]: **C.** *Utrobique*, "on both sides": taken as both testaments, or both the literal sense and the higher; Wyclif does not say which. E reads *rudem valde*, *capacitatem eorum*; D *apta verba*.
[^9]: **S.** The historiographers on Seth's separation from Cain's line and the mingling after the seventh generation: Peter Comestor, *Historia scholastica*, Gen. c. 31, *De causa diluvii* (PL 198, 1081A–D), followed closely: *Mortuo Adam, Seth separavit cognationem suam a cognatione Cain, quae redierat ad natale solum … in quodam monte proximo paradiso*, with Josephus's seventh generation and the *incubi daemones* who may have begotten the giants. In Comestor it is Cain's line that had returned to its native soil (LEDGER K02d-2). Methodius: the pseudo-Methodian *Revelationes* c. 1–2 (ed. Sackur 1898, pp. 60–62), which Comestor follows and calls *Methodius martyr* (PL 198, 1076B). There Seth takes his kindred *in quendam montem, proximos paradiso*, and the men of Cain's camp couple *in more animalium*, which is Wyclif's *brutalitas* (LEDGER K02d-3). On *incubi* Loserth refers to Du Cange III, 804 (LEDGER K02d-4). *Cognacio* here is family "kindred", not the GLOSSARY *cognacio* of Christ's members. *Mixta*: the bodies compounded of the elements. E reads *solium naturale*, *causa fuit*; C *miscuisse se*.
[^10]: **C.** *Pius pater*: "a loving father"; *pius* here is a father's tenderness, as the side-note's "God's mercy" takes it. D reads *non manebit*.
[^11]: **S.** Gen. 6:3; the Vulgate has *illius* for Wyclif's *eius*, as DE read. Douay–Rheims wording. C reads *centum et viginti*.
[^12]: **T/S.** DE read *Spiritus vindicandi*, "the spirit of avenging", for *Spiritus vivendi*; the print is translated. Noah's ages: compare Gen. 5:31 and 7:6. D reads *perpetue*, *ut sit*; E *subscribitur*.
[^13]: **S.** Gen. 6:5–6. Wyclif's *cuncta cogitacio hominis* departs from the Vulgate's *cuncta cogitatio cordis* (DR "all the thought of their heart"); his wording is translated. *Penituit eum quod hominem fecisset in terra* agrees with 6:6 (DR).
[^14]: **C.** *Penitere, id est, penam tenere*: Wyclif's etymology, "to hold the penalty", which reads God's repenting as the penalty Christ bears. *De presenti*: "of the present", taken of the present-tense *penitere* so expounded; God always knows himself to suffer, since he knows eternally what Christ suffers. D reads *quod est*, E *quasi est*.
[^15]: **S.** *Precavens in futurum* is not in the modern Vulgate, as Loserth's foot (43.25) says. It did stand in the medieval Vulgate of Gen. 6:6 (*poenituit eum quod hominem fecisset in terra, et praecavens in futurum, et tactus dolore cordis intrinsecus*), as quoted by Rupert of Deutz, *De Trinitate et operibus eius*, In Gen. (PL 167, 340B), and by Hugh of St Victor, *Adnotationes in Pentateuchon* (PL 175, 46B) (LEDGER K02d-5).
[^16]: **T.** *Sterilitas*, "barrenness", is the print; D reads *fertilitas*, "fertility", which agrees with the serene and healthy air as an occasion of wantonness. The print is translated. ABC read *fuerit*; E *eis*.
