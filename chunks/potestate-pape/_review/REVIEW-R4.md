# Review R4: *De potestate pape* chunks 12-f, 12-o, 12-r, comp-a

Reviewer: independent, fresh session, 2026-10-12. Method: sentence by sentence against Loserth's Latin, checked against `CARD.md` (four Thakkar classes, connective table, standing rules), CONVENTIONS §§6–9, 14, and GLOSSARY.md (grepped per term; every `{g:…}` key in the four chunks was matched against the glossary headwords and all exist). Sources and ledger rows were not re-verified. Connectives (*sed/autem*) were checked volume-wide before this review, so they are reported only where a note contradicts them. Quotation bounds were not checked against the italics. For comp-a, the renderings were compared with the chapter chunks (01-a, 01-d/e, 04-d, 06-e and others) where the compendium summarises the same passage. Line numbers marked "c." are placed from side-note, apparatus and plate-log anchors, because the chunk Latin is not lineated. No plates were read.

Watch-list result: *ingratus* does not occur in these four chunks. Every English sentence has a main verb. *Laxare* is "loose" in comp-a 398.9, matching 01-d/01-e. One note contradicts the English beside it (#3), and two more notes misdescribe the Latin beside them (#11, #18).

## Latin doubts (for the plates)

- **12-f 335.26** *quemque autem ex amore populi*. The parallel *Deus enim quandoque ex amore predestinati … hunc arguit …, quemque autem ex amore populi* strongly suggests *quandoque autem*. N. 12 says B's apparatus at this line is cut off in the scan. If the plate or apparatus gives *quandoque*, the English becomes: "and sometimes out of love for the people, whom he wills to be preserved from the poison of such a stain".
- **12-f c. 337.13** *igitur respondebam*. N. 20 reads it as an epistolary imperfect. *Respondeam* is possible. The English "I would therefore answer" serves either reading, so no change follows from this.
- **12-r c. 381.6** *ante ea* (n. 2). Possibly *antea*, split in the print. The sense is the same either way.
- **12-r c. 385.20** *et dispensacio pro pecunia et de cognacione potencie incurrit* (n. 18). *Incurrit* has no object; *intercurrit* or *occurrit* would construe. Check the plate and apparatus.
- **12-o 371.1** *constat sciolos* (n. 7). The accusative is irregular; *sciolis* may be meant.
- **12-o 372.17** *est pocius* (n. 11). A comparative may have dropped out.
- **comp-a c. 398.12** *quo tripliciter confirmat* (n. 5). Possibly *quod*.
- **comp-a c. 401.6** *cum ditacio non dissolveret amiciciam* (n. 17). Is *non* intrusive? The English depends on the answer.
