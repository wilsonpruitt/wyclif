# *De ecclesia*, chapter 21, part 6: order as perceptible sacrament, and the many characters in one order

## ¶1 · 510.27–512.25

> [p. 510] Sed tercio modo sumitur ordo pro sacramento sensibili quod est ordinancia episcopi ad sacrum officium supra laycos in ecclesia ministrandi, et si res abscondita huius sensibilis sacramenti est ordo vel caracter insensibiliter subiectatus in anima, et ordo est opus sensibile ab episcopo celebratum quod in verborum sacramentalium prolacione, in ritus, tactus aut unccionis ministracione et in circumcisione extrinsece, ut loci, [p. 511] temporis, persone ordinande et superiorum licencie attencione. Unde dicitur communiter quod regulariter in celebracione ordinum rite facta Deus graciose coassistit dando graciam. Et ideo necesse est peccatorem ordinandum conteri de peccatis. Deus enim dans potestatem ad ministrandum dat eciam graciam ad officium exequendum nisi indisposicio ordinati prepediat. Unde in baptismo et quocunque alio sacramento subducta assistencia Christi dantis graciam, que sit res sacramenti, fit ex indisposicione suscipientis sacramentum equivoce. Verumptamen Deus post sacracionem episcopi sepe facit ordinem reminiscere, ipse enim dat regulariter rem sacramenti, et sic facit sepe ordines nobis incognite. Unde vacuum videtur mihi quod sollicitemur ad querendum quando, qualiter et quibus verbis Deus ordinavit apostolos vel beatum Paulum aut alios discipulos. Cavere tamen debemus solicite ne pseudoclerici fingant se esse ordinatos a Deo ad tale officium clericatus. Ad hoc enim ordinatur ordinandi examinacio, episcopi celebracio et signorum sensibilium figuracio, ut sic innotescat ecclesie discolorum exclusio; utrobique tamen sepe contingit illusio. Et (ut sepe dixi) credendum est precipue operi legitimo ordinati. Istis premissis videtur mihi quantum ad primam conclusionem quod, sicut ordo acceptus tercio modo est aggregatum ex multis signis sensibilibus, eciam paulatim in diversis et intercisis temporibus celebratis: sic ordo superior acceptus secundo modo est aggregatum ex diversis caracteribus sive signaculis; quos Deus dat appropriate simul vel intercise, et sunt eius partes quantitative non quoad molem, sed quoad potestatem, ita quod, quotquot actus legitimos quis sufficit facere, tot caracteres habet datos a Deo compositos [p. 512] proporcionaliter cum se ipsis. Sed ne subditus diffidat de potestate sui prepositi, cum in cessacione cuiuscunque officii unus caracter extinguitur, Deus dedit caracterem superiorem, qui est limitacio ad sufficienciam dati generis ministerii indistincte, et talem caracterem non potest ordinatus deserere, quamdiu manet in eo potencia ad signa sensibilia ministrandum. Suspendi tamen potest a superiori propter causam racionabilem, ne ordinatus ponat tunc caracterem in effectum. Et si queratur necessitas ponendi tot caracteres, dicitur quod admissa significacione quid nominis, et supposita ordinacione divina de dividendo diversis hominibus diversas gracias officii gratis datas, sequitur inevitabiliter predicta diversitas caracterum. Unde ad fundandum istam sentenciam oportet primo supponere fidem scripture Iª Cor. XII°, 4—7: Divisiones graciarum sunt, idem autem spiritus; et divisiones ministracionum sunt, idem autem dominus, et divisiones operacionum sunt, idem autem Deus qui operatur omnia in omnibus. Unicuique autem datur manifestacio spiritus ad utilitatem. Et post in corpore ecclesie explicat novem gradus. Nec licet denegare talem ordinem concedendo corpus ecclesie vel eius ministros secundum iura canonica.

But in the third way order is taken for the [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} sacrament, which is the [ordination]{g:ordinancia} of a bishop to the sacred office of ministering in the [church]{g:ecclesia} above the laity; and if the hidden reality of this perceptible sacrament is order or [character]{g:caracter} [imperceptibly]{g:insensibilis} subjected in the soul, [then] order is also a perceptible work celebrated by the bishop, which [consists] in the utterance of the sacramental words, in the ministering of rite, touch or anointing, and outwardly in the circumscribing [of it], as by attention to place, [511] time, the person to be ordained and the licence of superiors.[^1] Hence it is commonly said that, as a rule, in a celebration of orders rightly performed God graciously assists together, giving grace. And therefore it is necessary that a sinner who is to be ordained be contrite for his sins. For God, giving power to minister, gives also grace for carrying out the office, unless the [unfitness]{g:indisposicio} of the ordained hinders [it]. Hence in baptism and in any other sacrament, when the assistance of Christ giving grace, which is to be the reality of the sacrament, is withdrawn, the sacrament is made [equivocally]{g:equivoce}, through the unfitness of the one receiving it.[^2] Nevertheless God, after the bishop's consecrating, often makes the order revive; for he regularly gives the reality of the sacrament; and so he often makes orders in a way unknown to us.[^3] Hence it seems to me vain that we should be anxious to inquire when, how and with what words God ordained the apostles, or blessed Paul, or other disciples. Yet we must carefully beware lest false clerics feign that they are ordained by God to such an office of the [clerical state]{g:clericatus}. For to this end are ordered the examination of the one to be ordained, the bishop's celebration and the figuring of perceptible signs, that thus the exclusion of the [froward]{g:discoli} may become known to the church; yet on both sides illusion often happens.[^4] And (as I have often said) one must believe chiefly the lawful work of the ordained. These things premised, it seems to me, as to the first [conclusion]{g:conclusio}, that, just as order taken in the third way is an aggregate of many perceptible signs, celebrated even little by little at diverse and interrupted times: so higher order taken in the second way is an aggregate of diverse characters or [seals]{g:signaculum}, which God gives as is fitting, together or at intervals; and they are its [quantitative parts]{g:pars quantitativa}, not as to bulk but as to power; so that as many lawful acts as a man suffices to do, so many characters he has, given by God, compounded [512] proportionally with one another.[^5] But lest a subject should distrust the power of his [superior]{g:prepositi}, since at the ceasing of any office one character is extinguished, God has given a higher character, which is an [assignment]{g:limitacio} to sufficiency for a given kind of ministry, without distinction; and such a character the ordained cannot desert so long as there remains in him the power to minister the perceptible signs. Yet he can be [suspended]{g:suspendere} by a superior for reasonable cause, lest the ordained should then put the character into effect.[^6] And if the need of positing so many characters is asked, [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that, granted the [signification]{g:significacio} of [what the name means]{g:quid nominis}, and supposing the divine [ordinance]{g:ordinacio} of dividing diverse graces of office, freely given, among diverse men, the aforesaid diversity of characters follows inevitably.[^7] Hence, to ground this [teaching]{g:sentencia}, one must first suppose the faith of scripture, 1 Corinthians 12:4–7: "There are diversities of graces, but the same Spirit; and there are diversities of ministries, but the same Lord; and there are diversities of operations, but the same God, who worketh all in all. And the manifestation of the Spirit is given to every man unto profit." And afterwards he sets out nine grades in the body of the church. Nor is it lawful to deny such an order while granting the body of the church, or its ministers according to the canon laws.[^8]

## ¶2 · 512.26–513.21

> [p. 512] Unde notanda est trimembris divisio textus apostoli. Aliqua enim donat Deus predestinatis que vocat gracias a spiritu sancto datas, quia predestinatis timentibus Deum omnia cooperantur in bonum. Unde quia cum istis donis stat mortale secundum presentem iusticiam, ideo vocantur gracie gratis date ex benevolencia spiritus predestinantis. Ministraciones autem sunt in qualibet creatura racionali predestinata [p. 513] vel prescita, quam oportet servire domino libere vel coacte; ideo adiungit apostolus illud secundum membrum ad dominum. Sed tercio omnis natura creata saltem per se existens habet apud creatorem suum officium operandi. Ideo notat illud tercium generale sub habitudine ad Deum creantem. Istis suppositis patet quod omnis clericus habet a Deo secundum suum gradum limitacionem officii ministrandi, et illam voco ego caracterem quem oportet omnem fidelem admittere cuicunque membro ecclesie et nullum alium. Quod autem alii capiunt terminum striccius in uno sensu et alii largius in alio, non est magna vis, cum sit terminus magistralis, verumptamen loquendum est (ut plures loquentes scripture et racioni conformius), et patet responsio quomodo aliquis caracter cessat completo officio, et talis dividitur proporcionaliter ut eius officium. Et ille vocatur caracter in actu, et alius caracter dicit potestatem ad confuse faciendum ministerium, sive sequatur sive non, et ille non dependet ab opere sed a sufficiencia operis quoad speciem terminantis, et iste modus loquendi videtur facilior.

Hence the threefold division of the apostle's text is to be noted. For some things God gives to the [predestinate]{g:predestinatus}, which he calls graces given by the Holy Spirit, because "to the predestinate that fear God all things work together unto good". Hence, since mortal [sin] stands with these gifts [according to present justice]{g:secundum presentem iusticiam}, they are therefore called graces freely given, from the benevolence of the predestinating Spirit.[^9] And ministries are in every rational creature, predestinate [513] or [foreknown]{g:prescitus}, which must serve the Lord freely or by compulsion; therefore the apostle joins that second [member]{g:membrum} to "the Lord". But thirdly every created nature, at least one existing by itself, has from its creator its office of operating. Therefore he marks that third, general [member] under the [condition]{g:habitudo} toward God creating.[^10] These things supposed, it is clear that every cleric has from God, according to his grade, an assignment of the office of ministering; and this I call the character, which every believer must grant to any member of the church whatever, and no other.[^11] And that some take the term more strictly in one sense and others more broadly in another is no great matter, since it is a [masters' term]{g:termini magistrales}; nevertheless one must speak (as the majority [do], speaking more conformably to scripture and reason); and the answer is clear, how some character ceases when the office is completed, and such [a character] is divided proportionally, as its office [is]. And that is called the character in act; and the other character signifies the power to perform the ministry [indistinctly]{g:confuse}, whether [the act] follows or not; and that does not depend on the work but on sufficiency for the work as to the species of what terminates it; and this way of speaking seems easier.[^12]

## ¶3 · 513.22–514.20

> [p. 513] Ulterius patet quod est dare septem ordines secundo modo dictos, distinctos specifice, sed nullus eorum est sensibile sacramentum, sed res vel fructus eius. Sacramentum autem ordinis quod sacerdos celebrat est quodammodo unum secundum magnam partem sui subiectatum in ordinante, quia finis a quo capitur eius unitas est totum sacerdocium tocius sacerdotis ecclesie, nec resisto quin sunt diversi ordines distincti specifice tam sacramenta quam res sacramenti; omnes tamen illi ordines sunt unus ordo, et sic sunt tantum septem sacramenta, licet sit tantum unum sacramentum, sicut Crisostomus, Omelia prima, dicit de septem ecclesiis. [p. 514] Et patet primo quod ordinans hominem primo et immediate in sacerdotem eo ipso dat sibi vel ministerialiter signo sacramentali ostendit quod habet septem ordines sive caracteres. Patet ex hoc quod eo ipso limitatur ad potestatem septemplicem quod est ordo. Non enim foret sacerdos univoce nisi haberet potestatem ad ministrandum quamcunque speciem spiritualis ministerii quo sufficit ministrare clericus graduatus inferior. Immo quot sunt species officii in quas graduatus potestative sufficit, tot sunt ordines sive caracteres. Omnes tamen sunt unus ordo cuius partes integrales vocari possunt partes potestative, sicut pars senarius communis ad quamcunque unitatem eius vocatur prima pars eius aliquota, et pars eius communis ad quemcunque eius binarium vel ternarium vocatur secunda et tercia pars eius aliquota, ex quibus adequate constat senarius. Further, it is clear that there are seven orders taken in the second way, distinct in species; but none of them is the perceptible sacrament, but its reality or fruit. And the sacrament of order which the [priest]{g:sacerdos} celebrates is in a way one, subjected for a great part of it in the one ordaining, since the end from which its unity is taken is the whole priesthood of the whole priest of the church; nor do I resist [the view] that there are diverse orders distinct in species, sacraments as well as reality of the sacrament; yet all those orders are one order; and so there are only seven sacraments, though there is only one sacrament, as Chrysostom, in the first homily, says of the seven churches.[^13] [514] And it is clear, first, that he who ordains a man first and immediately into a priest by that very fact gives him, or ministerially shows by a sacramental sign, that he has seven orders or characters. It is clear from this, that by that very fact he is assigned to a sevenfold power, which is order. For he would not be a priest univocally unless he had power to minister every species of spiritual ministry that a cleric of lower grade suffices to minister. Indeed, as many as are the species of office to which one who holds a grade suffices in power, so many are the orders or characters.[^14] Yet all are one order, whose integral parts can be called potestative parts; as the part of six common to any unit of it is called its first aliquot part, and its part common to any two or three of it is called its second and third aliquot part, of which six is adequately made up. ## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Ordinancia episcopi*: "ordination", the sacramental act (row *ordinacio; ordinancia*). *Res abscondita*: the hidden reality (*res sacramenti*) beneath the visible sign. *Insensibiliter*: imperceptibly. **T.** As printed, the period has no apodosis after *et si … in anima*, and the clause *quod in … prolacione …* has no verb; "[then]" and "[consists]" are supplied. *In circumcisione extrinsece*: so the codices and the print; Loserth notes "rectius *et intercisione*", "and in the interruption [of times]", comparing 511.29 and 511.31 (510.34). The print is translated, taking *circumcisio* as the circumscribing of the rite by its outward conditions. **R.** Side-note 510.27.

[^2]: **C.** *Regulariter*: as a rule. *Coassistit*: assists together [with the minister]. *Conteri*: to be contrite. *Que sit res sacramenti*: grace, which is to be the reality of the sacrament. *Sacramentum equivoce*: a sacrament in name only. **R.** Side-note 511.5.

[^3]: **T.** *Reminiscere*: so printed; "makes the order remember" gives no sense here. Translated "revive", as if *reviviscere*, the term for a sacrament that takes effect once the obstacle is removed (our emendation; the plate confirms *reminiscere* and Loserth has no note). **C.** *Post sacracionem episcopi*: after the bishop's consecrating act. *Incognite*: in a way unknown to us. **R.** A in the margin at 511.15: *Nota*.

[^4]: **C.** *Pseudoclerici*: false clerics. *Discolorum*: of the froward (1 Pet. 2:18). *Utrobique … illusio*: deception happens on both sides, in God's ordaining as men judge it and in the bishop's rite. **R.** Side-note 511.18.

[^5]: **C.** *Quantum ad primam conclusionem*: as to the first of the three doubts (509.1), how the characters make one order. *Appropriate*: as is fitting, not the canon-law *appropriare*. *Partes quantitative … quoad potestatem*: Wyclif grants the characters to be quantitative parts in power, against the doctors' denial (509.3). *Compositos proporcionaliter cum se ipsis*: compounded in proportion to one another. **R.** Side-note 511.26; A in the margin at 511.26: *Responsio*.

[^6]: **C.** *Caracterem superiorem*: the higher character, a general assignment to a kind of ministry, which outlasts the ceasing of any single act. *Indistincte*: without distinction of particular acts. *A superiori*: by a superior in office. **T.** B *in ea* (512.7). **R.** Side-note 512.2.

[^7]: **C.** *Admissa significacione quid nominis*: granted the signification of the word "character" as defined (what the name means). *Gracias officii gratis datas*: graces of office freely given, the scholastic *gratiae gratis datae*. **T.** B *signacione* (512.12), the same word (row *signacio / significacio*). **R.** Side-note 512.11; A in the margin at 512.11: *Questio*.

[^8]: **S.** 1 Corinthians 12:4–7: Wyclif has no *vero* in v. 4 (the Douay–Rheims "Now" is therefore dropped) and *autem* for *vero* in v. 6. *Novem gradus*: the nine set in the church at 1 Corinthians 12:28 (apostles, prophets, doctors, miracles, healings, helps, governments, tongues, interpretations). **C.** *Supponere fidem scripture*: to take as premiss what scripture teaches. *Nec licet … secundum iura canonica*: taken to mean that whoever grants the body of the church, or its ministers as the canons define them, may not deny such an order.

[^9]: **S.** Romans 8:28: Wyclif has *predestinatis timentibus* ("to the predestinate that fear") for the Vulgate *diligentibus* ("to them that love"); his Latin is translated (Loserth, 512.29: Rom. VIII, 28). **C.** *Trimembris divisio*: graces, ministries, operations (1 Cor. 12:4–6). *Mortale*: mortal [sin]. *Secundum presentem iusticiam*: as against predestination; since these gifts can stand with mortal sin, they are graces freely given, not the grace that makes pleasing.

[^10]: **C.** *Libere vel coacte*: willingly or under compulsion, as the predestinate and the foreknown serve. *Ad dominum*: to "the same Lord" of v. 5; *ad Deum creantem*: to "the same God" of v. 6. *Per se existens*: existing by itself, a substance. *Habitudo*: "condition toward", a relation (row).

[^11]: **C.** *Illam voco ego caracterem*: Wyclif's own definition, the character as the assignment of office (*limitacio*, as above). *Quem*: agrees with *caracterem*. *Et nullum alium*: and no other character. **T.** B lacks *ministrandi*, and AA₁ lack *ego* (513.8–9). **R.** Side-notes 513.6, 513.12; A and B in the margin at 513.7: *Quid est caracter*.

[^12]: **C.** *Terminus magistralis*: a term of the masters, of the schools rather than of scripture (row). *Ut plures loquentes*: the verb is understood, "as the majority [do]". *Caracter in actu*: the character answering to an office actually performed; *alius caracter*: the standing power. *Confuse*: "indistinctly", without determination to any one act (row). *Quoad speciem terminantis*: as to the species of the act in which the power terminates.

[^13]: **S.** Chrysostom, "first homily", on the seven churches (of the Apocalypse); Loserth gives no reference; not located (LEDGER E21f-1). **C.** *Quod sacerdos celebrat*: so printed, though the one who ordains is the bishop. *Subiectatum in ordinante*: having its subject in the one ordaining. *Tocius sacerdotis ecclesie*: taken as Christ, the whole priest of the church; it may mean every priest of the church. *Tantum septem … tantum unum*: the first *tantum* sits awkwardly ("only seven" and yet "only one"). **R.** Side-note 513.22; A in the margin at 514.1: *Crisostomus*.

[^14]: **C.** *Primo et immediate in sacerdotem*: ordained priest directly, without the lower orders (cf. 503.2–3). *Quod est ordo*: taken as "which is order", the sevenfold power; it may instead mean "by the very fact that [this] is order". *Univoce*: in the same sense as other priests. *Graduatus*: one who holds a grade of order, not an academic graduate. **T.** B *quamcunque pem* (514.7–8), so read in the OCR of the page foot. **R.** Side-note 514.1; A in the margin at 514.1: *Prima conclusio*.

[^15]: **C.** *Partes integrales … potestative*: the potestative parts may be called integral parts. *Pars senarius*: taken as *senarii*, "of six". The aliquot parts of six are one, two and three, which add to six. *Per impossibile*: by an impossible supposition. *Conficiendi … absolvendi*: of consecrating [the eucharist] and of absolving. *Si non equivoce*: except equivocally. The sentence runs on at 514.21.

[^16]: **C.** *Sicut res sacramenti ordinis*: as the orders' characters are bound into one, so the realities (the graces) of the several sacraments entail one another. **T.** B *consequentur* (514.22). **R.** Side-note 514.20; A in the margin at 514.20: *Secunda conclusio*.

[^17]: **S.** *Baptismo flaminis*: the baptism of the Spirit, as against that of water; cf. Wyclif, *Trialogus*, p. 285 (Loserth, 514.24; LEDGER E21f-2). **C.** The six realities answer to baptism, confirmation, matrimony (*sponsari capiti ecclesie*), penance, the eucharist and extreme unction, with order the seventh. *Ordo per accidens*: an order (a sequence) by accident, set by the church's institution. **T.** AA₁ *fructum finem* (514.26); B *gracie confirmate* for *consummate* (514.27).

[^18]: **C.** *Appreciando*: with *intendimus*, "in valuing [them]". *Intendimus*: "we attend", of purpose, not the scholastic "maintain". The sentence continues at 515.1. **R.** Side-note 514.31.
