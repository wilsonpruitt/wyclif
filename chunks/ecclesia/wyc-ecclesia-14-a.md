# *De ecclesia*, chapter 14, part 1: the endowment of the Roman church; Christ held no civil lordship

## ¶1 · 299.10–300.6

> [p. 299] Antequam respondeam ad magistrales obiectus, sunt duo dubia breviter transcurrenda. Primo, quomodo stat cum fide scripture dotacio Romane ecclesie. Videtur enim ex multis dictis superius expresse innui quod non sine gravi peccato altrinsecus potest tale privilegium temporale contra Christi privilegia introduci. Sed videtur probabile (ut alias sepe dixi) quod illa dotacio sicut et particularium ecclesiarum possunt sic racionabiliter colorari, quod seculares domini videntes quod vita exproprietaria qualem Christus et sui apostoli vixerant est seculari ac civili conversacione perfeccior, et sic ad exonerandum eos, ad accumulandum eis meritum, ad multiplicandum milites Christi qui distrahant mundum a vita mundana, cum talis sit ad multiplicandum religionem Christi habilior, dederunt titulo elemosine suum seculare dominium, non ut sacerdotes Christi civiliter aut seculariter dominentur, sed ut servantes humilitatem, paupertatem et penalitatem priorem fideliter ministrent pauperibus elemosinas ad hoc datas, ut vel sic distraheretur mundus a conversacione civili et ritu quem [p. 300] ante Christum observaverant mundiales. Nec credo quod ista condicione dimissa nec secularium dotacio nec cleri Christi admissio cum religione Christi sit compossibiliter sustinenda; talis autem dotacio, sonans in puram et perpetuam elemosinam, utrimque est tota trinitate auctorizabilis et fundabilis in scriptura.

Before I reply to the master's [objections]{g:objectum}, two doubts are to be run through briefly.[^1] First, how the [endowment]{g:dotacio} of the Roman [church]{g:ecclesia} stands with the faith of scripture. For from many things said above it seems to be expressly implied that such a temporal [privilege]{g:privilegium} cannot be brought in against Christ's privileges without grave sin on either side. But it seems probable (as I have often said elsewhere) that that endowment, as also that of particular churches, can [be given colour]{g:colorare} reasonably in this way:[^2] that secular [lords]{g:dominus}, seeing that the [propertyless]{g:exproprietarii} life such as Christ and his apostles had lived is more perfect than a secular and civil [manner of life]{g:conversacio}, and so, to unburden them, to heap up merit for them, to multiply the [soldiers]{g:milites} of Christ, who would draw the world away from worldly life (since such [a life] is more apt for multiplying the religion of Christ), gave their secular [lordship]{g:dominium} by [title]{g:titulus} of [alms]{g:elemosina},[^3] not that the [priests]{g:sacerdos} of Christ should [exercise lordship]{g:dominari} civilly or secularly, but that, keeping their former humility, poverty and [hardship]{g:penalitas}, they should faithfully minister to the poor the alms given for this, so that at least in this way the world might be drawn away from the civil manner of life and the rite which [300] worldly men had observed before Christ. Nor do I believe that, once this condition is dropped, either the endowment by [seculars]{g:secularis} or its acceptance by the [clergy]{g:clerus} of Christ can be [compossibly]{g:compossibilis} upheld with the religion of Christ; but such endowment, sounding in pure and [perpetual alms]{g:elemosina perpetua}, is on both sides authorizable by the whole Trinity and capable of being founded in scripture.[^4]

## ¶2 · 300.7–300.17

> [p. 300] Sed hic recalcitrant moderni prepositi, dicentes quod licet eis civiliter ac seculariter dominari, et quod secularis dominacio fuit iure poli debita Christi vicariis, cum Christus dominatus fuit seculariter toti mundo. Unde (ut inquiunt) Constantinus non dotavit ecclesiam, sed illud dominium quod ipse ac predecessores sic occuparunt tyrannice, humiliter et debite diviserunt, unde Christus, Petrus, Clemens et alii precedentes beatum Silvestrum habuerunt ius plenum ad totum imperium, sed non erant in possessione ante concessionem humilem Constantini.

But here the modern [superiors]{g:prepositi} kick back, saying that it is lawful for them to exercise lordship civilly and secularly, and that secular [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio} was due to Christ's [vicars]{g:vicarius Christi} by the law of heaven, since Christ exercised lordship secularly over the whole world.[^5] Hence (as they say) Constantine did not endow the church; but the lordship which he and his predecessors had thus occupied tyrannically they humbly and duly divided; hence Christ, Peter, Clement and the others who preceded blessed Silvester had full [right]{g:ius} to the whole empire, but were not in possession before the humble [grant]{g:concessio} of Constantine.[^6]

## ¶3 · 300.18–302.2

> [p. 300] Et inter omnes hereses dissipantes religionem christianam et fidem scripture hec est una precipua. Primo, inquam, claudicat in hoc quod dicit Christum habuisse ius ad dominandum civiliter, cum conversacione et sermone secundum testimonia sanctorum doctorum exponencium fidem evangelii docebat oppositum. Ipse enim dicit Luce, IX°, 58: Vulpes foveas habent et volucres celi nidos, filius autem hominis non habet ubi caput suum reclinet. Super quo dicit venerabilis Beda super Lucam lib. II°, c. IV°: Quid me propter divicias et seculi lucra cupis sequi, cum tante sim paupertatis ut nec hospiciolum quidem habeam et non meo utar tecto? Et ad istum sensum illud exponitur a beato Jeronymo nec contrarie ab aliquo sancto (ut alias explanavi). [p. 301] Item, si Christus et sui apostoli habuissent ius ad civiliter dominandum, et faciliter ex oblacione mundi potentum possent fuisse positi in possessione pacifica, ut patet de Evangelista et aliis sanctis: sequitur quod ex desidia fuissent valde culpabiles, cum possent ita faciliter quesivisse ius sibi et sue ecclesie et excusasse sibi iniuriantes a sua intrusione. Hoc autem est contra evangelium Luce XXII°, ubi decidens questionem de maioritate inter apostolos dicit quod opposito modo se habet ad ritum gentilem, cum maioritas inter eos attenditur penes maioritatem servicii ex humilitate maiori. Ex quo Iª Petri V°, 3, capit ipse pro principio quod sui clerici non sint dominantes in clero. Item, illud est expresse contra iura canonica, cum sub auctoritate Augustini, distinccione VIIIª canon sic loquitur: Quo iure defendis villas ecclesie divino vel humano? Divinum ius in divinis scripturis habemus, humanum ius in legibus regum. Unde quisquis possidet quod possidet? Nonne jure humano? Nam iure divino domini est terra et plenitudo eius. Pauperes et divites una terra supportat. Iure ergo humano dicitur: Villa hec mea est. Tolle, inquit, iura imperatoris et quis audet dicere: Villa hec mea est, meus est iste servus, mea est hec domus? Si autem ut teneantur ista ab hominibus, regum iura fecerunt, vultis reticeamus leges ut gaudeatis? Unde infra, XCVIª distinccione, Constantinus, incorporat ecclesia cartam cesaris sub forma qua dotavit ecclesiam. Cum ergo lege scripture, lege sanctorum doctorum et iuris canonici non sit fundabilis secularis dominacio cleri [p. 302] Christi, qua temeritate sompniarent discoli istam blasphemiam?

And among all the heresies that scatter the christian religion and the faith of scripture, this is one of the chief. First, I say, it limps in this, that it says Christ had a right to exercise lordship civilly, since in manner of life and in speech, according to the testimonies of the holy doctors who expound the faith of the gospel, he taught the opposite. For he himself says in Luke 9:58: "The foxes have holes, and the birds of the air nests: but the Son of man hath not where to lay his head." On which the venerable Bede says, on Luke, book II, chapter 4: "Why do you desire to follow me for the sake of riches and the gains of the world, when I am of such poverty that I have not even a little lodging and use no roof of my own?"[^7] And it is expounded in this sense by blessed Jerome, and not otherwise by any saint (as I have explained elsewhere).[^8] [301] Again, if Christ and his apostles had had a right to exercise lordship civilly, and could easily have been put in peaceful possession by the offering of the mighty of the world, as is clear of the Evangelist and other saints, [it follows]{g:sequitur} that they would have been very culpable through sloth, since they could so easily have sought the right for themselves and for their church, and have excused those who wronged them from their intrusion.[^9] But this is against the gospel, Luke 22, where, deciding the question of [greatness]{g:majoritas} among the apostles, he says that it stands with them in the opposite way to the gentile rite, since greatness among them is reckoned by greatness of [service]{g:servicium} out of greater humility.[^10] From this, in 1 Peter 5:3, he takes it as a principle that his clerics should not be "lording it over the clergy." Again, that is expressly against the canon laws, since, under the authority of Augustine, the canon in distinction 8 speaks thus: "By what right do you defend the estates of the church, divine or human? The divine right we have in the divine scriptures, the human right in the laws of kings. Whence does each one possess what he possesses? Is it not by human right? For by divine right 'the earth is the Lord's and the fulness thereof.' The poor and the rich one earth supports. By human right, therefore, it is said: This estate is mine. Take away, he says, the rights of the emperor, and who dares to say: This estate is mine, this servant is mine, this house is mine? But if the rights of kings have brought it about that these things are held by men, do you want us to keep silent about the laws, so that you may rejoice?"[^11] Hence below, in distinction 96, *Constantinus*, the church incorporates the charter of Caesar in the form in which he endowed the church.[^12] Since therefore by the law of scripture, by the law of the holy doctors and of canon law, the secular exercise of lordship by the clergy [302] of Christ cannot be founded, with what rashness would the [froward]{g:discoli} dream up this blasphemy?

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 299.10: Two doubts to be solved: 1. Whether the endowment of the Roman Church is consistent with the faith.
- 299.22: Probably in its first intention it was directed to help the clergy to minister more effectually;
- 300.1: and otherwise it is certainly not consistent with Christ's religion,
- 300.7: Modern prelates say that Christ was ruler over the world and they inherit his rights (as vicars).
- 300.18: This is a great heresy:
- 300.21: I. as contrary to Christ's teaching;
- 301.1: He and his apostles refused all civil power.
- 301.15: 2. It is against the canons.

## Notes

[^1]: **C/R.** *Magistrales obiectus*: the objections of a master (a doctor in theology) that Wyclif has undertaken to answer; A in marg. (299.16) marks the coming *Responsio*. A in marg. (299.10): *Materia pro dotacione ecclesie*.
[^2]: **C.** *Colorari*: to give colour, a plausible ground, in the period sense ("colour of title"), not "pretext". *Possunt* is plural because the endowments of the particular churches are counted with that of Rome.
[^3]: **C.** *Ad exonerandum eos … eis*: most likely the lords themselves (*eos* for *se*), whom the gift unburdens and enriches with merit; possibly the clergy. *Milites Christi* are the soldiers of Christ (2 Tim. 2:3), not the knightly estate.
[^4]: **C.** *Sonans in puram et perpetuam elemosinam*: the formula of tenure in free alms (frankalmoign). *Cleri Christi admissio*: the clergy's admitting, i.e. accepting, the endowment; *utrimque*, on the side of giver and receiver.
[^5]: **C.** *Ius poli*, "the law of heaven", against *ius fori*, the law of the court; the pair is Augustine's, *Sermo* 355.4.5 (PL 39, 1572: *jure fori, non jure poli*) (LEDGER E14a-1). The side-note (300.7) reads *prepositi* as "prelates".
[^6]: **T/C.** *Diviserunt* ("divided", with Constantine and his predecessors as subject) is printed without variant; the sense may want *dimiserunt* ("gave up"). Translated as printed (see the report). On the claim, Constantine merely restored to the pope what Christ already held by right.
[^7]: **S/T.** Luke 9:58 (DR); the manuscripts read *Luce VIII* (Loserth 300.24). Bede, *In Lucae evangelium expositio* III, on Luke 9:58 (PL 92, 460), cited by Wyclif as book II, chapter 4 (Loserth 300.27: *Opp.* V, 262; LEDGER E14a-2); Bede takes the words from Jerome, *In Matth.* I, on 8:20 (PL 26, 52–53; LEDGER E14a-3). A has *ut nec* followed by a gap; A₁ *meum hospiciolum* (300.29).
[^8]: **S.** Jerome on the same saying (Matt. 8:20), presumably his commentary on Matthew, book I: from memory, to be checked (LEDGER E14a-3).
[^9]: **C.** *De Evangelista*: which evangelist was offered possession by the mighty is not said; to be identified (LEDGER E14a-4). *Excusasse … a sua intrusione*: to have cleared those who wronged them of their intrusion (by recovering the right).
[^10]: **S.** Luke 22:24–27, paraphrased; 22:25 (DR) "The kings of the Gentiles lord it over them." The next sentence quotes 1 Pet. 5:3 (DR); *ipse* is Peter.
[^11]: **S/T.** Decretum D. 8 c. 1 (*Quo iure*), from Augustine, *Tract. in Ioh.* 6.25: Loserth (301.16), modern form ours (LEDGER E14a-5). Within it Ps. 23:1. A A₁ *scriptum habemus*, B *scriptis*, Loserth corrects from the canon (301.19); A A₁ *possideret* (301.20). *Reticeamus* ("keep silent about") is printed without note; Augustine and Gratian read *recitemus* ("recite") (LEDGER E14a-6). Translated as printed.
[^12]: **S/C.** Decretum D. 96 c. 14 (*Constantinus*), the Donation of Constantine: Loserth (301.28), modern form ours (LEDGER E14a-7). *Ecclesia* is taken as the subject (the church in its canon law); it may be a scar for *ecclesie*.
