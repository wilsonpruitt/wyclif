# *De potestate pape*, chapter 10, part 10: the king charged with heresy, and Lincoln on the cardinals

## ¶1 · 253.28–255.20

> [p. 253] Consolacio autem a Christo est in operando in causa ista fideliter et constanter, cum Matth. X, 25 dicat: Si patremfamilias Belzebub vocaverunt, quanto magis domesticos eius? Ideo ibant apostoli gaudentes quod digni habiti sunt pro nomine Jhesu contumeliam pati. Sed et consolacio quedam mundana exoritur, quod ad tantum insane prorumpunt satrape in suis sermonibus, quod imponunt [p. 254] principalem heresim regi suo. Rex, inquiunt, care conduxit stipendiarios contra sanctam ecclesiam; ex quo patenter implicant quod rex eorum sit heresiarcha non obstante ignorancia puerili. Apostolus enim non fuit excusatus a blasfemia, non obstante quod per ignoranciam stulte fecit, ut prima Timothei primo. Videtur itaque nimis magna presumpcio sic imponere heresim toti consilio tanti regni; predicarent enim iuxta suam opinionem atque scienciam de periculosiori heresi adversancium pape suo, et si umquam valet sciencia decretiste, valet ad cerciorandum ecclesiam in hac parte. Dicit enim quod ad illos singulariter pertinet cognoscere disposicionem veri pape et rectitudinem sue consuetudinis cum aliis annexis difficultatibus, sicut ad eos pertinet cognoscere quiditatem heresis et penam sui subditi. Doceant igitur in istis populum de fraude, quia (ut inquiunt) sine fide istius articuli non est salus; ostendant igitur artem suam in isto fideliter, quia maior et famosior est multitudo decretistarum eis contraria in hac parte et specialiter cuneus pseudocardinalium, quibus videbatur regi ecclesie tempore Gregorii excedentis decalogum. Ipsi enim in tradicionibus humanis periti, qui melius noverunt qualitatem eleccionis quam ipsimet celebrarunt, testantur nobis cartis et nunciis, quod eleccio eorum non fuit legitima, et sic (ut inquiunt) noster Urbanus, quem colimus infideliter, non est papa. Nostri autem theologi dicunt primo, quod licet eleccio eorum, ut vere creditur, fuerit maledicta, eleccio tamen passiva Urbani nostri ex eleccione activa Dei est valida, dum tamen fiat ab ipso electo in veris factis papalibus [p. 255] recompensa. Dicunt secundo quod nedum catholice sed melius quam modo conversaretur fidelis ecclesia quocumque papa cesareo subducto, quia Act. IV, 12 dicitur, quod non est aliud nomen sub celo, in quo oportet nos salvos fieri preter nomen domini Jhesu Christi. Et dicunt tercio quod pensando virtutum insignia probabiliter reputant Urbanum nostrum verum papam, cum omnes sancti episcopi erant pape, et Robertum non reputant explicite papam vel membrum sancte ecclesie, quia in De Verbis Domini sermone LIII sic scribitur: Caput humile et membrum superbum absit; non vult esse de corpore capitis Christi, quia amat superbiam. Unde cum secundum eundem Augustinum sermone L Christus vere loquens de quibuscumque pastoribus, cum in se habet omnes pastores bonos vere dicit: Ego sum unus, mecum omnes in unitate unum sunt. Prescitus autem cum sit membrum dyaboli, non compaginatur ordinate huic capiti.

And consolation from Christ lies in working in this cause faithfully and constantly, since Matt. 10:25 says: "If they have called the good man of the house Beelzebub, how much more them of his household?" Therefore the apostles went "rejoicing that they were accounted worthy to suffer reproach for the name of Jesus."[^1] But a certain worldly consolation also arises, in that the [satraps]{g:satrapa} break out so madly in their sermons that they impute [254] principal heresy to their king. "The king," they say, "has dearly hired mercenaries against holy [church]{g:ecclesia}"; from which they plainly imply that their king is a heresiarch, notwithstanding his boyish ignorance.[^2] For the apostle was not excused from blasphemy, notwithstanding that he acted foolishly through ignorance, as in 1 Timothy 1.[^3] It seems, then, [exceedingly]{g:nimis} great presumption so to impute heresy to the whole council of so great a [kingdom]{g:regnum}; for they should preach, according to their [opinion]{g:opinio} and knowledge, about the more dangerous heresy of those opposing their pope; and if the knowledge of a decretist is ever worth anything, it is worth it for certifying the church in this matter. For it says that it pertains to them uniquely to know the disposition of the true [pope]{g:papa} and the rightness of his [custom]{g:consuetudo}, with the other difficulties annexed, just as it pertains to them to know the [quiddity]{g:quiditas} of heresy and the penalty of their subject. Let them therefore teach the people in these things about the fraud, since (as they say) without faith in this article there is no salvation; let them therefore show their art faithfully in this, since the multitude of [decretists]{g:decretiste} contrary to them in this matter is greater and [better known]{g:famosior}, and especially the wedge of [pseudo-cardinals]{g:pseudocardinales}, by whom the church seemed to be ruled in the time of Gregory, who exceeded the decalogue.[^4] For they, skilled in human traditions, who knew best the quality of the election that they themselves conducted, testify to us by charters and messengers that their election was not lawful, and so (as they say) our Urban, whom we faithlessly honour, is not pope.[^5] But our theologians say, first, that although their election, as is truly believed, was accursed, yet the passive election of our Urban, from the active election of God, is valid, provided that, in true papal deeds, it is [255] repaid by the one elected himself.[^6] They say, second, that the faithful church would live not only in a catholic manner but better than now with any caesarean pope whatever withdrawn, since Acts 4:12 says that "there is no other name under heaven" in which we must be saved, except the name of the Lord Jesus Christ.[^7] And they say, third, that, weighing the marks of the virtues, they reckon [with probability]{g:probabiliter} that our Urban is the true pope, since all holy bishops were popes, and they do not reckon Robert explicitly pope or a [member]{g:membrum} of holy church, since in *On the Words of the Lord*, sermon 53, it is written thus: "Far be it [that there be] a humble [head]{g:caput} and a proud member; he does not wish to be of the body of the head, Christ, because he loves pride."[^8] Hence since, according to the same Augustine in sermon 50, Christ, speaking truly of all pastors whatever, since he has in himself all good pastors, truly says: "I am one; with me all are one in unity."[^9] And [the foreknown]{g:prescitus} man, since he is a member of the devil, is not fitted in due order to this head.[^10]

## ¶2 · 255.21–256.2

> [p. 255] Istam autem negativam dico opinando, sicut secta altera possit catholice dicere conformiter de Urbano. Ideo oportet, quod opera iusta dent fidem papatus, cum nichil sit fallacius quam humana eleccio et tradicio phariseica cum suo iudicio. Nam easdem tradiciones habet secta Roberti et nostra, et tamen in iudicio quo maxime servirent ecclesie sunt directe opposite, et indubie neutra pars noscit evidenciam vie sue; theologus autem neutram sectam dampnat, sed instar libertatis christianorum primeve ecclesie dicit quod utraque pars potest viare satis catholice, et sive uter eorum credat explicite, et quicumque [p. 256] theologus dixerit huius contrarium, a facultate sua degenerat.

And this negative I state as an opinion, just as the other sect could say the like of Urban in a catholic manner. Therefore it must be just works that give credence to a papacy, since nothing is more deceptive than human election and pharisaic tradition with its judgement.[^11] For the sect of Robert and ours have the same traditions, and yet in the judgement in which they would most serve the church they are directly opposed, and without doubt neither party knows the [evidence]{g:evidencia} for its way; but the theologian condemns neither sect, but, after the pattern of the freedom of the Christians of the primitive church, says that each party can [be a wayfarer]{g:viator} catholicly enough, whether or not either of them believes explicitly; and whatever [256] theologian says the contrary of this degenerates from his faculty.[^12]

## ¶3 · 256.3–257.10

> [p. 256] Tercio replicatur ex dicto Lynconiensis Epistola XXXVI, ubi sic loquitur: Sicut super mundi cardines mundus innititur eorumque fulcimine supportatur, sic super sancte Romane ecclesie cardinales orbis terrarum qui non commovebitur, id est universalis ecclesia que nullis commocionibus a stabilitate fidei deicitur, fixe innititur et ab eisdem forte et firmiter sustinetur. Ipsi enim sunt quos vocat psalmista fundamenta orbis terrarum, ipsi sunt de quibus in Yob scribitur: Sub quo curvantur qui portant orbem; dum enim curas ecclesie oneraque cunctorum tolerant, quasi sibi superimpositum orbem portant. Et sicut in mundo visibili sol iste conspicuus suo preeminenti lumine mundi tenebras purgat singulariterque mundum illustrat motuque suo ordinatissimo ceteros motus corporales naturales ordinat et regulat, sic in orbem ecclesie summus pontifex vicem solis obtinet precellenti luce doctrine et eximiorum operum, mundum purgans ab errorum tenebris, ad veritatis cognicionem singulari prerogativa eundem illuminans, omnes eciam motus accionum in universali ecclesia disposicione sua ordinans, regulans et gubernans. Quemadmodum igitur mundi status, decor et ordo post mundi conditorem angelicosque spiritus ad conditoris nutum administratorios debent subesse soli visibili mundique cardinibus, sic ut veraciter senciunt qui que sursum sunt sapiunt, post mundi conditorem et redemptorem curiamque celestem ex spiritibus beatis angelorum et sanctorum [p. 257] adunatam, status, decor et ordo universalis ecclesie debet subesse suo soli suisque cardinibus, hoc est, summo pontifici sibique assistentibus cardinalibus. Ideoque sancte Romane ecclesie debetur ab universis ecclesie filiis devotissima obediencia, honoratissima reverencia, id est, ferventissimus amor, subiectissimus timor. Hec et hiis similia ab eis debentur obligacius et forcius, qui pro sublimitate gradus ecclesiastici fastigio ecclesie, id est, summo pontifici et cardinalibus adherent proximius.

Thirdly, [it is rejoined]{g:replicatur}, from the saying of Lincoln in Letter 36, where he speaks thus:[^13] "Just as the world rests upon the hinges of the world and is supported by their prop, so upon the cardinals of the holy Roman church 'the world which shall not be moved', that is, the universal church, which is cast down from the stability of faith by no commotions, rests fixedly, and is sustained by them strongly and firmly.[^14] For they are those whom the psalmist calls 'the foundations of the world'; they are those of whom it is written in Job: 'Under whom they stoop that bear up the world'; for while they bear the cares of the church and the burdens of all, they carry, as it were, the world laid upon them.[^15] And just as in the visible world this conspicuous sun by its [preeminent]{g:preeminencia} light purges the darkness of the world and singularly illumines the world, and by its most ordered motion orders and regulates the other natural bodily motions, so in the orb of the church the highest pontiff holds the place of the sun, by the surpassing light of [doctrine]{g:doctrina} and of excellent works, purging the world from the darkness of errors, illuminating it with a singular prerogative to the knowledge of truth, ordering, regulating and governing also all the motions of actions in the universal church by his disposition. As, therefore, the [state]{g:status}, beauty and order of the world, after the world's maker and the angelic spirits ministering at the maker's nod, ought to be subject to the visible sun and the hinges of the world, so, as those truly perceive who 'mind the things that are above', after the maker and redeemer of the world and the heavenly court gathered [257] from the blessed spirits of angels and saints, the state, beauty and order of the universal church ought to be subject to its sun and its cardinals, that is, to the highest pontiff and the cardinals assisting him.[^16] And therefore to the holy Roman church is owed, by all the sons of the church, most devoted obedience, most honoured reverence, that is, most fervent love, most subject fear. These things, and things like them, are owed more bindingly and more strongly by those who, for the sublimity of their ecclesiastical rank, cling more closely to the summit of the church, that is, to the highest pontiff and the cardinals."

## ¶4 · 257.11–257.13

> [p. 257] Talia sunt multa dicta Lyncolniensis et aliorum doctorum, que ex defectu intelligencie inferiores infatuant et superiores faciunt insanire.

Many sayings of Lincoln and of other doctors are of this kind, which, through a defect of understanding, make fools of inferiors and make superiors go mad.

## ¶5 · 257.14–257.18

> [p. 257] Unde pro intentu dictorum huius sancti notandum, primo quod describitur in dictis qualis debet esse summus pontifex cum cardinalibus, cum, ut dicit in sermone suo ad papam, ipse gerit in militante ecclesia vicem Christi.

Hence, for the intent of the sayings of this saint, it is to be noted, first, that these sayings describe what kind of man the highest pontiff with the cardinals ought to be, since, as he says in his sermon to the pope, he bears in the [church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans} the place of Christ.[^17]

## ¶6 · 257.19–257.21

> [p. 257] Ex quo patet quod oportet verum papam conversari Christo similius et aliter est ex mendacio dolosissimus Antichristus.

From which it is clear that a true pope must live more like Christ, and otherwise he is, by [his] lie, a most deceitful Antichrist.

## ¶7 · 257.22–257.32

> [p. 257] Secundo notandum quod iste sanctus intelligit predicta re putative vel condicionaliter, et non assertive competere pape secum viventi, quia aliter foret nimis sompniferus contradictorius sibi ipsi. Ipse enim obedienter pape tamquam Antichristo restitit ad tribunal Christi ab eius iudicio appellando et post mortem eundem usque ad mortem quasi cuspide baculi vulneravit, ut dixi VIII capitulo. Et patet quod nichil insanius quam credere quod eo ipso quo quis fungitur vice pape iste condiciones sibi conveniunt.

Secondly, it is to be noted that this saint understands the foregoing to belong to the pope living in his time by estimation, or conditionally, and not assertively; for otherwise he would be exceedingly dreamy, contradictory to himself.[^18] For he obediently resisted the pope as Antichrist, appealing from his judgement to the tribunal of Christ, and after [his own] death wounded the same [pope] even to death, as it were with the point of his staff, as I said in chapter 8.[^19] And it is clear that nothing is more insane than to believe that, by the very fact that someone discharges the office of pope, these conditions belong to him.

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 254.1: Prelates go so far as to charge the king with heresy.
- 254.28: The cardinals elected Urban and then declared that his election was not lawful.
- 255.6: We think Urban is probably a true pope, and that Robert is not even a member of the Church.
- 255.21: Others may think the Opposite.
- 257.14: Grosseteste's claims for the pope are conditional on his being what he ought to be.

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** Acts 5:41; Wyclif cites only the phrase from *gaudentes*, and gives no reference.
[^2]: **R.** The king is Richard II, born 1367, hence "boyish ignorance"; the occasion of the charge about "mercenaries" has not been identified (LEDGER P10j-1). **G.** *satrape*: Wyclif's derogatory word for the officials of the church courts, as at its first use in this work.
[^3]: **S.** 1 Tim. 1:13, "who before was a blasphemer … but I obtained the mercy of God, because I did it ignorantly"; Wyclif turns the verse to say that ignorance did not clear Paul of the name of blasphemer.
[^4]: **T.** *cuneus* and *pseudocardinalium* are Loserth's; the manuscripts read *timens* and *pseudocarnalium* (B *carnalia*) (254.23). The print governs. **C.** *quibus videbatur regi ecclesie*: read as *regi* passive with *ecclesie* for *ecclesia*, "by whom the church seemed to be ruled"; the alternative, "to whom it seemed good to rule the church", is possible. Gregory: Gregory XI (see 252.1).
[^5]: **R.** The cardinals who elected Urban VI in April 1378 declared the election invalid in August 1378 (Anagni) and elected Robert of Geneva at Fondi (Walsingham, *Historia Anglicana* I, 369, 393; LEDGER P10j-2). **T.** B reads *de tradicionibus* for *in tradicionibus* (254.25).
[^6]: **C.** "Passive election", Urban's being elected; "active election", God's electing him: the cardinals' act may be void and God's choice still stand, provided Urban repays it in deeds.
[^7]: **S.** Acts 4:12; the Vulgate reads *nec enim aliud nomen est sub caelo datum hominibus, in quo oporteat nos salvos fieri* (DR "For there is no other name under heaven given to men, whereby we must be saved"). Wyclif's version drops *datum hominibus* and adds *preter nomen domini Jhesu Christi*; his Latin is translated, the words common to both in DR's wording.
[^8]: **S.** Augustine, *De verbis Domini*, sermon 53, as Wyclif cites it, i.e. *Sermo* 354.9 (*Ad continentes*), PL 39, 1568: *Caput humile, et membrum superbum? Absit. Non vult esse de corpore capitis humilis, qui amat superbiam* (Loserth: Opera, tom. V, col. 1375) (LEDGER P10j-3). **C.** *cum omnes sancti episcopi erant pape*: *papa*, "father", was once the title of every bishop. **R.** A notes in the margin at 255.12: *Prescitus non est membrum*.
[^9]: **S.** Augustine, *Sermo* 138.5, PL 38, 765 (Wyclif's "sermon 50" is an older numbering; Loserth: ibid., p. 671), on the pastors: *Ego sum, unus sum, mecum omnes in unitate unum sunt* (LEDGER P10j-4); cf. John 10:16. **C.** The sentence is an anacoluthon: *cum … Christus … vere dicit* has no main clause; the next sentence supplies the conclusion.
[^10]: **G.** *Prescitus*: "the foreknown", those God foreknows will be damned (glossed at the first occurrence in this work).
[^11]: **C.** *fidem papatus*: "credence to a papacy", i.e. ground for believing a man pope.
[^12]: **C.** *et sive uter eorum credat explicite*: an incomplete *sive* clause, taken as "whether or not either of them believes explicitly" [in his pope]; the faculty is the faculty of theology.
[^13]: **S.** Robert Grosseteste, *Epistolae*, ed. Luard, letter 36, to the cardinal Giles, p. 125 (Loserth) (LEDGER P10j-5). **R.** Both manuscripts note in the margin at 256.3: *Argumentum pro papa*, and at 256.6: *Cardinalium laus per Lincolniensem*. **T.** B omits *sicut super mundi* (256.4–5).
[^14]: **C.** Grosseteste's figure turns on *cardines* "hinges" (the poles on which the world turns) and *cardinales* "cardinals". **S.** "The world which shall not be moved": Ps. 92:1 (Vulgate numbering; also Ps. 95:10).
[^15]: **S.** Ps. 17:16 and Job 9:13 (Loserth).
[^16]: **S.** "Mind the things that are above": Col. 3:2; the "ministering spirits": Heb. 1:14. **T.** Loserth's foot to 257.2 reads *Recte: debet se soli suo* (so the plate) against the print's *debet subesse suo soli*; there is no such note at 256.28–29. The print is translated in both places.
[^17]: **S.** Grosseteste's sermon before Innocent IV at Lyon (1250; his visit to the curia, Matthew Paris, *Chronica majora* V, 96–98) (LEDGER P10j-7). **R.** Both manuscripts note in the margin at 257.14: *Papa qualis debet esse*.
[^18]: **C.** *sompniferus contradictorius*: two predicates with no connective; a lost *et* is possible. *secum viventi*: "living with him", i.e. contemporary. **R.** A notes in the margin at 257.22: *Cave*.
[^19]: **R.** Grosseteste's appeal against Innocent IV, and the story (Matthew Paris) that after his death he appeared to the pope and struck him with the point of his pastoral staff, from which the pope died (Higden, *Polychronicon* VIII, 240–42, as at chapter 8; Matthew Paris, *Chronica majora* V, 429–30, 470–71) (LEDGER P10j-8). **C.** *post mortem* is taken as Grosseteste's own death.
