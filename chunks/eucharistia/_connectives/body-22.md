# sed/autem extract, wave 22: chunks 08-e 08-f 08-g

### 08-e ¶1 · 247.29–249.11  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 247] Quantum ad materiam de augmentacione et diminucione, dictum est alibi quomodo in homine ad unitatem spiritus qui est persona hominis non est [p. 248] difficultas quomodo fit augmentacio per humidum cibale, generata parva forma in ipso, ut puta forma carnis, ossis vel talis partis simplicis ac composite, ymmo non obest animam vegetativam eciam animam sensitivam generari que subordinetur anime ultimate; nec valet quod illa forma sit pars quantitativa forme tocius, **sed** satis est quod sit forma subordinata forme tocius a qua ista parcialis substancia habeat quidditatem, sicut totum corpus habet quidditatem suam a forma tocius; de multiplicacione vero formarum tam animarum quam formarum mixtorum dictum est diffuse alibi, sicut de ordine augmentandi et quomodo non oportet formam ultimam maiorari vel minorari, licet forme parcium adveniant vel recedant. In lignis **autem** in quibus est anima vegetativa est super hec quedam diversitas, quoniam communiter est per processum temporis novum lignum; et illud successivum communiter augmentatur et diminuitur et illud successivum individuatur a suis elementis et tunc est maius nunc **autem** minus (ut hic supponitur tanquam alibi declaratum). Quoad auctoritatem Christi Math. XV patet quod exsufflandi sunt ut infidelissimi qui dicunt Jesum nostrum nescire philosophica in hac parte. Oportet enim ipsos cognoscere virtutem sermonis et sensum ad quem hec dixit ille, cui cuncta subserviunt; habet **autem** sermo Christi sic: Non intelligitis quod omne quod intrat in os in ventrem vadit et in secessum emittitur? que **autem** procedunt de ore, de corde exeunt et ea coinquinant hominem. Et narrat consequenter septem que coinquinant hominem; [p. 249] ubi patet primo quod Christus non reprobaret carenciam intellectus Petri cum suis sociis superaddendo eis expositivam sentenciam nisi foret subtilis, verissima et salubris; secundo patet quod primo loquitur de ore corporis correspondenter ad Judeos carnales qui ritus corporeos observabant; et tercio loquitur de ore mentis correspondenter ad patres legis nove qui debent per corpora immunda vel communia ut animalia et alia prohibita in lege veteri precavicionem a contagione peccati notare, ut docet Augustinus in libro contra Faustum.

ENGLISH: As to the matter of [augmentation]{g:augmentacio} and diminution, it has been said elsewhere[^1] how in a man, with regard to the unity of the spirit, which is the man's [person]{g:persona}, there is no [248] difficulty in how augmentation comes about through [the moisture of food]{g:humidum-cibale}, a small form being generated in it, for instance the form of flesh, of bone, or of some such part, simple or composite; indeed, nothing prevents a vegetative soul, and even a sensitive soul, from being generated, to be subordinated to the final soul. Nor is it sound [to argue] that that form is a [quantitative part]{g:pars-quantitativa} of the form of the whole; it is enough that it be a form subordinated to the form of the whole, from which this partial [substance]{g:substancia} has its quiddity, as the whole body has its quiddity from the form of the whole.[^2] And of the multiplication of forms, both of souls and of the forms of mixed bodies, it has been said at length elsewhere, as also of the order of augmenting, and how the final form need not be made greater or less, although the forms of the parts come or go. But in trees, in which there is a vegetative soul, there is a certain difference beyond these, since commonly there is new wood in the course of time; and that [successive entity]{g:successivum} is commonly augmented and diminished, and that successive entity is individuated by its elements, and is then greater and now less (as is supposed here, as having been [made clear]{g:declarare} elsewhere). As to the authority of Christ, Matt. 15, it is clear that those who say that our Jesus did not know philosophical matters[^3] in this part are to be [blown out]{g:exsufflare} as the most [unbelieving]{g:infidelis}. For they must know the proper force of the words and the [meaning]{g:sensus} to which he said these things, he whom all things serve; and Christ's word runs thus: "Do you not understand, that whatsoever entereth into the mouth, goeth into the belly, and is cast out into the privy? But the things which proceed out of the mouth, come forth from the heart, and those things defile a man" (Matt. 15:17–18). And he next relates seven things that defile a man;[^4] [249] where it is clear, first, that Christ would not reprove the lack of understanding of Peter with his companions, adding for them over and above an expository [teaching]{g:sentencia},[^5] unless it were subtle, most true and wholesome; second, it is clear that he first speaks of the mouth of the body, corresponding to the carnal Jews who observed bodily rites; and third, he speaks of the mouth of the mind, corresponding to the fathers of the new law, who ought, through bodies unclean or common, such as animals and other things forbidden in the old law, to note a [precaution]{g:precavicio} against the contagion of sin, as Augustine teaches in the book against Faustus.[^6]

### 08-e ¶2 · 249.12–249.26  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 249] Et tercio pro subtilitate sermonum Christi notandum primo quod Christus primo intelligit secundum distribucionem accomodam pertinentem quod omnis pars alimenti inmunda transiens per digestiones completas coinquinat hominem inquinamento corporali, cum purum alimenti separatum fuerit ab inpuro; ideo textus Marc. VIIᵐᵒ, 18, 19 habet sic: Non intelligitis, quia omne extrinsecus introiens in hominem non potest eum coinquinare, quia non introivit in cor eius **sed** in ventrem et secessum exit purgans omnes escas? Unde et Mattheus narrat hec septem de ore procedere, ut intelligamus os mentis. Et ponit exire cor interioris hominis quod est procedere extra limites racionis. Et patet sensus litteralis infringibilis veritatis.

ENGLISH: And third, for the subtlety of Christ's words, it is to be noted first that Christ first understands, by a fitting [accommodated distribution]{g:distribucio-accomoda},[^7] that every unclean part of food passing through the complete [digestions]{g:digestio} defiles a man with bodily defilement, once the pure of the food has been separated from the impure; therefore the text of Mark 7:18–19 runs thus: "Understand you not that every thing from without entering into a man cannot defile him: because it entered not into his heart, but goeth out into the belly and the privy, purging all meats?"[^8] Hence Matthew too relates that these seven proceed from the mouth, so that we understand the mouth of the mind.[^9] And he puts them as going out of the heart of the inner man, which is to proceed outside the [limits]{g:limites} of reason. And the literal sense, of unbreakable truth, is clear.

### 08-e ¶3 · 249.27–250.20  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 249] **Sed** est hoc verbum Christi gravidatum sensu morali multiplici. Primo notatur quod nemo maculatur mentaliter nisi macula originata fuerit a se ipso; secundo notatur quod carnalis observancia pro tempore [p. 250] legis veteris fuit licita **sed** nunc maculat, cum plene digesta transiit in secessum. Et tercio instruimur quod nullum corporale fetidum maculat mentem hominis nisi a puricia nature bone separatum fuerit et secundum racionem fetoris peccati per abusum innaturalem tanquam vomitus reassumptum. Mille alias subtilitates potest fidelis extrahere de hiis verbis Christi puris ab omni macula falsitatis. Non enim pertinuit sibi plus extense distribuere **sed** docere, ne sui sint occupati observanciis corporalis alimenti dimissa sollicitudine circa cibum anime spiritualem. Ideo dicit Apostolus Tym. primo: Omnia munda mundis. Et prima Thim. IVᵒ, 4: Omnis creatura Dei bona est et nichil reiciendum, quod cum graciarum accione percipitur, et Iᵃ Cor. Xᵒ, 25: Omne quod in macello venit manducate, nichil interrogantes propter conscienciam. Et hinc redarguendi sunt qui fingunt mendacia ut fidem katholicam quod signum corporis Christi quod est sacramentum blaspheme colatur et cultus signati corporis dominici remittatur.

ENGLISH: But this word of Christ is pregnant with a manifold moral sense. First, it is noted that no one is stained in mind unless the stain has originated from himself; second, it is noted that carnal observance for the time [250] of the old law was lawful, but now it stains, since, fully digested, it has passed into the privy. And third, we are instructed that nothing bodily and foul stains the mind of a man unless it has been separated from the purity of good nature[^10] and taken up again like vomit, through unnatural abuse, in the manner of the foulness of sin. A thousand other subtleties the faithful man can draw out of these words of Christ, pure of every stain of falsehood. For it did not pertain to him to distribute more extensively,[^11] but to teach that his own should not be occupied with observances of bodily food, care for the spiritual food of the soul being laid aside. Therefore the Apostle says, Tim. 1: "All things are clean to the clean."[^12] And 1 Tim. 4:4: "every creature of God is good, and nothing to be rejected that is received with thanksgiving"; and 1 Cor. 10:25: "Whatsoever is sold in the shambles, eat: asking no question for conscience' sake." And hence those are to be [refuted]{g:redarguere} who [feign]{g:fingere} lies as the catholic faith: that the [sign]{g:signum} of the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}, which is the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}, is to be [worshipped]{g:cultus} blasphemously, and the worship of the [Lord's body]{g:corpus-dominicum} that is signified [remitted]{g:remittere}.[^13]

### 08-e ¶4 · 250.21–251.21  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 250] **Sed** redeundo ad aliam sentenciam Doctoris Communis notandum primo quomodo discrepat ab aliis in materia de mensura; ponit enim multa que dicit alibi esse impossibilia et tunc sunt indubie explicite vel implicite heretica, ut ponit inpossibilitatem composicionis continui ex non quantis; possibilitatem **autem** immediacionis instancium videtur concedere. Scribitur enim ex parte Thome super IIᵒ Sentenciarum, distinccione IIIᵃ, questione XXVᵗᵃ: Dicendum, inquit, quod est assignare ultimum instans in quo angelus fuit bonus et primum in quo fuit malus; [p. 251] nec inter hec instancia fuit tempus medium, et quia tempus est formaliter numerus, non sequitur ipsum continuitas nisi ex parte motus. Unde cum vicissitudo affeccionum in angelo per quas est bonus et malus non est continua et ordinata ad aliquem motum continuum; numerus tamen earum dicetur tempus, quia secundum prius et posterius se habent, **sed** non est continuum. Unde inter duo instancia non necessario accipietur tempus medium, sicut nec inter duas unitates numerus. Istud **autem** dictum viciatum est; sophiste, inquam, Oxonienses dicerent quod vel bonitas angeli desinit esse per remocionem de presenti vel malicia sua cepit per remocionem de presenti, sic quod nec pro eodem instanti fuit sic bonus et malus, nec duo instancia erant immediata, quia per idem Petrus factus sanus immediate post egritudinem, cum sint contraria immediata, causaret immediacionem instancium, et per idem punctus continue fluens super duas lineas contiguas et generaliter mutari unius indivisibilis precedit per instans suum mutatum esse (ut patet alibi).

ENGLISH: But, returning to another [teaching]{g:sentencia} of the [Common Doctor]{g:doctor-communis}, it is to be noted first how he differs from others in the matter of measure. For he posits many things which he says elsewhere are impossible, and then they are without doubt explicitly or implicitly heretical: as he posits the impossibility of the composition of a continuum out of non-[quantified]{g:quantus} things, but seems to concede the possibility of [immediacy]{g:immediacio} of instants. For it is written on Thomas's part on the second [book] of the *Sentences*, distinction 3, question 25:[^14] "It must be said," he says, "that one may assign a last instant in which the angel was good and a first in which he was bad; [251] nor between these instants was there a middle time; and because time is formally number, continuity does not follow it except on the side of motion. Hence, although the alternation of affections in an angel, through which he is good and bad, is not continuous and ordered to some continuous motion, yet their number will be called time, because they stand according to before and after; but it is not continuous. Hence between two instants a middle time will not necessarily be taken, as neither [is] a number between two units." But this saying is faulty; the Oxford [sophists]{g:sophista}, I say, would say that either the angel's goodness ceases to be by removal of the present or his malice began by removal of the present,[^15] so that he was not thus good and bad for the same instant, nor were two instants [immediate]{g:immediatus}; for by the same token Peter, made healthy [without intermediary]{g:immediate} after sickness, since they are immediate contraries, would cause an immediacy of instants; and by the same token a point flowing continuously over two contiguous lines [would do so]; and generally the being-changed of one indivisible precedes, by its instant, its having-been-changed (as is clear elsewhere).[^16]

### 08-f ¶4 · 253.23–254.21  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 253] Similiter, una legio angelorum quam fides vocat unum (ut patet Luc. VIIIº, 3o) posset circulariter circumduci, et sic motus localis et tempus inde consequens foret vere continuum; ymmo videtur quod illa legio que est unus angelus foret quodammodo circularis, quia circumduccio foret circularis que requirit continuitatem et continua circularitas illius motus requirit continuitatem subiecti, quod est legio; [p. 254] patet igitur quod nec sensus nec racio indicat nunc continuitatem in mundo quin per idem et tunc foret continuitas utrobique. Nec dubium quin moto tunc continuo a sperico vel punctali foret tunc inmediacio motuum et instancium, sicut foret inmediacio situum indivisibilium; ex quibus componeretur utrobique continuum. Ymmo corpus cubicum haberet correspondenter ad eius punctalia acervum punctalium suppositum et undique circumductum, et omniquaque foret tanta continuitas et sic verum balbutivit hec via vocis sue ignara, quia fuit sibi ipsi manifeste contraria. Nunc itaque tenet Aristotelem nunc negat, nunc tenet leges hominum et nunc negat; nunc tenet decreta ecclesie et nunc negat, nec fundat se in sic variando in fide scripture, ideo vana est testificacio huius vie. Quantum ad dicta Aristotelis katholica de genere accidentis, patet quod ipsa renuit sine causa. **Sed** alia dicta Aristotelis de composicione continui ex non quantis, ut sentenciant Plato, Augustinus et Lincolniensis, ipsa concedit explicite ad favorem Aristotelis **sed** negat ipsa implicite.

ENGLISH: Likewise, one legion of angels, which faith calls one (as is clear, Luke 8:30),[^8] could be led round in a circle, and so the local motion and the time following from it would be [truly]{g:vere} continuous; indeed, it seems that that legion, which is one angel, would be in a manner circular, because the leading round would be circular, which requires continuity, and the continuous circularity of that motion requires continuity of the [subject]{g:subiectum}, which is the legion. [254] It is clear, therefore, that neither sense nor reason now indicates continuity in the world without there being, by the same token, continuity then also on both sides. Nor is there doubt that, a continuum being then moved by a spherical or punctual [thing], there would then be immediacy of motions and of instants, as there would be immediacy of indivisible sites, out of which a continuum would be composed on both sides. Indeed, a cubic body would have, corresponding to its punctuals, a heap of punctuals placed under it and led round it on every side, and everywhere there would be as much continuity. And so this way stammered the truth, ignorant of its own voice, because it was manifestly contrary to itself. So now it holds Aristotle, now denies him; now it holds the laws of men and now denies them; now it holds the [decrees]{g:decretum} of the [church]{g:ecclesia} and now denies them; nor in so varying does it ground itself in the faith of scripture; therefore the testimony of this way is vain. As to Aristotle's catholic sayings on the genus of [accident]{g:accidens}, it is clear that it rejects them without cause. But Aristotle's other sayings, on the composition of a continuum out of non-quantified things, as Plato, Augustine and [Lincoln]{g:lincolniensis} [pronounce]{g:sentenciare},[^9] it concedes explicitly in favour of Aristotle, but denies implicitly.

Quis ergo crederet tali viro in isto triplici devio et similibus tanquam testi?

Who then would believe such a man as a witness, in this threefold straying and the like?

### 08-f ¶7 · 255.1–255.23  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 255] **Sed** forte dicitur quod iste doctor ponit duplex tempus continuum et discretum; discretum componitur ex instantibus mensurantibus motus affeccionis evangelice. Et istam sentenciam tangit Thomas super II° Sentenciarum, distinccione secunda, questione XIIIᵃ: Dicendum, inquit, quod angelus potest considerari dupliciter: vel quantum ad suum esse quod est sine vicissitudine et sic mensuratur evo, vel quantum ad operacionem et sic quoad aliquam operacionem mensuratur eciam evo quoad operacionem qua intuetur res in verbo. In tali enim operacione non est vicissitudo aliqua **sed** secundum operacionem in qua est vicissitudo mensuratur tempore. **Sed** ipsum tempus est aliud a tempore quod mensurat motum primi mobilis, quia ista vicissitudo non ordinatur ad illam, nec illud tempus est continuum, **sed** est numerus discretus vicissitudinis non continue. Continuitas enim accidit tempori ex parte motus numerati. Et ita sicut ponit unum tempus mensurans totam universitatem sensibilem, sic ponit unum evum mensurans totam naturam angelicam secundum suum esse et intelligere permanens quod non est de genere successivi.

ENGLISH: But perhaps it is said that this doctor posits a twofold time, continuous and discrete; the discrete is composed of instants measuring the motions of angelic affection.[^13] And Thomas touches on this teaching on the second [book] of the *Sentences*, distinction 2, question 13:[^14] "It must be said," he says, "that an angel can be considered in two ways:[^15] either as to his [being]{g:esse}, which is without [alternation]{g:vicissitudo}, and so he is measured by [aeviternity]{g:evum}; or as to operation, and so, as to some operation, he is measured also by aeviternity, as to the operation by which he beholds things in [the Word]{g:verbum}. For in such operation there is no alternation; but according to the operation in which there is alternation he is measured by time. But that time is other than the time which measures the motion of [the first movable]{g:primum-mobile}, because this alternation is not ordered to that, nor is that time continuous, but it is the discrete number of a non-continuous alternation. For continuity befalls time on the side of the motion numbered." And so, as he posits one time measuring [the whole]{g:universitas} perceptible universe, so he posits one aeviternity measuring the whole angelic [nature]{g:natura} according to its permanent being and understanding, which is not of the genus of the successive.

### 08-g ¶1 · 255.24–256.5  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 255] Contra istam ficticiam arguitur primo per hoc, quod nichil est ponendum sine racione philosophica vel testimonio ex scriptura; **sed** neutrum istorum docet hanc duplicitatem temporis, ergo non est ponenda. Assumptum patet ex hoc quod aliter positum foret sompnium infundabile, irracionabile positum, cum pari evidencia infinita alia forent fingenda contra regulas nature que compendiosiori modo sine superfluitate [p. 256] semper agit, et Deus irracionabiliter oneraret ecclesiam fidelium ad credendum, et veritas oneraret falsitatem atque mendacium; et minor patet per consequens, hoc **autem** est commune argumentum temptativum, quo opinans cogitur dare evidenciam vie sue.

ENGLISH: Against this [fiction]{g:ficticia} it is argued, first, through this: that nothing is to be posited without philosophical reason or testimony from scripture; now neither of these teaches this doubleness of time; therefore it is not to be posited. [The assumption]{g:assumptum} is clear from this, that otherwise what is posited would be a dream [without foundation]{g:infundabilis}, a thing posited without reason, since by the same [evidence]{g:evidencia} infinitely many other things would have to be [feigned]{g:fingere} against the rules of nature, which [256] always acts in the more compendious way, without superfluity; and God would unreasonably burden the [church]{g:ecclesia} of the faithful with believing, and truth would burden falsehood and lying;[^1] and the minor is clear in consequence. And this is a common [tentative argument]{g:argumentum-temptativum},[^2] by which one who holds an opinion is forced to give evidence for his way.

### 08-g ¶3 · 256.22–257.14  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 256] Item, Deus de potencia sua absoluta posset permittere variacionem affeccionis angeli sine hoc quod produceret novum genus temporis, **sed** mensuraret affecciones sibi succedentes tempore communi; non ergo sequitur ex variacione talium affeccionum novum genus temporis; alias sic: Una affeccio potest esse permanens quantumcunque diuturna mensurata evo, post quam potest alia affeccio permanens succedere, et Deus non foret necessitatus pro mensura unius affeccionis nec pro mensura alterius nec pro instanti temporis medio novum genus temporis procreare, ergo hoc foret Deo possibile; nam commune evangelium [p. 257] sufficeret mensurare sequentem, nec pro instanti temporis potest tempus discretum subito secundum se totum produci, ymmo sicut dicit super distinccione XIᵃ, IVᵗⁱ Sentenciarum quod est dare ultimum tempus et non ultimum instans in quo manebit forma panis et primum instans, in quo erit corpus Christi sub illis accidentibus, ita videtur consequenter dicendum quod est de affeccione angelica variata sine creacione novi generis temporis, cum nec evum potest esse pars temporis nec duo vel finita instancia temporis possunt constituere tempus quantumcunque longum, quia sic foret tempus discretum, per accidens longum tempore nostro continue mensuratum; et patet quod ista ficticia est irracionabilis sine causa.

ENGLISH: Again, God by his [absolute power]{g:potencia-absoluta} could permit a variation of an angel's affection without producing a new genus of time, but would measure the affections succeeding one another by common time; therefore a new genus of time does not [follow]{g:sequitur} from the variation of such affections. Otherwise, thus: one affection can be permanent, however long-lasting, measured by [aeviternity]{g:evum}, after which another permanent affection can succeed; and God would not be necessitated, for the measure of the one affection, nor for the measure of the other, nor for the instant of time between, to procreate a new genus of time; therefore this would be possible to God. For the common aeviternity[^4] [257] would suffice to measure the following [affection]; nor at an instant of time can a discrete time be produced suddenly, whole according to itself. Indeed, as he says on distinction 11 of the fourth [book] of the *Sentences*, "that [one may posit]{g:est-dare} a last time and not a last instant in which the form of [bread]{g:panis} will remain, and a first instant in which the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} will be under those [accidents]{g:accidens},"[^5] so it seems one must consequently say that it is [so][^6] with the angelic affection varied without the creation of a new genus of time; since neither can aeviternity be a part of time, nor can two or finitely many instants of time constitute a time however long, because so it would be a discrete time, long [by accident]{g:per-accidens}, measured continuously by our time. And it is clear that this fiction is unreasonable, without cause.

### 08-g ¶5 · 258.22–259.18  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 258] Item, tempus discretum ex affeccione angelorum est prius ac dignius quam tempus nostrum continuum et eque sufficiens ad mensurandum omnem motum vel vicissitudinem sensibilem, ergo superfluit ponere aliud; per hoc enim quod duracio motus primi mobilis sufficit mensurare omnes motus alios ponit secta ista ipsam specialiter esse tempus. Si ergo duracio angelica sufficit prius mensurare ipsum motum, sequitur quod foret tempus ad ipsum et eius duracio solum quando. Nec valet dicere quod vicissitudo angelica non est continua, quia utraque eius medietas que est instans illius temporis est vere continua; cur ergo non totum, cum una pars sequitur immediate ad [p. 259] reliquam, et hoc sufficit ad continuacionem temporis; ymmo videtur quod in quolibet instanti temporis nostri est mutacio vicissitudinis affeccionis angelice; nam angelus videt nedum creaturas in verbo **sed** in genere proprio; cum ergo angelus videt continue noviter presenciam ac novitatem creature genite pro quolibet instanti temporis nostri, videtur quod continue habet novam noticiam vel modum sciendi. Sicut enim tempus discretum consequitur ad affeccionem vel volicionem angeli, ita sequitur ad intuicionem et quamcunque variacionem ex parte angeli, et ita (ut arguit Doctor Subtilis contra hanc viam) nedum simul forent multa tempora **sed** numeri angelorum variancium affecciones suas, que tempora ut subiecta et obiecta forent distincta in specie, **sed** idem angelus in numero secundum variaciones intencionum et affeccionum circa diversa obiecta in specie causaret simul diversa tempora in specie.

ENGLISH: Again, discrete time from the affection of angels is prior to and worthier than our continuous time, and equally sufficient to measure every [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} motion or alternation; therefore it is superfluous to posit another. For through this, that the duration of the motion of the first movable suffices to measure all other motions, this [sect]{g:secta} posits it specially to be time. If then the angelic duration suffices, prior [to it], to measure that motion, it follows that it would be time to it, and its duration only a "when".[^10] Nor is it sound to say that the angelic alternation is not continuous, because each half of it, which is an instant of that time, is [truly]{g:vere} continuous;[^11] why then not the whole, since one part follows [without intermediary]{g:immediate} upon [259] the other, and this suffices for the [continuation]{g:continuacio} of time? Indeed, it seems that in every instant of our time there is a change of alternation of angelic affection; for the angel sees creatures [not only]{g:nedum} in [the Word]{g:verbum} but in their proper genus; since, then, the angel continually sees anew the presence and newness of the creature begotten for every instant of our time, it seems that he continually has a new knowledge or mode of knowing. For as discrete time follows upon the affection or volition of an angel, so it follows upon intuition and any variation on the angel's side; and so (as [the Subtle Doctor]{g:doctor-subtilis} argues against this way)[^12] not only would there be many times at once, but [as many as] the numbers of angels varying their affections,[^13] which times would be distinct in species as [their] subjects and objects [are]; but the same angel in number, according to the variations of intentions and affections about objects diverse in species, would cause at once times diverse in species.
