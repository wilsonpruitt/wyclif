# *De potestate pape*, chapter 2, part 2: no man gives the Spirit, takes away sin, or punishes beyond God

## ¶1 · 26.9–32.8

> [p. 26] Quantum ad potestatem dandi Spiritum Sanctum vel graciam, videtur dicendum conformiter quod foret nimis magna presumpcio dicere quod homo peccator dat Spiritum Sanctum, quia Christo super quem specialiter descendit columba appropriatur baptizacio ista, ut patet Joh. primo, Ymo nullus talis habet potestatem servandi sibi Spiritum Sanctum, quomodo igitur ad dandum alteri, cum ignorat utrum pertineat ad columbam? Ymo apostoli, ad quorum ministerium dabatur Spiritus Sanctus, non presumpserunt asserere quod dederunt, sed, ut patet Act. VIII, 15, oraverunt pro illis ut acciperent Spiritum Sanctum. Ideo vel solus Deus habet potestatem dandi Spiritum Sanctum, vel quilibet laycus baptizando vel promerendo habet potestatem dandi Spiritum Sanctum; differenter tamen episcopus, secundum quod autoritative det donum tale, nimis multum excederet vicariam potestatem. Quis igitur concederet, quod habet potestatem super Deum vel eius graciam, et quomodocunque locuti fuerimus, maximum foret quod unus meretur alteri primam graciam, quia, cum in omni motu movens et motum sunt simul, nimis scandalosum foret quod Romanus episcopus tam distanter, tam generaliter et tam improporcionaliter ad [p. 27] Deum, alteraret homines, dando graciam, cum multorum talium spiritus, in quibus subiectaretur talis potestas, sunt miserabiliter damnandi non sufficientes iuvare se ipsos. Et si dicatur quod solum limitatur potestas eorum habilibus, cum solum talibus Deus potest dare graciam, revera habiles haberent a Deo graciam non eo plus vel minus, licet tales non sic menciantur se dare graciam Sicut igitur non habent potestatem faciendi filios Dei, dando graciam predestinacionis, sic nec aliquam graciam, qua Christianus foret gratus Deo. Nec est color si habent potestatem super antecedens, quod habent potestatem super omne consequens vel convertibile, eo quod sic essent potentatus pares Deo, et cum Psalmo LXXXIX, 10 dicitur: Si in potentatibus octoginta anni, et amplius eorum, labor et dolor. Et revera minus videtur errorem sapere quod sanitas corporalis subsit hominis potestati, quam quod gracia vel Spiritus Sanctus subsit, nam cum nichil est in potestate hominis dare, nisi quod subiacet sue potestati, nimis magnum foret christianum habere potestatem dandi Spiritum Sanctum; hoc enim est singulare donum sui principii, cum christianus non potest ipsum vendere. Et conformis est consideracio de absolucione; quondam enim reputabatur Deo proprium tollere peccata tam aput fideles quam aput infideles; unde singulariter dixit Baptista de Christo, ut legitur Joh. I, 29: Ecce agnus Dei, ecce qui tollit peccata mundi. Super quo dicit August. Omel. IV: Nemo sibi arroget et dicat, quod ipse aufert peccata [p. 28] mundi. Iam attendite contra quos superbos intendebat digitum Ioannes. Nondum erant nati heretici et iam ostendebantur, contra illos clamabat a fluvio. Unde sepe imposuerunt Judei Christo blasfemiam ex hoc quod peccata dimisit simpliciter, quia non dimittitur peccatum simpliciter nisi ex hoc, quod offensa in Deum laxatur, sed quis dimittit iniuriam, nisi vel factam sibi vel suo subdito? Deus enim dando homini potestatem huiusmodi, prius dimitteret suam iniuriam antequam vicarius suus dimitteret. Et ista videtur sentencia multorum doctorum, ut XXIII, q. V ex decreto beati Ambrosii sic habetur: Remittuntur, inquit, peccata per Dei filium, cuius levita interpres et quidam excusator est, quia, ut dicitur XV, q. I: Nec is: Levita minister remissionis est. Unde super illo decreto Archidiaconus ita scribit: Prelati quando ligant vel solvunt indignos, mirum est si tunc ligantur vel solvuntur in celis, sed intelligitur de illis, quorum merita ligari vel solvi postulant. Et illud notat XXIV, q. I Ubi sana et Quicunque. Nec dubium de ista sentencia, ne blasfemetur quod prelati nostri prevalent contra Deum; Deus enim non potest quemquam absolvere, nisi prius ipsum habilitet; cum igitur christianus nullum habilitat, nisi forsan orando vel merendo, patet quod oportet Deum habilitare hominem secundum meritum eius. Et de isto debet christianus confidere non autem in pseudochristi blasfemia, [p. 29] qua sompniat et facit ignaros credere de sua insensibili et infundabili potestate; unde non sinit quod scrutinium sue blasphemie in luce clareat, et licet sit tantum errabilis, blasfemat profundius quod Deus est necessitatus complere omnia sua iudicia. Sicut igitur sacerdotes legis veteris non fecerunt hominem sanum vel leprosum sed notificabant, ut patet IV Sentenciarum dist. XVIII et recitatur inferius, sic presbyteri legis nove. In cuius signum Christus suscitavit Lazarum et fecit Apostolos ipsum solvere, ut patet X, XI: et decem leprosi, dum ipsi irent, mundati sunt, ut dicitur Luce XVII, 12, 14 et Psalmus XXXI, 5: Dixi confitebor adversum me, et tu remisisti. Verum tamen amplius habent sacerdotes Christi quam sacerdotes legis veteris, quia plus est iudicare de macula spiritus quam de macula corporali, plus eciam est dignum et humile habere graciam et meritum ex subieccione ecclesie quam corporaliter infirmum esse notificatum in populo esse sanum; et sic licet in multis excellenciis sacerdocium legis veteris superat sacerdocium legis nove, ad sacerdocium tamen Christi non potest attingere, sed generaliter fallit, quando non sequitur arbitrium interni iudicis, ut patet XI, q. III Ad hoc vera est. Ista autem blasfema gentilitas, qua sacerdotes debiliores usurpant sibi opus, quod est Deo proprium, maculat matrem ecclesiam, ideo necesse foret eos cognoscere, quomodo quidam tantum a veritate degeneraverant quod negarunt accionem aliquam competere creature (ut patet IX Metaphysice Comment. [p. 30] VII). Alii autem magis blasfeme concesserant quod Deus omnem accionem illis ad extra communicat tamquam eius vicariis, et hii tamquam alter Nabuchodonosor. de quo Dan. IV. et alter Senacherib, de quo II. Paralip. XXXII vendicant se esse socios Trinitatis. Considerarent autem dictum Augustini II De Mirabilibus Scripture Sacre cap. IV quomodo sol et luna steterunt contra Gabaon, non humani imperii auctoritate sed Domini imperantis. Nil, inquit, propter iubentis hominis verbum creature faciunt, sed quod Dominus servi sui oracioni obediens precipit, et cap. XXIV dicit quod lepra Naaman sanata est in sermone imperantis Dei, et non in re aliqua sacramentum virtutis continebatur, et hinc Elizeus accipere munera recusavit. Et sic attribuerent sibi quod servo est proprium, promulgando, operando et promerendo, quod Deus dignatur per illos facere continue, sumendo ut servi humiliter de se ipsis. Ubi autem dubitant de suo opere, debent vere, religiose et secure totam accionem Deo ascribere, cum oportet eum facere omnem accionem creature. Sive utatur igitur in tali accione creatura sua ut organo sive non, sibi laus est operis tribuenda, laudem autem creature de quanto meretur oportet esse aput Deum absconditam, unde magnificantes suam potenciam notarent formam utendi et ascribendi potestatem in sanctis apostolis, et nimis magnum foret, quod nostri illos excederent. Ipsi autem sepe confitentur, quod non habent potestatem nisi ad edificacionem ecclesie, ut patet [p. 31] II. Corinth. X. et iterum confitentur quod ve illis nisi edificatorie usi fuerint potestate, ut patet I. Corinth. IX, 16: Ve mihi, si non evangelizavero, et tercio confitentur se non audere de se ipsis loqui vel aliis nisi quod Christus in eis loquitur. Nam Rom. XV, 18 ait Apostolus: Non, inquit, audeo aliquid loqui eorum, que per me non effecit Christus. Stulticia igitur foret, episcopum cecum infundabilia et inopinabilia de se ipso presumere. Quantumcunque itaque fuerit magnus Christi episcopus, non potest quemquam absolvere nisi de quanto contritus prius et absolutus fuerit apud Deum; et tunc videtur multis quod de tanto quilibet simplex sacerdos posset absolvere, ymo quasi contritus secure absolvitur, etsi humana absolucio non sequatur, dimittere autem suam iniuriam sicut peccatum in ipsum potest quilibet christianus eque plene ut papa, sicut ponit Matth. VI et XVIII. Quomodo igitur auderet aliquis dicere quod ipse habet singulariter potestatem absolvendi quemcumque peccatorem a pena et a culpa? Revera foret nimis damnabilis, qui ad detrimentum ecclesie tantum incarcerat huiusmodi potestatem. Et conformiter dicitur de potestate excommunicandi vel puniendi subiectos. Nemo enim habet ut membrum Christi potestatem puniendi aliquem, nisi de quanto Deus prius punierit, quod si excedit, punit ut tortor dyaboli. Et hinc dicit Augustinus in De Mirabilibus Scripture cap. XVII quod Isaac benedixit non Esau quem voluit, sed [p. 32] Jacob quem noluit benedicere, ut in benediccione non hominis ostenderet beneficium sed Dei benedicentis. Denique dictum est in Numeris ad Moysen et Aaron sacerdotes: Vos ponite nomen meum super filios Israhel, ego Dominus benedicam vos. Nec voluntas sacerdotis obesse aut prodesse possit ultra meritum benediccionem poscentis. Nec dubium fideli de ista sentencia.

As to the power of giving the Holy Spirit or grace, it seems that one must say, in conformity [with the foregoing], that it would be too great a presumption to say that a sinful man gives the Holy Spirit, since this baptism is appropriated to Christ, upon whom the dove specially descended, as is clear from John 1.[^1] Indeed no such man has [power]{g:potestas} to keep the Holy Spirit for himself; how then [has he power] to give it to another, since he [does not know]{g:ignorare} whether he belongs to the dove? Indeed the apostles, by whose ministry the Holy Spirit was given, did not presume to assert that they gave it, but, as is clear from Acts 8:15, they "prayed for them that they might receive the Holy Ghost". Therefore either God alone has power of giving the Holy Spirit, or any layman whatever, by baptizing or by meriting, has power of giving the Holy Spirit; yet it is otherwise with a [bishop]{g:episcopus}: insofar as he were to give such a gift by his own authority, he would far too much exceed vicarial power.[^2] Who, therefore, would grant that he has power over God or over his grace? And however we may have spoken, the most there could be is that one merits the first grace for another; for since "in every motion the mover and the moved are together", it would be too scandalous that [the Roman bishop]{g:romanus-episcopus}, so distant, so general and so out of proportion to [27] God, should alter men by giving grace, since the spirits of many such men, in whom such power would have its subject, are wretchedly to be damned, not sufficient to help themselves.[^3] And if it is said that their power is [limited]{g:limitare} only to the [qualified]{g:habilis}, since to such alone can God give grace, in truth the qualified would have grace from God neither more nor less on that account, even if such men did not lie in this way that they give grace. Just as, therefore, they do not have power of making sons of God by giving the grace of predestination, so neither [have they power to give] any grace by which a Christian would be pleasing to God. Nor is there [colour]{g:color} [for saying], if they have power over the [antecedent]{g:antecedens}, that they have power over every consequent or convertible, since in that way potentates would be equal to God; and as it is said in Psalm 89:10: "But if in the strong they be fourscore years: and what is more of them is labour and sorrow."[^4] And in truth it seems to savour less of error that bodily health should be subject to a man's power than that grace or the Holy Spirit should be; for since nothing is in a man's power to give except what lies under his power, it would be too much for a Christian to have power of giving the Holy Spirit; for this is the singular gift of its own principle, since a Christian cannot sell it.[^5] And the consideration of absolution is alike; for once it was reckoned proper to God to take away sins, among the faithful as well as among [unbelievers]{g:infidelis}; hence the Baptist said singularly of Christ, as is read in John 1:29: "Behold the Lamb of God. Behold him who taketh away the sins of the world." On which Augustine says, in Homily 4: "Let no one arrogate it to himself and say that he takes away the sins [28] of the world. Mark now against what proud men John was pointing his finger. The heretics were not yet born, and already they were being pointed out; against them he was crying from the river."[^6] Hence the Jews often charged Christ with blasphemy because he forgave sins [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}; for a sin is not forgiven without qualification except by the offence against God being released; now who forgives an injury, except one done either to himself or to his subject? For God, in giving a man power of this kind, would forgive his own injury before his vicar forgave it.[^7] And this seems to be the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of many doctors, as is had thus in [C.] 23 q. 5 from the decree of blessed Ambrose: "Sins," he says, "are [remitted]{g:remittere} through the Son of God, whose interpreter and a kind of excuser the Levite is"; for, as is said in [C.] 15 q. 1, *Nec is*: "The Levite is the minister of remission."[^8] Hence on that decree the Archdeacon writes thus: "When [prelates]{g:prelatus} bind or loose the unworthy, it is a wonder if they are then bound or loosed in heaven; but it is understood of those whose merits demand that they be bound or loosed." And [the gloss] notes this at [C.] 24 q. 1, *Ubi sana* and *Quicunque*.[^9] Nor is there doubt about this teaching, lest it be blasphemously said that our prelates prevail against God; for God cannot absolve anyone unless he first qualifies him; since, then, a Christian qualifies no one, except perhaps by praying or meriting, it is clear that God must qualify a man according to his merit. And in this a Christian ought to trust, but not in the blasphemy of the false christ, [29] by which he dreams, and makes the ignorant believe, of his [imperceptible]{g:insensibilis} and groundless power; hence he does not allow the scrutiny of his blasphemy to shine out in the light, and although he is so liable to error, he blasphemes more deeply that God is necessitated to fulfil all his judgments.[^10] Just as, therefore, the [priests]{g:sacerdos} of the old law did not make a man clean or leprous but made it known, as is clear from *Sentences* IV dist. 18, and is recounted below, so [too] the [presbyters]{g:presbiter} of the new law.[^11] In token of which Christ raised Lazarus and had the apostles loose him, as is clear from [John] 10, 11; and the ten lepers, "as they went, were made clean", as is said in Luke 17:12, 14; and Psalm 31:5: "I said I will confess against myself … and thou hast forgiven."[^12] Nevertheless the priests of Christ have more than the priests of the old law, since it is more to judge of a stain of the spirit than of a bodily stain; it is also more worthy and humble to have grace and merit from subjection to the [church]{g:ecclesia} than for one bodily sick to be made known among the people to be clean; and so, although in many excellences the priesthood of the old law surpasses the priesthood of the new law, yet it cannot reach to the priesthood of Christ; but it generally fails when it does not follow the judgment of the inward judge, as is clear from [C.] 11 q. 3, *Ad hoc vera est*.[^13] And this blasphemous paganism, by which feebler priests usurp to themselves the work that is proper to God, stains mother church; therefore it would be needful for them to know how certain men had degenerated so far from the truth that they denied that any action belongs to a creature (as is clear from *Metaphysics* IX, comment [30] 7). But others, more blasphemously, had granted that God communicates every outward action to them as his vicars; and these, like another Nebuchadnezzar, of whom Daniel 4, and another Sennacherib, of whom 2 Chronicles 32, claim to be partners of the Trinity.[^14] And let them consider the saying of Augustine, *On the Wonders of Holy Scripture* II, chapter 4, how the sun and moon stood still against Gibeon "not by the authority of a human command but of the Lord commanding. Creatures," he says, "do nothing on account of the word of the man who bids, but what the Lord, obeying the prayer of his servant, commands"; and in chapter 24 he says that Naaman's leprosy was healed "at the word of God commanding, and the sacrament of [power]{g:virtus} was not contained in any thing; and hence Elisha refused to accept gifts".[^15] And so they should attribute to themselves what is proper to a servant, proclaiming, working and meriting what God deigns to do continually through them, taking [the part] of servants humbly with regard to themselves. And where they are in doubt about their own work, they ought truly, religiously and safely to ascribe the whole action to God, since he must do every action of a creature. Whether, then, he uses his creature as an instrument in such an action or not, the praise of the work is to be given to him; and the praise of the creature, insofar as it merits, must be hidden with God; hence those who magnify their own [power]{g:potencia} should note the form of using and ascribing power in the holy apostles, and it would be too much that ours should exceed them.[^16] And they often confess that they have no power except for the building up of the church, as is clear from [31] 2 Corinthians 10; and again they confess that woe to them unless they have used power to build up, as is clear from 1 Corinthians 9:16: "Woe is unto me if I preach not the gospel"; and thirdly they confess that they do not dare to speak of themselves or of others except what Christ speaks in them. For in Romans 15:18 the Apostle says: "For I dare not," he says, "to speak of any of those things which Christ worketh not by me."[^17] It would therefore be folly for a blind bishop to presume groundless and incredible things of himself. However great, then, a bishop of Christ may be, he cannot absolve anyone except insofar as [the man] has first been contrite and absolved before God; and then it seems to many that to that extent any simple priest could absolve; indeed one who is as it were contrite is safely absolved, even if human absolution does not follow; and any Christian can forgive his own injury, as a sin against himself, as fully as the [pope]{g:papa}, as Matthew 6 and 18 lay down.[^18] How then would anyone dare to say that he singularly has power of absolving any sinner whatever from penalty and from guilt? In truth he would be most damnable who, to the detriment of the church, so imprisons power of this kind.[^19] And in conformity the answer is the same concerning the power of excommunicating or punishing subjects. For no one has, as a [member]{g:membrum} of Christ, power of punishing anyone except insofar as God has first punished; and if he exceeds this, he punishes as the devil's torturer. And hence Augustine says in *On the Wonders of Scripture*, chapter 17, that "Isaac blessed not Esau, whom he wished [to bless], but [32] Jacob, whom he did not wish to bless, so that in the blessing he might show the benefit not of man but of God who blesses. Finally it was said in Numbers to Moses and Aaron the priests: 'Put ye my name upon the children of Israel; I the Lord will bless you.' Nor can the will of the priest hinder or help beyond the merit of him who asks for the blessing."[^20] Nor is there doubt for one of the faithful about this teaching.

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 26.9: Man cannot give the Holy Spirit.
- 26.24: A bishop has a spiritual power but he cannot grant the Spirit at his will.
- 27.1: Nor can the pope; indeed many popes have themselves lacked it.
- 27.27: The power of absolution: Only God takes away sin.
- 28.21: God himself cannot give absolution save to one whom he has fitted to receive it.
- 29.6: The priests in the old law did not cure the leper but declared his state; so it is as to absolution in the new law.
- 29.26: How bad priests usurp the office of God,
- 30.17: They should attribute to themselves the office of a servant and ascribe all power to God.
- 30.31: Example set by the apostles.
- 31.10: A simple priest can declare absolution to the contrite as well as the Pope.
- 31.25: So as to excommunication, no man has the power of punishing thus unless God has already punished.

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** John 1:32–33: the Spirit descending as a dove on Jesus, "he it is that baptizeth with the Holy Ghost". "Belongs to the dove" (26.14) is whether one has the Spirit at all. A in marg.: *Dare Spiritum Sanctum*.

[^2]: **C.** *Differenter tamen episcopus* is elliptical: a layman may be said to give the Spirit by baptizing or meriting, but a bishop claiming to give it *autoritative*, by his own authority, would exceed a vicar's power. **T.** Loserth reports *Sed quod* from the codices at 26.26, apparently where he prints *secundum quod*; translated as printed.

[^3]: **S.** "In every motion the mover and the moved are together": Aristotle, *Physics* VII.2 (243a), the axiom of contact between mover and moved (LEDGER P02b-1). Hence a distant Roman bishop cannot "alter" men by giving grace. **C.** *In quibus subiectaretur talis potestas*: in whom such power would inhere as in its subject; the pronoun is taken with the spirits of the bishops.

[^4]: **S.** Ps. 89:10 (Vulgate numbering); Wyclif omits *autem*. **C.** The verse turns on *potentatibus*, which DR renders "the strong" and the Latin shares with *potentatus*, "potentates", just before: those who claim God's power have only labour and sorrow beyond their span. *Potentatus* is the person who holds power, as in 02-a.

[^5]: **C.** *Singulare donum sui principii*: a gift that belongs to its own source alone (the Spirit is given only by God); that a Christian cannot sell it recalls Simon Magus (Acts 8:18–20).

[^6]: **S.** John 1:29: the Vulgate has *peccatum mundi* (DR "the sin of the world"); Wyclif's *peccata* is followed. Augustine, *In Ioannis evangelium tractatus* 4 (Loserth: "cap. I, tract. IV, Opp. III, p. 316"; LEDGER P02b-2, wording to be checked). *Fluvio* is the Jordan.

[^7]: **S.** Mark 2:7, Luke 5:21: "Who can forgive sins, but God only?" A in marg. at 27.27: *Peccata tollit solus Christus*.

[^8]: **S.** Decretum C.23 q.5 c.49, from Ambrose (editor; form ours, LEDGER P02b-3); the passage is Ambrose, *De Cain et Abel* 2.4.15, which reads *per Dei verbum* and *executor* (LEDGER P02b-4, to be checked). Loserth notes *Rectius: per Dei verbum*. **T.** *Excusator* ("excuser") is translated as printed; the source's *executor* ("executor") may be the true reading. The second citation is Decretum C.15 q.1 c.11 (editor; codices *XVI*, corrected by Loserth); *Nec is* is taken as the canon's incipit.

[^9]: **S.** "The Archdeacon" is Guido de Baysio, *Rosarium* on the Decretum (Loserth: "Archid. l. c."; identification LEDGER P02b-5). Decretum C.24 q.1 cc.27 and 29 (editor; form ours, LEDGER P02b-6); the codices read *XXIV, q. I versus sua et quodcunque*. B in marg.: *Absolutio*. A reads *nec blasphemetur* (28.22).

[^10]: **C.** "The false christ" (*pseudochristi*, Matt. 24:24) is singular in the print, and the pronouns that follow refer to him; he is the claimant to such power. "Necessitated to fulfil all his judgments": that God must ratify whatever he binds or looses.

[^11]: **S.** Peter Lombard, *Sentences* IV d.18 (editor: "ed. Lugd. p. 41[.]"; LEDGER P02b-7), on the priests of the old law who declared, not made, a leper clean (Lev. 13–14). "Clean" renders *sanum* (whole, sound).

[^12]: **T.** *X, XI* (29.10) names no book; the raising of Lazarus and the command to loose him are John 11:44 ("Loose him, and let him go"), said to the bystanders, not the apostles. Probably *Joh. XI* with the book lost. **S.** Luke 17:14 (Wyclif adds *ipsi*); Ps. 31:5, abridged (DR "I said I will confess against my self my injustice to the Lord: and thou hast forgiven the wickedness of my sin").

[^13]: **C.** "It cannot reach" has as subject the priesthood of the old law, which in outward excellences surpasses the new but falls short of Christ's; "it generally fails" is taken of any priesthood that does not follow the inward judge. B reads *quantum non sequitur* (29.24–25). **S.** Loserth: no chapter *Ad hoc vera est* exists; the place is C.11 q.3 c. *Tunc* (editor; LEDGER P02b-8).

[^14]: **S.** Averroes, *In Metaphysicam* IX, comm. 7, on those who denied any action to creatures (the *loquentes*; LEDGER P02b-9). Dan. 4 (Nebuchadnezzar's boast); 2 Chron. 32 (Sennacherib's against the God of Israel).

[^15]: **S.** Augustinus Hibernicus, *De mirabilibus sacrae scripturae* II.4 (Josh. 10:12–13) and II.24 (4 Kings 5) (editor: Augustine *Opp.* III, 2, 1000; the work is pseudonymous; LEDGER P02b-10). Loserth corrects the codices' *III … cap. 3* and *Cap. 22*. **T.** "In any thing" renders Loserth's emendation *in re aliqua*; the codices read *non in aqua* ("the sacrament of power was not contained in the water"), which also construes and fits Naaman's washing.

[^16]: **C.** *Potencia* here is ordinary might, "power" (GLOSSARY *potencia*, CONTEXTUAL), beside *potestas*; Wyclif uses the two interchangeably. *Sumendo ut servi humiliter de se ipsis* (30.19) is compressed: taking the part of servants, humbly, as regards themselves. The codices read *continue sumando ut supera* (Loserth).

[^17]: **S.** 2 Cor. 10:8 ("our power, which the Lord hath given us unto edification"); 1 Cor. 9:16; Rom. 15:18, where the Vulgate has *efficit* (DR "worketh") for Wyclif's *effecit*. A in marg.: *Potestatem apostoli quam se dicunt habere*.

[^18]: **C.** *Quasi contritus* (31.13) is translated as printed, "one who is as it were contrite"; the sense required is "the truly contrite", and a scar or lost word is possible. **S.** Matt. 6:12–15, 18:21–35 (forgiving one's brother). B reads *ita fuerit* (31.10); A *similiter potestatem* (31.20); A and B in marg.: *Dimittere peccata*.
