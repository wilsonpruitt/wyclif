# *De potestate pape*, chapter 12, part 6: the caesarean clergy, Judas's field, and Jesus as patron

## ¶1 · 333.6–338.8

> [p. 333] Possunt autem iste due partes per hoc discerni potissime quod clerus cesareus instat attencius pro tradicionibus humanis et privilegiis que fastum et lucrum seculi sapiunt defendendis. Sed clerus Christi laborat assidue pro legibus Christi et eius privilegiis, quibus bonum spirituale acquiritur ostendendum; nec phas est fideli discredere quin ista pars sit recta et prior erronea, in tantum quod omnis innitens priori parti est infidelis hereticus, quia in affeccione preponderat bona temporalia bonis eternis, et cum pertinaciter defendit hunc errorem tamquam avarus, quod est ydolorum servitus, patet quod omnis talis sit hereticus infidelis. Et ex ista perfidia consurgit heresis, quod licet clericis et specialiter episcopis civiliter dominari, quod fulcitur ex hoc quod dominacio civilis est licita, licet difficilis; ille igitur qui haberet maiorem prudenciam ad cavendum periculum, ut habent episcopi, magis licite assumeret hanc vitam honorificam dominandi. Per hunc fucum concluditur quod convenientissimum est clericos bellare, uxorari et quemlibet statum periculosum assumere, cum virtus consistit circa difficile. Sed primo tollo istam versuciam; constat quidem quod multa sunt licita, que propter inferioritatem suam sunt Antichristi clericis aliena, sic quod ipsi peccarent graviter, dimissa operacione nobili, operacioni ignobili intendendo, et sic propter perfeccionem status foret turpissimum [p. 334] ipsos in talibus occupari, ut patet de Christo qui nescivit taliter facere, cum tunc sciret degradare se ipsum ad statum et ordinem laycalem, et per consequens sciret male facere et peccare. Prudencia itaque cleri stat in declinacione talis operis imperfecti, sicut foret bonum in laycis pure intendentibus scole Christi. Notaremus itaque quod duodecim episcopis, quos Christus in scola sua nutriverat, solus Scarioth se ipsum amando post apostolatum proprietati intenderat, nam Act. primo 18 dicit Petrus de Scarioth: hic possedit agrum de mercede iniquitatis. Nec phas est credere quod Petrus in hoc mentitus est, nec evangelium Matth. XXVI contradicit isti sentencie, quia cum Scarioth prius pro triginta argenteis vendidit pontificibus Salvatorem, assecuraturus de precio et credens verbis magistri, quod fuit in proximo moriturus, potuit providere sibi de redditu prope Jerusalem, ut frustratus a lucro quod videbatur habere ex furto communis pecunie, provideat sibi et suis possidendo illud proprietarie; nec repugnat quod accepit possessionem nuncupative dictam vel sessinam in predio, et post reportando pecuniam dandam pro agro, audiens magistrum condempnatum, infructuose penitens se suspendit. Judas enim non dormit, sed facit velociter facta sua, et consonabit quod illud maledictum commercium foret undique mendaciis pertractatum. Sic igitur secundum regulam, qua Augustinus concordat evangelia, credi debet tam Petri evangelio quam Matthei; nec caret misterio quod Yudas pontifex legis nove sic mercatus fuit abscondite cum pontificibus veteris testamenti, quia sic continuatur generacio eorum adultera; [p. 335] et docetur quomodo pseudopontifices in lege gracie exsuperant malicia pontifices veteris testamenti. Illi enim emerunt agrum cum dicta pecunia secundum religionem fictam ad sepeliendum extraneos, quia precium sanguinis licet effusi licite exhorrebant. Sed dicitur nostros pontifices mittere in corbonam precium seminis, quod est preciosius, et effusum vilius ac culpabilius, in quo videntur superare cupidine veteres sacerdotes. Nec dubium quin symoniace vendendo spiritualia, et sic Christum, nostri pontifices peccarent gravius precium inbursantes, et sic (ni fallor) dominacio secularis in nostris episcopis non fundabitur in lege nova vel veteri, nisi forte in Scarioth, Anania et Zaphira, de quibus Act. V; sed omnes hii tres exinde divino iudicio sunt occisi. Quod autem vindicta nunc non capitur de nostris, ut in primitiva ecclesia de minus peccantibus, nostre culpe et signo maioris periculi possumus imputare; Deus enim quandoque ex amore predestinati peccantis hunc arguit et castigat, quemque autem ex amore populi, quem vult a toxico talis macule preservari, nunc autem signum est quod ex peccati participio deseruit ut desperatos tam populum quam peccantes. Et istud videtur michi esse tempus periculosum, quale non fuit a mundi inicio a Christo et apostolo prophetatum. Vix enim invenies unum presbiterum vel potentem laycum, qui non sit irretitus opere vel consensu episcoporum symonica heresi plus vel minus. Et patet quam horrenda est desercio securitatis cleri de sorte Domini et preeleccio securitatis mundane, in qua mundus blasfeme Deo preponitur. Nec valet [p. 336] locus a simili captus a laycis, quia et hoc foret in seculari dampnabile, et mundana securitas Dei adiecta in ipso est licita et non sic in clerico legis Christi. Clericis enim legis veteris, quibus imponebatur sarcina intollerabilis, suffecit pars Domini de decimis, oblacionibus et privatis elemosinis, et tercia pars stricta suffecit Christo et suis apostolis. Nobis autem non sufficit decima pars Domini ampliata proditorie et iudaice vendicata. Sed terciam partem prediorum laycalium ex titulo possessionis Christi mendaciter vendicamus, et ex uno latere cum bonis pauperum quorum sumus nudi procuratores, non habentes a Domino ad hoc procuratorium, possessiones seculares incessanter perquirimus. Ex alio vero latere extendimus magnificantes tradiciones hominum que questum sapiunt, et ex hinc ad grave dampnum ecclesie est ipsa lex Dei nimium pretermissa. Dicimus enim quod nedum est licitum sed necessarium propter consilium cesaris postponere Christi consilia, sed necessario oportet quod fiat dispensacio cum illis tradicionibus onustatis quas Christus instituit, sed cum donacionibus hominum et ritibus sequentibus propter sacrilegium non licet Deo vel homini dispensare, in hoc indubie preponentes hominem vel verius mammonam Deo nostro. Consideraret itaque clerus, quomodo ad fornicandum spiritualiter cum bonis fortune est clerus proclivior, ad resipiscendum tardior, et omnino est dirupcio amoris Dei detestabilior. Ideo considerarent arguentes per locum de possibili ad periculum de inesse, quomodo quilibet claustralis in Anglia potest meritorie habere feminam in cubili et studio celatam, sic [p. 337] valet quod non abutatur femina, igitur debet; fornicacio (inquam) corporalis non est tam assidua, cum oportet quod carnalis fornicacio, quando natura deficit et invalescit avaricia, sit extincta et per consequens tamquam innaturalior et difficilior est a Domino plus odita. Ideo dicit Matth. XI, 24: Dico vobis, quod terre Sodomorum remissius erit in die iudicii quam tibi. Neque enim saltamus cum filio hominis, nec plangimus cum Johanne Baptista, sed, omittentes doctrinam Christi nobis facilem quam expressit in vita et moribus et verbis, de vita paupere et exproprietaria, sequimur sanctos novellos quorum canonizacio fuit empta, licet pro maiori parte vite sue vixerunt mendaciter; et addimus in commendacionem nostre sentencie quod non legimus de patronorum nostrorum resipiscencia, igitur respondebam. Constitueremus, inquam, nobis Jhesum patronum et regulam, postponendo alios nec obediendo nisi de quanto sequuntur ipsum. In cuius confirmacionem oraciones nostras, quas habemus de sanctis, singulariter ad ipsum dirigimus. Non simulemus igitur maniace stulticiam auditorii in nostris sermonibus, quia ipsum percipit sophisticaciones nostras subtilius quam sufficimus palliare. Materiam (inquam) fidei, que opinionem excedit, magnificare debemus, quantum sufficimus, Sic igitur tollendo istas notas perfidie de plus credendis factis et dictis unius peccantis prepositi quam verbo vel operi Jhesu nostri, debemus singulariter doctrine sue intendere, quia revera est infidelis et mundo suspecta excusacio in peccatis capere thema nostrum de Christo, et dimissa doctrina sua inniti uni erranti preposito licet sancto, et specialiter in hiis mundialibus, in [p. 338] quibus obliquant a Christo, addendo fallaciam; Ipsi sic sequuntur, igitur nos possumus licite ac meritorie sic viare. Et sequitur punctus fallacie, quando concluditur, igitur de facto nos servando naturam operis licet modo contrario nec iniuste facimus nec peccamus. Revera prevalente ista fallacia liceret cuilibet religioso qualibet seculari vita quomodolibet conversari.

And these two parts can be told apart chiefly by this, that the [caesarean clergy]{g:clerus-cesareus} presses more earnestly for the defending of human traditions and [privileges]{g:privilegium} that savour of the pride and gain of the world.[^1] But the clergy of Christ labours assiduously for the showing forth of the laws of Christ and his privileges, by which spiritual good is acquired; nor is it lawful for a believer to disbelieve that this part is right and the former erroneous, so much so that everyone who leans on the former part is an [unbeliever]{g:infidelis}, a heretic, because in his affection he weighs [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} above eternal goods; and since he obstinately defends this error as a covetous man, "which is a serving of idols", it is clear that every such man is a heretic, an unbeliever.[^2] And out of this faithlessness arises the heresy that it is lawful for clerics, and especially for [bishops]{g:episcopus}, to [exercise civil lordship]{g:dominari}; which is propped up by this, that the civil [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio} is lawful, though difficult; therefore he who had greater prudence for avoiding the danger, as bishops have, would the more lawfully take up this honourable life of exercising lordship. By this sham it is [inferred]{g:concludere} that it is most fitting for clerics to make war, to marry and to take up any dangerous [state]{g:status}, since [virtue]{g:virtus} is concerned with what is difficult.[^3] But first I [demolish]{g:tollere} this cunning. It is evident, indeed, that many things are lawful which, on account of their inferiority, are Antichrist's and foreign to clerics, so that they would sin gravely if, leaving noble work aside, they gave themselves to ignoble work; and so, on account of the perfection of their state, it would be most shameful [334] for them to be occupied in such things,[^4] as is clear in Christ, who knew not how to act so, since then he would have known how to degrade himself to the lay state and order, and consequently would have known how to do evil and to sin. The prudence of the clergy, therefore, consists in declining such imperfect work, as it would be good in lay people who give themselves purely to the school of Christ. We should note, then, that of the twelve bishops whom Christ had nurtured in his school, Iscariot alone, loving himself, had given himself to property after the apostolate; for in Acts 1:18 Peter says of Iscariot: "this man hath possessed a field of the reward of iniquity."[^5] Nor is it lawful to believe that Peter lied in this, nor does the gospel, Matthew 26, contradict this [teaching]{g:sentencia};[^6] for since Iscariot had earlier sold the Saviour to the [pontiffs]{g:pontifex} for thirty pieces of silver, meaning to make sure of the price, and believing his master's words that he was soon to die, he could have provided himself with a revenue near Jerusalem, so that, cheated of the gain he seemed to have from the theft of the common purse, he might provide for himself and his own by possessing it as a proprietor; nor [is it incompatible]{g:repugnare} that he took a possession so called in name, or [seisin]{g:sessiva} in the estate, and afterwards, bringing back the money to be given for the field, on hearing that his master was condemned, repenting fruitlessly, hanged himself.[^7] For Judas does not sleep but does his deeds quickly, and it will accord [with this] that that accursed [trade]{g:commercium} was handled throughout with lies.[^8] So then, according to the rule by which Augustine harmonizes the gospels, credence must be given to Peter's gospel as much as to Matthew's;[^9] nor is it without mystery that Judas, a pontiff of the new law, thus traded in secret with the pontiffs of the old testament, for so their adulterous generation is continued; [335] and it is taught how the false pontiffs under the [law of grace]{g:lex-gracie} surpass in malice the pontiffs of the old testament. For they bought a field with the said money, according to a feigned religion, "to be a burying place for strangers", because they shrank from the "price of blood", though the blood was lawfully shed.[^10] But it is said that our pontiffs "put into the corbona" the price of seed, which is more precious and is shed more vilely and more culpably; in which they seem to surpass the old priests in cupidity.[^11] Nor is there doubt that, selling spiritual things, and so Christ, simoniacally, our pontiffs would sin more gravely in pocketing the price; and so (unless I am mistaken) secular exercise of lordship in our bishops will not be founded in the new law or the old, unless perhaps in Iscariot, Ananias and Sapphira, of whom Acts 5 speaks; but all these three were slain for it by divine judgement. And that vengeance is not now taken on ours, as it was in the primitive [church]{g:ecclesia} on those who sinned less, we may impute to our fault and to a sign of greater danger. For God sometimes, out of love for a [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} sinner, rebukes and chastises him, and someone, again, out of love for the people, whom he wills to be preserved from the poison of such a stain; but now it is a sign that, by reason of [participation]{g:participium} in the sin, he has abandoned both the people and the sinners as desperate.[^12] And this seems to me to be the perilous time, such as has not been from the beginning of the world, foretold by Christ and the apostle.[^13] For you will scarcely find one [presbyter]{g:presbiter} or powerful layman who is not entangled, by the deed or the consent of the bishops, in the simoniacal heresy, more or less. And it is clear how horrible is the clergy's desertion of the security of the lot of the Lord, and the preferring of worldly security, in which the world is blasphemously set before God.[^14] Nor does [336] the [topic from likeness]{g:locus-a-simili} taken from the laity avail; for this too would be damnable in a [secular]{g:secularis}, and worldly security added to God's is lawful in him, and not so in a cleric of the law of Christ. For to the clerics of the old law, on whom an intolerable burden was laid, the Lord's portion of [tithes]{g:decime}, oblations and private [alms]{g:elemosina} sufficed, and a strait third part sufficed Christ and his apostles.[^15] But for us the Lord's tenth part does not suffice, enlarged treacherously and claimed in Jewish fashion. But we lyingly claim a third part of the lay estates by [title]{g:titulus} of Christ's possession; and on the one side, with the goods of the poor, of whom we are mere [proctors]{g:procurator}, not having from the Lord a proctor's mandate for this, we ceaselessly acquire secular possessions. And on the other side we extend, magnifying them, the traditions of men that savour of gain, and hence, to the grave harm of the church, the very law of God is far too much passed over. For we say that it is not only lawful but necessary, on account of Caesar's counsel, to set Christ's counsels after it; but that a [dispensation]{g:dispensacio} must necessarily be made in the case of those burdensome traditions which Christ instituted, but that in the case of the donations of men and the rites that follow them it is not lawful for God or man to dispense, on account of sacrilege; in this we without doubt set man, or more truly mammon, before our God.[^16] Let the clergy consider, therefore, how with the goods of fortune the clergy is more prone to fornicate spiritually, slower to come to its senses, and how the bursting of the love of God is altogether more detestable. Therefore those who argue by the topic from the possible to the danger [taken] [as a matter of fact]{g:de-inesse} should consider how [by that argument] any [cloisterer]{g:claustrales} in England can meritoriously have a woman hidden in his bed and study; so [337] it holds: "he can avoid abusing the woman; therefore he ought [to have her]".[^17] Bodily fornication (I say) is not so constant, since carnal fornication must be extinguished when nature fails and avarice grows strong; and consequently [spiritual fornication], as more unnatural and more difficult, is the more hated by the Lord.[^18] Therefore it says in Matthew 11:24: "I say unto you, that it shall be more tolerable for the land of Sodom in the day of judgment than for thee." For we neither dance with the Son of man nor mourn with John the Baptist,[^19] but, leaving aside Christ's [doctrine]{g:doctrina}, easy for us, which he expressed in life, manners and words, of the poor and [propertyless]{g:exproprietarii} life, we follow new saints whose canonization was bought, although for the greater part of their life they lived lyingly; and we add, in commendation of our teaching, that we do not read of our patrons' repentance. I would therefore answer:[^20] We should establish, I say, Jesus for ourselves as patron and rule, setting others after him and obeying them only in so far as they follow him. In confirmation of this we direct our prayers, those we have of the saints, singularly to him.[^21] Let us not, therefore, madly feign the stupidity of our audience in our sermons, for it perceives our sophistries more subtly than we are able to [cloak]{g:palliare} them. The matter of faith (I say), which exceeds [opinion]{g:opinio}, we ought to magnify as far as we are able. So then, removing these marks of faithlessness, of giving more credence to the deeds and words of one sinning [superior]{g:prepositi} than to the word or work of our Jesus, we ought singularly to give ourselves to his doctrine; for truly it is an unbelieving excuse in sins, and suspect to the world, to take our [theme]{g:thema} from Christ and, his doctrine set aside, to lean on one erring superior, though a saint, and especially in these worldly matters, in [338] which they bend away from Christ, adding the [fallacy]{g:fallacia}: "They follow thus; therefore we can lawfully and meritoriously [journey as wayfarers]{g:viator} thus."[^22] And the point of the fallacy follows when it is inferred: "therefore in fact we, keeping the nature of the work, though in a contrary manner, neither act unjustly nor sin." Truly, if this fallacy prevailed, it would be lawful for any [religious]{g:religiosi} to live any secular life in any way whatever.

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 333.20: Heresy of maintaining that bishops should rule because qualified by wisdom.
- 334.1: In ruling they forsake their office for a lower one.
- 335.14: Our bishops found their practices on those of Iscariot, Ananias and Sapphira,
- 336.17: Christ's laws set aside for gainful traditions.
- 337.9: Instead of obeying Christ's teaching we follow modern saints canonized for payment.
- 337.21: We should make Jesus our patron and address all prayers to him.

## Notes

[^1]: **T.** *Defendendis*: Loserth's emendation; the manuscripts read *defendendum* (333.9). We translate the print. **C.** The clergy of Christ's *ostendendum* (333.12), left by Loserth, answers it: both are taken as "for the defending / showing forth of".
[^2]: **S.** Ephesians 5:5. **C.** *Infidelis hereticus* and *hereticus infidelis*: the two nouns in apposition, the order reversed in the second.
[^3]: **S.** The maxim that virtue is concerned with the difficult is Aristotle's (*Nicomachean Ethics* 2.3, 1105a9: "art and virtue are always concerned with what is harder") (LEDGER P12f-1).
[^4]: **C.** *Sunt Antichristi clericis aliena*: the print (confirmed on the plate) is construed "are Antichrist's [things] and foreign to clerics". The sense would run more smoothly with *Christi clericis*, "foreign to the clerics of Christ"; we translate the print.
[^5]: **S.** Acts 1:18; Wyclif omits the Vulgate's *Et … quidem* (DR "And he indeed").
[^6]: **S.** The thirty pieces are Matthew 26:15; the purchase of the field by the chief priests is Matthew 27:3–8, which Wyclif cites as ch. 26.
[^7]: **G.** *Sessinam*: seisin, the feudal possession that perfects title (Loserth, after Matthew: "an English law term"). *Se suspendit* (Matthew 27:5) is "hanged himself"; here *suspendere* carries no pun. **C.** *Possessionem nuncupative dictam*: possession in name only, before the price was paid. **S.** "The theft of the common purse": John 12:6.
[^8]: **S.** "Does his deeds quickly" alludes to John 13:27, "That which thou dost, do quickly."
[^9]: **S.** Augustine, *De consensu evangelistarum*, whose rule is that one evangelist's omitting or telling otherwise what another says is no contradiction, so long as the truth of the facts is the same (e.g. II.19.47; III.17) (LEDGER P12f-2). Acts is called "Peter's gospel" because Peter speaks the verse.
[^10]: **S.** Matthew 27:6–7 (DR). **C.** *Licet effusi licite*: "though [the blood was] lawfully shed", their own judgement of Christ's death.
[^11]: **S.** Matthew 27:6 ("to put them into the corbona"). **C.** *Precium seminis*: the price of seed, i.e. money the bishops take on account of sins of the flesh, set against the "price of blood". What money is meant is not traced: fines or commuted penances for unchastity, or fees for reconciling a church polluted "by blood or seed" (Wyclif's own pairing, *Sermones* IV, 304) (LEDGER P12f-3).
[^12]: **C.** *Quemque autem*: taken as "someone, again" (*quemquam*, *quendam*); the apparatus at 335.26 (B) is cut off in the scan.
[^13]: **S.** 2 Timothy 3:1 ("dangerous times") and Matthew 24:21 ("such as hath not been from the beginning of the world"), by allusion.
[^14]: **S.** *Sors Domini*: the clergy as the Lord's lot (*kleros*), whose portion is the Lord (Numbers 18:20; Deuteronomy 18:2); the derivation is Jerome's, *Ep.* 52.5 (LEDGER P12f-4).
[^15]: **C.** *Tercia pars stricta*: "a strait third part", i.e. a narrower portion still; the sense of "third" is not certain. It answers "a third part of the lay estates" below, the share claimed for the clergy.
[^16]: **C.** *Tradicionibus onustatis*: "burdensome traditions" (as if *onustis*, "laden"), ironic: Christ's ordinances may be dispensed from, men's donations may not. The second *sed* corrects upward ("indeed"); the third is adversative.
[^17]: **C.** The argument parodied is the bishops' at 333.22–26: one can avoid the danger, therefore one ought to take up the life. *Sic valet*: "so it holds", introducing the inference as the cloisterer would draw it.
[^18]: **C.** The subject of *est … odita* is not expressed; it is taken as spiritual fornication (avarice), contrasted with bodily fornication, which nature ends.
[^19]: **S.** Matthew 11:17–19, by allusion ("We have piped to you, and you have not danced: we have lamented, and you have not mourned").
[^20]: **C.** *Igitur respondebam*: the epistolary imperfect, opening Wyclif's reply ("I would therefore answer"), which follows in *Constitueremus*. The "commendation" is ironic: we cannot read that our bought saints ever repented.
[^21]: **C.** The prayers of the saints' days end "through our Lord Jesus Christ"; *dirigimus* is taken as indicative, a fact that confirms the rule. Loserth's side-note reads it as an exhortation.
[^22]: **C.** *Ipsi sic sequuntur*: "they" are the erring saints, following their way. *Viare*: to make one's way as a wayfarer.
