# *De eucharistia*, chapter 6, part 5: false allegations for transubstantiation; three resolutions; what disposes a man for the sacrament

## ¶1 · 174.1–174.32

> [p. 174] Ideo fabulantes talia sompnia viderent primo quomodo sancti et philosophi loquuntur de descripcione et de distinccione substancie et accidentis. Multi enim locuntur de signis huiusmodi sine sensu, et multi iudaice fabricant allegaciones corruptas, ut allegant fundacionem transubstanciacionis sue in illo Math. VI°, 11: Panem nostrum supersubstancialem da nobis hodie, sed hii non minus delirant quam falsi reportatores Christi qui loco istius dicti Johannis II°, 19: Solvite templum hoc et in tribus diebus excitabo illud. Hic dixit, inquiunt, possum destruere templum Dei et post triduum reedificare illud Math. XXVI°, 61. Nam longe plus differunt panis ille substancialis et sua transubstanciacio quam differant solvere et destruere, excitare et reedificare. Pictores autem vocant colores corpora liquida que illiniunt corporibus solidis, que debent ex hoc colorari. Et ita videtur decretalem grosse loqui de accidentibus vocando grave liquidum inebriatum generativum vini quod inmiscet se vino aut aqua post consecracionem accidenter; quod alii specificant per quantitatem quam dicunt ternam dimensionem que potest esse vanum. Que quantitas foret eque activa ut vinum, sive fuerit per se sive in subiecto. Sed philosophi non sic locuntur de quantitate quod sit de genere activorum racione qualitatis active quod subiectet, sicut nec qualitates prime agunt acciones qualitatis secunde quam causant, nec potest quantitas secundum diffinicionem Augustini sic per se variari; nec umquam conceperunt priores sancti illud insensibile cotidianum miraculum, ymmo pocius vocatur irracionabilis Dei crudelitas sanctificare per se accidencia destruendo substanciam.

Therefore those who tell such dreams as fables should see first how the [saints]{g:sancti} and the philosophers speak of the [definition]{g:descripcio} and the distinction of [substance]{g:substancia} and [accident]{g:accidens}. For many speak of signs of this kind without sense, and many in Jewish fashion fabricate corrupt [allegations]{g:allegacio}, as they allege the foundation of their [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio} in that [text], Matt. 6:11: "Give us this day our supersubstantial bread";[^1] but these rave no less than the false reporters of Christ who, in place of that saying, John 2:19, "Destroy this temple; and in three days I will raise it up," [said]: "This man said," they say, "I am able to destroy the temple of God and after three days to rebuild it," Matt. 26:61.[^2] For that substantial bread and their transubstantiation differ far more than *solvere* and *destruere*, "raise up" and "rebuild", differ. Now painters call "colours" the liquid bodies which they smear on solid bodies that are to be coloured by this. And so the [decretal]{g:decretalis} seems to speak grossly of accidents, calling [accidents] a heavy, intoxicated liquid, generative of wine, which mingles itself with the wine or water after [consecration]{g:consecracio} accidentally;[^3] which others specify by [quantity]{g:quantitas}, which they say is the threefold dimension that can be a void. And that quantity would be as active as wine, whether it were [of itself]{g:per-se} or in a [subject]{g:subiectum}. But philosophers do not speak of quantity thus, as though it were of the genus of active things by reason of an active [quality]{g:qualitas} which it subjects, just as neither do the primary qualities perform the actions of the secondary quality which they cause; nor can quantity, according to Augustine's definition, vary thus of itself;[^4] nor did the earlier saints ever conceive that imperceptible daily miracle; indeed it is rather called an irrational cruelty of God, to sanctify accidents of themselves by destroying the substance.

## ¶2 · 175.1–175.5

> [p. 175] Et sic quantum ad omnes allegandos in contrarium, est triplex solucio. Prima quod sunt grossi pictores mendacii intelligentes per accidencia ipsas substancias quas scriptura sacra et sancti doctores intellexerunt per panem et vinum.

And so, as to all who are to be alleged to the contrary, there is a threefold [resolution]{g:solucio}. The first, that they are gross painters of falsehood, understanding by accidents the very substances which holy scripture and the holy doctors understood by [bread]{g:panis} and [wine]{g:vinum}.

## ¶3 · 175.6–175.10

> [p. 175] Secunda solucio est quod intelligunt quod accidens est sine subiecto ibi principaliter in suo genere existente; nam corpus Christi est ibi principalius quam panis aut vinum; cui corpori suspensa consideracione signorum est precipue attendendum.

The second resolution is that they understand "an accident is without a subject existing there [principally]{g:principaliter} in its own genus";[^5] for the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} is there more principally than bread or wine; and to that body, with consideration of the [signs]{g:signum} suspended, attention is chiefly to be paid.[^6]

## ¶4 · 175.11–175.24

> [p. 175] Tercia solucio est quod omnes adversarii veritatis intelligunt accidens esse sine subiecto condicionaliter, si Deus voluerit. Et illam condicionem oportet adversarium (ut docet beatus Jacobus) velit nolit intelligere. Nec habet dictum eorum in hoc efficacius testimonium, nam si per inpossibile Deus posset sic facere, adhuc non dicetur per hoc sic esse de facto; nec displiceret illis dicta glossa sancti Apostoli, cum sic glossatur Augustinus in dicto suo ad maximum hereticum, quando dicit quod Deus filius potuit produxisse ad intra alium filium. Non, inquit, non potuit sed non decuit, hoc est, potuit, si voluerit; et sic non plus quam sancti primitive ecclesie contradicimus in hoc divine potencie.

The third resolution is that all the adversaries of the truth understand "an [accident without a subject]{g:accidens-sine-subiecto}" conditionally: if God should will. And that condition the adversary (as blessed James teaches) must understand, whether he will or not.[^7] Nor has their saying a more efficacious testimony in this, for if, by an impossible supposition, God could do so, still it will not on that account be said to be so in fact; nor would the said gloss of the holy Apostle displease them, since Augustine glosses thus in his saying to Maximinus the heretic, when he says that God the Son could have produced another son within. "Not 'he could not'," he says, "but 'it was not fitting'; that is, he could, if he should will";[^8] and so we no more than the saints of the primitive [church]{g:ecclesia} contradict the divine [power]{g:potencia} in this.[^9]

## ¶5 · 175.25–176.2

> [p. 175] Secundo obicitur contra dicta de fragilitate indisponente hominem ad celebrandum hoc venerabile sacramentum. Nemo, inquam, capit hoc venerabile sacramentum quin sit infinitum fragilis et positus in adopcione bonum temporale ante virtutem eligeret; satis est igitur quod homo non sit mortali actualiter [p. 176] maculatus, licet oporteat quod pedes lavet (ut dicitur Joh. XIII°, 14).

Secondly, [it is objected]{g:obicitur} against what was said about the frailty that [makes]{g:indisponere} a man unfit to celebrate this venerable [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}. No one, I say, takes this venerable sacrament without being infinitely frail and [one who], placed in the [choice]{g:adopcio}, would choose a temporal good before virtue;[^10] it is enough, therefore, that a man not be actually [176] stained with mortal [sin], though he must wash his feet (as is said, John 13:14).

## ¶6 · 176.3–176.22

> [p. 176] Hic sunt multa dicenda; aliqua enim indisponunt hominem simpliciter et aliqua secundum quid, ut communicet cum infidelibus in sacramento altaris; nam peccatum mortale vel infidelitas presciti indisponit eum simpliciter et peccata venialia indisponunt secundum quid; unde quia quilibet nostrum debet presupponere quod sit predestinatus, debet supponere quod hoc sacramentum sibi proficiet, et licet sit indispositus secundum quid per veniale peccatum, tamen est simpliciter dispositus per graciam predestinacionis et per triplicem graciam consequentem (ut notatum est in principio huius VIᵗⁱ capituli). Loquor autem de veniali peccato ut alias; sine cuius noticia vanum est dubitare in ista materia; et sic nemo est infinitum fragilis, cum non attenditur penes hoc quod cicius ant facilius homo posset peccare. Sed multi positi in adopcione eligerent virtutem dimisso vicio; et ita non sufficit carencia mortalis peccati actualis cum malus habitus nimis indisponit hominem ad cultum huius sacramenti.

Here many things are to be said; for some things make a man unfit [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} and some [in a certain respect]{g:secundum-quid}, so that he communicates with [unbelievers]{g:infidelis} in the [sacrament of the altar]{g:sacramentum-altaris};[^11] for mortal sin, or the unbelief of the [foreknown]{g:prescitus}, makes him unfit without qualification, and venial sins make unfit in a certain respect; hence, because each of us ought to presuppose that he is [predestinate]{g:predestinatus}, he ought to suppose that this sacrament will profit him, and though he be unfit in a certain respect through venial sin, yet he is fit without qualification through the grace of predestination and through the threefold consequent grace (as was noted at the beginning of this sixth chapter).[^12] And I speak of venial sin as elsewhere; without knowledge of which it is vain to doubt in this matter; and so no one is infinitely frail, since that is not reckoned by this, that a man could sin more quickly or more easily.[^13] But many, placed in the choice, would choose virtue, leaving vice; and so the lack of actual mortal sin does not suffice, since a bad habit makes a man exceedingly unfit for the worship of this sacrament.

## ¶7 · 176.23–178.30

> [p. 176] Tria ergo sunt disponencia, scilicet Christi dileccio, pax cum proximo et actualis devocio; que tria exprimuntur in illo magno sermone Christi post dacionem huius sacramenti a capitulo XIII Joh. usque ad capitulum XVIII. Quoad dileccionem Christi et pacem proximi dicit Joh. XIII°, 34: Mandatum novum do vobis: ut diligatis invicem, sicut dilexi vos. Et quia illa dileccio convertitur cum dileccione et pace proximi, ideo capitulo XIV°, 21 explicat hanc dileccionem: [p. 177] Si, inquit, diligitis me, mandata mea servate. Et sequitur: Pacem relinquo vobis, pacem meam do vobis; ubi non dubium dat doctrinam tenerrimam ad colendum hoc sacramentum quod paulo ante dedit apostolis: Oportet, inquam, cultorem huius sacramenti radicari et fundari in caritate, sic quod habitualiter Deum amet pre omnibus et omnia in ordine ad ipsum. Unde nimis indisponitur qui peccato invidie, peccato avaricie vel carnis proponunt inordinate creaturas diligere; nam cultus eukaristie nocet tali et facit iniuriam cuilibet creature. Unde in signum plene mundicie observancie huius sacramenti creditur Christum post eius ministerium lavisse humillime pedes discipulorum quod significat mundiciam affeccionum. Unde Augustinus Omelia LVI dicit quod quantumcunque homo mundus fuerit in baptismo, verumtamen cum in rebus humanis postea vivitur et terra pede fedata calcatur: Ipsi, inquit, humani affectus sine quibus in hac mortalitate non vivitur quasi pedes sunt quibus humanis rebus afficimur, et sic afficimur quod si dixerimus quia peccatum non habemus nos ipsos seducimus et veritas in nobis non est. Ideo debemus lavare pedes mentis cotidie, cum oramus: Dimitte nobis debita nostra. Qui ergo est mundus totus secundum partes superiores anime carens mortali crimine indiget ut pedes lavet, cum solum beati sine macula sive ruga qui non calcant hanc terram peregrinancium et incolunt terram vivencium laverunt pedes simpliciter, ut dicitur Cant. Vᵗᵒ, 3. Valet ergo usus eukaristie ad memorandum gesta Christi, ut sit efficacius imitatus, sic quod quicunque celebrat missam vel [p. 178] audit cum oleo devocionis ungit devote corpus Christi cum Magdalena. Unde signanter dicit Christus Matth. XXVI°, 10 contra imitatores Scarioth qui remurmurant contra devotos quos vocant paplardos vel ypocritas, quod poterunt apcius prodesse pauperibus. Quid, inquit, molesti estis huic mulieri? Opus enim bonum operata est in me. Et sequitur: Amen dico vobis, ubicunque predicatum fuerit hoc evangelium, in toto mundo dicetur quod et hoc fecit in memoriam eius. Quicunque, inquam, sic ungit Dominum in sacramento altaris. Nichil debet esse in mundo quoad actus nostros famosius quam quod hoc debet facere in memoriam imitatoriam Jesu Christi; nam subvenire pauperibus est opus predicto inferius, ad quod semper est oportunitas temporis, dum hic vivitur. Sed oportet captare tempus ad sic singulariter celebrandum, quia solum dum est dolor ingratitudinis precedentis et devocio ad colendum corpus Christi pro nobis sic passi mortui et sepulti. Et hec racio quare nostri dicunt quod requiritur intencio consecrandi; requiritur, inquam, devota intencio ad memorandum sic sacramentaliter Salvatorem, quod si defuerit, est Christi irrisio et despectiva super Christum humoris fetentis infusio. Digne ergo manducatum corpus Christi valet pro vivis et mortuis, ut pena pro peccatis debita remittatur, ut vivus ex devocione amplius mereatur, et ut sanctum merendi propositum et per consequens ut gracia firmius et perseverancius habeatur, et sic utrobique delentur culpe tam veniales quam mortales occasione huius sacramenti.

There are therefore three things that dispose, namely the love of Christ, peace with one's neighbour, and actual devotion; which three are expressed in that great sermon of Christ after the giving of this sacrament, from chapter 13 of John to chapter 18. As to the love of Christ and peace with the neighbour, he says, John 13:34: "A new commandment I give unto you: That you love one another, as I have loved you." And because that love [is convertible]{g:converti} with the love of the neighbour and peace with him, therefore in chapter 14:21 he explains this love: [177] "If you love me," he says, "keep my commandments."[^14] And it follows: "Peace I leave with you: my peace I give unto you"; where without doubt he gives a most tender doctrine for worshipping this sacrament which a little before he had given to the apostles. The [worshipper]{g:cultor} of this sacrament, I say, must be rooted and founded in [charity]{g:caritas},[^15] so that habitually he loves God before all things and all things in order to him. Hence he is exceedingly unfit who, through the sin of envy, the sin of avarice or of the flesh, purpose to love creatures inordinately;[^16] for the worship of the eucharist harms such a man and does injury to every creature. Hence, in sign of the full cleanness of the observance of this sacrament, it is believed that Christ, after its ministry, most humbly washed the feet of the disciples, which signifies cleanness of the affections. Hence Augustine, Homily 56, says that however clean a man has been in baptism, nevertheless when one lives afterwards among human things, the earth too is trodden, [and] fouled by the foot:[^17] "The human affections themselves," he says, "without which one does not live in this mortality, are as it were feet by which we are affected toward human things, and we are so affected that 'if we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves and the truth is not in us.'" Therefore we ought to wash the feet of the mind daily, when we pray: "Forgive us our debts." He, therefore, who is wholly clean according to the higher parts of the soul, lacking mortal crime, needs to wash his feet, since only the blessed, without spot or wrinkle, who do not tread this earth of pilgrims and inhabit the land of the living, have washed their feet without qualification, as is said, Cant. 5:3.[^18] The use of the eucharist therefore avails for remembering the deeds of Christ, so that he may be more efficaciously imitated, so that whoever celebrates mass or [178] hears it with the oil of devotion devoutly anoints the body of Christ with the Magdalene. Hence Christ says pointedly, Matt. 26:10, against the imitators of Iscariot who murmur against the devout, whom they call [paplards]{g:paplardus} or hypocrites,[^19] [saying] that they could more fitly benefit the poor: "Why," he says, "do you trouble this woman? For she hath wrought a good work upon me." And it follows: "Amen I say to you, wheresoever this gospel shall be preached in the whole world, that also which she hath done shall be told for a memory of her."[^20] Whoever, I say, thus anoints the Lord in the sacrament of the altar. Nothing ought to be [better known]{g:famosius} in the world, as to our acts, than that one ought to do this in imitative memory of Jesus Christ;[^21] for to relieve the poor is a work inferior to the aforesaid, for which there is always opportunity of time while one lives here. But one must seize the time to celebrate thus singularly, because [it is the time] only while there is sorrow for the preceding ingratitude and devotion to worship the body of Christ, who thus suffered, died and was buried for us. And this is the reason why ours say that the intention of consecrating is required;[^22] there is required, I say, a devout intention to remember the Saviour thus [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter}, which if it be lacking, it is a mockery of Christ and a contemptuous pouring of stinking humour over Christ. The body of Christ worthily eaten, therefore, avails for the living and the dead, that the penalty owed for sins may be remitted, that the living may merit more from devotion, and that the holy purpose of meriting, and consequently grace, may be held more firmly and more perseveringly; and so [on both sides]{g:utrobique} faults both venial and mortal are blotted out on the occasion of this sacrament.

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 174.1: False arguments for transsubstantiation.
- 175.1: Three solutions of objections: I. Our opponents call accidents what Scripture calls bread and wine.
- 175.6: 2. Bread and wine are said not to exist in the sense that we need not care tor them.
- 175.11: 3. We do not limit God's power, but say that the acts ascribed to Him are contrary to His nature.
- 175.25: II. As to the disposition needed for the celebrant. (Cf. 163, 12.)
- 176.3: Not only actual mortal sin may indispose us, but a bad habit.
- 176.23: The right disposition is given by: 1. Love of Christ. 2. Peace with our neighbour. 3. Devoutness.
- 177.5: He who would communicate rightly must love God above all things and all things in relation to Him.
- 178.26: Great profit of the sacrament when rightly taken.

## Notes

[^1]: **S/T.** Matt. 6:11, the Vulgate's *supersubstantialem*, on which the allegation rests. 174.7 ABCD read *super subiectalem*. 174.4: the manuscripts lack *modi* of *huiusmodi*; the print supplies it.
[^2]: **S/T.** John 2:19 and Matt. 26:61, both in the Douay–Rheims, which renders both *solvere* and *destruere* "destroy"; Wyclif's comparison turns on the two Latin verbs, which are therefore left in Latin in the next sentence. 174.9: the manuscripts read *Johannis III*; Loserth corrected it to II.
[^3]: **C/S.** The object of *vocando* is unexpressed: the decretal is taken to call "accidents" this heavy liquid. The decretal is *X* 3.41.6, *Cum Marthe*, as at 171.16: *si post calicis consecrationem aliud vinum mittatur in calicem … accidentibus prioris vini commixtum … Ipsa tamen accidentia vinum appositum videntur afficere, quia, si aqua pura fuerit apposita, vini saporem assumit* (Friedberg II, 640) (LEDGER K06e-1). *Accidenter* "accidentally" is not the *per accidens* of the glossary. 174.19 ABCD *cum vino*; 174.21 D *trinam*.
[^4]: **S/C.** Augustine's definition of quantity is not located; the *Categoriae decem* then ascribed to him puts quantity among the accidents that *in ipsa usia sunt, et sine hac ipsa esse non possunt* (c. 8, PL 32, 1425); cf. *De quantitate animae* 4.6 (LEDGER K06e-2). *Per se* here and in *sanctificare per se accidencia* below is the CONTEXTUAL row's first sense, "of itself"; at 174.21 *sive fuerit per se sive in subiecto* it borders on the spatial sense, apart from a subject.
[^5]: **C.** *Sine subiecto ibi principaliter in suo genere existente*: the participle agrees with *subiecto*, so the accident is said to be without a subject that exists there principally in its own kind; the bread is not there as the principal thing. Proposition in indirect speech, so quoted.
[^6]: **T.** 175.10 *signorum*: the manuscripts read *signum*; Loserth's print.
[^7]: **S.** Jas. 4:15, "If the Lord will" (Loserth 175.14). "The holy Apostle" whose gloss is meant in the next sentence is taken to be James, the gloss being this condition.
[^8]: **S/T.** The print reads *ad maximum hereticum* (plate n246, no variant), "to the greatest heretic"; we translate *Maximinum* (our emendation): Augustine's work against the Arian bishop is titled *Contra Maximinum haereticum*, so *maximum* has lost a syllable; and the passage is *Contra Maximinum* II.12.3 (PL 42, 768): *Filius autem non genuit creatorem. Neque enim non potuit, sed non oportuit*; it is the Son who does not beget, so the print's *Deus filius* stands, and Wyclif's *non decuit* renders *non oportuit* (LEDGER K06e-3). 175.20 AB *quando dicunt*, CE *cum dicit*; 175.22 D *non potuit,*.
[^9]: **C.** *Potencia* is the CONTEXTUAL row potency / power: here God's power.
[^10]: **C.** *Positus in adopcione* (also 176.17): *adopcio* taken in its root sense, a choosing (*adoptare*, to choose), so "placed in the choice", given the option. The construction *quin sit … et … eligeret* joins a present subjunctive and an imperfect. 175.25–26 ABCE *indisposite*; 175.27 ABCE *venerabile* deest.
[^11]: **C.** *Ut communicet cum infidelibus*: taken as the result of being unfit, that he shares the sacrament as the unbelievers do. *Infidelitas presciti* is "the unbelief of the foreknown": *prescitus* is Wyclif's term for those whom God foreknows will be damned.
[^12]: **C.** The cross-reference is to the opening of this chapter, where the threefold grace is set out (Loserth's side-note at 175.25 points to 163.12).
[^13]: **C.** *Cum non attenditur penes hoc*: infinite frailty is not measured by how readily a man could sin. The print's *ant* is read as *aut*.
[^14]: **S.** The words are John 14:15; Wyclif's reference is 14:21, the verse that restates the same link of love and keeping the commandments. Then John 14:27 (Loserth 177.2).
[^15]: **S.** "Rooted and founded in charity": Eph. 3:17, given in Wyclif's own construction and so not quoted.
[^16]: **C.** *Qui … proponunt*: a singular subject with a plural verb in the print; translated as it stands. 177.21 ABDE read *peccata*.
[^17]: **S/C.** Augustine, *In Ioh.* tract. 56.4 (Loserth 177.15; LEDGER K06e-4); the quotation inside is 1 John 1:8. *Terra pede fedata calcatur*: *fedata* agrees with *terra*; the earth is trodden, and fouled by the foot. 177.17 ABCE lack *et*; C has *pede* in another hand.
[^18]: **S.** Cant. 5:3, "I have washed my feet"; "without spot or wrinkle" echoes Eph. 5:27, and "the land of the living" Ps. 26:13 (Vulgate numbering). 177.23 E *pedes* deest.
[^19]: **G.** *Paplardos*: the vernacular *papelard*, a hypocrite or sanctimonious flatterer, given beside *ypocritas*.
[^20]: **S.** Matt. 26:10 and 26:13, in the Douay–Rheims; Wyclif's *quod et hoc fecit* for the Vulgate *et quod haec fecit* does not change the sense. The anointing woman is identified with Mary Magdalene in the sentence before, as in the medieval reading.
[^21]: **G.** *Famosius*: here a plain comparative, "better known", not the formula "as best known" of the glossary row.
[^22]: **C/S.** *Nostri*, "ours", our doctors; the teaching that the intention of consecrating is required is the common one (e.g. Thomas, *ST* III q. 64 a. 8); the devout intention to remember the Saviour that follows is Wyclif's own gloss on it (LEDGER K06e-5). 178.1 ABC read *debite corpus* for *devote corpus*; 178.18 C has *colendum* in the margin in another hand.
