# *De potestate pape*, chapter 6, part 5: the eighth teaching: antichrist, and the first six conditions of the pseudo-pope

## ¶1 · 118.6–119.6

> [p. 118] Octava sentencia est: Romano pontifice vel quocumque alio fingente se gerere primatum vicarie potestatis Christi in terris, ab eius semita declinante secundum sensum scripture nullus plenius vel periculosius gerit de facto nomen et opera Antichristi. Pro declaracione istius sentencie notandum est primo secundum modum loquendi scripture: Quod quicumque est Christo vel legi sue contrarius, dicitur Antichristus, et concordat literalis composicio, cum Antichristus dicitur ab anti, quod est contra, et Christus quasi contra Christum. Et isto modo loquitur Evangelista in prima epistola sua canonica II cap. 18: Et sicut audistis, inquit, quia Antichristus venit, nunc Antichristi multi facti sunt. Et illos vocat Christus Matth. XXIV cap. Pseudochristos a pseudo quod latine dicitur falsus vel decipiens, et sic quilibet dyabolus vel membrum eius est realiter antichristus. Unde videtur michi illos esse culpandos qui preter auctoritatem scripture sompniant quod de tribu Dan in illo loco cum equis et curru igneis et ceteris ficticiis dominabitur Antichristus. Doctores autem qui sic loquuntur vel ad modum loquendi propheticum loquuntur mistice vel videntur ad modum concionatoris fingere. Certum est itaque quod persona prescitorum aggregata, est unus Antichristus, sicut quotlibet membrum eius, et illius erit unus capitalis in malicia plus [p. 119] accensus; ymo videtur predestinatum, dum crimine fuerit Christo contrarius, dici posse realiter Antichristum, modo quo ille qui dixit et facta sunt Mat. XVI propter adversacionem nominat Petrum sathanam. Est igitur christiano necessarium cognoscere Antichristum.

The eighth [teaching]{g:sentencia} is this: when the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex}, or anyone else who feigns that he bears the [primacy]{g:primatus} of the vicarial [power]{g:potestas} of Christ on earth, turns aside from his path, then, according to the sense of scripture, no one bears more fully or more dangerously, in fact, the name and the works of Antichrist.[^1] To make this teaching plain it is to be noted, first, according to scripture's manner of speaking, that whoever is contrary to Christ or to his law is called Antichrist; and the literal composition of the word agrees, since Antichrist is named from *anti*, which means "against", and *Christus*, as if "against Christ". And in this way the Evangelist speaks in his first canonical epistle, chapter 2:18: "And as you have heard," he says, "that Antichrist cometh, now there are become many Antichrists."[^2] And Christ in Matthew 24 calls them "false christs" (Matt. 24:24), from *pseudo*, which in Latin is said "false" or "deceiving"; and so every devil, or [member]{g:membrum} of the devil, is really antichrist. Hence it seems to me that those are to be blamed who, beyond the [authority]{g:auctoritas} of scripture, dream that Antichrist, of the tribe of Dan, will lord it in that place with fiery horses and chariot and the other [fictions]{g:ficticia}.[^3] But the doctors who speak so either speak mystically, after the prophetic manner of speaking, or seem to invent after the manner of a preacher. It is certain, then, that the aggregate person of [the foreknown]{g:prescitus} is one Antichrist, as is any member of it; and of that [Antichrist] there will be one chief, more [119] inflamed in malice;[^4] indeed it seems that [a predestinate]{g:predestinatus} [person], while by crime he is contrary to Christ, can really be called Antichrist, in the way in which he who "spoke, and they were made" calls Peter Satan for his opposition, Matthew 16 (16:23).[^5] It is therefore necessary for a Christian to know Antichrist.

## ¶2 · 119.7–119.30

> [p. 119] Secundo notandum, quod de clero necesse est Antichristum trahere suam originem; cum enim non omnes sacerdotes confirmati sunt, necesse est quod aliqui propter status excellenciam cadant profundius. Sic magnus Antichristus fuit Lucifer cum suis complicibus, sic magnus Antichristus fuit Scarioth cum avaris suis sequentibus, et ita de generacione nequam, que processit Cayn usque ad novissimum reprobandum, unde beatus Evangelista II epistola canonica concedit, quod multi seductores exierunt in mundum, qui non confitentur Jhesum Christum venisse in carne; hic est seductor et antichristus, unde prima epistola IV, cap.: Multi pseudoprophete exierunt in mundum; et sequitur eorum descripcio Omnis spiritus qui solverit Jhesum ex Deo non est, et hic est Antichristus de quo audistis quoniam venit, et declarat quod ille Antichristus sit mundo deditus et de clero prodiit. Cui conformiter loquitur Christus Matth. XXIV, 5: Multi, inquit, venient in nomine meo, dicentes quia ego sum Christus, et multos seducent, et illos nominat posterius Pseudochristos. Ex quibus videtur quod surgent de clero.

Second, it is to be noted that Antichrist must draw his origin from the [clergy]{g:clerus}; for since not all [priests]{g:sacerdos} are confirmed [in grace], it must be that some, because of the excellence of their state, fall the more deeply.[^6] So Lucifer with his accomplices was a great Antichrist; so Iscariot with his avaricious followers was a great Antichrist; and so of the wicked generation that has proceeded [from] Cain down to the last who is to be reprobated.[^7] Hence the blessed Evangelist in his second canonical epistle grants that "many seducers are gone out into the world who confess not that Jesus Christ is come in the flesh. This is a seducer and an antichrist" (2 John 7); hence the first epistle, chapter 4: "Many false prophets are gone out into the world" (1 John 4:1); and their [definition]{g:descripcio} follows: "Every spirit that dissolveth Jesus is not of God. And this is Antichrist, of whom you have heard that he cometh" (1 John 4:3); and he makes plain that that Antichrist is given to the world and has come forth from the clergy.[^8] In agreement with this Christ speaks in Matthew 24:5: "Many," he says, "will come in my name saying, I am Christ. And they will seduce many"; and later he names them "false christs" (24:24). From which it seems that they will rise from the clergy.

## ¶3 · 119.31–121.9

> [p. 119] Et concordat racio cum antichristus peccato gravissimo et magis subdolo erit Christo contrarius. Sed illud peccatum oportet esse traditoriam [p. 120] ypocrisim, quia illam scit dyabolus magis efficere, et simplices propter velamentum eam minus percipere. Et ad istum sensum vadunt verba Apostoli II epistola ad Thym., II Petri epistola, et epistola Jude cum aliis dictis propheticis, quia indubie talis est apcius instrumentum dyaboli ad simplices seducendum. Quando enim dyabolus videt constantem dileccionem Christi in laycis et cum pietate ceca ignoranciam legis Christi, ex alioque latere avariciam Scariothis in clero, non est sibi placencius crimen propter ingratitudinis et duplicitatis gravedinem, quam seducere christianos in propriis instrumentis; sic enim victus est a Christo. Unde sine assercione ymaginor tercio quod sit unus nominetenus papa pseudonomine, cui iste XI condiciones insint ex instinctu patris mendacii. Primo quod ubi est Christus veritas Joh. XIV, Pseudo ubique innititur mendacio, quod notum est esse contrarium veritati, innititur, inquam, mendacio in verbis, in scriptis et vita, dicendo se propinquissime sequi Christum tamquam eius fidelem vicarium, dum tamen sit ab eo in moribus maxime elongatus, aliter enim non palliaret seducendo populum, quomodo veniret in nomine crucifixi, ut Christus prophetavit Matth. XXIV et Joh. XV. Secundo, ubi Christus regularissime tenuit summam pauperiem, iste Pseudo irregulariter summe seculo innititur; aliter enim non esset de mundo, sed ex Deo, qui per pauperiem vicit mundum, ut prophetatum est I Joh. II et Joh. [p. 121] XVII, cum enim sibi desunt miracula et vite sanctitas; aliter valde pauci suo disciplinatui iungerentur. Modo autem ex instinctu dyaboli totus mundus est mundo deditus, sic ubi Christus renuit oblacione dyaboli omnia regna mundi, ut dicitur Matth. IV, iste Pseudo dicit maniace quod dominetur capitaliter super tota habitabili et per eius licenciam et pacienciam singuli reges regnant.

And reason agrees, since antichrist will be contrary to Christ by the gravest and more crafty sin. Now that sin must be treacherous [120] hypocrisy, for the devil knows it to be the more effective, and the simple, because of its veil, perceive it the less.[^9] And to this sense go the words of the Apostle in the second epistle to Timothy, of the second epistle of Peter and of the epistle of Jude, with other prophetic sayings; for without doubt such a one is the apter instrument of the devil for seducing the simple.[^10] For when the devil sees in the laity a constant love of Christ, and with blind [piety]{g:pietas} an ignorance of Christ's law, and on the other side the avarice of Iscariot in the clergy, no crime is more pleasing to him, for the gravity of its [ingratitude]{g:ingratitudo} and duplicity, than to seduce Christians by their own instruments; for so he was conquered by Christ.[^11] Hence, third, I imagine, without asserting it, that there is one [pope]{g:papa} in name only, by a false name, in whom these eleven conditions are present by the prompting of the father of lies (cf. John 8:44). First: whereas Christ is the truth (John 14:6), the Pseudo everywhere leans on a lie, which is known to be contrary to truth; he leans, I say, on a lie in words, in writings and in life, saying that he follows Christ most nearly as his faithful vicar, while yet he is in his manners most far removed from him. For otherwise he would not [cloak]{g:palliare} [himself] in seducing the people: how [else] would he come in the name of the Crucified, as Christ prophesied in Matthew 24 and John 15?[^12] Second: whereas Christ most regularly kept the highest poverty, this Pseudo irregularly leans in the highest degree on the world; for otherwise he would not be of the world but of God, who conquered the world through poverty, as is prophesied in 1 John 2 and John [121] 17; for since miracles and holiness of life are lacking to him, otherwise very few would be joined to his discipleship.[^13] But as it is, by the prompting of the devil the whole world is given over to the world: so, whereas Christ refused all the kingdoms of the world at the devil's offer, as is said in Matthew 4 (4:8–10), this Pseudo says like a madman that he lords it [as head]{g:capitaliter} over the whole inhabited earth, and that by his [licence]{g:licencia} and sufferance each king reigns.[^14]

## ¶4 · 121.10–121.28

> [p. 121] Tercio, ubi Christus anathemate gravi prohibuit quod non adderetur aliquid impertinens legi sue, ut patet Gal. primo et sepe alibi, iste Pseudo fabricat leges novas, que nusquam reperiuntur in scriptura sacra; in qua secundum Augustinum est omnis veritas, sed sunt legi Christi latenter contrarie. Nec mirum quia sacerdos et propheta paulative declinantes a lege Domini preposituram talis legiferi meruerunt, ut prophetatum est Psalmo IX, 21. Ubi propheta de Antichristo loquens secundum exposicionem sanctorum: Constitue, inquit, legislatorem super eos. Et istam legis translacionem oportet sequi extraneum sacerdotem, quia Heb. VII, 12 dicit Apostolus, quod translato sacerdocio necesse est ut legis translacio fiat. Et iste triplex funiculus, scilicet commentum mendacii, commentum favoris seculi et commentum tradicionis inique sunt basis tocius retis dyaboli.

Third: whereas Christ, under a grave anathema, forbade that anything not pertaining to his law be added to it, as is clear in Galatians 1 (1:8–9) and often elsewhere, this Pseudo fabricates new laws, which are nowhere found in holy scripture (in which, according to Augustine, is all truth), but are secretly contrary to [the law of Christ]{g:lex-christi}.[^15] Nor is it a wonder, for priest and prophet, turning aside little by little from the law of the Lord, have deserved such a lawgiver in the [office of superior]{g:prepositura} [over them], as is prophesied in Psalm 9:21, where the prophet, speaking of Antichrist according to the exposition of the saints, says: "Appoint a lawgiver over them."[^16] And this translation of the law must follow a foreign priest, for in Hebrews 7:12 the Apostle says that "the priesthood being translated, it is necessary that a translation be made of the law".[^17] And this threefold cord, namely the [fabrication]{g:commentum} of falsehood, the fabrication of the world's favour and the fabrication of wicked tradition, are the base of the devil's whole net.[^18]

## ¶5 · 121.29–122.3

> [p. 121] Quarto, ubi Christus elegit discipulos simplices idiotas, mundo pauperes et introitu ad suam religionem fecit plus pauperes, ut patet Matth. XIX; iste Pseudo eligit sibi plus inclitos, callidos et astutos, et prius mundo abiectos elevat in falsos dominos urbis et orbis. Aliter enim foret discipulus magistro nimis dissimilis et ex contrarietate [p. 122] foret societas desoluta, quia Ecclesiastici XIII, 3 scribitur: Quid communicabit cacabus ad ollam? quando enim se colliserint confringetur.

Fourth: whereas Christ chose as disciples simple [unlearned men]{g:idiota}, poor to the world, and on their entry into his religion made them poorer, as is clear in Matthew 19 (19:21, 27), this Pseudo chooses for himself the more renowned, the crafty and the astute, and raises those before cast off by the world into false lords of the city and of the world. For otherwise the disciple would be too unlike his master, and from the contrariety [122] the fellowship would be dissolved; for it is written in Ecclesiasticus 13:3: "What agreement shall the earthen pot have with the kettle? for if they knock one against the other, it shall be broken."[^19]

## ¶6 · 122.4–122.16

> [p. 122] Quinto, ubi Christus precepit suis discipulis ire in mundum universum et predicare indifferenter evangelium docereque omnes gentes servare quecumque eis mandaverat, ut patet Matth. ultimo, iste Pseudo docet et care conducit suos discipulos modo contrario servare oppositum, cum ipse residens in sumptuoso palacio constructo de bonis pauperum, ut alter Cosdroe, nulli predicat et discipulos tales optat, qui in castris conquestis mutescendo resideant nisi forte superbe, blasfeme, eciam scandalose in verba de potestate sua proruperint. Et istarum scolarum promulgata contrarietas confundit seculum.

Fifth: whereas Christ commanded his disciples to go into the whole world and preach the gospel without distinction, and to teach all nations to observe whatsoever he had commanded them, as is clear in the last [chapter] of Matthew, this Pseudo teaches his disciples, and hires them dearly, to observe the opposite in the contrary way; since he himself, residing in a sumptuous palace built from the goods of the poor, like another Chosroes, preaches to no one, and wants such disciples as sit in conquered castles keeping silent, unless perhaps they break out, proudly, blasphemously, even scandalously, into words about his power.[^20] And the published contrariety of these schools confounds the world.

## ¶7 · 122.18–122.29

> [p. 122] Sexto, ubi docuit Christus suos discipulos possidere et servare vitas suas quietas atque perpetuas in paciendo tribulaciones atque iniurias, ut patet Matth. V et Luce XXI, iste Pseudo docet verbo et opere suos discipulos ex immensitate sue dignitatis quoscumque eis contrarios debellare, quia aliter paulative periret ab eis dominium; et sic sunt in capitulo impiorum, de quibus Sapiencie II, 1 scribitur: Dixerunt impii cogitantes apud se non recte: Exiguum et cum tedio est tempus vite nostre, non est refrigerium in fine hominis, et cetera que sequuntur.

Sixth: whereas Christ taught his disciples to possess and keep their lives quiet and perpetual in suffering tribulations and injuries, as is clear in Matthew 5 and Luke 21 (21:19), this Pseudo teaches his disciples by word and deed, out of the immensity of his dignity, to war down whoever is contrary to them, because otherwise [lordship]{g:dominium} would little by little perish from them; and so they are in the chapter of the wicked, of whom it is written in Wisdom 2:1: "The wicked have said, reasoning with themselves, but not right: The time of our life is short and tedious, in the end of a man there is no remedy", and the rest that follows.[^21]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 118.6: A pontiff who claims to be Christ's Vicar while leaving his way is the worst of Antichrists.
- 118.29: The whole body of the reprobate are Antichrist; so is each of its members.
- 119.7: Antichrist must originate from the clergy.
- 119.31: The special sin of Antichrist is hypocrisy.
- 120.13: A man may be a pope in name, yet have the conditions of Antichrist.
- 120.21: 1. He says he is a follower of Christ, yet goes and leads others astray.
- 120.27: 2. He does not imitate Christ in his poverty,
- 121.1: but claims rule over the whole world.
- 121.10: 3. Against Christ's prohibition he makes new laws not founded in Scripture.
- 121.29: Christ chose poor simple men for his disciples; the pope chooses such as are illustrious or astute.
- 122.4: 5. Christ bade his disciples to go out through the world preaching; the false pope dwells in a rich palace and does not preach.
- 122.18: 6. Christ taught his disciples to submit to persecution; the pope teaches his to fight for dominion.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Romano pontifice … declinante* is an ablative absolute: the sentence states a condition ("when, or if, the Roman pontiff … turns aside") and then denies that anyone bears Antichrist's name more fully. *Eius semita* is Christ's path. AB in marg.: *Antichristus quis est secundum scripturam*.
[^2]: **S.** 1 John 2:18. Wyclif omits the Vulgate's *et* before *nunc* (DR "even now"); the English follows his Latin. B in marg. at 118.16: *Anti valet contra*.
[^3]: **S/C.** The legend of Antichrist's birth from the tribe of Dan is patristic (Irenaeus, *Adv. haer.* 5.30.2, on Jer. 8:16; LEDGER P06e-1). *In illo loco* names no place (perhaps Jerusalem or its temple). The fiery horses and chariot recall Elijah's (4 Kings 2:11). *Dominabitur*: "lord it", the pejorative sense (GLOSSARY *dominari*).
[^4]: **C.** *Persona … aggregata*: the foreknown taken together as one person, the body whose head is the devil. *Quotlibet* is read as *quodlibet*. Loserth's side-note renders *prescitorum* "the reprobate"; the GLOSSARY's "the foreknown" is kept. AB in marg. at 118.27: *De Antichristo*.
[^5]: **S/R.** "He spoke, and they were made": Ps. 148:5 (also 32:9), naming Christ as the creator Word. Matt. 16:23, "Go behind me, Satan". A in marg. at 119.1: *Nota contradicit sibi de ecclesia*, a reader's charge that calling a predestinate man Antichrist contradicts Wyclif's doctrine of the church.
[^6]: **C.** *Confirmati*: confirmed in grace, i.e. made unable to fall; "[in grace]" is supplied. Loserth compares Wyclif, *De Antichristo* = *Opus evangelicum* III, 6 (editor, LEDGER P06e-2). A in marg.: *Antichristus ex clero*.
[^7]: **T.** *Que processit Cayn* will not construe as printed, and the plate shows no lost word: a preposition (*a* or *de*) is wanting before *Cayn*. "[from]" is supplied (our emendation); no variant is recorded.
[^8]: **C.** *Declarat*: Wyclif takes the Evangelist to show that Antichrist is "given to the world" (1 John 4:3, "he is now already in the world") and came "from the clergy" (cf. 1 John 2:19, "they went out from us"). The second identification is ours.
[^9]: **C.** *Illam scit dyabolus magis efficere* is read "the devil knows it [hypocrisy] to accomplish more"; it may also mean "the devil knows how to work it the better". *Sed* brings the next premiss, hence "now".
[^10]: **S.** The texts meant are probably 2 Tim. 3:1–5 (those "having an appearance indeed of godliness"), 2 Pet. 2:1–3 and Jude 4; the identification is ours.
[^11]: **C.** *In propriis instrumentis* is ambiguous: the Christians' own instruments (their clergy), or Christ's own instruments turned against him. *Sic enim victus est a Christo* then gives the devil's motive: he repays Christ in kind for his own defeat. *Dileccio* is "love" (GLOSSARY *caritas*).
[^12]: **C/S.** *Pseudo*, the Greek prefix used as a noun, is kept as "the Pseudo" throughout the eleven conditions. The *quomodo* clause is read as a question ("how else"). Matt. 24:5; "John 15" as printed; the nearest text is John 5:43, "if another shall come in his own name" (our suggestion). The *ndelem* of the print is *fidelem*. Apparatus 120.18, 20: codd. *innitatur* for *innititur*; A *tantum* for *tamen*. Loserth compares Wyclif's other lists of Antichrist's conditions, *Opus evangelicum* III–IV and *De Christo et suo adversario Antichristo* cap. 11 (*Pol. Works* II, 680), which has twelve (editor, LEDGER P06e-3).
[^13]: **C.** *Regularissime … irregulariter* plays on *regula*, the rule of a religious life. *De mundo … ex Deo*: 1 John 2:15–16 and John 17:14–16 (our identification). The *cum … aliter* clause is loose: since he lacks miracles and holiness, only worldliness draws followers. *Disciplinatus*, "discipleship", the body of his disciples.
[^14]: **T/C.** Codd. *dicat* (apparatus 121.6); the print's *dicit* is translated. *Totus mundus est mundo deditus* plays on *mundus* as the whole race and as the world opposed to God. *Dominetur capitaliter*: "lords it as head", the pejorative sense of *dominari*. *Tota habitabili*: the inhabited earth (*oikoumene*).
[^15]: **S.** Gal. 1:8–9. Augustine: probably *De doctrina christiana* 2.42.63 (PL 34, 65): *quidquid homo extra didicerit, si noxium est, ibi damnatur; si utile est, ibi invenitur* (LEDGER P06e-4).
[^16]: **S.** Ps. 9:21; Wyclif omits the Vulgate's *Domine* (DR "Appoint, O Lord, a lawgiver over them"). The reading of the verse as Antichrist is Augustine's, *Enarr. in Ps.* 9.20 (PL 36, 125: *Videtur mihi Antichristum significare*; LEDGER P06e-5). *Preposituram talis legiferi*: literally "the superiorship of such a lawgiver".
[^17]: **S/C.** Heb. 7:12; Wyclif omits *enim* and *et* (DR "For … a translation also"). *Istam legis translacionem oportet sequi extraneum sacerdotem* may also be construed "a foreign priest must follow this translation of the law"; Hebrews sets the priesthood first and the law after it, which supports the rendering.
[^18]: **S/C.** The threefold cord is Eccles. 4:12 ("a threefold cord is not easily broken"). The three fabrications answer to the first three conditions: the lie, the world, the new laws. *Commentum* is "fabrication" (GLOSSARY).
[^19]: **S/T.** Matt. 19:21, 27. Ecclus. 13:3 agrees with the Vulgate; DR is quoted as it stands. Apparatus 122.3: codd. *colliderint*; B *refringentur*, A *confringentur*; the print is translated. The print's *m falsos* is *in falsos*.
[^20]: **S/R.** Matt. 28:19–20 combined with Mark 16:15 ("Go ye into the whole world"). Chosroes II of Persia, who in the legend of the Exaltation of the Cross sat enthroned as a god in a costly tower (Jacobus de Voragine, *Legenda aurea* cap. 137, *De exaltatione sanctae crucis*, 14 September, ed. Graesse p. 606; LEDGER P06e-6). B *Cosdree*. *Castra conquesta* are castles taken by conquest; not the friars' "Caim's castles" (*castra caynitica*).
[^21]: **S/C.** Luke 21:19, "In your patience you shall possess your souls". Wis. 2:1: Wyclif adds *impii* and omits *enim* and the second *et* (DR "For they have said … and in the end of a man"); the English follows his Latin. *Capitulum* is both an assembly (a chapter) and a chapter of the book: the wicked of Wisdom 2.
