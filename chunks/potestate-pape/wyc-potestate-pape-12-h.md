# *De potestate pape*, chapter 12, part 8: the saints against the laws of men; the sext's rule on rival provisions

## ¶1 · 342.19–343.31

> [p. 342] Et de ista subtraccione a libertate legis Christi post multiplicitatem legum Antichristi conquesti sunt sancti; quod patet in exemplo triplici, primo ex decreto beati Augustini ad inquisiciones Januarii superius recitato et ponitur in decretis XII distinccione: Omnia; secundo ex dicto beati Jeronymi epistola CXVIII ad Domicianum de morte Fabiole, ubi exponens illud evangelium Matth. XIX, 9, ubi dominus precepit viris uxorem non debere dimittere, excepta causa fornicacionis, et dimissam manere innuptam sic loquitur: Quicquid viris iubetur, hoc consequenter redundat ad feminas, neque enim adultera uxor dimittenda est [p. 343] et vir mechus tenendus. Si quis meretrici coniungitur unum corpus efficitur, igitur que scortatori impuroque sociatur, unum cum eo corpus efficitur. Alie, inquit, sunt leges cesarum, alie Ihesu Christi, aliud Papinianus, aliud Paulus noster precipit. Apud illos viris inpudicicie frena laxantur, et solo stupro atque adulterio condempnato, passim per lupanaria atque ancillulas libido permittitur, quasi culpam dignitas faciat, non voluptas; apud nos quod non licet feminis, eque non licet viris. Igitur in lege Christi oportet fidelem quiescere, et alias leges, nisi de quanto consonant legi Christi, contempnere, quod sepe oportet de vocata lege ecclesie. Unde beatus Bernhardus libro primo ad Eugenium sic effatur: Quid servilius indigniusque presertim summo pontifici, quem non dico omni die, sed pene omni hora insudare talibus rebus et pro talibus. Deinde quando oramus, quando docemus populos, quando edificamus ecclesiam, quando meditamur in lege? Et quidem cottidie perstrepunt in pallacio leges, sed Iustiniani non Domini. Si iustum istud, tu videris, nam certe lex Domini immaculata convertens animas. Hec autem non tam leges quam lites sunt et cavillaciones subvertentes iudicium. Tu igitur pastor et episcopus animarum, qua mente obsecro sustines coram te, semper silere illam, garrire istas? Fallor, si non movet tibi scrupulum perversitas hec, puto quod interdum compellat te clamor ad Dominum cum propheta: Narraverunt michi iniqui fabulaciones, sed non ut lex tua.

And of this withdrawal from the freedom of the law of Christ, in following after the multitude of Antichrist's laws, the saints have complained;[^1] as is clear in a threefold example: first from the decree of blessed Augustine to the inquiries of Januarius, recited above, which is placed in the Decretum, distinction 12, [the canon] *Omnia*;[^2] second from the saying of blessed Jerome in letter 118 to Domitianus on the death of Fabiola, where, expounding that gospel, Matthew 19:9, where the Lord commanded husbands that a wife ought not to be put away except for the cause of fornication, and that one put away should remain unmarried, he speaks thus:[^3] "Whatever is commanded to men redounds consequently to women; for neither is an adulterous wife to be put away [343] and an adulterous husband to be kept. If anyone is joined to a harlot, he is made one body; therefore she who is united to a fornicator and unclean man is made one body with him.[^4] The laws of the Caesars," he says, "are one thing, those of Jesus Christ another; Papinian commands one thing, our Paul another. Among them the reins of unchastity are loosened for men, and, with only seduction and adultery condemned, lust is permitted everywhere through brothels and slave-girls, as if rank, not pleasure, made the fault; with us what is not lawful for women is equally not lawful for men."[^5] Therefore the faithful man must rest in the law of Christ and despise other laws, except in so far as they accord with the law of Christ; which must often be done with the [so-called]{g:vocatus} law of the [church]{g:ecclesia}. Hence blessed Bernard, in the first book to Eugenius, speaks thus:[^6] "What is more servile and more unworthy, especially for the supreme pontiff, than to sweat, I do not say every day, but almost every hour, over such matters and for such [people]?[^7] Then when do we pray, when do we teach the peoples, when do we build up the church, when do we meditate on the law? And indeed every day laws resound in the palace, but Justinian's, not the Lord's. Whether this is just, you must see; for certainly 'the law of the Lord is unspotted, converting souls'. But these are not so much laws as lawsuits and cavils subverting judgment. You then, shepherd and bishop of souls, with what mind, I beseech you, do you bear it before you that the one is always silent and these chatter? I am mistaken if this perversity does not stir a scruple in you; I think that at times it compels you to cry out to the Lord with the prophet: 'The wicked have told me fables: but not as thy law.'"[^8]

## ¶2 · 344.1–344.25

> [p. 344] Ista autem secundum fidem scripture sunt a papa et quolibet episcopo meditanda, et postpositis cunctis legibus extraneis effectualiter exequenda. In extraneis enim legibus iacet a lege Christi et per consequens a Deo distraccio, utriusque testamenti offensio et periculosa ac latens iniuriacio. Quis (queso) dubitat, quin occupacio circa leges hominum, que omnes sunt conclusio ad viacionem superflue, de tanto distrahunt pusillam capacitatem anime a lege Domini? Quis insuper hesitat de legislatore Christo, quem oportet omnem salvandum sequi? Psalmus primo, 2 scribitur: In lege eius meditabitur die ac nocte, ymo Deutronomio VIII, 1 dicitur: Omne mandatum quod ego precipio tibi hodie, cave diligenter ut facias. Et quia ad hoc requiritur mandatorum noticia; ideo Deutronomio VI, 6, precipitur post precepcionem observancie omnium mandatorum erunt verba hec, que ego precipio tibi hodie, in corde tuo, et narrabis ea filiis tuis et meditaberis sedens in domo tua et ambulans in itinere, dormiens atque consurgens. Et Joh. XIV, 15 ab equo dicitur: Si quis diligit me, sermonem meum servabit. Nichil enim attencius mandatur a Domino, quam iugis meditacio legis Dei.

And these things, according to the faith of scripture, are to be meditated on by the [pope]{g:papa} and by every [bishop]{g:episcopus}, and, all foreign laws being set aside, carried out in effect. For in foreign laws lies a distraction from the law of Christ, and consequently from God, an offence against both testaments, and a dangerous and hidden wronging. Who, I ask, doubts that busying oneself with men's laws, which are all [*conclusio*] superfluous to [wayfaring]{g:viacio}, draws the small capacity of the soul away from the law of the Lord by so much?[^9] Who, moreover, hesitates about Christ the lawgiver, whom everyone who is to be saved must follow?[^10] In the first Psalm, [verse] 2, it is written: "on his law he shall meditate day and night"; indeed in Deuteronomy 8:1 it is said: "All the commandments, that I command thee this day, take great care to observe." And because for this a knowledge of the commandments is required, therefore in Deuteronomy 6:6, after the commanding of the observance of all the commandments, it is commanded: "these words which I command thee this day, shall be in thy heart: and thou shalt tell them to thy children, and thou shalt meditate sitting in thy house, and walking on thy journey, sleeping and rising."[^11] And in John 14[:23] it is said: "If any one love me, he will keep my word."[^12] For nothing is more carefully commanded by the Lord than continual meditation on the law of God.

## ¶3 · 344.26–345.10

> [p. 344] Et quantum ad tercium patet, quod in legibus humanis est fraudis et iniurie plenitudo. Patet hoc de principiis vel maximis legum ecclesie, et multo magis de conclusionibus ex illis sequentibus. Nam libro VI de constitucionibus datur pro regula, quod duobus super eadem provisione litteras a papa habentibus una die, is, cui papa [p. 345] canonicatum contulit preferendus est ei cui non dedit canonicatum, ignorante executore quis prius impetraverit. Si autem sint pares in gracia, quoad formam stili papalis, is qui prius presentavit erit pocior in prebenda. Et tercio si equales fuerint in hiis tribus, tunc canonici, quibus spectat collacio, vel maior pars eorum tenetur eligere reliquo carente fructu gracie nisi ex tenore papalium litterarum expresse appareat, quod papa utrique illorum voluerit providere.

And as to the third, it is clear that in human laws there is a fullness of fraud and injury. This is clear of the principles or maxims of the laws of the church, and much more of the conclusions following from them. For in the Sixth Book, [under] "On constitutions", it is given as a rule that "when two have letters from the pope on the same provision on one day, he on whom the pope [345] conferred a canonry is to be preferred to him on whom he did not bestow a canonry, if the executor does not know which [obtained]{g:impetrare} [his letters] first. But if they are equal in the grace, as to the form of the papal style, he who presented first shall have the stronger right in the prebend. And third, if they are equal in these three, then the canons to whom the [conferral]{g:collacio} belongs, or the greater part of them, are bound to choose, the other lacking the fruit of the grace, unless it appears expressly from the tenor of the papal letters that the pope wished to provide for both of them."[^13]

## ¶4 · 345.11–345.18

> [p. 345] Ista autem trimembris maxima videtur in toto esse principium contra Christum, quia supposito sicut in maiori parte contingit, quod prepositus in canonicatu vel tempore, in presentacione vel ostensione gracie, vel tercio in capitali eleccione sit reliquo quantumlibet indignior quoad Deum, tunc ex ista maxima Antichristi debet iudicari contra Christi iudicium.

Now this three-membered maxim seems to be wholly a principle against Christ; for supposing, as happens for the greater part, that the one preferred in canonry or in time, in presentation or in showing the grace, or, third, in the chapter's election, is ever so much more unworthy than the other as regards God, then by this maxim of Antichrist judgment must be given against the judgment of Christ.[^14]

## ¶5 · 345.19–345.21

> [p. 345] Ex quo manifeste sequitur quod hoc Antichristi porisma sit iniquum contra iusticiam et per consequens contra Christum.

From which [it follows]{g:sequitur} plainly that this [porism]{g:porisma} of Antichrist is wicked, against justice, and consequently against Christ.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Post multiplicitatem legum Antichristi*: *post* taken as "in following after" (the church goes after Antichrist's many laws); it could be temporal, "after the multiplying of".
[^2]: **S.** Augustine, *Ep.* 55 to Januarius (on the multiplying of observances), as *Decretum* D.12 c.12 *Omnia talia*: Loserth gives "Cap. XII" (342.24); the modern form is ours (LEDGER P12h-1). The identification of the letter is to be checked (LEDGER P12h-2).
[^3]: **S.** Jerome, letter on the death of Fabiola: Loserth (342.29) notes that it is *Ep.* 77 and is addressed to Oceanus, not *Ep.* 118 to Domitianus as Wyclif says (PL 22.691) (LEDGER P12h-3). Matthew 19:9; *manere innuptam*, "remain unmarried", is 1 Corinthians 7:11.
[^4]: **S.** Jerome's argument from 1 Corinthians 6:16. **T.** Both manuscripts read *scortatori imponitur sociatur* (343.2–3), and B omits *igitur* (343.2); the print's *impuroque* is Jerome's text.
[^5]: **T.** The manuscripts read *in viris pudicicie* (343.6); the print's *viris inpudicicie* is Jerome's. **R.** A marks the passage *De meretricio*, B *Meretricacio* (343.1). Papinian, the Roman jurist.
[^6]: **S.** Bernard, *De consideratione* 1.4: Loserth gives "Cap. IV. Opp. I, 732" (343.14); to be checked against PL 182 (LEDGER P12h-4). **R.** B notes in the margin (343.15) that human laws chatter above the law of Christ before the pope.
[^7]: **T.** The print has *quem non dico omni die … insudare*, which does not construe; Bernard's sentence requires *quam*, "than to sweat" (our emendation, after the source). B omits *et* at 343.18.
[^8]: **S.** Psalm 18:8 and Psalm 118:85 (Vulgate numbering), within Bernard's text. *Illam … istas*: the law of the Lord is silent, the lawsuits chatter.
[^9]: **T.** *Que omnes sunt conclusio ad viacionem superflue*: *conclusio* does not construe (a stray word or a misreading in the established text); it is left untranslated in brackets. **C.** *Occupacio … distrahunt*: a singular subject with a plural verb; we take the busying with the laws as the subject. *Viacio*: the state of the wayfarer on the road to the homeland.
[^10]: **T.** The manuscripts read *hesitat quin* (344.11); the print's *hesitat de* is translated.
[^11]: **S.** Psalm 1:2; Deuteronomy 8:1 (Wyclif quotes the first clause); Deuteronomy 6:6–7, where Wyclif omits *in eis*, "upon them", after *meditaberis*. **R.** Both manuscripts note in the margin (344.14) that the law of God ought to be studied most diligently.
[^12]: **T.** The print has *Joh. XIV, 15 ab equo*; the words quoted are John 14:23, which the English gives. *Ab equo* does not construe (perhaps for *ab eo*, "by him") and is not translated.
[^13]: **S.** *Liber Sextus* 1.3.14 *Duobus* (De rescriptis): Loserth (344.30) notes that Wyclif's "title II" (De constitutionibus) should be title III, chapter 14; the modern form is ours (LEDGER P12h-5). **G.** *Gracia*: the papal grace (the letters of provision). *Pocior in prebenda*, "the stronger right in the prebend". **T.** At 345.1 the manuscripts read *eo* for *ei*; at 345.3 B reads *impetravit*. **R.** Both manuscripts note (344.30): *Lex papalis patitur instanciam*.
[^14]: **C.** *Prepositus* is here the participle of *preponere*, "the one preferred", not *prepositi* "superiors". *Capitali eleccione*: the election by the chapter (*capitulum*), the third member of the rule. B reads *fit* at 345.16.
