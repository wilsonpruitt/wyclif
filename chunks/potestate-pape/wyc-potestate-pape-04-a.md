# *De potestate pape*, chapter 4, part 1: the vicar of Peter and what election is worth

## ¶1 · 62.10–63.8

> [p. 62] Supposito quod beatus Petrus habuit primatum inter apostolos ad regendum, arbitror quod non solum accepit primatum pro se ipso sed pro eius vicariis succedentibus in ecclesia militante. Cum enim Christus non potest ecclesiam suam deserere et oporteat ipsam militantem post Christum exemplari ab ecclesia apostolica, mandatum est quod, sicut Christus fuit capitaneus cetus apostolici et post ipsum Petrus, sic oportet continue esse capitaneum in ecclesia militante. Sed antequam discuciatur utrum oporteat ipsum esse Romanum pontificem necessarium, arbitror precognoscere aliquas sentencias premittendas. Et primo, qualem oportet esse beati Petri archivicarium et per quid constituitur Petri vicarius. Nec debet fideli verti in dubium quin sicut Petrus ex hinc per se fuit Christi vicarius; quod magistrum suum quoad actus et mores secutus fuit similius, sic oportet Petri vicarium similius sequendo Petrum de corpore ecclesie derivari. [p. 63] Vicarius enim in quantum huiusmodi gerit vicem superioris a quo capit vicariam potestatem. Si igitur in principio quod debet regulare sequencia Petrus non fuisset sic Christi vicarius nisi quia secutus fuit eum propinquius, per locum a sufficienti similitudine oportet quod Petri archivicarius de propinquiori sequatur Christum et per consequens Petrum in moribus.

Supposing that blessed Peter had the [primacy]{g:primatus} among the apostles for ruling, I think that he received the primacy not only for himself but for his vicars succeeding him in the [church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans}. For since Christ cannot desert his [church]{g:ecclesia}, and it must, militant after Christ, be modelled on the apostolic church, it is enjoined that, as Christ was [captain]{g:capitaneus} of the apostolic company, and after him Peter, so there must continually be a captain in the church militant.[^1] But before it is discussed whether he must necessarily be the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex}, I think [we must] know beforehand certain [teachings]{g:sentencia} to be set out first.[^2] And first, what sort of man the archvicar of blessed Peter must be, and by what a [vicar of Peter]{g:vicarius-petri} is constituted. Nor ought a believer to call into doubt that, just as Peter was in his own right [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius-christi} from this, that he followed his master more nearly in likeness as to acts and morals, so Peter's vicar must be derived from the body of the church by following Peter more nearly in likeness.[^3] [63] For a vicar, as such, bears the stead of the superior from whom he takes vicarial [power]{g:potestas}. If therefore in the beginning, which ought to regulate what follows, Peter would not have been thus vicar of Christ except because he followed him more nearly, then by the topic from sufficient likeness Peter's archvicar must follow Christ, and consequently Peter, more nearly in morals.[^4]

## ¶2 · 63.9–63.26

> [p. 63] Item, illius est homo vicarius cuius vicem gerit et a quo procuratoriam potestatem legitime accepit, sed nemo gerit vicem Christi vel Petri nisi sequatur eum in moribus, cum nulla alia sequela sit pertinens nec aliter recipit a Deo procuratoriam potestatem, igitur ad illud officium vicarium requiritur et morum conformitas et instituentis auctoritas. Cum enim Deus necessario agit omnem accionem creature, patet quod impossibile est habere quemquam talem vicariam potestatem nisi Deus ipsam dederit, sicut dicit Apostolus Rom. XIII, ymo cum illa potestas sit tam excellens dominium supernaturale, videtur quod oportet ipsum singulariter et supernaturaliter dari a Deo. Deus autem non daret provide potestatem huiusmodi illi quem sciret esse proditurum suam ecclesiam, sicut nec dat graciam predestinacionis illi quem scit finaliter periturum.

Again, a man is the vicar of him whose stead he bears and from whom he has lawfully received proctorial power; now no one bears the stead of Christ or of Peter unless he follows him in morals, since no other following is pertinent, nor does he otherwise receive proctorial power from God; therefore for that vicarial office there is required both conformity of morals and the authority of the one who institutes.[^5] For since God necessarily does every action of a creature, it is clear that it is impossible for anyone to have such vicarial power unless God has given it, as the Apostle says, Romans 13; indeed, since that power is so excellent a supernatural [lordship]{g:dominium}, it seems that it must be given by God singularly and supernaturally.[^6] Now God would not providently give power of this kind to one whom he knew would betray his church, just as he does not give the grace of predestination to one whom he knows will finally perish.

## ¶3 · 63.27–65.7

> [p. 63] Ex quibus patet quod humana eleccio nichil valet in eligendo talem prepositum; nisi forte de quanto promulgaverit conformiter ad divinum iudicium. Et ista est secunda sentencia supponenda. Item suppono quod electores devient eligendo unum inhabilem propter affeccionem persone, pecunie vel in penam peccati populi, non concordando divine sentencie, tunc cum Deus non approbat nec confirmat quod peccator [p. 64] quem eligunt sit eius vicarius, patet quod eorum activa eleccio non dat electo iusticiam quoad Deum. Sic enim possent electores perverti quod eligunt in Christi vicarium pessimum Antichristum. Et hinc credo quod ritus ecclesie religiose nominant talem electum permissione divina episcopum; non presumunt dicere approbacione vel gracia divina; quod sepius mentiretur. Si dicatur quod eo ipso quis eligitur aput Deum autorizantem eleccionem, quo in eum maior pars consenserit electorum, eo ipso subditi debent sequi eum quomodocumque viaverit, patet quod nichil infidelius, quia non solum membra dyaboli cogerent Deum ad malum; sed fides consequentis subverteret statum ecclesie, ideo absit a fidelibus hoc porisma; tunc enim posset currus Dei ex principiis fidei duci in precipicium, descendendo per latitudinem itineris Antichristi. Ideo debet esse maxima fideli quod precise de tanto debet subditus sequi suum privatum prepositum vel sibi aut sue prepositure legitime consentire, de quanto imitatus est Christum vel docuerit legem suam, aut conversacione et opere ostendit signum vivum sui officii. Patet ex decreto sancti pape Cypriani distinccione VIII Si solus Christus, et docet apostolus prima Cor. XI, 1: Estote imitatores mei sicut ego Christi. Ecce quam breviter forma exprimitur imitandi. Et hec racio quare apostoli, ne eclipsarent, sed declararent visionem ad sequendum Christi vestigia, non ediderunt novas leges sed legem quam a Christo acceperant sedule docuerunt; dixerunt se esse vacuos et dyasones quasi nichil, ut patet prima Cor. XIII, 2, [p. 65] et Christum non se vigilancius predicabant, ut patet II. Cor. IV. Nichil enim periculosius in ecclesia quam obumbrare viam sequendi Christi vestigia, ducendo populum ab eius semita, quia error talis secundum imaginacionem Aristotelis primo De Celo et Mundo cresceret in declinacionem maximam Antichristi.

From which it is clear that human election avails nothing in electing such a [superior]{g:prepositi}, unless perhaps in so far as it has promulgated in conformity with the divine judgement. And this is the second teaching to be supposed.[^7] Again, I suppose that electors go astray by electing an [unqualified]{g:inhabilis} man out of affection for a person or for money, or in punishment for the sin of the people, not agreeing with the divine sentence; then, since God neither approves nor confirms that the sinner [64] whom they elect is his vicar, it is clear that their active election does not give the one elected justice as regards God. For electors could be so perverted that they elect the worst Antichrist as vicar of Christ. And hence I believe that the [rites]{g:ritus} of the church religiously name such a one, once elected, "bishop by divine permission"; they do not presume to say "by divine approval" or "by divine grace", which would very often be a lie.[^8] If it is said that by the very fact that the greater part of the electors has consented to someone, he is elected before God, who [authorizes]{g:autorizare} the election, [and that] by that very fact the subjects must follow him however he has walked, it is clear that nothing is more faithless; for not only would [members]{g:membrum} of the devil compel God to evil, but faith in the [consequent]{g:consequens} would overturn the state of the church; therefore far be this [porism]{g:porisma} from the faithful; for then the chariot of God could, from principles of faith, be driven over a precipice, going down along the broadness of the road of Antichrist.[^9] Therefore it ought to be a maxim for the believer that a subject ought to follow his private superior, or lawfully consent to him or to his [office of superior]{g:prepositura}, precisely in so far as he has imitated Christ or taught his law, or by [manner of life]{g:conversacio} and work shows a living sign of his office.[^10] It is clear from the decree of the holy pope Cyprian, distinction 8, *Si solus Christus*, and the apostle teaches it, 1 Corinthians 11:1: "Be ye imitators of me, as I of Christ."[^11] See how briefly the form of imitating is expressed. And this is the reason why the apostles, so as not to eclipse but to make clear the vision for following Christ's footsteps, did not put out new laws, but diligently taught the law that they had received from Christ; they said that they were empty and *dyasones*, as it were "nothing", as is clear from 1 Corinthians 13:2, [65] and they preached Christ, not themselves, the more watchfully, as is clear from 2 Corinthians 4.[^12] For nothing is more dangerous in the church than to overshadow the way of following Christ's footsteps, leading the people away from his path, for such an error, according to Aristotle's image in the first book of *On the Heaven and the World*, would grow into the greatest deviation, [that] of Antichrist.[^13]

## ¶4 · 65.8–65.27

> [p. 65] Et ex isto quarto colligitur quod, sive electores bene vel male elegerint, operibus bonis debemus credere et quicumque modus perficiendi prepositum fuerit iudicio Christi conformior est eo melior. Nam ex dictis secundo capitulo de potestate eo ipso quo quis copiosius operatur meritorie habet a Deo ad hoc copiosius potestatem. Et hinc dicit Christus Joh. X, 38: Operibus credite, et hinc dicit Crisostomus in De laude Pauli: Si, inquit, magisterii atque primatus adipiscendi gracia ad procuranda tanta negocia apostoli prosiluissent, consequenter essent de ambicione culpabiles. Sequitur: Sicut enim, qui princeps fuerit ordinatus, nisi digne sibi impositum ministret officium, meretur eciam maiora supplicia, sic alter etsi non ordinetur, suscipiat tamen et sicut oportet impleat non dico sacerdocium sed solicitudinem curamque multorum, tunc profecto honore est dignissimus. Iste sanctus non ponderavit elecciones nostras sed recta officia.

And from this it is gathered, fourth, that whether electors have elected well or ill, we ought to believe good works, and whatever mode of making a superior is more in conformity with the judgement of Christ is by so much the better.[^14] For from what was said in the second chapter on power, by the very fact that someone works the more abundantly meritoriously, he has from God the more abundantly the power for it. And hence Christ says, John 10:38: "Believe the works"; and hence Chrysostom says in *De laude Pauli*: "If," he says, "the apostles had leapt forward to manage such great affairs for the sake of gaining [rule]{g:magisterium} and primacy, they would consequently be guilty of ambition." It continues: "For as he who has been ordained a prince, unless he administers worthily the office laid on him, deserves even greater punishments, so another, even if he is not ordained, should he yet take up and fulfil as he ought (I do not say the priesthood, but the solicitude and care of many), then he is surely most worthy of honour." This saint did not weigh our elections, but offices rightly done.[^15]

## ¶5 · 65.28–65.30

> [p. 65] Sed contra illud arguitur per hoc, quod iuxta istam sentenciam perirent de eleccionibus multe leges et multa legum principia.

But against that it is [argued by appeal to]{g:arguitur-per} this, that according to this teaching many laws on elections, and many principles of laws, would perish.

## ¶6 · 66.1–66.18

> [p. 66] Sed negetur illud, nisi forte vocentur leges hereses, quibus intricatur ecclesia. Nichil enim est magis hereticum quam pertinaciter defendere quod eo ipso quo electores vel maior pars eorum consenserit viva voce secundum ritus hominum in personam aliquam, eo ipso illa persona legittime est electa; oportet enim subducere subduccionem, quia stat in culpatis electoribus quod electus sit pessimus antichristus et stat illos amore vel odio eligere personam in quam Deus non consenserit, sicut stat ipsos male eligere personam cuius eleccionem passivam Deus approbat. Ideo idem hoc foret credere et electores extollere super Deum; quia ratificare per eos eleccionem quoad personam apud Deum inhabilem, quod Trinitas non potest facere. Ideo illi qui religiosius eligunt subducta revelacione solum probabili reputacione pronuncciant pro electo.

[66] But let that be denied, unless perhaps heresies, by which the church is entangled, are called laws. For nothing is more heretical than to defend obstinately that by the very fact that the electors, or the greater part of them, have consented by living voice, according to the rites of men, to some person, by that very fact that person is lawfully elected; for the exclusion must be made, since it can be, when the electors are culpable, that the one elected is the worst antichrist, and it can be that they elect, from love or hatred, a person to whom God has not consented, just as it can be that they elect wrongly a person whose passive election God approves. Therefore to believe this would be the same as to exalt the electors above God, since [it would be] to ratify through them an election as regards a person unqualified before God, which the Trinity cannot do. Therefore those who elect more religiously, revelation being absent, pronounce for the one elected only by a probable reckoning.[^16]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 62.10: Peter's primacy was continued to his successors.
- 62.21: What is required of Peter's vicar and what makes him such.
- 63.9: We must follow Peter in his way of life, and must have authority from God.
- 63.27: Human choice avails nothing unless it declares God's will.
- 64.20: Rulers are to be obeyed so far as they set forth the life and will of Christ.
- 65.8: The success of an election is to be judged by the good works of the elected.
- 66.1: Laws would not be set aside except so far as they were heretical.

## Notes

[^1]: **T/C/R.** *Mandatum est quod*, "it is enjoined that" (c. 62.15), is the print; it construes only loosely, and *manifestum est*, "it is manifest", would suit the argument better. *Post Christum* may go with *militantem* ("militant after Christ") or with *exemplari*. A in marg.: *Capitulum 4* (62.9). At 62.18 B reads or adds *oportet esse*; the note is unclear in the scan.

[^2]: **T/C.** *Arbitror precognoscere*: the infinitive has no subject; we supply "[we must]". B reads *permittendas* for *premittendas* (62.22).

[^3]: **C/R.** The print's semicolon after *vicarius* divides *ex hinc … quod*, "from this, that"; we construe across it. *Similius*, "more nearly in likeness", is the comparative of *similis*; *per se*, "in his own right". *Archivicarius*, "archvicar": Peter's chief vicar. B in marg.: *Qualis debet esse archivicarius Petri*; A in marg.: *Vicarius Petri per quid constituatur* (62.22).

[^4]: **C.** *Per locum a sufficienti similitudine*: the dialectical topic (*locus*) from likeness; what made Peter vicar at the beginning makes his successor vicar after him. *Mores*, "morals": conduct, way of life.

[^5]: **T/G.** *Sequela* (printed broken, 63.13) is here the following of Christ, not the logician's "what follows" (GLOSSARY §C), so it is untagged. B reads *fit* for *sit* (63.13). *Procuratoria potestas*, "proctorial power": the power of one who acts as proctor (*procurator*) for another.

[^6]: **S.** Rom. 13:1 (DR "there is no power but from God"). *Agit omnem accionem creature*: God as first agent in every act of a creature.

[^7]: **C.** *De quanto promulgaverit*: an election counts only as a declaration of what God has already judged. The first teaching is the one on the vicar's likeness to Peter (¶¶1–2).

[^8]: **T/G.** *Divine sentencie* is God's verdict on the person, so "sentence" (GLOSSARY, CONTEXTUAL), against "teaching" just before it. *Nominant* is Loserth's correction; the manuscript reads *nominat* (64.5–6). The manuscripts read *talem episcopum* (64.6–7), the print *talem electum … episcopum*: we translate the print. *Permissione divina* is set against *gracia divina*, the two formulas of a bishop's style.

[^9]: **C/S.** *Quomodocumque viaverit*, "however he has walked": whatever way of life he has taken. *Fides consequentis*: belief in the consequent of the conditional just stated (that subjects must follow the one so elected). The broad road: cf. Matt. 7:13 (DR "broad is the way that leadeth to destruction").

[^10]: **C.** *Suum privatum prepositum*: perhaps "his own particular superior"; *privatum* may also point to the superior of a *religio privata*, an order (GLOSSARY §B).

[^11]: **S.** Gratian, *Decretum* D.8 c.9, *Si solus Christus* (from Cyprian); Loserth: "Decreti Prima pars, dist. VIII, cap. IX" (64.25); the modern form is ours (LEDGER P04a-1). 1 Cor. 11:1: the Vulgate reads *Imitatores mei estote, sicut et ego Christi* (DR "Be ye followers of me, as I also am of Christ"); Wyclif changes the order and drops *et*, and we keep "imitators" for the "form of imitating" that follows.

[^12]: **T/S.** *Dyasones* (64.33) is the print, confirmed on the plate; it does not construe and no variant is given. It may be a corruption touching 1 Cor. 13:1 (*aes sonans*, DR "sounding brass"), and is left untranslated. 1 Cor. 13:2 (DR "I am nothing"); Loserth italicises *nichil*. 2 Cor. 4:5 (DR "For we preach not ourselves, but Jesus Christ our Lord").

[^13]: **T/S.** The manuscripts read *et ducendo* (65.4); the print omits *et*. Aristotle, *De caelo* I.5 (271b8–13): a small error at the outset becomes great at the end (LEDGER P04a-2, to be checked).

[^14]: **C/R.** *Ex isto quarto*: "fourth" counts the suppositions (the vicar's likeness to Peter; election as declaration only; the electors' error). A in marg.: *Officii explecio debita ostendit prelatum habere potestatem* (65.8).

[^15]: **S/G/R.** John 10:38 (DR "believe the works"). Chrysostom, *De laudibus sancti Pauli*; Loserth: *Opp.* (ed. Migne) I, 830, 931, cf. IX, 205 (65.16); for the second passage cf. *In ep. ad Titum* 1, hom. 2 (65.17) (LEDGER P04a-3, to be checked). *Magisterium* is "rule" here (GLOSSARY §D). *Sequitur* introduces the continuation of the quotation, not an inference, so it is untagged. B in marg.: *Nota* (65.17); A in marg.: *Ministrans sollicite ecclesie est honore dignissimus* (65.20). *Recta officia*: offices rightly discharged.

[^16]: **C.** *Oportet enim subducere subduccionem*, literally "one must subtract the subtraction": the rule holds only once the case of culpable electors is taken out (cf. *subducto errore instituentis*, 69.12). *Stat … quod*, "it can be that". *Eleccionem passivam*: the person's being elected, as against the electors' active election (64.1). *Quia ratificare*: the infinitive hangs on an understood *foret*; we supply "[it would be]". *Subducta revelacione*, "revelation being absent".
