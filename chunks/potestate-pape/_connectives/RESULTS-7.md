## Doubtful, left as they are

- **08-f, p. 185**: *Sed tercio videtur michi probabile quod debemus … inniti Christi consilio*, rendered "But thirdly". It is the third item in a series (Matt 24; *Et sic* 2 Thess 2; *Sed tercio*). The *sed* may only mark the next step, which would make it (a) "now" or a plain "and". Kept as (b) because the third point turns from prophecy to practical counsel.
- **08-f, p. 188**: *cum oppositorum est eadem disciplina, Antichristus **autem** et Christus sunt oppositi, oportet quod scriptura …*, rendered "since the same discipline is of opposites, and Antichrist and Christ are opposites, scripture … must". Formally this is the minor premiss (a). But it sits inside the *cum* clause, and English cannot take "now" there without the clause being recast. "and" joins the two premisses under "since", and that carries the logic.
- **08-h, p. 193**: *Sed cum ille qui creavit te sine te, non iustificat te sine te, necesse habemus doctrinam Apostoli … attendere … Oportet igitur preter redempcionem generalem …*, rendered "But since". An *igitur* follows, so under the look-ahead rule this could be (a) "now": the step that brings Ephesians 5 to bear. Kept as (b) because the conclusion's own *preter redempcionem generalem* makes the contrast with Christ's general redemption the point.
- **09-a, p. 196**: *Sed nec faciunt miracula nec predicant neque orant*, rendered "But they neither do miracles". It could be (c), extending the charge sheet (and the next step is *sic quod … nec Geysy nec Symon*). It could also be (b), setting these men against the Gehazi/Simon figures just named. "But" is defensible.
- **08-e, p. 182**: *Ille **autem** synodo collecta Leonem deposuit*, rendered "And he". Narrative continuation (c), though it could be read as (b) (John against Leo). "And" kept.
- **09-b, p. 199**: *Iste **autem** maledictus Liberius non foret plus … exilaratus*, rendered "And". It draws the moral of the story and may be (a), the premiss that closes *Et patet probabiliter maior*. "And" is defensible.
- **08-g, p. 191**: *distinccione XXI Nunc **autem** divina* is a canon incipit cited as a title and left in Latin, in italics. No connective to render.
