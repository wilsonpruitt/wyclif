# *De potestate pape*, chapter 8, part 6: the rival popes and the signs of antichrist

## ¶1 · 185.1–185.18

> [p. 185] Itaque infra quemcumque centenarium annorum a tempore dotacionis ecclesie facta est notanda contencio inter papas. Sed abhinc ex superhabundanti cautela dyaboli plus cecata est iniquitas Antichristi, cum autem apostoli habuerunt planiores revelaciones quam prophete veteris testamenti et maiorem zelum habeant ad profectum ecclesie, non licet discredere quin prophetaverant hos eventus precipuos ut periculum infidelitatis, ita ut tractus ad inferos caucius caveatur, quin indubie istorum antichristorum dissensio dicit patenter, si velimus attendere, quod gradus et vita eorum discordant; et displicent suis autoribus, Et causa dissensionis est honor mundanus vel globus temporalium, quia aliter non foret facile fingere, unde dissenciendo contendant pocius circa illud officium post dotacionem ecclesie quam perante.

So within every hundred years from the time of the [endowment]{g:dotacio} of the [church]{g:ecclesia} there has been notable contention among [popes]{g:papa}. But from then on, by an overabundant [wile]{g:cautela} of the devil, the iniquity of Antichrist has been the more obscured;[^1] and since the apostles had plainer revelations than the prophets of the old testament, and have greater zeal for the church's progress, it is not permitted to disbelieve that they prophesied these chief events as a danger of [unbelief]{g:infidelitas}, so that the drag toward hell might be more warily guarded against.[^2] Nay, undoubtedly the dissension of these antichrists says plainly, if we will attend, that their rank and their life are at odds; and they displease their authors. And the cause of the dissension is worldly honour or the mass of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia},[^3] for otherwise it would not be easy to imagine why, in dissenting, they contend over that office more after the endowment of the church than before it.

## ¶2 · 185.19–187.20

> [p. 185] Ex quibus ut supra colligo tamquam michi probabile, quod Christus summus propheta Matth. XXIV, 5 dicens, quod multi venient in nomine suo, dicentes se esse Christus, et multos seducent, intelligit de Romanis pontificibus blasfeme mencientibus, quod sunt eius vicarii, quos autorizat ad taliter dominandum. Et sic videtur michi probabile quod idem sit sensus Apostoli II. Tess. II, 3, 4 ubi prophetat, quod ante diem iudicii oportet, quod veniat dissensio primum et reveletur homo peccati, qui extollitur super omne quod dicitur Deus. Sed tercio videtur michi probabile quod debemus quoad hoc inniti Christi consilio, quod facta tali divisione iam diebus nostris quibusdam dicentibus: Ecce hic Avinione est Christus et aliis dicentibus, nolite credere, quia indubie [p. 186] opera fidelia oportet ostendere Christi vicarium, cui opinio vel eleccio humana est impertinens. Opera autem Christo contraria ostendunt multis quod uterque eorum sit antichristus et per consequens fugiendus, ymo credo indubie utroque clero, qui istis ambobus consensit, neuter clerus habebit contra reliquum evidenciam quod suus episcopus pro quo zelat sit papa, quia (ut alias ostendi) error eligencium non impedit, quin electus sit papa. Nec nos possumus contra illos ex operibus vel revelacione convincere, quod noster Romanus pontifex sit papa. Ideo videtur securum, cum melius foret ecclesie, quod nullus foret huiusmodi, quod vivamus ut ante dotacionem, quando ecclesia communiter regulabatur per socios, et pro neutro asseramus blasfema mendacia, cum tota vita sua debet capi relicto ambiguo, nam nichil nocius, quam quod stat utrumque istorum esse pseudopapam et per consequens Antichristum. Nec est racio vel auctoritas cogens quod alter istorum sit papa, igitur ista fides non est ecclesie imponenda; et nimis leve foret quod utraque istarum parcium imponat relique scisma vel heresim sine causa notoria, et specialiter cum persone aggregate religiosorum in ista sentencia sint divise. Stat enim quod homines inculpate eligant Antichristum, ut patet ex dictis; et stat quod homines viciosissime eligant quem Deus bene elegerit; ideo patet quod argumenta neutram partem necessitant ad credendum suum prepositum esse papam. Ideo nos Anglici debemus quiescere in antiqua religione Christi ut olym, neutri parti credendo nimis faciliter. Si enim uterque istorum est Antichristus, sine tali capite debet ecclesia regulari, sed quis scit hoc? Docet [p. 187] enim Augustinus in De Verbis Domini Sermone XLV ex triplici scripture testimonio signum evidentissimum Antichristi: primum est illud Apostoli Rom. X, 3: Ignorantes Dei iusticiam et suam volentes constituere, iusticie Dei non sunt subiecti. Unde quia illud signum concludit infallibiliter Antichristum; exponit Augustinus idem pertinencius: Quid, inquit, est aliud ignorare Dei iusticiam et suam velle constituere nisi dicere, ego sanctifico, ego iustifico, quod ego dedero sanctum est, dimitte Deo quod Dei est, agnosce, homo, quod hominis est; ignoras Dei iusticiam et tuam vis constituere, iustificare me vis, sufficit tibi ut mecum iustificeris. Vendicant enim quidam secundum leges suas regere totum mundum et tamen nesciunt secundum leges Dei se ipsos regere vel suos domesticos, et cum illi habent pseudodiscipulos publice asserentes quod ipsi melius et universalibus regunt ecclesiam quam Christus vel eius apostoli.

From which, as above, I gather as probable to me that Christ, the highest prophet, saying in Matthew 24:5 that "many will come" in his "name", saying that they are Christ, "and they will seduce many", means it of the [Roman pontiffs]{g:romanus-pontifex}, who blasphemously lie that they are his vicars, whom he [authorizes]{g:autorizare} to [lord it]{g:dominari} in this way.[^4] And so it seems probable to me that the sense of the Apostle is the same in 2 Thessalonians 2:3–4, where he prophesies that before the day of judgement there must "come a" dissension "first, and the man of sin be revealed", who "is lifted up above all that is called God".[^5] But thirdly it seems probable to me that in this we ought to lean on Christ's counsel, now that such a division has been made in our days, with some saying, "Lo here at Avignon is Christ", and others saying, "do not believe";[^6] for undoubtedly it is faithful works [186] that must show the [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius-christi}, to which human [opinion]{g:opinio} or election is [irrelevant]{g:impertinens}. Now works contrary to Christ show many that each of them is an antichrist and consequently to be fled. Indeed, I believe undoubtedly that, of the two [clergies]{g:clerus} that have consented to these two, neither clergy will have [evidence]{g:evidencia} against the other that its [bishop]{g:episcopus}, for whom it is zealous, is pope,[^7] because (as I have shown elsewhere) the error of the electors does not prevent the one elected from being pope. Nor can we prove against them, from works or from revelation, that our Roman pontiff is pope. Therefore it seems safe, since it would be better for the church that there were no one of this kind, that we live as before the endowment, when the church was commonly ruled through fellows,[^8] and that we assert blasphemous lies on behalf of neither, since each one's whole life must be taken [as the evidence], the ambiguous being left aside;[^9] for nothing is more harmful than this, that it can be that each of them is a [pseudo-pope]{g:pseudopapa} and consequently Antichrist. Nor is there a reason or [authority]{g:auctoritas} compelling [us to hold] that either of them is pope; therefore this faith is not to be imposed on the church. And it would be an [exceedingly]{g:nimis} light thing for each of these parties to charge the other with schism or heresy without notorious cause, and especially since the persons of [the religious]{g:religiosi}, taken together, are divided over this [teaching]{g:sentencia}.[^10] For it can be that men blamelessly elect Antichrist, as is clear from what has been said; and it can be that men most viciously elect one whom God has well chosen. Therefore it is clear that arguments necessitate neither party to believe its [superior]{g:prepositi} to be pope. Therefore we English ought to rest in the ancient religion of Christ as of old, believing neither party too readily. For if each of them is Antichrist, the church must be ruled without such a [head]{g:caput}; but who knows this? For [187] Augustine teaches in *On the Words of the Lord*, Sermon 45, from a threefold testimony of scripture, the most evident sign of Antichrist.[^11] The first is that of the Apostle, Romans 10:3: "Not knowing the justice of God, and willing to establish their own, they are not subject to the justice of God."[^12] Hence, because that sign [infers]{g:concludere} Antichrist infallibly, Augustine expounds the same more pertinently: "What else," he says, "is it [to be ignorant]{g:ignorare} of God's justice and to will to establish one's own, but to say, 'I sanctify, I justify; what I give is holy'? Leave to God what is God's; acknowledge, man, what is man's. You are ignorant of God's justice and will establish your own; you want to justify me; it is enough for you that you be justified with me."[^13] For some claim to rule the whole world according to their own laws, and yet do not know how to rule themselves or their households according to the laws of God; and since they have false disciples publicly asserting that they rule the church better and more universally than Christ or his apostles.[^14]

## ¶3 · 187.21–188.17

> [p. 187] Secundum testimonium est illud Joh. V, 43 tactum superius: Ego veni in nomine patris mei et non accepistis me, Si alius venerit in nomine suo, illum accipietis. Super quo dicit Augustinus: De Antichristo dictum est, et omnes sic intelligunt: Ego veni in nomine patris mei et non suscepistis me, si alius venerit in nomine suo, hunc suscipietis. Sed audiamus Johannem: Audistis, quia Antichristus venit, et nunc Antichristi multi facti sunt. Quid autem expavescimus in Antichristo, nisi quia nomen suum honoraturus est et nomen domini contempturus. Quid aliud facit, qui dicit: Ego iustifico? respondet eis subditus, ego ad Christum veni [p. 188] non pedibus sed corde; ubi evangelium audivi, ibi credidi, et ille: Non es mundus. Quare? quia non fui ibi. Hec Augustinus. Et patet, cum oppositorum est eadem disciplina, Antichristus autem et Christus sunt oppositi, oportet quod scriptura condiciones Christi detegens, indirecte condiciones detegat Antichristi. Ideo dicit capitulum predictum de Antichristo: Scrutamini scripturas, quia putatis in illis habere vitam eternam, et ille sunt que testimonium perhibent de me. Unde redarguendi sunt fingentes quod solum erit unus Antichristus, qui procreabitur ex fratre et moniali, quia certum est quod omnis dampnandus erit Antichristus, et scriptura docet nos sic loqui de illo; ideo fabule tales celant multos Antichristos et palliant eorum versucias, sic quod incaucius precaventur.

The second testimony is that of John 5:43, touched on above: "I am come in the name of my Father, and you have not received me: if another shall come in his own name, him you will receive."[^15] On which Augustine says: "It was said of Antichrist, and all understand it so: 'I am come in the name of my Father, and you have not received me; if another shall come in his own name, him you will receive.' But let us hear John: 'You have heard that Antichrist cometh, and now there are become many Antichrists.' And what do we dread in Antichrist, but that he will honour his own name and despise the name of the Lord? What else does he do who says, 'I justify'? The subject answers them: 'I came to Christ [188] not on foot but in heart; where I heard the gospel, there I believed.' And he: 'You are not clean.' Why? 'Because I was not there.'"[^16] Thus Augustine. And it is clear: since the same discipline is of opposites, and Antichrist and Christ are opposites, scripture, in [exposing]{g:detegere} the conditions of Christ, must indirectly expose the conditions of Antichrist.[^17] Therefore the aforesaid chapter says of Antichrist: "Search the scriptures, for you think in them to have life everlasting; and the same are they that give testimony of me."[^18] Hence those are to be [refuted]{g:redarguere} who imagine that there will be only one Antichrist, who will be begotten of a friar and a nun;[^19] for it is certain that everyone who is to be damned will be an antichrist, and scripture teaches us to speak so of him. Therefore such fables hide many antichrists and [cloak]{g:palliare} their cunning, so that they are guarded against less warily.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Plus cecata est*: "has been the more obscured" (*caecare*, to blind, to darken). The sense wanted may be "the more hidden" (*celata*); the print is translated.
[^2]: **T/C.** *Ita ut*: Loserth's text; the manuscripts read *ita et* (apparatus 185.10). The following *quin indubie* (185.10) is taken as corrective, "nay, undoubtedly"; the sentence does not hang well on what precedes, and a word may be missing.
[^3]: **G/C.** *Globus temporalium*: "the mass of temporal goods", *globus* of things (GLOSSARY §D). *Suis autoribus*: "their authors", most likely those who set them up; the referent is not stated.
[^4]: **S/C.** Matthew 24:5, in indirect speech (Vulgate *in nomine meo … Ego sum Christus*). The clause *quos autorizat ad taliter dominandum* is taken as part of the popes' lie (that Christ authorizes them); *dominari* is pejorative here.
[^5]: **S.** 2 Thessalonians 2:3–4. Wyclif reads *dissensio* where the Vulgate has *discessio* (DR "a revolt"), as Loserth notes (apparatus 185.28); the English follows Wyclif's word.
[^6]: **S/R.** After Matthew 24:23 ("Lo here is Christ, or there: do not believe him"), with Avignon put into the text. The "division" is the schism that began in 1378, with one claimant at Rome and one at Avignon (LEDGER P08f-1).
[^7]: **C.** *Utroque clero, qui istis ambobus consensit* is taken as a loose ablative of reference, "of the two clergies that have consented to these two"; it could also be read "with both clergies having consented".
[^8]: **C.** *Per socios*: "through fellows", i.e. by companions of equal standing, with no single head over them.
[^9]: **C.** *Cum tota vita sua debet capi relicto ambiguo*: construal doubtful. Taken as: each claimant's whole life is the evidence to be weighed, and what is ambiguous (the election) set aside.
[^10]: **C.** *Sentencia* (CONTEXTUAL) is "teaching" here: the position held on which claimant is pope. *Persone aggregate religiosorum*: the religious taken as bodies (the orders).
[^11]: **S.** Augustine, *De verbis Domini* sermo 45 in Wyclif's numbering; Loserth: Sermo 129, Opp. tom. V, 633 (PL 38, 720–25; LEDGER P08f-2).
[^12]: **S/T.** Romans 10:3 in Augustine's form (Vulgate *suam quaerentes statuere*, DR "seeking to establish their own … have not submitted themselves"). The manuscripts read "Rom. 1"; Loserth corrects to X (apparatus 187.4).
[^13]: **T.** *Ego iustifico*: Loserth's emendation; the codex reads *ego sanctifico* twice (apparatus 187.10).
[^14]: **T/C.** The clause *et cum illi habent pseudodiscipulos …* has no main verb: a word or clause may have been lost (187.18–20). *Universalibus* does not construe with *melius*; it is translated as *universalius*, "more universally".
[^15]: **S.** John 5:43, with Wyclif's *accepistis* (Vulgate *accipitis*, DR "you receive me not"); the English gives his perfect.
[^16]: **S/T.** Augustine, Sermo 129.6 (PL 38, 723; Loserth's reference repeated at 188.3; LEDGER P08f-3), quoting John 5:43 (with *suscepistis … suscipietis*) and 1 John 2:18 (Loserth's reference, 187.28). *Ibi credidi*: Loserth's emendation; the codex reads *et* (apparatus 188.1). The exchange is with an opponent who denies the subject is clean because he was not baptized "there", among his own party.
[^17]: **T/C.** *Oppositorum est eadem disciplina*: the scholastic maxim that the same science treats of opposites (Aristotle, *Metaph.* Γ.2, 1004a9–10: "it is the province of one science to study opposites"; LEDGER P08f-4). A omits *eadem* (apparatus 188.4). *Autem* here brings the minor premiss.
[^18]: **S.** John 5:39 (Loserth's reference), slightly reworded (Vulgate *quia vos putatis in ipsis vitam aeternam habere*); "the aforesaid chapter" is John 5, cited for 5:43.
[^19]: **R.** The tale that Antichrist will be born of a friar and a nun; Loserth gives no source and it has not been traced (LEDGER P08f-5, untraced).
