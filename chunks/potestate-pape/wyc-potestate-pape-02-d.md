# *De potestate pape*, chapter 2, part 4: the nine gifts: prophets, doctors, the second and third hierarchies, the body

## ¶1 · 36.28–37.11

> [p. 36] In secundo gradu ponuntur prophete, qui possunt intelligi secundum scienciam evangelizantes prophecias de preterito et futuro; predicatores quidem debent procul fari vulgo abscondita, et secundum scienciam aptare auditorio [p. 37] sermonem; et sic habuerunt apostoli alciorem propheciam, vivaciorem et magis fructiferam quam prophete veteris testamenti, ut patet II. Petri I; et illud credo esse supremum officium apostolorum ad extra post sapiencialem contemplacionem ad intra, ideo Christus et eius apostoli instanter exercuerunt illud officium, ut patet Marc. ult, et I. Corinth. I. Et ad istum sensum intelligunt quidam illud Matth. XXIII, 34: Ecce ego mitto ad vos prophetas, sapientes et scribas et apostolos et evangelistas.

In the second degree are placed the prophets, who can be understood as those who, according to knowledge, preach as gospel prophecies of the past and of the future; for preachers indeed ought to speak out from afar things hidden from the common people, and according to knowledge to fit their [37] sermon to the audience; and so the apostles had a higher prophecy, more lively and more fruitful, than the prophets of the old testament, as is clear from 2 Peter 1; and I believe this to be the highest office of the apostles outwardly, after wise contemplation inwardly; therefore Christ and his apostles exercised that office urgently, as is clear from the last chapter of Mark and from 1 Corinthians 1.[^1] And in this sense some understand that of Matthew 23:34: "Behold I send to you prophets and wise men and scribes and apostles and evangelists."[^2]

## ¶2 · 37.12–37.18

> [p. 37] In tercio autem gradu sunt doctores qui seorsum ad partem sive scolastice sive private docent evangelizatum vel prophetizatum publice. Et illis correspondet donum fidei, cum debent constanter docere rudes instar Christi, qui domi docebat suas predicatas parabolas, ut patet Marc. IV.

And in the third degree are the doctors, who apart, to one side, whether in the schools or privately, teach what has been preached as gospel or prophesied publicly. And to them corresponds the gift of faith, since they must steadfastly teach the unlearned after the likeness of Christ, who at home taught the parables he had preached, as is clear from Mark 4.[^3]

## ¶3 · 37.19–38.20

> [p. 37] Habito itaque pastu triplici secundum primam yerarchiam recipientem a Deo sibi tres gradus exemplariter ad primam yerarchiam celestem, consequenter ponit quomodo suffragatur virtus inferior. Et omnia ista manifestant quomodo Deus qui est spiritus rutilat varie in hominibus donis suis. Sicut enim fumus indicat ignem a quo efficitur, sic quelibet creatura habens variabiliter lumen virtutis sicut radius dicit lucem. Ideo Apostolus vocat hec dona manifestacionem spiritus. Alii autem habent virtutem ut Deus faciat per eos mirabilia; per hoc enim spiritus pascitur et virtus anime roboratur, sed hoc est modicum in comparacione ad priora, sicut modicum sit quod Spiritus Sanctus apparuit apostolis in linguis igneis, nisi eos illustrasset interius; [p. 38] talem autem virtutem habuerunt patres in primitiva ecclesia plus vel minus. Sed non ascribebant sibi virtutes nisi ut organis ex humilitate magis capacibus. In secundo vero gradu secunde yerarchie est gracia curandi corporaliter infirmos, et sic duo sensus extremi, scilicet visus et tactus, in ordine ad animam erant pasti. Unus autem sensus quoad ordinem dignitatis et propinquitatem ad spiritum est prior, sed alius sensus genere, generalitate et carnis propinquitate preponitur. Sed alie due habent virtutem opitulandi egentibus, pascendi medios cunctos sensus; et hoc fit firmitate fidei, qua auditur, adoratur et gustatur Christus in membris. Apostolus enim corporalis elemosyne pascit tres sensus medios quo auditur vox Christi Matth. XXV, 40: Quamdiu uni de minimis meis fecistis, mihi fecistis, tunc enim Christi bonus odor sumus Deo, si gustamus internam Christi dulcedinem.

Having thus [treated] the threefold feeding according to the first hierarchy, which receives from God three degrees on the pattern of the first heavenly hierarchy, he next sets out how the lower [power]{g:virtus} [aids]{g:suffragari}.[^4] And all these show how God, who is a spirit, shines variously in men by his gifts. For as smoke indicates the fire by which it is produced, so every creature that has the light of power in varying measure declares the light, as a ray does. Therefore the Apostle calls these gifts "the manifestation of the Spirit".[^5] And others have the power that God works wonders through them; for by this the spirit is fed and the power of the soul strengthened, but this is little in comparison with the former, just as it would be little that the Holy Spirit appeared to the apostles in tongues of fire, had he not enlightened them inwardly; [38] and such power the fathers in the primitive [church]{g:ecclesia} had, more or less. But they ascribed the powers to themselves only as to instruments made more capable by humility. And in the second degree of the second hierarchy is the grace of healing the sick bodily, and so the two outermost senses, namely sight and touch, were fed in their order to the soul. And the one sense, as to the order of dignity and nearness to the spirit, is prior, but the other sense is put before it in kind, in generality and in nearness to the flesh. But the other two have the power of helping the needy, of feeding all the middle senses; and this is done by the firmness of faith, by which Christ is heard, worshipped and tasted in his members.[^6] For the apostle of bodily alms feeds the three middle senses, in that the voice of Christ is heard, Matthew 25:40: "As long as you did it to one of my least, you did it to me"; for then "we are the good odour of Christ unto God" (2 Corinthians 2:15), if we taste the inward sweetness of Christ.[^7]

## ¶4 · 38.21–39.5

> [p. 38] Sed quoad terciam yerarchiam, qua populus a prepositis regulatur, Deus dedit quibusdam gubernaciones sicut prepositis utriusque membrorum ecclesie; ad quod requiritur discrecio donorum spiritualium et per consequens spirituum movencium ad illud officium, ponendo; servando et protegendo quemlibet in illo gradu, ad quem Deus per dona sua disposuit; hoc enim maxime pertinet ad officium prelatorum. Et quia homo est naturaliter animal communicativum in sermonibus et civile, ideo oportet secundo quod talis prepositure curatus habeat genera linguarum, [p. 39] quibus communicabit cum subditis, quia aliter foret eis barbarus. Nec hoc sufficit, cum avis posset voces formare, ideo requiritur ultimo interpretacio sermonum, nam viva vox habet ex cooperacione spiritus multum latentis energie.

Now as to the third hierarchy, by which the people is ruled by [superiors]{g:prepositi}, God gave to some governments, as to the superiors of both [kinds] of the church's [members]{g:membrum}; for this is required the discerning of spiritual gifts, and consequently of the spirits that move [men] to that office, by placing, keeping and protecting each one in that degree to which God has disposed him by his gifts; for this pertains most of all to the office of [prelates]{g:prelatus}.[^8] And since man is by nature an animal that communicates in speech, and a civil one, therefore it is needful secondly that the [curate]{g:curatus} of such an [office of superior]{g:prepositura} have kinds of tongues, [39] by which he will communicate with his subjects, since otherwise he would be a barbarian to them. Nor is this enough, since a bird could form sounds; therefore lastly the interpretation of speeches is required, for the living voice has, from the cooperation of the spirit, much hidden energy.[^9]

## ¶5 · 39.6–39.23

> [p. 39] Et aptantur hec novem dona spiritualia ad membra humani corporis, ita quod trinarius prime yerarchie correspondeat cerebro, in quo sunt tres ventriculi vasa lucidorum spirituum, et quinque sensuum interiorum, quibus sapiencia, id est Dei noticia, tamquam sol vel dies ad intra percipitur. Sed organa quinque spirituum situatorum in capite figurant secundum spiritum dicentem scienciam mundanorum, collum vero quod est organum vociferandi signat donum fidei ex auditu, sed in regione pectoralium cor, stomachus et pulmo ex proprietate correspondent secundo trinario. In regione vero nutritivorum splen et intestina correspondent tercie yerarchie, ymo sicut Apostolus factus est omnia omnibus, ut omnia lucrifaceret; sic non est membrum ecclesie, quin pertinenter possit cuicunque membro corporis cooperari.

And these nine spiritual gifts are fitted to the members of the human body, so that the triad of the first hierarchy corresponds to the brain, in which are three ventricles, vessels of the lucid spirits and of the five inner senses, by which wisdom, that is, the knowledge of God, is perceived inwardly as the sun or the day. But the organs of the five spirits situated in the head figure the second, the spirit that speaks the knowledge of worldly things; and the neck, which is the organ of uttering, signifies the gift of faith that comes by hearing; but in the region of the breast the heart, stomach and lung correspond by their property to the second triad.[^10] And in the region of the nutritive [members] the spleen and intestines correspond to the third hierarchy; indeed, as the Apostle "became all things to all", that he might gain all, so there is no member of the church that cannot fittingly cooperate with any member of the body whatever.[^11]

## ¶6 · 39.24–40.17

> [p. 39] Triplex tamen conveniencia et triplex differencia est notanda. Sicut enim membra constituunt unum corpus cui anima copulatur, et iterum sicut omne membrum est cuicunque necessarium, cum iuvant se reciproce in suis officiis, sic est de membris ecclesie ex vi communionis et vinculo caritatis. Tercio vero sicut membra se habent in suo regimine, sic et membra ecclesie, cum secundum Crist. Omel. XXII Operis Imperfecti homo sit liber quidam, cui tota religio christiana [p. 40] inscribitur. Sicut igitur est affinitas capitis ad pedes, sic racio et affeccio connectuntur, sicut eciam omne membrum nobile vel ignobile sine contencione servit spiritui, sic omne membrum ecclesie sine contencione minoritatis et obediencie servit Christo, et sicut membra preeminencia non de sua nobilitate superbiunt, sed faciunt suum officium secundum regimen anime ad suffragium singulorum, sic debet esse de membris ecclesie; oculi quidem et facies carent tali tegimine, ne velatum deturpent et preparent ad ruinam: sic Christus et apostoli ex fervore caritatis et alienacione a fervore cupiditatis non erant temporalibus seculariter involuti. Sed membra minus nobilia ut pudenda sunt absconsius, multiplicius et tenerius cooperta, et sic est de contemptibilibus per quos feces ecclesie expurgantur.

Yet a threefold agreement and a threefold difference must be noted. For as the members make up one body to which the soul is joined, and again as every member is necessary to every other, since they help one another in their offices, so it is with the members of the church by force of communion and the bond of [charity]{g:caritas}. And thirdly, as the members behave in their governance, so too do the members of the church, since according to Chrysostom, homily 22 of the *Opus imperfectum*, "man is a kind of book in which the whole Christian religion [40] is written".[^12] As therefore there is an affinity of the head to the feet, so reason and affection are joined; and as every member, noble or ignoble, serves the spirit without contention, so every member of the church serves Christ without contention over [lesser standing]{g:minoritas} and obedience; and as the members of preeminence do not grow proud of their nobility but do their office according to the governance of the soul for the [aid]{g:suffragium} of each, so it should be with the members of the church.[^13] The eyes indeed and the face lack such covering, lest a veil disfigure them and prepare them for ruin: so Christ and the apostles, from the fervour of charity and estrangement from the fervour of covetousness, were not entangled in [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} in a worldly way. But the less noble members, like the private parts, are covered more hiddenly, more manifoldly and more tenderly, and so it is with the contemptible persons by whom the dregs of the church are purged.[^14]

## ¶7 · 40.18–40.30

> [p. 40] Alii autem dividunt membra ecclesie in spiritualia vel dextera sicut clerum et membra corporalia vel sinistra ut laycos, et prima pars que debet esse ut tectum domus ecclesie, eo quod non capit in se incisionem dominii, remanet indivisa. Sed secunda pars dividitur in prepositos et operarios ut parietes et fundamentum ecclesie, et sic habemus tres yerarchias ecclesie cum suis subdivisionibus, in partem infimam, mediam et supremam. Alii autem ex textu apostoli trahunt novem partes ecclesie ad novem partes simplices et spermaticas in humano corpore et alii aptant ipsas ad organa propter conveniencias naturales.

And others divide the members of the church into spiritual or right-hand, such as the [clergy]{g:clerus}, and bodily or left-hand, such as the laity; and the first part, which ought to be like the roof of the house of the church, because it does not admit into itself the cutting up of [lordship]{g:dominium}, remains undivided. But the second part is divided into superiors and workers, like the walls and foundation of the church, and so we have three hierarchies of the church with their subdivisions, into a lowest, a middle and a highest part. And others draw from the Apostle's text nine parts of the church [answering] to the nine simple and spermatic parts in the human body, and others fit them to the organs on account of natural correspondences.[^15]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 36.28: 2. Prophets.
- 37.12: 3. Doctors.
- 37.19: A second hierarchy. 1. Lower members of the Church who manifest the Spirit by various virtues.
- 38.5: 2, Those who heal the sick,
- 38.12: 3. Who aid the poor.
- 38.21: Third hierarchy. Prelates who need 1. discernment of Spirits, 2. tongnes, 3. and their interpretation.
- 39.5: These gifts correspond to the members of the body.
- 39.24: Interdependence of all members;
- 40.4: all are bound, to serve Christ, each in his own way.

## Notes

[^1]: **C/S.** *Procul fari*, "to speak out from afar", plays on the etymology of *propheta* as one who speaks forth or from afar (cf. Isidore, *Etym.* 7.8.1, to be checked; LEDGER P02d-1). *Secundum scienciam* echoes "the word of knowledge, according to the same Spirit" (1 Cor. 12:8). Wyclif is following the order of 1 Cor. 12:28 (DR "first apostles, secondly prophets, thirdly doctors"). 2 Pet. 1:19 ("the more firm prophetical word"); Mark 16:20; 1 Cor. 1:17 ("Christ sent me not to baptize, but to preach the gospel"); Wyclif gives chapters only.

[^2]: **S.** Matt. 23:34. The Vulgate has only *prophetas, et sapientes, et scribas* (DR "prophets and wise men and scribes"); *et apostolos et evangelistas* is not in it (cf. Luke 11:49, *prophetas et apostolos*). Wyclif's added words are translated.

[^3]: **S.** The third degree of 1 Cor. 12:28 (DR "thirdly doctors"); the gift of faith, 1 Cor. 12:9; Mark 4:34 (DR "but apart, he explained all things to his disciples"). B omits *doctores*; A in marg.: *tercius* (37.12).

[^4]: **R/G.** The three degrees of the first hierarchy (apostles, prophets, doctors) are patterned on the first of the three angelic hierarchies of Pseudo-Dionysius, *De caelesti hierarchia* (to be checked; LEDGER P02d-2). "He" is the Apostle, whose list (1 Cor. 12:28) Wyclif now follows into the second hierarchy, DR "miracles … graces of healings, helps". *Virtus* here is "power" in the sense of those "miracles" (*virtutes*), the "working of miracles" (*operatio virtutum*) of 12:10. AB in marg.: *Ierarchia secunda* (37.29).

[^5]: **S.** 1 Cor. 12:7 (DR "the manifestation of the Spirit is given to every man unto profit"); John 4:24 ("God is a spirit"); the tongues of fire, Acts 2:3.

[^6]: **C.** *Alie due*, "the other two", does not fit the count: one gift (helps, *opitulaciones*) remains in this hierarchy, and the middle senses are three; the reference is uncertain. *Adoratur*, "worshipped", stands in a list of the middle senses (hearing, smell, taste) where *odoratur*, "smelled", is expected, and the next sentence turns on "odour"; the print is translated.

[^7]: **C/S.** *Apostolus … corporalis elemosyne*, "the apostle of bodily alms", is translated as printed; the phrase is doubtful (perhaps one who gives bodily alms). Matt. 25:40, where the Vulgate reads *uni ex his fratribus meis minimis* (DR "to one of these my least brethren"); Wyclif's shorter words are translated. 2 Cor. 2:15 is the reference printed in the margin (38.32).

[^8]: **C.** *Prepositis utriusque membrorum ecclesie*: "both" is taken to mean the two kinds of members, clergy and laity (cf. ¶7). The gifts are those of 1 Cor. 12:10, 28 (DR "the discerning of spirits"; "governments, kinds of tongues, interpretations of speeches"). AB in marg.: *Tertia ierarchia*; A in marg. sup.: *Membra ecclesie quam habent similitudinem ad membra corporis humani* (38.18).

[^9]: **S/G.** Aristotle, *Politics* 1.2 (man a political animal, endowed with speech), to be checked (LEDGER P02d-3); 1 Cor. 14:11 (DR "I shall be to him to whom I speak a barbarian"). *Curatus* here is the one charged with such an office of superior, not only a parish priest.

[^10]: **C.** *Figurant secundum spiritum dicentem scienciam mundanorum*: *secundum* is taken as "the second [gift]", knowledge, after wisdom (1 Cor. 12:8); it may instead be the preposition: "figure, according to the spirit, [the gift] that speaks the knowledge of worldly things". *Quinque spirituum* may stand for the five outer senses. "Faith that comes by hearing": Rom. 10:17.

[^11]: **T/S.** Loserth queries *coequari*, "be matched with", for the printed *cooperari* (39.23); the print is translated. 1 Cor. 9:22 (DR "I became all things to all men, that I might save all"); *ut omnia lucrifaceret* echoes *ut infirmos lucrifacerem* in the same verse. Wyclif's words are translated.

[^12]: **S.** Pseudo-Chrysostom, *Opus imperfectum in Matthaeum*, hom. 22; Loserth: Chrysostom, *Opera* (ed. Migne) VI (LEDGER P02d-4, to be checked). B in marg.: *Membrum* (39.30).

[^13]: **C.** *Sine contencione minoritatis et obediencie*: taken as contention over lesser standing and obedience (no member disputes its lower place); the genitives may instead describe the service itself, "without contention, in lowliness and obedience". A in marg. numbers the three agreements 1, 2, 3 (40.1, 40.4, 40.6).

[^14]: **S.** 1 Cor. 12:23 (DR "those that are our uncomely parts have more abundant comeliness"); *temporalibus seculariter involuti* recalls 2 Tim. 2:4 (DR "No man, being a soldier to God, entangleth himself with secular businesses").

[^15]: **C.** "Simple and spermatic parts": the homogeneous parts of the body (bone, flesh, nerve and the like), which medieval medicine held to be formed from the seed, as against the composite organs (*organa*) of the next clause.
