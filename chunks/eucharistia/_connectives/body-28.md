### 09-m ¶1 · 320.23–321.5  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 320] Tercio vero confirmatur ex factis regum Anglie qui crebrius pro levi delicto acceperunt in manus suas temporalia prelatorum; cum hoc vendicant ut regalia ad quam sine repugnancia episcopi sunt iurati; evidens videtur quod non reputabunt illud hereticum **sed** plane katholicum, cum aliter foret clerus in Anglia monstruosus dominus capitalis et proditor regis sui. Nec oportet plus timere excommunicacionem vel leges hominum sic agendo quam domini predicti timuerant, cum factum foret plus consonum legi Dei: ideo dicunt [p. 321] experti quod sacerdotes Antichristi non excommunicant ipsos qui eos persecuntur in faciem, cum non cederet eis ad lucrum **sed** remotos excommunicant cecatos per satrapas suos intrinsecos, quia per hoc putant iuvamine generacionis adultere lucrum eis accrescere.

ENGLISH: Thirdly, this is confirmed from the deeds of the kings of England, who quite often, for a slight offence, have taken the [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} of [prelates]{g:prelatus} into their hands; since they claim this as a [regality]{g:regalia}, to which the bishops are sworn without demur,[^1] it seems evident that they will not reckon it heretical but plainly catholic, since otherwise the clergy in England would be a monstrous [chief lord]{g:capitalis-dominus} and a traitor to its king. Nor need one fear excommunication or the laws of men more in acting so than the aforesaid lords had feared [them], since the deed would be more in accord with [God's law]{g:lex-dei}: therefore [321] men of experience say that the [priests]{g:sacerdos} of Antichrist do not excommunicate those who persecute them to their face, since it would not turn to their gain, but excommunicate those far off, who are blinded by their own [satraps]{g:satrapa} within;[^2] for they think that by this, with the help of an adulterous generation,[^3] gain accrues to them.

### 09-m ¶2 · 321.6–321.18  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 321] Secundo principaliter arguitur per hoc quod plus pensanda est salus et utilitas communioris ecclesie quam utilitas partis sue; **sed** clerus Anglicane ecclesie est modica pars respectu ecclesie tocius regni nostri; ergo debet eius utilitas plus pensari; cum ergo per hunc modum salvaretur totum regnum ab hostibus corporalibus (quod aliter faciliter perire poterat), videtur quod naturalis racio ad hoc cogit. Unde considerantes alcius statum regni dicunt quod regnum nostrum posset plene defendi ab intra per ministracionem temporalium que sunt in manu mortua; et insuper hoc foret medium multiplicandi homines qui in ista causa certare possent legitime.

ENGLISH: Secondly, [as a main head]{g:principaliter}, it is argued by appeal to this: that the welfare and advantage of the more common [church]{g:ecclesia} is to be weighed more than the advantage of its part; now the clergy of the English church is a small part in respect of the church of our whole [kingdom]{g:regnum}; therefore its advantage ought to be weighed more.[^4] Since, then, in this way the whole kingdom would be saved from bodily enemies (which otherwise could easily perish), it seems that natural reason compels to this. Hence those who consider the state of the kingdom more deeply say that our kingdom could be fully defended from within by the administration of the temporal goods that are in [the dead hand]{g:manus-mortua};[^5] and moreover this would be a means of multiplying men who could lawfully fight in this cause.

### 09-m ¶3 · 321.19–322.7  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 321] Nec valet fingere quod accresceret regno peccatum sacrilegii ex hoc facto quod necessitaret ad ruinam ecclesie nostre, quia ut dicit decretum beati Ambrosii XII, questione IIᵃ, Aurum habet ecclesia, pro alendis pauperibus, pro redimendis captivis et pro edificiis necessariis locis sanctis licet eciam vasa sacra confringere sine peccato. Cum ergo hoc sit maius quam omnia ista, simul nec oportet vasa sacra ad hoc vendere nec sustentacionem cleri vel eius religiositatem propterea minuere **sed** augere, videtur quod hoc undique sit salubre. Non enim videtur finis thesauri sic cumulati in clero qui debet mundum deserere, nisi quod ydolatrando congreget et parando [p. 322] conservet thesaurum regni forinsecis inimicis. Sic vero beatus Ambrosius dicit aurum collectum in templo Jerusalem esse causam quare Nabuchodonosor ipsam destruxerat. Et hec racio quare Anglia et multa alia regna sepius sunt destructa. Non ergo credi debet cecatis, dimissa fide scripture, quousque regnum nostrum irremediabiliter sit destructum.

ENGLISH: Nor is it any use to feign that the sin of sacrilege would accrue to the kingdom from this deed, such as would bring our church of necessity to ruin; for, as the decree of blessed Ambrose says (XII, question 2), "The church has gold":[^6] for feeding the poor, for redeeming captives and for buildings necessary to holy places it is lawful even to break up the [sacred]{g:sacer} vessels without sin. Since, then, this is greater than all those, and at the same time there is no need to sell the sacred vessels for it, nor on that account to lessen the sustenance of the clergy or its [religiousness]{g:religiositas}, but rather to increase [them], it seems that this is on every side wholesome. For there seems to be no end of treasure so heaped up in the clergy, which ought to forsake the world, except that by [idolatrizing]{g:ydolatrare} it gathers, and by preparing [322] keeps, the kingdom's treasure for foreign enemies.[^7] And so blessed Ambrose says that the gold collected in the temple of Jerusalem was the cause why Nebuchadnezzar had destroyed it.[^8] And this is the reason why England and many other kingdoms have often been destroyed. Therefore the blinded are not to be believed, with the faith of scripture set aside, until our kingdom is destroyed beyond remedy.

### 09-m ¶4 · 322.8–324.3  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 322] Item, (ut omnis fidelis fatetur) multiplicacio peccatorum est causa destruccionis regnorum, **sed** illa inter christianos potissime originatur a clero (ut docet Gregorius in Registro suo libro III, cap. XXIX), ergo causa destruccionis regnorum originatur ex peccato cleri. Cum ergo peccatum cleri potissimum sit avaricia que est ydolorum servitus (ut dictum est tractatu De Simonia), videtur quod remedium potissimum ad tollendum destrucciones regnorum foret tollere occasionem simonie in clero; que per hoc tolleretur potissime quod clero ut angelis vel Christo et aliis statum innocencie servantibus servarentur vite necessaria sine pluri; aliter enim inficiunt ministerium, consecrant eukaristiam ad sui iudicium et reddunt se utcumque dampnabiles per consensum. Ideo minus malum foret undique tale ministerium non adesse sacerdotes itaque hoc ministerium, quia debent in paupertate habendo omnia in communi propinquius sequi Christum. Quod si murmuraverimus contra dictam sentenciam que secundum legem Dei foret ad utilitatem ecclesie, indignificamus nos, etsi panem sacramentalem conficimus, refici pane vite. Deus enim [p. 323] communia bona preponderat, sicut ordinat in prosperitate communis populi stare valetudinem regni nostri. Quomodo ergo permitteremus avaris spoliacionem pauperis populi de elemosinis quibus vivimus, et habendo bona eius incastrata non ipsos superflue quoad Deum et homines foveremus. Colligendo ergo radices errorum in clero, patet quod tria sunt in quibus sophisticatur ecclesia, scilicet cleri dominacio, tradicionum suarum magnificacio et sanctitatis sue simulacio; et ex illis procedunt infinita sophismata regis superbie, ut excommunicacionis terrificacio, observacionis legis sue preponderacio et signorum in sacramentis ac sacramentalibus nimia attencio. Remedium contra primum est fides de Christi pauperie quam totus clerus debet sequi propinquius et consequenter exemplo suo ad hoc trahere populares. Remedium contra secundum est quod leges humane precise de tanto sunt valide, de quanto in lege Domini sunt fundate. Remedium contra tercium est credere vite et operibus conformibus legi Dei; nam res cui credimus est insensibilis; cui non pertinet sensum sensibile nisi de quanto fidem excitat ad credito secundum caritatis regulas adherendum. Et in isto tercio est magis velatum periculum, ut patet de sacramentorum sophisticacione supra fidem scripture et specialiter in illis que lucrum sapiunt, ut patet de eukaristia, penitencia et sepultura. Ideo debet fidelis theologus contra simulaciones istas constanter invehere. Finis itaque gracia cuius hec dixerim est ut tollendo errorem in signis fidelis memoretur meritorius signatorum. Nec credo esse aliquod genus hominum, quod memoratur Christum falsius tam speculative quam practice sequendo Scarioth quam facit communitas sacerdotum, quia in quantum sic [p. 324] celebramus memoriam Christi, debemus ipsum sequendo similius pacem et veritatem diligere; et nullus magis exorbitat in utriusque contrarium.

ENGLISH: Again (as every believer confesses), the multiplication of sins is the cause of the destruction of kingdoms; now among Christians it originates most of all from the clergy (as Gregory teaches in his *Register*, book 3, chapter 29);[^9] therefore the cause of the destruction of kingdoms originates from the sin of the clergy. Since, then, the [foremost]{g:potissimus} sin of the clergy is avarice, which is the [servitude of idols]{g:servitus-ydolorum} (as was said in the treatise *On Simony*),[^10] it seems that the foremost remedy for taking away the destructions of kingdoms would be to take away the occasion of [simony]{g:simonia} in the clergy; and this would be taken away most of all by this: that for the clergy, as for the angels, or for Christ and the others who kept the state of innocence, the necessaries of life were kept, and no more. For otherwise they infect the ministry, consecrate [the Eucharist]{g:eukaristia} to their own judgement,[^11] and render themselves in one way or another damnable by consent. Therefore it would be a lesser evil for such a ministry to be present nowhere; priests, then, [should hold] this ministry, because they ought, in poverty, having all things in common, to follow Christ more closely.[^12] And if we murmur against the said [teaching]{g:sentencia},[^13] which according to God's law would be for the advantage of the church, we make ourselves unworthy, even if we [consecrate]{g:conficere} the sacramental [bread]{g:panis}, to be refreshed by the bread of life. For God [323] gives common goods the greater weight, just as he ordains that the health of our kingdom stands in the prosperity of the common people. How then should we allow the avaricious the despoiling of the poor people, out of the alms on which we live, and, holding its goods locked away, not cherish them superfluously as regards God and men?[^14] Gathering, then, the roots of the errors in the clergy, it is clear that there are three things in which the church is [counterfeited]{g:sophisticare}:[^15] namely the clergy's [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio}, the magnifying of its traditions, and the feigning of its holiness; and from these proceed numberless [sophisms]{g:sophisma} of the king of pride,[^16] such as the terrifying with excommunication, the overweighting of the observance of its own law, and too much attention to the [signs]{g:signum} in the sacraments and [sacramentals]{g:sacramentalia}. The remedy against the first is faith concerning the poverty of Christ, which the whole clergy ought to follow more closely, and consequently by its example draw the people to it. The remedy against the second is that human laws are valid precisely so far as they are founded in the law of the Lord. The remedy against the third is to believe life and works conformable to God's law; for the thing that we believe is [imperceptible]{g:insensibilis}; and what is perceptible to sense does not pertain to it, except so far as it stirs up faith to cleave to what is believed according to the rules of [charity]{g:caritas}.[^17] And in this third lies the more veiled danger, as is clear from the [sophistry]{g:sophisticacio} of the sacraments beyond the faith of scripture, and especially in those that savour of gain, as is clear of the Eucharist, penance and burial. Therefore the faithful theologian ought to inveigh steadfastly against these feignings. The end, then, for the sake of which I have said these things is that, by taking away the error in the signs, the believer may more meritoriously remember the [things signified]{g:signatum}. Nor do I believe there is any kind of men that remembers Christ more falsely, both speculatively and practically, following Iscariot, than the community of priests does; for [insofar as]{g:in-quantum} we thus [324] celebrate the memory of Christ, we ought, following him more nearly in likeness, to love peace and truth; and none strays further into the contrary of both.[^18]

### 09-n ¶1 · 324.29–326.3  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 324] Quod **autem** digressus sum tractando difficultates huius materie scolastice, ad hoc valet ut sciatur errorem affectus errorem consequi intellectus. Sicut enim cultores signorum errant falsissime in accidentibus sacramenti, sic errant affectus circa temporalia [p. 325] clero accidencia, ut acquirat sibi merendo et populo veras divicias sempiternas, et sicut blasphemi dicunt quod virtute benediccionis Christi panis secundum se totum destruitur ad non esse, sic quod nulla pars eius remaneat, ita affectus in nostris benediccionibus est perversus, sic quod iuxta prophetam Deus prelatis contrariis maledicit suis benediccionibus et econtra. Et illud in facto ostenditur, cum confessio et usus sacramenti altaris debet precipue fieri propter pacem membrorum ecclesie constituendam et confirmandam, quam sacerdotes tam committendo quam permittendo magis dilacerant. Taceo **autem** bella et pugnas quas prelati in persona sua procurant et faciunt et voco temporalia que monstruose sibi accumulant et consumunt. Cum **autem** bonum hoc putridum sit causa bellorum, patet quod presbiteri et specialiter prelati sunt causa dissensionis et bellorum fidelium, et sic omissione dampnabili nec hortantur nec consulunt ad pacem regnorum **sed** bella, et sic degenerantes a vita et doctrina apostolorum celebrant falsissime missas suas. Procurarent enim verbo et opere quod omnes christiani eciam seculares domini, cum sint unus panis, et de uno pane edant tamquam membra unius ecclesie, quod superant omnia mundi temporalia et honores, antequam edant, lacerent vel turbent minimum membrum matris ecclesie. Quam leccionem si ex sacramento altaris perfecte didicerint, tunc attingendo finem sacramenti tamquam filii pacis vere celebrant missas suas. Et circa hoc intenti dimissis aliis sollicite studeremus; ergo per se finem huius sacramenti que [p. 326] est pax triplex concedat nobis Deus in nobis acquirere in matre nostra adiuvando Deum conficere et in Christo Jesu finaliter observare. Amen.

ENGLISH: And that I have digressed in treating the difficulties of this scholastic matter serves for this: that it may be known that the error of the affection follows the error of the understanding.[^1] For just as the [worshippers of signs]{g:cultores-signorum} err most falsely about the [accidents]{g:accidens} of the sacrament, so the affections err about the [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} [325] that befall the clergy, [given] so that it may acquire, by meriting, true and everlasting riches for itself and the people;[^2] and just as the blasphemers say "by virtue of Christ's blessing the [bread]{g:panis} is destroyed in its whole self to not-being, so that no part of it remains", so the affection in our blessings is perverse, so that, according to the prophet, God, to contrary [prelates]{g:prelatus}, "curses their blessings, and conversely".[^3] And that is shown in the fact: since confession and the use of [the sacrament of the altar]{g:sacramentum-altaris} ought chiefly to take place for establishing and strengthening the peace of the [members]{g:membrum} of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, which the [priests]{g:sacerdos} rather tear apart, as much by committing as by permitting. And I pass over the wars and battles that prelates procure and make in their own person, and I name the temporal goods that they monstrously heap up for themselves and consume.[^4] Now since this rotten good is the cause of wars, it is clear that [presbyters]{g:presbiter}, and especially prelates, are the cause of dissension and of the wars of the faithful; and so, by a damnable omission, they neither exhort nor counsel to the peace of kingdoms but to wars; and so, degenerating from the life and [doctrine]{g:doctrina} of the apostles, they celebrate their masses most falsely. For they should procure by word and deed that all Christians, even [secular lords]{g:seculares-domini}, since they "are one bread, and eat of one bread" as members of one church, rise above all the temporal goods and honours of the world before they eat, sooner than tear or trouble the least member of mother church.[^5] If they have learned this lesson perfectly from the sacrament of the altar, then, attaining the end of the sacrament, they [truly]{g:vere} celebrate their masses as sons of peace.[^6] And intent on this, with other things set aside, we should study diligently; therefore may God grant us to acquire the end [of itself]{g:per-se} of this sacrament, which [326] is a threefold peace: in ourselves to acquire it, in [our mother]{g:mater-nostra} by helping God to [make]{g:conficere} it, and in Christ Jesus finally to keep it.[^7] Amen.

### pen01 ¶1 · 329.2–330.18  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 329] Duo sunt sacramenta precipua in quibus ecclesia est illusa, scilicet sacramentum eukaristie et sacramentum penitencie. In sacramento eukaristie Antichristus paulative tantum seduxit ecclesiam quod fecit eam quiditatem huius sacramenti sensibilis ignorantem. Superaddit eciam quod ignorat quod quilibet sacerdos quantumcunque indignus fuerit conficiendo facit regulariter ultra hoc quod fecerunt patres veteris testamenti vel apostoli miracula infinita. **Sed** miraculum istud est imperceptibile atque inutile. Imperceptibile quidem, quia post consecracionem sensus ut prius renunciant quod est panis; et inutile est, quia per confeccionem suam (ut false inquiunt) panis destruitur, ymmo materia prima que est fundamentum nature secundum se totam desinit esse, ac si Deus in mundi principio materiam crearet superfluam vel aliter pro sacerdotibus Antichristi discipulis materiam primam creavit in mundi principio, ut ex ea blaspheme et irregulariter consecrent corpus Christi et postmodum sine causa miraculose ipsam adnichilent. Talia **autem** ridicula infinita que fideles evacuant, dicendo quod hoc [p. 330] sacramentum naturaliter est panis et sacramentaliter corpus Christi. Non enim vident causam quare Deus hoc fundamentum nature destrueret, sicut nec vident quod corpus Christi in natura sua est materialius, densius aut maius quam prius fuerat; nec vident in quid ista materia convertatur nisi secundum fantasiam suam erroneam in nichilum convertatur, quia utendo terminis de conversione (ut prius usa fuit ecclesia) illa materia non converteretur in materiam corporis Christi vel in corpus Christi aut eius partem nisi fieret id conversum, sicut nos dicimus quod panis convertitur in corpus Christi et prius iniustus in filium Dei est conversus; quod omnis talis conversio dicit aliquam faccionem, sic quod natura conversi sit accidentaliter de novo quod vel cuiusmodi prius non fuerat. Materiam **autem** istam non possunt fingere sic converti, ideo necessitantur dicere quod virtute sui miraculi est annichilata. **Sed** procul a fidelibus talis vesania.

ENGLISH: There are two chief [sacraments]{g:sacramentum} in which the [church]{g:ecclesia} has been deluded: namely the sacrament of [the Eucharist]{g:eukaristia} and the sacrament of penance. In the sacrament of the Eucharist Antichrist has little by little so far seduced the church that he has made it [ignorant of]{g:ignorare} the [quiddity]{g:quiditas} of this [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} sacrament.[^1] He adds besides that it is ignorant that any [priest]{g:sacerdos}, however unworthy he may be, in [consecrating]{g:conficere} does, [as a rule]{g:regulariter}, beyond what the fathers of the old testament or the apostles did, numberless miracles.[^2] But this miracle is imperceptible and useless. Imperceptible, for after the [consecration]{g:consecracio} the senses report as before "it is bread"; and useless it is, for by their [consecration]{g:confeccio} (as they falsely say) the [bread]{g:panis} is destroyed; indeed [prime matter]{g:materia-prima}, which is the foundation of [nature]{g:natura}, ceases to be in its whole self; as if God at the beginning of the world created matter superfluous, or else created prime matter at the beginning of the world for the priests, the disciples of Antichrist, so that out of it they might blasphemously and [irregularly]{g:irregulariter} consecrate [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} and afterwards, without cause, miraculously [annihilate]{g:adnichilare} it.[^3] And such are the numberless absurdities that the faithful do away with, by saying "this [330] sacrament is naturally bread and [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} the body of Christ". For they do not see a cause why God should destroy this foundation of nature, just as they do not see that the body of Christ is in its nature more material, denser or greater than it had been before; nor do they see into what this matter is converted, unless, according to their erroneous [imagination]{g:fantasia}, it is converted into nothing;[^4] for, using the terms about [conversion]{g:conversio} (as the church formerly used them), that matter would not be converted into the matter of the body of Christ, or into the body of Christ or a part of it, unless it became that into which it was converted, as we say "the bread is converted into the body of Christ" and "one formerly unjust has been converted into a son of God"; for every such conversion [signifies]{g:dicere} some [making]{g:faccio}, so that the nature of the thing converted is, accidentally, anew what, or of what sort, it had not been before.[^5] Now they cannot [feign]{g:fingere} this matter to be so converted; therefore they are compelled to say "by virtue of their miracle it has been annihilated". But far from the faithful be such madness.

### pen01 ¶2 · 330.19–331.22  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 330] In inpossibiles **autem** errores incidunt Antichristi filii de penitencia, cum nesciunt fundare istam penitenciam quam papa de novo instituit; evidenciam **autem** que fidem faceret vel scripture nescit; nescit eciam si penitens sit contritus vel peccatum suum deletum a Domino, sicut nescit quantitatem vel qualitatem penitencie iniungende. Communiter tamen limitatur quiditas huius penitencie quod est peccati ostensio sacerdoti cum contricione precedente et satisfaccione penitencie consequente; et sic (ut quidam inquiunt) sacramentum penitencie est in diversis generibus; nec est possibile tres eius partes, scilicet cordis contricionem, oris confessionem et operis satisfaccionem temporaliter [p. 331] esse simul. Spreta ista irregulari descripcione penitencie videtur in ipsam ulterius procedendum, et primo notanda est sentencia sepedicta quod non est simpliciter de necessitate salutis quod quilibet beatificandus eciam dum discrecionem attigerit confiteatur omnia peccata sua que commisserat proprio sacerdoti, quia antequam ista lex fuit edita multi sancti fuerant salvati tam de lege antiqua quam de lege gracie. Et sic videtur Spiritum Sanctum negligenter sacramentum tam necessarium obmisisse. Nam Actuum IIᵒ et infra legitur Petrum, Paulum et ceteros apostolos multa milia populi convertisse. Et non recolo aliquem conversum istud novum sacramentum a sacerdote aliquo accepisse, nec Christus nec Baptista nec aliquis apostolus ista sacramentali penitencia quam nos cognoscimus usus fuit. Ideo dicit doctor decretorum Johannes de Deo quod istud sacramentum a quadam institucione pape cepit originem, quem quidam dicunt fuisse Innocencium IIIᵘᵐ; et confirmacio patet De Penitenciis et Remissionibus capitulo Omnis utriusque sexus. Et ista confessio est alibi multipliciter impugnata.

ENGLISH: And the sons of Antichrist fall into impossible errors about penance, since they do not know how to ground this penance which the [pope]{g:papa} has newly instituted;[^6] and he does not know evidence that would make faith, or [evidence] of scripture; he does not know either whether the penitent is contrite or his sin blotted out by the Lord, just as he does not know the [quantity]{g:quantitas} or [quality]{g:qualitas} of the penance to be enjoined.[^7] Yet the quiddity of this penance is commonly [assigned]{g:limitare} thus: that it is the showing of sin to a priest, with contrition going before and the [satisfaction]{g:satisfaccio} of penance following; and so (as some say) the sacrament of penance is in diverse genera;[^8] nor is it possible for its three parts, namely contrition of heart, confession of mouth and satisfaction of work, to be together in time. [331] With this irregular [definition]{g:descripcio} of penance spurned, it seems we must proceed further into it; and first is to be noted the [teaching]{g:sentencia} often stated:[^9] that it is not [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} of the necessity of salvation that everyone who is to be beatified, even once he has reached discretion, confess all the sins he had committed to his own priest; for before this law was published many [saints]{g:sancti} had been saved, both of the old law and of [the law of grace]{g:lex-gracie}. And so it would seem that the Holy Spirit negligently left out so necessary a sacrament. For in Acts 2 and below one reads that Peter, Paul and the other apostles converted many thousands of the people.[^10] And I do not recall that anyone converted received this new sacrament from any priest; nor did Christ, nor the Baptist, nor any apostle use this sacramental penance that we know. Therefore the doctor of decrees John of God says that this sacrament took its origin from a certain institution of a pope, whom some say was Innocent III;[^11] and the confirmation is clear in [the title] On Penances and Remissions, the chapter *Omnis utriusque sexus*.[^12] And this confession has been attacked elsewhere in many ways.
