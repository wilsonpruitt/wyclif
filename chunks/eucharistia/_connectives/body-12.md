# sed/autem extract, wave 12: chunks 05-e 05-f 05-g

### 05-e ¶1 · 131.14–133.11  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 131] Unde, ut loquuntur Ambrosius, Hugo et alii veritatis discipuli quod eadem essencia que prius fuit non sacramentum post fit sacramentum virtute verborum Christi, et sic quoad figuracionem convertitur in corpus Christi, ut loquitur Anshelmus, et sic substancia panis et vini non remanet in actu consideracionis ut prius, sicut philosophi loquuntur, ponentes sensibile et tempus poni in actu per sensacionem et intelleccionem. Non enim intellexit quod sit accidens sine subiecto, cum in De Veritate capitulo ultimo ita scribit: Existente namque corpore colorem eius esse necesse est, et pereunte corpore colorem eius manere inpossibile est. Quamvis enim illud dicat in persona discipuli, tamen consequenter illud approbat ponendo diversitatem inter colorem et eius subiectum ac rectitudinem et eius signum. Rectitudo enim non dependet ab illo signo, sicut color dependet [p. 132] ab subiecto. Quando enim Augustinus et alii sancti in suis dyalogis loquuntur ut discipuli et pretereunt ac non reprobant, indubie illud quod ponunt tanquam discipuli ut magistri approbant. Unde in confirmacionem istius sentencie allegavi Augustinum superius quod accidens non potest esse sine subiecto tam in dyalogo suo quam alibi. Docet enim racio naturalis quod non potest esse accidens sine subiecto, quia non potest esse subiectum sine suo accidente, ergo multo magis econtra; nam secundum omnes qui intelligunt se ipsos maius vel tantum foret vinculum ac dependencia accidentis ad subiectum quam econtra. Et argumentum patet ex hoc quod si aliqua creatura est, tunc est dependencia eius ad Deum; quod est relacio inseparabilis; et sic non potest esse substancia materialis sine quantitate continua et discreta, sine relacione suarum parcium ad omnem eius punctum, et sic de infinitis aliis accidentibus. Numquid credimus quod analogum accidentis sit prius naturaliter quam genus substancie quod est basis cuilibet accidenti, sic quod accidens potest esse per totum mundum sine substancia, non econtra? unde genus substancie dependeret a genere accidentis, non econtra; ymmo species substancie materialis dependeret a corpore de genere quantitatis, non econtra, cum conveniunt invicem, ut quantitas potest quantumlibet rarefieri et condensari, ut coguntur dicere in eukaristia. Et sic substancia potest mutari in quantitatem sine acquisicione vel deperdicione quantitatis, cum quantitas ita potest; et sic punctus posset in infinitum maiorari, cum sicut pedalitas per accidens est pedalitas, sic quantitas per accidens est quantitas. [p. 133] **Sed** longe aliter sentit Augustinus de quantitate, ut patet Vᵗᵒ de Trinitate IXᵒ et XIIIᵒ, sic loquitur: Sicut ergo non dicimus tres essencias, ita non dicimus tres magnitudines. In rebus, inquit, que participacione magnitudinis magne sunt, quibus aliud est esse, aliud magnum esse, sicut magna domus et magnus mons et magnus animus, in hiis ergo rebus aliud est magnitudo, aliud quod ab ea magnitudine magnum est, et prorsus non hoc est magnitudo quod magna domus, **sed** vera magnitudo in Deo est ipse Deus et non sibi accidens.

ENGLISH: Hence,[^1] as Ambrose, Hugh and other disciples of the truth say, that the same [essence]{g:essencia} which before was not a [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} afterwards becomes a sacrament by virtue of the words of Christ, and so, as to [figuration]{g:figuracio}, is converted into [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}, as Anselm says;[^2] and so the [substance]{g:substancia} of the [bread]{g:panis} and [wine]{g:vinum} does not remain in act of consideration as before, as the philosophers say, who posit that the perceptible and time are put in act by [sensation]{g:sensacio} and intellection.[^3] For he did not understand[^4] that there is an [accident without a subject]{g:accidens-sine-subiecto}, since in *De veritate*, in the last chapter, he writes thus: "For when the body exists its colour must exist, and when the body perishes it is impossible that its colour remain."[^5] For although he says it in the person of the disciple, yet he afterwards approves it, by positing a difference between colour and its [subject]{g:subiectum}, and rectitude and its sign. For rectitude does not depend on that sign as colour depends [132] on the subject. For when Augustine and the other [saints]{g:sancti} speak in their dialogues as disciples, and pass on and do not reject [what is said], beyond doubt what they posit as disciples they approve as masters. Hence in confirmation of this [teaching]{g:sentencia}[^6] I [alleged]{g:allegare} Augustine above, that an [accident]{g:accidens} cannot be without a subject, both in his dialogue and elsewhere. For natural reason teaches that there cannot be an accident without a subject, because there cannot be a subject without its accident; therefore much more the converse; for according to all who understand themselves, the bond and dependence of accident on subject would be greater than, or as great as, the converse. And the argument is clear from this, that if any [creature]{g:creatura} is, then there is its dependence on God, which is an inseparable relation; and so a material substance cannot be without continuous and discrete [quantity]{g:quantitas}, without the relation of its parts to its every point, and so of infinitely many other accidents. Are we to believe that the [analogous term]{g:analogum} accident[^7] is naturally prior to the genus of substance, which is the base for every accident, so that accident can be throughout the whole world without substance, and not the converse? so that the genus of substance would depend on the genus of accident, not the converse; indeed, a species of material substance would depend on a body of the genus of quantity, not the converse, since they [agree]{g:convenire} with each other, as quantity can be rarefied and condensed to any degree, as they are forced to say in the eucharist. And so substance can be changed as to quantity[^8] without the acquiring or losing of quantity, since quantity can be so; and so a point could be enlarged to infinity, since just as [foot-length]{g:pedalitas} is foot-length [by accident]{g:per-accidens}, so quantity is quantity by accident. [133] But Augustine [holds]{g:sentire} far otherwise of quantity, as is clear in *De Trinitate* V, [chapters] 9 and 13;[^9] he speaks thus: "Just as therefore we do not say three essences, so we do not say three magnitudes." "In things," he says, "which are great by participation in magnitude, for which it is one thing to be and another to be great, as a great house and a great mountain and a great mind, in these things therefore magnitude is one thing, and that which is great from that magnitude another; and magnitude is not at all the same thing as a great house; but true magnitude in God is God himself, and not an accident to him."

### 05-e ¶2 · 133.12–135.2  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 133] Ex istis patet loyca huius sancti qua utitur probando distinccionem accidencium. Et patet secundo, quod non vocat accidencia res que poterunt per se esse (ut dictum est capitulo IIIᵒ). Et patet tercio error eorum qui ponunt quantitatem primi generis creature non distingui a sua substancia. Error enim in materia de eukaristia precipitat eos profundius ad implicandum quamlibet substanciam esse Deum, quia indubie, si non sit distinccio inter magnitudinem materialis substancie et suam essenciam, tunc (ut Augustinus arguit) ipsa est Deus; et credo quod error quo colitur accidens tanquam Deus induxit in errores huiusmodi cecans multos in loyca et methaphisica; et hii sunt qui dicunt sacramentum altaris esse quantitatem que est tempus maius aut minus, ut unam hostiam dicunt esse momentum et aliam diem dominicum, **sed** terciam dicunt septimanam vel mensem, hii magnificant tantum suam scienciam quod dicunt se scire modo distincte qua hora erit finale iudicium; oportet enim ipsos dicere quod hoc sacramentum [p. 134] sit quantitas successiva. Statuunt enim sibi tanquam principium hoc porisma dyaboli: Si aliquod ens creatum sit distinctum a substancia, tunc ipsum poterit per se esse, et ex isto profundant se in erroribus infinitis, dicentes quod accidens non potest esse nisi accidens, sicut substancia non potest esse nisi substancia, quia aliter quidditas nature sibi accideret, verumptamen accidens intelligi potest dupliciter, scilicet neutraliter vel adiective; neutraliter intelligendo sacramentum altaris esse accidens **sed** non secundo modo, cum nulli accidit; nec Deus supplet vicem cause materialis in isto, licet omnis creatura accidentaliter inest Deo, **sed** remanet in accidente per se posito quedam naturalitas ad formaliter inherendum; et sic quedam accidentalitas qualis non potest inesse substancie. **Sed** consequencius dicerent ponendo inesse cuiuslibet forme substanciali quandam naturalitatem remotam obediencialem, sicut cuilibet forme accidentali inest quedam remota naturalitas obediencialis, ut inhereat materiali substancie; nam ille qui potest sic mutare naturas ut forma accidentalis per se existat et agat, nedum producendo formas accidentales **sed** formas substanciales quod videtur naturali esse proprium substancie: ille, inquam, potest facere formas substanciales materiis inherere, potissime cum potest actuaciones formarum remanencium omnino suspendere; quod est plus quam appetitus et actuaciones remittere vel mutare; ideo homo istius scole timeret sibi ne blaspheme neget potenciam infinitam absolute necessariam Dei sui; unde ego utor hac arte, non admitto tales casus insolitos timendo blasphemias, nisi probetur mihi hoc esse de Dei potencia; et hoc facto paratus sum ad concedendum probatum [p. 135] et infinitum excellenciora quam ipsi ponunt Deo et sacramento suo competere.

ENGLISH: From these [words] the [logic]{g:loyca} of this saint is clear, which he uses in proving the distinction of the accidents. And it is clear, secondly, that he does not call accidents things that could be [of themselves]{g:per-se}[^10] (as was said in chapter 3). And, thirdly, the error is clear of those who posit that the quantity of a creature of the first genus is not distinguished from its substance.[^11] For the error in the matter of the eucharist casts them down deeper, to implying that every substance is God, because beyond doubt, if there is no distinction between the magnitude of a material substance and its essence, then (as Augustine argues) it is God; and I believe that the error by which an accident is worshipped as God has led into errors of this kind,[^12] blinding many in logic and metaphysics; and these are the ones who say "the sacrament of the altar is a quantity which is a greater or lesser time", as they say one [host]{g:hostia} is a moment and another the Lord's day, but a third they say is a week or a month; these so magnify their knowledge that they say they know now distinctly at what hour the final judgement will be; for they must say that this sacrament [134] is a [successive quantity]{g:quantitas-successiva}. For they set up for themselves as a principle this [porism]{g:porisma} of the devil: "If any created [being]{g:ens} is distinct from substance, then it can be of itself"; and from this they sink themselves in infinite errors, saying that an accident cannot be anything but an accident, just as a substance cannot be anything but a substance, because otherwise the quiddity of its nature would befall it;[^13] nevertheless "accident" can be understood in two ways, namely [as a neuter]{g:neutraliter} [noun] or as an adjective: understanding it as a neuter, the sacrament of the altar is an accident, but not in the second way, since it befalls nothing; nor does God [supply the place]{g:vicem-supplere} of the material cause in this, although every creature is accidentally in God, but there remains in the accident posited of itself a certain naturalness toward formally inhering; and so a certain [accidentality]{g:accidentalitas} such as cannot be in a substance. But they would speak more consistently by positing that there is in every[^14] [substantial form]{g:forma-substancialis} a certain remote [obediential naturalness]{g:naturalitas-obediencialis}, just as in every accidental form there is a certain remote obediential naturalness, that it should inhere in a material substance; for he who can so change natures that an accidental form exists and acts of itself, [not only]{g:nedum} producing accidental forms but substantial forms, which seems to be proper to natural substance: he, I say, can make substantial forms inhere in matters, most of all since he can wholly suspend the actuations of the forms that remain; which is more than to remit or change appetites and actuations. Therefore a man of this school would fear for himself lest he blasphemously deny the infinite, absolutely necessary [power]{g:potencia}[^15] of his God. Hence I use this art: I do not admit such unusual cases, fearing blasphemies, unless it is proved to me that this is within God's power; and that done, I am ready to grant what is proved, [135] and things infinitely more excellent than they posit to belong to God and to his sacrament.

### 05-f ¶1 · 135.3–136.11  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 135] Unde videtur mihi, quod doctor Bonaventura et alii inconsequenter sompniant quod propter honorem sacramenti et devocionem populi multa sunt ponenda de eukaristia que non sunt fundabilia in scriptura; habeo **autem** pauca adversus eos qui inconsequenter et nimis parce sompniant. Quamvis **autem** sit de natura sompnii dormientis quod sit irregularis et expers racionis, tamen sompnium vigilis quod est ymaginacio sine fundamento debet habere evidenciam racionis; posset ergo fingi quod ex fundamento huius materie quod omnes vires anime humane tam organice quam non organice sint per se sine subiecto, et quod ille sint plene speciebus, actibus et virtutibus, ut anime beatorum erunt in patria, sic quod habeant plenitudinem beatitudinis consequentis. Sint ergo infinicies infinite qualitates huiusmodi ornate beatitudinis cum disposicionibus predictis que sint sine subiecto, et ponantur in qualibet parte proporcionali hostie infinite. Ille **autem** qui negat hunc casum habet negare fundamentum fabulancium tanquam conditores legis in ista materia; ymmo habita possibilitate casus non evidencius fundabit veritatem opinionis sue de inesse quam fundatur de facto in scriptura casus iam positus; cum ergo tunc foret hostia infinitum plus sacra quam modo defenditur, quare non datur ista sanctitas et honor hostie? Nam quelibet pars talis hostie haberet infinitos per se [p. 136] fines, gracia quorum totum genus humanum racionabiliter laboraret, et quodlibet istorum accidencium foret formaliter beatum haberetque actus beatos perpetuos, et sic foret melius homine misero, nec foret racio quare non posset elicere alios actus, sicut clare videt deitatem et gaudet nimirum gaudio magno. Tale, inquam, gloriosum accidens comederet corporaliter homo in assumendo viaticum, sicut comedit corpus Christi. Infinita sunt talia deliramenta infidelia quibus ex errore invento de eukaristia mendaciter et blaspheme occupatur ecclesia.

ENGLISH: Hence it seems to me that doctor Bonaventure and others dream [inconsistently]{g:inconsequens}[^1] that, for the honour of the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} and the devotion of the people, many things are to be posited of the eucharist which cannot be founded in scripture; and I have a few things [to say] against those who dream inconsistently and too sparingly.[^2] And although it is of the nature of a sleeper's dream that it is irregular and devoid of reason, yet the dream of a waking man, which is imagination without foundation, ought to have the [evidence]{g:evidencia} of reason. It could therefore be feigned, from the foundation of this matter, that all the powers of the human soul, organic as well as non-organic, are [of themselves]{g:per-se} without a subject, and that they are fully [furnished] with species, acts and virtues,[^3] as the souls of the blessed will be in the [homeland]{g:patria}, so that they have the fullness of consequent blessedness. Let there be, then, infinitely infinite qualities of such an adorned blessedness, with the aforesaid dispositions, which are without a subject, and let them be posited infinitely[^4] in each [proportional part]{g:partes-proporcionales} of the [host]{g:hostia}. But he who denies this case has to deny the foundation of those who tell fables as founders of law[^5] in this matter; indeed, granted the possibility of the case, he will not found the truth of his [opinion]{g:opinio} [as a matter of fact]{g:de-inesse} more evidently than the case just posited is in fact founded in scripture. Since, then, the host would then be infinitely more [sacred]{g:sacer} than it is now maintained [to be], why is this holiness and honour not given to the host? For each part of such a host would have infinitely many [136] ends of themselves, for the sake of which the whole human race would reasonably labour; and each of these [accidents]{g:accidens} would be formally blessed and would have blessed acts perpetually, and so would be better than a wretched man; nor would there be a reason why it could not [elicit]{g:elicere} other acts, as it clearly sees the [Godhead]{g:deitas} and rejoices, assuredly, with great joy. Such a glorious accident, I say, a man would eat [bodily]{g:corporaliter} in receiving the viaticum, as he eats [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}. Infinite are such faithless [ravings]{g:deliramenta} with which, from the error invented about the eucharist, the [church]{g:ecclesia} is lyingly and blasphemously occupied.

### 05-f ¶2 · 136.12–137.6  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 136] Et pro isto potest intelligi sentencia Augustini in De Verbis Domini Sermone XXVIIIᵒ dicentis: Dixi vobis quod ante verba Christi quod offertur panis dicatur, **sed** cum verba Christi deprompta fuerint, iam non panis dicitur **sed** corpus appellatur. Iste sanctus voluit quod illud quod prius fuit panis foret corpus Domini virtute sue benediccionis; ideo dicit Sermone LIIIᵒ loquens de eukaristia: Sacramentum quidem pene omnes corpus Christi dicunt. Non, inquam, intendit sanctus panem destrui vel in essencia non manere, ut patet ex dictis, sicut homine facto papa et nomine novo imposito, non propterea nomen perditur, quod ad naturalem proprietatem rei consequitur **sed** sopitur. Nec dubium quin equivocant in predicacione tropica, vocando illum panem corpus Christi modo quo exponit se ipsum Sermone XLVIIIᵒ, notans quomodo Christus nunc dicitur esse in similitudinem carnis peccati et nunc dicitur peccatum. Solet, inquit, similitudo capere nomen rei cuius est similitudo, ut [p. 137] exemplificat de homine picto quod est evidencius de sacramento altaris. Intencio ergo sanctorum est quod in presencia corporis Christi consideracio nature hostie suspenditur et tota intencio sequendi Christum in moribus attendatur, cum natura docet nos consideracionem luminis astrorum diffugere in solis presencia.

ENGLISH: And for this [point] the [teaching]{g:sentencia}[^6] of Augustine can be understood, in *On the Words of the Lord*, sermon 28, where he says: "I told you that before the words of Christ what is offered is called bread; but when the words of Christ have been uttered, it is no longer called bread but is named the body."[^7] This saint meant that what before was bread would be [the body of the Lord]{g:corpus-domini} by virtue of his blessing; therefore he says in sermon 53, speaking of the eucharist: "The sacrament, indeed, almost all call the body of Christ."[^8] The saint did not, I say, [maintain]{g:intendere} that the bread is destroyed or does not remain in its essence, as is clear from what has been said; just as, when a man is made pope and a new name is imposed, the name that follows on the natural property of the thing is not lost on that account, but put to sleep. Nor is there doubt that they speak [equivocally]{g:equivoce} in [tropical predication]{g:predicacio-tropica},[^9] calling that bread the body of Christ in the way in which he expounds himself in sermon 48, noting how Christ is now said to be "in the likeness of sinful flesh" and now is said [to be] "sin".[^10] "A likeness," he says, "is accustomed to take the name of the thing whose likeness it is", as he [137] gives the example of a painted man; which is more evident of the sacrament of the altar. The intention of the [saints]{g:sancti}, therefore, is that in the presence of the body of Christ the consideration of the nature of the host is suspended and the whole intention of following Christ in conduct is attended to, since nature teaches us to turn away from considering the light of the stars in the presence of the sun.

### 05-f ¶3 · 137.7–138.4  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 137] Quarto replicatur per hoc quod multi sancti moriebantur in ista fide; difficile ergo et inopinabile foret impugnare istam fidem vel sanctos illos accusare qui scripserant et defenderant illam fidem. Scribit enim sanctus Thomas super IVᵒ, distinccione XIᵃ, articulo primo: Hec, inquit, posicio que ponit substanciam panis remanere simul cum corpore Christi est incompetens, inpossibilis et heretica. Incompetens quidem, quia impediret veneracionem debitam huic sacramento. Esset enim ydolatrie occasio, si substancia panis ibi remaneret. Esset eciam contra significacionem sacramenti, quia species que inducerent in verum corpus Christi significarent magis substanciam panis. Esset tercio contraria huic sacramento, quia cibus iste non esset pure spiritualis **sed** eciam corporalis. Et quod sit inpossibilis, patet quia inpossibile est aliquid esse nunc, ubi prius non fuit nisi ipso mutato vel alio in ipsum converso; non enim posset eciam per miraculum fieri quod esset animal racionale, mortale et non esset homo; sic ergo corpus Christi non posset aliter se habere nunc quam prius, nisi interveniat mutacio; cum ergo ex parte panis non [p. 138] foret mutacio, nec ex parte corporis Christi, quia sic idem corpus moveretur simul et semel ad diversa loca, quod est inpossibile, cum tunc motus contrarii inessent simul eidem.

ENGLISH: Fourthly it is [rejoined]{g:replicatur} by appeal to this, that many saints died in this faith; it would therefore be difficult and unthinkable to attack this faith or to accuse those saints who had written and defended that faith. For saint Thomas writes on the Fourth [book], distinction 11, article 1:[^11] "This [position]{g:posicio}," he says, "which posits that the [substance]{g:substancia} of the [bread]{g:panis} remains together with the body of Christ, is [unfitting]{g:incompetens}, impossible and heretical. Unfitting indeed, because it would impede the veneration due to this sacrament. For it would be an occasion of idolatry if the substance of bread remained there. It would also be against the [signification]{g:significacio} of the sacrament, because the [species]{g:species} which would lead into the true body of Christ would signify rather the substance of the bread.[^12] It would be, thirdly, contrary to this sacrament, because this food would not be purely spiritual but also bodily. And that it is impossible is clear, because it is impossible that anything be now where before it was not, unless it is itself changed or another is converted into it; for it could not be brought about even by a miracle that [something] should be a rational, mortal animal and not be a man; so therefore the body of Christ could not be otherwise now than before unless a change intervene; since therefore on the part of the bread there [138] would be no change, nor on the part of the body of Christ, because so the same body would be moved at once and together to diverse places, which is impossible, since then contrary motions would be in the same thing at once."[^13]

### 05-f ¶4 · 138.5–138.8  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 138] Quod **autem** sit heretica, patet ex hoc quod contradicit veritati scripture. Non enim esset verum dicere: Hoc est corpus meum, **sed** hic est corpus meum.

ENGLISH: "And that it is heretical is clear from this, that it contradicts the truth of scripture. For it would not be true to say 'This is my body', but 'Here is my body'."[^14]

### 05-f ¶5 · 138.9–138.21  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 138] Hic dicitur quod petitur assumptum, ut puta quod sanctus Thomas moriebatur in ista fide vel alius assignandus, quia nescitur qualem instinctum habuerunt in exitu, et sicut fabule laborant quod servaverunt hoc usque ad mortem, ita laborant fabule non minus probabiles, quod coram populo in mortis articulo retractarunt, quia aliter iuxta istam stulticiam dampnaretur sanctus Origenes cui imponitur ut impio heretico defendisse et in scriptis reliquisse quod nemo dampnabitur perpetuo **sed** ad tempus; ille **autem** qui sine revelacione asserit quod tam pius et tam sanctus sicut fuit hic Origenes sit dampnatus est stulto stulcior.

ENGLISH: Here [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that the [assumption]{g:assumptum} [is begged]{g:petitur},[^15] for instance that saint Thomas died in this faith, or another who might be named, because it is not known what prompting they had at their departure; and just as fables labour [to show] that they kept this until death, so fables no less probable labour [to show] that they retracted before the people at the point of death; for otherwise, according to this folly, saint Origen would be damned, against whom it is charged, as against an impious heretic, that he defended and left in writing that no one will be damned perpetually but for a time;[^16] and he who without revelation asserts that one so pious and so holy as this Origen was is damned is more foolish than a fool.

### 05-f ¶9 · 139.12–139.28  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 139] Et quantum ad illud allegatum de sancto Thoma, dicitur a multis quod pseudofratres post mortem eius corruperant ut plurimum eius scripta et hoc ideo, quia procurarunt esse inquisitores pravitatis heretice propter lucrum; ideo ut habeant laqueos plus subdolos ad involvendum simplices, quorum bona avare siciunt, seminarunt nimis spisse zizania contra fidem scripture; **sed** benedictus Dominus regnum nostrum liberatum est ab ista inquisicione heretice pravitatis, cum multi tam seculares quam religiosi sint longe subtiliores et sufficienciores ad inquirendum in regno nostro vel ubilibet, specialiter in materia de eukaristia, hereticam pravitatem. Cuius evidencia est quod defecerunt in ista materia, primo in visione corporis Christi in sacramento oculo corporali, secundo in materia de impanacione, **sed** in tercio signo magis defecerant.

ENGLISH: And as for that [allegation]{g:allegare} about saint Thomas, it is said by many that the [false friars]{g:pseudofrater} after his death corrupted his writings [for the most part]{g:ut-plurimum}, and this for the reason that they contrived to be inquisitors of [heretical depravity]{g:heretica-pravitas} for gain; therefore, that they might have more cunning snares to entangle the simple, whose goods they greedily thirst for, they sowed [cockle]{g:zizania} [exceedingly]{g:nimis} thick against the faith of scripture. But, blessed be the Lord, our [kingdom]{g:regnum} has been freed from this inquisition of heretical depravity, since many, [seculars]{g:secularis} as well as [religious]{g:religiosi}, are far subtler and more sufficient for inquiring into heretical depravity in our kingdom or anywhere, especially in the matter of the eucharist. The evidence of this is that they have failed in this matter:[^20] first in the vision of the body of Christ in the sacrament with the bodily eye, second in the matter of [impanation]{g:inpanacio}, but in the third [stage]{g:signa} they had failed more.

### 05-g ¶3 · 140.18–141.10  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 140] Iterum, scriptura sacra et antiqui sancti doctores ut Ambrosius, Augustinus et alii autores canonum dicunt regulariter quod hostia consecrata est panis et nunquam dicunt quod est accidens. Que ergo foret presumpcio sollempnisare hoc sine auctoritate ut fidem katholicam? sicut decretum Romane ecclesie tempore Nicholai IIⁱ decrevit quod post consecracionem panis et vinum remaneant (ut patet superius) et concordat plene decretum Ambrosii, ibidem, Omnia quecunque, et breviter quotcunque ponit quod panis erit post consecracionem corpus Domini; cum ergo [p. 141] illud decretum fuit diu ante Innocencium IIIᵘᵐ (ut patet per Cestrensem libro VIIᵒ), videtur quod ecclesia diu viavit in floribus fidei sine ista ficticia (non enim est evidens quod papa Nicolaus IIᵘˢ et tota sua ecclesia cum sanctis doctoribus qui nunquam sciverunt vel recoluerunt istam ficticiam **sed** asseruerunt tamquam per se notum eius contrarium, ut patet per beatum Augustinum superius allegatum de accidentibus) sic dampnata; nimis ceca itaque foret temeritas hereticare in hoc sanctos cum primitiva ecclesia.

ENGLISH: Again, holy scripture and the ancient holy doctors, such as Ambrose, Augustine and other authors of the canons, say [as a rule]{g:regulariter} that the [consecrated host]{g:hostia-consecrata} is bread, and never say that it is an [accident]{g:accidens}. What presumption, then, would it be to solemnize this without [authority]{g:auctoritas} as the catholic faith? as the [decree]{g:decretum} of the Roman church in the time of Nicholas II decreed that after consecration the bread and wine remain (as is clear above),[^6] and the decree of Ambrose, in the same place, *Omnia quecunque*, fully agrees, and, in short, all those who posit[^7] that the bread will be [the body of the Lord]{g:corpus-domini} after consecration; since therefore [141] that decree was long before Innocent III (as is clear from [the Chester chronicler]{g:cestrensis}, book 7),[^8] it seems that the church long [journeyed as a wayfarer]{g:viator}[^9] in the flowers of faith without this [fiction]{g:ficticia} (for it is not evident that Pope Nicholas II and his whole church, with the holy doctors who never knew or called to mind this fiction but asserted its contrary as known [of itself]{g:per-se}, as is clear through the blessed Augustine [alleged]{g:allegare} above on accidents, was so damned);[^10] exceedingly blind, therefore, would be the rashness to [pronounce the saints heretical]{g:hereticare} in this, together with the primitive church.

### 05-g ¶4 · 141.11–142.21  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 141] Ex quo videtur ulterius probabiliter supponendum quod ecclesia tempore Innocencii tercii loquens de transsubstanciacione locuta est de illa ad sensum famosorum doctorum, quo usa fuit ecclesia primitiva. Videtur enim Hugo loqui de transsubstanciacione panis ad sensum quo utitur Ambrosius de conversione in corpus Christi. Transitus **autem** ille vel illa conversio non potest salvari nisi ad illum sensum quo concedunt quod panis fit, erit et est corpus Christi, hoc est, post consecracionem figurat sacramentaliter corpus Christi. Hoc enim est maius miraculum, quam foret conversio de qua fit mencio in lege veteri, ymmo quam foret conversio desinente panis substancia, quia utrobique est corpus Christi eque vere et realiter ibi presens; et super hec tolluntur inconveniencia que ponit panis destruccio. Laudabilis ergo foret mutacio que ponit tot et plura bona et evacuat magis mala. Nec scio aliter concordare decretalem Innocencii et decretum. Non enim est racionabile quod decretalis sit immemor decreti prioris aut quod hereticet priorem ecclesiam sine causa. Unde patet [p. 142] quod stat cum isto sensu quod post istam transsubstanciacionem panis transsubstanciatus remaneat, verumptamen diffido quod ecclesia Avinonica cum suis satrapis significacionem istam acciperet. **Sed** que cura de hoc, cum prior ecclesia hoc approbans fuit gravior, sicut gravior fuit persona Innocencii tercii cum pluribus quam mille prelatis licet non episcopis **sed** abbatibus ydiotis quam ecclesia Gregorii IXⁱ cum satrapis secte sue? Ab illo namque dicitur quinque libros decretalium capere auctoritatem novam, priori sopita, sicut Machomet dicit legem suam ab ipso capere auctoritatem non a lege nova vel veteri. Sic inquam decretales epistole ex multis antiquis edicionibus decretalium prioris ecclesie sunt collecte dimissis partibus verioribus aut displicentibus et novis sive placentibus acceptatis; ideo est novum opus paris auctoritatis cum aliis epistolis pape de novo editis, quia aliter caperent iste ultime decretales ex multitudine paparum priorum longe maiorem auctoritatem quam evangelium. Nam prima epistola decretalis secundum ipsos est paris auctoritatis cum evangelio.

ENGLISH: From which it seems that it is further to be supposed, [with probability]{g:probabiliter}, that the church in the time of Innocent III, speaking of [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio}, spoke of it in the [meaning]{g:sensus} of the renowned doctors which the primitive church used.[^11] For Hugh seems to speak of the transubstantiation of the bread in the meaning which Ambrose uses of the [conversion]{g:conversio} into [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}.[^12] And that passing-over, or that conversion, cannot be saved except in that meaning in which they grant that "the bread becomes, will be and is the body of Christ", that is, after consecration it [figures]{g:figurare} [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} the body of Christ. For this is a greater miracle than would be the conversion of which mention is made in the old law, indeed than would be a conversion with the substance of the bread ceasing, because in both cases the body of Christ is equally [truly]{g:vere} and [really]{g:realiter} present there; and besides this the [absurdities]{g:inconveniens}[^13] that the destruction of the bread posits are [disposed of]{g:tollere}. Praiseworthy, then, would be a change that posits so many goods and more, and empties out more evils. Nor do I know any other way to make the [decretal]{g:decretalis} of Innocent and the decree agree. For it is not reasonable that the decretal should be unmindful of the earlier decree, or that it should pronounce the earlier church heretical without cause. Hence it is clear [142] that it is consistent with this meaning that after this transubstantiation the transubstantiated bread remains; nevertheless I doubt[^14] that the church of Avignon with its [satraps]{g:satrapa} would accept this signification. But what care [need we have] about this, since the earlier church approving this was weightier, just as the person of Innocent III, with more than a thousand prelates (though not bishops but [unlettered]{g:ydiota} abbots), was weightier than the church of Gregory IX with the satraps of his sect?[^15] For from him the five books of decretals are said to take a new authority, the earlier being put to sleep, as Mahomet says that his law takes its authority from himself, not from the new law or the old. So, I say, the decretal letters have been collected from many ancient editions of the decretals of the earlier church, the truer or displeasing parts being left out[^16] and new or pleasing ones accepted; therefore it is a new work, of equal authority with the pope's other letters newly issued, because otherwise these latest decretals would take from the multitude of earlier popes a far greater authority than the gospel. For the first decretal letter, according to them, is of equal authority with the gospel.[^17]

### 05-g ¶6 · 142.27–143.3  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 142] Quantum ad illud trimembre argumentum quo suadetur incompetencia huius sentencie, patet ad primum quo dicitur quod hoc posicio impedit veneracionem debitam huic sacramento, patet quod falsum assumitur, **sed** nititur destruere ydolatriam qua nimis multi colunt corpus mathematicum false [p. 143] fictum per se existere tanquam Deum, et sic nititur cultum erroneum signo exhibitum destruere et totum cultum Christo quem fide ibi videmus colligere.

ENGLISH: As to that three-membered argument by which [the case is made]{g:suadere} for the [unfittingness]{g:incompetencia} of this teaching, it is clear as to the first, in which it is said that "this [position]{g:posicio} impedes the veneration due to this [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}", it is clear that a falsehood is assumed;[^19] rather, it strives to destroy the idolatry by which exceedingly many worship as God a [mathematical body]{g:corpus-mathematicum} falsely [143] feigned to exist of itself; and so it strives to destroy the erroneous worship shown to a [sign]{g:signum}, and to gather the whole worship to Christ, whom by faith we see there.

### 05-g ¶7 · 143.4–143.26  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 143] Et quantum ad occasionem ydolatrie, dictum est superius quomodo Doctor Subtilis destruit hanc evidenciam usque ad fundamentum in ea; nam ita pronus est populus ad colendum hoc sacramentum quod ista via ponit inpossibiliter corpus de genere quantitatis, sicut est pronus colere panem illum ut corpus Christi quod foret ex maiori similitudine minus inconveniens in natura. Et preterea per hoc quod nominatur panis foret populus pronior ex naturali ingenio ad cognoscendum quod non est corpus Christi. Adversarii enim infideliter abscondunt naturalem quidditatem sacramenti sic quod populus non cognoscit distinccionem eius a corpore Christi, in tantum quod ista secta verecundatur detegere suam ficticiam. Unde conveniencius fantasiaret ponendo quod sacramentum non est nisi species vel similitudo rei qualis est in speculo vel medio quam ponendo unam talem rem insolidam ut panis sine subiecto, cum magnus doctor Augustinus ponit philosophice quod illa terna dimensio non potest esse sine subiecto. Ex ista sentencia efficaciter tolleretur falsus cultus ydolatrandi, quo signum vel ymago vel accidens peius ymagine colitur tamquam Deus; **sed** donate istam iniuriam.

ENGLISH: And as to the occasion of idolatry, it has been said above how the Subtle Doctor destroys this [evidence]{g:evidencia} down to the foundation in it;[^20] for the people are as prone to worship this sacrament, which this way impossibly posits [to be] a body of the genus of [quantity]{g:quantitas}, as they are prone to worship that bread as the body of Christ, which, from the greater likeness, would be less absurd in nature.[^21] And moreover, by this, that it is named bread, the people would be more prone, from natural wit, to know that it is not the body of Christ. For the adversaries faithlessly hide the natural quiddity of the sacrament, so that the people do not know its distinction from the body of Christ, so much so that this sect is ashamed to [expose]{g:detegere} its fiction. Hence it would [fancy]{g:fantasiare} more fittingly[^22] by positing that the sacrament is nothing but a [species]{g:species} or likeness of a thing, such as is in a mirror or a medium, than by positing one such [unsolid]{g:insolidus} thing as bread without a subject, since the great doctor Augustine posits philosophically that that threefold dimension cannot be without a subject.[^23] By this teaching the false worship of [idolatrizing]{g:ydolatrare} would effectively be disposed of, by which a sign or an image, or an accident worse than an image, is worshipped as God; but pardon this injury.[^24]
