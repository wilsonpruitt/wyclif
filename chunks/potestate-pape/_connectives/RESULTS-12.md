## Doubtful, left as they are

- **11-h ¶4, p. 292**: *Cum autem signa ista non sint nisi gracia signatorum*, rendered "And since". Either (c), opening the next remark on the signs, or (a), "Now since", the premiss for *signa sine signatorum correspondencia sunt venenum*. "And" is defensible.
- **11-h ¶6, p. 293**: *Diceret autem logicus*, rendered "And a logician would say". (c) as found; it is the first half of the contrast closed by *Sed decretiste*, which carries the "but".
- **11-i, p. 295** (changed above): *deminuere autem libertatem* could also be (c), the next item in what the pontiffs say ("and"); either way "and yet" was off the card. "But" follows the translator's own adversative reading.
- **11-k ¶1, p. 303**: *aliqua prosunt per se … aliqua autem prosunt per accidens*, rendered "while others profit". The correlative *aliqua … aliqua autem* is carried by "some … while others"; "but others" would also do.
- **11-h ¶5, p. 293** and **11-m ¶4, p. 313**: *sed longe evidencius tolleretur* and *Sed gravius peccant* are escalating *sed* ("but far more", "But … more gravely"), classed (b); "and" would also be defensible.
- **11-m ¶7, p. 316**: *Sed iste sanctus dicit*, rendered "But": returns from the friars to Augustine's Herius; (b) as found, though it is close to a resumptive "now".
