# *De potestate pape*, chapter 9, part 3: no middle order; election and the boast of the keys

## ¶1 · 200.30–202.2

> [p. 200] Item, prima Thym. Ill, 2 scribit sibi Apostolus quales condiciones debet habere episcopus: Oportet, inquit, episcopum esse irreprehensibilem unius uxoris virum. Et immediate subiungit: dyaconos autem pudicos, non bilingues, non multo [p. 201] vino deditos, non turpe lucrum sectantes. Si, inquam, inter ordinem episcopalem et ordinem dyaconatus foret ordo medius, iste magnus doctor gencium, qui complete et inmediate evangelium suum didicit a Domino Ihesu Christo, ut patet Gal. primo, distinccionem et regulas illius ordinis non pretermisisset; ymo nusquam in epistola aliqua ponit distinccionem illorum ordinum, sed concludit presbiteros, sacerdotes et episcopos sub nomine apostoli, quem gradum ecclesie inter novem nominat primum tamquam dignissimum, ut patet I. Cor. XII. Et idem dicit Ephes. IV. Unde et illud testantur alii sancti doctores, ut patet in decretis, distinccione XCIII Legimus sub auctoritate Jeronimi, ubi allegantur dicta testimonia Apostoli ad hunc sensum. Et adducitur duplex aliud testimonium, primum illud Apostoli Phil. primo: Paulus et Thymotheus, servus Jhesu Christi, omnibus Sanctis qui sunt Philippis cum episcopis et dyaconibus. Secundum exemplum est dictum Petri I. Pet. V, 1, 2: Seniores igitur, qui in vobis sunt obsecro, consenior et testis Christi passionum, pascite qui in vobis est gregem Dei. Per seniores intelligit iste sanctus cum decreto presbiteros, quibus interdicitur dominatus. Et distinccione XCV Olym sub auctoritate Jeronymi sic recitatur expressius: Olym idem presbiter qui et episcopus, et antequam instinctu dyaboli studia in religione fierent et diceretur in populis: Ego sum Pauli, ego sum Apollo, ego sum Cephe, communi consilio presbiterorum ecclesie gubernabantur. Sicut, inquit, presbiteri sciunt se esse ex ecclesie consuetudine ei, qui sibi prepositus fuerit, esse subiectos, ita episcopi noverint se magis consuetudine quam [p. 202] dispensacionis dominice veritate presbiteris esse maiores et in communi debere ecclesiam regere.

Again, in 1 Timothy 3:2 the Apostle writes to him what conditions a [bishop]{g:episcopus} must have: "It behoveth," he says, "a bishop to be blameless, the husband of one wife." And immediately he adds: "And deacons chaste, not double tongued, not given to [201] much wine, not greedy of filthy lucre."[^1] If, I say, there were a middle order between the episcopal order and the order of the diaconate, this great doctor of the gentiles, who learnt his gospel completely and immediately from the Lord Jesus Christ, as is clear from Galatians 1, would not have passed over the distinction and the rules of that order; indeed, nowhere in any epistle does he set down a distinction of those orders, but he comprehends [presbyters]{g:presbiter}, [priests]{g:sacerdos} and bishops under the name of apostle, the grade of the [church]{g:ecclesia} which he names first among nine, as the most worthy, as is clear from 1 Corinthians 12. And he says the same in Ephesians 4.[^2] Hence the other holy doctors also testify to it, as is clear in the decrees, distinction 93, *Legimus*, under the authority of Jerome, where the said testimonies of the Apostle are [alleged]{g:allegare} to this sense.[^3] And a twofold further testimony is adduced: first that of the Apostle, Philippians 1: "Paul and Timothy, servant of Jesus Christ, to all the saints who are at Philippi, with the bishops and deacons." The second example is the saying of Peter, 1 Peter 5:1–2: "The ancients therefore that are among you, I beseech, who am myself also an ancient and a witness of the sufferings of Christ: feed the flock of God which is among you."[^4] By the ancients this saint, with the decree, understands presbyters, to whom [lordly rule]{g:dominatus} is forbidden.[^5] And in distinction 95, *Olim*, under the authority of Jerome, it is recited more expressly thus: "Of old the presbyter was the same as the bishop; and before, by the devil's prompting, factions arose in religion and it was said among the peoples, 'I am of Paul, I of Apollo, I of Cephas', the churches were governed by the common counsel of the presbyters. Just as," he says, "presbyters know that by the [custom]{g:consuetudo} of the church they are subject to him who has been set over them, so let bishops know that they are greater than presbyters more by custom than by the truth of the Lord's [202] arrangement, and that they ought to rule the church in common."[^6]

## ¶2 · 202.3–202.8

> [p. 202] Tercio principaliter arguitur ad idem per hoc, quod precipuum officium pastoris suspenditur per elecciones novellas, sed suspenso tali officio non est pastor nisi equivoce, igitur expedicius foret ecclesie quod non foret ad talem dignitatem humana eleccio.

Thirdly, as a principal [argument], [it is argued]{g:arguitur-per} to the same [conclusion] by appeal to this: that the chief office of a pastor is [suspended]{g:suspendere} by the new elections; now when such an office is suspended there is no pastor except [equivocally]{g:equivoce}; therefore it would be more expedient for the church that there were no human election to such a dignity.[^7]

## ¶3 · 202.9–202.31

> [p. 202] Assumptum patet de pastu populi verbo Dei, ad quam ydoneitatem non attenditur, sed omnino postponitur in eleccione huiusmodi. Et constat quod per se ab illo pastu pastor ecclesie trahit nomen, non autem a pastu corporali, cum hoc sit officium laycale, ut pulchre declarat Augustinus in De Verbis Domini Sermone XLIX, exponens textum Joh. X de pastore mercenario et lupo. Mercenarius, inquit, videns aliquem loqui peccata aut sentire secundum perniciem anime sue vel aliquid facere sceleratum, et tamen quia videtur habere alicuius momenti personam in ecclesia, unde si sperat commodum, obmutescit. Videt, inquit, hominem perire in peccato, videt illum sequi lupum id est dyabolum, videt illius guttur morsu trahi ad supplicium, et tamen non illi dicit: Peccas, uec illum obiurgat, ne perdat commoda sua; talis mentaliter videt lupum venientem et fugit. Sed bonus pastor usque ad mortem facit contrarium. In quo secundum Augustinum est integra racio pastoralis officii. Securos, inquit, nos fecit Spiritus Sanctus per Ezechielem XXXIII. 7, quia limitavit nobis speculatoris officium.

The [assumption]{g:assumptum} is clear from the feeding of the people with the word of God, a fitness to which no attention is paid, but which is wholly set aside in an election of this kind. And it is evident that the pastor of the church takes his name essentially from that feeding, and not from bodily feeding, since this is a lay office, as Augustine finely declares in *On the Words of the Lord*, sermon 49, expounding the text of John 10 on the hireling shepherd and the wolf.[^8] "The hireling," he says, "seeing someone speak sins, or think to the ruin of his soul, or do something wicked, and yet, because he seems to have a person of some weight in the church, whence, if he hopes for advantage, keeps silent.[^9] He sees," he says, "a man perishing in sin, sees him following the wolf, that is the devil, sees his throat dragged by the bite to punishment, and yet does not say to him, 'You sin', nor rebukes him, lest he lose his advantages: such a one sees the wolf coming in his mind, and flees." But the good shepherd does the contrary even to death. In this, according to Augustine, lies the whole account of the pastoral office. "The Holy Ghost," he says, "has made us secure through Ezekiel 33:7, for he has [assigned]{g:limitare} to us the office of [watchman]{g:speculator}."[^10]

## ¶4 · 202.32–204.29

> [p. 202] Et mundum in testem invoco, quod ista papalis eleccio et sua beneficiorum distribucio seminat [p. 203] in ecclesia mercenarios nimis late, ex parte vero paparum dat occasionem nimis extollendi vicariam potestatem, nimis appreciandi seculi dignitatem et nimis affectandi fantasticam sanctitatem. Illi autem doctores, qui expectant remuneracionem temporalem a papa, dicunt quod est incomprehensibilis potestatis, quia potest omnia que Christus potuit humanitus et multa ultra, que concernunt regimen ecclesie activorum. Nec est aliqua opinio Antichristi magis heretica vel blasfema, quia certum est ex fide quod non habet aliquam potestatem nisi quam Deus dat sibi ad edificandum ecclesiam conformiter legi sue. Quod si habet tantam potestatem, adhuc reddit nimis se dampnabilem, quod non exponit scintillam eius ad regimen presentis ecclesie adeo vacillantis, ymo ut declarat Lumbardus IV sentenciarum distinccione XVIII cap. II et infra ex testimoniis sanctorum Augustini, Ambrosii. Jeronymi, divisus ab ecclesia, ut prescitus non habet potestatem huiusmodi; et quantumcumque verus sacerdos christianus non habet potestatem absolvendi, nisi ad promulgandum divinam sentenciam vel arbitrandum penitenciam aut acceptandum vel refutandum personam secundum quod digna fuerit aut indigna; quod si quis abutitur clavibus discordans ab ecclesia triumphante ostendit se expertem a clavibus sancte ecclesie; et creditur quod supercilium phariseicum ad tantum vibravit claves contra semitas immobiles ecclesie triumphantis, quod perit evidencia et surrepit diffidencia in opere clavium pretendencium se superiorem primatum gerere in ecclesia militante. Sed benedictus Yerarcha tocius ecclesie, quod [p. 204] satis est fidei in ipsum credere. Unde Jeronymus exponens illud Matth. XVI. 29: Tibi dabo claves regni celorum, hunc inquit, locum aliqui non intelligentes, sumunt aliquid de supercilio phariseorum, ut dampnare innoxios vel solvere se putent noxios, cum apud Deum non sentencia sacerdotum sed vita reorum queratur. Sed sicut Levit. XIV ostendere se sacerdotibus iubentur leprosi, quos illi mundos vel leprosos non faciunt, sed discernunt, ita et hic. Unde non video de quo prelatus sic posset pompare inanius quam de huiusmodi potestate, quia ignorat si habeat aliquam huiusmodi potestatem. Ignorat si exercens potestatem reddit se dampnabilem ex errore mendaci ab ecclesia triumphante. Ignorat tercio, si simplex subiectus suus habet eo quantumlibet amplius potestatis. Ideo indubie sacerdotalis potestatis pompacio fetet coram Deo et homine. Coram Deo, quia illi quorum potestas a Deo suspenditur per peccatum vel ex inhabilitate nunquam acceperant potestatem, inmiscent in Christum pompose blasfemias, dicentes se posse ultra hoc quod poterunt apostoli vel eciam Ihesus noster. Addunt eciam in palliacionem sue versucie horrendam heresim, quod Deus pepigit cum eis, quod ipsis assisteret quicquid pretenderent se solvere vel ligare, eo quod quicquid apostoli rite secundum claves ecclesie sic solverint vel ligaverint, Dei assistencia confirmabat.

And I call the world to witness that this papal election, and its distribution of benefices, sows [203] hirelings in the church far too widely, and on the part of the popes gives occasion for exalting the vicarial [power]{g:potestas} too much, for prizing the dignity of the world too much, and for [desiring]{g:affectare} an imaginary sanctity too much.[^11] And those doctors who expect temporal reward from the pope say that he is of incomprehensible power, since he can do all that Christ could do as man, and much beyond, which concerns the rule of the church of the active.[^12] Nor is there any [opinion]{g:opinio} of Antichrist more heretical or blasphemous, for it is certain, [as faith teaches]{g:ex-fide}, that he has no power except what God gives him for building up the church in conformity with his law. And if he has so much power, he still renders himself exceedingly damnable, in that he does not put forth a spark of it for the rule of the present church, wavering as it is; indeed, as Lombard declares in [book] 4 of the *Sentences*, distinction 18, chapter 2 and following, from the testimonies of the saints Augustine, Ambrose and Jerome, one divided from the church, as one [foreknown]{g:prescitus}, does not have power of this kind;[^13] and however true a Christian priest [he be], he does not have the power of absolving except to promulgate the divine [sentence]{g:sentencia}, or to assign penance by his judgement, or to accept or refuse a person according as he shall be worthy or unworthy; and if anyone abuses the keys, in discord with the [church triumphant]{g:ecclesia-triumphans}, he shows himself to have no part in the keys of holy church;[^14] and it is believed that pharisaic haughtiness has brandished the keys so far against the immovable paths of the church triumphant that [evidence]{g:evidencia} perishes and distrust creeps in, in the work of the keys of those who claim to hold a superior [primacy]{g:primatus} in the church militant.[^15] But blessed be the Hierarch of the whole church, that [204] it is enough for faith to believe in him. Hence Jerome, expounding that of Matthew 16:19, "I will give to thee the keys of the kingdom of heaven", says: "This place some, not understanding, take up something of the haughtiness of the Pharisees, so as to think they condemn the innocent or loose the guilty, since with God it is not the sentence of the priests but the life of the accused that is sought. But as in Leviticus 14 the lepers are commanded to show themselves to the priests, whom these do not make clean or leprous but discern, so here too."[^16] Hence I do not see of what a [prelate]{g:prelatus} could boast more emptily than of power of this kind, since he [is ignorant]{g:ignorare} whether he has any power of this kind. He is ignorant whether, in exercising the power, he renders himself damnable by a lying error, [cut off] from the church triumphant. He is ignorant, thirdly, whether his simple subject has however much more power than he.[^17] Therefore without doubt the boasting of priestly power stinks before God and man. Before God, because those whose power is suspended by God through sin, or [who] through lack of [qualification]{g:habilitas} never received the power, pompously mix blasphemies into Christ, saying that they can [do] beyond what the apostles could, or even our Jesus.[^18] They add also, for the [cloaking]{g:palliacio} of their craftiness, a horrible heresy: that God made a pact with them that he would stand by them in whatever they pretended to loose or bind, because whatever the apostles rightly, according to the keys of the church, so loosed or bound, God's assistance confirmed.[^19]

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** 1 Tim. 3:2, without the Vulgate's *ergo* ("therefore"), and 3:8, where Wyclif reads *autem* for *similiter* (DR "in like manner").
[^2]: **S.** Gal. 1:12, the gospel received "by the revelation of Jesus Christ"; 1 Cor. 12:28, "first apostles, secondly prophets, thirdly doctors", the list Wyclif counts as nine grades; Eph. 4:11. **C.** *Concludit*: "comprehends", not the logician's "infer" (GLOSSARY *concludere*).
[^3]: **S.** Decretum D. 93 c. 24 (*Legimus*), from Jerome's letter to Evangelus (Ep. 146); Loserth gives no reference here; form ours (LEDGER P09c-1, to be checked).
[^4]: **S.** Phil. 1:1: Wyclif reads *servus* (singular) for *servi* and omits *in Christo Jesu*. 1 Pet. 5:1–2, leaving out the end of v. 1 ("as also a partaker of that glory ...") and of v. 2.
[^5]: **S.** 1 Pet. 5:3: "Neither as lording it over the clergy" (*neque ut dominantes in cleris*). **C.** *Cum decreto*: with the canon *Legimus* just cited.
[^6]: **S.** Decretum D. 95 c. 5 (*Olim*): Loserth "Cap. V" at 201.27 (LEDGER P09c-2, editor; modern form ours). The canon is taken from Jerome's commentary on Titus 1 (LEDGER P09c-3, to be checked). The party cries are 1 Cor. 1:12. **C.** *Studia*: factions, partisan zeal. **T.** 202.2: the manuscripts lack *et* before *in communi*, supplied by Loserth.
[^7]: **C.** *Elecciones novellas*: the new elections of the present rite. **T.** 202.8: the manuscripts read *humanam*; Loserth prints *humana*, translated.
[^8]: **G.** *Pastor ... a pastu*: the pastor is named from feeding (*pastus*); English loses the pun. "Pastor" for the office, "shepherd" in John 10 and Augustine's figure. **S.** Augustine, Sermon 137 (Loserth: *Opp.* V, 669, Serm. CXXXVII), sermon 49 *De verbis Domini* in the old numbering (LEDGER P09c-4, editor); John 10:12. **R.** A and B in the margin (202.9): *Pastor dicitur a pastu*.
[^9]: **C.** The sentence has no finite verb until *obmutescit*; translated as it stands. *Unde si sperat commodum*: "from whom ... he hopes for advantage"; the *si* sits awkwardly, and the print is translated. **R.** A and B in the margin (202.17): *Mercenarius*.
[^10]: **S.** Ezek. 33:7: "I have made thee a watchman to the house of Israel". **T.** 202.30: the manuscripts read *XXXIV*; Loserth prints *XXXIII*, translated.
[^11]: **C.** *Vicariam potestatem*: the power the pope holds as vicar. *Fantasticam*: "imaginary", of the imagination (cf. GLOSSARY *fantasia*). **R.** A and B in the margin (202.26): *Beneficiorum distribucio papalis*.
[^12]: **C.** *Humanitus*: as man, in his humanity. *Regimen ecclesie activorum*: the rule of the church of those in the active life; or "of active men". **R.** A and B in the margin (203.3): *Potestas pape magna*.
[^13]: **S.** Peter Lombard, *Sentences* IV d. 18 c. 2 ff.; Loserth gives PL 192, 1106 (LEDGER P09c-5, editor). **C.** *Ut prescitus*: "as one foreknown", i.e. as the foreknown are; or "like a foreknown man".
[^14]: **C.** *Sentenciam*: the judicial "sentence" God passes (GLOSSARY *sentencia*, second sense), as *sentencia sacerdotum* at 204.6. *Quantumcumque verus sacerdos christianus*: even the truest priest.
[^15]: **C.** *Evidencia*: the credibility of the work of the keys. **T.** 203.31: one manuscript (the siglum is illegible in the scan's apparatus) lacks *perit evidencia*.
[^16]: **S.** Jerome, *Commentary on Matthew* III, on 16:19 (Loserth: PL 26, 118) (LEDGER P09c-6, editor). Lev. 14:2: "He shall be brought to the priest". **T.** The print gives Matthew "XVI, 29" (so the plate); the verse is 16:19, as in Loserth's note, and is given here. **C.** *Yerarcha*: Christ, the hierarch of the whole church.
[^17]: **C.** *Ex errore mendaci ab ecclesia triumphante*: a lying error that sets him apart from the church triumphant (cf. *discordans ab ecclesia triumphante*, 203.26). *Simplex subiectus suus*: a simple man under him. **R.** A and B in the margin (204.10): *Clavium abusio et pompa*.
[^18]: **C.** *Vel ex inhabilitate nunquam acceperant potestatem*: a second relative clause without *qui*. *Poterunt* for *potuerunt*, "could". *Inmiscent in Christum*: they mix blasphemies into [what they say of] Christ. **T.** 204.19: the manuscripts read *ille*; Loserth prints *illi*, translated.
[^19]: **C.** *Eo quod*: the ground they allege, God's confirmation of what the apostles bound and loosed *rite*, "rightly", stretched to *quicquid*, whatever these pretend to bind and loose.
