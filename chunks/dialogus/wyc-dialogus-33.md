# *Dialogus*, chapter 33: true and false trade, buying and selling

## Truth · 78.11–80.20

> [p. 78] Video quod adversarius noster non movet efficaciter pro parte sua racionibus vel scriptura, sed pocius irritat socios et contendit; nos autem talem consuetudinem non habemus, sicut nec ecclesia dei, ut docet paulus [1ª] Cor. [2°]. Ideo dimissis omnibus tradicionibus hominum vocatis legibus, et dictis scriptorum sequencium, que nec in fide scripture nec in racionibus sunt fundata, videtur quod in fine veritas catholica cum silencio sit loquenda. Sed primo pro declaracione dictorum de commercio sacerdotum et de simonia cum aliis commerciis hodie usitatis, videndum est quid sit emere et vendere. Et videtur quod ipsum sumptum analogice sit unam rem pro alia voluntarie commutare; et intelligo terminos analogice modo quo dicitur deum vendere beatitudinem et promerentem de congruo ipsam emere. Et isto modo videtur deum loqui ys 55°. Venite et emite absque argento et absque ulla commutacione vinum et lac. Et sic intelligendo commercium commune ad empcionem et vendicionem videtur quod empcio sit personam ementem facere [p. 79] aliquid esse suum, et vendicio sit personam vendentem facere suum esse persone ementis. Et sic deus analogice vendit beatitudinem, quia facit ipsam esse persone de congruo promerentis; et promerens emit beatitudinem, quia facit ipsam ex commutacione cum domino esse suam. Et sic commutacio intelligi potest dupliciter; primo modo proprie pro commutacione substancie quantum ad proprietatem possessionis, modo quo emens bovem commutat argentum vel aliud precium pro eodem (et isto modo videtur isayam loqui de commutacione in autoritate proposita, cum deus non potest mutari, nec aliquid substancie vel nature ementis beatitudinem perditur ab emente); 2° modo laxius pro commutacione cujuscunque rei substancie vel racionis, modo quo respectus et alie veritates dicuntur res, et sic beatificatus, licet habeat is deum pro substancia sue beatitudinis, habet tamen sibi propriam beatitudinem respectivam. Et sic commutat suum meritum ex dei gracia pro eadem. Et sic duplex videtur commercium; primum verum a deo tanquam principali vendente racionabiliter approbatum: secundum est commercium fantasticum sive falsum, et illud fit a diabolo tanquam capitaliter commercante, et illo modo omnis commercacio symoniaca sive illicita dicitur commercium a domino deacceptum. Istis premissis videtur mihi quod sacerdos potest licite cum suo corporali labore et mercede sensibili commercari, sed quod vendat suam oracionem vel meritum est racioni contrarium, quia valor sue oracionis vel meriti est supra suam possessionem in manu dei. Et sic quicunque vendunt beneficia ecclesiastica, vel literas fraternitatum aut meriti videntur commercari cum diabolo et contra deum blasfemare, cum fingunt se vendere quod deo est proprium. Ideo ne sacerdos incidat in istam blasfemiam videtur securum quod [p. 80] dicat in principio ipsum velle accipere alimenta et tegumenta pure titulo elemosyne, et velle e contra quantum deus acceptaverit in sacerdotali officio laborare. Et sic videtur quod papa vel patronus alius conferens beneficium ecclesiasticum debet pure ex titulo elemosine ad expressum spirituale curati officium limitare; et in principio convenire quod accipienda elemosina non est sua, sed dei et populi reverendi; ideo sine retribucione corporali reddenda sibi exspectat a deo premium, ut beneficiatur sincere et spiritualiter prosit ecclesie. Et multa talia signa sacerdotum et novorum ordinum hodie introducta videntur esse signa generacionis adultere et non a domino approbata. Et hec racio quare consecraciones corporum vel locorum per episcopos usitate, litere fraternitatum et alie commutaciones sacerdotum pro consecracione eucaristie in lege domini non fundantur, sed labor liber et utilis, modo quo foret in statu innocencie; et commercia secularia sunt prope periculum propter fraudem. Qui ergo haberet istam sentenciam clare discussam posset de simonia et aliis commerciis illicitis atque beatitudinis meritis clare loqui.

TRUTH.[^1] I see that our adversary does not argue effectively for his side by reasons or by
scripture, but rather provokes his fellows and contends; but we have no such
[custom]{g:consuetudo}, nor the [church]{g:ecclesia} of God, as Paul teaches, 1 Corinthians 2.[^2]
Therefore, setting aside all the traditions of men that are called laws, and the sayings of later
writers, which are founded neither in the faith of scripture nor in reasons, it seems that in the
end the catholic truth is to be spoken with silence.[^3] But first, to make clear what has been said
of the [trade]{g:commercium} of [priests]{g:sacerdos} and of [simony]{g:simonia}, together with the other trades
in use today, we must see what it is to buy and to sell. And it seems that this, taken analogically,
is voluntarily to [exchange]{g:commutacio} one thing for another; and I understand the terms analogically in the way
in which God is said to sell blessedness, and one who merits it [by congruity]{g:de-congruo} to buy
it. And in this way God seems to speak in Isaiah 55: "Come, buy wine and milk without money, and
without any price."[^4] And so, understanding trade as common to buying and selling, it seems that
buying is for the person buying to make [79] something be his own, and selling is for the person
selling to make what is his own be the buying person's. And so God analogically sells blessedness,
because he makes it belong to the person who merits by congruity; and the one who merits buys
blessedness, because he makes it his own by [exchange]{g:commutare} with the [Lord]{g:dominus}. And so exchange can
be understood in two ways: in the first way, properly, for an exchange of substance as regards the
ownership of a possession, in the way in which one buying an ox exchanges silver or some other price
for it (and in this way Isaiah seems to speak of exchange in the authority set forth, since God
cannot be changed, nor is anything of the substance or nature of the one buying blessedness lost by
the buyer);[^5] in the second way, more loosely, for an exchange of anything whatever, of substance
or of aspect, in the way in which [relations]{g:respectus} and other truths are called things; and so the blessed
one, although he has God as the substance of his blessedness, nevertheless has a relational
blessedness proper to himself. And so he exchanges his merit, [had] from God's grace, for it.[^6]
And so trade seems to be twofold: the first true, reasonably approved by God as the principal
seller; the second is fantastic or false trade, and that is done by the devil as chief trader, and
in that way all simoniacal or unlawful [trading]{g:commercari} is called trade disowned by the Lord.[^7] With these
premised, it seems to me that a priest may lawfully trade with his bodily labour and
with [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} wages; but that he should sell his prayer or merit is contrary to
reason, because the worth of his prayer or merit is above his possession, in the hand of God. And so
all who sell [ecclesiastical benefices]{g:beneficium-ecclesiasticum}, or [letters of fraternity]{g:litere-fraternitatum} or of
merit,[^8] seem to trade with the devil and to blaspheme against God, since they pretend to sell what
is proper to God. Therefore, lest a priest fall into this blasphemy, it seems safe that [80] he say
at the outset that he wishes to accept food and covering purely by [title]{g:titulus} of
[alms]{g:elemosina}, and in return to labour in the priestly office as much as God will have
accepted. And so it seems that the [pope]{g:papa} or other [patron]{g:patronus} conferring an ecclesiastical
benefice ought, purely by title of alms, to [assign]{g:limitare} [it] to the express spiritual office
of the [curate]{g:curatus}; and at the outset to agree that the alms to be received is not his, but
God's and the people's, who are to be revered; therefore, with no bodily recompense to be rendered to
him, he awaits a reward from God, so that [the curate] may be beneficed sincerely and may profit the
church spiritually.[^9] And many such signs of priests and of the new orders, introduced today, seem
to be signs of an adulterous generation,[^10] and not approved by the Lord. And this is the reason
why consecrations of bodies or of places customary among [bishops]{g:episcopus}, letters of
fraternity, and other exchanges of priests for the consecration of the eucharist are not founded in
the law of the Lord, but free and useful labour is, as it would be in the state of innocence; and
secular trades are near to danger on account of fraud. Whoever, therefore, had this
[teaching]{g:sentencia} clearly discussed could speak clearly of simony and of the other unlawful
trades, and of the merits of blessedness.

## Pollard's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 78.18: Before treating of the commerce of priests and simony we must see what is buying and selling.
- 78.27: Buying is the acquisition, and selling the transference, of property in any article.
- 79.3: God may thus analogically be said to sell bliss to a person deserving it de congruo, and he to buy it.
- 79.6: Barter may be understood in two ways: (i) strictly, of exchange in possession, while the thing remains unchanged, as man receives bliss, that is God, while God remains unchanged, and man loses nothing of his nature. (ii) loosely, when there is a change in the thing, or in its mode of being, as when merit by God's grace is exchanged for bliss.
- 79.21: All simoniacal barterings are of the second class, and in these the devil is chief trafficker.
- 79.24: A priest may traffic in his labour and in sensible things; but not in his prayers or merit, the worth of which rests with God.
- 79.28: To sell benefices, letters of fraternity or merit is devil's traffic.
- 79.31: Priests should therefore accept food and clothing only as alms;
- 80.5: and patrons confer benefices only as such.
- 80.6: Remembering that the alms are not theirs, but God's and the people's.
- 80.9: In presenting to a benefice the patron should look to God for the reward of profit to the Church.
- 80.12: The signs of the present system are the signs of an adulterous generation.
- 80.14: Consecration of bodies and places by bishops, letters of fraternity, priests' bargainings for masses have no Scripture authority, but free and useful labour has.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** The chapter opens with no speaker's name in the print. The speaker calls Falsehood
    "our adversary" and continues the argument of chapter 32, so it is Truth.

[^2]: **S/T.** The words quoted are 1 Cor. 11:16 (DR: "we have no such custom, nor the Church of
    God"), not chapter 2. The numbers *[1ª]* and *[2°]* are in the editor's brackets, and Pollard's
    margin gives "1 Cor. ii."; the English keeps the print's reference.

[^3]: **C.** *Cum silencio sit loquenda*: "is to be spoken with silence", i.e. without the
    contention just blamed on the adversary. It may also mean "while [all the rest] is kept silent",
    the traditions and later writers having been set aside.

[^4]: **S.** Isa. 55:1, which Wyclif quotes as the Vulgate with *et* added (*venite et emite*). DR
    renders *absque ulla commutacione* "without any price"; Wyclif's analysis turns on the Latin word,
    which in what follows is "exchange".

[^5]: **C.** The parenthesis takes Isaiah's "without any exchange" to deny exchange in this first,
    proper sense: nothing passes out of the buyer's substance, and God is not changed.

[^6]: **C.** *Is* (79.14) is taken as the pronoun "he", the blessed one. *Ex dei gracia* may attach
    to *meritum* ("his merit, [which is] from God's grace", so the side-note) or to *commutat* ("by
    God's grace he exchanges his merit"). *Pro eadem*: for the blessedness.

[^7]: **T.** *Deacceptum*, "not accepted, disowned" (plate: *de-|acceptum*). B, C read *deo* for
    *domino*, "disowned by God".

[^8]: **T.** *Aut meriti*: "or [letters] of merit". B had *merita*, corrected to *meriti*; B¹ reads
    *merita*, which would make it "or merits".

[^9]: **C.** *Sua*, *sibi* and *exspectat* refer to the patron (so the side-notes: "not theirs, but
    God's and the people's"; "the patron should look to God"). *Populi reverendi* is taken as "the
    people, who are to be revered"; *reverendi* may instead go with *dei* as well ("of God and the
    people, to be revered"). *Beneficiatur* passes to the curate as subject, as does *prosit*.

[^10]: **S.** Matt. 12:39, "an evil and adulterous generation seeketh a sign": Wyclif plays on
    *signa*, the outward signs of the priests and new orders.
