# sed/autem extract, wave 6: chunks 03-d 03-e 03-f

### 03-d ¶1 · 65.29–66.5  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 65] **Sed** contra istud arguitur primo per hoc quod nulla quantitas potest taliter maiorari, nam vel manet quantitas que maioraretur vel non. Si manet, vel est permixta cum quantitate que advenit; et patet [p. 66] quod tunc ad omnem punctum fit nova quantitas antiqua corrupta; si vero manet inpermixta, non illa fit maior **sed** generaliter nova que non est sacramentum, ex quibus fit sacra et non sacra quantitas bipartita.

ENGLISH: But against this [it is argued]{g:arguitur-per} first by appeal to the fact that no [quantity]{g:quantitas} can be enlarged in such a way; for either the quantity that would be enlarged remains or it does not. If it remains, either it is mixed with the quantity that comes to it, and it is clear [66] that then at every point a new quantity comes to be, the old one having been corrupted; but if it remains unmixed, it is not that quantity that becomes greater, but [there is] generally a new one, which is not the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}; and from these a quantity comes to be in two parts, [sacred]{g:sacer} and not sacred.[^1]

### 03-d ¶3 · 66.22–66.33  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 66] Tercio arguitur per hoc quod signata a terra pedali cui linitur pedalis quantitas, illa terra est et foret formaliter pedalis in illo casu possibili quo Deus anichilaret quantitatem illam **sed** servaret terram omnino inmotam in situ eodem; **sed** tunc remaneret formaliter pedalis, ergo est dare unam pedalitatem intrinsecam priorem illa linita qua formaliter est pedalis; et illa non potest per se esse, cum sit istam terram sic esse quantam; et distinccionem illius nemo potest destruere; et eadem est consideracio de qualitate quod non habet propriam raritatem, densitatem et similes qualitates distinctas a subiecto.

ENGLISH: Third, it is argued by appeal to the fact that, a foot-long [piece of] earth being designated, a,[^3] on which a foot-long quantity is [smeared]{g:linire}, that earth is, and would be, formally foot-long in that possible case in which God [annihilated]{g:anichilare} that quantity but kept the earth altogether unmoved in the same place; now then it would remain formally foot-long; therefore [one may posit]{g:est-dare} an intrinsic [foot-length]{g:pedalitas}, prior to that smeared one, by which it is formally foot-long; and that [foot-length] cannot be [of itself]{g:per-se}, since it is this earth's being so [quantified]{g:quantus}; and no one can destroy its distinctness. And the same consideration holds of [quality]{g:qualitas}: that it does not have its own rarity, density and like qualities distinct from the subject.[^4]

### 03-d ¶4 · 67.1–67.15  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 67] Item, secundum falsigraphos substancia potest manere quanta sine quantitate distincta et sic de omni respectu; nec est racio quin per idem sic senciendum est de toto genere qualitatis, ergo non est dare accidens preter substanciam; cum ergo constat ex sensu quod remanent post consecracionem res materiales, restat illis concedere quod remanent materiales substancie. Quid ergo foret istud corpus remanens nisi panis et vinum, ut dicit decretum Romane ecclesie? Cum **autem** hoc sit argumentum ad hominem, peto maiorem ut datam. Minor patet ex hoc quod eque ad quantitatem potest esse motus ut ad qualitatem; eque ponunt philosophi et probant raciones alia accidencia distingui ut qualitates, eque ponunt qualitates non posse separari a subiecto ut alia accidencia.

ENGLISH: Again, according to the [false writers]{g:falsigraphus}[^5] [substance]{g:substancia} can remain quantified without a distinct quantity, and so of every [relation]{g:respectus}; nor is there a reason why, by the same token, the same should not be thought of the whole genus of quality; therefore one may not posit an accident besides substance. Since, therefore, it is evident from sense that material things remain after the consecration, it remains for them to grant that material substances remain. What, then, would that remaining body be but bread and wine, as the [decree]{g:decretum} of the Roman [church]{g:ecclesia} says?[^6] And since this is an [argument to the man]{g:argumentum-ad-hominem}, I ask that the major be taken as granted. The minor is clear from this: that there can be motion to quantity equally as to quality; equally do the philosophers posit, and arguments prove, that the other accidents are distinguished [from substance] as qualities are; equally do they posit that qualities cannot be separated from a subject, as [they posit of] the other accidents.

### 03-e ¶1 · 69.22–71.3  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 69] Nec valet allegare in isto miracula ad colorandum quamcunque opinionem contrariam veritati; non enim fundatur fictum miraculum. Secundo quia sic accidens factum subiectum transsubstanciacionis crearet in casu et anichilaret et tamen cum sit res inanimata, non habens substanciam que sustentaret eius mutacionem, nulla una permanens qualitas posset pati vel agere. Assumptum sic arguitur: Posito quod calor per se agat in sacramento, quousque fiat alimentum [p. 70] et redeat eadem materia, patet quod diu erit antequam redibit materia panis (ut concedunt adversarii), et tota qualitas que iterum corrumperetur anichilabitur, sicut qualitas que interim producetur creabitur, cum non sit materia que suscipiat illam transmutacionem naturalem, sic quod qualitas non corrumpetur in potencia materie, **sed** corrumpetur in nichilum vel totaliter producitur ex nichilo, sicut agens naturale suscitat priorem materiam panis ex nichilo. Talis enim substancia non generatur ex accidente, ut patet quod, licet ecclesia sollempnizet hec signa supra substancias, sicut transfert ad contraria nomina ministrorum ecclesie, manente tamen disposicione quam Deus instituit vel variancia, non oportet quod natura rei proporcionaliter consequatur, ut posito quod anima vel quecunque forma materialis inmediate actuet huiusmodi accidens absolutum per se servatum foret substancia composita ut est modo et vel nulla creatura potest per se esse sine miraculo vel vocatum accidens potest per se esse sine novo miraculo vel fingi posset per idem miraculum. Unde tolleretur omnis descripcio creature. Sic enim fingunt sollempnes doctores quod generato verme de hostia anima sensitiva induceretur in accidencia sine materia; quare ergo non consumeretur ex tibi dubio tota materialis substancia facta generacione et motu inter talia accidencia, ymmo cum materia individuatur ab instanti sue creacionis, videtur quod nova creatur et non sic inevitabiliter antiqua sustentatur et sic quot substancie convertuntur in corpus Christi tot materie prime sunt perdite [p. 71] et nemo nostrum cognosceret huiusmodi materialem substanciam. Quomodo ergo auderet quisquam conficere vel iurare de actu materialis substancie.

ENGLISH: Nor does it avail to [allege]{g:allegare} miracles in this matter to [give colour to]{g:colorare} any [opinion]{g:opinio} contrary to the truth; for a feigned miracle is not grounded. Second, because in this way an [accident]{g:accidens} made the subject of [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio} would, in a given case, create and [annihilate]{g:anichilare}; and yet, since it is an inanimate thing, not having a [substance]{g:substancia} to sustain its change, no one permanent [quality]{g:qualitas} could be acted on or act. [The assumption]{g:assumptum} is argued thus. Suppose that heat acts [of itself]{g:per-se} in the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} until it becomes nourishment[^1] [70] and the same matter returns: it is clear that it will be long before the matter of the [bread]{g:panis} returns (as the adversaries grant), and the whole quality that would in the meantime be corrupted will be annihilated, just as the quality that will be produced in the meantime will be created, since there is no matter to receive that natural transmutation; so that the quality will not be corrupted into the potency of matter, but will be corrupted into nothing, or is produced wholly from nothing, just as the natural agent raises up the former matter of the bread from nothing. For such a substance is not generated from an accident; as is clear, because although the [church]{g:ecclesia} solemnizes these signs over substances, just as it transfers the names of the ministers of the church to their contraries,[^2] yet, whether the disposition which God instituted remains or varies,[^3] the nature of the thing need not follow in proportion. So, supposing that a soul or any material form immediately actuated such an [absolute accident]{g:accidens-absolutum} kept of itself, it would be a composite substance as it is now; and either no [creature]{g:creatura} can be of itself without a miracle, or the [so-called]{g:vocatus} accident can be of itself without a new miracle, or [anything] could be feigned by the same miracle. Hence every [definition]{g:descripcio} of a creature would be taken away. For in this way solemn doctors [feign]{g:fingere} that, when a worm is generated from the [host]{g:hostia}, a sensitive soul would be induced into the accidents without matter.[^4] Why then, [for all you know]{g:ex-tibi-dubio},[^5] would not the whole material substance be consumed, generation and motion having taken place among such accidents? Indeed, since matter is individuated from the instant of its creation, it seems that a new [matter] is created and the old is not so inevitably sustained; and so, as many substances as are converted into [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}, so many [prime matters]{g:materia-prima} are lost, [71] and none of us would know such material substance. How then would anyone dare to [consecrate]{g:conficere} or to swear about the act of material substance?

### 03-e ¶2 · 71.4–71.14  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 71] Item, ecclesia non est oneranda novitate tali insolita nisi vel fides scripture vel vivax racio vel efficax sanctorum testimonium ad hoc cogat, **sed** quodlibet istorum trium deficit predicto errori de sacramento altaris, ergo et racio sic onerandi ecclesiam. Assumptum patet ex multis testimoniis sanctorum et veritate ac sufficiencia legis Christi. Sic enim coleretur abhominacio desolacionis, quia accidens entitatis minime, tamquam Deus, vel saltem coleretur tamquam sacrum signum mendacissime accidens loco substancie et imponeretur Deo quod sit auctor mendacii.

ENGLISH: Again, the church is not to be burdened with such an unaccustomed novelty unless either the faith of scripture, or living reason, or the effective testimony of [the saints]{g:sancti} compels it to this; now each of these three is lacking to the aforesaid error about [the sacrament of the altar]{g:sacramentum-altaris}; therefore so is the reason for so burdening the church. The assumption is clear from many testimonies of the saints, and from the truth and sufficiency of [the law of Christ]{g:lex-christi}. For in this way "the abomination of desolation" would be worshipped,[^6] since an accident of the least entity [would be worshipped] as God; or at least an accident would be worshipped, most mendaciously, as a [sacred]{g:sacer} sign in place of substance, and it would be imputed to God that he is the author of a lie.

### 03-e ¶7 · 72.3–72.10  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 72] Unde quantum ad testimonia sanctorum, reduco ex multis triplex testimonium Augustini;. primo illud dictum de Quantitate Anime capitulo IVº, ubi dicit quod corpus non potest esse sine altitudine; prorsus, inquit, non dubito corpora omnia longitudine, latitudine et altitudine carere non posse, **sed** numquid potest cogitari ista tria non esse nisi in corporibus? Non intelligo, inquit, quomodo alibi esse possint.

ENGLISH: Hence, as to the testimonies of the saints, I bring forward, out of many, a threefold testimony of Augustine. First, that saying in *De quantitate animae*, chapter 4, where he says that a body cannot be without height: "I do not at all doubt," he says, "that all bodies cannot lack length, breadth and height; but can it be thought that these three are not, except in bodies? I do not understand," he says, "how they could be elsewhere."[^10]

### 03-e ¶8 · 72.11–72.27  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 72] Et quoad qualitatem scribit IIº Soliloquiorum XIIº capitulo: Esse aliquid in aliquo nos non fugit duobus modis dici. Uno modo localiter ut corpus seiungi atque alibi esse possit, ut hoc lignum in hoc loco et sol in oriente. Altero **autem** quo ita est aliquid in subiecto, ut ab eo nequeat separari, ut in hoc ligno forma et species quam videmus, ut in sole lux, ut in igne calor et in anima disciplina et si qua sunt alia. Ista, inquit, vetustissima nobis sunt et ab ineunte adolescencia studiosissime percepta et cognita, quia non possum de hiis interrogatus, quin ea sine ulla deliberacione concedam. Et sequitur: Illud vero quod interrogasti, quis concesserit aut cui posse fieri, videatur ut id quod est in subiecto maneat ipso intereunte subiecto? Monstruosum enim et a veritate alienissimum ut id quod non esset nisi in ipso esset, eciam cum ipsum non fuerit possit esse.

ENGLISH: And as to quality, he writes in *Soliloquies* II, chapter 12:[^11] "It does not escape us that something is said to be in something in two ways. In one way locally, so that it can be separated and be elsewhere, as this wood in this place, and the sun in the east. In the other, by which something is so in a subject that it cannot be separated from it, as in this wood the form and appearance which we see, as in the sun light, as in fire heat, and in the soul learning, and whatever others there are. These things," he says, "are very old to us, and from early youth most studiously grasped and known, because I cannot, being asked about them, fail to grant them without any deliberation."[^12] And [it follows]{g:sequitur}: "But as to what you asked, who would grant, or to whom would it seem possible, that what is in a subject should remain when the subject itself perishes? For it is monstrous and most foreign to the truth that that which would not be unless it were in it could be even when it itself is not."

### 03-f ¶1 · 73.19–75.4  (7 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 73] Quantum ad glosas et opiniones modernorum doctorum in ista materia, patet quod ut plurimum sunt frivole; ipsi enim vix corticem verborum sanctorum suorum priorum conceperant; et ideo deficiente intelligencia hii negant et imponunt erronee sanctis stulticiam et hii ad ostensionem maioris stulticie fingunt eis glosas nimis extraneas, ut patet de glosa Doctoris Communis ad dicta Augustini de potenciis anime, quomodo memoria, racio et voluntas non sunt essencia anime inseparabiles qualitates, Augustinus [p. 74] **autem** ponit in utroque oppositum. **Sed** movet me dictum illius magni doctoris et sciencioris modernis Hugonis, IIº De Sacramentis, Parte VIIIª in cuius IXº capitulo ita scribit: Per verba sanctificacionis vera panis et vini substancia in verum corpus Christi et sanguinem convertitur, sola specie panis et vini remanente et substancia in substanciam transeunte. Conversio **autem** ipsa non secundum unionem **sed** secundum transicionem credenda est: quoniam nequaquam essencia in augmentum essencie accidit ut per id quod accedit id ad quod accedit cum illo ad quod accedit unum fiat. Nec sic in pane corpus Christi consecrari dicimus, ut de pane corpus Christi esse accipiat, nec quasi novum corpus subito factum de mutata essencia, **sed** in ipsum corpus verum mutatam essenciam; nec ipsam substanciam panis et vini in nichilum redactam, quia desiit esse quod fuit, **sed** mutatam pocius, quia incepit esse aliud quod non fuit et ipsum quod cepit esse ex ea esse non accipiat, quia panis fuit, **sed** ipsa eius esse accepit quando desiit esse quod fuit. Hoc expressius distinximus propter eos qui ex sua racione fidei preiudicium faciunt et sensu suo incedentes asserere nituntur vel hoc esse solum quod cernitur vel tale esse quod creditur; hoc est, quia sola panis et vini species cernitur solam ibi esse panis et vini substanciam vel quia ibi substancia corporis et sanguinis esse [p. 75] creditur possibiliter ei inesse speciem et qualitatem panis et vini que cernitur quasi non possit species apparere cuius non assit substancia vel substancia latere cuius non appareat forma.

ENGLISH: As to the [glosses]{g:glosa} and [opinions]{g:opinio} of [the modern doctors]{g:moderni-doctores} in this matter, it is clear that [for the most part]{g:ut-plurimum} they are frivolous; for they had scarcely grasped the bark of the words of their own earlier [saints]{g:sancti}; and therefore, understanding failing them, some deny and erroneously impute folly to the saints, and others, to the display of greater folly, feign for them glosses [exceedingly]{g:nimis} strange; as is clear of the gloss of [the Common Doctor]{g:doctor-communis} on Augustine's sayings about the powers of the soul, how memory, reason and will are not the [essence]{g:essencia} of the soul [but] inseparable qualities,[^1] [74] but Augustine posits the opposite on both points. But I am moved by the saying of that great doctor, more knowing than the moderns, Hugh, in *De sacramentis* II, part 8, in whose ninth chapter he writes thus:[^2] "Through the words of sanctification the true [substance]{g:substancia} of [bread and wine]{g:panis} is converted into the true body of Christ and blood, the [species]{g:species} of bread and wine alone remaining, and substance passing into substance. And the [conversion]{g:conversio} itself is to be believed to be not by [union]{g:unio} but by transition: since in no way does essence come to the increase of essence so that, through that which accedes, that to which it accedes becomes one with that to which it accedes.[^3] Nor do we say that the body of Christ is consecrated in the bread in such a way that [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} receives its [being]{g:esse} from the bread, nor as if a new body were suddenly made from the changed essence, but [we say that] the changed essence [passes] into the true body itself; nor [do we say] that the substance itself of bread and wine is reduced to nothing, because it has ceased to be what it was, but rather that it is changed, because it has begun to be another thing which it was not; and that which it began to be does not receive being from it, because it was bread, but it itself received its being when it ceased to be what it was. This we have distinguished more expressly on account of those who out of their own reason prejudice the faith, and, going by their own sense, strive to assert either that this alone is which is seen, or that what is believed is such [as is seen]; that is: because only the species of bread and wine is seen, [they assert] that only the substance of bread and wine is there; or, because the substance of the body and blood is believed to be there, [75] [they assert] that the species and quality of bread and wine which is seen can possibly be in it; as if a species could not appear whose substance is not present, or a substance lie hidden whose form does not appear."[^4]

### 03-f ¶2 · 75.5–75.26  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 75] Ista verba doctoris incusserunt michi formidinem nec video quin doctor in illis intelligit quod fidelis suscitet intellectum ad credendum quod solum corpus Christi sit post consecracionem ibi principaliter pane et vino in principalitate illa corpori Christi cedentibus, unde propter amissionem illius principalitatis dicit nec panem nec vinum remanere **sed** in corpus Christi converti. Accidencia **autem** habent ibi principalem existenciam ad expergefaciendum sensus fidelium sine subiecto suo ibi principaliter existente; verumtamen ipsa informant panem et vinum, non **autem** corpus Christi vel sanguinem, **sed** illa informacio propter presencialitatem dignioris corporis est sopita, sicut Petro tenente in domo principalitatem dominii post successionem Christi, in eadem domo cui Petrus omnino succedit in servum nomen Petri tacetur et nomen Christi in hiis que sunt dignitatis vel principalitatis ibi erigitur. Sic est quodammodo de corpore Christi et pane qui post conversionem, licet remaneat secundum essenciam, tamen esse principalitatis ab eo tollitur, cum remanet secundarie serviens superiori corpori ut figura.

ENGLISH: These words of the doctor struck [misgiving]{g:formido} into me; nor do I see but that the doctor means in them that the faithful man should rouse his understanding to believe that the body of Christ alone is there [principally]{g:principaliter} after the consecration,[^5] the bread and wine [giving way]{g:cedere} to the body of Christ in that principality;[^6] hence, because of the loss of that principality, he says that neither bread nor wine remains, but [that it is] converted into the body of Christ. And the [accidents]{g:accidens} have there a principal existence, to awaken the senses of the faithful, without their subject existing there principally; nevertheless they [inform]{g:informare} the bread and wine, not however the body or blood of Christ; but that informing is lulled asleep because of the presence of the worthier body. So, when Peter holds the principality of [lordship]{g:dominium} in a house after succeeding Christ, in the same house, in which Peter wholly passes into [the place of] a servant,[^7] the name of Peter is silent, and the name of Christ is raised up there in the things that belong to dignity or principality. So it is in a certain way with the body of Christ and the bread, which after the conversion, although it remains as to its essence, yet has the being of principality taken from it, since it remains, secondarily, serving the superior body as a [figure]{g:figura}.

### 03-f ¶4 · 76.6–76.18  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 76] Et iterum, attribuimus in aliis terminacionem actus corpori principali, accessorio dimisso, ut dicimus nos emere vel vendere equum vel asinum et non materiam eius vel formam, sicut legiste dicunt se non excommunicare propter pecuniam **sed** propter inobedienciam consumatam; et cadit utrimque ex equivocacione illusio, potest tamen modus loquendi fundari ex illo Joh. IVº, 42: Mulieri dicebant quia iam non propter tuam loquelam credimus, ipsi enim audivimus et scimus quia hic est vere salvator mundi, ubi oportet intelligere negacionem super principalitatem muliebris evidencie; et tales sunt multi modi loquendi in scriptura.

ENGLISH: And again, in other things we attribute the terminus of an act to the principal body, the accessory being set aside: as we say that we buy or sell a horse or an ass, and not its matter or its form; just as the [legists]{g:legiste} say that they excommunicate not on account of money but on account of consummated disobedience;[^9] and on both sides [illusion]{g:illusio} falls out of equivocation. Yet the manner of speaking can be grounded on that of John 4:42: "They said to the woman: We now believe, not for thy saying: for we ourselves have heard him and know that this is indeed the Saviour of the world";[^10] where one must understand the negation [as falling] on the principality of the woman's [evidence]{g:evidencia}; and such are many manners of speaking in scripture.
