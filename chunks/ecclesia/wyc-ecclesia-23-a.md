# *De ecclesia*, chapter 23, part 1: indulgences: what the word means, and the canonists' limits on who may grant them

## ¶1 · 549.2–550.23

> [p. 549] Restat consequenter tractare de indulgenciis et absolucionibus a pena et culpa, in quibus videtur esse error apud simplices istam materiam ignorantes. In primis ergo suppono significacionem quid nominis huius signi indulgencia. Est enim omnis indulgencia actus indulgentis, ita quod indulgencia non sit aliud nisi habentem ad hoc potenciam indulgere, et est indulgere gratis concedere, operam dare sive remittere, et componitur de inde et algere (quod est frigere vel mortificari), sicut est de commissis oblivioni traditis quoad expeticionem vindicte. Et sic loquitur scriptura Judith VIII°, 14: Indulgenciam domini cum lacrimis postulemus, et sic sumitur Isaie XXVI°, LXI° et LXIII°. Sic autem indulgere peccatoribus est Deo proprium, sicut et peccata dimittere (ut patet capitulo ultimo quinti libri); sed ab istis sensibus extractus est terminus et baptizatus in sensum alium partim per doctores theologie et partim per canonistas; quandoque enim secundum eos connotat remissionem pene, ut secunda questione tercio capitulo Indulgencie, ut notat Archidiaconus, quod quandoque signat active concessionem, ut dominus papa indulsit subdito hoc vel illud, id est, concessit; aliquando autem signat dispensacionem, aliquando ius, ut notat Johannes, XXXIII, q. IIª. Interfectores; et aliquando accipitur pro remittere vel condonare, in De Penitenciis [p. 550] et Remissionibus, cap. Indulgencie in principio per Archidiaconum; unde omnes istas indulgencias trahunt et limitant ad prelatos ecclesie. Unde statuunt sibi pro regula quod solus papa potest dare quoad locum et tempus indulgencias generales, episcopi autem habent potestatem limitatam faciendi indulgencias. Nam cum dedicant ecclesias, non extenditur eorum indulgencia ultra annum, eciamsi a pluribus episcopis dedicetur. In aliis autem ultra numerum quadraginta dierum non potest episcopus indulgere, verumptamen episcopus confirmatus antequam sit sacerdos, sicut et legati non sacerdotes faciunt indulgencias, quia hec potestas magis eis ex iurisdiccione quam ex ordine. Inferiores autem ut abbates et presbiteri non possunt indulgencias tales concedere publice ex auctoritate iurisdicionis, sed private in foro penitencie (ut patet in De Penitenciis et Remissionibus, capitulo Indulgencie); super vero conceduntur per Innocentem et Archidiaconum: licet, inquit, restricta sit potestas de indulgenciis que fiunt publice, non tamen in foro penitenciali in quo sacerdos potest indulgere annos et dies dummodo discrete faciat et penitenciam iniunctam per superiorem non remittat.

It remains next to treat of [indulgences]{g:indulgencie} and of absolutions from punishment and guilt, in which there seems to be error among simple people who are ignorant of this matter.[^1] First, then, I suppose the signification of this sign *indulgencia*, [what the name means]{g:quid-nominis}. For every indulgence is an act of one who indulges, so that indulgence is nothing other than one who has the power for it indulging; and to indulge is to grant freely, to give one's effort, or to remit; and it is compounded of *inde* and *algere* (which is to be cold or to be mortified), as is the case with offences handed over to oblivion as regards the seeking of vengeance.[^2] And so scripture speaks in Judith 8:14: "Let us with tears beg the Lord's indulgence"; and so it is taken in Isaiah 26, 61 and 63.[^3] And to indulge sinners in this sense belongs to God alone, as also to forgive sins (as is clear in the last chapter of the fifth book);[^4] but the term has been drawn out of these senses and baptized into another sense, partly by the doctors of theology and partly by the canonists. For sometimes, according to them, it connotes remission of punishment, as in the second question, third chapter, *Indulgencie*, as the Archdeacon notes: that sometimes it signifies actively a grant, as "the lord pope indulged a subject this or that", that is, granted; and sometimes it signifies a dispensation, sometimes a right, as John notes on C.33 q.2 *Interfectores*; and sometimes it is taken for "to remit" or "to pardon", in *De penitenciis* [550] *et remissionibus*, chapter *Indulgencie*, at the beginning, by the Archdeacon.[^5] Hence they draw all these indulgences in and [limit]{g:limitare} them to the [prelates]{g:prelatus} of the [church]{g:ecclesia}. Hence they lay down for themselves as a rule that the [pope]{g:papa} alone can give indulgences general as to place and time, but bishops have a limited power of making indulgences. For when they dedicate churches their indulgence does not extend beyond a year, even if the dedication is made by several bishops; and in other cases a bishop cannot indulge beyond the number of forty days. Nevertheless a bishop confirmed before he is a priest, as also legates who are not priests, make indulgences, because this power is theirs from jurisdiction rather than from order.[^6] And inferiors, such as abbots and presbyters, cannot grant such indulgences publicly by authority of jurisdiction, but privately in the court of penance (as is clear in *De penitenciis et remissionibus*, chapter *Indulgencie*); and on the word *conceduntur*, by Innocent and the Archdeacon: "Although," he says, "the power is restricted concerning indulgences which are made publicly, yet not in the penitential court, in which a priest can indulge years and days, provided he does it discreetly and does not remit a penance enjoined by a superior."[^7]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 549.2: Indulgence to sinners belongs to God alone.
- 549.15: The canonists have given it another sense: of remission of punishment.
- 550.1: Such indulgences are reserved to prelates under certain limitations.

## Notes

[^1]: **T.** A heads the chapter *Cap. XXII.* (549.1). **C.** *A pena et culpa*: the formula of the plenary indulgence, "from punishment and guilt". *Simplices*: simple people, the unlearned. **R.** A in the margin at 549.2: *De indulgenciis*.

[^2]: **C.** *Significacionem quid nominis huius signi*: Wyclif begins from what the name means (row), as he began from *ecclesia* (5.11). *Indulgencia non sit aliud nisi habentem ad hoc potenciam indulgere*: an accusative and infinitive, "nothing other than one who has the power for it indulging". *Operam dare*: to give one's effort or service, one of the dictionary senses of *indulgere*. The derivation from *inde* and *algere* (to grow cold) is the grammarians' (not found in PL; LEDGER E23a-1): an offence handed to oblivion grows cold as to the seeking of vengeance. **T.** B *signacionem* (549.5); B *remittere componitur*, without *et* (549.9–10). **R.** B in the margin at 549.7: *Quid est indulgencia?* Cf. Hus, *Opera* I, 175 (Loserth, 549.5; LEDGER E23a-2).

[^3]: **S.** Judith 8:14: the Vulgate reads *indulgentiam ejus fusis lacrimis postulemus*, Douay–Rheims "with many tears let us beg his pardon"; Wyclif's *domini … cum lacrimis* is translated, and "indulgence" kept, since the word is his point. Isaiah 26:15 *Indulsisti genti* (Douay–Rheims "Thou hast been favourable to the nation"), 61:1 *praedicarem captivis indulgentiam* ("to preach a release to the captives"), 63:7 *secundum indulgentiam suam* ("according to his kindness"): Wyclif gives the chapters only; the verses are ours.

[^4]: **C.** *Deo proprium*: proper to God, his alone (side-note 549.2). *Capitulo ultimo quinti libri*: *De civili dominio* III c. 27, as at 547.17 (chapter 22, part 8), on suffrages and indulgences (Society ed. IV, 644); that only God remits sin is argued in its c. 26 (608: *hoc solus Deus potest*) (LEDGER E23a-3). **R.** Side-note 549.2; A in the margin at 549.20: *Nota quid est indulgere vel indulgencia; supra vide*.

[^5]: **C.** *Baptizatus in sensum alium*: the term is "baptized" into a new sense (B lacks *baptizatus*, 549.18). *Quod quandoque signat*: taken as reporting the Archdeacon's note. **S.** *Secunda questione tercio capitulo Indulgencie*: so the codices; Loserth corrects to *Liber Sextus* 5.10.3 *Indulgentiae*, with the gloss of the Archdeacon, Guido de Baysio (Loserth, 549.21; the form is ours; LEDGER E23a-4). *XXXIII, q. IIª. Interfectores*: Decretum C.33 q.2 c.5, Nicholas I to Archbishop Rodulf of Bourges (Loserth, 549.26; the form is ours; LEDGER E23a-5). Loserth would read *Nicolaus papa* for *Johannes*, the canon being Nicholas I's; but *ut notat Johannes* names the glossator Johannes Teutonicus: the ordinary gloss on c. 5, v. *Indulgentia*, weighs whether an indulgence is a dispensation or a *ius* (glossed Decretum, 1588), as Wyclif reports, so the emendation is not needed (LEDGER E23a-6). **T.** B *convocat* for *connotat* (549.20). **R.** Side-note 549.15.

[^6]: **G.** *Limitant*: "limit", in the sense of restrict (row *limitare*), not "assign". **C.** *Quoad locum et tempus*: general as to place and time. *Magis eis ex iurisdiccione quam ex ordine*: the verb is understood. **S.** The year for a dedication and the forty days otherwise are the rule of the Fourth Lateran Council, c. 62, X 5.38.14 *Cum ex eo* (LEDGER E23a-7). **R.** Side-note 550.1. A in the margin at 550.3, only partly legible in the page foot: *Indulgencia … potestas*, with *convenit* added; B in the margin at 550.14: *Potestas indulgendi est ex iurisdiccione et non ex ordine, ut hic patet*.

[^7]: **T.** *Super verbo* is our emendation for the print's *super vero*, which does not construe: the canonists cite a gloss "on the word" (cf. *in verbo*, 551.4), and the word glossed is taken as *conceduntur*. B *Inserentes autem* for *Inferiores autem* (550.14); A *discreta* (550.22). **C.** *In foro penitencie*: the court of penance, the confessional, set against public jurisdiction. *Inquit*: singular, though two glossators are named. The quotation is closed at *remittat*, where the print's italics end. **S.** Innocent IV and the Archdeacon on *Indulgentiae*, VI 5.10.3 (LEDGER E23a-8); Innocent's apparatus is on the Decretals of Gregory IX, so his gloss may be on X 5.38.14 (not traced: no readable copy of Innocent's or the Archdeacon's apparatus was found on archive.org, and the OCR of the glossed Sext of 1584 is unusable at that place). Sext 5.10.3 has *conceduntur*.
