# *De eucharistia*, chapter 2, part 1: the three opinions, and the faith of the ancient Roman church

## ¶1 · 29.25–30.5

> [p. 29] Premissa ista communi sentencia de isto venerabili sacramento restat scrutari quid ipsum sit realiter. In qua materia sunt tres vie: prima videtur esse sentencia sancti Thome cum suis sequacibus quod Eukaristia [p. 30] sit magnitudo de genere quantitatis, quia cum oportet ipsum esse accidens, quantitas autem est primum accidens post genus substancie, oportet illud sacramentum sensibile esse quantitatem continuam permanentem qua panis perante formaliter fuit quantus.

This common [teaching]{g:sentencia}[^1] about this venerable [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} having been set out first, it remains to scrutinize what it is [really]{g:realiter}. In this matter there are three ways: the first seems to be the teaching of St Thomas with his followers, that the eucharist [30] is a magnitude of the genus of [quantity]{g:quantitas}, because, since it must be an [accident]{g:accidens}, and quantity is the first accident after the genus of [substance]{g:substancia}, that [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} sacrament must be the continuous permanent quantity by which the [bread]{g:panis} was before formally [quantified]{g:quantus}.[^2]

## ¶2 · 30.6–31.20

> [p. 30] Secunda opinio videtur esse Scoti qui inprobat opinionem priorem per hoc quod hoc sacramentum potest rarefieri et condensari, quod non potest competere quantitati; ideo cum sit accidens consonum, videtur ipsum ponere qualitatem, cum oportet quod sit accidens absolutum. Unde aliqui istius secte dicunt quod est figura, aliqui quod est qualitas prima, et alii quod est qualitas secunda, ut color, sapor vel aliud huiusmodi sextuplex vel ex multis accidentibus aggregatum. Tercia opinio est Beringarii, et videtur esse decretum sancte sinodi. Constat quidem ex publica et sollempni confessione Beringarii posita in De Consecracione, distinccione 2ª, Ego Beringarius, quod ipse corrigendo errorem priorem confitetur panem et vinum esse hoc sensibile sacramentum. Nam (ut recitavi superius) ibi inscribitur sua confessio sub hiis verbis: Credo panem et vinum que in altari ponuntur post consecracionem non solum sacramentum sed eciam verum corpus et sanguinem domini nostri Jesu Christi esse sensualiter non solum sacramentum, sed in veritate manibus sacerdotum tractari et frangi et fidelium dentibus atteri. Ubi patet quod ille sensit quod idem panis et vinum quod ponebatur ante missam super altare remanet post consecracionem [p. 31] tam sacramentum quam corpus dominicum. Quod autem ista fuit tunc sentencia Romane ecclesie, patet ex tribus: primo quia in eodem decreto ex verbis notarii sic habetur: Hoc lecto et perlecto sponte subscripsi. Hanc confessionem sue fidei de corpore et sanguine domini nostri Jesu Christi a Beringario Rome coram centum 14ᶜⁱᵐ episcopis factam. Ex quo patet quod ecclesia tante sinodi non permitteret revocacionem prioris heresis et correpcionem suam sine vituperio sub hiis verbis nisi suam fidem tamquam katholicam approbaret. Secundo confirmatur ex eodem decreto quod ista fuit tunc Romane ecclesie sentencia. Nam ibidem sic scribitur: Misit papa Nicholaus per urbes Italie, Germanie Gallie et ad quecunque loca fama pravitatis eius pervenire ante potuit, ut ecclesie que prius doluerant de adverso atque diverso, post ea gauderent de reverso atque converso. Ex istis luculenter patet quod ecclesia tunc sensit sentenciam predictam Beringarii esse katholicam et sentenciam illi contrariam esse hereticam.

The second [opinion]{g:opinio} seems to be that of Scotus, who disproves the prior opinion by this, that this sacrament can be rarefied and condensed, which cannot belong to quantity;[^3] therefore, since it is an accident in keeping [with this], he seems to posit it to be a [quality]{g:qualitas}, since it must be an [absolute accident]{g:accidens-absolutum}.[^4] Hence some of this sect say that it is figure, some that it is a first quality, and others that it is a second quality, such as colour, taste or something else of this kind, sixfold, or aggregated out of many accidents.[^5] The third opinion is Berengar's, and it seems to be the [decree]{g:decretum} of the holy synod. For it is evident from the public and solemn confession of Berengar, placed in *De consecratione*, distinction 2, *Ego Berengarius*, that he, correcting his earlier error, confesses the bread and wine to be this perceptible sacrament.[^6] For (as I [recited]{g:recitare} above) his confession is there written in these words: "I believe that the bread and wine which are placed on the altar are after the consecration not only a sacrament but also the true body and blood of our Lord Jesus Christ, [sensually]{g:sensualiter} not only a sacrament, but in truth handled and broken by the hands of the priests and ground by the teeth of the faithful."[^7] Where it is clear that he [held]{g:sentire} that the same bread and wine which was placed on the altar before the mass remains after the consecration [31] both a sacrament and the Lord's body.[^8] Now that this was then the teaching of the Roman [church]{g:ecclesia} is clear from three [things]: first, because in the same decree, from the notary's words, it is held thus: "This having been read and read through, I have subscribed of my own accord. This confession of his faith concerning the body and blood of our Lord Jesus Christ, made by Berengar at Rome before one hundred and fourteen bishops."[^9] From which it is clear that the church of so great a synod would not have let the revocation of the earlier heresy and his [reproof]{g:correpcio} pass without censure in these words unless it approved his faith as catholic.[^10] Secondly, it is confirmed from the same decree that this was then the teaching of the Roman church. For it is written there thus: "Pope Nicholas sent [it] through the cities of Italy, Germany, Gaul, and to whatever places the fame of his depravity had been able to reach before, so that the churches which before had grieved over him turned against and turned aside might afterwards rejoice over him turned back and converted."[^11] From these it is luminously clear that the church then held the aforesaid teaching of Berengar to be catholic and the teaching contrary to it to be heretical.

## ¶3 · 31.21–32.4

> [p. 31] Tercio confirmatur idem per hoc quod in prima revocacione sua protestatus fuerat sub hiis verbis: Consencio autem sancte Romane ecclesie et apostolice sedi, et ore ac corde profiteor de sacramentis dominice mense eandem fidem me tenere quam dominus et venerabilis papa Nicholaus et hec sancta sinodus auctoritate evangelica et apostolica tenendam tradidit michique confirmavit. Et post confitetur sentenciam supradictam. Ex quibus colligitur quod sentenciam [p. 32] illam receperat a papa et sinodo confitendam; et patet quod olim fuit sensus ecclesie, ut de articulo fidei quod panis et vinum remanent post consecracionem Eukaristie sicut ante.

Thirdly, the same is confirmed by this, that in his first revocation he had [declared solemnly]{g:protestari} in these words: "And I consent to the holy Roman church and the apostolic see, and with mouth and heart I profess that I hold, concerning the sacraments of the Lord's table, the same faith which the lord and venerable [pope]{g:papa} Nicholas and this holy synod have delivered to be held by evangelical and apostolic [authority]{g:auctoritas} and have confirmed to me."[^12] And afterwards he confesses the teaching mentioned above. From which it is gathered that he had received that teaching [32] from the pope and the synod, to be confessed; and it is clear that it was once the mind of the church, as of an [article of faith]{g:articulus-fidei}, that the bread and wine remain after the consecration of the eucharist as before.

## ¶4 · 32.5–32.9

> [p. 32] Ex quibus colligitur quod altera istarum ecclesiarum erravit in fide vel equivocavit in loyca vel significacione, vel tercio quod in tempore successit credendi varietas, sic quod illud quod tunc fuit articulus fidei iam est falsum.

From which it is gathered that one or the other of these churches erred in faith, or equivocated in [logic]{g:loyca} or [signification]{g:significacio}, or thirdly that in time a variety of believing followed, so that what was then an article of faith is now false.[^13]

## ¶5 · 32.10–32.15

> [p. 32] Sed constat quoad hoc tercium, quod equa racione verum foret quod panis et vinum remanent post consecracionem in Eukaristia, sicut umquam fuit verum vel credendum; ideo cum equivocacio in materia fidei non excusat, videtur quod altera ecclesia erravit.

Now it is evident as to this third [way], that by equal reason it would be true that the bread and wine remain after the consecration in the eucharist, as ever it was true or to be believed; therefore, since [equivocation]{g:equivocacio} in a matter of faith does not excuse, it seems that one or the other church erred.

## ¶6 · 32.16–32.32

> [p. 32] Ex quo patet primo quod Romana ecclesia potest errare in articulis fidei, cum sic fecit; patet secundo quod non oportet credere quod si Romana ecclesia quidquam katholicat vel hereticat, ergo vere; et tercio videtur probabile quod prior ecclesia sane sensit in isto et posterior oberravit; tanta namque auctoritas, tanta sanctitas et tanta sapiencia vel maior lucebat in isto papa Nicholao II° secundum cronicas et istis 114ᶜⁱᵐ episcopis, sicut in eo qui composuit decretales. Quare ergo non crederetur tante vel plus sue sentencie sicut debiliori sentencie succedenti? Ecclesia enim deteriorando quoad fidem scripture procedit. Et iterum prior sentencia plus consonat sensui, racioni, sanctis doctoribus et scripturis, igitur cum illa non sit posterius abrogata vel inprobata, videtur quod ecclesia adhuc debet illam credere sicut primo.

From which it is clear, first, that the Roman church can err in articles of faith, since it did so; it is clear, second, that one need not believe that if the Roman church [declares]{g:catholicare} anything catholic or [pronounces]{g:hereticare} it heretical, then [it does so] [truly]{g:vere}; and third, it seems probable that the earlier church held soundly in this and the later went astray; for so great authority, so great holiness and so great wisdom, or greater, shone in that pope Nicholas II, according to the chronicles, and in those 114 bishops, as in him who composed the decretals.[^14] Why then should not as much credence, or more, be given to their teaching as to the weaker teaching that succeeded it?[^15] For the church proceeds by growing worse as to the faith of scripture. And again, the earlier teaching is more consonant with sense, reason, the holy doctors and the scriptures; therefore, since it has not afterwards been abrogated or disproved, it seems that the church ought still to believe it as at first.

## ¶7 · 33.1–33.8

> [p. 33] Quod autem plus consonat sensui et racioni patet inferius, et quod plus consonat sanctis doctoribus patet ex tribus: primo ex dicto beati Ambrosii, posito in De Consecracione, distinccione IIª Omnia quecunque: Licet figura panis et vini videatur, nichil tamen aliud quam caro Christi et sanguis post consecracionem credenda sunt. Ecce quod panis et vinum credenda sunt esse caro Christi et sanguis.

And that it is more consonant with sense and reason is clear below; and that it is more consonant with the holy doctors is clear from three [sayings]: first from the saying of blessed Ambrose, placed in *De consecratione*, distinction 2, *Omnia quecunque*: "Although the [figure]{g:figura} of bread and wine is seen, yet nothing other than the flesh and blood of Christ are to be believed [to be there] after the consecration."[^16] See: the bread and wine are to be believed to be the flesh and blood of Christ.

## ¶8 · 33.9–33.19

> [p. 33] Secundo confirmatur eadem sentencia ex dicto beati Augustini eadem distinccione, Qui manducant, ubi loquens de hoc sacramento sic dicit: Quod videtur, inquit, panis est et calix quod oculi renunciant; quod autem fides postulat instruenda, panis est corpus Christi et calix est sanguis. Ista ideo dicuntur sacramenta, quia in eis aliud videtur et aliud intelligitur. Quod videtur speciem habet corporalem, quod intelligitur fructum habet spiritualem. Ecce quod iste sanctus ponit panem et vinum esse sacramentum visibile.

Secondly, the same teaching is confirmed from the saying of blessed Augustine in the same distinction, *Qui manducant*, where, speaking of this sacrament, he says thus: "What is seen," he says, "is bread and the [chalice]{g:calix}, which the eyes report; but what faith, which is to be instructed, requires: the bread is the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} and the chalice is the blood. These are therefore called sacraments, because in them one thing is seen and another understood. What is seen has a bodily [species]{g:species}; what is understood has spiritual fruit."[^17] See: this saint posits the bread and wine to be the visible sacrament.

## ¶9 · 33.20–33.26

> [p. 33] Tercio confirmatur ex dicto Hilarii eadem distinccione, ubi sic scribitur: Corpus Christi quod sumitur de altari figura est, dum panis et vinum extra videntur, veritas autem, dum corpus Christi et sanguis in veritate interius creduntur. Quotlibet sunt dicta similia dictis istorum trium sanctorum que expresse dicunt sentenciam quam utrobique Beringarius confitetur.

Thirdly, it is confirmed from the saying of Hilary in the same distinction, where it is written thus: "The body of Christ which is taken from the altar is a figure, while the bread and wine are seen outwardly; but the truth, while the body and blood of Christ are believed inwardly in truth."[^18] There are any number of sayings like the sayings of these three [saints]{g:sancti} which expressly say the teaching that Berengar confesses in both places.[^19]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 30.1: 1. that of St. Thomas who says that it is a quantity;
- 30.6: 2. that of Scotus who says it is quality;

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Sentencia* (CONTEXTUAL): the position taught, "teaching", throughout this chunk; the *communis sentencia* is that of ch. 1.

[^2]: **S.** Thomas: the dimensive quantity of the bread as the subject of the remaining accidents. The place is *ST* III q. 77 a. 2 (*accidentia … esse sicut in subiecto in quantitate dimensiva panis vel vini remanente*), with *In IV Sent.* d. 12 q. 1 a. 1 qc. 3; Loserth's references (plate: *ST* III q. 76; *In IV Sent.* d. 10 q. 1 a. 2) do not treat it (LEDGER K02a-1).

[^3]: **S.** Scotus's argument from rarefaction and condensation; Loserth: Duns Scotus, *De Eucharistia* (Lyon 1639), scholium III (LEDGER K02a-2).

[^4]: **C.** *Cum sit accidens consonum*: "since it is an accident in keeping [with this]", that is, an accident that can be rarefied and condensed; D reads *consonum ipsum videtur*. The construal is uncertain.

[^5]: **C/T.** *Figura* here is the Aristotelian quality (shape), not the sacramental figure; untagged. *Sextuplex*, "sixfold", is added in C by another hand (30.14) and its reference is unclear; perhaps the kinds of second quality.

[^6]: **S.** Gratian, *De cons.* D. 2 c. 42, as at 25.19; Loserth's reference, the form ours (LEDGER K02a-3).

[^7]: **T/C.** Here the print has *non solum sacramentum* where 26.1 has *non solum sacramentaliter*; Loserth notes at 30.25 "Recte: nisi in solo sacramento", citing Berengar's confession. Translated as printed.

[^8]: **C.** *Sensit*: "held", the sense of opinion; not the row *sentire* "perceive", which is of the sacrament perceived. So *sensit* at 31.18 and *sane sensit* at 32.20.

[^9]: **T/S.** The notary's sentence breaks off at *factam*; it continues in the *Misit papa Nicholaus* clause quoted at 31.13 (Loserth: "Cetera infra sequuntur"). ABC read *sponte scripsi*. The number *centum 14ᶜⁱᵐ* (114) is the print's; the canon reads *CXIII* (113; Ivo's *Decretum* CXX), Friedberg, *De cons.* D. 2 c. 42; Wyclif's 114 recurs at 32.24 (LEDGER K02a-4).

[^10]: **C.** *Sine vituperio*: the synod would not have let Berengar's revocation and reproof stand uncensured "in these words" unless it approved the faith they state. ABC read *et concepcionem* for *et correpcionem* (31.9).

[^11]: **C.** *De adverso atque diverso … de reverso atque converso*: the canon's wordplay on *-versus*, kept as far as English allows.

[^12]: **T.** *Revocacione* (31.22): Herzberg-Fränkel read *ratificacione*, D *renovacione*. ABCE lack *auctoritate* (31.27), C adding it in the margin in another hand.

[^13]: **C.** *Altera istarum ecclesiarum*: one of the two, the ancient church of Nicholas II and the present church. The three options (error in faith, equivocation, a change of faith in time) are taken in reverse in 32.10–15.

[^14]: **S/R.** Nicholas II's Roman synod (1059), where Berengar swore the oath, "according to the chronicles"; Friedberg on c. 42: *Ex Concilio Romano hab. a. 1059* (LEDGER K02a-5). *Eo qui composuit decretales*: Wyclif names no one; the *Liber Extra* is Gregory IX's (promulgated 1234), compiled by Raymond of Peñafort; promulgated by the bull *Rex pacificus*, 5 Sept. 1234 (LEDGER K02a-6).

[^15]: **C.** *Sue sentencie*: the reflexive refers to Nicholas with the synod; rendered "their".

[^16]: **S.** *De cons.* D. 2 c. 74 (*Omnia quecunque*), under Ambrose's name; Loserth's reference (OCR "LXXTV"), the form ours, with *De apostasia* p. 64 (LEDGER K02a-7).

[^17]: **S/C.** *De cons.* D. 2 c. 58 (*Qui manducant*), under Augustine's name; Loserth's reference, the form ours (LEDGER K02a-8). The words quoted are Augustine, *Sermo* 272 (PL 38, 1246–1247); the canon's opening *Qui manducant* is *Sermo* 131.1 (LEDGER K02a-9). *Instruenda* agrees with *fides*: faith that is to be instructed. AD read *quod autem panis*; ABC *Ideo ista* (33.14).

[^18]: **S/T.** *De cons.* D. 2 c. 79, under Hilary's name; Loserth's reference, the form ours (LEDGER K02a-10). Loserth notes "Recte: videtur … creditur" (33.23–24), the singular verbs of the canon; the print's plurals are translated.

[^19]: **C.** *Utrobique*: "in both places", Berengar's two confessions (the oath and the first revocation, 31.21).
