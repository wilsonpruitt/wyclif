# *De ecclesia*, chapter 19, part 2: the predestinate are priests in the homeland; the foreknown minister the sacraments; first conclusion: clerical litigation should cease

## ¶1 · 445.31–448.13

> [p. 445] Unde videtur mihi quod nomina recitati officii eciam in predestinatis exhinc merentibus beatitudinem sunt quedam specialia, ut apostolatus et episcopatus. Nam missio ad evangelizandum et superintendencia solicita ad custodiendum videntur tantum pro statu [p. 446] vie officiariis sponsi competere. Sed sacerdocium videtur esse eternum, cum beati dant et docent sacra illuminando suos socios in eternum, et ad illud potest applicari illud Apoc. 1°, 6: Fecisti nos Deo nostro regnum et sacerdotes; et sic predestinati qui digne presunt subiectis videntur mihi esse eis in beatitudine preferendi, et sic in celesti ierarchia eis in perpetuo sacerdocio sunt prelati, ut innuit beatus Gregorius, Omelia XVIIª super illo Luce X°, 1: Post hec designavit dominus alios septuaginta, et Crisostomus expressius, Omelia XXXIV Operis Imperfecti Melior, inquit, est laicus qui in prima facie profitetur vitam secularem sed studet spiritualem, quam sacerdos profitens vitam spiritualem sed amplectitur vitam carnalem, et melior est laicus ante Deum penitenciam agens quam clericus permanens in peccatis. Laicus enim in die iudicii stolam sacerdotalem accipiet, dum eciam crismate omnes sic unguentur in sacerdocio; sacerdos autem peccator spoliatur sacerdocii dignitate quam habuit; et tunc erit inter infideles et ypocritas (ut dicit veritas Matthei XXIV°, 51). Omnes eciam beati erunt virgines spiritualiter (ut patet Apocalypsis XIV°, 4). Et omnes erunt martyres modo suo, cum Luce XIV°, 27, dicitur: Qui non baiulat crucem suam et venit post me, non potest meus esse discipulus. Omnes (inquam) erunt sacerdotes in patria, quia dantes sacra beatitudinis accidentalia conbeatis. Et hinc Abel a quo cepit humana ecclesia in triumpho, fuit expresse virgo, martir et sacerdos, ut notat Augustinus in De [p. 447] Mirabilibus Scripture Sacre, III° capitulo. Tota, inquit, iusticia, hec est virginitas, sacerdocium et martirium. Que triplex iusticia in Abel primo fuit qui et munera Deo placita obtulit, prosapiam generis in seculo non dereliquit et martirii coronam sanguinis effusione promeruit. Quilibet ergo salvandus debet esse spiritualiter virgo in hoc quod declinans a malo sequitur agnum sponsum ecclesie; debet secundo esse sacerdos, offerens Deo fructum meriti, et debet tercio connectendo hec duo ad invicem in istis perseverare finaliter tamquam martir. Et ideo signanter notat Christus Matthei XXIII°, 35 quomodo ecclesia dicitur cepisse ab Abel iusto cui secundum Augustinum Jesus commisit primatum iusticie. In hoc, inquit, quod non nominat Zachariam iustum, ut Abel iustum, hereditatem iusticie Abel tenere Zachariam ostendit, sicut in genealogia Christi scribitur primo et solitarie David fuisse regem, dum dicitur: Gesse autem genuit David regem; Salomoni autem et aliis non additur nomen regis, cum alii patres regnum iure hereditario tenuerunt. Sed notanda est alia causa (quam dixi superius) quod Abel fuit primus veteranus ecclesie triumphantis qui spe felici spiritualis nectaris missus est in locum refrigerii expurgandus. Et patet ad formam argumenti quod Deus quidquid creature racionali dederit, eam prius ad ipsum habilitat; et quantum ad decretum beati Gregorii, patet quod intelligitur abutentem potestate sua ut sic privare se illa, quantum ad racionem sibi merendi, dum tamen maneat potestas secundum racionem aliis promerendi. Et sic Deus dedit potestatem Iude propter causam multiplicem; ipse autem non fuit inhabilis ad recipiendum illam potestatem, licet [p. 448] ex culpa propria fuerit inhabilis ad sibi merendum beatitudinem; nec Christus ordinavit eum sic demereri sed permisit ordinando eum in hoc per accidens sponse sue proficere. Unde talis mixcio atque prefeccio prodest ex gracia ordinantis sancte matri ecclesie eciam plus quam si solum membrum ecclesie in ipsa preficeret. Prodest enim strenuo militi ad exercitum quod habeat rebellantem. Nec contradico sed assencio quod sepe evenit, ut pontifex fingat vel putet se signare sacerdotem caractere, ubi propter inhabilitatem ad ordines vel negligenciam ordinarii manet laycus non signatus (de quo alibi).

Whence it seems to me that, among the names of the office just recounted, even in the [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} who thereby merit blessedness, some are special, as apostleship and the episcopate.[^1] For the sending to preach the gospel and anxious oversight in guarding seem to belong to the officers of the [bridegroom]{g:sponsus} only for the state of [the way]{g:via}. [446] But priesthood seems to be eternal, since the blessed give and teach holy things, enlightening their fellows for ever; and to it can be applied that word of Apocalypse 1:6: "[Thou] hast made us to our God a kingdom and priests."[^2] And so the predestinate who worthily preside over subjects seem to me to be preferred to them in blessedness, and so in the heavenly hierarchy they are set over them in a perpetual priesthood, as blessed Gregory hints in Homily 17, on that of Luke 10:1, "After these things the Lord appointed other seventy";[^3] and Chrysostom more expressly, in Homily 34 of the *Opus imperfectum*: "Better," he says, "is a layman who on the face of it professes the secular life but strives after the spiritual, than a [priest]{g:sacerdos} who professes the spiritual life but embraces the carnal life; and better before God is a layman doing penance than a [cleric]{g:clericus} remaining in sins. For the layman will receive the priestly stole on the day of judgement, since all are then anointed with chrism in priesthood; but the sinful priest is stripped of the dignity of the priesthood that he had"; and then he will be among the [unbelievers]{g:infidelis} and hypocrites (as the Truth says, Matthew 24:51).[^4] All the blessed, too, will be virgins spiritually (as is clear from Apocalypse 14:4). And all will be martyrs in their own way, since it is said in Luke 14:27: "And whosoever doth not carry his cross and come after me cannot be my disciple." All, I say, will be priests in the [homeland]{g:patria}, since they give the holy things, the accidental [goods] of blessedness, to their fellow-blessed.[^5] And hence Abel, with whom the human [church]{g:ecclesia} in triumph began, was expressly virgin, martyr and priest, as Augustine notes in *On the [447] Wonders of Holy Scripture*, chapter 3: "The whole of justice," he says, "is this: virginity, priesthood and martyrdom. Which threefold justice was first in Abel, who both offered gifts pleasing to God, left no offspring of his race in the world, and merited the crown of martyrdom by the shedding of his blood."[^6] Every one, therefore, who is to be saved must be spiritually a virgin, in that, turning aside from evil, he follows the Lamb, the bridegroom of the church; he must, secondly, be a priest, offering to God the fruit of merit; and he must, thirdly, joining these two to each other, persevere in them to the end as a martyr. And therefore Christ notes pointedly, in Matthew 23:35, how the church is said to have begun from Abel the just, to whom, according to Augustine, Jesus committed the primacy of justice. "In this," he says, "that he does not name Zacharias 'just', as he names Abel 'just', he shows that Zacharias holds the inheritance of Abel's justice; as in the genealogy of Christ it is written first and alone that David was king, when it is said: 'And Jesse begot David the king'; but to Solomon and the others the name of king is not added, since the other fathers held the [kingdom]{g:regnum} by hereditary right."[^7] But another cause is to be noted (which I gave above): that Abel was the first veteran of the [church triumphant]{g:triumphans}, who, in happy hope of spiritual nectar, was sent to the place of refreshment, to be purged.[^8] And as to the form of the argument, it is clear that whatever God gives to a rational creature, he first fits it for that; and as to the decree of blessed Gregory, it is clear that it is understood [to mean] that one who abuses his power so deprives himself of it, as regards the aspect of meriting for himself, while yet the power remains under the aspect of meriting for others.[^9] And so God gave power to Judas for a manifold cause; and he was not [unqualified]{g:inhabilis} to receive that power, although [448] through his own fault he was unqualified to merit blessedness for himself; nor did Christ ordain him so to demerit, but permitted it, ordaining that he should in this profit his [bride]{g:sponsa} by accident.[^10] Whence such a mixing and setting-over profits holy [mother]{g:mater} church, by the grace of the one who ordains, even more than if he set over it only a [member]{g:membrum} of the church. For it profits a vigorous soldier, for exercise, that he have someone rebelling against him. Nor do I contradict, but assent, that it often happens that a bishop feigns or thinks that he signs a priest with the [character]{g:carecter}, where, through unfitness for orders or the negligence of the ordinary, he remains a layman, unsigned (of which elsewhere).[^11]

## ¶2 · 448.14–449.3

> [p. 448] Videtur autem mihi quod prescitus eciam in mortali peccato actuali ministrat fidelibus, licet sibi dampnabiliter, tamen subiectis utiliter sacramenta. Sic enim Balaam, Num. XXIII°, Cayphas, Joh. XI°, prophetarunt, et dyaboli sepe deiecti per Christum a personis quas obsederant confessi sunt publice dominum Jesum Christum. Et istam sentenciam diffinivit Salvator Matthei VII°, 22: Multi, inquit, dicent mihi in illa die: Domine, domine, nonne in nomine tuo prophetavimus et in nomine tuo demonia eiecimus et virtutes multas fecimus? Et tunc confitebor illis: Quia nunquam novi vos, ut patet de uno Marci IX° et Luce IX° quem apostoli increparunt, quia fecit miracula et non secutus fuit eos. Sed Christus approbavit quantum ad substanciam miraculi, ut non per se ex signis huiusmodi iudicemus filium Dei vel filium diaboli. Sic ergo sacerdotes mali iniuriantur sed non nocent piis fidelibus non demerentibus ministrando eis ecclesiastica sacramenta, ut patet in [p. 449] decretis Iª, q. I, Si fuerit, ex testimonio Augustini, et XVª, questione ultima, cap. ultimo, et III° Decretalium, De Cohabitacione clericorum et mulierum, Vestra.

And it seems to me that a [foreknown]{g:prescitus} man, even in actual mortal sin, ministers the sacraments to the faithful, though damnably for himself, yet profitably for his subjects. For so Balaam (Numbers 23) and Caiaphas (John 11) prophesied, and devils, often cast out by Christ from persons whom they had possessed, publicly confessed the Lord Jesus Christ.[^12] And the Saviour defined this [teaching]{g:sentencia} in Matthew 7:22: "Many," he says, "will say to me in that day: Lord, Lord, have not we prophesied in thy name, and cast out devils in thy name, and done many miracles? And then will I profess unto them, I never knew you"; as is clear of the one in Mark 9 and Luke 9 whom the apostles rebuked because he worked miracles and did not follow them. But Christ approved [him] as to the substance of the miracle, so that we should not judge, from signs of this kind of themselves, a son of God or a son of the devil.[^13] So therefore bad priests wrong the pious faithful who do not demerit, but do not harm them, in ministering to them the church's sacraments, as is clear in [449] the decrees, Causa 1, question 1, *Si fuerit*, from the testimony of Augustine, and Causa 15, last question, last chapter, and in book 3 of the Decretals, *On the [joint dwelling]{g:cohabitacio} of clerics and women*, *Vestra*.[^14]

## ¶3 · 449.4–449.15

> [p. 449] Ex istis videtur primo quod cessaret in clero omnis contencio circa temporalia que sub titulo cause ecclesiastice est tractata. Feci autem ad hoc raciones multiplices et allegavi ad hoc auctoritates scripture. Nam posita licencia taliter contendendi via paciendi iniurias foret securior, ergo generaliter sub pena peccati foret via illa eligenda; patet consequencia ex hoc quod via meritorior et facilior est generaliter eligenda, et ideo dicit Gregorius Omelia XVIIª: Qui locum predicacionis suscipit mala inferre non debet sed tolerare, ut ex ipsa sua mansuetudine iram seviencium mitiget et peccatorum vulnera in aliis ipse affliccionibus vulneratus sanet.

From these things it seems, first, that all contention in the [clergy]{g:clerus} about [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} that has been conducted under the [title]{g:titulus} of an ecclesiastical cause should cease.[^15] And I have made many arguments for this, and [alleged]{g:allegare} authorities of scripture for it. For, granted licence to contend in this way, the way of suffering wrongs would be safer; therefore, generally, under pain of sin, that way would have to be chosen. The [inference]{g:consequencia} is clear from this, that the more meritorious and easier way is generally to be chosen;[^16] and therefore Gregory says in Homily 17: "He who takes up the place of preaching ought not to inflict evils but to bear them, so that by his very meekness he may soften the anger of those who rage, and, himself wounded by afflictions, may heal the wounds of sins in others."[^17]

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Nomina recitati officii*: the offices named in the previous part (apostle, bishop, priest, prelate, 444–445). *Quedam specialia*: taken as "some [of these names] are special", proper to the state of the way, as Loserth's side-note reads it ("Some offices in the Church are temporal"); *exhinc merentibus*, "who thereby merit", by holding them. *Superintendencia*: oversight, the bishop's office.

[^2]: **S.** The words are Apocalypse 5:10 (DR); the print cites 1:6, DR "hath made us a kingdom, and priests to God and his Father". **C.** *Dant et docent sacra*: the priest's work, to give and teach holy things. **T.** At 446.1 B reads *sacerdocium vestrum*. **R.** A in the margin at 446.4: *Quilibet sanctorum in patria erit sacerdos*.

[^3]: **S.** Gregory, *Homiliae in Evangelia* 17, on Luke 10:1 (PL 76; the homily is right, the content loosely given; LEDGER E19b-1). Wyclif's *septuaginta* is translated; the Vulgate has *septuaginta duos*, DR "other seventy-two". **C.** *Prelati*: the participle, "set over", not the noun "prelates". **R.** A in the margin at 446.8: *Gregorius*; at 446.10: *Crisostomus*.

[^4]: **S.** *Opus imperfectum in Matthaeum*, hom. 34, then read as Chrysostom's (Loserth: *Opus Imperfectum l. c. pag. CXLV*; LEDGER E19b-2, editor). Matthew 24:51, DR "appoint his portion with the hypocrites"; *infideles* is from Luke 12:46, DR "with unbelievers". **C.** The print italicises the quotation through *quam habuit*; *et tunc erit inter infideles* is Wyclif's. **T.** At 446.12 B reads *laicis*. **R.** A in the margin at 446.13: *Laycus melior quam sacerdos*.

[^5]: **S.** Apocalypse 14:4, DR "for they are virgins. These follow the Lamb". **C.** *Martyres modo suo*: martyrs in their own way, by carrying the cross. *Sacra beatitudinis accidentalia*: the accidental [goods] of blessedness, the joys added to essential blessedness, which the blessed give one another as priests. *Conbeatis*: fellow-blessed, as at 444.

[^6]: **S.** Pseudo-Augustine, *De mirabilibus sacrae scripturae* (Loserth: *Aug. Opp. tom. III, App. pag. 5*, among the spuria; LEDGER E19b-3, editor), now given to the Irish "Augustinus Hibernicus"; PL 35 heads it *Auctor incertus* (LEDGER E19b-4, verified). The passage is book I, chapter 3. Abel: Genesis 4:4, 4:8. **T.** The manuscripts give chapter *IV°*; Loserth corrected it to *III°* (447.1). **C.** *Humana ecclesia in triumpho*: the human part of the church triumphant began with Abel, the first of the just to die. **R.** A in the margin at 446.29: *Augustinus*.

[^7]: **S.** Matthew 23:35, DR "from the blood of Abel the just, even unto the blood of Zacharias"; Matthew 1:6, DR "And Jesse begot David the king" (Vulgate *Iesse*, end of 1:5; Wyclif *Gesse*). The words given to Augustine (*In hoc … tenuerunt*) carry no reference in Loserth; they condense *De mirabilibus* I.3 (PL 35), *haereditatem iustitiae Abel tenere Zachariam … Iesse autem genuit David regem* (LEDGER E19b-5, verified). **T.** At 447.11 B reads *signanter nota*.

[^8]: **C.** *Quam dixi superius*: an earlier place, not located; cf. 391.4–393 (the church begins from Abel) (LEDGER E19b-6, open). *Veteranus*: one who has completed his service, the church's first discharged soldier. *Missus est in locum refrigerii expurgandus*: sent to the place of refreshment, there to be purged. **T.** At 447.24 B reads *qui sepe felici* and *iussus est*.

[^9]: **C.** The answer to the argument that opened the chapter (442, previous part): God gives no power to the unqualified. The decree of Gregory is the canon cited there (Decretum C.11 q.3 c.60; LEDGER E19a-2). *Racio*: the respect under which the power is taken, meriting for oneself or for others. **R.** B in the margin at 447.15: *Nota quomodo abutens sua potestate ea privatur*; A at 447.28: *Sacerdos quomodo se privat sacerdocio et quomodo manet sacerdos*.

[^10]: **T.** At 448.3 AA₁ read *promisit*; Loserth's *permisit* is translated. **C.** *Per accidens*: not by the nature of Judas's act but by God's ordering of it.

[^11]: **C.** *Mixcio atque prefeccio*: the mixing of the foreknown among the faithful and their being set over them. *Ad exercitum*: taken as "for exercise" (*exercitium*); "for the army" is possible. *Miles*: "soldier", the Pauline figure (GLOSSARY *milites*). *Pontifex*: the ordaining bishop; *ordinarius*, the ordinary, the bishop with jurisdiction. *De quo alibi*: not located. **R.** A in the margin at 448.8: *Episcopus non semper quando pretendit ordinare ordinat*; B there: *Presciti ministrant sacramenta*.

[^12]: **C.** *Prescitus*: one whom God foreknows will be damned (GLOSSARY). *In mortali peccato actuali*: in actual mortal sin, not only under the foreknowledge of damnation. **S.** Numbers 23; John 11:49–52; the devils' confession, as Mark 1:24, 3:11. **T.** At 448.17–18 AA₁ read *prophetavit*. **R.** A in the margin at 448.14: *Nota conclusionem bonam: Sacerdos malus est sacerdos et ministrat*.

[^13]: **S.** Matthew 7:22–23 (DR "done many miracles in thy name"): Wyclif omits the third "in thy name", and Loserth notes that *discedite a me*, "depart from me", seems to have fallen out after *novi vos* (448.25). Mark 9:37 and Luke 9:49, DR "we saw one casting out devils in thy name, who followeth not us". **C.** *Sentencia*: the position taught, "teaching" (GLOSSARY, CONTEXTUAL), not a judicial sentence. *Virtutes*: "miracles", with DR. *Per se*: taken with *ex signis*, "from signs … of themselves".

[^14]: **S.** Decretum C.1 q.1 c.30 (*Si fuerit*); C.15 q.8 c.5; Decretals X 3.2.7 (*Vestra*), under the title *De cohabitatione clericorum et mulierum* (Loserth: *Decr. II pars, causa I, q. I, cap. XXX et causa XV, q. VIII, c. V*; *Decret. Greg. IX, lib. III, tit. II, c. VII*; the modern form is ours; LEDGER E19b-7, E19b-8, E19b-9, editor). **C.** *Iniuriantur sed non nocent*: they do the faithful wrong, but it does them no harm. **R.** A in the margin at 448.30: *Nota*.

[^15]: **C.** *Contencio … sub titulo cause ecclesiastice*: litigation over temporal goods conducted as an ecclesiastical cause in the church's courts. *Feci … raciones*: Wyclif's earlier arguments, not located. **R.** A in the margin at 449.3: *Clericus vel sacerdos non debet litigare pro temporalibus. Prima conclusio*.

[^16]: **T.** At 449.9 the manuscripts read *elicienda*; Loserth's *eligenda*, "to be chosen", is translated. **C.** *Posita licencia*: even granting that such contending were licit.

[^17]: **S.** Gregory, *Homiliae in Evangelia* 17.4 (Loserth: *Greg. XL Hom. lib. I, Hom. XVII, 4*; LEDGER E19b-10, editor). **T.** At 449.14 AA₁ read *seviciencium*. The print closes the sentence with a comma; the next part opens with *Item*. **R.** A in the margin at 449.13: *Nota Gregorius*.
