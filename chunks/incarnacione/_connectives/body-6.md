### 06-b ¶2 · 90.6–90.32  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 90] 3°. confirmatur sic. Ad hoc quod satisfaciat⁴ pro peccato nature vel generis,⁵ oportet quod natura, que peccaverat, emerendo⁶ restituat:⁶ **sed** genus humanum peccavit in Adam et suis posteris: ergo oportet idem genus secundum aliquod eius suppositum ut satisfaciat⁷ pro peccato; **sed** non potuit secundum pure⁸ hominem: ergo relinquitur quod suppositum impeccabile satisfaciat assumendo eandem naturam in aliqua essencia singulari: quod et factum est per Verbum,⁹ ut superius dixerat Augustinus. Et illam¹⁰ racionem tangit Anselmus 2°· Cur Deus Homo ca°. 8. que, licet non sapiat negantibus universalia, saperet tamen cuicunque pio theologo.¹¹ Aliter enim non conveniencius assumendo humanitatem redemisset Deus peccatum hominis, quam peccatum angeli; cum plus convenit Christus post incarnacionem¹² secundum humanitatem angelo quam homini.¹² Nam ‘dii’ solum participative non plus in natura cum Deo conveniunt quam ante participacionem: **sed** tam equivoce dicitur ‘homo’ de Deo et de angelis¹³ quam equivoce ‘deus’ de creatura et natura divina dicitur: ergo tam equivoce disconveniunt Verbum et homo post incarnacionem sicut ante: **sed** ante plus conveniebat angelo quam homini: ergo¹⁴ modo. Quid ergo pertinuit¹⁵ Christi¹⁵ meritum humano generi a quo tante distitit? Non ergo satisfecit illud genus hominum quod peccavit; et per consequens manet in pleno debito sicut ante; quod est contra Apostolum ad Colos. 2. dicentem quod ‘in Christo Deus delevit cirographum decreti, quod erat nobis contrarium.’

ENGLISH: Third, this is confirmed thus. In order that [one] make satisfaction for the sin of a [nature]{g:natura} or of a kind, the nature that had sinned must restore by making amends:[^4] now the human kind sinned in Adam and his posterity: therefore the same kind must, as to some supposit of it, make satisfaction for the sin; now it could not as to a mere man: therefore it remains that a supposit unable to sin make satisfaction by [assuming]{g:assumere} the same nature in some singular essence; which was also done by the [Word]{g:verbum}, as Augustine had said above. And Anselm touches on that argument, *Cur Deus homo* II, chapter 8;[^5] which, although it has no savour for those who deny [universals]{g:universale}, would yet have savour for any pious theologian. For otherwise God would not more fittingly have redeemed the sin of man by assuming the humanity than the sin of an angel; since Christ after the incarnation agrees, as to the humanity, more with an angel than with a man.[^6] For those who are "gods" only [participatively]{g:participative} agree with God in nature no more than before the participation: now "man" is said as [equivocally]{g:equivoce} of God and of the others as "god" is said equivocally of a creature and of the divine nature:[^7] therefore the Word and man disagree as equivocally after the incarnation as before: now before, he agreed more with an angel than with a man: therefore [so he does] now. How, then, did Christ's merit pertain to the human kind, from which he stood so far apart?[^8] Therefore that kind of men which sinned did not make satisfaction; and consequently it remains in full debt as before; which is against the Apostle, Colossians 2, saying that "in Christ God blotted out the handwriting of the decree, which was contrary to us."[^9]

### 06-b ¶4 · 91.12–92.16  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 91] Item, si Christus non sit univoce homo cum aliis, tunc nullum accidens consequens eius humanitatem foret univoce accidens cum accidentibus correspondentibus in suis fratribus. Consequens interimeret totam fidem, spem, et caritatem de Christi incarnacione, et falsificaret scripturas eque nequiter velut Eutichiani et alii heretici supra dicti. Et consequencia patet ex hoc, quod omne accidens individuatur, specificatur, et habet totam essenciam suam a subiecto. Ergo, si subiecta in nullo univoco conveniunt, a maiori vel pari nec accidencia que secuntur; ut, si angelus non sit eiusdem speciei secundum copulatum corpus cum nostro corpore, tunc nec⁵ accidencia, que appropriate⁶ consecuntur nostra corpora, conveniunt eis nisi equivoce; ut, angelus non dormit, comedit, sentit, gignit, et sic de accidentibus competentibus toti homini: aliter enim essent procreati ab incubis et succubis filii diaboli naturales, et non filii hominum nec proximorum parentum; quod nemo dicit. Si ergo locucio animati⁷ psittaci⁸ vel⁸ inanimati organi non est univoce⁹ locucio cum humana, saltem secundum speciem specialissimam¹⁰ propter diversitatem specificam¹¹ subiectorum; multo magis nullum accidens Christum consequens foret eiusdem [p. 92] racionis cum accidente create substancie; cum subiecta nec specie, nec genere, nec in aliquo univoco convenirent. Non enim plus convenit Christus nobiscum quam deitas, ut sic opinantes concorditer confitentur; et per consequens, cum accidencia non sint nisi modi create substancie, sequitur quod nulla accidencia de genere¹ Iesu nostro² conveniant; ut non pocius conceptus est, vel natus humanitus, ambulavit, predicavit, paciebatur sitim, esuriem, opprobria vel flagella, quam deitas ipsa; et sic fuit obiectum maxime illusivum, cum nec fuit quantus, nec qualis, nec aliquod³ aliorum⁴ encium, cum non sit mobilis aut sensibilis eo quod omne tale accidens univocum cum aliis⁵ requirit primum subiectum univocum quod informat. **Sed** Christus in nullo substanciali est nobis similis, ut dicitur; ergo nec in aliquo accidentali: non enim tantum conveniret sicut elementum, minera, bufo, bestia, vel quelibet⁶ creatura.

ENGLISH: Again, if Christ is not man univocally with the others, then no accident consequent on his humanity would be an accident univocally with the corresponding accidents in his brethren. The consequent would destroy the whole faith, hope, and [charity]{g:caritas} concerning Christ's incarnation, and would falsify the scriptures as wickedly as the Eutychians and the other heretics spoken of above. And the inference is clear from this, that every accident is individuated, specified, and has its whole essence from its [subject]{g:subiectum}. Therefore, if the subjects agree in nothing univocal, then, by the argument from the greater or from the equal, neither do the accidents that follow them; as, if an angel is not of the same species with our body as to the body joined [to him], then neither do the accidents which follow our bodies as proper to them belong to them except equivocally; as, an angel does not sleep, eat, feel, beget, and so of the accidents that belong to the whole man: for otherwise there would be natural sons of the devil procreated by incubi and succubi, and not sons of men nor of their nearest parents; which no one says. If, then, the speech of an animate parrot or of an inanimate organ is not speech univocally with human [speech], at least as to the [most special species]{g:species-specialissima}, because of the specific diversity of the subjects,[^12] much more would no accident consequent on Christ be [of the same kind]{g:eiusdem-racionis} [92] with an accident of a created substance; since the subjects would agree neither in species, nor in genus, nor in anything univocal. For Christ agrees with us no more than the [Godhead]{g:deitas} does, as those who so opine confess with one accord; and consequently, since accidents are nothing but modes of created substance, it follows that no accidents in kind belong to our Jesus;[^13] so that he was no more conceived, or born [humanly]{g:humanitus}, walked, preached, suffered thirst, hunger, reproaches or scourges, than the Godhead itself; and so he was a most [deceptive]{g:illusivus} object, since he was neither [quantified]{g:quantus}, nor qualified, nor any of the other [beings]{g:ens}, since he is not movable or perceptible, because every such accident univocal with others requires a first univocal subject which it informs. Now Christ is like us in nothing substantial, as is said; therefore not in anything accidental either: for he would not agree [with us] as much as an element, a mineral, a toad, a beast, or any creature whatever.

### 06-c ¶1 · 92.17–94.22  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 92] Ideo dicunt sic opinantes consequenter, **sed** minus catholice, quod Christus non est mobilis vel accidentatus humanitus. Quod si verum est, tunc indubie non est homo in aliquo fratribus suis consimilis. Quomodo, rogo, haberet fratres, si non sit univoce filius hominis habens eundem patrem et matrem remotos cum filiis⁷ aliis, et sic temporaliter conceptus, genitus, atque natus, et sic consequenter, ut canit historia evangelica, conversatus? aut quomodo esset nedum in substancia vel natura vel in aliquo accidente fratribus suis consimilis, si nec⁸ in genere,⁸ nec in specie, nec in accidente univoco convenirent⁹? Pro certo dicat qui audet! quia ego non audeo tantum subvertere totum sensum historicum scripturarum, cum evangelium sicut et vetus testamentum expresse et absolute attribuit¹⁰ sibi denominaciones accidentales creatas. Quomodo, queso, crederemus¹¹ aliquid de Christo, si non¹² assercionem propriam, [p. 93] qua dicit, ‘palpate et videte, quoniam spiritus carnem et ossa non habet, sicut me videtis habere.’ Ioh. ult°.? ex qua¹ doctrina hauserat ille celicus paranymphus,² quando in principio capituli sui dicit, ‘Quod fuit ab inicio, quod audivimus, quod vidimus oculis nostris,³ quod perspeximus, et manus nostre contrectaverunt de Verbo vite, anunciamus vobis’? Et Apostolus ad Hebre. 4°. ‘habentes pontificem magnum, qui penetravit celos Iesum Filium Dei, teneamus spei nostre confessionem:⁴ non enim habemus pontificem, qui non possit compati⁵ infirmitatibus nostris; temptatum **autem** per omnia pro similitudine absque peccato.’ Quod dictum Apostoli ego nescio aliter exponere quam expositum est a sanctis doctoribus. Per hoc ergo quod Iesus est filius Dei, est ‘pontifex magnus⁶ superior Aaronitis.’⁶ Per hoc quod potestate propria ‘penetravit celos,’ solus ascendit⁷ trahens alios secum. Per hoc quod potest compati nobis in natura communi assumpta, factus est nobis propicius; quia ad Hebre. 5°. dicitur ‘cum esset Filius Dei, didicit ex his, que passus est, obedienciam.’ Experiencia enim temptacionis multimodis⁸ didicit⁹ humanitus, que aliter non sic sciret.¹⁰ Temptatus¹¹ enim erat tripliciter a dyabolo, ut patet Matth 4°; et quadrupliciter¹² a Iudeis de potencia, sapiencia, misericordia, et iusticia, ut patet ex decursu evangelii. Non **autem** intrinsece a seipso. Quarto fuit illa temptacio nobis pro similitudine exemplari ad docendum nos gratis compati et vincere temptatores,¹³ quia capitaneus apostolorum 1 Pet. 2°. vere testatur quod ‘Christus passus est pro nobis,¹⁴ vobis relinquens exemplum ut sequamini¹⁴ vestigia eius.’ Ista est exposicio sana, cui oportet nos insistere non superaddendo aliam¹⁵ pre timore impugnacionis sophistice de vi vocis. Non quod fuit in capite nostro¹⁶ ficta passio et non [p. 94] vera, ut dixerunt heretici supradicti; **sed** veram conversacionem, veram passionem, veram mortem, et alias predicaciones sustinuit; ut meminit Augustinus esse de substancia fidei in De Vera Religione 29°. et Enchiridion 4°¹ ca°. ‘Nos, inquit,² patimur egencias et dolores, sed³ indubie magis ipse; nam “pro nobis egenus factus est.”’ 2 Cor. 8°. Et cum in perfecta etate, tenerrima complexione,⁴ sensu vivacissimo,⁵ non mortificato aliquo membro, in media etate, non supernatante humido⁶ infantili⁷ sit passus, patet quod in nullo alio est pena vel ‘dolor tantus sicut fuerat dolor suus,’ ut cecinit Isa. 53°. Secundo fratres sui fossi sunt sub terra vel lapide, **sed** ipse plus, cum Dominus mundi sepultus est sumptibus alienis, ut meminit Augustinus in De Questionibus Veteris et Nove Legis ca° 59:⁸ et, ut breviter dicatur, quicquid debuit facere fecit ad summum, cum oporteat⁹ principia exemplaria esse ultima⁹; et eo fecit hec omnia excellencius, quo ipse fuit humanitas copulata ypostatice deitati. Cum ergo omnes ille maneries accidencium creatorum infuerunt sibi formaliter, et omne tale accidens requirit subiectum sibi proporcionatum; sequitur quod Christus subiectus eis sit idem in specie cum subiectis accidencium consimilis speciei.

ENGLISH: Therefore those who so opine say, consistently but less catholicly, that Christ is not [movable]{g:mobilis} or [subject to accidents]{g:accidentatus} [humanly]{g:humanitus}. And if this is true, then without doubt he is not a man like his brethren in anything. How, I ask, would he have brethren, if he is not [univocally]{g:univoce} a son of man, having the same remote father and mother with the other sons, and so conceived, begotten, and born in time, and so, consequently, as the gospel history sings, living his life among men? Or how would he be like his brethren [not only]{g:nedum} in substance or [nature]{g:natura} or in some accident, if they agreed neither in genus, nor in species, nor in a univocal accident? Let him who dares say it for certain! For I do not dare so far to overturn the whole historical sense of the scriptures, since the gospel, as also the old testament, expressly and absolutely attributes to him created accidental [denominations]{g:denominacio}. How, I beg, would we believe anything of Christ, if not his own assertion, [93] in which he says: "Handle, and see: for a spirit hath not flesh and bones, as you see me to have", John, the last chapter?[^1] From which teaching that heavenly [paranymph]{g:paranymphus} had drawn, when at the beginning of his chapter he says: "That which was from the beginning, which we have heard, which we have seen with our eyes, which we have looked upon and our hands have handled, of the word of life, we declare unto you"?[^2] And the Apostle, Hebrews 4: "Having a great high priest that hath passed into the heavens, Jesus the Son of God: let us hold fast the confession of our hope. For we have not a high priest who cannot have compassion on our infirmities: but one tempted in all things like as we are, without sin."[^3] That saying of the Apostle I do not know how to expound otherwise than it has been expounded by the holy doctors. By this, then, that Jesus is the Son of God, he is "a great high priest, higher than the Aaronites".[^4] By this, that by his own power he "passed into the heavens", he alone ascended, drawing others with him. By this, that he can have compassion on us in the common nature [assumed]{g:assumere}, he has become propitious to us; for in Hebrews 5 it is said: "whereas he was the Son of God, he learned obedience by the things which he suffered." For by experience of temptation, in its multitude, he learned humanly what he would not otherwise know in that way.[^5] For he was tempted in three ways by the devil, as is clear from Matthew 4; and in four ways by the Jews, as to power, wisdom, mercy, and justice, as is clear from the course of the gospel. But not inwardly, from himself. Fourth, that temptation was "like as we are" for us, as an exemplary likeness, to teach us freely to have compassion and to conquer the tempters,[^6] for the [captain]{g:capitaneus} of the apostles, 1 Peter 2, truly testifies that "Christ suffered for us, leaving you an example that you should follow his steps." That is the sound exposition, on which we must insist, not adding another for fear of a [sophistical]{g:sophistice} attack [by the force of the words]{g:de-vi-vocis}. Not that there was in the head a feigned passion and not [94] a true one, as the heretics spoken of above said;[^7] but he underwent a true [manner of life]{g:conversacio}, a true passion, a true death, and the other [predications]{g:predicacio}; as Augustine recalls is of the substance of the faith, in *De vera religione* 29 and *Enchiridion*, chapter 4: "We," he says, "suffer needs and pains, but without doubt he more; for 'for us he became needy.'" 2 Corinthians 8.[^8] And since he suffered in perfect age, of most delicate complexion, with the liveliest sense, with no member mortified, in the middle of life, with no infant moisture floating above,[^9] it is clear that in no other is there pain or "sorrow so great as his sorrow had been", as Isaiah 53 sang.[^10] Second, his brethren were buried under earth or stone, but he more, since the Lord of the world was buried at the expense of others, as Augustine recalls in *De quaestionibus Veteris et Novi Testamenti*, chapter 59;[^11] and, to say it briefly, whatever he ought to do he did to the utmost, since exemplary principles must be extremes; and he did all these things the more excellently in that he was the [humanity]{g:humanitas} joined [hypostatically]{g:ypostatice} to the [Godhead]{g:deitas}. Since, then, all those [kinds]{g:maneries} of created accidents were in him formally, and every such accident requires a subject proportioned to it, [it follows]{g:sequitur} that Christ, the [subject]{g:subiectum} of them, is the same in species with the subjects of accidents of like species.

### 06-c ¶2 · 94.23–94.33  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 94] **Sed** arguendo contra illud videtur primo tripliciter quod non debet concedi simpliciter quod Christus sit mobilis. Nam¹⁰ non foret evidencia concludendi quod Christus sit mobilis,¹⁰ nisi ex hoc quod secundum humanitatem est mobilis. **Sed** talis deduccio non procedit, cum arguitur a¹¹ secundum quid ad¹¹ simpliciter. Ergo non est medium concludendi¹² quod Christus sit mobilis. Sicut ergo non sequitur—Christus secundum humanitatem est creatura; ergo Christus est creatura—; ita non sequitur—Christus secundum humanitatem est mobilis; ergo Christus est mobilis.

ENGLISH: But arguing against that, it seems first, in three ways, that it ought not to be granted [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} that Christ is movable. For there would be no [evidence]{g:evidencia} for [inferring]{g:concludere} "Christ is movable" except from this, that [as to the humanity]{g:secundum-humanitatem} he is movable. Now such a deduction does not proceed, since it is argued from "[in a certain respect]{g:secundum-quid}" to "without qualification". Therefore there is no [middle term]{g:medium} for inferring "Christ is movable".[^12] As, then, it does not follow, "Christ as to the humanity is a [creature]{g:creatura}; therefore Christ is a creature", so it does not follow, "Christ as to the humanity is movable; therefore Christ is movable".

### 06-c ¶3 · 95.1–95.23  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 95] Hic dicitur concedendo quod Christus est mobilis cum humanitas sua sit mobilis. Et dicant, qui sciunt, quia pro certo non puto aliam creaturam, **sed** neque Deum cognoscere quod Deus sit mobilis, nisi ex hoc quod creatura est mobilis que Deo ypostatice copulatur. Et per locum a sufficienti similitudine concedi debet simpliciter quod Christus est creatura, quia tota humanitas eius sibi copulata ypostatice est creatura.¹ Sicut enim negacio includitur in racione creature, que negacio non potest competere divinitati; sic in motu et qualibet forma substanciali vel accidentali in genere: et per consequens, negato quod Christus est creatura, negari debet quodlibet per se inferius; ut, quod est homo, animal, corpus, anima, sive substancia, et multo evidencius quod sit mole magnus, accidentaliter² qualis, filius hominis, predicans,³ passus, locatus, temporaliter natus, sedens, vestitus; quia quodlibet istorum⁴ est inferius, indignius, et humilius⁵ quam esse substanciam, et⁶ presupponit substanciam tanquam subiectum dans eis esse accidentale in genere. Omne itaque per se in genere dicit esse suum possibile esse pro aliqua mensura, pro qua non habet existenciam actualem; et per consequens omne per se in genere dicit se esse creaturam includendo neminem esse primitatis divine.

ENGLISH: [95] [The answer is]{g:dicitur} here, by granting "Christ is movable", since his humanity is movable. And let those who know say it, for I certainly do not think that any other creature, nor even God, knows "God is movable" except from this, that a creature which is joined hypostatically to God is movable. And by the [topic from sufficient likeness]{g:locus-a-sufficienti-similitudine} it ought to be granted without qualification that Christ is a creature, since his whole humanity joined to him hypostatically is a creature. For just as a negation is included in the account of a creature, which negation cannot belong to the [divinity]{g:divinitas}, so [it is] in motion and in any substantial or accidental form in a genus: and consequently, if "Christ is a creature" is denied, anything [of itself]{g:per-se} inferior ought to be denied; as, that he is a man, an animal, a body, a soul, or a substance, and much more evidently that he is great in bulk, accidentally qualified, the son of man, preaching, having suffered, located, born in time, sitting, clothed; for each of these is inferior, less worthy, and humbler than being a substance, but presupposes substance as a subject giving them accidental being in a genus.[^13] So everything of itself in a genus [signifies]{g:dicere} its being to be possible being for some measure for which it does not have actual existence; and consequently everything of itself in a genus signifies itself to be a creature, by including that none of them is of the divine [firstness]{g:primitas}.[^14]

### 06-c ¶5 · 96.16–96.29  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 96] Item nichil habet Deum ut superiorem nisi creatura: Christus sic habet:⁵ ergo est creatura. Minor⁶ patet ex confessione Veritatis Matth. 27. ‘Deus meus’; et Ioh. 20. ‘vade ad fratres meos et dic eis: Ascendo ad Patrem meum et Patrem vestrum,⁷ Deum meum et Deum vestrum’⁷; et ad Ephes. 1°. ‘Deus Domini nostri Iesu Christi, Pater glorie, det vobis spiritum sapiencie’; et 1ᵃ ad Cor. 3°. ‘vos⁸ estis Christi, Christus **autem** Dei’; et infra 11°, ‘caput vero Christi Deus.’ Et ad idem sonat tota scriptura de humanitate Christi; et specialiter illud Veritatis ‘Pater maior me est.’ Ioh. 14⁹; et illud ‘seipsum exinanivit, formam servi accipiens’ ad Phil. 2, ut exponit Augustinus primo libro Contra Maximinianum. Non enim audebant¹⁰ sancti doctores inficere scripturam suspicione falsitatis, ut hodie.

ENGLISH: Again, nothing has God as superior except a creature: Christ so has [him]: therefore he is a creature. The minor is clear from the confession of the Truth, Matthew 27: "My God"; and John 20: "go to my brethren and say to them: I ascend to my Father and to your Father, to my God and to your God"; and Ephesians 1: "the God of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of glory, give unto you the spirit of wisdom"; and 1 Corinthians 3: "you are Christ's. And Christ is God's"; and below, 11: "the head of Christ is God." And the whole of scripture concerning the humanity of Christ sounds to the same effect; and especially that [saying] of the Truth, "the Father is greater than I", John 14; and that [saying], "[emptied]{g:exinanire} himself, taking the form of a servant", Philippians 2, as Augustine expounds it in the first book Against Maximinianus.[^21] For the holy doctors did not dare to taint scripture with suspicion of falsehood, as [men do] today.

### 06-d ¶1 · 96.30–97.9  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 96] Unde Doctor Subtilis super Distinccione 11. 3ⁱⁱ Sentenciarum dicit quod hec proposicio—Christus est creatura—est concedenda simpliciter, si bene intelligatur, **sed** negabatur propter hereses dicencium Christum esse pure creaturam: [p. 97] et fingit postmodum responsiones ei, cui non placet sic dicere. Concedit **autem** consequenter simpliciter cum Augustino quod Christus incepit esse¹ sicut generabatur ex tempore. Unde Doctor Bonaventura super eadem Distinccione dicit quod doctores magis hoc² respiciunt, quod potest esse via in falsitatem vel errorem, quam quod ipsa de se sit falsa vel erronea. Unde concedit³ consequenter quod Christus est homo creatus et non⁴ homo eternus, cum sit secundus Adam habens multos homines seniores.

ENGLISH: Hence the [Subtle Doctor]{g:doctor-subtilis}, on distinction 11 of the third [book] of the *Sentences*, says that this [proposition]{g:proposicio}, "Christ is a [creature]{g:creatura}", is to be granted [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}, if it is well understood, but that it was denied because of the heresies of those who said "Christ is purely a creature":[^1] [97] and afterwards he devises replies for one who is not pleased to speak so. And consequently he grants without qualification, with Augustine, "Christ began to be", as he was begotten in time.[^2] Hence the doctor Bonaventure, on the same distinction, says that the doctors look rather to this, that it can be a way into falsity or error, than that it is of itself false or erroneous. Hence he consequently grants that Christ is a created man and not an eternal man, since he is the second Adam, having many men older [than he].[^3]

### 06-d ¶2 · 97.10–98.3  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 97] Quandocunque ergo ego dico, quod Christus est creatus vel factus, intelligo catholice, sicut⁵ scriptura sacra et sancti doctores me edocent, quod Christus secundum humanitatem est creatus⁶ vel taliter temporaliter denominatus. Et illam regulam dat⁶ sanctus Thomas 4°. Contra Gentiles 48. ‘Nihil, inquit, Deo proprium enunciatur de Christo nisi racione humanitatis vel expressate vel subintellecte: nec⁷ e contrario quoad denominaciones eternas, quas homo ille recipit secundum divinitatem.’ Et sic eum gloso ac alios quosdam, quando dicunt quod Christus non est creatura; intelligunt enim quod Christus non est secundum divinitatem creatura: quod est necessarium, cum oppositum sit hereticum. Et quod ille sit sensus suus videtur, quia super 2ᵐ. Sentenciarum Dist. 11. Quest. 4⁸ dicit quod accidencia creata dicuntur de Filio Dei, non quod ipsa persona eterna his⁹ informatur, **sed** quod natura assumpta⁹ his¹⁰ informatur. Quod dictum ego nescio sane concipere nisi intelligendo quod persona Verbi non informetur¹¹ accidentibus creatis secundum deitatem, **sed** quod ipsa principaliter informetur eis secundum humanitatem. Nam Verbum predicat et agit actos humanos, informatur virtutibus et beatitudine, sicut substancialiter informatur anima intellectiva et creata racionabilitate.¹² Ideo certum est quod persona Verbi informatur istis accidentibus ac formis creatis, nisi coincidatur in opinionem negantem [p. 98] mobilitatem Christi simpliciter. Et illa glosa est evidencior et famosior quam glosa data a modernis ad scripturam sacram et dicta sanctorum.

ENGLISH: Whenever, therefore, I say "Christ is created" or "made", I understand [it] catholicly, as holy scripture and the holy doctors teach me: that Christ [as to the humanity]{g:secundum-humanitatem} is created, or is in such a way named temporally. And Saint Thomas gives that rule, *Contra Gentiles* IV, 48: "Nothing proper to God," he says, "is stated of Christ except by reason of the [humanity]{g:humanitas}, either expressed or [understood as implied]{g:subintelligere}; and conversely as to the eternal denominations, which that man receives as to the [divinity]{g:divinitas}."[^4] And so I [gloss]{g:glosare} him and certain others, when they say "Christ is not a creature"; for they understand "Christ is not a creature as to the divinity": which is necessary, since the opposite is heretical. Stulti, inquit, reprehendunt prophetam Abraham tamquam pure puerile gesserit in hoc dicto; **sed** cum credidit benediccionem sui seminis Messiam credidit de suo semine incarnandum.’

ENGLISH: But [it is objected]{g:obicitur-per} against this sense, by appeal to this: that Christ is not before the incarnation; and consequently the writer of this scripture did not intend, to the letter, the [movability]{g:mobilitas} of Wisdom incarnate. Hence the answer is that, whatever may be the case with the [consequent]{g:consequens}, the [inference]{g:consequencia} is not valid; for, since with God all things that have been are present, it is certain that the author of this scripture knew well enough how "Christ abideth for ever",[^1] according to that saying of his vessel of election, Hebrews 13: "Jesus Christ, today and tomorrow: and the same for ever."[^2] And this Jerome notably [makes clear]{g:declarare} in the letter On the Assumption of the Blessed Virgin, to Paula and Eustochium: "The incarnation of Christ indeed, that is, that Christ is incarnate in his time, the patriarch Abraham knew, according to the testimony of the Truth, John 8: 'Abraham your father rejoiced that he might see my [110] day', that is, the time of the incarnation and not only the day of eternity; 'he saw it and was glad.'"[^3] To which Augustine, in Homily 43, adds an argument.[^4] "When," he says, "Abraham, Gen. 24, sent his servant to seek a wife for his son Isaac, he bound him by this oath, that he should faithfully fulfil what he was bidden. For a great thing was being done when a marriage was sought for the seed of Abraham. But so that the servant might know what Abraham knew, that he did not desire grandsons carnally, nor savoured anything carnal concerning his own race, he said to the servant whom he was sending: 'Put thy hand under my thigh, and swear by the God of heaven.'[^5] What," he says, "has 'the God of heaven' to do with Abraham's thigh?" And he answers: "Since by the thigh the race is denoted, by that oath it was signified that the God of heaven would come in the flesh from the race of Abraham. Fools," he says, "reproach the prophet Abraham, as though he had done something merely childish in this saying; but when he believed the blessing of his seed, he believed the Messiah was to be incarnate of his seed."

### 07-b ¶3 · 110.27–111.25  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 110] Et patet ad obiectus garulos sophistarum, quibus invehunt contra oraciones ecclesie quibus rogat⁴ Dominum⁴ ‘per⁵ incarnacionem, nativitatem, circumcisionem, baptismum, ieiunium, passionem, mortem, resurreccionem et ascensionem a periculis liberari’; ‘Inanis, inquiunt, est oracio suggesta, cum omnia predicta nec sunt nec possunt esse.’ **Sed** constat quod unumquodque illorum, cum sit nobis preteritum, et futurum suo tempore patribus veteris testamenti, [quod]⁶ [p. 111] vere est pro suo tempore et per consequens causa misericordiam impetrandi: non quod quidquam scrupulosum¹ in nostra lege celamus,² quod non audeamus disputacioni cuiuscunque philosophantis ostendere. Et sic intelligendus est Augustinus cum aliis doctoribus quod eadem est fides patrum tam novi quam veteris testamenti. ENGLISH: And [from this] the answer is clear to the chattering objections of the sophists, with which they inveigh against the prayers of the [church]{g:ecclesia} in which it asks the Lord "by the incarnation, nativity, circumcision, baptism, fasting, passion, death, resurrection and ascension to be freed from dangers":[^7] "Empty," they say, "is the prayer put forward, since all the aforesaid neither are nor can be." But it is evident that each one of these, since it is past for us, and future in its time for the fathers of the old testament, [111] truly is for its time,[^8] and consequently is a cause of [obtaining]{g:impetrare} mercy: not that we hide anything doubtful in our law that we would not dare to show to the disputation of anyone who philosophises. And this mode of speaking Jerome teaches from that saying of Jude: "Jesus," he says, "having saved the people out of Egypt, did afterwards destroy them that believed not"; and the Apostle, 1 Cor. 10: "Neither let us tempt Christ, as some of them tempted";[^9] not that Jesus Christ was then already born of the virgin Mary, but because that [person]{g:persona}, who in his time is Christ, is eternal, and incarnate by the everlasting counsel of God, and the material [essence]{g:essencia} [was] always at hand. So, then, the holy fathers, in whatever time they were, knew truly "Christ is, was, and will be"; and the [propositions]{g:proposicio} formed by them would have been true, "Christ is, was, or will be incarnate", although it was not then so that Christ is incarnate. For, as I have often said, just as a proposition can be true here together with this, that its primary [significate]{g:significatum} is not here, but it is enough that it be somewhere: correspondingly a proposition can be true now together with this, that its primary [thing signified]{g:signatum} is not now, but it is enough that it be at some time.[^10] And so Augustine, with other doctors, is to be understood [when he says] that the faith of the fathers of the new as well as of the old testament is the same.[^11] For the incarnation, the death of Christ, and the coming to judgement are the faith that is believed, from which faith let falsity, or [fiction]{g:ficticia}, be far, etc.

### 07-b ¶5 · 112.20–112.32  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 112] Sunt **autem** multi alii sensus huic littere adaptati.⁹ Ut hi dicunt quod extensive loquendo de motu Sapiencia increata movetur obiective a cognitis terminantibus eius actum. ENGLISH: And there are many other senses adapted to this text. Thus some say that, speaking of motion extensively, uncreated Wisdom is moved objectively by the things known that terminate his act. Others say that, since there are as many kinds of motion as there are beings, and God at every point of the world continually acquires [lordships]{g:dominium}, he is, in that [kind]{g:maneries} of motion, [the motion] of relation, "supremely movable".[^17] And a third [group] say that in every motion [one may posit]{g:est-dare} an order of [priority]{g:prioritas} and posteriority according to the successive [naming]{g:denominacio} of the subject in the matter of the motion, and every such thing is, as to [intelligible being]{g:esse-intelligibile}, ordered and described eternally in uncreated Wisdom; and according to those reasons he is said figuratively [to be] "supremely movable". But the first exposition, since it is more literal, [in the proper sense of the words]{g:de-virtute-sermonis}, truer, and more conformed to the faith, would please more.

### 07-b ¶6 · 113.1–113.29  (6 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 113] Unde quamvis auctor scripture intenderit omnes istos sensus, primum tamen principalius, ut sit introitus ad alios consequentes. Et sic Augustinus de virtute sermonis salvat textus scripture plus imbrigabiles ut illud Ioh. 14,¹ ‘Sermonem quem audistis non est meus, **sed** eius, qui misit me, Patris.’ Dicit enim illud Ome. 76ᵃ. esse verum de virtute sermonis: ‘non, inquit, miremur, non paveamus:² non est minor Patre, vel impar sibi: non enim mentitus est; “qui me non diligit, sermones meos³ non servat:” non est hic sibi contrarius, **sed** forte non⁴ sine misterio ibi dixit pluraliter, hic singulariter; volens hic intelligere semetipsum, qui, cum sit Sermo vel Verbum Dei Patris non **autem** Sermo sui ipsius, verum dixit ad litteram.’ Quod si grammaticus ex accusativo casu offenditur, potest dici quod facta construccione recta non sit⁵ anthitesis. Nam qui non diligit Christum, non servat sermones suos, id est,⁶ sentencias vel veritates, quas precepit observari. **Sed** ut manifestet quid ex hoc sequitur, subiungit ‘et sermonem quem audistis’ (supple⁷ ‘non servat’); per quam copulacionem docemur methaphisicam quod omnes huiusmodi veritates eterne sunt idem essencialiter Verbo Dei. Et 3°, ut doceat quod ille Sermo sit idem essencialiter Deo Patri, licet personaliter⁸ distinguatur, subiungit ‘non est meus **sed** eius, qui misit me, Patris.’ Licet **autem** sensus catholicus posset esse quod sermo vocalis non sit Verbi ex se, modo quo Apostolus dicit solum Patrem habere immortalitatem;⁹ ut exponit Augustinus primo De Trinitate et primo contra Maximinianum caᵒ. 10; tamen¹⁰ prior sensus est subtilior et mihi¹¹ carior.

ENGLISH: [113] Hence, although the author of scripture intended all these senses, yet [he intended] the first more principally, so that it might be an entrance to the others that follow. And so Augustine, in the proper sense of the words, saves the more [troublesome]{g:imbrigabilis} texts of scripture, as that of John 14, "The word which you have heard is not mine; but the Father's who sent me." For he says in Homily 76 that it is true in the proper sense of the words: "Let us not," he says, "wonder, let us not be afraid: he is not less than the Father, or unequal to himself: for he did not lie: 'He that loveth me not keepeth not my words': he is not here contrary to himself, but perhaps not without a [mystery]{g:misterium} he spoke there in the plural, here in the singular; wishing here to understand himself, who, since he is the [Speech]{g:sermo} or [Word]{g:verbum} of God the Father, and not the Speech of himself, said what is true to the letter."[^18] And if the grammarian is offended by the accusative case, it can be said that, once the construction is made right, there is no [antithesis]{g:anthitesis}.[^19] For he who does not love Christ does not keep his words, that is, the [teachings]{g:sentencia} or truths which he commanded to be observed. Now, to make plain what follows from this, he adds, "and the word which you have heard" (supply "keepeth not"); by which conjunction we are taught the metaphysics that all such eternal truths are essentially the same as the Word of God. And thirdly, to teach that that Speech is essentially the same as God the Father, although personally distinguished, he adds, "is not mine; but the Father's who sent me." And although the catholic sense could be that [the spoken word]{g:sermo-vocalis} is not the Word's of himself, in the way in which the Apostle says that the Father alone has immortality, as Augustine expounds in De Trinitate 1 and in book 1 against Maximinus, chapter 10, yet the first sense is subtler and dearer to me.[^20]

### 07-c ¶1 · 113.30–115.21  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 113] Et sic intelligendus est sensus Apostoli ad Philipp. 2°. quod Christus,¹² ‘cum in forma Dei esset, non rapinam arbitratus est esse se equalem Deo; **sed** semetipsum exinanivit formam servi accipiens.’ Pro cuius intellectu notandum quod sicut forma [p. 114] Dei est deitas,¹ sic forma hominis est humanitas. Utraque enim est forma substancialis; cum quelibet persona divina sit illud quo² est Deus,³ quia Deus formaliter deitate;¹ sicut quelibet persona hominis est illud quo² est homo, quia⁴ est homo⁴ formaliter humanitate. Utramque **autem** harum formarum habet ut subiectus eis formaliter; unam tamen habet absolute necessario; et aliam accepit⁵ ex tempore. Et istam altissimam methaphisicam de formis vellem nostros theologos, etsi non in⁶ methaphisica Aristotelis, saltem in verbis Apostoli compendiose addiscere. ENGLISH: And so the sense of the Apostle, Philippians 2, is to be understood: that Christ, "being in the form of God, thought it not robbery to be equal with God: But [emptied]{g:exinanire} himself, taking the form of a servant." For the understanding of which it is to be noted that as the form [114] of God is the [Godhead]{g:deitas},[^1] so the form of man is the [humanity]{g:humanitas}. For each is a [substantial form]{g:forma-substancialis}; since each divine [person]{g:persona} is that which is God, because [it is] God formally by the Godhead; as each person of man is that which is man, because he is man formally by the humanity.[^2] And he has each of these forms as their [subject]{g:subiectum}, formally; yet the one he has absolutely, of necessity, and the other he received in time. And this most lofty metaphysics of forms I would wish our theologians to learn compendiously, if not in the Metaphysics of Aristotle, at least in the words of the Apostle. From which it is clear that it was not from his own [estimation]{g:reputacio}, or his own judgement, without truth beneath it, that he claimed himself to be equal to the Father, as if he were seizing for himself the honour due to the Father, as Satan does; since to be God belongs to him naturally and in common with the Father and the Holy Spirit. That it belongs jointly to him and to the Father he had shown when he said, "I and the Father are one." And Augustine frames such an argument:[^3] "Nothing that is natural and essential to a [supposit]{g:suppositum} is robbery: now for Christ to be equal to God is natural and belongs [of itself]{g:per-se} to the [Word]{g:verbum}: therefore this is not robbery.[^4] But by making himself to be a visible [nature]{g:natura} he mercifully emptied himself: 'himself', it says, on account of the identity of the person who is the son of man and the Son of God: 'emptied', it says, because he made himself to be, outwardly, a corporeal nature, which in the beginning of the world, being prior in origin to its having form, is empty."[^5] To which sense, as Augustine expounds in Confessions 12, scripture says in the beginning of Genesis, "the earth was void and empty".[^6] Nor do I see how [emptying]{g:exinanicio} can be better understood than as a thing made outwardly empty, essentially, so that it is emptied. And this is true of Christ to the letter, since he is each one of three [115] natures.[^7] And hence the Truth truly says, John 14, "the Father is greater than I": for, since Christ is so [equivocally]{g:equivoce} the [divinity]{g:divinitas} and the humanity, he can, with respect to an absolute predicate, be understood according to the [aspect]{g:racio} of divinity or according to the aspect of humanity, as the predicate requires. And it is clear from what was said above that there is no [incompatibility]{g:repugnancia} [between] "the Father is greater than Christ" and "the same Father is [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} equal to Christ": indeed, understanding the person of Christ equivocally as to two natures, there is no incompatibility between these: "the Father is greater than Christ" and "the same Father is not greater than Christ"; nor ought such [predications]{g:predicacio} to be rejected, [in the proper sense of the words]{g:de-virtute-sermonis}, as being figurative,[^8] for by the same token this would be denied, "I and the Father are one", [and] "the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit are one God", etc., since in the first there is [equivocation]{g:equivocacio}, and in the second a conception of persons. Indeed, if I am not mistaken, all our speech about God is figurative, as whenever it is said "God is movable, born, has suffered", etc., it is not a [synecdochic construction]{g:construccio-synecdochica} but the figure that is called *antropospatos*, namely when a human passion is attributed to God; from *antropos*, man, and *patos*, passion.[^9] For we have no names that signify God without figure.

ENGLISH: And if it is said that this response, since it savours of contradiction, destroys theological disputations, since it falls into [refutation]{g:redargucio}, which is the vilest goal of the respondent, the answer is that it would profit much that the sophistical manner of disputing, introduced from the sin of Lucifer chiefly for the sake of the arguer's show and the respondent's open refutation, be left aside in the schools of theology.[^12] In sign of which, the first question that scripture mentions was introduced by the devil: "Why," says the serpent, "hath God commanded you, that you should not eat of every tree of paradise?" And this is the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of Augustine, De doctrina christiana 2, [chapter] 31, where he teaches that in disputations one should beware of the lust for wrangling and frivolous, childish display.[^14] Nor do the [quibbles]{g:argucie} of the sophists lead theologians to any other goal, but [the theologian] exposes their reedy cunning, in which they had before [boasted]{g:pompare};[^15] and this from their own principles. ### 07-c ¶4 · 117.10–117.27  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 117] **Sed** ulterius arguitur quod Verbum Dei non sit simpliciter mobile racione assumpti hominis. Nam nulla persona movetur propter habitum vel² accidens essencialiter separatum²: **sed** humanitas Christi est habitus Verbo Dei accidentaliter copulatus: ergo propter eius laceracionem³ vel passionem non sequitur⁴ passio Verbi Dei. Confirmatur triplici exemplo. Primo ex hoc quod homo non laceratur vel comburitur, etsi vestimenta eius taliter⁵ paciantur. 2°. ex hoc quod humanitas conceditur per tempus notabile non fuisse: quando tamen non conceditur Verbum Dei conformiter non fuisse, non ergo sequitur—si ista humanitas movebatur post a non esse acquirendo essendi terminum,⁶ tunc et Verbum—. 3°. confirmatur ex figura Abrahe patriarche, qui cum voluit Gen. 22°. immolare⁷ filium suum Ysaac, non hunc occidit **sed** arietem, quem vidit post tergum⁸ herentem⁸ cornibus. Cum ergo passio Christi fuit per Ysaac allegorice figurata, videtur quod non persona Christi, **sed** eius humanitas, fuit passa.

ENGLISH: But further it is argued that the Word of God is not movable without qualification by reason of [the assumed man]{g:homo-assumptus}. For no person is moved on account of a habit or accident essentially separate: now the humanity of Christ is a habit accidentally joined to the Word of God: therefore on account of its laceration or passion the passion of the Word of God does not follow.[^18] This is confirmed by a threefold example. First from this, that a man is not torn or burned, even if his garments suffer so. Secondly from this, that the humanity is granted not to have been for a notable time, while yet it is not granted, correspondingly, that the Word of God was not: it does not, then, follow, "if this humanity was moved afterwards from not-being, acquiring the term of being, then the Word [was moved] too". Thirdly, this is confirmed from the figure of the patriarch Abraham, who, when he willed, Gen. 22, to sacrifice his son Isaac, did not kill him but the ram which he saw behind his back, sticking fast by the horns.[^19] Since, then, the passion of Christ was allegorically figured by Isaac, it seems that not the person of Christ, but his humanity, suffered.
