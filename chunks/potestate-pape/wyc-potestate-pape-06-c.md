# *De potestate pape*, chapter 6, part 3: the decretals are not scripture; the apostles' equal authority

## ¶1 · 110.20–111.2

> [p. 110] Similiter, tunc difficultaretur christiana religio ultra patres veteris testamenti vel infideles de secta Machometi, qui de paucioribus legibus sunt contenti; multo magis in secta Christi; qui Mat. XI, 30 promisit onus suum esse leve, et ille qui Mat. V, 17 dicit, quod non venit solvere legem sed implere, facit verbum abbreviatum Mat. XXII, 40 dicens, quod in dileccione Dei et proximi lex pendet et prophete. Similiter, Deus foret insufficiens et improvidus, si incomplete dedisset legem suam per eius cariores apostolos, relinquens ingracioribus eius presbiteris ex declinacione ab eius vita et verbis legis tam necessarie complementum. Sic enim fuissent patres primitive ecclesie frustrati a fructu [p. 111] finali legis dominice, cum nescierunt decretales epistolas.

Likewise, the Christian religion would then be made more difficult than [that of] the fathers of the old testament, or of the [unbelievers]{g:infidelis} of the [sect]{g:secta} of Mahomet, who are content with fewer laws; much more [should it be otherwise] in the sect of Christ, who in Matthew 11:30 promised that his "burden" is "light"; and he who in Matthew 5:17 says that he came not "to destroy" the law "but to fulfil" makes an abridged word in Matthew 22:40, saying that on the love of God and of neighbour "dependeth the whole law and the prophets".[^1] Likewise, God would be insufficient and improvident if he had given his law incompletely through his dearer apostles, leaving the completion of so necessary a law to his [presbyters]{g:presbiter}, who are more [displeasing]{g:ingratus} to him through declining from his life and words. For so the fathers of the primitive [church]{g:ecclesia} would have been cheated of the final fruit [111] of the Lord's law, since they did not know the decretal epistles.

## ¶2 · 111.3–113.5

> [p. 111] Similiter, non plus tenemur credere illis epistolis quam tenemur credere quod auctor eorum sit caput ecclesie militantis, sed hoc non tenemur credere absque formidine, non solum quia nescimus si moritur, verum quia nescimus si prescitus fuerit vel membrum sancte matris ecclesie. Sic enim electis duobus papis de primatu contendentibus christianismus foret perplexus et non sufficeret in Christum credere; non enim teneor plus credere dicto hominis quam teneor de illo credere sanctitatem vel quod Deus in ipso loquitur, sed non teneor pariter credere sanctitatem vite in nostris prepositis, sicut in sanctis apostolis igitur non tantum teneor credere suis scriptis; tunc enim tenerer credere quod sunt confirmati gracia predestinacionis. Similiter, ista sentencia inficeret totam scripturam sacram, cum Apostolus Gal. I, 8 audacter asserit quod si illis vel angelus de celo evangelizaret preterquam quod evangelizatum est, anathema sit. Modo autem multe gentes transferuntur cum Gal. doctrina Apostoli per epistolas decretales. Cum igitur per falsigraphum oportet tantum illis credere sicut apostolo, relinquitur quod oportet apostolo in isto discredere, cum licet eis ut referunt contra apostolum dispensare, eo quod ipsi auctorizant scripta apostoli. Item, Apoc. ultimo propter auctoritatem libri propheta sic loquitur: Si quis apposuerit ad hec, apponet Deus super illum plagas scriptas in libro [p. 112] isto, et si quis diminuerit de verbis libri prophecie huius, auferet Deus partem eius de libro vite et de civitate sancta. In veteri vero testamento est aliud duplex testimonium: primo Deutero. IV, 2 per sanctum Moysen Deus precipit Israeli: Non addetis ad verbum quod vobis loquor neque auferetis ex eo. Et Proverb. XXX dixit vir cum quo Deus est ne addas quicquam verbis illius et arguaris et inveniaris mendax. Cum igitur nostri non doceant quod hec dicit Dominus, illud videretur nimis magna presumcio parificare dicta illa legi scripture. In cuius confirmacionem unus Papa revocat, suspendit vel abrogat epistolas et bullas alterius, sed facta sua falsificat. Ideo vel oportet falsificare totam fidem scripture vel omnes satrapas sequentes in auctoritate postponere. Hoc tamen papa habet pre aliis, quod sibi ut pape plus credi debet propter vite sanctitatem, cum debet ut sic conformius sequi Christum, quod si manifeste declinat ab hac semita, nullus est simulacior, mendacior et quidquid tunc locutus fuerit de mendacio plus suspectus. Ideo absit michi plus credere cuicumque Romano pontifici, quam quatuor sanctis doctoribus vel eis similibus, primo quia facta et scripta multorum paparum plus discordant a lege Dei; secundo quia non debemus credere verbis hominis nisi de quanto debemus credere quod prima veritas in ipso loquitur. Fides inquam est, si quid sit veritas, verbum Domini ipsam loquitur, libencius autem loquitur sapiencia in iusto sibi benivolo, quam in mendace sibi contrario. Tercio quia criminosus mentitur quicquid dixerit, non auctoritate [p. 113] vel racione sui, sed racione sermonis primi dicentis, credenda est veritas quam ipse balbuciet menciendo. Revera qui convictus est mendax in opere vel sermone, antequam purificetur quicquid dixerit est suspectum.

Likewise, we are no more bound to believe those epistles than we are bound to believe that their author is [head]{g:caput} of the [church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans}; now we are not bound to believe this without [misgiving]{g:formido}, not only because we do not know whether he is dead, but because we do not know whether he has been [foreknown]{g:prescitus} or a [member]{g:membrum} of holy mother church.[^2] For so, when two popes have been elected contending over the [primacy]{g:primatus}, Christendom would be [perplexed]{g:perplexus}, and it would not suffice to believe in Christ;[^3] for I am not bound to believe a man's saying more than I am bound to believe holiness of him, or that God speaks in him; now I am not bound to believe holiness of life in our [superiors]{g:prepositi} equally as in the holy apostles; therefore I am not bound to believe their writings so much; for then I would be bound to believe that they are confirmed in the grace of predestination. Likewise, this [teaching]{g:sentencia} would infect the whole of holy scripture, since the Apostle, Galatians 1:8, boldly asserts that if even "an angel from heaven" should preach to them "besides that which" has been preached, "let him be anathema".[^4] Now many nations are "removed", with the Galatians, from the doctrine of the Apostle through the decretal epistles.[^5] Since, therefore, according to the [false writer]{g:falsigraphus} one must believe them as much as the apostle, it remains that one must disbelieve the apostle in this, since it is lawful for them, as they report, to dispense against the apostle, because they themselves [authorize]{g:autorizare} the writings of the apostle.[^6] Again, in the last [chapter] of the Apocalypse, for the sake of the [authority]{g:auctoritas} of the book, the prophet speaks thus: "If any man shall add to these things, God shall add unto him the plagues written in [112] this book. And if any man shall take away from the words of the book of this prophecy, God shall take away his part out of the book of life, and out of the holy city."[^7] And in the old testament there is another, twofold, testimony: first, Deuteronomy 4:2, through holy Moses God commands Israel: "You shall not add to the word that I speak to you, neither shall you take away from it." And in Proverbs 30 "the man" said, "with whom God is": "Add not any thing to his words, lest thou be reproved and found a liar."[^8] Since, therefore, our [prelates] do not teach that "thus saith the Lord", it would seem too great a presumption to make those sayings equal to the law of scripture. In confirmation of this, one [pope]{g:papa} revokes, [suspends]{g:suspendere} or abrogates the epistles and bulls of another, but [so] falsifies his acts.[^9] Therefore either one must falsify the whole faith of scripture, or put all the [satraps]{g:satrapa} who follow after [it] in authority.[^10] Yet the pope has this before others: that he, as pope, ought to be believed more on account of holiness of life, since as such he ought to follow Christ more conformably; but if he manifestly declines from this path, no one is more dissembling, more lying, and whatever he has then said is the more suspect of lying. Therefore far be it from me to believe any [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex} more than the four holy doctors or those like them: first, because the deeds and writings of many popes are more at odds with [God's law]{g:lex-dei}; second, because we ought not to believe the words of a man except in so far as we ought to believe that the first truth speaks in him. It is of faith, I say, that if anything is truth, the word of the Lord speaks it; and wisdom speaks more willingly in a just man well-disposed to her than in a liar contrary to her. Third, because a criminous man lies whatever he says; not by the authority [113] or by reason of him, but by reason of the word of the first speaker, is the truth to be believed which he stammers out while lying. Truly, he who has been convicted a liar in deed or word, whatever he says before he is purified is suspect.

## ¶3 · 113.6–114.12

> [p. 113] Item, si quilibet papa haberet tantam potestatem edificandi ecclesiam et quicquid aliud faciendi cum sanctis apostolis et cum non excederet Mattheum, Johannem et ceteros citra Petrum, videtur quod, sicut omnes alii apostoli, ut fingitur, erant minoris auctoritatis quam Petrus, sic et quilibet papa ipsum succedens. Si igitur papa habet auctoritatem canonizandi epistolas Pauli, quod ipse non habuit, quia tunc foret per se autenticus, videtur quod quilibet papa sequens omnem apostolum citra Petrum auctoritate excesserat, et sic decretales essent maioris autoritatis quam quatuor evangelia et paris auctoritatis cum Petri epistolis. Sed deliramenta sunt ista, cum tota scriptura capit parilitatem auctoritatis a Spiritu Sancto, quem credimus eius autorem. Nec credo beatum Petrum fuisse maioris auctoritatis quam ceteros, quia ore, digito et auctoritate Domini locuti sunt et fecerant facta sua. Ideo auctoritas Dei non cepit magis vel minus in ipsis. Nec aliquis apostolus in quantum illa privata persona sed in quantum homo divinus; ut loquitur Augustinus IV De Doctrina christiana cap. VI; vel in quantum Christus participative, ut loquuntur multi sancti, ut sic (inquam) sunt omnes paris auctoritatis, unus ad unum et alius ad aliud, secundum quod Deus eis diviserat dona sua, et quicquid aliquis apostolus ut talis dixerit est paris auctoritatis cum [p. 114] quolibet. Et hinc Petrus ut solidum membrum sancte matris ecclesie gaudebat de Pauli iusta correpcione; non enim spectat ad membra corporis usurpare vel remurmurare contra aliena officia. Et sic videtur michi quod quilibet sacerdos debet esse sub non gradu potestatis et auctoritatis secularis, et pure in nomine Domini precipere sic vel sic facere; quod si sit antichristus, membrum dyaboli vel aliter Scarioth, non habet aliquam huiusmodi potestatem. Unde ut sepe repecii, pure de tanto debet christianus implere quod precipit.

Again, if every pope had as great a [power]{g:potestas} of building the church, and of doing whatever else, as the holy apostles had, and since he would not exceed Matthew, John and the others short of Peter, it seems that, just as all the other apostles, as is feigned, were of less authority than Peter, so also is every pope who succeeds him.[^11] If then the pope has authority to [canonize]{g:canonizare} the epistles of Paul, which [Paul] himself did not have, because then he would be [authentic]{g:autenticus} of himself, it seems that every following pope had exceeded in authority every apostle short of Peter, and so the decretals would be of greater authority than the four gospels and of equal authority with Peter's epistles. But these are ravings, since the whole of scripture takes equality of authority from the Holy Spirit, whom we believe to be its author. Nor do I believe that blessed Peter was of greater authority than the rest, because they spoke and did their deeds by the mouth, the finger and the authority of the Lord. Therefore God's authority did not admit of more or less in them. Nor [does] any apostle [speak] as that private person, but as a divine man, as Augustine says, *On Christian Doctrine* book 4, chapter 6; or as Christ [does], [participatively]{g:participative}, as many saints say;[^12] as such, I say, they are all of equal authority, one for one thing and another for another, according as God had divided his gifts to them; and whatever any apostle as such has said is of equal authority with [114] [that of] any other. And hence Peter, as a solid member of holy mother church, rejoiced at Paul's just rebuke; for it does not belong to the members of the body to usurp or murmur against the offices of others.[^13] And so it seems to me that every [priest]{g:sacerdos} ought to be under no degree of secular power and authority, and to command purely in the name of the Lord that this or that be done;[^14] but if he is an antichrist, a member of the devil, or otherwise a Scarioth, he has no such power at all. Hence, as I have often repeated, a Christian ought to fulfil what he commands purely to that extent.

## ¶4 · 114.13–114.29

> [p. 114] Sed contra illud arguitur per hoc quod iuxta dicta nullus apostolus vel predestinatus citra Christum est maioris auctoritatis quam quilibet, quod confunderet gradus ecclesie et iura papalia, cum sicut Petrus et ceteri apostoli fuerunt paris auctoritatis, sic debet esse papa et quilibet sanctus episcopus, ymo quilibet predestinatus. Ubi igitur foret papalis sentencia de maioritate et obediencia? Similiter, iuxta dicta de sanctitate debet capi auctoritas, sed unus est reliquo sanccior, igitur maioris auctoritatis quam evangelium. Nec valet referre omnem auctoritatem pure ad Deum, quia sunt gradus auctoritatum, cum unus constitutus est sub alterius potestate, aliter enim periret regula obediencie humane et maioritas auctoritatis ecclesie.

But against this [it is argued by appeal to]{g:arguitur-per} the fact that, according to what has been said, no apostle or [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} [person] short of Christ is of greater authority than any other, which would confound the grades of the church and the papal [laws]{g:ius}, since, as Peter and the other apostles were of equal authority, so must the pope and every holy bishop be, indeed every predestinate [person]. Where, then, would the papal teaching on [greatness]{g:majoritas} and obedience be?[^15] Likewise, according to what has been said, authority must be taken from holiness; now one is holier than another; therefore of greater authority than the gospel.[^16] Nor does it avail to refer all authority purely to God, because there are grades of authorities, since one is set under the power of another; for otherwise the rule of human obedience and the greatness of the church's authority would perish.

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 110.29: It would imply that God gave an imperfect law to the apostles.
- 111.3: We are not bound to believe that the pope is head of the Church. What if rival popes are elected?
- 112.12: One pope revokes the decrees of another.
- 112.24: I would rather rely on the four great doctors.
- 113.6: If the pope's claims are allowed, the decretals would be of more authority than the gospels.
- 113.20: All the Scripture is of equal authority, as were all the apostles.
- 114.5: So should it be with priests now, who should speak in the name of the Lord,
- 114.13: Objection: 1. This would confuse all law and order in the Church.
- 114.22: 2. There must be degrees of authority, even if it rests on holiness.

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** Matt. 11:30 ("my burden light"), 5:17 ("I am not come to destroy, but to fulfil"), 22:40 ("On these two commandments dependeth the whole law and the prophets"), Douay–Rheims. "Abridged word" (*verbum abbreviatum*) is Rom. 9:28 (Douay "a short word shall the Lord make").
[^2]: **C.** *Si moritur*, literally "whether he dies": whether the author of the epistles is still living.
[^3]: **R.** The double papal election of 1378 (Urban VI and Clement VII), current when Wyclif wrote (LEDGER P06c-1).
[^4]: **S/T.** Gal. 1:8; Wyclif's Latin departs from the Vulgate (*licet nos aut angelus de caelo evangelizet vobis praeterquam quod evangelizavimus vobis*), so we translate his words, quoting the Douay where they agree. The print has *illis*, "to them"; the manuscripts read *illi* (apparatus 111.21), which would give "if they or an angel from heaven should preach".
[^5]: **S/C.** Gal. 1:6, "I wonder that you are so soon removed". *Cum Gal.* is "with the Galatians"; *doctrina* is an ablative of separation without a preposition, or an *a* has been lost before it.
[^6]: **C.** *Per falsigraphum*: "by [the reckoning of] the false writer", the one who sets the decretals level with scripture.
[^7]: **S.** Apoc. 22:18–19, Douay–Rheims (Wyclif stops before "and from these things that are written in this book").
[^8]: **S.** Deut. 4:2; Prov. 30:1, 6 (Loserth), Douay–Rheims.
[^9]: **C.** *Sed facta sua falsificat*: by revoking, one pope declares false what the other did. The *sed* is odd; *et* would be smoother.
[^10]: **C.** *Postponere*, "put after", against the faith of scripture just named.
[^11]: **C.** The protasis runs on with *et cum*: "if every pope had … and since he would not exceed …". *Citra Petrum*, "short of Peter", as *citra Christum*.
[^12]: **S/C.** Augustine, *De doctrina christiana* 4.6.9 (PL 34, 93: the scriptural authors are *viros summa auctoritate dignissimos planeque divinos*; LEDGER P06c-2). The sentence has no verb; we supply "[does] … [speak]" from the context of speaking with God's authority.
[^13]: **S.** Gal. 2:11–14, Paul's rebuke of Peter at Antioch; that Peter rejoiced at it is not in the text of Galatians; the nearest is Augustine, Ep. 82.22 (PL 33, 286), Peter accepting the correction *sanctae ac benignae pietate humilitatis* (LEDGER P06c-3; a source for *gaudebat* not found).
[^14]: **C.** *Sub non gradu*, literally "under a non-degree": taken as "under no degree". It may stand for *sub nullo gradu* or *sine gradu*.
[^15]: **T.** The print has *papalis sentencia*; the manuscripts read *papali* (apparatus 114.20). *Majoritas*, "greatness", as with *prioritas*, "priority".
[^16]: **C.** *Quam evangelium*: the objection draws an absurd consequence: if authority follows holiness, a holier man would have greater authority than the gospel. *Alius*, "than the other", would be the expected comparison.
