## Doubtful, left as they are

- **05-f, p. 135**: *Ille **autem** qui negat hunc casum habet negare fundamentum fabulancium*, rendered "But he who denies this case". Read as (b): the denier is set against the case just posited. It could be (a), the step securing the case on the way to *cum **ergo** tunc foret hostia infinitum plus sacra … quare non datur*, which would make it "Now he who denies". A real toss-up; left.
- **05-f, p. 135**: *habeo **autem** pauca adversus eos*, rendered "and I have a few things [to say]". Read as (c): it goes on from the judgment on Bonaventure to announce the reply. A contrast reading (b) is weak; not a premiss. Left.
- **05-e, p. 133**: *ut unam hostiam dicunt esse momentum et aliam diem dominicum, **sed** terciam dicunt septimanam vel mensem*, rendered "but a third they say is a week or a month". A *sed* in a list, close to continuative; "but" (weak contrast, sharpening the absurdity) is defensible and the card's "and" belongs to *autem*. Left.
- **05-f, p. 139**: *primo … secundo …, **sed** in tercio signo magis defecerant*, rendered "but in the third [stage] they had failed more". Read as (b), climactic contrast; left.
