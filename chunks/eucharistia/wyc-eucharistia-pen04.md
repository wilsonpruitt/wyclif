# *De eucharistia*, De eucharistia et penitencia, chapter 4: the keys, and the priest's ignorance of the penance due

## ¶1 · 336.18–338.18

> [p. 336] Superest solvere alios obiectus qui videntur sonare quod ista sacramentalis penitencia est necessario facienda. Concesso ergo quod hoc sacramentum tam proficit quam officit diversis hominibus, videndum est primo de clavibus quas Christus concesserat Petro et ceteris apostolis cum suis successoribus; et dictum est diffuse alibi quod iste claves non sunt potestates spirituales date specialiter nostris sacerdotibus ad tollendum peccata hominibus, quia hoc est agno proprium sed iste claves sunt potestates et sciencie ad deviantibus indicandum conformiter voluntati divine secundum iudicium legis Dei. Et non dubium quin si sacerdos a clavibus triumphantis ecclesie hic erraverit quod neque solvit neque ligat spiritualiter. Et de quanto illis clavibus se conformaverit solvit vel [p. 337] ligat apud ecclesiam triumphantem. Sed quid hoc ad istam sacramentalem penitenciam introductam? Sic sepe peccata sua publica plus nota sunt sacerdoti qui ipsa percipit quam ipsi peccatori qui secundum legem istam de propriis criminibus confitetur? Unde Act. XIII, 10 Paulus ex revelacione plus cognovit peccata illius magi qui dicitur Barien vel Elimas quam ipsemet cognoverat hic in via. Et (ut breviter dicatur) papa vel sacerdos alius non habet scienciam peccati gravedinem cognoscendi qualitercunque peccans secundum ultimum sue noticie dixerit confessori nisi hoc specialiter sibi fuerit revelatum, quia quomodo sciret alter eorum quomodo peccatum illud ex concomitancia pene vel gracie finaliter qualificatum fuerit, cum hoc sit viatoribus ex revelacione specialiter cognoscendum, et per consequens nullus talis sacerdos cognoscit quanta aut quali penitencia foret dignus; ideo scripta et practisata de penitenciis patule contradicunt. Nec dubium quin Deus nollet quod sacerdos suus iniungeret penitenciam nisi eius quantitatem et qualitatem sibi ostenderit. Unde cum sacerdotes absolventes hoc sepe nesciunt, ymmo si hec penitencia confesso suo profuerit vel fuerit voluntati Dei contraria, videtur quod Deus non regulariter approbat talem penitenciam sic iniunctam. Christus enim iniunxit adultere nolle amplius peccare, ut patet Johannis VIII, Paulus eciam iniunxit isti mago Barien penitenciam quod ad tempus corporaliter foret cecus. Nec habet ista ars fundacionem vel evidenciam quod a Deo approbatur. Ideo videtur [p. 338] quod sit presumpta cecitas preter legem attemptata. Et quantam ad dicta doctorum atque papalia, patet ex multis testimoniis Augustini quod non valent nisi ex racione vel lege Domini sint fundata. Ideo non omnia scripta doctorum de istis penitenciis et absolucionibus ut evangelium sunt credenda. Hoc tamen debet fidelis credere quod quantumcunque Deo deliquerit, dum tamen Deo humiliter confessus fuerit remissionem et delecionem peccati habere poterit. Et hoc discretus sacerdos ex sua prudencia poterit promovere. Et sic generalis confessio sepe induceret ut in lege veteri maiorem penitenciam et ruborem, quam ista confessio specialis. Nec ex dicto pape vel experiencia est notum fidelibus quod hec introducta penitencia magis bonum facit ecclesie quam humano generi infert malum: Et nullus nostrorum confessorum scit debitam satisfaccionem, cum soli Deo sit cognitum limitare.

It remains to [resolve]{g:solvere} the other objections, which seem to sound as if this sacramental [penance]{g:penitencia} must necessarily be made. Granting, then, that this [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} both profits and harms different men, we must first look at the keys which Christ had granted to Peter and the other apostles with their successors;[^1] and it has been said at length elsewhere that these keys are not spiritual [powers]{g:potestas} given specially to our [priests]{g:sacerdos} for taking away sins from men, since this is proper to the Lamb; but these keys are powers and knowledges for pointing out [the way] to those who stray, in conformity with the divine will, according to the judgement of the law of God.[^2] And there is no doubt that, if a priest here has strayed from the keys of the [church]{g:ecclesia} triumphant, he neither looses nor binds [spiritually]{g:spiritualiter}.[^3] And to the extent that he has conformed himself to those keys, he looses or [337] binds before the church triumphant. But what has this to do with this introduced sacramental penance? Are his public sins not thus often better known to the priest who perceives them than to the sinner himself who, according to this law, confesses his own crimes?[^4] Hence (Acts 13:10) Paul by revelation knew the sins of that magician who is called Barien or Elymas better than he himself had known them here on [the way]{g:via}.[^5] And (to put it briefly) the [pope]{g:papa} or another priest does not have the knowledge to know the gravity of the sin, however the sinner may have told it to the confessor to the utmost of his knowledge, unless this has been specially revealed to him; for how would either of them know how that sin has been finally qualified by the [concomitance]{g:concomitancia} of penalty or of grace, since this is to be known by [wayfarers]{g:viator} specially by revelation? And consequently no such priest knows of how great or what kind of penance [the sinner] would be worthy; therefore the things written and [put into practice]{g:practizare} about penances openly contradict [one another].[^6] Nor is there doubt that God would not wish his priest to enjoin a penance unless he had shown him its [quantity]{g:quantitas} and [quality]{g:qualitas}. Hence, since absolving priests often do not know this, indeed [do not know] whether this penance will have profited the one confessed to them or been contrary to the will of God, it seems that God does not [as a rule]{g:regulariter} approve such penance so enjoined.[^7] For Christ enjoined on the adulteress "to will to sin no more", as is clear in John 8; Paul also enjoined on that magician Barien the penance that he should be [bodily]{g:corporaliter} blind for a time.[^8] Nor has this art a foundation or [evidence]{g:evidencia} that it is approved by God. Therefore it seems [338] that it is a presumptuous blindness, attempted outside the law. And as for the sayings of the doctors and the papal ones, it is clear from many testimonies of Augustine that they are of no value unless they are founded on reason or the law of the Lord.[^9] Therefore not all the writings of the doctors about these penances and absolutions are to be believed as the gospel. Yet this the faithful man ought to believe: that however much he has offended against God, provided only that he has humbly confessed to God, he will be able to have [remission]{g:remissio} and blotting out of sin. And a discreet priest will be able to further this by his prudence. And so general [confession]{g:confessio} would often induce, as in the old law, greater penance and shame than this special confession. Nor is it known to the faithful, from the pope's saying or from experience, that this introduced penance does more good to the church than it brings evil on the human race: and none of our confessors knows the due [satisfaction]{g:satisfaccio}, since it is known to God alone to [assign]{g:limitare} it.[^10]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 336.18: The keys that Christ gives are power and knowledge to direct men in the right way.
- 337.2: A priest may know a man's open sins better than the man himself does.
- 337.9: Without special revelation no Pope or priest can tell how gravely a penitent has sinned.
- 337.22: Hence he cannot assign penance rightly
- 338.5: We should believe that if we humbly confess to God our sins will be forgiven.
- 338.9: A wise priest may help us in this.

## Notes

[^1]: **C/R.** *Hoc sacramentum*: penance. *Concesso ergo*: the concession of the preceding chapter, that the practice profits some, is now joined to its harm. E's margin: *De concessione clavium Petro etc.* The keys: Matt. 16:19, "I will give to thee the keys of the kingdom of heaven".

[^2]: **S/C/T.** *Agno proprium*: John 1:29, "Behold the Lamb of God. Behold him who taketh away the sin of the world"; Wyclif's *ad tollendum peccata* takes up *tollit peccatum*. *Ad deviantibus indicandum*: "for pointing out to those who stray", the object understood; DE read *ad deviandum*. *Dictum est diffuse alibi*: the place is not given.

[^3]: **C/T/R.** *Quin si … quod neque solvit*: the second *quod* repeats the first after the conditional; A lacks it (336.31). C's margin: *Errans a clavibus ecclesie triumphantis non absolvit neque ligat*.

[^4]: **T/C.** *Sunt* is Loserth's addition (*Codd.: sunt deest. Addidi*); the apparatus also reports *dixit sepe* at 337.2–3, siglum not legible. The print's question expects the answer yes, as Loserth's side-note takes it. A reads *confitentur*, BDEF *confiteri*, C *necit confiteri*.

[^5]: **S.** Acts 13:6–11; Wyclif's *Barien* is the manuscripts' form (*sic codd.*), for the Vulgate's *Bariesu* (DR "Barjesu"). *Ipsemet … hic in via*: the magician himself, while still on the way.

[^6]: **C/T.** *Alter eorum*: either the pope or the other priest. *Qualificatum*: given its final quality, as the sin is weighed with the penalty or the grace that comes with it. *Contradicunt*: without an object; read as contradicting one another, it could mean that they contradict this argument. ABC read *quomodocunque*, B *sensum ultimum*.

[^7]: **C/T.** *Confesso suo*: the one who confessed to him. AC lack *vel fuerit*; B reads *irregulariter* for *regulariter*; A *vellet* and *ostenderet*. *Absolventes*: absolving; *absolucio* "absolution" has no row.

[^8]: **S.** John 8:11, Vulgate *vade et iam amplius noli peccare* (DR "Go, and now sin no more"); Wyclif's infinitive *nolle amplius peccare* is translated. Acts 13:11, "thou shalt be blind, not seeing the sun for a time" (*usque ad tempus*). D reads *eciam quod*, lacking *penitenciam*.

[^9]: **S/T.** *Multis testimoniis Augustini*: no place is named; Augustine's rule that the writings of later authors bind only so far as they are grounded in scripture or reason is to be checked (LEDGER Kpen04-1). *Quantam* as printed, for *quantum*.

[^10]: **T.** AEF read *nullorum* for *nullus nostrorum*; A *introductiva*; D lacks *ex* (338.13); E *delinquerit*.
