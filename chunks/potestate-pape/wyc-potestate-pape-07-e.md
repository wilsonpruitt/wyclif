# *De potestate pape*, chapter 7, part 5: the fifth argument: the watchful vicar and the two who say they are popes

## ¶1 · 147.27–152.9

> [p. 147] Ad quintum patet ex illo Mar. XIV quod P. et quilibet eius vicarius debet cum Christo vigilare attencius in custodia gregis sui, quia Psalmo CXX, 4 dicitur quod non dormitabit neque dormiet, qui custodit Ysrahel, quod si nominetenus [p. 148] papa dormierit sompno spirituali letifero, nullus dampnabilius se ipsum condempnat et ad officium illud inhabilitat, cum sibi ipsi in vita et nomine contradicit. Nec debet communitas fidelium sequi talem; habet enim ex mendacio signum potissimum Antichristi, ut declarat Crysostomus Omelia VIII, exponens illud II. Thim. III, 1 In novissimis diebus instabunt tempora periculosa, ubi declarat processisse hereticos a mundi principio, dyabolo contra plantacionem Dei mendacium seminante. Et correspondenter de membris istorum causatum ut Caym contra Abel, filii Dei contra filios hominum, Abraham et Pharao, Yacob et Esau, Moyses et magi, prophete et pseudoprophete, apostoli et pseudoapostoli, Christus et Antichristus continue repugnarunt et continue opponebatur mendacium veritati. Dyabolus, inquit, et membra sua operibus nichil monstrant, sed verbis magna false promittunt, ut patet Genes. III, 5 ubi dicit dyabolus: Nequaquam moriemini, sed eritis sicut dii. Quod si de maioritate potestatis pompose contendimus et in vita ac sanctis operibus sumus steriles, absolucionem a pena et a culpa pro pecunia promittentes et tamen in vivendo Christo conformiter subditos non iuvantes, sumus indubie Antichristi. Quod si torpemus in declaracione talium, Spiritus Sanctus necessario dissipabit consilium Achitofel, dividens membra Antichristi ab invicem fidelibus precipiens ne sequantur. Nam Matth. XXIV, 5 dicitur: Multi venient in nomine meo, dicentes quia ego sum Christus et multos seducent, quod cum necesse sit esse verum ad literam, patet quod oportet hoc verificari de pretendentibus se esse Christi [p. 149] vicarios, unde quia sofisticacione dyaboli nimis seducitur populus, non credens signo abhominacionis stanti in loco sancto Dan. IX cap. prophetato, abbreviabit Deus dies propter electos faciendo divisionem inter apostatas, ut vel sic noscatur veritas. Et statim de cavendo periculo divisionis subiungit, tunc si quis dixerit vobis: Ecce hic est Christus aut illic, nolite credere. Surgent enim pseudochristi et pseudoprophete, et dabunt signa magna. Ecce, inquit, predixi vobis. Istam propheciam Christi omnis catholicus tenetur credere. Et videtur michi ipsam posse exponi de istis dicentibus hodie se esse papas; uterque enim dicit se esse Christum Deum, et maioris vel pluris potestatis quam Ihesus noster; in cuius nomine tamquam eius summos vicarios referunt se venire; et certum est quod ista divisio est ad instruccionem ecclesie. Ideo videtur salubris doctrina Christi, quod neque nudis verbis dicencium, quod nunc papa sit Rome, nunc quod papa sit Avinione, debemus credere, sed ipsis operibus Christo conformibus, ut docet Joh. X, 38 de seipso; quia certum est, quod nuda verborum promulgacio nec fallax et subdola satraparum eleccio facit fidem de Christi vicario, sed ut dicit beatus Crysostomus: Nudis foliis verborum ut signis generacionis adultere innititur Antichristus, quia hoc est magis contrarium verbo Dei. Unde uterque istorum, ut michi videtur, debet contentari de isto regnorum responso: ex quo non claret eis ex eleccione vel opere quis est papa, quiescunt in fide filii Dei, quousque ecclesia plene doceatur, quis eorum fuerit verus papa, et supponentes [p. 150] utrumque eorum esse bonum sacerdotem atque catholicum, parati sunt obedire utrique secundum formam, qua Apostolus obedivit debite beato Petro. Et quicumque extorserit maiorem obedienciam est suspectus. Petrus enim non peciit obedienciam maiorem a populo nisi quod credatur fides Christi et efficaciter opere compleatur. Non enim peciit dominacionem seculi, pecuniam vel vectigal. Nec subest alteri eorum auctoritas plus querendi, et specialiter de regnis, populis vel personis exigendo pecunias vel pinguium beneficiorum collaciones, nisi fundare sciverit ex racione vel scriptura quod Christus vel Petrus obedienciam huiusmodi vendicavit vel debuit vendicare. Si autem alter eorum regno vel populo fidem Christi predicare voluerit vel fidem susceptam instar Petri confirmare miraculis; videtur michi quod populus gratanter debeat obedire, dominacionem autem ut venenum debet expellere, gracias agens Deo, qui abbreviavit et detexit venenosam tyrannidem Antichristi. Nec moveat quod tempora ista sunt prospera, Christus autem dicit; quod est tribulacio magna, qualis nunquam fuit. Loquitur autem de miserabili tribulacione anime maxima que iam instat, quia dormicio Symonis et Yude vigilia in cautelis faciunt litargire quod multi sompno letifero insensibiliter moriantur. Paratus sum autem revocare istam sentenciam, docto quod sit contraria fidei christiane. Sed videtur michi indubie quod non amplius quam ostensum est; debet catholicus talem ducem sequi vel ei ut Christi vicario obedire; et nichil [p. 151] infidelius quam quod oportet de necessitate salutis christianum exercitum tali capite regulari. Et patet quod in isto quinto argumento facto pro papa debet ipse diligenter attendere quod non sit ad instar Petri reprobati fumo fastus vel avaricie soporatus; de quo sompno dictum est alibi. Nam habere solum nomen Petri vicarii vel pastoris est ex mendaci ypocrisi magis dampnabile quam si foret homo huius prepositure, cum Gregorius beatus primo libro De cura Pastorali II cap. exponendo illud Matth. XVIII, 6 Qui scandalizaverit unum de pusillis istis minimis, qui in me credunt, expedit ei ut suspendatur mola asinaria circa collum eius et demergatur in profundum maris: Pastores, inquit, perversi in moribus quod verbis predicant, moribus impugnant; unde fit, ut cum pastor per abrupta graditur, ad precipicium grex sequatur; quia cum layci dicta prelatorum cognoverint, per opera pervertuntur. Hinc scribitur per prophetam: Causa sunt ruine populi sacerdotes mali, de quibus dicit Dominus per prophetam: Facti sunt domui Ysrahel in offendiculum iniquitatis. Nemo quippe amplius in ecclesia nocet, quam qui perverse agens habet nomen vel ordinem sanctitatis, delinquentem autem hunc redarguere nullus audet; et in exemplum culpa vehementer extenditur, quando peccator pro reverencia ordinis honoratur. Indigni namque, tanti ponderis pericula fugerent, si Veritatis sentenciam sollicite cogitarent, qui scandalizaverit etc. Per molam quippe asinariam secularis vite circuitus [p. 152] ac labor exprimitur et per profundum maris extrema dampnacio designatur. Qui igitur ad sanctitatis speciem deductus vel verbo ceteros destruit vel exemplo, melius profecto fuerat, ut hunc ad mortem sub seculari habitu terrena acta constringerent, quam quod sacra officia in culpa ceteris imitabilem demonstrarent, quia nimirum si solus caderet hunc utrumque pena inferni tollerabilior cruciaret.

To the fifth [argument]: it is clear from that [passage] of Mark 14 that Peter and every vicar of his must keep watch with Christ more attentively in guarding his flock, because in Psalm 120:4 it is said that "he shall neither slumber nor sleep, that keepeth Israel". So if one who is [pope]{g:papa} in name only [148] sleeps the deadly sleep of the spirit, no one condemns himself more damnably or makes himself more [unqualified]{g:inhabilis} for that office, since he contradicts himself in life and in name.[^1] Nor must the community of the faithful follow such a one; for from his lie he has the chief sign of Antichrist, as Chrysostom declares in Homily 8, expounding that [text] of 2 Tim. 3:1, "in the last days shall come dangerous times", where he declares that heretics have come forth from the beginning of the world, the devil sowing a lie against God's planting.[^2] And correspondingly it has been brought about among the members of these [two] that Cain against Abel, the sons of God against the sons of men, Abraham and Pharaoh, Jacob and Esau, Moses and the magicians, prophets and false prophets, apostles and false apostles, Christ and Antichrist have continually [been incompatible]{g:repugnare}, and lie has continually been set against truth.[^3] "The devil," he says, "and his members show nothing in works, but in words falsely promise great things, as is clear in Genesis 3:5, where the devil says: By no means shall you die, but you shall be as gods."[^4] So if we contend pompously about [greatness]{g:majoritas} of [power]{g:potestas}, and are barren in life and in holy works, promising absolution from penalty and from guilt for money, and yet not helping our subjects to live in conformity with Christ, we are without doubt Antichrist's.[^5] And if we grow sluggish in declaring such things, the Holy Spirit will of necessity defeat the counsel of Achitophel, dividing the members of Antichrist from one another and commanding the faithful not to follow them.[^6] For in Matthew 24:5 it is said: "Many will come in my name saying, I am Christ. And they will seduce many"; and since this must be true to the letter, it is clear that it must be verified of those who claim to be [149] [vicars of Christ]{g:vicarius-christi}. Hence, because the people is too much seduced by the devil's sophistry, not believing the sign of the abomination standing in the holy place, prophesied in Daniel ch. 9, God will shorten the days for the sake of the elect by making a division among the apostates, so that even in this way the truth may be known.[^7] And at once, on guarding against the danger of the division, he adds: "Then if any man shall say to you, Lo here is Christ, or there: do not believe him. For there shall arise [false Christs]{g:pseudochristus} and false prophets and shall shew great signs." "Behold," he says, "I have told it to you, beforehand."[^8] Every catholic is bound to believe this prophecy of Christ. And it seems to me that it can be expounded of those who today say they are popes; for each of them says that he is Christ God, and of greater or more power than our Jesus, in whose name, as his highest vicars, they report that they come; and it is certain that this division is for the instruction of the [church]{g:ecclesia}.[^9] Therefore Christ's [doctrine]{g:doctrina} seems wholesome, that we must believe neither the bare words of those who say at one time that the pope is at Rome, at another that the pope is at Avignon, but the works themselves that are conformed to Christ, as he teaches of himself in John 10:38; for it is certain that neither a bare promulgation of words nor a deceitful and crafty election by [satraps]{g:satrapa} produces faith concerning the vicar of Christ; but, as blessed Chrysostom says, "Antichrist leans on the bare leaves of words, as on the signs of an adulterous generation", because this is more contrary to the word of God.[^10] Hence each of these two, as it seems to me, ought to be content with this answer of the [kingdoms]{g:regnum}: since it is not clear to them from election or from works which is pope, they rest in the faith of the Son of God until the church is fully taught which of them was the true pope; and, supposing [150] each of them to be a good and catholic [priest]{g:sacerdos}, they are ready to obey each according to the form in which the Apostle duly obeyed blessed Peter.[^11] And whoever extorts a greater obedience is suspect. For Peter did not seek a greater obedience from the people than that the faith of Christ be believed and effectually fulfilled in deed. For he did not seek [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio} in the world, money or tribute. Nor does either of them have [authority]{g:auctoritas} to seek more, and especially by exacting money or [conferrals]{g:collacio} of fat benefices from kingdoms, peoples or persons, unless he knows how to ground from reason or scripture that Christ or Peter claimed such obedience or ought to have claimed it. But if either of them is willing to preach the faith of Christ to a kingdom or a people, or to confirm with miracles the faith received, after Peter's pattern, it seems to me that the people should gladly obey; but the exercise of lordship it must expel like poison, giving thanks to God, who has shortened and uncovered the poisonous tyranny of Antichrist. Nor let it trouble [anyone] that these times are prosperous, whereas Christ says that "there is great tribulation, such as never was".[^12] And he speaks of the wretched tribulation of the soul, the greatest, which is now at hand; for the sleeping of Simon and the watching of Judas in [wiles]{g:cautela} make men so lethargic that many die [imperceptibly]{g:insensibilis} in deadly sleep.[^13] And I am ready to revoke this [teaching]{g:sentencia}, once it is shown that it is contrary to the Christian faith.[^14] But it seems to me without doubt that a catholic ought not to follow such a leader, or to obey him as vicar of Christ, any further than has been shown; and nothing [151] is more unbelieving than [to say] that the Christian army must, of necessity of salvation, be ruled by such a [head]{g:caput}.[^15] And it is clear that in this fifth argument made for the pope he himself must take diligent heed that he not be, like Peter when he was rebuked, lulled to sleep by the smoke of pride or of avarice; of which sleep it has been spoken elsewhere. For to have only the name of vicar of Peter or of shepherd is, by lying hypocrisy, more damnable than if he were a man of this [office of superior]{g:prepositura},[^16] since blessed Gregory, in the first book of the *Pastoral Care*, ch. 2, expounding that [text] of Matthew 18:6, "he that shall scandalize one of these least little ones that believe in me, it were better for him that a millstone should be hanged about his neck, and that he should be drowned in the depth of the sea", [says]:[^17] "Shepherds perverse in their morals attack by their morals what they preach in words; hence it comes about that, when the shepherd walks over steep places, the flock follows to the precipice; for when lay people have learned the sayings of [prelates]{g:prelatus}, they are perverted by their works. Hence it is written by the prophet: Evil priests are the cause of the people's ruin; of whom the Lord says by the prophet: They were a stumblingblock of iniquity to the house of Israel.[^18] For no one does more harm in the church than one who, acting perversely, has the name or the order of holiness; and no one dares to [refute]{g:redarguere} him when he transgresses, and the fault spreads violently as an example when the sinner is honoured out of reverence for his order. For the unworthy would flee the dangers of so great a burden if they pondered carefully the sentence of the Truth, he that shall scandalize etc.[^19] For by the millstone the round and toil [152] of secular life is expressed, and by the depth of the sea the uttermost [damnation]{g:dampnacio} is signified. Whoever, therefore, brought to the outward form of holiness, destroys others either by word or by example, it would surely have been better for him that earthly deeds should bind him unto death in a secular habit, than that sacred offices should show him to others as imitable in his fault; for doubtless, if he fell alone, a more tolerable pain of hell would torment him in both [respects]."[^20]

## Notes

[^1]: **S/C/R.** Mark 14:37, Christ to Peter asleep in Gethsemane: "Simon, sleepest thou? Couldst thou not watch one hour?" *Nomine tenus* (printed across the page turn) is *nominetenus*, "in name only", as in "clerics in name only". A and B note in the margin at 147.31 *Papa non est verus vicarius Christi, dum Christum non imitatur* ("the pope is not the true vicar of Christ while he does not imitate Christ").
[^2]: **S.** Chrysostom, *Homilies on 2 Timothy*, hom. 8, on 2 Tim. 3:1; Loserth: Opp. tom. XI, p. 643 (LEDGER P07e-1).
[^3]: **T/C.** *causatum* is Loserth's emendation; the manuscripts read *catum*. The clause has no finite verb: we take *causatum* [*est*] with an *ut*-clause of result ("it has been brought about that"); *ut* could also be "as", making the pairs examples. *Repugnarunt* keeps the glossary's "be incompatible", here of persons set against each other. The sons of God and of men: Gen. 6:2; Moses and the magicians: Exod. 7.
[^4]: **S.** Loserth: "loosely quoted" (Chrysostom, same homily; LEDGER P07e-2). His italic runs from *et membra sua* to *sicut dii*. The devil's words are Gen. 3:4–5 in a short form (Vulgate *Nequaquam morte moriemini … et eritis sicut dii*; DR "No, you shall not die the death … and you shall be as Gods").
[^5]: **C/R.** *sumus … Antichristi*: genitive, "Antichrist's", i.e. his members, as just said; the form could also be nominative plural, "we are antichrists". A and B note in the margin at 148.21 *Antichristi qui sunt*. "Absolution from penalty and from guilt" (*a pena et a culpa*) is the formula of plenary indulgences.
[^6]: **S.** 2 Kings 15:31 and 17:14 (DR; 2 Sam. in modern Bibles): David's prayer against the counsel of Achitophel, and its defeat.
[^7]: **S.** The abomination: Dan. 9:27, as cited at Matt. 24:15; the shortened days: Matt. 24:22, here applied to the division among the claimants.
[^8]: **S.** Matt. 24:23–25 (Loserth's foot reference). Wyclif stops 24:24 at *signa magna* (Vulgate *et prodigia …*, DR "and wonders, insomuch as to deceive (if possible) even the elect"); "he says" (*inquit*) is his insertion in 24:25.
[^9]: **R/T.** The two who "today say they are popes": Urban VI at Rome and Clement VII at Avignon, rival claimants since 1378 (so Loserth's introduction, on this passage; LEDGER P07e-3). *Christum Deum*: each claims to be "Christ" in the sense of Matt. 24:5. The manuscripts read *se hodie esse* and *summos vicarius*; Loserth prints *hodie se esse* and *vicarios*.
[^10]: **S/C.** John 10:38 ("believe the works"). The Chrysostom quotation has no reference at the foot and is not traced; it is not in hom. 8 on 2 Timothy, cited just above (LEDGER P07e-4, untraced); the adulterous generation that seeks a sign is Matt. 12:39. *facit fidem*: "produces faith", i.e. makes the claim credible.
[^11]: **C/R.** Loserth glosses *de isto regnorum responso* as *que regna eis respondere debeant*, how the kingdoms ought to answer them: the answer is the kingdoms', and "they" who rest in faith are the kingdoms. "Duly" (*debite*): Paul obeyed Peter only so far as was due (Gal. 2:11; cf. 157 below). A and B note in the margin at 150.5 *Obedire pape*.
[^12]: **S.** Matt. 24:21 in Wyclif's form (*est … qualis nunquam fuit*); Vulgate *erit enim tunc tribulatio magna, qualis non fuit ab initio mundi*, DR "For there shall be then great tribulation, such as hath not been from the beginning of the world".
[^13]: **C.** Simon is Peter asleep in Gethsemane (Mark 14:37), as in note 1, while Judas kept watch to betray; Peter "when he was rebuked" (151) is the same scene. *faciunt litargire quod*: literally "make [men] fall into lethargy, so that".
[^14]: **R.** Wyclif's declaration, an offer to retract. Loserth notes that it is missing from the *Protestationes* of Wyclif printed by Höfler (*Anna von Luxemburg*, Denkschriften der Wiener Akademie 1870/71, p. 147) (LEDGER P07e-5).
[^15]: **T/C.** The print sets a semicolon after *ostensum est*; we construe *non amplius quam ostensum est* with *debet … sequi … obedire*. B reads *regulare*. "Of necessity of salvation" echoes the bull *Unam sanctam* (1302), *Extrav. comm.* 1.8.1, *omnino esse de necessitate salutis* (Friedberg II, 1246; LEDGER P07e-6).
[^16]: **T.** Doubtful: as printed the comparison is weak, and a word may be lost (perhaps *non* before *foret*, "than if he were not a man of this office"). The manuscripts read *foret si homo*; Loserth prints *si foret homo*. Translated as printed.
[^17]: **T/S.** The manuscripts are corrupt here (A *prepositure tam Gregorius beatus, Gregorius primo libro De Cura …*; B *tam Gregorius beatus quam Gregorius primo libro …*); Loserth prints *cum Gregorius beatus primo libro*, and the clause has no main verb, so "[says]" is supplied. Gregory, *Regula pastoralis* I.2 (Loserth: *De cura pastor.*; LEDGER P07e-7). Matt. 18:6 in Wyclif's form adds *minimis* ("least") and reads *circa collum* for the Vulgate's *in collo*; *mola asinaria* is the millstone turned by an ass, which Gregory reads below as the round of secular life.
[^18]: **S.** "Evil priests are the cause of the people's ruin" is not a text of scripture in this form: Gregory here reads *Laqueus ruinae populi mei, sacerdotes mali* (Hos. 5:1; 9:8; *Reg. past.* I.2, PL 77, 16), and Wyclif's form is Gregory's own phrase in his letters (*causa sunt ruinae populi sacerdotes mali*, PL 77, 1031, 1209) (LEDGER P07e-8). "They were a stumblingblock …": Ezek. 44:12.
[^19]: **C/R.** *Veritatis sentenciam*: the judgement of the Truth (Christ) in Matt. 18:6, a threatened penalty, so "sentence", not "teaching" (the CONTEXTUAL row). A and B note in the margin at 151.10 *Nocent in ecclesia maxime mali sacerdotes*; B at 151.30 *Mola asinaria quid signet*. Loserth: Hus reuses the passage (*De ecclesia*, fol. 224b) (LEDGER P07e-9).
[^20]: **T.** The manuscripts read *suaverat ut hunc*; Loserth prints *fuerat*. B reads *constringerentur*. *hunc utrumque* does not construe well; Gregory's text reads *utcunque*, "at any rate" (*Reg. past.* I.2, PL 77, 16: *si solus caderet, utcunque hunc tolerabilior inferni poena cruciaret*; LEDGER P07e-10). Translated as printed.
