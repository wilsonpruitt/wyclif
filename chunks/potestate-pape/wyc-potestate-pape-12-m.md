# *De potestate pape*, chapter 12, part 13: the pope must be the holiest of wayfarers

## ¶1 · 360.19–361.11

> [p. 360] Non igitur sequitur: Multum malum exinde contingeret, igitur non hoc, sed eius contrarium est tenendum. Sic enim destrueretur maior pars legum et sermonum Christi et facienda forent mala ut bona eveniant. Propter talia igitur inconveniencia expedit, quod fidelis teneat se in limitibus legis Christi, videlicet non exigendo plus vel attencius de temporalibus quam hec docet. Quantum vero ad gravandos videtur quod a summo pontifice usque ad minimum sacerdotem omnes peccant in recipiendo bona pauperum et ecclesiasticas dignitates, nam quilibet papa in quantum talis tenetur esse sanctissimus viatorum, et sub illo colore accipit illud nomen; quod videtur [p. 361] esse nimis magna presumpcio. Nam iuxta decretum beati Jeronymi recitatum X capitulo et testimonium Bernhardi et Lyncolniensis papa debet esse qualis ibi reputative describitur; sed tunc foret sanctissimus viatorum, igitur ad hoc tenetur; non enim est evidens quod tam sancti et scioli doctores oleo adulacionis linirent pontificem menciendo de illo, pronunciantes de ipso laudes que nec insunt nec inesse debeant. Ideo planum est ex eorum sentencia quod papa debet esse sanctissimus et per idem quilibet papa.

It does not [follow]{g:sequitur}, therefore: "Much evil would come of this; therefore not this, but its contrary, is to be held." For so the greater part of the laws and sermons of Christ would be destroyed, and evils would have to be done that good may come.[^1] Because of such [absurdities]{g:inconveniencia},[^2] therefore, it is expedient that the faithful man keep himself within the [limits]{g:limites} of the [law of Christ]{g:lex-christi}, namely by not demanding more, or more eagerly, of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} than it teaches. And as to those who are to be charged,[^3] it seems that from the highest pontiff down to the least [priest]{g:sacerdos} all sin in receiving the goods of the poor and ecclesiastical dignities; for every [pope]{g:papa}, as such, is bound to be the holiest of [wayfarers]{g:viator}, and under that [colour]{g:color}[^4] he takes that name; which seems [361] to be too great a presumption. For according to the decree of blessed Jerome recited in chapter 10, and the testimony of Bernard and of Lincoln, the pope ought to be such as he is there described [by estimation]{g:reputative};[^5] but then he would be the holiest of wayfarers; therefore he is bound to this. For it is not evident that doctors so holy and so [knowing]{g:sciolus} would anoint the pontiff with the oil of flattery by lying about him, pronouncing of him praises which neither are in him nor ought to be. So it is plain from their [teaching]{g:sentencia} that the pope ought to be the holiest, and by the same token every pope.

## ¶2 · 361.12–361.23

> [p. 361] Item, beatus Gregorius libro II De Cura Pastorali cap. primo dicit quod pastor ecclesie debet se habere ad subditos sicut pastor bestiarum se habet ad bestias quas custodit. Sed constat quod nulla earum equiparatur pastori in prudencia vel virtute, igitur pastor ecclesie debet in virtute omnes subditos suos excellere, quia alia excellencia non est pertinens. Cum igitur papa sit pastor tocius residue universitatis fidelium, videtur quod debet supponi ipsum in prudencia et virtute cunctos illos excellere. Aliter enim eque maneret et pasceret eum subditus ut econtra.

Again, blessed Gregory, in book 2 of the *Pastoral Care*, chapter 1, says that the shepherd of the [church]{g:ecclesia} ought to bear himself toward his subjects as the shepherd of beasts bears himself toward the beasts he keeps.[^6] Now it is evident that none of them is equal to the shepherd in prudence or [virtue]{g:virtus}; therefore the shepherd of the church ought to excel all his subjects in virtue, since no other excellence is relevant. Since therefore the pope is shepherd of the [whole]{g:universitas-fidelium} remaining body of the faithful, it seems that he ought to be supposed to excel all of them in prudence and virtue. For otherwise the subject would equally abide [with] him and feed him, as the other way round.[^7]

## ¶3 · 361.24–362.5

> [p. 361] Item, papa consentit multipliciter, quod vocetur pater beatissimus et vendicat honores ultra omnes mortales alios; cum igitur supponi debet quod hec facit religiose et debite, vel quod sit apostata Antichristus, videtur quod approbantes hec facta papalia, tenentur in istis supponere veritatem et laudis talis materiam. Si igitur veritas est quod ipse est hic pater beatissimus, tunc est viator sanctissimus; et idem sequitur si sit maxime honorandus, cum honor sit ex quo premium virtutis, [p. 362] ut dicunt philosophi et beatus Jacobus II cap. Et patet ex hoc principio quod Deus honorat hominem proporcionaliter ad virtutem; videtur igitur quod papa, cum dicit se digne consentire antecedenti consentire debeat consequenti.

Again, the pope consents in many ways to being called "most blessed father",[^8] and claims honours beyond all other mortals; since therefore it must be supposed either that he does these things religiously and duly, or that he is an apostate Antichrist, it seems that those who approve these papal deeds are bound to suppose in them the truth and the matter for such praise. If therefore it is the truth that he is here the most blessed father, then he is the holiest wayfarer; and the same follows if he is to be honoured most, since honour is the reward of virtue,[^9] [362] as the philosophers say, and blessed James, chapter 2. And it is clear from this principle that God honours a man in proportion to his virtue; it seems therefore that the pope, since he says that he worthily consents to the [antecedent]{g:antecedens}, ought to consent to the [consequent]{g:consequens}.

## ¶4 · 362.7–363.13

> [p. 362] Item, papa (ut ipse et sui fautores asserunt) gerit singulariter vicariam vicem Christi m terris, sic quod ipse infinitum excedit alios quoscumque episcopos, sicut Christus infinitum excessit apostolos, ut patet de indulgenciis et equiparanciis quotquot aliis episcopis et sibi in excellencia; sed totum illud foret mendacium, nisi eos proporcionaliter excederet in virtute, igitur hoc debet supponi. Non enim debet supponi quod papa propter fastum et questum seculi mendaciter debeat ad talia aspirare. Ideo dicit Gregorius ubi supra: Oportet namque ut pastor metiri sollicite studeat, quanta tenende rectitudinis necessitate constringitur, sub cuius estimacione populus grex vocatur; igitur necesse est ut sit cogitacione mundus, accione precipuus, pre cunctis contemplacione suspensus. Item, ad esse veri pape requiritur originalis Dei eleccio, sed Deus non eligit ad papale officium nisi dignum et virtute cunctos ceteros preeminentem, igitur aliter non est papa. Maior ex fide sepe dicta supponitur; oportet enim Deum concurrere cum quolibet agente creato et omne opus creature legitimum primitus approbare; cum igitur secundum humanitatem non eligit regulariter papas, patet quod oportet eum secundum divinitatem eleccione eterna eligere [p. 363] quemlibet verum papam. Minor autem patet ex hoc, quod pastor (in quantum huiusmodi) debet esse medium inter Deum et populum et per consequens in virtute Deo propinquior, ut diffuse declarat beatus Gregorius ubi supra. Et patet ex hoc quod distribuere beneficia, decidere causas civiles et cetera, que non sunt preeminencie virtutum officia, sunt abiectum officium laycale, ideo iuxta beatum Gregorium ad papam non pertinent. Deus igitur eligit eum in papam qui ad iuvandum populum preeminet potestate, et aliter in isto deficiens est Antichristus et pseudopapa, ex ficcione mendacii et fraudacione officii.

Again, the pope (as he and his supporters assert) singularly bears the vicarial office of Christ on earth, so that he infinitely exceeds any other [bishops]{g:episcopus} whatever, as Christ infinitely exceeded the apostles, as is clear from the [indulgences]{g:indulgencie} and the comparisons of any number of other bishops with himself in excellence.[^10] Now all that would be a lie unless he exceeded them proportionally in virtue; therefore this ought to be supposed. For it ought not to be supposed that the pope, for the pride and gain of the world, should lyingly aspire to such things. So Gregory says in the place cited: "For the shepherd must study carefully to measure by what necessity of holding to uprightness he is bound, under whose estimation the people is called a flock; therefore it is necessary that he be pure in thought, foremost in action, lifted up in contemplation above all."[^11] Again, to the being of a true pope there is required God's original election; now God does not choose for the papal office any but one who is worthy and [preeminent]{g:preeminencia} in virtue over all the rest; therefore otherwise he is not pope. The major, often stated, is supposed [as faith teaches]{g:ex-fide};[^12] for God must concur with every created agent and first approve every lawful work of a creature; since therefore according to his humanity he does not [as a rule]{g:regulariter} elect popes, it is clear that he must, according to his [divinity]{g:divinitas}, choose by eternal election [363] every true pope. And the minor is clear from this, that the shepherd (as such) must be a mean between God and the people, and consequently nearer to God in virtue, as blessed Gregory declares at length in the place cited. And it is clear from this that distributing benefices, deciding civil causes and the rest, which are not offices of preeminence in the virtues, are an abject lay office; so according to blessed Gregory they do not pertain to the pope. God therefore chooses as pope him who is preeminent in [power]{g:potestas} for helping the people, and one who otherwise falls short in this is Antichrist and a [pseudo-pope]{g:pseudopapa}, by the [fiction]{g:ficcio} of a lie and the defrauding of the office.

## ¶5 · 363.14–364.33

> [p. 363] Ex istis videtur quod nemo sumeret sibi honorem papalem, ut dicit Apostolus, nisi a Deo revelacione abscondita sit vocatus; nam debet cognoscere quod nisi virtute preemineat est contrarius veritati. Sed quis scit hoc? Cum igitur gracia fastus vel questus accipiens dignitatem huiusmodi se ipsum excommunicat in principio, patet quam magnum periculum est in presumpcione talis officii. Ideo dicit Gregorius primo Pastoralium IX: Cum curam populi electus presul suscipit, quasi ad egrum medicus accedit. Si igitur adhuc in eius corpore passiones vivunt qua presumpcione percusso mederi properat, qui in facie vulnus portat. Et infra XI: Sollerter se quisque meciatur, ne locum regiminis assumere audeat, si adhuc in se vicium dampnabiliter regnat. Et hinc superna voce Levit. XXI. 17 ad Moysen dicitur, homo de semine tuo per familias qui habuerit maculam non offerat panes Deo suo nec accedat ad ministerium eius. Et forte si prelati forent a Deo examinati radicitus, cognosceretur quod ex [p. 364] perversa affeccione pocius preessendi quam promerendi suscipiunt illud onus. Quod evidenter convincitur ex hoc quod amplectentes temporalia dimittunt officium promerendi, quod oportet laycos si volunt memores esse salutis proprie iudicare. Et sic videtur melius fore ecclesie quod non foret prelatus cesareus. Sic enim Petrus et ceteri apostoli instar Christi preeminenciam illam aufugerant et ministerialiter propter meritum apud Deum absconditum subesse elegerant. Unde Gregorius primo Pastoralium III: Hec breviter diximus, ut quantum sit pondus regiminis monstraremus, ne temerare sacra regimina quisquis impar his est audeat et per concupiscenciam culminis ducatum suscipiat perdicionis. Hinc etenim Jacobus pie prohibet dicens: Nolite plures magistri fieri, fratres mei. Hinc mediator Dei et hominum percipere regnum vitavit in terris, qui supernorum spirituum scienciam sensumque transcendens ante secula regnat in celis, Scriptum quippe est, Ihesus cum cognovisset, quia venturi essent ut raperent et facerent eum regem, fugit in montem. Quis enim principari hominibus tam sine culpa potuisset quam is, qui inculpate regeret illos quos creavit? Sed quia idcirco in carne venerat, ut non solum nos per passionem redimeret, verum eciam per conversacionem doceret, exemplum se sequentibus prebens rex fieri noluit, ad crucis vero patibulum sponte convenit, oblatam gloriam culminis fugit, penam probrose mortis expeciit, ut membra eius discerent favores mundi fugere, terrores hominum non timere, pro veritate adversa diligere timendo prospera declinare.

From these things it seems that no one should take to himself the papal honour, as the Apostle says, unless he has been called by God by a hidden revelation;[^13] for he must know that unless he is preeminent in virtue he is contrary to the truth. But who knows this? Since therefore one who accepts such a dignity for the sake of pride or gain excommunicates himself at the outset, it is clear how great a danger lies in presuming to such an office. So Gregory says in *Pastoral [Care]* 1.9: "When a prelate who has been elected takes up the care of the people, he comes like a physician to the sick. If therefore passions still live in his body, with what presumption does he hasten to heal the stricken, who carries a wound on his own face?" And below, 11: "Let each one skilfully measure himself, lest he dare to assume the place of rule while vice still reigns damnably in him. And hence by the voice from above (Leviticus 21:17) it is said to Moses: 'Whosoever of thy seed throughout their families, hath a blemish, he shall not offer bread to his God, neither shall he approach to minister to him.'"[^14] And perhaps, if [prelates]{g:prelatus} were examined by God to the root, it would be known that they take up that burden out of [364] a perverse affection for presiding rather than for meriting.[^15] Which is evidently proved from this, that those who embrace temporal goods abandon the office of meriting; which laymen must judge, if they wish to be mindful of their own salvation. And so it seems that it would be better for the church that there were no caesarean prelate. For so Peter and the other apostles, after the pattern of Christ, had fled that preeminence, and had chosen to be subject in ministry for the sake of merit hidden with God. Hence Gregory, *Pastoral [Care]* 1.3: "These things we have said briefly, to show how great is the weight of rule, lest whoever is unequal to it dare to profane the sacred offices of rule, and through desire of eminence take up the leadership of perdition.[^16] For hence James piously forbids, saying: 'Be ye not many masters, my brethren.' Hence the mediator of God and men, who, transcending the knowledge and understanding of the spirits above, reigns in heaven before the ages, avoided receiving a kingdom on earth. For it is written: 'Jesus, when he knew that they would come to take him by force and make him king, fled into the mountain.'[^17] For who could have ruled over men so without fault as he who would rule blamelessly those whom he had created? But since he had come in the flesh for this purpose, not only to redeem us by his passion but also to teach us by his [manner of life]{g:conversacio}, offering an example to those who follow him he would not be made king, but went of his own accord to the gibbet of the cross; he fled the glory of eminence offered him, he sought the penalty of a shameful death, that his [members]{g:membrum} might learn to flee the favours of the world, not to fear the terrors of men, to love adversity for the truth's sake, and in fear to turn away from prosperity."

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 360.28: The pope is bound to be the holiest of men,
- 361.1: if not, he should not take the name of pope.
- 361.24: So too of the honours that he claims,
- 362.7: and of the powers that he is supposed to wield.
- 362.24: To be a true pope he must be chosen by God.
- 363.14: Hence none should take papal honours who does not know himself called of God; but none can know this.

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** Rom. 3:8 (DR "let us do evil that there may come good"), turned into Wyclif's own construction; so not quoted.
[^2]: **C.** *Inconveniencia* (CONTEXTUAL): here the absurd consequences of the argument just refuted, not troubles in affairs.
[^3]: **C.** *Ad gravandos*, "those who are to be charged", i.e. those to be held at fault in what follows; the verb can also mean "to be burdened", which fits the clergy laden with the goods of the poor less well.
[^4]: **C.** *Color*: a plausible ground, as in "colour of title"; the pope takes the name on the ground of the holiness it claims.
[^5]: **S.** Wyclif refers back to ch. 10 of this work, where a decree of Jerome is recited with Bernard (*De consideratione*) and Robert Grosseteste, bishop of Lincoln, on what a pope ought to be: the decree is C.24 q.1 c.14 *Hec est fides* (p. 250), Lincoln is cited at pp. 257–258, and Bernard's book *ad papam Eugenium* at p. 265. *Reputative*: by men's estimation, as against revelation.
[^6]: **S.** Gregory, *Regula pastoralis* 2.1 (PL 77.25D: *Tantum debet actionem populi actio transcendere praesulis, quantum distare solet a grege vita pastoris*), which Wyclif paraphrases; Loserth gives "Greg. l. c." at 361.12.
[^7]: **T.** The print's *eque maneret et pasceret eum* does not construe well: *manere* with an object is odd. Perhaps *minaret*, "would drive" (as a shepherd his flock); we translate the print.
[^8]: **R.** Loserth at 361.24 compares Hus, *Opera* I, 229a ("Cf. Hussii Opp. I, 229 a") (LEDGER P12m-3).
[^9]: **T.** The print reads *cum honor sit ex quo premium virtutis*; *ex quo* does not construe and is left untranslated (an intrusion, or a word lost). The maxim is Aristotle's, honour the prize of virtue (*Nic. Eth.* 4.3, 1123b35). James 2 is Wyclif's reference (Jas. 2:1, against respect of persons).
[^10]: **T.** Loserth marks this place corrupt (362.11, *Hic locus corruptus est*; B omits *et*). We translate the print: *equiparanciis quotquot aliis episcopis et sibi*, "comparisons of any number of other bishops with himself".
[^11]: **S.** Gregory, *Regula pastoralis* 2.1 (Loserth: "Cap. I"; PL 77.26D–27A); Wyclif leaves out the qualities Gregory lists between *actione praecipuus* and *prae cunctis contemplatione suspensus*.
[^12]: **C.** *Maior ex fide sepe dicta supponitur*: *dicta* may agree with *maior* ("the major, often stated") or with *fide* ("from the faith often stated"); we take the first.
[^13]: **S.** Heb. 5:4 (DR "Neither doth any man take the honour to himself, but he that is called by God, as Aaron was"), given in indirect speech.
[^14]: **S.** Gregory, *Regula pastoralis* 1.9 (PL 77.22D) and 1.11 (PL 77.23D–24A), as Wyclif cites them; Loserth gives no reference. Gregory reads *causam populi* and *in ejus opere* where this text has *curam populi* and *in eius corpore*. The scripture is Lev. 21:17–18, which Gregory introduces with *ad Moysen dicitur: Loquere ad Aaron* and quotes with the Vulgate's futures *offeret*, *accedet*; this text drops *Loquere ad Aaron* and has subjunctives.
[^15]: **T.** *Ex* (363.34) is Loserth's supply; the manuscripts omit it.
[^16]: **T.** *Ne temerare … quisquis impar his est audeat* is Loserth's text; the manuscripts read *ne intrare* (364.13) and *quisquis est is nuper audeat* (364.13–14). Gregory, *Regula pastoralis* 1.3 (PL 77.16B–C), which reads *ne temerare sacra regimina quisquis his impar est audeat* and so confirms Loserth's emendations.
[^17]: **S.** Jas. 3:1 (DR). John 6:15 (Loserth): the text omits the Vulgate's first *eum*, *iterum* and *ipse solus* (DR "fled again into the mountains, himself alone"). "Mediator of God and men" is 1 Tim. 2:5.
