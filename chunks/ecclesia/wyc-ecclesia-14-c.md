# *De ecclesia*, chapter 14, part 3: the clergy live on alms and owe spiritual alms in return

## ¶1 · 306.15–307.2

> [p. 306] Nec valet fingere quod dominacio illa non sit privilegium sed lex communis, quia est lex privata diu post fundacionem ecclesie Christi concessa singulariter clero suo. Ideo patet ex descripcione privilegii quod sit sibi privilegium notabile pro hac vita. Sic enim purgacio mortuorum est privilegium sancte ecclesie quam tamen multi non appetunt. Minor autem argumenti sic ostenditur: Non fuit in Christo falsitas, dolus sive duplicitas, cum secundum Apostolum, II Corinth. I°, 18, non fuit in ipso Est et non, sed fuisset cum paribus, si fuisset crucifixus, ut post clericatum sui imitatores seculariter dominentur. Ergo hoc non intenderat paciendo; ipse enim non potuit dominari seculariter sed vixit pauperem vitam summe exproprietarie; quod et docuit suos precipuos apostolos quos voluit esse exemplaria imitanda future ecclesie. Ipsis enim dixit quod haberent nolicionem possidendi aurum vel argentum, quod non diligerent mundum nec mundialia, sed quod omnino relinquerent cuncta [p. 307] que possident et tollentes crucem sequantur eum (ut patet Matthei X°, Luce XIV° et sepe alibi in evangelio).

Nor does it avail to pretend that that [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio} is not a [privilege]{g:privilegium} but common law, because it is a private law granted singly to his [clergy]{g:clerus} long after the foundation of Christ's [church]{g:ecclesia}. Therefore it is clear from the [definition]{g:descripcio} of privilege that it is a notable privilege for it for this life.[^1] For so the cleansing of the dead is a privilege of holy church, which many nevertheless do not desire. Now the minor of the argument is shown thus:[^2] there was in Christ no falsehood, guile or duplicity, since according to the Apostle, 2 Corinthians 1:18, there was not in him "It is and It is not";[^3] now there would have been, other things being equal, if he had been crucified so that after taking clerical state his imitators should [exercise lordship]{g:dominari} in secular fashion.[^4] Therefore he had not intended this in suffering; for he himself could not exercise secular lordship, but lived a poor life, supremely [without property]{g:exproprietarie}; and this he also taught his chief apostles, whom he willed to be patterns for the future church to imitate. For he told them that they should have an unwillingness to possess gold or silver,[^5] that they should not love the world nor worldly things, but that they should utterly leave all things [307] that they possess and, taking up their cross, follow him (as is clear from Matthew 10, Luke 14 and often elsewhere in the gospel).

## ¶2 · 307.3–307.33

> [p. 307] Cum ergo in facto movit apostolos suos ad facto et conversacione interpretandum hec mandata secundum vitam exproprietariam et penalem, nec unquam innuit eis dominacionem civilem, fuisset nimis magnus seductor, si cum istis paribus dominacionem civilem pro statu cleri moderni intendebat ipsis adicere. Nam capitalibus exemplaribus in quibus dedit nobis potestatem et regulam vivendi precipit et monuit exemplando oppositum, nec est credibile quod desereret clerum suum, tacens omnino necessarium ad conversacionem ipsorum secundum suum beneplacitum regulandam; tunc enim fuisset omnino negligens et subdolus, sic quod omnes defectus nostros possemus in suam negligenciam racionabiliter retorquere; quod cum sit nefas dicere, credamus ex fide scripture et testimonio sanctorum doctorum quod in apostolis docuit totum genus cleri sequentis vivere pure exproprietarie de elemosinis corporalibus laicorum et pro mutuo nexu caritatis habendo, non minus obligavit clerum suum ad retribucionem elemosine spiritualis, sic quod clerici sint necessariores et digniores in ecclesia quam econtra, et plus indigent seculares clericis quam econtra, sicut spiritualis elemosina est prestancior corporali, et sic oportet quod laici mendicent elemosinas spirituales a clericis ad quarum retribucionem clerici amplius obligantur, sicut oportet ut clerici mendicent elemosinas corporales a laicis ad quarum retribucionem multum valde sed non adeo laici sunt astricti, nec graviter feramus maioritatem obligacionis huiusmodi, quia hoc redundat in excellenciam clericorum.

Since therefore in deed he moved his apostles to interpret these commands, by deed and by [manner of life]{g:conversacio}, according to a life without property and of hardship, and never hinted civil lordship to them, he would have been an exceedingly great seducer if, these things being equal, he intended to add civil lordship to them for the state of the modern clergy.[^6] For in the chief patterns, in which he gave us the power and rule of living, he commanded and admonished the opposite by his example; nor is it credible that he would desert his clergy, keeping wholly silent about what was necessary for ruling their manner of life according to his good pleasure; for then he would have been altogether negligent and deceitful, so that we could reasonably turn all our failings back upon his negligence. Since this is wicked to say, let us believe [from the faith of scripture]{g:ex-fide} and the testimony of the holy doctors that in the apostles he taught the whole kind of the clergy that followed to live purely without property on the bodily [alms]{g:elemosina} of the laity; and for the having of a mutual bond of [charity]{g:caritas} he bound his clergy no less to the repayment of spiritual alms,[^7] so that clerics are more necessary and more worthy in the church than the reverse, and [seculars]{g:secularis} need clerics more than the reverse, as spiritual alms are more excellent than bodily. And so laymen must [beg]{g:mendicare} spiritual alms from clerics, to the repayment of which the clerics are the more bound, just as clerics must beg bodily alms from laymen, to the repayment of which the laymen are very greatly, but not so much, bound. Nor let us take ill the [greatness]{g:majoritas} of such an obligation, because this redounds to the excellence of the clerics.

## ¶3 · 308.1–309.7

> [p. 308] Christus enim est plus obligatus creature sue quam econtra, cum ex lege sua eterna et titulo gracie prevenientis constituit se debitorem suis servis fidelibus, non ut solum daret eis bonum aliquod temporale, sed ut daret eis se ipsum in premium sempiternum; tantum autem non potest creatura beata domino obligari, et sic tenetur sacerdos rependere subditis spirituales elemosinas. Ideo (ut dixi proximo capitulo) deficiens a satisfaccione illius debiti gravius excommunicatur a domino, et per consequens periculosius cadit in heresim in tantum quod, si Deus per impossibile deficeret a debito ad quod se obligavit, graciose promittendo et meritum proficiendo, ipse foret summus hereticus. Nec feramus graviter quod quantumcunque nobiles quoad mundum ad tantum se humilient pro Christo quod deveniant elemosinarii pauperum subditorum, tum quia Christus nobilissimus recepit ad sui egenciam relevandam tam a viris secularibus quam a devotis feminis elemosinas corporales, tum eciam quia ex tali humiliacione et ministerii complecione crescit prepositi maioritas quoad Deum. Sic ergo non est quoad hoc distinccio inter fratres, inter possessionatos et inter curatos, viventes de decimis et oblacionibus, quin omnes debemus vivere exproprietarie de elemosinis laicorum et omnes debemus esse procuratores, non civiles possessores bonorum pauperum, iuxta preceptum Christi Matthei X°, nec potest conventualis conversacio vel solitaria aut prelacia maior vel minor tollere hoc debitum, sed omnes tenemur esse fideles dispensatores thesauri spiritualis vel corporalis domini secundum mensuram donacionis Christi; illi autem sunt superiores quoad Deum qui instar apostolorum plus deserentes mundum seminant efficacius verbum Dei. Unde ritus moderni [p. 309] isti maxime religioni supperadditi alios iuvando facilitant et alios retardando difficultant, sed utrobique qui ex maiori caritate plus defenderit et docuerit legem domini accepcior erit illi; non enim est personarum accepcio apud Deum, sed quicunque ex maiori caritate pro illo plus passus fuerit, magis penaliter vixerit et sponse sue magis profecerit, sibi accepcior erit.

For Christ is more bound to his creature than the reverse, since by his eternal law and by [title]{g:titulus} of [prevenient grace]{g:gracia-preveniens} he made himself debtor to his faithful servants, not only to give them some temporal good, but to give them himself as an everlasting reward; but a blessed creature cannot be bound to the Lord to so great an extent; and so the [priest]{g:sacerdos} is bound to repay spiritual alms to his subjects. Therefore (as I said in the preceding chapter)[^8] one who fails in the satisfaction of that debt is more gravely excommunicated by the Lord, and consequently falls more dangerously into heresy, so much so that if God, to suppose the impossible, failed in the debt to which he bound himself by graciously promising and by making merit profitable,[^9] he would be the supreme heretic. Nor let us take it ill that men, however noble as regards the world, humble themselves for Christ so far as to become [almsmen]{g:elemosinarius} of their poor subjects: both because Christ, most noble, received bodily alms to relieve his need, from secular men as well as from devout women; and also because from such humbling and fulfilment of ministry the greatness of the [superior]{g:prepositi} grows as regards God. So therefore there is in this respect no distinction among friars, among [possessioners]{g:possessionati} and among [curates]{g:curatus} living on [tithes]{g:decime} and offerings, but that we all ought to live without property on the alms of the laity, and we all ought to be [proctors]{g:procurator}, not civil possessors, of the goods of the poor, according to Christ's command, Matthew 10;[^10] nor can conventual or solitary manner of life, or greater or lesser prelacy, take away this debt; but we are all bound to be faithful dispensers of the Lord's treasure, spiritual or bodily, "according to the measure of the giving of Christ";[^11] and those are superior as regards God who, like the apostles, forsaking the world the more, sow the word of God more effectively. Hence the modern rites added on [309] to this greatest religion make it easier for some by helping them and harder for others by holding them back;[^12] but in either case, whoever from greater charity has more defended and taught the law of the Lord will be more acceptable to him; for "there is no respect of persons with God",[^13] but whoever from greater charity has suffered more for him, lived more [in hardship]{g:penaliter}, and profited his [bride]{g:sponsa} more, will be more acceptable to him.

## ¶4 · 309.8–310.13

> [p. 309] Ex istis potest speculator perpendere quomodo perversa est elemosinancium intencio, et per consequens religio Christiana, nam ubi donata sunt clero temporalia non ut civiliter dominentur, sed ut ecclesiastica elemosina serviliter dispensetur; non ut proprietarie splendidi vel seculariter conversetur; sed ut sine personarum accepcione, postposito affectu carnis et sanguinis, elemosina divitum magis egentibus ministretur; non ut clerus deses in spirituali servicio contendat seculariter pro primatu, sed ut vigilancius ex maiori beneficio intendat humilius quomodo se et suos subditos liberet a reatu. Sed iudicet considerans statum seculi, que istarum parcium est pocior hiis diebus; nam ad tantum invaluit oleum peccatoris quod, ubi dicerentur christiani ex oleo gracie et humili imitacione regis justicie, iam nimis multi sunt Antichristi ex diffusione olei regis superbie ad cuius probacionem a nominetenus sanctuario Christi incipite. Nonne vendicat antipapa, vicarius Luciferi, quod debet super omnes imperatores, reges et principes seculariter dominari, et ob reverenciam ordinis sibi debet undique secundum modum seculi magis splendide ministrari; et ad hoc intorquet scripturam sacram et dicta sanctorum. Et idem vendicant multi eius discipuli, verecundantes vocari subditorum elemosinarii [p. 310] sive servi, sed conversando excessive seculariter introducunt se dominos reciproce nominari. Nam in primitiva ecclesia dixerunt se regulariter esse servos, tempore vero Augustini propter dominacionem insolencium destruendo vicia vocati sunt domini. Sed hodie plane nituntur omissione et commissione in conversacione ostendere quod pretendunt se esse dominos seculares; quod cum repugnat legi Dei et ordini, patet quanta monstruositas foret ex heresi circa fidem scripture sacre in ecclesiam introducta. Corrigendi ergo sunt blasphemi qui denigrant (quantum in eis est) causam Christi martirii, scilicet quod mortuus est pro dominacione seculari suis clericis adquirenda.

From these things the [watchman]{g:speculator} can weigh how perverted is the intention of the [almsgivers]{g:elemosinans}, and consequently the Christian religion. For whereas [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} were given to the clergy not that they should exercise civil lordship, but that the church's alms should be dispensed in the manner of a servant; not that they should be splendid as proprietors or live in secular fashion, but that, without respect of persons, the affection of flesh and blood set aside, the alms of the rich should be ministered to the more needy; not that the clergy, slothful in spiritual [service]{g:servicium}, should contend in secular fashion for primacy, but that, the more watchfully because of the greater benefice, it should attend the more humbly to how it may free itself and its subjects from guilt[^14] — but let him who considers the state of the world judge which of these sides is the stronger in these days. For the oil of the sinner has so prevailed that, where men would be called Christians from the oil of grace and humble imitation of the king of justice, now very many are Antichrists from the spreading of the oil of the king of pride;[^15] for the proof of which, "begin ye at" the sanctuary of Christ that is so in name only.[^16] Does not the antipope, the vicar of Lucifer,[^17] claim that he ought to lord it in secular fashion over all emperors, kings and princes, and that out of reverence for his order he ought everywhere to be served more splendidly after the manner of the world? And to this end he twists holy scripture and the sayings of the saints. And many of his disciples claim the same, ashamed to be called almsmen [310] or servants of their subjects; but by living excessively in secular fashion they bring it about that they are named lords in turn. For in the primitive church they called themselves, as a rule, servants; but in the time of Augustine, on account of the exercise of lordship in destroying the vices of the insolent, they were called lords.[^18] But today they plainly strive, by omission and commission in their manner of life, to show that they claim to be secular lords; and since this is repugnant to God's law and order, it is clear how great a monstrosity would be brought into the church from heresy about the faith of holy scripture. Therefore the blasphemers are to be corrected who blacken (as far as in them lies) the cause of Christ's martyrdom, namely [by saying] that he died to acquire secular lordship for his clerics.

## ¶5 · 310.14–310.29

> [p. 310] Et sic fingunt ingrati quod sanctus Thomas Cantuariensis obiit pro dominacione seculari ablata clero Anglie restituenda et dominacione civili habita confirmanda. Christus enim econtra mortuus est pro salvacione humani generis et ad docendum suos discipulos et specialiter clerum secularia desideria abnegare (ut dicit Apostolus ad Titum II°). Et sanctus Thomas mortuus est ad constanter corripiendum reges et dominos, ne precipitent clerum in officio seculari nec impediant quominus corripiatur a suo episcopo, et per consequens ad docendum episcopos nostre provincie ne ab ista correpcione, et per consequens a defensione privilegiorum et iuris ecclesie timore servili de amissione temporalium retrahantur. Et utinam sacerdotes superiores nostri regni addiscerent intellectu et affectu istam geminam leccionem.

And so men [displeasing]{g:ingratus} [to God] pretend that Saint Thomas of Canterbury died to restore the secular lordship taken from the clergy of England and to confirm the civil lordship it had.[^19] For Christ, on the contrary, died for the salvation of the human race and to teach his disciples, and especially the clergy, to deny worldly desires (as the Apostle says to Titus, chapter 2).[^20] And Saint Thomas died to rebuke kings and lords steadfastly, lest they cast the clergy headlong into secular office or hinder its being corrected by its [bishop]{g:episcopus}; and consequently to teach the bishops of our province not to be drawn back from this correction, and consequently from the defence of the privileges and [right]{g:ius} of the church, by servile fear of losing temporal goods.[^21] And would that the higher priests of our [kingdom]{g:regnum} would learn, with understanding and affection, this twofold lesson.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Lex privata*: a privilege is etymologically a "private law" (*privata lex*), the definition Wyclif invokes. *Sibi* is the clergy.
[^2]: **C.** The minor of the argument at 305.11–13: Christ did not suffer for the civil endowment of his clergy.
[^3]: **S.** 2 Cor. 1:18–19 (DR "was not, It is, and It is not"); Wyclif's words are closest to v. 19, *non fuit est et non*.
[^4]: **C.** *Cum paribus*, "other things being equal" (as *ceteris paribus*); so again at 307.8, *cum istis paribus*. *Post clericatum*: after they took clerical status.
[^5]: **S/C.** *Nolicio*, the act of not-willing (scholastic *nolitio*). Matt. 10:9 (*nolite possidere aurum neque argentum*); 1 John 2:15; Luke 14:27, 33; Matt. 10:38, as Wyclif's references allow.
[^6]: **C.** The hypothesis: that Christ, having taught a propertyless life, secretly meant his later clergy to hold civil lordship.
[^7]: **T.** A and A₁ omit *non* (307.30), which would reverse the sense; the print is translated.
[^8]: **S.** The editor refers to p. 296 ff. (308.8 foot), in chapter 13 (LEDGER E14c-1).
[^9]: **C.** *Meritum proficiendo*: "by making merit profitable", i.e. by binding himself to reward merit. *Per impossibile*: an impossible supposition, made for the argument.
[^10]: **S.** Matt. 10:9–10, the mission charge.
[^11]: **S.** Eph. 4:7 (DR "according to the measure of the giving of Christ"). *Fideles dispensatores* recalls 1 Cor. 4:1–2.
[^12]: **C.** *Isti maxime religioni*: the religion of Christ, "greatest" of religions; possibly "added to this religion above all". The modern rites are the orders' rules.
[^13]: **S.** Rom. 2:11.
[^14]: **C.** The sentence beginning *nam ubi* has no main clause of its own; it breaks off and Wyclif resumes with *Sed iudicet*. *Proprietarie splendidi vel seculariter conversetur* mixes plural and singular (the clergy); the sense is clear. *Ex maiori beneficio*: the greater the benefice, the greater the duty.
[^15]: **S.** Ps. 140:5, *oleum autem peccatoris non inpinguet caput meum* (DR "let not the oil of the sinner fatten my head"); the play is on *christiani* and the oil of anointing (chrism).
[^16]: **S.** Ezek. 9:6, *a sanctuario meo incipite* (DR "begin ye at my sanctuary").
[^17]: **R.** The editor identifies the antipope as Robert of Geneva, Clement VII (309.26 foot), elected September 1378 (LEDGER E14c-2).
[^18]: **C.** *Propter dominacionem insolencium destruendo vicia*: probably "on account of [their] lordship over the insolent, in destroying vices"; the genitive may also go with *vicia*. *Regulariter*, "as a rule", not "as regulars".
[^19]: **G/C.** *Ingrati* stands without a dative; the glossary's sense "displeasing [to God]" is taken. "The ungrateful" is possible. A notes *Ficcio* in the margin at 310.14.
[^20]: **S.** Titus 2:12 (DR "denying ungodliness and worldly desires").
[^21]: **R.** Thomas Becket (d. 1170), who resisted Henry II over the trial of criminous clerks. *Nostre provincie*: the province of Canterbury.
