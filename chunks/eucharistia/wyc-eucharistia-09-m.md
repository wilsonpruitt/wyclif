# *De eucharistia*, chapter 9, part 13: the kings' precedent, the roots of error, and the priest's bond to poverty

## ¶1 · 320.23–321.5

> [p. 320] Tercio vero confirmatur ex factis regum Anglie qui crebrius pro levi delicto acceperunt in manus suas temporalia prelatorum; cum hoc vendicant ut regalia ad quam sine repugnancia episcopi sunt iurati; evidens videtur quod non reputabunt illud hereticum sed plane katholicum, cum aliter foret clerus in Anglia monstruosus dominus capitalis et proditor regis sui. Nec oportet plus timere excommunicacionem vel leges hominum sic agendo quam domini predicti timuerant, cum factum foret plus consonum legi Dei: ideo dicunt [p. 321] experti quod sacerdotes Antichristi non excommunicant ipsos qui eos persecuntur in faciem, cum non cederet eis ad lucrum sed remotos excommunicant cecatos per satrapas suos intrinsecos, quia per hoc putant iuvamine generacionis adultere lucrum eis accrescere.

Thirdly, this is confirmed from the deeds of the kings of England, who quite often, for a slight offence, have taken the [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} of [prelates]{g:prelatus} into their hands; since they claim this as a [regality]{g:regalia}, to which the bishops are sworn without demur,[^1] it seems evident that they will not reckon it heretical but plainly catholic, since otherwise the clergy in England would be a monstrous [chief lord]{g:capitalis-dominus} and a traitor to its king. Nor need one fear excommunication or the laws of men more in acting so than the aforesaid lords had feared [them], since the deed would be more in accord with [God's law]{g:lex-dei}: therefore [321] men of experience say that the [priests]{g:sacerdos} of Antichrist do not excommunicate those who persecute them to their face, since it would not turn to their gain, but excommunicate those far off, who are blinded by their own [satraps]{g:satrapa} within;[^2] for they think that by this, with the help of an adulterous generation,[^3] gain accrues to them.

## ¶2 · 321.6–321.18

> [p. 321] Secundo principaliter arguitur per hoc quod plus pensanda est salus et utilitas communioris ecclesie quam utilitas partis sue; sed clerus Anglicane ecclesie est modica pars respectu ecclesie tocius regni nostri; ergo debet eius utilitas plus pensari; cum ergo per hunc modum salvaretur totum regnum ab hostibus corporalibus (quod aliter faciliter perire poterat), videtur quod naturalis racio ad hoc cogit. Unde considerantes alcius statum regni dicunt quod regnum nostrum posset plene defendi ab intra per ministracionem temporalium que sunt in manu mortua; et insuper hoc foret medium multiplicandi homines qui in ista causa certare possent legitime.

Secondly, [as a main head]{g:principaliter}, it is argued by appeal to this: that the welfare and advantage of the more common [church]{g:ecclesia} is to be weighed more than the advantage of its part; now the clergy of the English church is a small part in respect of the church of our whole [kingdom]{g:regnum}; therefore its advantage ought to be weighed more.[^4] Since, then, in this way the whole kingdom would be saved from bodily enemies (which otherwise could easily perish), it seems that natural reason compels to this. Hence those who consider the state of the kingdom more deeply say that our kingdom could be fully defended from within by the administration of the temporal goods that are in [the dead hand]{g:manus-mortua};[^5] and moreover this would be a means of multiplying men who could lawfully fight in this cause.

## ¶3 · 321.19–322.7

> [p. 321] Nec valet fingere quod accresceret regno peccatum sacrilegii ex hoc facto quod necessitaret ad ruinam ecclesie nostre, quia ut dicit decretum beati Ambrosii XII, questione IIᵃ, Aurum habet ecclesia, pro alendis pauperibus, pro redimendis captivis et pro edificiis necessariis locis sanctis licet eciam vasa sacra confringere sine peccato. Cum ergo hoc sit maius quam omnia ista, simul nec oportet vasa sacra ad hoc vendere nec sustentacionem cleri vel eius religiositatem propterea minuere sed augere, videtur quod hoc undique sit salubre. Non enim videtur finis thesauri sic cumulati in clero qui debet mundum deserere, nisi quod ydolatrando congreget et parando [p. 322] conservet thesaurum regni forinsecis inimicis. Sic vero beatus Ambrosius dicit aurum collectum in templo Jerusalem esse causam quare Nabuchodonosor ipsam destruxerat. Et hec racio quare Anglia et multa alia regna sepius sunt destructa. Non ergo credi debet cecatis, dimissa fide scripture, quousque regnum nostrum irremediabiliter sit destructum.

Nor is it any use to feign that the sin of sacrilege would accrue to the kingdom from this deed, such as would bring our church of necessity to ruin; for, as the decree of blessed Ambrose says (XII, question 2), "The church has gold":[^6] for feeding the poor, for redeeming captives and for buildings necessary to holy places it is lawful even to break up the [sacred]{g:sacer} vessels without sin. Since, then, this is greater than all those, and at the same time there is no need to sell the sacred vessels for it, nor on that account to lessen the sustenance of the clergy or its [religiousness]{g:religiositas}, but to increase [them], it seems that this is on every side wholesome. For there seems to be no end of treasure so heaped up in the clergy, which ought to forsake the world, except that by [idolatrizing]{g:ydolatrare} it gathers, and by preparing [322] keeps, the kingdom's treasure for foreign enemies.[^7] And so blessed Ambrose says that the gold collected in the temple of Jerusalem was the cause why Nebuchadnezzar had destroyed it.[^8] And this is the reason why England and many other kingdoms have often been destroyed. Therefore the blinded are not to be believed, with the faith of scripture set aside, until our kingdom is destroyed beyond remedy.

## ¶4 · 322.8–324.3

> [p. 322] Item, (ut omnis fidelis fatetur) multiplicacio peccatorum est causa destruccionis regnorum, sed illa inter christianos potissime originatur a clero (ut docet Gregorius in Registro suo libro III, cap. XXIX), ergo causa destruccionis regnorum originatur ex peccato cleri. Cum ergo peccatum cleri potissimum sit avaricia que est ydolorum servitus (ut dictum est tractatu De Simonia), videtur quod remedium potissimum ad tollendum destrucciones regnorum foret tollere occasionem simonie in clero; que per hoc tolleretur potissime quod clero ut angelis vel Christo et aliis statum innocencie servantibus servarentur vite necessaria sine pluri; aliter enim inficiunt ministerium, consecrant eukaristiam ad sui iudicium et reddunt se utcumque dampnabiles per consensum. Ideo minus malum foret undique tale ministerium non adesse sacerdotes itaque hoc ministerium, quia debent in paupertate habendo omnia in communi propinquius sequi Christum. Quod si murmuraverimus contra dictam sentenciam que secundum legem Dei foret ad utilitatem ecclesie, indignificamus nos, etsi panem sacramentalem conficimus, refici pane vite. Deus enim [p. 323] communia bona preponderat, sicut ordinat in prosperitate communis populi stare valetudinem regni nostri. Quomodo ergo permitteremus avaris spoliacionem pauperis populi de elemosinis quibus vivimus, et habendo bona eius incastrata non ipsos superflue quoad Deum et homines foveremus. Colligendo ergo radices errorum in clero, patet quod tria sunt in quibus sophisticatur ecclesia, scilicet cleri dominacio, tradicionum suarum magnificacio et sanctitatis sue simulacio; et ex illis procedunt infinita sophismata regis superbie, ut excommunicacionis terrificacio, observacionis legis sue preponderacio et signorum in sacramentis ac sacramentalibus nimia attencio. Remedium contra primum est fides de Christi pauperie quam totus clerus debet sequi propinquius et consequenter exemplo suo ad hoc trahere populares. Remedium contra secundum est quod leges humane precise de tanto sunt valide, de quanto in lege Domini sunt fundate. Remedium contra tercium est credere vite et operibus conformibus legi Dei; nam res cui credimus est insensibilis; cui non pertinet sensum sensibile nisi de quanto fidem excitat ad credito secundum caritatis regulas adherendum. Et in isto tercio est magis velatum periculum, ut patet de sacramentorum sophisticacione supra fidem scripture et specialiter in illis que lucrum sapiunt, ut patet de eukaristia, penitencia et sepultura. Ideo debet fidelis theologus contra simulaciones istas constanter invehere. Finis itaque gracia cuius hec dixerim est ut tollendo errorem in signis fidelis memoretur meritorius signatorum. Nec credo esse aliquod genus hominum, quod memoratur Christum falsius tam speculative quam practice sequendo Scarioth quam facit communitas sacerdotum, quia in quantum sic [p. 324] celebramus memoriam Christi, debemus ipsum sequendo similius pacem et veritatem diligere; et nullus magis exorbitat in utriusque contrarium.

Again (as every believer confesses), the multiplication of sins is the cause of the destruction of kingdoms; now among Christians it originates most of all from the clergy (as Gregory teaches in his *Register*, book 3, chapter 29);[^9] therefore the cause of the destruction of kingdoms originates from the sin of the clergy. Since, then, the [foremost]{g:potissimus} sin of the clergy is avarice, which is the [servitude of idols]{g:servitus-ydolorum} (as was said in the treatise *On Simony*),[^10] it seems that the foremost remedy for taking away the destructions of kingdoms would be to take away the occasion of [simony]{g:simonia} in the clergy; and this would be taken away most of all by this: that for the clergy, as for the angels, or for Christ and the others who kept the state of innocence, the necessaries of life were kept, and no more. For otherwise they infect the ministry, consecrate [the Eucharist]{g:eukaristia} to their own judgement,[^11] and render themselves in one way or another damnable by consent. Therefore it would be a lesser evil for such a ministry to be present nowhere; priests, then, [should hold] this ministry, because they ought, in poverty, having all things in common, to follow Christ more closely.[^12] And if we murmur against the said [teaching]{g:sentencia},[^13] which according to God's law would be for the advantage of the church, we make ourselves unworthy, even if we [consecrate]{g:conficere} the sacramental [bread]{g:panis}, to be refreshed by the bread of life. For God [323] gives common goods the greater weight, just as he ordains that the health of our kingdom stands in the prosperity of the common people. How then should we allow the avaricious the despoiling of the poor people, out of the alms on which we live, and, holding its goods locked away, not cherish them superfluously as regards God and men?[^14] Gathering, then, the roots of the errors in the clergy, it is clear that there are three things in which the church is [counterfeited]{g:sophisticare}:[^15] namely the clergy's [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio}, the magnifying of its traditions, and the feigning of its holiness; and from these proceed numberless [sophisms]{g:sophisma} of the king of pride,[^16] such as the terrifying with excommunication, the overweighting of the observance of its own law, and too much attention to the [signs]{g:signum} in the sacraments and [sacramentals]{g:sacramentalia}. The remedy against the first is faith concerning the poverty of Christ, which the whole clergy ought to follow more closely, and consequently by its example draw the people to it. The remedy against the second is that human laws are valid precisely so far as they are founded in the law of the Lord. The remedy against the third is to believe life and works conformable to God's law; for the thing that we believe is [imperceptible]{g:insensibilis}; and what is perceptible to sense does not pertain to it, except so far as it stirs up faith to cleave to what is believed according to the rules of [charity]{g:caritas}.[^17] And in this third lies the more veiled danger, as is clear from the [sophistry]{g:sophisticacio} of the sacraments beyond the faith of scripture, and especially in those that savour of gain, as is clear of the Eucharist, penance and burial. Therefore the faithful theologian ought to inveigh steadfastly against these feignings. The end, then, for the sake of which I have said these things is that, by taking away the error in the signs, the believer may more meritoriously remember the [things signified]{g:signatum}. Nor do I believe there is any kind of men that remembers Christ more falsely, both speculatively and practically, following Iscariot, than the community of priests does; for [insofar as]{g:in-quantum} we thus [324] celebrate the memory of Christ, we ought, following him more nearly in likeness, to love peace and truth; and none strays further into the contrary of both.[^18]

## ¶5 · 324.4–324.28

> [p. 324] Recolligendo ergo summatim finem intentum in isto tractatu videtur michi quod quociens sacerdos conficit tociens Deo se obligat profitens specialiter sequi Christum vita et moribus et per consequens vivendo vitam pauperem habere omnia in communi. Et ad illud obligantur omnes pure clerici a papa usque ad infimum sacerdotem. Curati enim licet dicuntur promoti et domini, debent instar Christi et apostolorum capere de elemosinis populi quem edificant vel alterius devoti hominis quantum est eis necessarium ad ministerium sacerdotis. Quod si quis eorum ad distribuendum capit elemosinas ulteriores a populo, caveat sibi de periculo adiacente. Si enim rex celorum sic vixit pauperrimus habens omnia in communi, quis aut qualis es tu sacerdos cui Christus precipit: Hoc quocienscunque feceritis, in meam memoriam facietis; et tamen faciendo signum suum signatum tanquam Christi proditor false negas; debes enim opere exemplari et verbo supereminenter edificare Christi ecclesiam, cum propterea illud officium tibi singulariter est commissum. Quod si plus voluptuose et seculariter conversaris, quis est falsior et infideliter, maior proditor Deo suo? Et hec est radix perturbacionis ecclesie, cum Veritas, caput eius, odit huiusmodi falsitatem.

Gathering up, then, in sum the end intended in this treatise, it seems to me that as often as a priest consecrates, so often he binds himself to God, professing in a special way to follow Christ in life and manners, and consequently, living a poor life, to have all things in common. And to that all who are clerics purely are bound, from the [pope]{g:papa} down to the lowest priest. For [curates]{g:curatus}, although they are called promoted men and lords, ought, after the pattern of Christ and the apostles, to take from the [alms]{g:elemosina} of the people whom they edify, or of some other devout man, as much as is necessary to them for the ministry of a priest. And if any of them takes further alms from the people for distributing, let him beware of the danger that lies close by. For if the king of heaven lived thus, poorest of all, having all things in common, who or what sort are you, priest, to whom Christ commands: "This, as often as you shall do it, you shall do in memory of me";[^19] and yet, in making his sign, you falsely deny the thing signified, as a traitor of Christ? For you ought by exemplary work and word to build up Christ's church pre-eminently, since for that reason that office has been committed to you [in a singular way]{g:singulariter}.[^20] And if you [live your life]{g:conversari} more voluptuously and in a worldly way, who is falser and, faithlessly, a greater traitor to his God?[^21] And this is the root of the church's disturbance, since Truth, its [head]{g:caput}, hates falsehood of this kind.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Ad quam sine repugnancia episcopi sunt iurati*: *quam* takes *regalia* as a feminine singular, "regality". *Sine repugnancia* is rendered "without demur"; the logical sense of the row (*repugnancia* "incompatibility") would give "sworn without incompatibility", i.e. the oath does not conflict with their office.

[^2]: **G/C.** *Satrapa*: Wyclif's word for the officers of the church courts. *Per satrapas suos intrinsecos*: *suos* takes the priests of Antichrist as its owner; *intrinsecos*, "within", the officers at home, set against the excommunicated "far off".

[^3]: **S.** *Generacionis adultere*: Matt. 12:39, "an evil and adulterous generation".

[^4]: **C/T.** *Eius utilitas*: the advantage of the church of the whole kingdom, the major premiss's "more common church". ABD read *debet eis*; in C *eius* stands on an erasure; AB lack *plus*, which C adds in the margin by another hand.

[^5]: **G.** *Manus mortua*, mortmain: lands held by the church, which never pass on by death or sale.

[^6]: **S.** *Decretum beati Ambrosii XII, questione IIᵃ*: Gratian, C. 12 q. 2 c. 70 (Loserth: *Decr.* Sec. Pars, Causa XII, qu. II, cap. LXX; the modern form is ours), *Aurum habet ecclesia* (LEDGER K09m-1), from Ambrose, *De officiis* II.28 (LEDGER K09m-2), §§137–142, PL 16, 140B–142A; Wyclif's *pro alendis pauperibus, pro redimendis captivis* follows Gratian's dictum before the canon, *pro alimonia pauperum, pro redemtione captivorum res sacras distrahi licet*. Loserth's italics end at *ecclesia*; the rest is Wyclif's summary of the canon and stands unquoted.

[^7]: **C.** *Parando conservet thesaurum regni forinsecis inimicis*: the clergy keeps the kingdom's treasure ready for foreign enemies to take; *forinsecis inimicis* is a dative of advantage on both verbs. The Latin's *finis* is the purpose of the treasure.

[^8]: **T/S.** *Sic vero* is Loserth's correction (322.1–2): ABC read *Sed nec*, DE *Sic nec*. Ambrose on the temple gold and Nebuchadnezzar: Loserth gives no source and refers to Wyclif, *De ecclesia* pp. 376–377 (LEDGER K09m-3); the passage is Ambrose, *De officiis* II.28.137 (PL 16, 140B), *An ignoramus quantum auri atque argenti de templo Domini Assyrii sustulerint?* (4 Kgs 24:13), with Joachim keeping the gold and losing it at §141; it is part of the canon *Aurum* just quoted, and the claim that the gold was the cause of the destruction is Wyclif's own (LEDGER K09m-4). *Ipsam* is feminine: Jerusalem.

[^9]: **S.** Gregory, *Registrum* III.29 as Wyclif cites it; Loserth gives no reference. It is the letter to Queen Brunhild, Ep. XI.69 in the Maurist numbering (PL 77, 1209B): *Nam causa sunt ruinae populi sacerdotes mali*; Wyclif quotes it under the same reference, *Registro libro 3, capitulo 29 regine Francie*, in *De simonia* c. 1, p. 7 (LEDGER K09m-5).

[^10]: **S.** *Avaricia que est ydolorum servitus*: Eph. 5:5 (Loserth), where the Vulgate has *avarus, quod est idolorum servitus*, Douay "covetous person (which is a serving of idols)"; Wyclif makes avarice the subject, so his Latin is translated. *Tractatu De Simonia*: Wyclif, *De simonia* p. 7 ff. (Loserth; LEDGER K09m-6). ABC read *ydolorum cultus*.

[^11]: **S.** *Ad sui iudicium*: 1 Cor. 11:29, "eateth and drinketh judgment to himself".

[^12]: **T.** The print, *Ideo minus malum foret undique tale ministerium non adesse sacerdotes itaque hoc ministerium*, does not construe after *adesse*; A reads *Hoc ministerium*. Loserth judges the place corrupt and proposes *Ideo minus malum foret undique tale ministerium non esse ad sacerdotes, quia debent …*, "a lesser evil that such a ministry should nowhere belong to priests, because they ought …". The print is translated as far as it goes, with [should hold] supplied (our emendation); Loserth's reading gives the opposite point, that the ministry should be kept from priests who do not live in poverty.

[^13]: **C.** *Sentencia*, "teaching": the position just set out on disendowment, not a judicial sentence. *Pane vite*: John 6:35, "I am the bread of life".

[^14]: **C/T.** A doubtful sentence. *Incastrata*, "locked away" (literally "put in a castle", perhaps glancing at the friars' houses); *ipsos* is taken as the avaricious, following Loserth's side-note ("for the benefit of rapacious spoilers"); it could be the poor people (*populi*, plural by sense). D reads *superfluos*, "cherish them as superfluous"; DE read *avare*, "avariciously", for *avaris*. The print has no question mark; the sense is a question under *quomodo*.

[^15]: **C.** *Sophisticatur ecclesia*: taken as the passive of *sophisticare*, "the church is counterfeited"; as the deponent *sophisticari* it would be "the church plays the sophist". ABC read *sublimacio* for one of the three nouns (323.10; Loserth: "an error legentis?").

[^16]: **S.** *Regis superbie*: Job 41:25, "he is king over all the children of pride".

[^17]: **T/C.** *Cui non pertinet sensum sensibile*: the accusative *sensum* does not construe; read as "what is perceptible to sense" (as if *sensui sensibile*). *Credere vite et operibus*: to trust a life and works that conform to God's law, against trust in the signs.

[^18]: **S/C.** *Pacem et veritatem diligere*: cf. Zech. 8:19, "only love ye truth and peace". *Sequendo similius*: the comparative adverb of *similis*, following him in closer likeness.

[^19]: **S.** *Hoc quocienscunque feceritis, in meam memoriam facietis*: the canon of the mass, *Haec quotiescunque feceritis, in mei memoriam facietis* (Sarum Missal, ed. Dickinson, col. 617), which Wyclif gives with *Hoc* and the Vulgate's *meam* (LEDGER K09m-7), not the Vulgate; cf. Luke 22:19 and 1 Cor. 11:25, "This do ye, as often as you shall drink, for the commemoration of me". D reads *commemoracionem*.

[^20]: **C.** *Singulariter*: not "alone", since the office is every priest's; "in a singular way", by the row's second sense.

[^21]: **T.** *Infideliter* is the print, as an adverb on *maior proditor*; BC read *infidelior*, "falser and more faithless", which the apparatus records beside the corrected *infideliter*. The print is translated. ABCD read *Qui si* for *Quod si* (324.24).
