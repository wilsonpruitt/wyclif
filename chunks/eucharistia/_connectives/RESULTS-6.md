# Connective check: *sed* / *autem*, De eucharistia, wave 6 (chunks 03-d, 03-e, 03-f)

Classes: (a) minor premiss / next step, "now" · (b) adversative, "but" · (c) continuative *autem*, "and";
*non … sed* (incl. *nec … sed*), "but".

## Changes

| chunk | page | Latin phrase | was | now | reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 03-f | 75 | *verumtamen ipsa informant panem et vinum, non **autem** corpus Christi vel sanguinem* | not however the body or blood of Christ | but not the body or blood of Christ | b: the *autem* sets the body and blood against the bread and wine that the accidents do inform. The class is rendered right but with the wrong word: the card gives "but" for the adversative, and "however" is not in the table. "But not" also matches the 03-c ruling on *non autem*. The "but" that follows (*sed illa informacio*) is a separate *sed* and keeps its own "but" |

## Doubtful, left as they are

- **03-d, p. 67**: *Cum **autem** hoc sit argumentum ad hominem, peto maiorem ut datam*, rendered "And since this is an argument to the man". The argument has just been stated, and here Wyclif turns to how its premisses are secured (major granted, minor proved). Read that way, (c) "and" stands. It could also be read as the next step of the proof, which would make it (a) "Now since". No *ergo* follows, so the look-ahead rule does not settle it. Left.
- **03-e, p. 72** (Augustine, *Soliloquia* II.12): *Uno modo localiter … Altero **autem** quo ita est aliquid in subiecto*, rendered "In one way locally … In the other, by which". The *autem* gets no word of its own. It is the second half of a correlative pair (*uno … altero autem*), and the English pair "In one way … In the other" carries it, so it is counted as (c) and not as an omission. "And in the other" would make it explicit. Left.
- **03-f, p. 75**: *Accidencia **autem** habent ibi principalem existenciam*, rendered "And the accidents". This carries on with what is "there" after the conversion: (c). Read as a contrast (the bread and wine lose principality, *but* the accidents have a principal existence), it could be (b) "But". Left.
- **03-f, p. 74** (Hugh, *De sacramentis* II.8.9): *Conversio **autem** ipsa non secundum unionem*, rendered "And the conversion itself". Hugh goes on from the fact of the conversion to its mode: (c). It could be read as his next step, (a) "Now". Left.
