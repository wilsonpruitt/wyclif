# Connective check: *sed* / *autem*, De eucharistia, wave 15 (chunks 06-c, 06-d, 06-e)

Classes: (a) minor premiss / next step, "now" · (b) adversative, "but" · (c) continuative *autem*, "and";
*non … sed* (incl. *nec / nedum / nemo … sed*), "but".

## Changes

| chunk | page | Latin phrase | was | now | reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 06-e | 175 | Augustine (*Contra Maximinum*): *Non, inquit, non potuit **sed** non decuit, hoc est, potuit, si voluerit* | "No," he says, "he could not, but it was not fitting; | "Not 'he could not'," he says, "but 'it was not fitting'; | *non … sed*, construed wrongly. The first *Non* negates *non potuit*: "not 'he could not' but 'it was not fitting'". The old English has Augustine say "he could not", which the next clause, *hoc est, potuit*, then contradicts. Corrected, the *sed* is "but" in its proper class and *hoc est, potuit* follows from it |

## Doubtful, left as they are

- **06-c, p. 166** (Gregory): *Sed agni caro nec cruda edenda est nec aqua cocta, quia …*, rendered "But the flesh of the lamb". Read as (b): after the glosses on "water" and "raw", it turns to the precept itself. It could be (a), since *Qui **ergo** paschalis gaudii sollempnitatem celebrare desiderat agnum nec aqua coquat nec crudum comedat* (p. 167) closes on it. But that *ergo* comes three sentences later and restates the precept as an exhortation rather than drawing a conclusion from a syllogism. Left.
- **06-c, p. 166** (Gregory): *Lactuce vero aggrestes valde amare sunt. Carnes **autem** agni cum lactucis aggrestibus sunt edende, ut …*, rendered "And the flesh of the lamb". Read as (c). It could be (a), since the bitterness just stated is brought to bear on the eating, but no inference is drawn. Left.
- **06-c, p. 168** (Gregory): *Quia igitur qualiter edendum sit pasca agnovimus, nunc **autem** a qualibus edi debeat agnoscamus*, rendered "Since, then, … let us now learn". There is no separate word for the *autem*. It marks the next step of the homily (a), and the construction carries it: the "Since …, let us now" frame together with *nunc*'s "now" gives the step. A second "now" would be redundant. Left, counted as carried, not omitted.
- **06-d, p. 170**: *Omnes **autem** istam dileccionem **sed** nimis mendaciter profitemur*, rendered "And we all profess this love, but exceedingly falsely". Read as (c). It could be (a), as the minor of an implicit argument (whoever loves anything more than the Lord is unworthy; we profess the love falsely; *Et talis indigne capit corpus Christi*). But no *ergo / ideo / igitur* closes it, and "but" follows at once. Left.
- **06-d, p. 171**: *contradiceretur decretis et glosse ecclesie **sed** blaspheme contradiceretur divine potencie*, rendered "but, what is more, would blasphemously contradict". The "but" is present and is (b), escalating. "What is more" is a supplement, not a "rather". The editor may want it bracketed or dropped. Left.
- **06-e, p. 174**: *Pictores **autem** vocant colores corpora liquida …*, rendered "Now painters call". Read as (a): it brings a new premiss (the painters' usage), and *Et ita videtur decretalem grosse loqui de accidentibus* draws on it. "And" (c) is defensible. Left.
- **06-e, p. 176**: *… cicius aut facilius homo posset peccare. **Sed** multi positi in adopcione eligerent virtutem …; **et ita** non sufficit carencia mortalis peccati*, rendered "But many". Read as (b). *Et ita* draws on it, but that is not the *ergo* of the look-ahead rule. Left.
