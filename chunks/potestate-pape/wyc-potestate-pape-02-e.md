# *De potestate pape*, chapter 2, part 5: body and church compared; power lost by mortal sin

## ¶1 · 40.31–42.5

> [p. 40] Sed quomodocunque loquimur, est notanda diversitas inter membra istorum corporum. Prima, cum partes ecclesie continuantur per graciam, non respiciunt posicionem vel ubicacionem situs [p. 41] corporei ut membra hominis; secunda cum membra sint mistica, non repugnat sed convenit quod unum membrum habeat diversorum generum officia; homo enim est quasi universitas, ideo sibi convenit instanter agere quantum potest. Tercia diversitas est, licet membra ecclesie habeant virtutes influxas a Christo, sicut membra corporis habent virtutes influxas ab anima, a quibus ponuntur in esse membrorum, tamen influencia prior et membrorum operacio est voluntaria et graciosa et meritoria, et secundo influencia anime et membrorum operacio sunt naturales et permanencius perdurantes; licet enim membrum ecclesie posset facillime mutari ex sua vertibilitate ab uno membro in aliud vel ex variacione operis desinere esse membrum vivum, hoc tamen non potest contingere ex differencia spiritus influentis, unde quia tota materia ista est insensibilis, est plena illusione zophistica. Sed quantum ad ultimum scilicet perdicionem potencie, videtur, cum potestas illa sit licencia operandi, quod generaliter perditur per mortale, cum sit illicitum tali aliquid operari. Et ista videtur sentencia beati Gregorii posita in decretis XI, q. III: Ipse, inquit, ligandi atque solvendi potestate se privat, qui hanc pro suis voluntatibus et non subditorum moribus exercet. Et sequitur: Iudicare digne de subditis nequeunt, qui in subditorum causis sua vel odio vel gracia sequuntur. Tunc, inquit vera est absolucio presidentis, cum interni sequitur arbitrium iudicis, quod bene quadriduani mortui resuscitacio illa signat, quia prius Dominus mortuum suscitavit [p. 42] et vivificavit dicens: Lazare, veni foras et postmodum is, qui vivus fuerat, egressus a discipulis est solutus. Et patet cum cursu ecclesie quomodo maior pars ecclesie tam in foro penitencie quam iuris caret potestate iudicii.

But however we speak, a diversity must be noted between the members of these bodies.[^1] The first: since the parts of the [church]{g:ecclesia} are continuous by grace, they do not regard the position or location of bodily [41] place, as the members of a man do; the second: since the members are mystical, it is not repugnant but fitting that one [member]{g:membrum} have offices of diverse kinds; for man is as it were a universe, therefore it befits him to act urgently as much as he can. The third diversity is that, although the members of the church have powers [flowed in]{g:influere} from Christ, as the members of the body have powers flowed in from the soul, by which they are set in the being of members, yet the former influence and the members' operation is voluntary, gracious and meritorious, and in the second the influence of the soul and the operation of the members are natural and endure more permanently; for although a member of the church could very easily be changed, by its changeability, from one member into another, or by a variation of its work cease to be a living member, yet this cannot happen from a difference in the spirit flowing in; hence, since this whole matter is [imperceptible]{g:insensibilis}, it is full of sophistical illusion.[^2] But as to the last [point], namely the loss of [power]{g:potencia}, it seems, since that [power]{g:potestas} is a licence to act, that it is generally lost by mortal [sin], since it is unlawful for such a one to do anything.[^3] And this seems to be the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of blessed Gregory placed in the Decretum, C. 11 q. 3: "He deprives himself," he says, "of the power of binding and loosing, who exercises it for his own wishes and not for the conduct of his subjects." And it follows: "They cannot judge worthily of their subjects, who in their subjects' cases follow their own hatred or favour." "Then," he says, "is the absolution of the one presiding true, when it follows the judgement of the inward judge; which that raising of the man four days dead well signifies, for the Lord first raised the dead man [42] and gave him life, saying, 'Lazarus, come forth', and afterwards he who had come out alive was loosed by the disciples."[^4] And it is clear from the course of the church how the greater part of the church, both in the [court of penance]{g:forum-penitencie} and in that of law, lacks the power of judgement.[^5]

## ¶2 · 42.6–42.16

> [p. 42] Confirmatur secundo ex hoc quod potestas talis cum dativo a Deo ad tam excellens ministerium, est quoddam singulare privilegium. Sed secundum beatum Gregorium ibidem: privilegium omnino meretur amittere qui permissa sibi abutitur potestate. Cum igitur quilibet potentatus abutitur sua potestate, dum illam exercuerit in mortali, videtur quod meretur potestatem talem amittere, et cum Deus qui dat insensibiliter potestatem non defert meritum sive dignum, videtur quod eo ipso Deus aufert ab homine potestatem.

It is confirmed secondly from this, that such power, [given] by God for so excellent a ministry, is a certain singular [privilege]{g:privilegium}.[^6] Now according to blessed Gregory in the same place, "he deserves to lose his privilege altogether, who abuses the power permitted him". Since therefore every [potentate]{g:potentatus} abuses his power while he exercises it in mortal [sin], it seems that he deserves to lose such power; and since God, who gives power imperceptibly, does not defer what is merited or deserved, it seems that by that very fact God takes the power away from the man.[^7]

## ¶3 · 42.17–42.33

> [p. 42] Item, Deus potest auferre licenciam vel potestatem ab homine pro delicto, sed quando auferret graciosus dominus si non quando se ipsum dampnificat per abusum ad quod non habet licenciam? ideo cum nemo potest abuti tali licencia nec virtute, videtur quod Gregorius equivocat vocando potestatem, posse vel potenciam aut liberum arbitrium, et alias vocat potestatem amissam ipsam licenciam. Et idem videtur ex decreto beati Augustini posito XXIV, q. prima, ubi dicit omnes hereticos, id est, peccato mortali ab ecclesia divisos, non habere aliquid potestatis. Sed notandum est; ut alias declaravi, quod opus heretici vertitur dignis ad commodum, sicut Deus per se supplet, quando prepositi excommunicati a Deo subditis suis deficiunt.

Again, God can take away a licence or power from a man for a fault; but when would a gracious lord take it away, if not when the man harms himself by an abuse for which he has no licence? Therefore, since no one can abuse such a licence or [power]{g:virtus}, it seems that Gregory speaks [equivocally]{g:equivoce}, calling "power" the capacity or power or [free choice]{g:liberum-arbitrium}, and elsewhere calling the power that is lost the licence itself.[^8] And the same seems [to follow] from the decree of blessed Augustine placed at C. 24 q. 1, where he says that "all heretics", "that is, those divided from the church by mortal sin, have nothing of power".[^9] But it must be noted, as I have declared elsewhere, that the work of a heretic is turned to the profit of the worthy, as God supplies by himself when [superiors]{g:prepositi} excommunicated by God fail their subjects.[^10]

## ¶4 · 43.1–43.9

> [p. 43] Et ex istis videtur quod potestas predestinacionis (de qua Joh. primo et sepe alibi) est maxima potestas, quam Deus communicat viatori. Potestas autem, quam Deus dat beato ad plene fruendum dominio, et in ipso qualis creatura est potestas hominis consumata, de qua quidam intelligunt illud Matth. ult: Data est mihi omnis potestas in celo et in terra, beati enim habent fructum cuiuslibet potestatis.

And from these things it seems that the power of predestination (of which John 1 and often elsewhere) is the greatest power that God communicates to the [wayfarer]{g:viator}.[^11] And the power which God gives to the blessed for the full enjoyment of [lordship]{g:dominium}, and in him, such as a creature is, is the consummated power of man, of which some understand that of the last chapter of Matthew: "All power is given to me in heaven and in earth"; for the blessed have the fruit of every power.[^12]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 40.31: Difference between membership of the body and of the Church.
- 41.20: Members who tall into mortal sin lose their power.
- 42.26: Although the wicked lose power, the righteous may profit by their ministrations,
- 43.1: The greatest power God grants is that of predestination.

## Notes

[^1]: **R.** B in marg.: *Diversitas in membris corporis organici et ecclesie* (40.31). The "last" point taken up in ¶1 is the loss of powers, announced earlier in the chapter.

[^2]: **G/C.** *Influxas*, "flowed in" (GLOSSARY *influere*); *influencia*, "influence". *Influencia prior … et secundo influencia anime*: "the former" is Christ's inflow into the church, "the second" the soul's into the body. *Universitas*: man as a whole world in little.

[^3]: **C.** *Perdicionem potencie … potestas illa*: Wyclif uses *potencia* and *potestas* of the same thing here, "power" both times. *Per mortale*: by mortal sin, as *in mortali* at 42.11. B in marg.: *Perdere potestatem*; A in marg.: *Potestatem homo perdit per peccatum mortale* (41.20).

[^4]: **S.** Gregory, *Hom. in Evang.* 26.5–6, as excerpted in the Decretum, C. 11 q. 3 cc. 60–62 (Loserth "Decreti secunda pars, Causa XI, q. III, cap. LX–LXII"; the modern form is ours; PL 187, 861B–862A; the homily is PL 76, 1200B–C; LEDGER P02e-1, P02e-2). John 11:43–44 (DR "Lazarus, come forth"; "Loose him"). The printed *Indicare* (41.27) is read as *iudicare*, "judge".

[^5]: **C.** *Cum cursu ecclesie*: "from the course of the church", its present practice. *Tam in foro penitencie quam iuris*: the court of penance and the court of law.

[^6]: **T.** *Cum dativo* (42.7) does not construe; the plate confirms the print. We take the sense to be "given" (perhaps for *cum detur* or *tamquam data*) and supply it in brackets.

[^7]: **S/C.** Gregory, as excerpted in the Decretum, C. 11 q. 3 c. 63 (*Idem in Regesto*; PL 187, 862A; Loserth "Ibid. cap. LXIII"; LEDGER P02e-3); the maxim is Pope Simplicius's, and Gratian gives it under his name at D. 74 c. 7. *Non defert meritum sive dignum*: "does not defer", i.e. delays no requital; it may instead mean "does not pay regard to".

[^8]: **C.** *Vocando potestatem, posse vel potenciam aut liberum arbitrium*: in one place Gregory's "power" names the bare capacity (*posse*, *potencia*), which no sin removes, and in another the licence, which is lost; *potencia* is "power" in the sense of capacity. *Nec virtute*: *virtus* as faculty.

[^9]: **S.** Decretum, C. 24 q. 1 c. 31 (*Didicimus*; Loserth, 42.27; PL 187, 1279C: *omnes omnino haereticos et schismaticos nil habere potestatis ac iuris*); Gratian's inscription is Cyprian, to Magnus, not Augustine as Wyclif has it (LEDGER P02e-4). His italics run over the gloss *id est … divisos*, which may be Wyclif's own; we follow them.

[^10]: **C.** *Ut alias declaravi*: the place is not identified (LEDGER P02e-5; not found by phrase search in the Society volumes).

[^11]: **S.** John 1:12 (DR "he gave them power to be made the sons of God") is the likeliest reference. A in marg.: *Potestas predestinacionis est maxima potestas viatoris*; B in marg.: *Potestas predestinacionis maxima* (43.1).

[^12]: **T/C/S.** *Dominio*: the manuscripts read *domino*, "for the full enjoyment of the Lord" (43.5); Loserth prints *dominio*, which we translate. *Et in ipso qualis creatura est*: taken as "in him, so far as a creature can have it"; the clause is compressed. Matt. 28:18 (Loserth). B in marg.: *Consummata potestas* (43.6).
