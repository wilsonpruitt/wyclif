# *De potestate pape*, chapter 10, part 7: cutting the root of cupidity; the caesarean order not needed for salvation

## ¶1 · 242.1–242.5

> [p. 242] Consideraremus itaqae, quantum sancti eciam posteriores et peiores apostolis ab isto peccato cupidinis se servarunt et ipsum in scriptis ac verbis vivacibus reprobarunt, ut specialiter patet in scriptis et decretis beati Gregorii.

[242] We should consider, then, how far the saints, even those later than the apostles and worse than they, kept themselves from this sin of cupidity and reproved it in writings and in living words, as is especially clear in the writings and decrees of blessed Gregory.[^1]

## ¶2 · 242.6–242.30

> [p. 242] Sed adhuc replicatur, quomodo melius foret corrigere cleri cupidinem quam devote collata auferre? cum ista possunt meritorie possideri et securitas ac copia temporalium confortat et auget clericos, ymo habent hoc, cum sit meritorium et iuste petitum ex titulo doni Dei; grave itaque foret cum tanta subtraccione perturbare ecclesiam. Hic videtur michi quod evidencia non procedit, cum melius foret clericos conversari ut clericos generaliter quoad quoscumque dotatos; optimum itaque foret radicitus corrigere cleri cupidinem, auferendo venenosam occasionem, sic quod clerus reductus ad perfeccionem status primevi allidat parvulos reatus ad petram iusticie. Ista itaque foret correccio cupiditatis cleri fructuosior, permanencior et complecior, quam blasfema extorsio humanitus palliata. Qui enim sanaret vulnus vel morbum hominis, destrueret radicalem causam; qua dimissa remaneret patens occasio frutices cupidinis producendi; oportet tamen prudenter auferre radicem istam pestiferam, quando ex peccato notorio datur occasio auferendi, specialiter incipiendo a fonte cupidinis producendi, quia non cessabunt cupiditatum litigia nisi ipse fuerit exsiccatus.

[242] But it is further rejoined that it would be better to correct the cupidity of the [clergy]{g:clerus} than to take away what was devoutly conferred,[^2] since these things can be possessed meritoriously, and the security and [plenty]{g:copia} of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} strengthens and increases the clerics; indeed they have this, since it is meritorious and justly sought by the [title]{g:titulus} of God's gift; so it would be a grave thing to disturb the [church]{g:ecclesia} with so great a withdrawal. Here it seems to me that the [evidence]{g:evidencia} does not go through, since it would be better for clerics to live as clerics generally, as regards all who are endowed whatever;[^3] so it would be best to correct the clergy's cupidity at the root, by taking away the poisonous occasion, so that the clergy, brought back to the perfection of its primeval [state]{g:status}, may "dash the little ones" of guilt "against the rock" of justice.[^4] This, then, would be a correction of the clergy's cupidity more fruitful, more lasting and more complete than a blasphemous extortion [cloaked]{g:palliare} in human fashion. For whoever would heal a man's wound or disease would destroy the root cause; if that were left, there would remain an open occasion of producing shoots of cupidity. Yet this pestilent root must be taken away prudently, when occasion of taking it away is given by a notorious sin, beginning especially from the fount of cupidity that is to be produced, for the lawsuits of cupidities will not cease unless that fount has been dried up.[^5]

## ¶3 · 242.31–246.21

> [p. 242] Sunt autem mille occasiones sanandi ecclesiam, sed cautela dyaboli sparsi sunt favores et pax, quam Christus venit dissolvere, et sic dona mamone [p. 243] sunt inter legistas et clericos eciam exproprietarios et dominos seminata. Sed oportet quod Deus gladio vindicte quem venit mittere modo sibi abscondito, ut pestilencia, guerra vel alia pena racionali dissipet istas fraudes; tunc enim foret clerus proporcionatus in numero et virtute, ubi iam nocivus est undique vivis et mortuis: vivis non faciendo clericale officium peccati populi destructivum, et mortuis non mitigando penam defunctorum quam ex stultis donariis paciuntur. Et ista argucia: Hoc bene potest fieri, igitur non debet corrigi, multos cecat; securitas autem debet confidenter exspectari a Deo non seculo, et in hoc debet sperari tam nobis quam laycis vera securitas, et ideo precipit nobis Apostolus Hebre, ultimo, quod sumus contenti presentibus, ita quod confidenter dicamus: Dominus michi adiutor et non timebo, quid faciat michi homo. Et hinc petimus in oracione dominica: panem nostrum cotidianum da nobis hodie. Sed et nos sacerdotes innitentes ritibus legis veteris confidenter annuatim sine thaurizacione diutina porcionem Domini scilicet oblaciones et decimas expectamus. Quantum ad dacionem Domini, patet quod in penam peccati dat indignis, quod eis obedit, ut dicit Augustinus in De Verbis Domini Sermone LIII: Deus, inquit, iratus dat creature ad eius voluntatem et alias placidus dat creature ad eius utilitatem, eciam quod non appetit habere. Triplex autem ponitur exemplum primi, nam [p. 244] Yob primo legitur quomodo Deus dedit dyabolo potestatem ad temptandum beatum Yob in sui confusionem et sancti probacionem, sic eciam demones exauditi sunt petendo se mitti in porcos Mar. V, et sic Israhelite exauditi sunt secundum suum desiderium, scilicit confusionem in escis carnalibus, ut patet Psalmus LXXVII, 30: Adhuc esce eorum erant in ore ipsorum et ira Dei ascendit super eos. Sed Apostolus exauditus est in contrario illius, quod peciit. Nam II. Cor. XII, 8 ita dicit: Ter Dominum rogavi, ut discederet a me angelus sathane, et dixit mihi: Sufficit tibi gracia mea, nam virtus in infirmitate perficitur. Sunt enim nonnulli qui ex carencia temptacionis superbiunt, et sunt nonnulli qui ex fortitudine temptacionis senciunt de se ipsis humilius et gloriosius coronantur; et ideo preter beatitudinem et alia per se bona debemus condicionaliter petere moderate, ut petendo temporalia debemus modum attendere; et condicionaliter petere, si et quatenus nobis prosunt. Et hec racio, quare petimus sub forma panis cottidiani temporalia necessaria hodie nobis dari, que Apostolus vocat presencia. Et constat, quod nimis levis est evidencia, Deus concedit eciam sanctis copiam temporalium et gloriam mundanam, igitur eis expedit vel est licitum sic habere, quamvis sint aliunde apud Deum laudabiles, verumtamen non in illo. Unde videtur quod nostri episcopi sunt Christo ingratiores quam Scarioth et legis veteris sacerdotes; ipsi enim tenuerunt se superficietenus, quoad possessiones in limitibus legis Dei, sed [p. 245] adulterati sunt abscondite in adulacione voti aut elemosine, ut patet Matth. XV; et ideo remansit penes eos palliata clavis sciencie; unde et Christus Joh. V, 39 dicit eos putare vitam eternam habere in scripturis. Et Matth. XXIII, 3: Omnia quecumque dixerint vobis, facite, quod non diceret hodie de nostris episcopis propter ingratiorem defectum sentencie, ideo videtur quod sunt plus debriati avaricia et ingratitudine ignorancie, sapiencie prime opposite, quam irreligiositas Scariothis. Melius utique foret semel ad sanitatem perfectam perturbare ecclesiam quam quod remaneat continue periculosius, irreligiosius et lacius perturbata. Ulterius notandum est tercio, quod necesse est paparum et episcoporum ordinem esse in ecclesia militante. Sed duplicem ordinem talium ministrorum considera, scilicet cesareum et divinum. Quamvis autem oporteat ex malicia ordinem cesareum quem Christus approbat secundum totum bonum virtutis in eo bonum utile vel nature, necesse tamen est in eo esse malum culpe quod Deus reprobat, et sic dat Deus tyrannis et blasfemis bona que occupant sine eorum vero dominio. Alium autem ordinem divinum oportet esse integre secundum ordinacionem divinam, cum non per se sapit peccatum ut prior. Ipse autem ex institucione humana capit gradus suos, secundum quod variantur gradatim mundi divicie vel honores. Alius autem ordo hec respuens respicit pure et mundo abscondite ordinacionem divinam ad plus vel minus prodessendum ecclesie; quando autem ordinacio humana concordat in persona quam Deus ad gradum talem elegerit, [p. 246] sapit minus peccatum et aliter colitur Antichristus. Unde nichil maius hereticum quam quod tam necessarius est iste ordo cesareus, ut nemo posset sine papa istius ordinis esse salvus, primo quod multis pseudopapis non existentibus, ut patet ex dictis, sancta duravit predestinatorum ecclesia, secundo quia trecentis annis et amplius, quibus sine dotacione crevit ecclesia, regulabatur sine tali primatu cesareo communi consilio sacerdotum. Pro quo tempore idem fuit sacerdos et episcopus, ut patet ex dictis. Tercio quod totus ordo talis fuit, institutus a cesare preter Christum, ut patet ex dictis VIII cap. Et nichil infidelius quam quod nemo potest modo salvari sine suo decreto tam extraordinario preter fidem. Sicut igitur conversi in Yndia et Grecia et aliis locis, ubi papatus noster non colitur, sunt christiani salvandi, eciam qui religiosius nobis vivunt: sic in Europa stat esse perfectos christianos secundum fidem Christi, etsi non cognoscant preeminenciam pontificis Constantini.

[242] And there are a thousand occasions of healing the church; but by the devil's [wile]{g:cautela} favours have been scattered, and the peace which Christ came to dissolve, and so the gifts of mammon [243] have been sown among [legists]{g:legiste} and clerics, even the [propertyless]{g:exproprietarii}, and lords.[^6] But God must, with the sword of vengeance which he came to send, in a way hidden with himself, as by pestilence, war or another reasonable penalty, scatter these frauds;[^7] for then the clergy would be proportioned in number and in [virtue]{g:virtus}, whereas now it is harmful on every side to the living and to the dead: to the living, by not doing the clerical office that destroys the people's sin; and to the dead, by not mitigating the punishment of the departed which they suffer for their foolish gifts.[^8] And this [quibble]{g:argucia}, "This can be well done, therefore it ought not to be corrected", blinds many. But security ought to be awaited confidently from God, not from the world, and in this true security is to be hoped for, by us as by the laity; and therefore the Apostle commands us (Hebrews, the last chapter) that we be content with present things, so that "we may confidently say: The Lord is my helper: [and] I will not fear what man shall do to me."[^9] And hence we ask in the Lord's prayer: "Give us this day our daily bread."[^10] And we priests too, relying on the rites of the old law, confidently await year by year, without long hoarding, the Lord's portion, namely offerings and tithes. As to the Lord's giving, it is clear that in punishment of sin he gives to the unworthy, in that he obeys them,[^11] as Augustine says in *On the Words of the Lord*, sermon 53: "God", he says, "when angry gives to a creature according to its will, and at other times, when pleased, gives to a creature according to its advantage, even what it does not desire to have."[^12] And a threefold example of the first is given: for [244] in Job 1 it is read how God gave the devil [power]{g:potestas} to tempt blessed Job, to the devil's confusion and the saint's proving; so also the demons were heard when they asked to be sent into the swine (Mark 5); and so the Israelites were heard according to their desire, namely to their confusion in fleshly foods, as is clear from Psalm 77:30: "As yet their meat was in their mouth: and the wrath of God came upon them."[^13] But the Apostle was heard in the contrary of what he asked. For in 2 Cor. 12:8 he says thus: "Thrice I besought the Lord that the angel of Satan might depart from me. And he said to me: My grace is sufficient for thee: for power is made perfect in infirmity."[^14] For there are some who grow proud from lack of temptation, and there are some who from the strength of temptation think more humbly of themselves and are more gloriously crowned; and therefore, apart from blessedness and the other goods in themselves, we ought to ask conditionally [and] moderately, so that in asking for temporal goods we ought to attend to measure, and to ask conditionally, if and in so far as they profit us.[^15] And this is the reason why we ask, under the form of daily bread, that the necessary temporal goods be given us today, which the Apostle calls "present things". And it is evident that this is too light an evidence: "God grants even to saints plenty of temporal goods and worldly glory, therefore it is expedient for them, or lawful, so to have them", although they are praiseworthy with God on other grounds; nevertheless not in that. Hence it seems that our [bishops]{g:episcopus} are more [ungrateful]{g:ingratus} to Christ than Iscariot and the priests of the old law;[^16] for these kept themselves outwardly, as to possessions, within the [limits]{g:limites} of God's law, but [245] were secretly adulterated in flattery of a vow or of alms, as is clear in Matt. 15; and therefore the key of knowledge remained with them, cloaked; hence Christ too (John 5:39) says that they think they have life everlasting in the scriptures.[^17] And Matt. 23:3: "All things whatsoever they shall say to you, do", which he would not say today of our bishops, because of the more displeasing defect of [teaching]{g:sentencia};[^18] therefore it seems that they are more drunk with avarice and with the [ingratitude]{g:ingratitudo} of ignorance, opposed to the first wisdom, than the [irreligion]{g:irreligiositas} of Iscariot [was].[^19] It would certainly be better to disturb the church once, to perfect health, than that it should remain continually disturbed, more dangerously, more irreligiously and more widely. Further, thirdly, it is to be noted that it is necessary that there be an order of popes and bishops in the [church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans}. But consider a twofold order of such ministers, namely the caesarean and the divine. Now although it is needful, out of malice, [that there be] the caesarean order, which Christ approves in respect of all the good of virtue in it, the useful good or [the good] of nature, yet it is necessary that there be in it the evil of fault, which God reproves; and so God gives to tyrants and blasphemers the goods which they occupy, without their true [lordship]{g:dominium}.[^20] But the other order, the divine, must be wholly according to the divine [ordinance]{g:ordinacio}, since it does not of itself savour of sin as the former does. And the former takes its grades from human institution, according as the riches or honours of the world vary by degrees.[^21] But the other order, spurning these things, looks purely, and hidden from the world, to the divine ordinance, for profiting the church more or less; and when the human ordinance agrees on a person whom God has chosen for such a grade, [246] it savours less of sin; and otherwise Antichrist is worshipped. Hence nothing is more heretical than [the claim] that this caesarean order is so necessary that no one could be saved without a [pope]{g:papa} of this order: first, because, with many [pseudo-popes]{g:pseudopapa} not being [popes], as is clear from what has been said, the holy church of the [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} endured;[^22] secondly, because for three hundred years and more, during which the church grew without [endowment]{g:dotacio}, it was ruled without such caesarean [primacy]{g:primatus}, by the common counsel of priests. For that time priest and bishop were the same, as is clear from what has been said. Thirdly, because the whole of such an order was instituted by Caesar, apart from Christ, as is clear from what was said in chapter 8. And nothing is more unbelieving than [the claim] that no one can now be saved without his decree, so extraordinary, beyond the faith.[^23] As, therefore, converts in India and Greece and other places, where our papacy is not honoured, are Christians to be saved, even those who live more religiously than we: so in Europe there may be perfect Christians according to the faith of Christ, even if they do not acknowledge the [preeminence]{g:preeminencia} of Constantine's pontiff.[^24]

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Consideraremus* (imperfect subjunctive) is hortatory, "we should consider". *Itaqae* is a misprint for *itaque*.

[^2]: **C/R.** *Quomodo* after *replicatur* is taken in its medieval use as "that", introducing the rejoinder (so Loserth's side-note at 242.6); the print's question mark would give "how would it be better …?", which inverts the sense. The manuscripts note in the margin *Dotacionis argumentum* (242.5) and *Ablacio temporalium* ("taking away of temporal goods", 242.8).

[^3]: **C.** *Generaliter quoad quoscumque dotatos*: construed "generally, with respect to all the endowed whoever they are", i.e. the remedy should apply to every endowed cleric. An alternative is to join *generaliter* to *clericos*: "for clerics in general to live as clerics".

[^4]: **S.** Ps. 136:9 (Loserth), DR "dash thy little ones against the rock"; Wyclif applies the verse allegorically, the little ones being the first stirrings of guilt.

[^5]: **C.** *A fonte cupidinis producendi*: literally "from the fount of cupidity to be produced", the source from which cupidity springs, i.e. the endowment itself. *Ipse* takes up *fons*.

[^6]: **S.** *Pax, quam Christus venit dissolvere*: after Matt. 10:34 (DR "I came not to send peace, but the sword"), to which the "sword of vengeance which he came to send" in the next sentence also alludes. "Mammon": Luke 16:9.

[^7]: **C.** *Modo sibi abscondito*: "in a way hidden with himself", i.e. known to God alone. *Pena racionali* is a penalty fitted by reason to the fault.

[^8]: **T/R.** 243.9 *destructivum*: Loserth's reading; the manuscripts read *destructum*. Margin of both manuscripts at 243.8: *Clerus quomodo nocet vivis et mortuis* ("how the clergy harms the living and the dead"). *Virtus* is taken as "virtue" beside *numerus*; "power" is possible.

[^9]: **S.** Heb. 13:5–6 (Loserth). Wyclif turns the Vulgate's *contenti praesentibus* (DR "contented with such things as you have") into a precept in the first person, *quod sumus contenti presentibus*, translated from his Latin; the rest is the Douay–Rheims (Wyclif's *et* before *non timebo* is supplied in brackets).

[^10]: **S.** Luke 11:3 (Loserth).

[^11]: **T/C.** 243.24 *dacionem*: B reads *dotacionem* "endowment". *Quod eis obedit*: "in that he obeys them", i.e. God grants the wish of the unworthy as a punishment; the margin of both manuscripts at 243.17: *Dat Deus indignis ad penam peccati* ("God gives to the unworthy for the punishment of sin"), and at 243.30, *Exaudiuntur mali a Deo sibi in penam* ("the wicked are heard by God to their own punishment").

[^12]: **S.** Augustine, *De verbis Domini* sermon 53, as Wyclif cites it; Loserth refers to Augustine's *Opera*, tom. V, col. 1378, where the words run *Aliquando Deus iratus dat quod petis et Deus propitius negat quod petis* ("sometimes God in anger gives what you ask, and God when gracious refuses what you ask"). Wyclif's wording is his own paraphrase (LEDGER P10g-1); the modern sermon number is to be checked (LEDGER P10g-2).

[^13]: **S/T.** Job 1:12; Mark 5:12–13 (Loserth gives Matt. 8:32); the quails, Num. 11:33; Ps. 77:30–31 in the Douay–Rheims wording. The manuscripts read *Psalm. LXXIX*; Loserth corrects to LXXVII. He notes the same three examples in Augustine's sermon (LEDGER P10g-3). *In sui confusionem*: *sui* is the devil.

[^14]: **S.** 2 Cor. 12:8–9. Wyclif writes *angelus sathane* (from v. 7) as the subject of *discederet*, where the Vulgate has no subject (DR "that it might depart from me"); the rest is the Douay–Rheims. The manuscripts divide *in firmitate*.

[^15]: **C.** *Debemus condicionaliter petere moderate*: the print sets two adverbs side by side; we join them with "[and]". The sentence may instead break after *petere*, making *moderate* begin the next clause ("moderately, so that …"); the sense is the same. *Per se bona* are goods in themselves, as against temporal goods, which are good only as they serve.

[^16]: **G/C.** *Christo ingratiores*: of persons toward someone, *ingratus* is "ungrateful", the bishops being set beside Iscariot; at 245.6 *ingratiorem defectum* is said of a thing and is "more displeasing".

[^17]: **S/C.** Matt. 15 is the passage on the Pharisees' vowed gift (15:5, *munus*), which lets a son escape the duty to his parents. *Adulacione* "flattery" is the print; *adulteracione*, matching *adulterati*, may have been meant (Latin doubt, not settled). "The key of knowledge" is from Luke 11:52 (DR). John 5:39 is given in indirect speech (DR "you think in them to have life everlasting"). *Re mansit* is read as *remansit*.

[^18]: **S.** Matt. 23:3; Wyclif omits the Vulgate's *ergo* and *servate et* (DR "All things therefore whatsoever they shall say to you, observe and do"), and his shorter text is translated.

[^19]: **C.** *Plus … quam irreligiositas Scariothis*: the comparison is between the bishops' state and Iscariot's irreligion, as the nominative shows: "than the irreligion of Iscariot [was]". *Sapiencie prime opposite* qualifies *ignorancie*: the ignorance opposed to the first wisdom.

[^20]: **T/C.** At 245.21 Loserth notes *Hic verbum deesse videtur* ("a word seems to be missing here"). The sentence lacks an infinitive after *oporteat*; we supply "[that there be]" (*esse*), in line with *necesse … est in eo esse* that follows. *Secundum totum bonum virtutis in eo bonum utile vel nature* is construed as a list of the goods Christ approves in the caesarean order: the good of virtue, the useful good, the good of nature. Margin of both manuscripts at 245.17: *Ordo duplex sacerdotum* ("the twofold order of priests").

[^21]: **C.** *Ipse* takes up *prior*, the caesarean order; *Alius autem ordo* in the next sentence is again the divine order.

[^22]: **C/R.** *Multis pseudopapis non existentibus*: an ablative absolute, literally "many pseudo-popes not existing [as popes]", i.e. while the see was held by men who were no true popes. The alternative "while many [true popes] did not exist" gives the same argument. Margin of both manuscripts at 246.2: *Sine papa potest homo salvari* ("without a pope a man can be saved").

[^23]: **C/G.** *Nichil infidelius*: the comparative of *infidelis* said of a claim, "more unbelieving", i.e. more contrary to faith. *Suo decreto*: the decree of the caesarean pope.

[^24]: **G.** *Pontificis Constantini*: "Constantine's pontiff", the pope as made by the Donation (cf. 237.6 *concessione Cesaris*). *Stat esse* is "it can be that there are".
