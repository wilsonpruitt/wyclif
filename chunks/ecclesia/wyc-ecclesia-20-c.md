# *De ecclesia*, chapter 20, part 3: the office of the valiant woman: the field, the girded loins, the lamp, the spindle, the open hand, the double garments (Prov. 31:16–21)

## ¶1 · 480.16–484.28

> [p. 480] Sed quia matrona prudens considerans quod cibaria vie non sufficiunt homini in eternum, ideo quinto subditur: Consideravit agrum et emit illum, de fructu manuum suarum plantavit vineam. Ager ille secundum Augustinum est regnum celorum in quo sunt fructus semper virentes, ut patet Apoc. ultimo, et Psalmo XLIX°, 11 dicitur: Pulchritudo agri mecum est. Illam autem patriam fidelis discursu fidei infuse considerat, et ex fide scripture pro levi tribulacionis momentanee ipsum sperat, et sic illud quod non videtur modo sensibiliter pondus eterne glorie operatur in nobis et voluntariam persecucionis pacienciam propter iusticiam que secundum vocem veritatis Matthei VI° est precium huius agri. Unde quid propter exemplum martirum et iustorum sancte vivencium et quid propter virtutes theologicas infusas desuper mater nostra in spe boni futuri plantavit vineam, hoc est, superiorem cleri ecclesiam que residuum agri letificet? Et hoc merito tocius ecclesie quod est fructus [p. 481] sue potencie promerendi. Et utinam non propter ocium sive demeritum vinea ista transeat in ortum olerum. Unde quia ager iste in racione colendi et possidendi requirit laborem sollicitum et ordinatum, ideo sexto subditur de muliere forti predicta quod accinxit fortitudine lumbos suos et roboravit brachium suum. Nam ancilla apparate serviens tanto sponso nedum stringit carnales concupiscencias zona precepti dominici, sed cuncta opera sua facit ad regulam inter habundanciam et defectum, ne spiritualiter adulteretur cum viro alio, sic quod ad omne opus agendum in agro dominico sit parata; et ad ista requiritur fortitudo resistendi et prevalendi cunctis spiritualibus inimicis. Et videtur posse intelligi in predicacione secundum causam per agrum predictum ipsum caput ecclesie, cuius participacione omnes predestinati sunt regnum celorum; qui licet sint secundum naturam sensibiles, racio tamen qua sunt ecclesia et in potencia beatificabiles est insensibilis. Ad reducendum potenciam istam ad actum requiritur labor assiduus, et illud exercicium fortificat potenciam operandi; et ideo signanter dicitur quod roboravit brachium suum, nec est ista exercitacio nimium tediosa. Ideo septimo subditur: Gustavit et vidit quia bona est negociacio eius; non extinguetur in nocte lucerna eius. Quibus, inquit Augustinus, est palatum quo gustatur sic indulcoratum terrestribus, ut videatur laborem pro beatitudine esse amarum eis, non est celum carum. Rectificat autem gustum anime consciencia bona, que si pungitur peccato amara videntur omnia quibus anima nutriretur. Que ergo dulciter [p. 482] delectatur in domino, non solum exponit exercicio laudis divine sua omnia sed se ipsam; talis enim interim gustat in actibus quam suavis est dominus, quousque infima dulcedo timentibus Deum abscondita sit illapsa. Nec potest obici quod hec mulier non vidit quomodo operandum sit propter noctis tenebras in qua viat, quia lucerna ardebit continue, dum semper habet oculum et affeccionem, sperando in domino, verbo domini consolacionem scripture et continuacionem beneficencie tamquam oleum continue largiente, et ex ista luce cum oleo devocionis exhilarata. Unde octavo subiungitur: Manum suam misit ad forcia et digiti eius apprehenderunt fusum; ubi ista bona matrona lanifica per manum et digitum specificat expressius quam supra applicacionem potencie et prudencie ad sedule operandum. Nam manus et digiti sunt propiores quam brachium actui operandi; et potest opus huius femine dici forte, quia collectim procedit ex viribus universalis ecclesie resistente toto corpore dyaboli cum eius omnibus instrumentis. Sed quantum ad sensum habendum de fuso dicit magnus doctor Augustinus quod non tacebit caritati nostre quod magister suus inspirando donaverit. Primo ergo supponit quod christianus scit bene quid fecerit in preteritum, sed ignorat quid aut qualiter faciet in futurum. Secundo supponitur quod pius christianus de futuro opere bono timet, de preterito gaudet, et ut perseveret in bonis operibus caute vigilat, ne forte negligens futurorum perdat preteritum, et sic habet firmam conscienciam de facto et non de fiendo. Tercio supponit quod in arte nendi lanifici involutum in colo nondum artificiatum est in forma fili, sed ab illo ad fusum trahitur, non econtra. [p. 483] Ex istis elicitur quod sancta mater ecclesia dum subtili operacione apprehendit opus suum preteritum ad sensum scripture, apprehendit digitis suis fusum, quia rem anteriorem posteriorat, ut contingit de nentibus; est tamen conneccio, quia in opere preterito debent firmari brachia, debet securari consciencia et fundari spes ad futura premia capienda: Quidquid, inquit, operaris tota firmitas animi sit ad fusum, quia quod pendet in colo ad fusum trahicitur, non econtra. Et secundum istam leccionem non sollicitaret fidelis nec aspiraret ad grandem statum seculi in futurum, sed instanter trahiceret opus meritorium in preteritum, confidens quod ut sic erit humilior ad futurum. Et istam sollicitudinem ad colum prohibet sponsus Matthei VI° capitulo. Jactemus ergo thesaurum meriti in memoriam sponsi nostri qui pendens in cruce noluit memoriam passionis in preteritum intranslate relinquere, quamvis Judei false dixerint: Si rex Israel est, descendat nunc de cruce et credimus ei Matthei XXVII°, 40. Et hinc conclusio sequitur pertinenter: Manum suam aperuit inopi et palmas suas extendit ad pauperem. Nam dicens beneficencie et communionis non oblivisci ex proposicione septima et sciens quod solum firma spes premii est de perfecta operacione plene preterita ex proposicione proxima dat operam pro ulteriori elemosina facienda. Sperans, inquit Augustinus, in domino sibi dicit: Da domine, quia dedi, dimitte, quia dimisi, fac mecum misericordiam, quia feci. Nam Jacobi IIª, 12 scribitur: Iudicium sine misericordia illi qui non fecit misericordiam. Habens, inquit, plenum sacellum apotecam vel orreum nendo salubriter a colo transferat ad fusum. Et multo magis operandum est instanter opus misericordie spirituale et notum ex textu quomodo ecclesia egenciori [p. 484] dat largius de eo quod indiget sed potestatem defensionis exercet in paupere. Ex istis decimo sequitur quod vir talis matrone non timebit domui sue a frigoribus nivis: omnes enim domestici eius vestiti sunt duplicibus. Quomodo, inquit Augustinus, timeret dominus de suis domesticis qui sunt membra talis matrone, cum Romanorum VIII°, 30 scribitur: Quos predestinavit ipsos vocavit et quos vocavit ipsos et iustificavit et quos iustificavit ipsos et glorificavit. Igitur nec Christus nec ecclesia timebit membris suis ex persecucione nivea in qua refrigescit caritas multorum (ut dicitur Matthei XXIV°, 12), cum omnes de domo ecclesie vestiti sunt duplicibus. Hoc vestimentum est preciosissimum, cum sit realiter ipse sponsus. Unde ad Galatos III°, 27 dicitur: Omnes qui in Christo baptizati estis Christum induistis. Illi ergo qui et predestinantur et virtuose superaddite operantur, digne laudantes Christum divinitus atque humanitus, sunt vestiti duplicibus. Heretici autem presciti qui baptizati sunt, habentes temporaneam graciam baptismalem, et post nude confitentur in Christo unam simplicem naturam (ut puta, solum divinitatem vel solum humanitatem, ut Fotiniani et Manichei) vestiti sunt tamquam servi ecclesie simplici vestimento, ne fingant se velle perseverare finaliter, si habuerint vestimentum. Sed mali secundum Augustinum vestiti sunt tantum forma sacramenti, boni vero opere et imitacione finali.

But since the prudent matron considers that the victuals of the [way]{g:via} do not suffice a man for eternity, therefore fifthly it is added: "She hath considered a field, and bought it: with the fruit of her hands she hath planted a vineyard."[^1] That field, according to Augustine, is the [kingdom]{g:regnum} of heaven, in which are fruits ever green, as is clear from the last [chapter of the] Apocalypse; and in Psalm 49:11 it is said: "With me is the beauty of the field." And the faithful [soul] considers that [homeland]{g:patria} by the discursive reasoning of infused faith, and [from the faith]{g:ex-fide} of scripture hopes for it in return for the light [burden] of a momentary tribulation; and so that which is not now seen [perceptibly]{g:sensibilis} works in us a weight of eternal glory, and [works in us] the willing patience of persecution for justice, which according to the voice of Truth, Matthew 6, is the price of this field.[^2] Hence why, both on account of the example of the martyrs and of the just who live holily, and on account of the theological [virtues]{g:virtus} infused from above, did [our mother]{g:mater-nostra}, in hope of the good to come, plant a vineyard, that is, the higher [church]{g:ecclesia} of the [clergy]{g:clerus}, to gladden the rest of the field? And this by the merit of the whole church, which is the fruit of [481] its power of meriting. And would that this vineyard may not, through idleness or demerit, pass into a garden of herbs.[^3]

Hence, since this field, in respect of tilling and possessing it, requires careful and ordered labour, therefore sixthly it is added of the aforesaid valiant woman that "she hath girded her loins with strength, and hath strengthened her arm." For a handmaid serving so great a [bridegroom]{g:sponsus} in readiness not only binds fleshly concupiscences with the girdle of the Lord's precept, but does all her works by the rule [that lies] between abundance and defect, lest she commit spiritual adultery with another man, so that she is ready for every work to be done in the Lord's field; and for these is required the strength to resist and prevail over all spiritual enemies.[^4] And it seems that by the aforesaid field can be understood, in predication by cause, the very [head]{g:caput} of the church, by participation in whom all the [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} are the kingdom of heaven; who, although according to nature they are perceptible, yet the aspect by which they are the church and potentially beatifiable is [imperceptible]{g:insensibilis}.[^5] To reduce this potency to act, assiduous labour is required, and that exercise strengthens the power of working; and therefore it is said expressly that "she hath strengthened her arm"; nor is this exercise too wearisome. Therefore seventhly it is added: "She hath tasted, and seen that her traffic is good: her lamp shall not be put out in the night." "Those," says Augustine, "who have a palate, by which one tastes, so sweetened by earthly things that labour for blessedness seems to be bitter to them: to them heaven is not dear."[^6] But a good conscience sets right the taste of the soul; and if [conscience] is pricked by sin, all the things by which the soul would be nourished seem bitter. The [soul], therefore, that sweetly [482] delights in the Lord exposes to the exercise of divine praise not only all her own things but herself; for such a one meanwhile tastes in [her] acts how sweet the Lord is, until the deepest sweetness, hidden for those that fear God, has flowed in.[^7] Nor can it be objected that this woman did not see how she was to work, because of the darkness of the night in which she makes her way; for the lamp will burn continually, while she always has [her] eye and affection [on it], hoping in the Lord, the word of the Lord continually bestowing, as oil, the consolation of scripture and the [continuity]{g:continuacio} of beneficence; and [she is] gladdened by this light with the oil of devotion.[^8]

Hence eighthly it is added: "She hath put out her hand to strong things, and her fingers have taken hold of the spindle"; where this good wool-working matron, by the hand and finger, specifies more expressly than above the application of power and prudence to working sedulously. For hands and fingers are nearer than the arm to the act of working; and the work of this woman can be called strong, since it proceeds collectively from the forces of the universal church, the whole body of the devil resisting with all its instruments.[^9] But as to the sense to be had concerning the spindle, the great doctor Augustine says that he will not keep silent from our [charity]{g:caritas} what his master has given [him] by inspiration. First, then, he supposes that a christian knows well what he has done in the past, but does not know what or how he will do in the future. Secondly it is supposed that the pious christian fears for the good work to come and rejoices over the past, and, that he may persevere in good works, watches cautiously, lest perhaps, neglecting the things to come, he lose the past; and so he has a firm conscience of what has been done, and not of what is to be done. Thirdly he supposes that in the art of spinning, what of the wool-work is wound on the distaff is not yet worked into the form of thread, but is drawn from it to the spindle, not the reverse. [483] From these it is drawn out that holy mother church, while by subtle working she takes hold of her past work according to the sense of scripture, takes hold of the spindle with her fingers, since she makes the earlier thing later, as happens with those who spin; yet there is a connection, since in past work the arms must be strengthened, the conscience made secure and hope founded for gaining the rewards to come: "Whatever you work," he says, "let the whole firmness of the mind be at the spindle, since what hangs on the distaff is passed to the spindle, not the reverse."[^10] And according to this lesson the faithful [man] would not be anxious nor aspire to a great state in the world in the future, but would earnestly pass meritorious work into the past, trusting that so he will be humbler for the future. And this solicitude for the distaff the bridegroom forbids in Matthew, chapter 6. Let us therefore cast the treasure of merit into the memory of our bridegroom, who, hanging on the cross, would not leave the memory of the passion untransferred into the past, although the Jews falsely said: "If he be the king of Israel, let him now come down from the cross: and we will believe him," Matthew 27:40.[^11] And hence the conclusion follows pertinently: "She hath opened her hand to the needy, and stretched out her hands to the poor." For [she], telling [herself] not to forget beneficence and communion, from the seventh proposition, and knowing, from the proposition next before, that the firm hope of reward is only of a perfect work fully past, applies herself to making further [alms]{g:elemosina}.[^12] "Hoping in the Lord," says Augustine, "she says to herself: 'Give, Lord, because I have given; forgive, because I have forgiven; deal mercifully with me, because I have done [mercy].'" For in James 2:12 it is written: "Judgment without mercy to him that hath not done mercy." "Let one who has," he says, "a full purse, storehouse or barn, spinning wholesomely, transfer from the distaff to the spindle." And much more is the spiritual work of mercy to be worked earnestly; and it is known from the text how the church gives more largely to the needier [484] of that which he needs, but exercises the power of defence in [the case of] the poor.[^13]

From these it follows tenthly that the husband of such a matron "shall not fear for [his] house in the cold of snow: for all [his] domestics are clothed with double garments." "How," says Augustine, "should the Lord fear for his domestics, who are [members]{g:membrum} of such a matron, since in Romans 8:30 it is written: 'Whom he predestinated, them he also called. And whom he called, them he also justified. And whom he justified, them he also glorified'?" Therefore neither Christ nor the church will fear for its members from the snowy persecution, in which "the charity of many shall grow cold" (as is said in Matthew 24:12), since all of the house of the church are clothed with double garments.[^14] This garment is most precious, since it is really the bridegroom himself. Hence in Galatians 3:27 it is said: "As many of you as have been baptized in Christ have put on Christ." Those, therefore, who are both predestined and work virtuously besides, worthily praising Christ divinely and humanly, are clothed with double garments. But [foreknown]{g:prescitus} heretics who are baptized, having a temporary baptismal grace, and afterwards barely confess in Christ one simple nature (for example, only the divinity or only the humanity, as the Photinians and Manichees), are clothed, as servants of the church, with a single garment, lest they pretend that they would will to persevere finally if they had had the garment.[^15] But the wicked, according to Augustine, are clothed only with the form of the sacrament, and the good with the work and final imitation.[^16]

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** Proverbs 31:16 (Wyclif *illum* for the Vulgate's *eum*). **C.** *Cibaria vie*: the "victuals" of verse 15 (480.13–14), food for the way, set against the field bought for eternity. **T.** AA₁ lack *matrona … ideo* (480.16). **R.** Side-note 480.16.

[^2]: **S.** Augustine, *Sermo* 37 on Proverbs 31 (LEDGER E20c-2). *Apoc. ultimo*: Revelation 22:2 (the tree of life "yielding its fruits every month"). Psalm 49:11 ("with me is the beauty of the field"). *Pro levi tribulacionis momentanee … pondus eterne glorie operatur in nobis*: 2 Corinthians 4:17 ("that which is at present momentary and light of our tribulation worketh for us … an eternal weight of glory"), worked into Wyclif's sentence. *Matthei VI°*: the codices read *Matth. V.* (Loserth, 480.29); the sense is Matthew 5:10 ("Blessed are they that suffer persecution for justice' sake: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven"). Translated as printed. **C.** *Operatur* governs two objects, *pondus* and *pacienciam*: the unseen glory works in us both the weight of glory and the patience that buys the field.

[^3]: **C.** *Quid … et quid* is taken as "why", introducing the printed question. It may instead be read as "both … and" (on account partly of the martyrs' example, partly of the virtues), with the sentence a statement. *Superiorem cleri ecclesiam*: the clergy as the vineyard planted within the field of the whole church. *Potencie promerendi*: the power of meriting; the "fruit of her hands" of the verse. **S.** *In ortum olerum*: 3 Kings 21:2, Achab's wish to turn Naboth's vineyard into "a garden of herbs". **T.** A reads *boni fuci* (480.32); B *vinea est* (481.2).

[^4]: **S.** Proverbs 31:17. From here Loserth notes that what follows is in Augustine's *Sermo* 37 on Proverbs 31, with some words changed (481.10) (LEDGER E20c-1). **C.** *Apparate*: in readiness. *Ad regulam inter habundanciam et defectum*: the rule of the mean, between excess and defect. *Zona precepti dominici*: the girdle of the verse read as the Lord's precept. **T.** B reads *super habundanciam* (481.10). **R.** Side-note 481.6.

[^5]: **C.** *In predicacione secundum causam*: predication by cause, in which a thing is named by its cause; so the field, which is the kingdom, can be said to be Christ the head, by participation in whom the predestinate are that kingdom. *Racio … qua sunt ecclesia*: the aspect under which they are the church, set against their perceptible nature. **T.** The codices read *per dictum* (481.15); B *verificabiles* for *beatificabiles* (481.19).

[^6]: **S.** Proverbs 31:18. Augustine, the same sermon, 37.10 (*Ubi est palatum, quod gustatur hoc?*); *non est celum carum* is Wyclif's (LEDGER E20c-3). **C.** *Quibus … est palatum … non est celum carum*: the relative *quibus* is resumed by *eis*; "to those whose palate … to them heaven is not dear." **R.** Side-note 481.24.

[^7]: **S.** *Gustat … quam suavis est dominus*: Psalm 33:9 ("O taste, and see that the Lord is sweet"). *Dulcedo timentibus Deum abscondita*: Psalm 30:20 ("the multitude of thy sweetness, O Lord, which thou hast hidden for them that fear thee"). **T.** The codices read *se ipsum*; *se ipsam* is Loserth's (482.2). **C.** *Que ergo*: the soul (*anima*). *Infima dulcedo*: as printed, "the lowest", that is, deepest, sweetness; *intima*, "inmost", would read more easily. *Illapsa*: flowed in, the word Wyclif uses of Christ flowing into the soul.

[^8]: **C.** *In qua viat*: in which she makes her way, as a wayfarer. *Verbo domini … largiente* is an ablative absolute: the word of the Lord supplies scripture's consolation and continued beneficence as the oil of the lamp. *Habet oculum et affeccionem*: keeps eye and affection fixed. **T.** B reads *inde qua viat* (482.7); AA₁ lack *oleum* (482.10).

[^9]: **S.** Proverbs 31:19. **T.** The codices read *Ut octavo* (482.11); A has the letter K in the lower margin (one of the Hebrew letters it sets against the verses, cf. 483.1). **C.** *Resistente toto corpore dyaboli* is printed as an ablative absolute, "the whole body of the devil resisting"; the sense may be that the church's forces resist the devil's body. **R.** Side-note 482.12.

[^10]: **S.** Augustine, the same sermon: the three suppositions and *Quidquid operaris … non econtra* (LEDGER E20c-4). **C.** *Caritati nostre*: the preacher's address to his hearers, "your charity", here in the first person plural. *De facto … de fiendo*: of what has been done, not of what is to be done. *Lanifici*: genitive of *lanificium*, wool-work. *Rem anteriorem posteriorat*: on the spindle the earlier work is put behind, as spun thread passes from distaff to spindle. *Trahicitur* (and *trahiceret* below): *traicitur*, is passed over. **R.** A in the margin at 482.23: *Triplex supposicio*. **T.** B reads *supponit quod pius* (482.24); A lacks the letter L (483.1).

[^11]: **S.** Matthew 6:34 ("Be not therefore solicitous for to morrow"). The words of the Jews are Matthew 27:42; Loserth's verse 40 is "If thou be the Son of God, come down from the cross." **C.** *Noluit memoriam passionis … intranslate relinquere*: Christ would not leave his passion unfinished, still on the distaff, but carried it through into the past. **T.** B reads *dixerunt* (483.18).

[^12]: **S.** Proverbs 31:20. *Beneficencie et communionis non oblivisci*: Hebrews 13:16 ("do not forget to do good and to impart"). **C.** *Ex proposicione septima*: verse 18, the lamp fed with the oil of beneficence (482.6–9); *ex proposicione proxima*: verse 19, the spindle. **R.** A in the margin at 483.20: *Conclusio*. Side-note 483.20.

[^13]: **S.** Augustine, the same sermon: *Da domine, quia dedi …* (LEDGER E20c-5) and *Habens plenum sacellum …* (LEDGER E20c-6). James 2:13 ("For judgment without mercy to him that hath not done mercy"); Loserth's verse is 12. **C.** The last sentence sets the two halves of the verse apart: to the needy (*inopi*) the church gives of what he lacks; for the poor (*pauperi*) she exercises the power of defence. **R.** B in the margin at 483.34: *Nota*.

[^14]: **S.** Proverbs 31:21: the Vulgate's subject is the woman; Wyclif makes it her husband. Augustine, Sermo 37.15, rephrased (LEDGER E20c-7); the print's italics run through *glorificavit*, so Romans 8:30 is inside the quotation. Romans 8:30 (Wyclif without *autem*); Matthew 24:12 (Wyclif *refrigescit* for *refrigescet*). **T.** B lacks *decimo* (484.3). **R.** Side-note 484.3.

[^15]: **S.** Galatians 3:27 (Wyclif *Omnes qui* for *Quicumque enim*). The Photinians held Christ to be man only, the Manichees to have had no true human nature, as in Sermo 37.17 (LEDGER E20c-8). **C.** *Divinitus atque humanitus*: as God and as man; the double garment is faith in both natures, with predestination. *Ne fingant … si habuerint vestimentum*: the foreknown receive only the single garment, so that they cannot claim they would have persevered had they had the double one. **T.** A reads *Fetiniani* (484.23).

[^16]: **S.** Augustine, Sermo 37.16: the good servants have put on Christ *non tantum in forma Sacramenti, sed etiam in opere exempli*, the others *usque ad Sacramentum* (LEDGER E20c-9). **C.** *Opere et imitacione finali*: by the deed and by imitation to the end.
