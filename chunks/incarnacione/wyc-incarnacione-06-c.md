# *De benedicta incarnacione*, chapter 6, part 3: that Christ is movable and a creature: scripture and the saints

## ¶1 · 92.17–94.22

> [p. 92] Ideo dicunt sic opinantes consequenter, sed minus catholice, quod Christus non est mobilis vel accidentatus humanitus. Quod si verum est, tunc indubie non est homo in aliquo fratribus suis consimilis. Quomodo, rogo, haberet fratres, si non sit univoce filius hominis habens eundem patrem et matrem remotos cum filiis⁷ aliis, et sic temporaliter conceptus, genitus, atque natus, et sic consequenter, ut canit historia evangelica, conversatus? aut quomodo esset nedum in substancia vel natura vel in aliquo accidente fratribus suis consimilis, si nec⁸ in genere,⁸ nec in specie, nec in accidente univoco convenirent⁹? Pro certo dicat qui audet! quia ego non audeo tantum subvertere totum sensum historicum scripturarum, cum evangelium sicut et vetus testamentum expresse et absolute attribuit¹⁰ sibi denominaciones accidentales creatas. Quomodo, queso, crederemus¹¹ aliquid de Christo, si non¹² assercionem propriam, [p. 93] qua dicit, ‘palpate et videte, quoniam spiritus carnem et ossa non habet, sicut me videtis habere.’ Ioh. ult°.? ex qua¹ doctrina hauserat ille celicus paranymphus,² quando in principio capituli sui dicit, ‘Quod fuit ab inicio, quod audivimus, quod vidimus oculis nostris,³ quod perspeximus, et manus nostre contrectaverunt de Verbo vite, anunciamus vobis’? Et Apostolus ad Hebre. 4°. ‘habentes pontificem magnum, qui penetravit celos Iesum Filium Dei, teneamus spei nostre confessionem:⁴ non enim habemus pontificem, qui non possit compati⁵ infirmitatibus nostris; temptatum autem per omnia pro similitudine absque peccato.’ Quod dictum Apostoli ego nescio aliter exponere quam expositum est a sanctis doctoribus. Per hoc ergo quod Iesus est filius Dei, est ‘pontifex magnus⁶ superior Aaronitis.’⁶ Per hoc quod potestate propria ‘penetravit celos,’ solus ascendit⁷ trahens alios secum. Per hoc quod potest compati nobis in natura communi assumpta, factus est nobis propicius; quia ad Hebre. 5°. dicitur ‘cum esset Filius Dei, didicit ex his, que passus est, obedienciam.’ Experiencia enim temptacionis multimodis⁸ didicit⁹ humanitus, que aliter non sic sciret.¹⁰ Temptatus¹¹ enim erat tripliciter a dyabolo, ut patet Matth 4°; et quadrupliciter¹² a Iudeis de potencia, sapiencia, misericordia, et iusticia, ut patet ex decursu evangelii. Non autem intrinsece a seipso. Quarto fuit illa temptacio nobis pro similitudine exemplari ad docendum nos gratis compati et vincere temptatores,¹³ quia capitaneus apostolorum 1 Pet. 2°. vere testatur quod ‘Christus passus est pro nobis,¹⁴ vobis relinquens exemplum ut sequamini¹⁴ vestigia eius.’ Ista est exposicio sana, cui oportet nos insistere non superaddendo aliam¹⁵ pre timore impugnacionis sophistice de vi vocis. Non quod fuit in capite nostro¹⁶ ficta passio et non [p. 94] vera, ut dixerunt heretici supradicti; sed veram conversacionem, veram passionem, veram mortem, et alias predicaciones sustinuit; ut meminit Augustinus esse de substancia fidei in De Vera Religione 29°. et Enchiridion 4°¹ ca°. ‘Nos, inquit,² patimur egencias et dolores, sed³ indubie magis ipse; nam “pro nobis egenus factus est.”’ 2 Cor. 8°. Et cum in perfecta etate, tenerrima complexione,⁴ sensu vivacissimo,⁵ non mortificato aliquo membro, in media etate, non supernatante humido⁶ infantili⁷ sit passus, patet quod in nullo alio est pena vel ‘dolor tantus sicut fuerat dolor suus,’ ut cecinit Isa. 53°. Secundo fratres sui fossi sunt sub terra vel lapide, sed ipse plus, cum Dominus mundi sepultus est sumptibus alienis, ut meminit Augustinus in De Questionibus Veteris et Nove Legis ca° 59:⁸ et, ut breviter dicatur, quicquid debuit facere fecit ad summum, cum oporteat⁹ principia exemplaria esse ultima⁹; et eo fecit hec omnia excellencius, quo ipse fuit humanitas copulata ypostatice deitati. Cum ergo omnes ille maneries accidencium creatorum infuerunt sibi formaliter, et omne tale accidens requirit subiectum sibi proporcionatum; sequitur quod Christus subiectus eis sit idem in specie cum subiectis accidencium consimilis speciei.

Therefore those who so opine say, consistently but less catholicly, that Christ is not [movable]{g:mobilis} or [subject to accidents]{g:accidentatus} [humanly]{g:humanitus}. And if this is true, then without doubt he is not a man like his brethren in anything. How, I ask, would he have brethren, if he is not [univocally]{g:univoce} a son of man, having the same remote father and mother with the other sons, and so conceived, begotten, and born in time, and so, consequently, as the gospel history sings, living his life among men? Or how would he be like his brethren [not only]{g:nedum} in substance or [nature]{g:natura} or in some accident, if they agreed neither in genus, nor in species, nor in a univocal accident? Let him who dares say it for certain! For I do not dare so far to overturn the whole historical sense of the scriptures, since the gospel, as also the old testament, expressly and absolutely attributes to him created accidental [denominations]{g:denominacio}. How, I beg, would we believe anything of Christ, if not his own assertion, [93] in which he says: "Handle, and see: for a spirit hath not flesh and bones, as you see me to have", John, the last chapter?[^1] From which teaching that heavenly [paranymph]{g:paranymphus} had drawn, when at the beginning of his chapter he says: "That which was from the beginning, which we have heard, which we have seen with our eyes, which we have looked upon and our hands have handled, of the word of life, we declare unto you"?[^2] And the Apostle, Hebrews 4: "Having a great high priest that hath passed into the heavens, Jesus the Son of God: let us hold fast the confession of our hope. For we have not a high priest who cannot have compassion on our infirmities: but one tempted in all things like as we are, without sin."[^3] That saying of the Apostle I do not know how to expound otherwise than it has been expounded by the holy doctors. By this, then, that Jesus is the Son of God, he is "a great high priest, higher than the Aaronites".[^4] By this, that by his own power he "passed into the heavens", he alone ascended, drawing others with him. By this, that he can have compassion on us in the common nature [assumed]{g:assumere}, he has become propitious to us; for in Hebrews 5 it is said: "whereas he was the Son of God, he learned obedience by the things which he suffered." For by experience of temptation, in its multitude, he learned humanly what he would not otherwise know in that way.[^5] For he was tempted in three ways by the devil, as is clear from Matthew 4; and in four ways by the Jews, as to power, wisdom, mercy, and justice, as is clear from the course of the gospel. But not inwardly, from himself. Fourth, that temptation was "like as we are" for us, as an exemplary likeness, to teach us freely to have compassion and to conquer the tempters,[^6] for the [captain]{g:capitaneus} of the apostles, 1 Peter 2, truly testifies that "Christ suffered for us, leaving you an example that you should follow his steps." That is the sound exposition, on which we must insist, not adding another for fear of a [sophistical]{g:sophistice} attack [by the force of the words]{g:de-vi-vocis}. Not that there was in the head a feigned passion and not [94] a true one, as the heretics spoken of above said;[^7] but he underwent a true [manner of life]{g:conversacio}, a true passion, a true death, and the other [predications]{g:predicacio}; as Augustine recalls is of the substance of the faith, in *De vera religione* 29 and *Enchiridion*, chapter 4: "We," he says, "suffer needs and pains, but without doubt he more; for 'for us he became needy.'" 2 Corinthians 8.[^8] And since he suffered in perfect age, of most delicate complexion, with the liveliest sense, with no member mortified, in the middle of life, with no infant moisture floating above,[^9] it is clear that in no other is there pain or "sorrow so great as his sorrow had been", as Isaiah 53 sang.[^10] Second, his brethren were buried under earth or stone, but he more, since the Lord of the world was buried at the expense of others, as Augustine recalls in *De quaestionibus Veteris et Novi Testamenti*, chapter 59;[^11] and, to say it briefly, whatever he ought to do he did to the utmost, since exemplary principles must be extremes; and he did all these things the more excellently in that he was the [humanity]{g:humanitas} joined [hypostatically]{g:ypostatice} to the [Godhead]{g:deitas}. Since, then, all those [kinds]{g:maneries} of created accidents were in him formally, and every such accident requires a subject proportioned to it, [it follows]{g:sequitur} that Christ, the [subject]{g:subiectum} of them, is the same in species with the subjects of accidents of like species.

## ¶2 · 94.23–94.33

> [p. 94] Sed arguendo contra illud videtur primo tripliciter quod non debet concedi simpliciter quod Christus sit mobilis. Nam¹⁰ non foret evidencia concludendi quod Christus sit mobilis,¹⁰ nisi ex hoc quod secundum humanitatem est mobilis. Sed talis deduccio non procedit, cum arguitur a¹¹ secundum quid ad¹¹ simpliciter. Ergo non est medium concludendi¹² quod Christus sit mobilis. Sicut ergo non sequitur—Christus secundum humanitatem est creatura; ergo Christus est creatura—; ita non sequitur—Christus secundum humanitatem est mobilis; ergo Christus est mobilis.

But arguing against that, it seems first, in three ways, that it ought not to be granted [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} that Christ is movable. For there would be no [evidence]{g:evidencia} for [inferring]{g:concludere} "Christ is movable" except from this, that [as to the humanity]{g:secundum-humanitatem} he is movable. Now such a deduction does not proceed, since it is argued from "[in a certain respect]{g:secundum-quid}" to "without qualification". Therefore there is no [middle term]{g:medium} for inferring "Christ is movable".[^12] As, then, it does not follow, "Christ as to the humanity is a [creature]{g:creatura}; therefore Christ is a creature", so it does not follow, "Christ as to the humanity is movable; therefore Christ is movable".

## ¶3 · 95.1–95.23

> [p. 95] Hic dicitur concedendo quod Christus est mobilis cum humanitas sua sit mobilis. Et dicant, qui sciunt, quia pro certo non puto aliam creaturam, sed neque Deum cognoscere quod Deus sit mobilis, nisi ex hoc quod creatura est mobilis que Deo ypostatice copulatur. Et per locum a sufficienti similitudine concedi debet simpliciter quod Christus est creatura, quia tota humanitas eius sibi copulata ypostatice est creatura.¹ Sicut enim negacio includitur in racione creature, que negacio non potest competere divinitati; sic in motu et qualibet forma substanciali vel accidentali in genere: et per consequens, negato quod Christus est creatura, negari debet quodlibet per se inferius; ut, quod est homo, animal, corpus, anima, sive substancia, et multo evidencius quod sit mole magnus, accidentaliter² qualis, filius hominis, predicans,³ passus, locatus, temporaliter natus, sedens, vestitus; quia quodlibet istorum⁴ est inferius, indignius, et humilius⁵ quam esse substanciam, et⁶ presupponit substanciam tanquam subiectum dans eis esse accidentale in genere. Omne itaque per se in genere dicit esse suum possibile esse pro aliqua mensura, pro qua non habet existenciam actualem; et per consequens omne per se in genere dicit se esse creaturam includendo neminem esse primitatis divine.

[95] [The answer is]{g:dicitur} here, by granting "Christ is movable", since his humanity is movable. And let those who know say it, for I certainly do not think that any other creature, nor even God, knows "God is movable" except from this, that a creature which is joined hypostatically to God is movable. And by the [topic from sufficient likeness]{g:locus-a-sufficienti-similitudine} it ought to be granted without qualification that Christ is a creature, since his whole humanity joined to him hypostatically is a creature. For just as a negation is included in the account of a creature, which negation cannot belong to the [divinity]{g:divinitas}, so [it is] in motion and in any substantial or accidental form in a genus: and consequently, if "Christ is a creature" is denied, anything [of itself]{g:per-se} inferior ought to be denied; as, that he is a man, an animal, a body, a soul, or a substance, and much more evidently that he is great in bulk, accidentally qualified, the son of man, preaching, having suffered, located, born in time, sitting, clothed; for each of these is inferior, less worthy, and humbler than being a substance, but presupposes substance as a subject giving them accidental being in a genus.[^13] So everything of itself in a genus [signifies]{g:dicere} its being to be possible being for some measure for which it does not have actual existence; and consequently everything of itself in a genus signifies itself to be a creature, by including that none of them is of the divine [firstness]{g:primitas}.[^14]

## ¶4 · 95.24–96.15

> [p. 95] Unde scriptura sacra⁷ anunciat Christum simpliciter esse factum, ut ad Gal. 4°. ‘misit Deus Filium suum factum ex muliere, factum sub lege.’ ‘Non⁸ sunt audiendi, inquit Beda, qui legunt “natum ex muliere”’⁹ Et credo quod nullus dicit hoc determinatum ‘ex muliere’ esse terminum distrahentem quin sequatur—factus ex muliere; ergo factus—; sicut sequitur—compositus ex corpore et anima; ergo compositus—. Et ideo dicit Augustinus Enchiridion 29, quod illam creaturam, quam virgo concepit et peperit, quamvis ad solam personam Filii pertinentem, tota Trinitas [p. 96] fecit; et illam creaturam declarat esse Filium Dei tempore¹ immediate sequente. Et idem dicit in Sermone de Vigilia Epiphanie; ‘magnum misterium fuit, quod creator creari voluit’: et in Sermone Domini in Monte; ‘voluit esse creatura, qui est creator.’ Item ad Hebre. 3°. scribit Apostolus: ‘considerate apostolum et pontificem confessionis nostre² Iesum, qui fidelis est ei qui fecit illum.’ Ecce hic dicit absolute quod Deus fecit Iesum. Unde Leo Papa in quodam sermone; ‘nova et inaudita convencio, Deus qui eternaliter erat, fit creatura.’ Et Lincolniensis in sermone De Natali Domini; ‘in spectaculo³ creacionis⁴ conspicitur creator faciens creaturam; in isto spectaculo³ conspicitur creator factus creatura.’ Et Johannes Damascenus ca°. 50. ‘Christus est creatus et increatus:’ unde et ca°. 92 concedit quod Christus est creatura.

Hence holy scripture announces without qualification that Christ was made, as Galatians 4: "God sent his Son, made of a woman, made under the law." "They are not to be heard," says Bede, "who read 'born of a woman', but 'made of a woman'."[^15] And I believe that no one says that this [determination]{g:determinacio} "of a woman" is a [diminishing]{g:distrahens} term, so that it would not follow, "made of a woman; therefore made", just as it follows, "composed of body and soul; therefore composed". And therefore Augustine says, *Enchiridion* 29, that that creature which the virgin conceived and brought forth, though belonging to the [person]{g:persona} of the Son alone, the whole Trinity [96] made;[^16] and he [makes clear]{g:declarare} that that creature is the Son of God in the time following [without intermediary]{g:immediate}.[^17] And he says the same in the Sermon on the Vigil of the Epiphany: "It was a great [mystery]{g:misterium} that the [creator]{g:creator} willed to be created"; and in the Lord's Sermon on the Mount: "He who is creator willed to be a creature."[^18] Again, in Hebrews 3 the Apostle writes: "consider the apostle and high priest of our confession, Jesus: who is faithful to him that made him."[^19] Behold, here he says absolutely that God made Jesus. Hence Pope Leo, in a certain sermon: "A new and unheard-of coming-together: God, who was eternally, becomes a creature." And [Lincoln]{g:lincolniensis}, in the sermon On the Nativity of the Lord: "In the spectacle of creation the creator is seen making the creature; in this spectacle the creator is seen made a creature." And John Damascene, chapter 50: "Christ is created and uncreated"; hence also in chapter 92 he grants "Christ is a creature".[^20]

## ¶5 · 96.16–96.29

> [p. 96] Item nichil habet Deum ut superiorem nisi creatura: Christus sic habet:⁵ ergo est creatura. Minor⁶ patet ex confessione Veritatis Matth. 27. ‘Deus meus’; et Ioh. 20. ‘vade ad fratres meos et dic eis: Ascendo ad Patrem meum et Patrem vestrum,⁷ Deum meum et Deum vestrum’⁷; et ad Ephes. 1°. ‘Deus Domini nostri Iesu Christi, Pater glorie, det vobis spiritum sapiencie’; et 1ᵃ ad Cor. 3°. ‘vos⁸ estis Christi, Christus autem Dei’; et infra 11°, ‘caput vero Christi Deus.’ Et ad idem sonat tota scriptura de humanitate Christi; et specialiter illud Veritatis ‘Pater maior me est.’ Ioh. 14⁹; et illud ‘seipsum exinanivit, formam servi accipiens’ ad Phil. 2, ut exponit Augustinus primo libro Contra Maximinianum. Non enim audebant¹⁰ sancti doctores inficere scripturam suspicione falsitatis, ut hodie.

Again, nothing has God as superior except a creature: Christ so has [him]: therefore he is a creature. The minor is clear from the confession of the Truth, Matthew 27: "My God"; and John 20: "go to my brethren and say to them: I ascend to my Father and to your Father, to my God and to your God"; and Ephesians 1: "the God of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of glory, give unto you the spirit of wisdom"; and 1 Corinthians 3: "you are Christ's. And Christ is God's"; and below, 11: "the head of Christ is God." And the whole of scripture concerning the humanity of Christ sounds to the same effect; and especially that [saying] of the Truth, "the Father is greater than I", John 14; and that [saying], "[emptied]{g:exinanire} himself, taking the form of a servant", Philippians 2, as Augustine expounds it in the first book Against Maximinianus.[^21] For the holy doctors did not dare to taint scripture with suspicion of falsehood, as [men do] today.

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** The words are Luke 24:39 (Vulgate *quia spiritus*; Wyclif *quoniam*), not John; Wyclif's "John, the last chapter" may have the touching of Thomas in view (John 20:27).

[^2]: **S.** 1 John 1:1, with "we declare unto you" from 1:3 (DR). The *paranymphus* (the bridegroom's friend) is here John the Evangelist; "his chapter" is the opening of the epistle.

[^3]: **S.** Heb. 4:14–15. The Vulgate has *teneamus confessionem* (DR "let us hold fast our confession"); *spei nostre* comes from Heb. 10:23 (DR "Let us hold fast the confession of our hope"). A B C lack *confessionem* (93.9).

[^4]: **S.** Not a Vulgate text but the exposition's own phrase, with Heb. 7:11 (the priest "not … called according to the order of Aaron") in view; C lacks *superior* and O *Aaronitis*, each leaving a gap (93.14).

[^5]: **T.** *Multitudine* "in its multitude" (93.20), with A B C and O (*multi^do*), against the print's *multimodis* "in manifold ways", which no manuscript reported at the foot gives. For *didicit* and *sciret* A B C have *dicit* and *foret*; the print follows O.

[^6]: **C.** *Pro similitudine* is Heb. 4:15's phrase (DR "like as we are"), now expounded as a likeness given us for an example; "fourth" continues the three heads of the verse (Son of God, passed into the heavens, compassion).

[^7]: **T.** *In capite* "in the head" (93.31), with A B C O, against the print's *in capite nostro* "in our head", which Harris inserted from M.

[^8]: **S.** Augustine, *De vera religione* 29 and *Enchiridion* 4 (O "40"), as Wyclif cites them. *De vera religione* 29 is 16.30–31, PL 34, 134–135: *in vero homine apparuit … pauper esse voluit … flagellatus atque cruciatus est … morte multatus est* (*verified*, LEDGER I06c-1). The print's *Enchiridion* 4 fits *Ench.* 4–5, PL 40, 232–233: *certum propriumque fidei catholicae fundamentum Christus est*, which the heretics have *nomine tenus* but not *re ipsa*. O's 40, on the grace of union, does not fit. The quoted words *Nos patimur egencias et dolores …* are not in the *Enchiridion*. Perhaps they are Wyclif's summary of *De vera religione* 16.31 (*untraced*, LEDGER I06c-2). The inner text is 2 Cor. 8:9, freely: Vulgate *propter vos egenus factus est* (DR "he became poor for your sakes").

[^9]: **C.** *Humidum infantile*: the physicians' moisture of infancy (cf. *humidum radicale*, "radical moisture"), abundant in the child; Christ suffered in the middle of life, when it no longer abounds.

[^10]: **S.** The words echo Lam. 1:12 (Vulgate *si est dolor sicut dolor meus*, DR "if there be any sorrow like to my sorrow"), not Isaiah 53, which speaks of the servant's sorrows (53:3–4).

[^11]: **S.** Ps.-Augustine (Ambrosiaster), *Quaestiones Veteris et Novi Testamenti*, as cited: c. 59 (O "79") (LEDGER I06c-3). The place is q. 75, PL 35, 2269: *nec enim Salvator aliquid possidebat in mundo, cum sit dominus mundi. Mortuus enim alienis impendiis sepelitur* (*verified*). Neither number is right, but O's 79 is nearer than the print's 59.

[^12]: **T.** The first premiss, "For there would be no evidence … is movable" (94.25–26), is lacking in A B C; the print has it from O. At 94.28 O reads *a quo ad simpliciter* for *a secundum quid ad simpliciter* (A B C, print); cf. 98.12–13, where the print keeps *a quo*.

[^13]: **T.** *Sed* "but" (95.17), with C O, against the print's *et* "and". For *humilius* "humbler" (95.17) A B C read *minus* "less", O *vmis*.

[^14]: **C.** *Dicit*: signifies (the logical use). Whatever falls in a category signifies a being that is possible for some time at which it is not actual, and so signifies a creature; *neminem*, "none", excludes it from the divine firstness.

[^15]: **S/T.** The words "but 'made of a woman'" (95.27, *sed factum ex muliere*) are added with C O against the print, which ends the quotation at *natum ex muliere*. Bede, on Gal. 4:4 as it stands in his exposition of Luke 11:27, *In Lucam* IV, PL 92, 480A (also *Hom.* on the same text, PL 94, 421D): *Neque enim audiendi sunt qui legendum putant, natum ex muliere, factum sub lege, sed, factum ex muliere*, which bears out C O (*verified*, LEDGER I06c-4).

[^16]: **S.** Augustine, *Enchiridion* 29 as cited (in the modern division c. 38), PL 40, 251: *illam creaturam quam Virgo concepit et peperit, quamvis ad solam personam Filii pertinentem, tota Trinitas fecerit* (*verified*, LEDGER I06c-5).

[^17]: **C.** *Tempore immediate sequente* "in the time following without intermediary" (96.1): the creature was the Son of God from the first moment of its being. O lacks *tempore*, which would give "he makes clear immediately after", of the next words in Augustine's text.

[^18]: **S.** Augustine, a sermon on the vigil of the Epiphany (not found among Augustine's sermons in the PL; *untraced*, LEDGER I06c-6), and *De sermone Domini in monte*. The line is not there, as Harris also found. Wyclif has it, with the attribution, from Scotus, *Ord.* III d. 11 q. 1 (Wadding VII/1, 240), where it is one of the authorities for the opinion Scotus reports: *per August. de sermone Domini in monte: voluit esse creatura, qui est creator* (*untraced* in Augustine, LEDGER I06c-7).

[^19]: **T.** C O read *confessionis vestre* "of your confession" (96.6); the print keeps *nostre* with A B and the Vulgate (Heb. 3:1).

[^20]: **S.** not Leo but Ps.-Augustine, *Sermo* 128.2, PL 39, 1998: *o nova et inaudita commixtio! Deus qui est et qui erat, fit; creator, creatura*. Wyclif has it, under Leo's name and with *conventio*, from Bonaventure, *In III Sent.* d. 11 a. 2 q. 1 arg. 1 (Quaracchi III, 249), whose editors identify it (*verified*, LEDGER I06c-8); Robert Grosseteste, bishop of Lincoln, a sermon on the Nativity, not traced (Grosseteste's sermons are largely unedited; LEDGER I06c-9); John Damascene, *De fide orthodoxa*, cc. 50 and 92 in Wyclif's (Latin) numbering. These are III.4 (Christ is called *created and uncreated*, as Harris notes) and IV.18 (*God the Word … is called creature*) in the Greek division (LEDGER I06c-10, I06c-11). Wyclif has both, with *creatus et increatus*, from Scotus, *Ord.* III d. 11 q. 1 (Wadding VII/1, 240), whose print reads *cap. 30* and *c. 97*.

[^21]: **S.** Augustine on Phil. 2:7 in "the first book Against Maximinianus", which is the *Collatio cum Maximino* (LEDGER I06c-12, *verified*). Here Augustine is speaking, *Collatio* 14, PL 42, 720: *semetipsum exinanivit, formam servi accipiens: agnovisti aequalem, jam incipe agnoscere minorem … Ecce qua forma major est Pater*.
