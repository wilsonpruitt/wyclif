# sed/autem extract, wave 31: chunks 02-f 02-g

### 02-f ¶1 · 48.23–50.27  (6 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 48] Hic videtur michi quod ecclesia primitiva illud non posuit, **sed** ecclesia novella, ut quidam infideliter et infundabiliter sompniantes baptizarunt terminum et fantasiarunt multa false ad onus ecclesie. Primo ergo videtur quod non pocius credendum est dicte decretali vel suis autoribus quam decreto ecclesie in persona Beringarii, quia (ut supra dictum est) in isto supereminebant sanctitate et sciencia papa cum sinodo magis credibili, qui locuti sunt conformius ad scripturas, ad sanctorum doctorum sentencias et plus consone racioni et preter hec expressius et [p. 49] facilius detexerunt credendum. Cum enim transsubstanciari sit terminus magistralis, licet fideli trahere eius significacionem ad quemcunque sensum katholicum particularem et sic videtur ambigue et incomplete innuere fidem ecclesie de Eukaristia. Aliud **autem** decretum explicat quomodo papa et sinodus decreverunt in Beringario quomodo panis et vinum que in altari ponuntur remanent post consecracionem non solum sacramentum **sed** eciam verum corpus Christi et sanguis. Unde illud credendum ut fidem misit papa iocunde diversis ecclesiis. Cum ergo signum materiale decretalium vel decretorum sit inpertinens fidei quam sentit ecclesia et tanta vel maior relucebat in ecclesia priori auctoritas, videtur quod tollitur evidencia capta ex decretali ecclesie de transsubstanciacione que tolleret naturam panis et vini post consecracionem hostie remanere. Quod si dicatur doctores glosantes ut glosam communem et Archidiaconum cum multis theologis intelligere per panem solummodo formam panis, patet quod coloracius diceret expositor prioris decreti quod isti expositores intelligunt per formam panis et vini realiter essenciam vel naturam panis et vini que est realiter ipsum corpus; et sic est locucio decreti expressior glosa sua. Confirmatur primo per hoc quod inevitabiliter sequitur quantumcunque rudi loico: Sub forma panis et vini est corpus Christi sacramentaliter, ergo ille panis et illud vinum sunt substancia illi forme et per consequens illa existente forma accidentali panis et vinum remanent maioris permanencie illa forma. Secundo per hoc quod CXIVᶜⁱᵐ episcopi non congregarentur ad discernendum quod panis et vinum que in altari ponuntur manerent post consecracionem, si intelligerent [p. 50] eius contradiccionem quod non panis et vinum remanent post consecracionem illud sacramentum altaris **sed** accidencia que quondam fuerunt panis et vinum; non enim illuderent populo, dicentes insolite quod accidencia ponuntur intendentes contradictorium dicti sui. Sic enim facile foret glosare quoscunque testes fidei per contradictorium, fidem ecclesie subvertendo; videat ergo primo glosator quomodo nullus diceret panem et vinum poni in altari ante consecracionem, hoc est, illa accidencia non illam substanciam; videat secundo quod eque falsum est illa accidencia esse corpus Christi et sanguinem sicut illas substancias, et eque vere figurant ille substancie corpus Christi ut accidencia; videat tercio quomodo scriptura sacra a qua exemplatur ista locucio non intelligit per panem et vinum illa accidencia **sed** ipsas essencias. Quare ergo non sic intelligeret decretum cum sanctis doctoribus? Tercio confirmatur ut sepe quod verba decreti magis concordant cum scriptura et sanctis doctoribus, cum racione et sensu; ideo tam maturi et sancti episcopi vel intelligerent famose per verba fidei vel exprimerent sensum suum. Sic enim explanat decretum multociens quomodo debet intelligi quod panis est corpus Christi, hoc est, signum sacramentale eius; qui modus loquendi fuit famosus in scriptura et tunc acceptus a populo, **sed** post declinavit ad predicacionem ydempticam.

ENGLISH: Here it seems to me that the primitive [church]{g:ecclesia} did not posit this, but [the newer church]{g:novella-ecclesia} did, as certain men, dreaming [faithlessly]{g:infideliter} and [without foundation]{g:infundabiliter}, baptized the term and [fancied]{g:fantasiare} many things falsely, to the burden of the church.[^1] First, then, it seems that the said [decretal]{g:decretalis} or its authors are not to be believed rather than the [decree]{g:decretum} of the church in the person of Berengar, because (as was said above) in this the [pope]{g:papa}, with a synod more worthy of belief, excelled in holiness and knowledge; and they spoke more conformably to the scriptures and to the [teachings]{g:sentencia} of the holy doctors, and more consonantly with reason, and besides this they uncovered what is to be believed more expressly and [49] more easily.[^2] For since "to be transubstantiated" is a [masters' term]{g:termini-magistrales}, the faithful man may draw its [signification]{g:significacio} to any particular catholic [meaning]{g:sensus}; and so it seems to intimate ambiguously and incompletely the faith of the church about [the Eucharist]{g:eukaristia}.[^3] But the other decree explains how the pope and synod decreed in [the case of] Berengar how the [bread]{g:panis} and [wine]{g:vinum} which are placed on the altar remain after the [consecration]{g:consecracio} not only a [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} but also the true [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} and blood. Hence the pope gladly sent this to various churches as the faith to be believed.[^4] Since, then, the material [sign]{g:signum} of decretals or decrees is not pertinent to the faith which the church [holds]{g:sentire}, and as great or greater [authority]{g:auctoritas} shone forth in the earlier church, it seems that the [evidence]{g:evidencia} drawn from the church's decretal on [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio}, which would take away the nature of bread and wine remaining after the consecration of the [host]{g:hostia}, [is disposed of]{g:tollere}.[^5] And if it is said that the doctors who [gloss]{g:glosare}, such as the common [gloss]{g:glosa} and the Archdeacon, with many theologians, understand by "bread" only the form of bread, it is clear that an expositor of the earlier decree would say [more colourably]{g:coloracior} that these expositors understand by "the form of bread and wine" [really]{g:realiter} the [essence]{g:essencia} or [nature]{g:natura} of bread and wine, which is really the body itself; and so the speech of the decree is more express than its gloss.[^6] This is confirmed, first, by this: that it [follows]{g:sequitur} inescapably, for a logician however crude: "Under the form of bread and wine is the body of Christ [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter}; therefore that bread and that wine are [substance]{g:substancia} to that form"; and consequently, that accidental form existing, the bread and wine remain, of greater permanence than that form.[^7] Second, by this: that a hundred and fourteen [bishops]{g:episcopus} would not be gathered to determine that the bread and wine which are placed on the altar remain after the consecration, if they understood [50] the contradiction of it: that it is not bread and wine that remain after the consecration as that [sacrament of the altar]{g:sacramentum-altaris}, but [accidents]{g:accidens} which were once bread and wine; for they would not [delude]{g:illudere} the people, saying in an unwonted way that accidents are placed [there], meaning the contradictory of what they said.[^8] For in this way it would be easy to gloss any witnesses of the faith by the contradictory, subverting the faith of the church. Let the glossator, then, see first how no one would say that bread and wine are placed on the altar before the consecration, that is, those accidents, not that substance; let him see secondly that it is as false that those accidents are the body and blood of Christ as that those substances are, and that those substances [figure]{g:figurare} the body of Christ as [truly]{g:vere} as the accidents; let him see thirdly how holy scripture, from which this way of speaking is drawn, does not understand by bread and wine those accidents but the essences themselves. Why then would he not understand the decree so, with the holy doctors?[^9] Thirdly, this is confirmed, as often, by this: that the words of the decree agree more with scripture and the holy doctors, with reason and sense; therefore bishops so mature and holy would either understand by the words of faith in the well-known way, or would express their own meaning. For so the decree explains many times how it must be understood that "the bread is the body of Christ", that is, its sacramental sign; which manner of speaking was well known in scripture and then accepted by the people, but afterwards declined into [identical predication]{g:predicacio-ydemptica}.[^10]

### 02-f ¶2 · 50.28–51.17  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 50] Unde hodie loquendum foret conformiter sensui exposito et scandalo in fide subiecto, ymmo Christus non omisit sic loqui propter scandalum, ut dicitur Joh. VIᵒ; **sed** subtiliter exponit se ipsum, ut docet decretum Augustini super Psalmo IVᵒ, quod ponitur [p. 51] in De Consecracione, distinccione IIᵃ, Prima quidem: Spiritualiter intelligite que locutus sum: Non hoc corpus quod videtis manducaturi estis nec bibituri sanguinem quem fusuri sunt qui me crucifigent. Sacramentum aliquod vobis commendavi, spiritualiter intellectum vivificabit vos. Unde ad intelligendum quod non illo modo quo ipsi putant erogat corpus suum dicit ipsum ascensurum in celum integrum. Cum videritis, inquit, filium hominis ascendentem, ubi erat prius, spiritus est qui vivificat. Antiquo ergo decreto patrum pocius credendum est, cum quinquagesima distinccione, Domino sancto, sub auctoritate Isidori sic habetur: Quocienscunque in gestis conciliorum discors sentencia invenitur, illius concilii magis teneatur sentencia cuius antiquior et pocior extat auctoritas. Cum ergo sentencia decreti sit propinquior fonti scripture, videtur quod illa in materia fidei pocius est tenenda.

ENGLISH: Hence today one ought to speak in conformity with the meaning set out, the scandal in the faith being brought under; indeed, Christ did not leave off speaking so because of scandal, as is said in John 6;[^11] but he subtly expounds himself, as the decree of Augustine on Psalm 4 teaches, which is placed [51] in *De consecratione*, distinction 2, *Prima quidem*: "Understand [spiritually]{g:spiritualiter} what I have spoken: you are not to eat this body which you see, nor to drink the blood which they will shed who will crucify me. I have commended to you a certain sacrament; spiritually understood, it will give you life." Hence, so that [they might] understand that he does not dispense his body in that manner in which they think, he says that he "will ascend into heaven whole": "When you shall see", he says, "the Son of man ascending where he was before, it is the spirit that quickeneth."[^12] The ancient decree of the fathers is therefore rather to be believed, since in the fiftieth distinction, *Domino sancto*, it is had thus under the authority of Isidore: "Whenever a discordant teaching is found in the acts of councils, let the teaching of that council be held rather whose authority stands more ancient and more powerful." Since, then, the teaching of the decree is nearer to the source of scripture, it seems that it is rather to be held in matter of faith.[^13]

### 02-g ¶1 · 51.18–53.4  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 51] Ulterius quantum ad descripcionem transsubstanciacionis, videtur quod capit calumpniam, supponendo (ut adversarii fatentur) quod illa conversio non ponit anichilacionem nec ydemptificacionem panis cum corpore Christi; nam omne accidens oportet habere subiectum, omnis talis transubstanciacio est accidens, ergo omnem talem oportet habere subiectum; licet **autem** fateantur accidens esse sine subiecto, tamen dicunt quod ipsum oportet habere subiectum in principio, ymmo (ut infecte loquendo dicunt) quod ipsum est subiectum ad quemlibet sui punctum, non ergo est sine subiecto, ymmo omne accidens respectivum cuiusmodi est omnis mutacio, concedunt non [p. 52] posse esse sine subiecto suo, ergo oportet dari aliquid quod subicitur transsubstanciacioni passive; illud non est accidens, quia ipsum manet non transsubstanciatum, nec est panis vel vinum, quia, cum oportet transsubstanciacionem illam esse fundatam, ne cogamur concedere corpus Christi vel hostiam successive confici, patet quod pro instanti transsubstanciacionis nec panis transsubstanciatus nec vinum est secundum aliquam sui partem, et per consequens nullum accidens tunc subiectat; illud deductum est in tractatu De Anichilacione movisse philosophos ad ponendum materiam primam generacioni subiectam, quia fingere unam rem prius inauditam sine fundamento foret illusio; ponens ergo transsubstanciacionem diceret quid est, ubi est et quomodo sustentatur; cum enim sit forma, oportet quod aliquod subiectum informet et pulcrificet, cum tam miraculose producitur. Unde licet quondam laboraverim ad describendum transsubstanciacionem concorditer ad sensum prioris ecclesie, tamen modo videtur michi quod contrariantur, posteriori ecclesia oberrante. Si enim transsubstanciacio sit cessio unius substancie alteri quoad locum, sic quod una substancia transsubstanciata remanet per eundem locum quo prius, et substancia dignior sit per illum locum sacramentaliter cui subordinatur substancia prior ut signum, tunc contingit quod panis transsubstancietur pro instanti quo non mutetur, **sed** remanet panis subiectus accidentibus post consecracionem; quod dicitur repugnare sentencie decretalis; ideo dimitto nunc concordancias legum istarum et voco transsubstanciacionem conversionem [p. 53] unius substancie in aliam, ut semen convertitur in corpus vivum, homo convertitur in terram, et sic generaliter quando unum corpus ex alio generatur.

ENGLISH: Further, as to the [definition]{g:descripcio} of [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio}, it seems that it is [open to challenge]{g:calumpnia}, supposing (as the adversaries admit) that that [conversion]{g:conversio} posits neither [annihilation]{g:anichilacio} nor [identification]{g:ydemptificacio} of the [bread]{g:panis} with the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi};[^1] for every [accident]{g:accidens} must have a [subject]{g:subiectum}; every such transubstantiation is an accident; therefore every such [transubstantiation] must have a subject. And although they admit that an accident is without a subject, yet they say that it must have a subject at the beginning; indeed (as they say, speaking imperfectly) that it is subjected at every point of itself; therefore it is not without a subject.[^2] Indeed, every [relational]{g:respectivus} accident, of which kind is every change, they grant cannot [52] be without its subject; therefore something must be given which is subjected to transubstantiation passively. That is not an accident, because it remains not transubstantiated; nor is it the bread or the [wine]{g:vinum}, because, since that transubstantiation must be founded, lest we be forced to grant that the body of Christ or the [host]{g:hostia} is [consecrated]{g:conficere} successively, it is clear that at the instant of transubstantiation neither the transubstantiated bread nor the wine is, according to any part of itself, and consequently it [is then the subject of]{g:subiectare} no accident.[^3] This, as was deduced in the treatise *On Annihilation*, moved the philosophers to posit [prime matter]{g:materia-prima} as subject to generation, because to [feign]{g:fingere} one thing previously unheard of, without foundation, would be [illusion]{g:illusio}. So whoever posits transubstantiation should say what it is, where it is and how it is sustained; for since it is a form, it must [inform]{g:informare} and beautify some subject, since it is produced so miraculously.[^4] Hence, although I once laboured to [define]{g:describere} transubstantiation in accord with the [meaning]{g:sensus} of the earlier [church]{g:ecclesia}, yet now it seems to me that they are contrary, the later church having strayed.[^5] For if transubstantiation is a giving-way of one [substance]{g:substancia} to another as to place, such that the one substance, transubstantiated, remains through the same place as before, and the worthier substance is through that place [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter}, to which the prior substance is subordinated as a [sign]{g:signum}, then it happens that the bread is transubstantiated at an instant at which it is not changed, but bread remains subject to the accidents after the consecration; which is said to [be incompatible]{g:repugnare} with the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of the [decretal]{g:decretalis}. Therefore I now let go the concordances of these laws, and I call transubstantiation the conversion [53] of one substance into another, as seed is converted into a living body, man is converted into earth, and so generally when one body is generated from another.[^6]

### 02-g ¶2 · 53.5–53.24  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 53] Ulterius quoad ydolatriam patet quod populus est instruendus ne credat illud accidentatum quod sentit esse ydemptice corpus Christi; et hoc est necessarium secundum omnem opinionem istius materie, quia ecclesia posterior fatetur quod remanet unum album, rotundum et durum, quod non potest esse corpus Christi; et revera cum illud sit per se sensibile et per consequens populus est ita pronus vel pronior ad credendum illud esse corpus Christi ut panem, patet quod non remanet illis punctus coloris ad putandum panem desinere secundum quamlibet eius partem, ne adorando panem ut corpus Christi ydolatria committatur; per idem enim tolleretur quodcunque accidens sensibile, ymmo modo superadditur illusio de subiecto et accidente suo; et maior pars populi credit infideliter et irreverencius quod illud remanens quod ipsi ponunt accidens sit ydemptice corpus Christi. Constat quidem quod accidens est magis extraneum a natura corporis Christi vel sanguinis, quam natura panis aut vini. De transsubstanciacione **autem** patet diffuse posterius.

ENGLISH: Further, as to [idolatry]{g:ydolatria}, it is clear that the people must be instructed not to believe that that [thing subject to accidents]{g:accidentatus} which it [perceives]{g:sentire} is [identically]{g:ydemptice} the body of Christ;[^7] and this is necessary according to every [opinion]{g:opinio} on this matter, because the later church admits that there remains a white, round and hard thing, which cannot be the body of Christ. And [in truth]{g:revera}, since that is [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} [of itself]{g:per-se}, and consequently the people is as prone or more prone to believe that it is the body of Christ as to believe the bread is, it is clear that there remains to them no point of [colour]{g:color} for thinking that the bread ceases according to every part of it, lest by adoring the bread as the body of Christ idolatry be committed;[^8] for by the same token any perceptible accident would be taken away; indeed, now an illusion about the subject and its accident is added over and above; and the greater part of the people believes [faithlessly]{g:infideliter} and more irreverently that that remaining thing, which they posit [as] an accident, is identically the body of Christ. It is evident indeed that an accident is more foreign to the [nature]{g:natura} of the body or blood of Christ than the nature of bread or wine. And about transubstantiation it is clear at length later.[^9]
