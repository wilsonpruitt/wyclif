# *Dialogus*, Epilogus, part 1: prescription, charters and custom

## The first argument and its answer · 86.2–88.6

> [p. 86] RESTAT finaliter perstringere totam istam sentenciam sub quodam epilogo, ut plus clareat ad honorem dei et utilitatem cleri, per consequens tocius ecclesie militantis. Intendimus enim, quod hec ecclesia, et specialiter clerus, in paupertate honesta secundum ducatum domini reguletur ad quod requiritur secundum providenciam ordinacionis legis Christi. Quod renunciet finaliter dominio seculari, patet ex testimonio duplici et interpretacione Christi, tam vita quam verbo, que interpretacio nec potest directe fallere neque falli. Argucie autem que fiunt in oppositum non forent digne memoria nisi propter infidelem ignoranciam cecatorum. Arguitur enim communiter quod domini temporales concesserunt clero, ad sui et progenitorum suorum meritum et honorem, amplos redditus temporales, et clerus erat in possessione pro tempore infra quod currit prescripcio. [Nemo] ergo dirimeret vel machinaretur dirimere illud donum nisi qui minoraret salutem tam animabus vivis quam mortuis, quam eciam prosperitatem et pacem regnorum. Sed quis hoc intendit nisi fuerit antichristus? Quantum ad istam arguciam dictum est sepe quod militat dirrecte pro veritate catholica supradicta, cum racio capta ex fide testatur quod error contrarius legi dei, temptatus per progenitores et defensatus per superstites, neutri parti proficit ad salutem, sed officit gravando peccatum, et specialiter quo ad salutem [p. 87] anime in beatitudine possidenda. Et quantum ad prosperitatem et pacem mundanam patet quod disponit ad istam de quanto superstites racionabiliter † peccatum legi dei contrarium, cum nemo ipsas appeteret nisi de quanto sunt disposiciones prosperitatis et pacis patrie inductive. Scimus tamen quod dyabolus ex naturali potencia et innata malicia potest in penam peccati prioris sophisticare pacem et prosperitatem seculi, que saluti anime sunt nocive et per consequens ipsi homini quantum ad beatitudinem acquirendam. Et verisimile est quod dyabolus temptaret istam prosperitatem et pacem sensibilem quantum sufficit, et inter viantes destrueret, ad finem quo impediat ne fideles exequantur et compleant legem ordinacionis divine; sic enim cognoscimus ex ewangelio quod dyabolus ex sompno perturbavit uxorem Pilati, timens quod mors Christi cederet fidelibus ad salutem. Sed fidelis, et specialiter attemptans hoc opus ewangelicum, debet supra opinionem et cautelas dyaboli fundari in solliditate fidei scripturarum, et tunc non debet cessare propter eventus tales dyabolicos, quia aliter ipsi solliditatem fidei superarent, quod convincerent indubitanter defectum fidei in vecordi taliter obmittenti. Et patet quod hec stulta dotacio, sicud non potest esse meritoria preteritis vel presentibus, licet multum boni faciat sed non tantum quantum † sic non potest currere efficax et salubris prescripcio coram deo. Quia ut dicunt de privilegiis regis Anglie, quod sit semper iuxta etatem potens iustificare iniuriam prius factam, sic authonomatice est de deo, cum ex fide concessit deus libertatem hominibus, quamdiu manent hic superstites, quod possunt in quocunque peccaverint deo satisfacere et pro crimine emendari, nisi fuerint in peccato illo finaliter indurati. Dyabolus sic arguens innuit quod proceres regni nostri sunt sicut clerici, et specialiter superiores prelati, in isto crimine dyabolice indurati. Sed excuciamus cordate [p. 88] istas fraudulentas sophisticaciones dyaboli et agamus viriliter ad defensionem legis et restitucionem ordinacionis domini Jesu Christi, quia ex fide viantes non sufficiunt sibi ostendere hic plus amoris, et secundum apostolum anathema incurrit qui non amaverit dominum Jesum Christum. Et patet studenti solucio ad formam et materiam argumenti.

It remains, finally, to draw this whole [teaching]{g:sentencia} together under a kind of epilogue, so that it may be clearer, to the honour of God and the benefit of [the clergy]{g:clerus}, and consequently of the whole [church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans}. For we [maintain]{g:intendere} that this [church]{g:ecclesia}, and especially the clergy, be ruled in honest poverty according to the Lord's leading, to which it is bound according to the providence of the [ordinance]{g:ordinacio} of [the law of Christ]{g:lex-christi}.[^1] That it should finally renounce secular [lordship]{g:dominium} is clear from the twofold testimony and from Christ's interpretation, as much by life as by word; and that interpretation can neither directly deceive nor be deceived.[^2] But the [quibbles]{g:argucie} made to the contrary would not be worth remembering, were it not for the [unbelieving]{g:infidelis} ignorance of the blinded. For it is commonly argued that the [temporal lords]{g:temporales-domini} granted the clergy ample temporal [revenues]{g:reditus}, for their own merit and honour and their forebears', and the clergy has been in possession for the time within which [prescription]{g:prescripcio} runs.[^3] [No one] therefore would dissolve, or scheme to dissolve, that gift, except one who would diminish salvation for souls both living and dead, and also the prosperity and peace of [kingdoms]{g:regnum}.[^4] But who intends this, unless he be antichrist? As to this quibble it has often been said that it fights directly for the catholic truth stated above, since reason drawn from faith testifies that an error contrary to [God's law]{g:lex-dei}, attempted by the forebears and defended by the survivors, profits neither party to salvation, but harms by aggravating the sin, and especially as regards the salvation [87] of the soul in the blessedness to be possessed. And as regards worldly prosperity and peace, it is clear that it disposes to that, in so far as the survivors reasonably † the sin contrary to God's law, since no one would desire them except in so far as they are dispositions leading on to the prosperity and peace of the [homeland]{g:patria}.[^5] Yet we know that the devil, by natural power and inborn malice, can, in punishment of a prior sin, [counterfeit]{g:sophisticare} the peace and prosperity of the world, which are harmful to the soul's salvation, and consequently to the man himself as regards attaining blessedness.[^6] And it is likely that the devil would attempt this [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} prosperity and peace as far as suffices, and would destroy it among [wayfarers]{g:viator}, to the end of hindering the faithful from carrying out and fulfilling the law of the divine ordinance;[^7] for so we know from the gospel that the devil troubled Pilate's wife through a dream, fearing that Christ's death would turn to the salvation of the faithful.[^8] But the faithful man, and especially one attempting this gospel work, ought to be grounded, above the [opinion]{g:opinio} and [wiles]{g:cautela} of the devil, in the solidity of the faith of the scriptures; and then he ought not to cease on account of such devilish outcomes, for otherwise they would overcome the solidity of faith, and so they would prove beyond doubt a defect of faith in the [faint-hearted]{g:vecors} man who so leaves off. And it is clear that, as this foolish [endowment]{g:dotacio} cannot be meritorious for those past or present, although it does much good, but not so much as †, so no effective and wholesome prescription can run before God.[^9] For as they say of the privileges of the king of England, that he is always able, according to his age, to set right an injury done before, so [by antonomasia]{g:autonomatice} it is with God, since, [as faith teaches]{g:ex-fide}, God has granted men freedom, as long as they remain here as survivors, that in whatever they have sinned they can make satisfaction to God and be amended for the crime, unless they have been finally hardened in that sin.[^10] The devil, arguing thus, implies that the nobles of our kingdom are, like [the clerics]{g:clericus}, and especially the higher [prelates]{g:prelatus}, devilishly hardened in this crime. But let us heartily shake off [88] these fraudulent sophistries of the devil, and act manfully for the defence of the law and the restoration of the ordinance of the Lord Jesus Christ; for, as faith teaches, wayfarers cannot show him more love here, and according to the apostle he incurs anathema who has not loved the Lord Jesus Christ.[^11] And the solution to the form and matter of the argument is clear to the student.

## The second argument · 88.7–88.27

> [p. 88] Secundo sic arguitur: illud quod transiit in preteritum non potest dirimi, cum secundum philosophos illo deus privatur de preterito facere non preteritum. Sed, ut testatur homo et carte regum et secularium dominorum, diu transiit in preteritum quod ecclesia fuerat sic dotata. Quis ergo vel deus illud preteritum dirimere potest? Per idem enim non facerent fidem carte de humanis redditibus, seculares domini de ruina sui dominii forent desolati. Et dicens quod non licet secularibus dominis istud concedere innuit quod non sunt pleni domini bonorum que possident. Confirmatur tripliciter, primo per hoc quod magna carta regis testatur quod rex cum unanimi consensu regni sui concessit clero suo privilegium, ad cuius defensionem rex in principio sue coronacionis specialiter est iuratus, et excommunicaciones tam trans mare quam cis mare in omnes infringentes hoc privilegium fulminantur. Secundo confirmatur per hoc quod multi sancti et canonisati ab ecclesia in defensione huius privilegii obierunt. Ideo ergo foret privilegium illum destruere subvertere totam ecclesiam. Tercio confirmatur per hoc quod papa, qui est caput universalis ecclesie, tam facto quam scripto confirmat multipliciter istud factum.

[88] Secondly it is argued thus: what has passed into the past cannot be undone, since according to the philosophers God is deprived of this: to make the past not past.[^12] Now, as man and the charters of kings and secular [lords]{g:dominus} testify, it has long since passed into the past that the church had been so endowed. Who then, or [even] God, can undo that past? For by the same token charters would not be proof concerning human revenues, [and so] secular lords would be left desolate by the ruin of their lordship.[^13] And one who says that it is not lawful for secular lords to grant this implies that they are not full lords of the goods they possess. It is confirmed in three ways: first by this, that the king's great charter testifies that the king, with the unanimous consent of his kingdom, granted his clergy a privilege, to whose defence the king is specially sworn at the beginning of his coronation; and excommunications are fulminated, both beyond the sea and on this side of it, against all who infringe this privilege.[^14] Secondly it is confirmed by this, that many saints, canonized too by the church, died in defence of this privilege.[^15] Therefore, then, to destroy that privilege would be to subvert the whole church. Thirdly it is confirmed by this, that [the pope]{g:papa}, who is head of the universal church, both by deed and by writing confirms this deed in many ways.

## The answer to the second argument · 88.28–90.4

> [p. 88] Ad istud dictum est alibi quod hoc principale argumentum sentenciat quod nemo potest pro culpa sua satisfacere cum peccaverit, cum sicut homo potest cessare ab errore quem inchoaverit, pro delicto satisfacere quod commiserat, sic in proposito possunt reges et domini temporales; in cuius [p. 89] confirmacionem rex sapiens Salomon ad pacificacionem regni sui deposuit summum sacerdotem et alium licenter instituit, ut patet ex fide scripture 3 Reg. 3°. Ista ergo que ibi scripta sunt ad nostram doctrinam scripta sunt. Non petimus quod deus vel rex faciat illum errorem non fuisse preteritum, sed quod pro futuro corrigat ne continuetur amplius ad dampnum ecclesie contra deum. Et quantum ad cartas dicunt fideles reges cognoscere quod non possunt per ipsas vel aliter quidquid licenter concedere nisi habita ad hoc licencia dei, qui est dominus capitalis. Hoc autem exemplatum est in humanis legibus; sed demonstrari potest ex fide certissime quod hoc de deo oportet catholicum sustinere. Ideo non superest cleri sufficiens excusacio in hac parte, nisi ostenderit ad hoc licenciam huius domini capitalis. Aliter enim non rex terrenus blasfemias tales secundum legem suam requireret, et de deo, in quo est causa vivacioris fidei, hoc negaret. Sed certi sumus ex fide quod persona verbi, que est deus et non mutatur, non potest licenciam talem concedere, quia non potest esse sibi ipsi contrarius vel dare licenciam ad peccandum. Et quantum ad istam simul finalem arguciam per locum a simili, constat quod aliud est ius poli faciendo elemosinam, et aliud ius soli concedendo hereditatem terrenam. Et licet seculares domini non sunt plene in hereditatibus talibus confirmati, tamen possunt habere evidenciam supra dubium quod habent tales hereditates pro se et suis heredibus, specialiter dum ipsas rexerint conformiter legi dei et fideliter dederint deo servicium debitum, quod stat potissime in defensione et servicio legis sue. Et quantum ad plenitudinem dominii certum est fide quod ad plenitudinem divini dominii non possunt attingere, nec deus wult tale dominium eis concedere, nisi sub condicione tacita vel expressa quod serviant sibi fideliter et per idem servicium fiant digni. Quantum ad primam confirmacionem dicitur [p. 90] quod oportet hanc magnam cartam sane intelligere. Et licet non intendimus nec sufficimus hanc cartam ex integro defendere tanquam fidem, cum unum ex fide capimus, quod non valet, nec servari debet, nisi de quanto consonat legi dei,

[88] To this it has been said elsewhere that this principal argument [pronounces]{g:sentenciare} that no one can make satisfaction for his fault once he has sinned; whereas, just as a man can cease from an error he has begun [and] make satisfaction for the offence he had committed, so in the case before us kings and temporal lords can. In [89] confirmation of this the wise king Solomon, for the pacification of his kingdom, deposed the high priest and licitly instituted another, as is clear from the faith of scripture, 3 Kings 3.[^16] Therefore these things that are written there "were written for our learning" (Rom. 15:4). We do not ask that God or the king make that error not to have been past, but that for the future he correct it, lest it continue any further to the harm of the church, against God. And as to charters, the faithful say that kings know that they cannot, by them or otherwise, grant anything licitly unless they have had leave for it from God, who is [chief lord]{g:capitalis-dominus}.[^17] And this is exemplified in human laws; but it can be demonstrated most certainly, as faith teaches, that a catholic must maintain this of God. Therefore no sufficient excuse remains for the clergy in this matter, unless it shows the leave of this chief lord for it. For otherwise it would not be [consistent that] an earthly king should prosecute such blasphemies under his law, and [that we] deny this of God, in whom there is cause of livelier faith.[^18] Now we are certain, as faith teaches, that the person of the Word, who is God and does not change, cannot grant such leave, since he cannot be contrary to himself or give leave to sin. And as to this final quibble as well, [argued] by [the topic from likeness]{g:locus-a-simili}, it is evident that the [law]{g:ius} of heaven in giving [alms]{g:elemosina} is one thing, and the law of the soil in granting an earthly inheritance is another.[^19] And although secular lords are not fully confirmed in such inheritances, yet they can have [evidence]{g:evidencia} beyond doubt that they hold such inheritances for themselves and their heirs, especially while they rule them in conformity with God's law and faithfully render God the [service]{g:servicium} due, which consists chiefly in the defence and service of his law. And as to fullness of lordship, it is certain by faith that they cannot attain to the fullness of divine lordship, nor does God will to grant them such lordship except under the condition, tacit or express, that they serve him faithfully and by that same service become worthy. As to the first confirmation, it is said [90] that this great charter must be understood soundly. ## The answer continued · 90.5–92.30

> [p. 90] aliter foret contraria fidei, quod omnis fidelis debet horrere. Sed quia nostrum non est illud supponere, ideo possumus sanum intellectum supponere huic carte et ipsam ad nostrum propositum retorquere. Non enim supponimus quod in carta predicta vocentur privilegia cleri Anglie que sunt ad detrimentum eius contraria legi Christi, quia sic forent privilegia abolenda. Cum igitur Christus et sui apostoli habuerunt a deo hec privilegia quod vivant pauperem vitam et humilem cum aliis promoventibus ad hanc vitam, absit in ista carta solempni supponere, quod deus, concitor eius, voluit in hac carta privilegia contraria istis intelligi. Et cum rex et regnum iurati sunt ad sensum catholicum huius carthe, videtur quod ex vi iuramenti sui tenentur dictum sensum eius catholicum defendere, et sic hec evidencia redit ad sensum catholicum quem ex fide scripture intendimus. Cayfas enim dicens quod expedit unum hominem mori pro populo ignoranter salubrem sentenciam prophetavit. Sic autem videtur quibusdam quod, dum illa carta fuerit fideliter assecuta, ex eius fideli sentencia clerus Anglie foret ad statum quem Christus instituit restitutus. Nec aliter videtur quod rex et eius proceres cum suis episcopis evaderent periurium in hac parte. Et conformiter dicitur quod intelligende sunt excommunicaciones in illa materia. Infidele quidem foret vertere in hac carta significaciones terminorum contra veritatem sensus scripture, vocando pravas leges privilegia que privant clerum de gracia et inducunt ipsum in maximam servitutem. Ad secundam confirmacionem dictum est sepius quod sic arguens similis est stulto desperanti de sua causa, lambenti aquam turbidam, currenti in paludibus, [p. 91] ubi posset facilius de fonte vivo bibere aquam claram, modo quo loquitur propheta huic proposito pertinenter. Consuetudo autem Christi est infringibilis, summe facilitatis et auctoritatis, et post ipsum apostolorum consuetudo currens in claris rivulis ab hoc fonte. Sed ipsas stultus hic arguens dimittit et capit remotas consuetudines tanquam aquas stacionarias ex luto et ardore terrenorum infectas. Et talis secundum doctrinam prophete est nimis stultus, negligens in hiis que concernunt salutem anime. Quis ergo dubitat quin talis peccat non mediocriter qui dimittit consuetudinem summe autenticam et salubrem et innititur consuetudini corrupte condicionis contrarie, dum priorem consuetudinem facilius securius et melius posset accipere? In illa autem stulticia sunt taliter arguentes, quia dum arguunt per argumentum topicum sive probabile, nesciunt si assumant conswetudinem huius dyaboli pro exemplo; sicut enim sacrificium in lege veteri a tempore Moysi usque Cayfam et Annam, qui Christum occiderant, fuit continue in peiorari, sic sacrificium in lege gracie a Christo et suis apostolis per cupidinem mundanorum continue fuit debilius, sic quod hodie possunt sacerdotes dicere evidencius quod descenderunt a principio generis Scariothis, qui pro 30ª argenteis vendidit veritatem, quam quod descendunt a paupere domino Jesu Christo, quia similitudo maior hoc indicat esse verum. Nos autem occidui Machometi qui sumus pauci inter fideles ecclesie, credimus quod ad nostrum iudicium reguletur et contremit totus mundus, cum tamen plures sunt sapientes Greci vel longe plures fideles Indii, qui tenent consuetudinem nobis oppositam in hac parte. Ideo conswetudo vel opinio nichil probat vel innuit nisi de quanto fundata fuerit in consuetudine salvatoris. Cum ergo nostri occidui, quantumcunque magni in sapiencia vel sanctitate elevati fuerint, non attingunt in hoc ad fidem scripture, vel vix attingunt argumentum topicum, quod sapienter fuerant sive sancti. Cum ergo in omni genere sit [p. 92] unum predicamentum quod fit mensura omnium aliorum, attendamus fideliter ad hoc predicamentum, quod hoc dicit dominus vel ex lege sua, ergo hoc est insolubiliter observandum. Sed quia conswetudines in isto deficiunt, ideo sunt nichil aut modicum a fidelibus ponderande. Erubescat ergo maniacus occiduus in ista argucia: talis consuetudo in nostra contracta sine approbacione domini est confirmata, ergo est katholice observanda. Et per hoc patet responsio ad terciam arguciam factam de papa, supponendo ut fidem quod ipse peccare et errare poterit ut ceteri viatores. Sic enim Christus ordinavit Petrum sic peccasse ante missionem spiritus sancti et post illam. Et credo quod nullus pro verecundia audebit dicere quod Petrus post tam sanctam vitam fuit magis peccabilis quam est papa. Si ergo genus cleri usque in adventum antichristi in deterius retrocedit, evidens est multis, quod sit recens et plenissimus antichristus. Quis ergo timeret istam arguciam: Ipse sine racione vel fundacione scripture sic asserit vel excommunicat, ergo est sue sentencie a cunctis fidelibus insistendum: cum sit argumentum topicum in quo racionabiliter contrarium concluditur? Fideles ergo sibi propinquiores magis percipiunt istam sentenciam, et patet quod falsum assumitur, quod papa sit capud universalis ecclesie vel Petri vicarius vel vicarius Jesu Christi, sed inter omnes mortales ab illis maxime elongatur. Ideo in isto, sicud in aliis argumentis prioribus, adversarius vel falsum assumit, vel in sequela deficit, vel silenter innuit nostram sentenciam esse veram. Quia aut, ut Averrois asserit, veritas testatur multipliciter sibi ipsi, et secundum fideles doctores de quanto veritas plus teritur, de tanto fidelibus plus redolescit.

But because it is not ours to suppose that, therefore we can put a sound understanding under this charter and turn it back to our purpose. For we do not suppose that in the aforesaid charter those things are called privileges of the clergy of England which are to its detriment, contrary to the law of Christ, since in that case they would be privileges to be abolished. Since therefore Christ and his apostles had from God these privileges, that they should live a poor and humble life, with others urging men on to this life, far be it from us to suppose in this solemn charter that God, its mover, willed privileges contrary to these to be understood in this charter.[^20] And since the king and the kingdom are sworn to the catholic sense of this charter, it seems that by force of their oath they are bound to defend its said catholic sense; and so this evidence comes back to the catholic sense which we maintain from the faith of scripture. For Caiaphas, saying "that it is expedient that one man should die for the people", unknowingly prophesied a wholesome teaching (John 11:50–51).[^21] And so it seems to some that, if that charter were faithfully followed, by its faithful teaching the clergy of England would be restored to the state which Christ instituted. And in conformity with this it is said that the excommunications in that matter are to be understood. It would indeed be a thing of unbelief to turn the meanings of the terms in this charter against the truth of scripture's sense, calling depraved laws privileges, which deprive the clergy of grace and lead it into the greatest servitude.[^22] As to the second confirmation, it has been said very often that one who argues thus is like a fool despairing of his cause, lapping troubled water, running in the marshes, [91] where he could more easily drink clear water from the living fountain, in the way the prophet speaks pertinently to this purpose.[^23] Now the [custom]{g:consuetudo} of Christ is unbreakable, of the highest ease and authority, and after him the custom of the apostles, running in clear rivulets from this fountain. But this fool who argues so leaves them and takes remote customs, like stagnant waters infected with the mud and heat of earthly things. And such a man, according to the prophet's [doctrine]{g:doctrina}, is exceedingly foolish, negligent in the things that concern the soul's salvation. Who then doubts that such a man sins not moderately, who leaves a custom supremely authentic and wholesome and leans on a custom of a corrupt, contrary condition, while he could take the prior custom more easily, more safely and better? And those who argue thus are in that folly, for while they argue by a [topical]{g:argumentum-topicum} or probable argument, they do not know whether they are taking the custom of this devil as their example.[^24] For as the sacrifice in the old law, from the time of Moses up to Caiaphas and Annas, who had killed Christ, was continually worsening, so the sacrifice in [the law of grace]{g:lex-gracie}, from Christ and his apostles, has continually grown weaker through the greed of worldly men, so that today [priests]{g:sacerdos} can say more evidently that they descended from the head of the stock, Iscariot, who sold the truth for thirty pieces of silver, than that they descend from the poor Lord Jesus Christ, since the greater likeness shows this to be true.[^25] But we western Mahomets, who are few among the faithful of the church, believe that the whole world is ruled by our judgment and trembles at it, although there are more wise Greeks, or far more faithful Indians, who hold a custom opposed to ours in this matter.[^26] Therefore custom or opinion proves or implies nothing except in so far as it has been founded in the custom of the Saviour. Since therefore our westerners, however great they may have been in wisdom or raised high in holiness, do not in this attain to the faith of scripture, or scarcely attain a topical argument, [namely] that they had been wise or holy.[^27] Since therefore in every genus there is [92] one [member of the] [category]{g:predicamentum} which is the measure of all the others, let us attend faithfully to this category: "This the Lord says", or [this is] from his law, "therefore this is to be observed unbreakably".[^28] Now because customs fall short in this, therefore they are to be weighed by the faithful as nothing or little. Let the western maniac therefore blush at this quibble: "Such a custom, contracted in our [church] without the Lord's approval, has been confirmed; therefore it is to be observed catholicly."[^29] And by this the reply is clear to the third quibble, made concerning the pope, by supposing as faith that he can sin and err like other wayfarers. For so Christ ordained that Peter should have sinned in this way, before the sending of the Holy Spirit and after it.[^30] And I believe that no one, for shame, will dare to say that Peter, after so holy a life, was more capable of sin than the pope is. If therefore the stock of the clergy goes backward into worse until the coming of antichrist, it is evident to many that he is a recent and most complete antichrist.[^31] Who then would fear this quibble: "He asserts or excommunicates thus without reason or foundation in scripture; therefore his teaching is to be adhered to by all the faithful", since it is a topical argument in which the contrary is reasonably [inferred]{g:concludere}? The faithful nearer to him, therefore, perceive this teaching the more; and it is clear that a falsehood is assumed, that the pope is head of the universal church, or Peter's vicar, or vicar of Jesus Christ; rather, among all mortals he is the farthest removed from them. Therefore in this, as in the other, earlier arguments, the adversary either assumes a falsehood, or fails in [what follows]{g:sequela}, or silently implies that our teaching is true. For, as Averroes asserts, truth bears witness to itself in many ways; and according to faithful doctors, the more truth is rubbed, the more fragrant it grows to the faithful.[^32]

- 86.12: It is argued that for the good of souls Lords have given clergy ample possessions to which their title is secured by prescription.
- 86.16: To rob them would hurt both dead and living, and the peace of the kingdom.
- 86.21: Answer—it cannot profit souls to perpetuate their mistakes.
- 87.1: Earthly peace is valuable only as leading to the peace of heaven.
- 87.6: The devil can produce a deceitful earthly peace, hurtful to the soul.
- 87.10: And is likely to try to produce it in order to hinder the faithful,
- 87.13: as he tried to prevent the death of Christ.
- 87.16: The faithful must have their root in scripture, so that they may not faint at what the devil may bring about.
- 87.21: As endowments cannot be meritorious, so there can be no prescription valid with God.
- 87.25: Just as an English king may always redress a previous wrong, so God allows men, unless finally hardened, to make satisfaction for their sins.
- 87.31: The devil would make out that the nobles *are* hardened, like the higher prelates.
- 88.1: Defence of Christ's law best shows love to Christ, without which we are anathema.
- 88.7: Second argument—(*a*) God Himself cannot undo the past, but the endowment is long past.
- 88.12: (*b*) to undo it would discredit charters and ruin lords.
- 88.14: (*c*) to deny the lords' power to grant is to impugn their full ownership.
- 88.17: These arguments are confirmed (i) by Magna Charta.
- 88.22: (ii) by the deaths of many saints in their defence.
- 88.25: (iii) by the authority of the pope, the head of the universal church.
- 88.28: Answer.—(*a*) Men have power to amend their wrongdoings.
- 89.1: For which we have the example of Solomon.
- 89.4: We do not ask God or king to undo past errors, but to correct them for the future.
- 89.8: (*b*) As to charters, kings can grant nothing save by leave of the Chief Lord.
- 89.13: And this leave the clergy must show.
- 89.17: Which is impossible, as God does not contradict Himself.
- 89.20: (*c*) The heavenly law as to alms differs from the earthly law of inheritance.
- 89.25: Lords have clear evidence as to their rights, especially while they serve God.
- 89.29: But their ownership must be subject to God's.
- 90.1: As to the confirmations—(i) Magna Charta is only to be defended as it accords with Christ's law, but, rightly construed, the privileges it upholds are the privileges of following Christ.
- 90.21: A faithful interpretation of the Charter would restore the clergy to the state Christ ordained.
- 91.1: (ii) As to the witness of Saints, it is absurd to reject Christ's for theirs.
- 91.16: As the priests of the Old Law, so those of the New, have continually deteriorated.
- 91.25: We western Mahomets think ourselves the rulers of the earth, but there are many Greeks and Indians whose custom is better.
- 92.8: (iii) As to the Pope, if Peter could sin, much more can he.
- 92.21: He is neither the head of the universal church, nor Peter's vicar, nor Christ's.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Ad quod requiritur*: *quod* taken with *paupertate honesta* by sense ("the poverty to which it is bound"); it could also be read absolutely, "ruled, as is required, according to the providence of the ordinance".
[^2]: **C.** *Testimonio duplici*: the testimony of the twofold [testament], as Pollard's side-note takes it ("Both Testaments").
[^3]: **R.** *Prescripcio*: the canon- and civil-law acquisition of title by possession for a set term; the objection is that the clergy's title is now unassailable. Pollard's side-note: "their title is secured by prescription".
[^4]: **T.** *[Nemo]* is Pollard's supply; B¹, C, D omit it. C reads *diminueret* for *dirimeret* and *diminuere* for *dirimere*; C, D *dominium* for *donum*.
[^5]: **T.** The print has a dagger (†) after *racionabiliter* at 87.3 (the extraction's "t", corrected): a verb is lost, probably one meaning "amend" or "correct", and the sentence cannot be construed with certainty; the subject of *disponit* is not expressed. Translated as printed, the gap marked. *Patrie*: C omits it. The "homeland" here is probably the heavenly one (Pollard: "the peace of heaven"), though the earthly sense is possible.
[^6]: **C.** *Sophisticare* here is transitive, "to counterfeit, adulterate" (Pollard: "a deceitful earthly peace"), not the glossary's *sophisticari*, "to play the sophist".
[^7]: **C.** *Temptaret*: taken as "attempt [to produce]", with Pollard ("likely to try to produce it"); it could also mean "assail", which fits *destrueret* better. D reads *destruere*.
[^8]: **S/R.** Matt. 27:19: the Vulgate says only that she suffered "per visum"; the devil as the dream's author, seeking to prevent the Passion, is a commentary tradition (LEDGER DEA-1).
[^9]: **T.** A second dagger (†) after *quantum* at 87.23, restored from the plate: the comparison is incomplete. D reads *antequam* for *tantum quantum*. B¹, C, D read *Sicud* for *sic* (87.23): "so ... just as".
[^10]: **R.** The argument is about prescription: the royal privilege meant is that time does not run against the king (*nec … currit tempus contra regem*, Bracton, ed. Woodbine–Thorne ii. 58), so that he can always recover what was wrongly taken. *Iuxta etatem* is obscure; it may point to the related rule that a king on coming of age may revoke grants made in his minority (LEDGER DEA-2). *Iustificare iniuriam*: "set right an injury".
[^11]: **S.** 1 Cor. 16:22, loosely: Vulgate "Si quis non amat Dominum nostrum Jesum Christum, sit anathema". *Non sufficiunt sibi ostendere hic plus amoris*: no greater love can be shown in this life. C, D invert *non suff. vian.*; B¹ adds *ex fide* after *sibi*.
[^12]: **S.** Aristotle, *Ethics* VI.2 (1139b10), quoting Agathon: God is deprived of this alone, to make undone what has been done (LEDGER DEA-3). C reads *diminui* for *dirimi*.
[^13]: **C.** The sentence is compressed: "if charters were not proof (*facere fidem*) concerning human revenues, secular lords would be left desolate". C reads *habet* for *licet* (88.14).
[^14]: **R.** The "great charter" is Magna Carta, whose first clause grants that the English church be free; the king's coronation oath to keep the church's liberties; and the sentences of excommunication against infringers of the charters (LEDGER DEA-4). C, D invert *coronacionis sue*.
[^15]: **R.** The saints are chiefly Thomas Becket, killed in defence of the church's liberties (LEDGER DEA-4). B¹ reads *defessione*. C, D reorder *priv. illum dest. foret* (88.23).
[^16]: **S.** Solomon's deposition of Abiathar and appointment of Zadok: 3 Kings (1 Kings) 2:26–27, 35. The printed *3°* (chapter 3) does not match; Pollard's margin reference is "1 Kings ii. 27" (LEDGER DEA-5). C adds *Abyatar* after *sacerdotem*. *Licenter*, "licitly", anticipates *licencia*, "leave", below.
[^17]: **C.** *Dicunt fideles reges cognoscere*: taken as "the faithful say that kings know"; also possible, "they say that faithful kings know". C, D read *caveat* for *corrigat* (89.6); B¹ *nec* for *nisi* (89.9).
[^18]: **C/R.** Literally "For otherwise not an earthly king would require such blasphemies according to his law, and deny this of God". Construed as: it would be absurd for an earthly king to prosecute unlicensed grants under his law while God's leave was held unnecessary. The human law meant is probably the English mortmain legislation, which required the king's licence for alienation to the church (LEDGER DEA-6).
[^19]: **G/T.** *Ius poli ... ius soli*: "the law of heaven ... the law of the soil", *ius* taken as body of law (Pollard: "heavenly law ... earthly law"). C, D read *fori* for *soli*, giving the familiar pair *ius poli* / *ius fori*, the law of heaven and the law of the court. C, D invert *fin. sim.* (89.20).
[^20]: **C/T.** *Et licet non intendimus … defendere* (89.31–90.5): the *licet* clause has no main clause of its own; *aliter foret contraria fidei* belongs inside the *quod* clause ("that it is not valid … except in so far as it accords with God's law, otherwise it would be contrary to faith"). The English keeps the anacoluthon. *Concitor*: "mover, instigator"; possibly an error for *conditor*, "founder, author". Translated as printed. *Cum aliis promoventibus ad hanc vitam*: "with others urging [men] on to this life"; the object is not expressed. C, D read *vocent* (90.9).
[^21]: **S.** John 11:50–51; Wyclif's *unum hominem mori pro populo* agrees in substance with the Vulgate; DR wording used. C, D omit *ad* (90.23 *ad statum*); C, D read *regnum* and B¹ *regni* at 90.16.
[^22]: **C.** The play is *privilegia que privant*: "privileges" that "deprive". C, D add after *privilegia*: *vel vocare cartas tales privilegia*.
[^23]: **S.** Jer. 2:13 ("the fountain of living water") and 2:18 ("to drink the troubled water"); DR wording for *aquam turbidam*. Pollard's side-note shows that the second confirmation, the witness of saints, is the "remote custom" answered here.
[^24]: **T.** *Huius dyaboli*: C, D read *unius* for *huius* ("of a devil") and *Si* for *sicut* (91.16).
[^25]: **C.** *In peiorari*, a gerund-like infinitive: "in worsening". *A principio generis Scariothis*: literally "from the beginning (i.e. the head) of the stock of Iscariot". Matt. 26:15 ("thirty pieces of silver"). C, D read *occiderunt* for *occiderant*.
[^26]: **R.** *Occidui Machometi*: Wyclif's jibe at the Latin church as a "western" power claiming world rule, against the Greek and Indian churches. At 91.24 C reads *occidui*, B¹ *occidium*, D *occidunt*.
[^27]: **C.** The *cum*-clause has no main clause. Read with the next sentence's argument: the westerners' holiness yields at most a probable argument, not faith. *Sapienter*, an adverb, stands where the sense wants *sapientes*. B¹ adds *vel* after *fidem* (91.32).
[^28]: **S.** The principle that the first in each genus is the measure of the rest: Aristotle, *Metaphysics* X.1 (LEDGER DEA-7). *Predicamentum* is "category" (GLOSSARY), here the standard member of a category. B¹ reads *sit* for *fit* (92.1).
[^29]: **C.** *In nostra contracta*: the feminine noun is unexpressed; "church" supplied, though "land" (*terra*) is possible. C, D read *vel* for *aut* (92.5).
[^30]: **S.** Peter's denial before Pentecost, and after it his rebuke at Antioch (Gal. 2:11, "he was to be blamed").
[^31]: **C.** *Quod sit recens et plenissimus antichristus*: the subject is unexpressed; taken as the pope, from the preceding comparison; also possible, "that there is a recent and most complete antichrist".
[^32]: **T/S.** *Quia aut* is odd; perhaps for *Quia autem*; translated as "For". Averroes's dictum that truth agrees with itself in every way (cf. Aristotle, *Prior Analytics* I.32 and *Ethics* I.8, "omni vero consonat verum"), and the figure of truth growing fragrant, like spice, the more it is rubbed (LEDGER DEA-8).
