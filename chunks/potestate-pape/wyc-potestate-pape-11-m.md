# *De potestate pape*, chapter 11, part 13: all the holy are priests; the two orders of the apostles

## ¶1 · 312.1–312.7

> [p. 312] Tercio obicitur per hoc, quod iuxta hic dicta nec oportet esse sacerdotes, nec oportet sacramentum ordinis iterari factum per unum non episcopum sed simplicem sacerdotem; et sic peccarent sacerdotes benedicendo aliquem, clericorum ordinem omittendo et subiectos ultra debitum onerando.

Thirdly [it is objected, by appeal to]{g:obicitur-per} this, that according to what is said here there need be no [priests]{g:sacerdos}, nor need the sacrament of order be repeated when it is conferred by one who is not a [bishop]{g:episcopus} but a simple priest; and so priests would sin in blessing anyone, omitting the order of clerics and burdening subjects beyond what is owed.[^1]

## ¶2 · 312.8–312.21

> [p. 312] Hic dicitur negando primam consequenciam, cum oportet esse Christum et suos apostolos, ymo omnes sacerdotes sacramentales et devotum populum. Scribit enim Augustinus XX de civitate Dei, capitulo XII super illo, quod dicit propheta Apoc. XX 4: Sederunt sacerdotes Dei et Christi et regnabunt cum ipso mille annis; non utique de solis episcopis et presbiteris dictum est, qui proprie iam vocantur in ecclesia sacerdotes, sed sicut omnes christianos dicimus propter misticum carisma, sic omnes sacerdotes, quoniam membra sunt unius sacerdotis, de quibus apostolus Petrus ait: Plebs sancta, regale sacerdocium. Et idem dicit Crysostomus Omelia XL in Imperfecto.

Here [the answer is]{g:dicitur} by denying the first [inference]{g:consequencia}, since there must be Christ and His apostles, indeed all sacramental priests and the devout people. For Augustine writes in book 20 of *The City of God*, chapter 12, on what the prophet says in Apoc. 20:4: "'Priests of God and of Christ sat, and they shall reign with him a thousand years': this is surely not said of bishops and [presbyters]{g:presbiter} alone, who are now properly called priests in the [church]{g:ecclesia}; but as we call all [men] Christians because of the mystical chrism, so [we call] all priests, since they are [members]{g:membrum} of the one priest; of whom the apostle Peter says: 'A holy people, a kingly priesthood.'"[^2] And Chrysostom says the same in the *Opus imperfectum*, homily 40.[^3]

## ¶3 · 312.23–312.26

> [p. 312] Ex istis patet, primo quod femina est sacerdos, et (sic ut dictum est) non probabitur ex iure canonico vel civili quod supradicta femina non sit papa vel quod non valeant opera eius papalia.

From these it is clear, first, that a woman is a priest; and (thus, as has been said) it will not be proved from canon or civil [law]{g:ius} that the woman mentioned above is not [pope]{g:papa}, or that her papal works are not valid.[^4]

## ¶4 · 312.27–314.29

> [p. 312] Patet secundo ex dictis quod sicut omnes sancti viri et femine membra Christi sunt sacerdotes, quia sacramentum dantes ex vi communionis sanctorum, sic solum predestinati sunt membra Christi vel pars sancte matris ecclesie et [p. 313] proprie christiani, ut pulcre docet Crysostomus in Imperfecto Omelia XIX: Solum enim continuatos corpori Christi dicit esse christianos; cum itaque omnia que evenient de necessitate evenient, patet quod necesse est esse hos sacerdotes triplices eciam sacerdotes Baal. Sed quantum ad intentum conceditur quod sine quocumque sacerdote cesareo ecclesia posset viare prosperius quam nunc viat. Ideo non credamus eis, qui iurant quod nisi iste episcopus foret papa, perirent omnia ecclesiastica sacramenta; starent enim melius quam modo, licet non foret aliquis papa cesareus, cum post diem iudicii manebit corpus Christi, et sic sacramentum et res sacramenti, cessantibus aliis onerosis ritibus cum istis viancium sacramentis. Quantum ad secundam difficultatem, credo quod sepe peccant tam ordinantes clericos quam ipsi clerici, quam eciam populares; qui enim vadunt ad curiam Romanam, ut sint episcopi eciam Nullatenses, sed vivendo vitam suffraganeam prelati cesarei, ordinant clericos, benedicunt ecclesias et vivunt seorsum vitam lautam, laborantes propter commodum temporale, peccant graviter et sunt multi apostate. Sed gravius peccant, qui prava intencione menciuntur se esse a papa ad hoc officium deputatos. Gravissime autem qui prava intencione menciuntur in talia, dum tamen non sint infra sacros ordines ecclesiastice constituti. Et correspondenter peccant clerici ordinati, dum tamen manserint in eis; apud Deum humilitas non obest, sed consonat quod per simplices sacerdotes presumentes et sic ordinando peccantes sint legittime ordinati. [p. 314] Unde notando peccata sic ordinancium et dicta sanctorum de symonia episcopi ordinantis pauci vel nulli forent clerici a prelato cesareo ordinati. Ideo non debemus timere de talibus, dum secundum puram conscienciam de iustis operibus observamus ordinem clericalem, quia tunc Deus supplet vicem episcopi et omnia que sequuntur ex istis sunt intrepide concedenda, ut confessum in gravi infirmitate de peccato pro quo sedes apostolica foret petenda, dum contritus fuerit et satisfaciens convalescens non oportet petere Romanam curiam vel prelatum supra simplicem sacerdotem. Et iterum ordinatum per unum sacerdotem simplicem non oportet timere de presbiteratu vel alias ordinari, dum tamen rite fecerit officium clericale, quia sufficit prudens reputacio cum diligenti officio. Deus enim tunc supplet, quia aliter quilibet recipiens sacramentum de suo preposito fluctuaret. Nec convenit aliquem presumere quod immediate recipit ordinem sacerdotalem a Deo, sicut fecerunt Moyses et apostoli, licet concedi debeat, quod taliter potest esse, quia sine revelacione sic presumere foret stulticia scandalosa; ymo videtur quod homo pocius pateretur symoniam prepositi, dicens tamen ordinanti periculum qui sic mercatur cum ministerio sacramenti, quam temere confideret in insensibili Dei ordinacione mundo abscondita.

It is clear, secondly, from what has been said, that just as all holy men and women, members of Christ, are priests, because they give a sacrament by virtue of the communion of saints, so only [the predestinate]{g:predestinatus} are members of Christ, or part of holy mother church, and [13] properly Christians, as Chrysostom finely teaches in the *Opus imperfectum*, homily 19: for he says that "only those joined to the body of Christ are Christians".[^5] Since, then, all things that will come about will come about of necessity, it is clear that these threefold priests must exist, even priests of Baal.[^6] But as to the point at issue, it is granted that without any caesarean priest whatever the church could [be a wayfarer]{g:viator} more prosperously than it now is.[^7] Therefore let us not believe those who swear that unless this bishop were pope all the ecclesiastical sacraments would perish; for they would stand better than now, even if there were no [caesarean pope]{g:papa-cesareus}, since after the day of judgement the body of Christ will remain, and so the sacrament and the [reality of the sacrament]{g:res-sacramenti}, the other burdensome [rites]{g:ritus} ceasing together with these sacraments of [those on the road]{g:viantes}.[^8] As to the second difficulty, I believe that often both those who ordain clerics sin, and the [clerics]{g:clericus} themselves, and the people as well; for those who go to [the Roman curia]{g:curia-romana} so as to be bishops, even [bishops "of Nowhere"]{g:episcopus-nullatensis}, but, living the suffragan life of a caesarean [prelate]{g:prelatus}, ordain clerics, bless churches, and live apart a luxurious life, labouring for temporal advantage, sin gravely, and many are apostates.[^9] But they sin more gravely who with wicked intention lie that they have been [deputed]{g:deputacio} by the pope to this office. And most gravely those who with wicked intention falsely take on such things, while they are not established within the holy orders of the church. And correspondingly the clerics so ordained sin, while they remain in them; with God humility does no harm, but it accords [with reason] that those [ordained] by simple priests who presume, and so sin in ordaining, are lawfully ordained.[^10] [14] Hence, noting the sins of those who so ordain, and the sayings of the saints on the simony of the ordaining bishop, few or no clerics would be ordained by a caesarean prelate.[^11] Therefore we ought not to fear about such things, so long as, according to a pure conscience about just works, we keep the clerical order; for then God supplies the place of the bishop, and all things that [follow]{g:sequitur} from these are to be granted fearlessly: as that one who has confessed in grave infirmity a sin for which the apostolic see would have to be sought, provided he was contrite and making satisfaction, need not, on recovering, seek the Roman curia or a prelate above a simple priest. And again, one ordained by a single simple priest need not fear about his presbyterate, or be ordained otherwise, provided he has duly performed the clerical office, for prudent estimation with diligent office suffices. For God then supplies, because otherwise anyone receiving a sacrament would be wavering about his [superior]{g:prepositi}. Nor is it fitting for anyone to presume that he receives the priestly order immediately from God, as Moses and the apostles did, although it must be granted that it can be so; for without revelation so to presume would be a scandalous folly; indeed it seems that a man should rather suffer the simony of the superior, telling the ordainer, however, the danger [to one] who so trades with the ministry of a sacrament, than rashly trust in an [imperceptible]{g:insensibilis} [ordination]{g:ordinacio} of God hidden from the world.[^12]

## ¶5 · 314.30–315.5

> [p. 314] In istis tamen illusionibus prelatorum propter manifestam et onerosam multitudinem inconveniencium oportet divinam iusticiam ipsas detegere et errores modo absconditos tempore suo destruere. Modo enim servatur ut evangelium, [p. 315] quod sint quinque ordines celebrati; in tempore autem apostoli non erant nisi duo ordines ad ordinacionem ecclesie, scilicet dyaconus et sacerdos, ut patet legenti epistolas Pauli, et meminit archidyaconus cum sanctis doctoribus.

Yet in these illusions of prelates, because of the manifest and burdensome multitude of [ill consequences]{g:inconveniencia}, divine justice must [expose]{g:detegere} them and in its time destroy the errors now hidden.[^13] For now it is kept as gospel [15] that there are five orders celebrated; but in the time of the apostle there were only two orders for the ordinance of the church, namely deacon and priest, as is clear to one who reads the epistles of Paul, and the Archdeacon recalls it, with the holy doctors.[^14]

## ¶6 · 315.6–315.27

> [p. 315] Ex veteri vero et novo testamento debent esse septem ordines, ideo eleccio quinarii medii videtur figurare fidelibus quod primus numerus superbus scilicet quinarius facit nostros prepositos taliter variare. Sicut enim omnis christianus et specialiter bonus presbiter est sacerdos, sic est spiritualiter hostiarius, ceroferarius, lector, exorcista, subdyaconus, dyaconus et sacerdos, ut alias diffuse exposui. Et sic videtur quod ecclesia posset viare facilius, contenta de duobus ordinibus, in quibus residua spiritualiter includuntur. Nec obest, quod Augustinus dicit in libello suo De Heresibus, quod Heriani ab Herio quodam sunt dicti, qui, cum esset presbiter, doluit fortiter quod episcopus non potuit ordinari, et in Arrianorum heresim lapsus propria queque doctrinata addidisse, nonnulla dicens offerri pro dormientibus non oportere, nec statuta sollempniter celebranda esse ieiunia, sed cum quibus voluerit esse ieiunandum, ne videatur esse sub lege. Dicebat eciam presbiterum ab episcopo nulla differencia debere discerni.

But from the old and new testament there ought to be seven orders; therefore the choice of the middle five seems to signify to the faithful that the first proud number, namely five, makes our superiors vary in this way.[^15] For just as every Christian, and especially a good presbyter, is a priest, so he is spiritually doorkeeper, taper-bearer, lector, exorcist, subdeacon, deacon and priest, as I have expounded at length elsewhere.[^16] And so it seems that the church could journey more easily, content with two orders, in which the rest are spiritually included. Nor is it an obstacle that Augustine says in his little book *On Heresies* that "the Herians are named from a certain Herius, who, when he was a presbyter, grieved greatly that he could not be ordained bishop, and, having fallen into the heresy of the Arians, [is said] to have added certain doctrines of his own, saying that one ought not to offer for the sleeping, nor that the appointed fasts should be solemnly kept, but that one should fast whenever one will, lest one seem to be under the law. He said also that a presbyter ought to be distinguished from a bishop by no difference."[^17]

## ¶7 · 315.28–316.23

> [p. 315] In capitulo Herrii videntur michi esse Fratres, qui sumptuose, laboriose et periculose aquirunt episcopatus officia, ut seculariter voluptuose et ociose dimissa prius commendata obediencia conversentur. Non enim sonat directe ad utilitatem ecclesie vel religionis augmentum quod sic vivunt, quia per idem quilibet frater aptus ad regimen [p. 316] sic dissolveret claustrale vinculum. Sed iuxta principia fidei dimitteret dotacionem et conversacionem cesaream proficiendo subiectis episcopaliter, sicut fecerunt apostoli. Hoc enim plus congrueret professioni sue primeve. Sed iste sanctus dicit quod Herius doluit, quod episcopus non potuit ordinari, quia noluit esse sub lege; et sic incidit in istum errorem, quod presbiterum simplicem et episcopum non oportet discrimen per potestatem iurisdicionis atque scienciam, quia tunc iste Herius propter inhabilitatem ad officium episcopale pateretur repulsam. Sed absit logicum istud credere illud dictum Augustini repugnare decreto Jeronymi: Quod olim idem fuit episcopus et sacerdos, olim enim erant pauci episcopi atque ydonei, qui seclusi a seculo non propter mundum, sed propter Deum et amorem ecclesie curam pastoralis officii perfecerunt; et sic error esset hodie negare presbiterum ab episcopo nulla differencia discerni, cum multi presbiteri instructi in lege Domini vivunt sancte, et multi episcopi ignari legis Dei vivunt contrarie. Ideo necesse est esse differenciam sacerdotum.

Under the heading of Herius, it seems to me, are the [friars]{g:frater}, who sumptuously, laboriously and dangerously acquire the offices of bishoprics, so that, having first abandoned the obedience committed to them, they may live in a worldly, voluptuous and idle way.[^18] For that they live so does not tend directly to the usefulness of the church or the increase of religion; for by the same token any friar fit for rule [16] would so dissolve the cloistral bond. But according to the principles of faith he would give up the [endowment]{g:dotacio} and the caesarean [manner of life]{g:conversacio}, profiting his subjects in the manner of a bishop, as the apostles did. For this would better suit his first profession. But that saint says that Herius grieved that he could not be ordained bishop, because he would not be under the law; and so he fell into this error, that a simple presbyter and a bishop need not be distinguished by the [power of jurisdiction]{g:potestas-iurisdiccionis} and by knowledge; for then this Herius, because of his lack of qualification for the episcopal office, would suffer rejection.[^19] But far be it from a logician to believe that saying of Augustine to [be incompatible]{g:repugnare} with the decree of Jerome, "that once bishop and priest were the same";[^20] for once there were few bishops, and suitable ones, who, secluded from the world, not for the world's sake but for God's and for love of the church, carried out the care of the pastoral office; and so it would be an error today to deny that a presbyter is distinguished from a bishop by no difference, since many presbyters instructed in the law of the Lord live holily, and many bishops ignorant of the law of God live the contrary way.[^21] Therefore there must be a difference among priests.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** The objection draws three consequences from the lay-baptism argument of 11-k and 11-l: priests are unnecessary; an ordination by a simple priest need not be repeated; and priests who bless (ordain) under the present rite sin by burdening their subjects. *Benedicendo aliquem*: blessing anyone into orders. A notes in the margin *Tercio arguitur*.
[^2]: **S.** Augustine, *De civitate Dei* 20.10; Wyclif says ch. 12, Loserth gives ch. 10 (Opp. VII, 588; PL 41, 676), rightly. The verse is Augustine's form: the Vulgate has "sat upon them" at Apoc. 20:4 and "they shall be priests of God and of Christ: and shall reign with him a thousand years" at 20:6 (DR). Peter's words are 1 Pet. 2:9 in Augustine's wording (DR "a kingly priesthood, a holy nation"). **T.** *Carisma* (312.16) is the print's spelling; Augustine's word is *chrisma*, chrism, on which the name "Christians" turns, so we render "chrism".
[^3]: **S.** Pseudo-Chrysostom, *Opus imperfectum in Matthaeum*, hom. 40; Loserth cites Chrysostom, Opp. VI, 856 (LEDGER P11m-2).
[^4]: **C.** "The woman mentioned above" is the woman pope of 11-l (309). **R.** Both manuscripts note at 312.23 *Femina sacerdos papa*. The OCR's *lure* (312.24) is the print's *iure*.
[^5]: **S.** Pseudo-Chrysostom, *Opus imperfectum*, hom. 19; Loserth "pag. 706 seqq." (313.2); PG 56, 739–40, where only those who hold Christ's truth, not merely his name, are true Christians; Wyclif summarises. The quotation bounds follow Loserth's italics, which leave *Solum enim* outside. Both manuscripts note at 312.8 *Omnes sancti christiani sunt sacerdotes*.
[^6]: **C.** *Hos sacerdotes triplices*: we take the three kinds as the sacramental priests, the holy who are priests as members of Christ, and the priests of Baal (the reprobate clergy); the text does not enumerate them.
[^7]: **G.** *Viare* "to be a wayfarer" (the verb of *viator*); at 315.13 the same verb is "journey" where "be a wayfarer more easily" would not read. **C.** *Sacerdos cesareus*: "caesarean priest", of the endowed clergy (GLOSSARY *prelatus / sacerdos cesarius*).
[^8]: **C.** *Iste episcopus*: "this bishop", one particular claimant to the papacy, unnamed. The sacrament that remains after judgement is the body of Christ as *res sacramenti*, the reality, the church itself; the sacraments of those on the road cease with the other rites.
[^9]: **R.** B notes in the margin at 313.31 *Vadentes curiam Romanam pro sacerdocio*; A at 313.32 *Cave*, "beware", and both at 313.29 *Nota hic*.
[^10]: **C.** The print's semicolon after *in eis* divides the clause; *in eis* may refer to the orders or to the sins. *Consonat*: we supply "with reason". *Menciuntur in talia*: "lie [their way] into such things", that is, falsely take on episcopal acts.
[^11]: **C.** The sense: if the simony of ordaining bishops voided orders, hardly any cleric ordained by a caesarean prelate would be ordained at all. **R.** A notes at 314.4 *Cave foveam*, "beware the pit".
[^12]: **C.** *Pateretur symoniam prepositi*: a man should rather submit to the simoniac superior's ordination, warning him of his danger, than claim an ordination direct from God. *Dicens tamen ordinanti periculum qui sic mercatur*: "telling the ordainer the danger [of one] who so trades"; the relative *qui* refers to the ordainer.
[^13]: **C.** *Inconveniencia* (CONTEXTUAL): here the burdensome ill consequences in the church's affairs, not absurdities in argument.
[^14]: **S.** The Archdeacon is Guido de Baysio, the canonist (*Rosarium* on the *Decretum*); Wyclif gives no place. The likeliest is the *Rosarium* on D. 93 c. 24 *Legimus* (Venice 1481, fol. image 222), where bishop and presbyter are one order in the early church: after Huguccio, "from the beginning there was a difference … but as to the name there was none", and after Laurentius, *ante enim episcopus et presbyter idem erant etiam quo ad sacra*, "for before, bishop and presbyter were the same even as to the sacred [rites]". His gloss on D. 60 c. 4 (Urban II on the two sacred orders) says nothing to the point (LEDGER P11m-4). **R.** B notes at 314.34 *Ordines clericorum quinque*.
[^15]: **C.** *Quinarius medius*: the five middle orders between the two extreme ones, taking seven in all. *Primus numerus superbus*: five, perhaps as the first "proud" (abundant?) number; the arithmetic sense is uncertain, and the construal is ours.
[^16]: **S.** "Elsewhere": a reference to Wyclif's own works, not identified; cf. *De ecclesia* c. 21 (p. 510), on the seven orders, and *De civili dominio* II.8 (pp. 73–74), on Christ exercising each of them (LEDGER P11m-5). **R.** A notes at 315.18 *Nota et tene*.
[^17]: **S.** Augustine, *De haeresibus* 53, on Aerius; Loserth: "Aeriani ab Aerio etc. Aug. Opp. tom. VIII, 18" (PL 42, 39–40). Wyclif spells the name *Herius*; we keep his spelling. **T.** Augustine's text reads *doluisse fertur … addidisse*: the print's *doluit fortiter* leaves *addidisse* without a governing verb, so we supply "[is said]" (LEDGER P11m-7). *Doctrinata* stands for Augustine's *dogmata*; the print's *cum quibus voluerit* ("with those with whom one will") is a corruption of Augustine's *cum quisque voluerit*, and the English follows Augustine: "whenever one will" (our emendation; LEDGER P11m-8). "The sleeping" (*dormientibus*) are the dead.
[^18]: **C.** *In capitulo Herrii*: "under the heading of Herius", the friar-bishops belong to the same class as Aerius, who sought a bishopric. *Commendata obediencia*: the obedience of the order committed to them.
[^19]: **C.** *Quia noluit esse sub lege* is Wyclif's gloss on Augustine's "lest he seem to be under the law". *Non oportet discrimen*: there need be no difference between presbyter and bishop. The *quia tunc* clause gives Aerius's motive: if there were such a difference, his unfitness would have barred him. **T.** At 316.16 Loserth's apparatus reports a variant *qui* / B *quia*; its place in the line is unclear.
[^20]: **S.** *Decretum* D. 95 c. 5 (Loserth "Decreti Prima Pars, Dist. XCV, cap. V"; form ours), from Jerome on Titus 1; the canon begins *Olim idem erat presbyter, qui et episcopus*.
[^21]: **C.** The double negative is the print's: to deny that there is no difference between presbyter and bishop would be an error today, because now the difference often runs in the presbyter's favour. Wyclif turns Aerius's thesis on its head rather than adopting it.
