# *De benedicta incarnacione*, chapter 3, part 2: the threefold descent and the despoiling of hell

## ¶1 · 32.25–33.6

> [p. 32] Unde videtur michi scripturas insolubiles appropriate¹² tribuere persone Christi descensum¹³ ad inferos et spoliacionem fidelium de potencia inimici. Nam Psalmo 67 dicitur ad litteram¹⁴ de Christo dividente spolia ‘Ascendisti¹⁴ in altum’ sed prius ‘cepisti captivitatem,’ et 3°. ‘dedisti dona hominibus.’ Cui¹⁵ sensui alludit Apostolus ad Ephes. 4. [p. 33] ‘Ascendens,¹ inquit, Christus in altum,² captivam duxit captivitatem.’ Et sequitur: ‘quod autem ascendit, quid³ est, nisi quia descendit primum ad partes inferiores terre?’ Et ut ostenderet nobis quod eadem sit persona, que hec cuncta perficiat, subiungit: ‘qui descendit ipse est et qui ascendit super omnes celos ut impleret omnia.’

Hence it seems to me that the scriptures, which cannot be loosed,[^1] appropriately attribute to the [person]{g:persona} of Christ the descent into hell and the [despoiling]{g:spoliacio} of the faithful out of the [power]{g:potencia}[^2] of the enemy. For in Psalm 67 it is said literally of Christ dividing the spoils, "Thou hast ascended on high," but first "thou hast led captivity captive," and third "thou hast given gifts to men."[^3] To this sense the Apostle alludes in Ephesians 4: [33] "Christ ascending on high," he says, "led captivity captive." And it follows: "Now that he ascended, what is it, but because he descended first into the lower parts of the earth?" And to show us that it is the same person who accomplishes all these things, he adds: "He that descended is the same also that ascended above all the heavens, that he might fill all things."[^4]

## ¶2 · 33.7–33.16

> [p. 33] Ex istis verbis Apostoli patet primo quod loquitur de Christo secundum humanitatem, secundum quam solum ascendere et descendere sibi conveniunt.⁴ 2°. probat illum hominem esse Deum, cum post ascensionem dedit spiritualia dona hominibus mittendo eis Spiritum Sanctum, ut testatur utrumque testamentum et rei eventus probaverat in effectu. 3°. intendit quod eadem persona secundum humanitatem eandem descendit et⁵ taliter⁵ ascendit. ‘Qui descendit, inquit, ipse et non alius ascendit,’ racionem autem subiungit ‘ut impleret omnia.’

From these words of the Apostle it is clear, first, that he speaks of Christ [as to the humanity]{g:secundum-humanitatem}, as to which alone ascending and descending belong to him. Second, he proves that that man is God, since after the ascension he gave spiritual gifts to men by sending them the Holy Spirit, as both testaments testify and the outcome of the matter had proved in effect. Third, he [maintains]{g:intendere} that the same person, as to the same [humanity]{g:humanitas}, descended and in such a way ascended. "He that descended," he says, "is himself, and not another, that ascended"; and he adds the reason, "that he might fill all things."

## ¶3 · 33.17–34.13

> [p. 33] Cum enim tres sint⁶ regiones mundi notabiles,⁷ in quibus oportuit Christum humanitus possessionem accipere vel sesinam,⁸ scilicet celestium, terrestrium, et infernorum; proporcionaliter⁹ descendit tripliciter benefaciens et repetens omagium de¹⁰ subiectis. Primo descendit per inanicionem secundum infirmitatem carnis, et¹¹ eum mundus infirmus sibi similis in carne¹¹ et anima eciam genus proprium non cognovit. 2°. Descendit ad patres in limbo qui erant tantum¹² in spiritu post triumphum sed proporcionaliter in anima,¹² et indubie fuit in illo triduo in sinu Abrahe per 36 horas, quibus passio¹³ continuata fuerat, non parva exaltacio capite coniuncto membris cum multitudine angelorum. Sicut enim sexto die creatum est genus humanum,¹⁴ sic sexto senario, qui est numerus perfectus ex ductu eius in se circulariter, [p. 34] fuerat recreatum; ut¹ per tantum tempus clarere² posset veritas et excludi³ ficticia mortis Chrsti. Resurrexit autem, ut confortaret ecclesiam de fide resurreccionis future, ut patet ad Cor. 15;⁴ et ut ‘primatum teneat’ ut abbas ordinis Christiani aperiendo⁵ patribus complete purgatis ianuam eternalem. Tempus autem continens purum integrum diem et duas seminoctes significat quod simpla mors Christi,⁶ que integraliter fuit lucida proporcionaliter ad vitam, fuit medium sufficiens ad nostram duplicem mortem semiplenam. Tercius vero⁷ fuit descensus⁸ corporis in sepulchro,⁹ qui nedum ostendit corpus vere mortuum anima ab eo¹⁰ elongata, sed per omnia factus nobis consimilis sanctificavit subterraneas requies beandorum.

For since there are three notable regions of the world in which Christ had [humanly]{g:humanitus} to take possession or [seisin]{g:sessiva}, namely [the regions] of the heavenly, the earthly and the infernal, proportionally he descended in three ways, doing good and claiming [homage]{g:omagium} from his subjects.[^5] First he descended by [emptying]{g:exinanicio}, according to the [weakness]{g:infirmitas} of the flesh, and the weak world, like him in flesh and soul, even his own kind, did not know him.[^6] Second, he descended to the fathers in [limbo]{g:limbus}, who were only in spirit after the triumph but proportionally in soul;[^7] and without doubt he was in those [three days]{g:triduum} in the bosom of Abraham for 36 hours, through which the passion had continued,[^8] no small exaltation, the head joined to the members, with a multitude of angels. For just as the human race was created on the sixth day, so in the sixth senary, which is a perfect number from its being multiplied into itself circularly, [34] it was re-created;[^9] so that through so long a time the truth could shine forth and the [fiction]{g:ficticia} of the death of Christ be excluded. And he rose again to strengthen the [church]{g:ecclesia} in the faith of the future resurrection, as is clear from 1 Corinthians 15; and so that "he may hold the [primacy]{g:primatus},"[^10] as abbot of the Christian order, opening the eternal gate to the fathers fully purged. And the time, containing one pure whole day and two half-nights, signifies that the single death of Christ, which was wholly bright in proportion to life, was a sufficient means for our double, half-full death.[^11] And the third was the descent of the body into the sepulchre, which [not only]{g:nedum} showed the body truly dead, the soul withdrawn from it, but, made in all things like to us, sanctified the underground resting-places of those who are to be blessed.

## ¶4 · 34.14–34.19

> [p. 34] Et correspondenter tres maneries hominum eripuit, et tres¹¹ dimisit. Extraxit enim, quasi mordens infernum, patriarchas prophetas et fideles alios, quorum peccata sunt plene purgata, relictis purgandis in purgatorio et dampnatis in inferno; sive sint infantes solis originalibus, si sit possibile, irretiti; sive adulti peccatis actualibus maculati.

And correspondingly he snatched out three [kinds]{g:maneries} of men, and left three. For he drew out, as if biting hell,[^12] the patriarchs, the prophets and other faithful whose sins were fully purged, leaving those to be purged in purgatory and the damned in hell, whether they be infants entangled in original [sins] alone, if that is possible, or adults stained with actual sins.

## ¶5 · 34.20–35.5

> [p. 34] Sed 3°. post privatam instruccionem apostolorum 40 diebus, ut patet Actuum 1°, ascendit gloriose in celum secundum utramque naturam summe beatus, ducens secum numerum insensibilem suo tempore captivorum; et sic tenendo primatum multiplicem inter omnia supposita humani generis, sicut est prior vel abbas omni dignitate: iuxta illud Baptiste: ‘qui post me venit ante me¹² factus est,’ Ioh. 1°. sic habuit primatum resurgencium, et virtute sui solius ascendentis omnia genera hominum ad se trahentis¹³ fuit primus celum intrancium. Nemo enim sufficit¹⁴ suo imperio portas huius¹⁵ aperire¹⁶ nisi filius hominis,¹⁷ qui notus hostiariis prius descenderat.¹⁷. Et sic indubie sicut illuminavit istas tres [p. 35] machinas sua humanitate impletas, ita suscepit omagium¹ vel obsequium utrobique: cum secundum Apostolum ad Philipp. 2°. ‘oportet quod in nomine Iesu omne² genu flectatur celestium, quos restituit, terrestrium, quos redemit, et infernorum,’ quos spoliavit.

But third, after the private instruction of the apostles for 40 days, as is clear from Acts 1, he ascended gloriously into heaven as to both [natures]{g:natura}, supremely blessed, leading with him a number of captives [imperceptible]{g:insensibilis} in his time;[^13] and so holding a manifold primacy among all the supposits of the human race, as a prior or abbot [is first] in every dignity: according to that [word] of the Baptist, "He that cometh after me was made before me," John 1,[^14] so he had the primacy of those who rise again, and by the [power]{g:virtus} of himself alone, ascending and drawing all kinds of men to himself, he was the first of those entering heaven. For no one suffices by his own [command]{g:imperium} to open the gates of this [place] but the Son of man, who, known to the doorkeepers, had first descended.[^15] And so without doubt, just as he illuminated these three [35] frames, filled with his humanity, so he received homage or [obeisance]{g:obsequium} in both places:[^16] since according to the Apostle in Philippians 2, "it behoves that in the name of Jesus every knee should bow, of those that are in heaven," whom he restored, "on earth," whom he redeemed, "and under the earth," whom he despoiled.[^17]

## ¶6 · 35.6–35.29

> [p. 35] Omnia quidem hec fecit humanitas Christi. Et ideo post tantum honorem triumphalem individui humane nature prohibuit nimirum Iohannem angelus seipsum vel³ dulia adorare, ut⁴ patet Apoc. ultimo capitulo: ante quidem satis permiserant,⁵ ut patet Gen. 18 et Iosue 5. Omnia quidem hec fecit idem filius hominis secundum eandem humanitatem continue⁶ ypostatice copulatum: qua unione habita patet quod oportet Christum esse continue creaturam et aliquid quod prius non fuerat. Sed quid, rogo, si non homo? Ipsa ergo humanitas fecit⁷ hec omnia, id est, ‘in opus ministerii, in edificacionem corporis Christi,’ ut dicit Apostolus ad Ephes. 4°., quia Ioh. 4°. dicitur: ‘meus cibus est ut faciam voluntatem eius, qui⁸ misit me, ut perficiam opus eius,’ id est,⁸ ut perfecte usque ad consummacionem faciam: inter que hoc videtur principium,⁹ descendere ad inferna et'extrahere patres captivos in limbo, sicut ante promiserat. Et huic consonat¹⁰ illud Zacharie¹¹ 9°. ubi post propheciam Messie quod veniet salvator et ipse ‘pauper et ascendens super asinam et super pullum asine’ alloquitur eundem Messiam in hec verba: ‘Tu quoque in sanguine testamenti tui emisisti vinctos tuos de lacu in quo non est aqua.’ Quod¹² exponitur ad litteram de patribus incarceratis in limbo. Et¹³ idem sonat illud Osee 13. verificatum ad litteram de Messia: ‘ero¹⁴ mors tua, o mors; ero morsus tuus, inferne.’¹⁵

All these things indeed the humanity of Christ did. And therefore, after so great a triumphal honour of the [individual]{g:individuum} of human nature, the angel, no wonder, forbade John to adore him even with [dulia]{g:dulia},[^18] as is clear from the Apocalypse, the last chapter; before, indeed, they had permitted it readily enough,[^19] as is clear from Genesis 18 and Joshua 5. All these things indeed the same Son of man did as to the same humanity, continuously [hypostatically]{g:ypostatice} joined: and with that [union]{g:unio} had, it is clear that Christ must continuously be a [creature]{g:creatura} and something that he had not been before. Now what, I ask, if not man? The humanity itself therefore did all these things, that is, "for the work of the ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ," as the Apostle says in Ephesians 4,[^20] for in John 4 it is said: "My meat is to do the will of him that sent me, that I may perfect his work," that is, that I may do it perfectly unto consummation: among which this seems the beginning, to descend into hell and draw out the fathers captive in limbo, as he had promised before. And with this agrees that [word] of Zechariah 9, where, after the prophecy of the Messiah, that the saviour will come, and he "poor, and riding upon an ass, and upon the foal of an ass," he addresses the same Messiah in these words: "Thou also by the blood of thy testament hast sent forth thy prisoners out of the pit, wherein is no water."[^21] Which is expounded literally of the fathers imprisoned in limbo. And that [word] of Hosea 13 sounds the same, [true]{g:verificari} literally of the Messiah: "O death, I will be thy death; O hell, I will be thy bite."

## ¶7 · 35.30–36.5

> [p. 35] Qui ergo sumus,¹⁶ ut impugnemus tenorem¹⁷ verborum fidei vel scripture,¹⁷ specialiter cum sanctioribus et approbatioribus [p. 36] doctoribus visa sit illa logica² subtilis et de virtute sermonis verissima?² Pro certo per idem possemus innovando falsificare reputacione totum corpus biblie³ scripture. Ideo expedit obstare principiis erroris⁴ ne error intollerabilis⁵ consequatur.

Who then are we, that we should impugn the tenor of the words of the faith or of scripture, especially since to holier and [more approved]{g:approbati-doctores} [36] doctors that logic has seemed subtle and most true [in the proper sense of the words]{g:de-virtute-sermonis}?[^22] For certain, by the same token we could, by innovating, falsify in [men's] estimation the whole body of the scripture of the Bible. Therefore it is expedient to withstand the beginnings of error, lest an intolerable error follow.[^23]

## ¶8 · 36.6–36.14

> [p. 36] Item iuxta sic loquentes sequitur quod Christus bis factus fuit homo, bis eciam incarnatus. Nam semel fuit incarnatus et humiliatus⁶ in utero virginis et in morte desiit⁷ esse homo et caro, et post pro instanti⁸ resurreccionis incepit esse homo corporeus: ergo conclusio. Si enim⁹ homo pro quacunque modica¹⁰ morula reticens post loquelam reloquatur,¹¹ bis loquitur; multo evidencius persona, que 33 annis¹² est homo et post per¹³ intercisum triduum non est homo ac tercio post est homo, bis evidenter est homo.

Again, according to those who speak thus, [it follows]{g:sequitur} that Christ was made man twice, twice also incarnate. For once he was incarnate and humbled in the womb of the virgin, and in death he ceased to be man and flesh, and afterwards, at the instant of the resurrection, he began to be a bodily man: therefore the [conclusion]{g:conclusio}. For if a man, keeping silent after speech for however small a pause, speaks again, he speaks twice; much more evidently a person who for 33 years is a man, and afterwards for an interrupted three days is not a man, and third, afterwards, is a man, is evidently twice a man.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** 32.25 *scripturas insolubiles*: "the scriptures, which cannot be loosed" (cf. John 10:35, *non potest solvi scriptura*), not the logicians' *insolubilia*.
[^2]: **C.** 32.27 *potencia inimici*: the enemy's might, so "power", not "potency".
[^3]: **S.** Ps. 67:19 (Hebrew 68:18): the Vulgate has *accepisti dona in hominibus* (DR "thou hast received gifts in men"); Wyclif's *dedisti dona hominibus* follows Eph. 4:8 *dedit dona hominibus*.
[^4]: **S.** Eph. 4:8–10. Wyclif adds *Christus*; Harris prints *Ascendens* with O and the Vulgate, A B C *ascendit*. In 4:9 he lacks the Vulgate's *et* ("also") and reads *ad partes inferiores* for *in inferiores partes*. The words quoted in the next paragraph, *ipse et non alius ascendit*, paraphrase 4:10.
[^5]: **C.** 33.20 *proporcionaliter descendit tripliciter*: the three descents answer to the three regions (cf. Phil. 2:10, quoted at 35.3–5). *Repetens omagium* is the lord's claim on his tenants, with *sesinam* above.
[^6]: **C.** *et eum mundus infirmus sibi similis in carne et anima eciam genus proprium non cognovit*: *genus proprium* is taken in apposition to *mundus* (cf. John 1:10–11). A B C read *et tunc eum mundus infernus similis in carne*.
[^7]: **T.** *qui erant tantum in spiritu post triumphum sed proporcionaliter in anima*: the print hardly construes. O omits *tamen post triumphum*; Harris asks whether to read *qui erant tunc tantum in spiritu sed post triumphum proporcionaliter in anima* ("who were then only in spirit, but after the triumph proportionally in soul"). The print is rendered.
[^8]: **T.** 33.27 *passio*: A B C *possessio* ("the possession had continued").
[^9]: **C.** 33.30 *sexto senario*: the sixth six, the 36 hours. Six is a perfect number, and its square, 36, ends again in 6 (multiplied "circularly").
[^10]: **S.** Col. 1:18.
[^11]: **S.** The single death of Christ set against our double death (of body and of soul) is Augustine's, *De Trinitate* IV.3.5–6, PL 42, 889–891, *Huic ergo duplae morti nostrae Salvator noster impendit simplam suam*; the thirty-six hours and the squared *senarius* are IV.6.10, PL 42, 895, *triginta sex horae sunt, qui est quadratus senarius* (*verified*, LEDGER I03b-1).
[^12]: **S.** 34.15 *quasi mordens infernum*: after Hos. 13:14, "O hell, I will be thy bite" (quoted at the end of the next paragraph but one).
[^13]: **C.** 34.23 *numerum insensibilem suo tempore captivorum*: "imperceptible in his time", that is, the captives were not seen to ascend with him; or "a number past perceiving".
[^14]: **S.** John 1:30 (cf. 1:15): the Vulgate reads *post me venit vir qui ante me factus est* (DR "After me there cometh a man, who is preferred before me").
[^15]: **T.** 34.31 *notus hostiariis*: O *notis hostiariis*; A B C *hostiarius prius descenderet* ("unless the Son of man, as doorkeeper, first descend").
[^16]: **C.** 35.1–2 *machinas ... utrobique*: the "frames" are the three regions of the world (*machina mundi*). *Utrobique* "in both places" stands, though three are named.
[^17]: **S.** Phil. 2:10, with *oportet quod* added ("it behoves that"); the three relative clauses are Wyclif's.
[^18]: **G.** *Dulia*: the veneration due to a creature, below *latria*, due to God alone. Rev. 22:9: "See thou do it not ... Adore God."
[^19]: **T.** *permiserant*: so Harris (*sc. angeli*, the angels); A B C *permiserat*, O *promisserant*. The angels received adoration at Gen. 18:2 and Josh. 5:15.
[^20]: **S.** Eph. 4:12: *in opus ministerii* (the DR text used here prints "for the word of the ministry"; the Vulgate's *opus* is "work").
[^21]: **S.** Zech. 9:9, 9:11. Wyclif reads *super pullum asine* for the Vulgate's *super pullum filium asinae* (DR "upon a colt, the foal of an ass").
[^22]: **T.** 36.1 *illa logica subtilis et de virtute sermonis verissima*: A B C read *lingua subtilis et de vi verbi sit vera* ("the language [is] subtle and true by the force of the word"). *Approbatioribus*: A B C *approbatis*, O *approbatoribus*.
[^23]: **S.** *Obstare principiis*: Ovid, *Remedia amoris* 91 (*principiis obsta*), *Principiis obsta; sero medicina paratur*, proverbial (*verified*, LEDGER I03b-2).
