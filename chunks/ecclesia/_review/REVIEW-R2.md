# Four-class review R2: *De ecclesia* chunks 05-f, 06-a, 06-d, 06-g

Reviewer: independent, fresh session, 2026-10-03. Method: each chunk read once, sentence by sentence, Latin against English, checked against CARD.md (four classes, connective table), CONVENTIONS §§6–9 and §14, GLOSSARY.md (grepped by term), the italics rule (M5-RESUME brief 45) and the standing emendation rule (M5-RESUME, Rulings 2026-10-02). Sources were not re-verified. Line numbers are taken from the plates where I opened them (pp. 112, 118, 119, 127, 137). Elsewhere they are placed from the side-notes and the apparatus and marked "c.".

Classes: 1 terms · 2 connectives · 3 medieval sense · 4 construal, or a note that contradicts the text. **Q** marks a quotation-bounds row under the italics rule. It is a class-4 matter, but I count it separately. Italics were checked on the plates for pp. 112, 118, 119, 127 and 137 only. The other pages of these chunks (113–117, 120, 126, 128–130, 138–141) were not checked for italics.

**Cross-chunk finding (markup).** `lint.py` checks glossary tags only where the key matches `\{g:([a-z-]+)\}`. A key with a space or a capital letter (`{g:ex fide}`, `{g:mater nostra}`, `{g:corpus Christi misticum}`) is never checked at all. CONVENTIONS §11 asks for keys in lower case with hyphens. The volume has **276** spaced keys, against 2,811 well-formed ones, so this is not peculiar to these chunks. The rows below list the ones in R2. A single mechanical pass that hyphenates and lower-cases every key, then lints, would clear the volume.

## State of each chunk

- **05-f**: Sound. The ten arguments are construed correctly and the doubtful spots carry C notes. The connectives are right except one *sed … sed* chain in the fourth argument. One tag and one note are stale against a ruling made on these lines.
- **06-a**: Sound English. Most of the rows are bookkeeping: tags and notes that predate the 2026-10-02 rulings (*capitatus*, *denominacio*) and quotation bounds on p. 118. The one error is in n. 20, which states the part/whole inference backwards. The English beside it is right.
- **06-d**: The hardest chunk of the four, and well noted. It has one real error: the *deducendo pure creaturam* clause is rendered opaquely, and n. 8 explains it backwards. Everything else is labelling.
- **06-g**: Good English. The remaining work is glossary conformity to rulings made after drafting (*res sacramenti*, the four graces, *denominacio*). Two silent construals need a note or a fix: *ad membra et omne*, and the quantifier in *omnis … non sit*.
