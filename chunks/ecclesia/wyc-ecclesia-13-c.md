# *De ecclesia*, chapter 13, part 3: two objections: the old law's perpetual alms, and the merit of larger and longer alms

## ¶1 · 283.1–283.10

> [p. 283] Contra dicta obicitur primo per hoc quod in veteri testamento erant multe tales elemosine, religiones et ritus perpetui approbati, cum ergo tante auctoritatis sit vetus testamentum ut novum, sequitur quod licitum est fundare tales elemosinas perpetuas auctoritate veteris testamenti. Nam ex hinc sumitur solucio decimarum, ut dictum est libro III° cap.... ex testimonio Henrici de Gandano et sancti Thome; et assumptum patet Levitici VI° et VII° Exodi XXVII° et sepe alibi in scriptura.

Against what has been said, [it is objected, by appeal to]{g:obicitur per} this, first, that in the old testament there were many such [alms]{g:elemosina}, religions and perpetual rites approved; since, then, the old testament is of as great authority as the new, it [follows]{g:sequitur} that it is lawful to found such perpetual alms on the authority of the old testament. For from here the payment of [tithes]{g:decime} is derived, as was said in book 3, chapter …, from the testimony of Henry of Ghent and of Saint Thomas; and the [assumption]{g:assumptum} is clear from Leviticus 6 and 7, Exodus 27, and often elsewhere in scripture.[^1]

## ¶2 · 283.11–284.21

> [p. 283] Hic patet quod obieccio ista non impugnat predicta, cum conclusio sit concedenda. Sed utinam sic arguentes attenderent ad sufficientem similitudinem utrobique, primo quomodo in veteri testamento ordinata est talis perpetuitas, singulariter a Deo et non ab homine. Unde libri legales et prophetales sic inchoant talia privilegia perpetua: Hec dicit dominus; et tunc cum auctor utriusque testamenti expressit voluntatem suam sufficienter in evangelio, non usurparent superaddere ordinacionem que videtur ordinacioni Dei contraria ipso domino inconsulto. Considerarent secundo quomodo omnes dicte elemosine vocate perpetue erant solum periodice, sicut patet in materia de cessacione legalium et persone privilegiate racione delicti a domino privilegiante proporcionaliter ammovente, ut patet de Datan et Abiron Numeri XVI°, de Hely et suis filiis 1° Regum II° et IV° capitulis, et de sacerdotibus, scribis et phariseis tempore Christi, qui omnes proporcionaliter ad abusum privilegii sunt puniti. Quomodo ergo vendicaremus colorate privilegium ab homine preter Deum, et super hoc exempcionem a tutela et regulacione illius privilegii ab illo [p. 284] qui privilegiat citra Deum? Locus enim a similitudine ex lege veteri hoc non petit; stet homo in limitibus privilegii unius persone et tunc limitari convenit regimen plenum illius privilegii ad illam personam singulariter concedentem. Tercio notaret sic arguens quomodo clerus veteris testamenti, quando vixit carnaliter, limitatus est per Deum sine solicitudine circa proventus vel redditus adquisitos vel datos humanitus, quid de oblacionibus, primiciis vel loco ad inhabitandum percipiet, quanto magis clerus spiritualis Christi pauperis et egeni excluderet se striccius a mundialibus de alimentis et tegumentis secundum regulam Apostoli 1° Tim. VI° contentatus? Nam Ezechiel. XLIV°, 28— 30, sic scribitur: Non erit autem sacerdotibus hereditas, ego hereditas eorum. Et possessiones non dabitis eis in Israel, ego enim possessio eorum. Victimam et pro peccato et pro delicto ipsi comedent et omne votum in Israel ipsorum erit. Et primitiva omnium pecorum primogenitorum et omnia libamenta ex omnibus que offerunt sacerdotum erunt.

Here it is clear that this objection does not impugn what was said before, since the [conclusion]{g:conclusio} is to be granted. But would that those who argue so would attend to a sufficient likeness on both sides: first, how in the old testament such perpetuity was ordained singularly by God and not by man. Hence the books of the law and of the prophets begin such perpetual [privileges]{g:privilegium} thus: "Thus saith the Lord"; and then, since the author of both testaments has expressed his will sufficiently in the gospel, they would not usurp [the right] to add on an [ordinance]{g:ordinacio} which seems contrary to the ordinance of God, without consulting the Lord himself.[^2] Let them consider, second, how all the said [so-called]{g:vocatus} perpetual alms were only for a period, as is clear in the matter of the cessation of the legal [observances], and of the privileged person, whom the privileging Lord removed by reason of an offence, in proportion [to it], as is clear of Dathan and Abiron, Numbers 16, of Heli and his sons, 1 Samuel chapters 2 and 4, and of the priests, scribes and Pharisees in the time of Christ, who were all punished in proportion to their abuse of the privilege.[^3] How, then, should we claim [with colour]{g:colorare} a privilege from man apart from God, and on top of this an [exemption]{g:exempcio} from the protection and regulation of that privilege by him [284] who privileges short of God? For [the topic from likeness]{g:locus a simili} drawn from the old law does not ask for this: let a man stay within the [limits]{g:limitare} of the privilege of one person, and then it is fitting that the full government of that privilege be assigned to that person who singularly grants [it].[^4] Third, let one who argues so note how the [clergy]{g:clerus} of the old testament, when it lived carnally, was assigned by God, without anxiety about income or [revenues]{g:reditus} acquired or given by men, what it should receive from offerings, first-fruits, or a place to dwell in: how much more would the spiritual clergy of Christ, poor and needy, exclude itself more strictly from worldly things, content with food and covering according to the Apostle's rule, 1 Timothy 6:8?[^5] For in Ezekiel 44:28–30 it is written thus: "And the priests shall have no inheritance, I am their inheritance: neither shall you give them possessions in Israel, for I am their possession. They shall eat the victim both for sin and for trespass: and every vowed thing in Israel shall be theirs. And the firstfruits of all the firstborn of the cattle, and all the libations of all things that they offer, shall be the priests'."[^6]

## ¶3 · 284.22–287.20

> [p. 284] Habuerunt autem loca pro inhabitacione et alendis pecoribus (ut patet Josue XXI° et ultimo). Si ergo clerus Aaronita tam carnalis, tam laboriose solicitus circa ceremonias legis tam limitate et stricte habuit temporalia, quanto magis clerus Christi qui exemplo conversacionis et verbo predicacionis docuit mundum relinquere et conversando in celestibus ad eorum annichilationem laicos edocere? Et hec racio legis Dei, Deuteronomii XVIII°, 1, quare precipit populo sub hiis verbis: Non habebunt sacerdotes et Levite et omnes qui de eadem tribu sunt partem et hereditatem cum reliquo populo Israel, quia sacrificia domini et oblaciones eius [p. 285] comedent et nichil aliud accipient de possessione fratrum suorum: Dominus enim ipse est hereditas eorum, sicut locutus est illis. Et sic intelligo scripturam sacram Numeri XVIII°, 20, cum dicitur: Dixitque dominus ad Aaron: In terra eorum nichil possidebitis nec habebitis partem inter eos, ego pars et hereditas tua. Haberent autem sacerdotes de optimis vescibilibus sine sollicitudine (ut patet ex Exodi XIX°, Levitici 1° et IV° et capitulis sequentibus). Ex istis potest colligi conclusio quod moderna cleri dotacio non est fundabilis in altero testamento. Secundo principaliter obicitur per hoc quod dare aliquantam elemosinam pro aliquanto tempore est cum paribus alicuius meriti; dare cum paribus tantam vel maiorem elemosinam per tempus diucius foret meriti amplioris, et per consequens pro tempore legis gracie, quando debet esse superhabundans caritas, debet dari elemosina largissima quoad quantitatem muneris, quoad libertatem donacionis et quoad diuturnitatem temporis. Sed cui donarent laici nisi clero Christi a quo recipiunt elemosinas ampliores? Ergo sancti reges et prelati, leges ecclesie et ostensiones miraculi docent cum racione de maioritate meriti quod talis dotacio ecclesie sit meritoria utrobique. Quis, inquam, dubitat quin, si Petrus meritorie dederit elemosinam et, servato merito, superaddiderit quomodolibet elemosinam ampliorem, quin eciam cresceret meritum aggregatum? Hic dicitur concedendo primam conclusionem: eo quod apostoli et alii cariores Christi discipuli dederunt omnia sua temporalia, sequendo Christum in evangelica paupertate, ita quod nec dacio nec recepcio est [p. 286] per se culpabilis sed carencia boni modi, notandum tamen quod maioritas meriti dantis elemosinam nec absolute attenditur penes quantitatem aut valorem dati nec penes quantitatem temporis pro quo datur, quia iuxta diffinicionem Christi, Luce XXI°, 2, Mulier paupercula mittendo quadrantem in gazophilacium plus omnibus misit. Ideo attendendum est ad caritatem a qua procedit elemosinacio corporalis. Et patet quod modus elemosinandi respicit discrecionem qua elemosinans relevet indigentem. Ideo evangelium non precipit ut relinquens bona sua det Christi apostolis sed pauperibus ut cecis, claudis atque debilibus. Secundo requirit rectitudo modi elemosine, ut tribuens observet servilem humiliacionem qua sciat se esse nudum ministrum Dei cui prestita sunt bona fortune, ut humiliter ac caritative distribuat. Et tercio requirit modus rectus elemosinandi ut elemosinans observet puram et simplicem intencionem non appetendo mundi gloriam. Et ideo docet Christus Matthei VI°, 2, 3, quod faciendo elemosinam non canes ante te tuba sicut ypocrite, sed nesciat sinistra, hoc est, intencio expectans retribucionem temporalem, quid faciat dextra; pars intencionis qua recte et pure spiritualis retribucio perpetua pro alio seculo expectatur et secundum quod ista plus adequantur cum maiori caritate est maior elemosina in donante. Ex quo patet cum veritate facti quod elemosine vocate perpetue in maiori parte vel semper habent minus de racione elemosine quam dande elemosine temporales. Nam taliter elemosinans deficit communiter in discrecione dando magis egentibus, deficit secundo in humiliacione credendo quod data sunt sua ad ordinandum quomodocunque voluerit sine superiori domino ipsum ut nudum servum suum et organum [p. 287] excitante, et demum communiter in elemosina tam corporali quam spirituali surrepit affeccio vane glorie vel boni utilis temporalis; et (si non fallor) oculus cesaris quodammodo infectus fuerat in hiis tribus. Quis rogo sciret docere quod cesar vel alius dominus secularis in fundando tam publice perpetuas elemosinas non intendit vane gloriam solempnitatis nominis sui et sui generis longis temporibus duraturam? Quod si illud preponderat intencioni dextere, faciendo elemosinas de alieno, peccat mortaliter. Et patet ad formam argumenti concedendo conclusionem et sequelam: Nam maxima elemosina in via est ut quis relinquat penitus civilem possessionem in mundo et ponat vitam presentem pro honore Dei et utilitate ecclesie dicente Christo Johannis XV, 13: Maiorem caritatem nemo habet quam ut animam suam ponat quis pro amicis suis, talis ei dat omnia bona sua fortune et omnia bona sua naturalia, et cum iusti sunt omnia, patet quod in tali dacione fundatur elemosina largissima; et patet conclusio.

And they had places to dwell in and to feed their cattle (as is clear from Joshua 21 and the last [chapter]). If, then, the Aaronite clergy, so carnal, so laboriously anxious about the ceremonies of the law, had [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} so limitedly and strictly, how much more the clergy of Christ, who by the example of [their] [manner of life]{g:conversacio} and by the word of preaching taught [men] to leave the world, and, by living in heavenly things, to teach the laity [to bring] those [goods] to nothing?[^7] And this is the reason of God's law, Deuteronomy 18:1, why it commands the people in these words: "The priests and Levites, and all that are of the same tribe, shall have no part nor inheritance with the rest of the people of Israel, because they shall eat the sacrifices of the Lord, and his oblations, [285] and they shall receive nothing else of the possession of their brethren: for the Lord himself is their inheritance, as he hath said to them." And so I understand holy scripture, Numbers 18:20, where it is said: "And the Lord said to Aaron: You shall possess nothing in their land, neither shall you have a portion among them: I am thy portion and inheritance." And the priests would have of the best things to eat without anxiety (as is clear from Exodus 19, Leviticus 1 and 4, and the chapters following).[^8] From these things the conclusion can be gathered that the modern [endowment]{g:dotacio} of the clergy cannot be founded in either testament.

Second, principally, it is objected, by appeal to this, that to give some alms for some time is, other things being equal, of some merit; to give, other things being equal, as much or greater alms for a longer time would be of more ample merit; and consequently in the time of the law of grace, when [charity]{g:caritas} ought to be superabundant, the most generous alms ought to be given as to the quantity of the gift, as to the freedom of the giving, and as to the long duration of the time. But to whom should laymen give, if not to the clergy of Christ, from whom they receive more ample alms? Therefore holy kings and [prelates]{g:prelatus}, the laws of the [church]{g:ecclesia} and the showings of miracle teach, with the reason from the [greatness]{g:majoritas} of merit, that such endowment of the church is meritorious on both sides.[^9] Who, I say, doubts that, if Peter has given alms meritorily and, the merit being kept, has added on in any way a more ample alms, the aggregate merit would also increase?[^10]

To this the answer is, granting the first conclusion (in that the apostles and other dearer disciples of Christ gave all their temporal goods, following Christ in evangelical poverty, so that neither giving nor receiving is [286] of itself culpable, but the lack of a good manner), that it must nevertheless be noted that the greatness of the merit of one who gives alms is not reckoned absolutely by the quantity or value of what is given, nor by the quantity of the time for which it is given; for according to Christ's determination, Luke 21:2–3, the poor little woman, by putting a farthing into the treasury, put in more than all.[^11] Therefore one must attend to the charity from which bodily almsgiving proceeds. And it is clear that the manner of giving alms has regard to the discretion by which the [almsgiver]{g:elemosinans} relieves the needy. Therefore the gospel does not command that one who leaves his goods should give them to the apostles of Christ, but to the poor, such as the blind, the lame and the feeble.[^12] Second, the rightness of the manner of alms requires that the giver observe a servile humbling by which he knows himself to be the bare minister of God, to whom the goods of fortune have been lent, so that he may distribute [them] humbly and charitably. And third, the right manner of giving alms requires that the almsgiver keep a pure and simple intention, not desiring the glory of the world. And therefore Christ teaches, Matthew 6:2–3, that in doing alms you should not sound a trumpet before you as the hypocrites [do], but let the left hand (that is, the intention that expects a temporal reward) not know what the right hand does: [the right hand being] the part of the intention by which a spiritual and perpetual reward for the other world is rightly and purely expected; and according as these are more matched with greater charity, the alms in the giver is greater.[^13] From this it is clear, with the truth of the fact, that so-called perpetual alms for the most part, or always, have less of the nature of alms than temporary alms to be given. For one who gives alms in that way commonly fails in the discretion of giving to those more needy; he fails, second, in humbling, believing that what is given is his own to order as he will, with no superior lord moving him as his bare servant and instrument; [287] and finally, commonly, in alms both bodily and spiritual, there creeps in an affection of vainglory or of a useful temporal good; and (if I am not mistaken) Caesar's eye was in some way infected in these three.[^14] Who, I ask, could teach that Caesar or another secular lord, in founding perpetual alms so publicly, does not aim at the vainglory of the solemnity of his name and of his line, to last for long ages? And if that outweighs the intention of the right hand, he sins mortally, making alms out of what is another's.[^15] And [the answer] to the form of the argument is clear, by granting the conclusion and [what follows]{g:sequela}: for the greatest alms on the [way]{g:via} is that one should leave civil possession in the world entirely and lay down his present life for the honour of God and the profit of the church, as Christ says, John 15:13: "Greater charity no man hath than that a man lay down his life for his friends"; such a one gives him all his goods of fortune and all his natural goods; and since all things are the just man's, it is clear that in such a giving the most generous alms is founded; and the conclusion is clear.[^16]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 283.1: It is objected that there were perpetual foundation under the O. T.
- 283.11: Answer: These were ordained by God,
- 283.21: who could and did withdraw them;
- 283.28: and do not authorise human foundations exempt from regulation.
- 284.6: The clergy of the O. T. was limited as to what it might receive.
- 284.22: Much more should the clergy of Christ set an example of unworldliness.
- 285.13: 2. It is objected a) that the more the alms, the greater the charity,
- 285.19: b) that alms can be given to none better than to the clergy.
- 285.30: Answer: a) True, if the alms are rightly directed;
- 286.2: but merit does not depend wholly on the amount given but on the spirit of the giver.
- 286.13: Alms must be given
- 286.15: 1. to the poor,
- 286.16: 2. as a service to God,
- 286.18: 3. without thought of worldly reward.
- 286.29: The founder of perpetual alms usually fails in all three points.

## Notes

[^1]: **C/S/T.** Side-note 283.1; A in the margin *Contra. Prima obieccio*. *Religiones*: religious institutions of the old law, untagged. After *cap.* the manuscripts leave a gap (Loserth, 283.7); which work's "book 3" is meant is not identified here (LEDGER E13c-1). Henry of Ghent: Loserth refers forward to p. 317; Thomas on tithes is to be checked (LEDGER E13c-6). Leviticus 6–7 and Exodus 27 are given by chapter only; the "perpetual" ordinances there are to be checked (LEDGER E13c-2).

[^2]: **C/T.** Side-note 283.11; A in the margin *Responsio*; A spells *inpungnat* (283.11). *Conclusio sit concedenda*: Wyclif grants that the old law had such perpetual alms, and denies that it follows for men now. *Hec dicit dominus*: the prophets' formula. *Ipso domino inconsulto*: without consulting the Lord.

[^3]: **S/C/T.** Side-note 283.21; A in the margin *Conclusio* (283.21). *Periodice*: for a period. *Cessacione legalium*: the ceasing of the legal (ceremonial) observances under the gospel. *Persone privilegiate … ammovente*: we construe the privileged person as the one the Lord removes; "and of the privilege removed from the privileged person" is possible. Numbers 16; 1 Samuel 2 and 4 (Vulgate 1 Kings). B reads *cum suis filiis* (283.27).

[^4]: **C/G/T.** Side-note 283.28. *Colorate*: with colour, that is, on a plausible ground (GLOSSARY §C, *color*), not "under a pretext". *Citra Deum*: short of God (cf. *citra Christum*). A and A₁ lack *unius … privilegii* (284.1). The argument: if the likeness with the old law is pressed, the one who grants a privilege keeps its government, so the likeness yields no exemption.

[^5]: **C/S.** Side-note 284.6. *Limitatus est … quid … percipiet*: it was assigned by God what it should receive (GLOSSARY §D, *limitare*). *Christi pauperis et egeni*: of Christ poor and needy. 1 Timothy 6:8 ("having food and wherewith to be covered, with these we are content", DR), alluded to, not quoted.

[^6]: **S/T.** Ezekiel 44:28–30 in Wyclif's form: *sacerdotibus* for the Vulgate's *eis*, *possessiones* for *possessionem*, *pecorum* added ("of the cattle"), *offerunt* for *offeruntur*; he stops before the end of 44:30. The Douay–Rheims is followed where he agrees. A in the margin *Racio* (284.14).

[^7]: **S/C.** Side-note 284.22. Joshua 21: the Levites' cities with their suburbs. *Et ultimo*: "and the last [chapter]", or "and lastly"; the reference is doubtful (LEDGER E13c-3). *Ad eorum annichilationem*: we take *eorum* of the world's goods, which the clergy teaches the laity to bring to nothing; "to their own self-emptying" is possible.

[^8]: **S/T.** Deuteronomy 18:1–2 and Numbers 18:20 are the Douay–Rheims; Wyclif adds *populo* (Deut. 18:1) and stops Numbers 18:20 before "in the midst of the children of Israel". B reads *fratruum* (285.3). Exodus 19 has no priestly portions; Exodus 29 (Aaron and his sons eating the consecration offerings) may be meant (to be checked, LEDGER E13c-4). A in the margin *Conclusio* (285.11). *In altero testamento*: in either testament.

[^9]: **C/T.** Side-notes 285.13, 285.19; A in the margin *Contra. Secunda obieccio* (285.13). *Cum paribus*: other things being equal (13-a, n. 1). *Ostensiones miraculi*: miracles shown in favour of endowed churches. *Utrobique*: for giver and receiver alike.

[^10]: **C.** *Petrus*: any man, the schoolmen's example name, not the apostle. The second *quin* repeats the first, a common pleonasm of the period.

[^11]: **S/T.** Side-notes 285.30, 286.2; A in the margin *Responsio* (285.30) and *Conclusio* (286.1); B reads *carencicia* (286.1). Luke 21:2–3 in Wyclif's words: the Vulgate has *vidua haec pauper plus quam omnes misit* (DR "this poor widow hath cast in more than they all"); the *quadrans*, "farthing", is Mark 12:42. *Diffinicionem*: determination.

[^12]: **S.** Side-notes 286.13, 286.15; A in the margin *Conclusio* (286.8). The list recalls Luke 14:13 ("the poor, the maimed, the lame and the blind", DR); the command to leave one's goods for the poor, Matthew 19:21.

[^13]: **S/T/C.** Side-notes 286.16, 286.18. The manuscripts give *Matth. V. 34*; Loserth prints *VI, 2, 3* (286.19). Matthew 6:2–3 in Wyclif's words (Vulgate *noli tuba canere ante te*); the gloss of the left and right hands as two intentions is his. *Ista … adequantur*: the right intention and the alms, matched with greater charity.

[^14]: **C/T.** Side-note 286.29. A and A₁ read *ut organum* for *et organum*. *Deficit … in discrecione dando magis egentibus*: he fails in the discretion that gives to the more needy. *Bona fortune*: goods of fortune. *Cesaris*: the emperor who endowed the church; Constantine is named at 288.13.

[^15]: **C.** A in the margin *Conclusio* (287.5). *Vane gloriam*: vainglory. *Intencioni dextere*: the right-hand intention of 286.24–25. *De alieno*: out of what is another's: the goods are God's, and the giver his bare minister (286.16–17).

[^16]: **S/R/T.** A in the margin *Ad argumentum*; A and A₁ read *concedo conclusionem* (287.11–12). John 15:13: Wyclif has *Maiorem caritatem … quam ut*, the Vulgate *Majorem hac dilectionem … ut* (DR "Greater love than this"); we keep "charity" for *caritas*. *Ei*: to God, it seems. *Cum iusti sunt omnia*: all things belong to the just, Wyclif's teaching on the lordship of the just (source to be checked, LEDGER E13c-5).
