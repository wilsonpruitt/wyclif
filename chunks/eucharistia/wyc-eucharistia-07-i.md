# *De eucharistia*, chapter 7, part 9: the third raving, Avignon, and the decree *Hoc est* with its gloss

## ¶1 · 221.31–222.17

> [p. 221] Hic tangitur deliramentum tercium quo ceci rimantur ficticias ad palliandum sua mendacia. Nam (ut [p. 222] supra) primum est transsubstanciacio in qua ponunt quod panis et vinum omnino destruitur. Secundum est ydemptificacio in qua ponunt unum suppositum ydemptice fieri aliud. Et tercium est inpanacio in qua ponunt non suppositum fieri suppositum, sed naturam assumi ypostatice a supposito, sic quod in prima ficticia ponunt de transsubstanciato nec remanere materiam nec formam sed totum desinere. In secunda vero ponunt tolerabilius quod panis et vinum remanent in suis suppositis; que supposita sunt panis et vinum et per viam ydemptificacionis fiunt corpus Christi et sanguis. Tercia via dicit coloracius quod non remanent supposita panis et vini, sed nature panis et vini servantur per ypostaticam unionem; et sic intelligunt quidam sentenciam triplicem quam recitat Magister IVᵒ Sentenciarum, distinccione XIᵒ, capitulo primo.

Here the third [raving]{g:deliramenta} is touched on, by which the blind pry out [fictions]{g:ficticia} to [cloak]{g:palliare} their lies.[^1] For (as [222] above) the first is [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio}, in which they posit that the [bread]{g:panis} and [wine]{g:vinum} is wholly destroyed. The second is [identification]{g:ydemptificacio}, in which they posit that one [supposit]{g:suppositum} [identically]{g:ydemptice} becomes another. And the third is [impanation]{g:inpanacio}, in which they posit not that a supposit becomes a supposit, but that a [nature]{g:natura} is assumed [hypostatically]{g:ypostatice} by a supposit; so that in the first fiction they posit that of the transubstantiated thing neither matter nor form remains, but the whole ceases. And in the second they posit more tolerably that the bread and wine remain in their supposits, which supposits are bread and wine, and by way of identification become the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} and his blood. The third way says [more colourably]{g:coloracior} that the supposits of bread and wine do not remain, but the natures of bread and wine are kept through [hypostatic]{g:ypostaticus} [union]{g:unio}; and so some understand the threefold [teaching]{g:sentencia} which [the Master]{g:magister} [recites]{g:recitare} in the fourth [book] of the *Sentences*, distinction 11, chapter 1.[^2]

## ¶2 · 222.18–223.14

> [p. 222] Sed arguendo contra istam sentenciam notandum est primo quod (ut referunt cronicantes) erat quidam frater predicator Parisius qui propter magnificacionem istius sentencie fuit ibi excommunicatus et appellando ad curiam Avinonicam fuit tempore Clementis quinti in ista sentencia condempnatus, sicut tempore Urbani quinti fuit alius predicator et inquisitor pravitatis heretice dampnatus, quia voluit defendere quod homo videt corpus Christi in hostia oculo corporali. Et racionabile est quod seminans in materia fidei ultra quam scit defendere vel fundare sit tanquam ecclesie inimicus defamandus, cum Spiritus Sanctus dedit sponse [p. 223] sue quid et quantum debet credere. Ideo odibiles sunt qui ad onus et illusionem ecclesie fingunt mendacia. Et utinam ecclesia Avinonica ponderaret istam sentenciam. Licet enim usque ad tempus Clementis Vᵗⁱ stetit episcopus Romanus (ut debuit) intra Romam vel suam diocesim, ut cronicat Cistrensis libro ultimo cap. XXXIV, et diu ante cepit generacio ista declinare ab antiqua fide de quidditate hostie, tamen tota illa ecclesia propter diuturnitatem more in irreligiosa lascivia potest dici Avinonica, sicut fingit se Romanam ecclesiam; si enim vendicat universalem iurisdiccionem, ut Petrus, debet secundum regulas Christi illuc accedere quo in plus populosa patria novit gentilitatem barbaram plus fervere.

But in arguing against this teaching it is to be noted first that (as the chroniclers report) there was a certain [friar]{g:frater} preacher at Paris who, for magnifying this teaching, was excommunicated there, and, appealing to [the Avignon curia]{g:curia-avinonica}, was condemned in this teaching in the time of Clement V;[^3] just as in the time of Urban V another preacher and inquisitor of [heretical depravity]{g:heretica-pravitas} was condemned, because he wished to defend that a man sees the body of Christ in the [host]{g:hostia} with the bodily eye.[^4] And it is reasonable that one who sows in the matter of faith beyond what he knows how to defend or found should be defamed as an enemy of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, since the Holy Spirit gave his [bride]{g:sponsa} [223] what and how much she ought to believe. Therefore those are hateful who, to the burden and [illusion]{g:illusio} of the church, [feign]{g:fingere} lies. And would that the Avignon church would weigh this teaching! For although until the time of Clement V the [Roman bishop]{g:romanus-episcopus} stood (as he ought) within Rome or his diocese, as the Cestrensis chronicles in his last book, chapter 34,[^5] and long before, this generation began to decline from the ancient faith about the [quiddity]{g:quiditas} of the host, yet that whole church, because of the long duration of its stay in irreligious wantonness, can be called Avignonese, as it feigns itself the Roman church; for if it claims universal [jurisdiction]{g:iurisdiccio}, as Peter [did], it ought according to the rules of Christ to go thither where, in a more populous country, it knows barbarous [paganism]{g:gentilitas} rages most.[^6]

## ¶3 · 223.15–224.2

> [p. 223] Hic enim dicitur symoniaca avaricia intoxicare contratas occiduas tamquam corvus quiescens in reperto cadavere. Notaret (inquam) quomodo a tempore apostolorum usque ad quatuor doctores ecclesie in fide florebat ecclesia, non introducendo de quidditate sacramenti altaris accidencia ignorata, et ex post continue retrocessit; et ponderaret equa lance evidencias que necessitarent ad seminandum istam sentenciam inintelligibilem et infundabilem tam contrariam antique sentencie. Si, inquam, introduxit scolam istius mendacii arroganter sine revelacione vel evidencia a priori, quis dubitat quin sit proditorius seductor ecclesie et precipuus discipulus Antichristi? Unde solebam ponere sociis meis quod simus in meditullio temporis antequam ista cecitate defamata fuit ecclesia et quod protunc faciant evidencias quod ab ecclesia taliter debet credi; licet enim rimari racionem fidei, quare ergo non racionem dicti Innocencii tercii, cum videtur [p. 224] contrarium racioni et per consequens fidei ac decreto Romane ecclesie.

For here simoniacal avarice is said to poison the western [districts]{g:contrata} like a crow resting on a carcass it has found. It would note (I say) how from the time of the apostles up to the four doctors of the church the church flourished in faith, not introducing unknown [accidents]{g:accidens} concerning the quiddity of [the sacrament of the altar]{g:sacramentum-altaris}, and afterwards continually went back;[^7] and it would weigh with an even balance the [evidences]{g:evidencia} that would necessitate sowing this unintelligible teaching, [without foundation]{g:infundabilis}, so contrary to the ancient teaching. If, I say, it introduced the school of this lie arrogantly, without revelation or evidence a priori, who doubts but that it is a [traitorous seducer]{g:proditorius-seductor} of the church and a chief disciple of Antichrist?[^8] Hence I used to put it to my companions that we should be in the midpoint of the time before the church was defamed with this blindness, and that they should then produce evidences that the church ought so to believe;[^9] for it is lawful to search out the reason of faith; why then not the reason of the saying of Innocent III, since it seems [224] contrary to reason, and consequently to faith and to the decree of the Roman church?[^10]

## ¶4 · 224.3–224.6

> [p. 224] Doctum est autem quod sentencia Thome sit nimis debilis in hac parte; nec dubito quin nulla nova sufficiencia subintrabit, quia non fundatur in scriptura vel maioribus doctoribus.

And it has been shown that the teaching of Thomas is [exceedingly]{g:nimis} weak in this part; nor do I doubt that no new sufficiency will enter in, because it is not founded in scripture or in the greater doctors.[^11]

## ¶5 · 224.7–225.6

> [p. 224] Secundo notandum quod inter omnia decreta sanctorum istud decretum cum glossa sua magis facit pro nostra sentencia. Augustinus enim distinguens inter sacrificium et sacramentum dicit quod sacrificium conficitur ex sacramento et re sacramenti que est corpus Christi et sanguis, ut est in persona Christi, non quod deitas et humanitas sint partes Christi sed eius quidditates, quarum utraque est totus Christus. Sic sacrificium dicit aggregative et non per viam composicionis ex partibus sacramentum sensibile et corpus Christi cum sanguine. Ulterius Augustinus approbans locucionem tropicam ita scribit: Caro Christi et quam sub forma panis opertam in sacramento accipimus, et sanguis eius quem sub vini specie ac sapore potamus, sic quod caro est corporis et sanguis est sanguinis sacramentum. Hic patet quod iste sanctus loquitur tropice ut scriptura et docet quod corpus Christi ibi absconditur sic quod non ibi videtur oculo corporali. Et tercio explanat et applicat hunc sensum expressius: Panis, inquit, celestis qui vere est caro Christi suo modo vocatur corpus Christi, cum revera sit sacramentum corporis Christi, quod exponit anterius per hoc quod istis signatur vera caro Christi et sanguis. Unde glossa dicit quod hoc celeste [p. 225] sacramentum, quando sic representat Christi carnem, dicitur corpus Christi sed inproprie, scilicet signandi misterio. Sic, inquit Augustinus, ipsa carnis inmolacio que fit manibus sacerdotis vocatur Christi passio, mors et crucifixio, non rei veritate sed signandi misterio, sicut sacramentum fidei baptismi est fides.

Second, it is to be noted that among all the [decrees]{g:decretum} of the saints this decree with its gloss makes most for our teaching.[^12] For Augustine, distinguishing between sacrifice and [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}, says that the sacrifice "is made up of the sacrament and the [reality of the sacrament]{g:res-sacramenti}, which is the body and blood of Christ",[^13] as it is in the [person]{g:persona} of Christ: not that [the Godhead]{g:deitas} and [the humanity]{g:humanitas} are parts of Christ, but his quiddities, each of which is the whole Christ. So the sacrifice [signifies]{g:dicere} [aggregatively]{g:aggregative}, and not by way of composition from parts, the [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} sacrament and the body of Christ with the blood. Further, Augustine, approving [tropical speech]{g:locucio-tropica}, writes thus: "The flesh of Christ, which we receive covered under the form of bread in the sacrament, and his blood, which we drink under the [species]{g:species} and savour of wine, so that the flesh is the sacrament of the body and the blood is the sacrament of the blood."[^14] Here it is clear that this saint speaks [tropically]{g:tropice}, as scripture does, and teaches that the body of Christ is hidden there, so that it is not seen there with the bodily eye. And third, he explains and applies this [meaning]{g:sensus} more expressly: "The heavenly bread," he says, "which is [truly]{g:vere} the flesh of Christ, is in its manner called the body of Christ, although [in truth]{g:revera} it is the sacrament of the body of Christ";[^15] which he expounds earlier by this, that by these the true flesh and blood of Christ are signified. Hence the gloss says that this heavenly [225] sacrament, when it thus represents the flesh of Christ, is called the body of Christ, but improperly, namely by a [mystery]{g:misterium} of signifying.[^16] "So," says Augustine, "the very immolation of the flesh, which is done by the hands of the priest, is called the passion, death and crucifixion of Christ, not in the truth of the thing but by a mystery of signifying"; just as the sacrament of faith, of baptism, is faith.[^17]

## ¶6 · 225.7–225.29

> [p. 225] In isto et aliis sibi similibus vellem emulos notare distinccionem predicacionis duplicis et sensum proposicionis sacramentalis, et tunc non mirarentur de sanctorum doctorum decretis cum ecclesie legibus nec de nobis ipsos tunc exponentibus. Quis (rogo) dubitat quin glossa ista sit duplex ex mendacio glossatorum sumpta que datur decreto Nicholai IIⁱ Ego Beringarius, cum textus sic loquitur: Profiteor panem et vinum que in altari ponuntur post consecracionem non solum sacramentum sed verum corpus Christi et sanguinem domini nostri Jesu Christi esse. Super quo textu dat glossa pro regula ut omnia referas ad species ipsas, ita quod iste sit sensus: profiteor non panem et vinum sed species panis et vini per se positas non solum esse sacramentum nec corpus Christi, sed sub illis contineri corpus Christi. Et sic confessio Beringarii est inpossibilis et heretica de virtute sermonis, sed debet glossari per suum contradictorium, cum hereticum sit quod panis et vinum remaneant post consecracionem sacramentum, sed sunt res aliene nature, non panis et vinum sed accidencia que non possunt esse corpus Christi, sed in illis est corpus Christi.

In this and in others like it I would wish the rivals to note the distinction of the twofold [predication]{g:predicacio} and the meaning of the sacramental [proposition]{g:proposicio},[^18] and then they would not wonder at the decrees of the holy doctors together with the [laws of the church]{g:leges-ecclesie}, nor at us when we expound them. Who (I ask) doubts but that this gloss is double, taken from the lie of the glossators, which is given to the decree of Nicholas II, *Ego Berengarius*,[^19] since the text speaks thus: "I profess that the bread and wine which are placed on the altar are after consecration not only a sacrament but the true body of Christ and the blood of our Lord Jesus Christ." On which text the gloss gives as a rule that you refer everything to the species themselves, so that this is the meaning: "I profess, not the bread and wine, but the species of bread and wine set [by themselves]{g:per-se}, to be not only a sacrament nor the body of Christ, but that under them the body of Christ is contained."[^20] And so the confession of Berengar is impossible and heretical [in the proper sense of the words]{g:de-virtute-sermonis}, but must be glossed by its contradictory, since it is heretical that bread and wine remain a sacrament after consecration; but they are things of an alien nature, not bread and wine but accidents, which cannot be the body of Christ, but in them is the body of Christ.[^21]

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Rimantur*: pry out, search through chinks (*rima*, the figure of 216.21 and 221.11). ABCE omit *Hic tangitur deliramentum*; B reads *quomodo* (221.31).
[^2]: **S.** Peter Lombard, *Sent.* IV d. 11 c. 1, the three opinions on the conversion (Loserth: "l. c."); the Lombard's three (PL 192, 861–862) are the substantial conversion *ut haec essentialiter fiat illa*, the body under the accidents with the bread resolved *in praejacentem materiam* or *in nihilum*, and the bread remaining with the body *ibidem*; they answer only loosely to Wyclif's identification, transubstantiation and impanation (LEDGER K07i-1). C has *naturam* in an erasure, AB *nullam*, D *in naturam* (222.5).
[^3]: **R/S.** Loserth identifies the friar preacher as John of Paris, O.P. (Werner, *Die nachscotistische Scholastik* II, 411; Natalis Alexander, *Hist. eccl.* saec. XIII–XIV, c. 5); Bishop William of Baufet forbade John to teach his *Determinatio de modo existendi corporis Christi* (1304), which allows presence by assumption of the bread; John appealed to Clement V and died at Bordeaux in 1306 before the appeal was decided, so "condemned" is Wyclif's chroniclers', not the record's (LEDGER K07i-2). ABE read *magnitudinem*, C *magnificacionem* in an erasure.
[^4]: **S.** The preacher and inquisitor condemned under Urban V (1362–70) is not named, and Loserth gives no foot; not traced (LEDGER K07i-3).
[^5]: **S.** *Cistrensis*: the monk of Chester, Ranulf Higden, *Polychronicon*, the last book (VII), c. 34 as Wyclif cites; in the Rolls Series it is VII.40 (vol. VIII, 292–294): *Hic quoque Clemens primus omnium papalem sedem de Roma usque Avinionam transtulit* (LEDGER K07i-4).
[^6]: **C.** *Ut Petrus*: as Peter, who went to Rome. The subject throughout is the church called Avignonese; ABC read *moris* (C corrected) for *more*. A and C read *reedificat* at 223.11.
[^7]: **C.** *Notaret … ponderaret*: the subject is still the Avignon church, continuing *utinam … ponderaret* (223.4). ABCD read *ignorancia* for *ignorata*; C has *accidencia* in the margin. The four doctors are Ambrose, Augustine, Jerome and Gregory.
[^8]: **C.** The subject of *introduxit* is taken as the same church (or its head); *a priori*: evidence from causes, before any appeal to effects.
[^9]: **C.** *Ponere*: to posit a case in disputation. Wyclif's case: suppose we stood in the middle time, before the church was so defamed; then let them produce the evidence. D reads *sumus*; C omits *temporis*. *Protunc*: read on the plate.
[^10]: **S.** The saying of Innocent III: his teaching that the accidents remain without a subject (*Cum Marthae*, X 3.41.6; *De sacro altaris mysterio* IV) is the likely referent; *De sacro altaris mysterio* IV.9 says it outright (PL 217, 862B: *post consecrationem accidens est sine subjecto … transit enim substantia, sed remanent accidentia*); the decree of the Roman church is *Ego Berengarius* (¶6) (LEDGER K07i-5). E omits *dicti* (223.32).
[^11]: **C.** *Doctum est*: shown above, in the earlier chapters. *Sufficiencia*: a sufficient ground.
[^12]: **S.** The decree is *De cons.* D. 2 c. 48 *Hoc est* (Loserth: *Decreti tertia pars*, dist. II, cap. XLVIII; modern form ours) (LEDGER K07i-6). C notes in the margin *scilicet quod potencia, De consecracione, distinccione IIa, Hoc est*; ABC read *fecit* for *facit* (224.8).
[^13]: **G.** *Conficitur* here is "is made up", the general sense, as at 221.24, not "consecrated".
[^14]: **T/S.** *Caro Christi et quam*: the *et* does not construe and is left untranslated; the decree reads *caro eius est quam*. *Corporis*: C corrects in another hand to *carnis*, the decree's reading (*caro carnis*); the print construes and is kept (224.21).
[^15]: **S.** The same canon, the *Panis celestis* sentence (*De cons.* D. 2 c. 48); c. 48 § 2 (PL 187, 1754): *coelestis panis, qui vere Christi caro est, suo modo vocatur corpus Christi, quum revera sit sacramentum corporis Christi*, verbatim (LEDGER K07i-7). C reads *figuratur* for *signatur* (224.29).
[^16]: **T/S.** *Quando sic representat*: ABDE read *quin sic*; Loserth notes that *quod* is right, after the ordinary gloss *ad v. Celestis* (*quod celeste sacramentum quod vere representat Christi carnem*). The print construes and is kept (LEDGER K07i-8). ABE read *signando* (C corrected).
[^17]: **C.** *Passio* is Christ's passion, not the category. *Sacramentum fidei baptismi est fides*: baptism, the sacrament of faith, is called faith, the close of c. 48 § 2 (PL 187, 1754: *sic sacramentum fidei, quod baptismus intelligitur, fides est*), after Augustine, *Ep.* 98.9 (PL 33, 364) (LEDGER K07i-9). ABE read *involucio* for *inmolacio* (C corrected); C reads *propriis manibus*.
[^18]: **C.** *Predicacio duplex*: the identical and the tropical predication (*predicacio ydemptica / tropica*). D reads *vere exponentibus* (225.11).
[^19]: **C.** *Duplex*: double, two-faced, taken from the glossators' lie; ABCD read *ficta que* for *sumpta que* (225.13). *Ego Berengarius*: *De cons.* D. 2 c. 42, which Wyclif ascribes to Nicholas II (LEDGER K07i-10).
[^20]: **S/G.** The ordinary gloss on *De cons.* D. 2 c. 42, *ad v.* *Panem* and *Christi esse* (Loserth) (LEDGER K07i-11). *Per se positas*: set apart by themselves, the spatial sense of *per se*. E reads *referis*; D *simul sed* (225.22).
[^21]: **C.** From *cum hereticum sit* the clause gives the glossators' ground, not Wyclif's: on their reading the things on the altar are accidents of an alien nature, in which the body is. D reads *de verbis sed* and *suam contradiccionem* (225.23–25).
