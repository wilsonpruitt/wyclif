## Doubtful, left as they are

- **10-a, p. 162**: *Dimissa autem contencione verbali … arguitur primo contra illud*, rendered "But, setting aside". The turn from the moderns' position to the arguments against it: either (b), set against Scotus's threefold negation, or (a), the next step ("Now, setting aside"). "But" is defensible.
- **10-a, p. 162; 10-d, p. 175; 10-e, p. 178**: intensive *Sed et hoc concedunt plurimi* / *Sed et hoc sonat vocabulum* / *Sed et hoc concedunt concorditer*, rendered "But … too". *Sed et* ("and indeed", "moreover") fits none of the classes cleanly; "but … too" keeps the *sed* and the *et*, and reads as a mild turn, not a contrast. Left.
- **10-a, p. 163**: *nec negacione; sed humanitate oportet ipsum esse singulare speciei humane formaliter: ergo per idem et alios*, rendered "but by humanity he must". An *ergo* follows, so (a) "now" is possible; but the *sed* answers the preceding *nec … nec* (not by alien suppositation nor by negation, but by humanity): a *non … sed*, so "but" stands.
- **10-b, p. 165**: *cum humanitas sit nomen … predicatum de suis suppositis et non homo, humanitas autem non potest predicari in recto de Christo*, rendered "but 'humanity' cannot be predicated". Inside the *cum* clause; either (b), set against "predicated of its supposits", or (a), the second premiss of *videtur quod*. "But" is defensible.
- **10-e, p. 177**: *Quod autem illa humanitas sit substancia racionalis, omnes concorditer confitentur*, rendered "And that". Either (c), extending the proof of the *assumptum* after the aside on the feigned negation, or (a), its next premiss ("Now that"). "And" is defensible.
- **10-e, p. 179**: *beatus, cuius est natura, quam elegisti …—ista autem natura non est beata, cum non sit suppositum*, rendered "and this nature is not blessed". The adversaries' gloss continued: either (c) or (b), "but this nature [itself] is not blessed" (the person whose nature it is is blessed, the nature is not). The *ergo* that follows (*illud ergo … non est beatum*) could also make it (a). A real toss-up.
