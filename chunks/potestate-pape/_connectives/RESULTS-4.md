## Doubtful, left as they are

- **05-e, p. 94**: *ipsi autem servabant vitam humilem*, rendered "while they themselves kept". Adversative (b) against the turmoil of temporal goods; "while" carries the contrast inside the *cum* period (same call as RESULTS-1, 01-a p. 3). Could be "but they themselves kept" if the card is read strictly.
- **05-f, p. 99**: *Sed tercio notet falsarius*, rendered "But thirdly let the falsifier note". Third item of a list (*Sed notet … Notet secundo … Sed tercio notet*), so arguably next step (a) or plain continuation; "But" keeps the rebuttal force against the objector and is defensible.
- **06-b, p. 108**: *Quod autem sacerdotes nostri temporis non habent tantam auctoritatem*, rendered "And that the priests of our time". Either (c), or (b) setting present priests against the apostle-authors ("But that"), or (a) the step to the thesis. "And" defensible.
- **06-c, p. 112**: *unus Papa revocat … epistolas et bullas alterius, sed facta sua falsificat*, rendered "but [so] falsifies his acts". The *sed* is near-intensive ("and so"); "but [so]" carries both readings.
- **06-c, p. 112**: *libencius autem loquitur sapiencia in iusto*, rendered "and wisdom speaks more willingly". Either (c), extending *Fides inquam est …*, or (a), the premiss that brings the second reason home ("now wisdom speaks"). Toss-up.
- **06-d, p. 115**: *Referendo autem auctoritatem ad Deum*, rendered "But, referring authority to God". First of three senses (*Secundo vero … Sed tercio*); (b) against "which some call authority", or (a) opening the distinction ("Now"). "But" defensible.
- **06-d, p. 116**: *Leges autem papales de maioritate et obediencia possunt intelligi*, rendered "And the papal laws". (c), or (a) the step that turns to the objection's papal laws ("Now the papal laws"). "And" defensible.
