# *De eucharistia*, chapter 5, part 6: the waking dream of blessed accidents, and the saints who died in this faith

## ¶1 · 135.3–136.11

> [p. 135] Unde videtur mihi, quod doctor Bonaventura et alii inconsequenter sompniant quod propter honorem sacramenti et devocionem populi multa sunt ponenda de eukaristia que non sunt fundabilia in scriptura; habeo autem pauca adversus eos qui inconsequenter et nimis parce sompniant. Quamvis autem sit de natura sompnii dormientis quod sit irregularis et expers racionis, tamen sompnium vigilis quod est ymaginacio sine fundamento debet habere evidenciam racionis; posset ergo fingi quod ex fundamento huius materie quod omnes vires anime humane tam organice quam non organice sint per se sine subiecto, et quod ille sint plene speciebus, actibus et virtutibus, ut anime beatorum erunt in patria, sic quod habeant plenitudinem beatitudinis consequentis. Sint ergo infinicies infinite qualitates huiusmodi ornate beatitudinis cum disposicionibus predictis que sint sine subiecto, et ponantur in qualibet parte proporcionali hostie infinite. Ille autem qui negat hunc casum habet negare fundamentum fabulancium tanquam conditores legis in ista materia; ymmo habita possibilitate casus non evidencius fundabit veritatem opinionis sue de inesse quam fundatur de facto in scriptura casus iam positus; cum ergo tunc foret hostia infinitum plus sacra quam modo defenditur, quare non datur ista sanctitas et honor hostie? Nam quelibet pars talis hostie haberet infinitos per se [p. 136] fines, gracia quorum totum genus humanum racionabiliter laboraret, et quodlibet istorum accidencium foret formaliter beatum haberetque actus beatos perpetuos, et sic foret melius homine misero, nec foret racio quare non posset elicere alios actus, sicut clare videt deitatem et gaudet nimirum gaudio magno. Tale, inquam, gloriosum accidens comederet corporaliter homo in assumendo viaticum, sicut comedit corpus Christi. Infinita sunt talia deliramenta infidelia quibus ex errore invento de eukaristia mendaciter et blaspheme occupatur ecclesia.

Hence it seems to me that doctor Bonaventure and others dream [inconsistently]{g:inconsequens}[^1] that, for the honour of the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} and the devotion of the people, many things are to be posited of the eucharist which cannot be founded in scripture; and I have a few things [to say] against those who dream inconsistently and too sparingly.[^2] And although it is of the nature of a sleeper's dream that it is irregular and devoid of reason, yet the dream of a waking man, which is imagination without foundation, ought to have the [evidence]{g:evidencia} of reason. It could therefore be feigned, from the foundation of this matter, that all the powers of the human soul, organic as well as non-organic, are [of themselves]{g:per-se} without a subject, and that they are fully [furnished] with species, acts and virtues,[^3] as the souls of the blessed will be in the [homeland]{g:patria}, so that they have the fullness of consequent blessedness. Let there be, then, infinitely infinite qualities of such an adorned blessedness, with the aforesaid dispositions, which are without a subject, and let them be posited infinitely[^4] in each [proportional part]{g:partes-proporcionales} of the [host]{g:hostia}. But he who denies this case has to deny the foundation of those who tell fables as founders of law[^5] in this matter; indeed, granted the possibility of the case, he will not found the truth of his [opinion]{g:opinio} [as a matter of fact]{g:de-inesse} more evidently than the case just posited is in fact founded in scripture. Since, then, the host would then be infinitely more [sacred]{g:sacer} than it is now maintained [to be], why is this holiness and honour not given to the host? For each part of such a host would have infinitely many [136] ends of themselves, for the sake of which the whole human race would reasonably labour; and each of these [accidents]{g:accidens} would be formally blessed and would have blessed acts perpetually, and so would be better than a wretched man; nor would there be a reason why it could not [elicit]{g:elicere} other acts, as it clearly sees the [Godhead]{g:deitas} and rejoices, assuredly, with great joy. Such a glorious accident, I say, a man would eat [bodily]{g:corporaliter} in receiving the viaticum, as he eats [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}. Infinite are such faithless [ravings]{g:deliramenta} with which, from the error invented about the eucharist, the [church]{g:ecclesia} is lyingly and blasphemously occupied.

## ¶2 · 136.12–137.6

> [p. 136] Et pro isto potest intelligi sentencia Augustini in De Verbis Domini Sermone XXVIIIᵒ dicentis: Dixi vobis quod ante verba Christi quod offertur panis dicatur, sed cum verba Christi deprompta fuerint, iam non panis dicitur sed corpus appellatur. Iste sanctus voluit quod illud quod prius fuit panis foret corpus Domini virtute sue benediccionis; ideo dicit Sermone LIIIᵒ loquens de eukaristia: Sacramentum quidem pene omnes corpus Christi dicunt. Non, inquam, intendit sanctus panem destrui vel in essencia non manere, ut patet ex dictis, sicut homine facto papa et nomine novo imposito, non propterea nomen perditur, quod ad naturalem proprietatem rei consequitur sed sopitur. Nec dubium quin equivocant in predicacione tropica, vocando illum panem corpus Christi modo quo exponit se ipsum Sermone XLVIIIᵒ, notans quomodo Christus nunc dicitur esse in similitudinem carnis peccati et nunc dicitur peccatum. Solet, inquit, similitudo capere nomen rei cuius est similitudo, ut [p. 137] exemplificat de homine picto quod est evidencius de sacramento altaris. Intencio ergo sanctorum est quod in presencia corporis Christi consideracio nature hostie suspenditur et tota intencio sequendi Christum in moribus attendatur, cum natura docet nos consideracionem luminis astrorum diffugere in solis presencia.

And for this [point] the [teaching]{g:sentencia}[^6] of Augustine can be understood, in *On the Words of the Lord*, sermon 28, where he says: "I told you that before the words of Christ what is offered is called bread; but when the words of Christ have been uttered, it is no longer called bread but is named the body."[^7] This saint meant that what before was bread would be [the body of the Lord]{g:corpus-domini} by virtue of his blessing; therefore he says in sermon 53, speaking of the eucharist: "The sacrament, indeed, almost all call the body of Christ."[^8] The saint did not, I say, [maintain]{g:intendere} that the bread is destroyed or does not remain in its essence, as is clear from what has been said; just as, when a man is made pope and a new name is imposed, the name that follows on the natural property of the thing is not lost on that account, but put to sleep. Nor is there doubt that they speak [equivocally]{g:equivoce} in [tropical predication]{g:predicacio-tropica},[^9] calling that bread the body of Christ in the way in which he expounds himself in sermon 48, noting how Christ is now said to be "in the likeness of sinful flesh" and now is said [to be] "sin".[^10] "A likeness," he says, "is accustomed to take the name of the thing whose likeness it is", as he [137] gives the example of a painted man; which is more evident of the sacrament of the altar. The intention of the [saints]{g:sancti}, therefore, is that in the presence of the body of Christ the consideration of the nature of the host is suspended and the whole intention of following Christ in conduct is attended to, since nature teaches us to turn away from considering the light of the stars in the presence of the sun.

## ¶3 · 137.7–138.4

> [p. 137] Quarto replicatur per hoc quod multi sancti moriebantur in ista fide; difficile ergo et inopinabile foret impugnare istam fidem vel sanctos illos accusare qui scripserant et defenderant illam fidem. Scribit enim sanctus Thomas super IVᵒ, distinccione XIᵃ, articulo primo: Hec, inquit, posicio que ponit substanciam panis remanere simul cum corpore Christi est incompetens, inpossibilis et heretica. Incompetens quidem, quia impediret veneracionem debitam huic sacramento. Esset enim ydolatrie occasio, si substancia panis ibi remaneret. Esset eciam contra significacionem sacramenti, quia species que inducerent in verum corpus Christi significarent magis substanciam panis. Esset tercio contraria huic sacramento, quia cibus iste non esset pure spiritualis sed eciam corporalis. Et quod sit inpossibilis, patet quia inpossibile est aliquid esse nunc, ubi prius non fuit nisi ipso mutato vel alio in ipsum converso; non enim posset eciam per miraculum fieri quod esset animal racionale, mortale et non esset homo; sic ergo corpus Christi non posset aliter se habere nunc quam prius, nisi interveniat mutacio; cum ergo ex parte panis non [p. 138] foret mutacio, nec ex parte corporis Christi, quia sic idem corpus moveretur simul et semel ad diversa loca, quod est inpossibile, cum tunc motus contrarii inessent simul eidem.

Fourthly it is [rejoined]{g:replicatur} by appeal to this, that many saints died in this faith; it would therefore be difficult and unthinkable to attack this faith or to accuse those saints who had written and defended that faith. For saint Thomas writes on the Fourth [book], distinction 11, article 1:[^11] "This [position]{g:posicio}," he says, "which posits that the [substance]{g:substancia} of the [bread]{g:panis} remains together with the body of Christ, is [unfitting]{g:incompetens}, impossible and heretical. Unfitting indeed, because it would impede the veneration due to this sacrament. For it would be an occasion of idolatry if the substance of bread remained there. It would also be against the [signification]{g:significacio} of the sacrament, because the [species]{g:species} which would lead into the true body of Christ would signify rather the substance of the bread.[^12] It would be, thirdly, contrary to this sacrament, because this food would not be purely spiritual but also bodily. And that it is impossible is clear, because it is impossible that anything be now where before it was not, unless it is itself changed or another is converted into it; for it could not be brought about even by a miracle that [something] should be a rational, mortal animal and not be a man; so therefore the body of Christ could not be otherwise now than before unless a change intervene; since therefore on the part of the bread there [138] would be no change, nor on the part of the body of Christ, because so the same body would be moved at once and together to diverse places, which is impossible, since then contrary motions would be in the same thing at once."[^13]

## ¶4 · 138.5–138.8

> [p. 138] Quod autem sit heretica, patet ex hoc quod contradicit veritati scripture. Non enim esset verum dicere: Hoc est corpus meum, sed hic est corpus meum.

"And that it is heretical is clear from this, that it contradicts the truth of scripture. For it would not be true to say 'This is my body', but 'Here is my body'."[^14]

## ¶5 · 138.9–138.21

> [p. 138] Hic dicitur quod petitur assumptum, ut puta quod sanctus Thomas moriebatur in ista fide vel alius assignandus, quia nescitur qualem instinctum habuerunt in exitu, et sicut fabule laborant quod servaverunt hoc usque ad mortem, ita laborant fabule non minus probabiles, quod coram populo in mortis articulo retractarunt, quia aliter iuxta istam stulticiam dampnaretur sanctus Origenes cui imponitur ut impio heretico defendisse et in scriptis reliquisse quod nemo dampnabitur perpetuo sed ad tempus; ille autem qui sine revelacione asserit quod tam pius et tam sanctus sicut fuit hic Origenes sit dampnatus est stulto stulcior.

Here [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that the [assumption]{g:assumptum} [is begged]{g:petitur},[^15] for instance that saint Thomas died in this faith, or another who might be named, because it is not known what prompting they had at their departure; and just as fables labour [to show] that they kept this until death, so fables no less probable labour [to show] that they retracted before the people at the point of death; for otherwise, according to this folly, saint Origen would be damned, against whom it is charged, as against an impious heretic, that he defended and left in writing that no one will be damned perpetually but for a time;[^16] and he who without revelation asserts that one so pious and so holy as this Origen was is damned is more foolish than a fool.

## ¶6 · 138.22–138.28

> [p. 138] Iterum, inter beatos Augustinum et Jeronimum fuit contrarietas de fide scripture Gal. IIᵒ utrum Petrus peccavit in simulando communicare cum gentibus; et licet non habeamus exceptis fabulis quod alter eorum revocavit errorem quem reliquit in sua epistola, tamen stultum est credere quod alter eorum propterea sit dampnatus.

Again, between the blessed Augustine and Jerome there was contrariety on the faith of scripture in Galatians 2, whether Peter sinned in feigning to keep company with the gentiles;[^17] and although we do not have, fables excepted, that either of them revoked the error he left in his letter, yet it is foolish to believe that either of them was damned on that account.

## ¶7 · 138.29–139.8

> [p. 138] Iterum tercio, inter beatum Bernhardum et alios in hoc sibi contrarios fuit repugnancia si beata virgo fuit concepta in originali peccato, sicut usque hodie [p. 139] est inter magnas sectas in isto contrarietas. Et tamen licet hoc expressius tangit fidem scripture quam remanencia panis post consecracionem, dementis est dicere quod propterea altera istarum sectarum est heretica et dampnanda. Cum ergo fides scripture cum rectitudine vite sit evidencia ad topice iudicandum, patet quod stultum est sine revelacione ista de quoquam asserere.

Again, thirdly, between the blessed Bernard and others opposed to him in this there was [incompatibility]{g:repugnancia} [as to] whether the blessed Virgin was conceived in original sin, as to this day [139] there is contrariety on this between great sects.[^18] And yet, although this touches the faith of scripture more expressly than the [remaining]{g:remanencia} of the bread after [consecration]{g:consecracio}, it is a madman's part to say that on that account one of these sects is heretical and to be damned. Since therefore the faith of scripture with rectitude of life is evidence for judging topically,[^19] it is clear that it is foolish without revelation to assert these things of anyone.

## ¶8 · 139.9–139.11

> [p. 139] Et patet quam nudum est illud argumentum ad probandum quod non remaneat natura panis in hostia consecrata.

And it is clear how bare that argument is for proving that the nature of the bread does not remain in the [consecrated host]{g:hostia-consecrata}.

## ¶9 · 139.12–139.28

> [p. 139] Et quantum ad illud allegatum de sancto Thoma, dicitur a multis quod pseudofratres post mortem eius corruperant ut plurimum eius scripta et hoc ideo, quia procurarunt esse inquisitores pravitatis heretice propter lucrum; ideo ut habeant laqueos plus subdolos ad involvendum simplices, quorum bona avare siciunt, seminarunt nimis spisse zizania contra fidem scripture; sed benedictus Dominus regnum nostrum liberatum est ab ista inquisicione heretice pravitatis, cum multi tam seculares quam religiosi sint longe subtiliores et sufficienciores ad inquirendum in regno nostro vel ubilibet, specialiter in materia de eukaristia, hereticam pravitatem. Cuius evidencia est quod defecerunt in ista materia, primo in visione corporis Christi in sacramento oculo corporali, secundo in materia de impanacione, sed in tercio signo magis defecerant.

And as for that [allegation]{g:allegare} about saint Thomas, it is said by many that the [false friars]{g:pseudofrater} after his death corrupted his writings [for the most part]{g:ut-plurimum}, and this for the reason that they contrived to be inquisitors of [heretical depravity]{g:heretica-pravitas} for gain; therefore, that they might have more cunning snares to entangle the simple, whose goods they greedily thirst for, they sowed [cockle]{g:zizania} [exceedingly]{g:nimis} thick against the faith of scripture. But, blessed be the Lord, our [kingdom]{g:regnum} has been freed from this inquisition of heretical depravity, since many, [seculars]{g:secularis} as well as [religious]{g:religiosi}, are far subtler and more sufficient for inquiring into heretical depravity in our kingdom or anywhere, especially in the matter of the eucharist. The evidence of this is that they have failed in this matter:[^20] first in the vision of the body of Christ in the sacrament with the bodily eye, second in the matter of [impanation]{g:inpanacio}, but in the third [stage]{g:signa} they had failed more.

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 135.3: The supporters of the independence of accidents do not carry their theory far enough.
- 136.12: Augustine, when he says that bread has become the Body, means that we are to think of it as such.
- 137.7: It is said that saints have died in this faith; e. g. St. Thomas.
- 138.8: We have no proof that they did die in it; nor, if they did, is such an error necessarily a cause of damnation.
- 139.12: The writings of St. Thomas are said to have been falsified after his death, in the interest of friar-inquisitors.

## Notes

[^1]: **T/S.** *Inconsequenter* is C's reading in the text, with *inconvenienter* in its margin; AB read *inconvenienter* (and at 135.8 A has *inconvenienter* in the text, *inconsequenter* in the margin). AB read *Bonaventure*. The place in Bonaventure is to be checked (LEDGER K05f-1).
[^2]: **C.** *Nimis parce*: "too sparingly", against the §D row's "exceedingly", because the fault charged is excess of restraint: they do not carry their dream far enough, and the reductio that follows carries it further.
[^3]: **C.** *Speciebus*: the species by which the powers of the soul know, not the sacramental species. *Plene* with the ablatives: "fully [furnished] with". The doubled *quod* (*fingi quod ex fundamento … quod omnes*) is resumptive.
[^4]: **C.** *Infinite* at the end of the clause is taken as the adverb; as a nominative plural it would repeat *infinite qualitates* ("let them be posited, infinite, in each proportional part").
[^5]: **C.** *Fabulancium tanquam conditores legis*: the genitive and the accusative do not agree; taken as "those who tell fables, as [if they were] founders of law".
[^6]: **C.** *Sentencia*, the CONTEXTUAL row, here "teaching": Augustine's position as Wyclif reads it.
[^7]: **S/T.** Loserth: Augustine, *Opera* V, Appendix, p. 152, sermon 84 (a sermon of the Maurist appendix), with Wyclif, *De apostasia*, p. 64 (LEDGER K05f-2). The print's second *quod* is resumptive; ABCD read *que offertur*.
[^8]: **S/T.** Wyclif's sermon 53: Loserth refers to the same volume of the Maurist *Opera* and to *De apostasia*; the page-foot figures are unclear in the scan, to be checked (LEDGER K05f-3). AB read *corpus esse dicunt*. At 136.17 the manuscripts read *fore* for *foret*, corrected in C's margin.
[^9]: **G/C.** *Predicacio tropica*: the bread is called the body by a trope (the row beside *ydemptica*). The subject of *equivocant* is the saints.
[^10]: **S.** Rom. 8:3; 2 Cor. 5:21. Wyclif's sermon 48: Loserth, *Opera* V, p. 653 (LEDGER K05f-4). C has *esse* in the margin.
[^11]: **S.** Loserth: Thomas, *Super IV Sent.* d. 11, q. 1 [the scan reads "q. II"], a. 1 (*Opera* X, 166–167, ed. Paris 1660), cf. *ST* III q. 75 a. 2; he notes that "this quotation varies much from the text of Thomas" (LEDGER K05f-5).
[^12]: **S.** Loserth gives Thomas's sense here as *quia species non ducerent in verum corpus Christi per modum signi sed magis in substanciam panis* ("because the species would not lead into the true body of Christ by way of a sign, but rather into the substance of the bread"); the print is translated. AB read *remaneret eciam*.
[^13]: **T.** Loserth marks a gap at 137.26: the step *Aliter enim se haberet nunc et prius, est idem quod moveri vel transmutari. Si ergo corpus Christi …* ("for to be otherwise now than before is the same as to be moved or changed. If therefore the body of Christ …") is wanting, so the closing *cum ergo* clause has no main clause. Translated as printed. B omits *eciam per*.
[^14]: **S/C.** Matt. 26:26. *Hic* taken as the adverb "here", set against the neuter pronoun *hoc*; as a masculine pronoun it would be "this one is my body".
[^15]: **C/T.** *Petitur assumptum*: the reply charges a begging of the question, the assumption being that the saints named died holding this faith. At 138.13 AB read *facile* for *fabule*, and C was corrected from *fabule* to *facile*; the print translated. ABCE read *sed coram*.
[^16]: **S/T.** The teaching charged against Origen, that punishment is not everlasting (*De principiis*), to be checked (LEDGER K05f-6). ACDE read *ut pio* ("as against a pious heretic"); E *in eternum*.
[^17]: **S.** Gal. 2:11–14. The dispute between Jerome (*Comm. in Gal.* on 2:11) and Augustine (*Epp.* 28, 40, 82) whether Peter's dissimulation was real; to be checked (LEDGER K05f-7). *Communicare cum gentibus*: Peter's eating with the gentiles (Gal. 2:12).
[^18]: **S.** Bernard, *Ep.* 174, to the canons of Lyons, against the feast of the Conception; to be checked (LEDGER K05f-8). The "great sects" of Wyclif's day are not named in the Latin.
[^19]: **G.** *Topice*: "topically", with probable, not demonstrative, force (cf. *argumentum topicum*, *evidencia topica*). D reads *tropice*.
[^20]: **C.** *Cuius evidencia*: *cuius* taken of the friar-inquisitors' insufficiency just stated. *Signum*: "stage" (the §D row); the third stage is not named here. E reads *ut multum* for *ut plurimum*.
