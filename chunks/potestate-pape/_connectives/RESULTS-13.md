## Doubtful, left as they are

- **12-a ¶8, p. 320**: *Sed nimis aliena est ab ista doctrina humana eleccio … Ideo oportet*, rendered "Now". (a), premiss closed by *Ideo*, is the card's reading; (b) "But" (against "the whole people ought to embrace him") is possible. "Now" stands.
- **12-b ¶1, p. 321**: *Sed nedum abest a nobis …*, rendered "But". (b), against the expectation that the act would be perceptible; or (a), the premiss that it is not perceived, closed by *videtur quod*. "But" defensible.
- **12-b ¶3, p. 322**: *Sed ubi expressius, quam Matth. XXIV*, rendered "But". Either (b), a turn from "Christ ought to have foretold it" to where he did, or (a), the next step bringing Matthew 24 to bear. No *ergo* follows; "But" stands.
- **12-b ¶4, p. 323**: *Communis autem modus loquendi concordat … ille locus debet esse sanctissimus*, rendered "And". Either (c), a further support, or (a), the premiss that the papal place is the "holy place" (then "Now"). Toss-up.
- **12-e ¶1, p. 332**: *Sed quantum ad primum punctum de capite causas ecclesiasticas terminante*, rendered "But". Either (b), turning to answer the curia's claim, or (a), the next step. "But" stands.
- **12-f ¶1, p. 335**: *quemque autem ex amore populi*, rendered "and someone, again,". (c) is carried by "and"; "again" is extra but marks the distributive (*quandoque … quemque*), not a connective swap. Left.
- **12-f ¶1, p. 336** (changed above): *sed necessario oportet quod fiat dispensacio* is cumulative (*sed* = "but also"), not cleanly adversative; "but" gives two successive "but that" clauses. "And further" would read better but is not a card rendering for *sed*; the coordinator may rule.
- **12-c ¶2, p. 326**: *Sed nunc quid detineat scitis* is a scripture quotation (2 Thess 2:6); Douay "But now" kept.
