# *De eucharistia*, chapter 8, part 5: growth, what defiles a man, and Thomas on immediate instants

## ¶1 · 247.29–249.11

> [p. 247] Quantum ad materiam de augmentacione et diminucione, dictum est alibi quomodo in homine ad unitatem spiritus qui est persona hominis non est [p. 248] difficultas quomodo fit augmentacio per humidum cibale, generata parva forma in ipso, ut puta forma carnis, ossis vel talis partis simplicis ac composite, ymmo non obest animam vegetativam eciam animam sensitivam generari que subordinetur anime ultimate; nec valet quod illa forma sit pars quantitativa forme tocius, sed satis est quod sit forma subordinata forme tocius a qua ista parcialis substancia habeat quidditatem, sicut totum corpus habet quidditatem suam a forma tocius; de multiplicacione vero formarum tam animarum quam formarum mixtorum dictum est diffuse alibi, sicut de ordine augmentandi et quomodo non oportet formam ultimam maiorari vel minorari, licet forme parcium adveniant vel recedant. In lignis autem in quibus est anima vegetativa est super hec quedam diversitas, quoniam communiter est per processum temporis novum lignum; et illud successivum communiter augmentatur et diminuitur et illud successivum individuatur a suis elementis et tunc est maius nunc autem minus (ut hic supponitur tanquam alibi declaratum). Quoad auctoritatem Christi Math. XV patet quod exsufflandi sunt ut infidelissimi qui dicunt Jesum nostrum nescire philosophica in hac parte. Oportet enim ipsos cognoscere virtutem sermonis et sensum ad quem hec dixit ille, cui cuncta subserviunt; habet autem sermo Christi sic: Non intelligitis quod omne quod intrat in os in ventrem vadit et in secessum emittitur? que autem procedunt de ore, de corde exeunt et ea coinquinant hominem. Et narrat consequenter septem que coinquinant hominem; [p. 249] ubi patet primo quod Christus non reprobaret carenciam intellectus Petri cum suis sociis superaddendo eis expositivam sentenciam nisi foret subtilis, verissima et salubris; secundo patet quod primo loquitur de ore corporis correspondenter ad Judeos carnales qui ritus corporeos observabant; et tercio loquitur de ore mentis correspondenter ad patres legis nove qui debent per corpora immunda vel communia ut animalia et alia prohibita in lege veteri precavicionem a contagione peccati notare, ut docet Augustinus in libro contra Faustum.

As to the matter of [augmentation]{g:augmentacio} and diminution, it has been said elsewhere[^1] how in a man, with regard to the unity of the spirit, which is the man's [person]{g:persona}, there is no [248] difficulty in how augmentation comes about through [the moisture of food]{g:humidum-cibale}, a small form being generated in it, for instance the form of flesh, of bone, or of some such part, simple or composite; indeed, nothing prevents a vegetative soul, and even a sensitive soul, from being generated, to be subordinated to the final soul. Nor is it sound [to argue] that that form is a [quantitative part]{g:pars-quantitativa} of the form of the whole; but it is enough that it be a form subordinated to the form of the whole, from which this partial [substance]{g:substancia} has its quiddity, as the whole body has its quiddity from the form of the whole.[^2] And of the multiplication of forms, both of souls and of the forms of mixed bodies, it has been said at length elsewhere, as also of the order of augmenting, and how the final form need not be made greater or less, although the forms of the parts come or go. But in trees, in which there is a vegetative soul, there is a certain difference beyond these, since commonly there is new wood in the course of time; and that [successive entity]{g:successivum} is commonly augmented and diminished, and that successive entity is individuated by its elements, and is then greater and now less (as is supposed here, as having been [made clear]{g:declarare} elsewhere). As to the authority of Christ, Matt. 15, it is clear that those who say that our Jesus did not know philosophical matters[^3] in this part are to be [blown out]{g:exsufflare} as the most [unbelieving]{g:infidelis}. For they must know the proper force of the words and the [meaning]{g:sensus} to which he said these things, he whom all things serve; and Christ's word runs thus: "Do you not understand, that whatsoever entereth into the mouth, goeth into the belly, and is cast out into the privy? But the things which proceed out of the mouth, come forth from the heart, and those things defile a man" (Matt. 15:17–18). And he next relates seven things that defile a man;[^4] [249] where it is clear, first, that Christ would not reprove the lack of understanding of Peter with his companions, adding for them over and above an expository [teaching]{g:sentencia},[^5] unless it were subtle, most true and wholesome; second, it is clear that he first speaks of the mouth of the body, corresponding to the carnal Jews who observed bodily rites; and third, he speaks of the mouth of the mind, corresponding to the fathers of the new law, who ought, through bodies unclean or common, such as animals and other things forbidden in the old law, to note a [precaution]{g:precavicio} against the contagion of sin, as Augustine teaches in the book against Faustus.[^6]

## ¶2 · 249.12–249.26

> [p. 249] Et tercio pro subtilitate sermonum Christi notandum primo quod Christus primo intelligit secundum distribucionem accomodam pertinentem quod omnis pars alimenti inmunda transiens per digestiones completas coinquinat hominem inquinamento corporali, cum purum alimenti separatum fuerit ab inpuro; ideo textus Marc. VIIᵐᵒ, 18, 19 habet sic: Non intelligitis, quia omne extrinsecus introiens in hominem non potest eum coinquinare, quia non introivit in cor eius sed in ventrem et secessum exit purgans omnes escas? Unde et Mattheus narrat hec septem de ore procedere, ut intelligamus os mentis. Et ponit exire cor interioris hominis quod est procedere extra limites racionis. Et patet sensus litteralis infringibilis veritatis.

And third, for the subtlety of Christ's words, it is to be noted first that Christ first understands, by a fitting [accommodated distribution]{g:distribucio-accomoda},[^7] that every unclean part of food passing through the complete [digestions]{g:digestio} defiles a man with bodily defilement, once the pure of the food has been separated from the impure; therefore the text of Mark 7:18–19 runs thus: "Understand you not that every thing from without entering into a man cannot defile him: because it entered not into his heart, but goeth out into the belly and the privy, purging all meats?"[^8] Hence Matthew too relates that these seven proceed from the mouth, so that we understand the mouth of the mind.[^9] And he puts them as going out of the heart of the inner man, which is to proceed outside the [limits]{g:limites} of reason. And the literal sense, of unbreakable truth, is clear.

## ¶3 · 249.27–250.20

> [p. 249] Sed est hoc verbum Christi gravidatum sensu morali multiplici. Primo notatur quod nemo maculatur mentaliter nisi macula originata fuerit a se ipso; secundo notatur quod carnalis observancia pro tempore [p. 250] legis veteris fuit licita sed nunc maculat, cum plene digesta transiit in secessum. Et tercio instruimur quod nullum corporale fetidum maculat mentem hominis nisi a puricia nature bone separatum fuerit et secundum racionem fetoris peccati per abusum innaturalem tanquam vomitus reassumptum. Mille alias subtilitates potest fidelis extrahere de hiis verbis Christi puris ab omni macula falsitatis. Non enim pertinuit sibi plus extense distribuere sed docere, ne sui sint occupati observanciis corporalis alimenti dimissa sollicitudine circa cibum anime spiritualem. Ideo dicit Apostolus Tym. primo: Omnia munda mundis. Et prima Thim. IVᵒ, 4: Omnis creatura Dei bona est et nichil reiciendum, quod cum graciarum accione percipitur, et Iᵃ Cor. Xᵒ, 25: Omne quod in macello venit manducate, nichil interrogantes propter conscienciam. Et hinc redarguendi sunt qui fingunt mendacia ut fidem katholicam quod signum corporis Christi quod est sacramentum blaspheme colatur et cultus signati corporis dominici remittatur.

But this word of Christ is pregnant with a manifold moral sense. First, it is noted that no one is stained in mind unless the stain has originated from himself; second, it is noted that carnal observance for the time [250] of the old law was lawful, but now it stains, since, fully digested, it has passed into the privy. And third, we are instructed that nothing bodily and foul stains the mind of a man unless it has been separated from the purity of good nature[^10] and taken up again like vomit, through unnatural abuse, in the manner of the foulness of sin. A thousand other subtleties the faithful man can draw out of these words of Christ, pure of every stain of falsehood. For it did not pertain to him to distribute more extensively,[^11] but to teach that his own should not be occupied with observances of bodily food, care for the spiritual food of the soul being laid aside. Therefore the Apostle says, Tim. 1: "All things are clean to the clean."[^12] And 1 Tim. 4:4: "every creature of God is good, and nothing to be rejected that is received with thanksgiving"; and 1 Cor. 10:25: "Whatsoever is sold in the shambles, eat: asking no question for conscience' sake." And hence those are to be [refuted]{g:redarguere} who [feign]{g:fingere} lies as the catholic faith: that the [sign]{g:signum} of the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}, which is the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}, is to be [worshipped]{g:cultus} blasphemously, and the worship of the [Lord's body]{g:corpus-dominicum} that is signified [remitted]{g:remittere}.[^13]

## ¶4 · 250.21–251.21

> [p. 250] Sed redeundo ad aliam sentenciam Doctoris Communis notandum primo quomodo discrepat ab aliis in materia de mensura; ponit enim multa que dicit alibi esse impossibilia et tunc sunt indubie explicite vel implicite heretica, ut ponit inpossibilitatem composicionis continui ex non quantis; possibilitatem autem immediacionis instancium videtur concedere. Scribitur enim ex parte Thome super IIᵒ Sentenciarum, distinccione IIIᵃ, questione XXVᵗᵃ: Dicendum, inquit, quod est assignare ultimum instans in quo angelus fuit bonus et primum in quo fuit malus; [p. 251] nec inter hec instancia fuit tempus medium, et quia tempus est formaliter numerus, non sequitur ipsum continuitas nisi ex parte motus. Unde cum vicissitudo affeccionum in angelo per quas est bonus et malus non est continua et ordinata ad aliquem motum continuum; numerus tamen earum dicetur tempus, quia secundum prius et posterius se habent, sed non est continuum. Unde inter duo instancia non necessario accipietur tempus medium, sicut nec inter duas unitates numerus. Istud autem dictum viciatum est; sophiste, inquam, Oxonienses dicerent quod vel bonitas angeli desinit esse per remocionem de presenti vel malicia sua cepit per remocionem de presenti, sic quod nec pro eodem instanti fuit sic bonus et malus, nec duo instancia erant immediata, quia per idem Petrus factus sanus immediate post egritudinem, cum sint contraria immediata, causaret immediacionem instancium, et per idem punctus continue fluens super duas lineas contiguas et generaliter mutari unius indivisibilis precedit per instans suum mutatum esse (ut patet alibi).

But, returning to another [teaching]{g:sentencia} of the [Common Doctor]{g:doctor-communis}, it is to be noted first how he differs from others in the matter of measure. For he posits many things which he says elsewhere are impossible, and then they are without doubt explicitly or implicitly heretical: as he posits the impossibility of the composition of a continuum out of non-[quantified]{g:quantus} things, but seems to concede the possibility of [immediacy]{g:immediacio} of instants. For it is written on Thomas's part on the second [book] of the *Sentences*, distinction 3, question 25:[^14] "It must be said," he says, "that one may assign a last instant in which the angel was good and a first in which he was bad; [251] nor between these instants was there a middle time; and because time is formally number, continuity does not follow it except on the side of motion. Hence, although the alternation of affections in an angel, through which he is good and bad, is not continuous and ordered to some continuous motion, yet their number will be called time, because they stand according to before and after; but it is not continuous. Hence between two instants a middle time will not necessarily be taken, as neither [is] a number between two units." But this saying is faulty; the Oxford [sophists]{g:sophista}, I say, would say that either the angel's goodness ceases to be by removal of the present or his malice began by removal of the present,[^15] so that he was not thus good and bad for the same instant, nor were two instants [immediate]{g:immediatus}; for by the same token Peter, made healthy [without intermediary]{g:immediate} after sickness, since they are immediate contraries, would cause an immediacy of instants; and by the same token a point flowing continuously over two contiguous lines [would do so]; and generally the being-changed of one indivisible precedes, by its instant, its having-been-changed (as is clear elsewhere).[^16]

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** *Dictum est alibi*: Wyclif's own earlier writing on growth; not identified here (LEDGER K08e-1).
[^2]: **C.** *Nec valet quod*: taken as "nor is it sound [to argue] that"; the point is that a partial form need be only subordinate to, not a quantitative part of, the form of the whole. *Ad unitatem spiritus* (247.30) is taken as "with regard to the unity"; it could be "for the sake of the unity".
[^3]: **T.** The print has *philosophica* (neuter plural, "philosophical matters"); BCDE read *philosophicam*, and Loserth's foot (248.23) proposes *philosophiam* ("philosophy"). The sense is the same; the print is translated.
[^4]: **S.** The seven of Matt. 15:19: evil thoughts, murders, adulteries, fornications, thefts, false testimonies, blasphemies. Peter's request and Christ's reproof are Matt. 15:15–16.
[^5]: **C.** *Sentencia* (CONTEXTUAL) is here "teaching": the explanation Christ adds (Matt. 15:17–20). At 250.21 it is Thomas's position taught.
[^6]: **S.** Augustine, *Contra Faustum*; Loserth's foot (249.11) cites "S. Aug. Opp. tom. VIII, 207, 210" (book VI c. 7, PL 42, 233–234: animals unclean *non natura, sed significatione*, and *ipsa a talibus carnibus abstinentia, tale vitium nos cavere praemonuit*) (LEDGER K08e-2). The *et tercio* of this sentence is the second limb of the *secundo*: the mouth of the mind against the mouth of the body.
[^7]: **G.** *Distribucio accomoda*: the universal *omne* is taken not of every food without restriction but of what the sense fits, here the unclean part that is purged. *Pertinentem* (C *partitive*, D *partinentem*) is rendered "fitting".
[^8]: **S.** Mark 7:18–19, in Wyclif's form: Vulgate *communicare* (he has *coinquinare*), *introit* (he has *introivit*, "entered"), and *sed in ventrem vadit, et in secessum exit* (he omits *vadit*). His Latin is translated, in the Douay–Rheims words where they agree.
[^9]: **T.** *Unde et Mattheus* is Loserth's text; all the manuscripts read *Unde in Math.* (249.22), "Hence in Matthew [he] relates". The sense is the same.
[^10]: **T.** *Puricia* is Loserth's text; the manuscripts read *quericia* (250.4), which does not construe.
[^11]: **C.** *Distribuere* picks up the *distribucio* of 249.13: Christ did not need to extend the universal *omne* further than his teaching required. C first wrote *tribuere*, corrected in the margin to *distribuere*.
[^12]: **S.** The words are Titus 1:15, not 1 Timothy, as Loserth notes at the foot ("Recte: Tit. I, 15").
[^13]: **C.** *Remittatur* is the slackening of a quality (intension and remission): the worship due to the Lord's body signified is lessened while the sign is worshipped. The decree's grounds are the opposed pair *signum / signatum*.
[^14]: **S.** Thomas, *In II Sent.*; the print reads d. 3 q. 25; Loserth's foot (250.29) gives "Super dist. II, lib. II Sent., Quaest. II" in his edition's tom. VIII p. 40. The words are *In II Sent.* d. 3 q. 2 a. 1 ad 5 (*est assignare ultimum instans in quo Angelus fuit bonus et primum in quo fuit malus … sicut nec inter duas unitates numerus*), so the print's distinction is right and Loserth's is not (LEDGER K08e-3).
[^15]: **C.** *Per remocionem de presenti*: the logicians' exposition of *incipit / desinit*, by denying the present state (it is not now, but immediately after it will be, or it is now, and immediately after it will not be). On that exposition there is no last instant of the one state next to a first instant of the other.
[^16]: **C.** *Mutari … precedit … mutatum esse*: the infinitives are the subject and object: the "being changed" of an indivisible comes before its "having been changed". *Ut patet alibi*: Wyclif's own writing, not identified here (LEDGER K08e-4).
