# sed/autem extract, wave 13: chunks 05-h 05-i 05-j

### 05-h ¶4 · 144.30–146.3  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 144] Hic miror, cum illud sacramentum secundum istam sectam sit corpus de genere quantitatis et habet dimensiones et acciones naturales in cibando et [p. 145] nutriendo, quod non foret cibus corporalis, cum non sit aliqua res nisi corporalis et ita grossa sicut panis alius consecrandus. Numquid credimus quod populus posset tantum seduci quod credat omnia bona adiacencia sacerdoti esse transaccidencia destructa tota substancia, et sic sacerdotes Baal viverent pro suo perpetuo sine cibo corporali? Revera deliramentum consimile foret dicere quod totum sacramentum quod Apostolus dicit ebriare et experiencia docet ipsum corporaliter nutrire sit cibus pure spiritualis et non corporalis. Pagani nimirum derident istas stulticias quibus sensus populi captivantur; tantum itaque sacerdos est dispositus ad iterum celebrandum cum paribus, si eque moderate et eque virtuose assumpserit tantumdem de accidentibus vel de pane vel vino non consecratis, sicut assumendo tantum de pane et vino consecratis, communiter tamen sacerdos non assumit hoc sacramentum intencione gule vel gracia nutricionis corporee, licet illa per accidens consequatur, ideo est ad celebrandum disposicior post assumpcionem sacramenti quam communiter est dispositus ad celebrandum post assumpcionem tanti cibarii non sacrati. Et patet quod ista via propter has tres argucias non est competens, **sed** competencia eius patet ex sanctis doctoribus qui declarant hoc fuisse competens propter sensum misticum quem panis et vini substancia representant. Hereticum itaque foret extinguere illum sensum, sicut stultum est credere quod propter ordinacionem ecclesie ut sumatur eukaristia per ieiunium, quamquam religiosius sumebatur a Christo et discipulis suis post prandium, per hoc, inquam, introducatur lex inpossibilis de accidentibus per se [p. 146] et dissolvatur de accidentibus necessaria lex nature; sufficit enim pro lege hominum quod ieiunium ecclesie sit servatum.

ENGLISH: Here I wonder, since that sacrament according to this [sect]{g:secta} is a body of the genus of [quantity]{g:quantitas} and has dimensions and natural actions in feeding and [145] nourishing, that it should not be bodily food, since it is not any thing but bodily, and as gross as other bread that is to be consecrated. Do we believe that the people could be so far seduced as to believe that all the goods lying beside the priest are [transaccidents]{g:transaccidencia},[^8] the whole substance destroyed, and that so the priests of Baal would live in perpetuity without bodily food?[^9] [In truth]{g:revera} it would be a like [raving]{g:deliramenta} to say that the whole sacrament, which the Apostle says makes drunk and experience teaches nourishes [bodily]{g:corporaliter},[^10] is purely spiritual food and not bodily. No wonder the pagans deride these follies by which the senses of the people are taken captive. So a priest is as much disposed to celebrate again, other things being equal, if he has taken, equally moderately and equally virtuously, just as much of accidents, or of bread or wine not consecrated, as by taking as much of consecrated bread and wine. Yet commonly a priest does not take this sacrament with the intention of gluttony or for the sake of bodily nourishment, though that follows [by accident]{g:per-accidens}; therefore he is more disposed to celebrate after the taking of the sacrament than he is commonly disposed to celebrate after the taking of so much food not [hallowed]{g:sacrare}.[^11] And it is clear that this way is not fitting by reason of these three quibbles, but its fittingness is clear from the holy doctors, who [make clear]{g:declarare} that this was fitting on account of the mystical sense which the substance of bread and wine represent.[^12] So it would be heretical to extinguish that sense, just as it is foolish to believe that, because of the [ordinance]{g:ordinacio} of the [church]{g:ecclesia} that the eucharist be [received]{g:sumpcio} fasting (although it was received more religiously by Christ and his disciples after the meal),[^13] by this, I say, an impossible law about accidents [by themselves]{g:per-se}[^14] [146] should be brought in, and the necessary law of nature about accidents be dissolved. For it suffices for the law of men that the church's fast be kept.

### 05-i ¶1 · 147.9–148.29  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 147] Item, maximum inconveniens ad quod deducit ex isto foret quod idem corpus moveretur simul et semel ad diversa loca, **sed** possibilitatem huius oportet viam istam concedere, nam positis sex sacerdotibus quorum quatuor deferant hostiam consecratam ad quatuor differencias posicionis, quintus vero elevet hostiam in manibus et sextus deprimat, patet quod corpus Christi movetur in illa hostia ad sex differencias posicionis simul et semel. Sicut enim anima movetur ad motum corporis, sic et corpus Christi in hostia consecrata, quia est continue noviter per diversa loca sine conversione ad hoc pertinente. Prima enim conversio Domini fuit ante hoc consumptum, ideo pro nunc non facit quod corpus Christi non movetur, quia sic non posset corpus Christi moveri in hostia; nec valet dicere quod non habet motum sibi proprium, quia hoc non requiritur, **sed** est possibile, tum quia sic posset fingi de possibili quod corpus Christi assumeret in celo diversa loca cum quibus sic moveatur per accidens ut anima, tum eciam quia corpus potest de Dei omnipotencia esse per diversa loca distancia cum hoc tamen quod non sit in loco medio, ut patet ex multiplici evidencia. [p. 148] Item, queratur de mendicacione istius termini excludentis motum localem conversio corporis acquirentis locum; patet, inquam, quod non est terminus philosophicus ad sensum illum, cum philosophi et subtiliores theologi negarent omnem talem conversionem de possibili. Et patet quod petitur descripcio motus; non enim est racio quare panis et vinum convertitur in corpus Christi vel sanguinem vel aliquam eius partem quin per idem in quamlibet et quidlibet assignandum. Videtur enim multis quod subducto omni motu locali vel conversione unius in aliud corpus posset quotlibet loca acquirere et alias perdere. Ideo inconveniens videtur descripcio mendicata. Si enim essent quotlibet loca occupata corporibus contiguis omnino quietis que subito continuentur anima Petri actuante eorum quotlibet, tunc Petrus esset per quotlibet loca de novo sine hoc quod moveretur ad aliquem eorum vel alicuius rei fiat in aliam conversio. Nam motus localis in Petro utrobique subducitur, et idem conceditur perditis partibus per discontinuacionem sine motu locali. Et eodem modo diceret pius philosophus pertinenter quod corpus habet esse intencionale per medium, capiens suam speciem, sicut habet esse spirituale per anime consideracionem in ipsa anima sine hoc quod corpus moveatur ad illa loca; quare non posset sic esse de esse sacramentali, quod habet corpus Christi in hostia per virtutem datam hostie sine hoc quod corpus Christi illinc localiter moveatur.

ENGLISH: Again, the greatest [absurdity]{g:inconveniens}[^1] to which he leads from this would be that the same body would be moved at one and the same time to different places. But this way must grant the possibility of this. For if six priests are posited, four of whom carry a [consecrated host]{g:hostia} toward the four [differences]{g:differencia} of [position]{g:posicio}, while the fifth elevates the host in his hands and the sixth lowers it, it is clear that the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} is moved in that host to six differences of position at one and the same time.[^2] For just as the soul is moved at the motion of the body, so also is the body of Christ in the consecrated host, since it is continually and newly through different places without a [conversion]{g:conversio} pertaining to this. For the first conversion of the Lord was completed before this; therefore for the present it does not make the body of Christ not be moved, since in that way the body of Christ could not be moved in the host.[^3] Nor does it avail to say that it does not have a motion proper to itself, because this is not required, but it is possible: both because one could in this way feign, as a possibility, that the body of Christ took on different places in heaven, with which it would so be moved [by accident]{g:per-accidens}, like the soul; and also because a body can, by the omnipotence of God, be through different distant places, with this however, that it is not in the middle place, as is clear from manifold [evidence]{g:evidencia}.[^4] [148] Again, let the question be put about the [begging]{g:mendicacio} of this term that excludes local motion, "conversion of a body acquiring place".[^5] It is clear, I say, that it is not a philosophical term in that [sense]{g:sensus}, since the philosophers and the subtler theologians would deny every such conversion as possible. And it is clear that the [definition]{g:descripcio} of motion is [begged]{g:petitur}. For there is no reason why the [bread]{g:panis} and [wine]{g:vinum} are converted into the body of Christ or [his] blood or any part of it, but that, [by the same token]{g:per-idem}, [they are converted] into any [part] whatever and anything whatever that might be assigned.[^6] For it seems to many that, with all local motion or conversion of one thing into another taken away, a body could acquire any number of places and at other times lose them. Therefore the begged definition seems absurd.[^7] For if there were any number of places occupied by contiguous bodies wholly at rest, which were suddenly [made continuous]{g:continuare},[^8] the soul of Peter actuating any number of them, then Peter would be through any number of places anew, without his being moved to any of them or any conversion being made of any thing into another. For local motion in Peter is taken away [on both sides]{g:utrobique}, and the same is granted when parts are lost through [discontinuation]{g:discontinuacio} without local motion. And in the same way a loving philosopher would fittingly say that a body has [intentional being]{g:esse-intencionale} through the medium, as it takes its [species]{g:species},[^9] just as it has spiritual [being]{g:esse} through the soul's consideration in the soul itself, without the body's being moved to those places. Why then could it not be so of the [sacramental being]{g:esse-sacramentale} which the body of Christ has in the host, through the [power]{g:virtus} given to the host, without the body of Christ being moved [locally]{g:localiter} from there?[^10]

### 05-i ¶2 · 148.30–150.2  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 148] Iterum, secundum Doctorem Subtilem et suam sectam Deus posset subito multiplicare idem corpus per quotlibet loca et alias demultiplicare; quod non [p. 149] posset esse, nisi idem corpus posset acquirere quotlibet loca sine motu vel conversione ad hoc requisita; ergo descripcio non est sana secundum eos. Quamvis **autem** hoc sit argumentum ad hominem et (ut dicetur posterius) procedit ex falso principio, grave tamen et ultra vires prioris secte foret hereticare in hoc omnes de secta secunda. Constat quidem quod si hoc sit simpliciter inpossibile in materia fidei, tunc est summe hereticum toti sacre scripture contrarium, cum omne tale non posset esse nisi Deus et omnis veritas non esset. Nec dubium quin innitens huic principio ad inprobandum panis substanciam remanere nitatur inpossibilitare et sic hereticare omnem viam que ponit multiplicacionem corpoream; ideo indubie altera istarum viarum in materia de existencia accidencium sine subiecto est inpossibilis atque heretica, sicut vel via Augustini que ponit generaliter distinccionem quantitatis a subiecto (ut recitavi superius) vel via contraria negans distinccionem quantitatis a subiecto. Unde illi qui inponunt scandalose sancto Thome super Tercium Sentenciarum quod pertinaciter viam que dicit Christum esse creaturam omnino esse inpossibilem **sed** non hereticam mirabiliter defamant ipsum, cum implicant ipsum in materia fidei esse contradictorium sibi ipsi et per consequens ante purgacionem nullibi esse credendum, et sic ponendo possibilitatem vacui non crederetur sibi, quia tunc terra pure posita in concavo orbis lune posset per vacuum subito acquirere locum terre quiescendo continue vel in celo vel in terra et tunc acquisicio situs foret formaliter pertinens motui vel in vacuo [p. 150] foret situs acquisicio vel duo instancia inmediata vel indivisibile moveretur in vacuo successive.

ENGLISH: Again, according to the [Subtle Doctor]{g:doctor-subtilis} and his sect,[^11] God could suddenly multiply the same body through any number of places and at other times demultiply it; which could not [149] be, unless the same body could acquire any number of places without the motion or conversion required for this. Therefore the definition is not sound according to them. And although this is an [argument to the man]{g:argumentum-ad-hominem} and (as will be said later) proceeds from a false principle, yet it would be grave, and beyond the strength of the former sect, to [pronounce heretical]{g:hereticare} on this point all those of the second sect.[^12] It is evident indeed that if this is impossible [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} in a matter of faith, then it is supremely heretical, contrary to the whole of holy scripture, since every such thing could not be unless God and all truth were not. Nor is there doubt that one who relies on this principle to disprove that the [substance]{g:substancia} of bread remains strives to [make out as impossible]{g:inpossibilitare}, and so to pronounce heretical, every way that posits bodily multiplication.[^13] Therefore without doubt one or other of these ways, in the matter of the existence of [accidents without a subject]{g:accidens-sine-subiecto}, is impossible and heretical: either the way of Augustine, which posits generally a distinction of [quantity]{g:quantitas} from its subject (as I [recited]{g:recitare} above), or the contrary way, denying the distinction of quantity from the subject.[^14] Hence those who scandalously impute to Saint Thomas, on the Third [book] of the *Sentences*, that [he holds] obstinately that the way which says "Christ is a [creature]{g:creatura}" is wholly impossible but not heretical, wonderfully defame him,[^15] since they imply that in a matter of faith he is contradictory to himself, and consequently, before purgation, is nowhere to be believed.[^16] And so, when he posits the possibility of a vacuum, he would not be believed; for then earth placed purely in the concave of the orb of the moon could, through a vacuum, suddenly acquire the place of the earth while continuously at rest either in the heaven or on earth; and then either the acquiring of situation would formally pertain to motion, or in a vacuum [150] there would be an acquiring of situation, or two immediate instants, or an indivisible would be moved successively in a vacuum.[^17]

### 05-i ¶4 · 150.18–151.23  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 150] Quantum ad ultimum Sancto impositum quod supradicta sentencia est heretica, quia scripture sacre contraria, cum tunc non diceretur: Hoc est corpus meum **sed** hic est corpus meum, frequenter musitavi si aliquis sciolus potuit istam stulticiam tam nudam asserere et demum credidi quod aliquis de pseudofratribus finxit istam demenciam contra Sanctum, quia (ut dicit Apostolus) periculum in falsis fratribus. Ponit enim scriptura sacra regulariter sacramentum istud esse panem, sicut sancti doctores sequentes et decretum Romane ecclesie. **Sed** falsa glossa adulatorum subintroeuncium maculavit istam sentenciam. Nam super IV Sentenciarum, distinccione XIᵃ, imponitur [p. 151] sancto Thome quod recitat Damascenum dicentem: Quia consuetudo est hominibus comedere panem et vinum, Christus coniugavit eis deitatem et fecit ea corpus suum et sanguinem. Et hoc dictum glossat consequenter: Dicendum, inquit, quod verbum Damasceni intelligendum est quantum ad species quibus corpus Christi divinitati coniunctum modo ineffabili coniungitur. Et ista glossa ad tantum dilatata est per patrem mendacii quod maculavit doctores decretorum precipuos, ut patet de glossa super additis, distinccione IIᵃ, De Consecracione primo; ergo patet quod ista glossa est contraria sibi ipsi, nam questione IVᵃ eiusdem distinccionis obicit per beatum Ambrosium dicentem: quod erat panis ante consecracionem est corpus Christi post consecracionem; hoc est, inquit, quod est sub speciebus panis, primo fuit panis et postea corpus Christi; ubi videtur quod loquitur de illo ante consecracionem quod ipsa substancia erit postea corpus Christi; quod est valde contrarium eidem sentencie, cum in fine eiusdem questionis dicit quod nichil panis umquam erit aliquid corporis Christi. Quis ergo crederet glossam istam que tam patenter se ipsam inficit?

ENGLISH: As to the last [charge] imputed to the Saint,[^20] that the aforesaid teaching is heretical, because contrary to holy scripture, since then it would not be said "This is my body" but "This [one] is my body":[^21] I have often pondered whether any [knowing]{g:sciolus} man could assert a folly so bare, and at last I came to believe that one of the [false friars]{g:pseudofrater} feigned this madness against the Saint, because (as the Apostle says) "peril from false brethren".[^22] For holy scripture [as a rule]{g:regulariter} lays it down that "this sacrament is bread", as do the holy doctors who follow it and the [decree]{g:decretum} of the Roman church.[^23] But the false gloss of flatterers creeping in[^24] has stained this teaching. For on the Fourth [book] of the *Sentences*, distinction 11, it is imputed [151] to Saint Thomas that he recites Damascene saying: "Because it is the custom of men to eat bread and wine, Christ joined the [Godhead]{g:deitas} to them and made them his body and blood."[^25] And he glosses this saying afterwards: "It is to be said," he says, "that the word of Damascene is to be understood as to the species, to which the body of Christ, joined to the [divinity]{g:divinitas}, is joined in an ineffable way." And this gloss has been spread so far by the father of lies[^26] that it has stained the chief doctors of the decrees, as is clear from the gloss on the additions, distinction 2, *De consecratione*, first.[^27] Therefore it is clear that this gloss is contrary to itself.[^28] For in question 4 of the same distinction he objects, by appeal to blessed Ambrose, who says: "What was bread before the [consecration]{g:consecracio} is the body of Christ after the consecration"; "that is," he says, "what is under the species of bread was first bread and afterwards the body of Christ". Here it seems that he speaks of that [which was] before the consecration, that the substance itself will afterwards be the body of Christ; which is very contrary to the same teaching, since at the end of the same question he says that "nothing of the bread will ever be anything of the body of Christ".[^29] Who then would believe this gloss, which so patently infects itself?

### 05-j ¶1 · 151.24–152.30  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 151] Secundo obiciunt dogmatisantes istius secte voce rigida et robusta **sed** ydiotica ut terreant ydiotas. Ista sentencia, inquiunt, est contra beatum Ambrosium et decretum ecclesie, Panis est in altari. **Sed** non timeant fideles hos torvos vultus vel voces rigidas, [p. 152] **sed** attendant sentenciam. Nam beatus Ambrosius dicit ibidem primo innuitive quod panis est corpus Christi. Ista vero secta dicit hoc esse inpossibile atque hereticum. Secundo dicit beatus Ambrosius in eodem decreto illud quod erat panis ante consecracionem iam corpus Christi est post consecracionem. Hoc, inquit ista glossa, est inpossibile et hereticum, **sed** quantitas per se significat corpus Christi. Tercio regulant glossatores istius secte quod generaliter per panem et vinum in dictis sanctorum istius materie intelligitur sua quantitas benedicta, et sic intelligitur quod ipsa erit corpus Christi, hoc est, ydemptificabitur corpori Christi ut ipsum sacramentaliter significat. Et sic glossant Apostolum et usum ecclesie in canone misse, quando orant ut hanc oblacionem Deus multipliciter benedicat, ut fiat ecclesie corpus et sanguis domini nostri Jesu Christi; hoc est, inquiunt, ut illa quantitas benedicatur et fiat corpus Christi et sanguis ad sensum expositum, cum panis et vinum non exspectant illam benediccionem, **sed** virtute benediccionis prioris totaliter destruuntur et quantitas per se remanens supplet vicem eorum in benediccione in sacramentali significacione et in virtuosa operacione. Non dubito quin glossa ista sit heretica, primis sanctis doctoribus et ecclesie Christi incognita, quousque glossatores magnificantes signa in quibus ponunt stare suam religionem ut in per se differencia subintrabant; propterea enim creditur dyabolum cecare per eos populum ut ydolatranda signa plus magnificent quam signata.

ENGLISH: Secondly, those of this [sect]{g:secta} who [teach as dogma]{g:dogmatisare} object, with a stiff and sturdy but [unlettered]{g:ydiota} voice, so as to terrify the unlettered: "This [teaching]{g:sentencia}," they say, "is against blessed Ambrose and the [decree]{g:decretum} of the [church]{g:ecclesia} *Panis est in altari*."[^1] But let the faithful not fear these grim faces or stiff voices, [152] but attend to the [sense]{g:sentencia-verborum}.[^2] For blessed Ambrose says there, first, by intimation, that "[bread]{g:panis} is the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}". But this sect says this is impossible and heretical. Secondly, blessed Ambrose says in the same decree that "what was bread before the [consecration]{g:consecracio} is now the body of Christ after the consecration". "This," says this [gloss]{g:glosa}, "is impossible and heretical; but [quantity]{g:quantitas} [by itself]{g:per-se} signifies the body of Christ."[^3] Thirdly, the glossators of this sect lay down as a rule that generally, by "bread and [wine]{g:vinum}" in the sayings of the [saints]{g:sancti} on this matter, its blessed quantity is understood; and so it is understood that it will be the body of Christ, that is, it will be [identified]{g:ydemptificare} with the body of Christ, as it [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} signifies it.[^4] And so they gloss the Apostle and the usage of the church in the canon of the mass, when they pray that God may bless "this oblation" manifoldly, "that it may become for the church the body and blood of our Lord Jesus Christ":[^5] "that is," they say, "that that quantity may be blessed and become the body and blood of Christ in the sense set out", since the bread and wine do not wait for that blessing, but by the power of the earlier blessing are wholly destroyed, and the quantity remaining by itself [supplies the place]{g:vicem-supplere} of them in the blessing, in the sacramental signification and in the powerful operation. I do not doubt that this gloss is heretical, unknown to the first holy doctors and to the church of Christ, until the glossators crept in, magnifying the [signs]{g:signum} in which they place their religion to stand as in its [differentia]{g:differencia} of itself;[^6] for on this account the devil is believed to blind the people through them, so that they magnify the signs that are to be [idolized]{g:ydolatrare} more than the things signified.[^7]

### 05-j ¶3 · 153.15–153.32  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 153] **Sed** replicatur per hoc quod responsio ista implicat locucionem istam esse tropicam: Hoc est corpus meum, quod est hereticum, cum tunc foret falsa de virtute sermonis. **Sed** stulti sic arguentes obliti racionis argumenti vel consequencie, ideo oportet acute respondere illis iuxta suam stulticiam, negando arguciam tam in materia quam in forma. Locucio **autem** tropica est verissima, summe katholica et miraculosissime conversiva. Nec est color ex istis concludere quemlibet talem modum loquendi per locum a simili vel quod quidditas aut natura panis et vini corrumpitur pocius quam si peccator convertitur in iustum, ergo natura illa destruitur. Sic, inquam, natura panis melioratur per benediccionem, quia post nudum esse naturale habet superadditum esse sacramentale, ut efficaciter figuret et faciat verum corpus Christi ad quemlibet eius punctum, et sic vere accipit **sed** tropice predicacionem corporis.

ENGLISH: But [it is rejoined]{g:replicatur}, on the ground that this answer implies that this speech is tropical, "This is my body", which is heretical, since then it would be false [in the proper sense of the words]{g:de-virtute-sermonis}. But the fools who argue so have forgotten the nature of an argument or [inference]{g:consequencia};[^9] so one must answer them sharply according to their folly,[^10] denying the quibble both in matter and in form. But [tropical speech]{g:locucio-tropica} is most true, supremely catholic, and most miraculously converting. Nor is there [colour]{g:color}[^11] to [infer]{g:concludere} from these [premisses] any such manner of speaking [by the topic from likeness]{g:a-simili}, or that the quiddity or [nature]{g:natura} of bread and wine is corrupted, any more than "if a sinner is converted into a just man, therefore that nature is destroyed".[^12] So, I say, the nature of the bread is bettered by the blessing, because after its bare natural [being]{g:esse} it has [sacramental being]{g:esse-sacramentale} added over it, so that it efficaciously figures and makes the true body of Christ at every point of it; and so it [truly]{g:vere}, but [tropically]{g:tropice}, receives the predication of the body.[^13]

### 05-j ¶4 · 154.1–154.8  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 154] **Sed** tercio replicatur per hoc quod doctores aliter intelligunt scripturam sacram, sanctos doctores et canones ecclesie, ut puta quod tota substancia panis et vini secundum se totam corrumpitur et remanent solum accidencia, de quibus solum locuntur auctoritates predicte, quando faciunt mencionem de sacramento altaris, cum aliter foret talis locucio tropica inpossibilis et heretica.

ENGLISH: But thirdly it is rejoined, on the ground that the doctors understand holy scripture, the holy doctors and the canons of the church otherwise: for instance, that the whole [substance]{g:substancia} of bread and wine is corrupted in its whole self and only the [accidents]{g:accidens} remain, of which alone the aforesaid authorities speak when they make mention of the [sacrament of the altar]{g:sacramentum-altaris}; since otherwise such tropical speech would be impossible and heretical.[^14]

### 05-j ¶7 · 155.8–155.15  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 155] **Sed** finaliter arguitur sequi ex dictis quod omnes pertinaciter defendentes sentenciam contra quam arguitur forent heretici, et cum per multa tempora papa et quasi tota ecclesia et specialiter scola theologorum et capitula religiosorum constanter defendit illud ut articulum fidei (ut patet ex scriptis editis in ista materia), sequitur quod omnes illi forent heretici, quod foret temerarium sustinere.

ENGLISH: But finally it is argued that it follows from what has been said that all who obstinately defend the teaching against which the argument is made would be heretics; and since for many times the [pope]{g:papa} and almost the whole church, and especially the school of theologians and the chapters of [the religious]{g:religiosi}, have constantly defended it as an [article of faith]{g:articulus-fidei} (as is clear from the writings published on this matter),[^21] it follows that all those would be heretics, which it would be rash to maintain.
