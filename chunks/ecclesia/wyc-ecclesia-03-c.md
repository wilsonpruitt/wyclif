# *De ecclesia*, chapter 3, part 3: three corollaries on suits and the malice of priests; Thomas: Christ head of all men

## ¶1 · 54.23–58.16

> [p. 54] Sciendum vero est quod sunt nonnulli quos caritatis gremio nutriens mater ecclesia tolerat, quousque ad spiritualis etatis incrementa perducat, qui nonnunquam et sanctitatis habitum tenent et perfeccionis meritum exequi non valent. Ad dona namque spiritualia minime assurgunt et idcirco hiis qui sibi coniuncti sunt ad tuenda ea que terrena sunt serviunt et nonnunquam in defensionis eiusdem iracundia excedunt. Et istos nomine religiosos declarat iste sanctus papa involvi duplici malicia, primo quia nedum infirmitate sed sciencia contra conscienciam suam peccant, secundo quia fingendo sanctitatem in defendendo [p. 55] bona ecclesie pro facinore querunt laudes. Ex istis conclusionibus huius sancti demonstrabilibus perpendere potest ecclesiasticus tam regularis quam secularis, si diligenter examinent conscienciam cum scriptura, palpando intencionem, volucionem et motus anime cum intencione supremi iudicis, quod non tam assidue contenderent pro vocatis bonis ecclesie. Patet secundo: Quomodo dominus papa non foveret sed extingueret lites huiusmodi secundum regulam evangelii: si enim committeretur regnis, provinciis et parrochiis examen vite clericorum, sic quod non ministrarentur eis stipendia subtracto sensibiliter eorum ministerio, viveretur superioribus prepositis tolerabilius et sacerdotibus cum populo melius quam est modo. Defectus autem patronorum et populi posset speculator superior facilius corrigere quam totum onus per se cum falsis testibus litem gignentibus supportare. Nec requiritur alia lex in distribuendo officia sacerdotum nisi cognicio a capite per testes probabiles, quod unus sit rector in ministerio sacerdotali, solicicior habiliorem preponere; de quo si non gauderet, depositus foret, ex ypocrisi ac cupiditate suspectus. Et patet tercio quod causa dissensionis et perturbacionis ecclesie sit malicia sacerdotum. Nam eorum virtuositas maxime pacificaret ecclesiam; ideo ex opposito eorum perversitas maxime perturbat ecclesiam, quia peccatum quod est per se causa perturbacionis ecclesie in ipsis est gravius. Dicunt enim contra fidem quod sunt viri sancte matris ecclesie, licet sint apostatantes partes diaboli, et per consequens, faciunt confusionem in fide, confusionem in sacramentis et subtraccionem spiritualis suffragii a Deo per ipsos instrumentaliter derivandi. [p. 56] Unde confidenter prenostico quod ante correccionem nostram secundum statum primevum non deficiet regnorum perturbacio. Cum igitur apostolus raptus fuit usque ad tercium celum, videns longitudinem cursus ecclesie quoad tempus, latitudinem quoad numerum prelatorum et profunditatem quoad sensum quo clerici sunt in sensu domini profundati, verti non debet in dubium quin prophetavit iuxta exposicionem beati Gregorii de ecclesia in instante, vocans eos ypocritas se ipsos amantes, verbum domini adulterantes et cauterizatam conscienciam habentes (ut patet I.Timotei IV°, 2). Signum autem precipuum foret quod clericus ad modum loquendi apostoli se ipsum diligat, si propter affeccionem proprietatis temporalium dimissa cura presbyteri se ipsum sollicitat. Que enim posset esse maior adulteracio verbi domini quam intelligere ipsum ad sensum diaboli iuxta quem defendatur esse hereticum et blasfemum, ymo quod licet ex auctoritate eius committere leve mendacium? Aut quid posset esse magis periculosum cauterium quam clericum habere conscienciam adustam cupidine, quod cessans ab operibus reprehensibilibus in facie ecclesie propter candentem amorem quem habet ad temporalia, publicet et defendat sine fructuosis operibus se esse membrum sancte matris ecclesie. Licet enim talis evacuet cauterio suo fluxum viciorum sensibilem, verumptamen diabolus auctor fluxus talis superflui tantum disseminat per meatus ypocritarum absconditos per totum residuum corporis fluxum peccati lubrici, et sic ex simulata consuetudine factus heresiarcha habet [p. 57] in capite, hoc est, in racione cauterium Antichristi. Nulli enim secundum beatum Gregorium, in Registro, secundo capitulo, periculosius vel amplius nocent in ecclesia sancti Dei. Sed redeundo ad difficultatem dimissam obicitur per sanctum Thomam de Christo, conclusione octava, articulo III°, ubi dicit quod Christus est caput omnium hominum, tam fidelium qui uniuntur sibi in actu per graciam, quam infidelium qui sunt solum in potencia eius membra, et post subdividit secundum predestinatos et prescitos qui recedentes ab hoc mundo totaliter desinunt esse membra Christi. Et probat hoc multiplici racione. Quantum ad hoc testimonium sancti Thome, videtur michi quod ipse equivocat vere dicendo quod Christus secundum deitatem est caput extrinsecum tocius humani generis; quod potest dici aggregative unum corpus naturale, cui eciam Christus benefacit sicut et toti mundo secundum humanitatem, cum virtute passionis Christi acquiritur quedam perfeccio secundaria toti mundo. Et sic secundum humanitatem benefacit toti humano generi, cum punit omnes dampnatos, vel ex infidelitate, ut eos qui non considerant in dominum Jesum Christum, vel ex desperacione qua dimissa debuerunt celestibus aspirare, vel tercio de iudicio temerario quo dimisso debuerunt domino Jesu Christo caritative finaliter adherere. Et sic intelligitur triplex causalis Christi Johannis XVI°, 8: Arguet mundum de peccato, de iusticia et [p. 58] de iudicio. De peccato infidelitatis que autonomatice dicitur peccatum, quia non crediderunt in patrem neque in me; de iusticia quam debuerunt habuisse, aspirando cum mediis congruis ad patriam naturalem, quia ad patrem vado; et de iudicio quo temere arbitrantur dogma mundi atque diaboli ardencius diligendum, cum princeps mundi iudicatus est per Christum docentem oppositum. Et sic opposita fidei, spei et caritatis que corresponderent per ordinem trinitati dampnant prescitos in finali iudicio (ut alias diffuse exposui). Non enim vidi in sancto Thoma vel alio doctore probabili quod totum illud genus sed pars eius predestinata sit sancta mater ecclesia, sponsa Christi et universalis vel catholica ecclesia, corpus Christi misticum de quo scriptura loquitur. Et de tali est nobis sermo.

But it must be known that there are some whom mother church, nursing them in the lap of
[charity]{g:caritas}, tolerates until she brings them to the growth of spiritual age; who sometimes
both hold the habit of sanctity and are not able to carry out the merit of perfection. For they hardly
rise to spiritual gifts, and therefore serve those joined to them in protecting the things that are
earthly, and sometimes go too far in the anger of that defence.[^1] And these, [religious]{g:religiosi}
in name, this holy [pope]{g:papa} declares to be entangled in a twofold malice: first, because they sin
not only from infirmity but from knowledge, against their conscience; second, because, feigning
sanctity in defending [55] the goods of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, they seek praise for a crime.[^2] From
these demonstrable conclusions of this saint a churchman, regular as well as secular, can weigh, if
they diligently examine their conscience with scripture, feeling out intention, volition and the
motions of the soul against the intention of the supreme judge, that they would not contend so
assiduously for the so-called goods of the church. It is clear, second, how the lord pope would not
foster but extinguish suits of this kind according to the rule of the gospel: for if the examination
of the life of [clerics]{g:clericus} were committed to kingdoms, provinces and parishes, so that
stipends were not ministered to them when their ministry was [perceptibly]{g:sensibilis} withdrawn,
life would be more tolerable for the higher [superiors]{g:prepositi}, and better for
[priests]{g:sacerdos} with the people, than it is now.[^3] And the failings of [patrons]{g:patronus}
and people a higher watchman could correct more easily than bear the whole burden by himself, with
false witnesses begetting suits. Nor is any other law required in distributing the offices of priests
than knowledge on the part of the head, through credible witnesses, that one man is [fit to be] rector
in the priestly ministry, [the head being] the more careful to set the abler man over [others]; and if
[the other] did not rejoice at this, he would be deposed, as suspected of hypocrisy and cupidity.[^4]
And it is clear, third, that the cause of the dissension and disturbance of the church is the malice of
priests. For their virtue would most pacify the church; therefore, conversely, their perversity most
disturbs the church, because the sin that is of itself the cause of the church's disturbance is graver
in them. For they say, against the faith, that they are men of holy mother church, although they are
apostatizing parts of the devil; and consequently they make confusion in faith, confusion in the
sacraments, and a withdrawal of the spiritual [suffrage]{g:suffragia} that is to be derived from God
instrumentally through them.[^5] [56] Hence I confidently foretell that, before our correction according
to the primitive state, the disturbance of kingdoms will not cease. Since, therefore, the apostle was
caught up to the third heaven, seeing the length of the church's course as to time, the breadth as to
the number of [prelates]{g:prelatus}, and the depth as to the sense in which clerics are sunk deep in the
sense of the Lord, it ought not to be called into doubt that he prophesied, according to the exposition
of blessed Gregory, of the church at the present instant, calling them hypocrites, lovers of
themselves, adulterating the word of the Lord and having their conscience seared (as is clear from 1
Timothy 4:2).[^6] And it would be a principal sign that a cleric loves himself, in the apostle's manner
of speaking, if, for an affection of ownership of temporal goods, leaving aside the care of a
[presbyter]{g:presbiter}, he busies himself about himself. For what greater adulteration of the word of
the Lord could there be than to understand it in the devil's sense, according to which it is maintained
to be heretical and blasphemous; indeed, that by its authority it is lawful to commit a light lie? Or
what cautery could be more dangerous than for a cleric to have a conscience burnt by cupidity, so that,
ceasing from works reprehensible in the face of the church, he, on account of the glowing love that he
has for [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, publishes and maintains, without fruitful works, that he is a
member of holy mother church?[^7] For although such a man by his cautery empties out the perceptible
flux of vices, yet the devil, author of such superfluous flux, spreads all the more, through the hidden
channels of hypocrites, through the whole remainder of the body, the flux of slippery sin; and so, made
a heresiarch by feigned [custom]{g:consuetudo}, he has [57] in the head, that is, in reason, the cautery
of Antichrist. For none, according to blessed Gregory in the *Register*, second chapter, do harm more
dangerously or more widely in the church of holy God.[^8] But, returning to the difficulty left aside, [it is objected, by appeal to]{g:obicitur-per} St Thomas,
*On Christ*, conclusion eight, article 3, where he says that "Christ is the head of all men, as much of
the faithful, who are united to him in act through grace, as of unbelievers, who are only in potency
his members"; and afterwards he subdivides according to the [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} and the
[foreknown]{g:prescitus}, who, departing from this world, wholly cease to be members of Christ. And he
proves this by many an argument.[^9] As to this testimony of St Thomas, it seems to me that he
equivocates, saying truly that Christ according to [the Godhead]{g:deitas} is the extrinsic head of the
whole human race, which can be called [aggregatively]{g:aggregative} one natural body, to which Christ
also does good, as also to the whole world, according to his humanity, since by virtue of Christ's
passion a certain secondary perfection is acquired for the whole world. And so according to his
humanity he does good to the whole human race, since he punishes all the damned: either for
[unbelief]{g:infidelitas}, as those who do not look to the Lord Jesus Christ; or for despair, which
they ought to have put away and aspired to heavenly things; or, third, for rash judgment, which they
ought to have put away and adhered finally, in charity, to the Lord Jesus Christ.[^10] And so is
understood Christ's threefold "because", John 16:8: "He will convince the world of sin and of justice
and [58] of judgment." Of sin, the sin of unbelief, which [by antonomasia]{g:autonomatice} is called sin:
"because they believed not in the Father nor in me"; of justice, which they ought to have had, aspiring
with fitting means to the natural [homeland]{g:patria}: "because I go to the Father"; and of judgment,
by which they rashly judge that the dogma of the world and of the devil is to be loved more ardently,
since "the prince of the world is judged" by Christ, who teaches the opposite.[^11] And so the opposites
of faith, hope and charity, which would correspond in order to the Trinity, damn the foreknown at the
final judgment (as I have set out at length elsewhere). For I have not seen in St Thomas, or in another
approved doctor, that that whole race, but [only] its predestinate part, is holy mother church, the
[bride]{g:sponsa} of Christ and the universal or catholic church,
[the mystical body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi-misticum} of which scripture speaks. And it is of such
[a church] that we are speaking.[^12]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 55.8: Litigation would be suppressed if the Pope asked rulers to withdraw the pay of those who neglected to minister.
- 55.18: The only needful rule being that men should be appointed for fitness and deposed if they deserve it.
- 55.28: Wickedness of priests troubles the Church.
- 56.1: 'Trouble will not cease until we return to the primitive order.
- 56.8: St Paul prophesied of "lovers of themselves" who care for wealth and not for their priestly duty;
- 56.16: "corrupters of the word", who wrest it to defend lying; "having a seared conscience", who proclaim themselves members of the Church but bring forth no fruit.
- 57.5: Objection: Christ is said by St. Thomas to be the head of all men.
- 57.14: In one sense this Is true as to his deity
- 57.20: and as to his humanity.
- 58.11: But St. Thomas does not say that the whole human race is the mystical body of Christ, but the part that is predestinate.

## Notes

[^1]: **S/T.** Gregory, *Moralia in Iob* 31.13 (§24), PL 76:586C, read this session (LEDGER E03c-1),
    word for word but *quod sunt* for *quia sunt*. The MSS read *bona* at 54.27; the print agrees with
    Gregory's *ea* (*ad tuenda ea que terrena sunt*), which is presumably the place. B *primo nedum* for
    *primo quia nedum*; AB margin: *Conclusio* (54.32).

[^2]: **C/S.** Gregory goes on to say that these men are *not* to be counted among the hypocrites, *quia
    aliud est infirmitate, aliud malitia peccare*, and sets against them those who know they act wickedly
    and swell with the name of sanctity (PL 76:586C–D). We take *istos nomine religiosos* as "these,
    religious in name [only]", Gregory's hypocrites, and the twofold malice as Wyclif's summary of that
    contrast; read with *istos* as the weak just described, Wyclif would be turning Gregory's
    distinction round. *Nomine*: plate. *Iste sanctus papa*: Gregory the Great. *Pro facinore querunt
    laudes*: Gregory's *de nomine sanctitatis intumescunt*.

[^3]: **T/C.** *Viveretur* is Loserth's emendation of the MSS' *videretur* (55.12); B *regnis regnis*
    (55.10), *ministrentur* (55.11–12). *Vocatis bonis ecclesie*: "so-called", as *vocate ecclesie*,
    62.7. *Examinent*: plural after the singular *ecclesiasticus*. *Subtracto … ministerio*: the stipend
    stops when the ministry is perceptibly withheld. *Dominus papa*: "the lord pope", the courtesy
    title, as *dominus episcopus* (GLOSSARY §B).

[^4]: **T/C.** A hard construal: *quod unus sit rector in ministerio sacerdotali, solicicior habiliorem
    preponere*. We take *solicicior* with the head (the bishop), and *de quo si non gauderet* of the
    man passed over, who resents the abler man's preferment. Alternatively: "that one is a more diligent
    rector in the priestly ministry, [it being the head's part] to prefer the abler", with *gauderet* of a
    rector who takes no joy in his ministry. A *cognito a capite* (55.19); AB *recto in* (55.20); B
    *humiliorem* for *habiliorem* (55.21). *Speculator*: the watchman of Ezek. 3:17, the bishop. A
    margin: *De eleccione ad prelacias*; B: *Nota de eleccione in preposituram qua forma fiet* (55.19).

[^5]: **T/C.** B *pacificari* (55.25). *Viri sancte matris ecclesie*: the churchmen's own claim (*viri*
    on the plate); against it Wyclif sets *apostatantes partes diaboli*. *Suffragii … derivandi*: the
    spiritual aid that should flow from God through priests as instruments.

[^6]: **S/C.** 2 Cor. 12:2 (the third heaven); length, breadth and depth from Eph. 3:18. *Se ipsos
    amantes*: 2 Tim. 3:2 (Loserth, 56.10); *adulterantes verbum Dei*: 2 Cor. 2:17 (Wyclif *domini*);
    *cauteriatam … conscientiam*: 1 Tim. 4:2, the place Wyclif cites. *Exposicionem beati Gregorii*:
    Loserth refers to Gregory's exposition of the fifth penitential psalm (56.10), not found in the PL text of that exposition (PL 79:601D–630C) (LEDGER E03c-2).
    *Sensus domini*: cf. Rom. 11:34. *Profunditatem … profundati*: we take it as stated; it may be
    ironic. *Confidenter*, *prenostico*: plate. A margin: *Nota bene* (56.1); B: *Qui dicunt se ipsos
    amare secundum apostolum* (56.8); A: *Verbum domini adulterantes et consciencia cauterizata*; B:
    *Adulteracio verbum (sic) dei nota. Nota habentes conscienciam cauteriatam (sic)* (56.10).

[^7]: **T/C.** AA₁ *Aut quod* for *Aut quid* (56.19–20); AB *defendet*, corrected in A to *defendat*
    (56.24). *Iuxta quem defendatur esse hereticum et blasfemum*: by the devil's sense, scripture itself
    is maintained to be heretical and blasphemous (false in its letter); *ymo … leve mendacium*: and a
    light lie is allowed on its authority. *Affeccionem proprietatis*: cf. *affeccio proprietaria*
    (GLOSSARY §B). *Cessans ab operibus reprehensibilibus in facie ecclesie*: the cautery stops only open
    sins, as the next sentence explains.

[^8]: **S/C.** The figure is surgical: a cautery stops the visible discharge while the matter spreads
    within. Gregory, *Regula pastoralis* 1.2: *Nemo quippe amplius in ecclesia nocet quam qui perverse
    agens nomen vel ordinem sanctitatis habet* (Loserth, 57.2; PL 77:16A), also in the *Decretum* D.83 c.2 (PL 187:400C; form
    ours) (LEDGER E03c-3); Wyclif paraphrases. Wyclif's *in Registro* names the work so. *Sancti Dei* is taken with
    *ecclesia*: "the church of holy God".

[^9]: **S/R.** Aquinas, *ST* III q.8 a.3 (Wyclif's *conclusione octava, articulo III°*; Loserth: Opp.,
    Venice 1774, V, 51) (LEDGER E03c-4). Loserth italicises *Christus est caput … eius membra* as
    quotation. *Difficultatem dimissam*: the question set aside, whether Christ is head of all men; the
    place is not identified. A margin: *Contra dicta. Nota: In equivoco conceditur quod Christus est capud
    tam fidelium quam infidelium omnium*; B: *S. Thomas*; AB: *Obieccio* (57.5–6).

[^10]: **T/C.** *Considerant* (57.23), as printed (plate n0094), hardly construes with *in dominum*: we
    translate "look to"; *crediderunt*, "believed in" (as at 58.2), is the likely sense. *Caput
    extrinsecum*: a head outside the body, not sharing its life. B *aggregare* for *aggregative* (57.16).
    *Qua dimissa debuerunt celestibus aspirare*: "which they ought to have put away and [so] aspired".
    AB margin: *Responsio* (57.13).

[^11]: **S/T.** John 16:8, 16:10, DR; 16:11 in Wyclif's clause (Vulgate *princeps huius mundi iam
    iudicatus est*, DR "the prince of this world is already judged"); 16:9 Wyclif *quia non crediderunt
    in patrem neque in me*, Vulgate *quia non crediderunt in me* (cf. John 16:3). *Triplex causalis*: the
    three *quia* clauses. The MSS read *autonomastice*; Loserth prints *autonomatice* (58.1). AB margin:
    *Joh. XVI* (57.29).

[^12]: **C.** *Opposita fidei, spei et caritatis*: unbelief, despair and rash judgment, the three sins
    above. *Ut alias diffuse exposui*: unidentified (LEDGER E03c-5). Construal: *quod totum illud genus
    sed pars eius predestinata sit*: "that that whole race, [and not rather] its predestinate part, is",
    rendered "rather than"; Loserth's side-note reads it so.
