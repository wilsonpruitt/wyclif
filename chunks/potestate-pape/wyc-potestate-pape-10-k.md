# *De potestate pape*, chapter 10, part 11: new articles of faith: no one must believe the pope is pope

## ¶1 · 257.33–258.18

> [p. 257] Notandum est tercio quod iste doctor videtur prudenter innuere quod nullus episcopus cesareus [p. 258] assumeret illud papale officium, quia obligat se ut sic quod sit in terris proximus sequax Christi, quod forte statui quem accipit est incompossibile. Ideo dicit Crysostomus Omelia XXVIII Operis Imperfecti: Quicumque, inquit, Domino humilitati studente iactancie studet, non est imago Christi, et qui Domino paupertatem amante diviciarum amator est in hoc seculo, repellit a se Christi similitudinem nec est vere discipulus qui non imitatur magistrum. Studeat igitur Romanus pontifex, si imitatur simillime vias Christi. Ideo dicit Epistola XLIII: Scio, inquit, domini pape et sancte sedis hanc esse potestatem, ut de omnibus beneficiis ecclesiasticis posset libere ordinare, scio quoque quod quisquis abutitur hac potestate edificat ad gehennam. Ideo (ut videtur multis) descripcio quam ponit de papa non convenit apostolis, sed solummodo pape summo.

It is to be noted, thirdly, that this doctor seems prudently to hint that no caesarean [bishop]{g:episcopus} [258] would take up that papal office, because he binds himself, as such, to be on earth the nearest follower of Christ, which is perhaps [incompossible]{g:incompossibilis} with the [state]{g:status} he takes on.[^1] Therefore Chrysostom says in Homily 28 of the *Opus imperfectum*: "Whoever," he says, "is zealous for boasting while the Lord was zealous for humility is not the image of Christ; and he who is a lover of riches in this world while the Lord loved poverty repels from himself the likeness of Christ; nor is he truly a disciple who does not imitate the master."[^2] Let the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex}, therefore, be zealous [to see] whether he imitates the ways of Christ most closely. Therefore he says in Letter 43: "I know," he says, "that this is the [power]{g:potestas} of the lord [pope]{g:papa} and of the holy see, that he could freely dispose of all ecclesiastical benefices; I know also that whoever abuses this power builds toward Gehenna."[^3] Therefore (as it seems to many) the [definition]{g:descripcio} that he gives of the pope does not fit the apostles, but only the highest pope.[^4]

## ¶2 · 258.19–259.8

> [p. 258] Unde propter talia verba quidam ad tantum insaniunt, quod dicunt neminem posse salvari nisi credat istum articulum fidei, quod quilibet papa sit per se causa tocius profectus spiritualis ecclesie. Unde Lyncolniensis Epistola XXXV: Ex generali debito subieccionis, quo non solum populus christianus sed totum humanum genus obligatur et sine cuius solucione nullus salutem consequitur, debitor sum sanctissime paternitati vestre excellentissimeque dominacioni plenitudinis, obediencie et reverencie, honoris et timoris. Et forte ex talibus verbis declamatoriis fuit iste doctor punitus a Domino per eum quem verbis floridis [p. 259] sic palpavit. Si enim iste sit tam necessarius articulus fidei, cum apostoli et omnes christiani ante dotacionem non crediderunt istum articulum, videtur quod absit quod eorum quilibet sit dampnandus. Sed dicti beati possunt de sic errantibus dicere irridendo: Novi recentesque articuli fidei per constitucionem imperatoris venerunt quos non coluerunt patres eorum.

Hence, because of such words, some go so far in madness that they say that no one can be saved unless he believes this [article of faith]{g:articulus-fidei}, that every pope is of himself the cause of all the spiritual profit of the [church]{g:ecclesia}.[^5] Hence Lincoln, Letter 35: "By the general debt of subjection, by which not only the Christian people but the whole human race is bound, and without the payment of which no one attains salvation, I am a debtor to your most holy paternity and most excellent [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio} of fullness of obedience and reverence, of honour and fear."[^6] And perhaps for such declamatory words this doctor was punished by the Lord through him whom he [259] so flattered with flowery words.[^7] For if this is so necessary an article of faith, since the apostles and all Christians before the [endowment]{g:dotacio} did not believe this article, it seems (God forbid) that every one of them is to be damned. But the said blessed ones can say mockingly of those who err so: "New and recent" articles of faith "have come" by the constitution of the emperor, "whom their fathers worshipped not".[^8]

## ¶3 · 259.9–259.23

> [p. 259] Item (ut patet ex decretis et cronicis tactis VIII capitulo) licencia et auctoritate cesaris factum vel fictum est quod generaliter suus episcopus sit caput ecclesie. Sed ille imperator non habet plus potestatem condendi novos articulos fidei quam quilibet alius christianus; sic enim posset condere novam legem Christi et legem conditam abrogare; non enim licet vel angelo de celo onerare sanctam ecclesiam novis articulis vel ritibus extra scripturam sacram a Spiritu Sancto doctam, ut patet in materia De Cessacione Legalium. Magna (inquam) libertas et privilegium fuit facilitas in qua Christus posuit suam ecclesiam, ut patet Gal. II; et hoc privilegium debent cuncti christiani defendere.

Again (as is clear from the decrees and chronicles touched on in chapter 8), it was by Caesar's [licence]{g:licencia} and [authority]{g:auctoritas} made, or feigned, that his bishop should generally be [head]{g:caput} of the church.[^9] Now that emperor has no more power of making new articles of faith than any other Christian; for so he could make a new law of Christ and abrogate the law already made; for it is not lawful even for an angel from heaven to burden holy church with new articles or rites outside holy scripture, taught by the Holy Spirit, as is clear in the matter *De cessatione legalium*.[^10] A great liberty and [privilege]{g:privilegium} (I say) was the lightness in which Christ set his church, as is clear in Gal. 2; and this privilege all Christians ought to defend.[^11]

## ¶4 · 259.24–259.32

> [p. 259] Item, non est possibile quod fidei orthodoxe sit falsitas substrata; sed sepe contingit quod Romanus pontifex non est papa, ymo posito quod sit; non est tantum necessarium ad salutem cuiuslibet christiani hoc credere, quod sine hoc non posset salvari, nam papa mortuo vel perverso non ex hoc titubat fides in quolibet christiano; et illud testarentur conversi per apostolos nostram nimirum demenciam deridendo.

Again, it is not possible that falsehood should underlie the orthodox faith; now it often happens that the Roman pontiff is not pope. Indeed, supposing that he is, it is not so necessary to the salvation of every Christian to believe this that without it he could not be saved; for when a pope is dead or perverted, the faith of any Christian does not totter on that account; and those converted by the apostles would testify to this, mocking, no doubt, our madness.[^12]

## ¶5 · 259.33–260.20

> [p. 259] Assumptum patet ex hoc quod aliter Deus necessitaret hominem ad menciendum et per consequens ad peccandum, et unus alius ymo sic [p. 260] illusus pie crederet eius contradiccionem, sicut christianus credit implicite esse verum; et sic oportet utramque sectam Urbani et Roberti dicere quod reliqua sit heretica, quia articuli fidei non limitantur sic ad loca, ut in ista villa teneantur credi et in proxima villa discredi. Nec valent sophismata quibus putantes subtiliare referunt quod papa potest facere novos articulos fidei et per consequens facere antiquos cessare, ymo facere christianos teneri credere falsitati, quia si quelibet veritas sit articulus fidei, tunc sacramenta potest facere, novos articulos ymo musca; sed et illum articulum sollempnizatum, quod iste sit papa, potest nedum imperator facere vel electores, sed quodcumque animatum vel inanimatum fuerit ad hoc movens. Sed evidencius foret, quod quis rogando Deum et perficiens papale officium, faciat se ipsum et plus timeat quod venenum peccati quam quod venenum corporis corrumpat istum christiane fidei articulum.

The [assumption]{g:assumptum} is clear from this, that otherwise God would necessitate a man to lie, and consequently to sin; and another man, indeed one so [260] deluded, would piously believe its contradictory, as a Christian implicitly believes it to be true; and so each sect, Urban's and Robert's, must say that the other is heretical, because articles of faith are not so limited to places that they are to be believed in this town and disbelieved in the next town.[^13] Nor do the [sophisms]{g:sophisma} avail by which those who think to refine report that the pope can make new articles of faith, and consequently make the old ones cease, indeed make Christians bound to believe a falsehood; for if every truth is an article of faith, then [one] can make sacraments, new articles, indeed a fly [can].[^14] And indeed that solemnized article, that this man is pope, not only the emperor or the electors can make, but whatever thing, animate or inanimate, has been moving to it. But it would be more evident that someone, by praying to God and carrying out the papal office, should make himself [pope], and should fear more that the poison of sin than that the poison of the body should corrupt this article of the Christian faith.[^15]

## ¶6 · 260.21–260.32

> [p. 260] In ista materia dixi alias quomodo quedam est credulitas explicita vel distincta, et quedam est implicita vel confusa; et isto modo tenetur omnis christianus credere singulas veritates, quia tenetur credere Deum esse, quod est omnis veritas. Hoc autem omnis fidelis tenetur pro aliquo tempore vie distincte credere, et sic quicumque in Deum finaliter crediderit salvabitur, etsi non plus de grano synapis habuerit. Alios autem oportet plus et alios minus de fide distincte credere secundum quod plus vel minus de gracia ad hoc obligante habuerit.

On this matter I have said elsewhere how one [credence]{g:credulitas} is explicit or distinct, and another implicit or [indistinct]{g:confusus}; and in this way every Christian is bound to believe each single truth, because he is bound to believe that God is, which is every truth.[^16] And this every believer is bound to believe distinctly for some time of [the way]{g:via}; and so whoever has finally believed in God will be saved, even if he has had no more than a grain of mustard seed.[^17] And some must believe more and others less of the faith distinctly, according as they have had more or less of the grace that binds them to it.

## ¶7 · 261.1–261.7

> [p. 261] Unde quidam vocant solum illas veritates catholicas quas oportet viatorem distincte credere articulos fidei, cuiusmodi sunt veritates expresse in symbolo; alii autem quamlibet veritatem catholicam articulum fidei, quia partem parvulam totalis fidei. Et illa concedit conclusiones sequentes, que equivocantibus videntur mirabiles.

Hence some call articles of faith only those catholic truths which the [wayfarer]{g:viator} must believe distinctly, such as are the truths expressed in the [creed]{g:symbolum}; but others [call] any catholic truth an article of faith, because [it is] a small part of the whole faith.[^18] And that [usage] grants the following conclusions, which seem marvellous to those who equivocate.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** "This doctor" is Grosseteste (Lincoln), whose sayings the chapter has been weighing since 256.3. *ut sic*: "as such", i.e. as pope. A "caesarean bishop" is the bishop endowed by Caesar (GLOSSARY *prelatus / sacerdos cesarius*).
[^2]: **S.** Pseudo-Chrysostom, *Opus imperfectum in Matthaeum*, Wyclif's "homily 28"; in Migne the passage closes hom. 35, on Matt. 20:20–28 (PG 56, 832) (Loserth: Opp. tom. VI, p. 771) (LEDGER P10k-1). **C.** *Domino humilitati studente* and *Domino paupertatem amante* are ablatives absolute, "while the Lord …".
[^3]: **S.** Wyclif cites "Letter 43"; Loserth identifies it as Grosseteste's Letter 49 (*Epistolae*, ed. Luard, p. 145) (LEDGER P10k-2). **C.** *Studeat … si imitatur*: *si* is "whether". The speaker of the letter is Grosseteste, "this doctor".
[^4]: **C.** *pape summo*, "the highest pope": the definition fits only the pope at the summit, perhaps Christ himself, whom alone it would fit without qualification; Wyclif does not say which.
[^5]: **R.** Both manuscripts note in the margin (Loserth, 258): *Papam dicunt esse necessarium ad salutem*; and at the letter: *Lincolniensis de papa nota bene*. **C.** *per se*: "of himself", as a cause in his own right.
[^6]: **S.** Grosseteste, Letter 35 (Luard, p. 123) (Loserth) (LEDGER P10k-3). **C.** *plenitudinis* is read with *obediencie … timoris*, "[a debtor] of fullness of obedience …"; the print's comma would instead join it to *dominacioni* ("lordship of plenitude"). **G.** *dominacio*, here a title of address, kept as "exercise of lordship" (GLOSSARY).
[^7]: **R.** Letter 35 is addressed to Gregory IX (Luard, p. 123); Wyclif seems to take the pope flattered and the pope who punished as one, presumably Innocent IV, whom Grosseteste later resisted and, after his own death, is said to have struck with his staff (257.27–30; chapter 8; Matthew Paris, *Chronica majora* V, 429–30, 470–71) (LEDGER P10k-4).
[^8]: **S.** Deut. 32:17, "gods … that were newly come up, whom their fathers worshipped not"; Wyclif sets "articles of faith" in the place of the gods. Not italic in the print; quoted here as scripture. **T.** B omits *per constitucionem imperatoris* (259.7). **C.** "The said blessed ones": the apostles and the Christians before the endowment.
[^9]: **R.** Chapter 8 rehearsed the imperial grants by which the Roman bishop came to be called head (Phocas, the Donation of Constantine) and the chronicles that report them: at 177–78 Wyclif cites Higden IV, 36 and V, 10 and the Decretum's *Constantinus* (D. 96 c. 14), and concludes *Quotlibet tales fideles cronice et decreta testantur …* (LEDGER P10k-5). **C.** *suus episcopus*: Caesar's bishop, the bishop of his capital.
[^10]: **S.** "Even … an angel from heaven": Gal. 1:8. *De cessatione legalium* is Grosseteste's treatise, which Wyclif cites by name at *De veritate sacrae scripturae* I, 135 (*Lincolniensis de cessacione legalium*); he has no work of that title, and uses the same phrase *in materia de cessacione legalium* at *De officio regis* 208 (LEDGER P10k-6). **C.** *doctam* is taken with *scripturam*, "scripture taught by the Holy Spirit"; it can agree with *ecclesiam*, "the church taught by the Holy Spirit". **T.** The manuscripts omit *a* (259.19), with the same sense.
[^11]: **S.** Gal. 2:4–5, "our liberty which we have in Christ Jesus", against those "that they might bring us into servitude". **C.** *facilitas*: the lightness of the burden Christ laid on his church.
[^12]: **C.** *sed* brings the minor premiss; the conclusion (that "this man is pope" is no article of faith) is left to the reader. *ymo posito quod sit* is read with what follows, against the print's semicolon.
[^13]: **C.** The assumption is the major premiss of ¶4. *unus alius ymo sic illusus*: "another man, indeed one so deluded", i.e. the believer of the other obedience, deceived as the first. **R.** Urban VI and Robert of Geneva (Clement VII), rival claimants since 1378. **T.** B reads *sic teneantur* (260.6), with the same sense.
[^14]: **T.** Loserth at 260.13: *Musca; hic aliquot verba exciderunt*, "some words have fallen out here". The print is translated as it stands, with *musca* taken as the subject of *potest*: if every truth is an article, whatever makes a truth (a fly landing) makes an article. **C.** *subtiliare*: here "to refine", to argue subtly, not the GLOSSARY's provisional "lighten" (from the *Dialogus*). **R.** Both manuscripts at 260.8: *Articuli novi fidei a papa*.
[^15]: **C.** *faciat se ipsum*: a complement ("pope") is understood or lost. A pope makes good the article "this man is pope" by prayer and the work of the office, and should fear sin, which would make it false, more than bodily poison.
[^16]: **S.** "I have said elsewhere": Loserth compares *Trialogus*, p. 413 (ed. Lechler, *Supplementum Trialogi* ch. 2: *aliqua creduntur explicite et aliqua implicite … sive quas confuse credimus*) (LEDGER P10k-7). **C.** "In this way": implicitly. *quod est omnis veritas*: God, who is all truth. **R.** Both manuscripts at 260.21: *Credulitas duplex*.
[^17]: **S.** Matt. 17:19, "if you have faith as a grain of mustard seed".
[^18]: **C.** *illa* (read on the plate) is taken as the second way of speaking; its conclusions begin at 261.8. **T.** B reads *pervulgam* for *parvulam* (261.5). **R.** Both manuscripts at 261.3: *Articuli fidei*.
