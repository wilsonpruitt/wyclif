# *De eucharistia*, chapter 5, part 11: who is a heretic, and the writings imputed to Thomas

## ¶1 · 155.16–157.14

> [p. 155] Hic dicitur quod multi sunt heretici, aliqui cogniti et aliqui ignoti, omnes enim presciti sunt heretici, sed aliqui sunt heretici in actu et alii in habitu, licet sint in gracia secundum presentem iusticiam. Aliqui eciam sunt heretici ad tempus solum secundum presentem iusticiam ut predestinati in mortali, et sic de multis divisionibus cum suis declaracionibus quas hic suppono, sed notum est quod stat multos homines tenere sentenciam hereticam sine hoc quod ipsi sint tunc heretici, quia multi opinative vel condicionaliter tenent multas sentencias parati ad revocandum docto opposito; et certum est quod nulli tales sunt ut sic heretici, quia tunc Doctor Communis fuisset nimis crebro hereticus. Unde videtur quod illi dogmatisantes qui deficientibus argumentis defamant universitatem in vulgari propter dicta scolastica et sanctorum sentencias sint publice puniendi; hoc autem optarem, [p. 156] cum ipsi sint manifeste heretici nisi probaverint quod divulgant aut quod non accipiant dignitatem in Oxonia antequam docuerint heresim quam imponunt; nam de natura accusacionis heretice est quod accusator obligat se ut sic ad penam talionis, cum credit katholicum eius contradictorium quod inpugnat; et idem est iudicium de toto capitulo quod consentit. Sic ergo multi sancti preterierunt istum articulum de sacris accidentibus tamquam fidei contrarium a nullis sciolis sui temporis attemptatum, ut patet de antiquis sanctis doctoribus. Et videtur quod ecclesia Avinonica ponens transsubstanciacionem non exhinc implicat substanciam panis destrui vel corrumpi. Sed conformiter ad antiquum decretum Romane ecclesie permittit panis substanciam remanere; unum tamen credo quod quicunque pertinaciter crediderit quod non remanent panis et vinum in hostia consecrata, sed quod ipsa est globus sacrorum accidencium sine subiecto, est manifeste hereticus a qualibet universitate fidelium expellendus. Contradicit eciam fidei quam magnus Augustinus confessus est cum Romana ecclesia. Nec usque hodie scivit fidelis vel infidelis inpugnare istam sentenciam Augustini. Sentencia autem ista que sancto Thome ascribitur est tam infirma et debilis quod nemo scit illam defendere vel probare; et credo quod non fient efficaciores argucie ad hereticandum predictam Augustini sentenciam quam in nomine Communis Doctoris recitate et patule sunt solute. Non enim consonat fidei Christi, quod vel sine causa aut ordine accusetur persona de heresi, vel quod eatur ad curiam mendaciter procurando katholicacionem [p. 157] omnium que scripsit sanctus Thomas. Primum non quia in hoc differt secta Machometi a secta Christi quod hic pseudo legem suam iubet defendi non racione sed gladio; Christus autem mandat in Petro quod de omni fide sua debent discipuli eius discrete, modeste et pacifice reddere racionem. Scarioth autem excitatus a dyabolo accepit cohortem et ministros pontificum, sed caute in tenebra, ut deprimeret veritatem, ut patet Joh. XVIIIᵒ, 3, sed pseudo Scariotis discipuli agitati a demonio meridiano publice in sermonibus incitant populum in vulgari, ut destruant tamquam hereticos simplices discipulos veritatis. Nec mirum quia eodem spiritu in fratres proprios tanquam Roberti discipuli magis callide machinantur.

Here [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that many are heretics, some known and some unknown, for all [the foreknown]{g:prescitus} are heretics;[^1] but some are heretics in act and others in habit, although they may be in grace [according to present justice]{g:secundum-presentem-iusticiam}. Some also are heretics for a time only, according to present justice, as [the predestinate]{g:predestinatus} in mortal [sin]; and so of many divisions with their explanations, which I here suppose. But it is known that it can stand that many men hold a heretical [teaching]{g:sentencia}[^2] without their being heretics then, for many hold many teachings [as an opinion]{g:opinative} or conditionally, ready to retract when the opposite is taught; and it is certain that none such are heretics as such, for then [the Common Doctor]{g:doctor-communis} would have been a heretic [exceedingly]{g:nimis} often. Hence it seems that those who [teach as dogma]{g:dogmatisare}, who, when arguments fail them, defame the university[^3] in the vernacular for scholastic sayings and the teachings of [the saints]{g:sancti}, should be publicly punished; and this I would wish, [156] since they are manifestly heretics unless they prove what they spread abroad, or [I would wish] that they not receive a dignity at Oxford before they have shown the heresy which they impute;[^4] for it is of the nature of an accusation of heresy that the accuser, as such, binds himself to the [penalty of retaliation]{g:pena-talionis}, since he believes the contradictory of what he attacks to be catholic;[^5] and the same judgement holds of the whole chapter that consents.[^6] So therefore many saints passed over this article of the sacred [accidents]{g:accidens} as contrary to faith, an article ventured by none of the [wise]{g:sciolus} of their time, as is clear of the ancient holy doctors.[^7] And it seems that the Avignon [church]{g:ecclesia}, positing [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio}, does not thereby imply that the [substance]{g:substancia} of the [bread]{g:panis} is destroyed or corrupted. But in conformity with the ancient [decree]{g:decretum} of the Roman church it allows the substance of the bread to remain;[^8] yet one thing I believe: that whoever obstinately believes "bread and [wine]{g:vinum} do not remain in the [consecrated host]{g:hostia}, but it is a mass of sacred accidents [without a subject]{g:accidens-sine-subiecto}" is manifestly a heretic, to be expelled from any [whole body of the faithful]{g:universitas-fidelium}.[^9] He also contradicts the faith which the great Augustine confessed with the Roman church. Nor to this day has believer or [unbeliever]{g:infidelis} known how to attack this teaching of Augustine. But this teaching which is ascribed to St Thomas is so weak and feeble that no one knows how to defend or prove it; and I believe that no more effective [quibbles]{g:argucie} will be made for [pronouncing heretical]{g:hereticare} the aforesaid teaching of Augustine than those which have been [recited]{g:recitare} in the name of the Common Doctor and openly [resolved]{g:solvere}. For it does not accord with the faith of Christ either that a person be accused of heresy without cause or order, or that one go to the curia, lyingly procuring the [declaration as catholic]{g:katholicacio} [157] of all that St Thomas wrote.[^10] Not the first, because the [sect]{g:secta} of Mahomet differs from the sect of Christ in this, that this false one orders his law to be defended not by reason but by the sword; but Christ commands in Peter that his disciples ought to render a reason of all their faith discreetly, modestly and peaceably.[^11] And Iscariot, stirred up by the devil, took a band and servants of the [pontiffs]{g:pontifex}, but warily, in the dark, to suppress the truth, as is clear from John 18:3; but the false ones, disciples of Iscariot,[^12] driven by the noonday devil, publicly in sermons incite the people in the vernacular, to destroy as heretics the simple disciples of the truth.[^13] Nor is it a wonder, for by the same spirit they plot more cunningly against their own brethren as disciples of Robert.[^14]

## ¶2 · 157.15–158.10

> [p. 157] Quantum ad secundum patet quod multe sentencie heretice sunt scriptis sancti Thome imposite; patet quod idem foret in tali involucro approbare totam sentenciam sancti Thome et in equivocacionem tocius evangelii et fidei katholice inducere dogmata Antichristi. Inponitur enim sibi Secunda Secunde, questione LXVII, articulo IIᵒ, quod asserit iudicem debere sentenciare secundum allegata et probata, licet sciverit illa esse falsa et iudicio divino contraria, quod est manifeste hereticum, pallians Pylati iudicium contra Christum. Ideo idem foret defendere istam sentenciam et defendere quod propter tradiciones humanas licet peccando facere contra Deum. Cum ergo beatus Augustinus et alii sancti doctores qui plus sciverunt quam mille tales doctores nunquam vendicarunt sed [p. 158] reprobarunt quod credatur de eis si hoc asserunt, ergo verum, qua fronte vendicarent fratres viciantes sic scripta Thome si ipse sic asserit, ergo verum? Nam Doctor Subtilis cui plus credendum est in speculacionibus quam sibi in pluribus plane detegit errores quos seminat. Cum ergo plane infirmate sunt raciones predicte, facile est tollere alias fiendas contra sentenciam Augustini et Romane ecclesie. Non ergo sequitur: Si Thomas hoc asserit, ergo hoc est populo predicandum.

As to the second, it is clear that many heretical teachings have been imputed to the writings of St Thomas;[^15] it is clear that, in such a [wrapping]{g:involucrum}, to approve the whole teaching of St Thomas would be the same as to bring in the dogmas of Antichrist, to the [equivocation]{g:equivocacio} of the whole gospel and of the catholic faith.[^16] For it is imputed to him, in the *Secunda Secundae*, question 67, article 2, that he asserts that a judge ought to [pronounce]{g:sentenciare} according to what is [alleged]{g:allegare} and proved, although he knows it to be false and contrary to divine judgement;[^17] which is manifestly heretical, [cloaking]{g:palliare} Pilate's judgement against Christ. Therefore it would be the same to defend this teaching and to defend "for the sake of human traditions it is lawful, by sinning, to act against God". Since, then, blessed Augustine and other holy doctors, who knew more than a thousand such doctors, never claimed, but [158] rejected, that it be believed of them "if they assert this, therefore it is true", with what face would the friars, who thus corrupt the writings of Thomas, claim "if he so asserts, therefore it is true"?[^18] For [the Subtle Doctor]{g:doctor-subtilis}, who is to be believed more than he in speculative matters, plainly [exposes]{g:detegere} in many places the errors which he sows.[^19] Since, then, the aforesaid arguments have been plainly weakened, it is easy to [demolish]{g:tollere} others that may be made against the teaching of Augustine and of the Roman church.[^20] Therefore [it does not follow]{g:sequitur}: "If Thomas asserts this, therefore this is to be preached to the people."

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 156.21: Augustine is on our side and is of much more weight than a saying ascribed to St. Thomas;

## Notes

[^1]: **G.** *Presciti*, "the foreknown": Wyclif's term for those God foreknows will be damned; not "the reprobate" (*reprobi*). *Hic dicitur* opens Wyclif's answer to the question who is a heretic.
[^2]: **C.** *Sentencia* is the CONTEXTUAL row: here, and throughout the chunk, the position taught ("teaching"), not a judicial sentence.
[^3]: **C.** *Universitatem*: the university, as the mention of Oxford in the next clause shows.
[^4]: **C.** Both clauses hang on *optarem*: Wyclif would wish them punished, or else barred from a *dignitas* (an academic degree or rank) at Oxford until they have shown (*docuerint*) the heresy they impute. ABCE read *heretici aut* and ABC *probaverunt*; ABDE lack *aut* (156.2); D *docuerant* (156.3). The print is translated.
[^5]: **S/C.** The *pena talionis*: the accuser who fails to prove his charge suffers the penalty he sought for the accused. That canon law bound an accuser of heresy in this way is to be checked (LEDGER K05k-1). ABD read *accionis* for *accusacionis* (156.4); C corrects in the margin.
[^6]: **C.** *Capitulum*: a chapter of a religious house or order that consents to the accusation; the referent is not named.
[^7]: **C.** *Tamquam fidei contrarium* goes with *articulum*: the saints passed the article over as contrary to faith, and *attemptatum* says no learned man of their time ventured it. AB read *sunt* for *sancti* (156.8); E *suis temporibus* (156.10).
[^8]: **S.** The "ancient decree of the Roman church" is presumably *Ego Berengarius* (Gratian, *De cons.* D. 2 c. 42; the form is ours), which the treatise calls the faith of the ancient Roman church; Loserth gives no reference here (LEDGER K05k-2).
[^9]: **C/T.** *Globus*: of things, "mass" (the §D row is for persons, "band"). *A qualibet universitate fidelium* may play on *universitas* "university" just above. E reads *maxime hereticus* (156.19); C has *fidelium* in the margin in another hand.
[^10]: **C.** *Katholicacio*, "declaration as catholic", is the noun of *catholicare*; the curia is the papal curia. Which proceeding Wyclif means is not said (LEDGER K05k-3). E reads *scripserit* (157.1).
[^11]: **S.** A paraphrase of 1 Pet. 3:15, DR "being ready always to satisfy every one that asketh you a reason of that hope which is in you"; Wyclif has "of all their faith" and adds *discrete, modeste et pacifice*. D reads *de Petro* (157.4); E lacks *omni* (157.5).
[^12]: **C.** *Pseudo Scariotis discipuli*: *pseudo* is taken as a noun, as *hic pseudo* of Mahomet just above ("the false ones, disciples of Iscariot"); it could also qualify *discipuli* ("the false disciples of Iscariot"). *Ministros pontificum* follows John 18:3 (DR "servants from the chief priests").
[^13]: **S/T.** *Demonio meridiano*: Ps. 90:6, DR "the noonday devil". ABC read *publico* (157.10); D *concitant* for *incitant* (157.11).
[^14]: **C/S.** *Tanquam Roberti discipuli* is taken with *fratres proprios*: the friars plot against their own brethren as being disciples of Robert. Which Robert (Grosseteste is a candidate) is not said (LEDGER K05k-4).
[^15]: **R/T.** *Quantum ad secundum* answers the second branch of 156.31–157.1 (going to the curia for all Thomas's writings). Loserth's side-note reads "ascribed"; *imposite* is rendered "imputed", as *inponitur* below. In C, *imposite — Thome* (157.16–18) is in the margin in another hand.
[^16]: **C/T.** *In equivocacionem*: so as to make the whole gospel and faith equivocal. D reads *enumeracionem* (157.18).
[^17]: **S/T.** Thomas, *ST* II-II q. 67 a. 2, "whether a judge may judge against the truth he knows" (Loserth's reference, ed. Venice 1774, III p. 291) (LEDGER K05k-5). DE read *asserat* (157.21). For *divino* (157.23) AD read *omnino*; B corrects in the margin; A in the margin in another hand *alias divino*. The print is translated.
[^18]: **T.** *Viciantes*, "corrupting", is Loserth's reading; the manuscripts have *vicinantes* (158.2). ABCD lack *doctores* and ABC read *vendicaverunt* (157.29); C reads *asseruunt* (158.1).
[^19]: **C/S.** *Sibi* and the subject of *seminat* are Thomas: Scotus is to be believed more than Thomas and exposes his errors. That Scotus criticizes Thomas in many places is Wyclif's general claim; no passage is named (LEDGER K05k-6).
[^20]: **T.** *Fiendas*, "that may be made", is the print; Loserth's apparatus says *rectius: ficticias* ("rather: fictitious"), which would give "other fictitious [arguments]". C has *alias* in the margin in another hand (158.7). The print construes and is translated.
