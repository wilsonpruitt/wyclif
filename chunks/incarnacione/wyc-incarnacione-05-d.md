# *De benedicta incarnacione*, chapter 5, part 4: the impossible cases set aside, and why the Word assumed this man

## ¶1 · 76.32–77.27

> [p. 76] Et utinam nos¹¹ scolastici, nescientes probare vel [p. 77] sophistice secundum vivacem¹ scintillam evidencie tales casus, insisteremus circa noticiam et deteccionem² substrate necessarie veritatis a nobis hodie plurimum occultate propter inanem occupacionem circa conclusiones impossibiles, que ex principiis impossibilibus consecuntur³: ut patet specialiter de casibus quibus ponitur Deum creaturam suam adnichilare, vel Verbum naturas prius assumptas dimittere ac alias personas in eisdem naturis assumendo communicare. Conceditur ergo quod, sicut libere contradictorie et per consequens contingentissime assumpsit hominem, sic potest absolute necessario nunc et semper facere vel⁴ causare quod neminem assumpsit, et quod non est vel⁵ fuit vel erit aliqua crcatura, ymo quod ipsummet non fuit vel⁶ erit amplius Verbum Dei. Patet hoc, posito quod non sit tempus. Sed absit ex isto concludere quod Deus potest quidquam adnichilare, vel facere tempus aut unionem predictam cessare. Talis enim non foret potencia contradiccionis ut ad affirmacionem et negacionem, sed potencia adnichilacionis,⁷ destruccionis, sive privacionis. Et patet solucio ad primam confirmacionem cum secunda. Deus enim necessitat se volicionibus⁸ eternis ad omne quod efficit, et tamen non potest efficere nisi summe libere contingenter, cum necessitas illa non sit coaccionis a causa superiori limitante; et per consequens nec pure naturalis inclinacionis, sed necessitas supposicionis, ut alias diffuse exposui. Et patet ad terciam quod Deus non est omnipotens, si posset vinculum dicte corrigie denodare.

Would that we schoolmen, who do not know how to prove such cases even [sophistically]{g:sophistice}[^1] by a lively spark of [evidence]{g:evidencia}, would apply ourselves to the knowledge and uncovering of the underlying necessary truth, which is today very much hidden from us on account of idle occupation with impossible conclusions that follow from impossible principles: as is clear especially of the cases in which it is posited that God [annihilates]{g:adnichilare} his [creature]{g:creatura}, or that [the Word]{g:verbum} [lays aside]{g:dimittere} natures earlier [assumed]{g:assumere} and, by assuming, communicates other persons in the same natures.[^2] It is granted, therefore, that just as he assumed a man [freely, as between contradictories]{g:libere-contradictorie}, and consequently most contingently, so he can, absolutely, necessarily, now and always, make or cause that he assumed no one, and that there is not, was not, or will not be any creature; indeed, that he himself was not, or will no longer be, the Word of God.[^3] This is clear on the supposition that there is no time. But far be it to [infer]{g:concludere} from this that God can annihilate anything, or make time or the aforesaid [union]{g:unio} cease. For such a power would not be a [power]{g:potencia} of contradiction, as to affirmation and negation, but a power of annihilation, destruction, or privation.[^4] And the answer to the first confirmation, together with the second, is clear. For God necessitates himself by eternal volitions to everything that he effects, and yet he cannot effect anything except in the highest degree freely [and] contingently, since that necessity is not one of coercion by a superior cause that limits [him]; and consequently not of purely natural inclination either, but [necessity of supposition]{g:necessitas-supposicionis}, as I have set out at length elsewhere. And it is clear, as to the third, that God is not omnipotent if he could untie the bond of the said thong.[^5]

## ¶2 · 77.28–78.6

> [p. 77] Et quoad argumenta fundata in loco a simili, vellem quod sic arguentes attenderent et stabilirent sufficientem similitudinem argumentis,⁹ et non quererent questiones novas induendo habitum responsalis vel dicendo negativas, quod ipsi nesciunt racionem diversitatis et alia multa verba preter [p. 78] scolastica. Unde quando assumitur quod tanta est distancia inter terminos creacionis sicut inter terminos adnichilacionis, pure petitur quod antea probaretur. Oporteret¹ ergo primo fundare existenciam adnichilacionis vel eius possibilitatem, et post sollicitari de eius terminis. Sed hoc non probabitur antequam humanitas Christi sit soluta.²

And as for the arguments founded on [the topic from likeness]{g:locus-a-simili}, I would wish that those who argue so would attend to and establish a sufficient likeness for the arguments, and not seek new questions by putting on the habit of [the respondent]{g:responsalis}, or by saying in the negative that they do not know the reason for the difference, and many other words beside the [78] scholastic ones. Hence, when it is assumed[^6] that the distance between the terms of creation is as great as that between the terms of annihilation, what ought first to have been proved is simply begged. It would therefore be necessary first to ground the existence of annihilation, or its possibility, and afterwards to be concerned about its terms. But this will not be proved before the [humanity]{g:humanitas} of Christ is [loosed]{g:solvere}.

## ¶3 · 78.7–78.15

> [p. 78] Ex istis videtur corelarie³ sequi primo quod neutra natura creata in Christo posset esse humanitas, nisi a Verbo ypostatice sit⁴ assumpta. Patet ex hoc quod aliter natura, que est Christus, dampnificari⁵ poterit; et sic Christus: si enim humanitas Christi posset fuisse⁶ individuum speciei hominis et non Christus, tunc posset peccasse ut unus homo fuisse⁷ alius. Quorum opposita hic suppono, ponendo quod assumpcio ypostatica a Verbo Dei consequitur⁸ ad hominem Iesum, sicut passio consequitur⁹ ad subiectum.

From these things it seems to follow, as a corollary, first, that neither created [nature]{g:natura} in Christ could be the humanity unless it were [hypostatically]{g:ypostatice} assumed by the Word.[^7] This is clear from the fact that otherwise the nature which is Christ could be [damaged]{g:dampnificare}, and so [could] Christ:[^8] for if the humanity of Christ could have been an [individual]{g:individuum} of the species man and not Christ, then it could have sinned as a single man, [and] have been another.[^9] The opposites of these I here suppose, positing that hypostatic assumption by the Word of God follows on the man Jesus as an [attribute]{g:passio} follows on its [subject]{g:subiectum}.

## ¶4 · 78.16–78.20

> [p. 78] 2°. sequitur quod nullam humanitatem aliam Verbum potuit ypostatice assumpsisse. Patet ex hoc, quod aliter Christus posset¹⁰ fuisse alius et¹¹ per consequens alia persona, ut post ostendetur.¹² Cum ergo¹³ consequens sit impossibile, patet quod antecedens inferius.¹⁴

Second, [it follows]{g:sequitur} that the Word could not have hypostatically assumed any other humanity. This is clear from the fact that otherwise Christ could have been another, and consequently another [person]{g:persona}, as will be shown later. Since, therefore, the [consequent]{g:consequens} is impossible, it is clear that the [antecedent]{g:antecedens} that infers it [is impossible too].[^10]

## ¶5 · 78.21–78.28

> [p. 78] 3°.¹⁵ sequitur quod nec Pater nec Donum incarnari potuit,¹⁶ quia aliter Filius posset¹⁷ esse humanitus pater patris. Cum ergo minimum inconsequens¹⁸ sit Deo impossibile absolute, ut capit Anselmus de Incarnacione Verbi cap 6°, patet intentum. Ista tamen dicta erunt magis evidencia postmodum, cum iuxta posite fuerint sentencie modernorum. Nam iste sunt conclusiones Anselmi Cur Deus Homo 6°. et de Incarnacione 7°. et 9°.

Third, it follows that neither the Father nor [the Gift]{g:donum} could be incarnate, since otherwise the Son could be [humanly]{g:humanitus} father of the father. Since, therefore, the least [inconsistency]{g:inconsequens}[^11] is absolutely impossible to God, as Anselm takes it in *On the Incarnation of the Word*, ch. 6, what was intended is clear. Yet these sayings will be more evident later, when the [teachings]{g:sentencia} of the moderns have been set beside them. For these are the conclusions of Anselm, *Cur Deus homo* 6 and *On the Incarnation* 7 and 9.[^12]

## ¶6 · 78.29–79.17

> [p. 78] Ad omnes¹⁹ auctoritates vel dicta auctorum in oppositum alleganda dicitur, sicut in materia De Adnichilacione, quod intelligenda sunt condicionaliter, quod Deus ex se habet [p. 79] potenciam sufficientem: unde potest in talia si voluerit, vel si illa possint¹ fieri. Sic enim dicit Augustinus contra Maximinianum, quod Deus potuit alium filium produxisse. Et isto modo loquendi sepe utitur venerabilis Anselmus, ut patet 2°. Cur Deus Homo cap. 5. Et ex hoc processerunt omnes sollicitaciones inanes² quid illo posito sequeretur.² Verumptamen magna consideracio est et nobis inscrutabilis,³ unde Deus voluit illum hominem pre ceteris assumpsisse, quod rememorat Augustinus Enchiridion 3°. Profecto modus iste, quo natus est Christus de Spiritu Sancto non ut eius filius de Maria virgine, sic⁴ insinuat⁴ nobis graciam Dei, qua homo, nullis precedentibus meritis in ipso exordio nature sue, quo esse cepit, Verbo Dei copularetur in tantam persone unitatem ut idem ipse esset Filius Dei qui filius hominis; ac sic nature humane suscepcione fieret quodammodo ipsa gracia illi homini naturalis que nullum peccatum posset admittere.

To all the authorities, or sayings of authors, to be [alleged]{g:allegare} to the contrary, [the answer is]{g:dicitur}, as in the matter *On Annihilation*,[^13] that they are to be understood conditionally: that God of himself has [79] sufficient power, so that he can do such things if he wills, or if they could be done. For so Augustine says, *Against Maximinus*, that God could have produced another Son.[^14] And the venerable Anselm often uses this manner of speaking, as is clear in *Cur Deus homo* 2, ch. 5.[^15] And from this have proceeded all the idle anxieties as to what would follow if that were posited. Nevertheless it is a great consideration, and unsearchable to us, why God willed to have assumed that man before others, which Augustine recalls, *Enchiridion* 3:[^16] "Truly this manner in which Christ was born of the Holy Spirit not as his son, [and] of the Virgin Mary as son,[^17] intimates to us the grace of God, by which a man, with no merits going before, in the very beginning of his nature, in which he began to be, should be joined to the Word of God in so great a unity of person that the same one should be Son of God who was son of man; and so, by the [taking-up]{g:suscipere} of human nature, that grace itself should become in a certain way natural to that man, which could admit no sin."

## ¶7 · 79.18–80.12

> [p. 79] Oportet ergo theologum capere tanquam principium quod Deus libere⁵ contradictorie⁶ assumpsit illum hominem sicut libere contradictorie⁶ produxit fabricam huius⁷ mundi, et huiusmodi effectuum⁷ causa est eterna⁸ Dei volicio,⁹ et per consequens essencia ac bonitas Dei nostri cuius non est querenda causa ulterior. Et sicut respondetur querenti,¹⁰ ‘Quare Deus produxit mundum in illo loco vel in illo tempore pocius quam quocunque alio?’— quod¹¹ hoc est necessarium ex supposicione, eo quod non posset illum mundum producere nisi ipsum produceret in illo loco et in illo tempore¹¹; ita respondetur querenti—‘ Quare Deus assumpsit illum hominem? ’—quod hoc est quia Deus voluit assumere hominem ad complecionem sue fabrice, et non potuit [p. 80] assumpsisse hominem, nisi illum hominem¹ assumpsisset. Sicut enim ille locus et illud tempus individuatur ab illo mundo, sic quod ubicunque vel quandocunque Deus² produxerit³ illum mundum, hoc fecisset in illo loco et in illo tempore. Sic illa humanitas, que est Iesus,⁴ sufficienter individuatur a Verbo, sic quod posita illa consequitur suppositacio sui a Verbo; et quamcumque aliam humanitatem per impossibile Verbum assumpsisset⁵ illa humanitas foret Iesus. Dimissis ergo casibus et conclusionibus ex illis inaniter coniecturatis,⁶ teneamus confessionem quod dicta ‘scriptura solvi non potest⁷’; quia, si non fallor, maior pars heresum circa incarnacionem Christi in isto devio titubavit.⁸

The theologian must therefore take as a principle that God, freely, as between contradictories, assumed that man, just as, freely, as between contradictories, he produced the fabric of this world; and the cause of this effect[^18] is the eternal volition of God, and consequently the [essence]{g:essencia} and goodness of our God, of which no further cause is to be sought. And just as [the reply]{g:respondetur} to one who asks, "Why did God produce the world in that place or at that time rather than in any other?" is that this is necessary on supposition, because he could not produce that world unless he produced it in that place and at that time: so the reply to one who asks, "Why did God assume that man?" is that this is because God willed to assume a man for the completion of his fabric, and could not [80] have assumed a man unless he had assumed that man. For just as that place and that time are individuated by that world, so that wherever or whenever God should produce that world, he would have done it in that place and at that time: so that humanity which is Jesus is sufficiently individuated by the Word, so that, it being posited, its [suppositation]{g:suppositacio} by the Word follows; and whatever other humanity the Word had, per impossibile, assumed, that humanity would be Jesus. Leaving aside, therefore, the cases and the conclusions idly conjectured from them, let us hold the confession that the said "scripture cannot be broken";[^19] for, unless I am mistaken, the greater part of the heresies about the incarnation of Christ have stumbled on this byway.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *vel sophistice*: *vel* taken as intensive, "even sophistically"; it could also open a second way of proving ("to prove, or [to prove] sophistically").
[^2]: **C.** *alias personas in eisdem naturis assumendo communicare*: the case that the Word, having laid aside the natures, should by assuming bring other persons to share in the same natures; the Latin leaves the construction of *communicare* loose.
[^3]: **C.** *absolute necessario*: the two adverbs stand unjoined and are both taken with *potest*. The power granted is the freedom of the eternal will "as between contradictories"; the next sentences deny that it is a power to annihilate.
[^4]: **C.** *potencia* here is God's power (the CONTEXTUAL row): a "power of contradiction" is a power over either of two contradictories, set against a power to destroy what is.
[^5]: **C.** *vinculum dicte corrigie*: the bond of the thong named in the third argument, earlier in the chapter; *denodare* "untie".
[^6]: **C.** *assumitur*: the logician's sense, "is taken as a premiss" (cf. *assumptum*), not the assumption of a nature.
[^7]: **C.** *neutra* "neither [of two]": the pair is not named (perhaps this humanity and any other, as in the second corollary); it may stand loosely for *nulla* "no created nature".
[^8]: **T.** *dampnificari* "be damaged", the print with O (*dąpni^ra*); A B C read *dampnari* "be damned". The argument of 65–68 turns on *dampnificare*.
[^9]: **C.** *ut unus homo fuisse alius*: taken as two consequents, "could have sinned as one man [does], and have been another [than Christ]", which *Quorum opposita* ("the opposites of these") requires. A B C omit *fuisse* ("could have sinned like one man or another").
[^10]: **T.** *inferens*, with A B C, against the print's *inferius* ("lower"), which does not construe: from an impossible consequent the antecedent that infers it is impossible. O is not reported and so agrees with the print; the departure rests on the argument.
[^11]: **T.** *inconsequens*, the print with A B C; O and M read *inconveniens* "absurdity", the word of Anselm's maxim that from the least absurdity in God impossibility follows (*Cur Deus homo* I.10; to be checked, LEDGER I05d-1). The print is kept.
[^12]: **S.** Anselm, *Epistola de incarnatione Verbi* 6, 7 and 9, and *Cur Deus homo* "6" (the book not given), by Wyclif's count; why neither the Father nor the Spirit was incarnate is argued in *De incarnatione Verbi* 10 and *Cur Deus homo* II.9. To be checked (LEDGER I05d-2).
[^13]: **S.** Wyclif's own *De adnichilacione* (the title in the print's italics); the place to be checked (LEDGER I05d-3).
[^14]: **S.** Augustine against Maximinus (Wyclif's three books "contra Maximinianum"); the place is not given, and the claim may be Maximinus' own, put to Augustine. To be checked (LEDGER I05d-4).
[^15]: **S.** Anselm, *Cur Deus homo* II.5. To be checked (LEDGER I05d-5).
[^16]: **S.** Augustine, *Enchiridion* 40 (c. 12) in the modern division, Wyclif's "3"; the quotation follows the text closely. The print does not mark it; the quotation marks are ours. To be checked (LEDGER I05d-6).
[^17]: **T.** *sicut filius insinuat*, with O, against the print's *sic insinuat*, which leaves *de Maria virgine* without its predicate; Augustine's sentence has *et de Maria virgine sicut filius, insinuat* (LEDGER I05d-6, to be checked).
[^18]: **T.** *huius* "this", with A B C O (A B C *huius effectus*, O *huius effectuum*), against the print's *huiusmodi effectuum* "effects of this kind"; rendered with A B C's singular.
[^19]: **S.** John 10:35 (Vulgate *et non potest solvi Scriptura*, in another order); Douay–Rheims. The bounds follow the print's italics.
