# *De potestate pape*, chapter 7, part 2: the keys are power and knowledge, given to every priest

## ¶1 · 138.32–141.15

> [p. 138] Quantum ad secundum argumentum de illo Matth. X, ubi recitatis nominibus apostolorum Petrus preponitur, indubie notatur primatus Petri, [p. 139] quem Christus intenderat, ut scilicet Petrus sit capitaneus et pugil precipuus ad ducendum Christi exercitum sibi similius, scilicet fide, humilitate et passione, ideo Petrus querendo de vita evangeliste signantis vitam contemplativam recepit responsum a Deo: Quid ad te, tu me sequere Joh. ultimo, 19. Istum igitur sensum comportant argumenta duo facta, ut sepe repecii, et non dominacionem vel honorem secularem, aut, ut dicit Bernhardus, potestatem seculariter castigandi; sicut caput verbis et intuitu, non autem corporali invasione vel instrumentali defensione, pascit et regit residuum corporis, quod si pape deficiunt visus, auditus et omnis sensus ad regendum capitaliter corpus Christi, quid sibi et pape vel capiti Christi ecclesie? Revera nulla eleccio vel approbacio humana potest facere quod talis fit caput vel articulus Christi ecclesie, sed mendaciter presumendo sic nomen fit caput ecclesie malignancium et synagoge sathane. Homo enim habet ex se potenciam vadendi et esse membrum dyaboli, sed non habet nisi a Deo esse membrum ecclesie; oportet enim proximum Christi vicarium esse instar sui spiritualem, ut docet Luce IX, 55, 56 de tribus apostolis appetentibus humanam vindictam: Nescitis, inquit Christus, cuius spiritus estis, filius hominis non venit animas perdere sed salvare. Miticia namque et non guerra possidebunt terram vivencium, ut docet Veritas Matth. V. Et hinc notat Crysostomus super primam epistolam Thym. Omelie V: Non, inquit, ait apostolus auctorabiliter neque dominative neque cum potestate, sed fili, ait, Thymothee, et ideo [p. 140] dicit Bernhardus, quod pascendas non premendas suscepit Eugenius oves Christi; debet enim pugnare tamquam spiritualis miles cum gladio spiritus, ad spiritualem Dei iniuriam vindicandam. Sed postquam mixta est cum religione sacerdotum civilitas, surrepsit in clero superbia, civilis exaccio et monstruosum dominium, ita quod illud Psalmi IX de Antichristo loquentis verificatur hodie: Dum superbit impius, incenditur pauper. Et quantum ad illud tactum de clavibus singulariter datis Petro, ut dictum est, nulli fideli licet ambigere; quin Petrus tamquam generalis attornatus gerebat personam ecclesie, ubi recepit potestatem non singulariter pro se et quocumque Romano pontifice sed generaliter et supereminenter pro quocumque sacerdote similius sequenti, cui est quicumque locus vel eleccio humana impertinens, quia donacio istarum clavium est divinum misterium. Sunt enim dicte claves potestas et sciencia ad edificandum ecclesiam, que signanter debent esse in quolibet sacerdote, et (ut credo) erant in Paulo excellencius quam in Petro. Cum enim impium videtur sine racione ponere in Petro ocium et potestatis abusum, Paulus autem ex fide scripture prima Cor. XV, 10 plus omnibus laboravit, videtur quod habuit amplius potestatis; laboravit, inquam, evangelizando, scribendo et persecuciones amplius paciendo, et clavis potestatis regiminis quam dicimus auctoritatem singulariter Petro competere non fundatur. Potestas enim ad regendum ecclesiam [p. 141] datur unicuique episcopo, quantum potest matri sue proficere, sive evangelizando sive sacramenta ministrando sive oracione, contemplacione, exemplacione edificando. Unde propter excellenciam, quam debemus habere, claves iste dicuntur singulariter sacerdotibus Christi competere, sic quod potestas sit materiale spiritualis clavis presbiteri, et formale est sciencia Dei; non sciencia mathematica, non logica vel disputacio scolastica, sed sciencia scripture, quam episcopus debet excellenter cognoscere et docere. Unde sicut materia prima non potest esse sine forma materiali nec econtra, sic videtur michi quod potestas clavium sacerdotalium non potest esse sine predicta sciencia nec econtra.

As to the second argument, from that [text] of Matthew 10, where in the listing of the names of the apostles Peter is put first, the [primacy]{g:primatus} of Peter is without doubt marked, [139] which Christ had intended: namely, that Peter should be [captain]{g:capitaneus} and chief champion, to lead Christ's army more closely like himself, that is, in faith, humility and suffering. So Peter, asking about the life of the evangelist, who signifies the contemplative life, received an answer from God: "What is it to thee? Follow thou me" (John 21 [the last chapter], 19).[^1] This sense, therefore, the two arguments put forward carry, as I have often repeated, and not [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio} or secular honour, or, as Bernard says, a power of chastising in secular fashion. Just as the [head]{g:caput} feeds and rules the rest of the body by words and by its gaze, but not by bodily assault or defence with weapons, so if the pope lacks sight, hearing and every sense for ruling the body of Christ [as head]{g:capitaliter}, what has he to do with "pope" or "head of Christ's [church]{g:ecclesia}"?[^2] In truth no human election or approval can make such a man become head or joint of Christ's church; but by lyingly presuming the name in this way he becomes head of "the assembly of the malignant" and of "the synagogue of Satan".[^3] For a man has of himself the [power]{g:potencia} of going off and of being a [member]{g:membrum} of the devil, but he has it only from God to be a member of the church; for the nearest [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius-christi} must be spiritual after his likeness, as Luke 9:55–56 teaches about the three apostles who desired human vengeance: "You know not," says Christ, "of what spirit you are. The Son of man came not to destroy souls, but to save." For meekness, and not war, shall possess the land of the living, as the Truth teaches in Matthew 5.[^4] And hence Chrysostom notes on the First Epistle to Timothy, homily 5: "The apostle does not speak," he says, "authoritatively, nor in lordly fashion, nor with power, but says 'son Timothy'"; and therefore [140] Bernard says that Eugenius received the sheep of Christ "to be fed, not to be oppressed".[^5] For he ought to fight as a spiritual [soldier]{g:milites} with the sword of the spirit, to avenge the spiritual injury done to God. But after [civil standing]{g:civilitas} was mingled with the religion of priests, pride crept into the clergy, civil exaction and monstrous [lordship]{g:dominium}, so that that [verse] of Psalm 9, which speaks of Antichrist, is verified today: "Whilst the wicked man is proud, the poor is set on fire."[^6] And as to the point touched on about the keys given singly to Peter, as has been said, it is not lawful for any of the faithful to doubt that Peter, as general attorney, bore the person of the church, where he received the [power]{g:potestas} not singly for himself and any [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex} whatever, but generally and preeminently for any [priest]{g:sacerdos} whatever who follows [Christ] more closely, to whom any place or human election whatever is irrelevant, because the giving of these keys is a divine mystery.[^7] For the said keys are power and knowledge for building up the church, which, significantly, ought to be in every priest, and (as I believe) were in Paul more excellently than in Peter. For since it seems impious to posit in Peter, without reason, idleness and abuse of power, but Paul, [from the faith of scripture]{g:ex-fide} (1 Corinthians 15:10), "laboured more than all", it seems that he had more of power: he laboured, I say, by preaching the gospel, by writing, and by suffering persecutions more abundantly. And that the key of the [power of rule]{g:potestas-regiminis}, which we call [authority]{g:auctoritas}, belongs singly to Peter has no foundation.[^8] For power to rule the church [141] is given to each [bishop]{g:episcopus}, as far as he can profit his mother, whether by preaching the gospel, or by ministering the sacraments, or by building up through prayer, contemplation and example. Hence, because of the excellence which we ought to have, these keys are said to belong singly to the priests of Christ, so that power is the material [element] of the [presbyter]{g:presbiter}'s spiritual key, and the formal [element] is the knowledge of God: not mathematical knowledge, not logic or scholastic disputation, but the knowledge of scripture, which a bishop ought to know and teach excellently. Hence, just as prime matter cannot be without a material form, nor the converse, so it seems to me that the power of the priestly keys cannot be without the aforesaid knowledge, nor the converse.[^9]

## ¶2 · 141.16–142.12

> [p. 141] Unde Johannes XXII et alii seculum sapientes, quia non excesserant in istis clavibus et earum officiis, finxerunt claves potestatis et auctoritatis, quas congregando pecunias simplices sue tyrannidi et ypocrisi subiecerunt. Sicut enim sunt due vie, una descendendi in viciis ad infernum et alia ascendendi in virtutibus ad ecclesiam triumphantem, sic sunt due porte sive introitus. Sacerdos autem debet habere claves ad serandum portas inferi a simplicibus sibi subditis et ad aperiendum simplicibus portas celi, et sic debet aperire pascendis ovibus thesaurum virtutum, et ipse primo virtuosis operibus sequelam ovium exemplantibus et excitantibus introire. Unde quotquot potestates vel sciencias tales prelatus habuit, tot claves habet, que omnes solum duas constituunt. Quod autem claves iste specificantur ad excomunicaciones et absoluciones, Deus huius seculi per fastum et avariciam cecando simplices introduxit. [p. 142] Et sic intelligo decretum XXIV, q. I, Quodcumque cum eis similibus, que omnia oportet intelligi potestate militantis ecclesie non errante; quod solum est verum quando iudicio triumphantis ecclesie est conformis; non enim potest aliter aperire ianuam regni celorum eciam sibi ipsi. Si autem claves iste quoad materiam sint verse in affeccionem terrenam et quoad formam declinant monstruose a sciencia apostolorum ad artem luciferi, necesse est quod tales suis clavibus aperiant portas suas et dogmatizent discipulorum suorum precipicium ad infernum.

Hence John XXII and others who savour the world, because they had not excelled in these keys and their offices, invented keys of power and authority, by which, in gathering money, they subjected the simple to their tyranny and hypocrisy.[^10] For just as there are two ways, one of descending by vices to hell and the other of ascending by virtues to the church triumphant, so there are two gates or entrances. And the priest ought to have keys to lock the gates of hell against the simple who are subject to him and to open the gates of heaven to the simple; and so he ought to open the treasure of virtues to the sheep that are to be fed, and himself to enter first, by virtuous works that give example to and stir up the [following]{g:sequela} of the sheep.[^11] Hence as many such powers or kinds of knowledge as a [prelate]{g:prelatus} has had, so many keys he has, and all of them make up only two. But that these keys are narrowed to excommunications and absolutions, the god of this world brought in, blinding the simple through pomp and avarice. [142] And so I understand the decree 24, q. 1, *Quodcumque*, with those like it: all of them must be understood [to hold] when the power of the church militant does not err, which is the case only when it is conformed to the judgement of the church triumphant; for otherwise it cannot open the door of the kingdom of heaven even to itself.[^12] But if these keys, as to their matter, have been turned into earthly affection and, as to their form, swerve monstrously from the knowledge of the apostles to the art of Lucifer, it must be that such men with their keys open their own gates and teach as dogma the headlong fall of their disciples into hell.[^13]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 139.1: Peter was not to maintain his primacy by force,
- 140.10: The gift of the keys was to Peter as attorney for the Church.
- 140.22: Paul held the keys (knowledge and power) even better than Peter.
- 141.16: John XXII an others forged keys which subjected simple men to their tyranny.
- 141.29: It is false that the keys mean excommunication and absolution.

## Notes

[^1]: **S/C.** Matthew 10:2 (DR "The first, Simon who is called Peter"). The words are John 21:22 (DR "what is it to thee? Follow thou me"); the printed verse 19 is where Christ first says "Follow me". The evangelist is John, the figure of the contemplative life. *Sibi similius*: "more like himself", that is, like Christ. *Capitaneus*: captain, kept apart from *caput*.

[^2]: **S/C.** Bernard's *potestatem seculariter castigandi* has no reference from Loserth here (LEDGER P07b-1, to-verify). *Quid sibi et pape vel capiti …*: the idiom of *quid mihi et tibi*, "what has he to do with" the titles "pope" or "head". *Sicut … quod si*: the comparison is completed by the conditional, construed "so if".

[^3]: **S/C.** Psalm 25:5 (DR "the assembly of the malignant"); Apocalypse 2:9 (DR "the synagogue of Satan"). *Articulus*: a joint or limb of the body. *Presumendo sic nomen*: construed "presuming the name in this way" (of head).

[^4]: **C/S.** *Potencia* here is ordinary power, a man's ability to go astray, not Aristotle's potency. Luke 9:55–56 (DR); Luke 9:54 names two apostles, James and John, where Wyclif says three. *Miticia … possidebunt terram vivencium*: a paraphrase of Matthew 5:4 (DR "Blessed are the meek: for they shall possess the land") with the "land of the living" of Psalm 26:13; left unquoted.

[^5]: **S.** Chrysostom, homily 5 on 1 Timothy: Loserth, *Opera* XI, 527 (139.31; LEDGER P07b-2, editor); the wording is to be checked (LEDGER P07b-3, to-verify). *Fili Thymothee*: 1 Timothy 1:18 (DR "O son Timothy"). Bernard, *De consideratione* II.6.13, Loserth's *ubi supra* (140.1), quoted already at 138.24 (LEDGER P07b-4, editor).

[^6]: **G/S.** *Miles*: the Pauline figure, "soldier" (Ephesians 6:17, DR "the sword of the Spirit"). Psalm 9:23 in the Vulgate (DR), which Loserth gives by the Hebrew numbering as Psalm 10:2 (140.9).

[^7]: **C/R/S.** The editor's semicolon after *ambigere* parts the verb from its *quin* clause; the two are construed together. Matthew 16:19. *Generalis attornatus*: the legal proctor who acts in another's person. A in marg. (139.30, again 140.19): "Peter received the keys not singly [139.30 *simpliciter*] for himself but generally for the church"; A in marg. 140.7 *Ad 2*, B "To the second in the second argument", A 140.10 "To 2 in the second argument": both readers mark the answer to the second argument.

[^8]: **S/C/R.** 1 Corinthians 15:10: Wyclif's *plus omnibus laboravit* for the Vulgate's *abundantius illis omnibus laboravi* (DR "I have laboured more abundantly than all they"). *Autem* here sets Paul against Peter inside the *cum* clause ("but"). *Clavis … quam dicimus auctoritatem singulariter Petro competere non fundatur* is construed with *competere* as what is unfounded; alternatively "the key …, which we say belongs as authority singly to Peter, is not founded". AB in marg. 140.19: "The keys [are] power and knowledge for building up the church"; 140.25: "Paul had more of power".

[^9]: **C.** *Materiale / formale*: the pair of matter and form: power is the matter of the key, knowledge its form. *Forma materialis*: the form that exists in matter. *Matri sue*: the church as mother.

[^10]: **T/C/R.** *Quia non excesserant*: Loserth suggests adding *parum* (141.17: "because they had gone not a little too far"); the print construes and is translated. *Seculum sapientes*: *sapere* "to savour". AB in marg. 141.17: "How the abuse of the keys comes about".

[^11]: **C.** *Sacerdos autem*: *autem* continues the figure of the gates ("and"). *Sequela* is the flock's following of Christ, not "what follows".

[^12]: **S/T/C.** 2 Corinthians 4:4 (DR "the god of this world hath blinded"). Gratian, Decretum C. 24 q. 1 c. *Quodcumque* (form ours; Loserth gives no reference; LEDGER P07b-5, to-verify); B reads *Quicunque* (142.1–2). *Potestate … non errante* is an ablative absolute, rendered "when the power … does not err".

[^13]: **C.** *Portas suas*: the gates of hell, the second of the "two gates" above. *Quoad materiam … quoad formam* takes up the matter (power) and form (knowledge) of the key.
