# *De potestate pape*, chapter 8, part 2: the lie of Christ's lordship, and the holiness of the place

## ¶1 · 169.32–171.14

> [p. 169] Econtra autem nos pro excusacionibus in peccatis fingimus de eo blasfemum mendacium [p. 170] quod dominatus fuit et apostolos suos dominari docuit summe civiliter. Et virtute istius mendacii coloramus nostrum monstruosum dominium. Non enim decuit regem seculorum assumere sibi ad perfeccionem humanam seculare dominium, nisi hoc faceret ad summum ultra quam alicui alteri bene potest competere Quomodo igitur ipse acciperet tam parce et indigne sessivam in dominio ad quod aspirat, relinquens mendacibus ab eo degenerantibus possessionem gloriosam et plenam super omnia regna mundi. Nonne (ut sepe repecii) leges papales convincunt, illud mendacium, primo XCVI distinccione Constantinus, incorporatur cartha Constantini et pape, testificans formam qua donavit cece partem illam imperii suo episcopo? Nonne secundo ex testimonio magni doctoris Augustini posito distinccione VIII Quo iure docetur expresse, quod clerus non habet proprietatem secularis dominii, nisi ex lege cesaris et dono secularium dominorum? Nonne tercio beatus Bernhardus libro II, III alloquitur Eugenium papam sub hiis verbis, ut recitavi diffuse superius: Quid dimiserit pape sanctus apostolus? quod habeo, inquit, hoc tibi do; quid illud? certe non est aurum neque argentum cum ipse dicat: Argentum et aurum non est michi. Si habere contingat, utere non pro libito, sed pro tempore, sic eris utens illis quasi non utens. Ipsa quidem quantum ad bonum anime spectat, nec bona sunt nec mala, usus tamen horum bonus, abusio mala, sollicitudo peior, questus turpior. Esto ut alia quacumque racione hec tibi venaices [p. 171] sed non apostolico iure; nec enim tibi dare quod non habuit, potuit; quod habuit, hoc dedit, sollicitudinem ut dixi super ecclesias. Numquid dominacionem? Audi ipsum. Non dominantes, ait, in clero sed forma facti gregis, et ne hoc dictum sola humilitate putes, non eciam veritate, vox domini est in evangelio: Reges gencium dominantur eorum, vos autem non sic. Planum est: apostolis interdicitur dominatus. Ecce hoc triplex testimonium manifeste convincit illud mendacium, lex propria, Augustinus, Bernhardus, qui manifeste dicit, quod non iure apostolico papa habet hec temporalia. Ideo dicit libro IV: In hiis successisti non Petro sed Constantino.

But we, on the contrary, by way of excuses for our sins, feign of him a blasphemous lie, [170] that he exercised lordship and taught his apostles to [exercise lordship]{g:dominari} in the highest degree civilly. And by virtue of this lie we [colour]{g:colorare} our monstrous [lordship]{g:dominium}. For it did not befit the king of ages to assume secular lordship to himself for human perfection unless he did it in the highest degree, beyond what can well belong to any other. How then would he take so sparingly and unworthily a seisin[^1] in the lordship to which he aspires, leaving to liars who degenerate from him a glorious and full possession over all the kingdoms of the world? Do not (as I have often repeated) the papal laws convict that lie? First, [in] Distinction 96, *Constantinus*, the charter of Constantine and the [pope]{g:papa} is incorporated, testifying the form in which he gave that part of the empire to his [bishop]{g:episcopus}.[^2] Is it not, secondly, expressly taught by the testimony of the great doctor Augustine, placed in Distinction 8, *Quo iure*, that the [clergy]{g:clerus} has no property of secular lordship except by the law of Caesar and the gift of [secular lords]{g:seculares-domini}?[^3] Does not, thirdly, blessed Bernard, book 2, [chapter] 3, address Pope Eugenius in these words, as I recited at length above:[^4] "What did the holy apostle leave to the pope? 'What I have,' he says, 'this I give thee.' What is that? Certainly it is not gold nor silver, since he himself says: 'Silver and gold I have none.' If you happen to have it, use it not at your pleasure but as the time requires; so you will be using them as though not using.[^5] As far as concerns the good of the soul, these things are indeed neither good nor bad; yet the use of them is good, the abuse bad, the anxiety worse, the gain more shameful. Granted that you may claim[^6] these for yourself on some other ground, [171] but not by apostolic [right]{g:ius}; for he could not give you what he did not have; what he had, this he gave: anxiety, as I said, over the churches.[^7] Was it [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio}? Hear him: 'Not lording it,' he says, 'over the clergy, but being made a pattern of the flock.' And lest you think this said only from humility and not also in truth, the voice of the Lord is in the gospel: 'The kings of the Gentiles lord it over them; but you not so.' It is plain: [lordly rule]{g:dominatus} is forbidden to the apostles."[^8] Behold, this threefold testimony manifestly convicts that lie: [the pope's] own law, Augustine, and Bernard, who manifestly says that the pope does not have these [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} by apostolic right. Therefore he says in book 4: "In these you have succeeded not Peter but Constantine."[^9]

## ¶2 · 171.16–171.28

> [p. 171] Et patet quam horribile est fingere talia blasfema mendacia propter dominia temporalia pallianda, Patet eciam, quod nec seculares nec clerici iuvantur per assistenciam talium antichristianorum sed profundius peiorantur, tam quoad bona temporalia quam spiritualia. Quoad temporalia, ut prophetatum est Dan. II et exponitur inferius, hoc fecit scissionem imperii. Et quoad spiritualia, patet quod mutuo se inficiunt ex consensu. Assistencia autem cleri tenentis se in statu primevo decoraret miliciam et econtra, ut patet de beatis Sebastiano, Martino et ceteris sed apostatarum assistencia deturparet.

And it is clear how horrible it is to feign such blasphemous lies for [cloaking]{g:palliare} temporal lordships. It is clear also that neither [seculars]{g:secularis} nor clerics are helped by the assistance of such antichristians, but are made deeply worse, as to temporal goods as well as spiritual. As to temporal goods, as was prophesied in Dan. 2 and is expounded below, this made the division of the empire.[^10] And as to spiritual goods, it is clear that they infect each other mutually by consent. But the assistance of a clergy keeping itself in the primitive [state]{g:status} would adorn the [knighthood]{g:milicia}, and conversely, as is clear of the blessed Sebastian, Martin and the rest;[^11] but the assistance of apostates would befoul [it].

## ¶3 · 171.29–172.2

> [p. 171] Quinta causa ficta est loci sanctitas ex multiplici christianorum martyrio, ubi decet sanctissimum caput ecclesie conversari. Sed constat quod locus non sanctificat inhabitantem sed econtra, ut dicitur II. Machabeorum V. Locus enim non est de genere activorum, cum sit quantitas et [p. 172] influencia celestis vel est sanctitati impertinens vel loca sunt alia magis apta.

The fifth cause feigned is the holiness of the place, from the manifold martyrdom of Christians, where it befits the most holy [head]{g:caput} of the [church]{g:ecclesia} to live.[^12] But it is evident that the place does not sanctify the inhabitant, but conversely, as is said in 2 Maccabees 5.[^13] For place is not of the genus of active things, since it is a quantity; and [172] celestial influence either is irrelevant to holiness, or other places are more apt.[^14]

## ¶4 · 172.3–172.13

> [p. 172] Similiter, si locus capit sanctitatem, per idem malediccionem ab incolis facinus committentibus in eodem, ut patet II. Regum primo de monte Gelboe. Sed Rome ab imperatoribus et carnificibus sibi subiectis, qui permanencius incolebant locum post et ante quam martyres, fuit multiplicius facinus perpetratum, igitur locus ille foret evidencius maledictus. Et in signum huius multi pape fugiunt illum locum, sicut Balaam propter influenciarum varietatem fecit altaria variari, ut dicitur Numeri XXIII.

Likewise, if a place takes on holiness, by the same token [it takes on] a curse from inhabitants who commit a [misdeed]{g:facinus} in it, as is clear from 2 Kings 1, of Mount Gelboe.[^15] Now at Rome, by the emperors and the executioners subject to them, who inhabited the place more permanently, after and before the martyrs, misdeeds were more manifoldly perpetrated; therefore that place would be more evidently cursed. And in sign of this many popes flee that place, as Balaam, because of the variety of influences, had the altars varied, as is said in Numbers 23.[^16]

## ¶5 · 172.14–172.30

> [p. 172] Similiter, si sanctitas martyrum fugat facinus et locum sanctificat, Jerusalem foret locus sanctissimus, cum ibi predicavit et passum fuit caput martyrum, ibi occisus est uterque Jacobus et multi martyres, ymo in veteri testamento occisi sunt plurimi, ut testatur Christus Matth. XXIII, 37 Jerusalem que occidis prophetas et lapidas eos qui ad te missi sunt, et IV. Regum XXIV, 4 scribitur quomodo Manasses effudit sanguinem innoxium et implevit Jerusalem cruore innocencium, et ob hanc rem noluit dominus propiciari. Difficile autem est sompniare, in qua istarum civitatum occisi sunt plures martyrio. Sed certum est quod Christus predicans, conversans et occisus in Jerusalem, infinitum excedit totum collegium martyrum Rome vel alibi occisorum, et preter hec locus est sanctitate antiquior.

Likewise, if the holiness of martyrs puts misdeed to flight and sanctifies the place, Jerusalem would be the most holy place, since there the head of martyrs preached and suffered; there each James was slain, and many martyrs; indeed in the old testament very many were slain there, as Christ testifies, Matt. 23:37: "Jerusalem, thou that killest the prophets and stonest them that are sent unto thee"; and in 2 Kings 24:4 it is written how Manasses shed innocent blood and filled Jerusalem with the blood of innocents, "and therefore the Lord would not be appeased."[^17] And it is difficult to dream in which of these cities more were slain in martyrdom. But it is certain that Christ, preaching, living and slain in Jerusalem, infinitely exceeds the whole college of martyrs slain at Rome or elsewhere; and besides this the place is older in holiness.

## ¶6 · 172.31–173.20

> [p. 172] Ex istis colligitur quod nec iste quinque raciones nec alie fingende stabiliunt quod oportet realiter Romanum pontificem esse papam. Multi enim Romani pontifices sicut et imperatores erant pessimi antichristi, et civitas illa cum gente [p. 173] sua modo prevalet et modo subditur, cum multe sunt hodie civitates, gentes et comunitates plus divites, inclite et amplius populose. Quomodo igitur apostoli, quibus preceptum est indiferenter omnes gentes, limitarent se ad illum populum, cum possent plus lucrifacere Christo in alio populo eciam non converso. Unde ut credo hec fuit una racio, quare apostoli non receperunt dotacionem fixam in terris, ut ubicumque terrarum spiritus eos moveret ad plus prodessendum ecclesie illuc tenderent indilate. Iuxta quem sensum scribit Apostolus Hebr. XIII, 14: Non habemus hic manentem civitatem sed futuram inquirimus, aliter enim dominium affixum retraheret affeccionem convertendi. Et hec racio, quare Christus non fuit affectus templo, ut patet Mar. XIII, sed predicavit sub divo, nunc in deserto, nunc in montibus, nunc in mari et sub verbis indifferentibus et generalibus, ut patet Matth. ultimo, precepit apostolis predicare.

From these things it is gathered that neither these five reasons nor others yet to be feigned establish that the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex} must really be pope. For many Roman pontiffs, like emperors too, were the worst antichrists; and that city with its [173] people at one time prevails and at another is subjected, since there are today many cities, peoples and communities richer, more renowned and more populous. How then would the apostles, who were commanded [to teach] all nations without distinction,[^18] [limit]{g:limitare} themselves to that people, when they could gain more for Christ in another people, even one not converted? Hence, as I believe, this was one reason why the apostles did not receive a fixed [endowment]{g:dotacio} on earth: so that wherever on earth the Spirit moved them, to profit the church more, they might go there without delay. In which sense the Apostle writes, Heb. 13:14: "We have not here a lasting city: but we seek one that is to come";[^19] for otherwise an affixed lordship would draw back the affection for converting. And this is the reason why Christ was not attached to the temple, as is clear from Mark 13,[^20] but preached [in the open air]{g:sub-divo}, at one time in the desert, at another on the mountains, at another on the sea; and in indifferent and general words, as is clear from the last [chapter] of Matthew, he commanded the apostles to preach.

## ¶7 · 173.21–173.32

> [p. 173] Quantum ad secundam ficticiam ad colorandum connexionem predictam Romani et summi pontificis per institucionem Petri; patet quod innititur foliis sine fructu; non enim plus sequitur: Petrus ordinavit Clementem episcopum Romanum, igitur ordinavit eum summum pontificem quam sequitur, ordinavit Marcum Anthiochie sibi succedere, igitur ordinavit eum sibi succedere in summum pontificem; locus autem (ut dictum est) est indifferens ad papatum et eque plenus papa fuit Petrus Anthiochie sicut Rome, et preterea Anthiochie accepit a populo insignia et non Rome.

As to the second [fiction]{g:ficticia}, for colouring the aforesaid connexion of the Roman and the [highest pontiff]{g:summi-pontifices} through Peter's institution: it is clear that it leans on leaves without fruit;[^21] for it no more [follows]{g:sequitur}, "Peter ordained Clement Roman bishop, therefore he ordained him highest pontiff", than it follows, "He ordained Mark to succeed him at Antioch, therefore he ordained him to succeed him as highest pontiff";[^22] and the place (as has been said) is indifferent to the papacy, and Peter was pope as fully at Antioch as at Rome; and moreover at Antioch he received the insignia from the people, and not at Rome.[^23]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 170.1: It is false that Christ ruled and that it becomes his disciples, to do so.
- 170.18: If the clergy hold property, they do it by Cesar's law.
- 171.16: Worldly gifts do not aid the Church but injure it.
- 171.29: Another plea is sanctity of Rome for its many martyrs,
- 172.3: Against this may be set its pollution by much wickedness.
- 172.14: Moreover Jerusalem has higher claims.
- 172.31: It is not proved that the bishop of Rome must be pope.
- 173.7: Endowment would have hindered the apostles' journeys.
- 173.21: That Peter ordained Clement proves nothing or the papacy might be at Antioch.

[^9]: **S.** Bernard, *De consideratione* 4.3.6 (Loserth: cap. 3, p. 776; PL 182, 776: *In his successisti, non Petro, sed Constantino*; LEDGER P08b-6).
[^10]: **S.** Dan. 2:41–43, the divided kingdom of iron and clay. "Below": no exposition of Dan. 2 has been found later in the treatise; the nearest is 379 (ch. 12), *ut patet Daniel II*, on the dissension of kingdoms (LEDGER P08b-7, untraced).
[^11]: **R.** Sebastian, the martyr of the Roman guard, and Martin of Tours, the soldier-bishop: holy men who stood beside the knighthood.
[^12]: **C.** "The fifth cause": Wyclif counts on from FitzRalph's three reasons (166.29–168.26) and a fourth not set out in these pages; cf. "these five reasons", 172.32.
[^13]: **S.** 2 Macc. 5:19, "God did not choose the people for the place's sake, but the place for the people's sake."

[^15]: **S.** 2 Kings (Vulgate 2 Regum, modern 2 Sam.) 1:21, "Ye mountains of Gelboe, let neither dew nor rain come upon you".
[^16]: **S.** Num. 23:1–3, 13–14, 27–30: Balaam has seven altars built at each of three places in turn.
[^17]: **S.** 2 Kings (Vulgate 4 Regum) 24:4. Wyclif paraphrases the verse; only its close is quoted in DR's words. In Matt. 23:37 he gives *Jerusalem* once.
[^18]: **T.** *Preceptum est indiferenter omnes gentes* lacks an infinitive; Matt. 28:19 (*docete omnes gentes*) supplies "to teach", which we give in brackets. A word may have been lost at 173.4.
[^19]: **S.** Wyclif's citation omits the Vulgate's *enim*; DR's "For" is dropped.
[^20]: **S.** Mark 13:1–2, the prophecy of the temple's destruction.
[^21]: **S.** The fig tree with leaves and no fruit, Matt. 21:19; Mark 11:13. **T.** The manuscripts read *ficticium* (Loserth).
[^22]: **R.** So the print: the common tradition makes Mark bishop of Alexandria and Evodius Peter's successor at Antioch (Eusebius, *Hist. eccl.* 2.16 and 3.22; LEDGER P08b-8).
