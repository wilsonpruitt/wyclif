# Connective check: *sed* / *autem*, De eucharistia, wave 8 (chunks 04-b, 04-c, 04-d)

Classes: (a) minor premiss / next step, "now" · (b) adversative, "but" · (c) continuative *autem*, "and";
*non … sed* (incl. *nedum … sed*), "but".

## Changes

| chunk | page | Latin phrase | was | now | reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 04-c | 91 | ***Sed** ab omnibus istis variat usus nostre ecclesie … Cum **ergo** secundum has quinque formas contingit vere conficere, patet conclusio* | But the use of our church varies from all these | Now the use of our church varies from all these | a: the look-ahead rule. The four gospel forms have been set out, and this *sed* brings in the fifth (the Roman use). That is the premiss the *ergo* needs for "these five forms", so it is the next step, not a contrast |

## Doubtful, left as they are

- **04-b, p. 88**: ***Sed** pro declaracione ulteriori sunt tria plus declaranda*, rendered "But for further clarification". This moves on to a new head (the three further points), not a new premiss, so it is not (a). "But" works as a transition. Left.
- **04-b, p. 89**: *In casu **autem** quo intelligatur proposicio non figurative …* "And in the case where", paired with *Si **autem** intelligatur proposicio figurative* "But if". The first of the two cases stays (c) and the second contrasts with it (b). "Now in the case" would also be defensible for the first. Left.
- **04-c, p. 93**: ***Sed** quia in nobis ex cibario causatur … ideo … accipiamus*, rendered "But because … therefore". The *ideo* closes its own *quia* clause, not a syllogism whose minor premiss this *sed* supplies. It contrasts fasting in body with the fasting from crime just required: (b). Left.
- **04-d, p. 97**: *Christus **autem** authonomatice effudit sanguinem suum*, rendered "and Christ, by antonomasia, shed". This carries the old-law type on into its fulfilment: (c). It could be read as a contrast (b, "but Christ") or as the step that applies the type (a, "now"). No *ergo* follows. Left.
- **04-d, p. 97**: *Evangelium **autem** Apostoli … habet simpliciter*, rendered "And the gospel of the Apostle". Luke's form and then Paul's form are set side by side: (c). A contrast reading (b) is possible, since *simpliciter* marks the difference. Left.
