# *De ecclesia*, chapter 21, part 7: order conferred by degrees, and the cleric's propertyless life

## ¶1 · 515.1–515.11

> [p. 515] ad folia quam ad fructus creditur facere in oculis Dei sacramenta nostra vilescere. Sic enim fuit tempore cessacionis legalium multipliciter ritibus humanitus adinventis, et patet via ad solvendum difficultatem qua queritur utrum, dyacono recipiente ab episcopo potestatem absolvendi et non conficiendi vel econtra per mortem aut infirmitatem episcopi vel causam aliam, utrum sit plene sacerdos. Dicitur enim communiter quod non imprimitur caracter sacerdocii ante complecionem verborum sacramentalium

to the leaves than to the fruits, is believed to make our sacraments grow cheap in the eyes of God. For so it was at the time when the [legal precepts]{g:legalia} were ceasing, [rites]{g:ritus} having been devised by men in many ways; and the way is clear to resolving the difficulty in which it is asked whether, when a deacon receives from the bishop the power of absolving and not of consecrating, or the reverse, through the death or infirmity of the bishop or some other cause, whether he is fully a [priest]{g:sacerdos}.[^1] For it is commonly said that the [character]{g:caracter} of priesthood is not imprinted before the completion of the sacramental words,

## ¶2 · 515.12–515.13

> [p. 515] sed veritas est quod ignoramus quando et qualiter Deus imprimit sacerdotalem caracterem

but the truth is that we [are ignorant of]{g:ignorare} when and how God imprints the priestly character;

## ¶3 · 515.14–515.19

> [p. 515] nec habent evidencie facte ex legibus ecclesie et scriptura vix corticem coloris (ut patet alibi). Ex istis tercio potest convinci quomodo interpolata celebracio sacramenti ordinis potest sive bene fieri sive male. Nam in primitiva ecclesia Christus immediate fecit apostolos suos sacerdotes, hoc est, episcopos, et post ascensionem

nor have the [evidences]{g:evidencia} drawn from the laws of the [church]{g:ecclesia} and from scripture more than barely the rind of [colour]{g:color} (as is clear elsewhere). From these, thirdly, it can be shown conclusively how an interrupted celebration of the sacrament of order can be done either well or badly. For in the primitive church Christ immediately made his apostles priests, that is, bishops; and after the ascension,[^2]

## ¶4 · 515.20–516.12

> [p. 515] tempore apostolorum constituerunt dyaconos, nec alios ordines clerici seorsum exhibitos legi illas columpnas ecclesie celebrasse. Et Deus scit utrum ritus ille foret hodie solempnior, facilior et ecclesie Christi utilior. Omnia enim que faciunt clerici inferioris ordinis possunt clerici ex vi potestatis sacerdotalis perficere et a probabili efficacius et humilius quam fiunt modo. Christus enim summus sacerdos exercuit omnia ista officia (ut alias explanavi). Ideo videri posset quod foret ad alleviamen ecclesie non habere tot ritus sacramentales et tot leges de clericis ordinatis per saltum, cum hoc quod foret ecclesie efficacius et brevius ministratum. Ex alio autem latere posset contingere quod episcopi postposito fastu de excellencia sui ordinis et cupiditate questus de sua preeminencia et necessitate ad regulandum [p. 516] ecclesiam in oculo puro et simplici considerent quod sincerius ministraretur ecclesie ordinatis clericis paulative. Sic enim foret examinacio discrecior, examinati appropriacio ex conversacione experta securior et omnino expulsio persone inhabilis et acceptacio persone habilis ad utilitatem ecclesie foret facilior. Illam autem potestatem ordinandi habent superiores prepositi ex dicto Apostoli, Romanorum XIV°, 19: Que edificacionis sunt invicem custodiamus. Debet autem supponi in istis prepositorum discrecio et per consequens acceptari eorum ordinacio, nisi fuerit ad oppositum vehemens suspicio.

in the time of the apostles, they appointed deacons; nor have I read that those pillars of the church celebrated other orders of the clergy conferred separately.[^3] And God knows whether that rite would today be more solemn, easier and more useful to the church of Christ. For all the things that clerics of a lower order do, clerics can perform by force of the priestly power, and probably more effectually and more humbly than they are done now. For Christ, the high priest, exercised all these offices (as I have explained elsewhere). Therefore it might seem that it would be for the relief of the church not to have so many sacramental rites and so many laws about clerics ordained *per saltum*, with this besides, that the church would be ministered to more effectually and more briefly. But on the other side it could happen that the bishops, setting aside pride in the excellence of their order and the greed of gain from their pre-eminence and from their necessity for ruling the [516] church, would consider with a pure and simple eye that the church would be more sincerely ministered to by clerics ordained by degrees. For so the examination would be more discerning, the [appropriation]{g:appropriacio} of the one examined, from his [manner of life]{g:conversacio} tried by experience, more secure, and altogether the expulsion of an [unqualified]{g:inhabilis} person and the acceptance of a [qualified]{g:habilis} person would be easier, to the benefit of the church.[^4] And this power of ordaining the higher [superiors]{g:prepositi} have from the saying of the apostle, Romans 14:19: "let us keep the things that are of edification, one towards another." Now in these matters the discretion of superiors ought to be supposed, and consequently their [ordination]{g:ordinacio} accepted, unless there should be a strong suspicion to the contrary.[^5]

## ¶5 · 516.13–517.26

> [p. 516] Quarto ex predictis potest colligi quomodo nomen clerici superaddit caracterem ordinis conversacionem exproprietariam. Nam habentes ordines inferiores coniugati et conversantes seculariter sunt laici, licet ex favore regni nostri habeant privilegium clericatus, et per consequens non sunt simul cum hoc clerici, cum status illi ex opposito distinguuntur (ut docet Hugo, De Sacramentis, libro secundo parte septima). Et potest confirmari ex decreto beati Jeronimi posito XIIª, q. I. Clericus, et infra, et Iª, q. II. Si quis et Sacerdos, cum multis legibus eis similibus. Et valet ad idem testimonium beati Bernardi libro ad papam Eugenium et testimonia sanctorum alibi sepius allegata. Sed efficacissime videtur mihi valere quadruplex testimonium de clericis veteris testamenti allegatum XIII° capitulo huius libri. Nec valet fingere quod leges veteris testamenti non obligant nos in novo; primo quia ille qui venit non legem solvere sed implere, cuius factum est optimus interpres quomodo vetus testamentum debet intelligi et servari, docuit [p. 517] quomodo ipse, sui apostoli et eorum vicarii debent vivere pauperem vitam exproprietarie; quod duravit per trecentos annos et amplius usque ad venenosam dotacionem cesaream, ad cuius crementum proporcionaliter decrevit possessionata ecclesia in virtute. Secundo confirmatur ex hoc quod omnia moralia iudicialium vel cerimonialium legis veteris sunt pro tempore legis gracie observanda; cum ergo conversacio pauper et exproprietaria sit moralis, videtur quod post Christum pauperem debet a suis clericis observari; sicut enim mater ecclesia est senior, sic debet esse specialiter in clero religiosior, et per consequens deserendo mundum sponso proprinquior. Tercio confirmatur per deducens ad inconveniens vel per locum a simili; nam decimas et illa que sonant in lucrum clericis accipimus non expressa auctoritate legis nove sed veteris; cum ergo amplectimus minus religiosum legis veteris, videtur sequi quod magis religiosum sit servandum nobis ex integro vel minus religiosum sic racionabiliter auferendum. Non enim licet nobis modo paganico colare nobis placitum de utroque testamento et abicere quod visum est nobis durum, per quod ut clavem Christi religio servaretur et potissime periclitante mundo per venenum avaricie. Sic enim introducta est ex consilio Sergii monachi secta nepharia Machometi.

Fourthly, from what has been said it can be gathered how the name of cleric adds to the character of order a [propertyless]{g:exproprietarii} manner of life.[^6] For those holding the lower orders who are married and live in a secular manner are laymen, although by the favour of our realm they have the [privilege]{g:privilegium} of the [clerical state]{g:clericatus}; and consequently they are not at the same time clerics as well, since those states are distinguished as opposites (as Hugh teaches, *On the Sacraments*, book 2, part 7).[^7] And it can be confirmed from the decree of blessed Jerome placed at C. 12 q. 1 *Clericus*, and further on, and C. 1 q. 2 *Si quis* and *Sacerdos*, with many laws like them. And to the same purpose avails the testimony of blessed Bernard in the book to Pope Eugenius, and the testimonies of the saints [alleged]{g:allegare} elsewhere quite often. But most effectual of all, it seems to me, is the fourfold testimony about the clerics of the old testament alleged in the thirteenth chapter of this book.[^8] Nor does it avail to pretend that the laws of the old testament do not bind us in the new; first, because he who came not to destroy the law but to fulfil it, whose deed is the best interpreter of how the old testament ought to be understood and kept, taught [517] how he himself, his apostles and their vicars ought to live a poor life [without property]{g:exproprietarie}; which lasted for three hundred years and more, up to the venomous caesarean [endowment]{g:dotacio}, in proportion to whose increase the [possessioned]{g:possessionatus} church decreased in virtue.[^9] Secondly, it is confirmed from this, that all the moral elements of the judicial or ceremonial precepts of the old law are to be observed in the time of the [law of grace]{g:lex-gracie}; since, then, a poor and propertyless manner of life is moral, it seems that after the poor Christ it ought to be observed by his clerics; for as mother church is older, so she ought to be, especially in her clergy, more religious, and consequently, by forsaking the world, nearer to the [bridegroom]{g:sponsus}.[^10] Thirdly, it is confirmed by [an argument] leading to an [absurdity]{g:inconveniens}, or by the [topic from likeness]{g:locus-a-simili}; for we take [tithes]{g:decime} and the things that make for gain to clerics on the express authority not of the new law but of the old; since, then, we embrace what is less religious in the old law, it seems to follow that what is more religious is to be kept by us in its entirety, or else that the less religious is on the same ground reasonably to be taken away. For it is not lawful for us, in a pagan manner, to strain out for ourselves what pleases us from each testament and to cast away what has seemed hard to us, through which, as by a key, the religion of Christ would be kept, and most of all when the world is imperilled by the venom of avarice. For so the abominable [sect]{g:secta} of Mahomet was introduced on the counsel of Sergius the monk.[^11]

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** The sentence began at 514.32: the irregularity "by which we attend more to the leaves than to the fruits" (the outward rite rather than the reality of the sacrament) "is believed to make our sacraments grow cheap". *Tempore cessacionis legalium*: when the legal precepts of the old law were about to cease; the ablative absolute *multipliciter ritibus humanitus adinventis* gives the like cause then, rites multiplied by human invention. The second *utrum* repeats the first after the long participial clause and is kept. *Potestatem absolvendi et non conficiendi*: the power of absolving but not of consecrating the eucharist; *vel econtra*, the reverse, the rite having been broken off by the bishop's death, infirmity or another cause. **R.** In the margin (the siglum is lost in the OCR of the page foot): *Questio*.

[^2]: **C.** *Nec … vix*: the negative is doubled, "nor … more than barely". *Corticem coloris*: the rind of colour, the outer husk of a plausible ground (row *color*). *Interpolata celebracio*: the sacrament of order conferred in stages, at intervals, rather than all at once. *Convinci*: to be shown conclusively. **T.** B *corticem colorum* (515.14). **R.** A in the margin at 515.15: [*Tercia*] *conclusio* (the OCR of the page foot gives only *conclusio*).

[^3]: **C.** *Legi*: "I have read" (perfect, first person), governing *illas columpnas ecclesie celebrasse*; the pillars are the apostles. *Ordines clerici seorsum exhibitos*: the orders below the priesthood, each conferred separately. The subject of *constituerunt* is the apostles. *A probabili*: probably. *Ut alias explanavi*: not located. **T.** B *fuit modo* for *fiunt modo* (515.25–26).

[^4]: **C.** *Per saltum*: "by a leap", ordination to a higher order without the lower ones. *Alleviamen*: relief. *Cum hoc quod*: "with this besides, that". *Ex alio … latere*: on the other side of the question. *Fastu de excellencia*: pride in the excellence. *Questus*: gain. *Paulative*: by degrees, through the orders in turn. *Examinati appropriacio*: the assigning of the examined candidate to the grade fitted to him, not the annexing of a church to a house (row *appropriare; appropriacio*, whose English is kept).

[^5]: **S.** Romans 14:19: Wyclif quotes only the second half and has *invicem* for the Vulgate *in invicem*. **C.** *Superiores prepositi*: the higher superiors, the bishops. *Ordinacio*: their ordaining of clerics, the sacrament (row: "ordination"). **T.** The codices read *debent*; the print has *Debet* (516.10). **R.** Side-note 516.9.

[^6]: **C.** *Superaddit caracterem ordinis conversacionem exproprietariam*: taken as "adds to the character of order a propertyless manner of life"; it might be read "adds the character of order and a propertyless manner of life". **T.** B reads *quomodo nulla clerici* (516.13–14). **R.** Side-note 516.13; A in the margin at 516.13: *Quarta conclusio*.

[^7]: **C.** *Privilegium clericatus*: the privilege of the clerical state, which the realm allowed to married men in lower orders. *Ex opposito distinguuntur*: are distinguished as opposites, clergy against laity. **S.** Hugh of St Victor, *De sacramentis* II, "part 7"; the passage is II.2.3 (PL 176), the clergy and laity *quasi duo latera corporis unius* (LEDGER E21g-1).

[^8]: **S.** Decretum C.12 q.1 c.5 *Clericus* and C.1 q.2 cc.8–9 *Si quis*, *Sacerdos* (Loserth, 516.22; the modern form is ours; LEDGER E21g-2, E21g-3). Bernard, *De consideratione*, addressed to Pope Eugenius III; cf. II.6.10 (*Argentum et aurum non est mihi*) and IV.2–3 (LEDGER E21g-4). The fourfold testimony: chapter 13, which Loserth locates at p. 284 above (516.28; LEDGER E21g-5). **T.** The OCR of the page foot appears to give A *22 cap.*, corrected to 13 (516.28).

[^9]: **S.** Matthew 5:17: Wyclif has *non legem solvere sed implere* for the Vulgate *non veni solvere, sed adimplere*; the Douay–Rheims "destroy … fulfil" is kept. **C.** *Cuius factum est optimus interpres*: Christ's deed is the best interpreter of the old law. *Trecentos annos*: down to Constantine. *Venenosam dotacionem cesaream*: Constantine's endowment of the church, at which, by the legend, poison was poured into the church (LEDGER E21g-6). **R.** Side-note 516.26; A in the margin at 516.30: *Ficcio*.

[^10]: **C.** *Moralia iudicialium vel cerimonialium*: the moral content of the judicial and ceremonial precepts, on the scholastic division of the old law into moral, judicial and ceremonial; the moral binds under the law of grace. *Mater ecclesia est senior*: the church is now older, grown from her youth; it may mean older than the synagogue. *Proprinquior*: so printed, for *propinquior*. **R.** Side-note 517.6.

[^11]: **C.** *Per deducens ad inconveniens*: so printed; *per* has no noun, and "[an argument]" is our supply (perhaps for *per deducere* or *deducendo*). *Inconveniens*: here the absurd consequence of a premiss (row, CONTEXTUAL). *Que sonant in lucrum*: that tend to gain. *Colare*: to strain out, to pick. *Per quod ut clavem*: so printed; *ut clavem* does not construe easily after *per quod*, and "as by a key" renders the print (*ut clavum*, "as by a helm", is possible). **S.** Sergius, the monk who in medieval polemic counselled Muhammad; to be checked (LEDGER E21g-7). **R.** Side-note 517.15.
