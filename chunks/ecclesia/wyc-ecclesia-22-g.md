# *De ecclesia*, chapter 22, part 7: exequies: what reaches the dead, Gregory, Chrysostom and Cyprian against mourning, and Augustine on the care of the dead

## ¶1 · 538.14–540.16

> [p. 538] aliis ritibus factis pro mortuis patet quod vel sunt purgandis contraria vel neutra vel minus utilia; contraria si sapiunt peccatum, minus utilia si in istis omissis contraria magis profecerint, et neutra supposito quod neque sint bona neque mala moraliter. Primum et tercium fiunt communius, unde prima particula ex hoc patet quod, sicut omnia meritorie facta pro mortuis capacibus eis prosunt, sic omnia demeritoria pretensa pro ipsis fieri profectui suo obsunt; non enim prodest corpori mortuo talis solempnitas, cum deficiat sibi sensus, nec prodest anime nisi sub racione qua meritum; aliter enim foret profectui suo indifferens, sicut est indifferens utilitati dyaboli vel beati. Illud ergo suppono ut maximam, et patet per Augustinum in De Cura agenda pro mortuis, et ponitur in decretis XIIIª, q. IIª: Non estimemus, inquit, ad mortuos pro quibus curam agimus pervenire nisi quod pro eis sive altaris sive oracionis sive elemosinarum sacrificiis supplicamus. [p. 539] Et sequitur: Corpori autem humando quidquid inpenditur, non est presidium salutis sed humanitatis officium. Sed et idem videtur per se notorium considerando quomodo proveniret profectus corpori mortuo vel spiritui separato. Et ex isto sequitur vivorum stulticia qui magis dolent pro morte corporali quam pro morte anime per peccatum, magis solicitantur pro solempnitate exequiarum proximi vel amici quam pro assiduitate conversacionum meritoriarum per quas solum suffragia insunt sibi. Unde decretum beati Gregorii XIIIª, q. IIª, Anime defunctorum. Curacio, inquit, funeris, condicio sepulture, pompa exequiarum magis sunt vivorum solacia quam subsidia mortuorum. Si aliquid prodest impio sepultura preciosa, oberit pio vilis aut nulla. Crisostomus super epistolam ad Hebreos, ut ibi exponitur: Ubicunque sepeliamur, domini est terra et plenitudo eius; secundum quod oportet fieri efficiatur. Lugere autem, deplorare et lamentari eos qui de hac vita decedunt ex pusillanimitate contingit. Hoc autem ex desperacione future resurreccionis intelligitur. Unde Apostolus non simpliciter ait: Nolumus vos ignorare de dormientibus, ut non contristemini, sed addit: Sicut et ceteri qui spem non habent. Et eadem sentencia patet in duobus capitulis sequentibus. Item, videtur quod murmur tale sit contra dominum quem debemus credere omnem hominem velle mori, sed voluntati Dei non est licitum taliter contrariare. Si enim scio quod est de Dei beneplacito et sic iustum, faciens ad ordinem [p. 540] et pulchritudinem universi quod Petrus nunc per dominum occidatur, quomodo licet mihi remurmurare contra istam iusticiam et pulchritudinem que adeo placet Deo. Unde XIIIª, q. IIª sub auctoritate Cypriani sic scribitur: Quam preposterum est, quamque perversum, ut cum Dei voluntatem fieri postulemus, quando evocat nos et accersit de hoc mundo, non statim voluntatis eius imperio pareamus et pervicacium more servorum ad conspectum domini cum tristicia et merore perducimur, exeuntes isthinc necessitatis vinculo, non voluntatis obsequio, quomodo volumus ab eo premiis celestibus honorari ad quem venimus inviti. Preceptum est, inquit, a domino ut contester assidue et publice predicarem fratres non esse lugendos accersione divina de seculo liberatos, cum sciamus non amitti sed premitti, recedentes de seculo.

other [rites]{g:ritus} performed for the dead, it is clear that they are either contrary to those who are to be purged, or neutral, or less useful: contrary, if they savour of sin; less useful, if, these being omitted, their contraries would have profited more; and neutral, supposing that they are neither good nor evil morally.[^1] The first and the third occur more commonly. Hence the first part is clear from this: that, as all things done meritoriously for the dead who are capable profit them, so all demeritorious things that are pretended to be done for them hinder their profit; for such solemnity does not profit the dead body, since it lacks sense, nor does it profit the soul except in so far as it is merit; for otherwise it would be indifferent to the soul's profit, as it is indifferent to the advantage of the devil or of one of the blessed.[^2] This, then, I suppose as a maxim; and it is clear through Augustine in *On the Care to Be Had for the Dead*, and it is set in the Decretum, C.13 q.2: "Let us not think," he says, "that anything reaches the dead for whom we have care, except what we supplicate for them by the sacrifices either of the altar, or of prayer, or of alms." [539] And it follows: "But whatever is spent on the body to be buried is not a safeguard of salvation but an office of humanity."[^3] But the same also seems evident of itself, considering how profit could come to a dead body or to a separated spirit. And from this follows the folly of the living, who grieve more for bodily death than for the death of the soul through sin, and are more anxious about the solemnity of the exequies of a neighbour or friend than about constancy in meritorious [manners of life]{g:conversacio}, through which alone [suffrages]{g:suffragia} belong to him.[^4] Hence the decree of blessed Gregory, C.13 q.2, *Anime defunctorum*: "The care of the funeral," he says, "the condition of the burial, the pomp of the exequies, are rather solaces of the living than aids of the dead. If a costly burial profits the impious at all, a mean one or none will harm the pious."[^5] Chrysostom on the Epistle to the Hebrews, as it is set out there: "Wherever we are buried, the earth is the Lord's and the fulness thereof; let it be done as it ought to be done. But to mourn, to bewail and to lament those who depart from this life comes from faint-heartedness. And this is understood to arise from despair of the future resurrection. Hence the Apostle does not say [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}, 'We will not have you ignorant concerning them that are asleep, that you be not sorrowful', but adds: 'even as others who have no hope.'" And the same [teaching]{g:sentencia} is clear in the two chapters following.[^6] Again, it seems that such murmuring is against the Lord, whom we must believe to will every man to die; but it is not lawful so to oppose the will of God. For if I know that it is of God's good pleasure, and so just, and makes for the order [540] and beauty of the universe, that Peter should now be slain by the Lord, how is it lawful for me to murmur back against this justice and beauty which so pleases God?[^7] Hence in C.13 q.2 it is written thus under the authority of Cyprian: "How preposterous it is, and how perverse, that while we ask that God's will be done, when he calls us forth and summons us from this world we do not at once obey the command of his will, and like stubborn servants are brought into the Lord's presence with sadness and grief, departing hence by the bond of necessity, not by the obedience of the will: how do we wish to be honoured with heavenly rewards by him to whom we come unwilling? It has been commanded," he says, "by the Lord, that I should constantly bear witness and publicly preach that the brethren freed from the world by the divine summons are not to be mourned, since we know that, departing from the world, they are not lost but sent before."[^8]

## ¶2 · 540.17–542.18

> [p. 540] In isto dicto videtur innuere triplicem racionem: Prima ex hoc quod nemo debet orare sibi ipsi contrarie, sed oramus Deum: Fiat voluntas tua undique, ideo non debemus velle huic contrarie. Secunda racio est ex hoc quod oportet salvandum meritorie et per consequens voluntarie pati mortem. Tercia racio stat in isto quod diligens se vel alterum debet gaudenter acceptare accessum eius proprium, sicut est mors cuilibet salvando, et prescito eciam est ad bonum, cum continue plus nocet ecclesie et amplius demeretur. In oppositum sunt consuetudo et leges ecclesie de cura agenda pro mortuis et ecclesiastica sepultura, unde oblaciones Jude Machabei laudat ecclesia, Machabeorum XII°, et sepultura mortuorum computatur inter opera misericordie, pro [p. 541] quo laudatur Tobias Tobie. Unde decretum recitat Augustinus De Cura agenda pro mortuis sub hiis verbis: Quamvis cure pro mortuis non pro omnibus pro quibus fiunt prosint, sed hiis tantum pro quibus dum viverent ut prosint meruerant; sed quia non discernimus qui sint illi, oportet illa pro regeneratis omnibus facere. Et assignat causam eius triplicem: Prima est profectus mortuorum capacium, secunda est excitacio affectus superstitum, et tercia est naturalis instinctus et meritorius, dum fuerit caritate formatus curam talem agencium. Pro prima sic scribit: Melius enim supererunt ista hiis quibus nec obsunt nec prosunt, quam eis deerunt quibus prosunt. Vult dicere quod oportet intendere orare generaliter pro cunctis fidelibus, subintelligendo et Deo limitante suffragium capacibus. Pro secunda causa patet quod ex hoc excitatur devocio superstitum et provocatur occupacio circa actum meritorium. Unde Augustinus: Diligencius facit hoc quisque pro amicis suis, ut pro illo fiat similiter. Et quoad terciam causam patet, cum necesse sit evacuare cadavera bestiarum, multo magis cadavera hominum, magis carorum et plus inficiencium. Unde Augustinus: Humanitatis, inquit, hoc est officium secundum affectum quo nemo unquam carnem suam odio habet. Unde oportet ut quam potest pro carne proximi curam gerat, cum spiritus inde recesserit qui hoc gerebat. Et si hoc faciunt qui carnis resurreccionem non credunt, quanto magis [p. 542] debent facere qui credunt ut corpori resurrecturo et eternaliter mansuro impensum officium sit quodammodo evidens eiusdem fidei testimonium. Et patet cum talis operacio non sit per se virtuosa, sed valde per accidens quod contingit eam nunc bene nunc male fieri. Cavere autem debemus de malicia ne fastui seculi principaliter intendamus, nec magis necessarium omittamus; nichil enim est magis necessarium obsequium agendum pro mortuis quam vivere virtuose. Ille ergo inordinate agit curam corporalem pro suo mortuo qui plus attendit ad illam quam ad superhabundanciam virtutis in animo. Vis ergo affectuose proficere anime defunctorum, exercita te ipsum in scola virtutum; quod quanto plus feceris, tanto cum paribus facis mortuis plus profectum; oportet ergo iuxta caritatis regulas quod superstes misereatur anime proprie placens Deo, et ad illud consequitur efficacissime suffragium mortuorum.

In this saying he seems to hint at a threefold reason. The first, from this: that no one ought to pray contrary to himself; now we pray God, "Thy will be done" everywhere; therefore we ought not to will contrary to this. The second reason is from this: that one who is to be saved must suffer death meritoriously, and consequently willingly. The third reason consists in this: that one who loves himself or another ought joyfully to accept his own advancement, as death is to everyone who is to be saved; and for the [foreknown]{g:prescitus} also it is for good, since he continually harms the [church]{g:ecclesia} more and demerits further.[^9] On the opposite side are the [custom]{g:consuetudo} and laws of the church about the care to be had for the dead and about ecclesiastical burial: hence the church praises the offerings of Judas Maccabeus, Maccabees 12, and the burial of the dead is counted among the works of mercy, for [541] which Tobias is praised in Tobias.[^10] Hence the decree cites Augustine, *On the Care to Be Had for the Dead*, in these words: "Although the cares taken for the dead do not profit all for whom they are made, but only those who, while they lived, had merited that they should profit them; yet because we do not discern who these are, it is fitting to do them for all the regenerate."[^11] And he assigns a threefold cause of it: the first is the profit of the dead who are capable; the second is the stirring of the affection of the survivors; and the third is the natural instinct, meritorious when it has been formed by [charity]{g:caritas}, of those who take such care. For the first he writes thus: "For it is better that these things be superfluous to those whom they neither harm nor profit, than that they be lacking to those whom they profit." He means that one must intend to pray generally for all the faithful, [understanding]{g:subintelligere} the condition as implied, and with God [assigning]{g:limitare} the suffrage to those who are capable.[^12] For the second cause, it is clear that by this the devotion of the survivors is stirred and their occupation with a meritorious act is called forth. Hence Augustine: "Each one does this more diligently for his friends, so that it may be done likewise for him." And as to the third cause it is clear, since it is necessary to clear away the carcasses of beasts, much more the corpses of men, who are dearer and infect more. Hence Augustine: "This," he says, "is an office of humanity, according to the affection by which no one ever hated his own flesh. Hence it is fitting that one take what care one can for a neighbour's flesh, when the spirit that took this care has departed from it. And if those do this who do not believe the resurrection of the flesh, how much more [542] ought those to do it who believe, so that the office spent on a body that will rise again and remain eternally may be in a way a manifest testimony of that same faith."[^13] And it is clear, since such a work is not virtuous of itself but very much by accident, that it happens to be done now well, now badly. And we must beware of malice, lest we aim principally at the pomp of the world, and lest we omit what is more necessary; for there is no more necessary duty to be done for the dead than to live virtuously. He therefore takes bodily care for his dead inordinately who attends more to that than to the superabundance of virtue in the soul.[^14] Do you wish, then, with affection to profit the soul of the departed? Exercise yourself in the school of the virtues; and the more you do this, the more profit, other things being equal, you make for the dead. It is needful, therefore, according to the rules of charity, that the survivor "have pity on his own soul, pleasing God"; and on that follows most effectually the suffrage of the dead.[^15]

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** The sentence begins at the end of part 6 (*De exequiis vero et*). *Purgandis*: the souls to be purged, in purgatory. *Contraria … neutra … minus utilia*: three possibilities; *contraria magis profecerint*: if the contrary acts, the good works omitted, would have profited more. **T.** B lacks *et neutra … unde* (538.17). **R.** Side-note 538.14.

[^2]: **C.** *Primum et tercium*: the contrary and the less useful, the first and third in Wyclif's list. *Prima particula*: the first part, that such rites can be contrary. *Pretensa pro ipsis fieri*: professed to be done for them. *Sub racione qua meritum*: only in so far as it is merit. *Utilitati dyaboli vel beati*: a devil or a blessed soul, who can gain nothing from it. **R.** Side-note 538.21.

[^3]: **S.** Augustine, *De cura pro mortuis gerenda* 18.22, as Decretum C.13 q.2 c.19 (Loserth, 538.29: *Decr. sec. pars, causa XIII, q. II, cap. XIX*; the form is ours; LEDGER E22g-1). The second sentence, *Corpori autem humando …*, is in the same chapter (LEDGER E22g-2). **T.** AA₁ lack *humanitatis … corpori* (539.2).

[^4]: **C.** *Per se notorium*: evident of itself. *Spiritui separato*: the soul separated from the body. *Per quas solum suffragia insunt sibi*: *sibi* taken of the dead neighbour or friend; it may refer to the living ("belong to them"). **R.** Side-note 539.6; A in the margin there: *Correlarium*.

[^5]: **S.** Decretum C.13 q.2 c.22 *Anime defunctorum* (Loserth, 539.11; the form is ours; LEDGER E22g-3). Loserth notes that *Curacio … nulla* (539.12–16) is from Augustine, *De cura pro mortuis gerenda* 2 (LEDGER E22g-4). **T.** The codices read *XII* (539.11). A *pompe* (539.13).

[^6]: **S.** Chrysostom on Hebrews, as Decretum C.13 q.2 c.26 (Loserth, 539.17: *ibid. cap. XXVI*; LEDGER E22g-5); the two following chapters are c.27 *Habent forsitan* and c.28 *Qui divina* (Loserth, 539.23; LEDGER E22g-6). Psalm 23:1 (Douay–Rheims); 1 Thessalonians 4:12 (Douay–Rheims, without "brethren"). **C.** *Ut ibi exponitur*: as it is set out there, in the canon. The quotation is closed after the Apostle's words; *Et eadem sentencia …* is Wyclif's. **T.** B *efficaciter* (539.17); the lemma is not legible in the page foot, perhaps *efficiatur*.

[^7]: **C.** *Murmur tale*: grumbling grief at a death. *Quem debemus credere omnem hominem velle mori*: God wills every man to die. *Petrus*: any man, the schools' stock name. *Faciens ad ordinem*: contributing to the order.

[^8]: **S.** Cyprian, as Decretum C.13 q.2 c.25 (Loserth, 540.4: *Cap. XXV*; LEDGER E22g-7); the words are from his *De mortalitate* 18 and 20 (PL 4), which read *contestarer* (LEDGER E22g-8). **T.** A *accerscit*; Loserth notes the subject *Deus*, as in the decree, which reads *et volumus* for *quomodo volumus* (540.7). The decree reads *ut contestarer assidue ut publice predicarem* (540.13), where the print has *ut contester … predicarem*; the print is translated as one clause. The decree adds *precedere* after *recedentes* (540.16), "that departing they go before". **R.** Side-note 540.5.

[^9]: **S.** Matthew 6:10 (Douay–Rheims); *undique*, "everywhere", is Wyclif's. **C.** *Sibi ipsi contrarie*: against what he himself prays. *Accessum eius proprium*: his own approach, his passage to God. *Prescito … est ad bonum*: death is a good even for the foreknown, since by living longer he harms the church more and adds to his demerit. **R.** A and B in the margin at 540.19: *Nota contra lugentes mortuos*.

[^10]: **S.** 2 Maccabees 12:43: the print has "Maccabees 12" without the book's number, and A lacks *XII* (540.30). Tobit (Tobias in the Douay–Rheims) 12:12, "when thou … didst bury the dead"; the codices give no chapter (541.1). **C.** *In oppositum*: on the opposite side, the church's practice against the authorities just cited. **R.** Side-note 540.26.

[^11]: **S.** Augustine, *De cura pro mortuis gerenda* 18.22, as Decretum C.13 q.2 c.19 (Loserth, 541.3: Aug. Opp. VI, 530; LEDGER E22g-9). **T.** A *quamvis cura … prosunt* (541.3); the decree reads *Quibus dum vivunt cooperantur ut prosint* (541.4). *Discernimus* and *sint* are Loserth's: AA₁ *decernimus*, B *decrevimus*; the codices *qui sunt* (541.6). **C.** *Decretum recitat Augustinus*: *Augustinus* is nominative in the print; taken as "the decree cites Augustine".

[^12]: **S.** *Melius … prosunt*: *De cura* 18.22 (LEDGER E22g-10). **C.** *Naturalis instinctus et meritorius*: an instinct that is natural, and meritorious when formed by charity. *Subintelligendo et Deo limitante suffragium capacibus*: the general prayer carries an implied condition, and God assigns its benefit to those capable of it. **R.** Side-note 541.13.

[^13]: **S.** *Diligencius … similiter* and *Humanitatis … testimonium*: Augustine, *De cura* 18.22 (LEDGER E22g-11). Ephesians 5:29 (Douay–Rheims), "no man ever hated his own flesh". **C.** The quotation is closed at *testimonium*. *Magis carorum et plus inficiencium*: men are dearer to us and infect more than beasts. *Evacuare*: to clear away. **R.** A in the margin at 541.18: *Augustinus*; at 541.24: *Nota pro sepultura proximi si et quomodo obligamur*.

[^14]: **C.** *Per se … per accidens*: of itself, by accident. *Fastui seculi*: the pomp of the world. *Obsequium*: "duty", the service done for the dead. *Superhabundanciam virtutis in animo*: virtue abounding in the survivor's soul. **R.** Side-note 542.3.

[^15]: **S.** Ecclesiasticus 30:24, "Have pity on thy own soul, pleasing God" (Douay–Rheims). **C.** *Cum paribus*: taken as *ceteris paribus*, "other things being equal" (as 548.22); the print may have lost *ceteris*. *Misereatur anime proprie*: by living well the survivor helps the dead most. **R.** A in the margin at 542.13: *Nota efficacissimum suffragium mortuorum*.
