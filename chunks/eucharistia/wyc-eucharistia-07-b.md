# *De eucharistia*, chapter 7, part 2: the absurdities of identification, and the argument that the sacrament is whiteness

## ¶1 · 193.21–194.5

> [p. 193] Item, plus accedendo ad difficultatem videtur quod omnis panis vel vinum transsubstanciatum in corpus et sanguinem manebit post conversionem ydemptificatum parti Christi, et cum nullam partem ex tali conversione deperdit Christus, sequitur quod post conversionem erit corpus Christi multiplicius; nam tantam partem quantus est panis aut vinum consecratus in toto mundo, Christus acquiret sine perdicione aliqua; quomodo ergo non habebit plus tam de materia quam de forma? Nam omnia ista habebit ut partes et nullam istarum iam habet; ergo quamlibet earum acquirit vel acquiret. Non enim dicetur quod ydemptificacio impediret formas syllogisticas, ita quod sequitur: [p. 194] totum istum panem et vinum Christus habebit partem suam et nullum istorum nunc habet, ergo multas partes habebit, quas nunc non habet; nam panis et vinum sunt substancialia et non accidentalia; et tunc patet consequencia.

Again, coming closer to the difficulty, it seems that all [bread]{g:panis} or [wine]{g:vinum} transubstantiated into the body and blood will remain, after the [conversion]{g:conversio}, [identified]{g:ydemptificare} with a part of Christ; and since Christ loses no part from such a conversion, [it follows]{g:sequitur} that after the conversion the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} will be more manifold; for Christ will acquire, without any loss, as great a part as is all the bread or wine consecrated in the whole world.[^1] How, then, will he not have more both of matter and of form? For he will have all these as parts, and none of them does he now have; therefore he acquires, or will acquire, each of them. For it will not be said that identification would block the syllogistic forms; so this follows:[^2] [194] "All this bread and wine Christ will have as his part, and none of these does he now have; therefore he will have many parts which he does not now have"; for bread and wine are substantial and not accidental [terms]; and then the [inference]{g:consequencia} is clear.

## ¶2 · 194.6–194.14

> [p. 194] Sed adversarius dicit quod omnes termini sunt accidentales, cum quilibet potest esse quidlibet, et ita non sequitur quidquid erit Petrus illud nunc est, sed asinus erit Petrus, ergo asinus ipse nunc est; ut in simili non sequitur: quidquid tempore nativitatis tue fuisti illud nunc es, sed infidelis ydiota tunc fuisti, ergo infidelis ydiota nunc es; et quam accidentale est tibi esse infidelem, tam accidentale est tibi esse hominem.

But the adversary says that all terms are accidental, since anyone can be anything; and so it does not follow: "whatever will be Peter, that now is; but an ass will be Peter; therefore the ass itself now is",[^3] as, in a like case, it does not follow: "whatever you were at the time of your birth, that you now are; but you were then an [unbelieving]{g:infidelis} [unlettered man]{g:ydiota}; therefore you are now an unbelieving unlettered man";[^4] and as accidental as it is to you to be an unbeliever, so accidental is it to you to be a man.

## ¶3 · 194.15–194.22

> [p. 194] Sed contra istud obicitur primo per hoc quod omne quod Christus umquam habebit partem suam, illud nunc habet partem suam; sed omne quod est vel erit panis aut vinum consecrandum, Christus habebit partem suam, cum ydemptificatur parti sue, ergo omne quod est vel erit, panis aut vinum consecrandum, Christus iam habet partem suam; ista quidem forma valet in terminis accidentalibus.

But against this [it is objected]{g:obicitur-per}, first, by appeal to this: that everything which Christ will ever have as his part, that he now has as his part; now everything that is or will be bread or wine to be consecrated, Christ will have as his part, since it is identified with his part; therefore everything that is or will be bread or wine to be consecrated, Christ already has as his part; and this form indeed holds in accidental terms.[^5]

## ¶4 · 194.23–194.27

> [p. 194] Capta ergo essencia que est panis consecrandus, si hoc erit corpus Christi et hoc differt nunc essencialiter a corpore Christi, cum nunc sit aliud ab ipso, sequitur quod aliquid quod iam essencialiter differt a corpore Christi erit corpus Christi.

Taking, then, the [essence]{g:essencia} which is the bread to be consecrated: if this will be the body of Christ, and this now differs essentially from the body of Christ, since it is now [another thing]{g:aliud} than it, it follows that something which already differs essentially from the body of Christ will be the body of Christ.

## ¶5 · 194.28–195.8

> [p. 194] Similiter, data ista sentencia perirent omnia putata, quia tam substancia que foret basis omni accidenti quam eciam distinccio accidencium; nam humanitas, animalitas et omne tale signabile accidit essencie, cum illa essencia que est homo vel animal potest esse [p. 195] non animal eciam non substancia, ymmo nichil foret per se aliquid nec aliquid per se vel singulare; quomodo ergo staret genus substancie? Et per idem quantitas potest esse qualitas, et sic de omni genere accidentis; et perirent quinque universalia, scilicet genus, species et substancialis differencia et per consequens omnis sciencia et omnis forma substancialis vel accidentalis.

Likewise, granted this [teaching]{g:sentencia},[^6] all the things supposed would perish: both the [substance]{g:substancia} that would be the base for every [accident]{g:accidens}, and also the distinction of accidents.[^7] For humanity, animality and every such [signifiable]{g:signabile}[^8] befalls the essence, since that essence which is a man or an animal can be [195] not an animal, even not a substance; indeed, nothing would be something [of itself]{g:per-se}, nor anything of itself or singular.[^9] How, then, would the genus of substance stand? And by the same token [quantity]{g:quantitas} can be [quality]{g:qualitas}, and so of every genus of accident; and the five [universals]{g:universale} would perish, namely genus, species and substantial [difference]{g:differencia},[^10] and consequently all knowledge and every form, substantial or accidental.

## ¶6 · 195.9–195.25

> [p. 195] Similiter iuxta hanc viam periret omnis motus vel accio naturalis, quia tam fundamentum quam materia motus deficeret; si enim scirem quod homo ambulat, scirem quod homini accidit ambulacio. Sed contra pro toto tempore ambulandi ex tibi dubio hoc fit non homo sed lapis vel aliud ydemptificatum membris Christi; non ergo scitur quod homo ambulat, cum tollitur omnis sciencia de rerum quidditate, distinccione vel transmutacione. Si enim materia motus foret continue ydemptificata cuilibet, quomodo specificaretur, distingueretur vel attenderetur motus? Sed totum hoc ignoras et dubitas, cum ydemptificacio sit habitudo reciproca que potest fieri insensibiliter ignorante homine subducto quocunque motu. Nichil itaque stulcius aut irracionabilius ista sentencia, cum ydemptificatur opinioni stultissime summe heretice et maxime detestande.

Likewise, on this way every natural motion or action would perish, because both the foundation and the matter of motion would fail; for if I knew that a man walks, I would know that walking befalls a man. Now, on the contrary, for the whole time of walking, [for all you know]{g:ex-tibi-dubio}, this becomes not a man but a stone or something else identified with the [members]{g:membrum} of Christ;[^11] therefore it is not known that a man walks, since all knowledge of the [quiddity]{g:quiditas}, distinction or transmutation of things is taken away. For if the matter of motion were continually identified with anything whatever, how would motion be specified, distinguished or observed? But all this you [are ignorant of]{g:ignorare} and doubt, since identification is a reciprocal [respect]{g:habitudo}[^12] which can come about [imperceptibly]{g:insensibiliter}, without a man's knowing, with any motion whatever withdrawn. Nothing, therefore, is more foolish or more irrational than this teaching, since it is identified with the most foolish [opinion]{g:opinio}, supremely heretical and most to be detested.[^13]

## ¶7 · 195.26–195.29

> [p. 195] Nec valet hec opinio pro fine quo fingitur, quia implicat panem et vinum remanere panem et vinum post consecracionem, ad quod evadendum fingit ydemptificacionem.

Nor does this opinion avail for the end for which it is [feigned]{g:fingere}, because it implies that the bread and wine remain bread and wine after the [consecration]{g:consecracio}; and to evade this it feigns identification.

## ¶8 · 195.30–196.3

> [p. 195] Sed pro ista via arguitur primo per hoc quod quidquid producit individuum speciei, ydemptificat [p. 196] illud nedum illi speciei sed cuilibet eius individuo, cum sit triplex ydemptitas, scilicet generalis, specialis et numeralis, est ergo ydemptificacio possibilis.

But for this way [it is argued]{g:arguitur-per}, first, by appeal to this: that whatever produces an [individual]{g:individuum} of a species identifies [196] it [not only]{g:nedum} with that species but with every individual of it, since identity is threefold, namely of genus, of species and numerical; identification is therefore possible.[^14]

## ¶9 · 196.4–196.22

> [p. 196] Sed pro isto non est difficultas quin talis ydemptificacio sit necessaria, sed difficultas loquitur de ydemptificacione numerali duorum individuorum que prius essencialiter et numeraliter sunt distincta, ut panis consecrandus et corpus Christi in celo sunt duo individua loco et forma multiplici distincta et virtute verborum conversionis ponitur eandem rem que prius fuit panis foret ydemptice corpus Christi; sed corpus Christi negatur fore ille panis: et hoc videtur fore difficile, cum ponitur formam substancialem panis et materiam primam ac quamlibet partem panis pro instanti conversionis desinere esse secundum se totam. Et ista foret mirabilis ydemptificacio in qua unum extremum secundum se totum destruitur et per idem non foret transitus, conversio vel transsubstanciacio panis in corpus Christi, nec corpus Christi fieret ex illo, sicut patet admittenti significaciones signorum antiquas. Et non valet baptisare terminos sic extranee et infundabiliter ad fingendum mendacium.

But as for this, there is no difficulty but that such identification is necessary, but the difficulty speaks of the numerical identification of two individuals which before are essentially and numerically distinct, as the bread to be consecrated and the body of Christ in heaven are two individuals, distinct in place and in manifold form, and it is posited that by virtue of the words of conversion the same thing which before was bread would be [identically]{g:ydemptice} the body of Christ; but the body of Christ is denied to be going to be that bread.[^15] And this seems to be difficult, since it is posited that the [substantial form]{g:forma-substancialis} of the bread and the [prime matter]{g:materia-prima} and every part of the bread cease to be in its entirety at the instant of conversion. And this would be a marvellous identification, in which one extreme is destroyed in its entirety; and by the same token there would be no passing, conversion or [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio} of the bread into the body of Christ, nor would the body of Christ come to be from it, as is clear to one who admits the ancient [significations]{g:significacio} of [signs]{g:signum}. And it is no use to baptize terms so strangely and [without foundation]{g:infundabiliter} in order to feign a lie.[^16]

## ¶10 · 196.23–196.28

> [p. 196] Conceditur tamen (ut dictum est in materia De Universalibus) quod omnis homo est idem quod quilibet, ymmo omnis substancia est idem cuilibet, non tamen propterea omnis homo est omnis homo, cum non oportet si iste homo sit idem isti homini quod sit propterea iste homo.

It is granted, nevertheless (as was said in the matter *On Universals*),[^17] that every man is the same as anyone, indeed every substance is the same as anything; yet not for that reason is every man every man, since it is not necessary, if this man is the same as this man, that he is for that reason this man.

## ¶11 · 196.29–197.9

> [p. 196] Secundo arguitur pro ista via per hoc quod illa sensibilis eukaristia est uni albedo sub qua videtur Christus corporali oculo et per idem est alii alia qualitas vel quantitas, secundum quod eius opinio de quidditate eukaristie variatur. Cum ergo quodlibet [p. 197] istorum sit Christus, videtur quod ydemptificacio multorum eciam diversorum in genere sit satis possibilis. Minor videtur ex hoc quod ex ultima protestacione Beringarii et confirmacione Romane ecclesie corpus Christi est in illo sacramento sensibile et de facto illud sacramentum (ut loquitur scriptura cum sanctis doctoribus). Quid ergo foret si non albedo, cum illa sit perfectissimum accidens, ibidem maxime propinquum intellectui ut sic signum?

Secondly it is argued for this way by appeal to this: that that [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} eucharist is to one man the whiteness under which Christ is seen with the bodily eye, and by the same token is to another some other quality or quantity, according as his opinion of the quiddity of the eucharist varies. Since, then, each [197] of these is Christ, it seems that the identification of many things, even diverse in genus, is quite possible. The minor appears from this:[^18] that by the last [declaration]{g:protestacio} of Berengar and the confirmation of the Roman [church]{g:ecclesia}[^19] the body of Christ is perceptible in that [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}, and in fact is that sacrament (as scripture speaks, with the holy doctors). What, then, would it be, if not whiteness, since that is the most perfect accident, there most proximate to the intellect, as being thus a sign?

## ¶12 · 197.10–197.22

> [p. 197] Item, cum illud sacramentum sit visibile vel est albedo vel album, sed non est album; ergo est albedo per se visibilis, non quantitas ut magnitudo vel superficies, vel non est hoc solum, quia potest manere idem album quantitate variata, ut patet de maioracione vel minoracione sacramenti in calice, ymmo sacramentum sub forma panis, sicut potest variari in albedine, sic potest per exspiracionem variari in quantitate. Sicut ergo quantitas per se existens est quanta, sic albedo per se existens est alba, quia aliter sensus nimis deciperetur in sensacione, cum prius videt album quam albedinem in abstracto, quia hoc est virtutis abstractive discernere.

Again, since that sacrament is visible, it is either whiteness or white; now it is not white; therefore it is whiteness, visible of itself, not quantity, as magnitude or surface, or it is not this alone, because the same white can remain with the quantity varied, as is clear from the increasing or lessening of the sacrament in the chalice; indeed the sacrament under the form of bread, as it can be varied in whiteness, so it can be varied in quantity by evaporation.[^20] As, then, quantity existing of itself is [quantified]{g:quantus}, so whiteness existing of itself is white; because otherwise sense would be [exceedingly]{g:nimis} deceived in [sensation]{g:sensacio}, since it sees the white before whiteness in the abstract, because to discern this belongs to the abstractive [power]{g:virtus}.[^21]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 193.21: If identification takes place, Christ must certainly be acquiring new parts.
- 194.6: Our opponent says that all terms are accidental.
- 194.15: We hold that whatever will be a part of Christ is so already.
- 194.23: In his theory something that differs now from Christ's body will be Christ's body.
- 194.28: If identity could thus be lost, all things would perish.
- 195.1: There would be no substance, no universals, no knowledge.
- 195.9: Action and change would cease, for there would be no specific thing to act; e. g. you could not say "a man walks" when the man might be a stone or any other creature.
- 195.30: Equivocation as to meaning of identification.
- 196.4: The identification we are dealing with is that of two numerically distinct bodies one is lost, in the other.
- 196.29: 2. Our opponents say: The host is a whiteness under which Christ is seen with the bodily eye;
- 197.3: for since Christ is the sacrament He must be whiteness, which is the most perfect accident.
- 197.10: We see the sacrament, and it must be either white or whiteness. But it is not white, so it must be whiteness, which indeed is white.

## Notes

[^1]: **T.** ABCD read *non tantam partem*, "not so great a part", which would undo the argument; E omits *Christi* (193.26). The print is translated.
[^2]: **C.** *Ita quod sequitur*: since identification does not block the syllogistic forms, the syllogism that follows stands. "Substantial and not accidental" are terms: the adversary's reply (¶2) is that all terms are accidental.
[^3]: **C.** *Illud nunc est* is taken bare, "that now is", matching *asinus ipse nunc est*: what will be Peter would already exist. It may also be read "that now is [Peter]".
[^4]: **T.** ABCD *in assimili*; ABD *tempore necessitatis* for *nativitatis* (A corrected in the margin, another hand).
[^5]: **T/C.** ABCD omit *obicitur*. *Ista quidem forma valet in terminis accidentalibus*: the form holds even on the adversary's own account of the terms.
[^6]: **C.** *Sentencia* is the position taught (GLOSSARY, CONTEXTUAL row): the identification opinion.
[^7]: **C.** *Omnia putata*: "all the things supposed", the things the schools hold to exist, as the *tam … quam* that follows lists them.
[^8]: **C.** *Signabile* (CONTEXTUAL row) in its logical sense, "signifiable": anything a term can signify.
[^9]: **T/R.** For *nec aliquid per se vel singulare* ABCD read *aliquid persona*, "nor anything a person or singular"; the print is translated. C adds at the head of the page in another hand: "because if anything were something of itself, then it could not be not that; which is false according to the one who posits the identification of all things whatever, and a person could be not a person, and the singular universal, and so of the rest."
[^10]: **C.** Of the five universals (genus, species, difference, property, accident) only three are named; the clause breaks off into its consequence.
[^11]: **C.** *Sed contra* brings the minor premiss of the argument closed by *non ergo scitur*, hence "now". *Ex tibi dubio*: "for all you know" (GLOSSARY).
[^12]: **C.** *Habitudo* (CONTEXTUAL row) in the sense "respect": identification as a relation holding both ways.
[^13]: **C.** *Cum ydemptificatur opinioni*: Wyclif turns the opinion's own word on it; the teaching "is identified with" the most foolish opinion.
[^14]: **G.** *Ydemptitas generalis, specialis et numeralis*: identity of genus, of species (cf. *ydemptitas specifica* "identity of species") and numerical identity (*ydemptitas numeralis*).
[^15]: **C/T.** *Corpus Christi negatur fore ille panis*: the identification may make the bread the body, but the body is not said to become that bread. ABCD omit *fore* after *videtur* (196.12); D reads *ydemptifice* at 196.11.
[^16]: **C.** *Baptisare terminos*: to give terms new names, as at a christening; cf. the "baptizers of the signs" (*baptiste signorum*, 81.29).
[^17]: **S.** Wyclif's *De universalibus* (*Tractatus de universalibus*); place not given (LEDGER K07b-1).
[^18]: **C.** The minor is "each of these is Christ" (196.32–197.1): it is proved from Berengar's oath that the body is perceptible in the sacrament and is the sacrament.
[^19]: **S/G.** The oath *Ego Berengarius* (Rome, 1059), Gratian *De cons.* D. 2 c. 42 (to be checked, LEDGER K07b-2). *Protestacio* takes its row "declaration"; here it is Berengar's sworn confession, not Wyclif's offer to retract. ABC read *et protestacione* (197.4).
[^20]: **C.** *Per exspiracionem*: by breathing out, i.e. evaporation of the consecrated wine or the drying of the host; D *per exspisacionem*.
[^21]: **C.** *Virtus* in the sense of a faculty, "power" (GLOSSARY): the abstracting power that discerns whiteness in the abstract after sense has seen the white thing.
