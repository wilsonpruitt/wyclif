# sed/autem extract, wave 18: chunks 07-d 07-e 07-f

### 07-d ¶1 · 200.1–202.8  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 200] **Sed** limitatum est nobis pro fugandis heresibus quod loquamur de predicacione ydemptica, et illam oportet scire distinguere a predicacione tropica ad intelligendum sanctos. Scribit enim Jeronimus in epistola ad Helbidiam De XII Questionibus questione IIᵃ: Nos, inquit, audiamus panem quem fregit Dominus deditque discipulis suis esse corpus Domini salvatoris, ipso dicente ad eos: Accipite et comedite; hoc est corpus meum. Ecce quod iste sanctus cui mille doctores et pape non equiparantur in fide scripture asserit quod panis demonstratur pronomine, et usque hodie omnes eciam infideles satrape non audebant hereticare huius sancti sentenciam. Unde Augustinus exponit eam expressius epistola XII ad Bonifacium episcopum: Si, inquit sacramenta quandam similitudinem rerum earum quarum sacramenta sunt non haberent, omnino sacramenta non essent. Ex hac **autem** similitudine plerumque eciam ipsarum rerum nomina accipiunt; sicut ergo secundum quendam modum sacramentum corporis Christi corpus Christi est, sacramentum sanguinis Christi sanguis Christi est, ita et sacramentum fidei fides est. Ideo docet Augustinus Sermone LV° quomodo non debemus contemptibiliter vel irracionabiliter accipere sacramentum propter suum signatum: Non tibi videatur vile, inquit, quod vides; quod vides transit, **sed** quod signatur in ipso visibili non transit **sed** permanet; ecce signum accipitur, comeditur, consumitur: numquid [p. 201] corpus Christi consumitur? Absit. Distinguunt enim sancti inter hoc signum et corpus Christi ut inter Deum et creaturam signantem, nec umquam sciverunt concipere quod qualitas sit sine subiecto, ita quod omnes vires anime plene virtute, beatitudine et gracia sint per se in patria adorande tanquam considerantes et compacientes de nostra miseria si tamen per se forent, pocius hoc facerent qualitates: fides, spes, karitas, felicitas, virgines quas oramus, quia de sua natura haberent perfecciones, quas per accidens accomodant sanctis quasi fines suarum perfeccionum, quas in se habent perfeccius, cum nichil accomodat alteri perfeccionem ulteriorem quam ex se habeat; virtus **autem** talis cum sit intuitiva, abstractiva et quomodocunque natura humana est secundum animam operativa, foret valde perfecta ex illa operacione sibi propria et advocatrix valde compaciens et miseri iuvativa. Nec dicetur racio quare qualitates corporee possunt per se habere omnem accionem quam possunt habere in corpore quin a pari vel evidencius qualitates anime ita possunt; nec est facile fingere quomodo possunt communicare acciones suas corporibus quibus per accidens sunt linite, quin evidencius Deus posset communicare potenciam creandi et omnem accionem suam ad intra cuilibet creature; et ita sicut ille qualitates anime insensibiles communicant corpori denominaciones quas per se sibi retinent, ita possent de Dei omnipotencia communicare ligno vel lapidi, et sic omnis materialis substancia foret de Dei omnipotencia intellectiva subtilissime et beatificabilis de obedienciali potencia. Ille enim qui potest [p. 202] suspendere informacionem qua qualitas spiritualis informat corpus et alias facere quod informet eciam efficacius corpus ex natura sua minus dispositum, ipse posset extendere potenciam informativam virtutis et potenciam passivam subiecti; et sic quelibet materialis substancia foret de Dei potencia quomodocunque activa et per consequens nulla foret substancialis differencia.

ENGLISH: But it has been [assigned]{g:limitare} to us, for the putting to flight of heresies, that we speak of [identical predication]{g:predicacio-ydemptica}, and one must know how to distinguish it from tropical predication in order to understand [the saints]{g:sancti}.[^1] For Jerome writes in the letter to Hedibia, *On Twelve Questions*, question 2: "Let us," he says, "hear that the [bread]{g:panis} which the Lord broke and gave to his disciples is the body of the Lord the Saviour, since he himself says to them: Take ye and eat. This is my body."[^2] See: this saint, to whom a thousand doctors and popes are not equal in the faith of scripture, asserts that the bread is demonstrated by the pronoun; and to this day even all the unbelieving [satraps]{g:satrapa} have not dared [to pronounce]{g:hereticare} this saint's [teaching]{g:sentencia} heretical.[^3] Hence Augustine expounds it more expressly in letter 12 to Bishop Boniface: "If [sacraments]{g:sacramentum}," he says, "did not have a certain likeness to the things of which they are sacraments, they would not be sacraments at all. And from this likeness they for the most part also receive the names of the things themselves; as therefore in a certain mode the sacrament of the body of Christ is [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}, the sacrament of the blood of Christ is the blood of Christ, so also the sacrament of faith is faith."[^4] Therefore Augustine teaches in Sermon 55 how we ought not to receive the sacrament contemptibly or irrationally on account of the [thing it signifies]{g:signatum}: "Let not what you see," he says, "seem cheap to you; what you see passes, but what is signified in the visible thing itself does not pass but remains. See, the sign is received, eaten, consumed: is [201] the body of Christ consumed? Far from it."[^5] For the saints distinguish between this sign and the body of Christ as between God and the signifying [creature]{g:creatura}, nor did they ever know how to conceive that a [quality]{g:qualitas} is without a subject; so that [on that view] all the powers of the soul, full in virtue, blessedness and grace, would [of themselves]{g:per-se} be adored in [the homeland]{g:patria} as considering and having compassion on our misery. If indeed they existed of themselves, the qualities would rather do this: faith, hope, charity, happiness, the virgins to whom we pray;[^6] for of their own nature they would have perfections, which [by accident]{g:per-accidens} they lend to the saints as the ends of their perfections, which they have more perfectly in themselves, since nothing lends to another a perfection further than it has of itself. And such a [virtue]{g:virtus}, since it is intuitive, abstractive, and operative in whatever way human [nature]{g:natura} is operative as to the soul, would be very perfect from that operation proper to it, and a very compassionate advocate, and helpful to the wretched.[^7] Nor will a reason be given why bodily qualities can of themselves have every action which they can have in a body, without the qualities of the soul being able to do so equally or more evidently; nor is it easy to feign how they can communicate their actions to the bodies on which they are by accident [smeared]{g:linire}, without God's being able, more evidently, to communicate the power of creating and every action of his *ad intra* to any creature whatever; and so, just as those [imperceptible]{g:insensibilis} qualities of the soul communicate to the body [namings]{g:denominacio} which of themselves they retain for themselves, so they could by God's omnipotence communicate [them] to a log or a stone, and so every material [substance]{g:substancia} would by God's omnipotence be most subtly intellective, and beatifiable by obediential [potency]{g:potencia}.[^8] For he who can [202] [suspend]{g:suspendere} the [informing]{g:informacio} by which a spiritual quality informs the body, and otherwise bring it about that it inform, even more efficaciously, a body less disposed by its nature, could himself extend the informative power of the virtue and the passive potency of the subject; and so any material substance whatever would by God's power be active in any way whatever, and consequently there would be no substantial difference.

### 07-d ¶2 · 202.9–202.19  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 202] **Sed** deliramenta sunt ista, cum nulla qualitas sit nisi substanciam esse qualem, nec aliqua forma est nisi illa que est substanciam esse formatam vel formosam; ideo idem est ponere quod qualitas sit per se sine substancia et ponere quod sit ista quod substancia sit qualis sine hoc quod sit substancia; et sic includit formalem contradiccionem quod sit accidens sine substancia subiecta, in tantum quod forma substancialis qua corpus hominis est formaliter vivum est vita que est accidentalis tam corpori quam illi spiritui. Et illi uniuntur in eadem persona hominis, ut hic supponitur.

ENGLISH: But these are [ravings]{g:deliramenta}, since no quality is anything but substance's being qualified, nor is any form anything but that which is substance's being formed or shapely;[^9] therefore it is the same to posit that a quality is of itself without substance and to posit that it is this: that substance is qualified without its being substance; and so it includes a formal contradiction that there be an [accident]{g:accidens} without a subject substance, insofar that the [substantial form]{g:forma-substancialis} by which a man's body is formally alive is a life which is accidental both to the body and to that spirit. And these are united in the same [person]{g:persona} of the man, as is here supposed.

### 07-d ¶3 · 202.21–202.28  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 202] Quantum ad triplicem evidenciam tactam in principio argumenti patet quod decretum loquitur tropice quod panis sacramentaliter fiat corpus Christi et non ydemptice quod natura panis sit natura corporis Christi, et sic licet albedo sit sacramentum, non tamen est per se sacramentum, **sed** sacramento illi inherens; et illud subiectum est infinitum perfeccius quam quantitas vel albedo.

ENGLISH: As to the threefold [evidence]{g:evidencia} touched on at the beginning of the argument, it is clear that the [decree]{g:decretum} speaks [tropically]{g:tropice}, "the bread [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} becomes the body of Christ", and not [identically]{g:ydemptice}, "the nature of bread is the nature of the body of Christ";[^10] and so, although whiteness is a sacrament, yet it is not of itself a sacrament, but inhering in that sacrament; and that subject is infinitely more perfect than [quantity]{g:quantitas} or whiteness.

### 07-d ¶5 · 202.31–203.17  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 202] Ulterius pro denominacione accidentis notandum quod triplex est denominacio, scilicet subiectiva, [p. 203] respectiva et intrinseca. Prima denominacione sola substancia denominatur, cum omne accidens sit substanciam esse accidentatam, ita quod nichil est subiectum accidentis nisi substancia; accidens **autem** est fundamentum et causa alteri **sed** non subiectum. Denominacio **autem** respectiva non exigit denominatum illud esse subiectum accidentis denominantis, quod satis est quod sit fundamentum ad quod accidens absolutum respiciat, causando in ipso habitudinem respectivam, ut patet de septem respectibus fundatis in quantitate et qualitate. Et isto modo dicunt Augustinus V De Trinitate in fine et Anshelmus in Monologion XXVII quod Deus est fundamentum habitudinis relative, que quia non requirit fundamentum suum per eius acquisicionem moveri, vocatur ab aliquibus relacio racionis. Non enim requirit in illius subiecto fundamentum esse accidens absolutum.

ENGLISH: Further, for the naming of an accident it is to be noted that naming is threefold, namely subjective, [203] relational, and intrinsic. By the first naming substance alone is named, since every accident is substance's being [subject to accident]{g:accidentatus}, so that nothing is the subject of an accident but substance; but an accident is a foundation and a cause to another, but not a subject. And [relational naming]{g:denominacio-respectiva} does not require that the thing named be the subject of the naming accident, since it is enough that it be a foundation to which an [absolute accident]{g:accidens-absolutum} looks, causing in it a [relational]{g:respectivus} [respect]{g:habitudo}, as is clear of the seven relations founded in quantity and quality.[^11] And in this way Augustine, *On the Trinity* V at the end, and Anselm in *Monologion* 27 say that God is the foundation of a [relative respect]{g:habitudo-relativa}, which, because it does not require its foundation to be moved by its acquisition, is called by some a [relation of reason]{g:relacio-racionis}. For it does not require that the foundation in its subject be an absolute accident.[^12]

### 07-d ¶6 · 203.18–204.8  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 203] Tercia **autem** denominacio intrinseca est qua idem denominat se per se, quod potest esse multipliciter; nam Deus per se excludendo omnem aliam causam denominat se sapientem, iustum, potentem etc.: ideo ista non sunt in Deo accidencia. Quantitas eciam dicitur per se quanta, licet equivoce a substancia; sic eciam albedo alba et ita de aliis accidentibus eciam respectivis. Aliter enim foret processus in infinitum, ut qualitas foret distincta intensione intensa, et sic in infinitum, nisi foret dare unum per se intensum. Non tamen video quod quantitas denominatur subiective albedine vel econtra, **sed** sicut superficies est alba vel albata, ut locuntur philosophi, ita qualitas [p. 204] est mole magna, ita quod neutrum istorum accidencium est subiectum alterius, **sed** denominant se reciproce respective, quia unum concomitatur reliquum in eodem subiecto, ut quia albedo est per partem substancie superficialem aut tenuem; ideo dicitur albedo superficialis, et quia qualitas est per pedalem quantitatem subiecti, ideo dicitur pedalis, sic quod nec quantitas subiectat qualitatem nec econtra.

ENGLISH: And the third, intrinsic, naming is that by which the same thing names itself of itself, which can be in many ways; for God, of himself, excluding every other cause, names himself wise, just, powerful and so on: therefore these are not accidents in God. Quantity, too, is said to be of itself [quantified]{g:quantus}, though [equivocally]{g:equivoce} with substance; so too whiteness [is] white, and so of the other accidents, even the relational ones.[^13] For otherwise there would be a process to infinity, as that a quality would be intense by a distinct intension, and so to infinity, unless [one may posit]{g:est-dare} one thing intense of itself. Yet I do not see that quantity is named subjectively by whiteness or the reverse; but just as a surface is white or whitened, as the philosophers speak, so a quality [204] is great in bulk, so that neither of these accidents is the subject of the other, but they name each other reciprocally, relationally, because the one accompanies the other in the same subject: as, because whiteness is through the superficial or thin part of the substance, therefore it is called superficial whiteness; and because a quality is through the foot-long quantity of the subject, therefore it is called foot-long; so that neither [is]{g:subiectare} quantity the subject of quality nor the reverse.

### 07-d ¶7 · 204.9–204.12  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 204] **Sed** qualitas multiplicius denominatur intensa, activa, quanta etc., quam denominatur ipsa quantitas, licet sit secundum speciem et non individuum fundamentum et basis in subiecto qualitati corporee.

ENGLISH: But quality is named intense, active, quantified and so on, in more ways than quantity itself is named, although [quantity] is, according to species and not [according to] the [individual]{g:individuum}, the foundation and base in the subject for bodily quality.[^14]

### 07-d ¶8 · 204.13–204.32  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 204] Sacramentum **autem** est per se subiectum tam quantitati quam qualitati, cum adversarii concedunt quod accidente albi vini infuso in accidens rubei vini consecrati utraque quantitas et qualitas movetur, cum sacramentum erit maius et alterum; ideo necesse est ponere ibi subiectum prius quantitate et qualitate, quia certum est quod fasciculus accidencium est posterius utroque; et patet in parte quomodo accidencia in denominacione sese respiciunt. Et per hec patet responsio ad tercium argumentum. Nam qualitates secunde denominant qualitates primas, materiam et formam, eciam Deum denominacione respectiva. **Sed** denominacione subiectiva solum denominant materialem substanciam per quam extenduntur. Denominacionem **autem** respectivam facit accidens in quantumcunque distante in quo non est subiective, ut albedo Petri Rome facit ipsum esse similem Johanni Anglie, ideo causat ad omnem punctum propinquitatem atque distanciam et quodammodo facit quod Deus est causa eius.

ENGLISH: Now the sacrament is of itself the subject both of quantity and of quality, since the adversaries grant that when the accident of white [wine]{g:vinum} is poured into the accident of consecrated red wine, both the quantity and the quality are moved, since the sacrament will be greater and other; therefore it is necessary to posit there a subject prior to quantity and quality, for it is certain that the [bundle of accidents]{g:fasciculus-accidentis} is posterior to both;[^15] and it is clear in part how accidents look to each other in naming. And by these things the reply to the third argument is clear. For second qualities name the [first qualities]{g:qualitas-prima}, matter and form, even God, by relational naming. But by subjective naming they name only the material substance through which they are extended. And an accident makes relational naming in a thing however distant in which it is not subjectively: as the whiteness of Peter at Rome makes him like John in England; therefore it causes nearness and distance to every point, and in a manner brings it about that God is its cause.[^16]

### 07-e ¶3 · 205.11–205.20  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 205] Et tercio oportet notare divisiones et descripciones predictas ad tollendum sophismata et errores. Ad tantum enim desipiunt quidam magni, quod credunt accidens denominare subiectum quantumcunque extrinsecum sine aliqua denominacione accidentis eciam respectivi in illo sic denominato, ut exemplificat de visione, de tempore, de motu. **Sed** illi parum ponderant visionem passivam que est videri predicamentum ubi et quando causatum a tempore cum denominacione formali et effectuali.

ENGLISH: And thirdly one must note the divisions and [definitions]{g:descripcio} given above, [to dispose of]{g:tollere} [sophisms]{g:sophisma} and errors. For certain great men are so far out of their wits that they believe that an accident names a subject however extrinsic, without any naming by an accident, even a [relational]{g:respectivus} one, in the thing so named, as he exemplifies of vision, of time, of motion. But they weigh too little passive vision, which is "being seen", the [category]{g:predicamentum} *where* and *when* caused by time, with formal and effectual naming.[^2]

### 07-e ¶4 · 205.21–206.2  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 205] **Sed** redeundo replicatur (ut tactum est) quod homo videndo quodcunque videt Deum oculo corporali, quia omne sensibile vel insensibile ipsum significat. Et confirmatur ex testimonio scripture multiplicis, nam Genes. XXXII, 30 dicitur quod Jacob vidit Dominum facie ad faciem; et Ysaie VI, 1 dicit quod vidit Dominum sedentem super solium excelsum et elevatum. Et Joh. VIIIᵛᵒ, 56 dicit Christus quod Abraham pater Iudeorum exultavit, ut videret diem Messie, vidit et gavisus est; et sic de multis similibus. Si ergo homo, potest tractare et tangere corpus Christi in hostia, [p. 206] multo magis videre potes ipsum ibidem, cum visus sit sensus subtilissimus et supremus.

ENGLISH: But, to return, [it is rejoined]{g:replicatur} (as was touched on) that a man, in seeing anything whatever, sees God with the bodily eye, because everything [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} or [imperceptible]{g:insensibilis} signifies him. And this is confirmed from the testimony of manifold scripture: for in Gen. 32:30 it is said that Jacob "saw the Lord face to face"; and Isaiah 6:1 says that he "saw the Lord sitting upon a throne high and elevated". And in John 8:56 Christ says that "Abraham, the father of the Jews, rejoiced that he might see the day of the Messiah: he saw it and was glad"; and so of many like [texts].[^3] If therefore, O man, one can handle and touch [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} in the [host]{g:hostia}, [206] much more can you see it there, since sight is the subtlest and highest sense.[^4]

### 07-e ¶5 · 206.3–207.10  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 206] Hic oportet cognoscere equivocaciones pro veritatis scripture misteriis cognoscendis. Est enim visio corporalis, visio ymaginaria et visio intellectualis. Visio corporalis est qua vigil sentit visibile secundum figuram forme visibilis in organo sensus visus. Unde ut loquar clarius in ista materia exprimo quod nemo videt ibidem corpus Christi oculo corporali, quod debet fidelibus esse certum. Unde Doctor Sollempnis primo quodlibeto, questione VIᵗᵃ, dubitando si fidelis videt corpus Christi in hostia oculo corporali declarat quod non, eciam quod oculus Christi in hostia non videt se ibidem propter limitacionis carenciam tam in visibili quam in specie visibilis quam in virtute visiva. Et idem dicit lex ecclesie satis sepe, ut III Decretalium, De Celebracione missarum, capitulo Cum Marthe dicitur quod forma visibilis panis et vini est sacramentum **sed** non res sacramenti; et aliud est quod creditur quam cernitur, quia bene corpus Christi creditur et cernitur eius species que est solummodo sacramentum. Unde mirantur multi qua fronte infundabiliter quis audet dicere quod videt corpus Christi in hostia oculo corporali. Non enim sequitur: Petrus intellectu vel fide videt Deum in hostia per corporalem oculum, quia per eius ministerium, ergo videt Deum ibidem oculo corporali. Sic enim arguunt sophiste quod homo videt per talum, per digitos et [p. 207] cetera organa inpertinencia visui Deum et omne sensibile vel insensibile, quia illa sunt in casu instrumenta movencia, ut mente concipiat noscibile quod absconditum est ab omni materiali veritate; **sed** vident quod ista est argucia deridenda, cum sic dicto: Petrus videt hoc oculo corporali, notatur quod oculus corporalis informatur virtute visiva apprehendente hoc per eius speciem naturalem, quomodo solum color, lux vel eius subiectum aut horum privacio videtur, sicut patet philosopho noscenti limites visionis.

ENGLISH: Here one must know the [equivocations]{g:equivocacio}, in order to know the [mysteries]{g:misterium} of the truth of scripture. For there is [bodily vision]{g:visio-corporalis}, imaginary vision and intellectual vision. Bodily vision is that by which one awake [perceives]{g:sentire} a visible thing according to the figure of the visible form in the organ of the sense of sight. Hence, to speak more clearly in this matter, I state that no one sees the body of Christ there with the bodily eye, which ought to be certain to the faithful. Hence the Solemn Doctor, *Quodlibet* I, question 6, raising the doubt whether a believer sees the body of Christ in the host with the bodily eye, [makes clear]{g:declarare} that he does not; even that the eye of Christ in the host does not see itself there, for lack of limitation, as much in the visible thing as in the species of the visible as in the visual [power]{g:virtus}.[^5] And the law of the [church]{g:ecclesia} says the same often enough: as in *Decretals* III, *De celebratione missarum*, the chapter *Cum Marthe*, it is said that "the visible form of [bread]{g:panis} and [wine]{g:vinum} is the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} but not the [reality of the sacrament]{g:res-sacramenti}; and what is believed is other than what is seen, for the body of Christ is indeed believed, and its [species]{g:species}, which is only the sacrament, is seen".[^6] Hence many wonder with what face anyone dares [without foundation]{g:infundabiliter} to say that he sees the body of Christ in the host with the bodily eye. For [it does not follow]{g:sequitur}: "Peter by understanding or by faith sees God in the host through the bodily eye, because through its ministry; therefore he sees God there with the bodily eye." For so the [sophists]{g:sophista} argue that a man sees God and everything perceptible or imperceptible through the ankle, through the fingers and [207] the other organs [irrelevant]{g:impertinens} to sight, because these are, in the case [put], moving instruments, so that he conceives in mind the knowable which is hidden from all material truth. But they see that this is a [quibble]{g:argucia} to be laughed at, since, when it is said thus, "Peter sees this with the bodily eye", it is signified that the bodily eye is [informed]{g:informare} by the visual power apprehending this through its natural species, in the way in which only colour, light, or its subject, or the privation of these, is seen, as is clear to the philosopher who knows the [limits]{g:limites} of vision.[^7]

### 07-e ¶6 · 207.11–207.18  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 207] Secunda maneries visionis vocatur visio ymaginaria que est visio qua secundum virtutem ymaginativam est visibile formaliter apprehensum; et differt a visione priori in tribus: Primo in hoc quod omnis visio corporalis sive per rectam intuicionem sive per reflexionem sive per refraccionem fit per limitacionem speciei presentis visibilis in organo sensus visus; non sic **autem** visio ymaginaria, cum sepe fit in obiecti absencia.

ENGLISH: The second [kind]{g:maneries} of vision is called imaginary vision, which is the vision by which the visible is formally apprehended according to the imaginative power; and it differs from the former vision in three ways. First in this, that every bodily vision, whether by direct intuition or by reflection or by refraction, comes about through the limitation of the species of the present visible thing in the organ of the sense of sight; but not so imaginary vision, since it often comes about in the absence of the object.

### 07-e ¶7 · 207.20–207.28  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 207] Secunda differencia est quod visio corporalis solum inest videnti dum vigilat, **sed** visio ymaginaria inest tam dormienti quam vigili, nam in sompnis fiunt quecunque sompnia in visione ymaginaria, sicut multe prophecie inperfecte fiunt in vigili per misterium visionis ymaginarie, ut patet in Daniele qui tam vigil quam dormiens vidit visione ymaginaria sompnium Nabuchodonosor et per hoc vidit prophetice ultra regem interpretacionem sompnii, ut patet Daniel. IIᵒ.

ENGLISH: The second [difference]{g:differencia} is that bodily vision is in the one seeing only while he is awake, but imaginary vision is in one asleep as much as in one awake: for in sleep all dreams whatever come about in imaginary vision, just as many imperfect prophecies come about in one awake through the mystery of imaginary vision, as is clear in Daniel, who, as much awake as asleep, saw by imaginary vision the dream of Nebuchadnezzar, and by this saw prophetically, beyond the king, the interpretation of the dream, as is clear in Dan. 2.[^8]

### 07-e ¶8 · 207.29–208.28  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 207] Tercio differunt in hoc quod visio ymaginaria est tam quoad obiectum quam tempus visione corporali extensior. Nemo enim videt corporaliter nisi colorem aut lucem presentem vel subiectum aut eorum privacionem, [p. 208] **sed** homo videt ymaginarie illa, que non habent existenciam, ut montem aureum et multa ymaginabilia que nunquam existunt. Sepe eciam videt homo ymaginarie pro dando signo temporis illa que tunc non existunt **sed** post vel ante; primo modo legitur Act. Xᵒ, 11 12, quomodo Petrus vidit in mentis excessu celum apertum et descendens vas quoddam velud lintheum quatuor iniciis submitti de celo in terram in quo erant omnia quadrupedia et serpencia terre et volatilia celi; que scriptura est verissima ad sensum positum. Secundo modo loquitur scriptura Act. IX, 11 quod Ananias ex mandato Domini alloqueretur Paulum: Vade, inquit, in vicum qui vocatur Rectus et quere in domo Jude Saulum nomine Tharsensem. Ecce enim orat. Et vidit virum Ananiam nomine introeuntem et inponentem sibi manus ut visum recipiat. Ecce quod Paulus vidit ymaginarie futurum factum Ananie, ut videtur probabiliter ex textu. Et ista visione vidit Heliseus Gesi valde distantem localiter, quomodo recepit simoniace a Naaman munera propter graciam spiritualem quam Deus contulit Heliseo (ut patet IVᵒ Reg. Vᵒ). Et ita videtur quod triplex sit visio ymaginaria, sicut triplex est visio corporalis, scilicet visio ymaginabilis non existentis preteriti vel futuri, visio ymaginabilis non existentis **sed** visui possibilis, et visio cuiuscunque estimabilis sicut apprehensio estimative fantastice vel memorative organice.

ENGLISH: Thirdly they differ in this, that imaginary vision is more extensive than bodily vision as much as to object as to time. For no one sees [bodily]{g:corporaliter} anything but present colour or light, or the subject, or their privation; [208] but a man sees imaginarily those things which have no existence, such as a golden mountain and many imaginable things which never exist. Often, too, a man sees imaginarily, for the giving of a sign of time, those things which do not then exist but [exist] afterwards or before.[^9] Of the first mode we read in Acts 10:11–12 how Peter "saw" in an ecstasy of mind "the heaven opened and a certain vessel descending, as it were a linen sheet let down by the four corners from heaven to the earth: wherein were all manner of four-footed beasts and creeping things of the earth and fowls of the air"; which scripture is most true in the sense laid down.[^10] Of the second mode scripture speaks in Acts 9:11, of how Ananias at the Lord's command was to speak to Paul: "Go," he says, "into the street that is called Strait and seek in the house of Judas, one named Saul of Tarsus. For behold he prayeth. And he saw a man named Ananias coming in and putting his hands upon him, that he might receive his sight."[^11] See: Paul saw imaginarily the future deed of Ananias, as appears [with probability]{g:probabiliter} from the text. And by this vision Elisha saw Gehazi, far distant [in place]{g:localiter}, how he received gifts simoniacally from Naaman, on account of the spiritual grace which God conferred on Elisha (as is clear in 4 Kings 5).[^12] And so it seems that imaginary vision is threefold, as bodily vision is threefold: namely, vision of an imaginable thing not existing, past or future; vision of an imaginable thing not existing but possible to sight; and vision of any estimable thing whatever, such as the apprehension of the estimative, the [phantastic]{g:fantasticus} or the memorative organic [power].[^13]

### 07-e ¶9 · 208.29–209.3  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 208] **Sed** tercia est visio intellectualis, qua primo intellectu videtur visibile; et est in beatis pura sine visione inferiori corporali vel tardante ymaginaria. In nobis [p. 209] **autem** sunt iste tres visionum species comitantes, ut simul video hiis tribus virtutibus sacramentum sensibile triplici visione.

ENGLISH: But the third is intellectual vision, by which the visible is seen first by the understanding; and in the blessed it is pure, without lower bodily vision or a retarding imaginary [vision].[^14] But in us [209] these three species of vision accompany one another, as I see at once with these three powers the perceptible sacrament by a threefold vision.

### 07-e ¶10 · 209.4–209.23  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 209] Est **autem** triplex visio intellectualis, scilicet intuitiva, abstractiva et enigmatica. Prima visione vident Deum beati in patria, sicut vident angelos et sensibilia, et cum actus specificantur ab obiectis, ponitur quod iste sunt tres species intuicionum, sicut aliter intuetur beatus sensibile et aliter viator. Intuicio **autem** intellectualis habet hanc racionem quod per se causatur ex capacitate intellectus et presencia rei vise, ut posito visibili cum sua applicacione que exprimitur per presenciam et sufficienti capacitate intellectus consequitur intuicio. **Sed** sic non est de visione abstractiva, quia ad illam requiritur discursus racionis, sicut philosophi naturaliter noverunt Deum ex effectibus (ut dicit Apostolus Rom. Iᵒ). Et illam dicunt doctores requiri ad actuale meritum viantis, et hanc neminem posse mereri in primo instanti in quo incipit preter Christum. Oportet enim tale meritum procedere a voluntate libere mota per racionem ad meritorie operandum quod requirit tempus in quolibet alio preter Christum.

ENGLISH: And intellectual vision is threefold, namely intuitive, [abstractive]{g:abstractive} and enigmatic. By the first vision the blessed see God in [the homeland]{g:patria}, as they see angels and perceptible things; and since acts are specified by their objects, it is posited that these are three species of intuitions, just as the blessed man intuits a perceptible thing in one way and the [wayfarer]{g:viator} in another. And intellectual intuition has this account: that it is caused [of itself]{g:per-se} by the capacity of the intellect and the presence of the thing seen, so that, when the visible is posited with its application, which is expressed by "presence", and a sufficient capacity of the intellect, intuition follows. But it is not so with abstractive vision, for to it the discourse of reason is required, as the philosophers by nature knew God from his effects (as the Apostle says, Rom. 1[:20]).[^15] And this, the doctors say, is required for the actual merit of [one on the road]{g:viantes}, and that no one except Christ can merit this in the first instant in which he begins.[^16] For such merit must proceed from a will freely moved by reason to work meritoriously, which requires time in anyone else whatever except Christ.

### 07-f ¶1 · 209.24–210.8  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 209] Tercia **autem** visio intellectualis est enigmatica, que est de pure credito infusa obscura noticia, modo quo loquitur Apostolus I. Cor. XIIIᵒ, 12: Videmus nunc per speculum in enigmate, tunc **autem** facie ad faciem. Et noticiam istius modi habent tam boni quam mali in quolibet inicio noticie rei insensibilis, sic quod quandoque simul currunt iste tres noticie. Et communiter precedit visio enigmatica, cum oportet nos procedere ab imperfecto ad perfectum. Sic ergo novem modis satis equivoce sumitur visio in scriptura. [p. 210] Quandoque **autem** sumitur visio aggregative pro multis visionibus; unde in Deo distinguitur visio intuicionis qua Deus videt existencia presencia, preterita et futura. Omnia enim illa intuetur, cum existunt sibi presencia, **sed** possibilia que non existunt videt Deus noticia simplicis apprehensionis. Unde quamvis oporteat illa esse, non tamen oportet illa existere (sicut patet alibi diffuse).

ENGLISH: And the third intellectual vision is enigmatic, which is an infused, obscure knowledge of what is purely believed, in the way the Apostle speaks, 1 Cor. 13:12: "We see now through a glass in a dark manner: but then face to face." And knowledge of this kind both good and bad men have at every beginning of knowledge of an [imperceptible]{g:insensibilis} thing, so that sometimes these three knowledges run together. And commonly [enigmatic vision]{g:visio-intuitiva} goes first, since we must proceed from the imperfect to the perfect. So therefore vision [is taken]{g:sumitur-pro} in scripture in nine ways, [equivocally]{g:equivoce} enough.[^1] [210] And sometimes vision is taken [aggregatively]{g:aggregative} for many visions; hence in God there is distinguished the vision of intuition, by which God sees existing things, present, past and future. For he intuits all those things, since they exist present to him; but possible things which do not exist God sees by the knowledge of simple apprehension. Hence although it must be that they are, yet it is not necessary that they exist (as is clear elsewhere at length).[^2]

### 07-f ¶2 · 210.9–210.17  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 210] Et per istam distinccionem potest intelligi scriptura que videtur contradicere sibi ipsi. Nam Exodi XXXIIIᵒ, 20 capitulo dicit Deus: Non me videbit homo et vivet, hoc est, non tunc vivet vitam naturalem, dum deitatem fuerit intuitus, ut fuit de Moyse et Paulo in raptibus, et ut evenit de beatis; vivunt enim tunc aliam vitam spiritualem mortificati mundo. Et hec est racio quare debemus mundum relinquere et non implicare nos terrestribus **sed** spiritualibus inhiare.

ENGLISH: And by this distinction scripture can be understood which seems to contradict itself. For in Exodus 33:20 God says: "Man shall not see me, and live"; that is, he will not then live the natural life while he has intuited [the Godhead]{g:deitas}, as it was with Moses and Paul in their raptures, and as happens with the blessed; for they then live another, spiritual life, mortified to the world.[^3] And this is the reason why we ought to leave the world and not entangle ourselves in earthly things but gape after spiritual ones.

### 07-f ¶3 · 210.18–211.20  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 210] Et sic intelligitur illud Johannis Iᵒ, 18: Deum nemo vidit unquam. Et illud Apostoli I. Cor. IIᵒ, 9: Nec oculus vidit nec auris audivit nec in cor hominis ascendit cum eis similibus. Notandum tamen quod tales negative quandoque excedunt claram intuicionem divine essencie et quandoque equalitatem visibilitatis divine. Quamvis enim beati clare videbunt divinam essenciam, non tamen secundum ultimum sue visibilitatis, cum hoc soli Deo sit proprium. Necesse itaque est tales distincciones cognoscere, nam propter talem ignoranciam et alias malicias dicitur Manassen regem Juda impetere Isaiam super heretica pravitate et ipsum usque ad mortem serrare. Dicit enim (ut supra [p. 211] exposui): Vidi dominum sedentem super solium excelsum. Et Deus dicit Moysi: Non videbit me homo et vivet. **Sed** concordantur per hoc quod propheta vidit Dominum visione abstractiva cum visione alia quam habuit de ymagine vel figura; per hoc enim quod Ysaias vidit talem ymaginem oculo corporali et visio deitatis fuit sibi infusa cum visione abstractiva quam Deo movente elicuit, Deum super alios intellexit, et sic generaliter in lege veteri, quando dicitur quod videt vel audit Dominum, intelligitur de sensacione in signo cum noticia abstractiva divine essencie, non quod tunc intuitus est Deum, ut sepe declarat Augustinus et specialiter in libro Sermonum Sermone primo: Apparere, inquit, per substanciam suam, sicuti est, nonnisi mundis cordibus dignatur, ut dicit evangelium; oculis **autem** corporalibus sanctorum. Si quando Deus voluit apparere, non per se ipsum, **sed** per creaturam sensibilem, quantum ista carne sentiri potest apparuit. Et post diffuse declarat istam sentenciam.

ENGLISH: And so is understood that of John 1:18: "No man hath seen God at any time." And that of the Apostle, 1 Cor. 2:9: "Eye hath not seen, nor ear heard: neither hath it entered into the heart of man", with the like. Yet it is to be noted that such negatives sometimes exceed clear intuition of the divine essence and sometimes the equality of the divine visibility.[^4] For although the blessed will clearly see the divine essence, yet not according to the utmost of its visibility, since this is proper to God alone. It is therefore necessary to know such distinctions; for on account of such ignorance, and other malices, Manasses king of Judah is said to have attacked Isaiah on [a charge of] [heretical depravity]{g:heretica-pravitas} and to have sawn him through to death.[^5] For he says (as I expounded [211] above): "I saw the Lord sitting upon a throne high." And God says to Moses: "Man shall not see me, and live." But they are reconciled by this: that the prophet saw the Lord by abstractive vision together with another vision which he had of an image or [figure]{g:figura}; for by this, that Isaiah saw such an image with the bodily eye, and the vision of the Godhead was infused into him together with the abstractive vision which he elicited by God's moving, he understood God above others. And so generally in the old law, when it is said that one sees or hears the Lord, it is understood of [sensation]{g:sensacio} in a sign together with [abstractive]{g:abstractive} knowledge of the divine essence, not that he then intuited God, as Augustine often [makes clear]{g:declarare}, and especially in the book of Sermons, Sermon 1: "To appear," he says, "through his substance, as he is, he deigns only to the clean of heart, as the gospel says; but to the bodily eyes of the [saints]{g:sancti}, if ever God willed to appear, he appeared not through himself but through a [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} [creature]{g:creatura}, so far as he can be [perceived]{g:sentire} by this flesh."[^6] And afterwards he makes this [teaching]{g:sentencia} clear at length.

### 07-f ¶4 · 211.21–212.6  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 211] Et patet quod sicut nemo sufficit Deum videre oculo corporali, sic nec corpus Christi in hostia consecrata, quia si sufficeret videre sic illud corpus ibi, sufficeret videre sic illud corpus esse ibi, et discernere cuiusmodi est ibi, quod est contra experienciam et determinacionem Urbani quinti cum suis cardinalibus. Videt, inquam, fide et intellectu corpus Christi in hostia consecrata, et sic per istam distinccionem de visione intelligende sunt alie scripture ut Math. Vᵗᵒ, 8, ubi promittitur pro finali premio beati mundo corde quoniam ipsi Deum videbunt. [p. 212] Ibi patet quod loquitur de intuicione in patria. **Sed** Joh. XIVᵒ, 9 dicit: Philippe, qui videt me, videt et patrem meum; ubi videtur loqui de visione abstractiva et visione infusa. Qui enim videt Dei filium naturalem sub racione qua talis, videt abstractive Deum patrem; et illa visio Christi pertinet ad fidelem supra bestiam.

ENGLISH: And it is clear that, just as no one is able to see God with the bodily eye, so neither [can he see] [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} in the [consecrated host]{g:hostia-consecrata}; for if he were able to see that body there in this way, he would be able to see in this way that that body is there, and to discern of what kind it is there, which is against experience and the [determination]{g:determinacio} of Urban V with his cardinals.[^7] He sees, I say, the body of Christ in the consecrated host by faith and understanding; and so by this distinction about vision other scriptures are to be understood, as Matt. 5:8, where there is promised as the final reward: "Blessed are the clean of heart: they shall see God." [212] There it is clear that it speaks of intuition in [the homeland]{g:patria}. But John 14:9 says: "Philip, he that seeth me seeth my Father also"; where it seems to speak of abstractive vision and infused vision.[^8] For he who sees the natural Son of God under the aspect by which he is such sees God the Father abstractively; and that vision of Christ belongs to the believer, above the beast.[^9]

### 07-f ¶5 · 212.7–212.16  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 212] **Sed** replicatur contra istud primo per hoc quod oculus corporalis est causa visionis Dei a nobis, ergo nos videmus Deum oculo corporali. **Sed** sic arguens remittendus est ad grammaticos pro cognoscendo regimine ablativi casus, ut dixi sepius. Illi enim talo et pedibus senciunt et intelligunt quidquid apprehenderint sicut fingunt; ideo oportet volentes loqui in ista materia notare grammaticam, loycam et philosophiam cum distinccione visionis predicta, et aliter sunt a disputacione istius materie exsufflandi.

ENGLISH: But against this [it is rejoined]{g:replicatur}, first, by this: that the bodily eye is a cause of the vision of God by us; therefore we see God with the bodily eye. But one who argues so is to be sent back to the grammarians to learn the government of the ablative case, as I have said more than once.[^10] For they, as they feign, perceive and understand with the ankle and the feet whatever they have apprehended; therefore those who wish to speak in this matter must attend to grammar, logic and philosophy, with the distinction of vision given above, and otherwise they are to be [blown out]{g:exsufflare} of the disputation of this matter.

### 07-f ¶7 · 212.20–212.28  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 212] **Sed** patet quod concedentes recte assumptum sunt sic glosandi: Hostia sacra figurat sacramentaliter corpus Christi; **sed** supposito quod ecclesia admittat loycam istam ut olim, patet quod propter equivocacionem perit illacio, cum sic arguitur: Hostia figurat corpus Christi et ipsa sentitur ibidem, ergo et corpus Christi. Sic enim argueretur quod deitas sit sensibilis quolibet quinque sensuum quandocunque aliquid sentiretur, omnis namque creatura figurat et dicit deitatem.

ENGLISH: But it is clear that those who rightly grant [the assumption]{g:assumptum} are [to be glossed]{g:glosare} thus: "The [sacred]{g:sacer} host [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} [figures]{g:figurare} the body of Christ";[^11] but, supposing that the [church]{g:ecclesia} admits this [logic]{g:loyca} as formerly, it is clear that through [equivocation]{g:equivocacio} the [illation]{g:illacio} perishes, when it is argued thus: "The host figures the body of Christ, and it is perceived there; therefore the body of Christ [is perceived] too." For so it would be argued that the Godhead is perceptible by any of the five senses whenever anything were perceived; for every creature figures and [signifies]{g:dicere} the Godhead.[^12]

### 07-f ¶8 · 212.29–213.16  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 212] Tercio replicatur per hoc quod nulla substancia videtur oculo corporali nisi quia suum accidens videtur oculo corporali per se, cum ergo indumentum vel [p. 213] armatura corporis Christi per se videtur in hostia, videtur quod corpus Christi videbitur consequenter. Confirmatur ex predicta sentencia Augustini dicentis quod Deus in ista carne potest sentiri oculis corporalibus, sicut dicimus parietem colorari et videri per spissas picturas parieti collinitas. Istam **autem** arguciam magnificant ydiote, asserentes quod solum accidens vere sentitur. **Sed** ignorant philosophiam quomodo nullum accidens potest sentiri nisi in virtute substancie sustentantis, cum nullum accidens sit nisi substanciam accidentari, hoc est, modus accidentalis substancie. Accidens tamen videtur per se et subiectum per accidens; cum ergo nullum accidens corporis Christi in hostia videtur oculo corporali, manifestum est quod ipsum non videtur sic per accidens **sed** ipsa hostia.

ENGLISH: Thirdly it is rejoined by this: that no [substance]{g:substancia} is seen with the bodily eye except because its [accident]{g:accidens} is seen [of itself]{g:per-se} with the bodily eye; since, therefore, the garment or [213] [armament]{g:armatura} of the body of Christ is seen of itself in the host, it seems that the body of Christ will be seen in consequence. This is confirmed from Augustine's teaching mentioned above, who says that God can be perceived in this flesh by bodily eyes, just as we say that a wall is coloured and seen through thick paintings [smeared on]{g:linire} the wall.[^13] And this [quibble]{g:argucia} the [unlettered men]{g:ydiota} magnify, asserting that only the accident is [truly]{g:vere} perceived. But they do not know philosophy, how no accident can be perceived except in the power of the sustaining substance, since no accident is anything but substance's being accidented, that is, the accidental mode of substance. Yet an accident is seen of itself and the subject [by accident]{g:per-accidens}; since, therefore, no accident of the body of Christ is seen in the host with the bodily eye, it is manifest that it is not seen so by accident, but the host itself [is].[^14]
