# *De eucharistia*, chapter 6, part 8: Rabanus and the saints, that bread and wine are the sacrament

## ¶1 · 187.1–188.23

> [p. 187] Revera glossantes decreta et decretales opinantur honorabilius quod panis et vinum sunt hoc sacramentum, quod non dampnant nec reprobant sed confirmant. Et sic fecerunt sancti doctores concorditer. Unde Rabanus libro De Naturis rerum, libro Vᵒ, capitulo XI declarat quare Deus voluit se ipsum coli sacramentaliter in pane et vino et in fine sic loquitur: Aliud est sacramentum, aliud virtus sacramenti; sacramentum enim ore percipitur, virtute sacramenti homo interior saciatur; igitur quia panis corpus confirmat, ideo ille corpus Christi congruenter nuncupatur; vinum autem quia sanguinem operatur in carne, ideo ad sanguinem Christi refertur; hec autem dum sunt visibilia sanctificata per Spiritum Sanctum, in sacramentum divini corporis transeunt. Ecce plane sentencia quam predixi. Unde ante sic loquitur: Maluit enim Dominus corporis et sanguinis sacramenta fidelium ore percipi et in partem eorum redigi, ut per visibile opus invisibilis ostenderetur effectus. Ecce quod iste magnus doctor dictat cum experiencia quod panis et vinum que sunt sacramentum corporis et sanguinis Domini rediguntur in partem hominis quoad corpus, ut figuretur pastus interioris hominis per rem huius sacramenti, ut sepe declarat in libro suo De corpore et sanguine Domini. Et idem allegatum est ex testimonio Augustini in De Consecracione, distinccione IIᵃ, Corpus, et Johannis Damasceni et aliorum, quamvis ista sentencia sit per novos recentesque qui venerunt nimis sophistice palliata. [p. 188] Antiqua itaque sanctorum est sentencia quod forma, species vel figura panis et vini sunt hoc sacramentum; et per hec tria nomina intelligunt essenciam vel quidditatem panis et vini. Sic enim loquitur Apostolus Hebr. I, 3, quando dicit Christum eternaliter fuisse in forma Dei, cum sit splendor patris et figura substancie eius. Non enim intelligunt sancti quod hoc sacramentum quod vocant figuram panis sit qualitas de IVᵗᵃ specie qualitatis, quia illa est debilissime entitatis. Nec dubium alicui loyco quin sequitur: Christus sumitur in forma panis et vini, ergo ille panis et vinum sunt, ut patet per argumentum Christi quo (ut dicitur Luc. XX) convicerat Saduceos. Et illi qui dicunt quod false vocatur forma panis et vini, quia est forma per se stans, que olim fuit accidens panis et vini, que iam non sunt secundum aliquam partem sui, nituntur sophisticare sensus populi, ne credat illud quod plane sentit sed cogatur credere false fictum incredibile, ut hoc sit via ad populum amplius seducendum. Unde solebam replicare contra hos glossatores quod ista est sentencia unius falsi et heretici ydiote, quia est sentencia eorum, qui olim fuerunt huiusmodi, quia ante baptisacionem suam; et utinam huiusmodi non sint modo.

[In truth]{g:revera} those who gloss the [decrees]{g:decretum} and [decretals]{g:decretalis} are of the more honourable opinion that the [bread and wine]{g:panis} are this [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}, which they do not condemn or reprove but confirm.[^1] And so the holy doctors did with one accord. Hence Rabanus, in the book *On the Natures of Things*, book 5, chapter 11, makes clear why God willed himself to be worshipped [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} in bread and wine, and at the end speaks thus: "The sacrament is one thing, the power of the sacrament another; for the sacrament is received by the mouth, by the power of the sacrament the inner man is filled; therefore, because bread strengthens the body, so it is fittingly named the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}; and wine, because it works blood in the flesh, is therefore referred to the blood of Christ; and these, while they are visible things sanctified by the Holy Spirit, pass into the sacrament of the divine body."[^2] Behold plainly the [teaching]{g:sentencia} that I spoke of before.[^3] Hence before [this] he speaks thus: "For the Lord preferred that the sacraments of his body and blood be received by the mouth of the faithful and be brought into a part of them, so that by a visible work an invisible effect might be shown." Behold, this great doctor lays down, with experience, that the bread and wine which are the sacrament of the [body of the Lord]{g:corpus-domini} and his blood are brought into a part of man as to the body, so that the feeding of the inner man by the [reality of this sacrament]{g:res-sacramenti} may be [figured]{g:figurare}, as he often makes clear in his book *On the Body and Blood of the Lord*.[^4] And the same has been [alleged]{g:allegare} from the testimony of Augustine in *De consecratione*, distinction 2, *Corpus*, and of John Damascene and others,[^5] although this teaching has been [cloaked]{g:palliare} [exceedingly]{g:nimis} [sophistically]{g:sophistice} by "new and fresh ones that are come".[^6] [188] So the ancient teaching of [the saints]{g:sancti} is that the form, [species]{g:species} or [figure]{g:figura} of bread and wine are this sacrament; and by these three names they understand the essence or quiddity of bread and wine. For so the Apostle speaks, Heb. 1:3, when he says that Christ was eternally in the form of God, since he is the brightness of the Father and "the figure of his substance".[^7] For the saints do not understand that this sacrament, which they call the figure of bread, is a [quality]{g:qualitas} of the fourth species of quality, since that is of the feeblest entity.[^8] Nor is it doubtful to any logician that [it follows]{g:sequitur}: "Christ is received in the form of bread and wine, therefore that bread and wine are", as is clear by the argument of Christ by which (as is said in Luke 20) he had [proved conclusively]{g:convincere} against the Sadducees.[^9] And those who say that it is falsely called the form of bread and wine, because it is a form standing [of itself]{g:per-se}, which once was an [accident]{g:accidens} of the bread and wine, which now are not in any part of themselves, strive [to counterfeit]{g:sophisticare} the senses of the people, so that it may not believe what it plainly [perceives]{g:sentire}, but be forced to believe an incredible thing falsely feigned, that this may be a way to seducing the people further. Hence I used [to rejoin]{g:replicatur} against these glossators that this is the teaching of a false and heretical [unlettered man]{g:ydiota}, because it is the teaching of those who once were such, because [they were so] before their baptism; and would that they are not such now.[^10]

## ¶2 · 188.24–189.28

> [p. 188] Ex istis probabiliter creditur quod episcopi tenentur sub pena amissionis omnium temporalium suorum (quia sub pena amissionis regni celorum) cognoscere quid sit hoc sacramentum, quia aliter sunt inepti fideliter informare populum; et omnes qui auctorisant et defendunt tales cifras integre incurrunt perdicionis periculum. Unde utinam gens Roberti quereret diligenter ab ipso cum suis cardinalibus quid de quidditate hostie sit credendum. Hoc enim secundum beatum Petrum est precipuum prelatorum officium parate et [p. 189] fideliter reddere racionem fidei et illam mundo postposito defendere et docere. Videtur, inquam, quod omne sensibile accidens in sacra hostia iuxta descripcionem sacramenti sit sacramentum vel saltem accidens adiuvans sacramenti; sed fides tractantis sacramentum et sanctificacio Dei spiritualiter iuvant et faciunt sacramentum. Cum autem omne per accidens sit reducibile ad aliquod per se, patet quod est dare substancialem essenciam vel naturam accidentibus illis substratam; sed ad racionem illam debemus parum attendere sed in racionem sui signati totam cogitacionem nostram suspendere. Et hec est racio quare sancti dicunt panem converti in corpus Christi vel sacramentum, ymmo panem esse corpus Christi, sicut dicimus peccatorem converti a membro dyaboli in filium Dei; que conversio est magis mirabilis quam conversio sacramenti. Sed nullibi in istis miraculis oportet quod Deus destruat substancialem essenciam, sed mutat ipsam in melius servando naturam. Et hoc servaremus nos qui signa tantum attendimus; Christus enim ordinavit nos comedere et bibere hostiam in hiis rebus, ut figurarent pastum anime tanquam finem. Et utinam tales cultores accidencium non sint a substancia corporis ecclesie exsufflandi; ponunt enim religioni Christi accidere totam regulam quam Christus tradidit et sic in accidentibus involvuntur; plus enim appreciantur terrena que adiacent quam virtutas.

From these things it is believed [with probability]{g:probabiliter} that the [bishops]{g:episcopus} are bound, under pain of the loss of all their [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} (because under pain of the loss of the [kingdom]{g:regnum} of heaven), to know what this sacrament is, because otherwise they are [inept]{g:ineptus} to [inform]{g:informare} the people faithfully; and all who authorize and defend such [ciphers]{g:cifra} wholly incur the danger of perdition.[^11] Hence would that the people of Robert would diligently ask of him, with his cardinals, what is to be believed about the quiddity of the [host]{g:hostia}.[^12] For this, according to blessed Peter, is the chief office of [prelates]{g:prelatus}: readily and [189] faithfully to render an account of the faith, and, the world [set aside]{g:postponere}, to defend and teach it.[^13] It seems, I say, that every [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} accident in the sacred host, according to the [definition]{g:descripcio} of the sacrament, is the sacrament, or at least an accident assisting the sacrament;[^14] but the faith of the one handling the sacrament and the [sanctification]{g:sanctificacio} of God [spiritually]{g:spiritualiter} help and make the sacrament. Now since everything [by accident]{g:per-accidens} is reducible to something of itself, it is clear that [one may posit]{g:est-dare} a substantial essence or nature underlying those accidents; but to that [aspect]{g:racio} we ought to attend little, but hang our whole thought on the aspect of what it signifies. And this is the reason why the saints say that the bread is converted into the body of Christ, or the sacrament, indeed that the bread is the body of Christ, just as we say that a sinner is converted from a [member]{g:membrum} of the devil into a son of God; which [conversion]{g:conversio} is more wonderful than the conversion of the sacrament. But nowhere in these miracles must God destroy the substantial essence, but he changes it for the better, preserving the nature. And this we should preserve, we who attend only to the signs;[^15] for Christ ordained us to eat and drink the host in these things, so that they might figure the feeding of the soul as the end. And would that such [worshippers of accidents]{g:cultores-accidencium} be not [blown out]{g:exsufflare} from the substance of the body of the [church]{g:ecclesia};[^16] for they posit that the whole rule which Christ handed down befalls the religion of Christ [as an accident], and so they are wrapped up in accidents; for they prize the earthly things that lie beside more than virtues.[^17]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 188.24: Bishops are bound to know the true doctrine.
- 189.17: We never find that God in His miracles destroys substance.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Quod non dampnant* is taken as a relative on the proposition ("that the bread and wine are this sacrament"), which the glossators confirm. *Sunt* is kept as "are", in Wyclif's order: bread and wine subject, "this sacrament" predicate.

[^2]: **S.** Loserth: Rabanus Maurus, *De universo* (= *De rerum naturis*) V.11 (PL 111), with a pointer to Wyclif, *De apostasia* c. 16, p. 207 (LEDGER K06h-1). Quotation bounds ours (no italics recorded). E reads *saturatur* for *saciatur* (187.10).

[^3]: **C.** *Sentencia* (CONTEXTUAL): "teaching", the position taught, here and throughout the chunk.

[^4]: **S.** Wyclif's ascription to Rabanus of a book *De corpore et sanguine Domini*; which work he means is to be checked (LEDGER K06h-2). The second quotation (*Maluit enim Dominus …*) is from the same chapter of *De universo* (LEDGER K06h-3).

[^5]: **S.** Loserth: Augustine in *De cons.* D. 2 c. 60 (*Corpus*), modern form ours (LEDGER K06h-4); John Damascene, *De fide orthodoxa* IV.13 (LEDGER K06h-5).

[^6]: **S.** Deut. 32:17 (Vulgate *novi recentesque venerunt*; DR "that were newly come up"), said there of new gods; Wyclif's *novos recentesque qui venerunt* is translated, with the Vulgate's sense.

[^7]: **S.** Heb. 1:3 (DR "the brightness of his glory and the figure of his substance"): Wyclif has *splendor patris* for *splendor gloriae*, and *in forma Dei* is Phil. 2:6 (DR "being in the form of God"). Only the words that agree with the Vulgate are quoted.

[^8]: **S.** Figure (shape) is the fourth species of quality in Aristotle, *Categories* 8 (LEDGER K06h-6).

[^9]: **S/C.** Luke 20:37–38 (God "is not the God of the dead, but of the living"). The inference concludes to existence ("that bread and wine are"), as Christ's argument concluded from "the God of Abraham" that Abraham lives; *sunt* kept as "are". D reads *convincerat* (188.13).

[^10]: **C/T.** The rejoinder is obscure: the glossators' teaching is that of an unlettered heretic, since it is that of men who were "such" (unbelievers) before their baptism; translated as printed. The manuscripts lack *modi* at 188.22–23, reading *huius*; *huiusmodi* is Loserth's. E reads *volebam* for *solebam* (188.20).

[^11]: **C/T.** *Cifras*: "ciphers", zeros, the accidents that stand for nothing; *inepti* "inept", kept apart from *indispositus* "unfit". D and E read *integres* (188.29).

[^12]: **R.** *Gens Roberti*: the following of Robert of Geneva, elected at Fondi in 1378 as Clement VII; the allusion dates the passage after the schism began (LEDGER K06h-7). D reads *ab eo* (188.31).

[^13]: **S/T.** 1 Pet. 3:15 (DR "being ready always to satisfy every one that asketh you a reason of that hope which is in you"): Wyclif has *racionem fidei*, "an account of the faith". ABCD read *beatorum prelatorum*, E *parare et* (188.33).

[^14]: **C/T.** *Accidens adiuvans sacramenti*: the participle governs a genitive; translated as "assisting the sacrament". C has *sit — sacramenti* in the margin by another hand (189.5).

[^15]: **C.** *Nos qui signa tantum attendimus*: taken as Wyclif's "we" (those who attend to the bread and wine as signs only, not as the thing signified); the other construal, an ironic "we" of the sign-worshippers, is possible.

[^16]: **C.** *Exsufflandi*: "blown out", the exsufflation of the baptismal exorcism; would that the church need not cast them out.

[^17]: **T.** The print has *virtutas* (189.28), which is not a Latin form; translated as *virtutes*, "virtues", and reported. Some manuscripts read *tantam regulam* (189.23).
