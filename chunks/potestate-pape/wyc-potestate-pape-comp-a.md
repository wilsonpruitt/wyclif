# *De potestate pape*, Compendium, part 1: the compendium, chapters one to seven

## ¶1 · 398.2–398.2

> [p. 398] Sentencia libri ultimi in compendio sic habetur:

The [teaching]{g:sentencia} of the last book is had in compendium thus:[^1]

## ¶2 · 398.3–398.27

> [p. 398] Capitulum primum premittens equivocacionem quadruplicem potestatis dividit ipsam, declarans quomodo ex scriptura non docetur, quod est potestas preter racionalis nature potenciam, et quomodo aliquid alterius subiacet potestati. Secundo descendendo ad divisionem Hugonis elicit descripcionem potestatis secularis et spiritualis et obiciendo dissolvit. Tercio principaliter arguitur quod superfluit ponere aliam potestatem preter liberum arbitrium, secundo quod potestas ordinis, cum nunc potest laxari et nunc restringi sufficit sine potestate regiminis vel iurisdicionis superaddita. Et tercio arguitur per hoc, quod creatura non cooperatur cum Deo comproducendo quantitatem indivisibilem in mente, quo tripliciter confirmat et respondet, quod potestas nunc accipitur simpliciter et nunc aggregative superaddita humana promulgacione autentica, et illam necesse est ponere post lapsum, quando Deus autenticat, et sic sunt multe potestates, que non fuissent in statu innocencie; secundo docet quomodo potest augeri vel minui, et tercio docet quid creatura coefficit cum Deo et quid non, et exponendo scripturas multiplices notat qualiter Christus tacuit, Joh. XIX tacendo doctrinas multiplices.

The first chapter, setting out first a fourfold [equivocation]{g:equivocacio} of [power]{g:potestas}, divides it, declaring how it is not taught from scripture that there is a power besides the [potency]{g:potencia} of rational nature, and how one thing is subject to the power of another.[^2] Second, coming down to Hugh's division, it draws out a [definition]{g:descripcio} of secular and spiritual power, and, raising objections, resolves them.[^3] Third, as a main head, it is argued that it is superfluous to posit another power besides [free choice]{g:liberum-arbitrium}; second, that the [power of order]{g:potestas-ordinis}, since at one time it can be [loosed]{g:laxare} and at another restricted, suffices without a [power of rule]{g:potestas-regiminis} or of [jurisdiction]{g:iurisdiccio} added over it.[^4] And third it is argued, [by appeal to]{g:arguitur-per} this, that a creature does not cooperate with God in co-producing an indivisible quantity in the mind; which it confirms in three ways, and it answers that power is taken at one time [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} and at another [aggregatively]{g:aggregative}, with an [authentic]{g:autenticus} human promulgation added over it, and that this [power] must be posited after the fall, when God authenticates [it]; and so there are many powers which would not have been in the state of innocence.[^5] Second, it teaches how [power] can be increased or diminished; and third it teaches what a creature co-effects with God and what not; and, expounding many scriptures, it notes how Christ was silent (John 19), by keeping silent [teaching] many [doctrines]{g:doctrina}.[^6]

## ¶3 · 399.1–399.18

> [p. 399] Capitulum secundum obicit tripliciter et dissolvit, primo suadet tripliciter, quod nemo habet potestatem ad faciendum aliud nisi quod Deus intendit quod faciat, quod concedit declarans sensum, et quomodo potestas talis perditur ad non gradum. Secundo obiciens de potestate faciendi miracula ostendit quid est miraculum, et non potest creature ut talis competere, docet quomodo est de miraculis senciendum, secundo principaliter docet quomodo senciendum est de potestate spiritus sancti et gracie, et tercio diffuse declarat quomodo sacerdos non tollit peccata, sed dum fuerit conformis divino iudicio quodammodo remittit et absolvit hominem a peccato. Quarto declarat potestatem excomunicandi et puniendi subiectos, et quinto tractat de modo committendi potestates, de origine et perdicione earum et que sit alia maior.

The second chapter raises objections in three ways and resolves them. First, it makes the case in three ways that no one has power to do anything other than what God intends that he do; which it grants, declaring the sense, and [it declares] how such power is lost to the point of no degree.[^7] Second, objecting about the power of working miracles, it shows what a miracle is, and [that it] cannot belong to a creature as such, [and] teaches how one should think about miracles. Second, as a main head, it teaches how one should think about the power of the Holy Spirit and of grace; and third it declares at length how a [priest]{g:sacerdos} does not take away sins, but, while he is conformed to the divine judgement, in a certain manner [remits]{g:remittere} and absolves a man from sin. Fourth, it declares the power of excommunicating and punishing subjects; and fifth it treats of the manner of committing powers, of their origin and their loss, and which [power] is greater than another.

## ¶4 · 399.19–399.30

> [p. 399] Capitulum tercium probat via triplici, quod P. habuit primatum inter apostolos primo octuplici testimonio evangelii, et exponit, quomodo sathan expetivit ut cribraret apostolos. Secundo probat illud octuplici testimonio factorum Petri, et tercio octuplici testimonio sanctorum. Quarto vero narrando triplicem fructum huius sentencie declarat quod Petrus propter humilitatem in moribus constitutus est a Christo caput ecclesie, et declarando quomodo Petrus precellebat fide, humilitate et caritate, declarat quomodo Petrus Johanne magis dilexerat.

The third chapter proves in a threefold way that P[eter] had [primacy]{g:primatus} among the apostles: first by an eightfold testimony of the gospel; and it expounds how Satan desired to sift the apostles.[^8] Second, it proves it by an eightfold testimony of Peter's deeds, and third by an eightfold testimony of the saints. And fourth, recounting a threefold fruit of this teaching, it declares that Peter, for his humility in conduct, was appointed by Christ [head]{g:caput} of the [church]{g:ecclesia}; and, declaring how Peter excelled in faith, humility and [charity]{g:caritas}, it declares how Peter had loved more than John.[^9]

## ¶5 · 399.31–401.27

> [p. 399] Capitulum quartum declarat primo quod Petrus non solum accepit illum primatum pro se ipso, sed pro eius vicariis succedentibus in ecclesia militante. Secundo declarat quod, sicut Petrus exhinc fuit per se Christi vicarius, quod suum [p. 400] magistrum quoad actus et mores secutus fuit conformius, sic oportet P. vicarium similius sequi ipsum. Tercio docet, quod humana eleccio non valet, nisi de quanto promulgaverit conformiter ad divinam. Quarto quod precise de tanto debet subditus sequi suum privatum prepositum, de quanto ipse sequitur Ihesum nostrum. Quinto sive electores bene vel male elegerint operibus meritoriis est credendum, et quicumque modus noster eligendi prepositum fuerit eleccioni Dei conformior est eo melior, et obicit quadrupliciter et dissolvit, declarans quod de quatuor modis eligendi prepositum eleccio per sortem foret modo securior. Sexto notat, quod non docetur ex scriptura, quomodo preeminencia Petri super alios apostolos stat in universali iurisdicione super totam militantem ecclesiam, aut quod particularis potestas iurisdicionis debet esse in alios apostolos per illam potestatem ad locum et populum limitata. Capitulum quintum declarat quintupliciter sextam sentenciam, quod Romanus pontifex debet racione officii vicarii sequi Christum in altissima paupertate, et post obicit tripliciter et dissolvit. Primo per hoc quod papa non posset perficere suum officium nec honorifice tenere statum papalem sine tali dominio, et declarat oppositum per Bernhardum. Secundo obicit per hoc, quod multi sancti episcopi approbarunt dotacionem huiusmodi et dissolvit, declarans quantum malum contingit ex isto porismate, cum sequimur mixtim bona secundum racionem qua sunt mala. Tercio obicit per hoc, quod est distinccio inter potestatem iurisdicionis et ordinis, eo quod solus papa habet potestatem super universalem ecclesiam; non enim est aliud proprium pape officium, et dissolvit, concedens distinccionem et negans [p. 401] illam universalem potestatem et obiciendo tripliciter ex domino Ardmacano dissolvit, declarans quod Paulus non habuit a Petro sed a Christo licenciam predicandi, et concludit quod, sicut apostoli erant socii et amici, sic debet esse hodie, cum ditacio non dissolveret amiciciam, eo quod Christus non inveniret amicos inter nostros episcopos conversando et declarat in tribus conclusionibus pastoris officium. Capitulum sextum connectit cum declaracione septimam sentenciam et octavam, quarum prior dicit sic: Quod sicut nullus apostolorum habuit tantam potestatem quantam habuit Christus humanitus, sic nec omnis papa habuit tantam potestatem quantam habuit beatus Petrus vel alius Christi apostolus, et declarando utrumque membrum tripliciter obicit et dissolvit. Octava sentencia dicit quod Romano pontifice vel quocumque alio fingente se gerere primatum vicarie potestatis Christi in terris ab eius semita declinante secundum sensum scripture nullus plenius vel periculosius gerit nomen et opus Antichristi; quod probat ex condicionibus undecim, annectens materiam de quiditate et divisione status, quomodo componitur ex non quantis et, declarans quomodo status potest alterari, tangit quomodo subditus potest prelatum corripere et dissolvit.

The fourth chapter declares, first, that Peter received that primacy not only for himself but for his vicars succeeding [him] in the church militant. Second, it declares that, as Peter was of himself the [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius-christi} on this ground, that he followed his [400] master more conformably in acts and conduct, so P[eter]'s vicar must follow him more closely.[^10] Third, it teaches that human election is of no avail except in so far as it has proclaimed in conformity with the divine [election]. Fourth, that a subject ought to follow his private [superior]{g:prepositi} precisely so far as he follows our Jesus.[^11] Fifth, that, whether the electors have chosen well or ill, credence is to be given to meritorious works; and whichever of our ways of electing a superior is more conformed to God's election is by that the better; and it raises objections in four ways and resolves them, declaring that of the four ways of electing a superior, election by lot would now be the safer.[^12] Sixth, it notes that it is not taught from scripture how Peter's [preeminence]{g:preeminencia} over the other apostles consists in a universal jurisdiction over the whole church militant, or that a particular [power of jurisdiction]{g:potestas-iurisdiccionis} ought to be in the other apostles, [assigned]{g:limitare} by that power to a place and a people. The fifth chapter declares in five ways the sixth teaching, that the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex} ought, by reason of the office of vicar, to follow Christ in the highest poverty; and afterwards it raises objections in three ways and resolves them.[^13] First, [it objects] by appeal to this, that the [pope]{g:papa} could not fulfil his office nor honourably maintain the papal state without such [lordship]{g:dominium}; and it declares the opposite by Bernard.[^14] Second, it objects by appeal to this, that many holy [bishops]{g:episcopus} approved an [endowment]{g:dotacio} of this kind; and it resolves it, declaring how much evil comes from this [porism]{g:porisma}, when we follow mixed goods in the respect in which they are evil.[^15] Third, it objects by appeal to this, that there is a distinction between the power of jurisdiction and [the power] of order, in that the pope alone has power over the universal church; for there is no other office proper to the pope; and it resolves it, granting the distinction and denying [401] that universal power; and, objecting in three ways out of [the lord of Armagh]{g:dominus-ardmacanus}, it resolves [the objections], declaring that Paul had his [licence]{g:licencia} to preach not from Peter but from Christ;[^16] and it [infers]{g:concludere} that, as the apostles were companions and friends, so it ought to be today, since enrichment ought not to dissolve friendship, in that Christ, keeping company among our bishops, would [otherwise] not find friends; and it declares in three [conclusions]{g:conclusio} the office of a shepherd.[^17] The sixth chapter joins together, with a declaration, the seventh teaching and the eighth, of which the former says thus: that, as none of the apostles had as much power as Christ had in his humanity, so neither has every pope had as much power as blessed Peter or another apostle of Christ had; and, declaring each member [of the comparison], it raises objections in three ways and resolves them.[^18] The eighth teaching says that, when the Roman pontiff, or anyone else who feigns that he bears the primacy of Christ's [vicarial power]{g:potestas-vicaria} on earth, turns aside from his path, according to the sense of scripture no one bears more fully or more dangerously the name and work of Antichrist; which it proves from eleven conditions, annexing the matter of the [quiddity]{g:quiditas} and division of [state]{g:status}, how it is composed of things without quantity; and, declaring how a state can be altered, it touches on how a subject can [reprove]{g:corripere} a [prelate]{g:prelatus}, and resolves [the objection].[^19]

## ¶6 · 401.28–402.33

> [p. 401] Capitulum septimum discurrit per illa octo argumenta, quibus probatur ex scriptura quod Petrus debet habere primatum super alios apostolos et per consequens papa super alios episcopos; primo igitur declarat quod primatus iste non infert dominacionem vel preeminenciam secularem, quod probat ex triplici testimonio Bernhardi, Augustini et Crysostomi. Secundo declarat [p. 402] quod papa debet esse capitaneus, precedens in spirituali exercitu pacificando, et declarat quod est clavis ad sensum scripture et sanctorum doctorum. Tercio ostendit quomodo papa debet ambulare super aquas ad Dominum instar Petri, et exponit propheciam Apocalipsis XII de dracone stante ante mulierem parituram. Quarto exponit ad quadruplicem sensum quomodo Christi vicarius debet cum Petro se mittere in mare. Quinto declarat quomodo papa debet vigilare cum Christo, et quod aliter est secundum Crysostomum Antichristus, et quomodo illud Matth. XXIV: Multi venient in nomine meo, dicentes quia ego sum Christus, verificatur de falsis Christi vicariis et quomodo regna istis papis concertantibus racionabiliter responderent, et quomodo pastores ex dicto beati Gregorii periculosius maculant gregem Dei. Sexto declarat prelati periculum ex dictis Lyncolniensis et quod foret remedium, et quomodo CLIII magni pisces signant totam ecclesiam Christi, et quomodo papa niteretur facere similiter. Septimo declaratur superioritas pape per hoc, quod in prima synodo, de qua Act. XV, primo sentenciavit pro libertate ecclesie, in quo sunt tria pape diligencius annotanda, sicut et ex precepcione Christi Matth. XVIII in qua correpcio fraterna singulariter Petro precipitur. Octavo docetur quomodo papa debet preeminenter amare Christi ecclesiam et stare pro privilegio vite exproprietarie quod Christus iniunxerat et describendo vitam exproprietariam probat tripliciter quod nemo habet a Deo potestatem clericos degradandi.

The seventh chapter runs through those eight arguments by which it is proved from scripture that Peter ought to have primacy over the other apostles, and consequently the pope over the other bishops. First, then, it declares that this primacy does not imply [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio} or secular preeminence, which it proves from a threefold testimony, of Bernard, Augustine and Chrysostom.[^20] Second, it declares [402] that the pope ought to be a [captain]{g:capitaneus}, going before in the spiritual army by making peace; and it declares that this is the key to the sense of scripture and of the holy doctors. Third, it shows how the pope ought to walk upon the waters to the Lord after the pattern of Peter; and it expounds the prophecy of Apocalypse 12 of the dragon standing before the woman "who was ready to be delivered".[^21] Fourth, it expounds in a fourfold sense how the vicar of Christ ought with Peter to "cast himself into the sea".[^22] Fifth, it declares how the pope ought to watch with Christ, and that otherwise, according to Chrysostom, he is Antichrist; and how that [word] of Matthew 24, "Many will come in my name saying, I am Christ", is verified of the false vicars of Christ; and how kingdoms would reasonably respond while these popes contend with one another; and how shepherds, from the saying of blessed Gregory, more dangerously stain the flock of God.[^23] Sixth, it declares the danger of a prelate from the sayings of Lincoln, and that there would be a remedy; and how the 153 great fishes signify the whole church of Christ, and how the pope should strive to do likewise.[^24] Seventh, the superiority of the pope is declared by appeal to this, that in the first synod, of which Acts 15 [tells], he first [pronounced]{g:sentenciare} for the liberty of the church, in which there are three things for the pope to note more diligently; as also from the command of Christ in Matthew 18, in which [fraternal correction]{g:correpcio-fraterna} is enjoined on Peter singly.[^25] Eighth, it is taught how the pope ought preeminently to love Christ's church and to stand for the [privilege]{g:privilegium} of the [propertyless]{g:exproprietarie} life which Christ had enjoined; and, describing the propertyless life, it proves in three ways that no one has from God the power of degrading [clerics]{g:clericus}.

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

## Notes

[^1]: **C/G.** *Sentencia*: "teaching", the position taught (CONTEXTUAL; not a judicial sentence). *Libri ultimi*: "of the last book", presumably this treatise as the last of a series in which it was copied; the heading Loserth prints is *Sentencia libri in compendio*. What follows is a medieval summary, chapter by chapter, in the third person ("it declares", the chapter being the subject).
[^2]: **C.** *Potencia* is CONTEXTUAL: here the Aristotelian potency of a rational nature, so "potency"; elsewhere "power". The clause itself says that scripture teaches no *potestas* beyond that *potencia*, and the treatise uses the two words interchangeably.
[^3]: **S.** Hugh of St Victor's division of power into secular and spiritual, *De sacramentis* II.2.4 (PL 176, 418A–B), which the treatise quotes in ch. 1, p. 7 (LEDGER P01b-6; P13a-1). *Obiciendo dissolvit*: literally "by objecting resolves", the compendium's recurring formula for raising objections and answering them.
[^4]: **C.** *Principaliter*: "as a main head", marking the chapter's principal divisions as against the subordinate counts (*secundo*, *tercio*) within them; so also at 399.10. *Nunc … nunc*: "at one time … at another".
[^5]: **C.** *Quo tripliciter confirmat*: the relative *quo* has no fitting antecedent; we take it as "which [argument] it confirms in three ways" (as if *quod*). *Superaddita* agrees with *potestas*: power taken "aggregatively" is the natural power together with an authentic human promulgation added over it.
[^6]: **S/C.** John 19:9 (DR "But Jesus gave him no answer"), Christ's silence before Pilate. *Tacendo doctrinas multiplices*: we supply "teaching"; the gerund may also mean "by keeping silent about many doctrines".
[^7]: **C.** *Suadet*: "makes the case" (persuades), not "proves". *Perditur ad non gradum*: "lost to the point of no degree", i.e. lost wholly.
[^8]: **S/T.** Luke 22:31 (DR "Satan hath desired to have you, that he may sift you as wheat"). The manuscripts read *et expetivit* (Loserth's apparatus, 399.22); the print drops *et*. At 399.29 A writes *P.* where the print has *Petrus*.
[^9]: **S/C.** John 21:15 (DR "lovest thou me more than these?"). *Johanne* is an ablative of comparison: Peter loved [Christ] more than John did. *Constitutus est … caput ecclesie*: the compendium's words for the treatise's ch. 3; they are translated as they stand.
[^10]: **C.** *Exhinc … quod*: "on this ground, that". *P. vicarium*: "Peter's vicar". *Sequi ipsum*: "follow him", most likely Christ (the *magistrum* just named), possibly Peter.
[^11]: **C.** *Privatum prepositum*: one's own particular superior; *privatus* may also carry the sense of *religio privata*, a superior of a private religion.
[^12]: **T.** The print's *persortem foretmodo* is read *per sortem foret modo*, "by lot would now be". *Securior*: "safer".
[^13]: **C.** *Sextam sentenciam*: the sixth of the numbered teachings the treatise sets out (the sixth is the one just stated for ch. 4; the seventh and eighth follow in ch. 6).
[^14]: **S.** Bernard, *De consideratione* IV.3.6 (PL 182, 776A: *In his successisti, non Petro, sed Constantino*), quoted in the treatise's ch. 5 at 86–87 (LEDGER P05c-1; P13a-2).
[^15]: **C.** *Mixtim bona*: goods mixed [with evil]; *secundum racionem qua sunt mala*, "in the respect in which they are evil", i.e. following the endowing saints in what was evil in their act.
[^16]: **S.** Richard FitzRalph, *Summa in quaestionibus Armenorum* VII.6–7 (Paris 1512, scan p. 100); the compendium names no book. The three are those answered in the treatise's ch. 5 (96.21–99): that someone must rule as head, that much was said to Peter alone (Matt. 16:19), and that Paul had his charge of the gentiles from Peter (Gal. 2:9; FitzRalph VII.7: *paulus gentem certam in suam curam … de voluntate petri accepit*) (LEDGER P13a-3). On Paul's commission, cf. Gal. 1:1 (DR "not of men, neither by man, but by Jesus Christ").
[^17]: **C.** *Cum ditacio non dissolveret amiciciam*: we take the subjunctive as "ought not to dissolve", and *eo quod … non inveniret* as the reason: else Christ, keeping company among our bishops, would find no friends. If the *non* of *non dissolveret* is intrusive, the sense is "since enrichment would dissolve friendship", which suits *eo quod* more easily.
[^18]: **C.** *Nec omnis papa*: "neither has every pope", i.e. not every pope, not "no pope". *Membrum*: each limb of the comparison, not a member of the church.
[^19]: **C/G.** The ablatives *Romano pontifice … declinante* are absolute: "when the Roman pontiff … turns aside". *Quiditas*: "quiddity", what a thing is. *Status* is the state of a man (of disposition, of virtue) that ch. 6 analyses; *ex non quantis*, "of things without quantity".
[^20]: **S.** Bernard, *De consideratione* II.6.10–13 and III.1.2 (PL 182, 748–49, 759), and Augustine, *Sermo* 67.7 (PL 38, 436), in the treatise's reply to the first argument (ch. 7, 135–138); Chrysostom, hom. 5 on 1 Timothy, follows in the reply to the second (139.31) (LEDGER P07a-3, P07a-4, P07a-7, P07b-3; P13a-4). The print's semicolon after *Bernhardi* is read as a comma.
[^21]: **S.** Matt. 14:29 (DR "walked upon the water to come to Jesus"); Apoc. 12:4 (DR "the dragon stood before the woman who was ready to be delivered").
[^22]: **S.** John 21:7 (DR "cast himself into the sea").
[^23]: **S/C/R.** Matt. 26:38 (DR "watch with me"). Matt. 24:5: Vulgate *Multi enim venient … dicentes: Ego sum Christus*; Wyclif lacks *enim* and has recitative *quia*. Chrysostom, hom. 8 on 2 Tim. 3:1, cited in the treatise's ch. 7 at 148 (LEDGER P07e-1; P13a-5); Gregory, *Regula pastoralis* I.2 (PL 77, 15D–16B), cited in the treatise's ch. 7 at 151 (LEDGER P07e-7; P13a-6). *Istis papis concertantibus* is absolute, "while these popes contend": the rival popes of the schism of 1378, Urban VI at Rome and Clement VII at Avignon, as in the treatise's ch. 7 (149: *nunc papa sit Rome, nunc quod papa sit Avinione*; LEDGER P07e-3, P08f-1; P13a-7).
[^24]: **S/C.** Lincoln: Robert Grosseteste, letter to Hugh of Pateshull (ed. Luard, p. 99), quoted in the treatise's ch. 7 at 152 (LEDGER P07f-2; P13a-8). John 21:11 (DR "great fishes, one hundred and fifty-three"). *Quod foret remedium*: "that there would be a remedy"; *quid*, "what the remedy would be", is possible. *Facere similiter*: as Peter drew the net to land.
[^25]: **S/C.** Acts 15:7. The subject of *sentenciavit* is Peter, unexpressed. Matt. 18:15–22: the command to reprove a brother, and Peter's question on forgiving (18:21).
