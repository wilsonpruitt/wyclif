## For the coordinator to decide

1. **Wyclif's catena in 06-c ¶4 is borrowed.** He took Damascene cc. 50 and 92, *creatus et increatus*, and the *De sermone Domini* line from Scotus, *Ord.* III d. 11 q. 1 n. 6. Scotus is there reporting an opinion that Wadding gives to William of Ware, with the same authorities and Jerome on Eph. 2:10. He took "Leo, *nova et inaudita conventio*" from Bonaventure's d. 11 a. 2 q. 1 arg. 1, where the words are really Ps.-Augustine, *Sermo* 128. Wyclif's misattributions (Leo, *De sermone Domini*) are inherited from them. This may deserve a convention or introduction note, alongside the "Damascene numbers via Scotus" note from RESULTS-4.
2. **97.15 (06-d ¶2): the translation may need changing.** The print is sound against Thomas. But "Nothing proper to God is stated of Christ except by reason of the humanity" reads wrongly in English. Thomas's sense is that nothing proper to the [divine] person is said of Christ, by reason of the humanity, except with the qualification expressed or understood. You could render it that way, or gloss it in n4. The *nec/et e contrario* choice is unaffected: Thomas has no converse clause.
3. **Wyclif's *Enchiridion* numbering is not a constant offset.** His "29" is PL c. 38. His "4" fits PL c. 4–5 only by subject. Do not try to correct his chapter numbers mechanically.
4. **I06d-1:** Wyclif ascribes to the Subtle Doctor an opinion Scotus reports with *aliter dicitur*, followed by the replies for one who dislikes it. Wyclif's "fingit postmodum responsiones" describes that structure exactly, so no change to the English is needed. The n1 replacement above says so.
5. **Bede (I06c-4)** confirms the C O addition at 95.27 (*sed factum ex muliere*), which the English already follows.
