# *De eucharistia*, chapter 1, part 1: the three things in the sacrament, and the objections of the pagans and of our own

## ¶1 · 11.2–11.16

> [p. 11] Tractando de Eukaristia oportet premittere quedam communia plus famosa et primo utrum sacramentum altaris sit realiter corpus Christi. In qua materia dixi sepe populo quod in sacramento altaris est tria considerare, scilicet nudum sacramentum sic quod non rem sacramenti ut hostiam consecratam; secundo sacramentum et rem sacramenti ut verum corpus Christi et sanguinem; et tercio rem sacramenti et non sacramentum ut unionem Christi cum corpore suo mistico quod est ecclesia; hoc enim nusquam est sensibile et per consequens non est alicubi sacramentum. Per hanc fidem tolluntur instancie paganorum. Arguunt enim quod sus, canis vel mus potest comedere Deum nostrum, quia corpus Christi quod est Deus.

In treating of the Eucharist we must first set out certain common [points] that are better known, and first whether [the sacrament of the altar]{g:sacramentum-altaris} is [really]{g:realiter} the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}. In this matter I have often told the people that in the sacrament of the altar there are three things to consider: namely, the bare sacrament, in such a way that [it is] not the [reality of the sacrament]{g:res-sacramenti}, as the [consecrated host]{g:hostia-consecrata}; second, the sacrament and the reality of the sacrament, as the true body and blood of Christ; and third, the reality of the sacrament and not the sacrament, as the union of Christ with his [mystical body]{g:corpus-christi-misticum}, which is the [church]{g:ecclesia}; for this is nowhere [perceptible]{g:sensibilis}, and consequently it is not anywhere a sacrament.[^1] By this faith the [objections]{g:instancia} of the pagans are [disposed of]{g:tollere}. For they argue that a pig, a dog or a mouse can eat our God, because [it can eat] the body of Christ, which is God.[^2]

## ¶2 · 11.17–12.2

> [p. 11] Sed respondemus eis iuxta fidem predictam quod infideliter falsum assumunt, cum tales bestie possunt comedere hostiam consecratam que est nudum sacramentum et non corpus Christi vel sanguis. Ymmo sicut leo comedens corpus hominis non comedit eius animam, licet sit ad omnem punctum illius corporis, sic intelligendum est de corpore Christi in sacramento altaris. Ipsum enim est totum sacramentaliter, spiritualiter [p. 12] vel virtualiter ad omnem punctum hostie consecrate, sicut anima est in corpore.

But we reply to them, according to the faith stated before, that they faithlessly assume a falsehood, since such beasts can eat the consecrated host, which is the bare sacrament and not the body or blood of Christ.[^3] Indeed, just as a lion eating the body of a man does not eat his soul, although it is at every point of that body, so it is to be understood of the body of Christ in the sacrament of the altar. For it is whole, [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter}, [spiritually]{g:spiritualiter} [12] or [virtually]{g:virtualiter}, at every point of the consecrated host, as the soul is in the body.[^4]

## ¶3 · 12.3–12.6

> [p. 12] Secundo obiciunt per hoc quod nos sacerdotes frangimus corpus Christi et per idem caput, collum, brachia et singula eius membra; quod foret maxime horribile facere Deo nostro.

Second, they object on the ground that we [priests]{g:sacerdos} break the body of Christ, and by the same token his head, neck, arms and each of his members; which would be most horrible to do to our God.

## ¶4 · 12.7–12.17

> [p. 12] Sed nos respondemus iuxta fidem priorem quod infideliter falsum assumunt, cum frangimus sacramentum vel hostiam consecratam, non autem corpus Christi, cum distinguuntur, sicut non frangimus radium solis, licet frangamus vitrum vel lapidem cristallinum. Et hec videtur sentencia cantus ecclesie quo canitur: Fracto demum sacramento Non vacilles sed memento Tantum esse sub fragmento Quantum toto tegitur.

But we reply, according to the former faith, that they faithlessly assume a falsehood, since we break the sacrament or consecrated host, but not the body of Christ, since they are distinguished; just as we do not break a ray of the sun, although we break the glass or the crystal stone. And this seems to be the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of the church's chant, in which it is sung: "When the sacrament is broken at last, do not waver, but remember that there is as much beneath a fragment as is covered in the whole."[^5]

## ¶5 · 12.18–12.24

> [p. 12] Cum ergo negare non possumus quin sacramentum frangatur, ut docet usus ecclesie, et sensus forent aliter illusi ex sophismate veritatis, et corpus Christi non frangitur, manifestum est quod illud sacramentum quod frangitur, non est corpus Christi, quia aliter querenti quid frangitur minus vere diceretur quod corpus Christi: quid enim querit de substancia rei.

Since, therefore, we cannot deny that the sacrament is broken, as the use of the church teaches, and the senses would otherwise be deceived by a [sophism]{g:sophisma} of the truth, and the body of Christ is not broken, it is manifest that that sacrament which is broken is not the body of Christ; for otherwise, to one asking what is broken, it would be said less [truly]{g:vere} that [it is] the body of Christ: for "what" asks about the [substance]{g:substancia} of the thing.[^6]

## ¶6 · 12.25–12.29

> [p. 12] Tercio obiciunt per hoc quod nisi corpus Christi foret hostia consecrata, nos non videremus corpus Christi nec comederemus, sicut nec dente conterimus, et per idem non assumeremus; quod foret inconveniens christianis.

Third, they object on the ground that unless the body of Christ were the consecrated host, we would not see the body of Christ nor eat it, just as we do not grind it with the tooth either, and by the same token we would not receive it; which would be an [absurdity]{g:inconveniens} for Christians.[^7]

## ¶7 · 13.1–13.17

> [p. 13] Sed hic respondemus per distinccionem quod est dare duplicem visionem, duplicem esum et duplicem sumpcionem, scilicet corporalem et spiritualem; et sic concedimus quod non videmus in sacramento illo corpus Christi oculo corporali sed oculo mentali, scilicet fide per speculum in enigmate. Et sicut ymago est integra ad omnem punctum speculi, sic quod videri potest vel secundum partem vel totum ab aliquo oculo corporali alicubi posito: sic intelligendum est in parte de corpore Christi in hostia consecrata ut speculo. Et eodem modo dicitur quod non tangimus vel capimus corpus Christi tactu corporeo, sicut nec corporaliter ipsum comedimus. Et iste est sensus cantus ecclesie quo dicitur: Quod non capis, quod non vides Animosa firmat fides Preter rerum ordinem.

But here we reply by a distinction: that [one may posit]{g:est-dare} a twofold vision, a twofold [eating]{g:esus} and a twofold [reception]{g:sumpcio}, namely bodily and spiritual; and so we grant that we do not see the body of Christ in that sacrament with the bodily eye but with the eye of the mind, namely by faith, "through a glass in a dark manner".[^8] And just as an image is whole at every point of a mirror, so that it can be seen either in part or whole by some bodily eye placed somewhere, so it is to be understood in part of the body of Christ in the consecrated host, as in a mirror.[^9] And in the same way the answer is that we do not touch or take the body of Christ by bodily touch, just as we do not eat it [bodily]{g:corporaliter} either. And this is the sense of the church's chant, in which it is said: "What you do not take, what you do not see, a spirited faith confirms, beyond the order of things."[^10]

## ¶8 · 13.18–13.24

> [p. 13] Nec conterimus corpus Christi dentibus sed spiritualiter integrum ipsum accipimus. Et sic intelligimus istum cantum quo dicimus: Nulla rei fit scissura, Signi tantum fit fractura Qua nec status nec statura Signati minuitur.

Nor do we grind the body of Christ with the teeth, but spiritually we receive it whole. And so we understand this chant, in which we say: "No rending of the thing is made; a [breaking]{g:fractio} of the [sign]{g:signum} alone is made, by which neither the state nor the stature of the [thing signified]{g:signatum} is lessened."[^11]

## ¶9 · 13.25–14.15

> [p. 13] Sed hic obiciunt quidam de nostris quod ista non sunt dicenda laycis qui nec ipsa concipiunt nec observant, cum ex istis fidem priorem amitterent. Sed nichil obiecto isto insanius; nam tam layci quam clerici nimis multi sunt tantum infideles in ista materia quod credunt paganis deterius quod hostia [p. 14] illa consecrata sit Deus illorum; et tunc indubie concludunt predicta argumenta paganica. Ille ergo qui non concipit ista male conciperet fidem de Trinitate vel incarnacione. Nec est fides predicta layca fides qua placeretur domino veritatis, sed infidelitas pessima quia ydolatria qua creatura valde abiecta non assumpta coleretur infideliter tamquam Deus; et sic secundum istam stulticiam nunquam doceretur vel argueretur aliquis error in fide, sed ne deterius contingat christianus valde disciplinabilis in infidelitate sordesceret, ac si diceretur quod spina in pede, telum in carne vel quecunque venenosa causa morbi in corpore non debet extrahi, ne forte contingat deterius. Episcoporum itaque est destruere has hereses, quia aliter ipsi forent heretici ex consensu.

But here certain of our own object that these things are not to be said to lay people, who neither conceive them nor observe them, since from them they would lose their former faith. But nothing is more insane than this objection; for laymen as well as clerics, [exceedingly]{g:nimis} many, are so unbelieving in this matter that they believe, worse than the pagans, "[14] that consecrated host is their God"; and then the pagan arguments mentioned before undoubtedly [conclude]{g:concludere}.[^12] He, therefore, who does not conceive these things would ill conceive the faith concerning the Trinity or the incarnation. Nor is the aforesaid lay faith a faith by which the Lord of truth would be pleased, but the worst [unbelief]{g:infidelitas}, because [it is] idolatry, by which a very abject [creature]{g:creatura}, not [assumed]{g:assumere}, would be worshipped unbelievingly as God;[^13] and so, according to this folly, no error in the faith would ever be taught or reproved, but, lest something worse should happen, a very [teachable]{g:disciplinabilis} Christian would grow foul in unbelief; as if it were said that a thorn in the foot, a dart in the flesh, or any poisonous cause of disease in the body ought not to be drawn out, lest perhaps something worse should happen.[^14] It belongs to the [bishops]{g:episcopus}, therefore, to destroy these heresies, because otherwise they themselves would be heretics by consent.

## ¶10 · 14.16–14.18

> [p. 14] Secundo obiciunt per hoc quod promulgata laicis ista sentencia vel vilesceret vel saltem minoraretur honor et devocio populi ad hoc venerabile sacramentum.

Second, they object on the ground that, if this teaching were promulgated to the laity, the honour and devotion of the people toward this venerable sacrament would either grow cheap or at least be lessened.

## ¶11 · 14.19–15.7

> [p. 14] Sed ista ficticia currit sub eadem perfidia. Nam Apostolus dicit Rom. III°, 8: Absit ut faciamus mala ut eveniant bona, ergo multo magis fidelis diceret: Absit ut committamus ydolatriam, ut ex falsa et infideli devocione populus amplius seducatur, quia revera paulo minus evidenter crederetur a populo, quod calix in quo est sanguis Christi et lignum per quod colitur crucifixus sunt realiter Deus noster. Sicut enim viso calice in tantum cultum prorumpimus sicut visa hostia consecrata, non propter ipsum calicem consecratum a pontifice sed propter sacramentum eximium in vase absconditum: sic visa [p. 15] hostia debemus credere quod ipsa non sit corpus Christi, sed ipsum corpus Christi est sacramentaliter in ipsa absconditum. Et iste sensus ecclesie est quem canit: Sub diversis speciebus, Signis tantum et non rebus, Latent res eximie.

But this [fiction]{g:ficticia} runs under the same perfidy. For the Apostle says, Rom. 3:8: "God forbid that we should do evil that good may come"; therefore much more would a faithful man say: "God forbid that we commit idolatry, so that the people may be the further seduced by a false and unbelieving devotion";[^15] for [in truth]{g:revera} it would be believed by the people only a little less evidently that "the [chalice]{g:calix} in which is the blood of Christ, and the wood through which the crucified is worshipped, are really our God". For just as, at the sight of the chalice, we break out into as great worship as at the sight of the consecrated host, not on account of the chalice itself, consecrated by a [pontiff]{g:pontifex}, but on account of the excellent sacrament hidden in the vessel: so at the sight of the [15] host we ought to believe that it is not the body of Christ, but that the body of Christ itself is sacramentally hidden in it.[^16] And this is the sense of the church which it sings: "Under diverse [species]{g:species}, signs only and not things, excellent things lie hidden."

## ¶12 · 15.8–15.17

> [p. 15] Et patet quod destructo errore ydolatrie plus et purius coleretur Deus quam modo colitur, quia credendum est quod Christus in sacramentis illis presens absconditur. Quare ergo non coleremus Christum visa hostia, sicut facimus viso calice et sicut fideles devocius colunt maiestatem divinam visa qualibet creatura? Detecta ergo ista sentencia foret cultus verus et debitus placens Deo, ymmo iam est cultus mendacii abhominabilis Deo vero. Et ista est vera sentencia licet displiceat pontificibus ydolorum.

And it is clear that, once the error of idolatry is destroyed, God would be worshipped more, and more purely, than he is worshipped now, because it is to be believed that Christ is hidden, present, in those sacraments. Why, then, should we not worship Christ at the sight of the host, as we do at the sight of the chalice, and as the faithful more devoutly worship the divine majesty at the sight of any creature whatever? Once this teaching, therefore, were uncovered, there would be true and due worship pleasing to God; rather, there is now a worship of falsehood, abominable to the true God.[^17] And this is the true teaching, although it displeases the pontiffs of idols.

## ¶13 · 15.18–15.26

> [p. 15] Tercio obiciunt per hoc quod sic vilesceret sacerdotalis potestas cum non habet potestatem consecrandi vel faciendi corpus Christi vel sanguinem, ymmo videtur quod sicut laycus videndo quamlibet creaturam videt in fide deitatem que est plus quam corpus Christi, sic videndo quamlibet creaturam fideliter sufficienter accipit corpus Christi. Sed quis tunc audiret missam, missantes care conduceret vel acciperet more ecclesie sacramentum?

Third, they object on the ground that the priestly [power]{g:potestas} would thus grow cheap, since it does not have the power of consecrating or making the body or blood of Christ; indeed, it seems that, just as a layman in seeing any creature whatever sees in faith the [Godhead]{g:deitas}, which is more than the body of Christ, so in seeing any creature whatever faithfully he sufficiently receives the body of Christ. But who then would hear mass, hire [mass-priests]{g:missantes} dearly, or receive the sacrament in the church's manner?[^18]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 11.2: Three things to be considered in the Sacrament: 1. The Sacrament or symbol: the host;
- 11.7: 2. the Sacrament and res sacramenti: Christ's body; 3. the res sacramenti: the union of Christ with the Church.
- 11.16: A beast may eat the host but does not eat the body.
- 12.3: Nor does the priest break Christ's body.
- 12.25: We must not confuse spiritual and bodily reception.
- 13.25: These truths may be preached to laymen to save them from heathen errors.
- 14.16: Such preaching is said to lessen the honour paid to the Sacrament.
- 14.19: We must not commit idolatry under pretence of increasing devotion.
- 15.8: We should recognize that Christ is hidden in the Sacrament.
- 15.18: This does not really lessen the priestly power.

## Notes

[^1]: **S/C.** *Dixi sepe populo*: Wyclif's own preaching. Loserth compares *Trialogus* IV.2, p. 248 (LEDGER K01a-1). The three members (sacrament only; sacrament and reality; reality only) are the schoolmen's division, from the Lombard, *Sent.* IV d. 8 c. 4 (LEDGER K01a-2). *Hoc* is neuter: it takes up the third member, not *unio* (feminine). ABE read *et verum* (11.8), ABCE *et unionem* (11.10).

[^2]: **C.** *Quia corpus Christi quod est Deus*: the clause has no verb (the plate confirms the stop after *Christi*); "[it can eat]" is supplied from *comedere*. It may be the objectors' compressed syllogism or a word lost at the paragraph's end.

[^3]: **C.** *Infideliter falsum assumunt*: they take a false premiss (that the host is the body of Christ), "faithlessly", i.e. against the faith just set out. *Cum* is causal.

[^4]: **C.** *Ipsum* is neuter, the body of Christ. The three mode adverbs are kept apart, joined as the Latin joins them (*sacramentaliter, spiritualiter vel virtualiter*); *totum*, "whole", is the predicate. CE read *ideo sicut* (11.20–21).

[^5]: **S/C.** The sequence *Lauda Sion* of Thomas Aquinas (Loserth: Daniel, *Thes. hymnol.* II, 98) (LEDGER K01a-3); the stanzas quoted at 13.15–17, 13.22–24 and 15.5–7 are from the same sequence (the stanzas *Quod non capis*, *Nulla rei fit scissura* and *Sub diversis speciebus*) (LEDGER K01a-4). *Sentencia* (CONTEXTUAL): "teaching", what the chant teaches; "sense" (the meaning of its words) is the alternative, and Wyclif says *sensus cantus* at 13.15. AE read *fragmentis*, B *quanto* (12.16–17).

[^6]: **C.** *Ex sophismate veritatis*: the senses would be deceived by a sophism made by the truth itself, if what they see broken were not broken; the construal is ours. *Minus vere*: "less truly", which may be the medieval litotes for "not truly". *Quid* is the question-word "what", which asks the substance. C has *manifestum — frangitur* in the margin in another hand (12.21–22).

[^7]: **C.** *Inconveniens* (CONTEXTUAL): an absurdity in argument. *Assumeremus*: "receive", the *sumpcio* of 13.2, not the *assumere* of the incarnation. D reads *assumendum* (12.28).

[^8]: **S/R.** 1 Cor. 13:12. Loserth compares Hus, *De corpore Christi*, *Opp.* I, fol. 174 (13.1) (LEDGER K01a-5). D reads *per divisionem* (13.1), *oculo carnali* (13.5).

[^9]: **C.** *Speculum* is the same word as the "glass" of 1 Cor. 13:12 just quoted: a mirror. *In parte*: the likeness holds only in part.

[^10]: **C.** *Capis* is rendered "take", as *capimus* just before; in the hymn it also bears "grasp". AE read *animosis* (13.16).

[^11]: **C.** *Res* / *signum* / *signatum*: thing / sign / thing signified. *Rei* is the thing itself (the body), kept apart from *res sacramenti*.

[^12]: **C/R.** *Credunt paganis deterius*: "worse than the pagans" (ablative of comparison); the proposition under discussion is kept as quoted. *Concludunt*: the pagan arguments of 11.14–16 conclude validly once the host is held to be God. E omits *nimis* (13.29); ABC omit *illa* (14.1).

[^13]: **C.** *Nec est fides predicta layca fides*: or "nor is the aforesaid faith a lay faith by which …". *Non assumpta*: not assumed, as Christ's humanity was assumed by the Word. D reads *qua idolatria* and *valide* (14.6).

[^14]: **C.** *Doceretur vel argueretur*: taught [to be an error] or reproved. B reads *christianis valde disciplinatis* (14.10), AB *spina in fide* (14.11).

[^15]: **S.** Rom. 3:8. Wyclif's *Absit ut faciamus mala ut eveniant bona* departs from the Vulgate *et non … faciamus mala ut veniant bona* (Douay–Rheims: "let us do evil that there may come good"), as Loserth notes; ABCD read *Rom. VI* (14.20).

[^16]: **C.** *A pontifice*: "by a pontiff", the bishop who consecrates the chalice; "pontiff" is kept for *pontificibus ydolorum* (15.17). *Viso calice … visa hostia*: "at the sight of". C has *Christi est* in the margin (15.2).

[^17]: **C.** *Ymmo iam est*: "rather, there is now": the present worship set against the true worship that would follow. D reads *quod iam* (15.15).

[^18]: **C.** *Missantes*: those who say mass; *care conducere*: to hire at a dear price (ABC *caris*, 15.25). The reply to this third objection follows at 15.27.
