# *De eucharistia*, chapter 4, part 5: the third objection: lay consecration, the force of the words, and the matter

## ¶1 · 98.15–99.22

> [p. 98] Sed tercio obicitur per hoc quod nedum laycus potest conficere, sed sacramentum posset confici sola mente, vel non est confeccio et per consequens eque in fermento ut azimis, et sic eque in aliis liquoribus ut in vino; ymmo cum omne vinum nostrum sit mixtum aqua, non oportuit inmiscere aquam sed simul benedicere aquam et vinum. Et videtur ulterius quod corpus Christi movetur de celo ad loca volita secundum arbitrium sacerdotis. Quantum ad istud, patet quod aliud est querere de posse quod se extendit ad potenciam Dei absolutam et aliud est secundum testimonium scripture querere de licere. Nullus (inquam) fidelis dubitat quin Deus posset dare layco potenciam conficiendi, sicut laycus cum possit esse sacerdos (ut dicunt loyci) possit conficere. Ymmo videtur iuxta testimonium Augustini, Crisostomi et aliorum sanctorum quod omnis predestinatus laycus est sacerdos, et multo magis devotus laycus conficiens, [p. 99] cum daret ecclesie sacrum ministerium, haberet racionem sacerdotis. Verumptamen sicut ecclesia racionabiliter variavit in verbis confeccionis ab omnibus hiis quatuor evangelistis servando sentenciam, quod patet ex fide scripture debere fieri, sic racionabiliter ordinavit quod soli sacerdotes propter religiositatem et dignitatem in moribus hoc sacramentum conficerent. Levis autem fundacio valet ad hoc quod Christus dedit singulariter illud officium suis apostolis, nec occurrit mihi efficax testimonium quod apostoli gerebant in hoc typum universalis ecclesie sed singulariter sacerdotum; nec oportet propter necessitatem ministerium accidencium huius hostie plus extendi, cum stat fidelem laycum laborando qui nunquam vidit hostiam accipere presbitero efficacius corpus Christi, cum secundum Augustinum super Johannem Omelia XXV accipere corpus Christi sit pie in ipsum credere: Ut quid, inquit, paras dentes et ventrem? crede et manducasti. Oportet enim quod sit spiritualis manducacio, ut dictum est, que consistit in pia consideracione et grata, quomodo Christus passus est pro humano genere.

But, third, it is objected, by appeal to this: that not only can a layman [consecrate]{g:conficere}, but the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} could be consecrated by the mind alone, or else there is no [consecration]{g:confeccio}; and consequently [it could be made] equally in leaven as in unleavened [bread], and so equally in other liquids as in [wine]{g:vinum}; indeed, since all our wine is mixed with water, there was no need to mingle water in, but only to bless water and wine together.[^1] And it seems, further, that [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} is moved from heaven to the places willed, at the [priest]{g:sacerdos}'s discretion. As to this, it is clear that it is one thing to ask about what can be, which extends to God's [absolute power]{g:potencia-absoluta}, and another to ask, according to the testimony of scripture, about what is lawful.[^2] No believer, I say, doubts that God could give a layman the power of consecrating, just as a layman, since he can be a priest, can (as the logicians say) consecrate.[^3] Indeed, it seems, by the testimony of Augustine, Chrysostom and other [saints]{g:sancti}, that every [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} layman is a priest; and much more would a devout layman consecrating, [99] since he would be giving the [church]{g:ecclesia} a sacred ministry, have the account of a priest.[^4] Nevertheless, just as the church reasonably varied, in the words of consecration, from all these four evangelists while keeping the [sense]{g:sentencia} (which it is clear [from the faith of scripture]{g:ex-fide} ought to be done), so it reasonably ordained that priests alone, for their [religiousness]{g:religiositas} and dignity in conduct, should consecrate this sacrament.[^5] And a light foundation serves for this: that Christ gave that office to his apostles [alone]{g:singulariter}; nor does any effective testimony occur to me that the apostles bore in this the type of the universal church, but only of priests.[^6] Nor is there need, on account of necessity, for the ministry of the [accidents]{g:accidens} of this [host]{g:hostia} to be extended further, since it can stand that a faithful layman, labouring, who has never seen the host, receives the body of Christ more effectively than a [presbyter]{g:presbiter}; since according to Augustine on John, homily 25, to receive the body of Christ is to believe in him [piously]{g:pie}: "Why," he says, "do you make ready teeth and belly? Believe, and you have eaten."[^7] For it must be a spiritual [eating]{g:manducacio}, as has been said, which consists in a pious and grateful consideration of how Christ suffered for the human race.

## ¶2 · 99.23–100.10

> [p. 99] Sed graviter ferunt plurimi de nimis levi efficacia verborum sacramenti, cum illis nihil conficitur vel mutatur, quod est contra decreta sanctorum et specialiter beati Ambrosii in libro suo De Officiis et ponitur in De Consecracione, distinccione II, Forte dicis, et in libro suo De Sacramentis, ut consequenter ponitur eadem distinccione, capitulo Panis est, ubi dicit quod de pane fit caro Christi; quod probat per [p. 100] hoc quod verbum Christi suffecit mutare aliquid a puro non esse ad existenciam naturalem, ut patet de creacione Genes. I°; ergo per locum a maiori illud verbum potest facere, ut quod erat panis ante consecracionem sit corpus Christi per consecracionem; et idem confirmat doctor per exempla scripture, ut patet eadem distinccione, capitulo sequente Revera, ubi probat conversionem illam esse possibilem per locum a maiori per illa que fiebant a prophetis in veteri testamento.

But a great many take it hard that the efficacy of the words of the sacrament is made [exceedingly]{g:nimis} light, since by them nothing is consecrated or changed; which is against the [decrees]{g:decretum} of the saints, and especially of blessed Ambrose in his book *On Duties*, as is set down in *De consecratione*, distinction 2, *Forte dicis*, and in his book *On the Sacraments*, as is set down next in the same distinction, chapter *Panis est*, where he says that from the bread the flesh of Christ is made.[^8] This he proves by [100] this, that the word of Christ sufficed to change something from pure not-[being]{g:esse} to natural existence, as is clear of the creation, Genesis 1; therefore, by [the topic from the greater]{g:locus-a-maiori}, that word can bring it about that what was [bread]{g:panis} before the [consecration]{g:consecracio} is the body of Christ by the consecration.[^9] And the doctor confirms the same by examples of scripture, as is clear in the same distinction, the following chapter, *Revera*, where he proves that [conversion]{g:conversio} to be possible by the topic from the greater, through the things that were done by the prophets in the old testament.[^10]

## ¶3 · 100.11–101.7

> [p. 100] Sed quantum ad omnia ista, patet quod iste sanctus non intendit dicere quod panis iste fiet ydemptice corpus Christi, sed quod fiet eius figura efficax modo dicto; et ad hoc vadunt tam argumenta quam sentencia. Et sic conversio illa non destruit naturam panis nec mutat naturam corporis, inducendo in materiam aliam quidditatem, sed facit presenciam corporis Christi et tollit principalitatem panis, ut in corpore Christi colligatur tota intencio adorantis; et istam conversionem probat sanctus per locum a maiori possibilem, et de ista mirabili et miraculosa conversione debet presbiter contentari. Et patet quod falsum assumitur, cum panis mutetur insensibiliter, ut figuret efficaciter corpus Christi; quod sancti intelligunt per hoc quod panis fit corpus Christi et quod post consecracionem panis est corpus Christi. Et qui ignorat istam predicacionem tropicam vel figurativam nedum male intelliget sanctos doctores et decreta ecclesie sed fidem scripture. Nam Christus dicit Joh. XV°, 5: Ego sum vitis vera, et Joh. XI°, 14, [p. 101] ac XIV°, 6: Ego sum pastor bonus. Ego sum via, etc., sicut eciam Luc. XXI°, 33 dicit quod celum et terra transibunt, sed verba sua non transibunt; ubi non dubium intelligit per verba que sunt signa inconstantissima signatam sentenciam; et isto tropo sepe utitur, sicut post eum utuntur sancti doctores et leges ecclesie.

But as to all these things, it is clear that this saint does not mean to say that this bread will become [identically]{g:ydemptice} the body of Christ, but that it will become its effective [figure]{g:figura} in the way stated; and to this go both the arguments and the teaching.[^11] And so that conversion does not destroy the [nature]{g:natura} of the bread nor change the nature of the body by bringing another [quiddity]{g:quiditas} into the matter, but it makes the presence of the body of Christ and takes away the [principality]{g:principalitas} of the bread, so that the whole intention of the worshipper is gathered in the body of Christ; and this conversion the saint proves possible by the topic from the greater, and with this wonderful and miraculous conversion the presbyter ought to be content. And it is clear that a falsehood is assumed, since the bread is changed [imperceptibly]{g:insensibiliter}, so as to [figure]{g:figurare} the body of Christ effectively; which the saints understand by [saying] that the bread becomes the body of Christ and that after the consecration the bread is the body of Christ.[^12] And whoever [is ignorant of]{g:ignorare} this [tropical]{g:predicacio-tropica} or [figurative predication]{g:predicacio-figurativa} will misunderstand [not only]{g:nedum} the holy doctors and the decrees of the church but the faith of scripture. For Christ says, John 15:5, "I am the true vine," and John 10:14 [101] and 14:6, "I am the good shepherd," "I am the way," etc.;[^13] as also, Luke 21:33, he says that heaven and earth shall pass away but his words shall not pass away; where without doubt he understands by the words, which are most inconstant signs, the [thing signified]{g:signatum}, the sense; and he often uses this [trope]{g:tropus}, as after him the holy doctors and [the laws of the church]{g:leges-ecclesie} use it.[^14]

## ¶4 · 101.8–101.19

> [p. 101] In isto autem tropo sunt gradus multiplices, quorum mirabilis et plus miraculosus est quod loco panis succedit corpus Christi sacramentaliter, ut probat sanctus Ambrosius Deo esse possibile et credibile, non quod panis destruitur, sed quod significat corpus Domini ibi presens in sacramento, cui oportet panis consideracione dimissa plene attendere. Unde hec est porta lapsus multorum qui ignorantes voces proprias baptizant terminos: aliqui ponentes transsubstanciacionem ad sensum expositum, alii ponentes ydemptificacionem, et alii ponentes inpanacionem, ut patet inferius.

And in this trope there are many degrees, of which the wonderful and more miraculous one is that in the place of the bread the body of Christ succeeds [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} (as saint Ambrose proves to be possible to God and credible): not that the bread is destroyed, but that it signifies [the body of the Lord]{g:corpus-domini} present there in the sacrament, to which one must attend fully, the consideration of the bread being laid aside.[^15] Hence this is the gate of the fall of many who, ignorant of the proper words, baptize terms: some positing [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio} in the [meaning]{g:sensus} set out, others positing [identification]{g:ydemptificacio}, and others positing [impanation]{g:inpanacio}, as is clear below.[^16]

## ¶5 · 101.20–102.14

> [p. 101] Sed redeundo conceditur quod Deus ex omnipotencia sua posset concedere fideli potenciam conficiendi eukaristiam pia mente, subducta voce. Sed de hoc non queritur, ymmo quid Deus ordinavit in lege scripture sue debere fieri; et patet quod ipse docendo verba confeccionis predicta docuit ut nos faciamus similiter. Et sic videtur domino Armachano probabile libro IX°, capitulo V°, De Questionibus Armenorum, quod Christus non illis verbis sed ante illorum prolacionem confecit in sacramento presenciam sui corporis et sanguinis. Sic enim ultimate instituit [p. 102] originaliter alia sacramenta, et ita levi evidencia sed probabili credimus quod sacerdos ex virtute Dei conficit illis verbis; et sic licet sacerdos posset habere virtutem conficiendi panem et non vinum, panem triticeum et non ordeaceum vel alterius speciei et econtra et sic de aliis variacionibus tam in pane quam in vino, ymmo verba quantumcunque varia possent esse pars huius sacramenti et sacramentum posset esse nude sacra hostia sine verbis, tamen formam quam observamus credimus a Domino institutam, sicut credimus profunditatem ordinacionis divine habere racionem, propter quam oportuit eum taliter ordinare, sed vias istas nobis investigabiles oportet transcurrere.

But, to return, it is granted that God by his omnipotence could grant a believer the [power]{g:potencia} of consecrating the eucharist with a pious mind, the voice withdrawn.[^17] But that is not what is asked; rather, [what is asked is] what God has ordained in the law of his scripture ought to be done; and it is clear that he, in teaching the aforesaid words of consecration, taught that we should do likewise. And so it seems probable to [the lord of Armagh]{g:ardmacanus}, in book 9, chapter 5, of *On the Questions of the Armenians*, that Christ effected the presence of his body and blood in the sacrament not by those words but before uttering them.[^18] For in this way, in the last resort, he instituted [102] the other sacraments at their origin; and so on light but probable [evidence]{g:evidencia} we believe that the priest consecrates by those words by the [power]{g:virtus} of God.[^19] And so, although a priest could have the power of consecrating bread and not wine, wheaten bread and not barley or of another kind, and conversely, and so of other variations in the bread as in the wine (indeed words however varied could be part of this sacrament, and the sacrament could be barely the [sacred]{g:sacer} host without words), yet we believe the form we observe was instituted by the Lord, just as we believe that the depth of the divine [ordinance]{g:ordinacio} has a reason for which he had to ordain thus; but we must pass over these ways, unsearchable to us.[^20]

## ¶6 · 102.15–102.28

> [p. 102] Ulterius quantum ad dissensionem inter nos et Grecos de confeccione in azimis vel fermento, dicitur quod confeccio in azimis quam nos observamus est conveniencior textui utriusque testamenti (ut dictum est alibi) et apcior ad figuram; dicit enim Apostolus primo Cor. V°, 8 quod epulari debemus non in fermento veteri malicie aut nequicie sed in azimis sinceritatis et veritatis. Utrum autem Greci vere conficiunt vel utrum foret confeccio in vino non lymphato, non aceto vel vino de aggresta aut liquido tali confecto, est dubium apud multos. Sed videtur mihi quod non tam necessaria est ista confeccio, quod oportet ipsum conficere in forma vel materia aliena, et sic vel Greci errant vel nos.

Further, as to the dissension between us and the Greeks over consecration in unleavened bread or in leaven, [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that the consecration in unleavened bread which we observe is in better [agreement]{g:conveniencia} with the text of both testaments (as has been said elsewhere) and apter to the figure; for the Apostle says, 1 Corinthians 5:8, that we ought to feast not in the old leaven of malice or wickedness but in the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth.[^21] But whether the Greeks [truly]{g:vere} consecrate, or whether there would be consecration in wine not mixed with water, in vinegar, or in wine of verjuice or a liquid made in such a way, is doubtful among many.[^22] But it seems to me that this consecration is not so necessary that one must consecrate it in a foreign form or matter; and so either the Greeks err or we do.[^23]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 98.15: Objection: The whole order of ministering the sacrament would be destroyed.
- 99.2: The order of the Church is rightly to be followed.
- 99.8: Yet it is not evident from Scripture that the power of consecration is strictly limited to priests.
- 99.23: We are said to take away the efficacy of the sacramental words.
- 100.11: These words make the host an efficient figure of Christ, so that our whole mind is to be fixed an Christ's body.
- 101.8: It is the most wonderful and miraculous of figures.
- 101.20: As to the words of consecration, it is enough that God bids them to be spoken.
- 102.15: Concerning the matter of the sacrament; doubts as to leavened bread, vinegar, verjuice etc.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** The objection draws these consequences from Wyclif's position (Loserth's side-note: "the whole order of ministering the sacrament would be destroyed"); *vel non est confeccio* is the alternative horn, "or else there is no consecration at all". *Conficere* is "consecrate" here and through the chunk; the words of consecration are *verba confeccionis*.
[^2]: **C.** *De posse* and *de licere*: the infinitives used as nouns, "what can be" (by God's absolute power) against "what is lawful" (by scripture). The distinction governs the whole answer (101.20, and 04-g at 109.1).
[^3]: **C.** The logicians' point: "a layman can consecrate" is true in the sense that a layman can become a priest, and a priest can consecrate; it is not a claim that he can consecrate while a layman.
[^4]: **S.** Augustine and Chrysostom on every predestinate layman as a priest are not identified at the page foot. Probably Augustine, *De civ. Dei* XX.10 (PL 41, 676: *sic omnes sacerdotes, quoniam membra sunt unius sacerdotis*), and the Chrysostom of the *Opus imperfectum* hom. 43 (Gratian D. 40 c. 12: *non omnis sacerdos sanctus, sed omnis sanctus sacerdos*). Loserth's note at 98.31 sends to Wyclif, *De ecclesia* p. 457, a parallel on lay reception that names neither father (LEDGER K04e-1). **G.** *Haberet racionem sacerdotis*: *racio* as the account of what a thing is (the row *racio individui / persone*), i.e. would meet what makes a priest.
[^5]: **G.** *Sentencia* here is the sense of the words (the row *sentencia verborum*), not a teaching: the church kept the sense of the evangelists' words while varying the words. *Sacerdos* is "priest", *presbiter* "presbyter" (99.17, 100.17), as the Latin alternates.
[^6]: **C.** *Singulariter* (CONTEXTUAL): "alone, only" in both places in this sentence. The *autem* continues the explanation of the church's ordinance; the "light foundation" is the ground for keeping consecration to priests.
[^7]: **S.** Augustine, *In Ioh.* tr. 25.12 (PL 35, 1602); Loserth: *Opp.* III.2, 489 (LEDGER K04e-2). **C.** *Cum stat … accipere*: "since it can stand (be the case) that … receives"; *presbitero* is the ablative of comparison, "than a presbyter".
[^8]: **S/T.** Ambrose, cited through Gratian: *De cons.* D. 2 c. *Forte dicis* (the page foot reads c. 43, OCR "XLIII"; form ours) and c. *Panis est* (c. 55; form ours) (LEDGERs K04e-3, K04e-4). The Latin assigns the first to Ambrose's *De officiis*; Gratian inscribes the chapter *in libro de officiis*, which Wyclif follows; the text is really *De sacramentis* VI.1.2 (PL 16, 453–454) (LEDGER K04e-5). Loserth (99.27–28): the incipit is properly *Forte dicas*. At 99.30 ABCE read *fit corpus*; the print's *caro* is translated.
[^9]: **C.** *Per locum a maiori*: the topic from the greater (cf. the row *a maiori vel pari*): if the word could make something from nothing, it can change one thing into another.
[^10]: **S.** *De cons.* D. 2 c. *Revera* (c. 69, Loserth 100.7; form ours; from Ambrose, *De mysteriis* 8.47–9.51; not the chapter next after c. 55, despite *sequente*) (LEDGER K04e-6). At 100.8 ABCD lack *esse*, which does not change the sense. *Conversio* is the eucharistic "conversion" (GLOSSARY).
[^11]: **C.** *Sentencia* (CONTEXTUAL) here leans to "teaching": Ambrose's position, beside his arguments. *Fiet* at 100.12 (B *fiat*, E *fieret*; D omits *non*) is translated as printed.
[^12]: **C.** *Falsum assumitur*: the objection's premiss at 99.24, "by them nothing is … changed". Wyclif answers that the bread is changed, imperceptibly, in being made an effective figure. At 100.24 E reads *figurat*.
[^13]: **S/T.** The print gives "John 15:5" for *Ego sum vitis vera*, which is John 15:1 (15:5 reads *Ego sum vitis*); the English keeps Wyclif's reference. *Ego sum pastor bonus* is John 10:14; the print's *XI°* is reported as a doubt (Loserth 100.30: the codices read *Joh. X ac XIV*, *correxi*). John 14:6 DR "I am the way, and the truth, and the life." At 101.1 ABCD read *bonus etc.*
[^14]: **S.** Luke 21:33 (DR "Heaven and earth shall pass away: but my words shall not pass away"), given in indirect speech with *sua* for *mea*, so left unquoted. At 101.3 ABCE read *transient*. **C.** *Signatam sentenciam*: "the thing signified, the sense": *sentencia* is the sense (row *sentencia verborum*) and *signata* marks it as what the words signify.
[^15]: **T.** At 101.8–9 the manuscripts disagree: ABC *quorum magis et plus*; C in the text *magis et mirabilis*, in the margin *mirabilis et plus miraculosus*; D and E *magis mirabile*. The print follows C's margin and is translated. **S.** Ambrose, *De sacramentis* IV.4.14–15, PL 16, 441A (Loserth 101.11) (LEDGER K04e-7).
[^16]: **C.** *Baptizant terminos*: they give the terms new names, as one names at baptism (cf. *baptiste signorum*, GLOSSARY). *Voces proprias*: the proper words for the thing. The three positions are taken up in the later chapters (*inferius*).
[^17]: **C.** *Potencia* (CONTEXTUAL): "power" here, of the power God could give, not Aristotle's potency. *Subducta voce*: without the words spoken.
[^18]: **S.** FitzRalph, *De questionibus Armenorum* IX.5 (Paris 1512, ff. 67v–68r: conversion before the words *probabile videtur*, either way *sine periculo*) (LEDGER K04e-8). **C.** *Confecit … presenciam* is "effected the presence": *conficere* in its general sense of making, since what Christ does is bring the presence about.
[^19]: **C.** *Ultimate … originaliter*: construed as "in the last resort … at their origin", i.e. the other sacraments, too, were first instituted by Christ's act and not by a fixed form of words. *Ita levi evidencia sed probabili*: the ground for the priest's consecrating by the words is light but probable (cf. *levis fundacio*, 99.8).
[^20]: **S.** *Profunditatem* and *vias … investigabiles* echo Rom. 11:33 (Vg. *O altitudo … investigabiles viae eius*; DR "how unsearchable his ways"); no quotation is made. **T.** At 102.9 E reads *nuda*, and C lacks *nuda sacra*; at 102.10 ABC read *forma*; at 102.12 E reads *habere potenciam*. The print is translated.
[^21]: **S.** 1 Cor. 5:8 in Wyclif's indirect form (*epulari debemus … malicie aut nequicie*); the Vulgate reads *Itaque epulemur non in fermento veteri, neque in fermento malitiae et nequitiae: sed in azymis sinceritatis et veritatis* (DR "let us feast, not with the old leaven, nor with the leaven of malice and wickedness: but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth"). The English follows his Latin. *Ut dictum est alibi*: probably *De veritate sacrae scripturae* c. 12 (ed. Buddensieg I, 276–277), where the Greeks are told that Christ, eating the Pasch by Exod 12, consecrated *in azimis* (LEDGER K04e-9).
[^22]: **C.** *In vino non lymphato, non aceto*: the second *non* is construed as continuing the series of doubtful matters ("in vinegar"), not as "in wine that is not vinegar"; reported as a doubt. *Aggresta*: verjuice, the juice of green grapes (Loserth 102.24; E *de aggresto*). At 102.23–25 A has *in vino … confecto* in the margin.
[^23]: **C.** The sentence is compressed: construed as "consecration is not so bound to one form that a consecration in a foreign form or matter is required (or excluded); so it is either the Greeks or we who are in error, not both". *Ipsum* (the sacrament) as the object of *conficere*. At 102.26 B reads *tam materia*, A *tam necessaria* in a second hand; at 102.27 D reads *quam oportet* and *forma lingua*.
