# *De eucharistia*, chapter 4, part 3: the five forms of the words, the sense before the language, and fasting communion

## ¶1 · 90.23–93.20

> [p. 90] Ulterius quoad formam verborum in lingua latina in qua Romana ecclesia habet fidem scripture, videtur quod non sit aliqua forma verborum ad confeccionem eukaristie generaliter requisita; nam usus ecclesie nostre variat ab omnibus illis quatuor, sicut et omnes illi variant ab invicem plus vel minus. Nam Mattheus XXVIº, 28 habet ista verba consecracionis vini: Hic est enim sanguis meus novi testamenti qui pro multis effundetur in remissionem peccatorum; Lucas vero XXIIº, 20 ponit sic: Hic est calix novum testamentum in meo sanguine qui pro vobis fundetur. Marcus vero XIVº, 24 [p. 91] varians a Mattheo dimittit enim et in remissionem peccatorum. Paulus vero tradens nobis sentenciam quam accepit a Domino I. Cor. XI° sic loquitur: Hic calix novum testamentum est in meo sanguine; hoc facite quocienscunque biberitis in meam commemoracionem. Sed ab omnibus istis variat usus nostre ecclesie, ut patet tercio decretalium Cum Marthe, titulo De Celebracione Missarum. Cum ergo secundum has quinque formas contingit vere conficere, patet conclusio. Per hanc itaque variacionem quintimembrem docemur preponderanter ad sensum attendere et quod talis variacio verborum non est de substancia sacramenti; nam contingit (sicut de facto contigit) per verba istarum quinque formarum conficere, quia aliter ante istam ordinacionem quintam ex defectu scripture deficerent ecclesie sollempnes, ymmo forent ydolatre decipientes se ipsos et populum ex falsitate scripture, et sic ex narracione evangelistarum tocius et omnium titubaret auctoritas, et per consequens superflue laboraret Augustinus per totum librum De Concordia Evangelistarum. Nec valet dicere quod quintus ordo est necessarius post publicacionem usus ecclesie Romane et non ante, quia sic liceret pape contra evangelium dispensare, eius errores corrigere et per consequens novum evangelium de toto condere; quod suppono hic a iuribus fidelium esse horribile, cum decreta Romanorum pontificum in fide sepius titubaverunt, ut patet distinccione XVI, capitulo VI. Sinodus, prima distinccione, capitulo I, De Penitencia [p. 92] quasi per totum et specialiter in ista materia. Quando enim quis peccando mortaliter deficit in moribus deficit et in fide, ideo inprovida et nimis levis videtur diffinicio eorum qui dicunt quod dimittendo verbum vel quamcunque aliam formam qua utitur ecclesia Romana sacerdos non conficit, quia diverse lingue habent diversum modum sonandi eciam latinum, ut patet in sonacione huius littere ‘C’; ymmo oportet diversimode organisantes secundum disposicionem figure celestis diversimode sonare; ideo idem foret dicere quod oportet omnem sacerdotem vel populum istam formam sonorum in confeccione huius sacramenti attendere et dicere quod oportet omnem salvandum esse istius complexionis et plage, quod foret infidelis accepcio personarum; ideo (ut dicit doctor) satis est quod fideles concordent in sentencia ante linguam, ut quod panis figurative est corpus Christi et vinum eius sanguis; et sic salvantur dicti quatuor evangeliste a mendacio, quando asserunt Dominum sic dixisse, hoc est, dixisse istam sentenciam sive sensum. Concordare tamen debet sacerdos cum patria et usu ecclesie in istis et ritibus similibus stante substancia sentencie ut in neutris. Unde usus ecclesie quem habemus licet sit colatus ex scriptura sacra, non est melior quam quilibet quatuor usuum evangelistarum; et Deus scit utrum sapuit peccatum illa varietas introducta. Teneamus ergo sentenciam requisitam; illud autem reputamus requisitam sentenciam in qua omnis isti quatuor evangeliste concordant et illam in qua variant esse necessariam ad [p. 93] mores, sed preter essenciam sacramenti; et utinam nimis observantes Judayce ritus et consuetudines hominum quos ultra veritatem Dei colunt attenderent ad istam sentenciam, nam phariseice colant culicem et degluciunt camelum, exigentes quod populus sequatur ritus et precepta et mores eorum; sed dicunt stultum esse attendere ad consilia Christi, ad vitam vel conversacionem Christi ac Petri, cum oportet propter distanciam et variacionem temporis nunc observare contrarium, et sic observantes paleam dimittimus granum purum; ideo oportet quod nos vertiginosi non excuciamus religionem quam Christus instituit, quia Eccli. XIX°, i dicitur: Qui spernit modica paulatim decidet; debemus ergo postponendo ritus novellos observare mores Christi et opera, quia secundum Gregorium I° Pastoralium III° idcirco Jesus in carne venerat ut non solum nos per passionem in carne redimeret, verum eciam per conversacionem doceret; ideo ve nobis, si latemus in tenebris vel retrocedendo vel declinando ipsum non sequimur.

Further, as to the form of the words in the Latin language, in which the Roman [church]{g:ecclesia} has the faith of scripture, it seems that there is no form of words generally required for the [consecration]{g:confeccio} of the eucharist; for the use of our church varies from all those four, just as all those also vary from one another, more or less. For Matthew 26:28 has these words of the consecration of the [wine]{g:vinum}: "For this is my blood of the new testament, which shall be shed for many unto remission of sins"; and Luke 22:20 puts it thus: "This is the [chalice]{g:calix}, the new testament in my blood, which shall be shed for you."[^1] And Mark 14:24, [91] differing from Matthew, leaves out "for" and "unto remission of sins".[^2] And Paul, handing down to us the [sense]{g:sentencia-verborum} which he received from the Lord, speaks thus in 1 Cor. 11: "This chalice is the new testament in my blood. This do ye, as often as you shall drink, for the commemoration of me."[^3] Now the use of our church varies from all these, as is clear in the third [book] of the [decretals]{g:decretalis}, *Cum Marthe*, under the title *De celebratione missarum*.[^4] Since, therefore, it is possible to [consecrate]{g:conficere} [truly]{g:vere} according to these five forms, the [conclusion]{g:conclusio} is clear. By this [fivefold]{g:quintimembris} variation, then, we are taught to attend preponderantly to the [meaning]{g:sensus}, and that such variation of words is not of the [substance]{g:substancia} of the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}; for it is possible (as in fact it has happened) to consecrate by the words of these five forms, because otherwise, before this fifth [ordinance]{g:ordinacio}, the solemn churches would have failed through a defect of scripture; indeed, they would be idolaters, deceiving themselves and the people through the falsity of scripture; and so, from the narration of the evangelists, the [authority]{g:auctoritas} of the whole and of all would totter,[^5] and consequently Augustine would have laboured superfluously through the whole book *On the Agreement of the Evangelists*. Nor is it valid to say that the fifth order is necessary after the publication of the use of the Roman church and not before, because then it would be lawful for the pope to dispense against the gospel, to correct its errors, and consequently to found a new gospel altogether; which I here suppose, from the laws of the faithful, to be horrible, since the [decrees]{g:decretum} of the [Roman pontiffs]{g:romanus-pontifex} have quite often tottered in faith, as is clear in distinction 16, chapter 6, *Sinodus*, the first distinction, chapter 1, *De penitencia*, [92] almost throughout, and especially in this matter.[^6] For when anyone, by sinning mortally, fails in morals, he fails also in faith; therefore the [definition]{g:diffinicio} of those seems improvident and [exceedingly]{g:nimis} light who say that, by leaving out a word or any other form which the Roman church uses, a [priest]{g:sacerdos} does not consecrate; because diverse tongues have a diverse manner of sounding even Latin, as is clear in the sounding of this letter 'C'; indeed, those who [form]{g:organisare} [sounds] with their organs diversely, according to the disposition of the figure of the heavens, must sound diversely.[^7] Therefore it would be the same to say that every priest or people must attend to this form of sounds in the consecration of this sacrament, and to say that everyone who is to be saved must be of this [complexion]{g:complexio} and [region]{g:plaga}; which would be a faithless [respect of persons]{g:accepcio-personarum}. Therefore (as the doctor says) it is enough that the faithful agree in the sense before the language, as that the [bread]{g:panis} is [figuratively]{g:figurative} the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} and the wine his blood;[^8] and thus the said four evangelists are saved from falsehood when they assert that the Lord spoke thus, that is, that he spoke this sense, or meaning. Yet the priest ought to agree with his [homeland]{g:patria} and the use of the church in these and similar [rites]{g:ritus}, the substance of the sense standing, as in [neutral things]{g:neutra}. Hence the use of the church which we have, although it is strained out of holy scripture, is not better than any one of the four uses of the evangelists; and God knows whether that variety, when introduced, savoured of sin.[^9] Let us therefore hold the required sense; and we reckon that to be the required sense in which all these four evangelists agree, and that in which they vary to be necessary for [93] morals, but outside the [essence]{g:essencia} of the sacrament. And would that those who exceedingly observe, Jewishly, the rites and [customs]{g:consuetudo} of men, which they cultivate beyond the truth of God, attended to this sense; for pharisaically they strain the gnat and swallow the camel,[^10] demanding that the people follow their rites and precepts and morals; but they say that it is foolish to attend to the counsels of Christ, to the life or [manner of life]{g:conversacio} of Christ and of Peter, since, because of the distance and variation of time, the contrary must now be observed; and so, observing the chaff, we let go the pure grain. Therefore it is needful that we giddy ones do not shake off the religion which Christ instituted, for in Ecclus. 19:1 it is said: "He that contemneth small things, shall fall by little and little." We ought therefore, [setting aside]{g:postponere} the newer rites, to observe the morals and works of Christ, because according to Gregory, *Pastoral Rule* 1.3, "for this reason Jesus had come in the flesh, that he might not only redeem us by his passion in the flesh, but also teach us by his manner of life";[^11] therefore woe to us if we lurk in darkness, or, going back or turning aside, do not follow him.

## ¶2 · 93.21–93.24

> [p. 93] Sed contra istud instatur tripliciter; videtur primo, cum Christus instituit et precepit capi hoc sacramentum in cena, quod nos debemus ipsum sequi et apostolos eius celebrando post prandium.

But against this [it is objected]{g:instatur} in three ways. It seems, first, since Christ instituted and commanded that this sacrament be taken at supper, that we ought to follow him and his apostles by celebrating after dinner.

## ¶3 · 93.25–93.29

> [p. 93] Hic dicitur quod conclusio est vera; sed pro materia notandum est quod, sicut Christus cum apostolis sumpsit hoc sacramentum ieiunus a crimine, sic oportet nos in hoc sacramento eorum discipulos sequi ipsos.

Here [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that the conclusion is true; but for the matter it must be noted that, as Christ with the apostles [received]{g:sumpcio} this sacrament fasting from crime, so we, their disciples, must follow them in this sacrament.[^12]

## ¶4 · 93.30–94.15

> [p. 93] Sed quia in nobis ex cibario causatur communiter ventris ingluvies, ideo pia religione ieiuni corpore [p. 94] sacramentum istud accipiamus; et ne paulativa declinacio inducat periculum, statuimus nobis pro regula quod omnino ieiuni in corpore celebramus et nimis prepostere plus ponderamus illud quam imitacionem Christi in ieiunio a peccato, et sic de ritu quoad locum et tempus ac alias observancias circa hoc sacramentum racionabiliter ordinatas. Illas autem laudo sed preponderancias earum et nimietatem detestor; unde cecitas pharisaica est qua putamus Christum pocius sequendum in ritibus quam virtute, ut licet Christus sic prandebat cum duodecim quorum unus fuit traditor et sic de infinitis circumstanciis: non tamen oportet nos illas attendere, cum non per se dicant virtutes secundum imitacionem Christi quoad genus virtutum omnimode conservare.

But because in us gluttony of the belly is commonly caused by food, therefore by a pious religion let us receive [94] this sacrament fasting in body; and lest a gradual declining bring on danger, we have laid down for ourselves as a rule that we celebrate wholly fasting in body, and exceedingly preposterously we weigh that more than the imitation of Christ in fasting from sin; and so of the rite as to place and time, and other observances about this sacrament reasonably ordained. And these I praise, but their [preponderances]{g:preponderancia} and excess I detest; hence it is pharisaic blindness by which we think that Christ is to be followed rather in rites than in [virtue]{g:virtus}: as, although Christ thus dined with the twelve, of whom one was a traitor, and so of infinite circumstances, yet we need not attend to these, since they do not [of themselves]{g:per-se} [signify]{g:dicere} virtues to be kept in every way, according to the imitation of Christ, as to the kind of virtues.[^13]

## ¶5 · 94.16–94.28

> [p. 94] Tres autem assignant homines causas quare Christus secundum hunc ordinem sacramentum istud instituit; prima quia oportuit esum agni paschalis, cum fuit figura huius sacramenti, precedere; secunda ut Christus doceat nos ordinaciones futuras imitacione sua in moribus minus attendere; et tercia ut legislator et auctor doceat ex ista varietate in ipso esse preeminenciam potestatis; ideo cum debemus sequi Christum, non precedere vel ex equo viare cum ipso, debemus gaudere de privilegio quod habet ut Deus et ut primogenitus ex vi gracie unionis et utrobique sequi ipsum vel proporcionaliter vel similiter secundum moralem congruenciam nostri status.

And men assign three causes why Christ instituted this sacrament in this order: the first, because the [eating]{g:esus} of the paschal lamb, since it was a [figure]{g:figura} of this sacrament, had to precede; the second, that Christ might teach us, by imitation of him in morals, to attend less to the ordinances to come; and the third, that as lawgiver and author he might teach by this variety that there is in him a [preeminence]{g:preeminencia} of [power]{g:potestas}. Therefore, since we ought to follow Christ, not to go before him or [be a wayfarer]{g:viator} level with him, we ought to rejoice in the [privilege]{g:privilegium} which he has as God and as firstborn by force of the [grace of union]{g:gracia-unionis}, and on both sides to follow him, either proportionally or similarly, according to the moral [fittingness]{g:congruencia} of our [state]{g:status}.[^14]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 90.23: The exact words of consecration are not essential, since they differ in Scripture.
- 91.20: The use of the Roman Church cannot make one form essential.
- 92.6: There are different pronunciations even of Latin.
- 92.16: What we have to attend to is the sense, in which the evangelists agree, although they differ in the words.
- 93.21: Christ did not ordain fasting communion.
- 93.25: It is more important to fast from sin, but to communicate fasting is a good practice.
- 94.16: Three reasons why Christ did not ordain us to fast before communion.

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** Matt. 26:28 and Luke 22:20 (Douay–Rheims). Wyclif's *in meo sanguine* for the Vulgate's *in sanguine meo* in Luke leaves the English unchanged; BCD read *effundetur* in Luke (90.33), the Vulgate *fundetur*.

[^2]: **C/S.** *Dimittit enim et in remissionem peccatorum* is read as naming two omissions: Mark 14:24 (Vulgate) lacks both Matthew's *enim* and his *in remissionem peccatorum*. C omits *et* (91.1), which would make *enim* Wyclif's own connective: "for he leaves out 'unto remission of sins'". The editor's italics mark only the second phrase.

[^3]: **C/S.** *Sentencia* (CONTEXTUAL): through this paragraph "sense", the meaning of the words set against their wording (*sentenciam sive sensum*, 92.20), not "teaching"; *sensus* is rendered "meaning" to keep the two words apart. *Tradens … quam accepit a Domino* echoes 1 Cor. 11:23; the words quoted are 1 Cor. 11:25 (Douay–Rheims), Wyclif's *biberitis* for the Vulgate's *bibetis* giving the same English.

[^4]: **S.** *Cum Marthe*, X 3.41.6, under the title *De celebratione missarum* (Loserth: *Decret. Greg. IX*, lib. III, tit. XLI, cap. VI; the modern form is ours; LEDGER K04c-1). The codices read *Misse* in the title (91.8).

[^5]: **C/S.** *Tocius et omnium*: "of the whole and of all", most likely the whole narration and all four evangelists; the Latin leaves the nouns unexpressed. The book is Augustine's *De consensu evangelistarum*, under Wyclif's title *De concordia* (LEDGER K04c-2).

[^6]: **S/T.** Loserth: Gratian, D. 16 c. 6 (*Habeo librum*) and *De pen.* D. 1 c. 1; the modern form is ours (LEDGER K04c-3, K04c-4). *Sinodus* stands as printed after *capitulo VI*: whether it names the matter of the canon (a synod) or opens the next reference is open. *Prima* is Loserth's correction: the codices read *XXXI* (91.29).

[^7]: **C.** *Organisantes*: those who form sounds with their organs of speech ("sounds" supplied). *Figura celestis*: the configuration of the heavens, which disposes a body; hence *complexionis et plage* below, "complexion and region". *Accepcio personarum*: the Douay "respect of persons".

[^8]: **C/T.** *Doctor* is unnamed; the nearest named authority is the lord of Armagh, cited for this point at 90.5 (LEDGER K04c-5). The example *ut quod panis figurative est corpus Christi* may be Wyclif's rather than the doctor's. D *panis est figurative* (92.17).

[^9]: **T/C.** 92.23–24: ABC read *Unde sensum quem*, which C corrected to *usus ecclesie*; D *Unde sensus quem*. Loserth prints *usus ecclesie*, which construes with *quam quilibet quatuor usuum*. *Colatus*: "strained", as liquid through a cloth. For *Deus scit* C has *dominus* in the margin (92.26).

[^10]: **S.** Alludes to Matt. 23:24 (DR "who strain out a gnat and swallow a camel"); not quoted, since the Latin is recast. *Colant* "strain" plays on *colunt* "cultivate" just before; D reads *colunt* here too, E *orant* (93.4).

[^11]: **S.** Ecclus. 19:1 (Douay–Rheims). Gregory, *Regula pastoralis* I.3 (Loserth: *Opp.* II, 4–5; LEDGER K04c-6), quoted to the editor's italics.

[^12]: **C/T.** *Conclusio est vera*: the objection's conclusion, that we ought to follow Christ and the apostles, is granted, and the following is moved from the meal to fasting from sin. *Ieiunus a crimine*: D *anime* (93.27); E *cepit* for *sumpsit*.

[^13]: **C.** *Cum non per se dicant virtutes … omnimode conservare*: the infinitive has no expressed governor. Taken here with *virtutes*, "virtues to be kept"; it may instead depend on *oportet* (we must keep in every way, by imitating Christ, the kind of virtues), and a word may be lost (94.14–15). *Dicant*: *dicere* in its logical sense, "signify".

[^14]: **C.** *Imitacione sua*: "by imitation of him", *sua* referring to Christ; "by his own example" is the alternative. *Utrobique*: "on both sides", as God and as firstborn. *Ordinaciones futuras*: the ordinances to come after him.
