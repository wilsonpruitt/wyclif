# *Dialogus*, chapter 14: prayers to the saints, and implicit faith

## Falsehood and Truth · 26.28–28.30

> [p. 26] MENDACIUM. Iterum manifeste innuis non esse ut fidem [p. 27] credendum illos esse sanctos qui in missis, in letaniis, et aliis ecclesie serviciis inseruntur; ad quid ergo diceremus de illis nisi crederemus quod in patria suntbeati? cum spes meriti super fide stabili in credentibus est fundanda. VERITAS. Hic dictum est sepius quod de sanctis quos canonizat ecclesia supponimus, sed non ut fidem credimus, quod sunt beati in patria. Undecim tamen apostolos cum paulo, magdalene et ceteris quos fides scripture implicat esse sanctos, credendum est ut fidem esse beatos, et alios, quantumcunque magna sanctitate in celis prepolleant, credimus esse in beatitudine, verumtamen citra fidem. Et ideo ordinavit ecclesia quod quecumque oracio porrecta ad sanctum ad deum principaliter dirigatur. Et sic quelibet talis oracio implicat tacite condicionem, si et quatenus placet deo per talis sancti meritum nobis miseris suffragari. Et ut videtur multis nulla oracio porrecta specialiter sancto Christi est laudabilis, nisi de quanto acuit devocionem in Christum, sic quod si omnes intenciones et oraciones uniri possent in Christum, sincerius directe unite in Christum, esset utilius Christiano omnes oraciones specialiter Christo porrigere. Nec valet false credere quod Christus propter celsitudinem sui dominii non potest a nobis miseris faciliter exorari; ideo est necessarium habere alias personas mediantes; Christus enim assistens pontifex immediate illabitur viatori et cum hoc immediate conjungitur deitati. Ideo nullus sanctus est misericordius aut facilius auditivus miseri quam est Christus. Nec valent oraciones aliorum sanctorum, nisi de quanto Christus prius ex sua gracia has acceptat. Ideo dicunt quidam quod porrecciones oracionum ad istos sanctos sepe [p. 28] sunt superflue vel nocive. Conceditur ergo quod non est articulus fidei appropriare cuilibet sancto de quo dicimus specialem articulum fidei sed beatitudinem illorum supponimus secundum majorem evidenciam vel minorem. Unde absit quod omnes legende sanctorum in ecclesiam introducte sint fides catholica, cum in festo corporis Christi de sancto sanctorum scribitur quod accidencia sunt in eucaristia sine subjecto, quod non fuit fides tempore augustini. Ideo dicit in de sermone Domini in monte quod multa vera, licet in legendis talibus inserantur non sunt fides catholica; sicut exemplificat de ministro convivii cujus unum brachium a cane rapido est abstractum. Sufficit ergo ad tales oraciones secundarias habere probabilem supposicionem, et de Christo qui fundamentaliter oratur habere cum aliis virtutibus firmam fidem; et patet solucio. In ista tamen materia dicunt scolastici quod aliqua est fides explicita vel expressa et aliqua est fides implicita sive tacita. Fidem autem implicitam dicunt esse quamcunque veritatem juxta illud apostoli prima cor. Caritas omnia credit, et tamen infinite sunt veritates de quibus non oportet nos hic habere fidem explicitam; cum sufficit fideli quod credat quamcunque veritatem secundum gradum et formam qua deus vult ipsum hanc credere. Ut carnis resurreccionis est articulus fidei cuilibet Christiano, et tamen non oportet de qualibet persona hominis habere expressum articulum fidei quod resurget. Sed deus vult quod per istam fidem generalem sua militans ecclesia excusetur. Non enim vult quod sua ecclesia cum veritatibus inutiliter oneretur. Communis autem carnis resurreccio erit tempore sempiterno adhuc futura, et postmodum tempore suo presens; sed post diem judicii est elapsa.

FALSEHOOD. Again you plainly hint that it is not to be believed as faith [27] that those are saints who are inserted in masses, in litanies, and in the other services of the [church]{g:ecclesia}. To what end, then, would we speak of them, unless we believed that they are blessed in the [homeland]{g:patria}?[^1] since the hope of merit must be founded, in believers, upon stable faith.

TRUTH. It has been said here more than once that of the saints whom the church [canonizes]{g:canonizare} we suppose, but do not believe as faith, that they are blessed in the homeland.[^2] Yet the eleven apostles, with Paul, Magdalene, and the others whom the faith of scripture implies to be saints, are to be believed as faith to be blessed; and the others, however great the sanctity in which they excel in heaven, we believe to be in blessedness, but yet [short of faith]{g:citra-fidem}. And therefore the church has ordained that whatever prayer is offered to a saint be directed principally to God. And so every such prayer tacitly implies a condition: if and in so far as it pleases God, through the merit of such a saint, [to aid]{g:suffragari} us wretches [by intercession]. And, as it seems to many, no prayer offered specially to a saint of Christ is praiseworthy except in so far as it sharpens devotion toward Christ; so that if all intentions and prayers could be united in Christ, more purely, directly united in Christ, it would be more profitable for a Christian to offer all prayers specially to Christ.[^3] Nor does it avail to believe falsely that Christ, because of the loftiness of his [lordship]{g:dominium}, cannot easily be entreated by us wretches, and that therefore it is necessary to have other persons mediating; for Christ, being come an high priest,[^4] [flows]{g:illabi} immediately into the [wayfarer]{g:viator}, and with this is immediately joined to [the Godhead]{g:deitas}. Therefore no saint is more mercifully or more readily disposed to hear a wretch than Christ is. Nor do the prayers of the other saints avail, except in so far as Christ first, of his grace, accepts them. Therefore some say that the offerings of prayers to these saints [28] are often superfluous or harmful. It is granted, therefore, that it is not [an article of faith]{g:articulus-fidei} [to appropriate]{g:appropriare} to every saint of whom we speak a special article of faith; but we suppose their blessedness according to greater or lesser [evidence]{g:evidencia}.[^5] Hence far be it that all the legends of the saints brought into the church should be the catholic faith, since in [the office of] the feast of Corpus Christi it is written of the holy of holies that the accidents are in the eucharist without a [subject]{g:subiectum}, which was not the faith in Augustine's time.[^6] So [Augustine] says in *On the Lord's Sermon on the Mount* that many true things, although they are inserted in such legends, are not the catholic faith; as he gives the example of the servant at a banquet whose one arm was carried off by a ravening dog.[^7] It suffices, therefore, for such secondary prayers to have a probable supposition, and, of Christ, who is fundamentally prayed to, to have firm faith together with the other virtues; and the solution is clear. Yet in this matter the schoolmen say that some faith is [explicit]{g:fides-explicita} or express, and some faith is implicit or tacit. And implicit faith they say to be [faith in] any truth whatever, according to that [word] of the apostle, 1 Corinthians: "[Charity]{g:caritas} believeth all things"; and yet there are infinitely many truths of which we need not here have explicit faith, since it suffices for the faithful man that he believe any truth according to the degree and form in which God wills him to believe it. Thus [that] of the resurrection of the flesh is an article of faith for every Christian, and yet one need not hold, of every human person, an express article of faith that he will rise again.[^8] But God wills that his [church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans} be excused through this general faith. For he does not will that his church be burdened uselessly with truths. And the common resurrection of the flesh is, in everlasting time, still to come, and afterwards, in its own time, present; but after the day of judgement it is past.[^9]

- 27.5: Ver. answers:
- 27.6: We do not hold as of faith the sanctity of any but Scripture saints.
- 27.11: Prayers are addressed mediately to saints, ultimately to God.
- 27.17: And are only good, if at all, in so far as they quicken devotion to Christ.
- 27.25: Who is more ready to hear than any saint.
- 27.28: Prayers to saints are often harmful.
- 28.3: The sanctity of saints is to be believed or not according to the evidence.
- 28.5: Their legends are not of faith.
- 28.7: Just as the existence of accidents without a substance in the Eucharist is a doctrinal accretion.
- 28.11: So are many of these legends.
- 28.13: It is enough to believe tentatively in prayers to saints, absolutely in Christ.
- 28.16: Explicit (or specific) differs from implicit (or indefinite) faith.
- 28.22: We must believe in the resurrection of the body, but not separately in that of this or that man's body.

## Notes

[^1]: **T.** B, C add *horarum officia* after *diceremus*: "to what end would we say the offices of the hours of them".

[^2]: **C.** *Ut fidem credere*, "believe as faith", is set against *supponere*, "suppose", and *citra fidem*, "short of faith": the saints of scripture are held as faith, the canonized saints on supposition.

[^3]: **C/T.** A compressed period, translated as printed; *sincerius ... unite in Christum* is taken as restating *uniri in Christum*. Over one *Christum* in this line B adds *scilicet sanctum* in another hand, and B1 and C read *ipsum*, which gives "if all intentions and prayers that could be united in [the saint] were more purely, directly united in Christ". B1, C read *est* for *esset*.

[^4]: **S.** *Assistens pontifex*, from Heb. 9:11, "Christ, being come an high priest".

[^5]: **T/C.** B, B1, C add after *fidei*: *adaptare quod in patria sit beatus*, "to fit to him that he is blessed in the homeland". *Appropriare* here is "attribute to", not the canon-law annexing of a church.

[^6]: **S/C.** The office of Corpus Christi, first nocturn, second lesson (Pollard's margin), which has the accidents existing without a subject (LEDGER D14-1). *De sancto sanctorum* is taken as "of the holy of holies", the sacrament; "by the holiest of saints" is grammatically possible.

[^7]: **S/T.** Augustine, *De sermone Domini in monte* I (Pollard: "i. 65"), on the apocryphal story of the man who struck an apostle at a feast (LEDGER D14-2). B, B1, C read *rabido*, "rabid", for *rapido*.

[^8]: **C/T.** *Carnis resurreccionis* is genitive in the print, construed as "[the article] of the resurrection of the flesh". B, B1 read *resurreccionem et alios articulos*, "the resurrection of the flesh and the other articles".

[^9]: **T.** Translating *est in* (B, B1, C) for the print's *erit*, which gives "will be, for everlasting time, still to come". The resurrection is true as future, then as present, then as past.
