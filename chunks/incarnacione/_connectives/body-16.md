### 10-g ¶1 · 182.32–184.14  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 182] Tercio confirmatur ex hoc quod nichil sufficit redimere genus humanum perfecte, ut modo redimitur, nisi ipsum sit [p. 183] Deus: humanitas Christi sic redimit: ergo est Deus. Ipsa¹ enim fuit passa omni passione, qua Christus dedit sanguinem in redempcionem ut Christus, et sic de posicione anime ac commercio quo mercatus est Christus. Cum ergo per² passionem et tales formas fiebat redempcio, sequitur quod ipsa fecit redempcionem; aliter enim³ non esset illa humanitas tante⁴ diligenda nec latria adoranda. Cum tamen indubie quilibet⁵ Christianus debet tantum diligere humanitatem Christi quantum⁵ et Christum sub racione qua homo: ymo hec idem sunt et colenda eadem pietate in numero, ut ostendit Magister 3°⁶ Sentenciarum Dist. 8. ex⁷ testimonio beati Augustini. Christus enim ex hoc quod est ista humanitas et per consequens creatura adorari debet yperdulia, et omnis alia creatura venerari debet tantum⁸ dulia. Nam excellens honor debet⁸ Christo attribui, qui ex unione ypostatica est creator ac eciam creatura; et illa vocatur significanter yperdulia; et cum hoc, cum idem Christus sit creator, debet latria soli deo debita adorari. Correspondenter humanitas Christi cum sit Verbum, debet adorari latria,⁹ et cum in se sit creatura debet adorari⁹ yperdulia sicut Christus. Et sic tam Christus quam ista humanitas, que est personaliter ipse Christus, debet secundum duplicem racionem eadem tam latria quam yperdulia adorari: **sed** absit hoc, nisi illa humanitas foret¹⁰ Christus! Ex quo patet quod non solum deitati, **sed** soli deo est latria exhibenda. Nec est putandum quod humanitas Christi adoranda sit latria solum secundum ordinem ad alterum, sicut crux, ymago, vel pars Christi quantitativa, sed¹¹ tanquam corpus animatum, sumptum de virgine, passum pro nobis, dolens et merens, et per consequens vere positum precium pro redempcione humani generis: ‘empti enim estis¹² precio [p. 184] magno,’ ut dicit Apostolus prima ad Cor. 6° et 7°: et ad id precium atque commercium explanat expressius: ‘Deus, inquit, proprio Filio non pepercit, **sed** pro nobis omnibus tradidit illum’: et indubie sic fecit tota Trinitas: unde ad Gal. 2°, ‘in fide vivo Filii Dei, qui dilexit me et tradidit semetipsum pro me’: et indubie precium traditum fuit Christi humanitas; unde¹ si per impossibile humanitas Christi dimissa¹ fuerit personalitati proprie conversans nobiscum ut proximis, ego diligerem eum ut salvatorem et redemptorem meum, adoraremque eum latria sicut prius: nulli enim alteri hominum adeo potero obligari. Nec video quin iste homo dimissus, sine peccato vel merito decedens, foret supra angelos et omnes alios homines merito exaltandus propter meritum quo meruit ypostatice copulatus.

ENGLISH: Thirdly, this is confirmed from this: that nothing suffices to redeem the human race perfectly, as it is now redeemed, unless it is [183] God; Christ's [humanity]{g:humanitas} so redeems: therefore it is God. For it suffered every suffering by which Christ gave his blood for redemption, as Christ [did]; and so of the [laying-down of the soul]{g:posicio-anime} and the [trade]{g:commercium} by which Christ made his purchase.[^1] Since, therefore, the redemption was made through the passion and such forms, it follows that it made the redemption; for otherwise that humanity would not be so greatly to be loved, nor to be adored with [latria]{g:latria}; whereas, nevertheless, every Christian undoubtedly ought to love Christ's humanity as much as [he loves] Christ too, under the aspect by which he is a man: indeed these are the same, and to be worshipped with the same [piety]{g:pietas} in number, as the [Master]{g:magister} shows, [book] 3 of the *Sentences*, dist. 8, from the testimony of blessed Augustine. For Christ, from this, that he is this humanity, and in consequence a [creature]{g:creatura}, ought to be adored with [hyperdulia]{g:yperdulia}, and every other creature ought to be venerated only with [dulia]{g:dulia}.[^2] For an excelling honour ought to be ascribed to Christ, who from the [hypostatic]{g:ypostatice} [union]{g:unio} is creator and also creature; and that is called, significantly, hyperdulia; and together with this, since the same Christ is creator, he ought to be adored with latria, owed to God alone. Correspondingly Christ's humanity, since it is the [Word]{g:verbum}, ought to be adored with latria, and since in itself it is a creature it ought to be adored with hyperdulia, as Christ is. And so both Christ and this humanity, which is personally Christ himself, ought, according to a twofold aspect, to be adored with the same, both latria and hyperdulia: but far be this, unless that humanity were Christ! From which it is clear that latria is to be shown not only to the [Godhead]{g:deitas}, but to God alone.[^3] Nor is it to be thought that Christ's humanity is to be adored with latria only according to its order to another, as the cross, an image, or a [quantitative part]{g:pars-quantitativa} of Christ, but as a body animated, taken from the virgin, suffering for us, grieving and mourning, and in consequence truly laid down as the price for the redemption of the human race:[^4] "For you are bought with a great [184] price," as the Apostle says, 1 Corinthians 6:20 and 7:23; and he sets out that price and trade more expressly: "God," he says, "spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all";[^5] and undoubtedly the whole Trinity did so. Hence Galatians 2:20: "I live in the faith of the Son of God, who loved me and delivered himself for me"; and undoubtedly the price delivered was Christ's humanity. Hence if, by an impossible [supposition], Christ's humanity had been [laid aside]{g:dimittere} to a [personality]{g:personalitas} of its own, [living its life]{g:conversari} with us as neighbours, I would love him as my saviour and redeemer, and would adore him with latria as before: for to no other of men shall I be able to be so bound. Nor do I see but that this man, laid aside, dying without sin or merit, would deservedly be exalted above the angels and all other men, for the merit by which he merited when hypostatically joined.[^6]

### 11-a ¶2 · 185.2–185.22  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 185] Superest¹ videre evidencias ex quibus fulcitur opinio huic sentencie² mee in verbis contraria.² Declarat **autem** Doctor Subtilis istam opinionem in principio 3ⁱⁱ Sentenciarum satis subtiliter. ‘Accidens, inquit, duplicem habitudinem habet ad subiectum: scilicet, informantis ad informatum; et talis racio includit imperfeccionem subiecti, quia potenciam ad actum quo subiectum perficitur: aliam **autem** habitudinem ut naturaliter posterius dependens a subiecto causaliter; et ista habitudo attestatur perfeccionem subiecti et imperfeccionem forme dependentis. Sic, inquit, simillime natura assumpta habet duplicem habitudinem ad Verbum, vel sub racione causandi sicut generaliter omnis creatura dependet a tota Trinitate; et illa habitudo presupponit in creatura posse naturale imperfeccionem sapiens, quod oportet habere actum causancie divine inseparabiliter comitantem. 2ᵃ **autem** habitudo est, qua natura assumpta secundum potenciam obediencialem potest esse natura Verbi, et illa potencia non potest actuari nisi per miraculum, et dicit quodammodo perfeccionem in persona assumente³; terminare itaque dependenciam creature secundum istam racionem est ypostatice assumere dictam creaturam.’

ENGLISH: It remains to see the [evidences]{g:evidencia} by which the [opinion]{g:opinio} contrary in words to this teaching of mine is propped up. And the [Subtle Doctor]{g:doctor-subtilis} [makes clear]{g:declarare} this opinion at the beginning of the third [book] of the *Sentences*, subtly enough.[^1] "An [accident]{g:accidens}," he says, "has a twofold [respect]{g:habitudo} to its [subject]{g:subiectum}: namely, of the informing to the informed; and such an aspect includes imperfection of the subject, because [it includes] potency to the act by which the subject is perfected; and the other respect, as naturally posterior, depending causally on the subject; and this respect attests the perfection of the subject and the imperfection of the dependent form.[^2] So," he says, "most like this, the assumed [nature]{g:natura} has a twofold respect to the [Word]{g:verbum}: either under the aspect of [causing]{g:causancia}, as generally every [creature]{g:creatura} depends on the whole Trinity; and this respect presupposes in the creature a natural capacity savouring of imperfection, which must have the act of the divine causing inseparably accompanying [it]. And the second respect is that by which the assumed nature, according to obediential [potency]{g:potencia}, can be the nature of the Word; and this potency cannot be actualised except by miracle, and it [signifies]{g:dicere} in a way perfection in the [assuming]{g:assumere} [person]{g:persona}; and so to terminate the [dependence]{g:dependencia} of the creature according to this aspect is [hypostatically]{g:ypostatice} to assume the said creature."[^3]

### 11-a ¶3 · 185.23–186.17  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 185] Et ista sentencia placet michi. Nam omne subiectum accidentis includit in se analogice racionem causantis ad [p. 186] suum causatum quod est in Deo equivoce perfeccionis simpliciter. Verumptamen omnis causancia, qua subiectum causat inherens accidens, sapit imperfeccionem subiecti, quia causanciam materialem vel subiectivam qua est in¹ potencia perfici¹ tali forma. Unde non est dare aliam singularem habitudinem, qua subiectum potest causare accidens sine hoc, quod ipso informetur. **Sed** quelibet singularis racio vel causa includit racionem analogam, que secundum illud² quod est perfeccionis simpliciter, soli Deo³ potest competere. Nec sequitur ex miraculosa transsubstanciacione in eucaristia quod vel qualitas vel inherens quantitas⁴ possit per se⁵ existere sine informacione et essenciali dependencia ad subiectum. Ad tantum ergo valet illa sentencia, quod vere ponit in qualibet racione creata lucere potenciam unde Deus potest humanitati Christi⁶ ypostatice copulari, **sed** quod aliam naturam possit assumere probari non poterit vel exemplariter edoceri.

ENGLISH: And this teaching pleases me. For every subject of an accident includes in itself, analogically, the account of a causer toward [186] its caused, which in God is, [equivocally]{g:equivoce}, [a matter] of perfection [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}.[^4] Nevertheless every causing by which a subject causes an inhering accident savours of imperfection of the subject, because [it is] material or subjective causing, by which it is in potency to be perfected by such a form. Hence [one may]{g:est-dare} not [posit] another singular respect by which a subject can cause an accident without being informed by it. But every singular account or cause includes an analogous account, which, according to that which is of [perfection without qualification]{g:perfeccio-simpliciter}, can belong to God alone. Nor does it follow from the miraculous [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio} in the eucharist that either quality or inhering quantity can exist [of itself]{g:per-se} without informing and essential dependence on a subject. To this much, therefore, that teaching avails: that it truly posits that in every created account there shines a potency whereby God can be hypostatically joined to Christ's [humanity]{g:humanitas}; but that he could assume another nature cannot be proved, or taught by example.[^5]

### 11-a ¶6 · 186.23–187.16  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 186] **Sed** in ista racione falsum petitur; cum personalis unio, qua humanitas fieret natura hominis, implicaret⁹ illam humanitatem fieri unum personaliter cum sumente. Natura enim completa substancialis cuicumque supposito est generaliter idem suppositum. Et si humanitas sit natura completa Verbi, tunc est essencia¹⁰ et substancia Verbi eo quod natura perfecta et essencia convertuntur, et si est essencia vel substancia Verbi, tunc est¹⁰ Verbum; cum cuiuslibet rei create vel increate idem est essencia vel substancia et [p. 187] res cuius est essencia. Ideo Verbum non potest facere humanitatem esse naturam Verbi, nisi fecerit ipsam esse essenciam ac substanciam Verbi, et per consequens ipsum Verbum. Nec sequitur—ista natura humana est natura Dei; ergo est natura divina—quia talia abstracta cum huiusmodi possessivis convocant¹ possessionem sui primitivi cum quadam racione reduplicandi; ut, si humanitas Christi sit essencia, natura, vel substancia divina, tunc est natura Dei sub racione qua Deus, quod solum est deitas; et si deitas sit natura humana, tunc est natura humana sub racione qua homo; et sic deitate foret Verbum formaliter homo, et humanitate Deus; quod est impossibile. Et patet quod multum refert² dicere—deitas est natura hominis— et—ipsa est natura humana; humanitas est natura Dei— et—ipsa est natura divina—; cum ille nature tam dispares conveniunt in eodem supposito.

ENGLISH: But in this argument the false is [begged]{g:petitur}; since the personal [union]{g:unio} by which the humanity would become the nature of a man would imply that that humanity becomes one personally with the one taking [it].[^7] For the complete substantial nature to any [supposit]{g:suppositum} whatever is generally the same supposit. And if the humanity is the complete nature of the Word, then it is the [essence]{g:essencia} and substance of the Word, because perfect nature and essence are [convertible]{g:convertibilis}; and if it is the essence or substance of the Word, then it is the Word; since of any thing whatever, created or uncreated, the essence or substance and [187] the thing whose essence it is are the same. Therefore the Word cannot make the humanity be the nature of the Word unless he made it be the essence and substance of the Word, and in consequence the Word himself. Nor does it follow—"this human nature is the nature of God; therefore it is the divine nature"—because such abstract [terms] with possessives of this kind call up the possession of their primitive with a certain account of reduplicating:[^8] as, if Christ's humanity is the divine essence, nature or substance, then it is the nature of God under the aspect by which he is God, which is only the [Godhead]{g:deitas}; and if the Godhead is human nature, then it is human nature under the aspect by which he is a man; and so by the Godhead the Word would formally be a man, and by the humanity God; which is impossible. And it is clear that it matters much to say "the Godhead is the nature of a man" and "it is human nature"; "the humanity is the nature of God" and "it is the divine nature"; since those natures, so disparate, agree in the same supposit.

### 11-a ¶8 · 187.22–188.13  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 187] Sine dubio verum concluditur, **sed** infidelis diceret quod minor foret pura peticio. Unde quia videmus, quod Deus producit per se multos effectus ut substancias, sicut fide capimus de mundi principio; eliciunt quidam tanquam principium quod deitas vicem cuiuscunque cause secunde supplere poterit. Et ex illo pullulant plurime vanitates; quia certum est quod nullius cause materialis vel subiective vicem deitas supplere poterit, hoc est, causare formam eadem causacione sive simillima subducto subiecto; quia tunc deitas posset esse ignea et quomodolibet informata; quod dementis est dicere. Ideo causare materialiter vel subiective non potest sibi competere; ymo nullum accidens vel formam materialem potest Deus sine causancia⁴ [p. 188] substancie per se producere: ut, sicut non potest producere motum meum, nisi ego movear hoc causando, sic de qualibet forma materiali. Ymo de accidente spirituali idem dicitur; ut non potest actum volendi creature producere nisi ipsa creatura nedum concurrat cum eo subiective causando, **sed** et eciam coagendo. Ymo si ultra perspicitur, nullius secundi agentis potest deitas vicem supplere agendo univoce. Ideo vicis supplecio, cum dicit inferioritatis condicionem vicariam, non potest deitati competere. Quando ergo per se miraculose opus perficit,¹ quod posset facere agens secundum² alia² accione in genere cum suo regimine instrumentaliter efficere, non supplet vicem agentis; nec e contra cum conformantur in genere.

ENGLISH: Without doubt something true is [inferred]{g:concludere}, but an [unbeliever]{g:infidelis} would say that the minor would be a pure begging [of the question]. Hence, because we see that God produces of himself many effects, such as substances, as we take by faith concerning the beginning of the world, some draw out as a principle that the Godhead can [supply the place]{g:vicem-supplere} of any second cause whatever. And from that sprout very many vanities; for it is certain that the Godhead can supply the place of no material or subjective cause, that is, cause a form by the same [causation]{g:causacio}, or a most similar one, with the subject withdrawn; for then the Godhead could be fiery and informed in any way whatever; which it is a madman's part to say. Therefore to cause materially or subjectively cannot belong to it; indeed God cannot produce of himself any accident or material form without the causing [188] of the substance: as, just as he cannot produce my motion unless I am moved, causing this, so of any material form. Indeed the same is said of a spiritual accident: as, he cannot produce a creature's act of willing unless the creature itself [not only]{g:nedum} concurs with him by causing subjectively, but also by co-acting. Indeed, if one looks further, the Godhead can supply the place of no second agent by acting [univocally]{g:univoce}. Therefore the supplying of a place, since it signifies a vicarious condition of inferiority, cannot belong to the Godhead.[^10] When, therefore, he of himself miraculously performs a work which a second agent could do, [and which he could] effect instrumentally, with his governance, by an action other in kind, he does not supply the place of the agent; nor conversely when they are conformed in kind.[^11]

### 11-a ¶10 · 188.17–188.25  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 188] **Sed** hic videtur michi quod consequencia sit neganda. Nam iuxta illud sequeretur quod quilibet potest esse quilibet. Nam Petrus prius origine est essencia quam substancia; ergo pro illo priori Deus posset facere eum nonsubstanciam; et, cum prius sit substancia quam corpus, et prius corpus quam animal, et prius animal quam homo, eo quod omne superius est causa sui inferioris; sequitur quod posset indifferenter fieri cuiuscunque speciei substancie tam secundum corpus quam animam.

ENGLISH: But here it seems to me that the [inference]{g:consequencia} is to be denied. For according to that it would follow that anyone can be anyone. For Peter is, prior in origin, an essence before a substance; therefore for that prior [moment] God could make him a non-substance; and, since he is a substance before a body, and a body before an animal, and an animal before a man, because every superior is cause of its inferior, it follows that he could indifferently be made of any species of substance whatever, as much as to body as to soul.

### 11-a ¶11 · 188.26–189.28  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 188] **Sed** ulterius pro materia argumenti dicitur quod, quam primo quoad consequenciam humanitas Christi est ista singularis natura, tam primo est ipsa persona Verbi. Non enim posset derelinqui non assumpta, nec post assumpcionem dimitti; quia tunc indubie, sicut incepcio humanitatis presupponeretur prioritate consequente assumpcionem,³ ita personacio creata, cum esse individuum racionalis nature sit esse personam. Et patet quod Christus prius est persona, suppositum, vel ypostasis quam ipse est natura singularis; [p. 189] **sed** generaliter e¹ contra primo, sicut substancia est natura singularis date speciei, ut Christus,¹ eque primo est suppositum humane nature, sicut est persona hominis. Ideo Christus prius est persona quam est persona hominis vel substancia singularis. Verumptamen non est ordo inter divinum suppositum² et personam divinam tanquam inter generalius et contraccius, sicut est in personalitatibus creatis. **Sed** et in illis non oportet, si Petrus sit prius suppositum substancie quam persona humane nature, quod potest³ esse suppositum non persona, ut patet ex dictis superius. Singularitas ergo Christi est posterior sua personalitate, et causatur ex condicionibus individuantibus, ut puta ex illo corpore et illa anima cum illa unione ypostatica et aliis que secuntur. Ex quo videtur michi quod Christus non potest aliam naturam assumere, quia tunc posset esse alius homo; et per consequens ista persona, ut post deducetur; quod est impossibile. Et consequencia patet ex hoc quod, si alia fuerint principia individuancia, tunc foret aliud individuum speciei humane et per consequens alius homo. Nec est color quod tempus aut aliud accidens requisitum individuat substanciam,⁴ nisi ut multo evidencius unio ypostatica individuaverat dominum Iesum Christum, ut dicit Anselmus in De Incarnacione 7°. Nec obest quod Christus sit prius suppositum et individuum speciei humane, quam habeat predictam unionem; quia, sicut prius est homo quam iste homo, sic prius est individuum speciei humane, quam hoc individuum, et prius est ista⁵ unio quam ipse sit hoc⁵ individuum.

ENGLISH: But further, for the matter of the argument, the answer is that, as early, as to inference, as Christ's humanity is this singular nature, so early is it the person of the Word itself. For it could not be left unassumed, nor be [laid aside]{g:dimittere} after the [assumption]{g:assumpcio}; for then undoubtedly, just as the [beginning]{g:incepcio} of the humanity would be presupposed [to] the assumption by a priority of consequence, so would a created [personation]{g:personacio} be, since to be an [individual]{g:individuum} of rational nature is to be a person.[^13] And it is clear that Christ is a person, supposit or [hypostasis]{g:ypostasis} before he is a singular nature; [189] but generally the reverse: as early as a substance is the singular nature of a given species, like Christ, equally early is it the supposit of human nature, as it is the person of a man. Therefore Christ is a person before he is a person of a man or a singular substance. Nevertheless there is no order between the divine supposit and the divine person as between the more general and the more contracted, as there is in created [personalities]{g:personalitas}.[^14] But in those too it is not necessary, if Peter is a supposit of substance before a person of human nature, that he can be a supposit [and] not a person, as is clear from what was said above. Christ's singularity, therefore, is posterior to his personality, and is caused from the individuating conditions, for instance from that body and that soul, with that hypostatic union and the other things that follow. From which it seems to me that Christ cannot assume another nature, because then he could be another man; and in consequence this person [could], as will be deduced later; which is impossible.[^15] And the inference is clear from this: that if there were other individuating principles, then there would be another individual of the human species, and in consequence another man. Nor is there [colour]{g:color} [for saying] that time or another requisite accident individuates the substance, except that much more evidently the hypostatic union had individuated the lord Jesus Christ, as Anselm says in *On the Incarnation*, [chapter] 7.[^16] Nor does it stand in the way that Christ is a supposit and individual of the human species before he has the said union; for, as he is a man before he is this man, so he is an individual of the human species before this individual, and this union is prior to his being this individual.[^17]

### 11-b ¶1 · 189.34–191.16  (7 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 189] Hic dicitur quod loquendo de accidente inherente, ipsum [p. 190] non poterit per se esse, cum omne tale sit modus substancie, **sed** cum¹ omnis creatura sit accidens Deo adheretque² sibi tam essencialiter sicut accidens ad subiectum; patet quod accidens potest Deus facere per se esse, non sic quin alicui, scilicet Deo, adhereat, **sed** quod nulli formaliter inhereat; sicut est de toto predicamento substancie, quod non potest sic formaliter inherere. Si tamen racio predicta procederet, argueret direccius quod homo et quelibet alia substancia poterit inherere, sicut quantitas et qualitas creduntur hodie per se esse: quod ego non video. Ideo alias dixi quod, sicut³ univocando de accidente respectivo, cuiusmodi sunt⁴ relacio, accio, passio etc., nullum illorum poterit esse nisi insit substancie ut subiecto: sic nec qualitas nec quantitas, cum sint univoce accidencia sicut illa. De qualitate consentit sanctus Thomas, quod ipsa non potest esse nisi insit quantitati. Et de quantitate est michi evidencius, cum sit substanciam esse quantam, et non potest exspectare rarefaccionem et condensacionem et ceteras mutaciones, quas in benedicta eucaristia oculariter experimur. Ideo, ut alias dixi, esset nimis magna⁵ sensus illusio, nisi esset in eucaristia unum quantum diversimode qualificatum et accidentatum ac mobile. Et illud subicitur quantitati, qualitati et ceteris accidentibus que videmus. **Sed** quoad questionem—quid est de tali essencia ?—non sollicitor; licet sint quotlibet dicta sanctorum que sonant quod sit panis vel vinum; que forte intelligenda sunt, quod essencia subiecta illis accidentibus post transsubstanciacionem est panis⁶ ante transsubstanciacionem, et virtute transsubstanciacionis desinit esse quid vel substancia, et manet eadem essencia conformiter accidentata. Illa **autem** non fit corpus Christi **sed** fit signum signans nobis ineffabiliter quod ad omnem punctum sui sit sacramentaliter corpus Christi et concomitanter⁷ anima et omnia alia Christi accidencia absoluta. Nec est [p. 191] de substancia fidei viatorum¹ scire quid est illa essencia; **sed** satis est cognoscere questionem si est de ista cum transsubstanciacione et ceteris veritatibus que sacramento eucaristie sunt annexe. Non ergo sequitur—si Christus potest facere corpus suum virtute verborum sacramentalium esse ad omnem punctum illius essencie; ergo potest facere quod humanitas non² sit suppositum, vel quomodocunque aliter per terminos magistrales aliter³ extraneetur racio—⁴ in omnibus enim similibus evidenciis est pura peticio.⁴ Illud **autem** quod de eucaristia fides orthodoxa artat nos credere potest catholicus philosophice sustinere. Si enim secundum perspectivos corpus multiplicatur intencionaliter vere presens,⁵ ubicunque species eius agitur, et habet ibi efficaciam operandi; quod magisterium Deo facere corpus suum esse ad omnem punctum hostie sacramentaliter et effectus spirituales efficaciter operari!

ENGLISH: Here [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that, speaking of an inhering [accident]{g:accidens}, it [190] cannot be of itself, since every such [accident] is a mode of substance; but since every [creature]{g:creatura} is an accident to God, and adheres to him as essentially as an accident to its [subject]{g:subiectum}, it is clear that God can make an accident be [of itself]{g:per-se}: not so that it does not adhere to something, namely to God, but so that it formally inheres in nothing; as is the case with the whole [category]{g:predicamentum} of substance, that it cannot so formally inhere.[^1] If, nevertheless, the argument stated proceeded, it would argue more directly that a man and any other substance whatever could inhere, as quantity and quality are believed today to be of themselves: which I do not see. Therefore I have said elsewhere that, as, speaking univocally of a [relational]{g:respectivus} accident, of which kind are relation, action, [being-acted-on]{g:passio}, etc., none of them can be unless it is in a substance as subject: so neither quality nor quantity, since they are accidents [univocally]{g:univoce} as those are.[^2] Of quality, Saint Thomas agrees that it cannot be unless it is in quantity. And of quantity it is more evident to me, since it is the substance's being [quantified]{g:quantus}, and it cannot admit rarefaction and condensation and the other changes which in the blessed eucharist we experience with our eyes.[^3] Therefore, as I have said elsewhere, there would be an [exceedingly]{g:nimis} great illusion of the sense, unless there were in the eucharist one quantified [thing], variously qualified and [subject to accidents]{g:accidentatus} and [movable]{g:mobilis}. And that is subjected to quantity, quality and the other accidents which we see. But as to the question—"what is such an [essence]{g:essencia}?"—I am not anxious; although there are any number of sayings of the [saints]{g:sancti} which sound [as if] it is bread or wine; which perhaps are to be understood [thus]: that the essence subject to those accidents after the [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio} is the bread [that was] before the transubstantiation, and by virtue of the transubstantiation ceases to be a what, or a substance, and remains the same essence, conformably subject to accidents.[^4] And it does not become the body of Christ, but becomes a [sign]{g:signum} signifying to us ineffably that at every point of itself it is sacramentally the body of Christ, and concomitantly the soul and all the other [absolute accidents]{g:accidens-absolutum} of Christ. Nor is it [191] of the substance of the faith of [wayfarers]{g:viator} to know what that essence is; but it is enough to know the question "whether it is" of it, together with the transubstantiation and the other truths which are annexed to the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} of the eucharist.[^5] It does not [follow]{g:sequitur}, therefore—"if Christ can make his body, by virtue of the sacramental words, be at every point of that essence; therefore he can make the [humanity]{g:humanitas} not be a [supposit]{g:suppositum}," or in whatever other way the account is [estranged]{g:extraneare} by [masters' terms]{g:termini-magistrales}—for in all similar [evidences]{g:evidencia} there is pure [begging]{g:peticio} [of the question].[^6] And what the orthodox faith constrains us to believe of the eucharist, a catholic can uphold philosophically. For if, according to the [perspectivists]{g:perspectivi}, a body is multiplied intentionally, truly present wherever its species is driven, and has there efficacy of working; what a [feat of mastery]{g:magisterium} for God to make his body be at every point of the host sacramentally and to work spiritual effects efficaciously![^7]

### 11-b ¶3 · 191.22–192.8  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 191] **Sed** hic dicitur quod loquendo de unione ypostatica oppositum sequitur ex assumpto. Unde omnes iste evidencie confirmare possunt fidelem credere quod duas naturas potest Deus unire personaliter in eadem ypostasi ad sensum expositum. **Sed** quod Verbum assumens non sit natura assumpta, nec iste raciones nec alie possibiles⁷ poterunt⁷ edocere. Unde quam mirum est quod natura divina et humana tam distantes sint utraque eadem persona, tam mirum est et idem in numero, quod Deus sit homo. Unde accidencium ad subiecta non est unio ypostatica, cum sint essencie a subiecto generaliter condistincte sine communicacione ydiomatum, ut substancia fiat tale accidens vel e contra. Ideo sicut in materia de Trinitate trinitas creata [p. 192] memorie, racionis, et voluntatis anime est inter omnes hucusque inventas simillima pro Trinitate increata viatoribus cognoscenda; sic in materia de Incarnacione ypostatica unio creati spiritus ad naturam corpoream, ut utrumque sit eadem persona hominis, est inter alias plus propinqua; nec possunt,¹ si non fallor, fieri evidencie ut, vel probetur talis impossibilitas unionis, vel quod Deus aliam naturam possit assumere, aut assumptam dimittere.

ENGLISH: But here the answer is that, speaking of the [hypostatic]{g:ypostatice} [union]{g:unio}, the opposite follows from the [assumption]{g:assumptum}.[^9] Hence all these evidences can confirm a believer in believing that God can unite two natures personally in the same [hypostasis]{g:ypostasis}, in the sense set out. But that the [assuming]{g:assumere} [Word]{g:verbum} is not the assumed nature, neither these arguments nor other possible ones will be able to teach. Hence, as wonderful as it is that the divine and the human nature, so distant, are each the same [person]{g:persona}, so wonderful it is, and the same in number, that God is a man.[^10] Hence there is no hypostatic union of accidents to subjects, since they are essences generally distinct from the subject, without [communication of idioms]{g:communicacio-ydiomatum}, so that a substance becomes such an accident or conversely. Therefore, as in the matter of the Trinity the created trinity [192] of memory, reason and will of the soul is, among all found hitherto, the most similar for the uncreated Trinity to be known by wayfarers; so in the matter of the Incarnation the hypostatic union of a created spirit to a bodily nature, so that each is the same person of a man, is nearer than the others; nor can evidences be made, if I am not mistaken, either that such a union be proved impossible, or that God could assume another nature, or [lay aside]{g:dimittere} the one assumed.[^11]

### 11-b ¶4 · 192.9–192.14  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 192] **Sed** instatur per hoc, quod gracia unionis foret gracia maxima possibilis creature, ut illa humanitas fiat Deus; et omnis gracia creata presupponit inesse tam gratificatum quam ipsum gratificans; ergo gracia unionis presupponit naturam assumptam, et per consequens ipsa natura prius est quam de facto assumitur.

ENGLISH: But [it is objected]{g:instatur} by this: that the [grace of union]{g:gracia-unionis} would be the greatest grace possible to a creature, that that humanity becomes God; and every created grace presupposes, as being, both the one made pleasing and that which itself makes pleasing; therefore the grace of union presupposes the assumed nature, and in consequence the nature itself is before it is in fact assumed.[^12]

### 11-b ¶5 · 192.15–193.16  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 192] Hic dicitur quod conclusio est concedenda, cum omne per se superius sit prius natura vel origine quam suum inferius; et per consequens et² prius quam passio vel accidens sibi inest. **Sed** ex hoc non sequitur quod illa humanitas potest esse non assumpta, etsi sit non assumpta pro illa mensura nature sive originis. **Sed** notandum quod duplex est gracia unionis. Prima increata, qua Verbum voluit eternaliter humanitatem illam sibi ypostatice copulare; et illa potest intelligi tripliciter, scilicet essencialiter, personaliter, sive formaliter, ut patet alibi. 2ᵈᵃ **autem** est gracia creata qua humanitas est sic formaliter³ grata Deo. Et ista gracia licet sit natura⁴ vel origine posterior⁵ quam subiectum, non tamen est possibile quod dicta humanitas temporaliter ipsam procedat sic quod mereatur graciam unionis; sicut Augustinus innuit Enchiridion 29°, cum non sit possibile ipsam manere per instans temporis⁶ non assumptam. Et sicut in aliis hominibus presupponitur gracia preveniens ad meritum, sic in Christo gracia unionis presupponitur naturaliter ad Christi meritum, etsi meritum [p. 193] pro instanti unionis naturaliter consequatur. Et istum credo esse sensum Augustini 17° De Civitate Dei ca°... ‘In rebus, inquit, per tempus exortis summa gracia est quod Deus in unitate persone homini nullis precedentibus meritis copulatur;’ non quod gracia sit membrum in corpore vel anima colligans unionem; **sed** quia ista unio sit gracia non preventa habitu disponente. Unde illa unio cum sit relacio racionis est posterius subiecta humanitate quoad genus cause materialis, et tamen est efficienter¹ prius quam Christus est hoc singulare vel individuum hominis, ut tangitur superius. **Sed** caveatur de prioritate² temporis aut prioritate² quoad consequenciam, cum omnia ista pro omni tempore Christi se mutuo consequantur. Unde Augustinus Enchiridion dicit quod ista unio fuit illi homini quodammodo naturalis, cum iste homo non poterit esse sine illa.

ENGLISH: Here the answer is that the [conclusion]{g:conclusio} is to be granted, since every superior of itself is prior by nature or origin to its inferior; and in consequence also prior to the [attribute]{g:passio} or accident's being in it. But from this it does not follow that that humanity can be unassumed, even if it is unassumed for that measure of nature or origin. But it is to be noted that the grace of union is twofold. The first uncreated, by which the Word willed eternally to join that humanity hypostatically to himself; and this can be understood in three ways, namely essentially, personally or formally, as is clear elsewhere. And the second is the created grace by which the humanity is so formally pleasing to God. And this grace, although it is posterior by nature or origin to its subject, yet it is not possible that the said humanity should precede it in time so that it might merit the grace of union; as Augustine intimates, *Enchiridion* 29, since it is not possible that it should remain for an instant of time unassumed.[^13] And as in other men [prevenient grace]{g:gracia-preveniens} is presupposed to merit, so in Christ the grace of union is presupposed naturally to Christ's merit, even if the merit [193] follows naturally for the instant of the union. And this I believe to be the sense of Augustine, *The City of God* 17, chapter …: "In things arisen through time," he says, "the highest grace is that God is joined to man in unity of person, with no merits going before";[^14] not that grace is a member in body or soul binding the union; but because this union is a grace not preceded by a disposing habit. Hence that union, since it is a [relation of reason]{g:relacio-racionis}, is posterior to the subject, the humanity, as to the genus of material cause, and yet it is efficiently prior to Christ's being this singular or [individual]{g:individuum} of a man, as is touched on above. But let one beware of priority of time or priority as to [inference]{g:consequencia}, since all these, for every time of Christ, follow on one another mutually. Hence Augustine in the *Enchiridion* says that this union was to that man in a way natural, since this man could not be without it.[^15]

### 11-b ¶6 · 193.17–193.31  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 193] Et si queritur de subiecto eius primo dicitur quod anima est subiectum primum unionis creati spiritus, non quod sit infusus habitus, **sed** habitudo relativa. Alia **autem** unio corporis ad Verbum subiectatur multiplicative in corpore sicut filiacio temporalis, que est habitudo secundum quam cognitum³ causatum naturaliter est productum a suo simili in natura. Et per istas particulas patet quod Spiritus sanctus non est Filius Patris, nec vermis est filius hominis, ut docet Augustinus Enchiridion 3°, nec inanimatum est filius sui naturaliter generantis. Correspondenter unio est habitudo, secundum quam una natura alteri ydemptitate ypostatica copulatur; et illa dupliciter secundum diversitatem nature increate assumentis et nature create suppositis copulate. Nec est extraneum vocare illam unionem graciam cum sit donum perfectissimum gratis datum.

ENGLISH: And if it is asked about its first subject, the answer is that the soul is the first subject of the union of the created spirit, not that it is an infused habit, but a relative [respect]{g:habitudo}. And the other union, of the body to the Word, is subjected [multiplicatively]{g:multiplicative} in the body, like temporal [sonship]{g:filiacio}, which is the respect according to which a cognitive caused thing is naturally produced from its like in nature.[^16] And through these particles it is clear that the Holy Spirit is not the Son of the Father, nor is a worm the son of a man, as Augustine teaches, *Enchiridion* 3, nor is an inanimate thing the son of what naturally generates it.[^17] Correspondingly, union is the respect according to which one nature is joined to another by [hypostatic identity]{g:ydemptitas-ypostatica}; and that [is] twofold, according to the diversity of the uncreated nature assuming and of the created nature joined to the supposits. Nor is it strange to call that union a grace, since it is a most perfect gift freely given.

ENGLISH: Thirdly, it is said in consequence that at the end of that hour any number of men will come to be, and yet there will not come to be anything, nor anything positive of any kind, on the supposition that any number of natures now assumed [195] are laid aside, without being moved, first at the end of the hour. Hence, with every change taken away except such alternate laying-aside and re-assumption, there would be time and a wondrous [successive]{g:successivum} motion. Nor will they deny that God can, with any other motion whatever taken away, alternate changes of this kind, since they depend only on the free volition of God; and without doubt, since one would be prior to the other [not only]{g:nedum} by nature but in time, there would be a continuous temporal succession.[^6]

### 11-c ¶7 · 195.18–195.27  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 195] 5to concedunt in casu quod⁷ in ista hora generabuntur vel fient infiniti homines et nichil illorum generabitur vel fiet ab aliquo, **sed** nec Deus sciet quando aliquis illorum hominum erit, ut divisa⁸ ista hora in suas partes proporcionales minores versus ultimum instans et quod in cuiuslibet illarum parcium prima medietate Deus dimittat unam, et in secunda reassumat reliquam dimittendo, servando in eis omnia positiva: tunc patet conclusio, cum nunquam erit ita⁹ quod isti homines sunt, et per consequens non erit aliquid de numero istorum hominum.

### 11-c ¶9 · 196.12–196.30  (2 sed/autem)

For God the Father cannot be generated divinely, nor would he be generated [humanly]{g:humanitus} except at the generation of the nature for the time at which it was assumed, which does not befit [one] old or blessed. As, then, the Son acquires places by moving humanly, so the Father, acquiring and filling those places bodily, would in a way be moved. ### 11-c ¶10 · 196.31–197.29  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 196] 8° concedunt de possibili quod Petrus in infinitum [p. 197] remittetur¹ inmerito² et nunquam intendetur³ quousque fuerit infinitum Deo carior quam est modo; Paulus **autem** per idem tempus in infinitum intendetur³ inmerito et tamen⁴ in eodem fine temporis⁵ sine suo demerito erit Deo quantumlibet minus⁶ carus; ex quo videtur quod apud Deum sit excepcio personarum; posito quod Petrus per istam horam remittetur usque ad non gradum meriti exclusive et in fine assumatur a Verbo ypostatice; Paulus **autem** per idem tempus assumptus continue mereatur et in fine dimittatur: tunc patet conclusio, petito hoc notorio principio quod non est possibile Deum tantum diligere naturam hominis non assumptam sicut naturam propriam quam assumit: tunc posito quod infinitum bonus sit aliquis homo non assumptus, adhuc melior et Deo carior est quecunque humanitas quam assumit. Et revera⁷ talia videntur michi dissona: scilicet quod homo Petrus demereatur continue, quousque fuerit infinitum Deo carus; et Paulus continue mereatur, quousque decidat a summa amicicia Dei sui; cum sceleratissimus peccator contingentissime, ut asserunt, potest assumi⁸; et cum ignorare poterit assumpcionem propriam, non est evidencia dans fidem quin quelibet creatura ypostatice unibilis sit assumpta. Et tunc non video quomodo quis convinceretur ex ydololatria vel cognosceret quiditatem aliquam creature. Concedunt enim quod Deus potest esse lapis et speciei abiectissime creature, potest esse dampnatus, quod est perfeccius. **Sed** procul a fidelibus ista ludicra venenosa. Unde Augustinus 83 Vet.⁹ et Nov. Test.⁹ Questionum questione 5ª dicit quod nullum animal irracionale potest esse beatum, et multo evidencius nulla inanimata substancia.

ENGLISH: Eighthly, they grant as possible that Peter will be [197] [remitted]{g:remitti} in merit to infinity and never [intensified]{g:intendi} until he is infinitely dearer to God than he is now; and Paul through the same time will be intensified in merit to infinity, and yet at the same end of the time, without demerit of his own, will be as much less dear to God as you please; from which it seems that with God there is exception of persons:[^12] on the supposition that Peter through this hour is remitted down to no degree of merit, exclusively, and at the end is assumed by the Word [hypostatically]{g:ypostatice}; and Paul, assumed through the same time, continually merits and at the end is laid aside. Then the conclusion is clear, granting this well-known principle: that it is not possible that God should love a nature of man not assumed as much as the nature, his own, which he assumes.[^13] Then, supposing that some man not assumed is infinitely good, still better and dearer to God is any humanity whatever which he assumes. And in truth such things seem to me discordant: namely that the man Peter demerits continually until he is infinitely dear to God, and Paul continually merits until he falls from the highest friendship of his God; since the most wicked sinner can, most contingently, as they assert, be assumed; and since he could be ignorant of his own assumption, there is no [evidence]{g:evidencia} giving faith against any creature whatever that can be hypostatically united being assumed.[^14] And then I do not see how anyone would be convicted of idolatry, or would know the quiddity of any creature. For they grant that God can be a stone, and [of] the species of the most abject creature, [and] can be damned, which is more perfect.[^15] But far from the faithful be these poisonous games. Hence Augustine, in the Eighty-three Questions, question 5, says that no irrational animal can be blessed, and much more evidently no inanimate substance.[^16]

### 11-d ¶1 · 197.30–199.2  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 197] 9° videtur de possibili concedendum quod Deus faciendo tres homines eternos, qui non possunt a Deo fieri, tantum perfecit genus humanum, sicut potuit de sua potencia absoluta, licet non plures homines quam illos tres potuit produxisse. [p. 198] Assigno totam multitudinem hominum a Deo producibilium¹ quoad intellectum divinum, et pono² quod tota Trinitas quamlibet talem naturam assumat ypostatice; tunc patet quod natura divina fecit trinitatem, tres homines, quos oportet sic opinantes dicere eternos homines, cum non habeant limitandum peryodum temporalem. Et secunda pars gratis conceditur cum dicunt hominem Iesum non posse fieri. Et tercia pars³ patet ex hoc quod tam perfectum est genus sicut aliquod individuum speciei; et⁴ infinitum perfectus est omnis homo in casu posito; ergo infinitum perfectum genus hominum; ymo, si consideretur, omnis homo foret infinitum perfectus humanitus, quia infinitam perfectam multitudinem tam animarum⁵ quam corporum tanquam partes proprias contineret; ergo infinitum perfectus humanitus foret omnis homo, quia infinitum perfeccior quam data anima vel corpus finitum; patet itaque quod non meliores homines⁶ vel magis graciosos plus Deum amantes concorditer aut plus beatos in corpore et anima omnino impeccabiles et imperfectibiles perfecciores omnimode quam poterit esse aliqua creatura. Et ultima particula patet illis, qui ponunt naturam assumptam esse hominem. Nam si Deus non potest assumere materiam primam vel formam materialem in unitatem suppositi nisi ydemptificaverit⁷ se illi nature, eo quod non est aliquid cuius ipsa est natura; per idem non potest aliam naturam assumere nisi ydemptificaverit se eidem, cum indifferenter cuiuslibet nature create quiditas sit natura. **Sed** iuxta sentenciam sancti Thome ponent⁸ Verbum posse esse multos homines per idem: nam infiniti homines possunt esse isti tres homines, et per consequens non plures istis tribus hominibus possunt esse. 3° confirmatur ex hoc quod unus illorum trium hominum cum sit Pater in divinis, est omnino incausabilis⁹ et improducibilis:⁹ [p. 199] ergo non plures quam isti tres poterunt esse; quia, data affirmativa¹ opposita, deficit positum.²

ENGLISH: Ninthly, it seems it must be granted as possible that God, by making three eternal men, who cannot be made by God, perfected the human race as much as he could by his [absolute power]{g:potencia-absoluta}, although he could not have produced more men than those three.[^1] [198] I assign the whole multitude of men producible by God as to the divine intellect, and I suppose that the whole Trinity [assumes]{g:assumere} each such nature [hypostatically]{g:ypostatice}: then it is clear that the divine [nature]{g:natura} made a trinity, three men, whom those who so opine must call eternal men, since they have no temporal period to be bounded.[^2] And the second part is granted freely, since they say "the man Jesus cannot be made". And the third part is clear from this: that the race is as perfect as any [individual]{g:individuum} of the species, and every man is infinitely perfect in the case supposed; therefore the race of men is infinitely perfect. Indeed, if it is considered, every man would be infinitely perfect [humanly]{g:humanitus}, since he would contain an infinitely perfect multitude of souls as well as of bodies as his own parts; therefore every man would be infinitely perfect humanly, since [he would be] infinitely more perfect than a given soul or finite body. It is clear, then, that [there could] not [be] better men, or more gracious, loving God more concordantly, or more blessed in body and soul, wholly unable to sin and unable to be perfected [further], more perfect in every way than any [creature]{g:creatura} could be.[^3] And the last particle is clear to those who hold "the assumed nature is a man". For if God cannot assume [prime matter]{g:materia-prima} or a material form into the unity of a [supposit]{g:suppositum} unless he has [identified]{g:ydemptificare} himself with that nature, because there is nothing of which it is the nature, by the same token he cannot assume another nature unless he has identified himself with it, since indifferently of any created nature the [quiddity]{g:quiditas} is [its] nature.[^4] But according to the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of St Thomas they will hold "the [Word]{g:verbum} can be many men" by the same token: for infinitely many men can be these three men, and consequently not more [men] than these three men can be.[^5] Thirdly, this is confirmed from this: that one of those three men, since he is the Father [among the divine]{g:in-divinis} [persons], is wholly [uncausable]{g:incausabilis} and unproducible: [199] therefore not more than these three can be; for, the opposite affirmative being granted, what was posited fails.[^6]

### 11-d ¶2 · 199.3–200.4  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 199] 10° eliciuntur in casu sic opinanti possibili denominaciones inconpossibiles³ de eodem; posito quod tota Trinitas assumat ypostatice universitatem creabilem vel creatam. In quo casu videtur primo quod, posita eius possibilitate, non debet a catholico negari⁴ simpliciter: cum catholicus debet sustinere ut maximam quod, sicut Deus sit tam bonus intrinsecus sicut aliquid potest intelligi vel eciam cogitari, sic tante perficit universitatem creatam quante potest sustineri quod ipsam perficiat sine⁵ repugnancia racionis, experiencie, vel scripture.⁵ **Sed** sine repugnancia alicuius istorum sustineri poterit quod Deus universitatem creatam ad tantum perficiat; ergo quilibet catholicus debet illam sentenciam sustinere. Non enim⁶ repugnat racioni pocius quam ipso de possibili posito repugnaret; nec experiencie repugnat cum nullus sensus humanus sciat discutere utrum Deus sit⁷ lapis, asinus,⁷ et sic de ceteris creaturis; nec scriptura de dampnacione hominum vel peccatis aut aliis obviat ne sic fiat, quia post peccatum potest Deus dampnandum assumere; eo quod minus videtur Deo contrarium quod sit racionalis homo vel angelus, quam quod sit omnis bestia hic sit quantificatus, figuratus, senex, agens, motus, et sic de aliis denominacionibus tam substancialibus quam accidentalibus, ibi **autem** opposite formatus. Nec valet dicere quod posito hoc de facto scriptura sacra illud exprimeret, quia Paulus ‘audivit archana que non licet homini loqui’: ymo ut diceretur ex scriptura hoc sequitur,⁸ cum Deus summe potens et non invidus vel avarus ex bonitate sua non potest se ab ista beneficiencia continere.⁹ Nam iuxta argumentum Veritatis Matt. 7. ‘si vos, cum sitis mali, nostis¹⁰ bona data dare filiis vestris; quanto magis Pater celestis dabit spiritum bonum petentibus se?’ ista consequencia non valeret, si [p. 200] homo posset dimittere beneficienciam quam facillime posset perficere instar¹ Dei: tunc enim posset Deus ex libertate de obligacionis nulli hominum² sequencium² tribuere quamlibet promerenti.

ENGLISH: Tenthly, there are drawn out, in a case possible for one who so opines, [incompossible]{g:incompossibilis} [namings]{g:denominacio} of the same [thing]: supposing that the whole Trinity assumes hypostatically the creatable or [created totality]{g:universitas-creata}.[^7] In which case it seems, first, that, its possibility being posited, it ought not to be denied by a catholic [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}: since a catholic ought to maintain as a maxim that, as God is as good intrinsically as anything can be understood or even thought, so he perfects the created totality as greatly as it can be maintained that he perfects it without [incompatibility]{g:repugnancia} with reason, experience, or scripture.[^8] Now without incompatibility with any of these it could be maintained that God perfects the created totality to that degree; therefore every catholic ought to maintain that teaching. For it is not incompatible with reason, any more than it would be were it itself posited as possible; nor is it incompatible with experience, since no human sense knows how to determine whether God is a stone, an ass, and so of the other creatures; nor does scripture, on the damnation of men or on sins or other things, stand in the way of its being so, since after sin God can assume one who is to be damned; because it seems less contrary to God that he be a rational man or angel than that he be every beast, here quantified, shaped, old, acting, moved, and so of the other namings, substantial as well as accidental, but there formed in the opposite way.[^9] Nor does it avail to say that, if this were posited in fact, holy scripture would express it, since Paul "heard secret [things] which it is not granted to man to utter": indeed, as might be said, this follows from scripture, since God, supremely powerful and not envious or avaricious, cannot from his goodness hold himself back from this beneficence.[^10] For according to the argument of the Truth, Matthew 7, "if you, being evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will the heavenly Father give the good Spirit to them that ask him?", that [inference]{g:consequencia} would not hold if [200] a man could forgo a beneficence which he could very easily perform after the manner of God: for then God could, out of a freedom from obligation, grant to none of the men who follow, however much he deserved.[^11]

### 11-d ¶3 · 200.5–201.17  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 200] Tales **autem** dilapsus vani ficti ex amplitudine divine potencie forent nidi³ hereticis ad hereses periculosissimas seminandum. Descendendo **autem** in speciali concedunt in tali casu quod Deus est quelibet eius pars quantitativa infinitum modica, et infinitum longa et lata; et ita, sicut est ‘primus et novissimus,’ sic est maximus et minimus possibilis, longissimus, latissimus, brevissimus, et indivisibilis quoad molem. Cum enim sit omnis ignis et omne corpus omogenium et⁴ ethrogenium, patet quod foret totus mundus multiplicatus dimensionaliter ubique, et sic nichil per se in genere foret in denominacione aliqua maius aut minus reliquo; cum quidlibet tale sit Deus, et per consequens nichil haberet plures partes quantitativas quam tres, que sunt Trinitas increata, super quibus cuncta sunt posita. Addunt eciam quod in casu posito acciones et passiones et cetere forme personales multiplicarentur concomitanter ubique consequenter ad immensam co-existenciam Trinitatis; et sic non est motus successivus; et per consequens neque tempus, cum prius ac posterius in situ deficiant; et sic de multis quibus ego noc sufficerem assentire. Quomodo queso quelibet pars quantitativa corporis foret essencialiter⁵ idem cuilibet? cum tunc pars foret non-pars; quia omne corpus indivisibile, et nulla pars minor aut maior⁶ reliqua; quia essencia divina et Trinitas benedicta, et sic mundus et quelibet pars eius quantitativa summe perfecta; quia realiter ipse Deus et sic tota Trinitas, si foret quelibet pars quantitativa corporis, differret quotlibet differenciis a seipsa. Deduccio omnium istorum patet ex possibilitate casus et principiis opinionis. Videtur enim multis quod [p. 201] sicut Verbum¹ potest esse dominus² sibimet et minister, ac tota generis humani cognacio, sic potest esse totus ignis et quelibet eius pars quantitativa: nec credo racionem diversitatis possibiliter assignandam. 2do dicunt quod, sicut Verbum est tantum unus homo,³ sic quod non multi, ponendo quod mille vel quotlibet humanitates assumpsit sic est tantum unus ignis licet infinitas igneitates assumpserit. Quod non dicunt illi qui ponunt Verbum in casu illo⁴ esse multos homines. Et patet plane ranga⁵ inconveniencium supradicta, cum omnis substancia per se in genere foret Pater, Filius, vel Spiritus sanctus: et sic utrobique illa tria⁶ supposita simul omnes substancie et omnia accidentibus informata sic quod, ubicunque est unus ignis, sunt tres ignes solummodo, qui sunt omnes. Infinitatem **autem** inconveniencium que secuntur videtur michi stultum deducere, cum gignit sollicitudinem apparencie sine fructu.

ENGLISH: And such vain slips, feigned from the breadth of the divine [power]{g:potencia}, would be nests for heretics for the sowing of most dangerous heresies.[^12] And, descending to the particular, they grant that in such a case God is each [quantitative part]{g:pars-quantitativa} of it, infinitely small, and infinitely long and broad; and so, as he is "the First and the Last", so he is the greatest and least possible, longest, broadest, shortest, and indivisible as to bulk. For since he is every fire and every body, homogeneous and heterogeneous, it is clear that the whole world would be multiplied dimensionally everywhere, and so nothing [that is] [of itself]{g:per-se} in a genus would in any naming be greater or less than another; since every such thing is God, and consequently nothing would have more quantitative parts than three, which are the uncreated Trinity, on which all things are set.[^13] They add, too, that in the case supposed actions and [beings-acted-on]{g:passio} and the other personal forms would be multiplied concomitantly everywhere, consequently on the immense co-existence of the Trinity; and so there is no successive motion, and consequently no time either, since before and after in position would be lacking; and so of many things to which I could not bring myself to assent.[^14] How, I ask, would each quantitative part of a body be essentially the same as each? since then a part would be a non-part; for every body [would be] indivisible, and no part less or greater than another; for [each would be] the divine essence and the blessed Trinity, and so the world and each quantitative part of it supremely perfect; for really God himself, and so the whole Trinity, if he were each quantitative part of a body, would differ from himself by any number of differences. The [deduction]{g:deduccio} of all these is clear from the possibility of the case and the principles of the [opinion]{g:opinio}. For it seems to many that, [201] as the Word can be lord to himself and servant, and the whole kindred of the human race, so he can be the whole fire and each quantitative part of it: nor do I believe a reason for the difference can possibly be assigned.[^15] Secondly, they say that, as the Word is only one man, so that [he is] not many, supposing that he assumed a thousand or any number of [humanities]{g:humanitas}, so he is only one fire, although he assumed infinitely many [firenesses]{g:igneitas}. Which those do not say who hold that the Word in that case is many men.[^16] And the row of [absurdities]{g:inconveniens} above is plainly clear, since every substance of itself in a genus would be Father, Son, or Holy Spirit: and so on both sides those three supposits [would be] at once all substances and all things informed by accidents, so that wherever there is one fire there are three fires only, which are all [the fires]. But to deduce the infinity of absurdities that follow seems to me foolish, since it begets anxiety for appearance without fruit.[^17]

### 12-a ¶2 · 201.18–202.27  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 201] **Sed** descendendum est ad trimembrem posicionem modernorum qui inani sollicitudine venantur⁷ quid contingeret si Christus multas humanitates assumeret. Falsitas enim [p. 202] dissonat sibi ipsi, teste Gregorio 12 Moralium ca° 14. ‘Alius, inquit, relicta veritate mentiri deliberat ut audiencium animum fallat. **Sed** quantus labor est custodire sollicite, ne ipsa eius fallacia deprehendi queat! Ponit quippe ante oculos quid aliis veritatem scientibus responderi possit; et cum magno cogitatu pertractat quomodo per argumenta falsitatis documenta¹ veritatis exsuperet; hinc inde se circumtegit; et contra hoc, ubi deprehendi potuerit, veritati similem responsionem querit; qui si utrobique verum diceret, hucusque² sine labore potuisset. Plana quippe est veritatis via et grave est iter mendacii.’³ Et indubie, sicut in agilibus, sic in speculabilibus verificatur ista sentencia; et patet tam exemplariter quam eciam racione. Si, inquam, consideremus pictacias quibus negantes universalia, extensionem temporis, et veritates de anima cum sibi similibus pregravantur, in quot errores incidunt, quot subterfugia querunt, et quam onerose locuntur, ut sub falsitatis pallio simulent veritatem; patesceret in speculabilibus sentencia Gregorii supradicta. **Sed** et racio illud idem exigit. Veritas enim obiective movet⁴ et instruit ad ipsam vere cognoscendum in animo et proporcionaliter proferendum: sed⁵ ad exprimendum⁵ falsitatem deficit obiectum huiusmodi in natura. Ideo difficultatur falsidicus pallians non existens, ac si absque fundamento vel exemplari aliquis⁶ intendit unum non ens vel vacuum fabricare. Unde et illud potest patescere in varietate sentenciarum de incarnacione hodie ventilata.

ENGLISH: But we must descend to the three-membered [position]{g:posicio} of the [moderns]{g:moderni}, who with empty anxiety hunt for what would happen if Christ assumed many humanities.[^1] For falsehood [202] is discordant with itself, as Gregory witnesses, *Morals* 12, chapter 14: "Another," he says, "having abandoned the truth, resolves to lie so as to deceive the mind of his hearers. But how great a labour it is to guard anxiously lest his very deceit be detected! For he sets before his eyes what could be answered by others who know the truth; and with great thought he works out how, by the arguments of falsehood, he may overcome the proofs of truth; he covers himself on this side and that; and against this, where he could be detected, he seeks an answer like the truth; who, if he spoke the truth on both sides, could surely have [done it] without labour. For plain is the way of truth, and heavy is the road of lying."[^2] And without doubt, as in matters of action, so in speculative matters this teaching [holds true]{g:verificari}; and it is clear both by example and also by reason. If, I say, we consider the patches with which those who deny [universals]{g:universale}, the extension of time, and the truths about the soul, with their like, are weighed down; into how many errors they fall, how many subterfuges they seek, and how burdensomely they speak, so as to simulate truth under the cloak of falsehood: Gregory's teaching above would become plain in speculative matters.[^3] But reason too demands the same. For truth moves objectively, and instructs toward knowing it truly in the mind and uttering it proportionately; now for expressing falsehood such an object is lacking in nature. Therefore the false speaker, [cloaking]{g:palliare} what does not exist, is put in difficulty, as if someone, without foundation or exemplar, intended to build a non-being or a void.[^4] Hence this too can become plain in the variety of teachings on the incarnation aired today.

### 12-a ¶3 · 202.28–203.6  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 202] Ponunt enim hi quod quamlibet naturam creatam quelibet persona divina indifferenter potest assumere: hi quod omnem et solam substanciam per se entem: et hi quod omnem et solam creaturam racionalem beatificabilem: et sic de quotlibet subdicionibus et evidenciis, quas nobis, qui ponimus solum Verbum pure Dominum Iesum Christum posse assumere, [p. 203] non expedit recitare; **sed** in difficultate, si Verbum multas humanitates assumeret, moderni doctores senciunt satis opposite. **Sed** pro ulteriori declaracione huius materie iuxta¹ ponam istorum sentencias, ut videatur quomodo exorbitantes a seipsis dissonant, et veritas rectissime in omnibus consonat sibi ipsi.

ENGLISH: For these hold that any divine [person]{g:persona} can indifferently [assume]{g:assumere} any created [nature]{g:natura}; these, every and only substance that is [of itself]{g:per-se}; and these, every and only rational creature that can be beatified: and so of any number of subdivisions and [evidences]{g:evidencia}, which it is not expedient for us, who hold that only the [Word]{g:verbum} can assume, [and] purely the Lord Jesus Christ, [203] to recite; but on the difficulty, if the Word assumed many humanities, the [modern doctors]{g:moderni-doctores} feel quite oppositely.[^5] But for the further making clear of this matter I shall set their teachings side by side, so that it may be seen how those who leave the track are discordant with themselves, and truth is most rightly in harmony with itself in all things.[^6]

### 12-a ¶5 · 203.24–204.10  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 203] **Sed** quia sanctus Thomas sicut et alii moderni doctores discordant a principiis iam assumptis, ideo oportet arguere contra possibilitatem suppositi aliis evidenciis. Videtur enim quod Christus posset esse quantumlibet monstruosus, quod esse non potest, cum minimum inconveniens sit Deo impossibile; et consequencia videtur ex hoc; quod, mille capitibus incommunicantibus existentibus in eodem corpore⁷ humano et⁷ proporcionaliter de aliis membris organicis, foret ille homo evidentissime monstrum magnum; **sed** hoc potest esse de Christo, cum omnem humanitatem potest [p. 204] simul assumere: ergo conclusio. Si enim Verbum totam unam cognacionem assumpserit tam maris quam femine, adhuc foret per adversarios solummodo unus homo, et per consequens tantum unum animal ac unum corpus; et super isto forent omnia dicta membra¹ monstruosa² ut estimo. Unde credo quod sanctus Thomas negans multiplicacionem dimensionalem eiusdem corporis, negaret in casu isto quod Christus est solummodo unus homo; nedum quod tunc foret hermofrodita,³ pater et filius, et tota cognacio; verum quia idem⁴ corpus foret dimensionaliter per loca distancia.

ENGLISH: But because St Thomas, like the other modern doctors too, departs from the principles already assumed, it is therefore necessary to argue against the possibility of the [case] supposed with other evidences.[^10] For it seems that Christ could be as monstrous as you please, which cannot be, since the least [absurdity]{g:inconveniens} is impossible for God; and the [inference]{g:consequencia} appears from this: that, with a thousand heads [that do not communicate]{g:incommunicans} existing in the same human body, and proportionately of the other organic members, that man would most evidently be a great monster; now this can be [so] of Christ, since he can assume every humanity [204] at once: therefore the [conclusion]{g:conclusio}. For if the Word assumed the whole of one kindred, male as well as female, he would still, according to the adversaries, be only one man, and consequently only one animal and one body; and on this [body] all the said members would be monstrous, as I reckon.[^11] Hence I believe that St Thomas, denying the [dimensional multiplication]{g:multiplicacio-dimensionalis} of the same body, would deny in this case "Christ is only one man"; [not only]{g:nedum} because he would then be a hermaphrodite, father and son, and the whole kindred, but because the same body would be dimensionally in distant places.[^12]

### 12-a ¶6 · 204.11–204.14  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 204] **Sed** hic dicitur quod monstruositas et impossibilitas multiplicacionis dimensionalis intelligitur secundum idem corpus; **sed** in casu posito foret secundum corpora multum disparia habitus talium situum et membrorum.

ENGLISH: But here [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that monstrosity and the impossibility of dimensional multiplication are understood as to the same body; but in the case supposed [the having]{g:habitus} of such positions and members would be as to very disparate bodies.[^13]

### 12-a ¶8 · 205.15–205.24  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 205] Item, si Christus potest simul habere⁶ humanitates tam dispariter accidentatas cum hoc quod maneat idem homo, tunc posset pro diversis sitibus habere denominaciones contrarias; et per consequens, coextensis illis humanitatibus, forent accidencia contraria coextensa; et sic idem simul et semel contrariis accidentibus informatum. **Sed** hoc modicum videtur modernis, si secundum diversas naturas eidem supposito ista insint. Unde concedunt quod qualescunque forme contrarie per eundem situm et idem suppositum, **sed** secundum diversas naturas, coextendi poterunt.

ENGLISH: Again, if Christ can at once have humanities so disparately subject to accidents, with this: that he remains the same man, then he could, for diverse positions, have contrary namings; and consequently, those humanities being coextended, contrary accidents would be coextended; and so the same [thing] would at once and together be informed by contrary accidents. But this seems a small matter to the moderns, if these things are in the same supposit as to diverse natures. Hence they grant that any contrary forms whatever could be coextended through the same position and the same supposit, but as to diverse natures.[^18]
