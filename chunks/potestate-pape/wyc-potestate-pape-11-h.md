# *De potestate pape*, chapter 11, part 8: the prelates' ornaments perverted; the third argument, from order

## ¶1 · 290.28–291.32

> [p. 290] Et sic sentitur de cruce, per quam sequela Christi in passione saltem carnis crucifixionis notatur, prepositis cesareis auream crucem habentibus, laucius, sumptuosius et studiosius carne pastis. Preciositas vero anuli aurei ingemmata non signat vere desponsacionem prelati cum sponso ecclesie aut Christi ovibus, sed affidacionem cum [p. 291] seculo et lana ac funo balancium. Nec sandalia, que in missis prelatus induit, signant precellenter preparacionem affeccionum in evangelium pacis, ut grex ordinatus sequatur eum faciendo similiter. Sed propter linicionem cesaream sopitur affeccio, quo ad illud officium et duabus viis in volucione serpentina titubando proceditur. Mitra vero gemmata non signat vere utriusque testamenti secundum duos semicirculos perfectam noticiam pastoralem, sed mundanam prudenciam, que est terrena, animalis et dyabolica ad fraudandum tam priores, preteritos quam sequentes. Cyrotece vero que signarent operacionis mundiciam signant potestatis velacionem et sanguinosa opera prelatorum. Pallium vero archiepiscopo novello adiectum, non signat plenitudinem fortitudinis et potestatis cum sciencia, cum vite asperitate atque prudencia ad regendum, sed econtra fortitudinem humane prudencie, ad tribulaciones pro temporalibus sufferendum et ut cingulum equinum ad onus equi et mulicum prudencia seculi supportandum. Et utinam decretales epistole, de quibus primo decretalium: De autoritate et usu pallii. Ex tenore cum locis similibus rectificarent usum spiritualis pallii, dimisso venali pallio corporali. Sic enim pervertuntur mendaciter signa generacionis adultere, quod minus accusabile nobis foret tantum committere et omittere de signato sine accusacione mendacii, quam quod tantum mendacii sit in signis. Est enim perversio prelatorum simpliciter signis contraria, ut contingit in isto metrico:

And the same is to be thought of the cross, by which the [following]{g:sequela}[^1] of Christ in his passion, at least the crucifixion of the flesh, is signified, while caesarean [superiors]{g:prepositi} have a golden cross and are fed on flesh more lavishly, more sumptuously and more studiously. And the costliness of the golden ring set with gems does not truly signify the espousal of the [prelate]{g:prelatus} with the [bridegroom]{g:sponsus} of the [church]{g:ecclesia} or with Christ's sheep, but his [affiance]{g:affidacio} with the [291] world and with the wool and the *funo* of the bleating flock.[^2] Nor do the sandals which the prelate puts on at masses pre-excellently signify the preparation of the affections for "the gospel of peace", so that the flock, set in order, may follow him by doing likewise.[^3] But because of the caesarean anointing, affection is lulled to sleep as regards that office, and one goes forward tottering on "two ways" in a serpent's winding.[^4] And the jewelled mitre does not truly signify, by its two half-circles, the shepherd's perfect knowledge of both testaments, but worldly prudence, which is "earthly, sensual, and devilish",[^5] for defrauding those going before, those past, as well as those following. And the gloves, which should signify cleanness of working, signify the veiling of [power]{g:potestas} and the bloody works of prelates. And the pall conferred on a new archbishop does not signify fullness of strength and power, with knowledge, with harshness of life and prudence for ruling, but on the contrary the strength of human prudence, for suffering tribulations for the sake of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, and, like a horse's girth, for bearing the load of horse and mule[^6] with the prudence of the world. And would that the decretal letters dealt with in the first book of the Decretals, *On the authority and use of the pall*, *Ex tenore*, with similar places, would correct the use of the spiritual pall, the saleable bodily pall being set aside.[^7] For the signs of "an adulterous generation" are so lyingly perverted that it would be less blameworthy for us to commit and omit so much regarding the thing signified, without the charge of a lie, than that there should be so much lying in the signs.[^8] For the perversion of the prelates is [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} contrary to the signs, as happens in this metrical verse:

## ¶4 · 292.3–292.28

> [p. 292] Cum autem signa ista non sint nisi gracia signatorum, signata per se sufficiunt sine signis, sed signa sine signatorum correspondencia sunt venenum; nec morarer circa hec signa in novo testamento ad onus ecclesie introducta nisi ad detegendum sensum misticum ornamentorum sacerdotalium legis veteris, quibus omnia in figura contigerant ad ostendendum sensum, quem nos utentes talibus signis debemus preponderanter attendere et ad convincendum mendacium philosophice signa ista gerencium. Ad tantum enim quidam in istis vane subtiliant, quod totum officium misse et sacerdotalis officii cum signis suis ac eciam partes basilice cum suis accidentibus mistice moralizant, ut ventilogium in summitate campanilis ecclesie dicunt signare curatum illius ecclesie, qui debet esse in conversacione superior, contemplacione celesti ecclesie triumphanti propinquior et contra omnes ventos temptacionum ecclesie tamquam gallus ad predicandum paracior. Sed Antichristus cum magistro suo Lucifero habet sensum contrarium in hiis signis, ut quod prelatus in fastu sit alcior, ad omnem ventum temptacionis vertibilior et tamquam canis mutus non valens latrare, ymo ut nuda galli statua ad fallendum subiectos mendacior.

And since these signs exist only for the sake of the things signified, the things signified suffice by themselves without the signs, but signs without the correspondence of the things signified are poison; nor would I dwell on these signs, introduced in the new testament to the burden of the church, except to [expose]{g:detegere} the mystical sense of the priestly ornaments of the old law, to whom "all things happened in figure",[^10] in order to show the sense to which we who use such signs ought to attend preponderantly, and to convict philosophically of lying those who wear these signs. For some [refine]{g:subtiliare} so vainly on these things that they moralize mystically the whole office of the mass and of the priestly office with its signs, and even the parts of the church building with their accidents: thus they say that the weathervane at the top of the church's bell-tower signifies the [curate]{g:curatus} of that church, who ought to be higher in [manner of life]{g:conversacio}, nearer in heavenly contemplation to the [church triumphant]{g:ecclesia-triumphans}, and, like a cock, readier to preach against all the winds of the church's temptations.[^11] But Antichrist with his master Lucifer has the contrary sense in these signs: that the prelate is higher in pride, more apt to turn with every wind of temptation, and like "a dumb dog not able to bark",[^12] indeed, like the bare statue of a cock, more lying for deceiving his subjects.

## ¶5 · 292.29–293.11

> [p. 292] Tercio principaliter arguitur per hoc quod nisi papa haberet plenitudinem potestatis et alii episcopi gradatim, ut in ecclesia ordinantur, periret ordo confessionis et timor committendi peccata, ymo per idem non observaretur ritus vel usus alicuius ecclesie, nisi qui foret expresse fundabilis [p. 293] in scriptura, et per consequens nemo observaret aliquem modum in serviendo Deo, sed longe evidencius tolleretur ordo secularium dominorum, sic quod non oportet esse cesarem, cum necessarius foret quod esset dignitas et distinccio clericalis. Ad hec igitur oportet elecciones procedere, sicut dictant leges et usus ecclesie. Non igitur sequitur: Christus et apostoli sic sequuntur, ergo non licet nobis sequentibus facere contrarium, cum pro variacione loci et temporis diversimode est agendum.

Thirdly and principally, [it is argued by appeal to]{g:arguitur-per} this: that unless the [pope]{g:papa} had [plenitude of power]{g:plenitudo-potestatis}, and the other [bishops]{g:episcopus} by degrees, as they are ordered in the church, the order of confession would perish, and the fear of committing sins; indeed, by the same token, no [rite]{g:ritus} or usage of any church would be observed except one that could be expressly founded [293] in scripture, and consequently no one would observe any mode in serving God; but far more evidently the order of [secular lords]{g:seculares-domini} would be done away, so that there need be no Caesar, since it would be more necessary that there be clerical dignity and distinction.[^13] To these ends, therefore, elections must proceed as the laws and usages of the church dictate. Therefore [it does not follow]{g:sequitur}: "Christ and the apostles proceed in this way, therefore it is not lawful for us who follow them to do the contrary", since one must act in different ways according to the variation of place and time.[^14]

## ¶6 · 293.12–294.2

> [p. 293] Quantum ad illud patet quod est expediens quod sit papa, sed ad aggravandum difficultatem loquamur limitate de papa cesareo. Et tunc videtur michi quod multum officit esse talem, ut patet inferius. Diceret autem logicus quod quilibet apostolus et quilibet sacerdos apostolorum vicarius habet in gradu suo plenitudinem potestatis. Sed decretiste videntur intelligere quod quilibet papa habet in infinitum maiorem potestatem iurisdicionis sive regiminis quam aliquis alius episcopus non Romanus, quia (ut sepe dictum est) cum sit infundabile mendacium in materia fidei est nimis hereticum. Petrus enim non habuit tantum aut talem excessum super alios apostolos sibi consocios; nec imperatoris est talem potestatem concedere. Quomodo igitur foret utile tale mendax et monstruosum ydolum ducem tocius corporis Christi fingere? Nec periret ex defectu talis monstri ordo confitendi, ordo regiminis vel alterius boni ecclesie, quia sicut in primitiva ecclesia regulabatur sine tali graduacione dominii in clero ex communi sacerdotum officio, qui omnes erant episcopi, quod exemplatum est [p. 294] in ecclesia apostolica, et tunc crevit ecclesia, sic posset esse hodie ad maiorem utilitatem ecclesie.

As to that, it is clear that it is expedient that there be a pope; but, to make the difficulty heavier, let us speak restrictedly of the [caesarean pope]{g:papa-cesareus}. And then it seems to me that it does much harm that there be such a one, as is clear below. And a logician would say that every apostle, and every [priest]{g:sacerdos} who is a vicar of the apostles, has in his degree plenitude of power. But the [decretists]{g:decretiste} seem to understand that every pope has infinitely greater [power of jurisdiction]{g:potestas-iurisdiccionis} or [of rule]{g:potestas-regiminis} than any other bishop not Roman; for (as has often been said), since this is a lie [without foundation]{g:infundabilis} in a matter of faith, it is exceedingly heretical.[^15] For Peter did not have so great or such an excess over the other apostles, his fellows; nor is it the emperor's part to grant such power. How then would it be useful to feign such a lying and monstrous idol as leader of the whole body of Christ? Nor would the order of confessing, the order of rule, or that of any other good of the church perish for want of such a monster; for as in the primitive church it was regulated without such a gradation of [lordship]{g:dominium} in the [clergy]{g:clerus}, by the common office of priests, who were all bishops (which was exemplified [294] in the apostolic church, and then the church grew), so it could be today, to the greater benefit of the church.[^16]

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Sequela* in its second sense, the following of Christ (GLOSSARY, CONTEXTUAL). The sentence plays on *carnis* and *carne*: the cross signifies the crucifixion of the flesh, while those who carry it are fed on flesh (meat).
[^2]: **T.** *Funo* (291.1) is not a Latin form, and the plate confirms the print. Loserth marks the place "Hic locus corruptus esse videtur" (apparently corrupt). *Fumo* "smoke" or *fimo* "dung" would construe; cf. Ezek. 34:3, the shepherds who eat the milk and are clothed with the wool. Left untranslated.
[^3]: **S.** Eph. 6:15, "your feet shod with the preparation of the gospel of peace", applied to the bishop's sandals.
[^4]: **C.** *Linicio* is read as "anointing" (from *linire*, smear); *lenicio* "soothing" is possible, and *sopitur* would suit either. **S.** "Two ways": Ecclus. 2:14, "the sinner that goeth on the earth two ways".
[^5]: **S.** Jas. 3:15, said there of wisdom (*sapientia*); Wyclif applies it to worldly prudence.
[^6]: **T.** The print has *mulicum* (291.21; the plate confirms it), not a Latin word; we read *muli cum* "of the mule, with" (our emendation).
[^7]: **S.** *Decretales* 1.8 (*De auctoritate et usu pallii*); Loserth gives cap. 5 and adds that the chapter is rather *Ex tuarum*, not *Ex tenore* (modern form ours; LEDGER P11h-1).
[^8]: **S.** Matt. 12:39, "an evil and adulterous generation seeketh a sign"; Wyclif plays on *signum*.
[^9]: **C.** A retrograde verse: read word by word backwards it gives the contrary, *Sacrificabo macrum, non dabo pingue sacramentum* ("I will sacrifice a lean one, I will not give a fat sacrament"). It illustrates *perversio* as a turning of the signs backwards. Loserth prints the two lines in italic as verse.
[^10]: **S.** 1 Cor. 10:11, "all these things happened to them in figure"; Wyclif's *quibus* takes the place of *illis*, referring to the men of the old law.
[^11]: **S.** The weathercock as the preacher belongs to the allegories of the church building of the kind collected in Durandus, *Rationale divinorum officiorum* 1.1, to be checked (LEDGER P11h-2).
[^12]: **S.** Isa. 56:10, "dumb dogs not able to bark"; Wyclif puts the plural into the singular.
[^13]: **C.** *Necessarius* is read as the neuter comparative ("more necessary"); cf. *necessarior* at 299.13. The objector's point is that if gradation in the clergy fell, gradation among secular lords would fall still more surely.
[^14]: **C.** *Sic sequuntur* is read "proceed in this way" (the manner of choosing ministers in the gospel); the verb may equally echo *sequentibus* ("follow"), as the next words do.
[^15]: **C.** *Quia* does not give a cause here; it introduces Wyclif's verdict on the decretists' view and may stand for *quod* ("which, as has often been said, … is exceedingly heretical").
[^16]: **C.** "Gradation" renders *graduacio*, which GLOSSARY gives as academic "graduation"; here it is rank among lords.
