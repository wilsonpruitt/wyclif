# *De ecclesia*, chapter 19, part 7: works, not names; relics and the devil's signs; seventh conclusion: baptism and the foreknown

## ¶1 · 464.31–465.13

> [p. 464] Nec sequitur ex isto confusio policie ecclesie, quia ecclesia militans operibus sui curati debet credere, et [p. 465] non nudo nomini vel prefecture in facie ecclesie, quia talis foret evidencia nimis fallax. Obediendum est tamen tali pretenso preposito salva semper obediencia legi Christi; et in ista cecitate tantum turbatur ecclesia quod quidam plus credunt signis mortis factis in vestigio mortuorum defunctorum quam operibus vivacibus superstitum quantumcunque conformiter legi Christi vivencium. Et credo quod illusio diaboli in talibus signis excecat populum; post illum ergo in quo claruit viva vite sanctitas probabiliter credi potest ad ostendendum eius sanctitatem signorum claritas, sed adhuc non tam evidenter sicut in uno opere fructuoso.

Nor does confusion of the church's polity follow from this; for the [church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans} ought to believe the works of its [curate]{g:curatus}, and [465] not the bare name or the prefecture in the face of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, since such [evidence]{g:evidencia} would be too fallacious.[^1] Yet such a pretended [superior]{g:prepositi} is to be obeyed, obedience to [the law of Christ]{g:lex-christi} being always preserved; and in this blindness the church is so troubled that some believe signs of death, done at the traces of dead men departed, more than the living works of survivors, however conformably to the law of Christ they live.[^2] And I believe that the illusion of the devil in such signs blinds the people; after one, then, in whom the living holiness of life has shone, the brightness of signs can probably be believed [to come] to show his holiness; but even so not as evidently as in one fruitful work.[^3]

## ¶2 · 465.14–468.31

> [p. 465] Unde talis culpanda cecitas, inordinatus ac cupidus cultus circa reliquias faciunt in penam peccati populum multum falli. Unde in multis patriis cupido pecunie facit in multis ecclesiis quod pars persone empte, ut canonizetur pro confessore vel martire, plus honoretur peregrinacione, sumptuosa oblacione et sepulcri ornacione auro et lapidibus preciosis quam corpus matris Dei, apostolorum Petri et Pauli vel alterius notorie beati. Et evidencia talium stat in isto quod talia possunt fieri vere ac meritorie, et prelati quibus hoc est incognitum approbant quod sit ita; nullum tale factum reprobo et pauca vel nulla approbo, quia peregrinantes, reliquias adorantes et pecunias congregantes nedum possent occupari utilius hoc dimisso, sed ex factis in lege Christi et scriptura videtur sequi quod omnes sic colentes debent protunc melius occupari et per consequens peccant graviter melius omittendo. Dimitto peccata que contingunt altrinsecus in hoc facto et quomodo ipsum est factum pharisaicum patrum veteris testamenti, sed non ex lege [p. 466] nova efficaciter nobis fundatum, sed ut plurimum ambiguum et vel ex parte colencium vel ex parte occupancium reliquias viciatum. Sicut enim in lege veteri Judeis declinantibus a cultu insensibilium invaluerunt signa generacionis adultere, sic in nova lege torpente affeccione celestium oportet tradiciones humanas et ritus ceremoniarum sensibilium dimissa religione et affeccione insensibilium prevalere, et ad hoc movent tres hostes anime. Nam necesse est dyabolum illusionibus signorum sensibilium seducere ypocritas sub specie sanctitatis, quia peccata facta in populo hoc requirunt, et dyabolus videt quod populus ad seduccionem illam est pronior. Sic enim legitur I° Regum XXVIIIᵛᵒ quod Saul consuluit phitonissam que arte diaboli suscitavit sibi dyabolum pretendentem effigiem Samuel, qui dyabolus multas veritates commiscuit, ut Saulem in desperacionem induceret et caucius in aliis erroribus seduceret post et ante. Declarat autem Augustinus libro II° De Mirabilibus Sacre Scripture, cap. X°, quod fuit dyabolus, non spiritus Samuelis, primo quia mulier tam malifica non haberet arte diaboli potestatem ad imperandum tam sancte anime; secundo quia tyrannus Saul non meruit tantam illustracionem et ministracionem a bono spiritu inquietato per duos homines tam indignos, et tercio quia dicit ille spiritus: Cras tu et filii tui mecum eritis. Saul autem et filii eius creduntur fuisse reprobi et sic cum diabolo et non cum spiritu Samuelis. Ista movent hunc sanctum [p. 467] ad credendum hoc totum factum fuisse illusione diaboli, et credo quod nemo faciet evidenciam quin licet fidelibus opinari quod feretrum pape bellantis translatum fuit in ecclesiam arte dyaboli. Nec sequitur ex sentencia Augustini quod scriptura I° Regum XXVIIIᵛ° sit falsa, quia dicit quod nomen Samuelis signat translative dyabolum figurantem; sic enim dicitur I Johannis V°, 8 quod spiritus, aqua et sanguis unum sunt in officio, designando trinitatem divine essencie, et in communi modo loquendi pretendens personam alterius vocatur nomine illius cuius vicem pretendit. Sic enim Johannes dicitur Helyas, et angelus vicarius Dei loquitur in lege veteri in persona Dei cuius sentenciam representat. Redeundo ergo ad propositum, patet quod necesse est sacerdotes Christi dimittere ritus gentiles de maioritate civili et omnino attendere quomodo copiosius atque humilius fructuose servient in ecclesia sancta Dei scientes ex fide quod secundum hoc laus et maioritas sunt a Deo. Ideo secundum Crisostomum sicut membra humani corporis nobiliora non impugnant minus nobilia sed iuvando se reciproce complent sua officia, sic debet esse de membris sancte matris ecclesie: unusquisque sine pompa maioritatis vel eius solicitudine debet iuvando et amando quemlibet operari in sua vocacione subducta contenciosa adversacione. Septimo videtur quod nullus prescitus recipit rite baptismum quo deleatur simpliciter suum originale peccatum; patet ex dictis XXVII° capitulo libri Vⁱ. Nam omnis prescitus manet perpetuo in defectu finalis perseverancie, quod est peccatum gravissimum et indelebile, et per consequens non tollitur simpliciter per baptismum. Verumptamen sicut aliqua est gracia predestinacionis [p. 468] et aliqua gracia secundum presentem iusticiam, sic aliquod est peccatum presciencie vel finalis impenitencie, et aliquod peccatum secundum presentem iniusticiam, et quia termini videntur multis extranei et preter significacionem verbalem, ideo alias illos exposui, declarans quomodo ista dicuntur gracie et peccata equivoce et quomodo ista sunt alternatim sibi opposita. Nam omnis prescitus habet perpetuum et originalem defectum, ille tamen ad tempus in malicia sua suspenditur per graciam secundum presentem iusticiam, ut graciam baptismalem et alias, et ideo dixi quod peccatum originale non deletur simpliciter. Et si queratur quid meretur prescitus existens in gracia secundum presentem iusticiam, dicitur quod aput rectiloquos et non equivocantes nunquam meretur beatitudinem, quia nunquam est dignus beatitudine sed meretur perpetuam mitigacionem pene eterne cum aliis bonis temporalibus; et sic Deus redemit prescitum non ad beatitudinem sed ad pene eterne miticiam, sicut redemit omnes dampnatos. Et correspondenter prescitus absolvitur a peccato, non ut omnino non perpetuo puniatur sed ne tam graviter perpetuo puniatur. Et sic (ut dicit Lincolniensis) peccata redemit non actus sed defectus quorum malicia per temporalem graciam est suspensa. Et sic presciti, sive seculares sive clerici, quantumcunque iuste videantur prefici bonis Dei, hoc est, dumtaxat secundum quid dum sunt in gracia temporali et iniuste simpliciter dum carent omnino momentanea caritate. Ex quo patet quod iudices tam seculares quam ecclesiastici deficiunt persepe in suis iudiciis a regulis

Hence such blameworthy blindness, an inordinate and covetous cult around relics, make the people, in punishment of sin, be much deceived. Hence in many [homelands]{g:patria} covetousness of money brings it about in many churches that part of a person bought so that he may be canonized as confessor or martyr is honoured more, with pilgrimage, costly offering and the adornment of the tomb with gold and precious stones, than the body of the mother of God, of the apostles Peter and Paul, or of another who is notoriously blessed.[^4] And the evidence for such things stands in this, that such things can be done truly and meritoriously, and prelates to whom this is unknown approve that it is so; I reprove no such deed and I approve few or none, since pilgrims, those who adore relics and those who gather money could not only be occupied more usefully, this being left aside, but from what is done in the law of Christ and scripture it seems to follow that all who worship so ought then to be better occupied, and consequently sin grievously by omitting the better.[^5] I leave aside the sins that occur on both sides in this matter, and how it is a pharisaic deed of the fathers of the old testament, but not efficaciously founded for us from the new [466] law, but [for the most part]{g:ut-plurimum} ambiguous, and vitiated either on the side of the worshippers or on the side of those who hold the relics.[^6] For as in the old law, when the Jews declined from the worship of [imperceptible]{g:insensibilis} things, the signs of an adulterous generation prevailed, so in the new law, when affection for heavenly things grows torpid, human traditions and [rites]{g:ritus} of [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} ceremonies must prevail, the religion and affection of imperceptible things being given up; and to this the three enemies of the soul move [men].[^7] For it is necessary that the devil seduce hypocrites under the appearance of holiness by illusions of perceptible signs, since the sins done among the people require this, and the devil sees that the people is more prone to that seduction. For thus it is read in 1 Kings 28 that Saul consulted a pythoness, who by the devil's art raised up for him a devil putting forward the likeness of Samuel; and that devil mixed in many truths, so as to lead Saul into despair and to seduce him more craftily into other errors, after and before.[^8] And Augustine declares in book 2 of On the Wonders of Holy Scripture, chapter 10, that it was a devil and not the spirit of Samuel: first, because a woman so evil-doing would not have power, by the devil's art, to command so holy a soul; secondly, because the tyrant Saul did not merit so great an enlightening and ministry from a good spirit disturbed by two men so unworthy; and thirdly, because that spirit says: "To morrow thou and thy sons shall be with me." But Saul and his sons are believed to have been reprobate, and so with the devil and not with the spirit of Samuel.[^9] These things move this saint [467] to believe that this whole deed was done by illusion of the devil; and I believe that no one will produce evidence that it is not lawful for the faithful to hold the opinion that the bier of the warring [pope]{g:papa} was translated into the church by the devil's art.[^10] Nor does it follow from Augustine's [teaching]{g:sentencia} that the scripture of 1 Kings 28 is false; for he says that the name of Samuel signifies, by transference, the devil who figured him; for so it is said in 1 John 5:8 that the spirit, the water and the blood "are one" in office, designating the trinity of the divine [essence]{g:essencia}; and in the common way of speaking one who puts forward the person of another is called by the name of him whose place he puts forward. For so John is called Elias, and an angel, the vicar of God, speaks in the old law in the person of God, whose teaching he represents.[^11] Returning, then, to the point, it is clear that the [priests]{g:sacerdos} of Christ must give up the gentile rites of civil [greatness]{g:majoritas} and attend altogether to how they may serve more abundantly and more humbly, fruitfully, in the holy church of God, knowing, [as faith teaches]{g:ex-fide}, that according to this are praise and greatness from God. Therefore, according to Chrysostom, as the nobler [members]{g:membrum} of the human body do not attack the less noble but, helping one another reciprocally, fulfil their offices, so it ought to be with the members of holy mother church: each one, without the pomp of greatness or solicitude for it, ought, by helping and loving each, to work in his own calling, contentious opposition being withdrawn.[^12] Seventhly, it seems that no [foreknown]{g:prescitus} [person] duly receives a baptism by which his original sin is blotted out [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}; it is clear from what was said in chapter 27 of book 5. For every foreknown [person] remains perpetually in the defect of final perseverance, which is a most grievous and indelible sin, and consequently is not taken away without qualification by baptism.[^13] Nevertheless, as there is a grace of predestination [468] and a grace [according to present justice]{g:secundum-presentem-iusticiam}, so there is a sin of [foreknowledge]{g:presciencia} or of final impenitence and a sin according to present injustice; and since the terms seem strange to many and beyond the verbal signification, I have expounded them elsewhere, declaring how these are called graces and sins [equivocally]{g:equivoce} and how they are alternately opposed to one another.[^14] For every foreknown [person] has a perpetual and original defect; yet for a time that [defect] is [suspended]{g:suspendere} in its malice by grace according to present justice, such as baptismal grace and others; and therefore I said that original sin is not blotted out without qualification. And if it is asked what the foreknown [person] existing in grace according to present justice merits, [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that, among those who speak correctly and do not equivocate, he never merits blessedness, since he is never worthy of blessedness, but he merits a perpetual mitigation of eternal punishment, with other temporal goods; and so God redeemed the foreknown not to blessedness but to the mildness of eternal punishment, as he redeemed all the damned.[^15] And correspondingly the foreknown [person] is absolved from sin, not so that he is not punished perpetually at all, but so that he is not punished so grievously perpetually. And so (as Lincoln says) he redeemed sins, not acts but defects, whose malice is suspended by temporal grace.[^16] And so the foreknown, whether [seculars]{g:secularis} or clerics, however justly they seem to be set over the goods of God, are so only [in a certain respect]{g:secundum-quid}, while they are in temporal grace, and unjustly without qualification, while they altogether lack momentary [charity]{g:caritas}. From which it is clear that judges, secular as well as ecclesiastical, very often fail in their judgements from the rules[^17]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 465.5: Excessive trust in miracles.
- 465.14: Evil of relic worship.
- 466.10: The devil has always drawn men to hypocrisy by means of sensible signs.
- 467.14: Christ's priests should care only to do service to the Church.
- 467.27: Baptism does not altogether destroy original Sin in the foreknown.
- 468.13: The profit of present righteousness to the foreknown is that they lessen their punishment,
- 468.26: and gain a qualified right to temporal goods.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Policia*: the church's polity, its order of government. *Prefectura in facie ecclesie*: an office of command as it appears before the church, outward appointment as against works. *Sui curati*: the priest with the cure of its souls (GLOSSARY *curatus*).

[^2]: **C.** *Signis mortis factis in vestigio mortuorum defunctorum*: miracles reported at the remains and tombs of the dead, set against the works of the living. **T.** B reads *quod quidem* (465.5) and lacks *mortuorum* (465.6), which would remove the doubling *mortuorum defunctorum*. **R.** Side-note 465.5.

[^3]: **C.** *Post illum … in quo claruit*: only after a man whose life has shone with holiness may signs probably be believed to show that holiness, and even then they are weaker evidence than one fruitful work.

[^4]: **C.** *Patriis*: here countries, the earthly sense of *patria* (GLOSSARY). *Pars persone empte, ut canonizetur*: a relic of a person whose canonization was bought; *empte* could instead agree with *pars*, "a part bought". **R.** A in the margin at 465.14: *Nota de peregrinacione*. Side-note 465.14.

[^5]: **C.** *Evidencia talium*: the ground alleged for such cults. *Nullum tale factum reprobo et pauca vel nulla approbo*: Wyclif condemns no particular case outright and approves few or none. *Protunc*: at that time. **R.** A in the margin at 465.19: *Nota de colentibus reliquias sanctorum* (Loserth: below 192ᵈ, without mark of reference).

[^6]: **C.** *Altrinsecus*: on both sides, of the worshippers and of the keepers of relics. *Occupancium reliquias*: those who hold the relics.

[^7]: **S.** *Generacionis adultere*: Matthew 12:39 ("An evil and adulterous generation seeketh a sign"). **C.** *Insensibilium*: of imperceptible things, set against *sensibilium*. *Tres hostes anime*: the world, the flesh and the devil. *Dimissa religione*: ablative absolute. **R.** A in the margin at 466.10: *Diabolus sepe seducit homines per corpora mortuorum et de Samuele, an ipse fuisset suscitatus* (Loserth: above 193ª, without mark of reference). Side-note 466.10.

[^8]: **S.** 1 Kings 28 (1 Samuel 28). **C.** *Phitonissa*: a woman with a divining spirit. *Pretendentem effigiem Samuel*: presenting Samuel's likeness. *Post et ante*: before and after the consultation. **T.** AA₁ read *pretendem* (466.16). **R.** B in the margin at 466.20: *Nota secundum Augustinum quod phitonissa suscitavit diabolum Sauli*.

[^9]: **S.** *De mirabilibus sacrae scripturae* II.10 (II.11, *De Samuele suscitato a Pythone*, in PL 35), under Augustine's name (Loserth: Aug. Opp. III, pars II, p. 18 of the appendix; LEDGER E19g-1, editor); for the author see LEDGER E19b-4. 1 Kings 28:19. **C.** *Duos homines tam indignos*: Saul and the woman. *Reprobi*: "the reprobate", not *presciti*. **R.** A in the margin at 466.21: *Samuel*; *Augustinus*.

[^10]: **C.** *Hunc sanctum*: Augustine. *Feretrum pape bellantis*: the bier of "the warring pope", carried into a church amid reported signs; the pope, not named here, is Gregory VI (1045–6), whose bier, by Higden's story, a whirlwind drove through the closed doors of St Peter's (*Polychronicon* VI. 22; Wyclif, *De civili dominio* II. 17, pp. 253, 257, the place cited at 460.3–4). *Nemo faciet evidenciam quin*: no one will show that it is not lawful. **R.** B in the margin at 467.7: *Nota de papa bellatore*.

[^11]: **C.** *Translative*: by transference of the name. *Dyabolum figurantem*: the devil who took Samuel's figure. *Sentenciam*: the mind or teaching of God that the angel conveys. **S.** 1 John 5:8 ("these three are one"); *in officio* and the trinitarian reading are Wyclif's. Matthew 11:14 ("he is Elias").

[^12]: **S.** Chrysostom on the members of the body; Loserth gives no reference; not located; not in the *Opus imperfectum*; perhaps the homilies on 1 Corinthians 12 (LEDGER E19g-3). **C.** *Ritus gentiles de maioritate civili*: gentile customs of civil greatness. *Copiosius atque humilius fructuose*: three adverbs, the last without a connective. **T.** B lacks *Ideo* (467.19). **R.** A in the margin at 467.19: *Crisostomus*. Side-note 467.14.

[^13]: **C.** *Prescitus*: one whom God foreknows will be damned (GLOSSARY); the conclusion turns on the difference between grace for the present and the grace of predestination. *Ex dictis XXVII° capitulo libri V*: Wyclif's cross-reference to an earlier book; to be checked (LEDGER E19g-4). **T.** AA₁ read *quomodo deleatur* (467.28). **R.** A in the margin at 467.27: *Septima conclusio. Nullus prescitus recipit rite baptismum quo deleretur peccatum originale*. Side-note 467.27.

[^14]: **C.** *Peccatum presciencie*: the sin that answers to foreknowledge, final impenitence, against sin "according to present injustice". *Preter significacionem verbalem*: *preter* is Loserth's reading of an abbreviation in the codices (468.5). *Alias … exposui*: a cross-reference to another work; to be checked (LEDGER E19g-5). *Alternatim*: crosswise, each kind of grace opposed to its kind of sin. **T.** AA₁ read *sicut aliquod* (468.2). **R.** A in the margin at 468.2: *Nota quid meretur prescitus per temporalem iniusticiam* (so the OCR of the page foot; Loserth: above 193ª, without mark of reference).

[^15]: **C.** *Ille … suspenditur*: *ille* is the defect. *Et alias*: Loserth adds *scil. gracias* (468.11). *Rectiloqui*: those who speak strictly, without equivocation. *Miticia*: mildness. **R.** A in the margin at 468.13: *Nota*. Side-note 468.13.

[^16]: **S.** Lincoln is Robert Grosseteste, bishop of Lincoln; Loserth gives only A's margin; not located (LEDGER E19g-6). **C.** *Peccata redemit non actus sed defectus*: the sins redeemed are defects, not acts. **T.** B reads *peccata redeunt* (468.24). **R.** A in the margin at 468.23: *Lincolniensis*.

[^17]: **C.** *Prefici bonis Dei*: set over God's goods as stewards. *Momentanea caritas*: charity for the moment, as against final charity. The sentence continues in the next part. **R.** A in the margin at 468.30: *Conclusio. De iudiciis sepe dubiis iudicancium*. Side-note 468.26.
