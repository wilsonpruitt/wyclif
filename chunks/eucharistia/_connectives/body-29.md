# sed/autem extract, wave 29: chunks pen02 pen03 pen04

### pen02 ¶1 · 331.23–331.28  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 331] **Sed** quia locucio in ista materia est valde periculosa ex consuetudine antiquata et multi ac magni venerantur hoc sacramentum, ac si sine illo non poterit homini esse salus, ideo procedendo securius dicendum in principio quid sit de hoc sacramento invento ex tradicione hominum concedendum.

ENGLISH: But since speech on this matter is very dangerous because of long-established [custom]{g:consuetudo}, and many great men venerate this [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} as if without it there could be no salvation for a man, therefore, to proceed more securely, we must say at the outset what is to be granted about this sacrament, devised out of the tradition of men.[^1]

### pen02 ¶3 · 332.28–333.9  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 332] Ideo respondendo ad istos obiectus dicitur primo quod necesse est peccatori cuius peccata delenda sunt contricione animi penitere; **sed** ex hoc non infertur [p. 333] quod sit tam necessaria verbalis confessio sacerdoti. Concedo tamen quod necessaria est verbalis confessio sacerdoti conversanti cum peccatore **sed** non est simpliciter necessaria cuicunque, cum multis martyribus deest copia vel presencia sacerdotis; et multi tempore utriusque legis sine confessione tali in patria sunt salvati. Cum **autem** omnia que eveniunt de necessitate evenient et talis confessio prodest pluribus, patet quod talis confessio est necessario facienda.

ENGLISH: Therefore, in reply to these objections, the answer is, first, that it is necessary for a sinner whose sins are to be blotted out to repent with [contrition]{g:contricio} of mind; but it is not inferred from this [333] that verbal [confession]{g:confessio} to a priest is so necessary. I grant, nevertheless, that verbal confession is necessary to a priest who [lives his life]{g:conversari} with the sinner; but it is not necessary [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} for anyone whatever, since many martyrs lack the [access]{g:copia-sacerdotis} or the presence of a priest;[^6] and many in the time of both laws have been saved in the [homeland]{g:patria} without such confession. And since all things that happen will happen of necessity, and such confession profits many, it is clear that such confession is necessarily to be made.[^7]

### pen02 ¶4 · 333.10–333.32  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 333] Et iterum, cum auricularis et ista sacramentalis confessio ex institucione hominum prosit multis, patet quod illa confessio est necessario facienda proprio sacerdoti. Non **autem** dico quod est necessario facienda a quolibet viatore semel in anno, si salvabitur, cum Baptista et multi alii sancti salvantur, qui nunquam confessione huiusmodi erant usi. Nec subiacet potestati hominum huiusmodi sacramentum; Deus enim libertavit suam ecclesiam quod ex sua gracia et cordis contricione fidelium sine ista penitencia sint salvati, ut patet de Petro, Paulo et Magdalena cum multis ceteris. Nam isti tres sunt salvati ex fide scripture, et tamen quod ista sacra confessione mundati fuerant, non docetur. Et certum videtur quod Christus ecclesie sue foret negligens si hoc sacramentum foret sibi tam necessarium et tamen non foret usque ad solucionem sathane ecclesie declaratum. Nam blasphemum videtur asserere quod sancti usque ad edicionem istius decretalis in obmittendo istam penitenciam peccaverunt. Et iterum certum videtur quod si huius legis edicio fuit ita racionabilis tempore Innocencii IIIⁱⁱ, quod illa racio stetit prius et sic homines ex obmissione talis penitencie antea peccaverunt.

ENGLISH: And again, since this auricular and sacramental confession, by the institution of men, profits many, it is clear that that confession is necessarily to be made to one's own priest. But I do not say that it must necessarily be made by every [wayfarer]{g:viator} once a year if he is to be saved, since the Baptist and many other saints are saved who never made use of confession of this kind. Nor is a sacrament of this kind subject to the power of men; for God has so freed his [church]{g:ecclesia} that by his grace and by the contrition of heart of the faithful they are saved without this penance, as is clear of Peter, Paul and the Magdalene, with many others. For these three are saved [from the faith of scripture]{g:ex-fide}, and yet it is not taught that they had been cleansed by this [sacred]{g:sacer} confession. And it seems certain that Christ would be negligent of his church if this sacrament were so necessary to it and yet were not [made clear]{g:declarare} to the church until the loosing of Satan.[^8] For it seems blasphemous to assert that the [saints]{g:sancti}, until the issuing of this decretal, sinned in omitting this penance. And again it seems certain that, if the issuing of this law was so reasonable in the time of Innocent III, that reason held before, and so men sinned before by omitting such penance.[^9]

### pen03 ¶1 · 334.2–335.11  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 334] Videtur rimandum ulterius quis habet potestatem condere tales leges. Et videtur multis probabile quod solus dominus temporis qui est Deus, quia solus iste habet potestatem super longitudinem terre, super racionem legis et super genus hominum obligandum. Ideo papa non habet potestatem condendi hanc legem, sicut non Deus voluit ipsam condere, cum concessit libertatem homini penitencie huiusmodi repugnantem. Hortari **autem** posset papa vel alius ad taliter penitendum. **Sed** quod omnis homo qui discrecionem attigerit et proprium sacerdotem paratum habuerit, confiteatur semel in anno omnia peccata sua proprio sacerdoti sub pena dampnacionis, non subiacet potestati papali, cum nec potest dampnare hominem nec salvare, licet filii diaboli et filii Dei sunt occasio dampnacionis multorum et quare alii sunt salvati. Nec habet papa potestatem ordinandi quod perpetuo sint tales sacerdotes ydonei aut parati; nec habet potestatem obligandi racionem quod semel in anno sit sibi sufficiens, quin per idem semel in biennio vel quolibet alio tempore et de multis preservatis ex Dei gracia pro nullo tempore hoc oportet. Ideo dicunt aliqui quod verificatum in isto loco est dictum Apostoli II Thess. II quod Antichristus extollitur super omne quod dicitur Deus, eo quod Christus istam legem ordinare non potuit, si volebat. Sic ergo in quatuor religionibus privatis sepedictis licet quedam facere que regule istarum religionum precipiunt stante [p. 335] semper Domini libertate et non licet istis tradicionibus ut legi Domini taliter obligari: sic senciendum est de hac tradicione pape qui est patronus prime secte. Observet ergo fidelis christianus hanc libertatem taliter confitendi quod confiteatur taliter sacerdoti, si et quatinus prosit sibi et ecclesie. Et istam libertatem habet concessam a Domino quam papa non potest destruere neque dare. Et per hoc patet solucio ad dicta Augustini et sanctorum similium quod homo debet presbytero confiteri, quando Deus movet peccantem humiliter confiteri de peccatis propriis que commisit, tunc debet taliter confiteri.

ENGLISH: It seems we must search out further who has the [power]{g:potestas} to make such laws. And it seems probable to many that [it is] only the [lord]{g:dominus} of time, who is God; for he alone has power over the length of the earth, over the reason of the law, and over the race of men that is to be bound.[^1] Therefore the [pope]{g:papa} does not have the power to make this law, just as God did not will to make it, since he granted to man a freedom [incompatible]{g:repugnare} with penance of this kind. But the pope or another could exhort [men] to do penance in this way. But that "every man who has reached discretion and has his own [priest]{g:sacerdos} ready should confess all his sins once a year to his own priest", on pain of [damnation]{g:dampnacio}, is not subject to papal power, since he can neither damn a man nor save him, although sons of the devil and sons of God are the occasion of the damnation of many and [the reason] why others are saved.[^2] Nor has the pope power to ordain that there always be such priests, fit or ready; nor has he power to bind reason [to hold] that once a year is sufficient for him, without its being needed, by the same token, once in two years or at any other interval; and for many who are preserved by the grace of God this is needed at no time at all.[^3] Therefore some say that in this place the saying of the Apostle, 2 Thess. 2, [is true]{g:verificari}, that "Antichrist is lifted up above all that is called God", in that Christ could not ordain this law, if he willed it.[^4] Therefore, as in the four [private religions]{g:religio-privata} so often mentioned it is lawful to do certain things which the rules of those religions command, the freedom of the Lord [335] always standing, and it is not lawful to be bound to these traditions in this way as to the law of the Lord: so we must think of this tradition of the pope, who is the [patron]{g:patronus} of the first [sect]{g:secta}.[^5] Let the faithful Christian therefore keep this freedom of confessing in such a way that he confesses so to a priest if and insofar as it profits him and the [church]{g:ecclesia}. And this freedom he has as granted by the Lord, which the pope can neither destroy nor give. And by this the [resolution]{g:solucio} is clear to the sayings of Augustine and of [saints]{g:sancti} like him, that a man ought to confess to a [presbyter]{g:presbiter}: when God moves the sinner to confess humbly the sins of his own that he has committed, then he ought to confess in this way.[^6]

### pen03 ¶2 · 335.12–336.17  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 335] **Sed** videtur multis quod generalis confessio et publica est melior quam confessio hec privata, utraque tamen est bona, licet in lege veteri et lege gracie prima sit magis fundabilis quam secunda; nec solum presbiteris est illa confessio facienda **sed** discretis tam viris quam feminis et omnimode Deo nostro. Et de illa generali confessione videtur loqui Jacobus, quando dicit: Confitemini alterutrum peccata vestra; et aliter usus ecclesie quod sacerdos confitetur peccata sua tam viris quam feminis ipsis existentibus quandoque solum in missarum suarum iniciis foret culpabiliter introductus. Et sic quidam sophisticant cum lege papali audientes missas proprii sacerdotis quandoque solitarii, dicentes quod fuerunt tunc confessi omnia peccata sua proprio sacerdoti. **Sed** videtur multis quod sophisticacio ista non sufficit cuicunque, cum multi necessitantur ostendere peccata sua discreto presbytero et recipere ab ipso salubre consilium quomodo [p. 336] de peccato preterito satisfaciant et pro peccato futuro de possibili precavebunt. Et tales fuerunt multi presbyteri tempore Augustini. Et sic concedo dictum Augustini quod homo debet, ymmo necessitatur confiteri peccata sua secundum formam quam Deus limitat. **Sed** absit ex isto concludere quod racionabile est omnem hominem confiteri semel in anno omnia peccata sua proprio sacerdoti, ut salubre est homini audire tempore congruo verbum Dei, sicut salubre est homini quoad corpus pasci cibario corporali. **Sed** insanum foret homini tempus statuere in quo sub pena mortis taliter sermonem audiat sive edat. Deus enim dedit homini liberum arbitrium et multis specialem instinctum, quando et qualiter debent taliter operari. Ideo nimia extollencia super Christum foret sic generaliter condere talem legem.

ENGLISH: But it seems to many that general and public [confession]{g:confessio} is better than this private confession, though each is good, although in the old law and [the law of grace]{g:lex-gracie} the first is better founded than the second; nor is that confession to be made only to presbyters, but to discreet persons, men as well as women, and in every way to our God.[^7] And of that general confession James seems to speak when he says: "Confess your sins one to another";[^8] and otherwise the [use]{g:usus} of the church, by which the priest confesses his sins to men as well as women when they are present, sometimes only at the beginnings of his masses, would have been culpably introduced.[^9] And so some [play the sophist]{g:sophisticari} with the papal law: hearing the masses of their own priest, sometimes alone, they say that they had then confessed all their sins to their own priest. But it seems to many that this [sophistry]{g:sophisticacio} does not suffice for everyone, since many are necessitated to show their sins to a discreet presbyter and to receive from him wholesome counsel on how [336] they may [make satisfaction]{g:satisfacere} for past sin and, as far as possible, guard against future sin. And such were many presbyters in the time of Augustine. And so I grant the saying of Augustine that a man ought, indeed is necessitated, to confess his sins according to the form that God [assigns]{g:limitare}. But far be it to [infer]{g:concludere} from this that it is reasonable for every man to confess all his sins once a year to his own priest, as it is wholesome for a man to hear the word of God at a fitting time, just as it is wholesome for a man, as to the body, to be fed with bodily food.[^10] But it would be insane to fix for a man a time in which, on pain of death, he must so hear a sermon or eat. For God has given man [free choice]{g:liberum-arbitrium}, and to many a special prompting, when and how they ought to act so. Therefore it would be excessive [exaltation]{g:extollencia} above Christ to make such a law so generally.[^11]

### pen04 ¶1 · 336.18–338.18  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 336] Superest solvere alios obiectus qui videntur sonare quod ista sacramentalis penitencia est necessario facienda. Concesso ergo quod hoc sacramentum tam proficit quam officit diversis hominibus, videndum est primo de clavibus quas Christus concesserat Petro et ceteris apostolis cum suis successoribus; et dictum est diffuse alibi quod iste claves non sunt potestates spirituales date specialiter nostris sacerdotibus ad tollendum peccata hominibus, quia hoc est agno proprium **sed** iste claves sunt potestates et sciencie ad deviantibus indicandum conformiter voluntati divine secundum iudicium legis Dei. Et non dubium quin si sacerdos a clavibus triumphantis ecclesie hic erraverit quod neque solvit neque ligat spiritualiter. Et de quanto illis clavibus se conformaverit solvit vel [p. 337] ligat apud ecclesiam triumphantem. **Sed** quid hoc ad istam sacramentalem penitenciam introductam? Sic sepe peccata sua publica plus nota sunt sacerdoti qui ipsa percipit quam ipsi peccatori qui secundum legem istam de propriis criminibus confitetur? Unde Act. XIII, 10 Paulus ex revelacione plus cognovit peccata illius magi qui dicitur Barien vel Elimas quam ipsemet cognoverat hic in via. Et (ut breviter dicatur) papa vel sacerdos alius non habet scienciam peccati gravedinem cognoscendi qualitercunque peccans secundum ultimum sue noticie dixerit confessori nisi hoc specialiter sibi fuerit revelatum, quia quomodo sciret alter eorum quomodo peccatum illud ex concomitancia pene vel gracie finaliter qualificatum fuerit, cum hoc sit viatoribus ex revelacione specialiter cognoscendum, et per consequens nullus talis sacerdos cognoscit quanta aut quali penitencia foret dignus; ideo scripta et practisata de penitenciis patule contradicunt. Nec dubium quin Deus nollet quod sacerdos suus iniungeret penitenciam nisi eius quantitatem et qualitatem sibi ostenderit. Unde cum sacerdotes absolventes hoc sepe nesciunt, ymmo si hec penitencia confesso suo profuerit vel fuerit voluntati Dei contraria, videtur quod Deus non regulariter approbat talem penitenciam sic iniunctam. Christus enim iniunxit adultere nolle amplius peccare, ut patet Johannis VIII, Paulus eciam iniunxit isti mago Barien penitenciam quod ad tempus corporaliter foret cecus. Nec habet ista ars fundacionem vel evidenciam quod a Deo approbatur. Ideo videtur [p. 338] quod sit presumpta cecitas preter legem attemptata. Et quantam ad dicta doctorum atque papalia, patet ex multis testimoniis Augustini quod non valent nisi ex racione vel lege Domini sint fundata. Ideo non omnia scripta doctorum de istis penitenciis et absolucionibus ut evangelium sunt credenda. Hoc tamen debet fidelis credere quod quantumcunque Deo deliquerit, dum tamen Deo humiliter confessus fuerit remissionem et delecionem peccati habere poterit. Et hoc discretus sacerdos ex sua prudencia poterit promovere. Et sic generalis confessio sepe induceret ut in lege veteri maiorem penitenciam et ruborem, quam ista confessio specialis. Nec ex dicto pape vel experiencia est notum fidelibus quod hec introducta penitencia magis bonum facit ecclesie quam humano generi infert malum: Et nullus nostrorum confessorum scit debitam satisfaccionem, cum soli Deo sit cognitum limitare.

ENGLISH: It remains to [resolve]{g:solvere} the other objections, which seem to sound as if this sacramental [penance]{g:penitencia} must necessarily be made. Granting, then, that this [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} both profits and harms different men, we must first look at the keys which Christ had granted to Peter and the other apostles with their successors;[^1] and it has been said at length elsewhere that these keys are not spiritual [powers]{g:potestas} given specially to our [priests]{g:sacerdos} for taking away sins from men, since this is proper to the Lamb; but these keys are powers and knowledges for pointing out [the way] to those who stray, in conformity with the divine will, according to the judgement of the law of God.[^2] And there is no doubt that, if a priest here has strayed from the keys of the [church]{g:ecclesia} triumphant, he neither looses nor binds [spiritually]{g:spiritualiter}.[^3] And to the extent that he has conformed himself to those keys, he looses or [337] binds before the church triumphant. But what has this to do with this introduced sacramental penance? Are his public sins not thus often better known to the priest who perceives them than to the sinner himself who, according to this law, confesses his own crimes?[^4] Hence (Acts 13:10) Paul by revelation knew the sins of that magician who is called Barien or Elymas better than he himself had known them here on [the way]{g:via}.[^5] And (to put it briefly) the [pope]{g:papa} or another priest does not have the knowledge to know the gravity of the sin, however the sinner may have told it to the confessor to the utmost of his knowledge, unless this has been specially revealed to him; for how would either of them know how that sin has been finally qualified by the [concomitance]{g:concomitancia} of penalty or of grace, since this is to be known by [wayfarers]{g:viator} specially by revelation? And consequently no such priest knows of how great or what kind of penance [the sinner] would be worthy; therefore the things written and [put into practice]{g:practizare} about penances openly contradict [one another].[^6] Nor is there doubt that God would not wish his priest to enjoin a penance unless he had shown him its [quantity]{g:quantitas} and [quality]{g:qualitas}. Hence, since absolving priests often do not know this, indeed [do not know] whether this penance will have profited the one confessed to them or been contrary to the will of God, it seems that God does not [as a rule]{g:regulariter} approve such penance so enjoined.[^7] For Christ enjoined on the adulteress "to will to sin no more", as is clear in John 8; Paul also enjoined on that magician Barien the penance that he should be [bodily]{g:corporaliter} blind for a time.[^8] Nor has this art a foundation or [evidence]{g:evidencia} that it is approved by God. Therefore it seems [338] that it is a presumptuous blindness, attempted outside the law. And as for the sayings of the doctors and the papal ones, it is clear from many testimonies of Augustine that they are of no value unless they are founded on reason or the law of the Lord.[^9] Therefore not all the writings of the doctors about these penances and absolutions are to be believed as the gospel. Yet this the faithful man ought to believe: that however much he has offended against God, provided only that he has humbly confessed to God, he will be able to have [remission]{g:remissio} and blotting out of sin. And a discreet priest will be able to further this by his prudence. And so general [confession]{g:confessio} would often induce, as in the old law, greater penance and shame than this special confession. Nor is it known to the faithful, from the pope's saying or from experience, that this introduced penance does more good to the church than it brings evil on the human race: and none of our confessors knows the due [satisfaction]{g:satisfaccio}, since it is known to God alone to [assign]{g:limitare} it.[^10]
