# *De potestate pape*, chapter 7, part 4: a fourth sense: riches make the mind giddy

## ¶1 · 146.34–147.26

> [p. 146] Nec obest talis sensus varietas, cum aqua nunc signat divicias, nunc tribulaciones et nunc populum [p. 147] in scriptura. Posset et quartus sensus moralis elici ex isto opere secundum doctrinam Crysost, super secundo capitulo, II. Thym, morali V, scilicet quod vadentes in mundi diviciis tamquam vertiginosi habent de spiritualibus incertum iudicium. Constat quidem naturalibus quomodo in vertiginosis spiritus, qui sunt vehicula interiorum sensuum, circumducti faciunt apparenciam eciam quieti corporis, ac si corrotaretur homine stante fixo. Sic cogitacione et fantasia fixis in temporalibus detecto timore Dei inmobilis, diviciis se sperificantibus ad corrupcionem sensibilium instar aque, necesse est fantasiam et affeccionem vertiginosi proporcionaliter circumduci; et talis non foret bonus dux itineris ad sequendum. Unde erivatis multis malis avaricie imaginatur iste sanctus animam talis extrahi et peccata sua in qualitates sensibiles commutari. Videres tunc, inquit, animam pallidam trementem, verecundantem, velatam, agoniam habentem, se ipsam miserabiliter contempnentem. Et hec racio quare Christus voluit duces sue ecclesie instar sui a mundialibus elongari, ne fantasia corrupta contempnant veras divicias, et talis indubie licet reputacione humana, tamen non auctoritate Domini foret papa.

Nor does such variety of sense stand in the way, since in scripture water signifies at one time riches, at another tribulations, and at another a people. [147] A fourth, moral sense could also be drawn from this deed, according to the [doctrine]{g:doctrina} of Chrysostom on the second chapter of 2 Timothy, the moral part of homily 5: namely, that those who walk in the world's riches have, like men with vertigo, an uncertain judgement about spiritual things.[^1] For it is evident to natural philosophers how in men with vertigo the spirits, which are the vehicles of the inner senses, being whirled round, produce the appearance, even in a body at rest, that it is turning round with them while the man stands fixed. So, with thought and imagination fixed on [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, the fear of the immovable God stripped away, and riches spinning themselves round like a sphere to the corruption of [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} things, like water, the imagination and affection of the giddy man must be whirled round in proportion; and such a man would not be a good leader of the journey for others to follow.[^2] Hence, having traced many evils to avarice,[^3] this saint imagines the soul of such a man drawn out and its sins changed into perceptible qualities. "You would see then," he says, "the soul pale, trembling, ashamed, veiled, in agony, miserably despising itself."[^4] And this is the reason why Christ willed that the leaders of his [church]{g:ecclesia}, after his likeness, be removed far from worldly things: lest, with a corrupted imagination, they despise the true riches. And such a man without doubt, though by human reputation, would yet not be [pope]{g:papa} by the Lord's [authority]{g:auctoritas}.[^5]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 147.4: A man engaged in worldly pursuits sees spiritual things confusedly.

## Notes

[^1]: **C/S/T.** *Nunc … nunc*: "at one time … at another". *Opere*: Peter's walking on the water. Chrysostom, homily 5 on 2 Timothy, ch. 2: Loserth, *Opera* XI, 628 (147.3; LEDGER P07d-1, editor). *Morali V* is construed as the moral exhortation that closes the homily. The manuscripts read *vadantes*, Loserth *vadentes* (147.4).

[^2]: **C/T.** *Naturalibus*: the natural philosophers. *Spiritus*: the bodily spirits of medieval physiology. *Detecto timore Dei inmobilis*: construed as an ablative absolute, "the fear … stripped away", with *inmobilis* agreeing with *Dei*; the participle is doubtful. *Sperificantibus* (Loserth, 147.12): B reads *ad modum spere se rotantibus* ("rotating themselves like a sphere"), added also in A's margin; the sense is the same. *Ad corrupcionem sensibilium*: the corruption of the things the senses perceive.

[^3]: **T.** The print has *erivatis* (147.16, so the plate); we read *derivatis*, "traced" (our emendation).

[^4]: **S/C.** The quotation is Chrysostom's, same homily (Loserth p. 628, 147.19; LEDGER P07d-2, editor; NPNF: "you would see it pale and trembling, ashamed, hiding its head, anxiously fearful, and self-condemned", which Wyclif's six terms follow; read in English only, LEDGER P07d-3). *Commutari* is "changed into", not the exchange of trade.

[^5]: **T/C.** *Tamen*: A reads *tantum* ("would be pope only by human reputation, not by the Lord's authority", 147.25); the print construes and is followed. *Licet … tamen* pairs "though … yet".
