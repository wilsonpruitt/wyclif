## Latin doubts (for the plates)

- **176.14** (08-d, n. 1): *quod semper sit iuste*. B has *fit* and the English follows B. *Sit/fit* is the classic long-s confusion, and the print may itself read *fit* (or the OCR may have misread it). If the plate shows *fit*, n. 1's "B is translated" should become "the print". If it shows *sit*, the card's rule needs a word of cause: the print (*quod semper sit iuste*, "which is always [so] justly") construes awkwardly but does construe, so taking a single manuscript's reading needs the stated reason.
- **c. 177.17** (08-d): *Sed cum tres ficciones non sufficiunt*. Ch. 8 so far (08-a to 08-c, and 08-d itself) numbers no three *ficciones*, and the English "three fictions" has no antecedent. Check the plate (n0238) for *tales* (abbreviated *t'les*/*tal's*) or *iste* misread as *tres*. If the print does read *tres*, a **C** note should say what the three are, or that they are not identified.
- **c. 294.7** (11-i): *minus diffusa*. Already plate-confirmed as printed (n. 2). Listed only so the coordinator sees that row 9 depends on an archetype error, not an OCR scar.

## Outside the four classes (not counted)

- **GLOSSARY query, *signabile* (11-k 305.5, *in quocumque profectu signabili*, "signifiable").** The rendering follows the row ruled at *Eccl.* 398.29. But in scholastic Latin *signabilis* with *quocumque* is normally "assignable" (*in quocumque instanti signabili*, "at any assignable instant"), and that is the sense here: any advancement one could name. The coordinator may wish to review the row. The chunk is not flagged, because GLOSSARY governs.
- 08-d n. 16 glosses *sub colore* as "a pretext". The *color* row says "never 'pretext'". Here the phrase is not *non est color*, and the English correctly has "under colour". The note's word could be changed to "a plausible ground (here a pretext in all but name)" or left. Raised only for consistency.
