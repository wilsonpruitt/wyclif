## Doubtful, left as they are

- **09-g ¶1, p. 297**: *ideo … paratus sum stare decreto sancte matris ecclesie; horreo autem inducere hunc tantum extraneum*, rendered "But I shudder". Either (b), his readiness to stand by the decree set against his horror of bringing in the accident-sense, or (c), the same stance extended ("And I shudder"). "But" is defensible.
- **09-h ¶2, p. 301**: *et hoc forte movet eos qui dicunt quod vident … sed evangelium dicit quod fuit singulariter concessum … ideo … menciuntur*, rendered "now". Either (a), the premiss closed by *ideo* (the card's look-ahead rule), or (b), the gospel set against the claim to see Christ's body with the bodily eye. "Now" is defensible and follows the card's *ergo/ideo* rule.
- **09-h ¶7, p. 303**: *In istis autem suppono ex loyca quod …*, rendered "And". Either (c), a note added to the arguments just made, or (a), the logical premiss those arguments rest on ("Now in these I suppose"). "And" is defensible.
- **09-i ¶2, p. 307** (Augustine quoted): *Sed et concio illa in qua Stephanus stans Christum conspexit … Constat igitur*, rendered "And besides". *Sed et* is the additive idiom ("moreover"), and "besides" carries it; but it brings a second premiss to a conclusion closed by *igitur*, so (a) "Now, besides, that assembly …" would match the card. A real toss-up within a quotation; left.
