# *De ecclesia*, chapter 3, part 7: no one may claim the obedience of all as head; the bride and the hireling

## ¶1 · 66.3–69.3

> [p. 66] Ex quibus concluditur quod periculosum foret cuiquam christiano propter fastum vel questum singularem pretendere quod sibi tamquam capiti universalis ecclesie sit necessarium omnes christianos singulariter obedire, dicente Augustino (ubi supra) quod ecclesie non preerat Faustinus episcopus, sed parti preerat (ut dicit epistola ad Dardanum). Christus congregavit ecclesiam suam in unum spiritum et unum corpus cuius unum caput est Christus. Et Bernardus super Cantica sermone XII°: Quisquis et inebriat verbis et fragrat beneficiis, sibi dictum putet: quia meliora sunt ubera tua vino et fragrancia unguentis optimis. Et ad hec quis idoneus? Quis nostrum unum illorum saltem integre perfecteque possideat, ut interdum non in dicendo sterilior et in operando tepidior sit? Sed est que merito et non ficte hoc preconio glorietur, ecclesia utique cui nunquam de universitate sua deest et unde inebriet et unde fragret; quod enim sibi deest in uno, habet in altero secundum mensuram donacionis Christi ac moderacionem spiritus dividentis singulis prout vult. Fragrat ecclesia in hiis qui sibi faciunt amicos de mamona iniquitatis, inebriat in ministris verbi qui vino leticie spiritualis infundunt terram et inebriant eam et fructum referunt in paciencia. Ipsa audaciter secureque sese nominat sponsam, tamquam que vere habet ubera meliora vino ac fragrancia unguentis [p. 67] optimis. Quod etsi nemo nostrum sibi arrogare presumat, ut animam suam audeat sponsam domini appellare, quoniam tamen de ecclesia sumus que merito hoc nomine et re nominis gloriatur, glorie huius participium usurpamus. Quod enim simul omnes plene integreque possidemus, hoc singuli sine contradiccione participamus. Gracias tibi domine Jesu qui nos karissime ecclesie tue aggregare dignatus es, non solum ut fideles essemus, sed eciam ut tibi vice sponse in amplexus iocundos castos eternosque copularemur. Ecce quomodo sancti sub dubio presumpserunt se esse membra ecclesie, sed nonnisi ex presupposita imitacione sponsi ecclesie. Unde sermone XIII° dicit Bernhardus: Si sponsa utique de omni virtute sua vel gracia misericordie audeat sibi quippiam arrogare, quanto minus adolescentule que forte nos sumus? Ecce quomodo dicit forte. Dicamus proinde et nos sponse vestigia insectantes: Non nobis domine, non nobis, sed nomini tuo da gloriam. Dicamus non verbo et lingua tantum, sed opere et veritate: quia aliter non sumus iuvencule, iuxta illud Psalmi LXXVIIⁱ, 36, 37 Quoniam dilexerunt eum in ore suo et lingua mentiti sunt ei; cor autem eorum non erat rectum cum eo, nec fideles habiti sunt in testamento eius. Ecce iste sanctus plane sentenciat quod, nisi habuerimus vitam contemplativam vel activam quam Deus acceptat ad beatitudinem aliter non sumus partes ecclesie. Ideo instar sanctorum decertaremus, quis posset esse in fructuoso ministerio matris sue operosior atque humilior. Et ex hinc iuxta fidem scripture, Matthei XVIII°, 1, sequitur quod sit maior. Christus enim non potuit esse caput ecclesie sine humillimo [p. 68] et pauperrimo ministerio sponse sue. Quomodo ergo eius vicarius preerit uni adolescentule, nisi gerat in moribus vicem Christi, non exigens subieccionem civilem quam Christus non potest exigere? Periculum autem predictum de presumpcione stupida exterreret sensatos, ne contendant circa primatus sancte matris ecclesie vel tamquam in ea aut ex ea capitanei exigant seculariter bona eius. Talis enim presumpcio excluderet contenciosos, ne sint vel membra abiecta huius corporis, cum secundum omnes eius naturas sit compaginata proporcione tali armonica; quod non est possibile quod secundum aliquas eius partes sit contrariacio perpetua, cum oportet ad ultimum quodlibet membrum eius consentanee iuvare quodlibet (ut docet apostolus Ia Cor. XII°). Nam finis pro quo nunc militamus est ut unanimes uno ore sine usurpacione alieni officii in quiete perpetua conletemur; que leticia a nullo queritur, nisi in via tendunt ad finalem concordiam. Temporalia enim abusa tenetur prelatus restituere (ut patet per Archidiaconum XLIVª distinccione et declaravi sexto capitulo....). Et primatum non debet clericus principaliter querere sed laborem; aliter enim est mercenarius, non pastor, ut allegatum est in principio huius capituli ex decreto VIIIª, q. I: Sunt in ecclesia. Super quo dicit Archidiaconus quod in quolibet episcopatu sunt duo, scilicet dignitas et opus ad quod dignitas vere consequitur. Et sic quicunque prepostere ambiunt dignitates, ut sint tamquam mercenarii et non ut membra ecclesie, se reddunt dampnabiles. Et forte in isto capitulo sunt quidam hodie qui non tantum appreciantur lucrum fructus operis evangeliste [p. 69] sicut voluptates temporales, decimas vel honores; et omnes tales sunt venenosi heretici (ut patet libro sexto, tractatu de heresi).

From this it is [inferred]{g:concludere} that it would be dangerous for any Christian, out of pride or
for singular gain, to claim that it is necessary for all Christians singly to obey him as
[head]{g:caput} of the universal [church]{g:ecclesia}; since Augustine says (in the place cited above)
that bishop Faustinus did not preside over the church, but presided over a part (as the letter to
Dardanus says). Christ gathered his church into one spirit and one body, whose one head is Christ.[^1]
And Bernard, on the Canticles, sermon 12: "Whoever both inebriates with words and is fragrant with
benefits, let him think it said to himself: 'For thy breasts are better than wine, smelling sweet of
the best ointments.' And for these things who is sufficient? Which of us possesses even one of them
wholly and perfectly, so as not at times to be more barren in speaking and more lukewarm in working?
But there is one who may deservedly and not feignedly glory in this praise: the church, to whom, from
her whole body, there is never lacking both whence to inebriate and whence to be fragrant; for what she
lacks in one, she has in another, 'according to the measure of the giving of Christ' and the
apportioning of the Spirit, 'dividing to every one according as he will.'[^2] The church is fragrant in
those who 'make unto themselves friends of the mammon of iniquity'; she inebriates in the ministers of
the word, who pour the wine of spiritual gladness upon the earth and inebriate it, and 'bring forth
fruit in patience.' She boldly and securely names herself [bride]{g:sponsa}, as one who truly has
breasts better than wine and smelling sweet of the best [67] ointments. And although none of us would
presume to arrogate this to himself, so as to dare to call his own soul the bride of the Lord, yet,
since we are of the church, which deservedly glories in this name and in the reality of the name, we
claim a share in this glory. For what all of us together possess fully and wholly, this each of us
shares without contradiction. Thanks be to thee, Lord Jesus, who hast deigned to join us to thy most
dear church, not only that we might be faithful, but also that, in the place of a bride, we might be
united to thee in joyful, chaste and eternal embraces."[^3] See how the saints, under doubt, presumed
themselves to be members of the church, but only on the presupposition of their imitating the church's
[bridegroom]{g:sponsus}. Hence in sermon 13 Bernard says: "If the bride, indeed, should dare to
arrogate anything to herself of all her virtue or the grace of mercy, how much less the young maidens,
which perhaps we are?" See how he says "perhaps."[^4] "Let us therefore also, following the bride's
footsteps, say: 'Not to us, O Lord, not to us; but to thy name give glory.'" Let us say it "not in
word and tongue" only, "but in deed and in truth"; for otherwise we are not young maidens, according to
that [text] of Psalm 77:36–37: "For they loved him with their mouth: and with their tongue they lied
unto him: but their heart was not right with him: nor were they counted faithful in his covenant."[^5]
See: this saint plainly [pronounces]{g:sentenciare} that unless we have a contemplative or active life
which God accepts for blessedness, otherwise we are not parts of the church. Therefore, after the
manner of the saints, we should contend who could be more laborious and humbler in the fruitful
ministry of his mother. And from this, according to the faith of scripture, Matthew 18:1, it
[follows]{g:sequitur} that he is the greater. For Christ could not be head of the church without the
humblest [68] and poorest ministry to his bride. How, then, will his vicar preside over one young
maiden unless he bears in his conduct the place of Christ, not exacting civil subjection, which Christ
cannot exact?[^6] And the aforesaid danger of stupid presumption would terrify the sensible, lest they
contend over the primacies of holy mother church, or, as [captains]{g:capitaneus} in her or from her,
exact her goods in a secular manner. For such presumption would exclude the contentious, so that they
are not even outcast members of this body; since according to all its natures it is joined together in
such harmonic proportion that it is not possible that, according to some of its parts, there should be
perpetual contrariety, since in the end every member of it must help every other in agreement (as the
apostle teaches, 1 Corinthians 12). For the end for which we now fight is that, "with one mind and with
one mouth," without usurping another's office, we may rejoice together in perpetual rest; which joy is
sought by no one unless on the [way]{g:via} they tend toward final concord.[^7] For a
[prelate]{g:prelatus} is bound to restore [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} abused (as is clear from the
Archdeacon on distinction 44, and I have declared in the sixth chapter …). And a
[cleric]{g:clericus} ought not principally to seek primacy but labour; for otherwise he is a hireling,
not a shepherd, as was alleged at the beginning of this chapter from the Decretum, [causa] 8, question
1, *Sunt in ecclesia*.[^8] On which the Archdeacon says that in every bishopric there are two things,
namely the dignity and the work, upon which the dignity truly follows. And so whoever canvass for
dignities preposterously, so as to be as hirelings and not as members of the church, render themselves
damnable. And perhaps in this chapter there are some today who do not value the gain of the fruit of
the work of an evangelist [69] as much as temporal pleasures, [tithes]{g:decime} or honours; and all
such are venomous heretics (as is clear in the sixth book, in the treatise on heresy).[^9]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 66.3: It is dangerous for any one to claim the obedience of all Christians.
- 67.11: Thus the Saints hesitated to call themselves members of the Church.
- 68.1: Christ's vicar must not ask a submission which Christ cannot ask.
- 68.25: A bishopric should be desired for the work, not for the dignity.

## Notes

[^1]: **S/T/C.** *Ubi supra*: Augustine, *In Ioh. tr.* 6 (03-f; Loserth: *Opp.* III/2, 340), and the
    letter to Dardanus (ep. 187; Loserth: *Opp.* II, 678) (LEDGER E03g-1). *Faustinus* (66.8) is
    Augustine's (*tr.* 6.25, PL 35:1436: *non Ecclesiae praeerat Faustinus, sed parti praeerat*). The
    letter to Dardanus supplies the sentence on Christ's one body (*Ep.* 187.37, PL 33:846: *in unum
    corpus, cujus unum caput est Christus*). B lacks *suam* (66.10).

[^2]: **T/S.** Bernard, *Sermones super Cantica* 12 (Loserth: *Opp.* 523; B *cap. XII*) (LEDGER
    E03g-2). *Cantica* is Loserth's; the MSS *canonica* (66.12). *Unde fragret* (66.21) is Loserth's,
    with Bernard; the MSS *bene fragret*. Song 1:1–2, DR (Wyclif *et fragrancia*); 2 Cor. 2:16, DR
    without "so"; Eph. 4:7, DR; 1 Cor. 12:11, DR. A margin: *Ecclesia que et quomodo fragrat unguentis
    optimis* (66.13).

[^3]: **S/C.** Luke 16:9, DR (third person for Bernard's *sibi*); Luke 8:15, DR (*referunt* for
    *afferunt*). *Participium*: here "a share" (for *participacionem*). B reads *Oportet eciam ut tibi
    vicem* (67.9). *Vice sponse*: in the place of a bride, as the church is the bride and we only of it.

[^4]: **T/C.** Bernard, *Super Cant.* 13 (LEDGER E03g-2). *Audeat* (67.15), as printed (plate): Bernard
    has *minime audet*, "dares not at all" (*Super Cant.* 13.9, PL 183:838D–839A), which the *quanto
    minus* argument needs; we translate Wyclif's Latin as it stands, as with scripture. *Adolescentule*: the "young maidens" of Song 1:2. A margin: *Sancti cum formidine et sub spe
    dixerunt se esse membra sancte ecclesie*; B: *Nemo audeat se asserere membrum ecclesie* (67.11).

[^5]: **S.** Ps. 113:9 (Vulgate numbering; Heb. 115:1), DR. *Non verbo et lingua … sed opere et
    veritate*: 1 John 3:18 (DR "not … in word nor in tongue, but in deed and in truth"). Ps. 77:36–37,
    DR; Wyclif *Quoniam* for *Et*. Loserth: in Bernard the psalm is introduced *Ne forte (quod nimis
    vereor) dicatur et de nobis: Quoniam …* (*Opp.* 527); *quia aliter non sumus iuvencule* is Wyclif's.

[^6]: **S/T/C.** Matt. 18:1–4: who is "the greater" in the kingdom; he who humbles himself (18:4).
    *Ministerio sponse sue*: "ministry to his bride" (objective genitive); "of his bride" is possible.
    B margin: *Matth. XVIII*
    (67.31).

[^7]: **T/S.** B *exerceret* for *exterreret* (68.6). *Capitanei*: captains, not heads (GLOSSARY §A).
    *Quod non est possibile quod*: result clause after *tali*. 1 Cor. 12:12–27 (B margin: *1 Cor. XII*,
    68.15); Rom. 15:6, DR "with one mind and with one mouth". A margin: *Membra sancte ecclesie sunt
    unita* (68.6).

[^8]: **T/S.** The Archdeacon: Guido de Baysio, archdeacon of Bologna, *Rosarium* on D.44 (Loserth)
    (LEDGER E03g-3). After *capitulo* (68.21) the MSS have a lacuna (Loserth); *sexto capitulo* is
    chapter 6 of this book. Decretum C.8 q.1 c.19 *Sunt in ecclesia* (Loserth: *Decreti secunda pars
    causa VIII, q. I, cap. XIX*; the modern form is ours) (LEDGER E03g-4); *mercenarius, non pastor*:
    John 10:12. *In principio huius capituli*: the earlier citation in chapter 3 is unchecked (LEDGER
    E03g-4, to-verify). A margin: *Prelatus*; AB: *Archidiaconus* (68.24).

[^9]: **C/S/R.** *Ad quod dignitas vere consequitur*: the dignity follows on the work (cf. Augustine,
    *Civ. Dei* 19.19, *nomen est operis, non honoris*; LEDGER E03g-5, to-verify). *In isto capitulo*:

    evangeliste*: 2 Tim. 4:5, "do the work of an evangelist". *Libro sexto, tractatu de heresi*: not
