# *De ecclesia*, chapter 5, part 6: ten arguments against a doctor's definition of the church

## ¶1 · 112.10–114.12

> [p. 112] In ista vero materia est quidam doctor describens ecclesiam sub hac forma: Ecclesia, inquit, est universalis multitudo omnium christianorum, habencium ex integro rectam et eandem fidem per Christum et apostolos divulgatam et a sanctis patribus et consiliis generalibus declaratam. Et patet quod loquitur de una sancta ecclesia catholica, quia aliter non assumeret pro genere quod ipsa ecclesia sit multitudo omnium christianorum. Primo, inquam, videtur quod nulli patres veteris testamenti fuerunt de illa ecclesia, quia illa non fuit antequam Christus eam genuit per effluxum sanguinis et aque de suo latere dormientis in cruce (ut dicitur Joh. XIX° 34), sed hoc non fuit pro tempore patrum veteris testamenti, ergo nec illa ecclesia. Ex quo sequitur quod cum extra illam ecclesiam non est salus (ut patet in extravagante Bonifacii VIIIⁱ. Unam sanctam), nullus patrum veteris testamenti salvabitur. Non enim foret pertinens decreto dicere quod extra unam sanctam et catholicam ecclesiam non est salus nisi intenderit quod nemo salvabitur nisi fuerit de illa. Secundo sequitur quod Christus non sit de illa ecclesia quia non est de universali multitudine omnium christianorum, eo quod non est [p. 113] christianus, sed ipse Christus a quo dicitur quilibet christianus, et per consequens, nec est de numero salvandorum nec caput nec aliqua pars ecclesie catholice. Nam a Christo ut capite denominantur fideles servi Christi christiani (ut patet Act. XI°, 26). Ergo sicut Romanus non est Roma nec Israhelita ipse Israhel, et breviter, nulla generacio passiva est ipsum principium generans, sic nullus christianus est Christus sed Christi, et periret fides scripture de capite et de membris ecclesie; et multo evidencius sequitur quod beata virgo non fuit membrum illius ecclesie, quia in sancto triduo non fuit illa ecclesia nec fuit posterius sibi incorporata. Tercio sequitur quod nulla sit talis ecclesia, quia nulla multitudo omnium christianorum habet ex integro eandem rectam fidem, tum quia defective et corrupte habet multitudo omnium christianorum fidem Christi, tum eciam quia nullam unam fidem habent omnes illi christiani nec pro specie nec pro aggregato. Nec valet hoc sophisma: Isti habent fidem, sed nullam fidem habent. Nec valet fingere fidem que creditur, quia talia negantur a sic describente, et illis positis inculte aggregantur in descripcione tam anxie elaborata. Nam multi christiani habent fidem valde peccatis obliquatam, tam presciti quam predestinati, et multi simpliciter infideles habent fidem creditam valde rectam. Quarto sequitur quod nec apostoli nec alii fideles erant de ecclesia, antequam fides fuit per Christum et apostolos divulgata et a sanctis patribus in consiliis generalibus declarata; quod fuit diu post ascensionem domini et mortem apostolorum. Nam nemo fuit de ecclesia catholica, antequam illa fuit, sed illa non fuit, antequam fuit ita quod illa est: sed cum non fuit ita quod illa [p. 114] est, antequam dicta conscripcio sibi compeciit, sequitur quod tunc primo fuit ecclesia; quod esset valde inconveniens, primo quia Christus ex latere suo non formasset ecclesiam, secundo quia nulli fideles fuissent de ecclesia ante consilia dicta generalia, et sic beatus Stephanus et multi alii christiani fuissent mortui extra fidem ecclesie; tercio quia complentes prima consilia post ascensionem fecissent primo ecclesiam catholicam, quia posito quod essemus per diem ante prima consilia, post ascensionem foret nobis verum quod nondum est ecclesia catholica, quia nondum verificata est descripcio.

But on this matter there is a certain doctor who defines the [church]{g:ecclesia} in this form:
"The church," he says, "is the universal multitude of all Christians, who hold wholly the right and
same faith spread abroad by Christ and the apostles and declared by the holy fathers and the general
councils."[^1] And it is clear that he speaks of the one holy catholic church, for otherwise he would
not take for genus that the church itself is the multitude of all Christians. First, I say, it seems
that no fathers of the old testament were of that church, because that [church] did not exist before
Christ begot it by the outflow of blood and water from his side as he slept on the cross (as is said
in John 19:34); now this was not in the time of the fathers of the old testament; therefore neither
was that church.[^2] From which [it follows]{g:sequitur} that, since outside that church there is no
salvation (as is clear in the extravagant of Boniface VIII, *Unam sanctam*), none of the fathers of
the old testament will be saved. For it would not be pertinent for the decree to say that outside the
one holy and catholic church there is no salvation, unless it meant that no one will be saved unless
he was of it.[^3] Secondly it follows that Christ is not of that church, because he is not of the
universal multitude of all Christians, in that he is not [113] a Christian, but Christ himself, from
whom every Christian is so called; and consequently he is neither of the number of those to be saved,
nor [head]{g:caput}, nor any part of the catholic church. For it is from Christ as head that the
faithful servants of Christ are named Christians (as is clear from Acts 11:26). Therefore, just as a
Roman is not Rome, nor an Israelite Israel himself, and in short no passive generation is the
generating principle itself, so no Christian is Christ, but Christ's; and the faith of scripture
concerning the head and the members of the church would perish; and much more evidently it follows
that the blessed Virgin was not a member of that church, because in the holy three days that church
did not exist, nor was she afterwards incorporated into it.[^4] Thirdly it follows that there is no
such church, because no multitude of all Christians has wholly the same right faith: both because the
multitude of all Christians has the faith of Christ defectively and corruptly, and also because all
those Christians have no one faith, neither as a species nor as an aggregate. Nor does this
[sophism]{g:sophisma} avail: "These have faith, but they have no faith." Nor does it avail to feign
the faith which is believed, because such things are denied by the one who defines thus, and, if they
are posited, they are crudely heaped together in a [definition]{g:descripcio} so anxiously worked
out.[^5] For many Christians, the [foreknown]{g:prescitus} as well as the
[predestinate]{g:predestinatus}, have a faith much bent by sins, and many who are
[unbelievers]{g:infidelis} [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} have a believed faith that is very
right.[^6] Fourthly it follows that neither the apostles nor other faithful were of the church before
the faith was spread abroad by Christ and the apostles and declared by the holy fathers in the general
councils; which was long after the ascension of the Lord and the death of the apostles. For no one was
of the catholic church before it existed; but it did not exist before it was the case that it is; but
since it was not the case that it [114] is before the said written [definition] applied to it, it
follows that it was then first the church.[^7] This would be very [absurd]{g:inconveniens}: first,
because Christ would not have formed the church from his side; secondly, because no faithful would
have been of the church before the said general councils, and so blessed Stephen and many other
Christians would have died outside the faith of the church; thirdly, because those who held the first
councils after the ascension would first have made the catholic church; for supposing that we were
[living] a day before the first councils after the ascension, it would be true for us that there is
not yet a catholic church, because the definition has not yet been verified.[^8]

## ¶2 · 114.13–114.22

> [p. 114] Quinto sequitur quod, ut generabuntur et morientur fideles, erit nova et nova ecclesia catholica, et per consequens, forent plures quam mille ecclesie catholice; quod est contra decretalem Bonifacii Unam sanctam. Et consequencia videtur ex hoc quod ut sic erunt nove et nove multitudines christianorum, et per consequens, non manebit per tot et tantas transmutaciones eadem ecclesia in numero, sicut patet querenti diligenter quid foret illa ecclesia que sic continue remaneret.

Fifthly it follows that, as the faithful are born and die, there will be a new and ever new catholic
church, and consequently there would be more than a thousand catholic churches; which is against
Boniface's decretal *Unam sanctam*. And the [inference]{g:consequencia} appears from this: that in this
way there will be new and ever new multitudes of Christians, and consequently the same church in
number will not remain through so many and so great changes, as is clear to one who asks diligently
what that church would be which would thus continuously remain.[^9]

## ¶3 · 114.23–114.30

> [p. 114] Sexto sequitur quod omnes presciti in gracia forent membra ecclesie catholice, et nulli predestinati dum erraverint in fide forent partes sancte matris ecclesie; quorum primum videtur falsum de Scarioth et aliis hereticis, et secundum videtur fuisse falsum de David, Petro, Paulo et eis similibus pro tempore quo fuerunt infideles. Quorum utrumque est contra sanctorum sentencias et raciones factas superius.

Sixthly it follows that all the foreknown in grace would be members of the catholic church, and no
predestinate, while they erred in the faith, would be parts of holy mother church; of which the first
seems false of Iscariot and other heretics, and the second seems to have been false of David, Peter,
Paul and those like them for the time in which they were unbelievers. Each of these is against the
[teachings]{g:sentencia} of the saints and the reasons given above.[^10]

## ¶4 · 114.31–115.33

> [p. 114] Septimo videtur quod non sit ecclesia triumphans, eo quod in patria non habuit fidem ut dicitur. Et sic nulla multitudo erit perpetuo ecclesia sponsa Christi, vel aliter ecclesia ad sui perfeccionem fiet non ecclesia quod est contra scripturam et racionem. [p. 115] Octavo videtur quod ecclesia catholica nullibi sit nec aliquam partem habet, cum nichil sit, et sic ecclesia Romana non foret dignior aut indignior quam ecclesia catholica. Istas evidencias facio, ut dicatur seriose ad materiam et formam racionum, nullibi declinando a veritate de virtute sermonis, quia sentenciam quam ego exposui de ecclesia volo defendere de virtute sermonis, communicando capciosissime sophiste et cuicunque fideli vel infideli volenti quantumlibet protervire. Si autem ista descripcio non sit valida, ad quid ponitur pro cognoscenda sancta matre ecclesia? Nono videtur, supposita communi logica iam currente, quod ecclesia universalis non sit aliquid, quia nec substancia nec accidens, et per consequens, cum nulla sit pars universalis ecclesie, ipsa foret tam quantitative quam qualitative indivisibilis, et per consequens, nec dominus papa nec Romana ecclesia foret caput universalis ecclesie, eo quod non sequitur: Si ipse exercet officium capitis in aliquos istorum, ergo in istos, quia pari evidencia diabolus foret caput eiusdem ecclesie et haberet quotlibet capita tam bona quam mala. Decimo et ultimo videtur quoad proprietates et accidencia ecclesie quod ipsa non habet sexus distinccionem, virginitatem, etatem vel alia accidencia que fides scripture sibi attribuit, et per idem, non habet privilegium vel peccatum, temporalia vel regulam vivendi, cum non magis tendat ad unitatem quam membra diaboli, cum in illis sit pax quam Christus Matthei X°, 34, dicit venire dissolvere. Istas instancias pueriles contra rudimenta fidei oportet fidelem clare dissolvere, quia in principiis errorum oportet diligenter obstare, cum eorum admissio abducit in devium.

Seventhly it seems that there is no [church triumphant]{g:ecclesia triumphans}, in that in the
[homeland]{g:patria} it has not had faith, as is said. And so no multitude will perpetually be the
church, the [bride]{g:sponsa} of Christ; or else the church at its perfection will become not-church,
which is against scripture and reason.[^11] [115] Eighthly it seems that the catholic church is
nowhere, nor has any part, since it is nothing; and so the Roman church would be neither worthier nor
less worthy than the catholic church. I set out these [evidences]{g:evidencia} so that the answer may
be made seriously to the matter and form of the arguments, nowhere turning aside from the truth in the
proper sense of the words; for the teaching which I have expounded concerning the church I am willing
to defend in the proper sense of the words, sharing it with the most captious sophist and with anyone,
believer or unbeliever, who wishes to be as wanton as he likes.[^12] But if this definition is not
valid, to what purpose is it set down for knowing holy mother church? Ninthly it seems, supposing the
common logic now current, that the universal church is not anything, because [it is] neither
substance nor accident; and consequently, since there is no part of the universal church, it would be
indivisible both quantitatively and qualitatively; and consequently neither the lord
[pope]{g:papa} nor the Roman church would be head of the universal church, since it does not follow:
"If he exercises the office of head over some of these, therefore over these"; for by equal evidence
the devil would be head of the same church, and it would have any number of heads, good as well as
bad.[^13] Tenth and last, it seems, as to the properties and accidents of the church, that it does not
have distinction of sex, virginity, age or the other accidents that the faith of scripture attributes
to it; and by the same token it does not have privilege or sin, [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} or a
rule of living, since it tends no more to unity than the members of the devil, since in them is the
peace which Christ, Matthew 10:34, says he came to dissolve.[^14] These childish
[objections]{g:instancia} against the rudiments of the faith the faithful man must clearly dissolve,
because one must diligently resist errors at their beginnings, since admitting them leads astray.[^15]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 112.10: Erroneous definition of the Church as the multitude of Christians who keep whole the true faith.
- 112.20: This definition excludes the O. T. Saints
- 112.31: and Christ himself,
- 113.4: so that he would not be the head of the Church.
- 113.13: The mass of Christians never keeps the faith quite whole.
- 113.26: The apostles would be out of the Church, with all who lived before general Councils had declared the faith.
- 114.13: Death and birth would be always making a new Church.
- 114.23: The Church would include all the foreknown in present grace and exclude all predestinate who were in error.
- 114.31: There would be no Church triumphant.
- 115.1: The Catholic Church would be nothing.
- 115.5: These opinions really follow from the premisses.
- 115.13: There would be no single head to the Church.
- 115.23: The Church would lose all the attributes that Scripture assigns to her.

## Notes

[^1]: **T/R/S.** A margin *Quedam descripcio ecclesie*; B margin, another hand, *Diffinicio ecclesie*
    (112.10). *Describens*: "defining", the verb of *descripcio*. The doctor is not identified by
    Loserth here, nor by us (LEDGER E05f-1). *Consiliis*: the codd. mostly write *consiliis* for
    *conciliis* (Loserth, 113.29): councils.

[^2]: **S/T/C.** John 19:34, DR "one of the soldiers with a spear opened his side: and immediately
    there came out blood and water"; *dormientis* (Christ asleep in death) is Wyclif's. A
    *assummeret* (112.16–17). *Sed hoc non fuit*: the minor premiss.

[^3]: **S/T/R.** Boniface VIII, *Unam sanctam*, *Extrav. comm.* 1.8.1 (the form is ours) (LEDGER
    E05f-2). *VIIIⁱ*: the raised ordinal *i* is the print's (plate). 112.23–26: A
    *patrinum*, B *patuum*, A₁ corrected *patrum*; A B lack *quod*, A₁ *sequitur quod*; B *nullus
    patruum*, A₁ *nullum patrum*. B margin, a recent hand: *Extra ecclesiam non est salus*.

[^4]: **C/S/T.** *Christi*: "Christ's", the genitive of belonging; *generacio passiva*: what is
    generated, as against the principle that generates it. Acts 11:26, DR "at Antioch the disciples
    were first named Christians". A A₁ *capite et membris* (113.9); A A₁ lack *et multo … ecclesie*
    (113.10). *In sancto triduo*: the three days from the Passion to the Resurrection.

[^5]: **T/C.** B *hoc solum: Isti* (113.20); A A₁ *fingere que creditur*, without *fidem* (113.21).
    *Fides que creditur*: the faith that is believed (its content), as against the faith by which one
    believes (cf. 110.24, 05-e). *Talia negantur … aggregantur*: we take it that the doctor denies
    any such common faith, and that if it is posited it is heaped crudely into his definition; the
    construal is doubtful. A margin, printed below 144a without mark (113.27): *Argumenta contra
    quandam descripcionem ecclesie continue infra*.

[^6]: **C.** *Simpliciter infideles*: unbelievers without qualification, outside the faith
    altogether; *fidem creditam valde rectam*: what they believe is in great part right. *Obliquatam*:
    bent, made crooked.

[^7]: **C.** *Fuit ita quod illa est*: "it was the case that it is", that is, that the definition
    held of it. *Conscripcio* as printed: "the written [definition]" (Latin doubts); *compeciit*, from
    *competere*, "applied to".

[^8]: **C/T.** *Inconveniens* here is an absurdity in argument (CONTEXTUAL, GLOSSARY). B *stetissent*
    (114.8). We read *ante prima consilia post ascensionem* together, as in the clause before; the
    print's comma after *consilia* would give "after the ascension it would be true for us …".
    Stephen died before any council (Acts 7).

[^9]: **T/C.** A A₁ lack *et per consequens … catholice* (114.15–16). *Eadem … in numero*:
    numerically the same. *Unam sanctam* teaches one church; on the doctor's definition there would be
    as many as there are changes in the multitude.

[^10]: **C.** *In gracia*: in present grace (cf. Loserth's side-note, 114.23). *Scarioth*: Judas
    Iscariot. *Infideles*: unbelievers, for the time of their fall, David, Peter, Paul (the persecutor).

[^11]: **T/C.** A₁ *habent fidem* (114.32). In the homeland faith has given way to sight, so the church triumphant has no
    faith and on this definition is no church.

[^12]: **T/C.** The *V* before *Istas* (115.5) is not Wyclif's (an alinea or margin letter) and is not
    translated. *De virtute sermonis*: in the proper sense of the words (proposed for GLOSSARY).
    *Communicando*: putting [the teaching] to any disputant; *protervire*: to argue wantonly.

[^13]: **T/C.** B *ipse exercitet* (115.19). *In aliquos istorum, ergo in istos*: from headship over
    some members to headship over them all together. If the church were nothing, whoever is head of
    some of its members would be its head, so the devil too, head of the foreknown.

[^14]: **S/T.** Matt. 10:34, DR "I came not to send peace, but the sword"; Wyclif's *pacem … venire
    dissolvere* paraphrases it. A₁ *temporale* (115.27). *Dissolvere* comes back in the next sentence
    for answering the objections; "dissolve" keeps the echo.

[^15]: **T/S/R.** A₁ *pluries* for *pueriles* (115.31). *In principiis … obstare* echoes the
    commonplace *principiis obsta* (Ovid, *Remedia amoris* 91, to be checked; LEDGER E05f-3). Loserth
    on chapter 5 (115.33): cf. Hus, *De ecclesia*, fol. 205 (LEDGER E05f-4).
