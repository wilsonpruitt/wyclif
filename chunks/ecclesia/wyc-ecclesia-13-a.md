# *De ecclesia*, chapter 13, part 1: temporary alms better than perpetual: the definitions, and the proof that they are more authentic

## ¶1 · 274.26–276.17

> [p. 274] Ex istis incidit declarare sentenciam quam dixi superius scilicet quod humana elemosina temporalis foret elemosina humana perpetua tam clero quam elemosinanti cum paribus magis autentica, magis meritoria et altrinsecus plus secura. [p. 275] Et ne equivocetur in termino, supponatur quod humana elemosina perpetua sit elemosina corporalis, data ab homine simpliciter sub nomine elemosine perpetue, sicut in cartis regum Anglie sepe fit mencio. Elemosina enim humana temporalis est elemosina data ab homine sine tali titulo expressata, sicut dantur bona vocata mobilia communitatibus vel personis egentibus. Prima autem elemosina fit communiter de redditibus secularibus, de proventibus ecclesiarum vel de impensionibus annuis ad perpetuitatem secundum traditiones humanas signo sensibili obligatorio limitatis. Suppono secundo positivum cuiuscunque partis conclusionis proposite; nam contingit secularem existentem in gracia pie dare tales elemosinas perpetuas, ergo contingit eas esse elemosinas autenticas, meritorias et securas; nec contendet amicus vel adversarius circa probacionem huius, cum leges ecclesie approbant dotaciones, fundaciones et appropriaciones perpetuas factas clero. Non autem sic approbarent factum illud, nisi fuerit autenticum, meritorium et securum; ergo conclusio. Unde adversarii clamant quod iustissime, necessariissime et decentissime ecclesia taliter est dotata, in tantum quod non licuit dominis temporalibus sine excommunicacione et dampnacione perpetua taliter non dotasse. Istis suppositis probatur conclusio per hoc quod Christus expresse facto exemplavit et verbo docuit temporalem elemosinam, non autem dictam elemosinam perpetuam; ergo conclusio. Illud autem quod docetur expressius in scriptura, quod procedit signancius a persona magis autentica et in quo plus crescit ecclesia, habet tres condiciones superius recitatas. Nam negligencia vel fatuitas fuisset in Christo, si dedisset legem ad gubernandum suam ecclesiam, et regulam abiectam [p. 276] vel minus necessariam expressius declarasset, regulam autem plus necessariam occultasset; ideo antiqui doctores ceperunt ex fide scripture quod testamentum Christi datum est ecclesie, tam in materia quam in forma, secundum quod est necessarium et expedicius, ut dirigat viantes per artam viam ducentem ad patriam, et per consequens, ut doceat quomodo viator est disposicior ad beatitudinem acquirendam. Illa autem que scriptura tacet, ut de nomine canis Tobie, de die iudicii et de futuris persecucionibus seculi, expedit particulariter ignorare. Cum ergo Christus et eius apostoli ceperunt ab homine dumtaxat elemosinas temporales et docuit suos discipulos precise tales recipere, ut patet Matthei X° et Luce X° ut dictum est XI° capitulo de privilegiis, manifestum videtur quod Christus preponderavit in ecclesia sua altrinsecus illum modum dandi et recipiendi elemosinas creature.

From these things it falls [to me] to declare the [teaching]{g:sentencia} which I stated above, namely that [temporary]{g:elemosina-temporalis} human alms would be, other things being equal, more [authentic]{g:autenticus}, more meritorious, and on either side more secure than perpetual human alms, both for the [clergy]{g:clerus} and for the [almsgiver]{g:elemosinans}.[^1] [275] And lest there be equivocation in the term, let it be supposed that perpetual human alms is bodily alms given by a man [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} under the name of perpetual alms, as is often mentioned in the charters of the kings of England. For temporary human alms is alms given by a man without such a [title]{g:titulus} expressed, as [so-called]{g:vocatus} movable goods are given to communities or to needy persons.[^2] And the first alms is commonly made from secular [revenues]{g:reditus}, from the income of churches, or from annual payments [assigned]{g:limitare} in perpetuity according to human traditions by a [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} obligatory sign.[^3] Second, I suppose the positive of each part of the proposed [conclusion]{g:conclusio}: for it is possible that a [secular]{g:secularis} in grace [piously]{g:pie} gives such perpetual alms; therefore it is possible that they are authentic, meritorious and secure alms; nor will friend or adversary dispute over the proof of this, since the laws of the church approve perpetual [endowments]{g:dotacio}, foundations and [appropriations]{g:appropriacio} made to the clergy. Now they would not so approve that deed unless it were authentic, meritorious and secure; therefore the conclusion.[^4] Hence the adversaries cry out that most justly, most necessarily and most fittingly the church has been so endowed, so much so that it was not lawful for the [temporal lords]{g:temporales-domini} not to have so endowed [it] without excommunication and perpetual damnation. These things supposed, the conclusion is proved by this, that Christ expressly exemplified by deed and taught by word temporary alms, but not the said perpetual alms; therefore the conclusion. Now that which is taught more expressly in scripture, which proceeds more markedly from a more authentic person, and in which the church grows more, has the three conditions recited above.[^5] For it would have been negligence or folly in Christ if he had given a law to govern his church, and had declared more expressly a lowly [276] or less necessary rule, but had hidden the more necessary rule; therefore the ancient doctors took it from the faith of scripture that the testament of Christ was given to the [church]{g:ecclesia}, both in matter and in form, according as is necessary and more expedient, to direct [those on the road]{g:viantes} along the strait way leading to the [homeland]{g:patria}, and consequently to teach how a [wayfarer]{g:viator} is better disposed to acquire blessedness.[^6] And the things on which scripture is silent, such as the name of Tobias's dog, the day of judgment, and the future persecutions of the world, it is expedient [for us] [to be ignorant of]{g:ignorare} in particular. Since, then, Christ and his apostles took from man only temporary alms, and he taught his disciples to receive precisely such, as is clear from Matthew 10 and Luke 10, as was said in the eleventh chapter, on privileges, it seems manifest that Christ gave greater weight in his church, on either side, to that manner of giving and receiving the alms of a creature.[^7]

## ¶2 · 276.18–276.23

> [p. 276] Et confirmacio huius est quod per illas elemosinas trecentis annis et amplius plus viguit ecclesia christiana. Privilegia ergo tam singulariter et signanter concessa a principe pacis pro quibus noluit suos contendere (ut dictum est in fine proximi capituli) sunt secularibus privilegiis pociora.

And the confirmation of this is that through those alms the Christian church flourished the more for three hundred years and more. Therefore the [privileges]{g:privilegium} granted so singularly and markedly by the prince of peace, for which he did not wish his own to contend (as was said at the end of the last chapter), are of more worth than secular privileges.[^8]

## ¶3 · 276.24–278.6

> [p. 276] Sed particularius descendendo probatur specialius particulatim conclusio, et primo quod elemosina temporalis foret seculari elemosinanti magis autentica, nam talis elemosinacio foret tam operi quam doctrine verbali regis regum similior; cum ergo operacio membri ecclesie sit eo magis autentica quo est capiti similior, quia de tanto est ab auctore approbacior, sequitur conclusio. Loquor autem de opere cum suis circumstanciis secundum quod plus sonant in moralem iusticiam (in quantum huiusmodi), sic quod de se habet plus rectitudinis operis boni de genere; et hoc intelligo [p. 277] per istam particulam cum paribus. Contingit autem per accidens occisionem hominis vel eius punicionem esse magis autenticam, quia plus Deo placentem quam data liberacio a morte vel restitucio ad prosperitatem in bonis nature vel fortune, supposito quod precedat ex maiori caritate. Verumptamen cum operacio que secundum se et secundum suas circumstancias est exemplari suo in Christo similior, debet correspondenter esse caritativior, suppono quod loquamur de autenticacione operis (in quantum huiusmodi), et cum omnis autenticacio operis nostri correspondet proporcionaliter approbacioni Christi, patet conclusio. Nemo enim debet facere opus misericordie nisi secundum doctrinam quam didicit a Christo, sed cum Christus docuit nos dare temporalem elemosinam et non sic perpetuam, sequitur quod temporalis elemosinacio sit magis autentica. Item, proporcionaliter ut operacio meritoria sapit maiorem humilitatem, est Deo placencior, et per consequens plus autentica apud magistrum qui est mitis et humilis corde; sed sic est de elemosinacione temporali respectu elemosinacionis perpetue. Ergo illa est magis autentica. Minor probatur ex hoc quod solius domini temporis qui est Deus est per se concedere donum perpetuum, unde (ut alias declaravi) non subiacet potestati hominis concedere donum pro aliquo tempore, nisi pro tempore quo ipse potest habere vel dominari eidem, cum Deus non potest concedere ius extensius sive uberius quam ipsemet potest habere. Cum ergo non sit in potestate secularis domini habere perpetuo tale donum, videtur quod non sufficit per se tam extense quicquam concedere. Et confirmacio huius est quod homo non potest ratificare perpetuitatem dominii suo heredi naturali cui magis afficitur tamquam magis capaci. Quomodo ergo rectificabit elemosinam ab approbacione Christi magis extraneam? Sicut ergo Edgarus et sanctus Edwardus [p. 278] non habuerunt potestatem ad dandum suis heredibus naturalibus dominium regni nostri, sicut patet ex conquestu sequente, sic videtur quod non suffecerunt donare accessorium persone tam extranee, ymmo sicut Willielmus conquestor non tenebatur restituere principale sic nec accessorium.

Now, descending more particularly, the conclusion is proved more specially, part by part; and first, that temporary alms would be more authentic for the secular almsgiver; for such [almsgiving]{g:elemosinacio} would be more like both the work and the verbal teaching of the King of kings; since, then, the activity of a [member]{g:membrum} of the church is the more authentic the more like it is to the [head]{g:caput}, because to that extent it is more approved by its author, the conclusion [follows]{g:sequitur}.[^9] And I speak of the work with its circumstances, according as they tend more toward moral justice (as such), so that of itself it has more of the rightness of a work [good in kind]{g:bona-de-genere}; and this I understand [277] by that phrase "other things being equal".[^10] But it can happen accidentally that the killing of a man, or his punishment, is more authentic, because more pleasing to God, than a deliverance from death granted [him] or a restoration to prosperity in the goods of nature or of fortune, supposing that it proceeds from greater [charity]{g:caritas}. Nevertheless, since an activity which in itself and in its circumstances is more like its exemplar in Christ ought correspondingly to be more charitable, I suppose that we are speaking of the authentication of the work (as such); and since every authentication of our work corresponds proportionally to Christ's approval, the conclusion is clear.[^11] For no one ought to do a work of mercy except according to the [doctrine]{g:doctrina} he has learned from Christ; now since Christ taught us to give temporary alms and not so perpetual, it follows that temporary almsgiving is more authentic. Again, in proportion as a meritorious activity savours of greater humility, it is more pleasing to God, and consequently more authentic with the master who is meek and humble of heart; now so it is with temporary almsgiving in respect of perpetual almsgiving. Therefore it is more authentic.[^12] The minor is proved from this, that it belongs to the lord of time alone, who is God, of himself to grant a perpetual gift; hence (as I have declared elsewhere) it does not lie in the power of man to grant a gift for any time except for the time during which he himself can have it or [exercise lordship]{g:dominari} over it, since God cannot grant a [right]{g:ius} more extensive or more ample than he himself can have.[^13] Since, then, it is not in the power of a secular lord to have such a gift in perpetuity, it seems that he does not suffice of himself to grant anything so extensively. And the confirmation of this is that a man cannot ratify the perpetuity of [lordship]{g:dominium} to his natural heir, to whom he is more attached as to one more capable. How then will he rectify an alms further estranged from Christ's approval? As, then, Edgar and Saint Edward [278] did not have power to give the lordship of our [kingdom]{g:regnum} to their natural heirs, as is clear from the conquest that followed, so it seems that they did not suffice to give the accessory to a person so foreign; indeed, just as William the Conqueror was not bound to restore the principal, so neither [was he bound to restore] the accessory.[^14]

## ¶4 · 278.7–278.11

> [p. 278] Et confirmacio est ex hoc quod multas tales elemosinas Saxonum transmutavit et monachos Normanie introduxit; quod si omnino fuisset illicitum, maior pars cleri possessionati in Anglia alienum bonum sine titulo usurparet.

And the confirmation is from this, that he transferred many such alms of the Saxons and brought in monks of Normandy; and if this had been wholly unlawful, the greater part of the [possessioner]{g:possessionati} clergy in England would be usurping another's good without title.[^15]

## ¶5 · 278.12–278.32

> [p. 278] Item, videtur quod multe carte perpetue elemosine sapiunt ex parte elemosinantis et male interpretantis blasphemiam et superbiam, cum multi domini seculares presumunt quod est in potestate eorum dare simpliciter in perpetuam elemosinam signatum redditum, qualibet condicione elemosinarii pretermissa; quod declaratum est proximo capitulo Deo non posse competere, cum 1° Regum II° legitur quod Deus condicionem Hely notaverat, sic quod non obstante quacunque promissione non tenet concessio, nisi servicium Dei virtuose peragat. Si ergo Deus qui potest iustificare impium et scit condiciones ac dignitates tocius generis succedencium subintellegit condicionem, ceca presumpcio videretur in homine qui nec potest donare per tantam periodum nec iustificare aliquem eius datarium, ut asserat se concedere datam elemosinam cuilibet illius generis absolute; in hoc enim implicat se sic concedere, sive Deus voluerit sive noluerit; quod non potest, cum nemo potest quicquam concedere, nisi prius Deus ipsum concesserit: ideo racione talis mendacii dissolvuntur multe elemosine vocate perpetue.

Again, it seems that many charters of perpetual alms savour of blasphemy and pride on the part of the almsgiver and of the one who interprets [them] wrongly, since many secular lords presume that it is in their power to give without qualification, in perpetual alms, a designated revenue, passing over every condition of the [almsman]{g:elemosinarius}; which was declared in the last chapter cannot belong to God, since in 1 Samuel 2:30 we read that God had noted the condition of Heli, so that, notwithstanding any promise, the [grant]{g:concessio} does not hold unless he performs the [service]{g:servicium} of God virtuously.[^16] If, then, God, who can justify the ungodly and knows the conditions and deserts of the whole line of successors, [understands]{g:subintelligere} a condition [as implied], it would seem blind presumption in a man, who can neither give for so long a period nor justify any grantee of his, that he should assert that he grants the given alms to anyone of that line absolutely;[^17] for in this he implies that he so grants whether God wills or not; which he cannot, since no one can grant anything unless God has first granted it: therefore, by reason of such a lie, many so-called perpetual alms are dissolved.[^18]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 274.26: Temporary alms are better than perpetual.
- 275.1: Definition of each.
- 275.12: Let us suppose perpetual alms in themselves to be good;
- 275.26: still temporary are better, since they have Christ's sanction,
- 276.18: and were practised in the primitive church.
- 276.24: 1. Temporary alms are more in God's way of working, and are so far, better.
- 277.18: 2. Temporary alms imply more humility.
- 277.24: Man's power over things is temporary and he cannot give rights that he does not possess.
- 278.15: Many perpetual alms are given without conditions as to the character of the grantee.

## Notes

[^1]: **C/R/T.** Chapter 13 begins at 274.25. A in the margin *Utrum elemosina humana corporalis perpetua sit fundanda* (274.26) and *Conclusio* (274.28); side-note 274.26. *Temporalis*: temporary, for a time, against *perpetua*; not "temporal" (*temporalia*, temporal goods). *Cum paribus*: other things being equal, explained at 276.33–277.1. *Altrinsecus*: on either side, for clergy and giver alike. *Autentica*: authentic, carrying authority (Christ's approval, 276.28–30). *Sentenciam*: the thesis taught (GLOSSARY, CONTEXTUAL: teaching / sentence). *Sentenciam quam dixi superius*: the place is Wyclif's and not identified here (LEDGER E13a-1). A and A₁ read *plus secatur* (274.30).

[^2]: **R/G.** Side-note 275.1. *Sub nomine elemosine perpetue*: the grant "in perpetual alms" of English charters, the tenure in frankalmoign (*libera eleemosina*, as in Glanvill XIII, the assize *utrum*; LEDGER E13a-2). *Sine tali titulo expressata*: without such a title expressed. *Bona vocata mobilia*: so-called movable goods, chattels.

[^3]: **C/T.** B reads *de pencionibus* (275.10). *Impensionibus annuis*: annual payments, pensions. *Signo sensibili obligatorio*: the charter and seal, the perceptible sign that binds. *Limitatis* agrees with *impensionibus*, perhaps with all three sources of the alms.

[^4]: **C/T.** A₁ comments in the margin *possibilitatem vel posicionem* (275.12–13); side-note 275.12. *Positivum*: the positive degree ("authentic, meritorious, secure") of each part, the conclusion asserting the comparative. *Contingit*: it is possible. A and A₁ lack *perpetuas* (275.14).

[^5]: **C/T.** A in the margin *Racio* (275.26); side-note 275.26. *Probatur conclusio*: the minor, that Christ taught temporary alms, is stated first (with *ergo conclusio*), the major after it: what is so taught has the three conditions. *Signancius*: more markedly.

[^6]: **S/T.** A and A₁ read *necessarius* (276.5). *Ceperunt ex fide scripture*: "took from the faith of scripture", with the verb governing *ex* (GLOSSARY §C, *ex fide*). *Artam viam ducentem ad patriam*: cf. Matthew 7:14 ("strait is the way that leadeth to life", DR). *Antiqui doctores*: unnamed. *Tam in materia quam in forma*: in content and in form.

[^7]: **S/T.** Side-note 276.18 (at the next paragraph). *Nomine canis Tobie*: the dog of the book of Tobias, whose name scripture does not give. *Matthei X° et Luce X°*: Christ's charge to the twelve and to the seventy-two (Matthew 10:9–10, Luke 10:7). B reads *XX. cap.* for *XI° capitulo* (276.16); the cross-reference is to 11-e, 247–48 (Matt. 10:9, Luke 10:7, Christ's privileges to the disciples); B's XX is wrong (LEDGER E13a-3). *Elemosinas creature*: alms from a creature, as against God's gifts. *Preponderavit … altrinsecus*: gave more weight, for giver and receiver alike.

[^8]: **C.** *Trecentis annis et amplius*: the church's first three centuries, before its endowment. *Principe pacis*: Christ (cf. Isaiah 9:6). *In fine proximi capituli*: 273.17–274.4 (12-f). *Pociora*: of more worth.

[^9]: **C/T.** A in the margin *Confirmacio* (276.24); side-note 276.24. *Particulatim*: part by part, taking the three comparatives in turn; this is the first. *Regis regum*: Christ. A and B abbreviate *approbacior*; A₁ reads *approbabilior*; B reads *de tanto ab* (276.30). *Operacio*: activity, the doing of the work.

[^10]: **C.** *Plus sonant in moralem iusticiam*: tend more toward moral justice. *In quantum huiusmodi*: as such, i.e. considered as almsgiving. *Rectitudinis operis boni de genere*: the rightness of a work good in kind (by its object), before the further goodness charity gives. *Cum paribus*: 274.28.

[^11]: **C.** *Per accidens*: by the circumstances of a case, not by its kind. *Data liberacio a morte*: a pardon from death. *Bonis nature vel fortune*: goods of nature (life, health) or of fortune (wealth). *Exemplari suo in Christo*: its pattern in Christ's life. *Caritativior*: more charitable, fuller of charity (GLOSSARY §A, *caritas*). *Autenticacione*: the being authentic.

[^12]: **S.** Side-note 277.18. Matthew 11:29 ("because I am meek, and humble of heart", DR). *Sapit maiorem humilitatem*: savours of greater humility.

[^13]: **C/R.** Side-note 277.24. *Solius domini temporis*: of God alone as lord of time, who alone can grant for all time. *Ut alias declaravi*: perhaps *De civili dominio* (to be checked, LEDGER E13a-4). *Cum Deus non potest concedere ius extensius …*: the premise argues downward from God: not even God grants a right beyond what he can have, so neither can man. *Ius*: a right (GLOSSARY §B).

[^14]: **R/C/T.** *Edgarus et sanctus Edwardus*: King Edgar and Edward the Confessor, remembered as benefactors of the monasteries, Westminster among them (their privileges are quoted at 205.3–206.26, 10-a). *Conquestu sequente*: the Norman conquest of 1066. *Accessorium … principale*: in law the accessory follows the principal: the endowment follows the lordship of the kingdom. *Persone tam extranee*: the endowed church, a stranger to the giver's line. B reads *extranie* (278.4). *Rectificabit*: will make good.

[^15]: **R/T.** William's transfer of Saxon endowments to Norman houses and monks (context to be checked, LEDGER E13a-5). B lacks *fuisset … titulo* (278.9). *Cleri possessionati*: the possessioner clergy, the endowed religious (GLOSSARY §B).

[^16]: **S/T.** Side-note 278.15. *Signatum redditum*: a designated revenue. *Declaratum est proximo capitulo*: cap. 12, 12-b, 256–57 (LEDGER E13a-6). 1 Samuel 2:30 (Vulgate 1 Kings): the Lord's promise to Heli's house withdrawn. A₁ corrects to *teneret*; B reads *servicio* (278.21). A in the margin *Racio* (278.22).

[^17]: **S/T/G.** *Qui potest iustificare impium*: cf. Romans 4:5 ("him that justifieth the ungodly", DR). A and A₁ read *subintellexit* (278.24) and *durare per tantam* (278.25–26). *Subintellegit condicionem*: understands a condition as implied (GLOSSARY §C). *Generis*: the line of successors to the grant. *Datarium*: grantee.

[^18]: **C.** *Implicat se sic concedere*: he implies that he grants in this way, the lie that dissolves the grant. *Nisi prius Deus ipsum concesserit*: unless God has first granted it [to the granter].
