### 03-a ¶2 · 28.17–29.6  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 28] Ex⁸ istis plane consequitur quod Christus fuit verus homo in triduo inter eius mortem et resurreccionem. Probatur sic. Cuiuslibet⁹ hominis tam corpus quam anima est inseparabiliter idem homo: **sed** tam corpus Christi quam eius anima per dictum triduum manet: ergo Christus fuit per dictum triduum verus homo. Maior patet ex dictis. Nam quomodocunque scriptura vel alii doctores catholici hoc intellexerunt sive figurative sive aliter, suppono me intelligere; sic tamen quod maneat verum de virtute¹⁰ sermonis: et sic oportet me concedere aliqua¹¹ que secuntur. Nam licet illud argumentum det michi plenam fidem ad conclusionem propositam, verumtamen pro intellectu scripture et veritatum annexarum huic [p. 29] materie cum dictis doctorum oportet illam materiam ulterius triplici medio¹ dilatare. Veritas enim testatur omnimode sibi ipsi, nec aliquid ex veritate consequens offenderet pias aures. Quicquid ergo ex hoc principio perfectus² philosophus sciret deducere concedi debet tanquam catholicum, et oppositum abici³ tanquam dissonum fidei Christiane.

ENGLISH: From these things it plainly follows that Christ was a true man in the three days between his death and resurrection. It is proved thus. The body as well as the soul of any man whatever is inseparably the same man; now both the body of Christ and his soul remain through the said three days; therefore Christ was a true man through the said three days. The major is clear from what has been said. For however scripture or other catholic doctors understood this, whether figuratively or otherwise, I suppose that I understand it; yet in such a way that it remains true [in the proper sense of the words]{g:de-virtute-sermonis}:[^1] and so I must grant some things that follow. For although that argument gives me full faith in the proposed [conclusion]{g:conclusio}, nevertheless, for the understanding of scripture and of the truths annexed to this [29] matter together with the sayings of the doctors, I must extend that matter further by a threefold [middle term]{g:media}.[^2] For truth bears witness to itself in every way, nor would anything that follows from truth offend pious ears. Whatever therefore a perfect philosopher would know how to deduce from this principle ought to be granted as catholic, and the opposite rejected as discordant with the Christian faith.

### 03-a ¶3 · 29.7–29.24  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 29] Ut primo sequitur ex eius opposito quod Christus non descendisset ad inferos, nec iacuisset sepultus in sepulcro, sicut tamen⁴ superius allegatur ex fide. Si enim descendit ad inferos, vel secundum deitatem vel secundum humanitatem. Non secundum⁵ deitatem, cum non secundum illam sit mobilis; et si secundum humanitatem, tunc illa fuit servata⁶ pro tunc descendens ad inferos. Nec sufficit hoc, nisi illa pro tunc fuerit sibi ypostatice copulata: quia persone non est attribuenda pars⁷ denominacionis⁸ pro tempore pro quo ipsa non est pars persone: cum tunc omne corpus racione materie foret perpetue,⁹ et omnis homo racione anime eque post mortem viveret sicut ante. Quod negant illi, quibus non sapit, quod Christus vixit humanitus in dicto triduo: unde dicunt quod est impossibile de vi vocis quod Christus fuit mortuus aut sepultus vel quod descendit ad inferos: **sed** sic intelligitur: corpus, quod quondam fuit Christi, sepultum est, et anima, que quondam fuit Christi, descendit ad inferos, **sed** non Christus,¹⁰ cum tunc non fuit ita quod Christus est.¹⁰

ENGLISH: As, first, [it follows]{g:sequitur} from its opposite that Christ would not have descended into hell, nor lain buried in the sepulchre, as is nevertheless [alleged]{g:allegare} above from faith.[^3] For if he descended into hell, [he did so] either [as to the Godhead]{g:secundum-deitatem} or as to [the humanity]{g:humanitas}. Not as to the Godhead, since as to that he is not movable; and if as to the humanity, then that [humanity] was preserved, at that time descending into hell.[^4] Nor does this suffice, unless it was at that time joined to him [hypostatically]{g:ypostatice}: because a part is not to be attributed to the [person]{g:persona} in [naming]{g:denominacio}[^5] for a time for which it is not a part of the person: since then every body would be perpetual by reason of its matter, and every man by reason of his soul would live after death just as before. This is denied by those to whom it does not make sense[^6] that Christ lived [humanly]{g:humanitus} in the said three days: hence they say that "Christ was dead," or "buried," or "descended into hell" is impossible [by the force of the words]{g:de-vi-vocis}; but it is understood thus: the body that was once Christ's was buried, and the soul that was once Christ's descended into hell, but not Christ, since then it was not so that Christ is.

### 03-a ¶4 · 29.25–30.22  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 29] **Sed** revera ista ficticia superaddit perfidiam Iudeorum. Nam turba Iudaica Ioh. 12. dixit Iesu: ‘nos audivimus ex lege quod Christus manet in eternum’: quia Iesaie¹¹ 9° scribitur: ‘super solium David et super regnum eius sedebit, ut confirmet illud et corroboret a modo¹² et usque in sempiternum,’ id est, a tempore quo ‘puer natus est nobis.’ Probatur sic. Iesus natus est rex humanitus, ut patet Ioh. 18¹³ ‘Tu dicis quia rex sum ego: nam ego¹⁴ in hoc natus sum.’ Cum ergo per mortem non fuit intercisio regni sui, cum fuit spirituale [p. 30] a corpore independens; ergo mansit perpetuo. Confirmatur: quia Daniel 7 scribitur de humanitate Christi: ‘potestas eius eterna que non auferetur,¹ et regnum eius quod non corrumpetur.’ Non ergo potest ille Dominus eciam² secundum humanitatem perdere regnum suum: quia Michee 4^to. dicitur, ‘regnabit super eos ex hoc³ nunc et usque in seculum.’ Et patet⁴ ex hoc quod non est⁵ alia regnacio hominis interioris⁵ in Christo nisi que est⁶ regnacio Christi Dei: **sed** anima regnavit a principio sue creacionis continue: ergo Christus. Non enim dictus rex Israel secundum humanitatem ut corporaliter regnet **sed** spiritualiter super illos: ut exponit Augustinus Ome. 50⁷ exponens illud⁷ Ioh. 12 ‘Benedictus qui venit in nomine Domini, rex Israel.’ ‘Non enim,⁸ inquit Augustinus, rex Israel fuit Christus ad exigendum tributum vel ferro exercitum armandum hostesque⁹ visibiliter debellandos; **sed** rex Israel, quod mentes regat, quod in eternum consulat, quod in regnum celorum credentes sperantes amantesque perducat.’ Cum ergo illa¹⁰ potestas spiritualis, existens subiective in anima independenter¹¹ a corpore, non fuit spoliata ab anima Christi in morte; videtur quod eciam¹² in morte fuit humanitas vel spiritus¹³ Christi rex Israel¹⁴ sicut ante.

ENGLISH: But in truth this [fiction]{g:ficticia} outdoes the faithlessness of the Jews.[^7] For the Jewish crowd, John 12, said to Jesus: "We have heard out of the law that Christ abideth for ever": because in Isaiah 9 it is written: "he shall sit upon the throne of David, and upon his kingdom; to establish it and strengthen it from henceforth and for ever,"[^8] that is, from the time at which "a child is born to us." It is proved thus. Jesus was born a king humanly, as is clear from John 18: "Thou sayest that I am a king: for I was born for this."[^9] Since therefore through death there was no interruption of his [kingdom]{g:regnum}, since it was spiritual, [30] independent of the body, therefore it remained perpetually. This is confirmed: for in Daniel 7 it is written of the humanity of Christ: "his [power]{g:potestas} is eternal, which shall not be taken away: and his kingdom that shall not be destroyed." Therefore that Lord cannot, even as to the humanity, lose his kingdom: for in Micah 4 it is said, "he will reign over them from this time now and for ever."[^10] And it is clear from this that there is no other [reigning]{g:regnacio} of the inner man in Christ but that which is the reigning of Christ God: now the soul reigned continuously from the beginning of its creation: therefore Christ [reigned]. For he is not called king of Israel as to the humanity so that he reigns bodily, but [so that he reigns] spiritually over them: as Augustine expounds in Homily 50,[^11] expounding that [text] of John 12, "Blessed is he that cometh in the name of the Lord, the king of Israel." "For Christ," says Augustine, "was not king of Israel for exacting tribute, or arming an army with the sword and subduing enemies visibly; but king of Israel because he rules minds, because he counsels for eternity, because he leads into the kingdom of heaven those who believe, hope and love." Since therefore that spiritual power, existing in the soul as in its [subject]{g:subiectum}[^12] independently of the body, was not stripped from the soul of Christ in death, it seems that even in death the humanity or spirit of Christ was king of Israel as before.

### 03-a ¶6 · 31.3–32.1  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 31] Item nemo sapiens et plene potens incipit opus laudabile in propria persona perficere; et, dum perfecerit¹ in propria persona rudimenta² operis,³ committit suo subdito operis complementum. Cum ergo edificacio ecclesie et liberacio patrum⁴ ab inferis sit opus summe laudabile; sequitur quod Christus in propria persona non perficiet imperfectam disposicionem, et committet spiritui creato ypostatice separato belli victoriam, que est operis complementum. Indignius namque⁵ est temptari a dyabolo, pati, et corporaliter disponere ad edificacionem ecclesie, quam⁶ potenter spoliare Tartara⁷ a dyabolo devicto. Si ergo Christus in propria persona passus fuerat et temptatus, quia in carne et⁸ anima ypostatice copulata: per idem in propria persona spoliavit infernum⁹ extrahendo patriarchas ab inferis, specialiter cum non reliquit unionem anime vel corporis quod assumpsit: dicente Augustino Ome. 47. ‘Si enim dixerimus quod Verbum Dei posuit animam suam et iterum sumpsit eam, metuendum est, ne subintret¹⁰ prava cogitacio et dicatur :—Ergo¹¹ aliquando anima illa separata est a Verbo, et aliquando Verbum illud, ex quo suscepit illam animam¹² fuit sine anima.—Video enim fuisse sine anima humana Verbum cum “in principio erat Verbum;”¹³ **sed** ex quo “Verbum¹³ caro factum est et habitavit in nobis;” et susceptus est¹⁴ a Verbo totus homo, id est,¹⁵ anima et caro. Quid fecit passio, quid fecit mors, nisi corpus ab anima separavit, animam vero a Verbo non separavit?’ Et sequitur. ‘Fidelis latronis animam¹⁶ non deserebat? et deserebat¹⁷ suam? Absit.’ Et quia minus intelligens potest¹⁸ ex dictis intelligere quod Verbum solum per graciam affuit illi anime et non hypostatica¹⁹ unione [p. 32] subdit¹ differenciam: ‘latronis vero¹ animam custodivit, suam vero inseparabiliter habuit.’²

ENGLISH: Again, no one who is wise and fully able begins to accomplish a praiseworthy work in his own person and, when he has accomplished the rudiments of the work in his own person, commits the completion of the work to his subordinate. Since therefore the building of the [church]{g:ecclesia} and the delivering of the fathers from hell is a supremely praiseworthy work, it follows that Christ will not accomplish the imperfect disposing [of it] in his own person and commit to a [created]{g:creatus} spirit, hypostatically separated, the victory of the battle, which is the completion of the work. For it is more unworthy to be tempted by the devil, to suffer, and bodily to dispose [things] for the building of the church, than mightily to [despoil]{g:spoliare} Tartarus, the devil being vanquished.[^15] If therefore Christ had suffered and been tempted in his own person, because [he did so] in flesh and soul hypostatically joined, by the same token in his own person he despoiled hell by drawing the patriarchs out of hell, especially since he did not relinquish the [union]{g:unio} of the soul or of the body that he [assumed]{g:assumere}: as Augustine says in Homily 47:[^16] "For if we say that [the Word]{g:verbum} of God laid down his soul and took it again, it is to be feared lest a wicked thought creep in and it be said: Then at some time that soul was separated from the Word, and at some time that Word, after he took up that soul, was without a soul. For I see that the Word was without a human soul when 'in the beginning was the Word'; but from the time that 'the Word was made flesh and dwelt among us,' and the whole man was taken up by the Word, that is, soul and flesh, what did the passion do, what did death do, but separate the body from the soul? but the soul it did not separate from the Word." And it follows: "He did not forsake the soul of the faithful thief: and did he forsake his own? Far be it." And because one of less understanding can understand from what has been said that the Word was present to that soul only by grace and not by hypostatic union, [32] he adds the difference: "the thief's soul he guarded, but his own he held inseparably."

### 03-a ¶7 · 32.2–32.13  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 32] Et potest illud confirmari ex hoc, quod aliter patres in limbo³ tantum tenerentur creature, que non est Deus, spoliando³ eos de manu dyaboli, sicut Christo secundum humanitatem: quod est impossibile. Cum ergo persone debetur accio, anima fuit tunc anima persone agentis.⁴ Unde sicut anima Christi non fuit⁵ spoliata gracia per mortem, et per consequens non⁶ gracia unionis; sic non fuit spoliata potestate spirituali regia, caractere,⁷ vel sacerdocio, quod est qualitas indelebilis secundum doctores. Unde sanctus Thomas concedit⁸ quod Christus fuit sacerdos secundum animam in triduo iuxta textum Apostoli ad Hebre 8. ‘Iesus **autem** eo quod maneat⁹ in eternum sempiternum habet sacerdocium.’

ENGLISH: And this can be confirmed from this, that otherwise the fathers in [limbo]{g:limbus} would be beholden only to a [creature]{g:creatura}, which is not God, for [despoiling]{g:spoliacio} them out of the hand of the devil, as [they are beholden] to Christ as to the humanity: which is impossible.[^17] Since therefore action is owed to the person, the soul was then the soul of the person acting.[^18] Hence, just as the soul of Christ was not stripped of grace by death, and consequently not of [the grace of union]{g:gracia-unionis}, so it was not stripped of the spiritual royal power, the [character]{g:caracter}, or the priesthood, which is an indelible quality according to the doctors. Hence saint Thomas grants that Christ was a priest as to the soul in the three days, according to the text of the Apostle to the Hebrews 8: "But Jesus, for that he continueth for ever, hath an everlasting priesthood."[^19]

### 03-a ¶8 · 32.14–32.23  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 32] Et revera non sapit¹⁰ quod illa anima sit rex et sacerdos, nisi sit persona; quia actus huius officii sint signantissime personales. Et Doctor Subtilis vere dicit quod Christus mansit Christus in illo triduo. Sicut ergo nichil quod non est Deus sufficit genus humanum redimere, sic nichil quod non est Deus suffecit tam potenter de manu inimici patres eripere. **Sed** si hoc posset¹¹ in virtute Dei creatura ab eo ypostatice separata; per idem posset passio vel meritum cuiuscunque hominis a Deo personaliter seiuncti auctoritate Dei pro quocunque peccato hominis facere recompensam.

ENGLISH: And in truth it makes no sense that that soul is king and priest unless it is a person; because the acts of this office are most markedly personal. And the Subtle Doctor truly says that Christ remained Christ in those three days.[^20] Just as therefore nothing that is not God suffices to redeem the human race, so nothing that is not God sufficed so mightily to snatch the fathers out of the hand of the enemy. But if a creature hypostatically separated from him could do this by the [power]{g:virtus} of God, by the same token the passion or merit of any man whatever, personally disjoined from God, could by the [authority]{g:auctoritas} of God make recompense for any sin of man whatever.

### 03-b ¶1 · 32.24–33.6  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 32] Unde videtur michi scripturas insolubiles appropriate¹² tribuere persone Christi descensum¹³ ad inferos et spoliacionem fidelium de potencia inimici. Nam Psalmo 67 dicitur ad litteram¹⁴ de Christo dividente spolia ‘Ascendisti¹⁴ in altum’ **sed** prius ‘cepisti captivitatem,’ et 3°. ‘dedisti dona hominibus.’ Cui¹⁵ sensui alludit Apostolus ad Ephes. 4. [p. 33] ‘Ascendens,¹ inquit, Christus in altum,² captivam duxit captivitatem.’ Et sequitur: ‘quod **autem** ascendit, quid³ est, nisi quia descendit primum ad partes inferiores terre?’ Et ut ostenderet nobis quod eadem sit persona, que hec cuncta perficiat, subiungit: ‘qui descendit ipse est et qui ascendit super omnes celos ut impleret omnia.’

ENGLISH: Hence it seems to me that the scriptures, which cannot be loosed,[^1] appropriately attribute to the [person]{g:persona} of Christ the descent into hell and the [despoiling]{g:spoliacio} of the faithful out of the [power]{g:potencia}[^2] of the enemy. For in Psalm 67 it is said literally of Christ dividing the spoils, "Thou hast ascended on high," but first "thou hast led captivity captive," and third "thou hast given gifts to men."[^3] To this sense the Apostle alludes in Ephesians 4: [33] "Christ ascending on high," he says, "led captivity captive." And it follows: "Now that he ascended, what is it, but because he descended first into the lower parts of the earth?" And to show us that it is the same person who accomplishes all these things, he adds: "He that descended is the same also that ascended above all the heavens, that he might fill all things."[^4]

### 03-b ¶2 · 33.7–33.16  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 33] Ex istis verbis Apostoli patet primo quod loquitur de Christo secundum humanitatem, secundum quam solum ascendere et descendere sibi conveniunt.⁴ 2°. probat illum hominem esse Deum, cum post ascensionem dedit spiritualia dona hominibus mittendo eis Spiritum Sanctum, ut testatur utrumque testamentum et rei eventus probaverat in effectu. 3°. intendit quod eadem persona secundum humanitatem eandem descendit et⁵ taliter⁵ ascendit. ‘Qui descendit, inquit, ipse et non alius ascendit,’ racionem **autem** subiungit ‘ut impleret omnia.’

ENGLISH: From these words of the Apostle it is clear, first, that he speaks of Christ [as to the humanity]{g:secundum-humanitatem}, as to which alone ascending and descending belong to him. Second, he proves that that man is God, since after the ascension he gave spiritual gifts to men by sending them the Holy Spirit, as both testaments testify and the outcome of the matter had proved in effect. Third, he [maintains]{g:intendere} that the same person, as to the same [humanity]{g:humanitas}, descended and in such a way ascended. "He that descended," he says, "is himself, and not another, that ascended"; and he adds the reason, "that he might fill all things."

### 03-b ¶3 · 33.17–34.13  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 33] Cum enim tres sint⁶ regiones mundi notabiles,⁷ in quibus oportuit Christum humanitus possessionem accipere vel sesinam,⁸ scilicet celestium, terrestrium, et infernorum; proporcionaliter⁹ descendit tripliciter benefaciens et repetens omagium de¹⁰ subiectis. Primo descendit per inanicionem secundum infirmitatem carnis, et¹¹ eum mundus infirmus sibi similis in carne¹¹ et anima eciam genus proprium non cognovit. 2°. Descendit ad patres in limbo qui erant tantum¹² in spiritu post triumphum **sed** proporcionaliter in anima,¹² et indubie fuit in illo triduo in sinu Abrahe per 36 horas, quibus passio¹³ continuata fuerat, non parva exaltacio capite coniuncto membris cum multitudine angelorum. Sicut enim sexto die creatum est genus humanum,¹⁴ sic sexto senario, qui est numerus perfectus ex ductu eius in se circulariter, [p. 34] fuerat recreatum; ut¹ per tantum tempus clarere² posset veritas et excludi³ ficticia mortis Chrsti. Resurrexit **autem**, ut confortaret ecclesiam de fide resurreccionis future, ut patet ad Cor. 15;⁴ et ut ‘primatum teneat’ ut abbas ordinis Christiani aperiendo⁵ patribus complete purgatis ianuam eternalem. Tempus **autem** continens purum integrum diem et duas seminoctes significat quod simpla mors Christi,⁶ que integraliter fuit lucida proporcionaliter ad vitam, fuit medium sufficiens ad nostram duplicem mortem semiplenam. Tercius vero⁷ fuit descensus⁸ corporis in sepulchro,⁹ qui nedum ostendit corpus vere mortuum anima ab eo¹⁰ elongata, **sed** per omnia factus nobis consimilis sanctificavit subterraneas requies beandorum.

ENGLISH: For since there are three notable regions of the world in which Christ had [humanly]{g:humanitus} to take possession or [seisin]{g:sessiva}, namely [the regions] of the heavenly, the earthly and the infernal, proportionally he descended in three ways, doing good and claiming [homage]{g:omagium} from his subjects.[^5] First he descended by [emptying]{g:exinanicio}, according to the [weakness]{g:infirmitas} of the flesh, and the weak world, like him in flesh and soul, even his own kind, did not know him.[^6] Second, he descended to the fathers in [limbo]{g:limbus}, who were only in spirit after the triumph but proportionally in soul;[^7] and without doubt he was in those [three days]{g:triduum} in the bosom of Abraham for 36 hours, through which the passion had continued,[^8] no small exaltation, the head joined to the members, with a multitude of angels. For just as the human race was created on the sixth day, so in the sixth senary, which is a perfect number from its being multiplied into itself circularly, [34] it was re-created;[^9] so that through so long a time the truth could shine forth and the [fiction]{g:ficticia} of the death of Christ be excluded. And he rose again to strengthen the [church]{g:ecclesia} in the faith of the future resurrection, as is clear from 1 Corinthians 15; and so that "he may hold the [primacy]{g:primatus},"[^10] as abbot of the Christian order, opening the eternal gate to the fathers fully purged. And the time, containing one pure whole day and two half-nights, signifies that the single death of Christ, which was wholly bright in proportion to life, was a sufficient means for our double, half-full death.[^11] And the third was the descent of the body into the sepulchre, which [not only]{g:nedum} showed the body truly dead, the soul withdrawn from it, but, made in all things like to us, sanctified the underground resting-places of those who are to be blessed.

### 03-b ¶6 · 35.6–35.29  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 35] Omnia quidem hec fecit humanitas Christi. Et ideo post tantum honorem triumphalem individui humane nature prohibuit nimirum Iohannem angelus seipsum vel³ dulia adorare, ut⁴ patet Apoc. ultimo capitulo: ante quidem satis permiserant,⁵ ut patet Gen. 18 et Iosue 5. All these things indeed the same Son of man did as to the same humanity, continuously [hypostatically]{g:ypostatice} joined: and with that [union]{g:unio} had, it is clear that Christ must continuously be a [creature]{g:creatura} and something that he had not been before. Now what, I ask, if not man? The humanity itself therefore did all these things, that is, "for the work of the ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ," as the Apostle says in Ephesians 4,[^20] for in John 4 it is said: "My meat is to do the will of him that sent me, that I may perfect his work," that is, that I may do it perfectly unto consummation: among which this seems the beginning, to descend into hell and draw out the fathers captive in limbo, as he had promised before. And with this agrees that [word] of Zechariah 9, where, after the prophecy of the Messiah, that the saviour will come, and he "poor, and riding upon an ass, and upon the foal of an ass," he addresses the same Messiah in these words: "Thou also by the blood of thy testament hast sent forth thy prisoners out of the pit, wherein is no water."[^21] Which is expounded literally of the fathers imprisoned in limbo. And that [word] of Hosea 13 sounds the same, [true]{g:verificari} literally of the Messiah: "O death, I will be thy death; O hell, I will be thy bite."

### 03-c ¶1 · 36.15–37.4  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 36] **Sed** consequens videtur¹⁴ falsum tripliciter.¹⁵ Primo ex hoc quod tunc resurreccio Christi foret eius nativitas vel generacio. Et sic Christus haberet triplicem nativitatem et generacionem multiplicem: nativitatem unam eternam et duas alias temporales.¹⁶ Nam generacio Christi prima¹⁷ in utero ideo est generacio quia est produccio a non esse hominem ad esse hominem. Cum ergo sic sit pro instanti¹⁸ resurreccionis, sequitur quod illa sit generacio, et per idem nativitas ex¹⁹ sepulcro, sicut foret posito quod semen virginis esset per se positum²⁰ in sepulcro, et²¹ successive vel subito lineatum,²² ac creata anima copulata.²² Nec est locus nativitati corporali pertinens, nisi forte racione conservacionis, sicut nec est pertinens nativitati ex spiritu, de qua Ioh. 3°., **sed** utrobique habita generacione renati consequitur proporcionaliter²³ [p. 37] nativitas. Oportet ergo ad resurreccionem persone quod eadem persona, que cecidit sopore mortis sive¹ intercisione humanitatis, resurgat; cum spiritus racionalis persone² sit humanitas et sic homo.

ENGLISH: But the [consequent]{g:consequens} seems false in three ways. First from this, that then the resurrection of Christ would be his nativity or generation. And so Christ would have a threefold nativity and a manifold generation: one nativity eternal and two others temporal. For the first generation of Christ, in the womb, is a generation for this reason, that it is a production from not being a man to being a man. Since therefore it is so at the instant of the resurrection, [it follows]{g:sequitur} that that is a generation, and by the same token a nativity out of the sepulchre, as it would be if it were posited that the seed of the virgin had been placed by itself in the sepulchre, and successively or suddenly given its lineaments, and a [created]{g:creatus} soul joined [to it].[^1] Nor is place relevant to bodily nativity, except perhaps by reason of preservation, just as it is not relevant to the nativity from the spirit, of which John 3 [speaks]; but on both sides, once the generation is had, the nativity of the one reborn follows proportionally.[^2] [37] It is necessary therefore, for the resurrection of a [person]{g:persona}, that the same person who fell in the sleep of death, or by the interruption of [the humanity]{g:humanitas}, rise again; since the rational spirit of the person is the humanity and so man.[^3]

### 03-c ¶3 · 37.20–39.9  (8 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 37] Tercio confirmatur illud ex⁶ testimonio scripture. Nam Ioh. 19 scribitur: ‘ad Iesum **autem** cum venissent,⁷ ut⁸ viderunt eum iam mortuum, non fregerunt eius⁹ crura; **sed** unus militum lancea¹⁰ latus eius¹¹ apperuit,¹² et exivit sanguis¹² et aqua: et qui vidit, inquit Iohannes, testimonium perhibuit;¹³ et verum est testimonium eius.’ Rogo, quomodo venissent ad Iesum mortuum,¹⁴ nisi ille homo mortuus esset Iesus? aut quomodo miles lancea latus eius Iesu aperiret,¹⁵ si non tunc fuerat latus Jesu? Numquid credimus quod evangelista, tam signanter asserens¹⁶ se ista vidisse ultra omnia alia¹⁶ gesta Christi, finxit mendacium? Et si dicatur quod littera occidit, sensus **autem** spiritualis et rectus vivificat, [p. 38] cuiusmodi est talis ?—cum venissent ad illud corpus mortuum, quod non¹ potuit esse Iesus, sed¹ prius, non tunc fuit corpus Iesu; tunc unus militum, non latus eius, nec univoce latus alicuius aperuit, **sed** partem unius corporis mortui, quod non tunc fuit Christus vel pars eius²; et exivit, non sanguis redempcionis et aqua baptismatis de latere Christi, quia tunc non fuit Christus. Et sic spiritus, aqua,³ et sanguis non sunt nec fuerunt testes humanitatis Christi, quia non simul fuerunt, sicut videtur Iohannes dicere I Ioh 4°. Nec est verum ad verba illud quod dicit Apostolus ad Ephes. 5°. quod ‘Christus sic dilexit ecclesiam quod tradidit semetipsum pro illa, ut sanctificaret⁴ mundans eam lavacro aque in verbo vite;’ cum ecclesia per totum triduum fuit cum sponso vel capite Christo.⁵ Nec est illud Apostoli ad Ephes 5°. verum ad verba, quando illud Gen. 2.⁶ dictum⁷ ad sensum litteralem de primo Adam et de uxore⁷ sua, exponit ad sensum misticum de Christo et ecclesia: ‘hoc, inquit textus, nunc os ex ossibus meis et caro de carne mea: propter hoc relinquet⁸ homo patrem et matrem suam et adherebit uxori sue, et erunt duo in carne una.’ ‘Sacramentum,⁸ inquit Apostolus, hoc magnum est: ego **autem** dico in Christo et in ecclesia.’ Illa **autem** exposicio Apostoli est impossibilis, scilicet quod,⁹ sicut ex costa Adam resoluti¹⁰ in soporem¹¹ edificata est sponsa eius secundum membra ossea solida et tenera, sic ex apertura¹² lateris secundi Adami edificata est ecclesia sponsa Christi secundum varietatem¹³ membrorum ecclesie. Nec reliquit¹⁴ Christus Patrem semetipsum¹⁵ exinaniendo¹⁶ et exeundo a¹⁷ Patre, ut dicitur Ioh. 16°.; et ‘matrem synagogam in qua natus’ est Isa. 50;¹⁸ et adherebit uxori sue scilicet ecclesie, ut dicitur Osee 2°. Nec erant duo coniugata in eadem natura communi tam secundum carnem¹⁹ quam [p. 39] spiritum ac¹ affeccione tam sensitiva quam intellectiva concordes; ut exponit Augustinus et alii sequentes Apostolum. **Sed** quelibet illarum particularum est impossibilis de vi vocis cum Christus non passus est mortem in cruce; quia pro² instanti mortis Christus non est Christus, nec fuit illud sacramentum in Christo et ecclesia: cum pro toto triduo non fuit Christus, **sed** sponsa exspectavit usque ad instans resurreccionis sponsalia. ENGLISH: Third, this is confirmed from the testimony of scripture. For in John 19 it is written: "But after they were come to Jesus, when they saw that he was already dead, they did not break his legs. But one of the soldiers with a spear opened his side: and there came out blood and water. And he that saw it," says John, "hath given testimony: and his testimony is true."[^6] I ask, how would they have come to Jesus dead, unless that dead man was Jesus? Or how would the soldier with a spear have opened his side, Jesus' side, if it was not then the side of Jesus? Do we believe that the evangelist, so markedly asserting that he saw these things beyond all the other deeds of Christ, made up a lie? And if it is said that the letter kills, but the spiritual and right sense quickens,[^7] [38] of what sort is such [a sense]? — "When they were come to that dead body, which could not be Jesus, but before, not then, was the body of Jesus,[^8] then one of the soldiers opened, not his side, nor [univocally]{g:univoce} the side of anyone, but a part of one dead body, which was not then Christ or a part of him; and there came out, not the blood of redemption and the water of baptism from the side of Christ, since then it was not Christ." And so the spirit, the water and the blood are not and were not witnesses of the humanity of Christ, since they were not together, as John seems to say, 1 John 4.[^9] Nor is that which the Apostle says in Ephesians 5 true to the words, that "Christ so loved the [church]{g:ecclesia} that he delivered himself up for it: that he might sanctify, cleansing it by the laver of water in the word of life";[^10] since the church through the whole three days was with [her] bridegroom or head, Christ. Nor is that [word] of the Apostle in Ephesians 5 true to the words, when he expounds that [word] of Genesis 2, said in the literal sense of the first Adam and his wife, in the mystical sense of Christ and the church: "This now," says the text, "is bone of my bones, and flesh of my flesh: for this cause shall a man leave his father and mother: and shall cleave to his wife. And they shall be two in one flesh." "This," says the Apostle, "is a great [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}:[^11] but I speak in Christ and in the church." And that exposition of the Apostle is impossible,[^12] namely that, as from the rib of Adam, dissolved in sleep, his bride was built according to the bony members, solid and tender, so from the opening of the side of the second Adam the church, the bride of Christ, was built according to the variety of the members of the church. Nor did Christ leave the Father, [emptying]{g:exinanire} himself and going out from the Father, as is said in John 16; and "his mother the synagogue, in which he was born," Isaiah 50;[^13] and [nor] will he cleave to his wife, namely the church, as is said in Hosea 2. Nor were the two joined in wedlock in the same common [nature]{g:natura}, as to the flesh as well as [39] the spirit, and concordant in affection both sensitive and intellective, as Augustine and others following the Apostle expound.[^14] But each of those clauses is impossible [by the force of the words]{g:de-vi-vocis}, since Christ did not suffer death on the cross; for at the instant of death Christ is not Christ; nor was that sacrament in Christ and the church, since for the whole three days there was no Christ, but the bride waited for the espousals until the instant of the resurrection.[^15] These are [ravings]{g:deliramenta}, and like the words of children.

### 03-c ¶5 · 39.22–40.15  (1 sed/autem)

ENGLISH: Therefore I grant this with these doctors in the first part, since it is of faith; and I grant in the fullest [proper sense of the words]{g:de-virtute-sermonis} that Christ lay in the sepulchre, since that dead body was the person of the Word:[^18] not that every [quantitative part]{g:pars-quantitativa} of that body was generated at the instant of death, so that Christ was then generated by assuming a new nature, but, remaining continuously the same body, before living and afterwards dead, it is subject perpetually to the same [hypostatic]{g:ypostatice} union. For through the whole three days Christ remained man, as to the body as well as as to the soul; and he truly suffered death at the [40] instant of separation, since the dead flesh, which then was Christ, suffered privation of life; and truly from the side of Christ there came out water and blood of Christ, for the duration of its issuing;[^19] for Christ for the whole three days is the same dead body, from which, as its blood and spirits flowed out so that the soul might naturally be separated, the subtler part of the blood was miraculously reserved, with the ichor,[^20] for celebrating the espousals between the bridegroom, living gloriously in the three days after the triumph, and his mystical body, which is the church. For the scriptures must be interpreted of Christ's humanity according to the sense which the holy doctors have diligently searched out in them.[^22]
