### 01-a ¶2 · 1.15–2.22  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 1] Potestas enim est universale instrumentum operandi et communicandi in ista materia. Describitur **autem** potestas ab Aristotele V Metaphysice quod est principium motus vel mutacionis aut in altero aut in quantum alterum. Et [p. 2] sic est communis ad omnem activam potestatem; quo modo videtur scripturam loqui Apocalypsis IX, 3 dicente: Quod data est potestas locustis, sicut habent potestatem scorpiones terre; racione cuius potestatis tenet dominus Ardmacanus II de questionibus Armenorum, capitulo IV, quod potestas est extensior quam nature racionalis potencia. **Sed** quia iuxta Aristotelem (ubi supra) potestas equivocatur ad quatuor, scilicet generaliter ad activam potenciam iam descriptam, que est vis transmutandi aliquid motu proprie dicto vel mutacione quantumlibet extensiva; et sic est vis generandi atque corrumpendi. Nec solum dicitur respectu essencie extra potens **sed** eadem essencia, in quantum activa, dicitur habere potenciam agendi intra se ipsam, in quantum est passiva, vel racione indisposicionis contrarie aut racione terminandi quomodolibet faccionem propriam ut passiva. Sic enim est potestas rectificandi se in virga, potestas frigefaciendi se in aqua, et potestas intelligendi se in anima; et sic de similibus.

ENGLISH: For power is the universal instrument of working and of communicating in this matter. And power
is defined by Aristotle, *Metaphysics* V, as that which "is the principle of motion or change
either in another or in so far as [it is] other".[^3] And [2] so it is common to every active
power; and in this way scripture seems to speak in Apocalypse 9:3, saying "that power was given to
the locusts, as the scorpions of the earth have power";[^4] by reason of which power the lord of
Armagh holds, in book II of the *Questions of the Armenians*, chapter 4, that "power is wider than
the [power]{g:potencia} of rational nature".[^5] But since, according to Aristotle (in the place cited), power
is said equivocally in four ways: namely, generally, of the active potency already defined, which
is the force of transforming something by motion properly so called, or by change however
extended; and so it is the force of generating and corrupting.[^6] Nor is it said only with
respect to an essence outside the one that has the power; but the same essence, in so far as it is
active, is said to have a potency of acting within itself, in so far as it is passive, either by
reason of a contrary [unfitness]{g:indisposicio} or by reason of terminating in any way its own
making, as passive.[^7] For in this way there is a power of straightening itself in a rod, a power
of cooling itself in water, and a power of understanding itself in the soul; and so of like cases.

### 01-a ¶3 · 2.23–3.2  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 2] Quamvis **autem** transmutatum in istis non sit aliud quam ipsum transmutans, tamen vel est alterum, vel ut terminans potenciam est alterius racionis. Et sic intelligendo potenciam patens est eius divisio, cum aliqua sit increata, et aliqua potencia est creata. Increata potencia potest intelligi dupliciter, vel personaliter ut pro patre in divinis, cuius actum terminat Verbum suum, qui non est aliud **sed** alter, et alius quam dicta potencia, quia genitus et causatus; vel essencialiter [p. 3] ut tota trinitas est una potencia intelligendi ac volendi se ipsam.

ENGLISH: And although what is transformed in these cases is not another thing than the transformer itself,
yet either it is other,[^8] or, as terminating the potency, it is of another [aspect]{g:racio}. And
understanding potency in this way, its division is plain, since some is uncreated, and some potency
is created.[^9] Uncreated potency can be understood in two ways: either personally, as [it is taken]
for the Father in divine matters, whose act is terminated by his Word, who is not another thing
but another, and other than the said potency, because begotten and caused; or essentially, [3] as
the whole Trinity is one potency of understanding and of willing itself.[^10]

### 01-a ¶5 · 3.11–3.23  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 3] Potestas **autem** creata vel est corporalis vel spiritualis, et sic subdividendo secundum differencias activorum, in quibus sunt multe differencie, quia spiritualis potestas racionalis creature est perpetua indelebilis **sed** restringibilis in effectu, corporalis **autem** potestas est delebilis et acquiribilis, cum sit qualitas de secunda specie qualitatis non inseparabiliter connaturalis substancie cui inest. Secundo vero modo sumitur potestas in textu Aristotelis, pro passiva potencia creature, secundum quam dicitur recipere congrue activi extrinseci accionem.

ENGLISH: And created power is either corporeal or spiritual, and so [one goes on] subdividing according to
the [differences]{g:differencia} of active things, among which there are many differences; for
the spiritual power of a rational creature is perpetual, indelible, but restrictable in effect,
while corporeal power is delible and acquirable, since it is a quality of the second species of
quality, not inseparably connatural to the substance in which it is.[^12] And in the second way
power [is taken]{g:sumitur-pro} in Aristotle's text for the passive potency of a creature,
according to which it is said to receive fittingly the action of an extrinsic agent.

### 01-a ¶6 · 3.24–5.21  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 3] **Sed** tercio pertinencius accipitur potestas pro principio faciendi aliquid, non quocunque modo, **sed** bene, quod Aristoteles dicit fieri secundum voluntatem. Et ista videtur mihi potencia rationalis; de qua nono Methaphisice 30 et 10. Et illa subdividitur secundum homines et angelos bonos vel malos; et illa potestas dicitur vel intrinsece vel extrinsece, proporcionaliter ut dictum est de potencia divina; unde potencia racionalis inseparabilis [p. 4] a creatura racionali servandi rectitudinem vocatur a venerabili Anshelmo, liberum arbitrium, ut patet in libello suo De Libero Arbitrio. Liberum, inquit, arbitrium est potestas servandi rectitudinem voluntatis propter ipsam rectitudinem; tales **autem** potenciae impediri potuerunt **sed** non extingui. Verumtamen potestas vel potencia sumitur quandoque pro sufficiencia meritorie operandi, vel incitantibus resistendi, et talis per peccatum potest diminui atque tolli. Quarto vero modo accipit Aristoteles potestatem sive potenciam pro habitu accidenter aquisito, secundum quod eius subiectum potens est alterius mutatum, ut ars medicinalis vel alii habitus operandi. Nec distat multum sufficiencia iam dicta ab ista potencia. Secundum hoc itaque inest homini duplex maneries potestatis, scilicet supernaturalis et civilis. Supernaturalis est potestas, quam Deus dat homini ad spiritualiter proficiendum ecclesie, cuiusmodi est potestas sacerdotalis in conficiendo vel ministrando sacramenta aut alia spiritualia bona ecclesie. Potestas **autem** civilis est potestas dominativa ad cohercendum rebelles ecclesie secundum penam sensibilem et dicit libertatem in potentatu et subdicione in extrinseco terminante. Et isto modo videtur Salvator dicere Math. XX, 25: Principes gencium dominantur eorum; et qui maiores sunt potestatem exercent in eos. Et Jeremie XXXIV, 9 refert Propheta quomodo Judei dimiserant servos suos et ancillas, ut essent liberi sue potestatis; quando [p. 5] enim homo habet libertatem sine coactivo preposito ad faciendum cum suis quod foret sibi expedicius, tunc dicitur sub nullius redigi potestate, ut loquitur Apostolus I. Corinth. VI, 12; et sic videtur Apostolum loqui I. Corinth. VII, 4 quando dicit quod coniuges non habent sui corporis potestatem, eo quod oportet eos ex debito servire sibi reciproce in actibus carnalibus, quando esset utilius contemplari. Et sic servitus et obligacio ad carnalem copulam in coniugio sunt status imperfecti quodammodo. Preter istas significaciones sumitur potestas in scriptura nunc pro sexto ordine angelorum, ut Colos. I, 16 et aliquando pro ipso potentatu, ut Rom. XIII, Omnis anima potestatibus sublimioribus subdita sit. Et quandoque sumitur equivoce pro potestate pretensa vel false palliata, ut Luce XXII, 53 dicit Christus suis captoribus: Hec est hora vestra et potestas tenebrarum. Et istas equivocaciones potestatum oportet diligenter notare secundum pertinencia materie ventilate.

ENGLISH: But in the third way, and more pertinently, power is taken for the principle of doing something,
not in any manner whatever, but well, which Aristotle says "comes about according to
will".[^13] And this seems to me to be rational potency, of which [he treats in] *Metaphysics* IX,
3 and 1.[^14] And it is subdivided according to men and angels, good or evil; and that power is
spoken of either intrinsically or extrinsically, proportionally as was said of the divine potency;
hence the rational potency of keeping rectitude, inseparable [4] from a rational creature, is
called by the venerable Anselm [free choice]{g:liberum-arbitrium}, as is clear in his little book
*On Free Choice*. "Free choice," he says, "is the power of keeping rectitude of will for the sake
of rectitude itself";[^15] and such powers could be hindered but not extinguished.
Nevertheless, power or potency[^16] is sometimes taken for the sufficiency to work meritoriously, or to
resist those who incite [to sin], and such [power] can be diminished and taken away through sin.
And in the fourth way Aristotle takes power or potency for a habit accidentally acquired, according
to which its subject, [itself] changed, is capable of [changing] another, as the art of medicine or
other habits of working.[^17] And the sufficiency just mentioned differs little from this potency.
Accordingly, then, there is in man a twofold kind of power, namely supernatural and civil.
Supernatural power is the power which God gives a man for profiting the church spiritually, of
which kind is priestly power in confecting or ministering the sacraments or other spiritual goods
of the church. And civil power is lordly power for [coercing]{g:cohercere} rebels against the
church by [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} penalty, and it implies freedom in the one who holds
sovereignty and subjection in the extrinsic party in which it terminates.[^18] And in this way the Saviour seems to
speak in Matthew 20:25: "The princes of the Gentiles [lord it over]{g:dominari} them; and they that
are the greater, exercise power upon them." And in Jeremiah 34:9 the prophet relates how the Jews
had let their menservants and maidservants go, "that they might be free, in their own power"; for
[5] when a man has freedom, without a [coactive]{g:coactivus} superior, to do with his own what
would be more expedient to him, then he is said to be "brought under the power of none", as the
Apostle speaks in 1 Corinthians 6:12; and so the Apostle seems to speak in 1 Corinthians 7:4 when
he says that "spouses have not power of their own body", because they must, out of the
[marriage debt]{g:debitum}, serve one another in carnal acts when it would be more profitable to contemplate. And so servitude
and obligation to carnal union in marriage are in a certain way imperfect states.[^19] Besides
these significations, power is taken in scripture at one time for the sixth order of angels, as in
Colossians 1:16, and at another for sovereignty itself, as in Romans 13[:1], "Let every soul be
subject to higher powers."[^20] And sometimes it is taken [equivocally]{g:equivoce} for a power
pretended or falsely [cloaked]{g:palliare}, as in Luke 22:53 Christ says to his captors: "This is
your hour and the power of darkness." And these equivocations of powers must be carefully noted,
according to what pertains to the matter under discussion.

### 01-b ¶1 · 5.22–8.2  (7 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 5] Quamvis **autem** nesciam fundare efficaciter ex scriptura, quod aliqua sit potestas nisi racionalis potencia, tamen quia videtur Aristotelem et doctores nostros loqui extensius, et nomen est vox sensitiva ad placitum, satis possunt admitti omnes significaciones termini supradicte, nam beatus Johannes intelligit in primo libro prophetico homines bestialiter conversantes. In cuius signum Apocal. IX describit bestias descripcionibus, quales non possunt corporaliter competere animali. Ut patet de locustis, quas dicit habere caudas similes scorpionum, et aculei erant in caudis earum. [p. 6] Et sic intelligitur de potestate equorum, qui post tubacionem sexti angeli exierunt, et conformiter intelligitur capitulo XIII, 4 de potestate, quam draco dedit bestie. Unde propter defectum auctoris non audeo dicere quod Deus habet potestatem producendi quicquam ad intra **sed** solum ad extra, licet habeat potenciam ad omnia producibilia sui intrinseca producendum, nec personam aut actum in Deo sue dicimus subici potestati. Omnes tamen actus nostri liberi arbitrii, sicut opera extrinseca per hos facta, dicuntur nostre subici potestati, ut patet I. Retractacionum XXXII, III De libero arbitrio et in De spiritu et anima XXVI. Unde non sequitur: si unum convertibilium non sit in potestate nostra, tunc nec reliquum vel e contra; antecedens non est in potestate nostra, igitur nec consequens vel e contra. Et sic argumentando affirmative ex opposito: in potestate, inquit Augustinus, nostra non est, nisi quod nostram sequitur voluntatem. Sequitur enim: Deus predestinavit me igitur habeo virtutem finalis perseverancie et e contra. Sequitur eciam: placet mihi de hoc quod digero alimentum, igitur sic digero; antecedens est in potestate mea et consequens non, quia ex II. Ethicorum propter illa que non sunt in potestate nostra non sumus laudandi moraliter nec culpandi. Supposito igitur, quod fiat restriccio potestatis a racionalem potenciam liberam accidentalem homini, patet quod tales potestates habent subdivisionem [p. 7] multiplicem et terminos magistrales, ut alia est potestas ordinis, alia regiminis vel iurisdiccionis, alia potestas autentica, et alia vicaria vel commissa. Et breviter secundum distinccionem operum est distinccio potestatum. Famosior **autem** distinccio potestatis pertinens huic proposito est illa quam tangit Hugo II De Sacramentis, parte II capitulo IV, dicens quod sicut duplex est vita hominis; vita terrena corporalis vel naturalis, alia celestis spiritualis vel supernaturalis, sic duplex est potestas. Prima vita corpus vivit ex anima, secunda vero vita anima vivit ex Deo illapso per graciam, et utraque vita suum bonum habet quo vegetatur et nutritur, ut possit subsistere; ut **autem** in utraque vita iusticia servetur, est dare secundum duos populos, scilicet clerum et laicos, duplicem potestatem, scilicet spiritualem et secularem; et in utraque diversi sunt gradus et ordines potestatum, sub uno tamen capite ecclesie utrimque distributi, et quasi ab uno principio deducti et ad unum relati. Terrena potestas caput habet regem in terris, sicut spiritualis potestas summum pontificem, **Sed** difficultas scolastica est quomodo iste potestates due describi debeant; et videtur Hugonem innuere quod spiritualis potestas sit potestas clerici ad dirigendum ecclesiam, ut bene vivat vita spirituali. Potestas **autem** secularis vel terrena sit potestas laici ad dirigendum ecclesiam, ut bene vivat vita naturali. Et habent iste potestates suas leges ac bona sua, de quibus principaliter intromittunt, ut laicus [p. 8] bona nature et fortune, **sed** clericus bona gracie et virtutis.

ENGLISH: Although, however, I cannot ground effectively from scripture that there is any [power]{g:potestas} other than a rational [potency]{g:potencia},[^1] yet because Aristotle and our doctors seem to speak more broadly, and a name is a perceptible sound by convention,[^2] all the significations of the term set out above can well be admitted; for blessed John, in the first prophetic book, understands men who live like beasts.[^3] In sign of this, Apocalypse 9 describes beasts in terms such as cannot bodily fit an animal, as is clear of the locusts, which he says have "tails like to scorpions: and there were stings in their tails". [6] And so it is understood of the power of the horses which went out after the sixth angel sounded his trumpet, and in like manner it is understood, in chapter 13:4, of the "power which the dragon gave to the beast".[^4] Hence, for want of an authority, I do not dare to say that God has power of producing anything within [himself], but only outside; although he has power to produce all the producibles intrinsic to himself, nor do we say that a person or an act in God is subject to his power.[^5] Yet all the acts of our [free choice]{g:liberum-arbitrium}, like the extrinsic works done through them, are said to be subject to our power, as is clear in *Retractations* I.32, *On Free Choice* III, and *On the Spirit and the Soul* 26.[^6] Hence it does not follow: if one of two convertibles is not in our power, then neither is the other, or conversely; the [antecedent]{g:antecedens} is not in our power, therefore neither is the [consequent]{g:consequens}, or conversely. And so, arguing affirmatively from the opposite: "there is nothing in our power", says Augustine, "but what follows our will". For [it follows]{g:sequitur}: God has predestined me, therefore I have the [virtue]{g:virtus} of final perseverance, and conversely. It also follows: it pleases me that I digest food, therefore I so digest; the antecedent is in my power and the consequent not, because, from *Ethics* II, "on account of those things which are not in our power we are not to be praised morally nor blamed".[^7] Supposing therefore that the restriction of power be made to a free rational potency accidental to man,[^8] it is clear that such powers have a manifold subdivision [7] and [masters' terms]{g:termini-magistrales}, as one is the [power of order]{g:potestas-ordinis}, another of rule or [jurisdiction]{g:potestas-iurisdiccionis}, one [authentic]{g:autenticus} power and another vicarious or committed. And, briefly, according to the distinction of works is the distinction of powers.[^9] And the [best-known]{g:famosior} distinction of power pertaining to this purpose is the one Hugh touches on, *On the Sacraments* II, part 2, chapter 4, saying that "as the life of man is twofold, the earthly, bodily or natural life, the other heavenly, spiritual or supernatural, so power is twofold. By the first life the body lives from the soul, but by the second life the soul lives from God, who flows into it by grace; and each life has its own good by which it is quickened and nourished, so that it may subsist; and so that justice may be kept in each life, there is, according to the two peoples, namely [the clergy]{g:clerus} and the laity, a twofold power, namely spiritual and secular; and in each there are different grades and orders of powers, yet distributed on both sides under one [head]{g:caput} of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, and as it were drawn from one principle and referred to one. The earthly power has as its head the king on earth, as the spiritual power has the highest pontiff."[^10] But the scholastic difficulty is how these two powers ought to be defined; and Hugh seems to hint that spiritual power is the power of a cleric to direct the church so that it lives well by the spiritual life, and secular or earthly power the power of a layman to direct the church so that it lives well by the natural life. And these powers have their own laws and their own goods, with which they chiefly concern themselves, as the layman [8] with the goods of nature and fortune, but the cleric with the goods of grace and virtue.

### 01-b ¶2 · 8.3–8.17  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 8] Iste **autem** descripciones videntur perplexe, cum clerici nostri videntur habere potestatem secularem concessam eis a regibus. Sicut ad reges pertinet subditos suos in virtute instruere, specialiter cum per se finis vite naturalis sit ut ornetur virtutibus, nec aliter est regnum vel populus, nec aliter vivit legaliter. Cum igitur quicquid potestas potest inferior, potest superior, non e contra, potestas **autem** spiritualis excedit aliam ut anima corpus, patet secundum doctorem quod potestas spiritualis habet aliam instruere, et quando deviat iudicare; spiritualis, inquit, potestas a solo Deo debet iudicari iuxta illud I. Corinth. II, 15: Spiritualis iudicat omnia, et ipse a nemine iudicatur.

ENGLISH: But these [definitions]{g:descripcio} seem perplexed, since our clerics seem to have secular power granted to them by kings. So too it belongs to kings to instruct their subjects in virtue,[^11] especially since the end of natural life, of itself, is that it be adorned with virtues, and not otherwise is there a [kingdom]{g:regnum} or a people, nor does it otherwise live lawfully. Since, therefore, whatever a lower power can do a higher can, and not conversely, and since now "the spiritual power exceeds the other as the soul the body", it is clear according to the doctor that the spiritual power is to instruct the other, and to judge it when it goes astray; "the spiritual power", he says, "ought to be judged by God alone, according to that of 1 Corinthians 2:15: 'the spiritual man judgeth all things: and he himself is judged of no man.'"[^12]

### 01-b ¶4 · 8.29–9.5  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 8] Utilitas maioritatis eius ex hoc evidet, quod Potestas spiritualis per se sufficit ad regimen populi, ut patet de Israhele usque ad tempora Saul sine potestate regia salubriter regulato. [p. 9] Potestas igitur spiritualis cum sit de bonis optimis et per se sufficientibus, excedit potestatem terrenam, cum illa nichil valet sine potestate spirituali principaliter regulante. **Sed** e contra ista per se sufficit sine illa.

ENGLISH: The usefulness of its [greatness]{g:majoritas} is evident from this[^15], that spiritual power by itself suffices for the rule of the people, as is clear of Israel, governed to its health without royal power until the times of Saul.[^16] [9] Spiritual power, therefore, since it concerns the best goods and those sufficient of themselves, exceeds earthly power, since that is worth nothing without spiritual power principally governing it. But, conversely, this suffices by itself without that.

### 01-c ¶1 · 9.11–10.24  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 9] Quantum ad antiquitatem, patet quod sacerdocium gentile vel naturale precessit sacerdocium leviticum. Et correspondenter de potestate dicitur, ut tangitur XXI capitulo V libri. Unde illo modo creditur Adam et Cayn eius progenitum ante peccatum fuisse quodammodo naturaliter sacerdotes. et sic potestas spiritualis benedicendi et consecrandi, videtur dignitate, tempore et utilitate precedere potestatem civiliter coactivam, ymmo potestas sacerdotalis levitica videtur potestatem regiam Israheliticam antiquitate precedere; non **autem** potestatem regiam gentilem, cum tempore Abrahe legitur novem reges fuisse in valle congregatos. Dignitate **autem** et utilitate videtur sacerdotalis potestas levitica esse prior. In Deo **autem** videtur racio potestatis regie increate precedere potestatem sacerdotalem; sicut secunda persona origine precedit terciam. et opus sustentacionis; opus confirmacionis. que correspondent istis officiis, et per consequens sacerdos eternus primos parentes matrimonio copulavit, licet spirituale matrimonium Christi et ecclesie ipsum precesserit. [p. 10] Redeundo igitur ad descripciones potestatum predictas, videtur quod capiunt multas calumpnias; primo quia eidem persone insunt ambe potestates simul; que persona fuit layca in veteri testamento, ut patet de rege Salem Melchisedech Genes. XIV, de Moyse et aliis iudicibus qui non erant sacerdotes levitici, in tantum quod videtur multis ipsos fuisse populares vel laicos. Secundo quia in novo testamento videtur multis quod clerici habeant simul utramque potestatem, cum habent secularem dominacionem; ut fingitur. Et tercio quia reges temporales habent spiritualem ad destruendum vicia et plantandum ac augendum virtutes in se et in aliis, quotquot **autem** receperunt Christum per fidem, dedit eis potestatem racione filiacionis spiritualis, ad impendendum opera spiritualia misericordie que indubie est potestas spiritualis; quia potestas data divinitus immediate ad vitam spiritualem gignendam et alendam. Nec potest christianus sine hac potestate sanctificare sabbatum; quam potestatem auferre a quolibet Christiano foret contradicere legi Dei; ut patet Exod. XX. Omnis enim potestas qua homo sanctificiat se vel tempus est potestas spiritualis.

ENGLISH: As to antiquity, it is clear that the gentile or natural priesthood preceded the Levitical priesthood. And correspondingly it is said of [power]{g:potestas}, as is touched on in chapter 21 of the fifth book.[^1] Hence in that way Adam and Cain his offspring are believed to have been in some way naturally priests before sin.[^2] And so the spiritual power of blessing and consecrating seems to precede in dignity, time and usefulness the civilly [coactive]{g:coactivus} power; indeed the Levitical priestly power seems to precede the Israelite royal power in antiquity; but not the gentile royal power, since in the time of Abraham nine kings are read to have been gathered in the valley (Genesis 14). But in dignity and usefulness the Levitical priestly power seems to be prior. But in God the notion of the uncreated royal power seems to precede the priestly power, as the second person precedes the third in origin, and the work of sustaining the work of confirming, which correspond to these offices;[^3] and consequently the eternal priest joined the first parents in matrimony, although the spiritual matrimony of Christ and the [church]{g:ecclesia} preceded it. [10] Returning, therefore, to the [definitions]{g:descripcio} of the powers given above, they seem to [be open to many challenges]{g:calumpnia}: first, because both powers are in the same person at once, a person who was lay in the old testament, as is clear of the king of Salem, Melchizedek, Genesis 14, and of Moses and the other judges, who were not Levitical priests, so much so that it seems to many that they were of the people, or lay. Secondly, because in the new testament it seems to many that clerics have both powers at once, since they have secular [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio}, as is feigned. And thirdly, because temporal kings have spiritual [power] to destroy vices and to plant and increase virtues in themselves and in others; and "as many as received" Christ by faith, "he gave them power",[^4] by reason of spiritual sonship, to perform the spiritual works of mercy, which is undoubtedly spiritual power, since it is a power given by God immediately for begetting and nourishing the spiritual life. Nor can a Christian sanctify the sabbath without this power; to take this power away from any Christian would be to contradict [God's law]{g:lex-dei}, as is clear in Exodus 20. For every power by which a man sanctifies[^5] himself or a time is spiritual power.

### 01-c ¶2 · 10.25–11.16  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 10] Et patet impugnacio descripcionis supradicte. Ideo ut descripcio dictarum potestatum sit nocior et ecclesie consentaneor, potest dici quod potestas spiritualis est potestas spiritus per se immediate ordinata, ut creatura racionalis secundum bona gracie dirigatur, et sic individuatur tam a subiecto quam obiecto. Est **autem** omnis homo spiritus, cum sit duarum naturarum utraque, ut loquitur scriptura Luce IX; 55: Nescitis cuius [p. 11] spiritus estis, et I. Joh. IV, 3: Omnis spiritus, qui solvit Jhesum, ex Deo nou est, spiritus ibi est subtilis hereticus. Et sic sive analogizetur potestas ad Deum et creaturas racionales, sive restringatur ad potestates hominum et angelorum verum est, quod omnis potestas spiritualis est potestas spiritus. Et quamvis homo non det graciam, ministrat tamen sacramenta, ut subditus secundum bona gracie dirigatur. Potestas **autem** corporalis, quamvis sit gracia bonorum gracie; tamen immediate est; ut creatura Dei ordinetur secundum bona naturalia vel fortune. Et ita videtur omnis homo habere duplicem potestatem, cum omnis homo debet habere potestatem super actus membrorum, et preterea habet potestatem viandi in gracia.

ENGLISH: And the impugning of the definition given above is clear. Therefore, so that the definition of the said powers may be better known and more consonant[^6] with the church, it can be said that spiritual power is a power of a spirit, ordered of itself immediately to this, that a rational creature be directed according to the goods of grace; and so it is individuated both by its subject and by its object.[^7] Now every man is a spirit, since he is of two natures, both of them,[^8] as scripture speaks in Luke 9:55: "You know not of what [11] spirit you are"; and 1 John 4:3: "Every spirit that dissolveth Jesus is not of God": spirit there is a subtle heretic.[^9] And so, whether power is analogized to God and to rational creatures, or restricted to the powers of men and angels, it is true that every spiritual power is a power of a spirit. And although man does not give grace, yet he ministers the sacraments, so that the subject may be directed according to the goods of grace. But bodily power, although it exists for the sake of the goods of grace, yet is immediately [ordered] so that God's creature may be ordered according to natural goods or those of fortune.[^10] And so every man seems to have a twofold power, since every man must have power over the acts of his members, and moreover he has the power of making his way in grace.[^11]

### 01-d ¶1 · 12.1–14.10  (10 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 12] Doc., consul, castiga, solare, remitte, fer. ora, quia (ut dictum est) quotquot receperunt Christum fide formata, dedit eis potestatem sic filios Dei fieri, ut liceat eis dirigere se et fratres suos in via patris sui Christi, eciam corripiendo, sicut patet Matth. XVIII. Unde correspondenter ad potestatem spiritualem potestas secularis est duplex, scilicet civilis et communis. Civilis autoritativa solum domino civili competit, **sed** civilis vicaria convenit ballivis vel aliis gerentibus vicem secularium dominorum. Potestas **autem** communis secularis est qua quilibet potest secundum bona nature et fortune dirigere se et suos. Et ita sicut homo non potest esse sine anima et corpore, nec filius Dei adoptivus sine bonis nature et gracie, sic non stat viatorem esse nisi habeat tam secularem quam spiritualem potestatem communem, quamvis in infantibus et morionibus sit ligata. Spiritualis **autem** potestas est utrobique perfeccior, **sed** et sacerdotalis potestas spiritualis superaddit spiritualem communem. Quod **autem** limitatur potestas clericis super bonis suis temporalibus, seclusa potestate secularium, videtur esse presumpcio et avaricia sacerdotum. Videtur enim quod Deus non potest [p. 13] ordinare medium multiplicius pro fine qui eque perfecte fieret per medium brevius, cum omnis talis ordinacio foret frustra; **sed** omnis finis pro quo ordinaretur potestas tam multiplex eque perfecte si non perfeccius fieret per unicam simplicem potestatem, que est liberum arbitrium, sicud per quotlibet potestates superadditas, igitur superfluit ponere tam multas. Minor probatur ex hoc, quod omnis potestas creata est propter opus perfeccionis universi secundum ecclesiam vel aliam eius partem, **sed** in omne tale opus sufficit liberum arbitrium sine potestate accidentali, nam in statu innocencie foret solum talis potestas, sicut post statum innocencie illi qui vixerunt religiosius caruerunt huiusmodi potestate, quid (queso) sufficit homo ordinare vel facere, quod non sufficit ex puro libero arbitrio? Videtur enim quod palliata potestas superaddita pocius perturbat ecclesiam. Item, eadem potestas in numero potest nunc laxari et nun restringi, ut patet de potestate ordinis pari in quolibet sacerdote, licet in prelatis suscipiat in laxacione et restriccione gradus multiplices; igitur per idem naturalis potestas potest nunc laxari et nunc restringi quantum oportet pro regimine ecclesie, igitur cum in tali laxacione non acquiritur nova potestas, nec in restriccione deperditur, sequitur quod non oportet superaddere novam huiusmodi potestatem. Minor patet, tum quia potestas non componitur ex suis partibus intensivis, tum eciam quia aliter non foret eadem potestas restricta vel laxata **sed** omnino vel antiqua perdita vel nova genita. Et illud evidencius movet quod non oportet in sacerdote ponere distinccionem specificam inter [p. 14] potestatem ordinis et iurisdiccionis, **sed** dans potestatem ordinis dat ut sic potestatem iurisdiccionis, quia aliter foret nuda potestas ordinis nimis superflua, quia insufficiens ut sit in materiam quantumcunque exercita. Christus **autem** quando dedit discipulis potestatem predicandi dixit Math. ultimo generaliter: Euntes igitur docete omnes gentes, baptizantes eos; quando igitur conceditur principale, conceditur accessorium sine quo ipsum non posset proficere.

ENGLISH: "Teach, counsel, chastise, comfort, forgive, bear, pray";[^1] for (as has been said) "as many as received" Christ by formed faith, "he gave them power" so "to be made the sons of God" that it is lawful for them to direct themselves and their brothers in the way of their father Christ, even by correcting them, as is clear in Matthew 18.[^2] Hence, corresponding to spiritual [power]{g:potestas}, secular power is twofold, namely civil and common. Civil authoritative [power] belongs only to the civil [lord]{g:dominus}, but civil vicarious [power] befits bailiffs or others acting in place of the secular lords. And common secular power is that by which anyone can direct himself and his own according to the goods of nature and fortune. And so, just as a man cannot be without soul and body, nor an adopted son of God without the goods of nature and of grace, so it does not hold that one is a [wayfarer]{g:viator} unless he has both secular and spiritual common power, although in infants and fools it is bound. And spiritual power is the more perfect in both cases, and priestly spiritual power indeed adds over and above the common spiritual [power]. But that power over their temporal goods is [assigned]{g:limitare} to clerics, the power of seculars being shut out, seems to be the presumption and avarice of priests.[^3] And so there are any number of masters' divisions of powers, which the curiosity of the church has devised, as an archdeacon and a bishop-elect are said to have [power of jurisdiction]{g:potestas-iurisdiccionis}, even for absolving and excommunicating, although they do not have the [power of order]{g:potestas-ordinis} for these; and laymen are said to have such power in the contentious court by commissions of prelates. Against what has been said of the multiplicity of human power it is argued.[^4] For it seems that God cannot [13] ordain a more manifold means for an end that would be done equally perfectly by a shorter means, since every such ordinance would be in vain; now every end for which so manifold a power would be ordained would be done equally perfectly, if not more perfectly, by a single simple power, which is [free choice]{g:liberum-arbitrium}, as by any number of superadded powers; therefore it is superfluous to posit so many. The minor is proved from this, that every created power is for the work of the perfection of the universe according to the church or another part of it; now for every such work free choice suffices without accidental power, for in the state of innocence there would be only such power, just as after the state of innocence those who lived more religiously lacked power of this kind. What (I ask) is man sufficient to ordain or do that he is not sufficient for out of pure free choice? For it seems that a [cloaked]{g:palliare} superadded power rather disturbs the church. Again, the same power in number can at one time be [loosed]{g:laxare} and at another restricted[^5], as is clear of the power of order, equal in every priest, although in prelates it takes on manifold degrees in loosing and restriction; therefore, by the same token, natural power can at one time be loosed and at another restricted as much as is needed for the rule of the church; therefore, since in such loosing no new power is acquired, nor is one lost in restriction, [it follows]{g:sequitur} that it is not necessary to superadd a new power of this kind. The minor is clear, both because power is not composed of its intensive parts, and also because otherwise it would not be the same power restricted or loosed, but either the old one wholly lost or a new one begotten. And this moves more evidently [to the view] that it is not necessary to posit in a priest a specific distinction between [14] power of order and of jurisdiction, but he who gives the power of order gives as such the power of jurisdiction, since otherwise the bare power of order would be too superfluous, being insufficient to pass into its matter however much it were exercised.[^6] Now Christ, when he gave the disciples the power of preaching, said generally, Matthew, the last chapter: "Going therefore, teach ye all nations: baptizing them";[^7] when, therefore, the principal is granted, the accessory is granted, without which it could not profit.

### 01-d ¶4 · 14.30–16.21  (2 sed/autem)

Et conceditur quod potentatus male accipiunt occasionem ad perturbandum ecclesiam ex ficticia potestatis; oportet tamen Deum dare potestates spirituales ad ministrandum sacramenta, ad cohercendum seculariter rebelles ecclesie, preter liberum arbitrium, ut patet ex scriptura multiplici.

ENGLISH: Likewise, supposing that the lord [pope]{g:papa} commits power by bulls into England to Peter the archbishop, to absolve Paul, a criminal, in [15] cases reserved to the curia, and I suppose that the lord pope dies before the bulls arrive in England,[^11] then the lord pope neither at the instant of his purpose to confer such power on Peter suddenly imprints such power in Peter, nor at any later [instant] that can be designated. And the same judgment [holds] of any creature that would give power to another; therefore there is no such power except a feigned one. For the [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius-christi} cannot at will imprint a quality in the soul at so great a distance, since then it would be superfluous to [authorize]{g:autorizare} such power by bulls, and continuously afterwards the pope's action would be irrelevant, as it would be, he being dead, at the instant the bulls became known. And by the same token it follows that it is not subject to his power to revoke power given by God, nor to loose power previously bound, just as it is not subject to his power that, whenever he claims to give such power, God concurs, principally providing the power. And otherwise it is certain that such power is not given by man; as, therefore, power is not given to the [unqualified]{g:inhabilis} or the unwilling, so neither does a potentate give power to another if he claims to; for otherwise men could try out a [trade]{g:commercium} in powers among themselves, and the general volition by which every pope wills that all the [qualified]{g:habilis} priests of Christ, and only such, have power would cause power far otherwise than the church reckons. In this matter it seems to me that power at one time means absolutely a [potency]{g:potencia} for ruling, and at another means, [aggregatively]{g:aggregative}, such a potency together with [authentic]{g:autenticus} notification or promulgation;[^12] and since [16] the senses are equally known, it is clear that it is not repugnant that there is no power except from God, and yet that something other than God gives power, that is, authentic promulgation in the face of the church that a created person has such power from God; such an aggregate is indeed given in part by man, but only if God principally authorizes it, as is clear below. To the first, the [inference]{g:consequencia} is granted with the assumption, but the minor is denied, since after the fall it is necessary to have power promulgated by man according to servile fear for [coercing]{g:cohercere} rebels, as it was not needed in the state of innocence nor in the rule of the piously obedient, since such a law is set because of transgressors, which the humble Christian will not fear.[^13] And it is granted that potentates wrongly take occasion to disturb the church from the [fiction]{g:ficticia} of power; yet God must give spiritual powers for ministering the sacraments, for coercing the rebels of the church in secular fashion, besides free choice, as is clear from manifold scripture.

### 01-e ¶3 · 18.10–18.29  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 18] Unde inter omnes locuciones blasfemas quibus christianismus illuditur, maximus cathaclismus mendacii est de collacione. subtraccione vel restriccione huiusmodi potestatum. ENGLISH: Hence, among all the blasphemous ways of speaking by which Christianity is mocked, the greatest cataclysm of lying is about the [conferral]{g:collacio}, withdrawal or restriction of such powers.[^7] For when God and reason compel, for the usefulness of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, that it be so done by a man, then and not otherwise God gives such power. Therefore in the primitive church the apostles did not use such civil speech, because then power would be simoniacal, saleable and purchasable like an office; therefore, because of papal bulls, power is not attributed to a subject more or less, since God gives power to the [qualified]{g:habilis} for the edification of the church independently of such a perceptible sacrament as has been introduced, and rightness of work is a more evident sign of such power than the letter or the sacraments of a [prelate]{g:prelatus}; nor is it inferred from this that it would be lawful to assume power to oneself in civil fashion to work in such a way, since no one would assume power to himself, but would receive it from another when God necessitates.

Discamus ergo quatuor veritates; quas pastor in responsione sua docuerit, primo docet dicendo, quod non haberet potestatem nisi datum esset sibi desuper, quod per Apostolum suum docuit; quod non est potestas nisi a Deo.

ENGLISH: Hence Simon Magus did not presume to assume apostolic power to himself, because he would then fear for himself that the effectual efficacy of the work would not follow;[^8] [19] therefore he wished to buy the power with money, as is clear in Acts 8. But our [superiors]{g:prepositi}, quieted in the rind of signs, seeking only honour and the gain of the world, are simoniacs more hateful to God;[^9] and so it is granted, as is touched on in the third confirmation, that too many sophistries fly about in this matter, since all are such that cannot be grounded in scripture, since it contains every single truth.[^10] And the Apostle seems to ground the secular power of ruling civilly in Romans 13:1, when he calls the [temporal lords]{g:temporales-domini} powers; and he says that they "bear not the sword in vain", that is, civilly [coactive]{g:coactivus} power, saying that here it "would be to resist God" and them as to the execution of such power, since (as he says) "there is no power but from God".[^11] This must be understood altogether [to mean] that God alone gives such power, or that it is not given by a rational creature unless God has given it first, so that this sign *nisi* is taken for *si non*, in scripture's manner of speaking.[^12] And Christ spoke this sense in John 19:10–11, where it is read that, Christ being silent, Pilate said: "Knowest thou not that I have power to crucify thee, and I have power to release thee?" To whom Jesus: "Thou shouldst not have any power against me, unless it were given thee from above." On which Augustine, Homily 116, notes first how the Lord often kept silent and often answered.[^13] He was silent before Pilate, before the priests and Herod, for three [reasons]: first, that the scripture of Isaiah 53:7 might be fulfilled: "like a lamb without voice before his shearer, so openeth he not his mouth";[^14] secondly, [20] that his gentleness might be shown, recited in Matthew 12:19: "neither shall any man hear his voice in the streets"; and thirdly, to teach how verbal contention is often useless, and therefore, when it is expedient, one must keep silence. Therefore, to [dispose of]{g:tollere} [objections]{g:instancia} from negatives of this kind, Augustine says that in general he did "not" keep dumb "as one guilty or deceitful, but as a lamb, that is, simple and innocent, he opened not his mouth". For he never spoke excusing himself of his crime, just as he never kept silent for his guilt; and so on both sides both his silence and his answer are to be noted for the hidden mystery: "where", he says, "he did not answer, as a sheep he kept silence, and where he answered, as a shepherd he taught. Let us learn, then", four truths which the shepherd taught in his answer. First he teaches, by saying "that he would not have power unless it were given him from above", what he taught through his Apostle: that "there is no power but from God".[^15]

### 01-e ¶6 · 20.25–20.27  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 20] Et patet tercio, quomodo vanum est excusare Pilatum a crimine **sed** magis est vanum excusare maioritatem peccati in sacerdote.

ENGLISH: And it is clear, thirdly, how vain it is to excuse Pilate from the crime, but it is more vain to excuse the [greatness]{g:majoritas} of the sin in the priest.[^17]

### 01-e ¶7 · 20.28–21.13  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 20] Quarto vero innuitur quomodo quis est sub alterius potestate; et quod potentatus superior non est eo ipso culpandus quo inferior excedit [p. 21] limites potestatis talis: Talem, inquit, Deus Pilato dederat potestatem, ut esset eciam sub cesaris potestate. Unde quidam intelligunt Christum non solum intelligere dacionem potestatis Pilati desuper non solum a Deo **sed** cesare, quia cesarem plus timuit ut autorem potestatis sue quam occidere regem gentis aliene. **Sed** timor ille fuit servilis, cum postposita regula potestatis divine, non timuit occidere innocentem; excusacionem **autem** non habuit, cum excessit potestatem quam habebat a cesare, qui potuit pretendere se dare potestatem adversum Dominum, **sed** non Deus.

ENGLISH: And fourthly it is intimated how one is under another's power, and that a superior potentate is not to be blamed by the very fact that an inferior exceeds [21] the [limits]{g:limites} of such power: "Such power", he says, "God had given Pilate, that he should be also under Caesar's power." Hence some understand Christ to mean not only the giving of Pilate's power from above not only by God but [by] Caesar,[^18] because he feared Caesar more, as the author of his power, than [he feared] to kill the king of a foreign nation. But that fear was servile, since, the rule of divine power being set aside, he did not fear to kill an innocent man; and he had no excuse, since he exceeded the power which he had from Caesar, who could claim to give power against the Lord, but God could not.[^19]

### 01-e ¶8 · 21.14–21.31  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 21] Et videtur mihi quinto quod magister docet misterium metaphysice per hoc, quod dicit nisi datum esset tibi desuper, non solum quod cesar non dat nisi Deus prius dederit, verum eciam quod potestas non est quid absolutum, quod posset per se existere, **sed** potestas dicitur dari ab autore, quando datum est a Deo creature, ut ministro Dei, quod sic aut sic sufficiat facere ad populum in Dei nomine regulandum, et credere quod hoc sit res que posset per se esse foret nimis magna stulticia; et sic per hoc participium neutrum datum convenienter exprimitur res verbi exprimenda per complexum, ut concessum est creature, quod legittime possit in nomine Domini regulare, et illa licencia est potestas, quam Deus nunc dat immediate per se, et nunc per sacramentale ministerium ad notificandum populo Dei licenciam.

ENGLISH: And it seems to me, fifthly, that the master teaches a mystery of [metaphysics]{g:metaphisica}[^20] by saying "unless it were given thee from above": not only that Caesar does not give unless God has given first, but also that power is not something absolute that could exist of itself; rather, power is said to be given by its author when it is given by God to a creature, as to a minister of God, that it be sufficient to do this or that for ruling the people in God's name; and to believe that this is a thing that could be of itself would be too great a folly. And so by this neuter participle *datum*, "given", there is fittingly expressed the thing of the verb, which is to be expressed by a complex: namely that it has been granted to the creature that it may lawfully rule in the name of the Lord; and that licence is power, which God gives sometimes immediately by himself, and sometimes through sacramental ministry, to notify the licence to the people of God.[^21]

### 02-a ¶1 · 22.2–22.7  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 22] **Sed** adhuc videtur, cum omnis potestas sit a Deo, quod nemo habet potestatem ad faciendum aliquid, nisi quod Deus intendit ut faciat. Ex quo videtur quod sacerdotes ociantes excusantur, cum non habent potestatem ad faciendam aliquid quod omittunt.

ENGLISH: But it still seems, since all [power]{g:potestas} is from God, that no one has power to do anything except what God intends that he do. From this it seems that idle [priests]{g:sacerdos} are excused, since they do not have power to do anything that they omit.[^1]

### 02-a ¶4 · 22.17–22.21  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 22] Item; recipiens talem potestatem a Deo est ut sic obligacior, **sed** oportet Deum cognoscere excessum obligacionis, et per consequens, quid adequate potentatus debet facere ex illa potestate, et sic Deus intendit ut illud faciat.

ENGLISH: Again: one who receives such power from God is, as such, the more bound; now God must know the excess of the obligation, and consequently what the potentate ought adequately to do from that power; and so God intends that he do it.[^4]

### 02-a ¶5 · 22.22–23.3  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 22] **Sed** notandum est, quod sicut aliud est Deum velle quod Petrus sit salvus et aliud Deum velle quod ipse est salvus, sic aliud est Deum intendere, quod Petrus faciat hoc et aliud Deum intendere quod ipse facit hoc; quia eo ipso quo Deus obligat Petrum sub obtentu premii vel incursu pene quod Petrus faciat hoc; Deus intendit quod faciat hoc; et sic utrobique solum intendit et vult veritatem, cum veritas est quod [p. 23] Petrus debet facere hoc; licet non sit verum, quod hoc facit, et per consequens non intentum vel volitum a Deo.

ENGLISH: But it must be noted that, just as it is one thing for God to will that Peter be saved and another for God to will that he is saved, so it is one thing for God to intend that Peter do this and another for God to intend that he does this; for by the very fact that God binds Peter, under the gaining of reward or the incurring of pain, that Peter do this, God intends that he do this; and so in both cases he intends and wills only truth, since the truth is that [23] Peter ought to do this, although it is not true that he does it, and consequently [that] is not intended or willed by God.[^5]

### 02-a ¶6 · 23.4–23.11  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 23] Et patet quod non excusatur **sed** accusatur ocium sacerdotum dicente Apostolo: Hortamur vos ne in vacuum graciam Dei recipiatis. Quicunque enim aliquam potestatem a Deo receperit, per quam liceat sibi opus Dei facere et ex negligencia pretermittit, in vanum graciam Dei recipit. Et in hoc imitamur eos, qui ex omissione instanter operando bonum peccaverunt continue.

ENGLISH: And it is clear that the idleness of priests is not excused but accused, as the Apostle says: "We exhort you that you receive not the grace of God in vain."[^6] For whoever has received any power from God by which it is lawful for him to do God's work, and through negligence passes it over, receives the grace of God in vain. And in this we imitate those who sinned continually by omission of urgently working good.[^7]

### 02-a ¶7 · 23.12–23.23  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 23] In hoc tamen est duplex culpa. prima est negligencia potentatus, et secunda maior, crebrior et periculosior est vetacio sathane, qua adversans impedit ne potentatus exerceat potestatem; et hoc contingit per mille meandros tradicionis humane, et specialiter per ficta de iurisdiccione. Quando **autem** non stat per illum qui immediate habet potestatem **sed** per alium irracionabiliter restringentem, tunc non sibi **sed** alii est debitum imputandum; **sed** hoc raro contingit, cum causa martirii foret hominem se exponere pro distribucione prudenti thesauri Domini.

ENGLISH: In this, however, there is a twofold fault. The first is the negligence of the potentate; and the second, greater, more frequent and more dangerous, is Satan's forbidding, by which the adversary prevents the potentate from exercising his power; and this happens through a thousand windings of human tradition, and especially through fictions about jurisdiction. But when it does not rest with the one who immediately has the power but with another who unreasonably restrains him, then the debt is to be imputed not to him but to the other; but this rarely happens, since it would be a cause for martyrdom for a man to expose himself for the prudent distribution of the Lord's treasure.[^8]

### 02-a ¶8 · 23.24–23.33  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 23] Unde videtur multis, quod Deus non intendit ut potens faciat tale opus; nec habet potestatem ad hoc; **sed** habet potestatem ut mereatur ex hoc quod nititur ad sic agendum. Alii **autem** dicunt quod habet potestatem remotam ad sic faciendum, hoc est, potestatem mediate; qua potest sic facere, et tamen Deus non intendit simpliciter quod ita faciet, et sic habet potestatem sic faciendi, **sed** non ad finem ut sic faciat.

ENGLISH: Hence it seems to many that God does not intend that the one who has power do such a work, nor does he have power for it; but he has power to merit from the fact that he strives to act so. But others say that he has remote power to do so, that is, power mediately, by which he can do so, and yet God does not intend [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} that he will do so; and so he has power of doing so, but not to the end that he do so.

### 02-a ¶9 · 24.1–24.29  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 24] **Sed** dimissa ista dissensione logica, videtur quod omnis potestas secularis diminuetur usque ad non gradum, quia solum durat pro tempore vie, ut docet Apostolus prima Corinth. XV, 24: Deinde, inquit, finis, cum tradiderit regnum Deo et Patri cum evacuaverit omnem principatum et potestatem et virtutem. Ymo videtur, cum Christus limitat omni sacerdoti suo formam et numerum obiectorum, quod hodie sunt prepositi potestatis minoris quam erant in primitiva ecclesia, et sic usque ad non gradum. Et id considerarent qui stulte et nescie suam magnificant potestatem. Videtur enim quod proporcionabiliter ut plus meritorie nituntur ad edificandum ecclesiam, sic habent amplius potestatis, cum secundum illam mensuram Deus dat potestatem hominibus; quilibet **autem** clericus vel laicus habet potestatem sufficientem edificandi ecclesiam, si voluerit esse Dei adiutor, faciens quod Deus destinavit eum ad taliter faciendum. Noverunt, inquam, logici quod est in libera potestate hominis facere quod Deus destinavit eum vel ordinavit eum ad fines huiusmodi. **Sed** aliter loquuntur qui ponunt globum potestatis quam Deus ubilibet potest per se ponere. Secundo principaliter arguitur de potestate faciendi miracula, de potestate dandi Spiritum Sanctum vel graciam, de potestate ampla absolvendi vel dispensandi alia sacramenta.

ENGLISH: But, setting aside this logical dissension, it seems that all secular power will be diminished down to no degree, since it lasts only for the time of [the way]{g:via}, as the Apostle teaches, 1 Corinthians 15:24: "Afterwards," he says, "the end: when he shall have delivered up the [kingdom]{g:regnum} to God and the Father: when he shall have brought to nought all principality and power and virtue." Indeed it seems, since Christ [assigns]{g:limitare} to every priest of his the form and number of objects, that today [superiors]{g:prepositi} are of less power than they were in the primitive [church]{g:ecclesia}, and so down to no degree.[^9] And those who foolishly and unknowingly magnify their own power should consider this. For it seems that in proportion as they strive more meritoriously to build up the church, so they have more power, since God gives power to men according to that measure; and any [cleric]{g:clericus} or layman whatever has power sufficient to build up the church, if he will be God's helper, doing what God destined him to do in such a way. Logicians know, I say, that it is in the free power of a man to do what God has destined him, or ordained him, to [do for] ends of this kind.[^10] But they speak otherwise who posit a mass of power which God can by himself place anywhere.[^11] In the second principal place, it is argued about the power of working miracles, about the power of giving the Holy Spirit or grace, and about the ample power of absolving or of dispensing the other sacraments.

### 02-a ¶10 · 24.30–26.8  (7 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 24] Quantum ad primum videtur mihi quod miraculum potest dici factum vel faccio quam Deus appropriate facit, et sic solus Deus agit miracula et longe crebrius quam putamus, quia quicunque creat licet solita, quia non miramur ea non [p. 25] vocamus miracula, ut declarat Augustinus super Joh. Omel., et sic quelibet creatura, sicut quilibet actus creandi potest dici miraculum, sic et quelibet faccio que Deo est propria. Unde idem esset creaturam vendicare potestatem miraculose agere, et vendicare quod sit alter Deus; quod Deus non potest creature concedere. Creare **autem** non potest Deus communicare creature, nec annichilare potest alicui competere, ut dictum est in materia de universalibus. Et patet quomodo debemus iudicare intellectu et fide miracula. Periculosum **autem** est instante tempore Antichristi magnificare signa sensibilia, cum Antichristi discipuli extollunt se super omne quod dicitur Deus, de principatu, de potestate, et virtute, quas Apostolus dicit evacuandos per Christum, Deus **autem** ad meritum hominis tam vivi quam mortui sepe facit miracula. Ad quem sensum dicitur corpora mortuorum facere miracula, ymo truncos lapides et multa putrida finguntur facere miracula propter lucrum. Sic **autem** equivocando in faccione miraculi, videtur quod omne membrum sancte matris ecclesie, de quanto sic meretur ut Deus operetur miraculose, facit miraculum, et pauci mortui qui sunt in statu merendi beati in patria. Illi **autem** qui dicunt quod lapis, herba et alia natura corporea habet virtutem faciendi miracula, videntur michi nimis confuse loqui, quia sic omnia forent miracula, cum nemo posset facere nisi miraculum quoad Deum. Alii **autem** dicunt quod oportet [p. 26] esse opus mirabile, et sic sunt illis quibus sunt mira insolita opera nature miracula, aliis **autem** non forent miracula, et sic de multis ficticiis infundabiliter adinventis. Vel ergo dicamus quod solus Deus habet potestatem faciendi miracula vel equivoce concedamus quod omnis homo promerens aput Deum, ut faciat ad preces suas miracula, facit quodammodo ipsa miracula.

ENGLISH: As to the first, it seems to me that a miracle can be called a deed or making which God does as proper to himself; and so God alone works miracles, and far more often than we think; for whatever he creates, although customary, we do not [25] call miracles, because we do not wonder at them, as Augustine declares on John, in a homily;[^12] and so every creature, as every act of creating, can be called a miracle, and so too every making that is proper to God. Hence it would be the same for a creature to claim power to act miraculously and to claim that it is another God, which God cannot grant to a creature. And God cannot communicate creating to a creature, nor can annihilating belong to anyone [but him], as has been said in the matter of universals.[^13] And it is clear how we ought to judge miracles, by understanding and by faith.[^14] But it is dangerous, with the time of Antichrist at hand, to magnify [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} signs, since the disciples of Antichrist "lift themselves up above all that is called God" on the score of principality, of power and of virtue, which the Apostle says are "to be brought to nought by Christ".[^15] But God often works miracles for the merit of a man, living as well as dead. In this sense the bodies of the dead are said to work miracles; indeed stocks, stones and many rotten things are feigned to work miracles for the sake of gain. And speaking [equivocally]{g:equivoce} in this way about the making of a miracle, it seems that every [member]{g:membrum} of holy mother church, insofar as he so merits that God works miraculously, works a miracle, and few of the dead who are in the state of meriting, the blessed in [the homeland]{g:patria}.[^16] But those who say that a stone, a herb and other bodily nature have the [power]{g:virtus} of working miracles seem to me to speak too [indistinctly]{g:confuse}, since in that way all things would be miracles, since no one could make anything but a miracle with respect to God. And others say that it must [26] be a wonderful work; and so the unusual works of nature are miracles to those to whom they are wonders, but to others they would not be miracles; and so with many [fictions]{g:ficticia} groundlessly invented. Either, therefore, let us say that God alone has power of working miracles, or let us grant equivocally that every man who merits with God that he should work miracles at his prayers does in some manner work the miracles himself.[^17]

### 02-b ¶1 · 26.9–32.8  (17 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 26] Quantum ad potestatem dandi Spiritum Sanctum vel graciam, videtur dicendum conformiter quod foret nimis magna presumpcio dicere quod homo peccator dat Spiritum Sanctum, quia Christo super quem specialiter descendit columba appropriatur baptizacio ista, ut patet Joh. primo, Ymo nullus talis habet potestatem servandi sibi Spiritum Sanctum, quomodo igitur ad dandum alteri, cum ignorat utrum pertineat ad columbam? Ymo apostoli, ad quorum ministerium dabatur Spiritus Sanctus, non presumpserunt asserere quod dederunt, **sed**, ut patet Act. VIII, 15, oraverunt pro illis ut acciperent Spiritum Sanctum. Ideo vel solus Deus habet potestatem dandi Spiritum Sanctum, vel quilibet laycus baptizando vel promerendo habet potestatem dandi Spiritum Sanctum; differenter tamen episcopus, secundum quod autoritative det donum tale, nimis multum excederet vicariam potestatem. Quis igitur concederet, quod habet potestatem super Deum vel eius graciam, et quomodocunque locuti fuerimus, maximum foret quod unus meretur alteri primam graciam, quia, cum in omni motu movens et motum sunt simul, nimis scandalosum foret quod Romanus episcopus tam distanter, tam generaliter et tam improporcionaliter ad [p. 27] Deum, alteraret homines, dando graciam, cum multorum talium spiritus, in quibus subiectaretur talis potestas, sunt miserabiliter damnandi non sufficientes iuvare se ipsos. Et si dicatur quod solum limitatur potestas eorum habilibus, cum solum talibus Deus potest dare graciam, revera habiles haberent a Deo graciam non eo plus vel minus, licet tales non sic menciantur se dare graciam Sicut igitur non habent potestatem faciendi filios Dei, dando graciam predestinacionis, sic nec aliquam graciam, qua Christianus foret gratus Deo. Nec est color si habent potestatem super antecedens, quod habent potestatem super omne consequens vel convertibile, eo quod sic essent potentatus pares Deo, et cum Psalmo LXXXIX, 10 dicitur: Si in potentatibus octoginta anni, et amplius eorum, labor et dolor. Et revera minus videtur errorem sapere quod sanitas corporalis subsit hominis potestati, quam quod gracia vel Spiritus Sanctus subsit, nam cum nichil est in potestate hominis dare, nisi quod subiacet sue potestati, nimis magnum foret christianum habere potestatem dandi Spiritum Sanctum; hoc enim est singulare donum sui principii, cum christianus non potest ipsum vendere. Et conformis est consideracio de absolucione; quondam enim reputabatur Deo proprium tollere peccata tam aput fideles quam aput infideles; unde singulariter dixit Baptista de Christo, ut legitur Joh. I, 29: Ecce agnus Dei, ecce qui tollit peccata mundi. Super quo dicit August. Omel. IV: Nemo sibi arroget et dicat, quod ipse aufert peccata [p. 28] mundi. Iam attendite contra quos superbos intendebat digitum Ioannes. Nondum erant nati heretici et iam ostendebantur, contra illos clamabat a fluvio. Unde sepe imposuerunt Judei Christo blasfemiam ex hoc quod peccata dimisit simpliciter, quia non dimittitur peccatum simpliciter nisi ex hoc, quod offensa in Deum laxatur, **sed** quis dimittit iniuriam, nisi vel factam sibi vel suo subdito? Deus enim dando homini potestatem huiusmodi, prius dimitteret suam iniuriam antequam vicarius suus dimitteret. Et ista videtur sentencia multorum doctorum, ut XXIII, q. V ex decreto beati Ambrosii sic habetur: Remittuntur, inquit, peccata per Dei filium, cuius levita interpres et quidam excusator est, quia, ut dicitur XV, q. I: Nec is: Levita minister remissionis est. Unde super illo decreto Archidiaconus ita scribit: Prelati quando ligant vel solvunt indignos, mirum est si tunc ligantur vel solvuntur in celis, **sed** intelligitur de illis, quorum merita ligari vel solvi postulant. Et illud notat XXIV, q. I Ubi sana et Quicunque. Nec dubium de ista sentencia, ne blasfemetur quod prelati nostri prevalent contra Deum; Deus enim non potest quemquam absolvere, nisi prius ipsum habilitet; cum igitur christianus nullum habilitat, nisi forsan orando vel merendo, patet quod oportet Deum habilitare hominem secundum meritum eius. Et de isto debet christianus confidere non **autem** in pseudochristi blasfemia, [p. 29] qua sompniat et facit ignaros credere de sua insensibili et infundabili potestate; unde non sinit quod scrutinium sue blasphemie in luce clareat, et licet sit tantum errabilis, blasfemat profundius quod Deus est necessitatus complere omnia sua iudicia. Sicut igitur sacerdotes legis veteris non fecerunt hominem sanum vel leprosum **sed** notificabant, ut patet IV Sentenciarum dist. XVIII et recitatur inferius, sic presbyteri legis nove. In cuius signum Christus suscitavit Lazarum et fecit Apostolos ipsum solvere, ut patet X, XI: et decem leprosi, dum ipsi irent, mundati sunt, ut dicitur Luce XVII, 12, 14 et Psalmus XXXI, 5: Dixi confitebor adversum me, et tu remisisti. Verum tamen amplius habent sacerdotes Christi quam sacerdotes legis veteris, quia plus est iudicare de macula spiritus quam de macula corporali, plus eciam est dignum et humile habere graciam et meritum ex subieccione ecclesie quam corporaliter infirmum esse notificatum in populo esse sanum; et sic licet in multis excellenciis sacerdocium legis veteris superat sacerdocium legis nove, ad sacerdocium tamen Christi non potest attingere, **sed** generaliter fallit, quando non sequitur arbitrium interni iudicis, ut patet XI, q. III Ad hoc vera est. Ista **autem** blasfema gentilitas, qua sacerdotes debiliores usurpant sibi opus, quod est Deo proprium, maculat matrem ecclesiam, ideo necesse foret eos cognoscere, quomodo quidam tantum a veritate degeneraverant quod negarunt accionem aliquam competere creature (ut patet IX Metaphysice Comment. [p. 30] VII). Alii **autem** magis blasfeme concesserant quod Deus omnem accionem illis ad extra communicat tamquam eius vicariis, et hii tamquam alter Nabuchodonosor. de quo Dan. IV. et alter Senacherib, de quo II. Paralip. XXXII vendicant se esse socios Trinitatis. Considerarent **autem** dictum Augustini II De Mirabilibus Scripture Sacre cap. IV quomodo sol et luna steterunt contra Gabaon, non humani imperii auctoritate **sed** Domini imperantis. Nil, inquit, propter iubentis hominis verbum creature faciunt, **sed** quod Dominus servi sui oracioni obediens precipit, et cap. XXIV dicit quod lepra Naaman sanata est in sermone imperantis Dei, et non in re aliqua sacramentum virtutis continebatur, et hinc Elizeus accipere munera recusavit. Et sic attribuerent sibi quod servo est proprium, promulgando, operando et promerendo, quod Deus dignatur per illos facere continue, sumendo ut servi humiliter de se ipsis. Ubi **autem** dubitant de suo opere, debent vere, religiose et secure totam accionem Deo ascribere, cum oportet eum facere omnem accionem creature. Sive utatur igitur in tali accione creatura sua ut organo sive non, sibi laus est operis tribuenda, laudem **autem** creature de quanto meretur oportet esse aput Deum absconditam, unde magnificantes suam potenciam notarent formam utendi et ascribendi potestatem in sanctis apostolis, et nimis magnum foret, quod nostri illos excederent. Ipsi **autem** sepe confitentur, quod non habent potestatem nisi ad edificacionem ecclesie, ut patet [p. 31] II. Corinth. X. et iterum confitentur quod ve illis nisi edificatorie usi fuerint potestate, ut patet I. Corinth. IX, 16: Ve mihi, si non evangelizavero, et tercio confitentur se non audere de se ipsis loqui vel aliis nisi quod Christus in eis loquitur. Nam Rom. XV, 18 ait Apostolus: Non, inquit, audeo aliquid loqui eorum, que per me non effecit Christus. Stulticia igitur foret, episcopum cecum infundabilia et inopinabilia de se ipso presumere. Quantumcunque itaque fuerit magnus Christi episcopus, non potest quemquam absolvere nisi de quanto contritus prius et absolutus fuerit apud Deum; et tunc videtur multis quod de tanto quilibet simplex sacerdos posset absolvere, ymo quasi contritus secure absolvitur, etsi humana absolucio non sequatur, dimittere **autem** suam iniuriam sicut peccatum in ipsum potest quilibet christianus eque plene ut papa, sicut ponit Matth. VI et XVIII. Quomodo igitur auderet aliquis dicere quod ipse habet singulariter potestatem absolvendi quemcumque peccatorem a pena et a culpa? Revera foret nimis damnabilis, qui ad detrimentum ecclesie tantum incarcerat huiusmodi potestatem. Et conformiter dicitur de potestate excommunicandi vel puniendi subiectos. Nemo enim habet ut membrum Christi potestatem puniendi aliquem, nisi de quanto Deus prius punierit, quod si excedit, punit ut tortor dyaboli. Et hinc dicit Augustinus in De Mirabilibus Scripture cap. XVII quod Isaac benedixit non Esau quem voluit, **sed** [p. 32] Jacob quem noluit benedicere, ut in benediccione non hominis ostenderet beneficium **sed** Dei benedicentis. Denique dictum est in Numeris ad Moysen et Aaron sacerdotes: Vos ponite nomen meum super filios Israhel, ego Dominus benedicam vos. Nec voluntas sacerdotis obesse aut prodesse possit ultra meritum benediccionem poscentis. Nec dubium fideli de ista sentencia.

ENGLISH: As to the power of giving the Holy Spirit or grace, it seems that one must say, in conformity [with the foregoing], that it would be too great a presumption to say that a sinful man gives the Holy Spirit, since this baptism is appropriated to Christ, upon whom the dove specially descended, as is clear from John 1.[^1] Indeed no such man has [power]{g:potestas} to keep the Holy Spirit for himself; how then [has he power] to give it to another, since he [does not know]{g:ignorare} whether he belongs to the dove? Indeed the apostles, by whose ministry the Holy Spirit was given, did not presume to assert that they gave it, but, as is clear from Acts 8:15, they "prayed for them that they might receive the Holy Ghost". Therefore either God alone has power of giving the Holy Spirit, or any layman whatever, by baptizing or by meriting, has power of giving the Holy Spirit; yet it is otherwise with a [bishop]{g:episcopus}: insofar as he were to give such a gift by his own authority, he would far too much exceed vicarial power.[^2] Who, therefore, would grant that he has power over God or over his grace? And however we may have spoken, the most there could be is that one merits the first grace for another; for since "in every motion the mover and the moved are together", it would be too scandalous that [the Roman bishop]{g:romanus-episcopus}, so distant, so general and so out of proportion to [27] God, should alter men by giving grace, since the spirits of many such men, in whom such power would have its subject, are wretchedly to be damned, not sufficient to help themselves.[^3] And if it is said that their power is [limited]{g:limitare} only to the [qualified]{g:habilis}, since to such alone can God give grace, in truth the qualified would have grace from God neither more nor less on that account, even if such men did not lie in this way that they give grace. Just as, therefore, they do not have power of making sons of God by giving the grace of predestination, so neither [have they power to give] any grace by which a Christian would be pleasing to God. Nor is there [colour]{g:color} [for saying], if they have power over the [antecedent]{g:antecedens}, that they have power over every consequent or convertible, since in that way potentates would be equal to God; and as it is said in Psalm 89:10: "But if in the strong they be fourscore years: and what is more of them is labour and sorrow."[^4] And in truth it seems to savour less of error that bodily health should be subject to a man's power than that grace or the Holy Spirit should be; for since nothing is in a man's power to give except what lies under his power, it would be too much for a Christian to have power of giving the Holy Spirit; for this is the singular gift of its own principle, since a Christian cannot sell it.[^5] And the consideration of absolution is alike; for once it was reckoned proper to God to take away sins, among the faithful as well as among [unbelievers]{g:infidelis}; hence the Baptist said singularly of Christ, as is read in John 1:29: "Behold the Lamb of God. Behold him who taketh away the sins of the world." On which Augustine says, in Homily 4: "Let no one arrogate it to himself and say that he takes away the sins [28] of the world. Mark now against what proud men John was pointing his finger. The heretics were not yet born, and already they were being pointed out; against them he was crying from the river."[^6] Hence the Jews often charged Christ with blasphemy because he forgave sins [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}; for a sin is not forgiven without qualification except by the offence against God being released; now who forgives an injury, except one done either to himself or to his subject? For God, in giving a man power of this kind, would forgive his own injury before his vicar forgave it.[^7] And this seems to be the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of many doctors, as is had thus in [C.] 23 q. 5 from the decree of blessed Ambrose: "Sins," he says, "are [remitted]{g:remittere} through the Son of God, whose interpreter and a kind of excuser the Levite is"; for, as is said in [C.] 15 q. 1, *Nec is*: "The Levite is the minister of remission."[^8] Hence on that decree the Archdeacon writes thus: "When [prelates]{g:prelatus} bind or loose the unworthy, it is a wonder if they are then bound or loosed in heaven; but it is understood of those whose merits demand that they be bound or loosed." And [the gloss] notes this at [C.] 24 q. 1, *Ubi sana* and *Quicunque*.[^9] Nor is there doubt about this teaching, lest it be blasphemously said that our prelates prevail against God; for God cannot absolve anyone unless he first qualifies him; since, then, a Christian qualifies no one, except perhaps by praying or meriting, it is clear that God must qualify a man according to his merit. And in this a Christian ought to trust, but not in the blasphemy of the false christ, [29] by which he dreams, and makes the ignorant believe, of his [imperceptible]{g:insensibilis} and groundless power; hence he does not allow the scrutiny of his blasphemy to shine out in the light, and although he is so liable to error, he blasphemes more deeply that God is necessitated to fulfil all his judgments.[^10] Just as, therefore, the [priests]{g:sacerdos} of the old law did not make a man clean or leprous but made it known, as is clear from *Sentences* IV dist. 18, and is recounted below, so [too] the [presbyters]{g:presbiter} of the new law.[^11] In token of which Christ raised Lazarus and had the apostles loose him, as is clear from [John] 10, 11; and the ten lepers, "as they went, were made clean", as is said in Luke 17:12, 14; and Psalm 31:5: "I said I will confess against myself … and thou hast forgiven."[^12] Nevertheless the priests of Christ have more than the priests of the old law, since it is more to judge of a stain of the spirit than of a bodily stain; it is also more worthy and humble to have grace and merit from subjection to the [church]{g:ecclesia} than for one bodily sick to be made known among the people to be clean; and so, although in many excellences the priesthood of the old law surpasses the priesthood of the new law, yet it cannot reach to the priesthood of Christ; but it generally fails when it does not follow the judgment of the inward judge, as is clear from [C.] 11 q. 3, *Ad hoc vera est*.[^13] And this blasphemous paganism, by which feebler priests usurp to themselves the work that is proper to God, stains mother church; therefore it would be needful for them to know how certain men had degenerated so far from the truth that they denied that any action belongs to a creature (as is clear from *Metaphysics* IX, comment [30] 7). But others, more blasphemously, had granted that God communicates every outward action to them as his vicars; and these, like another Nebuchadnezzar, of whom Daniel 4, and another Sennacherib, of whom 2 Chronicles 32, claim to be partners of the Trinity.[^14] And let them consider the saying of Augustine, *On the Wonders of Holy Scripture* II, chapter 4, how the sun and moon stood still against Gibeon "not by the authority of a human command but of the Lord commanding. Creatures," he says, "do nothing on account of the word of the man who bids, but what the Lord, obeying the prayer of his servant, commands"; and in chapter 24 he says that Naaman's leprosy was healed "at the word of God commanding, and the sacrament of [power]{g:virtus} was not contained in any thing; and hence Elisha refused to accept gifts".[^15] And so they should attribute to themselves what is proper to a servant, proclaiming, working and meriting what God deigns to do continually through them, taking [the part] of servants humbly with regard to themselves. And where they are in doubt about their own work, they ought truly, religiously and safely to ascribe the whole action to God, since he must do every action of a creature. Whether, then, he uses his creature as an instrument in such an action or not, the praise of the work is to be given to him; and the praise of the creature, insofar as it merits, must be hidden with God; hence those who magnify their own [power]{g:potencia} should note the form of using and ascribing power in the holy apostles, and it would be too much that ours should exceed them.[^16] And they often confess that they have no power except for the building up of the church, as is clear from [31] 2 Corinthians 10; and again they confess that woe to them unless they have used power to build up, as is clear from 1 Corinthians 9:16: "Woe is unto me if I preach not the gospel"; and thirdly they confess that they do not dare to speak of themselves or of others except what Christ speaks in them. For in Romans 15:18 the Apostle says: "For I dare not," he says, "to speak of any of those things which Christ worketh not by me."[^17] It would therefore be folly for a blind bishop to presume groundless and incredible things of himself. However great, then, a bishop of Christ may be, he cannot absolve anyone except insofar as [the man] has first been contrite and absolved before God; and then it seems to many that to that extent any simple priest could absolve; indeed one who is as it were contrite is safely absolved, even if human absolution does not follow; and any Christian can forgive his own injury, as a sin against himself, as fully as the [pope]{g:papa}, as Matthew 6 and 18 lay down.[^18] How then would anyone dare to say that he singularly has power of absolving any sinner whatever from penalty and from guilt? In truth he would be most damnable who, to the detriment of the church, so imprisons power of this kind.[^19] And in conformity the answer is the same concerning the power of excommunicating or punishing subjects. For no one has, as a [member]{g:membrum} of Christ, power of punishing anyone except insofar as God has first punished; and if he exceeds this, he punishes as the devil's torturer. And hence Augustine says in *On the Wonders of Scripture*, chapter 17, that "Isaac blessed not Esau, whom he wished [to bless], but [32] Jacob, whom he did not wish to bless, so that in the blessing he might show the benefit not of man but of God who blesses. Finally it was said in Numbers to Moses and Aaron the priests: 'Put ye my name upon the children of Israel; I the Lord will bless you.' Nor can the will of the priest hinder or help beyond the merit of him who asks for the blessing."[^20] Nor is there doubt for one of the faithful about this teaching.

### 02-c ¶1 · 32.9–33.5  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 32] **Sed** tercio principaliter arguitur de modo committendi potestates nec non de ordine et perdicione potestatum ecclesie; et que potestas sit alia prestancior? Quantum ad primum patet, cum Deus dat omnem potestatem vicariam, qualem solum Deus communicat creature, non refert sive in signo sensibili, sive inspiracione interna Deus potestatem talem communicaverit creature quia oportet omnino quod insensibiliter communicet sive sacramentum sensibile affuerit sive non. Unde vana est concencio, quando et quomodo apostoli facti fuerant sacerdotes, ut Ardmacanus libro II De Questionibus Armenorum recitat septem opiniones in ista materia. Prima dicit quod, quando Christus dixit eis Math. XVIII, 18: Quodcunque ligaveritis super terram erit ligatum et in celis, secunda dicit, quando in cena dixit apostolis de confeccione eukaristie, hoc facite in meam commemoracionem, de quo Luce XXII, 19; tercia opinio dicit quod, quando Christus inflavit in eos dicens: Accipite Spiritum Sanctum, quorum remiseritis peccata, remittentur eis, Joh. XX, 22, 23. Quarta opinio dicit quod die Penthecostes, quando Spiritus Sanctus apparuit eis in linguis [p. 33] igneis Act. II; quinta opinio dicit quod in ascensione, quando dixit: Ite, docete omnes gentes, baptizantes eos Matth, ult.; sexta opinio dicit quod, quando primo misit apostolos ad predicandum Matth. X, 8 dicit: Infirmos curate.

ENGLISH: But in the third principal place it is argued about the manner of committing powers, and also about the order and the loss of the [powers]{g:potestas} of the [church]{g:ecclesia}; and which power is more excellent than another. As to the first it is clear: since God gives every vicarial power, such as God alone communicates to a creature, it makes no difference whether God has communicated such power to a creature in a [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} sign or by inward inspiration, since he must altogether communicate it [imperceptibly]{g:insensibilis}, whether a perceptible sacrament is present or not. Hence the contention is vain over when and how the apostles were made [priests]{g:sacerdos}, as Armachanus, in book 2 *On the Questions of the Armenians*, recites seven opinions on this matter.[^1] The first says [it was] when Christ said to them, Matthew 18:18: "Whatsoever you shall bind upon earth, shall be bound also in heaven"; the second says, when at the supper he said to the apostles, of the confection of the eucharist, "Do this for a commemoration of me", of which Luke 22:19; the third opinion says [it was] when Christ breathed on them, saying: "Receive ye the Holy Ghost. Whose sins you shall forgive, they shall be forgiven them", John 20:22–23.[^2] The fourth opinion says [it was] on the day of Pentecost, when the Holy Spirit appeared to them in tongues [33] of fire, Acts 2; the fifth opinion says [it was] at the ascension, when he said: "Go, teach ye all nations: baptizing them", Matthew, the last [chapter]; the sixth opinion says [it was] when he first sent the apostles to preach, Matthew 10:8, [where] he says: "Heal the sick."[^3]

### 02-c ¶2 · 33.6–33.14  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 33] **Sed** doctor reprobat omnes istas sex opiniones per hoc quod, quando fecit eos apostolos dedit eis potestatem sui officii, que est potestas sacerdocii; nec sequitur quod primo, quando precipit eis hec facere. habuerunt huiusmodi potestatem. Nam Joh. IV, legitur quod vivente Baptista apostoli baptizarunt et sic predicarunt et multa sacerdotis officia ante mortem Christi exercuerant, cum Yudas fuit episcopus, ut dicitur Actuum primo.

ENGLISH: But the doctor rejects all these six opinions on the ground that, when he made them apostles, he gave them the power of their office, which is the power of the priesthood; nor does [it follow]{g:sequitur} that they first had power of this kind when he commanded them to do these things. For in John 4 it is read that while the Baptist was living the apostles baptized, and so they preached and had exercised many offices of a priest before Christ's death, since Judas was a [bishop]{g:episcopus}, as is said in Acts 1.[^4]

### 02-c ¶3 · 33.15–34.6  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 33] In ista materia videntur mihi tria dicenda: Primo quod sentencia ista doctoris sit satis probabilis, secundo videtur mihi vanum nimis discutere quando apostoli baptizabantur, ordinabantur in sacerdotes vel sacramenta alia ministraverant sive receperant ultra quod est in evangelio expressatum. Scivit enim Christus quod infideles christiani signa querentes ad sacramentalia sensibilia, dimissa re sacramenti, attenderent; ideo de ministerio sacramentorum huiusmodi facit modicam mencionem, **sed** ut ego credo insensibiliter sine sacramento sensibili, tamquam primus magister ordinavit suos apostolos sacerdotes. Sic enim insensibiliter ordinavit Moysen in veteri testamento; ipse **autem** fuit sacerdos, licet non Aaronita sensibiliter ordinatus; et ita ignoramus locum; tempus et formam, quibus apostoli ordinati fuerant sacerdotes; debemus tamen credere quod si fuisset plus necessarium ad fidem [p. 34] ecclesie, Spiritus Sanctus in evangelio expressisset. Unde vana curiositas videtur, plus quam evidenter ex scriptura elicitur, tractare huiusmodi questiones, **sed** ignoranciam nostram confitendo humiliter debemus gracias agere Deo; qui noticiam nostram sic salubriter moderavit.

ENGLISH: On this matter it seems to me that three things are to be said. First, that this [teaching]{g:sentencia} of the doctor is quite probable; second, it seems to me too vain to discuss when the apostles were baptized, were ordained priests, or ministered or received other sacraments, beyond what is expressed in the gospel.[^5] For Christ knew that [faithless]{g:infidelis} Christians, seeking signs, would attend to perceptible [sacramentals]{g:sacramentalia}, letting go the [reality of the sacrament]{g:res-sacramenti}; therefore he makes little mention of the ministry of sacraments of this kind; but, as I believe, he ordained his apostles priests imperceptibly, without a perceptible sacrament, as the first master. For so he imperceptibly ordained Moses in the old testament; and he was a priest, although not an Aaronite perceptibly ordained; and so we [are ignorant of]{g:ignorare} the place, time and form in which the apostles were ordained priests; yet we ought to believe that, if it had been more necessary to the faith [34] of the church, the Holy Spirit would have expressed it in the gospel.[^6] Hence it seems a vain curiosity to treat questions of this kind beyond what is evidently drawn from scripture; but, confessing our ignorance humbly, we ought to give thanks to God, who has so wholesomely moderated our knowledge.

### 02-c ¶4 · 34.7–34.11  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 34] **Sed** tercio videtur doctorem arguere ex inconveniencia quod oportuit simul et plene fieri sacerdotes. quia aliter (ut inquit) sacramentum sacerdocii foret partibile et non indivisibile ut reliqua sacramenta.

ENGLISH: But thirdly the doctor seems to argue from an [absurdity]{g:inconveniencia} that they had to be made priests at once and fully, since otherwise (as he says) "the sacrament of priesthood would be divisible and not indivisible, like the other sacraments".[^7]

### 02-c ¶5 · 34.12–35.16  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 34] **Sed** hic videtur mihi iuxta dicta de potestate ordinis quod, quotquot sunt genera officiorum presbyteri, tot sunt genera potestatum, ymo respectu cuiuscunque actus quem debet facere ex potestate huiusmodi est dare unam insensibilem; et omnes ille tamquam partes integrales vel aggregata eidem subiecto unam potestatem constituunt. Nec videtur illud insensibile **sed** signum sensibile quod facit ordinans esse ecclesiasticum sacramentum. Nec est inconveniens **sed** veritati consonum quod quotlibet parcialia constituunt unum sacramentum, licet sint disparium specierum, nam per se notum videtur fidelibus quod Deus posset dare homini potestatem ad unum et subtrahere potestatem ad aliud. Ex quibus sequitur cum dictis de quiditate potestatis, quod oportet dare particulares potestates secundum numerum obiectorum terminancium, quia diceretur aliter quod preter liberum arbitrium nemo acquireret vel perderet potestatem. Ulterius videtur incertum quantam potestatem oporteret viantem habere, ad hoc quod sit sacerdos, vel si omnes sacerdotes sunt paris [p. 35] potestatis ordinis, quia certum est quod Deus dat vel non dat, ut sibi placuerit, ordinis potestatem. Unde caracterem sacerdotalis ordinis supponimus communiter consequi par officium sacerdotis. In qua materia vellem nostros solempnizantes constituciones humanas ad fidem scripture et puram racionem attendere et non amplius fingere ad onus ecclesie, nisi quod exinde scirent clare deducere, quia potissimum in talibus quod summus sacerdos potest facere, est mereri vel preconizare quod tale sit Dei iudicium, a quo sensu sepe exorbitat; ideo sepe creditur quod absolvit vel excommunicat, et tamen totum contrarium evenit aput Deum, et dum nos viantes sumus ecclesie triumphanti contrarii, necesse est quod sit controversia in universali ecclesia.

ENGLISH: But here it seems to me, in keeping with what was said about the [power of order]{g:potestas-ordinis}, that as many as are the kinds of offices of a [presbyter]{g:presbiter}, so many are the kinds of powers; indeed, with respect to each act which he ought to do from power of this kind, there is one imperceptible [power] to be posited; and all of them, as integral parts, or as aggregated to the same subject, constitute one power.[^8] And it seems that not that imperceptible [power] but the perceptible sign which the one ordaining makes is the ecclesiastical sacrament. Nor is it an absurdity, but consonant with truth, that any number of partial [powers] constitute one sacrament, although they are of disparate species; for it seems self-evident to the faithful that God could give a man power for one thing and withdraw power for another. From these, together with what has been said about the [quiddity]{g:quiditas} of power, it follows that one must posit particular powers according to the number of the terminating objects; for otherwise it would be said that apart from [free choice]{g:liberum-arbitrium} no one would acquire or lose power.[^9] Further, it seems uncertain how much power [one on the road]{g:viantes} would need to have in order to be a priest, or whether all priests are of equal [35] power of order, since it is certain that God gives or does not give the power of order as it pleases him. Hence we commonly suppose that the [character]{g:caracter} of priestly order follows upon an equal office of priest.[^10] In this matter I would wish our men who solemnize human constitutions to attend to the faith of scripture and to pure reason, and not to feign more, to the burden of the church, than they could clearly deduce from there; for the most that the highest priest can do in such things is to merit, or to proclaim, that such is God's judgment, from which sense he often strays; therefore it is often believed that he absolves or excommunicates, and yet the whole contrary comes about with God; and while we who are on the road are contrary to the [church triumphant]{g:ecclesia-triumphans}, there must be controversy in the universal church.[^11]

### 02-c ¶6 · 35.17–36.27  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 35] Quantum ad tercium, scilicet ordinem potestatum, dicitur communiter quod sicut sunt novem ordines angelorum, sic sunt novem ordines potestatum; et hoc elicitur ex Apostolo I. Corinth. XII, 6 ubi declarans unitatem corporis ecclesie ostendit, primo quod divisio graciarum, ministracionum et operacionum procedit ab uno spirituali Domino, qui operatur in omnibus; oportet enim graciam precedere, que sit principium ministrandi in clericis et operandi in laycis. Spiritus **autem** dat graciam, Dominus recipit ministracionem, Deus exigit ministracionem. Alii, inquit, per Spiritum datur sermo sapiencie, alii vero sermo sciencie secundum eundem Spiritum. Alteri fides in eodem Spiritu, alii gracia sanitatum in uno Spiritu, alii [p. 36] operacio virtutum, alii prophecia, alii discrecio Spirituum, alii genera linguarum, alii interpretacio sermonum. Et hec novem videtur Apostolus consequenter ordine suo in subiectis suis exprimere. Quosdam, inquit, Deus posuit in ecclesia, primo apostolos, secundo prophetas, tercio doctores, demum virtutes, exinde gracias curacionum, opitulacioues, gubernaciones, genera linguarum, interpretaciones sermonum. Que novem videntur aptanda novem prioribus. Apostoli **autem** sunt primi post Christum, quorum officium est preeminenter secundum sapienciam et missionem capitis ministrare, et eorum officium gerunt episcopi, sicut dicitur papam gerere vicem Christi. Nec oportet contendere circa gradum disposicionis requisite ad esse apostoli, quia certum est quod est latitudo supereminentis disposicionis ad statum apostolicum requisita, qua deficiente non est apostolus sive episcopus; nec requiritur quod Christus viando ipsum elegerit, ut patet de Paulo, Barnaba, Luca, Marco et Matthia; nec obest quod habeat multa officia, cum debeat propinquius sequi Christum assumentem summe ministratorie officium septem ordinum, ut patet libro IV capitulo VIII. Ideo officia cleri debent esse supereminenter in apostolis aggregata, sicut sapiencia excedit alia bona.

ENGLISH: As to the third, namely the order of powers, it is commonly said that, just as there are nine orders of angels, so there are nine orders of powers; and this is drawn from the Apostle, 1 Corinthians 12:6, where, declaring the unity of the body of the church, he shows first that the division of graces, of ministries and of operations proceeds from one spiritual Lord, "who worketh all in all"; for grace must come first, which is the principle of ministering in clerics and of working in laymen.[^12] And the Spirit gives grace, the Lord receives ministry, God demands ministry.[^13] "To one indeed," he says, "by the Spirit, is given the word of wisdom: and to another, the word of knowledge, according to the same Spirit: to another, faith in the same spirit: to another, the grace of healing in one Spirit: to another [36] the working of miracles: to another, prophecy: to another, the discerning of spirits: to another, diverse kinds of tongues: to another, interpretation of speeches." And these nine the Apostle seems to express in sequence, in their order, in his subjects. "God," he says, "hath set some in the church; first apostles, secondly prophets, thirdly doctors: after that miracles: then the graces of healings, helps, governments, kinds of tongues, interpretations of speeches."[^14] Which nine seem to be fitted to the nine before. And the apostles are first after Christ; their office is to minister pre-eminently according to the wisdom and the mission of the head; and the bishops bear their office, as the [pope]{g:papa} is said to bear the place of Christ.[^15] Nor need one contend about the degree of disposition required for being an apostle, since it is certain that there is a latitude of surpassing disposition required for the apostolic state, and where it is lacking there is no apostle or bishop; nor is it required that Christ chose him while on the way, as is clear of Paul, Barnabas, Luke, Mark and Matthias; nor does it stand in the way that he has many offices, since he ought to follow more nearly Christ, who took on himself, in the highest and most ministering way, the office of the seven orders, as is clear in book IV, chapter 8.[^16] Therefore the offices of [the clergy]{g:clerus} ought to be aggregated in the apostles in a surpassing way, as wisdom exceeds the other goods.
