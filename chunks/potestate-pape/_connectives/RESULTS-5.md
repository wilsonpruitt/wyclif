## Doubtful, left as they are

- **06-g ¶3, p. 129**: *Ille autem perfecciones more universalium non possunt augmentari*, rendered "Now". Either (a), the premiss that explains why the first perfection is the greater and leads to *et sic … congruit clero Christi*, or (c), a gloss extending the distinction ("And those perfections"). "Now" is defensible.
- **06-h ¶2, p. 133**: *Ipse autem infinitum excedit status alios … et patet ad primum obiectum*, rendered "And". Could be (a), the step that closes the reply to the first objection; read as (c), extending "of such a state is our discourse". "And" is defensible.
- **07-a ¶1, p. 135**: *Sed ulterius transcurrendum est per argumenta*, rendered "But further". A transition to the next set of arguments, so arguably the "next step" ("Now further"); but it brings no premiss to bear, and "But" as a turn of topic is the plainer reading.
- **07-a ¶3, p. 135**: *debet autem primas post Christum esse minor*, rendered "and the primate … ought to be the lesser". Either (c), continuing *Constat autem* (both premisses together, "now … and …"), or (a), the separate step applying the rule to the primate. The English "now … ; and …" carries the pair as one premiss; left.
