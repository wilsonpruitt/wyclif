# *De eucharistia*, chapter 3, part 3: no ground for transubstantiation; the second argument, from the account of accident

## ¶1 · 61.14–62.10

> [p. 61] Item, secundum adversarium foret possibile quod corpus Christi maneret sacramentaliter sub pane, sicut iam manet sub nudis accidentibus et nec scriptura sacra nec racio nec revelacio docet aliquem quod sit talis transsubstanciacio; ergo stultum foret talem sensum inducere. Cum enim secundum Augustinum IIIᵒ De Doctrina Christiana in fine, omnis veritas sit in scriptura sacra, pertineret prepositis perscrutari fundamentum suum ibidem, ne ex falsitate illudant et onerent ecclesiam plus quam debent; et istud movet multos, cum nemo debet credere eciam pape in materia fidei, nisi de quanto se fundaverit in scriptura. Sed nec scriptura nec racione nec revelacione potest ecclesia Avinonica fundare predictam transsubstanciacionem, ergo non pocius tenemur ipsam credere quam credidit ecclesia primitiva; sic eciam [p. 62] posset ecclesia onerari mille fallaciis tanquam fide, et posset processu temporis induci lex Antichristi; creditur, inquam, quod in edicione ultima illius decretalis De Fide catholica, capitulo Firmiter in fine, non concurrerunt papa et CXIIIᶜⁱᵐ episcopi, ut contigit in sinodo qua decretum est quod panis et vinum remanent; ideo (ut dixi superius) non est racio quare debemus eis credere plus quam illis, potissime cum fabricatores mendacii de novo subrepserant, quos creditur istam ficticiam invenisse.

Again, according to the adversary it would be possible for the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} to remain [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} under [bread]{g:panis}, just as it now remains under bare [accidents]{g:accidens}; and neither sacred scripture nor reason nor revelation teaches anyone that there is such a [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio}; therefore it would be foolish to bring in such a sense. For since, according to Augustine, *On Christian Doctrine* III at the end, all truth is in sacred scripture,[^1] it would belong to [superiors]{g:prepositi} to search out their foundation there, lest out of falsehood they deceive and burden the [church]{g:ecclesia} more than they ought; and this moves many, since no one ought to believe even the [pope]{g:papa} in a matter of faith except insofar as he has grounded himself in scripture. Now neither by scripture nor by reason nor by revelation can the church of Avignon ground the aforesaid transubstantiation; therefore we are no more bound to believe it than the primitive church believed [it]. In this way, too, [62] the church could be burdened with a thousand [fallacies]{g:fallacia} as though with faith, and in process of time the law of Antichrist could be brought in. It is believed, I say, that in the last edition of that decretal *De fide catholica*, chapter *Firmiter*, at the end, the pope and the hundred and thirteen bishops did not concur, as happened in the synod in which it was decreed that the bread and the [wine]{g:vinum} remain;[^2] so (as I said above) there is no reason why we should believe these more than those,[^3] especially since fabricators of falsehood had newly crept in, who are believed to have invented this [fiction]{g:ficticia}.

## ¶2 · 62.11–63.22

> [p. 62] Cum ergo subtiliores doctores moderni vident et asserunt quod nichil cogit hoc credere nisi predicta decretalis et ipsa non daret per se fidem fidelibus, videtur quod in isto deficit huius fidei fundamentum. Oportet enim legistas fundantes tam sollempniter novum festum baptismo dimisso aliquid novum atque mirabile inculcare. Unde omnes legiste et eorum auctores nesciunt describere transsubstanciacionem et quomodo ab anichilacione distinguitur cum annexis difficultatibus, quibus exinde ecclesia est involuta. Conceditur tamen quod accidencia illa que quidam vocant panem et vinum sunt honoranda ut signum corporis Christi et sanguinis. Sed quantum patres legis veteris cavebant colere ymagines tanquam Deum, ut patet in epistola Jeremie missa Babiloniam per Baruch, tantum debet christianus cavere ne colat illud quod moderni vocant accidens et prior ecclesia [p. 63] vocavit panem et vinum tanquam verum corpus et sanguinem Jesu Christi, quia sicut ille ymagines non poterant se iuvare vel suos cultores contra hostes, latrones vel aves, ut docet Jeremias Baruch ultimo, revera sic nec dictum accidens. Ideo illo dimisso signatum suum quod est corpus Christi et sanguis, ymmo tota Christi humanitas, quantum univoce sufficimus est colenda; et licet illud signum habeat maiorem efficaciam quam signa legis veteris et nostre ymagines (ut patebit posterius), tamen nimis magna infidelitas foret credere illud tam abiectum in natura esse Deum; ymmo sic immolans infideliter facit abhominacionem et despectum Deo sicut alii infideles ydolatre; et illud predicandum foret laycis, ne stulta pietate precipitarentur infideliter in ydolatriam. Nec videtur quod aliqua pars panis aut vini convertitur in aliquam partem corporis Christi nisi per idem in quamlibet et sic in sanguinem et animam et quelibet accidencia corporis dominici absoluta; nam racione istius conversionis sunt omnia de novo ibi presencia; et hoc sufficit ad conversionem huiusmodi, ut videtur.

Since therefore the subtler [modern doctors]{g:moderni-doctores} see and assert that nothing compels [one] to believe this except the aforesaid decretal,[^4] and this would not of itself give faith to the faithful, it seems that in this the foundation of this faith fails. For the [legists]{g:legiste}, founding so solemnly a new feast, with baptism set aside, must inculcate something new and wonderful.[^5] Hence all the legists and their authors do not know how to [define]{g:describere} transubstantiation, and how it is distinguished from [annihilation]{g:anichilacio}, with the annexed difficulties in which the church has since been entangled. It is granted, nevertheless, that those accidents which some call bread and wine are to be honoured as a [sign]{g:signum} of the body of Christ and of [his] blood. But as much as the fathers of the old law took care not to worship images as God, as is clear in the letter of Jeremiah sent to Babylon by Baruch, so much ought a Christian to take care not to worship that which the [moderns]{g:moderni} call an accident and the prior church [63] called bread and wine as the true body and blood of Jesus Christ; because, just as those images could not help themselves or their worshippers against enemies, robbers or birds, as Jeremiah teaches, Baruch, the last [chapter],[^6] so [in truth]{g:revera} neither [can] the said accident. Therefore, that being set aside, its [thing signified]{g:signatum}, which is the body of Christ and the blood, indeed the whole [humanity]{g:humanitas} of Christ, is to be worshipped as far as we [univocally]{g:univoce} suffice;[^7] and although that sign has greater efficacy than the signs of the old law and our images (as will be clear later), yet it would be [exceedingly]{g:nimis} great [unbelief]{g:infidelitas} to believe that thing, so abject in nature, to be God; indeed, one who sacrifices so commits, unbelievingly, an abomination and contempt toward God, like other unbelieving idolaters; and that ought to be preached to lay people, lest by foolish [piety]{g:pietas} they be cast headlong, unbelievingly, into idolatry. Nor does it seem that any part of the bread or wine is [converted]{g:conversio} into any part of the body of Christ unless, by the same token, into every [part], and so into the blood and the soul and all the [absolute accidents]{g:accidens-absolutum} of the Lord's body; for by reason of that conversion they are all newly present there; and this suffices for a conversion of this kind, as it seems.[^8]

## ¶3 · 63.23–63.29

> [p. 63] Secundo principaliter arguitur de racione accidentis: nam omne accidens formaliter inherens substancie non est nisi veritas que est substanciam esse accidentaliter alicuius modi, ut hic supponitur, sed nulla talis veritas potest esse sine substancia, sicut nulla creatura potest esse sine Deo, ergo non est cumulus talium accidencium sine subiecto que sit hostia consecrata.

Secondly, [as a main head]{g:principaliter}, it is argued from the account of accident:[^9] for every accident formally inhering in a [substance]{g:substancia} is nothing but a truth, which is "the substance is accidentally of some mode," as is supposed here; now no such truth can be without substance, as no [creature]{g:creatura} can be without God; therefore there is no heap of such [accidents without a subject]{g:accidens-sine-subiecto} that is the consecrated [host]{g:hostia}.

## ¶4 · 64.1–64.11

> [p. 64] Item, omne accidens predicamentale est univoce accidens cum quolibet, sed aliquod accidens, ut omnis respectus in aliquo septem generum, non potest esse nisi informet subiectum suum, ergo nec aliquod; assumptum videtur ex hoc quod, sicut racio unica essendi sit et accidendi, quia aliter non cognosceretur a nobis analoga descripcio vel distinccio accidentis (nam substancia potest inesse et alias separari), ideo videtur quod analoga racio accidentis sit inesse substancie formaliter, non constituendo ipsam quid nec habendo potenciam per se esse.

Again, every accident of the [categories]{g:predicamentum} is univocally an accident with any other; now some accident, as every [relation]{g:respectus} in any of the seven genera, cannot be unless it informs its [subject]{g:subiectum}; therefore neither [can] any.[^10] The [assumption]{g:assumptum} seems [clear] from this: that, just as there is a single account of being and of being-accidental (because otherwise the analogous [definition]{g:descripcio} or distinction of accident would not be known by us; for a substance can be in [something] and otherwise be separated),[^11] so it seems that the analogous account of accident is to be in substance formally, not constituting it as a what, nor having a [potency]{g:potencia}[^12] to exist [of itself]{g:per-se}.

## ¶5 · 64.12–64.29

> [p. 64] Item, ponendo quantitatem et qualitatem linitas substancie adhuc oportet ponere informacionem earum qua immediate et formaliter substancia sit quanta et qualis; et illa non potest per se esse, ne sit processus in infinitum, ergo per idem nec aliquod accidens absolutum; assumptum patet eo quod informacio istorum accidencium potest suspendi ipsis manentibus (ut inquiunt), et per consequens oportet dare informacionem que sit vinculum quo substancia et tale accidens colligantur, quia aliter indubie non probabitur quantitas vel qualitas distincta a substancia, sed sufficerent partes quantitative et quidditas substancie; per hoc ergo quod substancia esset non tanta nec talis, alias autem est contrarie se habens, concludimus utrumque accidens. Et consimili argumento oportet dare informacionem distinctam que sit quantitas et qualitas, non autem ficta accidencia sic linita.

Again, positing [quantity]{g:quantitas} and [quality]{g:qualitas} [smeared]{g:linire} on the substance,[^13] one must still posit their [informing]{g:informacio}, by which [without intermediary]{g:immediate} and formally the substance is [quantified]{g:quantus} and qualified; and that cannot exist of itself, lest there be a process to infinity; therefore by the same token neither [can] any absolute accident. The assumption is clear from this, that the informing of these accidents can be [suspended]{g:suspendere} while they remain (as they say), and consequently one must posit an informing that is the bond by which the substance and such an accident are tied together; because otherwise, without doubt, quantity or quality distinct from substance will not be proved, but the [quantitative parts]{g:pars-quantitativa} and the quiddity of the substance would suffice. From this, therefore, that a substance would be not so great nor such, and at another time is disposed contrariwise, we [infer]{g:concludere} each accident. And by a like argument one must posit a distinct informing which is quantity and quality, and not feigned accidents smeared on in this way.

## ¶6 · 64.30–65.17

> [p. 64] Item, cum illa accidencia non superfluunt sed subiacent tantum divine potencie, poterunt coextendi [p. 65] punctus, linea, superficies; et multo magis forma substancialis poterit per se esse; ex quo generantur mille difficultates, ut puta quod tam quantitas quam qualitas componitur ex suis partibus intensivis, quia coextensis illis et continuatis ad omnem punctum non restat quomodo componerentur, nisi intensive fuit integrum; et de quantitate ac tempore, de materia et forma suasum est alibi multipliciter quod obviant veritati. Videtur eciam contra adversarios quod continuum componitur ex suis partibus indivisibilibus, cum Deo auferente a celo extimam superficiem sine pluri foret residuum mundi finitum et habens superficiem extimam eadem racione qua prius, sicut Deus posset linire unum tale cum alio et continuare partes ad unum punctum; quibus compositis forent duo indivisibilia inmediata in eodem continuo.

Again, since those accidents do not overflow but lie subject only to the divine power,[^14] point, line [and] surface could be [65] coextended; and much more could [substantial form]{g:forma-substancialis} exist of itself. From this a thousand difficulties are generated: for instance, that quantity as well as quality is composed of its intensive parts, because, these being coextended and [made continuous]{g:continuare}[^15] at every point, there remains no way in which they would be composed, unless it was intensively whole; and about quantity and time, about matter and form, it has been urged elsewhere in many ways that they go against the truth. It seems also, against the adversaries, that the continuum is composed of its indivisible parts, since, if God took away from the heaven its outermost surface and nothing more, the rest of the world would be finite and would have an outermost surface by the same reasoning as before; just as God could smear one such [world] together with another and make the parts continuous at one point; and these being so put together, there would be two indivisibles without intermediary in the same continuum.[^16]

## ¶7 · 65.18–65.28

> [p. 65] Item, cum quantitas in hostia consecrata sit alba, rotunda et aliter diversimode qualificata, oportet quod illa sit subdita qualitati (ut dicit Thomas), primo quia nulla qualitas est alba vel sic qualificata, secundo quia est prioritas naturalis quantitatis ad qualitatem et ad alia accidencia que sequuntur, et tercio quia illa hostia potest alterari, ut patet ad sensum secundum valde disparem qualitatem; oportet ergo subiectum talis alteracionis esse aliud ab huius qualitate, quia secundum nullam partem potest ipsa taliter transmutari.

Again, since the quantity in the consecrated host is white, round and otherwise diversely qualified, it must be that it is set under quality (as Thomas says):[^17] first, because no quality is white or so qualified; secondly, because there is a natural [priority]{g:prioritas} of quantity to quality and to the other accidents that follow; and thirdly, because that host can be altered, as is clear to sense, according to a very disparate quality; therefore the subject of such alteration must be other than the quality of this, because according to no part can the quality itself be so transmuted.[^18]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 61.14: Neither reason nor revelation teaches of such transsubstantiation.
- 62.11: We must be on our guard against idolatry.
- 63.4: To worship the elements is like worshipping an image.
- 63.23: An accident is only the truth that a substance is of such a sort. No collation of such accidents can be the host.
- 64.1: The accident is formally in the subject, but cannot give it quiddity nor exist by itself.
- 64.12: Quality and quantity are only accidents in forming a subject.
- 65.18: Quantity, according to St. Thomas, is the subject of qualities, which may vary in it from time to time.

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** Augustine, *De doctrina christiana*, cited by Wyclif as book III "at the end"; Loserth: Opp. III/1, 63, with *De baptismo contra Donatistas* (Opp. IX, 98) and Wyclif's *Trialogus* p. 240 (LEDGER K03c-1; the passage and book to be checked, LEDGER K03c-2).

[^2]: **S/T.** The decretal *Firmiter* of the Fourth Lateran Council, *X* 1.1.1 (Loserth: Decret. Greg. IX lib. I tit. I cap. I; the modern form is ours, LEDGER K03c-3). The synod with a hundred and thirteen bishops is that of *Ego Berengarius*, *De cons.* D. 2 c. 42 (Loserth's "Cf.", LEDGER K03c-4; the number of bishops to be checked, LEDGER K03c-5). *Capitulo Firmiter* is C's reading in rasura; the manuscripts otherwise have *ergo firmiter* (62.4). ABC read *concurrunt* (62.5).

[^3]: **C.** *Eis … illis*: "these" are the authors of the last edition of *Firmiter*, "those" the pope and bishops of the synod.

[^4]: **T.** *Cogit* is C's reading in rasura, which Loserth prints; the manuscripts have *credit* (62.12).

[^5]: **C.** *Novum festum*: the Latin names no feast. *Baptismo dimisso*, "with baptism set aside", is translated as printed; its point (a feast newly founded while an older sacrament is passed over) is our construal.

[^6]: **S.** Baruch 6, the letter of Jeremiah (6:1 sends it to Babylon); the images' helplessness against robbers and thieves is 6:56, the birds 6:21. Not quoted. Loserth cites Baruch VI, 1 and VI, 56. The chapter is "the last" of Baruch in the Vulgate.

[^7]: **T/C.** *Quantum univoce sufficimus*: printed so; C in rasura and D read *unite* (63.7), and D also *totaliter*. We translate the print, which is hard to construe: perhaps "as far as we are able in one and the same sense [of worship]". See Latin doubts.

[^8]: **C.** The argument: if the conversion makes any part of the bread pass into any part of the body, it must make it pass into every part, the soul and the accidents included, since all of these alike become newly present there.

[^9]: **C.** *De racione accidentis*: *racio* as the account of what an accident is, as in *racio speciei*; not the row "aspect", which is *racio* set against *substancia*. The proposition the truth consists in is given as quoted.

[^10]: **C.** *Univoce accidens cum quolibet*: every categorial accident is an accident in the same sense as every other; so what holds of the relations (that they cannot be without informing a subject) holds of all. ABCD read *aliquod argumentum* at 64.4–5.

[^11]: **C.** *Nam substancia potest inesse et alias separari*: printed so. As printed it says of substance what is usually said of an accident that can be present or absent; we translate the print and flag it (Latin doubts). D reads *una racio unica*, C *univoca*, ABE *unica racio unica* (64.5–6).

[^12]: **C.** *Potencia* is "potency" here, the capacity to exist of oneself, within the Aristotelian analysis; not "power", as of God's power at 64.31.

[^13]: **C.** *Linitas*, past participle of *linire*, "smear, coat": quantity and quality spread on the substance like a coating. The same verb returns at 64.29 (*linita*) and 65.13 (*linire unum tale cum alio*). C has *unitas* in the margin by another hand, D *trinitas* (64.12).

[^14]: **C.** *Non superfluunt sed subiacent tantum divine potencie*: we take *superfluere* literally, "overflow", that is, go beyond the reach of God's power; the alternative "are not superfluous" gives weaker sense with *sed subiacent*.

[^15]: **G.** *Continuare*: make continuous, join into one continuum; not "carry on".

[^16]: **C.** The thought experiment: God removes the outer surface of the heaven, so the remainder has a new outermost surface; two such worlds joined at one point would give two indivisibles with nothing between, which the adversaries' account of the continuum excludes. *Sicut* is loose here ("just as", "and so").

[^17]: **S/C.** Thomas: the quantity of the host as subject of the other accidents (*ST* III q. 77 a. 2, from memory, to be checked, LEDGER K03c-6). *Subdita qualitati* "set under quality": underlying it as its subject, as the side-note reads it.

[^18]: **C.** *Ipsa*: the quality. A quality is not altered part by part, so what is altered must be other than the quality, namely the quantity.
