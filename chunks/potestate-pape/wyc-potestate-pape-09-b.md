# *De potestate pape*, chapter 9, part 2: the poison of endowment; presbyter and bishop once the same

## ¶1 · 197.29–200.12

> [p. 197] Maior patet ex hoc, quod ritus humani servantur ut evangelium, igitur longe evidencius ritus Dei. Non enim licuit sine gravi offensa filiis Ysrahel petere sibi regem, sed rege dato eis a Domino propter offensam graviter sunt puniti, ut patet I. Reg. VIII et infra; multo magis christiani sine licencia Domini, qui contra evangelium sibi constituunt plus quam regem quem papam nominant. Unde decrementum ecclesie; quod [p. 198] continue plus invaluit, et cronice istius materie confirmant eandem sentenciam: Narrat enim Cestrensis libro 86 cap. quomodo ex tunc ecclesia Romana quanto plus cepit ditari, facta est magis instabilis, adepta plus sollicitudinis et subieccionis, quam spiritualia devocionis, plus exterioris assecuta pompositatis quam interioris ut creditur felicitatis. Proinde et hostis antiquus facta per Constantinum hac publica largicione ecclesiis legitur publice in aere proclamasse hodie infusum est venenum in ecclesia Unde Jeronimus scripsit in vitis patrum, ecclesia ex quo crevit in possessionibus: decrevit in virtutibus. Nec moveat, quod videtur dyabolum patrem mendacii sic clamasse, quia certum est quod oportet demones, qui letantur cum male fecerint, ministrare Deo et esse nuncios veritatis; et decet Deum per mamonam preconizare plebi periculum. Alia autem pericula ex hoc, quod sequuntur post correpcionem ecclesie; scimus stultissime applicare ad subtraccionem voluptatum ecclesie, sed vindictas prevaricacionem sequentes quas est evidens ex scriptura et sanctis doctoribus ex hinc sequi abnuimus inde sequi. Ex hinc enim oportet debilitari ecclesiam atque imperium, ut prophetatum est Daniel II. Ex hinc eciam debilitati sunt episcopi in virtute et populus consequenter, nam, ut patet ex cronicis predictis, tunc debilitati sunt Romani episcopi in tantum; quod instar magorum Pharaonis, qui in tercio signo defecerant, magnus hereticus fuit papa tercius post Silvestrum. Narrat [p. 199] enim Cestrensis libro suo IV quomodo post Silvestrum successit Marcus, cui successit Julius, qui passus est exilium decennale, et post hunc successit Liberius, de quo cap. 91 lib. IV Cestrensis dicit, quod iussu Constantini exulat tribus annis, quia Arrianis favere noluit. De cuius consilio clerus Romanus ordinavit interim Felicem papam. Qui celebrato consilio duos presbiteros Arrianos condempnans eiecit. Hoc audito Liberius ab exilio revocatur, qui exilii diuturnitate devictus et papatus resuscepcione exhilaratus, ad hereticam pravitatem consensit et Felice deiecto ecclesiam Petri et Pauli et Laurencii tenuit violenter, ita ut clerici et sacerdotes Felici faventes Liberio non prohibente in ecclesia cruciarentur, Felix vero martyrizatur. Iste autem maledictus Liberius non foret plus de papatu exilaratus et cadens in heresim pocius quam pape ante dotacionem nisi propter innaturalem papatus pinguedinem. Et patet probabiliter maior secundi argumenti. Minor autem ex hoc evidet, quod ante dotacionem ecclesie non fuerunt nomina cleri taliter baptizata et per consequens nec dignitatum officia. Idem enim fuit ante dotacionem ecclesie presbiter, episcopus et sacerdos, ut dominus Ardmacanus ostendit libro XI De Questionibus Armenorum V cap. ex scriptura multiplici, primo ex illo ad Tytum I, 5, 6, Reliqui te Crete, ut ea que desunt corrigas, et constituas per civitates presbiteros, si quis sine crimine est, unius uxoris vir, filios habens fideles, non in accusacione luxurie aut non subditos. Et subdit causam condicionis presbiteri. Oportet, [p. 200] inquit, episcopum sine crimine esse, sicut Dei dispensatorem. Ecce quod eundem quem prius vocat presbiterum, id est seniorem, vocat secundo episcopum; id est superintendentem. Item, Act. XX. 27— 28 legitur, quod Apostolus vocans ad se maiores natu ecclesie Ephesiorum, dicit eis: Non subterfugi quominus nunciarem consilium Dei vobis. Attendite vobis et universo gregi, in quo Spiritus Sanctus posuit vos episcopos regere ecclesiam Dei, quam aquisivit sanguine suo. Ecce quod omnes maiores natu id est seniores vel presbiteros vocat episcopos.

The major is clear from this, that human [rites]{g:ritus} are kept as the gospel; therefore far more evidently God's rites. For it was not lawful, without grave offence, for the children of Israel to ask a king for themselves; but when the king had been given them by the Lord they were gravely punished for the offence, as is clear from 1 Samuel 8 and following; much more Christians, without the Lord's [licence]{g:licencia}, who against the gospel appoint for themselves more than a king, whom they name [pope]{g:papa}.[^1] Hence the decline of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, which [198] has continually grown stronger; and the chronicles of this matter confirm the same [teaching]{g:sentencia}. For the Chester [chronicler] relates in book 86, chap., how "from then on the Roman church, the more it began to be [enriched]{g:ditare}, the more unstable it became, having gained more of anxiety and subjection than of the spiritual things of devotion, having attained more of outward pomp than, as is believed, of inward happiness. Accordingly the ancient enemy too, when this public largess had been made to the churches through Constantine, is read to have proclaimed publicly in the air: 'Today poison has been poured into the church.' Hence Jerome wrote in the *Lives of the Fathers*: the church, since it grew in possessions, has decreased in virtues."[^2] Nor let it trouble [anyone] that the devil, the father of lies, seems to have cried out so; for it is certain that the demons, who rejoice when they have done evil, must minister to God and be messengers of the truth; and it befits God to herald the danger to the people through mammon.[^3] And other dangers, because they follow after the correction of the church, we know how, most foolishly, to put down to the withdrawal of the church's pleasures; but the vengeances that follow [transgression]{g:prevaricacio}, which it is evident from scripture and the holy doctors follow from this, we deny follow from that.[^4] For from this the church and the empire must be weakened, as was prophesied, Daniel 2.[^5] From this, too, the bishops were weakened in [virtue]{g:virtus}, and the people consequently; for, as is clear from the aforesaid chronicles, the [Roman bishops]{g:romanus-episcopus} were then weakened so far that, like Pharaoh's magicians, who had failed at the third sign, the third pope after Silvester was a great heretic.[^6] For [199] the Chester [chronicler] relates in his book 4 how "after Silvester Mark succeeded, and Julius succeeded him, who suffered an exile of ten years, and after him Liberius succeeded", of whom the Chester [chronicler] says in chapter 91 of book 4 that by Constantine's command "he is exiled for three years, because he would not favour the Arians. On his counsel the Roman clergy meanwhile ordained Felix pope. He, having held a council, condemned and cast out two Arian [presbyters]{g:presbiter}. When this was heard, Liberius is recalled from exile; overcome by the length of his exile and elated by the resumption of the papacy, he consented to [heretical depravity]{g:heretica-pravitas}, and, Felix having been thrown out, held the church of Peter and Paul and Laurence by violence, so that clerics and [priests]{g:sacerdos} who favoured Felix were tortured in the church, Liberius not forbidding it; and Felix is martyred."[^7] And this accursed Liberius would not have been more elated over the papacy, and fallen into heresy, rather than the popes before the [endowment]{g:dotacio}, except for the unnatural fatness of the papacy. And so the major of the second argument is clear [by probable argument]{g:probabiliter}.[^8] And the minor is evident from this, that before the endowment of the church the names of the [clergy]{g:clerus} had not been baptized in this way, and consequently neither had the offices of the dignities.[^9] For before the endowment of the church presbyter, [bishop]{g:episcopus} and priest were the same, as [the lord of Armagh]{g:dominus-ardmacanus} shows in book 11 of the *Questions of the Armenians*, chapter 5, from manifold scripture:[^10] first from that of Titus 1:5–6: "I left thee in Crete, that thou shouldest correct the things that are wanting, and shouldest ordain presbyters in every city: if any be without crime, the husband of one wife, having faithful children, not accused of riot or unruly." And he adds the reason for the condition of the presbyter: "A bishop," [200] he says, "must be without crime, as the steward of God."[^11] Behold, the same man whom he first calls presbyter, that is elder, he calls in second place bishop, that is [overseer]{g:superintendencia}. Again, in Acts 20:27–28 we read that the Apostle, calling to him the ancients of the church of the Ephesians, says to them: "I have not spared to declare unto you the counsel of God. Take heed to yourselves and to the whole flock, wherein the Holy Ghost hath placed you bishops, to rule the church of God, which he hath purchased with his own blood." Behold, he calls all the ancients, that is elders or presbyters, bishops.[^12]

## ¶2 · 200.13–200.29

> [p. 200] Item, eidem consonat dictum Apostoli prima Thym. Ill, 1: Si quis episcopatum desiderat, bonum opus desiderat, quasi diceret episcopatus non dicit supra presbiteratum nisi opus superintendencie, unde V cap. specificat dicens: Qui bene presunt presbiteri, duplici honore digni habeantur, maxime quo laborant in verbo et doctrina Dei. Et sequitur: Adversus presbiterum accusacionem noli recipere nisi sub duobus vel tribus testibus. Presbiteros dicit episcopos; quia aliter non diceret IV cap. 14, quod gracia data est sibi cum imposicione manuum presbiteri. Cum igitur ad solos episcopos pertinet manus imponere, ut patet Act. VIII, patet quod Apostolus exprimit indifferenter sacerdotes nomine episcopi et nomine presbiteri; tunc enim non erat coactiva iurisdiccio vel temporalium dotacio que faceret diversitatem.

Again, the saying of the Apostle in 1 Timothy 3:1 agrees with this: "If a man desire the office of a bishop, he desireth good work"; as if he were to say, the episcopate does not signify, beyond the presbyterate, anything but the work of oversight; hence in chapter 5 he specifies, saying: "Let the presbyters that rule well be esteemed worthy of double honour: especially they who labour in the word and [doctrine]{g:doctrina} of God." And there follows: "Against a presbyter receive not an accusation, but under two or three witnesses."[^13] By presbyters he means bishops; for otherwise he would not say in chapter 4:14 that grace was given him "with imposition of the hands of the presbyter".[^14] Since, therefore, it belongs to bishops alone to lay on hands, as is clear from Acts 8, it is clear that the Apostle expresses priests indifferently by the name of bishop and by the name of presbyter; for then there was no [coactive]{g:coactivus} [jurisdiction]{g:iurisdiccio} or endowment of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} that would make a difference.[^15]

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Maior patet*: the major of the second principal argument (197.21), that the church may not depart from the apostolic observances. **S.** 1 Sam. 8 (1 Kings in the Vulgate): at 8:7 the Lord tells Samuel, "they have not rejected thee, but me, that I should not reign over them".
[^2]: **S.** Ranulf Higden, monk of Chester (*Cestrensis*), *Polychronicon*: Loserth gives the Rolls edition, V, 130, where Higden has the passage almost word for word, with the devil's cry and the saying of Jerome (book 4, ch. 26) (LEDGER P09b-1). **T.** 198.3: the print reads *libro 86 cap.*, with no chapter number (so the plate); Loserth: "recte: libro IV, cap. XXVI". Translated as printed. At 198.5 Higden reads *plus inde saecularis adepta sollicitudinis*: the codices' *temporalia* may hide an adjective (*temporalis*), and Loserth's *plus* is his supplement. 198.5: the manuscripts read *adepta temporalia sollicitudinis*; Loserth corrects to *adepta plus sollicitudinis*, translated; at 198.6 A reads *contra spiritualia*. **R.** A and B in the margin (198.2): *Ecclesia Romana a tempore dotacionis facta est instabilis*.
[^3]: **S.** The saying ascribed to Jerome is the gist of *Vita Malchi* 1 (PL 23, 53): *postquam ad Christianos principes venerit, potentia quidem et divitiis major, sed virtutibus minor facta sit*. The *Vita Malchi* circulated among the *Lives of the Fathers*, and Wyclif takes both the saying and the title from Higden (Rolls V, 130: *Jeronimus scripsit in vitis patrum: Ecclesia ex quo crevit possessionibus decrevit virtutibus*) (LEDGER P09b-2). **R.** A and B in the margin (198.12): *Ecclesia crescens in possessionibus decrescit in virtutibus*. **C.** *Per mamonam*: through mammon, i.e. the devil speaking as the voice of riches; *preconizare*, "herald" (cf. *preco*).
[^4]: **C.** Construed: men foolishly blame the dangers that follow a correction of the church (the taking away of its endowment) on the loss of its pleasures, but deny that the punishments following the transgression come from the endowment. *Ex hinc ... inde* both point to the endowment. **T.** 198.19–20: Loserth queries *in corrupcionem* for *correpcionem* ("the dangers that follow upon the church's corruption"); the print is translated.
[^5]: **S.** Dan. 2:42: "the kingdom shall be partly strong, and partly broken".
[^6]: **S.** Exod. 8:18: the magicians "practised in like manner, to bring forth sciniphs, and they could not", the third plague. **T.** 198.29: the print's *magcrum* is a broken *magorum* (so the plate), translated "magicians".
[^7]: **S.** Higden, *Polychronicon* IV: Loserth gives the Rolls edition, V, 146, 148, 158, and says the chapter is now 27. The Liberius entry is on p. 158 (*Hic tribus annis exulat quia noluit favere Arianis*), in book 4, ch. 27 (LEDGER P09b-3). **T.** 199.4: the print reads *cap. 91* (so the plate). 199.5: *Constantini* as printed; Liberius was exiled under Constantius II; Higden himself goes on *Constantius imperator Liberium … de exsilio revocavit* (Rolls V, 160), and two of his manuscripts read *jussu Constantii* at the exile (LEDGER P09b-4). **C.** *Celebrato consilio*: *consilium* for *concilium*, "council". **R.** A and B in the margin (199.2): *Depositio pape heretici*.
[^8]: **C.** *Pinguedinem*: the fatness (wealth) of the papacy after the endowment. *Patet probabiliter*: the major is shown as probable, not demonstrated.
[^9]: **C.** *Baptizata*: figurative, the titles of rank had not yet been "christened" in this way. *Minor autem*: the minor of the second principal argument (197.24), that before the endowment the priests ruled as companions.
[^10]: **S.** FitzRalph, *Summa in quaestionibus Armenorum* XI.5 (Paris 1512, fol. 84r–v): *nec in scripturis euangelicis aut apostolicis invenitur aliqua distinctio inter episcopos et sacerdotes simplices qui presbyteri appellantur*. He proves it from Titus 1:5–7, Acts 20:17–28, 1 Tim. 3 and 5, then Gal. 1, 1 Cor. 12 and Eph. 4, the order Wyclif follows here and on p. 200. Loserth gives no reference but compares *Trialogus* p. 276 (LEDGER P09b-5). **R.** A and B in the margin (199.26): *Episcopus presbiter et sacerdos olim fuit idem*.
[^11]: **S.** Titus 1:5–7. Wyclif omits the Vulgate's opening *Hujus rei gratia* ("For this cause"), *sicut et ego disposui tibi* ("as I also appointed thee"), and the *enim* of v. 7. DR has "set in order" for *corrigas* and "priests" for *presbyteros*: here "presbyters", since the argument turns on the word; DR's "steward" is kept for *dispensatorem*. **T.** 199.29: B lacks *per civitates*.
[^12]: **S.** Acts 20:17 (*maiores natu*, DR "the ancients"), 20:27–28; in v. 27 Wyclif omits *enim* and *omne*, "all". **C.** *Seniorem*: "elder", the sense of *presbyter*; *superintendentem*: "overseer", the sense of *episcopus*.
[^13]: **S.** 1 Tim. 3:1, without the Vulgate's *Fidelis sermo* ("A faithful saying"); 5:17, where Wyclif adds *Dei* and reads *quo* for *qui*; 5:19, *vel* for *aut*. DR has "priests" for *presbyteri*.
[^14]: **S.** 1 Tim. 4:14: the Vulgate has *presbyterii*, DR "the priesthood"; Wyclif's *presbiteri* is translated as he reads it, a singular. **C.** The point: only bishops lay on hands (next sentence), so the "presbyter" who laid hands on Timothy was a bishop.
[^15]: **S.** Acts 8:14–18: Peter and John lay on hands at Samaria. **T.** 200.24: the manuscripts lack *ad* (*ad solos episcopos*), supplied by Loserth; 200.28: B reads *erant* for *erat*.
