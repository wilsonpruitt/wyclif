# *De potestate pape*, chapter 12, part 12: the minister's stipend and the people's withholding of tithes

## ¶1 · 356.27–358.3

> [p. 356] Hic conceditur totum, cum utrobique veritas confuse tegitur; nam Christo et apostolis debebantur de lege Dei stipendia officio spiritualis ministerii necessaria, et lex Dei atque suum iudicium plene excuciunt istorum iniurias, quia faciat sacerdos spirituale officium sibi debitum; et incompossibile est quod desint sibi vite necessaria ad dampnum anime. Patet de prophetis legis veteris, de Christo et suis apostolis et de [p. 357] vitis patrum ut sanctis monachis. Si autem talis patitur penam vel defectum corporis, ut sepe contigit de Christo et suis apostolis, hoc servata paciencia redundat in lucrum anime, ideo Christus non tradidit legem in talibus reluctandi, sed humiliter iniurias paciendi, ut patet Luce IX: sed infideliter istam legem Christi contempnimus. Cum igitur discolis non desunt hodie temporalia, quanto magis Deus non deficiet in necessariis servo suo. Sed oportet in isto confuso involucro quod christianus caveat de triplici periculo, primo quod clericus Christi non ocietur accipiendo avide stipendium sine ministerio; hoc enim secundum legem Dei et hominum est iniustum; nec ad illud extinguendum ordinatur aliquod suffragium. Secundo quod in mensura et non in superhabundancia accipiatur de fimo temporalium, iuxta doctrinam Apostoli prima Thym. VI. 8 habentes, inquit, alimenta et quibus tegamur hiis contenti simus. Unde beatus Crysostomus super primo cap. ad Hebreos Hom. II dicit, quod christianus debet sapere in penuria, non in diviciis, quoniam qui in paucis potest vivere, eo qui non potest multo maior et melior est. Ut si vocatorum ad regalem civitatem hii nec subiugalibus egent nec famulis nec palliis nec deversoriis nec calligis nec vasis, sed sufficiat eis panem habere et ex fontibus aquam accipere; illi vero dicunt nisi vehicula, stramenta mollia, numerosam familiam habeamus, non possumus, et adhuc remissis brevibus et interpolatis passibus, civitatem attingere. Si insuper dum civitatem attigerint, sint secundum celeritatem et expedicionem ad civitatem [p. 358] cum ipsam intraverint premiandi, quis dubitat, quin primi excedent secundos? Correspondenter est de viantibus ad patriam senciendum.

Here the whole is conceded, since on both sides the truth is covered [indistinctly]{g:confuse};[^1] for to Christ and the apostles were owed, by the law of God, the stipends necessary to the office of spiritual ministry, and God's law and his judgment fully shake off the wrongs of those [men], provided the [priest]{g:sacerdos} does the spiritual office owed by him; and it is [incompossible]{g:incompossibilis} that the necessaries of life should be lacking to him to the harm of his soul. It is clear from the prophets of the old law, from Christ and his apostles, and from the [357] lives of the fathers, as [those of] holy monks.[^2] And if such a one suffers pain or want of body, as often happened with Christ and his apostles, this, patience being kept, redounds to the gain of the soul; therefore Christ did not give a law of struggling back in such things but of humbly suffering wrongs, as is clear in Luke 9; but we unfaithfully despise this law of Christ.[^3] Since, then, [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} are not lacking today to the [froward]{g:discoli}, how much more will God not fail his servant in necessaries. But in this indistinct wrapping a Christian must beware of a threefold danger: first, that the [cleric]{g:clericus} of Christ be not idle, greedily accepting a stipend without ministry; for this is unjust according to the law of God and of men; nor is any [aid]{g:suffragium} ordained for extinguishing it.[^4] Second, that he take of the dung of temporal goods in measure and not in superabundance, according to the [doctrine]{g:doctrina} of the Apostle, 1 Timothy 6:8: "Having," he says, "food and wherewith to be covered, with these we are content."[^5] Hence blessed Chrysostom, on the first chapter to the Hebrews, Homily 2, says that "a Christian ought to be wise in penury, not in riches, since he who can live on little is much greater and better than he who cannot."[^6] As if, of those called to the royal city, these need neither beasts of burden nor servants nor cloaks nor inns nor boots nor vessels, but it suffices them to have bread and to take water from the springs; but those say: "Unless we have carriages, soft bedding and a numerous [household]{g:familia}, we cannot, even with slack, short and halting steps, reach the city." If, moreover, when they have reached the city, they are to be rewarded according to their speed and dispatch to the city [358] when they have entered it, who doubts that the first will surpass the second? Correspondingly one must judge of [those on the road]{g:viantes} to the [homeland]{g:patria}.[^7]

## ¶2 · 358.4–358.24

> [p. 358] Sed caveatur tercio ne ad querendum nobis bona superflua et itineris ad patriam tardativa menciamur blasfeme de scriptura, dicentes quod de lege Domini debemus anathematizare strictissime pro temporalium negacione, et sic sollicitemur sollicite exequi tradiciones ad hoc conditas, quia hoc nimis ex Antichristi dogmate tardaret a patria, patet destruccio huius heresis Luce IX et per legem pape Gregorii, XXIII, questione IV, Inter querelas, ubi docetur non sic pro temporalibus vindicandum. Sed tercio obicitur per hoc quod iuxta hanc viam nedum detinerentur decime et proventus ecclesie a cardinalibus et alienigenis indignis nec solum ab ecclesiis appropriatis religiosis subtrahentibus debitum rectoris officium, sed ab assiduis rectoribus vigilanter residentibus, cum aperta sit via laycis ad iudicandum de ministerio sui prepositi et subtrahendum de stipendio secundum suum iudicium diminuerentur proventus ecclesie ad non quantum.

But, third, let care be taken lest, to seek for ourselves superfluous goods that slow the journey to the homeland, we blasphemously lie about scripture, saying that by the law of the Lord we ought to anathematize most strictly for the denial of temporal goods, and so be anxiously solicited to carry out the traditions made for this purpose; because this, out of Antichrist's dogma, would hold [us] back too much from the homeland. The destruction of this heresy is clear from Luke 9 and by the law of Pope Gregory, [Causa] 23, question 4, *Inter querelas*, where it is taught that one is not thus to take vengeance for temporal things.[^8] But, third, [it is objected, by appeal to]{g:obicitur-per} this: that on this way the [tithes]{g:decime} and proceeds of the [church]{g:ecclesia} would be withheld not only from cardinals and unworthy foreigners, nor only from churches [appropriated]{g:appropriare} to the [religious]{g:religiosi}, who withdraw the office owed by a rector, but from assiduous rectors vigilantly resident; since the way is open to laymen to judge of the ministry of their [superior]{g:prepositi} and to withdraw from his stipend according to their own judgment, the proceeds of the church would be diminished to nothing.[^9]

## ¶3 · 358.25–359.15

> [p. 358] Quoad istud argumentum videtur michi quod primo deficientibus satrapis in casibus duobus prioribus, et secundo deficiente rege ac secularibus dominis, qui ex officio prohiberent ne decime regni et bona pauperum taliter ad discolos devolvantur; populus debet decimas et oblaciones suas ab eis concorditer et constanter subtrahere et in alios pios usus expendere, cum predicti discoli Antichristi discipuli non sunt illi, quibus [p. 359] decime et obligaciones sunt debite. Ideo omnes tradiciones ecclesie malignancium non possunt fundare quod propter istam decenciam populus per censuras ecclesiasticas ad sui dispendium est terrendus, quin pocius propter omissionem negligentem defensionis legum ecclesie et consensum vecordem tradicionibus sathane est a Christo nisi resipiscendo peniteat condempnandus. Unde regi et regno debet esse principium quod quicumque clericus vel laycus intraneus vel extraneus istam veritatem edoctus et remanens mediator vel procurator subtraccionem istam precipuorum bonorum regni procurans post monicionem legitimam tamquam proditor vel hostis regni subdolus est censendus.

As to this argument it seems to me that, first, if the [satraps]{g:satrapa} fail in the two former cases, and secondly if the king and the [secular lords]{g:seculares-domini} fail, who by their office should prevent the tithes of the kingdom and the goods of the poor from passing thus to the froward, the people ought, with one accord and steadfastly, to withdraw their tithes and offerings from them and spend them on other pious uses, since the aforesaid froward, the disciples of Antichrist, are not those to whom [359] tithes and obligations are owed.[^10] Therefore all the traditions of the church of the malignant cannot ground [the claim] that, on account of this seemly [act], the people is to be terrified by ecclesiastical [censures]{g:censura} to its own loss;[^11] but rather, for the negligent omission of the defence of the church's laws and [faint-hearted]{g:vecors} consent to the traditions of Satan, it is to be condemned by Christ, unless it comes to its senses and repents. Hence it ought to be a principle for the king and the kingdom that any cleric or layman, native or foreign, who has been taught this truth and remains a mediator or [proctor]{g:procurator} procuring this withdrawal of the chief goods of the kingdom, is, after lawful monition, to be judged as a traitor or a subtle enemy of the kingdom.[^12]

## ¶4 · 359.16–359.22

> [p. 359] Ad destruendum itaque hanc antichristianam heresim debet tota ecclesia laborare. Et idem videtur michi iudicium de appropriatis elemosinis vel prebendis, quibuscumque communitatibus vel personis extraneis vel intraneis debitum ecclesie officium notabiliter subtrahentibus, ymo de perpetuis redditibus collatis clericis, ut sepe tetigi.

To destroy this antichristian heresy, therefore, the whole church ought to labour. And the same judgment seems to me [to hold] of [alms]{g:elemosina} or prebends appropriated to whatever communities or persons, foreign or native, that notably withdraw the office owed to the church; indeed, of perpetual [revenues]{g:reditus} conferred on clerics, as I have often touched on.[^13]

## ¶5 · 359.23–360.18

> [p. 359] Et quantum ad ultimum membrum, in quo iacet difficultas, conceditur quod occasione male accepta decimantes erranter excederent. Sed in parte altera iacet incomparabiliter maius periculum ut, posito quod tollatur a clero decimacio, sicut tollebatur a Christo et suis apostolis, cum fuit legale legis veteris, cum non debuit tunc remanere nisi secundum moralitatem inclusam, adhuc viveret clerus secundum privatas elemosinas ut tempore Christi, et postmodum magis fructificans nec tot episcopis egerent, ut modo propter penuriam; et sic concesso maximo inconvenienti huius partis foret modicum in comparacione [p. 360] ad inconveniens quod insurgit ex legibus et practicis circa ecclesiasticas porciones. Quamvis autem oportet quod senex sacerdos sit sumptuosior, tamen non vidi senem talem sine pena peccati sui a Domino quoad temporale subsidium derelictum. Ille enim, qui non derelinquit in bonis maximis non potest deficere in bonis minimis. Presbiter igitur talis decrepitus debet cavere de avaricia, de omni assistencia familiari vel ministeriali superflua, et dare se oracioni, contemplacioni et omni modo quo sufficit prodesse ecclesie; et sic vite sanctitas ac ecclesie edificacio erit carta, procuratorium ac medium interpellans ad Deum et populum, propter temporale subsidium acquirendum, sic quod sacerdos moriatur clericus, deserto seculo in spe spiritualis porcionis dominice, sicut vixit corporaliter ex porcione domini temporali.

And as to the last member, in which the difficulty lies, it is conceded that, on an occasion wrongly taken, tithe-payers might err and go too far. But on the other side lies an incomparably greater danger; as, supposing that tithing were taken away from the clergy, as it was taken away from Christ and his apostles, since it was a [legal precept]{g:legalia} of the old law, since it ought not then to remain except according to the morality included [in it], the clergy would still live on private alms, as in the time of Christ, and afterwards more fruitfully; nor would they need so many bishops, as now [they do] because of penury;[^14] and so, granting the greatest [ill consequence]{g:inconveniens} on this side, it would be small in comparison [360] with the ill consequence that arises from the laws and practices concerning ecclesiastical portions.[^15] And although an old priest must be more costly, yet I have not seen such an old man, without the penalty of his own sin, forsaken by the Lord as to temporal support.[^16] For he who does not forsake [us] in the greatest goods cannot fail in the least. Such a decrepit [presbyter]{g:presbiter}, therefore, ought to beware of avarice, of all superfluous attendance of household or ministers, and give himself to prayer, contemplation and every way in which he is able to profit the church; and so holiness of life and the building up of the church will be his charter, [proctor's mandate]{g:procuratorium} and mediating means, interceding with God and the people for acquiring temporal support, so that the priest may die a cleric, having forsaken the world, in hope of the Lord's spiritual portion, as he lived bodily from the Lord's temporal portion.[^17]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 356.27: We are bound to support God's ministers.
- 357.11: The clergy should not take stipends without ministering.
- 357.18: They should not accept too much.
- 358.4: Most of all they must avoid cursing because temporals are refused.
- 358.15: It is said that if the laity judge they will deprive even the good clergy of their support.
- 358.25: All money withdrawn should be applied to pious uses.
- 359.16: Appropriations should cease where duty is not done.
- 359.25: Tithepayers might err, but there is more danger on the other side. In the worst case the clergy might be maintained like Christ and the apostles.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *confuse*: "indistinctly", held in the whole without the distinctions that follow (the three dangers). *Excuciunt*: "shake off", i.e. repel. *Quia faciat sacerdos*: the subjunctive is taken as a proviso. **R.** The manuscripts' margin at 356.27: *Predicans non deficit in cibo* ("a preacher does not go short of food"; the apparatus OCR reads *dejicit*).
[^2]: **C.** *vitis patrum*: the *Vitae patrum*, the lives of the desert monks; *ut sanctis monachis* "as [those of] holy monks".
[^3]: **S.** Luke 9: the print gives the chapter only; probably 9:3 (DR "Take nothing for your journey") and 9:55, the rebuke of the disciples who would call down fire (DR "you know not of what spirit you are").
[^4]: **C.** *nec ad illud extinguendum ordinatur aliquod suffragium*: *illud* taken as the injustice of the idle stipend; no aid of law or privilege is ordained to wipe it out.
[^5]: **S.** 1 Timothy 6:8, agreeing with the Vulgate (*simus*); Douay-Rheims. *De fimo temporalium*: "the dung of temporal goods", cf. Philippians 3:8.
[^6]: **S.** Chrysostom, *Homilies on Hebrews*, hom. 2 (on ch. 1), in the medieval Latin version: to be checked (LEDGER P12l-1). **T.** The manuscripts read *Morali II* (357.21); the print's *Hom. II* is followed. **R.** Both manuscripts' margin at 357.18: *Victus parcitas et sumptus*, where A adds *secundum beatum Crisogonum*, which Loserth marks with "!".
[^7]: **T.** *deversoriis* "inns" (classical *deversorium*; the print sets *de | versoriis*, split by the folio mark); the manuscripts read *Diversionis* (357.26). **C.** *remissis brevibus et interpolatis passibus*: "slack, short and halting steps", going with *attingere*.
[^8]: **S.** Decretum C.23 q.4 c.27, *Inter querelas* (Gregory), as Loserth gives it (his "Causa XXII" in the apparatus; modern form ours) (LEDGER P12l-2). Luke 9:55, as in note 3. **T.** At 358.11 B lacks *tardaret*. **R.** Both manuscripts' margin at 358.4: *Exigere per excommunicacionem decimas*.
[^9]: **C.** *tercio obicitur*: the third objection (the first at 354.33, the second at 356.21), naming three cases: cardinals and foreigners, appropriated churches, resident rectors. *Ad non quantum*: "to nothing". **T.** At 358.16 B reads *detineretur*.
[^10]: **C.** *in casibus duobus prioribus*: the cardinals and foreigners, and the appropriated churches. *Obligaciones* (359.1): one expects *oblaciones* "offerings", as in the line before; translated as printed.
[^11]: **S.** *ecclesie malignancium*: Psalm 25:5 (DR "the assembly of the malignant"). **C.** *istam decenciam*: "this seemly [act]", the people's withholding; the word is doubtful.
[^12]: **C.** *subtraccionem istam precipuorum bonorum regni*: here the drawing of the kingdom's chief goods away (to unworthy and foreign holders), not the people's withholding of tithes.
[^13]: **C.** *quibuscumque communitatibus vel personis*: dative with *appropriatis*, "appropriated to whatever communities or persons". *Perpetuis redditibus*: the endowments of the perpetual-alms argument.
[^14]: **C.** *ultimum membrum*: the third case, the resident rectors. *Legale legis veteris*: tithes as a legal precept of the old law, binding now only by the moral precept included in it (both manuscripts' margin at 359.29: *Decime sunt legales legis veteris*). *Nec tot episcopis egerent, ut modo propter penuriam* is obscure: taken as "nor would they need so many bishops"; the alternative reading *episcopi* would give "nor would so many bishops be in want, as now through penury". **T.** At 359.25 the manuscripts read *excedere*; the print's *excederent* is followed.
[^15]: **C.** *inconveniens*: here the ill consequence in affairs, not the absurdity of an argument.
[^16]: **S.** Cf. Psalm 36:25 (DR "I have not seen the just forsaken"). *Senex sacerdos* "old priest", and then *presbiter* "presbyter", as printed.
[^17]: **C.** *carta, procuratorium*: the documents of a suit: holiness of life will be the priest's charter and his proctor's mandate. **T.** At 360.10 A reads *instrumentali*, B *in sacramentali*; the apparatus does not name the word they stand for.
