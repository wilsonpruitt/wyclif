# *Dialogus*, chapter 32: glebe and church

## Falsehood · 73.27–74.10

> [p. 73] MENDACIUM. Sed demum recensius arguunt populares quod gleba et ecclesia adeo connectuntur quod non possunt separari ab invicem, sicut nec substancia et passio vel accidens quod philosophi vocant inseparabile. Relinquat ergo sic opinans oblaciones et decimas vel acceptet simul [p. 74] dotatas ecclesias, sicut sancti priores secundum leges ecclesie acceptarunt; nam contra istud remurmurans cum debet perfecte restituere quod injuste tenuit, debet valorem tocius glebe sue reddere dominis, quia aliter iuxta ficticium suum foret propter injuste tentum debitum condempnandus. Quare ergo non posset dici quod quelibet privata persona ecclesiastica sit procuratrix et non domina secularis, sed ipsa tota ecclesia militans mater nostra super omnibus bonis collatis ecclesie dominatur? Talia sunt multa sophismata antichristi per que conturbat multos simplices sacerdotes.

FALSEHOOD. But at last, and more freshly, the people argue that the glebe and the [church]{g:ecclesia}
are so joined that they cannot be separated from one another, just as neither can a substance and
its [attribute]{g:passio}, or the accident that philosophers call inseparable.[^1] Let him who holds
this [opinion]{g:opinio}, therefore, give up offerings and [tithes]{g:decime}, or else accept along
with them [74] [endowed]{g:dotacio} churches, as the holy men before us accepted them according to
the laws of the church; for one who murmurs against this, since he must perfectly restore what he
held unjustly, must render the value of his whole glebe to the [lords]{g:dominus}, because otherwise,
by his own fiction, he would be condemned for a debt unjustly held.[^2] Why then could it not be said
that every private ecclesiastical person is a proctor and not a secular lord, but that the whole
[church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans} itself, [our mother]{g:mater-nostra},
[exercises lordship]{g:dominari} over all the goods conferred on the church?[^3] Such are many
[sophisms]{g:sophisma} of Antichrist, by which he throws many simple [priests]{g:sacerdos} into
confusion.[^4]

## Truth · 74.11–78.9

> [p. 74] VERITAS. Sed hic dicit fidelis constanter secundum doctrinam quam Christus docuit tam opere quam sermone quod clerus omnino debet vivere vitam pauperem et exproprietariam, tanquam heres regni celestis, propinquus vite innocencie, sicut Christus. Per hoc enim, quod clerus sic vivit de parte domini, differt sensibiliter a domino seculari. Et quantum ad primum objectum patet quod vocando ecclesiam ipsam basilicam vel fideles illam incolentes in diebus festivis, sive regimen rectoris ipsum populum gubernantis, satis sensibiliter differunt ipsa ecclesia atque gleba. Nec habet antichristus potenciam sic connectendi ista adinvicem quod unum non poterit ab alio separari, cum Christus et sui apostoli gesserunt curam excellencius quam nostri prepositi, licet caruerant tali gleba. Ideo istud verbum est manifestum mendacium antichristi ubi vult glebam proponere ut subjectum perfeccius, et curam spiritualem postponere, tanquam accidens imperfectum, cum notum sit fideli, qui non est mundana cupidine philocaptus, quod tam ipsa basilica quam spiritualis cura animarum qualem habuit [p. 75] Christus cum suis notorie differt a gleba, sive intellecta fuerit temporale sic possessum sive ipsa dominacio super illo. Ideo rude verbum est et infidele quod mundani balbuciunt in hac parte, aliquid tamen pronosticat, scilicet quod spiritualis cura exemplata a domino est postposita, et secundum ordinacionem antichristi affeccio temporalium antecedit. Quoad secundum objectum dictum est quod curatus culpabiliter glebam hanc detinens debet deo contricione restituere, cum deus sit dominus capitalis; et occurrente opportunitate debet satisfacere egenis subditis quos antea defraudavit. Non tamen video quod secundum legem aliquam debet hoc restituere patrono domino seculari, cum secundum legem divinam patronatus talis non est fundabilis, et secundum legem humanam patronus talis illi curato contulit ipsam glebam. Si autem curatus recepit fructum istius glebe, respuendo dominacionem mundanam in animo, fidelis ministracio potest ipsum quoad deum et homines excusare. Hoc tamen videtur quod fidelis curatus cum opportunitatem habuerit debet renunciare isti dominio seculari. Sic enim narrat beatus possidonius de magistro suo beato augustino, quod rogavit cives iponences reaccipere predia que sibi antea donaverunt, sic quod posset vivere pure de elemosinis, ut vixerunt Christus et sui apostoli, vel de decimis et oblacionibus, ut vixerunt sacerdotes et levite veteris testamenti. Et licet cives ipsi istud renuerent, tamen verisimile est quod augustinus non fecit hic ut canis rediens ad priorem vomitum, sed quod vivens pure de elemosinis, ministrans hec predia pauperibus fuit de vita paupere elemosinaria contentatus. Ideo hec via non est recens, infundabilis in scriptura, cum in lege domini sit fundata. Unde dicant sic balbucientes, si sciverint, quam possessionem vel glebam [p. 76] habuerunt hii patroni ordinum istorum recencium, Augustinus, benedictus, dominicus, aut franciscus. Sed sicut secta Christiana deseruit pauperiem patroni sui Christi, quam hii patroni suscitasse dicuntur, licet culpabiliter et indocte, sic sequaces istorum patronorum glebam temporalium avidius acceptarunt. Et sic in patronis istis et in lege ordinacionis eterne antiqui dierum ista renunciacio est antiquata, nec solum in istis patronis et in fratribus, licet sophistice fuit continue practizata, sed multi rectores habuerunt instinctu dei ocultam sinderisim, sicut legentes utramque legem domini ex ipsa patencius vel oculcius conceperunt. Modus autem sic possidendi seculariter et quasi hereditarie temporalia est magis culpabilis quam usus eorum, et sic potest homo virtuose accipere oblaciones et decimas in mensura, dum tamen ministret fideliter ecclesie indigenti. Fideliter, dico, omnino in spiritualibus et in temporalibus dum occurret oportunitas taliter ministrandi. Nec musitet curatus ubi cubabit, vel ponet decimas, si non habuerit domum propriam quasi hereditariam, quia cubet in meridie fervoris caritatis, sequens Christum qui sic accidenter et incognite suis apostolis dormiebat, et totam suam solicitudinem proiciat, conversando in celestibus ad beatitudinem adquirendam et de mediis serviendi deo et ecclesie ac sequendi Christum. Et non sit solicitus circa oblaciones vel decimas corporales, Christus enim ex summa sapiencia ordinavit quod curati sui careant certitudinem vel hereditatem horum temporalium ut celestibus plus anelent. Sed antichristus istum divinum ordinem dimutavit. Quantum ad tercium objectum patet quod militans ecclesia quoad 2ᵐ partem et membra ejus singula dominatur, et sic tota ecclesia secundum illam partem et hinc est imperfecta a celestibus, et statu innocencie [p. 77] elongata. Limitando autem ecclesiam militantem ad clerum Christi peregrinantem in hoc seculo patet quod ab illo est secularis dominacio commode interdicta, sicut capud ejus Christus docuit tam exemplo conversacionis quam verbali doctrina. Unde clerus vivens contrarie videtur esse de sorte diaboli sub capitaneo antichristi. Ipse autem est nuncupative capitalis dominus secularis et viventes sub suo vexillo videntur esse procuratores principis tenebrarum, qui habent hoc proprium quod non sunt illis bona ista mundana communia, cum non dividuntur singulis prout cuique opus erat, ut fratres mendicantes et possessionati alii contestantur, sed iniquissime ad contencionem magnam illius cleri hec bona sunt partita. Ideo non dubium istud sophisma diaboli, sicut multa similia, docet quomodo a parte domini sunt divisi: unde notet fidelis claustrales non solum diversorum ordinum, nec solum ejusdem ordinis diversorum domuum, sed ejusdem ordinis et domus variorum morum, et videbit quomodo temporalia sua sunt iniquissime dispartita; que omnia figurantur per scarioth qui, licet habuit capitaneum fontem gracie et tam graciosos socios, tamen ex avaricia et peccatis ceteris tantum contra dominum delinquebat. Ideo apostoli post missionem spiritus sancti non audebant talem confederacionem religionis inire, cum ecclesia debet de triplici amoris vinculo contentari. Videat ergo fidelis quod in fide scripture docetur, et illud secure teneat, et quicquid reliquum fuerit illud omittat. Ideo fideles quidam religiose sustinent quod nulla tradicio humana est acceptanda, sive papalis, sive imperialis, sive provincialis alia, nisi de quanto in lege domini est fundata. Patet ex hoc quod lex Christi est gravissima, [p. 78] quia dei sufficientissima et completissima cuiuslibet hominis directiva. Ideo clerus, qui debet illam legem cognoscere, nimis degenerat qui attendit tradicionibus aut consuetudinibus humanis in dei legibus non fundatis. Vel si fundantur implicite dei legibus clerus non degenerans debet aquam fontalem illius sapiencie de fonte illo omnino habiliori accepere, et aquam stagnalem humanam postponere. Et servato illo modo laudabili purificaretur lex dei et purgaretur ecclesia ab istis erroribus per diabolum introductis.

TRUTH. But here the faithful man says steadfastly, according to the [doctrine]{g:doctrina} that Christ
taught in deed as well as in word, that [the clergy]{g:clerus} ought altogether to live a poor and
[propertyless]{g:exproprietarii} life, as heir of the heavenly [kingdom]{g:regnum}, near to the life
of innocence, as Christ [was]. For by this, that the clergy so lives of the Lord's portion, it differs
[perceptibly]{g:sensibilis} from a secular lord.[^5] And as to the first objection, it is clear that,
whether we call "church" the building itself, or the faithful who frequent it on feast days, or the
rule of the rector governing the people, the church and the glebe differ perceptibly enough. Nor has
Antichrist the power so to join these to one another that the one cannot be separated from the other,
since Christ and his apostles bore the cure more excellently than our superiors, although they lacked
such a glebe. Therefore this saying is a manifest lie of Antichrist, where he wants to put the glebe
forward as the more perfect [subject]{g:subiectum}, and to put the spiritual cure after it as an
imperfect accident;[^6] since it is known to the faithful man, who is not [besotted]{g:philocaptus}
with worldly greed, that both the church building itself and the spiritual cure of souls such as
[75] Christ had with his own differ notoriously from the glebe, whether it be understood as the
temporal thing so possessed or as the very [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio} over it. Therefore
what worldly men stammer on this point is a crude and [unbelieving]{g:infidelis} saying; yet it
forecasts something, namely that the spiritual cure of which the Lord gave the pattern is put after,
and that, according to Antichrist's [ordinance]{g:ordinacio}, affection for
[temporal goods]{g:temporalia} goes before. As to the second objection, it has been said that a
[curate]{g:curatus} who culpably keeps back this glebe must make restitution to God by contrition,
since God is the [chief lord]{g:capitalis-dominus}; and, when opportunity arises, he must make
satisfaction to the needy subjects whom he defrauded before. Yet I do not see that by any law he must
restore this to the patron, the secular lord, since by divine law such patronage cannot be founded,
and by human law such a patron conferred the glebe itself on that curate. And if the curate has
received the fruit of this glebe, rejecting worldly exercise of lordship in his mind, faithful
ministration can excuse him before God and men. Yet this seems [true]: that a faithful curate, when he
has the opportunity, ought to renounce this secular [lordship]{g:dominium}. For so blessed Possidius
tells of his master, blessed Augustine, that he asked the citizens of Hippo to take back the estates
which they had given him before, so that he could live purely on [alms]{g:elemosina}, as Christ and
his apostles lived, or on tithes and offerings, as the priests and Levites of the old testament
lived.[^7] And although the citizens themselves refused this, yet it is likely that Augustine did not
here act like "a dog returning to his former vomit",[^8] but that, living purely on alms and
ministering these estates to the poor, he was content with a poor life of alms. Therefore this way is
not new, unfoundable in scripture, since it is founded in the law of the Lord. Hence let those who
stammer so say, if they know, what possession or glebe [76] these patrons of these recent orders had:
Augustine, Benedict, Dominic, or Francis. But as the Christian [sect]{g:secta} abandoned the poverty
of its patron Christ, which these patrons are said to have revived, though culpably and unlearnedly,
so the followers of these patrons have accepted the glebe of temporal goods the more greedily.[^9]
And so in these patrons, and in the law of the eternal ordinance of "the ancient of days", this
renunciation has been made ancient;[^10] and not only in these patrons and in the
[friars]{g:frater}, although it was practised sophistically all along, but many rectors have had by
God's prompting a hidden synderesis, as those who read either law of the Lord have conceived [it]
from that law, more openly or more obscurely.[^11] But the manner of so possessing temporal goods, in
a secular way and as if by inheritance, is more culpable than the use of them; and so a man can
virtuously accept offerings and tithes in measure, provided that he minister faithfully to the church
in its need. Faithfully, I say, altogether, in spiritual things and in temporal, whenever occasion
arises of so ministering. Nor let the curate mutter about where he will lie down, or where he will
put his tithes, if he has no house of his own as though by inheritance; for let him lie "in the
midday" of the fervour of [charity]{g:caritas}, following Christ, who so slept, as it fell out and
unknown, among his apostles; and let him cast away all his care, having his
[manner of life]{g:conversacio} in heavenly things, for the gaining of blessedness and about the means
of serving God and the church and of following Christ.[^12] And let him not be anxious about offerings
or bodily tithes; for Christ in his highest wisdom ordained that his curates should lack certainty or
inheritance of these temporal goods, so that they might pant the more after heavenly things. But
Antichrist has changed this divine order. As to the third objection, it is clear that the church
militant, as to its second part and its individual members, exercises lordship; and so the whole
church, according to that part and to that extent, is imperfect, [falling short] of heavenly things,
and [77] removed from the state of innocence.[^13] But assigning the church militant to Christ's
clergy, who are [pilgrims]{g:peregrinari} in this world, it is clear that secular exercise of lordship
is fittingly forbidden to it, as its head, Christ, taught by the example of his manner of life as well
as by spoken doctrine.[^14] Hence a clergy living contrariwise seems to be of the devil's lot, under
Antichrist as captain.[^15] And he is by name the secular chief lord, and those living under his
banner seem to be proctors of the prince of darkness; who have this as their property, that these
worldly goods are not common to them, since they are not divided to every one "according as he had
need", as the mendicant friars and the other [kind], the [possessioners]{g:possessionati}, bear
witness; but these goods have been parted most unjustly, to the great contention of that
clergy.[^16] Therefore, no doubt, this sophism of the devil, like many similar ones, teaches how they
are divided from the Lord's portion: hence let the faithful man note the
[cloisterers]{g:claustrales}, not only of different orders, nor only of the same order but of
different houses, but of the same order and house [and yet] of different manners, and he will see how
their temporal goods are most unjustly parcelled out; all of which are figured by Iscariot, who,
although he had the fountain of grace as his captain and such gracious companions, yet out of avarice
and his other sins offended so greatly against the Lord. Therefore the apostles, after the sending of
the Holy Spirit, did not dare to enter such a confederation of religion, since the church ought to be
content with the threefold bond of love.[^17] Let the faithful man therefore see what is taught in
the faith of scripture, and hold that securely, and whatever remains, let him leave it aside.
Therefore certain faithful men religiously maintain that no human tradition is to be accepted,
whether papal, or imperial, or other provincial, except in so far as it is founded in the law of the
Lord. It is clear from this that the law of Christ is of the greatest weight, [78] because, being
God's, it is the most sufficient and most complete director of every man. Therefore the clergy, which
ought to know that law, degenerates too far, that clergy which attends to human traditions or
[customs]{g:consuetudo} not founded in God's laws.[^18] Or if they are founded implicitly in God's
laws, a clergy that does not degenerate ought to take the spring water of that wisdom from that
altogether fitter source, and put the stagnant human water after it. And were that praiseworthy
manner kept, [God's law]{g:lex-dei} would be purified and the church purged of these errors brought in
by the devil.[^19]

## Pollard's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 73.27: *Mend.* objects:
- 73.28: Glebe and Church are inseparable.
- 74.1: If restitution be made at all, the whole value of the glebe must be surrendered to the temporal lords.
- 74.5: Why may it not be held that each ecclesiastic is only a proctor, but that the Church as a whole holds lordship?
- 74.11: *Ver.* answers.
- 74.16: As to the inseparability of Church and glebe, the word Church denotes not only (i) the building, but (ii) the congregation, and (iii) the rule of the rector, which are distinct enough from the glebe. Christ and his apostles ministered without any glebe.
- 74.28: The faithful will not confuse the cure of souls with glebe.
- 75.5: Those who do so show that they think less of the spiritual care than of its temporalities.
- 75.8: As to restitution, curates must make this to God as chief Lord, and, as occasion serves, to the poor they have robbed.
- 75.12: But not to the secular lord, who has no claim by God's law or man's.
- 75.15: A good curate may keep his glebe.
- 75.18: But must surrender it on fit occasion.
- 75.20: Following the example of Saint Augustine.
- 75.31: What possessions had the founders of the new Orders?
- 76.4: Their followers have degenerated.
- 76.8: The renunciation of temporalities has thus good authority, of which many rectors also have a secret consciousness.
- 76.12: It is chiefly the manner of holding temporalities that is wrong, a minister may thus receive moderate offerings and tithes, so he be faithful, alike in spiritual matters and temporal.
- 76.19: But let him not take thought of house or barn, but only of serving God.
- 76.25: The uncertainty as to his tithes and offerings is of Christ's ordinance.
- 76.28: As to the suggestion that individual ecclesiastics are proctors, and the Church as a whole holds lordship, the lordship even of the second Order is an imperfection. And such lordship is absolutely forbidden to the clergy.
- 77.6: Who by assuming it take Antichrist as their captain and Lord in chief,
- 77.10: and do not share the goods they hold of him equally.
- 77.15: Thus the goods of monks are unequally divided, even among brethren of the same house.
- 77.19: All which is typified in Iscariot.
- 77.21: The apostles founded no such communities.
- 77.24: The faithful should hold fast by Scripture and nothing else.
- 77.26: So that some faithful men maintain that no papal or imperial tradition is to be accepted save as resting on Scripture.
- 78.1: The law of Christ is complete and sufficient for the guidance of souls.
- 78.3: The clergy then either degenerate in accepting unfounded traditions,
- 78.6: or, if the traditions have foundation, should go straight to their source.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Populares*: "the people", the common folk, not a party. *Recensius* (= *recencius*) is
    the glossary's "more freshly"; Truth answers it at 75.29 (*hec via non est recens*) and 76.1
    (*ordinum istorum recencium*). *Passio* is the proper attribute of a subject (GLOSSARY §C); the
    three "objections" Truth answers at 74.17, 75.7 and 76.28 are the three steps of this speech.

[^2]: **T.** *Perfecte* (74.2–3) is divided *per-|fecte* on the plate; the extraction dropped the
    second half, now restored (OVERRIDE): "perfectly restore". *Ficticium suum*: "his own fiction", i.e. the opponent's claim that glebe and church can
    be parted. *Propter injuste tentum debitum*: "for a debt unjustly held"; *debitum* could also be
    taken with *tentum* as "what is owed, held unjustly".

[^3]: **C.** *Procuratrix* and *domina* are feminine to agree with *persona*: "a proctor, not a
    [lady and] owner". Pollard's side-note gives "proctor". The claim is that a single cleric only
    administers, while the church as a whole is the lord.

[^4]: **T.** Pollard's apparatus (74.9–10): "the scribe seems stupidly to have given the words
    *Talia ... sacerdotes* to Falsehood" (*Videtur scriba noster verba Talia ... sacerdotes stolide
    Mendacio transtulisse*); they read as Truth's verdict. B¹, C, D omit *Veritas* at 74.11. We keep
    the print's speaker division.

[^5]: **C.** *De parte domini*: "of the Lord's portion", the Levites' inheritance (Num. 18:20). *Pars*
    can also be "party, side", and the phrase returns at 77.14 (*a parte domini sunt divisi*), where
    both senses are live. B¹, C read *dominio* for *domino*: "from secular lordship".

[^6]: **C.** *Mendacium antichristi*: "a lie of Antichrist"; *Mendacium* is also the speaker
    Falsehood's name. *Proponere ... postponere*: "put forward ... put after", the spatial pair, not the
    disputation verb "propose" (GLOSSARY §C). B¹, C, D omit *proponere* and add *et glebam istam
    preponere* after *postponere*.

[^7]: **S.** Possidius, *Vita Augustini* 23 (Pollard's margin; LEDGER D32-1). B¹, C, D read *anima*
    for *antea* (75.22) and *discipuli* for *apostoli* (75.23).

[^8]: **S.** Prov. 26:11; 2 Pet. 2:22. Wyclif's *canis rediens ad priorem vomitum* is his own wording;
    the Vulgate has *canis qui revertitur ad vomitum suum* (Prov.), *canis reversus ad suum vomitum*
    (2 Pet.).

[^9]: **C.** *Licet culpabiliter et indocte* is taken with *suscitasse*: the founders revived Christ's
    poverty, but culpably, by founding private orders. It could instead qualify *deseruit*: the
    Christian sect abandoned it "culpably and unlearnedly".

[^10]: **C.** *Antiquata*: classically "annulled, made obsolete". Pollard's side-note ("has thus good
    authority") takes it as "made ancient, of long standing", playing on *antiqui dierum* (Dan. 7:9,
    DR "the ancient of days"); translated so. B¹, C, D read *eciam* for *et* (76.8).

[^11]: **R/C.** *Sinderisis*: synderesis, the scholastic name for the natural habit of conscience;
    Pollard: "a secret consciousness". *Utramque legem*: both laws, the old and the new; *ex ipsa* is
    taken of the law they read.

[^12]: **S.** "In the midday": Cant. 1:6 (*ubi cubes in meridie*). Christ asleep among his apostles:
    Matt. 8:24 (B¹, C, D read *sub* for *suis*). "Cast away all his care": cf. 1 Pet. 5:7 (*omnem
    sollicitudinem vestram projicientes*). *Conversando in celestibus*: cf. Phil. 3:20 (*nostra
    conversatio in caelis est*). **C.** *Et de mediis* depends loosely on *conversando*; construed
    "[taking thought] about the means".

[^13]: **C.** *2ᵐ partem*: the second part of the church militant, which Pollard's side-note calls "the
    second Order", i.e. the secular lords; B¹, C, D read *terciam*. *Et hinc*: "and to that extent".
    *Imperfecta a celestibus*: "imperfect, [falling short] of heavenly things"; B¹, C, D read *quod in
    perfecta*.

[^14]: **C.** *Limitando ecclesiam militantem ad clerum*: "assigning [the name] church militant to the
    clergy", i.e. restricting its reference; the glossary's *limitare*, "assign", is kept. B, D read
    *vocali* for *verbali*.

[^15]: **C.** *De sorte diaboli*: "of the devil's lot" turns the etymology of *clerus* (Greek *klēros*,
    lot) against the clergy: the clergy should be the Lord's lot (LEDGER D32-2).

[^16]: **S/C.** Acts 4:35, DR "distribution was made to every one, according as he had need"; Wyclif
    puts it in the negative. *Hoc proprium*: "this as their property", catching the pun on
    *proprietas*. *Possessionati alii*: "the other [kind], the possessioners", the endowed orders set
    beside the mendicants.

[^17]: **C.** *Confederacio religionis*: a league of a "religion", i.e. a religious order (GLOSSARY:
    *religio privata*). *Triplici amoris vinculo*: the threefold bond of love is not specified here;
    compare the "threefold cord" of Eccl. 4:12 (*funiculus triplex*).

[^18]: **C.** The period has two relatives: *clerus, qui debet ... cognoscere, nimis degenerat qui
    attendit*. The second *qui* is taken as restrictive, "that clergy which attends", so the
    degeneracy is limited to those who follow unfounded traditions.

[^19]: **T.** *Et* (78.8, *Et servato*) was dropped by the extraction and is restored from the plate
    (OVERRIDE).
