# sed/autem extract 07: chunks 17-g–19-g

### 17-g ¶1 · 410.30–412.14  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 410] Ad quartum argumentum respondetur quadrupliciter pro declaracione huius quod nec apostoli nec [p. 411] alii fideles erant de ecclesia, antequam fides fuit a sanctis patribus in conciliis generalibus declarata. Et suppono quamlibet istarum responsionum validam, cum aliter nimis superflue globarentur. Prima responsio et secundum partem validissimam dicit quod ante mortem Christi fuit doctrina fidei nostre per Christum et suos apostolos sufficienter divulgata et declarata per sanctos patres (supple per Christum et apostolos) ante mortem Christi in conciliis generalibus celebratis, et quod ista concilia ante mortem Christi habita inter Christum et apostolos cum suis discipulis de hiis que pertinent ad fidem fuerunt generalia. Patet per descripcionem concilii generalis positam in glosa Johannis XVIIª dist. cap. Generalia, ubi dicitur quod illud est generale concilium quod a papa vel eius legato aut locum eius tenente cum omnibus episcopis celebratur. Cum ergo Matthei X° dicitur quod Christus convocavit discipulos suos et, exponens eis fidem, dedit eis privilegia et multiplices potestates, videtur quod ipse celebravit tunc concilium generale; et idem patet Matthei XV° et Marci VI° et sepe ante mortem Christi, ubi loquitur discipulos convocasse. Istud **autem** dictum est in parte catholicum, cum tota trinitas eterno consilio decrevit decursum ecclesie consuluitque patriarchis, prophetis et capiti ecclesie domino Jesu Christo, secundum quem cursum procederet sancta mater ecclesia. Verumptamen non oportet omnes episcopos ad tale concilium congregari, nec facit per se multitudo personarum consulencium ad generalitatem concilii, cum generalissimum quia principium omnibus aliis sit concilium increatum. Verumptamen in conciliis apostolorum et aliis conciliis generalibus quoad multitudinem episcoporum quatuor apostolorum [p. 412] concilia tenuerunt primatum, et post quatuor concilia principalia, de quibus XVIª dist. Omnibus, et quatuor fuerunt sollempnes sinodi (ut patet XVI. dist. Omnibus). Omnibus tamen istis hoc imperfeccionis fuit quod unusquisque tamquam ignarus consulendo quesivit ab alio. Non sic **autem** in concilio increato nec in concilio quod Christus cum apostolis celebravit; ipse enim omnisciens non consuluit eos in sibi ambiguis **sed** per se diffinivit et auctorizavit quid foret ecclesie faciendum; quod pape non potest competere. Ideo concilia posterius solempnizata non possunt nisi equivoce dici concilia vel generalia aut decreta, inter illa tamen quatuor concilia apostolorum racionabiliter habent preeminenciam dignitatis.

ENGLISH: To the fourth argument, [made] to declare this, that neither the apostles nor [411] the other faithful were of [the church]{g:ecclesia} before the faith was declared by the holy fathers in general councils, [the reply is]{g:respondetur} fourfold.[^1] And I suppose each of these replies valid, since otherwise they would be heaped up too superfluously. The first reply, and in its most valid part, says that before the death of Christ the [doctrine]{g:doctrina} of our faith was sufficiently spread abroad by Christ and his apostles, and declared by holy fathers (supply: by Christ and the apostles) before the death of Christ in general councils celebrated; and that those councils held before the death of Christ between Christ and the apostles with their disciples, about the things which pertain to faith, were general.[^2] It is clear by the [definition]{g:descripcio} of a general council set down in the [gloss]{g:glosa} of John on Distinction 17, chapter *Generalia*, where it is said that that is a general council which is celebrated by the [pope]{g:papa}, or his legate, or one holding his place, with all the [bishops]{g:episcopus}.[^3] Since therefore in Matthew 10 it is said that Christ called together his disciples and, expounding the faith to them, gave them [privileges]{g:privilegium} and manifold powers, it seems that he then celebrated a general council; and the same is clear in Matthew 15 and Mark 6, and often before the death of Christ, where it speaks of his having called the disciples together.[^4] And this saying is in part catholic, since the whole Trinity by eternal counsel decreed the course of the church, and took counsel for the patriarchs, the prophets and the [head]{g:caput} of the church, the Lord Jesus Christ, [as to] the course according to which holy mother church should proceed. Nevertheless it is not necessary that all the bishops be gathered to such a council; nor does the multitude of persons taking counsel of itself contribute to the generality of a council, since the most general, because it is the principle for all the others, is the uncreated council.[^5] Nevertheless, among the councils of the apostles and the other councils general as to the multitude of bishops, four councils of the apostles [412] held the primacy, and after [them] four principal councils, of which Distinction 16, *Omnibus*; and there were four solemn synods (as is clear, Distinction 16, *Omnibus*).[^6] Yet all these had this much of imperfection, that each, as one ignorant, inquired of another by taking counsel. But it is not so in the uncreated council, nor in the council which Christ celebrated with the apostles; for he, all-knowing, did not consult them in things doubtful to himself, but of himself defined and authorized what was to be done by the church; which cannot belong to a pope.[^7] Therefore councils solemnized later can only equivocally be called councils, or general, or decrees; among them, however, the four councils of the apostles reasonably have the pre-eminence of dignity.[^8]

### 17-h ¶1 · 412.15–415.7  (13 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 412] **Sed** videamus fel commixtum cum mellis dulcedine. In morte (inquit) Christi cepit esse ecclesia, a qua morte sacramenta sumpserunt efficaciam. Patet ex dictis in prima responsione et secunda quomodo error iste nedum tolleret honorem sancte matris ecclesie ex sua antiquitate fundata in fide scripture, **sed** innuit interempcionem meriti matris nostre et tollit fidem patrum veteris testamenti dehonorando sanctam matrem ecclesiam. Item manifeste responsio obviat sibi ipsi, quia ante mortem Christi fuit ita quod est multitudo omnium christianorum habencium ex integro rectam et eandem fidem per Christum et suos apostolos promulgatam et a sanctis patribus in conciliis generalibus declaratam, ut declaratur in responsione. Ideo ante mortem Christi fuit ita quod est ecclesia. Non enim incepit ecclesia in triduo, quando dicitur [p. 413] Christum non esse, nec sapit incarcerantibus presens tempus quod a morte Christi sacramenta sumpserunt suam originem, sicut patet querendo que sacramenta et quando sumpserunt a dicta morte originem. Nec valet alia responsio qua dicitur quod descripcio predicta ecclesie non compeciit ecclesie in sancto triduo, tum quia tunc descripcio non valeret que tam cito deficeret in floribus ecclesie primitive, tum secundo quia iuxta declarata descripcio ecclesie compeciit christianis ante mortem Christi et per idem in sancto triduo. Non ergo deserenda est tam cito dicta descripcio, cum in fine adicitur quod stabit non obstantibus meis obiectibus in eternum. **Sed** revera fingere descripcionem per quam excluderetur a racione ecclesie tam sanctum apostolorum collegium cum suo abbate pro toto tempore sue viacionis videtur presumpcio, cum presciti dicuntur esse de sancta matre ecclesia et in principio mundi ante creacionem hominis sicut et lapsis ambobus parentibus fuit ecclesia (ut patet Encheridion LXIV°). Ulterius respondendo ad quintam raciunculam dicitur quod sicut continue manet eadem species hominum non obstante quod individua variarentur, sic manet continue eadem universalis ecclesia non obstante quod membra alia generentur et alia corrumpantur. Sic (inquit) manebit idem homo in numero non obstante quod partes equales alie generentur et alie corrumpantur. Quare rogo non sic erit de corpore Christi mistico quod est ecclesia? Et revera illud est bene dictum dummodo consequenter. Oportet enim ecclesiam catholicam esse unam personam sponsam et non formaliter speciem vel universale quod sit multorum singularium, **sed** [p. 414] est una persona successiva a principio mundi usque ad diem iudicii, et post habebit illa persona omnia eius membra, simul perpetuo beata et illius persone nunc una pars est militans et nunc alia, **sed** sicut nulla pars est reliqua, sic tota illa ecclesia non est pars eius aliqua **sed** una totalis et gloriosa incommunicabilis persona. Non enim capit ingenium humanum quod iste pauce persone militantes quas falsarius vocat universalem ecclesiam sint alie persone coniunctim vel divisim, ymmo ad alietatem cuiuscunque simplicis persone sequitur alietas multitudinis que ipsam personam caruerit a multitudine que ipsam personam tamquam partem habuerit. Ideo illa ecclesia catholica que durat a principio usque in finem mundi non est persone vel multitudo fidelium que nunc est secundum se totam, **sed** illa multitudo est pars illius ecclesie catholice que est in magno tempore et semper post diem iudicii, **sed** in nullo instanti temporis est per totum ante novissimum electum nec in aliqua parte talis temporis, et talis ecclesia augeri potest **sed** non minui, non quod eadem multitudo predestinatorum in numero sit nunc plures homines et nunc pauciores, **sed** quod idem commune ad sanctam totalem ecclesiam militantem sit nunc maius et nunc minus, sicut loquitur scriptura Act. VI° de crescente numero discipulorum. Sicut ergo idem tempus in numero non maioratur vel minoratur, sic nec eadem ecclesia in numero nisi accipiendo ecclesiam communiter pro capite et quacunque multitudine totali membrorum suorum. Sic enim maioratur ecclesia, ut homo stante unitate capitis ut anime. **Sed** ista veritas necessitat ad ponendum quod omnia que fuerunt vel erunt sunt pro suo tempore causancia unum integrum, cuius pars est pro parte temporis totalis [p. 415] ipsum integrum mensurantis. Et sic concedi potest quod ista nunc sunt, demonstrando omnia que fuerunt vel erunt, intelligendo parcialiter collective. **Sed** coloracius quoad logicam concedi potest quod hoc totum existens ista est nunc **sed** non ista, quia tunc infinita instancia forent simul in eodem instanti (de qua logica patet alibi).

ENGLISH: But let us see the gall mixed with the sweetness of honey. "At the death of Christ," he says, "[the church]{g:ecclesia} began to be, from which death the sacraments took their efficacy."[^1] It is clear from what was said in the first reply and the second how this error not only would take away the honour of holy mother church from her antiquity, founded in the faith of scripture, but implies the destruction of the merit of [our mother]{g:mater-nostra}, and takes away the faith of the fathers of the old testament by dishonouring holy mother church.[^2] Again, the reply manifestly contradicts itself; for before the death of Christ it was so that there is a multitude of all Christians having, entire, the right and same faith, promulgated by Christ and his apostles and declared by holy fathers in general councils, as is declared in the reply. Therefore before the death of Christ it was so that there is a church.[^3] For the church did not begin in the three days, when Christ is said [413] not to be; nor does it make sense to those who imprison the present time that the sacraments took their origin from the death of Christ, as is clear by asking which sacraments took their origin from the said death, and when.[^4] Nor is the other reply of any avail, by which it is said that the aforesaid [definition]{g:descripcio} of the church did not apply to the church in the holy three days: both because then a definition would not be valid which would fail so soon in the flowers of the primitive church, and secondly because, according to what has been declared, the definition of the church applied to Christians before the death of Christ, and by the same token in the holy three days. Therefore the said definition is not to be deserted so soon, since at its end it is added that it will stand for ever notwithstanding my objections.[^5] But truly, to feign a definition by which so holy a college of the apostles, with its abbot, would be excluded from the account of the church for the whole time of its [wayfaring]{g:viacio} seems presumption, since the [foreknown]{g:prescitus} are said to be of holy mother church; and in the beginning of the world, before the creation of man, as also when both parents had fallen, there was a church (as is clear, *Enchiridion* 64).[^6] Further, in reply to the fifth [little argument]{g:raciuncula}, [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that, as the same species of men remains continuously notwithstanding that the individuals vary, so the same universal church remains continuously notwithstanding that some [members]{g:membrum} are generated and others corrupted. So (he says) the same man in number will remain, notwithstanding that equal parts are, some generated and others corrupted. Why, I ask, will it not be so with the [mystical body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi-misticum}, which is the church?[^7] And truly that is well said, provided [it is said] consistently. For the catholic church must be one person, a [bride]{g:sponsa}, and not formally a species or [universal]{g:universale} which is of many singulars; but it [414] is one successive person from the beginning of the world to the day of judgement, and afterwards that person will have all her members together, perpetually blessed; and of that person now one part is militant and now another; but as no part is the remaining [part], so that whole church is not any part of itself, but one total and glorious incommunicable person.[^8] For human wit does not grasp that these few militant persons, whom the falsifier calls the universal church, should be other persons, jointly or severally; indeed, upon the otherness of any single person there follows the otherness of a multitude which lacked that person from a multitude which had that person as a part.[^9] Therefore that catholic church which lasts from the beginning to the end of the world is not, as to its whole self, the persons or the multitude of the faithful that now is; but that multitude is a part of that catholic church, which is in the great time and always after the day of judgement, but at no instant of time is it wholly [present] before the last elect, nor in any part of such time.[^10] And such a church can be increased but not diminished: not that the same multitude of the [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} in number is now more men and now fewer, but that the same common [term] for the holy total [church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans} is now greater and now less, as scripture speaks in Acts 6 of the number of the disciples increasing.[^11] As therefore the same time in number is not made greater or less, so neither is the same church in number, except by taking the church commonly for the [head]{g:caput} and any total multitude whatever of its members. For so the church is made greater, as a man is, the unity of the head standing, as [that] of the soul.[^12] But this truth necessitates positing that all things which have been or will be are, for their time, causing one integral whole, of which a part is for a part of the total time [415] measuring that integral whole. And so it can be granted that these now are, pointing to all things that have been or will be, understanding [it] partially and collectively. But [with more colour]{g:colorare} as regards logic it can be granted that this whole, existing as these things, is now, but not these things; for then infinite instants would be together in the same instant (of which logic it is clear elsewhere).[^13]

### 18-a ¶1 · 415.9–418.1  (6 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 415] Ulterius restat approbando veritatem et reprobando falsitatem transcurrere per responsiones ad alias quinque raciunculas que supersunt. Prima **autem** responsio dicit, primo quod omnes presciti renati baptismate sunt membra sancte ecclesie catholice; secundo dicit quod nulli predestinati errantes a recta fide sunt membra sancte matris ecclesie, et tercio dicit quod nec Petrus nec Paulus nec David nec aliquis alius pro tempore pro quo deviat a recta fide est pars ecclesie catholice. Ista est venenosa triplicitas: prima tamen suadetur est sentencia sancti Thome super III° Sentenciarum dist. XIIIª, q. IIª, dicentis quod, sicut in corpore naturali membra uniuntur et sunt unum per colligacionem quandam nervorum et iuncturarum, ut fiant unum continuacione, sic (inquit) in corpore Christi mistico omnes qui colligati sunt ad invicem per fidem rectam sunt unum in corpore ecclesie; et sic infidelis non pertinet ad unionem corporis ecclesie quod est unum simpliciter, nec paganorum, hereticorum et separatorum ab ecclesia est Christus proprie [p. 416] caput nisi in potencia. Ista **autem** verba non faciunt pro supradicta sentencia, cum solum predestinati colligati sunt per fidem rectam, eo quod omnes alii continue peccato indelebili peccant mortaliter, et sic sunt perpetuo heretici qui soli simpliciter separantur a corpore Christi mistico. **Sed** quod dicitur sanctum Thomam in solucione ad tercium argumentum plane dicere quod presciti existentes in gracia sunt vera membra ecclesie, sicut manus abscindenda est membrum, quousque fuerit actualiter abscisa, dicitur quod hoc debet intelligi de ecclesia secundo modo dicta, ut includit granum et paleam; que ecclesia secundum Augustinum III° De Doctrina Christiana XXXII° non est corpus domini nec eciam corpus domini bipertitum, et sic presciti baptizati habent secundum presentem iusticiam incorporacionem in illa mixta ecclesia et quandam adnascenciam cum vera ecclesia, sicut habet stercus in venis miseraicis, **sed** nunquam sunt pars corporis Christi; et ista est sentencia Augustini epistola XXXIIª ad Bonifacium. Quantum ad secundum errorem, patet quod capit instanciam de omnibus predestinatis a fide errantibus qui quamvis a fide ad tempus devient, Deus tamen ex eleccione predestinacionis eos semper eque diligit. Et hinc dicit Christus Johannis X, 16: Alias oves habeo que non sunt ex hoc ovili et vocem meam audient. Super quo textu notat Augustinus super Johan. Omelia XLVª quomodo Christus dicit se habere oves de gentibus adhuc convertendis; hec, inquit, secundum [p. 417] predestinationem dicta sunt. Ideo (ut dixi superius) ponit Ricardus de Sancto Victore tres gradus membrorum ecclesie. Quamvis **autem** predestinati deficiunt a fide et gracia secundum presentem iusticiam, habent tamen fidem infusam secundum graciam predestinacionis. Si ergo secundum doctorem prescitus existens in mortali secundum fidem infusam sit membrum sancte matris ecclesie, licet omne peccatum mortale concommunicetur infidelitas, multo magis omnis predestinatus foret membrum sancte matris ecclesie. Ad aliam raciunculam primo dicitur quod non est eadem racio ecclesie militantis et ecclesie triumphantis; secundo dicitur quod ecclesia triumphans infinitum distat in perfeccione essenciali ab ecclesia militante; tercio dicitur quod nullo modo est una ecclesia ex hiis, cum tantum differunt sicut substancia et accidens que non sunt unum aliquo modo. Et ista dicitur esse sentencia Augustini, Epistola XXXIIª, ad Bonifacium, ubi distinguens inter ecclesiam militantem et triumphantem ponit ecclesiam triumphantem esse sine macula vel ruga, triumphans **autem** est immunis ab omni culpa. Isti tres errores sunt manifestiores prioribus, quia equus cecus continue profundius sese precipitat. Nam verum est (ut sepe docet Augustinus ex fide scripture) quod ecclesia est maculata et rugosa dum militat, **sed** omnino sine mortali vel veniali dum triumphat. **Sed** omnino dementis foret ex istis arguere diversitatem specificam illarum ecclesiarum, vel quod non fit una ex illis, vel quod una infinitum excedit aliam in perfeccione essenciali. Nam beatus viator et dampnatus sunt eiusdem speciei specialissime, componunt unum genus humanum et sunt pares in perfeccione essenciali; et tamen sunt illius condicionis quod inferior est plenus macula et rugosus, et secundus est omnino sine macula vel ruga. Accidens ergo non variat tantum unam substanciam ab alia. Nec valet dicere quod est dare unum aggregatum ex substancia et accidente, ut puta ex habitu fidei infuso et substancia aggregatis, tum quia negatur talis unitas in fundamento responsionis, tum eciam quia talis habitus fidei manens perpetuo migraret de subiecto in subiectum et reciperet peregrinas predicaciones formales alienas a natura universalium, unde in confirmacionem supradicte sentencie. Pertinenter dico, primo quia cardinalis, secundo quia papa et tota curia approbavit suam sentenciam in hac parte, et tercio ex affeccione nominis et cognominis, cum fuit Johannis Monachi. Ubi non dubium quin loquitur limitate de ecclesia que est universitas predestinatorum, nam illam redemit, illam glorificabit post diem iudicii, illa erit non habens [p. 440] tunc maculam sive rugam, et illa est corpus Christi quod fovet et diligit; et nullo modo verificatur ista de numero prescitorum, quantumcunque magna sanctitate ad tempus appareat prepollere.

ENGLISH: For although at the instant at which the first man was made that [church]{g:ecclesia} was constituted, yet in a new way, after the ascension, its lower part was set above its upper part in the heavenly places. And by its lower part I understand the whole of human nature, which, according to its part which is the humanity of Christ, [439] is so exalted.[^1] And to destroy these errors in the faith the bishops should labour, whose principal office should be to teach and defend that faith, and also, setting aside their care for the things that savour of gain, to declare the errors of those who stray in this faith.[^2] For because of the blinding by temporal goods the church of the angels is almost forgotten, although in the Enchiridion 61 the holy doctor Augustine writes thus: "And this church which is in the holy angels and powers of God will become known to us as it is when we are joined to it at the end, to have everlasting blessedness; but this which is a pilgrim from it on earth is better known to us in that we are in it, and because it is of men, which we too are."[^3] And that none of the [foreknown]{g:prescitus} are [members]{g:membrum} of that church he declares thus: "This, by the blood of the mediator, who had no sin, is redeemed from all sin." Now it is evident that the sin of final impenitence, such as is in every foreknown [man], cannot be redeemed or blotted out, as Augustine teaches in a like case in *On the Gift of Perseverance* 6: "Many," he says, "can not have final perseverance, but no one can lose it."[^4] Whence, since the holy catholic church is called the church [by antonomasia]{g:autonomatice}, therefore among the authors of scripture and the holy doctors it is specially understood when the church is named; as in Ephesians 5:25–27: "Husbands, love your wives, as Christ also loved the church and delivered himself up for it: that he might sanctify it, cleansing it by the laver of water in the word of life: that he himself might present a glorious church, not having spot or wrinkle or any such thing; but that it should be holy and without blemish." Where there is no doubt that he speaks restrictedly of the church which is [the whole body]{g:universitas} of the [predestinate]{g:predestinatus}; for that he redeemed, that he will glorify after the day of judgement, that will [440] then be not having spot or wrinkle, and that is the body of Christ which he cherishes and loves; and in no way is this verified of the number of the foreknown, however much they may appear for a time to excel in great holiness.[^5]

### 18-g ¶2 · 440.5–440.23  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 440] Nec vidi evidenciam unde crederetur quod ante mortem Christi non erat ecclesia, nisi forte per locum ab insufficienti similitudine, capta ex textu Apostoli Ephes. V°, 31: Erunt, inquit, duo in carne una. Sacramentum hoc magnum est, ego **autem** dico in Christo et in ecclesia. Sicut enim ex latere primi Adam resoluti in soporem formata est Eva uxor sua (ut patet Gen. II°), sic ex latere secundi Adam dormientis in cruce formata ecclesia, uxor sua (ut patet Joh. XX°). **Sed** ante priorem formacionem non fuit Eva, ergo ante secundam formacionem non fuit ecclesia. Ista informis argucia induceret ex omnimoda similitudine in errores tocius fidei christiane, ut puta quod Christus non fuit mortuus, quod ecclesia habuit esse materiale ex sanguine et aqua effluxis de latere Christi, et quod Christus non formavit ecclesiam de suo latere, **sed** ipso soporato et ignorante solum passive contulit ad formacionem ecclesie. Et sic non foret Deus redemptor vel sponsus ecclesie.

ENGLISH: Nor have I seen [evidence]{g:evidencia} from which it should be believed that before the death of Christ there was no church, unless perhaps by the [topic]{g:locus} from insufficient likeness, taken from the text of the Apostle, Ephesians 5:31[–32]: "They shall be," he says, "two in one flesh. This is a great sacrament: but I speak in Christ and in the church."[^6] For just as from the side of the first Adam, sunk in sleep, Eve his wife was formed (as is clear from Genesis 2), so from the side of the second Adam, sleeping on the cross, the church was formed, his wife (as is clear from John 20).[^7] Now before the former formation there was no Eve; therefore before the second formation there was no church. This unformed [quibble]{g:argucia} would lead, from likeness in every respect, into errors against the whole christian faith: for instance, that Christ was not dead; that the church had material [being]{g:esse} from the blood and water that flowed out of Christ's side; and that Christ did not form the church from his side, but, he being asleep and unaware, contributed only passively to the formation of the church. And so God would not be the redeemer or [bridegroom]{g:sponsus} of the church.[^8]

### 18-g ¶3 · 440.24–441.25  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 440] Ideo oportet intelligere hoc sacramentum ad literam iuxta sensum historicum expressum de primis parentibus, et supra hunc sensum oportet intelligere hoc sacramentaliter vel mistice de Christo et ecclesia secundum catholicam similitudinem a Christo et sanctis doctoribus expressatam. Christus enim secundum deitatem sicut ordinavit domum sponse sue mundum sensibilem, sic prius dignitate ordinavit sponsam ecclesiam sibi domum, **sed** utrumque factum est in principio mundi. Et sicut Deus immisit soporem in [p. 441] Adam muliere formata ex eo sine priori contractu mutuo ad assensum, sic primus Adam secundum deitatem ex eternis racionibus exemplaribus formavit in principio mundi eciam ecclesiam sine previa consideracione consensus meritorii, quia (ut dicit Augustinus Enchiridion LVI°) ecclesia celestis semper ex quo condita est cohesit domino. Nam tam angelus quam homo creabatur in gracia gratificante, posterius **autem** in plenitudine temporis formavit ecclesiam suam humanitus que per multa millia annorum fuit prius, dum dormiens in cruce mortuus fecit eam formosam ex principiis eam mundantibus, virtute quorum tota prior ecclesia, sicut et ecclesia sequens accepit vitam spiritualem et pulchritudinem vite naturali superadditam. Et sicut preter materiam coste Adam oportet multiplicari spiritualiter, quia miraculose nobis abscondite aliam materiam ex quibus constituitur mater Eva, sic principaliter mundiciam vel virtutem corporalem sanguinis et aque redempcionis humane oportet dare ampliorem spiritualem mundiciam et virtutem ex eis originatam que se extendit ad totam ecclesiam preteritam et futuram; **sed** procul undique hoc venenum pestiferum, ut credatur ante mortem Christi non fuisse ecclesiam, **sed** Christum cum suis apostolis fuisse viando solummodo synagogam.

ENGLISH: Therefore this sacrament must be understood according to the letter, following the historical sense expressed of the first parents; and above this sense it must be understood sacramentally or mystically of Christ and the church, according to the catholic likeness expressed by Christ and the holy doctors.[^9] For Christ according to his deity, just as he ordained the [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} world as the house of his [bride]{g:sponsa}, so, first in dignity, he ordained his bride the church as a house for himself; but both were made in the beginning of the world. And just as God sent a deep sleep upon [441] Adam, the woman being formed from him without a prior mutual contract to assent, so the first Adam, according to his deity, formed from eternal exemplar reasons, in the beginning of the world, the church also, without previous consideration of a meritorious consent;[^10] for (as Augustine says, Enchiridion 56) "the heavenly church has always, since it was founded, cleaved to the Lord". For both angel and man were created in grace that makes pleasing; but later, in the fullness of time, he formed in human wise his church, which had existed for many thousands of years before, when, sleeping on the cross, dead, he made it beautiful from principles that cleanse it, by the power of which the whole prior church, as also the following church, received spiritual life and a beauty added over natural life.[^11] And just as, beyond the matter of Adam's rib, other matter, from which mother Eve is constituted, had to be multiplied spiritually, since [it was done] miraculously, in a way hidden from us, so principally the bodily cleanness or power of the blood and water of human redemption must give a fuller spiritual cleanness and power, originating from them, which extends itself to the whole church, past and future. But far, on every side, [be] this pestilent poison, that it should be believed that before the death of Christ there was no church, but that Christ with his apostles was, in his wayfaring, only the synagogue.[^12]

### 19-a ¶1 · 441.27–442.25  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 441] Supposito ex fide scripture elaborata a sanctis doctoribus quod solum predestinati sint membra sancte matris ecclesie, restat dubitandum ulterius si [p. 442] presciti gerant ordines et officia illius ecclesie. Et videtur ex dictis quod non; nam Deus summe sapiens non daret potestatem inhabili. **Sed** nullus talis habet potestatem huiusmodi si non per Deum, ergo nullus talis habet huiusmodi potestatem. Est **autem** inhabilis, quia in mortali peccato necessario dampnabili continue, ut suppono ex dictis XXVI° capitulo libri Vⁱ. Confirmatur ex Decreto, XIª, q. III, ubi ex dicto beati Gregorii sic habetur: Ipse ligandi atque solvendi potestate se privat qui hanc pro suis voluntatibus et non subditorum moribus exercet. Si ergo pro leviori peccato habens potestatem ordinis ipsa privatur, multo magis pro graviori peccato ipse qui reciperet huiusmodi potestatem ab ea excluditur; culpandus itaque foret Christus, si ecclesie sue preficeret membrum diaboli tam inhabile et nocivum. Non ergo habet talis caracterem indelebilem, ut sit de exercitu christiano. Hic videtur mihi indubie quod nullus prescitus est pars vel gerens officium tamquam de sancta matre ecclesia; habet tamen intra illam ecclesiam ad sui dampnacionem et ecclesie utilitatem certa officia ministrandi, ut patet de Scarioth et multis eius vicariis, et ad tantum Deus eligit tales dans eis ad tale officium potestatem. Nam Johannis VI°, 71 scribitur: Nonne ego duodecim vos elegi et unus ex vobis diabolus est?

ENGLISH: Supposing, [from the faith of scripture]{g:ex} as worked out by the holy doctors, that only the [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} are [members]{g:membrum} of holy [mother]{g:mater} [church]{g:ecclesia}, it remains to ask further whether [442] the [foreknown]{g:prescitus} hold the orders and offices of that church.[^1] And it seems from what has been said that they do not; for God, supremely wise, would not give power to one [unqualified]{g:inhabilis}. Now no such [man] has power of this kind if not through God; therefore no such [man] has power of this kind. And he is unqualified, because he is continually in mortal sin, necessarily damnable, as I suppose from what was said in the twenty-sixth chapter of book 5.[^2] It is confirmed from the Decretum, Causa 11, question 3, where, from a saying of blessed Gregory, it is had thus: "He deprives himself of the power of binding and loosing who exercises it according to his own wishes and not according to the conduct of his subjects." If therefore for a lighter sin one having the power of order is deprived of it, much more for a graver sin is he who would receive power of this kind excluded from it; Christ would therefore be to blame if he set over his church a member of the devil so unqualified and harmful. Such a man therefore does not have the indelible [character]{g:carecter}, so as to be of the Christian army.[^3] Here it seems to me without doubt that no foreknown [man] is a part, or bears an office as being of holy mother church; yet within that church he has, to his own damnation and the church's profit, certain offices of ministering, as is clear of Iscariot and his many vicars; and to that extent God chooses such men, giving them power for such an office.[^4] For in John 6:71 it is written: "Have not I chosen you twelve? And one of you is a devil."

### 19-a ¶2 · 442.26–444.5  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 442] Super quo Augustinus, Omelia XXVª, dubitat quomodo Christus elegit dyabolum, et respondet quod electus est iste de quo nolente et nesciente magnum aliquid boni fieret. Hoc, inquit, est proprium Dei, iniquis contrarium quod, sicut iniqui male utuntur [p. 443] bonis Dei operibus, sic econtra Deus bene utitur malis operibus iniquorum. Et ponendo multa exempla conclusionaliter sic concludit: Omnibus ergo bonis Dei, ne per singula currere longum sit, male utitur malus. Et hinc sepe dixi quod existens in mortali continue abutitur bonis Dei, et per consequens, quicquid sic fecerit peccat mortaliter; econtra de malis: malis malorum, inquit Augustinus, bene utitur bonus. Et quid tam bonum, ut Deus, de quo Marci X°, 18 dicitur: Nemo bonus nisi solus Deus? Quanto ergo ille melior tanto melius utitur malis nostris. Quid Iuda peius inter apostolos cui loculi commissi sunt et dispensacio pauperum distributa? **Sed** quia ingratus tanto beneficio et honori accepit pecuniam, perdidit iusticiam: mortuus tradidit vitam; quem ut discipulus secutus, ut inimicus persecutus est; toto hoc malo Iude bene usus est dominus, cum prodicione eius dampnabili nos redemit. Sic ergo habuit Iudas officium in ecclesia, non de illa, sicut stercus et apostemata habent officia in humano corpore, non de illo. Modo, inquit Augustinus, corpus Christi mixtum est tamquam area; **sed** novit Deus qui sunt eius, sicut tu nosti quid trituras, quia ibi est latens massa, nec consumit trituracio quod purgatura est ventilacio. **Sed** difficultas est utrum tales presciti habent caracterem ordinis, utrum sint sacerdotes episcopi vel prelati. Quoad primum est dissensio inter multos de quiditate caracteris. Aliqui enim dicunt quod est qualitas de secunda specie qualitatis sicut naturalis potencia. Alii **autem** dicunt quod non est per se in genere **sed** per reduccionem, sicut alie veritates consimiles extra genus, ita quod caracter sit deputacio [p. 444] passiva creature racionalis ad serviendum Deo et ecclesie secundum gradum ordinis dignitatis; nec contendo circa quiditatem talis caracteris, cum certus sum quod non sit res ut serta regis vel aliquod signum inpressum quod poterit per se esse.

ENGLISH: On which Augustine, in Homily 25, asks how Christ chose a devil, and answers that this one was chosen through whom, unwilling and unknowing, some great good would be done. "This," he says, "is proper to God, contrary to the wicked: that, as the wicked use the good works of God [443] badly, so conversely God uses well the bad works of the wicked."[^5] And setting out many examples, he [concludes]{g:concludere} by way of conclusion thus: "All the good things of God, therefore (lest it be long to run through them one by one), the bad man uses badly." And hence I have often said that one who is in mortal [sin] continually abuses the goods of God, and consequently whatever he so does, he sins mortally. Conversely of evils: "The evils of the evil," says Augustine, "the good man uses well. And what is so good as God", of whom it is said in Mark 10:18, "None is good but God alone"? "The better he is, therefore, the better he uses our evils. What was worse than Judas among the apostles, to whom the purse was committed and the dispensing for the poor assigned? But because, ungrateful for so great a benefit and honour, he took money, he lost justice; dead, he betrayed life; whom he followed as a disciple, him he persecuted as an enemy. All this evil of Judas the Lord used well", since by his damnable betrayal he redeemed us.[^6] So therefore Judas had an office in the church, not of it, as dung and [abscesses]{g:apostema} have offices in the human body, not of it. "At present," says Augustine, "the body of Christ is mixed, like a threshing-floor; but God knows who are his, as you know what you thresh, since there is a mass lying hidden there, and the threshing does not consume what the winnowing is to purge."[^7] But the difficulty is whether such foreknown [men] have the character of order, whether they are [priests]{g:sacerdos}, [bishops]{g:episcopus} or [prelates]{g:prelatus}. As to the first there is dissension among many about the [quiddity]{g:quiditas} of the character. For some say that it is a quality of the second species of quality, like a natural capacity. But others say that it is not of itself in a category but by reduction, like other similar truths outside a category, so that the character is a passive deputation [444] of a rational creature to serve God and the church according to the degree of the order of dignity.[^8] Nor do I contend about the quiddity of such a character, since I am certain that it is not a thing, like a king's crown, or some sign imprinted that could exist of itself.[^9]

### 19-a ¶3 · 444.6–445.29  (7 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 444] Tenendo ergo secundam viam de quiditate caracteris dico ulterius quod alius est necessario delebilis, ut caracter ecclesiasticus in prescitis, eo quod non ultra diem iudicii durabit in eis officium dignitatis. In predestinatis **autem** oportet esse caracterem omnino indelebilem, cum dignitas sequendi agnum mutuo illuminandi conbeatum et percipiendi fructum vivificum non posset deleri in eis, et de tanto habent caracterem ordinis vel caracterem baptismalem. Et patet quod predestinati et presciti secundum diversitatem officii equivoce habent caracterem. Et correspondenter dicitur quod equivoce habent ordinem, episcopatum vel aliam ecclesiasticam prelaciam, quia presciti ascribuntur ad tale officium solummodo temporale, non ut mereantur beatitudinem, **sed** ut mitigando penam per accidens prosint ecclesie. Et ita conceditur quod Scarioth et eius vicarii erant apostoli, presbiteri, episcopi et prelati, habentes proporcionaliter ad suum officium potestatem, et ad tantum aput Deum sunt habiles. Quod **autem** Iudas fuit apostolus, patet Matthei X°, ubi inter duodecim quos Christus elegerat computatur, et beatus Petrus testatur quod sortitus est sortem ministerii apostolatus et quod de illo scriptum est Psalmo CVIII°, 8: Fiant dies eius pauci et episcopatum eius accipiat alter; et sic cum singulum istorum sit nomen officii, patet quod non sequitur: Iudas est apostolus, episcopus vel sacerdos, ergo est membrum vel de sancta ecclesia. [p. 445] Sufficit quod sit in illa tamquam palea temporaliter ministrans finaliter comburenda. Unde non dicimus quod ad serviendum Deo in inferno perpetuo paciendo oportet servum indelebiliter habere caracterem, nisi forte equivocetur de caractere bestie quam meminit Johannis Apoc. XIX°, 20, ubi dicitur: Seduxit eos qui acceperunt caracterem bestie et qui adoraverunt ymaginem eius. Et de isto caractere fit mencio Apocalypsis XIII° et XIV°. Caracterem **autem** transferimus in bonum pro scriptura predestinacionis in libro vite (ut loquitur Christus Luce X°, 20 et Apocalypsis XIII°, 8). Quandoque vero accipitur caracter pro aggregato ex predestinacione et limitacione per signum sacramentale, et sic secundum limitacionem signi caracteris est varie respondendum. Nam caracter predestinatorum et caracter prescitorum qui sunt de synagoga sathane (ut loquitur scriptura Apoc. II°) est indelebilis, quia primus est calculus candidus vel nomen novum, quod nemo scit nisi qui accipit (ut loquitur scriptura Apoc. II°, 9). **Sed** secundus est nomen finalis impenitencie vel comitans caracter bestie, caracter **autem** temporalis quando est limitacio ad officium temporale; et in talibus terminis magistralibus oportet responsalem quoad significacionem et quiditatem in principio stabiliri. Caracter enim communiter in malum sumitur in scriptura, **sed** aliter loquimur hic de caractere agni quoad dignitatem activam ministerii ad quam assignantur predestinati differenter a servis dyaboli in ordine vel baptismo.

ENGLISH: Holding therefore the second way about the quiddity of the character, I say further that one [character] is necessarily delible, as the ecclesiastical character in the foreknown, because the office of dignity will not last in them beyond the day of judgement.[^10] But in the predestinate the character must be altogether indelible, since the dignity of following the Lamb, of mutually enlightening one's fellow-blessed, and of receiving the life-giving fruit could not be blotted out in them; and to that extent they have the character of order or the baptismal character. And it is clear that the predestinate and the foreknown, according to the diversity of office, have the character equivocally. And correspondingly it is said that they have order, the episcopate or another ecclesiastical prelacy equivocally, because the foreknown are enrolled for such an office as a merely temporal one, not that they may merit blessedness, but that, by mitigating their punishment, they may by accident profit the church.[^11] And so it is granted that Iscariot and his vicars were apostles, [presbyters]{g:presbiter}, bishops and prelates, having power in proportion to their office, and to that extent they are qualified before God. And that Judas was an apostle is clear from Matthew 10, where he is counted among the twelve whom Christ had chosen; and blessed Peter testifies that he "had obtained part of the ministry of the apostleship", and that of him it is written in Psalm 108:8: "May his days be few: and his bishopric let another take". And so, since each of these is the name of an office, it is clear that it does not [follow]{g:sequitur}: "Judas is an apostle, bishop or priest; therefore he is a member of holy church, or of it."[^12] [445] It suffices that he be in it as chaff, ministering for a time, to be burned in the end. Whence we do not say that, to serve God in hell by suffering perpetually, a servant must have a character indelibly, unless perhaps "character" is used equivocally of the character of the beast which John mentions in Apocalypse 19:20, where it is said: "He seduced them who received the character of the beast and who adored his image." And of this character mention is made in Apocalypse 13 and 14.[^13] But we transfer "character" to the good, for the writing of predestination in the book of life (as Christ speaks in Luke 10:20, and Apocalypse 13:8). And sometimes "character" is taken for the aggregate of predestination and assignment through a sacramental sign; and so, according to the assignment of the sign of the character, the answer must vary.[^14] For the character of the predestinate and the character of the foreknown who are of the synagogue of Satan (as scripture speaks, Apocalypse 2) is indelible: for the first is the white counter or new name, "which no man knoweth but he that receiveth it" (as scripture speaks, Apocalypse 2:9); but the second is the name of final impenitence, or the accompanying character of the beast; and the character is temporal when it is an assignment to a temporal office. And in such masters' terms the respondent must be fixed at the outset as to signification and quiddity. For "character" is commonly taken in a bad sense in scripture; but here we speak otherwise, of the character of the Lamb, as to the active dignity of ministry to which the predestinate are assigned, differently from the servants of the devil, in order or in baptism.[^15]

### 19-b ¶1 · 445.31–448.13  (10 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 445] Unde videtur mihi quod nomina recitati officii eciam in predestinatis exhinc merentibus beatitudinem sunt quedam specialia, ut apostolatus et episcopatus. Nam missio ad evangelizandum et superintendencia solicita ad custodiendum videntur tantum pro statu [p. 446] vie officiariis sponsi competere. **Sed** sacerdocium videtur esse eternum, cum beati dant et docent sacra illuminando suos socios in eternum, et ad illud potest applicari illud Apoc. 1°, 6: Fecisti nos Deo nostro regnum et sacerdotes; et sic predestinati qui digne presunt subiectis videntur mihi esse eis in beatitudine preferendi, et sic in celesti ierarchia eis in perpetuo sacerdocio sunt prelati, ut innuit beatus Gregorius, Omelia XVIIª super illo Luce X°, 1: Post hec designavit dominus alios septuaginta, et Crisostomus expressius, Omelia XXXIV Operis Imperfecti Melior, inquit, est laicus qui in prima facie profitetur vitam secularem **sed** studet spiritualem, quam sacerdos profitens vitam spiritualem **sed** amplectitur vitam carnalem, et melior est laicus ante Deum penitenciam agens quam clericus permanens in peccatis. Laicus enim in die iudicii stolam sacerdotalem accipiet, dum eciam crismate omnes sic unguentur in sacerdocio; sacerdos **autem** peccator spoliatur sacerdocii dignitate quam habuit; et tunc erit inter infideles et ypocritas (ut dicit veritas Matthei XXIV°, 51). Omnes eciam beati erunt virgines spiritualiter (ut patet Apocalypsis XIV°, 4). Et omnes erunt martyres modo suo, cum Luce XIV°, 27, dicitur: Qui non baiulat crucem suam et venit post me, non potest meus esse discipulus. Omnes (inquam) erunt sacerdotes in patria, quia dantes sacra beatitudinis accidentalia conbeatis. Et hinc Abel a quo cepit humana ecclesia in triumpho, fuit expresse virgo, martir et sacerdos, ut notat Augustinus in De [p. 447] Mirabilibus Scripture Sacre, III° capitulo. Tota, inquit, iusticia, hec est virginitas, sacerdocium et martirium. Que triplex iusticia in Abel primo fuit qui et munera Deo placita obtulit, prosapiam generis in seculo non dereliquit et martirii coronam sanguinis effusione promeruit. Quilibet ergo salvandus debet esse spiritualiter virgo in hoc quod declinans a malo sequitur agnum sponsum ecclesie; debet secundo esse sacerdos, offerens Deo fructum meriti, et debet tercio connectendo hec duo ad invicem in istis perseverare finaliter tamquam martir. Et ideo signanter notat Christus Matthei XXIII°, 35 quomodo ecclesia dicitur cepisse ab Abel iusto cui secundum Augustinum Jesus commisit primatum iusticie. From which it is clear that that power was infinitely stronger than that possession and did more for the profit of the church.[^12] For otherwise Christ, infinitely more powerful than Caesar, having disciples more prudent for ministering the goods of fortune, would be notably to be rebuked for not having endowed the church more than Caesar. For this would then have been better, because of the lesser avarice of the world, and consequently, from what has been said, as good as the power that Christ had.

ENGLISH: Sixthly, it seems that no cleric should contend about his [greatness]{g:majoritas} over his subjects in the ecclesiastical hierarchy; [464] for a man having a revelation of it would not contend about it, but would think humbly of himself; if he has no revelation of this, since he is then ignorant whether he is of holy mother church, he would contend in vain whether he is greater in that church, since he lacks proof whether he is of that church (as was touched on in the first [conclusion]{g:conclusio}).[^13] And greatness in the church and not of it would be useless to the one contending, and contemptible to the church, as something irrelevant or contrary. And from these things it seems that it would be too great a madness to urge the church to believe that every [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex} is [head]{g:caput} of his church; for according to what has been said, any such [pontiff] could be a [bishop]{g:episcopus} in the church without being of it.[^14] As, then, it is not of faith that every Roman pontiff is [predestinate]{g:predestinatus}, so it is not of the substance of faith that he is head or member of holy mother church. And it is clear that it does not follow that faith in the right sacramental ministry of the [priest]{g:sacerdos} perishes in lay people; for it suffices for the believer to believe the catholic faith, and that God, for no error of a spiritual [superior]{g:prepositi}, damages the subject unless his own demerit is the cause.[^15] The subject, then, ought not to despair over the sin of the superior; for, however bad the priest may be, provided the subject believes in Christ, he will be saved, and the damnable ministry of the sacrament by his [curate]{g:curatus} will profit him, as the decree shows under the authority of Augustine, in De consecratione, distinction 4: "In the way," he says, "that many wolves also imprint that very [character]{g:carecter} on wolves who seem to be within; yet they do not belong to that sheep which is one out of many."[^16]
