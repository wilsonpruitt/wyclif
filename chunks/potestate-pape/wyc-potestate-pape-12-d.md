# *De potestate pape*, chapter 12, part 4: not every pope antichrist; canonization a matter of opinion

## ¶1 · 328.21–329.17

> [p. 328] Sed absit fidelem credere, si papa sit Antichristus, tunc omnis papa est Antichristus, ut magnus Antichristus fuit Cayfas, et tamen non omnis episcopus; magnus Antichristus fuit Nero et tamen non omnis secularis dominus; magnus Antichristus fuit Sergius et tamen non omnis monachus. Et ita in mediis opinabilibus stat duos meritorie credere expresse contraria et credulitatem fidelis propter evidencias variari, ut de sanctis canonizatis credo quosdam absque formidine quia sunt beati in celo, ut apostolos, martyres, confessores primitive ecclesie; qui non [p. 329] sunt victi cum mundo, alios autem opinor vel reputo beatos, ut posteriores de quorum vita habeo ad hoc evidenciam, quia narracio sanccionis vocati miraculi vel canonizacio Romane curie est nimis tenuis ad fundandum fidem ut mortuus sit in cathalogo beatorum. Potest enim in talibus vocatis miraculis esse multiplex seduccio, et potest curia Romana esse veritatis ignara ex seduccione testium qui deponunt, ymo potest esse cecata propter pecuniam vel favorem. Ideo absit fidelem credere quod sit articulus fidei credendus quicquid papa in canonizacione, in absolucione vel bullari attestacione decreverit. Cum papa attestatur suam crebram decepcionem in talibus, et Deus non est auctor vel fautor mendacii, ideo absit ecclesiam premi serviliter tali fide.

But God forbid that a believer should believe that, if a [pope]{g:papa} is Antichrist, then every pope is Antichrist: as Caiaphas was a great Antichrist, and yet not every [bishop]{g:episcopus}; Nero was a great Antichrist, and yet not every [secular lord]{g:seculares-domini}; Sergius was a great Antichrist, and yet not every monk.[^1] And so in middle matters open to opinion it can stand that two persons meritoriously believe expressly contrary things, and that a believer's [credence]{g:credulitas} varies on account of the [evidence]{g:evidencia}:[^2] as, of the [canonized]{g:canonizare} saints, I believe some without [misgiving]{g:formido} to be blessed in heaven, such as the apostles, martyrs and confessors of the primitive [church]{g:ecclesia}, who were not [329] overcome with the world; but others I opine or reckon blessed, such as later ones of whose life I have evidence to that end; for the narrative of the sanctioning of a [so-called]{g:vocatus} miracle, or the canonization of the [Roman curia]{g:curia-romana}, is too slender to ground faith that a dead man is in the catalogue of the blessed. For in such so-called miracles there can be manifold deception, and the Roman curia can be ignorant of the truth through the deception of the witnesses who depose; indeed, it can be blinded by money or favour. Therefore God forbid that a believer should believe that whatever the pope has decreed in a canonization, in an absolution, or in an attestation by bull is an [article of faith]{g:articulus-fidei} to be believed. Since the pope attests his own frequent deception in such matters, and God is not the author or abettor of a lie, therefore God forbid that the church be servilely weighed down with such a faith.[^3]

## ¶2 · 329.18–329.32

> [p. 329] In istis igitur licet opinari vel reputare unam partem vel aliam, secundum quod evidencie variantur. Cavere tamen debemus ne racione peccati in evidenciis seducamur, nec quod nimis sollicitemur in talibus preter fidem. Multi enim seducuntur per evidencias captas a curia et multi dimittunt propter questum, affeccionem carnalem vel cecum favorem cognoscere et colere fidem Christi. Alii autem faciunt raciones topicas ad assenciendum quod pseudopapa sit potissimus Antichristus, primo per hoc quod quicumque homo est profundior in inferno, fit pro tempore vie profundior Antichristus, sed aliquis papa a probabili post dotacionem ecclesie meruit esse profundior Antichristus.

In these matters, therefore, one may opine or reckon one side or the other, according as the evidence varies. Yet we must beware lest by reason of sin we be led astray in the evidence, and that we be not over-anxious in such things beyond faith. For many are led astray by evidence taken from the curia, and many, for gain, carnal affection or blind favour, leave off knowing and cultivating the faith of Christ. And others make topical arguments for assenting that a [pseudo-pope]{g:pseudopapa} is the foremost Antichrist:[^4] first by this, that whatever man is deeper in hell becomes, for the time of the [way]{g:via}, a deeper Antichrist; now some pope, in all probability, since the [endowment]{g:dotacio} of the church, has deserved to be a deeper Antichrist.[^5]

## ¶3 · 329.33–330.2

> [p. 329] Assumptum patet ex hoc, quod correspondenter ad peccati gravedinem est prescitus profundius in inferno; sed constat quod quanta est [p. 330] gravedo peccati est ipsa contra veritatem, que est Christus.

The [assumption]{g:assumptum} is clear from this, that the [foreknown]{g:prescitus} man is deeper in hell in correspondence with the gravity of his sin; now it is evident that as great as is the [330] gravity of a sin, so far is it against the truth, which is Christ.[^6]

## ¶4 · 330.3–330.6

> [p. 330] Quod confirmatur tripliciter, primo per hoc quod quanto gradus est alcior, ideo casu supposito secundum legem datam de Lucifero est gravior.

This is confirmed in three ways: first by this, that the higher the degree, by so much, the case being supposed, according to the law given concerning Lucifer, is [the sin] graver.[^7]

## ¶5 · 330.7–330.12

> [p. 330] Similiter, omnis talis papa est mendacissimus et blasfemus ypocrita, quod prima Veritas summe odit; papa igitur talis ex gravedine obligacionis, ex superaddita blasfemia ypocrisi et ex ducatu innumerosi populi, quem ducit proditorie contra Christum, videtur esse gravius condempnandus.

Likewise, every such pope is a most lying and blasphemous hypocrite, which the first Truth supremely hates; such a pope, therefore, by reason of the gravity of his obligation, of the blasphemy added on top of hypocrisy, and of his leading of a numberless people, whom he leads treacherously against Christ, seems to deserve the heavier condemnation.[^8]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 328.21: Because one pope is Antichrist and does not follow that all popes are.
- 329.3: We need not believe all canonized to be Saints.
- 329.18: In such matters we must depend on evidence.
- 330.3: The higher the degree the greater the sin.
- 330.7: A wicked pope is the worst of hypocrites.

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** Sergius: the monk whom medieval polemic made the teacher of Muhammad (the Bahira legend); so Wyclif himself below, 374.28–30, where Muhammad's two accomplices are a Jew learned in the Old Law and 'Sergius, a crafty Christian monk' (LEDGER P12d-1). Each pair answers the inference from one to all: one wicked holder of an office does not make every holder of it wicked.
[^2]: **C.** *Stat* with the infinitive is the schoolmen's "it can stand", i.e. it is consistent. *Media opinabilia* are matters between what faith requires and what it forbids.
[^3]: **C.** *Suam crebram decepcionem*: his being often deceived, not his deceiving. Probably the papal decretal *A nobis* (X 5.39.28, Innocent III): the church's judgement 'follows opinion, which often happens both to deceive and to be deceived' (LEDGER P12d-2).
[^4]: **C.** *Potissimus*: "foremost", the superlative of *potis*; the chapter's *pocior Antichristus* (331.12) is its comparative, "greater".
[^5]: **C.** The minor premiss says "a deeper Antichrist" where the major's middle term ("deeper in hell") is expected; perhaps *in inferno* is understood, perhaps lost. We translate the print.
[^6]: **C.** *Quanta est gravedo peccati est ipsa contra veritatem*: the measure of a sin's gravity is how far it is against the truth; since the truth is Christ, the graver sinner is the more anti-Christ.
[^7]: **C.** *Quanto … ideo*: *ideo* stands where *tanto* would answer *quanto*. The "law given concerning Lucifer" is his fall from the highest degree (Isa. 14:12–15, by allusion); the other two confirmations follow at 330.7 and 330.13 (Pseudo-Augustine, Chrysostom) and 331.13 (Lincoln).
[^8]: **C.** *Superaddita blasfemia ypocrisi*: *ypocrisi* taken as dative ("added on top of hypocrisy"); it could be a second ablative ("from added blasphemy, from hypocrisy").
