# *De ecclesia*, chapter 21, part 4: the second fabrication: Bonaventure on distinct characters in the several orders

## ¶1 · 502.10–503.31

> [p. 502] Quarto arguitur per hoc quod caracter est signaculum perpetuum, manens in anima separata, ergo non debet imprimi nisi ad actum perpetuum; sed actus ministerialis istorum ordinum inferiorum non est perpetuus. Ergo etc. Dicitur, inquit, quod non oportet ut maneat semper quantum ad usum ministerii quod iste usus vel abusus convertitur in premium vel mercedem. Unde secundo querit et tenet quod in diversis ordinibus diversi imprimuntur caracteres distincti, et hoc specifice, sicut ordines, ut oportet eum dicere, et arguendo quadrupliciter ad oppositum respondet. Dicendum, inquit, quod inter opinantes in omnibus ordinibus esse caracterem duplex opinio esse consuevit. Quidam enim dicere volunt quod omnes ordines in uno caractere uniuntur; differunt tamen secundum maiorem implecionem et minorem, sicut si sigillum imprimatur in cera tenui, deinde forcius vel amplius, paulative procedendo secundum plus et plus quousque perveniatur ad statum perfectum. Si autem queras que sit racio talis impressionis, respondeo breviter quod hoc est propter [p. 503] caracteris imperfeccionem; et tripliciter reprobat istam opinionem satis vivaciter. Primo, inquit, si hoc esset, tunc esset impossibile promoveri ad sequentem ordinem dimisso priori; quod est contra iura. Item, tunc non esset differencia inter ordines nisi secundum maius et minus; quod est manifeste falsum, cum potestates in diversis ordinibus sunt ad diversa ordinate. Et tercio, si caracter sic susciperet magis, per idem posset suscipere minus, et per consequens foret delebilis, et per idem caracteres aliquorum sacramentorum magis et minus reciperent, ut dicatur baptizatus vel confirmatus magis et minus; quod est omnino inconveniens. Ergo et posicio. Alia possunt fieri argumenta quod per idem caracter baptismi crescere posset ad qualemcunque caracterem. Oportet enim in casu concedere quod duorum acolitorum caracter sacerdotalis fuit impressus in uno, et in alio non, posito quod alter cresceret in posterum in sacerdotem et alter maneat tantum acolitus, et foret confusio in sacerdocio secundum magis et minus. Ideo dicit secunda opinio melius quod in diversis ordinibus imprimuntur diversi caracteres, eo quod non solum qui ad Dei cultum assumuntur, distinguuntur ab aliis, ymmo inter ministrantes est ordo atque distinccio, et ulterius gradus illi et potestates ministrandi, si simul dantur, nunquam auferuntur, eciamsi ad alcius ministerium elevatur qui in minori ministraverat; et ideo in diversis ordinibus diversa sigilla debent imprimi secundum que nobis diversitas potestatum ministrandi et perpetuitas innotescat; tale autem signaculum est caracter.

Fourthly it is [argued by appeal to]{g:arguitur-per} this: "that a [character]{g:caracter} is a perpetual [seal]{g:signaculum}, remaining in the separated soul; therefore it ought not to be imprinted except for a perpetual act; now the ministerial act of these lower orders is not perpetual." Therefore etc. "[The answer is]{g:dicitur}," he says, "that it need not remain always as to the use of the ministry, [but in this,] that this use or abuse is turned into reward or wage."[^1] Hence he asks secondly, and holds, that "in diverse orders diverse distinct characters are imprinted, and this specifically, as the orders [are], as he must say"; and, having argued in four ways to the opposite, he answers.[^2] "It must be said," he says, "that among those who hold that there is a character in all orders there have customarily been two opinions. For some wish to say that all the orders are united in one character, yet differ according to a greater and a lesser filling, as if a seal were impressed in wax lightly, then more strongly or more fully, proceeding little by little by more and more until the perfect state is reached. And if you ask what is the reason of such an impression, I answer briefly that this is on account of the [503] imperfection of the character."[^3] And he rejects this opinion in three ways, quite vigorously. "First," he says, "if this were so, then it would be impossible to be promoted to the following order with the earlier passed over; which is against the laws. Again, then there would be no difference between the orders except according to greater and less; which is manifestly false, since the powers in diverse orders are ordered to diverse things. And thirdly, if the character thus admitted more, by the same token it could admit less, and consequently would be delible; and by the same token the characters of some sacraments would receive more and less, so that a man might be called more or less baptized or confirmed; which is an utter [absurdity]{g:inconveniens}." Therefore the position [is absurd] too.[^4] Other arguments can be made, that by the same token the character of baptism could grow into any character whatever. For in the case [put] one would have to grant that, of two acolytes, the priestly character was imprinted in the one and not in the other, supposing that the one should grow afterwards into a priest and the other remain only an acolyte; and there would be confusion in the priesthood according to more and less.[^5] Therefore the second opinion says better that "in diverse orders diverse characters are imprinted, because not only are those who are taken up to the worship of God distinguished from the others, but indeed among those who minister there is order and distinction; and further, those grades and powers of ministering, if they are given together, are never taken away, even if one who had ministered in a lesser [ministry] is raised to a higher ministry; and therefore in diverse orders diverse seals ought to be imprinted, by which the diversity of the powers of ministering, and their perpetuity, may become known to us; now such a seal is a character."[^6]

## ¶2 · 504.1–506.12

> [p. 504] Ideo in diversis ordinibus imprimuntur diversi caracteres, et concedende sunt raciones hoc ostendentes. Ad primum in oppositum quo sic arguitur: in sacramento baptismi non imprimitur nisi unus caracter; similiter in sacramento confirmacionis, ergo pari racione in sacramento ordinis. Dicendum, inquit, quod non est simile pro eo quod non ponit ita diversitatem, sicut ponit ordinis sacramentum quod de sui racione implicat diversitatem graduum. Ad secundum quo sic arguitur sacramentum ordinis essencialius respicit caracterem quam aliquod aliud sacramentum in quo caracter imprimitur; si ergo ordo unum est sacramentum quantumcunque plurificetur, videtur quod unus caracter in eo imprimitur. Dicendum quod verum est, sed nonnisi prout comparatur ad ulterius signaculum. Sicut ergo una litera est scripta et due et tres et quattuor, et tamen multarum literarum aggregacio ad unam sentenciam exprimendam non est nisi una scriptura: sic in proposito intelligitur quod omnis talis figuracio ordinatur ad hoc quod ei inscribatur signaculum secundum quod homo Dei minister perfectus esse iudicatur, et cum sacramentum dicat quid ad alterum, et eciam caracteres uniuntur secundum quid ad alterum, ideo plures caracteres unum faciunt sacramentum. Tercio sic arguitur: Si plures caracteres in eadem anima imprimuntur, aut ergo differunt specie vel solum numero; non secundo modo, quia oportet recipiens denudari a natura rei recepte, nec primo modo, quia ex diversis secundum formam et speciem manentibus non fit unum nisi unum materiale. Ad illud dicendum, inquit, quod dupliciter est loqui de [p. 505] caracteribus vel in se vel prout uniuntur in unitate et integritate subiecti. Si in se, sic differunt, sicut diverse species numeri, si secundo modo, tunc sicut binarius et quaternarius constituunt senarium et habent racionem partis constitutive et complementum ponitur in senario, sic intelligendum est in proposito suo modo, licet non sit omnino simile. Quarto arguitur per hoc quod natura non facit per plura quod facere potest per unum, ergo autor nature non imprimit in ordinato multos caracteres, cum solum unus sufficit, sicut evenit in baptismo. Que ergo necessitas ponendi in ordine tam distinctos caracteres? Dicendum, inquit, quod hoc verum est, quando aliquid potest per unum ita complete fieri ut per plura; hoc autem non fit in proposito pro eo quod oportet ordinatos non solum distingui ab aliis, sicut fit caracter baptismi, sed oportet ipsos distingui plus quam materiali differencia in se ipsis. Expergiscat rogo auditorium et consideret si iste locus et sermo doctorum dat efficax testimonium ad fantasiandum quod solum in ordine sacerdotali caracter imprimitur, cum tam spissim manifeste testatur oppositum. Et revera homo cui ars menciendi non foret laudabilis verecundaretur de tam patente mendacio. Ille autem qui vel intenderit omnia dicta sua capi yronice vel mendacium sibi ascriberet ad honorem, daret occasionem ut non credatur amplius allegacioni vel testimonio suo eciam quicquid iuraverit. Ex primo igitur commento colligitur quod doctor iste vel falsificat multipliciter testem suum, vel quod testificacio sequens sit omnino mendosa et nimis inutilis, cum ipse et testis suus tam crebro et tam patule contradicunt. Quomodo queso invocarem racionabiliter suffragium testimonii [p. 506] doctoris in eadem opinione et eodem libro quem in eadem opinione implico cencies esse mendacem? per inde quidem foret allegandus dyabolus in testem sentencie, cum notum sit quod ex cautela et subtilitate ingenii plurima vera dicit. Et hec racio quare quietor in allegacione scripture que mentiri non poterit et sanctorum doctorum quorum sentenciam volo ex integro sustinere. Alios autem doctores nolo sic allegare nisi arguendo ad hominem, cui foret talis doctoris testificacio argumentum, et sic allegarem contra mendacem oratorem dyaboli ipsum patrem mendacii, sed non ut sit mihi testis ydoneus.

[504] "Therefore in diverse orders diverse characters are imprinted, and the reasons showing this are to be granted. To the first in opposition, by which it is argued thus: In the sacrament of baptism only one character is imprinted; likewise in the sacrament of confirmation; therefore by parity of reason in the sacrament of order. It must be said," he says, "that the case is not alike, because it does not posit diversity in the way that the sacrament of order posits it, which by its own nature implies a diversity of grades.[^7] To the second, by which it is argued thus: The sacrament of order regards the character more essentially than any other sacrament in which a character is imprinted; if then order is one sacrament, however much it be multiplied, it seems that one character is imprinted in it. It must be said that this is true, but only as it is compared to a further seal. Just as, then, one letter is written, and two, and three, and four, and yet the aggregate of many letters for expressing one [sentence]{g:sentencia} is only one writing: so in the matter at hand it is understood that every such figuring is ordered to this, that there be inscribed in it the seal according to which a man is judged to be a perfect minister of God; and since a sacrament signifies something relative to another, and the characters too are united as something relative to another, therefore several characters make one sacrament."[^8] Thirdly it is argued thus: "If several characters are imprinted in the same soul, then either they differ in species or only in number. Not in the second way, since the recipient must be stripped of the nature of the thing received; nor in the first way, since from things that remain diverse in form and species nothing one is made except a material one.[^9] To that," he says, "it must be said that there are two ways of speaking of [505] characters: either in themselves, or as they are united in the unity and integrity of the subject. If in themselves, then they differ as diverse species of number; if in the second way, then, just as two and four make up six, and have the nature of a constitutive part, and the completion is placed in the six, so it is to be understood in the matter at hand in its own way, though it is not altogether alike."[^10] Fourthly it is argued by appeal to this: "that nature does not do by many what it can do by one; therefore the author of nature does not imprint many characters in one who is ordained, since one alone suffices, as happens in baptism. What need is there, then, of positing such distinct characters in order? It must be said," he says, "that this is true when something can be done as completely by one as by many; but this is not so in the matter at hand, because the ordained must not only be distinguished from others, as is done by the character of baptism, but they must be distinguished among themselves by more than a material difference."[^11] Let the audience wake up, I beg, and consider whether this passage and discourse of the doctors gives effectual testimony for fantasizing that a character is imprinted only in the priestly order, since it testifies the opposite so thickly and so manifestly.[^12] And truly a man to whom the art of lying were not praiseworthy would be ashamed of so open a lie. And he who either should intend all his sayings to be taken ironically, or should ascribe a lie to himself as an honour, would give occasion that no more credit be given to his [allegation]{g:allegacio} or testimony, whatever he may swear. From the first [fabrication]{g:commentum}, therefore, it is gathered that this doctor either falsifies his witness in many ways, or that the testimony that follows is altogether lying and quite useless, since he and his witness contradict each other so often and so openly.[^13] How, I ask, could I reasonably invoke the [aid]{g:suffragium} of the testimony [506] of a doctor, in the same opinion and the same book, whom in the same opinion I convict a hundred times of being a liar? On the same footing, indeed, the devil would have to be alleged as a witness to a teaching, since it is known that from [wile]{g:cautela} and subtlety of wit he says very many true things. And this is the reason why I rest in the allegation of scripture, which cannot lie, and of the holy doctors, whose teaching I wish to sustain in its entirety. But other doctors I will not allege in this way except by [arguing to the man]{g:argumentum-ad-hominem}, for whom such a doctor's testimony would be an argument; and so I might allege against the devil's lying [advocate]{g:orator} the father of lies himself, but not so that he should be a fit witness for me.[^14]

## ¶3 · 506.13–506.20

> [p. 506] In isto autem secundo commento absque tergiversacione patet primo mendacium allegantis; patet secundo quod non solvitur argumentum quod feci supponendo descripcionem clerici, per hoc quod mendaciter allegatur quod solum in sacerdocio caracter ordinis imprimatur; et tercio quod ista sentencia conviccionis mendacii foret medium doctori isti mihi et aliis cavendi caucius de mendosis allegacionibus in futurum.

And in this second fabrication, without evasion, there is clear, first, the lie of the one who alleges; it is clear, secondly, that the argument I made by supposing the [definition]{g:descripcio} of the [cleric]{g:clericus} is not answered by the lying allegation that the character of order is imprinted only in the priesthood; and thirdly, that this sentence of conviction for lying would be a means for this doctor, for me and for others, of being more wary of lying allegations in future.[^15]

## ¶4 · 506.21–506.30

> [p. 506] Scola quidem mendacii est per se a Deo et tota ecclesia detestanda dicente venerabili Anshelmo primo: Cur Deus homo cap. XII°: Non, inquit, sequitur si Deus vult mentiri, iustum est mentiri (supple si non ex possibili), sed pocius sequitur dominum illum non esse. Nam nequaquam potest velle mentiri voluntas, nisi in qua corrupta est veritas, ymmo que deserendo veritatem corrupta est. Quid ergo sequitur ex ista responsione mendacii? Revera mendacium mendacio cumulatum.

The school of lying is indeed in itself to be detested by God and the whole [church]{g:ecclesia}, as the venerable Anselm says, [book] I of *Cur Deus homo*, chapter 12: "It does not follow," he says, "if God wills to lie, that it is just to lie" (supply: if not from a possible [antecedent]), "but rather it follows that that Lord does not exist. For in no way can a will will to lie, except one in which truth is corrupted, indeed one which by deserting truth has been corrupted." What then follows from this answer of the lie? Truly, lie heaped upon lie.[^16]

[^1]: **C.** Bonaventure's fourth objection to his first question and its answer, continuing 500.26–502.9 (chunk 21-c). *Premium vel mercedem*: reward (of those who used the order well) or wage (of those who abused it), as at 502.8–9. **T.** As printed, *non oportet ut maneat … quod iste usus … convertitur* does not construe; the print shows no loss and Loserth marks none; a word such as *sed* is wanted before *quod*, and "[but in this,]" is our supply. B *ministralis* for *ministerialis* (502.13). **R.** A in the margin at 502.14: *Responsio*.

[^2]: **S.** Bonaventure, *In IV Sent.* d. 24, p. 2, a. 1, whether diverse characters are imprinted in diverse orders (Loserth, 503.23: "Bonav. l. c. pag. 652 seqq."; LEDGER E21d-1). **C.** *Specifice, sicut ordines*: the characters differ in species, as the orders do. *Ut oportet eum dicere*: italic in the print, so Loserth takes it as Bonaventure's own words ('as one must say' of him who holds it). The print's italics run on through *et*. *Arguendo quadrupliciter ad oppositum*: Bonaventure sets out four arguments for the opposite and answers them (504.2–505.17). **T.** B *diversis imprimuntur* (502.18). **R.** A in the margin at 502.17: *Opinio*.

[^3]: **C.** *Opinantes in omnibus ordinibus esse caracterem*: the holders of the third opinion of the first question (500.19–25). *Secundum maiorem implecionem et minorem*: by a greater and a lesser filling-up, as the impression of the seal grows. *Respondeo*: the first person is that of the first opinion as Bonaventure reports it. **T.** Bonaventure reads *complexionem* (Loserth, 502.25). **R.** A in the margin at 502.23 and 502.25: *Duplex opinio*; *Prima*.

[^4]: **C.** *Dimisso priori*: with the earlier order passed over, as in ordination to a higher order without the lower. *Contra iura*: against the canons. *Inconveniens*: "absurdity", the absurd consequence in argument (CONTEXTUAL row). *Ergo et posicio*: elliptical, "therefore the position [is absurd] too". **T.** AA₁ lack *satis vivaciter* (503.2); B lacks *reciperent … minus* (503.11).

[^5]: **C.** Wyclif's own addition to Bonaventure's three arguments: the print sets it in roman (plate n0540). *In casu*: in the case supposed. The point: if characters differed only by degree, the acolyte's character would already be the priestly one in whichever acolyte later became a priest.

[^6]: **S.** Bonaventure, ibid. [^7]: **C.** *Non est simile*: the case of baptism and confirmation is unlike that of order; the subject of *non ponit* is taken to be either of those sacraments. *De sui racione*: by its own nature. **R.** A in the margin at 504.6: *Responsio*.

[^8]: **C.** *Prout comparatur ad ulterius signaculum*: order is one only as it is related to a further seal, the priestly character, which completes the minister. *Sentencia*: here a "sentence" of writing, not a teaching (CONTEXTUAL row). *Dicat quid ad alterum … secundum quid ad alterum*: a sacrament signifies something relative, and the characters are one by relation; *secundum quid* here is not the opposite of *simpliciter*. **T.** B *purificetur* for *plurificetur* (504.13–14). The codices read *sciencie figuracio* (504.20) and *Sed quidem ad alterum* (504.23), and AA₁ lack *secundum quid … caracteres*; the print is translated. [^9]: **C.** *Oportet recipiens denudari a natura rei recepte*: the recipient must lack the nature of what it receives, so one soul cannot receive two characters of the same species. *Nisi unum materiale*: only a material unity, a heap. **T.** AA₁ *ac ergo* (504.27); B *unum sit* (504.31); the codices *Ad aliud* for *Ad illud* (504.31–32). **R.** A in the margin at 504.32: *Responsio*.
