## Doubtful, left as they are

- **03-e, p. 48**: *Quod autem Magister Distinctione 22 aliter loquitur*, rendered "And that the Master". Either (c), continuing the reply, or (b), setting aside the counter-authority ("But that the Master speaks otherwise"). "And" is defensible.
- **04-a, p. 49**: *Sed Doctor Subtilis dilatat … amplius*, rendered "But". The sense is "and further", Scotus widening Bonaventure's point, not opposing it; but *sed* introducing a new authority reads as (b) here, and "but" is the card's rendering.
- **04-c, p. 57**: *Sed concesso consequente … sequitur*, rendered "Now, the consequent granted". (a), the next step of the reductio; (b) "But" would mistake it for an objection. "Now" kept.
- **04-d, p. 64**: *Sed quia putrefaccio … non profuisset …: ideo inconsonum … fuisset*, rendered "But because … therefore". The *ideo* closes the *quia* clause inside one sentence, not a syllogism whose minor the *sed* brings, so (b): lying dead set against decaying. "But" kept.
