# Blind review: *Dialogus* chunks 02, 14, 30, versions A and B

Checked sentence by sentence against the Latin, using CARD.md (the four error classes and the
connectives table) and GLOSSARY.md. Classes: **1** terms · **2** connectives · **3** construal ·
**4** a note that misdescribes the Latin or the English. Severity: **error** means the English says
something the Latin doesn't, or drops a step of the argument. **weak** means defensible but loses a
nuance the argument uses. **style** covers prose, tagging mechanics and layout. Differences in the
notes' bibliographic detail (editions, columns, locators) are ignored, as instructed. Line numbers are
Pollard's, placed from the side-notes and the notes' own locators, so ±1 in places.

**Verdict: equivalent.** Neither version has a construal error, and both handle the hard points

little stiffer. The margin is too small to count.

## Chunk 14 (26.28–28.30): prayers to saints and implicit faith

| ver. | page.line | Latin | English | cl. | sev. | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 27.4 | *cum spes meriti … est fundanda* | "For hope of merit must be founded …" (new sentence) | 2 | style | *Cum* causal should be "since", and "For" is *enim*'s word. N. 2 explains the choice, so the logic is kept. |
| A | 27.23 | *ideo est necessarium habere alias personas mediantes* | "and that therefore it is necessary …" | 3 | style | Both versions rightly keep this inside the false belief. A adds "and that" without brackets and has no C note. B brackets "[and]" and gives n. 4. |
| A | 28.16 | *aliqua est fides explicita vel expressa et aliqua est fides implicita sive tacita* | "there is some explicit faith, or express, and some implicit faith, or tacit" | – | style | Awkward. B: "some faith is explicit or expressed and some faith is implicit or tacit". |

| B | 27.2 | *ad quid ergo diceremus de illis* | "To what purpose, then, should we speak of them" | 3 | weak | *Dicere de* + saint means to say [a mass or office] of him, which picks up the *missis … letaniis* just before. | B | 27.4 | *…beati? cum spes …* | "…homeland? since the hope …" | – | style | A lower-case clause after a question mark. |
| B | 27.14 | *suffragari* | "[to aid]{g:suffragari} us wretches" | 1 | weak | GLOSSARY has "to aid by intercession". The English drops "by intercession", which is the half that makes this about the saints. N. 2 restores it, but only in the note. |
| B | 27.24 | *immediate illabitur viatori … immediate conjungitur deitati* | "flows immediately into … is immediately joined" | 1/3 | weak | The point is "without a mediator", against *personas mediantes*. English "immediately" reads as time. A: "without intermediary", with n. 4 saying it is not temporal. |
| B | 27.24 | *assistens pontifex* | "being come a high priest" | – | style | Not in quotation marks, and it departs from DR "an high priest" (card: quotations in marks, DR wording). |
| B | 27.26 | *nullus sanctus est misericordius aut facilius auditivus miseri* | "more merciful, or more ready to hear a wretch" | 3 | weak | *Misericordius* is the adverb (the adjective with *sanctus* would be *misericordior*), and both adverbs modify *auditivus*. A: "more mercifully or more readily disposed to hear". |
| B | 28.1 | (one ¶, 26.28–28.30) | English split into two paragraphs at "Therefore it is granted" | – | style | The card says never split a paragraph. |
| B | 28.16–19 | *fides explicita … implicita* | tag on the third mention; `g:implicita` | 1 | style | The tag should go on the first occurrence ("some faith is explicit"). |
| B | 28.22 | *Ut carnis resurreccionis est articulus fidei* | "As, there is an article of faith of the resurrection of the flesh" | – | style | The construal is right (see A above), but the comma after "As" makes the English stumble. |
| B | 28.28 | *erit tempore sempiterno adhuc futura* | "will be, in everlasting time, still future … after the day of judgement it is past" | 3 | weak | This translates the print. That leaves an English that contradicts itself ("in everlasting time still future" / "past"), even though a variant (*est in*, B B¹ C) is available and given in n. 11. **Counts.** A: 0 errors, 1 weak, 3 style. B: 0 errors, 5 weak, 5 style.

**Verdict: A is moderately better.** Every one of B's weak points is where the argument lives:

- **Mediation.** The mediator-against-no-mediator contrast (*immediate* against *mediantes*) is blurred.
- **Liturgy.** The liturgical sense of *dicere de* is lost.
- **Intercession.** The "by intercession" half of *suffragari* is dropped.
- **Grammar.** The adverb *misericordius* is misconstrued.

A's one weak point is a note that wrongly calls the genitive faulty; its English is unaffected. B's
note there is the better one, and its explicit/implicit-faith sentence is better prose.

**Verdict: A is clearly better.** B's weak points bunch at the logical joints:

- *Stat*.
- *Licet*.
- *Non est evidencia asinina*.
- *Inductiva*.
- *Simealis*, which drops the jibe that 68.27 answers.

It also misses several §B–C tags or puts them on the wrong word. A's losses are mostly *cum* rendered
"For" and a few sentence splits. It has one real construal slip, the 66.21–24 question limb, where B
is right. B's notes identify some sources A doesn't (Gal. 2:6 at 66.18, and n. 9 on *multo magis*),
and its long periods are closer to Wyclif's shape. But at the difficult cruxes, A's notes do more of
the construal work (*cum habuerunt*, *talem*, *inductiva*, *derelinquerant*, *propter eius
inperfeccionem*).

## Overall verdict

| chunk | A (error / weak / style) | B (error / weak / style) | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 02 | 0 / 0 / 4 | 0 / 0 / 8 | equivalent |
| 14 | 0 / 1 / 3 | 0 / 5 / 5 | A moderately better |
| 30 | 0 / 1 / 8 | 0 / 8 / 14 | A clearly better |
| **total** | **0 / 2 / 15** | **0 / 13 / 27** | **A better** |

Neither version has an outright error: neither says something the Latin doesn't, or drops a step of
the argument. A is the better translation, and its lead grows as the Latin gets harder. It keeps
GLOSSARY's renderings where they carry the argument (*suffragari*, *simealis* "apish", *stat*,
*immediate* "without intermediary"), construes the grammar more exactly (*misericordius*, *licet*,
*servitus istis clericis*, *derelinquerant*), and tags more consistently. A's own recurring
weakness is rendering causal *cum* as "For" and splitting Latin periods. It also has one construal
slip (30, 66.21–24) and one note that misdescribes the Latin (14, n. 9).

On readability as prose, the two are close. A is smoother. B keeps Wyclif's long periods better but
leaves more stumbles ("Correspondingly to", "As, there is", "infinite are the truths", a sentence
fragment at 68.20).
