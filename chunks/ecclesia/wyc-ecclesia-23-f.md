# *De ecclesia*, chapter 23, part 6: against the treasury: the pope's power would reach heaven, purgatory and hell, and the saints' merits are already assigned by God

## ¶1 · 564.32–566.25

> [p. 564] Item, omnibus beatis diffinitum est a Deo premium beatitudinis correspondenter ad omnia merita sua, [p. 565] que adhuc manent in fructu, sed aliqua merita, requisita ad beatitudinem patriarcharum, non subiacent potestati huius Gregorii sic distribuere racione premii ex eis limitati; ergo nec alia. Assumptum patet, tum quia nihil est ociosum in meritis beatorum, tum quia beati habent gaudium et sic premium accidentale de quocunque bono quod fecerint. Minor autem sic ostenditur: In facta distribucione meriti stricte requisiti ad beatitudinem essencialem diminueretur de illa beatitudine correspondenter, quam iniuriam non potest papa facere, sicut nec Christus potest concives suos sic depauperare. Sed dicitur quod meritum beatorum, cum sit spirituale, non excommunicacione tali minuitur sed pocius perficitur. Sed (rogo) ad quid limitantur tunc merita supererogata ultra mensuram debitorum suorum vel superhabundanciam meritorum, cum merita stricte ad beatitudinem requisita eque sufficerent ad distribucionem huius faciendam, aut quomodo est necessarium ponere nunc tantam copiam talium meritorum? Videtur multis fidelibus quod non est modo numerus talium meritorum, vel aliter omnia illa et longe plura si fierent non sufficerent ad aliquem gradum beatitudinis promerentis, nisi quod Deus ex infinitate gracie sue acceptat merita illa de congruo. Item, iuxta istam sentenciam sequitur quod potestas iudiciaria cuiuscunque pape se extendit ad omnes animas in purgatorio et omnes beatos in celo; consequens impossibile, et consequencia sic probatur: papa eciam prescitus habet potestatem ad imperciendum merita bonorum cuicunque et quandocunque voluerit, ut dicit posicio; sed longe minor potestas sufficit ad fundandum iudiciariam potestatem, ergo papa habet iudiciariam potestatem super beatos eciam super Christum. Potest enim facere quod beatus [p. 566] mereatur cuicunque affecto suo quantumcunque voluerit, quia potest dare sibi meritum alterius, quod est idem. Ille enim qui habet potestatem super tam secretum thesaurum anime habet potestatem super personam quoad inferius corporale; in cuius signum Deus dedit dyabolo potestatem primo super substanciam beati Job, secundo super corpus. Verumptamen, inquit, animam eius serva, Job II°, 6. Et multo evidencius sequeretur quod papa preest cuicunque spiritui in purgatorio vel dampnato, et hoc videtur doctorem intendere, cum dicit quod bona communia multitudinis distribuuntur singulis de illa multitudine secundum arbitrium eius qui illi multitudini preest, et multo evidencius sequitur quod papa preest dampnatis, cum preest personis dignioribus, ut puta, purgandis spiritibus et beatis; qui enim habet potestatem cassandi opus dyaboli et tollendi ab eo predam quam tantum appetit, habet potestatem in eum, sed papa habet potestatem ad dandum quantamlibet indulgenciam captivo dyaboli, ne sufficiat amplius valere in eum, ut dicitur. Dicitur et Luce IX° quod Christus dedit discipulis potestatem super omnia demonia, sed nimis ampla foret pape iurisdiccio, qui instar Christi habet potestatem super multitudinem celestium, terrestrium et infernorum.

Again, for all the blessed a reward of blessedness has been defined by God, corresponding to all their merits, [565] which still remain in their fruit; now some merits, required for the blessedness of the patriarchs, are not subject to the power of this Gregory to distribute in this way, by reason of the reward [assigned]{g:limitare} from them; therefore neither are the others.[^1] The [assumption]{g:assumptum} is clear, both because nothing is idle in the merits of the blessed, and because the blessed have joy, and so accidental reward, from whatever good they have done. Now the minor is shown thus: if a distribution were made of merit strictly required for [essential blessedness]{g:beatitudo essencialis}, that blessedness would be diminished correspondingly; which injury the [pope]{g:papa} cannot do, just as Christ cannot so impoverish his fellow citizens.[^2] But it is said that the merit of the blessed, since it is spiritual, is not diminished by such communication but rather perfected. But (I ask) to what end, then, are supererogated merits assigned beyond the measure of their debts, or a superabundance of merits, since the merits strictly required for blessedness would equally suffice for making this distribution? Or how is it necessary to posit now so great a [plenty]{g:copia} of such merits?[^3] It seems to many of the faithful that there is now no number of such merits; or else all those, and far more if they were done, would not suffice for any degree of blessedness for the one meriting, except that God, of the infinity of his grace, accepts those merits [by congruity]{g:de congruo}.[^4] Again, according to this [teaching]{g:sentencia} it follows that the judicial power of any pope whatever extends to all the souls in purgatory and all the blessed in heaven; the [consequent]{g:consequens} is impossible, and the [inference]{g:consequencia} is proved thus: a pope, even a [foreknown]{g:prescitus} one, has power to impart the merits of the good to whomever and whenever he wishes, as the position says; now a far lesser power suffices to ground judicial power; therefore the pope has judicial power over the blessed, even over Christ.[^5] For he can bring it about that a blessed one [566] merits for anyone he favours as much as he wishes, because he can give him another's merit, which is the same thing. For he who has power over so secret a treasure of the soul has power over the person as regards the lower, bodily part; in token of which God gave the devil power first over the substance of blessed Job, secondly over his body: "But yet," he says, "save his life," Job 2:6.[^6] And much more evidently it would follow that the pope is set over every spirit in purgatory or damned; and this the doctor seems to [maintain]{g:intendere}, when he says that the common goods of a multitude are distributed to the single members of that multitude according to the judgment of him who is set over that multitude; and much more evidently it follows that the pope is set over the damned, since he is set over worthier persons, as, say, the spirits to be purged and the blessed.[^7] For he who has power to quash the work of the devil and to take from him the prey which he so desires has power over him; now the pope has power to give an indulgence as great as he pleases to the devil's captive, so that the devil may no longer prevail against him, as it is said. And it is said in Luke 9 that Christ gave the disciples power over all devils; but the pope's jurisdiction would be far too ample, who, like Christ, has power over the multitude of those in heaven, on earth and under the earth.[^8]

## ¶2 · 566.26–566.33

> [p. 566] Ex istis colligitur, cum tot inconveniencia sequuntur ex dicta palliacione potestatis papalis, quod radix istorum inconveniencium foret fidelibus venenosum. Quod autem ista sint inconveniencia, patet primo ex hoc quod secundum apostolum Roman. XIII°, 1 non est potestas nisi a Deo. Ipse autem non reliquit in lege sua papis huiusmodi potestatem, ergo non est fingenda, nam per idem foret infinitum amplianda.

From these things it is gathered, since so many [absurdities]{g:inconveniencia} follow from the said cloaking of papal power, that the root of these absurdities would be a poison to the faithful. And that these are absurdities is clear, first, from this: that according to the apostle, Romans 13:1, "there is no power but from God." Now he did not leave such power to popes in his law; therefore it is not to be feigned; for by the same token it would have to be amplified without end.[^9]

## ¶3 · 566.34–567.13

> [p. 566] Secundo per hoc quod pape singuli citra Christum sunt paris potestatis; cum ergo par in parem non [p. 567] habet imperium, videtur quod papa superstes non habet potestatem super omnes papas in purgatorio et in celo. Illi enim sunt excellenciores quia Deo propiores et cariores. Ideo oportet quod papa superstes serviat eis et non imperatorie concedat quod Petrus vel alius beatus mereatur amico suo quantumcunque sibi placuerit limitare. Et tercio per hoc quod papa superstes non habet tantam potestatem super corpus vel opus proprium, ergo multo magis non super opus Dei tam sibi alienum; homo enim quadam naturalitate dominatur super actibus et operibus propriis, nec autor nature dedit pape potestatem adversantem ordini naturali.

Secondly, by this: that the popes, each one, [short of Christ]{g:citra Christum}, are of equal power; since, then, "an equal has no rule over an equal," [567] it seems that the surviving pope has no power over all the popes in purgatory and in heaven. For they are more excellent, because nearer and dearer to God. Therefore the surviving pope must serve them, and not grant imperiously that Peter or another blessed one should merit for his friend as much as it has pleased him to assign.[^10] And thirdly, by this: that the surviving pope has not so great a power over his own body or work; therefore much more not over the work of God, so foreign to him; for a man by a certain naturalness [exercises lordship]{g:dominari} over his own acts and works, nor did the author of nature give the pope a power adverse to the natural order.[^11]

## ¶4 · 567.14–568.4

> [p. 567] Assumptum patet ex hoc quod tunc posset facere opus suum esse quantumcunque meritorium sibi placuerit, quia limitare beatis quod sibi assisterent quantumcunque voluerit promerendo. Stultus enim foret qui propter fastum vel questum plus benefaceret alteri minus digno, quam eciam benefaceret sibi ipsi, et si dicatur quod potestas non datur ut sic benefaciat sibi ipsi, patet quod Deus dat potestatem solum ad bonum suo predestinato. Ideo daret suo vicario potestatem, ut sibi retribuat tantum bonum. Sed Hostiensis (ut recitavi superius) horrebat istam ficticiam, quia certum videtur quod, si haberet tantam potestatem super demonia et super cunctos subditos suos quoad corpus et animam, haberet eciam potestatem super eorum substanciam, et tunc multi pape (ut creditur) fecissent miracula, mandando spiritibus afferre sibi thesauros absconditos et acquisivissent sibi dominia adeo affectata. Sed (ut innuitur Act. III, 6), quia Petrus non habuit possessionem auri vel argenti, ideo habuit potestatem sanandi [p. 568] infirmum, sed Simon, quia affectabat temporalia, caruit apostolica potestate (ut patet Act. VIII°). Apostoli enim denudati ab affeccione temporalium habuerunt super eis spiritualem potestatem atque dominium.

The assumption is clear from this: that then he could make his own work as meritorious as it pleased him, because [he could] assign to the blessed that they should assist him by meriting as much as he wished. For he would be a fool who, for pride or gain, did more good to another, less worthy, than he would do even to himself; and if it is said that the power is not given that he may so do good to himself, it is clear that God gives power only for the good of his [predestinate]{g:predestinatus}. Therefore he would give his vicar power that he might repay himself so much good.[^12] But Hostiensis (as I recounted above) shuddered at this [fiction]{g:ficticia}, because it seems certain that, if he had so great a power over devils and over all his subjects as regards body and soul, he would also have power over their substance; and then many popes (as is believed) would have done miracles, commanding the spirits to bring them hidden treasures, and would have acquired for themselves the lordships they so [desired]{g:affectare}.[^13] But (as is hinted in Acts 3:6), because Peter had no possession of gold or silver, therefore he had power to heal [568] the sick man; but Simon, because he desired [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, lacked apostolic power (as is clear in Acts 8). For the apostles, stripped of affection for temporal goods, had spiritual power and [lordship]{g:dominium} over them.[^14]

## ¶5 · 568.5–568.24

> [p. 568] Item, secundum doctorem beati non fecerunt determinate huiusmodi superhabundancia opera satisfactoria pro isto vel isto qui remissione indiget, ideo restat ut Deus derelinquat vicario suo in terris distributivam potestatem illius thesauri egenis ecclesie; sed illud videtur multipliciter palliatum, ergo posicioni deficit fundamentum. Minor probatur primo, per hoc quod eque probabiliter contingit sompniare quod tales beati determinate fecerunt ista opera supererogacionis pro illo qui eis participet, sicut quod reliquit papam, quod ipsa distribuat; ideo videtur peti ignotum per ignocius. Secundo quia dicti sancti fecerunt opera sua ex maiori gracia quam fuit illa in qua communiter vivunt pape, ideo verisimile est quod fecerunt ipsa principaliter pro ecclesia, relinquendo distribucionem divino arbitrio; hoc enim est habilitatis, sapiencie et necessitatis. Sed tunc fecissent determinate pro illis ad quos Deus limitat, ergo verisimile est quod meruerunt determinate pro illis, et hoc est certum quoad determinacionem divinam cui oportet stare.

Again, according to the doctor, the blessed did not do such superabundant satisfactory works determinately for this one or that one who needs remission; therefore it remains that God leaves to his vicar on earth the power of distributing that treasury to the needy of the [church]{g:ecclesia}; now that seems cloaked in many ways; therefore the position lacks a foundation.[^15] The minor is proved, first, by this: that one may with equal probability dream that such blessed ones did these works of [supererogation]{g:supererogacio} determinately for the one who shares in them, as that he left the pope to distribute them; therefore the unknown seems to be sought through the more unknown.[^16] Secondly, because the said saints did their works out of a greater grace than that in which popes commonly live, therefore it is likely that they did them principally for the church, leaving the distribution to the divine judgment; for this belongs to [qualification]{g:habilitas}, wisdom and necessity. Now they would then have done them determinately for those to whom God assigns [them]; therefore it is likely that they merited determinately for those, and this is certain as regards the divine determination, by which one must abide.[^17]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 565.1: God has rewarded the Saints according to their merits.
- 565.9: To distribute the merits would be to diminish the rewards.
- 565.21: Merits would not suffice to the Saint himself but for God's grace.
- 565.26: The power claimed makes the Pope judge of all souls, even in purgatory and heaven;
- 566.10: much more in hell.
- 566.30: All powers are of God, who has left no such power to the Pope.
- 567.2: An undue power is claimed for the actual Pope over his predecessors.
- 567.10: The Pope might appropriate all merits to himself;
- 567.26: and would have demons at his service.
- 568.5: We do not know that these merits have not been assigned;
- 568.20: as indeed they probably have been by God.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Item*: the second objection against the teaching ascribed to St Thomas. *Diffinitum est*: has been defined, fixed (cf. row *diffinicio*). *Manent in fructu*: remain in their fruit, the reward. *Huius Gregorii*: Gregory XI, named at 564.15. *Racione premii ex eis limitati*: because a reward has been assigned (row *limitare*) from those merits. *Ergo nec alia*: if not the merits required for the patriarchs' blessedness, then not the others either, since by the major every merit has its reward. **T.** AA₁ *districtum est a Deo* for *diffinitum est a Deo* (564.32). B lacks *requisita … potestati* (565.2). **R.** Side-note 565.1.

[^2]: **C.** *Assumptum*: the major, that God has defined a reward for every merit (row *assumptum*). *Premium accidentale*: accidental reward, beside row *beatitudo accidentalis*. *In facta distribucione*: if a distribution were made. *Concives suos*: his fellow citizens, the blessed. **R.** Side-note 565.9.

[^3]: **T.** The print reads *excommunicacione*, "by such excommunication", which does not construe with the argument (the sharing out of merit). We translate *communicacione*, "communication, sharing" (our emendation). **C.** *Sed dicitur*: the defenders' reply. *Ad quid limitantur*: to what end are assigned (row *limitare*). *Vel superhabundanciam meritorum*: a second subject of *limitantur*. *Copia*: plenty (row).

[^4]: **C.** *Non est modo numerus talium meritorum*: taken as "there are now no such merits to be counted"; it may mean only that their number is not fixed. *Promerentis*: of the one meriting. *De congruo*: by congruity (row): the merits fall short of condign merit, and God accepts them of grace. **R.** Side-note 565.21.

[^5]: **C.** *Potestas iudiciaria*: judicial power. *Consequens … consequencia*: the consequent (row) is denied, then the inference (row) is proved. *Papa eciam prescitus*: even a pope who is one of the foreknown (row *prescitus*). *Ut dicit posicio*: as the position under attack says. **R.** Side-note 565.26.

[^6]: **C.** *Cuicunque affecto suo*: for anyone he favours. *Quod est idem*: to give a blessed one's merit to another is the same as making the blessed one merit for him. *Thesaurum anime*: the treasure of the soul, its merit. *Quoad inferius corporale*: as regards the lower, bodily part. **S.** Job 1:12 (power over all he has) and Job 2:6 (Douay–Rheims, "but yet save his life"); Wyclif's argument takes *animam* as the soul. **T.** The codices read *Job primo*; Loserth corrected it to *II°* (566.8; LEDGER E23f-2).

[^7]: **C.** *Preest*: is set over. *Doctorem*: St Thomas, whose teaching is under attack (564.8). His words are given in indirect speech, from *In IV Sent.* d. 20 q. 1 a. 3 qc. 1 co. (= *Suppl.* q. 25 a. 1), quoted in 23-b (LEDGER E23f-1). *Intendere*: to maintain (row). *Purgandis spiritibus*: the spirits still to be purged. **R.** Side-note 566.10.

[^8]: **C.** *Cassandi opus dyaboli*: to quash the devil's work. *Ne sufficiat amplius valere in eum*: the subject is taken as the devil, who can no longer prevail against his captive. *Ut dicitur*: as the defenders say. *Super multitudinem celestium, terrestrium et infernorum*: the words of Philippians 2:10, "of those that are in heaven, on earth, and under the earth" (Douay–Rheims). **S.** Luke 9:1, "power … over all devils" (Douay–Rheims). **T.** B *Et Luce IX° dicitur*.

[^9]: **C.** *Inconveniencia*: CONTEXTUAL (absurdities / troubles); here absurdities in argument. *Palliacione*: cloaking, disguise. *Radix … venenosum*: a neuter predicate with a feminine subject, "a poisonous thing". *Ipse*: God. *Infinitum amplianda*: to be extended without end. **S.** Romans 13:1 (Douay–Rheims). **R.** Side-note 566.30.

[^10]: **C.** *Pape singuli citra Christum*: every pope, short of Christ (row *citra Christum*). *Par in parem non habet imperium*: the legal maxim, as in Nicholas III's *Exiit qui seminat* (Sext 5.12.3, *quum non habeat imperium par in parem*) (LEDGER E23f-3). *Papa superstes*: the surviving, living pope. *Imperatorie*: imperiously, as an emperor. *Limitare*: to assign (row). **R.** Side-note 567.2.

[^11]: **C.** *Quadam naturalitate dominatur*: by a kind of natural right exercises lordship (row *dominari*, here neutral) over his own acts. *Autor nature*: God.

[^12]: **C.** *Assumptum*: that the surviving pope has no such power over his own work (567.9). It is proved by its consequence: if he had power over the merits of the blessed, he could make his own work as meritorious as he pleased. *Quia limitare*: *posset* is understood. *Propter fastum vel questum*: for pride or gain. *Ut sibi retribuat*: that he might repay himself. **R.** Side-note 567.10. B in the margin at 567.10: "Again, it seems hard to say that such merits of the good in the homeland, which the lord pope claims to impart at his will, still remain unrewarded, at least since the Lord rewards beyond what is condign and punishes short of it."

[^13]: **S.** *Hostiensis (ut recitavi superius)*: Wyclif reported Hostiensis earlier, chapter 22, pp. 519–522 (LEDGER E23f-4). **C.** *Ficticia*: fiction (row). *Substanciam*: their goods, as Job's "substance" above. *Dominia adeo affectata*: lordships so much desired (row *affectare*). **R.** Side-note 567.26.

[^14]: **C.** *Ut innuitur*: as is hinted: Acts 3:6 does not say that Peter's poverty was the cause of his power. *Sanandi infirmum*: to heal the lame man of Acts 3. *Super eis*: taken as over the temporal goods; it may refer to the devils (567.20). **S.** Acts 3:6 (alluded to, "Silver and gold I have none"); Acts 8:18–20 (Simon).

[^15]: **C.** *Item*: a further objection. *Secundum doctorem*: according to St Thomas, *In IV Sent.* d. 20 q. 1 a. 3 qc. 1 co. (= *Suppl.* q. 25 a. 1) (LEDGER E23f-5). *Determinate*: for a definite person. *Distributivam potestatem illius thesauri*: the power to distribute the treasury of the church. *Egenis ecclesie*: to the church's needy. *Palliatum*: cloaked. *Minor*: taken as *illud videtur multipliciter palliatum*. **T.** The codices read *huius*; *huiusmodi* is Loserth's reading (568.6). **R.** Side-note 568.5.

[^16]: **C.** *Pro illo qui eis participet*: for whoever shares in them. *Sicut quod reliquit papam, quod ipsa distribuat*: the subject of *reliquit* is God, and *ipsa* the works. *Peti ignotum per ignocius*: the fault of seeking the unknown through the more unknown.

[^17]: **C.** *Ex maiori gracia*: out of greater grace. *Habilitatis*: of qualification (row *habilitas*): fitness for the task. *Ad quos Deus limitat*: to whom God assigns them. *Cui oportet stare*: by which one must abide. **R.** Side-note 568.20.
