# *De eucharistia*, chapter 9, part 1: the first objection, the decretal of Innocent III

## ¶1 · 272.12–272.22

> [p. 272] Pro complemento huius operis transcurrendum est per septem obiectus, ut fides de eukaristia plus patescat. Et primo notandum quod moderni ferunt graviter quod panis post consecracionem remaneat vel quod sacramentum non sit accidens sine subiecto. Primo ergo arguitur per hoc quod decretalis Innocencii tercii ad cuius edicionem fuerunt mille CCC et XV prelati dicit oppositum. Cum ergo secundum Augustinum nullus crederet evangelio nisi prius credat ecclesie, sequitur quod omnis catholicus debet credere isti decreto tam gravi ecclesie.

To complete this work we must run through seven [objections]{g:objectum}, so that the faith concerning the [Eucharist]{g:eukaristia} may lie more open. And first it is to be noted that the [moderns]{g:moderni} take it hard that the [bread]{g:panis} should remain after [consecration]{g:consecracio}, or that the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} should not be an [accident without a subject]{g:accidens-sine-subiecto}. First, then, [it is argued by appeal to]{g:arguitur-per} this: that the [decretal]{g:decretalis} of Innocent III, at whose issuing there were a thousand three hundred and fifteen [prelates]{g:prelatus}, says the opposite.[^1] Since, then, according to Augustine no one would believe the gospel unless he first believed the [church]{g:ecclesia},[^2] it follows that every catholic ought to believe this [decree]{g:decretum} of so weighty a church.

## ¶2 · 272.23–273.4

> [p. 272] Hic sunt multe instancie; prima loycorum que dicit quod non docetur efficaciter quod ista fuit sentencia dicti Innocencii cum tot prelatis. Secunda quod non docetur quod ipse vel illi fuerunt membra ecclesie, nam solum predestinatus est membrum ecclesie, sed ex tibi dubio nullus istorum fuit predestinatus, ergo [p. 273] ex tibi dubio nullus istorum fuit membrum ecclesie. Qualis ergo racio: Si homo debet credere ecclesie, ergo illis? De ista materia, quomodo fides data ecclesie iniciat fidem catholicam, patet alibi.

Here there are many [objections]{g:instancia}:[^3] the first, the logicians', which says that it is not effectively shown that this was the [teaching]{g:sentencia}[^4] of the said Innocent with so many prelates. The second, that it is not shown that he or they were [members]{g:membrum} of the church; for only a [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} is a member of the church; now, [for all you know]{g:ex-tibi-dubio}, none of these was predestinate; therefore, [273] for all you know, none of these was a member of the church. What sort of reasoning is it, then: "If a man ought to believe the church, therefore [he ought to believe] them"? On this matter, how faith given to the church begins catholic faith, it is clear elsewhere.

## ¶3 · 273.5–273.13

> [p. 273] Tercia instancia est quod transsubstanciacio est, quando una substancia transit in aliam, ut panis fit corpus Christi ad sensum expositum. Et quantum ad illud de existencia accidencium sine subiecto, dictum est quomodo multipliciter potest vere intelligi; et si replicetur quod dicta ecclesia non sic sentit, dicitur quod hoc docto consenciendum est veritati quod dogma foret nimis difficile; ideo hoc habito dicitur quod non est credendum in hoc illi ecclesie.

The third objection is that [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio} is when one [substance]{g:substancia} passes into another, as the bread becomes the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} in the [meaning]{g:sensus} that has been set out.[^5] And as to that [point] about the existence of [accidents]{g:accidens} without a subject, it has been said in how many ways it can [truly]{g:vere} be understood; and if [it is rejoined]{g:replicatur} that the said church does not so [hold]{g:sentire}, the answer is that, once this is shown, one must consent to the truth that the dogma would be [exceedingly]{g:nimis} difficult; so, granting this, the answer is that in this that church is not to be believed.[^6]

## ¶4 · 273.14–274.4

> [p. 273] Unde quarta instancia vere dicit quod nulli ecclesie vel angelo de celo credendum est in materia fidei, nisi de quanto super racione se fundaverit vel fide scripture. Cum ergo sensus ab adversariis expositus neutro istorum modorum fundatur, sequitur quod ille sensus propter nullam ecclesiam est credendus. Nam nullus istorum fuit subtilior quam sancte pagine professor notabiliter errans in ista materia. Quis ergo color, si omnes illi prelati fuerunt sacre pagine professores vel episcopi, quod decretum eorum carebat calumpnia? Nonne Sarraceni, Tartari, ymmo Asiani plures quam mille millia consenciunt in oppositum dicte sentencie, et sicut nos dicimus quod habemus fidem rectam nobisque est credendum, ita dicunt Asiani de se ipsis et contrarium de nobis; ymmo hodie possent colligi multa millia episcoporum et doctorum cum episcopo Avinonico que in ista materia forent contraria veritati; itaque non est color, si tot congregati sic diffinierant, ergo verum et verecundum [p. 274] est illis asserere quod oportet Deum eis assistere sic quod non errent in fide, cum continue sic faciunt et fecerunt. Sic enim fantasiarunt Donatiste coloracius et multi alii infideles.

Hence the fourth objection truly says that no church or angel from heaven is to be believed in a matter of faith except so far as it has founded itself on reason or on the faith of scripture.[^7] Since, then, the meaning set out by the adversaries is founded in neither of these ways, it follows that that meaning is to be believed on account of no church. For none of these was subtler than a professor of the holy page who erred notably in this matter.[^8] What [colour]{g:color},[^9] then, [is there for saying], if all those prelates were professors of the sacred page or [bishops]{g:episcopus}, that their decree was free of [challenge]{g:calumpnia}? Do not Saracens, Tartars, indeed Asians, more than a thousand thousand, agree in the opposite of the said teaching?[^10] And as we say that we have the right faith and are to be believed, so the Asians say of themselves, and the contrary of us. Indeed, today many thousands of bishops and doctors could be gathered with the bishop of Avignon[^11] who in this matter would be contrary to the truth. So there is no colour [in saying]: "If so many gathered together so defined, therefore [it is] true"; and it is shameful [274] for them to assert that God must assist them so that they do not err in faith, since they continually do so and have done so.[^12] For so the Donatists [fancied]{g:fantasiare}, [more colourably]{g:coloracior}, and many other [unbelievers]{g:infidelis}.

## ¶5 · 274.5–276.8

> [p. 274] De ista materia dixi alibi; ideo si verum est quod dicit Doctor Subtilis quod nichil cogit sic dicere nisi decretales epistole supradicte, nimis vacuus est baculus arundineus cui adversarii innituntur. Unde doctor quidam dubitat quare tantum innituntur moderni isti decretali dimissa scriptura sacra cum quatuor sanctis doctoribus; et respondet quod oportet populum plus in errore et malicia profundari; ideo argumentum Sathane cecantis sordentes est quod debent inniti sentencie huius pape, credendo istam blasphemiam, quia ipse inter omnes papas maxime stetit secundum scripturas pro dignitate ecclesie; ideo magnificemus ut evangelium scripta sua. Quo contra fidelis dicit ecclesie et specialiter regibus quod iste inter omnes papas maxime palliavit scripturas ad subtrahendum honorem et dominia secularium dominorum et accumulando sibi et clero suo contra religionem Christi primatus seculi, ut primo decretalium De Maioritate et Obediencia, capitulo Solite, habetur, quomodo respondendo ad litteram imperatoris palliavit dictum pape Petri quo dicit prima Petri IIᵒ, 13: Subditi estote omni humane creature propter Deum sive regi etc., quod hoc debet intelligi de simplici populo subiecto Petro et non de sacerdotibus qui sunt excellenciores quam aliqui domini [p. 275] seculares (ut diffuse exposui in tractatu de Rege capitulo VIᵗᵒ); et ista sentencia pervertendo sensum scripture molitur a clero evertere subieccionem et obedienciam in qua fovetur religio christiana, ut patet XI, questione prima, Si tributum, et capitulo Magnum quippe, et XXIII, questione VIII De episcopis. Unde sic loquitur decretum: Medium magnum quippe est et spirituale documentum quod christiani viri sublimioribus potestatibus docentur debere esse subiecti, ne quis constitucionem terreni regis putet esse solvendam. Si enim censum Filius Dei solvit, quis tu tantus es qui non putas esse solvendum? Et sequitur in decreto sentencia beati Ambrosii et beatus Petrus apostolus generaliter omnibus fidelibus scribit: Estote subditi dominis vestris. Que sentencia cum racione videtur contraria glosse pape. Quomodo ergo non plus crederetur in isto beato Ambrosio quam sibi iuxta capitulum primum distinccione XX? Secundo idem papa in eadem decretali exponit idem Matth. XVI: Nobis, inquit, in beato Petro sunt oves commisse dicente Domino: Pasce oves meas, non distinguens inter has oves et alias ut alienum demonstraret a suo ovili, qui Petrum et successores eius magistros non recognosceret et pastores, ut illud tanquam notissimum omittamus quod Dominus dixit ad Petrum et in Petro dixit ad omnes successores ipsius: Quodcunque ligaveris super terram, nichil excipiens, quando [p. 276] dixit: Quodcunque. Ecce quam cece et presumptuose concluditur cuicunque Romano pontifici pertinere universalem preeminenciam super tota ecclesia et sic sibi. Multi namque sunt fideles qui multos Romanos pontifices nec cognoscunt pastores eorum nec membra ecclesie, quia ipsis degenerantibus a Petro alii episcopi magis proficiunt toti ecclesie militanti (ut exposui in tractatu de papa).

On this matter I have spoken elsewhere; therefore, if it is true, as the [Subtle Doctor]{g:doctor-subtilis} says, that nothing compels [one] to say so except the decretal letters mentioned above,[^13] exceedingly empty is the reed staff on which the adversaries lean.[^14] Hence a certain doctor wonders why these moderns lean so much on this decretal, leaving aside holy scripture with the four holy doctors; and he answers that the people must be sunk deeper in error and malice.[^15] So the argument of Satan, who blinds the filthy, is that they ought to lean on the teaching of this pope, believing this blasphemy, because he among all popes stood most, according to the scriptures, for the dignity of the church; therefore let us magnify his writings as the gospel. Against this the faithful man says to the church, and especially to kings, that this [pope] among all popes most [cloaked]{g:palliare} the scriptures, so as to withdraw the honour and lordships of [secular lords]{g:seculares-domini}, and heaping on himself and his clergy, against the religion of Christ, the [primacies]{g:primatus} of the world, as is found in the first [book] of the Decretals, *De maioritate et obedientia*, chapter *Solitae*:[^16] how, answering the letter of the emperor, he cloaked the saying of Pope Peter where he says (1 Pet. 2:13), "Be ye subject to every human creature for God's sake: whether it be to the king" etc.,[^17] [saying] that this ought to be understood of the simple people subject to Peter, and not of [priests]{g:sacerdos}, who are more excellent than any [275] secular lords (as I have set out at length in the treatise *On the King*, chapter 6).[^18] And this teaching, perverting the meaning of scripture, labours to overturn from the clergy the subjection and obedience in which the Christian religion is nourished, as is clear in [Causa] 11, question 1, *Si tributum*, and the chapter *Magnum quippe*, and [Causa] 23, question 8, *De episcopis*.[^19] Hence the decree speaks thus: "It is indeed a great and spiritual lesson, that Christian men are taught that they ought to be subject to the higher powers, lest anyone think the ordinance of an earthly king is to be dissolved. For if the Son of God paid the tax, who are you, so great, that you think it is not to be paid?"[^20] And there follows in the decree the teaching of blessed Ambrose: "and blessed Peter the apostle writes generally to all the faithful: Be ye subject to your lords."[^21] This teaching, with reason, seems contrary to the pope's [gloss]{g:glossa}. How, then, would blessed Ambrose not be believed in this more than he, according to the first chapter in Distinction 20?[^22] Secondly, the same [pope]{g:papa} in the same decretal expounds Matthew 16 likewise: "To us," he says, "in blessed Peter the sheep were committed, when the Lord said: Feed my sheep; not distinguishing between these sheep and others, so as to show him a stranger to his fold who would not acknowledge Peter and his successors as masters and shepherds; to omit, as most well known, that the Lord said to Peter, and in Peter said to all his successors: Whatsoever thou shalt bind upon earth, excepting nothing, when [276] he said: Whatsoever."[^23] See how blindly and presumptuously it is [inferred]{g:concludere} that universal [preeminence]{g:preeminencia} over the whole church belongs to every [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex}, and so to himself! For there are many faithful who acknowledge many Roman pontiffs neither as their shepherds nor as members of the church, because, as these degenerate from Peter, other bishops profit the whole [church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans} more (as I have set out in the treatise *On the Pope*).[^24]

## ¶6 · 276.9–276.20

> [p. 276] Ulterius vero considera quo tendit exposicio huius sententie: Quodcunque ligaveris super terram erit ligatum et in celo, nedum quod intelligitur singulariter de Petro, de ipso, et quocunque Romano pontifice, sed quod sit excepcio de quocunque quod ipse in temporalibus vel spiritualibus decreverit faciendum. Quod si per inpossibile sit verum, quilibet Romanus pontifex extollitur super omne quod dicitur Deus, cum sit Deus in terris et inpeccabilis Antichristus. Et inde dictus papa modificat quomodo imperator dominatur laycis et non clericis qui a iurisdiccione Cesaris simpliciter sunt exempti.

And further, consider where the exposition of this saying tends, "Whatsoever thou shalt bind upon earth, it shall be bound also in heaven": [not only]{g:nedum} that it is understood of Peter [alone]{g:singulariter}, of himself, and of any Roman pontiff whatever,[^25] but that the exception is about whatever he has decreed to be done in temporals or spirituals.[^26] And if, per impossibile, this were true, every Roman pontiff "is lifted up above all that is called God", since he would be God on earth and an Antichrist unable to sin.[^27] And thence the said pope qualifies how the emperor [exercises lordship]{g:dominari} over laymen and not over clerics, who are exempt [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} from the [jurisdiction]{g:iurisdiccio} of Caesar.

## Notes

[^1]: **S/T.** The decretal is not named in the Latin; Innocent III's Fourth Lateran Council (1215), canon 1 *Firmiter* (X 1.1.1), which speaks of the bread "transubstantiated", is meant, as at 141.11–29, where Wyclif sets Innocent III's decretal against the decree of Nicholas II (LEDGER K09a-1). For the 1315 prelates Loserth compares Martinus Polonus, *Chronicon* (MGH SS XXII, 438: *summa prelatorum 1315*) (LEDGER K09a-2). ABDE read *decretales*; C corrects.
[^2]: **S.** Augustine, *Contra epistolam fundamenti* 5.6 (*ego vero evangelio non crederem, nisi me catholicae ecclesiae commoveret auctoritas*); PL 42, 176; Loserth gives no reference (LEDGER K09a-3).
[^3]: **C.** *Instancia*, "objection" (GLOSSARY): the objections are raised against the argument just stated and serve as the first answers to it; the fifth is called *responsio* (278.25, the next chunk).
[^4]: **C.** *Sentencia*: the position taught, "teaching", the first sense of the CONTEXTUAL row; so throughout the chunk.
[^5]: **C.** *Ad sensum expositum*: taken as "in the meaning set out" earlier in the treatise (Loserth's side-note: "Transsubstantiation has many meanings"); the bare phrase could also be "exposed to sense", which the context does not favour.
[^6]: **C/T.** The period is compressed. Taken as: if the church's meaning were shown, one must yield to the truth that its dogma would be exceedingly hard, and so it is not to be believed in this. Alternatively *quod* is causal: "one must consent to the truth, because the dogma would be exceedingly difficult". ABCD read *ymmo hoc docto* ("indeed, once this is shown") and *sensit* for *sentit* ("did not so hold").
[^7]: **S.** The angel from heaven recalls Gal. 1:8, alluded to, not quoted (LEDGER K09a-16).
[^8]: **C/T.** *Nullus istorum*: none of the council's prelates was subtler than a professor of theology who erred notably in this matter, so their number and rank give no guarantee; the Latin names no professor. D reads *nullus sanctorum*. *Sancte pagine* "holy page" and *sacre pagine* "sacred page" are kept apart, as *sanctus / sacer*.
[^9]: **C.** *Color* (GLOSSARY): colour, a plausible ground.
[^10]: **C/T.** *Asiani*: the Latin's word; whom it means is not said. "The said teaching" is the decree's. D reads *Nonne racione Saraceni*; ABC *senciunt* ("hold") for *consenciunt*; ABC lack *nos*, AB *se* (273.26).
[^11]: **R.** *Episcopus Avinonicus*: the pope at Avignon, after 1378 the claimant of the Clementine obedience (LEDGER K09a-4).
[^12]: **C/T.** *Ergo verum* is taken as closing the rejected inference (*si … diffinierant, ergo verum*) and *et verecundum est* as a new clause; *sic faciunt*, "do so", is to err. ABCD read *Sic autem* (274.3), D *dogmatiste* for *Donatiste*.
[^13]: **S.** Scotus, *In IV Sent.*; Loserth: "lib. IV, dist. VIII, q. II" (LEDGER K09a-5). The passage is Ord. IV d. 11 q. 3 (Wadding VIII, 616–617): what *principaliter videtur movere* is the Roman church's teaching in *Ad abolendam* (X 5.7.9) and *Firmiter* (X 1.1.1), Wyclif's *decretales epistole*. Loserth's d. 8 q. 2 cites *Cum Marthae* on the form of consecration (LEDGER K09a-5, K09a-6). *Decretales epistole supradicte*: the Latin does not name them further.
[^14]: **S.** The reed staff: Isa. 36:6, "this broken staff of a reed … upon which if a man lean, it will go into his hand", alluded to, not quoted.
[^15]: **S/C.** *Doctor quidam* is not named, and Loserth gives no foot (LEDGER K09a-7). *Dubitat*: raises the question, "wonders". *Sathane cecantis sordentes*: Satan "who blinds the filthy" (B *cecitantis*).
[^16]: **S.** X 1.33.6 (*Solitae*), Innocent III's answer to the emperor's letter; Loserth: "Decret. Greg. lib. I, tit. XXXIII, cap. VI"; the modern form is ours (LEDGER K09a-8).
[^17]: **S/T.** 1 Pet. 2:13; the Vulgate has *Subjecti igitur estote*, DR "Be ye subject therefore"; Wyclif's Latin lacks the connective, and so the English. C omits *propter Deum* and *etc.*; ABCE *subiecti*.
[^18]: **S/T.** Wyclif, *De officio regis*, ch. 6; Loserth gives the page as "i3q", that is, p. 139 (ed. Pollard and Sayle), where c. 6 quotes *Si tributum* and *Magnum quippe*; p. 126 sets *Solitae* against them (LEDGER K09a-9). The manuscripts lack *in* before *tractatu*; it is Loserth's.
[^19]: **S.** Gratian, C. 11 q. 1 c. 27 (*Si tributum*) and c. 28 (*Magnum quippe*); C. 23 q. 8 d.a.c. 1 (*De episcopis vero*); Loserth's references, the modern form ours (LEDGER K09a-10).
[^20]: **T/S.** The print reads *Medium magnum quippe est*; DE *Meum magnum*; C lacks *est* (A has it in the margin). Neither *Medium* nor *Meum* construes, and the chapter's incipit, just cited, is *Magnum quippe*; the English leaves the word out (our emendation). It may be a corruption of the canon's inscription in Gratian, *Idem* (*Idem in commentariis ad c. 5 Lucae*), copied into the text; a conjecture only. Friedberg's c. 28 reads *Magnum quidem est et spiritale documentum, quo* (Ambrose, *Expos. in Luc.* IV.73, PL 15, 1634); Wyclif has *quippe* here and in *De officio regis* c. 6, p. 139 (LEDGER K09a-11). The tax: Matt. 17:23–26. *Solvendam / solvendum*: one verb, "dissolved" of the ordinance and "paid" of the tax.
[^21]: **S/T.** The sentence is Gratian's dictum after C. 11 q. 1 c. 28 (*Item Petrus Apostolus generaliter omnibus fidelibus scribit*), which the manuscripts do not mark off from Ambrose's canon (Friedberg); Wyclif reads it as Ambrose's (LEDGER K09a-12). The words are 1 Pet. 2:13 with *dominis vestris* from 2:18 (DR "Servants, be subject to your masters with all fear"); the decree's form is translated. ABC lack *sentencia*; E reads *sancti* and adds *et Bernhardi*.
[^22]: **S/C.** Gratian, D. 20 d.a.c. 1 (Loserth: "Dist. XX, II Pars, § 1"), which in the exposition of scripture sets the sayings of Augustine, Jerome and others above the constitutions of some pontiffs (Loserth quotes it at 280.2) (LEDGER K09a-13). *Sibi*: the pope.
[^23]: **S/T.** *Solitae* § 6 (Loserth: "In eadem decretali, i. e. Solitae; ib. § VI") (LEDGER K09a-14), with John 21:17 and Matt. 16:19. "Matthew 16" is the Latin's, though *Pasce oves meas* is John 21. *Omittamus* is Loserth's correction (ABDE *amittamus*, C *admittamus*); ABCE read *Quod enim* and lack *Dominus*.
[^24]: **S.** The manuscripts read *de papa in tractatu*; Loserth: "rectius (ut videtur) De Potestate pape" (LEDGER K09a-15).
[^25]: **C/T.** *Singulariter*: "alone", the first sense of the CONTEXTUAL row. *De ipso*: of the pope himself. ABC read *et de*.
[^26]: **C/T.** *Sed quod sit excepcio de quocunque*: the sense is doubtful. Taken as: the word *quodcunque* is made to hold, as an exception from every limit, of whatever the pope decrees; a negative may have been lost (*nulla sit excepcio*, "there is no exception"), as the pope's *nichil excipiens* (275.27) suggests. C reads *fit excepcio*; ABCE *ipsi … decreverunt* ("they have decreed").
[^27]: **S/R.** 2 Thess. 2:4 (Loserth "I. Thess. II, 4"). C has *Antichristus* in the margin in another hand.
