# *De potestate pape*, chapter 11, part 3: the Virgin, a woman, a layman as pope; the old priesthood no ground for primacy

## ¶1 · 271.22–273.3

> [p. 271] Hic dicitur quod beata virgo fuit papa secundum extensionem nominis doctam superius; fuit enim excellenter mirabilis sanctitate, sicut et fuit sacerdos; et concordat genus gramatice cum sanctis peritis linguarum sic de papa loquentibus. Limitando autem signum ad propositum, dictum est quod papa est quicumque viator, qui sequitur Christum similius, ubi femella excluditur, quia quantumcumque sit sancta femina, propter infirmitatem sexus non permittitur evangelizare publice in ecclesia nec orare detecto capite, ut dicit Apostolus prima Cor. XI. Ideo mulier non est ydoneus capitaneus ecclesie nec [p. 272] viator simillimus sequax Christi. Et sic excluditur tam mulier quam angelus a pape nomine. De papa vero Agnete, de qua Cestrensis libro V, que post vocata est Johannes Anglicus, cum ceteris pseudopapis, patet quod false vendicaverat nomen pape. Quoad secundum obiectum de laycis, patet logico quod laycus potest esse papa. Et videtur de Dei potencia absoluta possibile quod omnis nunc vians foret membrum dyaboli vel quod desit clerus ecclesie, quod esset magis mirabile. Ego autem credo quod est necessarium ex supposicione quod sint multi viantes continue a tempore ascensionis usque ad diem iudicii sancciores ceteris, qui teneant statum et ordinem clericalem, sic quod ordinacioni divine repugnat, quin toto clero perverso usque ad laycos Deus subito illuminaret aliquem eorum, sic quod viveret pure exproprietarie et sancte de sorte Domini: et tunc foret clericus, licet nec aliqua septem arcium sit imbutus nec secundum ritus episcopales sit humanitus ordinatus. Ista autem evidencia non probat quod sic est de facto, sed videtur suadere quod sic potest esse de Dei potencia absoluta, Eleccionem autem Dei in talibus oportet omnem fidelem concedere, quia homo non eligit racionaliter alium ad aliquam dignitatem, nisi quia reputat eum dignum, sed si est dignus ad aliquid, est a Deo preelectus, quod debet credere; ideo nemo debet eligere quemquam ad aliquid, nisi reputet eum a Domino preelectum, et hoc est notum apud eos qui vident quod necesse est Deum ad [p. 273] omnem accionem creature concurrere preagendo. Et patet ex lege triplici ad idem ex IX cap. allegata.

The answer here is that the blessed Virgin was pope according to the extension of the name taught above; for she was excellently wonderful in holiness, as she was also a priest; and the grammatical gender agrees with the saints skilled in languages who speak thus of the pope.[^1] But, limiting the sign to the matter in hand, it has been said that the [pope]{g:papa} is whatever [wayfarer]{g:viator} follows Christ more nearly; and there the female is excluded, because however holy a woman may be, on account of the weakness of the sex she is not permitted to preach the gospel publicly in the church nor to pray with uncovered head, as the Apostle says, 1 Cor. 11.[^2] Therefore a woman is not a fit [captain]{g:capitaneus} of the church, nor [272] the wayfarer who most nearly follows Christ. And so both a woman and an angel are excluded from the name of pope. But of Pope Agnes, of whom the [Chester]{g:cestrensis} [chronicler speaks] in book 5, who was afterwards called John the Englishman, with the other [pseudo-popes]{g:pseudopapa}, it is clear that she had falsely claimed the name of pope.[^3] As to the second [objection]{g:instancia}, about laymen, it is clear to a logician that a layman can be pope. And it seems possible by God's [absolute power]{g:potencia-absoluta} that every [one] now on the way should be a [member]{g:membrum} of the devil, or that the church should lack [clergy]{g:clerus}, which would be more wonderful. But I believe that it is [necessary on supposition]{g:ex-supposicione} that there are continually, from the time of the ascension to the day of judgement, many [on the road]{g:viantes} holier than the rest, who hold the clerical [state]{g:status} and order; so that it [is incompatible]{g:repugnare} with the divine [ordinance]{g:ordinacio} that, were the whole clergy perverted, God should not, [reaching] down as far as the laity, suddenly enlighten one of them, so that he lived purely [without property]{g:exproprietarie} and holily from the Lord's lot:[^4] and then he would be a cleric, although he were neither versed in any of the seven arts nor ordained in human fashion according to the episcopal [rites]{g:ritus}. But this [evidence]{g:evidencia} does not prove that it is so in fact, but seems to persuade that it can be so by God's absolute power. And every believer must grant God's election in such [cases], because a man does not rationally elect another to any dignity except because he reckons him worthy; now if he is worthy of anything, he is pre-elected by God, which he must believe; therefore no one ought to elect anyone to anything unless he reckons him pre-elected by the Lord. And this is known among those who see that God must concur with [273] every action of a creature by acting beforehand. And it is clear from the threefold law [alleged]{g:allegare} for the same [point] from chapter 9.[^5]

## ¶2 · 273.4–273.11

> [p. 273] Sed ulterius pro materia argumenti miror in tribus, primo quomodo adversarius non erubescit concludere primatum cuiuscumque Romani pontificis ex primatu sacerdotis Aaronitici in veteri testamento, secundo quomodo concluderetur officiorum singularis preeminencia ex lege veteri, et tercio quomodo tot ritus et ornatus episcopales ex legalibus legis veteris surrepserunt.

But further, on the matter of the argument, I wonder at three things: first, how the adversary does not blush [to infer]{g:concludere} the [primacy]{g:primatus} of any [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex} whatever from the primacy of the Aaronic priest in the old testament; second, how a singular [preeminence]{g:preeminencia} of offices would be inferred from the old law; and third, how so many episcopal rites and ornaments crept in from the [legal precepts]{g:legalia} of the old law.[^6]

## ¶3 · 273.12–273.16

> [p. 273] Quantum ad primum, cum legalia cessare debuerant, summum autem sacerdocium cum ritibus suis figuravit Dominum, qui iam venit, quare igitur non debuerunt cessare in sacerdocio Christi, nisi forte adhuc expectetur infideliter Antichristus?

As to the first: since the legal precepts had to cease, and now the high priesthood with its rites figured the Lord, who has already come, why then ought they not to have ceased in the priesthood of Christ, unless perhaps Antichrist is still faithlessly awaited?[^7]

## ¶4 · 273.18–273.25

> [p. 273] Item, non videtur racio quare sequi debemus summum sacerdotem legis in hiis ritibus, quin per idem in aliis iam omissis, ut puta in uxoracione et successione hereditaria summi pontificis in suo oneroso officio et in parva porcione de sorte Domini. Non enim est racio diversitatis sufficiens, quod Machomed et Sergius colarunt de utroque testamento eis placitum, cetera displicibilia omittentes.

Again, there seems no reason why we should follow the [high priest]{g:summus-sacerdos} of the law in these rites and not, by the same token, in others now omitted: for instance, in the marrying and the hereditary succession of the [high priest]{g:summi-pontifices} in his burdensome office, and in the small portion from the Lord's lot. For it is no sufficient reason for the difference that Mahomet and Sergius strained out of both testaments what pleased them, omitting the rest, which displeased them.[^8]

## ¶5 · 273.27–274.33

> [p. 273] Item, in novo testamento debet ecclesia magis dimittere figuras et tamquam etate proveccior evacuando que erant parvuli, plus simulari ecclesie triumphanti; sed in beatis cessat omnis talis figura atque exaccio, cum sint ad ultimum unicordes; igitur in ecclesia apostolica debent ultra legem veterem ad illum statum accedere. Et hec racio, quare apostoli docuerunt in facto, verbo et [p. 274] scripto, quod cessarunt huiusmodi legalia; nec inducerentur nova ad onus ecclesie, ut patet Act. XV, ymo apostoli fuissent nimis immemores future ecclesie pretermittendo exempla in facto, verbo et scripto, quomodo successores sui episcopi militarent. Unde pro ulteriori sensu notandum quod omnis summus sacerdos legis veteris figuravit Christum in omnibus factis suis legalibus. Ideo autonomastice et singulariter ipse dicitur summus sacerdos et episcopus animarum et hinc illa multitudo sacerdotum et suorum officiorum fuit in Christo unico consumata, ut pulchre docet Apostolus Heb. IX, et Heb. VII dicitur: plures facti sunt sacerdotes secundum legem etc. Et hec racio quare apostoli non vocarunt se papas sanctissimos, capita universalis ecclesie vel universales pontifices, sed habendo secum summum pontificem usque ad consumacionem seculi, vocarunt se servos Christi in tribulacione, socios et ministros ecclesie; unde ista religio servata fuit tunc tempore beati Gregorii, et ex decreto suo incorporata legi ecclesie distinccione XCIX Prime sedis et cap. Ecce. Quare igitur non magis periculoso tempore plus indigni sine divisione vel phariseica expectacione inniterentur unite pontifici. Revera videtur iuxta distinccionem predictam et veritatem facti paparum sequi quod vel sunt personaliter Ihesus noster, vel quod sunt infidelitate peiores sacerdotibus legis veteris figuraliter expectantibus Ihesum Christum, cum propinquissime preparant viam Antichristo. Non igitur sequitur hec dominativa precellencia ex lege veteri.

Again, in the new testament the church ought rather to put away figures and, as one more advanced in age, putting away the things of a child, be made more like the [church triumphant]{g:ecclesia-triumphans};[^9] now in the blessed all such figure and exaction ceases, since at the last they are of one heart; therefore in the apostolic church they ought to come nearer that state, beyond the old law. And this is the reason why the apostles taught in deed, word and [274] writing that such legal precepts had ceased; and new ones were not to be brought in to burden the church, as is clear from Acts 15;[^10] indeed, the apostles would have been too unmindful of the future church had they left out examples, in deed, word and writing, of how their successors the bishops should serve as soldiers. Hence, for the further sense, it is to be noted that every high priest of the old law figured Christ in all his legal acts. Therefore [by antonomasia]{g:autonomatice} and singularly he is called the high priest and "bishop of souls"; and hence that multitude of priests and of their offices was consummated in the one Christ, as the Apostle finely teaches, Heb. 9; and in Heb. 7 it is said: "Many were made priests according to the law", etc.[^11] And this is the reason why the apostles did not call themselves most holy popes, heads of the universal church or universal pontiffs, but, having with them the highest pontiff "even to the consummation of the world", called themselves servants of Christ, partners in tribulation, and ministers of the church.[^12] Hence this observance was kept then, in the time of blessed Gregory, and by his decree incorporated into the law of the church, Distinction 99, *Prime sedis* and the chapter *Ecce*.[^13] Why then should not the more unworthy, in a more perilous time, rest in unity on the pontiff, without division or pharisaic expectation?[^14] In truth it seems to follow, according to the aforesaid distinction and the truth of the popes' deeds, that either they are personally our Jesus, or they are worse in [unbelief]{g:infidelitas} than the priests of the old law who awaited Jesus Christ in figure, since they prepare the way for Antichrist most nearly. This lordly pre-excellence, therefore, does not follow from the old law.[^15]

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Concordat genus gramatice*: *papa* has the form of a first-declension (mostly feminine) noun, and Wyclif notes that the grammar fits a woman; the "saints skilled in languages" are not named. The construal is uncertain; the sentence may instead mean only that their usage ("pope" in the wide sense of holiness) agrees with the grammar.
[^2]: **S.** 1 Cor. 11:5 (DR "every woman praying or prophesying with her head not covered disgraceth her head"); the bar on public speaking is 1 Cor. 14:34 ("it is not permitted them to speak"). *Similius*, "more nearly", is a comparative where the next sentence has the superlative *simillimus*.
[^3]: **S/R.** *Cestrensis*: Ranulf Higden, *Polychronicon* V, 32 (Rolls Series VI, 330–32), the tale of Pope Joan, whom Higden, after Martin of Troppau, calls *Johannes Anglicus, natione Maguntinus* (LEDGER P11c-1). Codd. in marg. at 272.2: *Papa Agnes mulier*. Loserth's foot compares Hus, *De ecclesia* fol. 220 (ch. 13, quoting Higden on Agnes), and Wyclif's *Cruciata* (*Polemical Works*, ed. Buddensieg, 619), where she is called Anna (LEDGER P11c-2).
[^4]: **C.** *Toto clero perverso usque ad laycos Deus … illuminaret aliquem eorum*: *eorum* taken of the laity: if the clergy failed entirely, God would reach to the laity and make one of them a cleric. "The Lord's lot" (*sors Domini*) is the clergy's share, the root of *clerus*.
[^5]: **T/S.** B reads *Exo.* at 273.2, possibly "Exodus 9" for *ex IX cap.*; the print, "from chapter 9" (of this treatise), is followed. The threefold law there is not identified here.
[^6]: **T/R.** A reads *singularum* at 273.9 ("a preeminence of singular offices"). Codd. in marg.: *Primatus pape non concluditur ex similitudine*.
[^7]: **C.** *Summum autem sacerdocium*: the *autem* brings the minor premiss (the high priesthood was a figure) inside the *cum* clause; *igitur* draws the inference as a question.
[^8]: **C/S.** *Non enim est racio … quod Machomed et Sergius colarunt*: i.e. choosing some rites and dropping others is the method of Mahomet and the monk Sergius (the medieval legend of Muhammad's Christian teacher), not a reason. Wyclif names him so elsewhere: *Sergium monachum Machometi … dydascalum* (*De civili dominio* II, ch. 13), and, of the same picking from both laws, *tamquam alter Sergius, seductor Machometi, volunt eligere carni placencia de lege utraque* (*De blasphemia*, ch. 6, p. 84) (LEDGER P11c-3). The small portion: Num. 18:20 (DR "I am thy portion and inheritance").
[^9]: **S.** *Evacuando que erant parvuli*: 1 Cor. 13:11 (DR "I put away the things of a child"). *Simulari* = *assimilari*, "be made like".
[^10]: **S.** Acts 15:28 (DR "to lay no further burden upon you"). *Militarent*: the Pauline soldiering of 2 Tim. 2:3–4.
[^11]: **S.** "Bishop of souls": 1 Pet. 2:25 (DR "the shepherd and bishop of your souls"). Heb. 7:23, Vulg. *Et alii quidem plures facti sunt sacerdotes* (DR "And the others indeed were made many priests"); Wyclif adds *secundum legem*. Heb. 9:11–12 on Christ the high priest.
[^12]: **S/R.** Matt. 28:20 (DR "even to the consummation of the world"); "partners in tribulation", Apoc. 1:9. Codd. in marg. at 274.11: *Apostoli non vocarunt se sanctissimos vel capita*. Loserth's foot at 274.17 compares Hus, *Opera* 219: Hus, *De ecclesia* ch. 12, copies the passage almost word for word (LEDGER P11c-4).
[^13]: **S/G.** Gratian, *Decretum* D.99 c.3 (*Prime sedis*) and c.5 (*Ecce*, Gregory refusing the title "universal pope"); Loserth gives "Cap. III", "Cap. V"; the modern form is ours (PL 187, 471B, 472B) (LEDGER P11c-5). *Religio* here is "observance" (scruple about titles), not the "religion" of the orders.
[^14]: **T/C.** The print has no question mark; the sentence is taken as a question after *quare igitur*. *Unite*, adverb, "in unity", "united"; *unico*, "the one [pontiff]", would read more easily and may lie behind it. The pontiff is Christ.
[^15]: **G.** *Precellencia*: rendered "pre-excellence", kept apart from *preeminencia* "preeminence" (proposed for GLOSSARY). *Dominativa*, "lordly", as *potestas dominativa*.
