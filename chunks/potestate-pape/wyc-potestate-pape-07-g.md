# *De potestate pape*, chapter 7, part 7: the seventh argument: Peter at the council and how a vicar should correct

## ¶1 · 156.26–160.4

> [p. 156] Per hec patet responsio ad septimum, in quo probatur preeminencia Petri per illud Act. XV, quod Petrus humiliter se pretulit ad laborem. Non fuit tunc inter apostolos de prepositura contencio, sed octuplex vera humilitas necessitavit Petrum respondere pre aliis. Et utinam papa modernus vellet exercere illam clavem sciencie fidei instar Petri, tunc enim non temptaret imponere [p. 157] fidelibus graves cerimonias eciam ultra ritus iudaycos, sed sine illis vel obediencia exacta a Petro diffiniret libertatem eorum stare in isto, quod eis et gentibus suffecit ad salutem gracia domini Ihesu Christi. Et post hec Jacobus socialiter et domestice alloquitur populum in hec verba: Viri fratres, audite me, Symon narravit quomodo Deus visitavit sumere ex gentibus populum nomini suo; propter quod ego iudico non inquietari eos, qui ex gentibus convertuntur. Ubi notum est quod Spiritus Sanctus non scribit eum beatissimum papam Petrum, sed vocat eum Symonem ex humilitate socium, et Jacobum non ex auctoritate Petri, sed Domini sic locutum, et ideo singulariter dixisse, ego iudico, quod verbum foret hodie graviter ut rusticum et inconsonum reprobatum. Nec valet allegare contra modernos episcopos Jacobi sanctitatem, quia tantum distat papa a sanctitate Petri, qui nec in hoc reprobavit Jacobum nec vendicavit quod ex sua auctoritate Jacobus sic loquatur. Sicut igitur episcopi longe sunt a sanctitate Jacobi, sic pape nimis distant a sanctitate Petri. Unde igitur pape auctoritas ad vendicandum de episcopis quod non est exemplatum in iurisdicione Petri super alios sanctos apostolos vel conversos? Petrus autem nullam dominacionem vendicavit super alios fratres suos, sed apostolo reprehendenti ipsum humiliter obedivit. Recolat igitur papa quomodo nemo debet sibi obedire, nisi de quanto ut Dei organum dixerit legem suam, nec tenetur sibi credere, nisi de quanto tenetur credere quod Deus in ipso loquitur; et hoc movebit eum ad allegandam legem Domini, in qua est omnis [p. 158] veritas et ad innitendum preeminencie sanctitatis, quia aliter est ut Antichristus maxime discredendus et a comitiva fidelium precipue fugiendus. Et tercio recolat quod Roboam ex dominacione pompatica, quam didicit ex consilio iuvenum dixit false, quod minimus digitus suus fuit potencior dorso patris, et per hoc divisum est regnum, ut patet III. Reg. XII, 10 et notat Crysostomus Omelia prima Operis Imperfecti. Et applicet ad hoc verba sua et opera, si implicat se extollere super omne quod dicitur Deus, exigens dominacionem quam Christus non potest exigere, et videbit quod racione huius precipue christianismus dividitur, nam fideles ab eo recesserant propter fetens eius dominium, et hinc impeditur utrimque conversio infidelium ad legem Domini; tam extranea, inquam, videtur conversacio eius a Christo quod longe plus variatur ab eo quam conversacio sacerdotum veteris legis a Moyse, qui sensu, verbis et doctrina remanserant, sed vita et opere recesserunt. Iste autem papa videtur extraneus a Christo, qui tam vita quam doctrina ab eo discordat, nam Matth. XXIV, 11 Christus dicit quod predicabitur hoc evangelium de pseudochristo in universo mundo in testimonium omnibus gentibus, pseudo autem nedum est interpositus, ut sit testis Christo in terris, sed persequitur illos, qui hoc evangelium attestantur; unde videtur quod non de illis Christus tantum confidit, ut de sacerdotibus legis veteris, cum Matth. XXIII, 3 suis precipit: Omnia quecumque dixerint vobis facite, secundum opera eorum nolite facere, dicunt enim et non faciunt. Dicitur autem quod nostri prelati ex maiori elongacione a Christo per superinductam dotacionem cesaream nec dicunt nec faciunt. [p. 159] Nam ubi beatus Gregorius ex animo iter arripuit ad Anglicos convertendum, papa modernus ut legem sibi limitat quod non predicet ne cadat in heresim, propter quam est solummodo deponendus, ut videtur ex XL distinccione: Si papa. Sed scandalosa sunt ista, primo quia papa sciret cavere sibi de heresi antequam acciperet huiusmodi dignitatem, secundo quia papa, qui dicitur habere tantam potestatis plenitudinem, diffiniret quod non foret heresis quicquid dixerit, vel saltem protestaretur, quod non habet animum a lege Domini declinandi. Tercio heresis periculosior est in vite discordia, ymo per idem nullus prepositus predicaret, cum pro heresi et specialiter reali sit dignus deponi, et papa propter causam multiplicem cicius aufugeret illud periculum. Ideo non est nisi antichristiana et accusatoria excusacio in peccatis. Accusatoria dico, quia ut sic patenter iudicat, quod sit alienus a vicario Christi, et in aliis dicitur comuniter quod peccare non poterit. Et sic notaret Petri vicarius illud septimo replicatum, quomodo Matth. XVIII propter primatum fuit dictum singulariter Petro, quod fratrem suum corriperet, ubi sunt tria Petri vicario annotanda, primo cum debet fratrem suum corripere, debet primo omnium irreprehensibilem se servare, nam caritas debet incipere a se ipsa. Secundo notandum est quod Petri vicarius debet esse in modo corripiendi prudens, assiduus et intentus, quod non excomunicet ante finem correpcionis tercie in quo viderit fratrem suum non esse apcius corrigendum. Tercio debet notare, quod sit socius et minister ecclesie; aliter enim [p. 160] non adhiberet sibi multitudinis testimonio, et quarto diceret ecclesie ut maiori, sic enim missus est a parva apostolorum ecclesia, ut patet Act. VIII.

By these things the reply to the seventh is clear, in which Peter's [preeminence]{g:preeminencia} is proved by that [passage] of Acts 15, that Peter humbly put himself forward for the labour.[^1] There was not then among the apostles any contention about the [office of superior]{g:prepositura}; but an eightfold true humility compelled Peter to answer before the others.[^2] And would that the modern [pope]{g:papa} were willing to exercise that key of the knowledge of the faith after Peter's pattern; for then he would not try to impose [157] heavy ceremonies on the faithful, even beyond the Jewish [rites]{g:ritus}, but, without them and without obedience exacted by Peter, would define their liberty to stand in this: that the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ sufficed them and the Gentiles for salvation.[^3] And after this James, as a companion and familiarly, addresses the people in these words: "Men, brethren, hear me. Simon hath related how God visited to take of the Gentiles a people to his name. For which cause I judge that they are not to be disquieted, who from among the Gentiles are converted."[^4] Here it is noteworthy that the Holy Spirit does not write him "the most blessed pope Peter", but calls him Simon, a companion, out of humility; and [writes] that James spoke thus not by Peter's [authority]{g:auctoritas} but by the Lord's, and therefore said in the singular "I judge", a word that today would be severely condemned as boorish and discordant. Nor is it valid to [allege]{g:allegare} James's holiness against modern [bishops]{g:episcopus}, for the pope is just as far from the holiness of Peter, who neither reproved James in this nor claimed that James spoke so by his authority. As, then, the bishops are far from the holiness of James, so the popes are very far from the holiness of Peter. Whence, then, does the pope have authority to claim from bishops what is not exemplified in Peter's [jurisdiction]{g:iurisdiccio} over the other holy apostles or converts? Now Peter claimed no [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio} over his other brethren, but humbly obeyed the apostle who rebuked him.[^5] Let the pope therefore recall that no one ought to obey him except in so far as he has spoken God's law as God's instrument, nor is [anyone] bound to believe him except in so far as he is bound to believe that God speaks in him; and this will move him to allege the law of the Lord, in which is all [158] truth, and to rely on the preeminence of holiness; for otherwise he is, as Antichrist, most of all to be disbelieved, and above all to be fled by the company of the faithful. And thirdly let him recall that Rehoboam, out of the pompous exercise of lordship which he learned from the counsel of the young men, said falsely that his "little finger" was mightier "than the back of" his "father", and through this the [kingdom]{g:regnum} was divided, as is clear in 3 Kings 12:10, and as Chrysostom notes in the first homily of the *Unfinished Work*.[^6] And let him apply his words and works to this, [to see] whether it implies that he exalts himself "above all that is called God", demanding an exercise of lordship which Christ cannot demand; and he will see that it is above all by reason of this that Christendom is divided, for the faithful had withdrawn from him because of his stinking [lordship]{g:dominium}, and hence on both sides the conversion of [unbelievers]{g:infidelis} to the law of the Lord is hindered.[^7] So foreign, I say, does his [manner of life]{g:conversacio} seem from Christ, that it varies from him far more than the manner of life of the [priests]{g:sacerdos} of the old law [varied] from Moses, who had remained [with him] in sense, words and [doctrine]{g:doctrina}, but withdrew in life and work. But this pope seems foreign to Christ, since he disagrees with him both in life and in doctrine; for in Matt. 24:11 Christ says that "this gospel" about the [false christ]{g:pseudochristus} "shall be preached in the whole world, for a testimony to all nations";[^8] but the [Pseudo]{g:pseudo}, far from being set there to be a witness to Christ on earth, persecutes those who bear witness to this gospel. Hence it seems that Christ does not trust these as much as the priests of the old law, since in Matt. 23:3 he commands his own: "All things whatsoever they shall say to you, do: but according to their works do ye not. For they say, and do not."[^9] And it is said that our [prelates]{g:prelatus}, from their greater distance from Christ through the caesarean [endowment]{g:dotacio} brought in upon [them], neither say nor do. [159] For whereas blessed Gregory set out with all his heart on the journey to convert the English, the modern pope [assigns]{g:limitare} himself as a law that he is not to preach, lest he fall into heresy, for which alone he is to be deposed, as appears from distinction 40, *Si papa*.[^10] But these things are scandalous: first, because the pope would know how to guard himself against heresy before he accepted such a dignity; second, because the pope, who is said to have so great a [plenitude of power]{g:plenitudo-potestatis}, could define that whatever he said would not be heresy, or at least could [declare solemnly]{g:protestari} that he has no mind to turn aside from the law of the Lord.[^11] Third, heresy is more dangerous in discord of life; indeed, by the same token no [superior]{g:prepositi} would preach, since for heresy, and especially real heresy, he deserves to be deposed; and the pope, for a manifold cause, would sooner flee that danger. Therefore it is nothing but an antichristian and self-accusing "excuse in sins".[^12] Self-accusing, I say, because as such he openly judges that he is a stranger to [being] [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius-christi}; and in other matters it is commonly said that he cannot sin. And so the [vicar of Peter]{g:vicarius-petri} should note what was repeated in the seventh, how in Matt. 18, because of the [primacy]{g:primatus}, it was said to Peter singly that he should correct his brother;[^13] where three things are to be noted by the vicar of Peter: first, since he must correct his brother, he must first of all keep himself irreprehensible, for [charity]{g:caritas} must begin from itself. Second, it is to be noted that the vicar of Peter must be prudent, assiduous and attentive in the manner of correcting, so as not to excommunicate before the end of the third correction, at which he sees that his brother is not to be corrected more fitly [otherwise].[^14] Third, he must note that he is a companion and minister of the [church]{g:ecclesia}; for otherwise [160] he would not bring in for himself the testimony of the many, and, fourth, tell it to the church as the greater; for so he was sent by the small church of the apostles, as is clear in Acts 8.[^15]

## Notes

[^1]: **S/R.** Acts 15:7–11. A has a marginal note at 156.26 whose text is cut off in the scan of the apparatus (presumably *Ad 7*).
[^2]: **T/C.** *octuplex*, "eightfold", is unexplained and doubtful; translated as printed. The contention about greatness: Luke 22:24.
[^3]: **S/C.** Acts 15:10–11, Peter against laying the yoke of the law on the Gentile converts. "The key of knowledge": Luke 11:52; *diffiniret libertatem eorum stare in isto*: "define their liberty to consist in this".
[^4]: **S.** Acts 15:13–14, 19 in Wyclif's form: he omits *primum* ("first") in 15:14 and *ad Deum* ("to God") in 15:19.
[^5]: **S/C.** Gal. 2:11–14. *notum est* above: "it is noteworthy"; "the most blessed pope" is the style of papal address.
[^6]: **S.** 3 Kings 12:10 (DR; 1 Kings in modern Bibles) in Wyclif's indirect form, *potencior* for the Vulgate's *grossior* (DR "My little finger is thicker than the back of my father"). Chrysostom: the pseudo-Chrysostom *Opus imperfectum in Matthaeum*, hom. 1; Loserth, foot to 158.9: "Opp. tom. VI (Ed. Migne) p. 622" (LEDGER P07g-1).
[^7]: **S/C.** 2 Thess. 2:4. *si implicat se extollere*: we take *implicat* as "implies"; it could be "whether he involves himself in exalting himself". "On both sides": of the divided Christendom.
[^8]: **S/T.** The words are Matt. 24:14, printed "XXIV, 11". Wyclif reads *hoc evangelium de pseudochristo* where the Vulgate has *hoc Evangelium regni* ("this gospel of the kingdom"), and *in universo mundo* for *in universo orbe*: "about the false christ" is his gloss, taking the gospel to be the warning of Matt. 24:23–24. *pseudo* is the Pseudo, his name in ch. 6 for the false pope.
[^9]: **S.** Matt. 23:3 without the Vulgate's *ergo* and *servate et* (DR "All things therefore whatsoever … observe and do").
[^10]: **S/C/R.** Gregory the Great set out to convert the English before he was pope: Bede, *Hist. eccl.* II.1, has his request and offer but says the Romans would not let him go; the journey itself (three days out, then recalled) is John the Deacon, *Vita Gregorii* I.22–24 (PL 75, 72) (LEDGER P07g-2). Decretum D.40 c.6 *Si papa*: the pope is judged by none "unless he is found to deviate from the faith" (Loserth, foot: "Cap. VI"; the modern form is ours; PL 187, 215, under Boniface's name: *a nemine est judicandus, nisi deprehendatur a fide devius*; LEDGER P07g-3). *limitat* takes the glossary's "assign"; the sense is "prescribes". A and B note in the margin at 159.5 *Papa quare non predicat*.
[^11]: **C.** *sciret*, *diffiniret*, *protestaretur* are potential subjunctives ("would know", "could define", "could declare"): the argument is ironic, since a pope who claims plenitude of power could remove the danger by a definition.
[^12]: **S/C.** "Excuse in sins": Ps. 140:4 (*ad excusandas excusationes in peccatis*, DR "to make excuses in sins"). *heresis realis*: heresy in deed, as against heresy in words; cf. *in vite discordia* just before.
[^13]: **S/C/T.** Matt. 18:15–17 is addressed to the disciples; Wyclif reads it as said to Peter (cf. Peter's question at 18:21). *septimo replicatum*: "repeated in the seventh", i.e. the seventh argument. B reads *ut* for *ubi* (159.24). The print says "three things" but four follow (Loserth's side-notes number them 1–4). A and B note in the margin at 159.21 *Vicarius Petri debet notare dicta Petro*.
[^14]: **C.** *apcius*: "more fitly"; the third correction is the telling to the church (Matt. 18:17). "Charity must begin from itself": ordered charity begins with oneself.
[^15]: **S/C.** Matt. 18:16–17 (the witnesses; "tell the church"). Acts 8:14: the apostles at Jerusalem sent Peter and John to Samaria. *multitudinis testimonio* is dative or ablative where *testimonium* is expected; translated by sense.
