# Connective check: *sed* / *autem*, De eucharistia, wave 14 (chunks 05-k, 06-a, 06-b)

Classes: (a) minor premiss / next step, "now" · (b) adversative, "but" · (c) continuative *autem*, "and";
*non … sed* (incl. *nec … sed*), "but".

## Changes

| chunk | page | Latin phrase | was | now | reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 06-a | 159 | Augustine, *Enchiridion* 53: *Quidquid **autem** gestum est in cruce Christi* | Whatever was done in the cross | And whatever was done in the cross | The *autem* was omitted. It belongs to Augustine's sentence and is (c) there: it carries his account of the creed's articles on into their application to the Christian life. It does not bring a premiss, so it is not "now" |
| 06-b | 165 | Gregory, *Hom.* : *quid namque est testa ante ignem, nisi molle lutum? **Sed** ei ex igne agitur ut solidetur, virtus **igitur** humanitatis eius velut testa exaruit* | But it is acted on by fire | Now it is acted on by fire | (a) by the card's look-ahead rule. The clause brings the step that the *igitur* closes on: clay is soft before the fire; it is made solid by fire; so his strength "dried up like a potsherd", i.e. was hardened by the fire of the passion. The *sed* is the premiss the conclusion uses, not a contrast that stands by itself |

## Doubtful, left as they are

- **05-k, p. 155**: *… quas hic suppono, **sed** notum est quod stat multos homines tenere sentenciam hereticam …*, rendered "But it is known that". Read as (b): it sets the point that matters against the divisions just set aside. It could be (a), since *Unde videtur quod illi dogmatisantes … sint publice puniendi* draws on it, but *unde* applies the point and does not close a syllogism. Left.
- **06-b, p. 162**: *… **sed** signata obmittimus. Ad signa **autem** que ritum et fastum sapiunt … nimis attendimus*, rendered "And to the signs". Read as (c): it carries the indictment forward. It could be (b), since it sets "signs" against "things signified" ("but to the signs …"). With "but" in the sentence just before, this is a real toss-up. Left.
- **06-b, p. 165** (the change above): if the reviewer hears the *sed* as the adversative to *molle lutum* ("soft clay, but solid by fire"), "But" is defensible. The change follows the card's look-ahead rule for the *igitur*.
