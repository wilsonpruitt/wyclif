# *De ecclesia*, chapter 22, part 2: the art of mockery, Hostiensis on the keys, and the fifth lie

## ¶1 · 520.15–522.31

> [p. 520] Et correspondenter potest dici doctori mendacii cum omnibus suis complicibus quod scrutetur testimonia omnium scripturarum, ut lumen sciencie et fervor caritatis descendat ut ignis ad istam sentenciam mendacii illustrandam, et certus sum quod licet incidant se cultris et lanceolis quantumcunque super cotem acutis, donec perfundantur sanguine, ut ibi dicitur de sacerdotibus Baal, non invenient conveniens testimonium ad suum mendacium colorandum, solum in quantum unum adductum testimonium ex Psalmo V°, 7: Perdes omnes qui loquuntur mendacium. Et quia testimonium illud non est conveniens, ideo in dolo allegatur Augustinus pro indole (ut alias diffuse exposui). Sic eciam Jesus noster exprobrat scribas et phariseos Ve octuplici verbis asperis et irrisoriis, ut patet Matthei XXII°, ex cuius doctrina sumpsit Apostolus disciplinam ad dicendum principi sacerdotum: Percuciet te Deus, paries dealbate Act. XXIII°, 3, unde utile foret introducere et docere [p. 521] artem irridendi et exprobrandi, cum in illo homo potest nunc superhabundare, nunc deficere et nunc tenere medium virtuosum; non sic autem in arte mendacii. Quando autem quis ex caritate ad vindicandum Dei iniuriam et ad excludendum errores a sancta ecclesia prorumpit in verba exprobratoria et irrisoria subducto zelo vindicte et laudis proprie, fit opus laudabile quod si integre sensero in me ipso, dicerem ironice cum Apostolo, IIª Corinth. XII° cap., 13: Donate mihi istam iniuriam. Quantum ad exprobracionem Hugucii, patet quod fuit ignarus logice, philosophie et veritatis fidei, ideo non mirum si intulit contumelias veritatis catholice professori. Unde Hostiensis respondet ad obiectum suum fatuum: Hii, inquit, qui sunt in purgatorio possunt dici boni et mali diversis respectibus. Boni vero sunt qui salvabuntur, mali autem sunt qui mala sustinent. Nec dubium theologo quin idem in numero sit simul bonum bonitate nature et malum malicia culpe. Ymmo eadem persona est bona bonitate purgacionis sive iusticie et cum hoc mala malicia pene. Unde Augustinus Enchiridion XIV°, 17°: Quapropter in hiis contrariis que bona et mala vocantur illa dialecticorum regula deficit qua dicunt nulli rei simul inesse duo contraria. Cum, inquit, bona et mala nullus ambigat esse contraria, non solum simul esse possunt, sed eciam mala omnino sine bonis et nisi in bonis esse non possunt, quamvis econtra bonum poterit sine malo. Constat quidem philosophis quod bonum et malum nunc dicuntur analogice, nunc equivoce et nunc contrarie, privative aut positive. Unde illi fuerunt ignari dyalectici de secta Manichei de quibus loquitur Augustinus. Contingit [p. 522] enim specialiter sic sumere bonum et malum quod sint sibi ipsis contraria incompossibilia, ut loquitur propheta Isaie V° cap., 20: Ve qui dicunt bonum malum et malum bonum. Illi enim qui dicunt in facto bonitatem moralem esse maliciam viciosam sunt in illa malediccione prophetica. Item, Hostiensis infra, De Remissionibus, capitulo Quibus prosunt, loquens de indulgenciis episcoporum dicit quod mortuis in purgatorio caritate continuatis corpori Christi prosunt ad suffragia merita militantis ecclesie, sed non secundum racionem absolvendi. Potestas, inquit, clavium non habet ibi locum, nec obstat quod ecclesia post mortem ligat et solvit XXIVª, q. IIª. Sane. Nam ibi fit absolucio ad consolacionem vivorum fidelium et confusionem infidelium, et ut ecclesia oret pro mortuo absoluto, sic et equivoce absolvitur mortuus, id est, antequam morietur, absolutus fuisse contricione monstratur, et sic quoad mortuos nihil iam operatur. Nam ubi te invenero, ibi te iudicabo, et sive arbor cadat ad austrum sive ad aquilonem, ibi erit; sed ecclesia militans sepe fallit et fallitur, ut dicit decretalis, De Sentencia Excommunicacionis, A nobis. Nam non mortuos sed vivos astringit potestas clavium, cum solum illi subsunt ecclesie militanti; et sequitur in fine tituli diffinicio doctoris. In inferno vero nihil operantur iste indulgencie, quia ibi nulla est redempcio. Non enim est ibi aliquod bonum quoad penam sed omne malum. Ibi enim non est pena meritoria nec purgatoria nec transitoria sed tantum punitoria et eterna. Hugucius tamen dicit quod ibi est alleviacio pene,

And correspondingly it can be said to the doctor of the lie, with all his accomplices, that he should search the testimonies of all the scriptures, so that the light of knowledge and the fervour of [charity]{g:caritas} may come down like fire to illuminate this [teaching]{g:sentencia} of the lie; and I am certain that, although they cut themselves with knives and lancets however sharpened on the whetstone, till they are covered with blood, as is said there of the priests of Baal, they will not find a fitting testimony [to give colour to]{g:colorare} their lie, save only insofar as one testimony is brought from Psalm 5:7: "Thou wilt destroy all that speak a lie."[^1] And because that testimony is not fitting, therefore Augustine is [alleged]{g:allegare} in guile on "promise" (as I have expounded at length elsewhere).[^2] So also our Jesus reproaches the scribes and Pharisees with an eightfold Woe, in harsh and mocking words, as is clear in Matthew 22, from whose [doctrine]{g:doctrina} the apostle took the lesson for saying to the chief of the priests: "God shall strike thee, thou whited wall", Acts 23:3; whence it would be useful to introduce and teach [521] the art of mocking and reproaching, since in it a man can now exceed, now fall short, and now keep the virtuous mean; but not so in the art of lying.[^3] And when someone out of charity, to avenge the injury done to God and to shut errors out of holy [church]{g:ecclesia}, breaks out into reproachful and mocking words, the zeal of revenge and of his own praise being withdrawn, a praiseworthy work is done; and if I should feel this wholly in myself, I would say ironically with the apostle, 2 Corinthians 12:13: "Pardon me this injury."[^4] As to the reproach of Huguccio, it is clear that he was ignorant of logic, philosophy and the truth of the faith; therefore it is no wonder if he brought insults upon a professor of catholic truth.[^5] Hence Hostiensis replies to his foolish [objection]{g:objectum}: "Those," he says, "who are in purgatory can be called good and bad in different [relations]{g:respectus}: good indeed, in that they will be saved; bad, in that they suffer evils." Nor is it doubtful to a theologian that the same thing in number is at once good with the goodness of nature and bad with the badness of guilt. Indeed, the same person is good with the goodness of purgation or justice, and with this bad with the badness of punishment.[^6] Hence Augustine, *Enchiridion* 14, 17: "Wherefore in these contraries which are called good and evil that rule of the dialecticians fails, by which they say that two contraries are present to no thing at once. For although," he says, "no one doubts that goods and evils are contraries, not only can they be at once, but evils can by no means be without goods, nor except in goods, though conversely good can be without evil." It is evident indeed to philosophers that good and evil are said now analogically, now [equivocally]{g:equivoce} and now contrarily, privatively or positively. Hence those of the sect of Manichaeus of whom Augustine speaks were ignorant dialecticians.[^7] For one can [522] take good and evil in a special way, so that they are contraries incompossible with each other, as the prophet speaks, Isaiah 5:20: "Woe to them that call good evil, and evil good." For those who say that in deed moral goodness is vicious badness are under that prophetic curse.[^8] Again, Hostiensis below, *On Remissions*, in the chapter *Quibus prosunt*, speaking of the [indulgences]{g:indulgencie} of bishops, says that to the dead in purgatory, continued by charity with the body of Christ, the merits of the [church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans} profit by way of [suffrages]{g:suffragia}, but not by way of absolving.[^9] "The power of the keys," he says, "has no place there; nor does it stand in the way that the church binds and looses after death (C. 24 q. 2 *Sane*)." For there absolution is done for the consolation of the living faithful and the confusion of [unbelievers]{g:infidelis}, and so that the church may pray for the dead man absolved; and thus the dead man too is absolved equivocally, that is, he is shown to have been absolved by contrition before he died; and so as regards the dead it now effects nothing. For 'where I find thee, there will I judge thee', and 'whether the tree fall to the south or to the north, there it shall be'; but the church militant often deceives and is deceived, as the decretal says, *On the Sentence of Excommunication*, *A nobis*. For the power of the keys binds not the dead but the living, since only they are subject to the church militant.[^10] And at the end of the title follows the doctor's [definition]{g:diffinicio}: in hell, however, these indulgences effect nothing, because there is no redemption there. For there is there no good as to punishment, but all evil. For there the punishment is not meritorious nor purgatorial nor transitory, but only punitive and eternal. Huguccio, however, says that there is there an alleviation of punishment;

## ¶2 · 523.1–524.17

> [p. 523] in paradiso autem nihil operantur, quia anime ibi existentes non egent nostris suffragiis sed econtra. Quintum et ultimum mendacium est accepcio testimonii Augustini de refrigerio dampnatorum et primo per illud Enchiridion CX°, ubi dicit quod mediocriter malis qui sunt dampnati prosunt suffragia, et glossa exponit istam quadrimembrem divisionem quod non est orandum pro valde bonis nec valde malis; non pro valde bonis, quia statim advolant et (ut dicitur libro III°, De Celebracionibus Missarum, Cum Marthe) iniuriam facit martiri qui orat pro martire; nec pro valde malis, quia non meruerunt sic iuvari. Mediocriter vero bonis prosunt, ut cicius liberentur ab igne purgatorio et mediocriter malis prosunt ut micius puniantur, nunquam tamen liberabuntur. Ex quo patet quod orandum est specialiter pro illis qui decesserunt in mortali, dummodo non sint valde mali vel summe mali. Sed dictum est superius in quarto commento mendacii quomodo doctores tam theologi quam iuris canonici exponunt utrumque membrum mediocriter bonorum et malorum de predestinatis in purgatorio penitenciam expectantibus. Quamvis enim omnes illi sint tam boni quam mali et ad penam temporalem dampnati, tamen alia est racio qua sunt boni, et alia racio qua sunt mali, sicut alia est racio qua simpliciter liberantur et alia racio qua micius puniuntur. Nec valet dicta logica glosse communis, cum nihil sit summe malum nisi peccatum, vel forte primus apostata cum omnibus membris suis; quod si non sit orandum pro istis, non est orandum pro prescito; et idem apparet in eadem questione cap. Pro obeuntibus, ubi sub auctoritate beati Gregorii decretum sic loquitur: Congruit ut pro mortuis catholicis sacerdos memoriam faciat et [p. 524] intercedat, non tamen pro impiis (quamvis christiani fuerint) tale quid agere licebit. Super quo dicit eadem glossa: Si est evidens aliquem decessisse in mortali, ut cum quis suspendit se vel precipitat aut alio modo se interficit, pro eo non est orandum, ut XXIIIª, q. V°, Placuit, et q. VIIIª, Quicunque clericus. Secus est, si est dubium: unde pro occiso in ebrietate non oratur (ut Extra., De Reliquiis, Audivimus). Quomodo queso ordinarentur tot leges ad non orandum pro mortuo in mortali secundum suspicionem fallibilem, si debemus specialiter orare pro illo quem scimus dampnatum perpetuo? vel quomodo specificaret ecclesia oracionem pro mortuis in missa dicendo: Memento domine famulorum tuorum qui nos precesserunt cum signo fidei et dormiunt in sompno pacis, si generaliter orandum est pro illis quibus pax ex perpetuitate vermis consciencie est incompossibilis?

in paradise, however, they effect nothing, since the souls existing there do not need our suffrages, but the reverse.[^11] The fifth and last lie is the taking of Augustine's testimony on the refreshment of the damned; and first through that text, *Enchiridion* 110, where he says that suffrages profit the moderately bad who are damned; and the gloss expounds this fourfold division thus: that one is not to pray for the very good nor for the very bad; not for the very good, because they fly off at once, and (as is said in book 3, *On the Celebrations of Masses*, *Cum Marthe*) he does injury to a martyr who prays for a martyr; nor for the very bad, because they did not merit to be so helped. To the moderately good, however, they profit, that they may be freed more quickly from the fire of purgatory, and to the moderately bad they profit, that they may be punished more mildly; yet they will never be freed.[^12] From which it is clear that one is to pray specially for those who died in mortal sin, provided they are not very bad or supremely bad. But it was said above, in the fourth [fabrication]{g:commentum} of the lie, how the doctors, theologians as well as those of canon law, expound each member, of the moderately good and of the moderately bad, of the [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} awaiting penance in purgatory. For although all of them are both good and bad, and condemned to temporal punishment, yet one is the ground on which they are good and another the ground on which they are bad, just as one is the ground on which they are freed [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} and another that on which they are punished more mildly. Nor does the said logic of the common gloss avail, since nothing is supremely bad except sin, or perhaps the first apostate with all his [members]{g:membrum}; and if one is not to pray for these, one is not to pray for one [foreknown]{g:prescitus};[^13] and the same appears in the same question, chapter *Pro obeuntibus*, where the decree, under the authority of blessed Gregory, speaks thus: "It is fitting that the priest make remembrance of the catholic dead and [524] intercede for them; yet it will not be lawful to do any such thing for the impious (although they were Christians)." On which the same gloss says: "If it is evident that someone has died in mortal sin, as when someone hangs himself or throws himself down or kills himself in some other way, one is not to pray for him, as C. 23 q. 5 *Placuit*, and q. 8 *Quicunque clericus*. It is otherwise if it is doubtful: hence one does not pray for one slain in drunkenness (as Extra, *On Relics*, *Audivimus*)."[^14] How, I ask, would so many laws be ordained against praying for one dead in mortal sin on a fallible suspicion, if we ought specially to pray for him whom we know to be damned forever? Or how would the church specify its prayer for the dead in the mass, saying: "Remember, O Lord, thy servants who have gone before us with the sign of faith and sleep in the sleep of peace", if one is generally to pray for those with whom peace is incompossible because of the perpetuity of the worm of conscience?[^15]

Psalm 5:7, Douay–Rheims. **C.** *Doctori mendacii*: the opponent. *Descendat ut ignis*: as the fire came down on Elijah's sacrifice (3 Kings 18:38). *Colorare*: give colour to (row). **T.** A *inscidant* (520.20). **R.** Side-notes 520.15, 520.23.

[^2]: **C.** *In dolo … pro indole*: a pun. Augustine is alleged "in guile" (*in dolo*) on the word *indoles*, "promise", in the argument from the midwives (row *indoles*, where Comestor reads *de dolo*); Loserth refers to pp. 163–164 above (520.27; LEDGER E22b-1).

[^3]: **S.** The eight woes are Matthew 23:13–29; the print gives chapter XXII. Acts 23:3, Douay–Rheims; Paul spoke to the high priest Ananias (Acts 23:2). **C.** *Ex cuius doctrina*: from Christ's doctrine. *Principi sacerdotum*: the singular, the high priest. *Medium virtuosum*: the mean of virtue, between excess and defect. **T.** A and B *octiplici* (520.29); A and B *deus peries* for *Deus, paries* (520.32).

[^4]: **S.** 2 Corinthians 12:13: Wyclif has *istam* for the Vulgate *hanc*. **C.** *Subducto zelo vindicte et laudis proprie*: ablative absolute, the zeal for revenge and for one's own praise being removed. *Si integre sensero in me ipso*: if I found this wholly so in myself, that is, if my motive were pure. *Ironice*: Paul's words are ironic.

[^5]: **C.** *Exprobracio Hugucii*: Huguccio's reproach that the theologians' opinion is close to heresy (518.20). *Veritatis catholice professori*: taken as the theologian who teaches catholic truth, the theologians' party; it may be Augustine, whose division they follow. **R.** Side-note 521.11; A in the margin at 521.11: *An dampnatis prosunt suffragia*.

[^6]: **C.** *Obiectum*: Huguccio's objection (row *instancia; objectum*). *Qui salvabuntur … qui mala sustinent*: the relatives are causal, "in that". The quotation of Hostiensis is ended at *sustinent* by sense. *Idem in numero*: numerically the same. *Bonitate nature … malicia culpe*: good by nature, bad by guilt. **T.** *Persona*: Loserth's expansion, with a query; the codices have *pe.* (521.20).

[^7]: **S.** Augustine, *Enchiridion* 4.14 (PL 40); the codices give "XVII" (521.22), and the print's "17°" is not explained (LEDGER E22b-2). The Manichees are named only in the heading of ch. 10, not here (LEDGER E22b-3). **C.** *Analogice … contrarie, privative aut positive*: good and evil are said as analogues, as equivocals, or as contraries, and as contraries either by privation or positively. *Unde illi fuerunt ignari dyalectici …*: taken as "those of the sect … were ignorant dialecticians"; it may be read "those ignorant dialecticians of whom Augustine speaks were of the sect of Manichaeus". **R.** B in the margin at 521.29: *Bonum accipitur tripliciter*.

[^8]: **S.** Isaiah 5:20: Wyclif has *dicunt bonum malum et malum bonum*, third person and the pair reversed, for the Vulgate *dicitis malum bonum, et bonum malum*; his Latin is translated. **C.** *Contingit*: it is possible. *Incompossibilia*: that cannot be together (cf. row *compossibilis*). *In facto*: in deed, by what they do.

[^9]: **S.** Hostiensis, *Summa aurea* V, *De remissionibus*, § *Quibus prosint* (*nec mortuis prosunt remissiones huiusmodi … potestas clavium non habet ibi locum*) (LEDGER E22b-4). **C.** *Caritate continuatis corpori Christi*: joined by charity in continuity with the body of Christ. *Ad suffragia*: by way of suffrages, as intercession; *secundum racionem absolvendi*, by way of the act of absolving. **R.** Side-note 522.7.

[^10]: **S.** Decretum C.24 q.2 c.6 *Sane* (Loserth, 522.14; LEDGER E22b-5). *Ubi te invenero, ibi te iudicabo*: a saying ascribed to the Lord, not in the Vulgate; current in the twelfth century (LEDGER E22b-6). The modern forms are ours. **C.** *Equivoce absolvitur*: absolved in name only, the absolution showing what contrition had already done. [^11]: **C.** *Diffinicio doctoris*: Hostiensis's determination at the end of the title (row *diffinicio*). *Redempcio*: here release from punishment, as in the saying "in hell there is no redemption".
