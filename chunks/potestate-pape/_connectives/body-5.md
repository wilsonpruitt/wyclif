# sed/autem extract, wave 5: chunks 06-e 06-f 06-g 06-h 06-i 07-a

### 06-e ¶1 · 118.6–119.6  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 118] Octava sentencia est: Romano pontifice vel quocumque alio fingente se gerere primatum vicarie potestatis Christi in terris, ab eius semita declinante secundum sensum scripture nullus plenius vel periculosius gerit de facto nomen et opera Antichristi. Pro declaracione istius sentencie notandum est primo secundum modum loquendi scripture: Quod quicumque est Christo vel legi sue contrarius, dicitur Antichristus, et concordat literalis composicio, cum Antichristus dicitur ab anti, quod est contra, et Christus quasi contra Christum. Et isto modo loquitur Evangelista in prima epistola sua canonica II cap. 18: Et sicut audistis, inquit, quia Antichristus venit, nunc Antichristi multi facti sunt. Et illos vocat Christus Matth. XXIV cap. Pseudochristos a pseudo quod latine dicitur falsus vel decipiens, et sic quilibet dyabolus vel membrum eius est realiter antichristus. Unde videtur michi illos esse culpandos qui preter auctoritatem scripture sompniant quod de tribu Dan in illo loco cum equis et curru igneis et ceteris ficticiis dominabitur Antichristus. Doctores **autem** qui sic loquuntur vel ad modum loquendi propheticum loquuntur mistice vel videntur ad modum concionatoris fingere. Certum est itaque quod persona prescitorum aggregata, est unus Antichristus, sicut quotlibet membrum eius, et illius erit unus capitalis in malicia plus [p. 119] accensus; ymo videtur predestinatum, dum crimine fuerit Christo contrarius, dici posse realiter Antichristum, modo quo ille qui dixit et facta sunt Mat. XVI propter adversacionem nominat Petrum sathanam. Est igitur christiano necessarium cognoscere Antichristum.

ENGLISH: The eighth [teaching]{g:sentencia} is this: when the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex}, or anyone else who feigns that he bears the [primacy]{g:primatus} of the vicarial [power]{g:potestas} of Christ on earth, turns aside from his path, then, according to the sense of scripture, no one bears more fully or more dangerously, in fact, the name and the works of Antichrist.[^1] To make this teaching plain it is to be noted, first, according to scripture's manner of speaking, that whoever is contrary to Christ or to his law is called Antichrist; and the literal composition of the word agrees, since Antichrist is named from *anti*, which means "against", and *Christus*, as if "against Christ". And in this way the Evangelist speaks in his first canonical epistle, chapter 2:18: "And as you have heard," he says, "that Antichrist cometh, now there are become many Antichrists."[^2] And Christ in Matthew 24 calls them "false christs" (Matt. 24:24), from *pseudo*, which in Latin is said "false" or "deceiving"; and so every devil, or [member]{g:membrum} of the devil, is really antichrist. Hence it seems to me that those are to be blamed who, beyond the [authority]{g:auctoritas} of scripture, dream that Antichrist, of the tribe of Dan, will lord it in that place with fiery horses and chariot and the other [fictions]{g:ficticia}.[^3] But the doctors who speak so either speak mystically, after the prophetic manner of speaking, or seem to invent after the manner of a preacher. It is certain, then, that the aggregate person of [the foreknown]{g:prescitus} is one Antichrist, as is any member of it; and of that [Antichrist] there will be one chief, more [119] inflamed in malice;[^4] indeed it seems that [a predestinate]{g:predestinatus} [person], while by crime he is contrary to Christ, can really be called Antichrist, in the way in which he who "spoke, and they were made" calls Peter Satan for his opposition, Matthew 16 (16:23).[^5] It is therefore necessary for a Christian to know Antichrist.

### 06-e ¶3 · 119.31–121.9  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 119] Et concordat racio cum antichristus peccato gravissimo et magis subdolo erit Christo contrarius. **Sed** illud peccatum oportet esse traditoriam [p. 120] ypocrisim, quia illam scit dyabolus magis efficere, et simplices propter velamentum eam minus percipere. Et ad istum sensum vadunt verba Apostoli II epistola ad Thym., II Petri epistola, et epistola Jude cum aliis dictis propheticis, quia indubie talis est apcius instrumentum dyaboli ad simplices seducendum. Quando enim dyabolus videt constantem dileccionem Christi in laycis et cum pietate ceca ignoranciam legis Christi, ex alioque latere avariciam Scariothis in clero, non est sibi placencius crimen propter ingratitudinis et duplicitatis gravedinem, quam seducere christianos in propriis instrumentis; sic enim victus est a Christo. Unde sine assercione ymaginor tercio quod sit unus nominetenus papa pseudonomine, cui iste XI condiciones insint ex instinctu patris mendacii. Primo quod ubi est Christus veritas Joh. XIV, Pseudo ubique innititur mendacio, quod notum est esse contrarium veritati, innititur, inquam, mendacio in verbis, in scriptis et vita, dicendo se propinquissime sequi Christum tamquam eius fidelem vicarium, dum tamen sit ab eo in moribus maxime elongatus, aliter enim non palliaret seducendo populum, quomodo veniret in nomine crucifixi, ut Christus prophetavit Matth. XXIV et Joh. XV. Secundo, ubi Christus regularissime tenuit summam pauperiem, iste Pseudo irregulariter summe seculo innititur; aliter enim non esset de mundo, **sed** ex Deo, qui per pauperiem vicit mundum, ut prophetatum est I Joh. II et Joh. [p. 121] XVII, cum enim sibi desunt miracula et vite sanctitas; aliter valde pauci suo disciplinatui iungerentur. Modo **autem** ex instinctu dyaboli totus mundus est mundo deditus, sic ubi Christus renuit oblacione dyaboli omnia regna mundi, ut dicitur Matth. IV, iste Pseudo dicit maniace quod dominetur capitaliter super tota habitabili et per eius licenciam et pacienciam singuli reges regnant.

ENGLISH: And reason agrees, since antichrist will be contrary to Christ by the gravest and more crafty sin. Now that sin must be treacherous [120] hypocrisy, for the devil knows it to be the more effective, and the simple, because of its veil, perceive it the less.[^9] And to this sense go the words of the Apostle in the second epistle to Timothy, of the second epistle of Peter and of the epistle of Jude, with other prophetic sayings; for without doubt such a one is the apter instrument of the devil for seducing the simple.[^10] For when the devil sees in the laity a constant love of Christ, and with blind [piety]{g:pietas} an ignorance of Christ's law, and on the other side the avarice of Iscariot in the clergy, no crime is more pleasing to him, for the gravity of its [ingratitude]{g:ingratitudo} and duplicity, than to seduce Christians by their own instruments; for so he was conquered by Christ.[^11] Hence, third, I imagine, without asserting it, that there is one [pope]{g:papa} in name only, by a false name, in whom these eleven conditions are present by the prompting of the father of lies (cf. John 8:44). First: whereas Christ is the truth (John 14:6), the Pseudo everywhere leans on a lie, which is known to be contrary to truth; he leans, I say, on a lie in words, in writings and in life, saying that he follows Christ most nearly as his faithful vicar, while yet he is in his manners most far removed from him. For otherwise he would not [cloak]{g:palliare} [himself] in seducing the people: how [else] would he come in the name of the Crucified, as Christ prophesied in Matthew 24 and John 15?[^12] Second: whereas Christ most regularly kept the highest poverty, this Pseudo irregularly leans in the highest degree on the world; for otherwise he would not be of the world but of God, who conquered the world through poverty, as is prophesied in 1 John 2 and John [121] 17; for since miracles and holiness of life are lacking to him, otherwise very few would be joined to his discipleship.[^13] But as it is, by the prompting of the devil the whole world is given over to the world: so, whereas Christ refused all the kingdoms of the world at the devil's offer, as is said in Matthew 4 (4:8–10), this Pseudo says like a madman that he lords it [as head]{g:capitaliter} over the whole inhabited earth, and that by his [licence]{g:licencia} and sufferance each king reigns.[^14]

### 06-e ¶4 · 121.10–121.28  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 121] Tercio, ubi Christus anathemate gravi prohibuit quod non adderetur aliquid impertinens legi sue, ut patet Gal. primo et sepe alibi, iste Pseudo fabricat leges novas, que nusquam reperiuntur in scriptura sacra; in qua secundum Augustinum est omnis veritas, **sed** sunt legi Christi latenter contrarie. Nec mirum quia sacerdos et propheta paulative declinantes a lege Domini preposituram talis legiferi meruerunt, ut prophetatum est Psalmo IX, 21. Ubi propheta de Antichristo loquens secundum exposicionem sanctorum: Constitue, inquit, legislatorem super eos. Et istam legis translacionem oportet sequi extraneum sacerdotem, quia Heb. VII, 12 dicit Apostolus, quod translato sacerdocio necesse est ut legis translacio fiat. Et iste triplex funiculus, scilicet commentum mendacii, commentum favoris seculi et commentum tradicionis inique sunt basis tocius retis dyaboli.

ENGLISH: Third: whereas Christ, under a grave anathema, forbade that anything not pertaining to his law be added to it, as is clear in Galatians 1 (1:8–9) and often elsewhere, this Pseudo fabricates new laws, which are nowhere found in holy scripture (in which, according to Augustine, is all truth), but are secretly contrary to [the law of Christ]{g:lex-christi}.[^15] Nor is it a wonder, for priest and prophet, turning aside little by little from the law of the Lord, have deserved such a lawgiver in the [office of superior]{g:prepositura} [over them], as is prophesied in Psalm 9:21, where the prophet, speaking of Antichrist according to the exposition of the saints, says: "Appoint a lawgiver over them."[^16] And this translation of the law must follow a foreign priest, for in Hebrews 7:12 the Apostle says that "the priesthood being translated, it is necessary that a translation be made of the law".[^17] And this threefold cord, namely the [fabrication]{g:commentum} of falsehood, the fabrication of the world's favour and the fabrication of wicked tradition, are the base of the devil's whole net.[^18]

### 06-f ¶1 · 122.30–123.8  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 122] Septimo, ubi Christus prohibet suis ferire cum gladio **sed** pati ut ipse, cum potuit hostes suos faciliter superare; ut patet Joh. XVIII, iste Pseudo docet et conducit milites de bonis pauperum [p. 123] ad cumulandum sibi superfluitates stercorum et discipulos suos, qui severius exequuntur illud officium, plus commendat et promovet ad seculi dignitatem, sic quod Christo conversante nobiscum non foret suis cum illis societas vel amicicia nisi inter homines prope pares, Et secundum Apostolum II Cor. VI, 15 non est communicacio Christi ad Belial, ut tangebam superius.

ENGLISH: Seventh: whereas Christ forbids his own to strike with the sword, but [bids them] suffer as he did, although he could easily have overcome his enemies, as is clear in John 18 (18:11), this Pseudo teaches and hires [soldiers]{g:milites} from the goods of the poor [123] to heap up for himself superfluities of dung; and those of his disciples who carry out that office the more severely he commends the more and promotes to the world's dignity; so that, were Christ living among us, his own would have no fellowship or friendship with them, [for there is none] except between men nearly equal.[^1] And according to the Apostle, 2 Corinthians 6:15, there is no communion of Christ with Belial, as I touched on above.[^2]

### 06-f ¶2 · 123.9–123.20  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 123] Octavo, ubi Christus prohibuit cariores eius discipulos Jacobum et Johannem inprecari ignem de celo descendere instar Helye, IV Reg primo ad vindicandum de Samaritanis negantibus eis necessaria, cibum et tegumina, ut patet Luce IX, 56, iste Pseudo deficiente materiali gladio fingit fulmen anathematis in eos, qui nolunt sue tyrannidi obedire, sic quod ubi Christus dicit suos discipulos esse spirituales nulli obnoxios, cum ipse non venit animas perdere **sed** salvare, ut ibi dicitur, iste Pseudo pro nichilo perdit utramque vitam multiplicium animarum.

ENGLISH: Eighth: whereas Christ forbade his dearer disciples, James and John, to call down fire from heaven after the manner of Elijah (4 Kings 1) to take vengeance on the Samaritans who denied them necessaries, food and shelter, as is clear in Luke 9:56, this Pseudo, when the material sword fails him, forges the thunderbolt of anathema against those who will not obey his tyranny; so that, whereas Christ says that his disciples are spiritual, hurtful to no one, since he "came not to destroy souls, but to save", as is said there, this Pseudo for nothing destroys both lives of many souls.[^3]

### 06-f ¶5 · 125.1–125.31  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 125] Undecimo et ultimo patet quod Christus abnuit omnem sentenciam, in qua necesse foret facere alicui iniuriam, ideo Luce XII, 14 dicit cuidam petenti eius iudicium: Quis me constituit iudicem aut diuisorem super vos? Unde et eius apostoli abalienati sunt a divisione cuiuscumque prepositure, que lucrum temporale sapiunt, quia didicerant a magistro Matth. X, 8: Gratis accepistis gratis date. Iste **autem** Pseudo cum discipulis suis palliat, quod ad eum solum spectat in solidum, omnes preposituras sapientes pinguedinem temporalem tribuere; nec potest propter plenitudinem potestatis symoniam committere, **sed** propter plenitudinem dominii oportet quemcumque secundum pinguedinem sui beneficii de thesauro Pseudo afferre; et de plenitudine potestatis facit reservaciones mentales absconditas, et virtute earum plus offerens preponetur, cum potest anteriorare vel posteriorare tempus ad libitum, ymo non obstante quod Christus sit cum suis apostolis omnibus diebus usque ad consumacionem seculi, ut dicit Matth. ultimo, iste tamen Pseudo potest dispensando tempus redimere et mutare, sic quod quando est maxime necessarium Christo et sue ecclesie consilia Christi servare, liceat sibi consilia ipsa suspendere, privilegia quecumque propter questum concedere, et quantumcumque Christo inhabiles tamquam habiles approbare, et quicquid fecerit debet tota militans ecclesia, quam oportet esse sub dicione ut legem Christi ratum credere.

ENGLISH: Eleventh and last: it is clear that Christ refused every [sentence]{g:sentencia} in which it would be necessary to do wrong to anyone; therefore, Luke 12:14, he says to one who asked for his judgment: "Who hath appointed me judge or divider over you?"[^10] Hence his apostles too were estranged from the distribution of any [office of superior]{g:prepositura} that savours of temporal gain, for they had learned from the master, Matthew 10:8: "Freely have you received, freely give." But this Pseudo with his disciples [cloaks]{g:palliare} [the matter, saying] that it belongs to him alone, in full, to bestow all offices of superior that savour of temporal fatness; and that he cannot commit simony, because of the [plenitude of power]{g:plenitudo-potestatis}, but because of the plenitude of [lordship]{g:dominium} everyone must bring [payment] out of his treasure to the Pseudo, according to the fatness of his [benefice]{g:beneficium-ecclesiasticum};[^11] and out of the plenitude of power he makes hidden mental reservations, and by virtue of them the one who offers more will be preferred, since he can put the time earlier or later at pleasure.[^12] Indeed, notwithstanding that Christ is with his apostles "all days, even to the consummation of the world", as he says in the last [chapter] of Matthew (28:20), yet this Pseudo can by dispensing redeem and change the time, so that, when it is most necessary for Christ and his church to keep the counsels of Christ, it is lawful for him to suspend those very counsels, to grant any [privileges]{g:privilegium} whatever for gain, and to approve as [qualified]{g:habilis} men however [unqualified]{g:inhabilis} for Christ; and whatever he has done, the whole [church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans}, which must be under [his] sway, ought to believe ratified, as [it believes] the law of Christ.[^13]

### 06-g ¶1 · 125.32–127.31  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 125] Multe sunt versucie velate sub istis XI, in quibus falluntur simplices versuciis Pseudochristi, et indubie quicumque fuerit talis Pseudo vel ei [p. 126] consenciens est secundum quod plus vel minus istis condicionibus participat Antichristus. Ideo docet beatus Evangelista epistola sua canonica; postquam Antichristum descripserit: Nolite, inquit, eum recipere in domum vestram, nec ave ei dixeritis; quod oportet intelligi nec in persona sua propria nec in membris, qui, inquit; dixerit illi ave, communicabit operibus eius malignis, et iste si est talis est heresiarcha solvens Christum infideliter in naturis persone, in Christi legibus et in membris, cuius scolam oportet cordatos theologos affeccione donorum suorum et timore suorum supplicacionum postpositis animose detegere. Et ipsum describunt omnes libri sapienciales vel prophetici alterius testamenti; et propter nostrorum vecordiam et membrorum suorum crescenciam in brevi tempore magis invaluit. Nec dubium quin sit infidelis Christo, qui aliquo quinque modorum consentit homini contra Christum. Sunt **autem** multa sophistica quibus homo iste illudit simplicibus christianis; dicitur enim primo quod status cleri sui est infinitum perfeccior quam status aliquis laycalis, ideo non pertinet ipsos de statu cleri cognoscere. Dicit secundo palpatus in metaphisica quod omnis status hominis est realiter ipse homo. Contra utrumque istorum invexi sepius: contra primum est decretum Crysostomi Imperfecto Omelia 26; ubi docet; quod homo debet iudicare de operibus manifestis, quia aliter precluderet lex Christi naturale iudicium et foret sibi ipsi contraria, [p. 127] cum ipsemet docet Matth. VII, 15 attendere a falsis prophetis ex fructibus cognoscendis, aliter enim necessario cecaret laycos ad elemosinas suas Antichristi discipulis improvide parciendum. Contra secundum patet quod repugnat priori, cum tunc omnis clericus foret infinitum perfeccior quam laycus; quod soli Christo potest competere; iterum omnis homo vel angelus maneret continue in eodem statu, sicut manet constanter in eadem humanitate, et sic nullus status posset maius aut minus suscipere. Et iterum periret status et christianorum distinccio, ymo propter casum Luciferi non foret status angelorum deterior, nisi forte dicatur quod status sit qualitas anime vel peccatum. Ideo certum est quod status iuxta methaphisicam scripture verissimam est denominacio, qua natura racionalis dicitur formaliter stare respectu finis ultimi, et sic in homine aliquis est status corporis, aliquis status anime et aliquis mixtum; aliquis eciam est status quoad mores et aliquis quoad ritus vel religionem, aliquis totalis et aliquis parcialis. Unde de statu corporali et statu mentali racionali vel integra individua potest intelligi illud Yob. XIII quod homo miserabilior lapsus nunquam in eodem statu permanet continue, nam secundum statum integrum variatur, ut patet de etate, de morum varietate et de circumstanciis statum integrantibus, que communiter variantur; manet tamen idem status communis et individuis successivis intercisus vel continuus.

ENGLISH: Many are the wiles veiled under these eleven, in which the simple are deceived by the wiles of the [false christ]{g:pseudochristus}; and without doubt whoever is such a Pseudo, or [126] consents to him, is Antichrist according as he shares more or less in these conditions.[^1] Therefore the blessed Evangelist teaches in his canonical epistle, after he has described Antichrist: "Receive him not," he says, "into your house, nor say to him: God speed you" (which must be understood neither in his own person nor in his [members]{g:membrum}); "he who shall say to him: God speed you," he says, "shall communicate with his wicked works."[^2] And this man, if he is such, is a heresiarch, faithlessly dissolving Christ in the natures of his person, in Christ's laws and in his members; and stout-hearted theologians must courageously [expose]{g:detegere} his school, setting aside affection for his gifts and fear for their supplications.[^3] And all the sapiential or prophetic books of the other testament describe him; and through the [faint-heartedness]{g:vecordia} of our men and the growth of his members he has in a short time grown the stronger. Nor is there doubt that he is [faithless]{g:infidelis} to Christ who in any of the five ways consents to a man against Christ.[^4] And there are many sophistries by which this man mocks simple Christians. For it is said, first, that the state of his clergy is infinitely more perfect than any lay state, and therefore it does not pertain to [the laity] to take cognizance of the state of the clergy. He says, second, having groped about in [metaphysics]{g:metaphisica}, that every state of a man is really the man himself.[^5] Against both of these I have often inveighed. Against the first is the decree of Chrysostom, *Opus imperfectum*, homily 26, where he teaches "that a man ought to judge of manifest works, since otherwise the law of Christ would shut out natural judgment and would be contrary to itself",[^6] [127] since he himself teaches, Matthew 7:15, to "beware of false prophets", who are to be known "by their fruits"; for otherwise it would of necessity blind lay people into sharing out their alms improvidently to the disciples of Antichrist.[^7] Against the second, it is clear that it is [incompatible]{g:repugnare} with the former, since then every cleric would be infinitely more perfect than a layman, which can belong to Christ alone. Again, every man or angel would remain continually in the same state, as he remains constantly in the same humanity; and so no state could admit more or less. And again the distinction of states and of Christians would perish; indeed, through Lucifer's fall the state of the angels would not be worse, unless perhaps it be said that a state is a quality of the soul, or sin.[^8] Therefore it is certain that a state, according to the most true metaphysics of scripture, is a [naming]{g:denominacio} by which a rational nature is said formally to stand in [relation]{g:respectus} to its last end; and so in man there is some state of the body, some state of the soul, and some mixed; there is also some state as to manners and some as to [rites]{g:ritus} or religion, some total and some partial.[^9] Hence of the bodily state, and of the mental, rational or integral individual state, can be understood that [saying] of Job 13, that man, fallen [and] more wretched, "never continueth in the same state"; for he varies according to his integral state, as is clear of age, of the variety of manners and of the circumstances that make up the state, which commonly vary; yet the same common state remains, broken off or continuous in [successive]{g:successivum} individuals.[^10]

### 06-g ¶3 · 129.4–129.31  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 129] Istam materiam diffuse prosecutus sum alibi cum aliis dubiis annexis, ideo transeo dictum repetens superius, quod status exproprietarie vivendi est quocumque statu civili perfeccior, sicut status virginitatis est quocumque statu coniugati perfeccior, natura angelica est naturaliter natura humana superior, vita contemplativa est vita activa perfeccior et sexus femineus est sexu masculino fragilior; et tamen constat quod multi coniugati sunt multis virginibus meliores, multi homines superiores dyabolis gracia non natura, multi activi sunt multis contemplativis sancciores, et multe femine virgines in iuvenili etate multis viris eciam decrepitis plus constantes, ut sepe meminit Augustinus in scriptis istius materie. Ideo necesse est volentem istam materiam intelligere considerare perfeccionem quam habet homo in quantum exproprietarie vivit, et perfeccionem quam habet in quantum civilis proprietarius, et illarum perfeccionum quas voco perfecciones simpliciter supra perfecciones individuas, primam inveniet plus perfectam. Ille **autem** perfecciones more universalium non possunt augmentari vel minui, **sed** perfecciones vel status individui secundum quod illis alia commoda sunt adiecta, et sic, ut sepe repecii, status exproprietarie vivendi propter excellenciam perfeccionis, congruit clero Christi.

ENGLISH: This matter I have pursued at length elsewhere, with other doubts annexed to it; therefore I pass on, repeating what was said above: that the state of living [without property]{g:exproprietarie} is more perfect than any civil state, as the state of virginity is more perfect than any state of the married, angelic nature is by nature superior to human nature, the contemplative life is more perfect than the active life, and the female sex is frailer than the male sex.[^17] And yet it is evident that many married people are better than many virgins, many men superior to devils by grace, not by nature, many active people holier than many contemplatives, and many women, virgins of youthful age, more constant than many men, even decrepit ones, as Augustine often recalls in his writings on this matter.[^18] Therefore whoever wishes to understand this matter must consider the perfection that a man has inasmuch as he lives without property, and the perfection that he has inasmuch as he is a civil [proprietary]{g:proprietarius} [owner]; and of those perfections, which I call perfections [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}, over and above individual perfections, he will find the first more perfect. Now those perfections, after the manner of [universals]{g:universale}, cannot be increased or diminished; but individual perfections or states [can], according as other advantages are added to them.[^19] And so, as I have often repeated, the state of living without property, because of the excellence of its perfection, befits the [clergy]{g:clerus} of Christ.

### 06-h ¶1 · 129.32–131.34  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 129] Et istam sentenciam approbat tota Trinitas cum tota ecclesia triumphante, tota natura bestiarum [p. 130] cum condicione status innocencie et saniores monachi, qui propter perfeccionem decreverunt statum illum eligere. Et patet probabilitas cronice, quam narrat Cestrensis libro IV, cap. LXXXVI quomodo tempore dotacionis ecclesie audita est angelica vox in aere, dicens quod hodie effusum est venenum in ecclesia sancta Dei. Nam quomodocumque hoc fuerit, hoc est verum, cum clerus a statu perfecciori decidit sine equivalente recompensa, ut eventus patulus rei probat. Unde manifestum signum foret Pseudochristi dampnantis ut hereticum quod genus illorum qui sic maculaverunt matrem nostram possunt eam purgare de macula quam ex eorum stulticia sic contraxit. Unde capitalis defendens pertinaciter istam sentenciam contra perfeccionem membrorum Christi et sancte matris ecclesie, heresiarcha racionaliter potest dici. **Sed** dubitatur si homo sit simul in multis statibus. Et videtur planum quod sic de statibus tam corporis quam anime compossibilibus, sicut homo habet simul denominaciones multorum actuum, ut puta qualitatum. Similiter differendum est utrique preposito peccanti propter dignitatem status, ut patet LXXXII distinccione cap. Presbiter et sepe alibi; et cum hoc valet efficaciter testimonium Augustini in multis locis, ut ubi docet quod in Pharaone, in Nabuchodonosor propter statum et dignitatem regalem ad regimen populi subiecti Deus inspiraverat propheciam, et propter sacerdotalem [p. 131] dignitatem Deus dedit Cayphe propheciam, ut dicit Augustinus in de Questionibus veteris et nove legis XXXV, et hinc David rite pepercit Sauli, ut patet I Reg. XXIV. Similiter, aliter nullius status celsitudo gravaret peccatum, quod est contra illud Sapiencie VI, 6 Durissimum iudicium hiis qui presunt, fiet, ymo aliter cuiuscumque prepositi status extingueretur, antequam quicquam boni vel mali faceret, que contradicunt scripture, cum ecclesie legibus, communi modo loquendi et sanctis doctoribus. Supposita igitur constancia statuum et facta limitacione ad statum quem preter nudos ritus humanos Deus ordinat, est dubium quomodo status variatur propter actus statui dissonos vel peccatum. Videtur enim quod status cum sit accidens tam parve constancie quod sit inalterabilis et per idem immobilis, cum manet eius dignitas cum peccato; aliter enim status sacerdotalis posset per alteracionem subiecti equiparari statui laycali et quilibet status cuilibet. Similiter, cum dignitas status hominis sit impertinens suis moribus, videtur quod stat eundem esse simul quantumcumque dignum et indignum et propter multiplicitatem statuum foret impertinens discernere per statum de hominis dignitate, nec sufficit homo cognoscere statum totalem aggregatum, tum quia infinitus est statuum numerus, tum quia aliquem statum proximi oportet viatorem quomodolibet ignorare. Similiter, cum status denigrari et debilitari poterit et alias serenari, videtur quod omne mortale statum quem invenit inficit, cum secundum Augustinum facit hominem esse nichil.

ENGLISH: And this [teaching]{g:sentencia} the whole Trinity approves, with the whole [church]{g:ecclesia} triumphant, the whole nature of beasts [130] together with the condition of the state of innocence, and the sounder monks, who for the sake of perfection decided to choose that state.[^1] And the probability of the chronicle is clear which Cestrensis tells in book 4, chapter 86: how at the time of the [endowment]{g:dotacio} of the church an angelic voice was heard in the air, saying that "today poison has been poured out into the holy church of God".[^2] For however this may have been, this is true, since the clergy fell from a more perfect state without equivalent recompense, as the open outcome of the matter proves. Hence it would be a manifest sign of a [false christ]{g:pseudochristus}, condemning as heretical [the teaching] that the kind of those who so stained [our mother]{g:mater-nostra} can cleanse her of the stain which she thus contracted from their folly. Hence the chief man who defends this teaching pertinaciously against the perfection of the [members]{g:membrum} of Christ and of holy mother church can reasonably be called a heresiarch.[^3] But it is asked whether a man may be in many states at once. And it seems plain that he may, as to states of body as well as of soul that are [compossible]{g:compossibilis}, just as a man has at once the [namings]{g:denominacio} of many acts, for instance of qualities. Likewise, deference is to be shown to each [superior]{g:prepositi} who sins, on account of the dignity of his state, as is clear in distinction 82, chapter *Presbiter*, and often elsewhere;[^4] and with this the testimony of Augustine holds good effectively in many places, as where he teaches that in Pharaoh and in Nebuchadnezzar God had inspired prophecy on account of the royal state and dignity, for the rule of the subject people, and on account of the priestly [131] dignity God gave Caiaphas prophecy, as Augustine says in *On Questions of the Old and New Law* 35; and hence David rightly spared Saul, as is clear in 1 Kings 24.[^5] Likewise, otherwise no loftiness of state would aggravate sin, which is against that word of Wisdom 6:6, "a most severe judgment shall be for them that bear rule"; indeed, otherwise the state of any superior whatever would be extinguished before he did anything good or bad; and these things contradict scripture, together with the laws of the church, the common way of speaking, and the holy doctors.[^6] Supposing, therefore, the constancy of states, and the [limitation]{g:limitacio} being made to the state which God ordains beyond bare human [rites]{g:ritus}, there is a question how a state is varied on account of acts discordant with the state, or of sin. For it seems that a state, although it is an accident of such slight constancy, is unalterable and by the same token immovable, since its dignity remains along with sin; for otherwise the priestly state could, through alteration of the subject, be made equal to the lay state, and any state to any other.[^7] Likewise, since the dignity of a man's state is irrelevant to his morals, it seems that it is consistent for the same man to be at once however worthy and unworthy; and on account of the multiplicity of states it would be irrelevant to judge of a man's dignity by his state; nor is a man able to know the total aggregate state, both because the number of states is infinite, and because a [wayfarer]{g:viator} must in some way be ignorant of some state of his neighbour. Likewise, since a state can be blackened and weakened and at another time made bright, it seems that every mortal [sin] infects the state it finds, since according to Augustine it makes a man to be nothing.[^8]

### 06-h ¶2 · 132.1–133.25  (4 sed/autem)

Hic oportet supponere descripcionem et distinccionem supradictas de statibus. Et secundo oportet notare quomodo dupliciter aliquid dicitur transmutari, vel subiective vel gracia concomitancie. Subiective ut sola substancia transmutatur; gracia concomitante omnis status vel res alia inexistens homini melioratur vel peioratur propter concomitanciam virtutis aut vicii. Et sic res in natura sua continue nunc est melior et nunc peior, et sic fides et spes possunt nunc formari caritate et nunc remanere informes, cum ipsa non permittit secum crimen ut suprema virtus et simplex. Quantum ad primum trium argumentorum sequencium patet ex distinccione iam posita, quomodo status non alteratur subiective **sed** con- comitative. Ipse **autem** infinitum excedit status alios, eciam status vivendi sive officii, qui dicuntur racione concomitancie virtutis vel vicii alterari; et patet ad primum obiectum. ENGLISH: From which it seems that, just as a man is in doubt about his neighbour whether he is in mortal [sin] or [foreknown]{g:prescitus}, so he would be in doubt about the excellence of any state of [one on the road]{g:viantes}. And secondly one must note how something is said to be changed in two ways: either subjectively or by reason of concomitance. Subjectively, as the substance alone is changed; by reason of concomitance, every state or other thing existing in a man is bettered or worsened on account of the concomitance of virtue or vice.[^11] And so a thing in its nature is continually at one time better and at another worse; and so faith and hope can at one time be formed by [charity]{g:caritas} and at another remain unformed, since charity, as the supreme and simple [virtue]{g:virtus}, does not allow crime alongside it.[^12] And it is clear that the threefold first argument strengthens the truth. As to the first of the three following arguments, it is clear from the distinction now set down how a state is altered not subjectively but concomitatively. And sometimes it is extinguished through sin, which it belongs to him to know who, incomprehensibly to us, ordains all states. And although the matter of states is of very wide compass (as the physician treats of the state of the body as it leads on to the felicity or misery of [the way]{g:via}, and the politician [133] considers the state of a part of the people according as it regards the peace of the commonwealth), yet the theologian and the [curate]{g:curatus}, who ought to be the physician of souls, considers the state of a man according to virtues or vices, as it regards the state of blessedness; and of such a state is our discourse.[^13] And this state infinitely exceeds the other states, even the states of living or of office, which are said to be altered by reason of the concomitance of virtue or vice; and the answer to the first [objection]{g:objectum} is clear. To the second the answer is that a temporal state is irrelevant to morals so far as concerns the nature of the state, or in itself [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}, which only those conceive who consider the natures of universals and reduplicative [natures]; nevertheless, as to the [relational]{g:respectivus} naming of the concomitance of the state, such states bear very relevantly on virtues and vices; and hence I call these states of disposition, and the others states of virtue. And so I grant that it is consistent for the same man to be worthy and unworthy [equivocally]{g:equivoce}, as regards the dignities of morals and of state; therefore it is pertinent to specify of which dignity of a man we speak when we judge a man's dignity from his state. And further it is granted that here we are not able to know the total state of a man except imperfectly, because we are ignorant of the outcome.[^14]

For this is to root out evil beasts from your borders, so that your flocks may be led out safe to pasture. You will tame the wolves, but you will not lord it over the sheep. You took them up to be fed, not to be oppressed." For this saint feared, from the exercise of lordship of that see, the subversion of the [so-called]{g:vocatus} church.[^15]
