# *De ecclesia*, chapter 19, part 3: the church's judges cannot judge suits over temporal goods; the forfeited stipend and the precarial grant

## ¶1 · 449.16–450.4

> [p. 449] Item, iudex generaliter ignorat que sit voluntas Dei quoad partes contendentes, que occuparet temporalia vendicata; sed nullus iudex ecclesiasticus iudicare debet pro parte nisi pro qua scit Deum discernere, quia aliter foret Deo contrarius iudicando. Ergo nullus iudex ecclesiasticus iudicaret in foro cleri contencioso nisi forte quod utraque pars cessaret a lite, intendens circa officium a domino limitatum. Illi enim quibus Christus limitat non portare saculum neque peram, sed, propter preponderanciam spiritualis officii ad quod primo accelerant, neque calciamenta ferre neque hominem per viam salutare neque quomodolibet occupari unde gravius officium discipuli Christi prepediat (ut docetur Luce X°, 4), valde alieni sunt, ut circa contenciones temporalium occupentur. Salutaciones [p. 450] enim per viam sunt nimis periculose et nimis a predicacione evangelii tardative. Nec valet ficcio qua dicitur temporalia pro quibus contenditur esse Christi discipulo pro suo officio necessaria

Again, a judge is generally [ignorant]{g:ignorare} what the will of God is as regards the contending parties, [that is,] which should occupy the temporal goods claimed; now no ecclesiastical judge ought to judge for a party except for the one for which he knows that God decides, for otherwise he would be contrary to God in judging. Therefore no ecclesiastical judge would judge in the contentious court of the [clergy]{g:clerus}, unless perhaps [to rule] that each party should cease from the suit, attending to the office [assigned]{g:limitare} by the Lord.[^1] For those to whom Christ assigns not to carry purse or scrip, but, because of the preponderance of the spiritual office to which they hasten first, neither to wear shoes nor to salute any man by the way, nor to be occupied in any way whereby he would hinder the weightier office of a disciple of Christ (as is taught in Luke 10:4), are far removed from being occupied about contentions over [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}.[^2] For salutations [450] by the way are too dangerous, and delay too much from the preaching of the gospel. Nor does the [fiction]{g:ficcio} avail by which it is said that the temporal goods contended for are, for a disciple of Christ, necessary

## ¶2 · 450.5–450.16

> [p. 450] instrumenta, quia exequatur suum officium sine retrospeccione ad temporalia, et non est compossibile Deum sibi deficere in temporalibus necessariis non ad onus. Ideo dicit Apostolus Heb. ultimo: Sint mores sine avaricia, contenti presentibus. Ipse enim dixit: Non te deseram neque derelinquam, ita ut confidenter dicamus: Dominus mihi adiutor, non timebo quid faciat mihi homo. Ideo dicit beatus Gregorius Omelia XVIIª Predicatori tanta debet esse in Deo fiducia ut presentis vite sumptus quamvis non provideat, tamen hos non sibi deesse certissime sciat, ne dum mens eius occupetur ad temporalia, minus aliis provideat eterna.

instruments for his office; for let him carry out his office without looking back to temporal goods, and it is not [compossible]{g:compossibilis} that God should fail him in necessary temporal goods that are not a burden.[^3] Therefore the Apostle says in Hebrews, the last chapter: "Let your manners be without covetousness, contented with such things as you have. For he hath said: I will not leave thee: neither will I forsake thee. So that we may confidently say: The Lord is my helper: I will not fear what man shall do to me." Therefore blessed Gregory says in Homily 17: "The preacher ought to have such trust in God that, though he does not provide for the expenses of the present life, he yet knows most certainly that they will not fail him, lest, while his mind is busied with temporal things, he provide the less of eternal things for others."[^4]

## ¶3 · 450.17–450.28

> [p. 450] Item, eo ipso quo quis est non membrum sancte matris ecclesie est indignus habere patrimonium Crucifixi titulo vel iure viri ecclesiastici, sed omnino iudex ecclesiasticus cui non sit de hoc specialis revelacio ignorat si dicta persona contendens sit membrum sancte matris ecclesie, ergo ignorat si sit dignus habere patrimonium vendicatum. Assumptum patet eo quod nullius alterius ecclesie persona nisi persona sancte matris ecclesia habet titulo illius ecclesie colorem aliquid vendicandi, cum sit de civitate et corpore dyaboli, contrarius prime iusticie. Et maior patet ex hoc quod nullus iudex ecclesiasticus subducta revelacione predicta

Again, by the very fact that someone is not a [member]{g:membrum} of holy [mother]{g:mater} [church]{g:ecclesia}, he is unworthy to have the patrimony of the Crucified by the [title]{g:titulus} or [right]{g:ius} of a churchman; now any ecclesiastical judge at all, to whom there is no special revelation about this, is ignorant whether the said person contending is a member of holy mother church; therefore he is ignorant whether he is worthy to have the patrimony claimed.[^5] The [assumption]{g:assumptum} is clear from this, that the person of no other church, except the person of holy mother church, has by the title of that church any [colour]{g:color} for claiming anything, since he is of the city and body of the devil, contrary to the first justice.[^6] And the major is clear from this, that no ecclesiastical judge, the said revelation withdrawn,

## ¶5 · 451.1–451.3

> [p. 451] ex quacunque probacione humana discernere, videtur quod propter ambiguitatem offendendi in Dei iusticiam cessaret omnis talis contencio.

[451] to discern by any human proof whatever, it seems that, because of the uncertainty of offending against God's justice, all such contention should cease.

## ¶6 · 451.4–453.8

> [p. 451] Nam si fundamentum tituli foret mendacium, quomodo non foret omne iudicium innitens illi fundamento iniquum? Aliter enim sufficeret cuicunque layco vel mechanico habere pretensionem vel apparenciam iuris ad iuste vendicandum et obtinendum quamcunque possessionem ecclesiasticam vel civilem; ex quo absurdissima sequerentur. Cum ergo pars Antichristi non sufficit probare quod sit vir ecclesiasticus, et iudex debet cavere omnino ne discrepet a divino iudicio, cum nullum nisi illud sit iustum simpliciter, videtur quod debet cessare omnino tale iudicium; et qui nituntur generaliter excusare curias ecclesiasticas in talibus iudiciis a peccatis lavant lateres et proditorie inpinguant inimicos iusticie oleo peccatoris. Nam Christus cum suis apostolis renuit talia placita secularia atque iudicia, et sic omnes curati vel clerici non cecati possessione et lege cesarea, de quibus loquitur Gregorius Omelia XVII: Multi, inquit, cum regiminis iura suscipiunt, ad lacerandum subditos inardescunt, terrorem potestatis exhibent, et quibus prodesse debuerant nocent, et quia caritatis viscera non habent, domini appetunt videri, patres se esse minime recognoscunt, humilitatis locum in elacionem dominacionis immutant, et si quandoque extrinsecus blandiuntur, intrinsecus seviunt. De quibus veritas dicit: Veniunt ad vos in vestimentis ovium, intrinsecus autem sunt lupi rapaces. Et forte sicut in tempore sancti Gregorii, sic sunt hodie in ecclesia quidam tales, et [p. 452] cum leges ecclesie licet sopite cum suis doctoribus illud dicunt concorditer, (ut patet III° Decretalium, cap. Fraternitatem), nec aliter possumus excusari de tanta occupacione temporalium differenter a Christo et suis apostolis, nisi quod sumus ministri eorum et procuratores pauperum quorum sunt bona que servamus non ut domini seculares, et tamen servare illam racionem in facto vel audire loqui de illa in verbo contempnimus ut venenum, sed tam sermone quam opere aspiramus ad dominium seculare. Item, ad repellendum tale humanum iudicium confert quod iudex ut plurimum ignorat si exactor interim cecidit a iure suo propter omissionem spiritualis ministerii subditis rependendum, sed stante illa iniusticia iniuste ferretur pro tali iudicium: ergo temerarium foret dare pro tali iudicium. Minor patet pro eo quod continuatim creatorum spirituale ministerium est magis debitum quam aliquod debitum corporale, ergo illis subtrahentibus magis debitum iniuste exigerent minus debitum et sub condicione quod retribuant magis debitum. Ymmo videtur quod excedunt in malicia servum nequam qui post magnam misericordiam factam maioris debiti minus sibi debitum exegit crudeliter (ut patet Matthei XVIII°). Talis enim curatus exigit a subditis sibi indebitum et hoc horribiliter per censuras et quomodocunque clerici circa prelacias contenderint, demum temporalia exigunt a viduis, a subiectis. Unde et istam iniuriam tangit Decretum, XIIª, q. II. Sepe fit, ubi ex decreto septimi Tolnensis concilii sic habetur: Si quis clericorum stipendium de rebus ecclesie cuiuscunque episcopi perceperit largitate, [p. 453] sub precarie nomine debeat professionem scribere, ne per retencionem diuturnam preiudicium afferat ecclesie, et quicunque in usu perceperit debet utiliter laborare, ut nec res divini iuris videantur aliqua occasione negligi et ut subsidium ab ecclesia cui deserviunt percipere possint clerici. Quod si quis eorum contempserit facere, ipse se stipendio suo videbitur privare.

For if the foundation of the title were a lie, how would every judgement resting on that foundation not be unjust? For otherwise it would suffice for any layman or craftsman to have a pretension or appearance of right in order justly to claim and obtain any possession whatever, ecclesiastical or civil; from which the most absurd things would follow. Since, therefore, Antichrist's party is not able to prove that [such a one] is a churchman, and the judge must altogether beware lest he differ from the divine judgement, since none but that is just [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}, it seems that such judgement ought altogether to cease;[^8] and those who strive to excuse the ecclesiastical courts generally from sins in such judgements are washing bricks, and treacherously fattening the enemies of justice with the oil of the sinner.[^9] For Christ with his apostles refused such secular pleas and judgements, and so do all [curates]{g:curatus} or clerics not blinded by possession and caesarean law; of whom Gregory speaks in Homily 17: "Many," he says, "when they take up the rights of rule, burn to tear their subjects; they display the terror of power, and harm those whom they ought to have profited; and because they have not the bowels of [charity]{g:caritas}, they desire to seem [lords]{g:dominus}, and do not at all acknowledge themselves to be fathers; they change the place of humility into the elation of the [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio}; and if sometimes they flatter outwardly, they rage inwardly." Of whom the Truth says: "They come to you in the clothing of sheep, but inwardly they are ravening wolves."[^10] And perhaps, as in the time of St Gregory, so today there are some such in the church; and [452] since the laws of the church, though lulled asleep, together with their doctors say this with one accord (as is clear in book 3 of the Decretals, chapter *Fraternitatem*), we cannot be excused otherwise for so great an occupation with temporal goods, so different from Christ and his apostles, except that we are their ministers and the [proctors]{g:procurator} of the poor, whose are the goods that we keep, not as secular lords; and yet we despise as poison keeping that account in deed, or hearing it spoken of in word, but in speech as in deed we aspire to secular [lordship]{g:dominium}.[^11] Again, it contributes to rejecting such human judgement that the judge [for the most part]{g:ut-plurimum} is ignorant whether the claimant has meanwhile fallen from his right through omission of the spiritual ministry that he is to render in return to his subjects; now while that injustice stands, judgement would be given unjustly for such a one: therefore it would be rash to give judgement for such a one.[^12] The minor is clear from this, that the spiritual ministry [owed] continually to creatures is more owed than any bodily debt; therefore, while they withhold what is more owed, they would unjustly exact what is less owed, and that on the condition that they repay what is more owed.[^13] Indeed it seems that they exceed in malice the wicked servant who, after great mercy had been shown him over a greater debt, cruelly exacted a lesser debt owed to him (as is clear in Matthew 18). For such a curate exacts from his subjects what is not owed to him, and that horribly, through [censures]{g:censura}; and however clerics may have contended about prelacies, in the end they exact temporal goods from widows, from subjects.[^14] Whence the Decretum also touches on this wrong, Causa 12, question 2, *Sepe fit*, where from a decree of the seventh council of Toledo it is had thus: "If any of the clerics has received a stipend from the goods of the church by the largesse of any bishop whatever, [453] he must write a profession under the name of a precarial grant, lest by long retention he bring prejudice to the church; and whoever has received [it] for use must labour usefully, so that the things of divine law may not seem on any occasion to be neglected, and that the clerics may receive support from the church that they serve. But if any of them scorns to do this, he will be seen to deprive himself of his stipend."[^15]

## ¶7 · 453.9–453.28

> [p. 453] Iura autem nature, iura scripture et iura ecclesie contestantur quod ecclesiasticus omnino omittens laborare pertinenter ad suum officium non debet accipere a subditis quibus non proficit sed officit temporale stipendium, quia tunc deberent laici conducere apostatas ad eis nocendum. Sed (ut sepe tetigi) contencio est quis exequeretur subtraccionem talem iustam, et videtur mihi non esse tantum sacramentum, quin illi qui stipendium ministrandi conferrent vacanti subtraherent, nec nocet fidelibus excommunicacio vel censura alia in hac parte (ut patet XIª, q. III. Quomodo sacerdos). Sumitur precaria nunc stricte pro dono vel stipendio temporali ad preces hominis pro tempore limitato, et nunc large pro quacunque donacione gratuita, eciam perpetua quoad usufructum donatore reservante penes se dominium, et sic in primitiva ecclesia tota dotacio censebatur precaria, ne elemosinarius prescriberet contra patronum; in defectu vero officii non cadit prescripcio sicut nec fit prescripcio contra Deum.

And the laws of nature, the laws of scripture and the laws of the church attest that an ecclesiastic who altogether omits to labour as pertains to his office ought not to receive a temporal stipend from subjects whom he does not profit but harms; for then lay people would have to hire apostates to harm them.[^16] But (as I have often touched on) the dispute is who should carry out such a just withdrawal; and it seems to me that it is no such great mystery but that those who conferred the stipend for ministering should withdraw it from the one who is idle; nor does excommunication or other censure harm the faithful in this matter (as is clear in Causa 11, question 3, *Quomodo sacerdos*).[^17] A precarial grant [is taken for]{g:sumitur-pro}, sometimes strictly, a gift or temporal stipend at a man's prayers for a limited time, and sometimes broadly any gratuitous donation whatever, even a perpetual one as to the [usufruct]{g:usufructus}, the donor reserving the lordship with himself; and so in the primitive church the whole [endowment]{g:dotacio} was reckoned a precarial grant, lest the [almsman]{g:elemosinarius} should acquire by [prescription]{g:prescripcio} against the [patron]{g:patronus}; and where the office fails, prescription does not run, just as there is no prescription against God.[^18]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 449.16: The clergy should not act as judges.
- 450.3: It is a mere pretence to say that the clergy need temporal possessions for their duties .
- 450.17: None can rightly hold the patrimony of 1 the Crucified but members of the Church,
- 451.1: but the judge cannot know who is such,
- 451.6: Since the ground of just judgments cannot be reached, it were better to give up such trials.
- 452.11: A claimant may have forfeited his right to dues by not ministering,
- 453.9: in which case all laws shew that he should not be paid.
- 453.15: The real question is who should withdraw the stipend? I say the patron.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Que occuparet*: "which [party] should occupy", an indirect question resuming *que sit voluntas Dei*. *Sed* brings the minor premiss. *Nisi forte quod utraque pars cessaret*: the only judgement allowed is that both parties drop the suit. *Intendens*: "attending to", taken with *utraque pars*; not GLOSSARY *intendere* "maintain". **R.** A in the margin at 449.16: *Iudex ecclesiasticus non debet iudicare inter litigantes pro temporalibus*.

[^2]: **S.** Luke 10:4, DR "Carry neither purse, nor scrip, nor shoes: and salute no man by the way"; Wyclif paraphrases. **C.** *Limitat*: "assigns", prescribes to them (GLOSSARY *limitare*). *Unde … prepediat*: no subject is expressed; taken as the disciple himself. *Saculum*: so the print, for *sacculum*.

[^3]: **C.** The sentence runs on from the previous paragraph (*necessaria / instrumenta*). *Quia exequatur*: the subjunctive taken as an injunction, "let him carry out". *Non ad onus*: taken with *necessariis*, necessaries that are no burden to him; or "not so as to be a burden [to others]". **R.** A in the margin at 450.3: *Ficcio*.

[^4]: **S.** Hebrews 13:5–6 (DR). Gregory, *Homiliae in Evangelia* 17.5 (Loserth: *Greg. XL. Hom. lib. I, Hom. XVII, 5*; LEDGER E19c-1, editor). **T.** At 450.10 B omits *te*. **R.** A in the margin at 450.11: *Gregorius. Nota qualem spem debet habere predicator ad Deum*.

[^5]: **C.** *Vir ecclesiasticus*: "churchman", one who claims the church's goods as a cleric. *Omnino iudex*: "any judge at all", *omnino* taken with *iudex*; or adverbial, "is altogether ignorant". *Patrimonium Crucifixi*: the church's goods as the patrimony of Christ.

[^6]: **C.** *Assumptum* here proves the first premiss (the non-member's unworthiness) and *maior* the second (the judge's ignorance), the reverse of the usual labels; translated as printed. *Sancte matris ecclesia* (p. 450): *ecclesia* is taken as the genitive *ecclesie*. *Colorem*: a plausible ground for a claim (GLOSSARY *color*). *Contrarius prime iusticie*: taken with the non-member, opposed to God, the first justice; or with the devil.

[^7]: **C.** *Incorporacio*: being incorporated as a member into holy mother church, the ground of any title. *Docere*: "to show", as by proof.

[^8]: **C.** *Pretensionem*: so the print, a claim put forward. *Pars Antichristi non sufficit probare quod sit vir ecclesiasticus*: *sit* has no expressed subject; taken as a member of Antichrist's party pleading as a churchman.

[^9]: **S.** *Lavant lateres*, "they wash bricks": the proverb of wasted labour; Loserth refers to Terence, *Phormio* 186, and Erasmus, *Adagia*, *Chil. I*, 370 (LEDGER E19c-2, editor). The oil of the sinner: Psalm 140:5, DR "let not the oil of the sinner fatten my head".

[^10]: **C.** *Placita*: pleas, suits at law (cf. GLOSSARY *placitare*). *Lege cesarea*: the caesarean law, the civil law under which the clergy hold as of Caesar (cf. [^11]: **S.** Decretals book 3, *Fraternitatem* (Loserth: *vid. Decret. Greg. IX, lib. III, tit. XXVIII, cap. III*).
