# *De ecclesia*, chapter 3, part 4: equivocation on church and head; three proofs that Christ is head of all; four senses of saving

## ¶1 · 58.17–60.4

> [p. 58] Non repugnat ergo equivocando vocare corpus diaboli ecclesiam Christi, ut posito quod una communitas nominetenus clericorum prescita credatur esse particularis ecclesia, licet committat peccata quantumcunque enormia. Tunc vulgus reputat eam esse ecclesiam, ymo quilibet eorum asserit quod propter ordinem est vir sancte matris ecclesie, racione cuius tituli excecat populum, in fide, officio et ypocrisi indubie menciendo, quia sic idem foret simul membrum Christi et membrum diaboli, ecclesia sanctorum et ecclesia malignancium. Tales enim dicit Thomas eciam membra ecclesie in potencia, licet nunquam reducantur ad actum. Ac si diceretur illud habet potenciam ex sufficiencia passionis Christi et potestate sui arbitrii esse membrum Christi, cum tamen nude sit membrum congregacionis habencium fidem informem, licet nunquam sit membrum corporis Christi mistici. Sed ne ego laborem in istis [p. 59] equivocis, limitavi nomen ecclesie, ut signat sponsam Christi. Tres tamen evidencie fiunt ad probandum hanc sentenciam. Primo dictum apostoli I. Timothei IV°, 10 Salvator est omnium et maxime fidelium; secundo per illud I. Johannis, II° cap., Ipse propiciacio pro peccatis nostris, non autem pro nostris tantum est, sed eciam pro tocius mundi. Sed in quantum salvat et propiciatur hominibus, est caput eorum: ergo Christus est caput omnium hominum. Tercio per hoc quod omnes homines sunt fideles, quia habent fidem de Deo. Patet quod ista argumenta procedunt ex equivoco: nam concedi potest cum sancto Thoma quod Christus quoad esse naturale est caput eciam diaboli, et per consequens, caput membrorum suorum, non solum quoad esse naturale, sed eciam quia sunt fratres Christi quoad naturam specificam, quorum omnia peccata ad eorum dampnacionem et penam cedunt, sanctis vero ad gloriam. Et sic misericorditer punit fratres suos ad bonum eorum (ut alias declaravi). Loqui autem sic generaliter atque equivoce est preter propositum nostrum, quia loquimur de capite et membris unius hominis ex sponso et sponsa, de quo Joh. III°, 13 dicitur quod nemo ascendit in celum, nisi qui descendit de celo (ut Augustinus exponit, Joh. Omelia XIIª). Et quantum ad dictum apostoli, dicitur communiter quod esse salvatorem vel salvare est equivocum ad quatuor: primo generaliter accipitur pro apponere medicinam ad infirmum cuius virtute egrotus sanari poterit, et isto modo salvavit Christus totum mundum humani generis, cum apposuit medicinam passionis, que suffecit redimere multos mundos. Istam autem [p. 60] medicinam apposuit Christus vel obtulit humano generi, cuius septima pars nunquam suscepit eam, sicut semper infideles quos Christus dicit Joh. III°, 18, iam iudicatos, et illos dicit sanctus Thomas esse membra

It is not repugnant, therefore, by equivocation, to call the body of the devil the church of Christ:
as, supposing that one community of clerics in name only, [foreknown]{g:prescitus}, is believed to be a
particular [church]{g:ecclesia}, although it commits sins however enormous. Then the common people
reckon it to be the church; indeed, each of them asserts that by reason of his order he is a man of holy
mother church, by reason of which title he blinds the people, lying beyond doubt in faith, office and
hypocrisy; for so the same [thing] would be at once a member of Christ and a member of the devil, the
church of the saints and the church of the malignant.[^1] For Thomas calls such men also members of the
church in potency, although they are never brought to act. As if it were said: that [one] has the
potency, from the sufficiency of Christ's passion and the power of his own choice, to be a member of
Christ, while yet he is barely a member of the congregation of those who have
[unformed faith]{g:fides-informis}, although he is never a member of
[the mystical body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi-misticum}.[^2] But, lest I labour over these [59]
equivocal [senses], I have [assigned]{g:limitare} the name church [to it] as it signifies the
[bride]{g:sponsa} of Christ. Yet three [pieces of evidence]{g:evidencia} are brought to prove this
[teaching]{g:sentencia}. First, the saying of the apostle, 1 Timothy 4:10: "He is the Saviour of all,
especially of the faithful"; second, by that of 1 John, chapter 2: "He is the propitiation for our
sins: and not for ours only, but also for those of the whole world." Now, in so far as he saves men and
is propitiation for them, he is their head: therefore Christ is the head of all men. Third,
by this: that all men are faithful, because they have faith about God.[^3] It is clear that these
arguments proceed from equivocation: for it can be granted with St Thomas that Christ, as to natural
being, is head even of the devil, and consequently head of his members, not only as to natural being,
but also because they are brothers of Christ as to specific nature; all of whose sins turn to their
damnation and punishment, but for the saints to glory. And so he punishes his brothers mercifully, for
their good (as I have declared elsewhere).[^4] But to speak so generally and equivocally is beside our
purpose, because we are speaking of the head and members of one man [made] of bridegroom and bride, of
which it is said in John 3:13 that "no man hath ascended into heaven, but he that descended from heaven"
(as Augustine expounds, on John, homily 12).[^5] And as to the saying of the apostle,
[the answer]{g:dicitur} commonly given is that to be a saviour, or to save, is equivocal in four [ways]:
first, it is taken generally for applying to the sick a medicine by whose virtue the sick man could be
healed; and in this way Christ saved the whole world of the human race, since he applied the medicine
of the passion, which sufficed to redeem many worlds. And this [60] medicine Christ applied or offered to
the human race, a seventh part of which never received it, as always the unbelievers, whom Christ, John
3:18, calls "already judged"; and these St Thomas calls members of

## ¶2 · 60.5–60.10

> [p. 60] Christi in potencia, non in actu. Alii autem recipiunt medicinam sed finaliter recidivant, ut presciti. Tercii vero receperunt medicinam et post lapsum ad sanctam matrem ecclesiam revertuntur, ut peccatores predestinati. Quarti quippe sanantur virtute meriti Christi nec unquam post incidunt in mortale.

Christ in potency, not in act. And others receive the medicine but finally relapse, as the
[foreknown]{g:prescitus}. But the third have received the medicine and after a fall return to holy
mother church, as [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} sinners. The fourth, indeed, are healed by virtue of
Christ's merit and never afterwards fall into mortal [sin].[^6]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 59.3: Objectors give three proofs of their opinions: 1. St. Paul's words;
- 59.8: 2, St. John's words;
- 59.10: 3. All men are believers since all believe about God. All these arguments rest on a double use of language.
- 59.26: Four uses of the word to save:
- 59.27: I. To some salvation is offered but refused.
- 60.5: Others receive it but fall away. A third sort after falling away return. The fourth class receive and hold to it.

## Notes

[^1]: **C/T.** *Equivocando*: using the word in another sense. *Nominetenus clericorum*: clerics in name
    only; *prescita* agrees with *communitas*. *Excecat*: the subject is each cleric. *Ecclesia
    malignancium*: cf. Ps. 25:5 (Vulgate *odivi ecclesiam malignantium*). B *ecclesie Romane cuius* at
    58.23 (the words it replaces are not clear from the apparatus). A margin: *Equivocando conceditur
    corpus diaboli esse ecclesiam Christi*; B: *Ecclesia Christi nuncupative* (58.16).

[^2]: **S/C.** Aquinas, *ST* III q.8 a.3 co. and ad 1 (LEDGER E03d-1): those who are members in a potency never
    brought to act. *Illud*: neuter, "that [one]", the foreknown cleric or community. *Fides informis*:
    faith not formed by charity (GLOSSARY §A); B *formem* (58.32). A margin: *Presciti quomodo sunt in
    potencia membrum (sic) Christi secundum beatum Thomam*; B: *Sanctus Thomas* (58.27).

[^3]: **S/T.** *L* (59.2) and *M* (59.26) were Loserth's alinea letters, since removed from the Latin. B
    *literavi* for *limitavi* (59.1). 1 Tim. 4:10, DR without "men" (Wyclif lacks *hominum*); 1 John
    2:2, DR; A *nostris non tantum est* (59.7); B *per idem* (59.6). The two texts are the *sed contra*
    of *ST* III q.8 a.3; the third proof is not (LEDGER E03d-1). AB margin: *Triplex
    obieccio* (59.3); B: *I. Tim. 4, I. Joh. 2* (59.4).

[^4]: **S/C.** That Thomas grants Christ to be head "even of the devil" as to natural being is not
    verified: it is not in *ST* III q.8 (a.7 makes the devil head of the wicked) nor in *In III Sent.* d.13 q.2 (nearest, a.2 qc.2 ad 6: the wicked share with the members only natural life) (LEDGER E03d-2). *Natura
    specifica*: the human species, which the devil's members share with Christ. *Ut alias declaravi*:
    unidentified (LEDGER E03d-4). A margin: *Responsio* (59.13); *Nota* (59.21).

[^5]: **S.** John 3:13, DR. Augustine, *In Iohannis evangelium tractatus* 12.8–9, PL 35:1488–89, read
    this session: none ascends but he who descended, because Christ descended *ut in illo et cum illo
    unus essent* (LEDGER E03d-3; Loserth: Opp. III/2, 387). *Unius hominis ex sponso et sponsa*: Christ
    and the church as one person. B margin: *Augustinus* (59.25).

[^6]: **C/R.** The four senses of *salvare* (59.26) are four sorts of men offered the medicine; Wyclif
    numbers only the first and then *alii … tercii … quarti*. *Septima pars* (60.1), as printed: the
    fraction is not explained. John 3:18, DR "is already judged". *Mortale*: mortal [sin]. AB margin:
    *Salvacio triplex* (59.25), against Wyclif's four; A: *Christus salvavit totum mundum quoad
    sufficienciam* (59.29); B: *Joh. III* (60.3).
