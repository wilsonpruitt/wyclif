# *De ecclesia*, chapter 23, part 3: the treasury of merits in Bonaventure and the decretists; three arguments for the opinion; how one may make satisfaction for another

## ¶1 · 554.23–555.15

> [p. 554] Secundo modo meritum Christi cooperatur in sacramentis et potestate clavium, et per hoc remittitur pars pene. Et tercio modo fit meritum ad fugiendum malum ex vi unitatis ecclesiastice. Et correspondenter fit triplex pene condonacio, ut sic currat misericordia cum iusticia. Et in qualibet tali condonacione est commutacio maioris in minus, unde pena eterna primo [p. 555] mutatur in temporalem in remissione pene et culpe. Secundo pena temporalis magna et supra vires mutatur in penam viribus competentem in sacerdotis absolucione. Tercio vero commutatur pena ad quam pertinet iudicio ecclesie in minorem relaxacionem in virtute residui meritorum ecclesie. Et omnia ista originantur ex merito passionis et satisfaccionis Christi, nullibi preiudicando iusticie. Et ita dicit quod indulgencie fiunt de meritis supererogacionis membrorum ecclesie, quem thesaurum pertinet ad solos episcopos saltem publice dispensare, quia illi sunt vicarii succedentes in loco Christi fratris senioris mortui, ut suscitent semen suum generando sibi filios spirituales. Et uterque istorum doctorum respondet ad obiectus in contrarium.

"Secondly, the merit of Christ cooperates in the sacraments and the power of the keys, and by this a part of the punishment is remitted. And thirdly, merit for fleeing evil arises by virtue of the church's unity. And correspondingly there arises a threefold pardon of punishment, so that thus mercy may run with justice. And in each such pardon there is an [exchange]{g:commutacio} of the greater into the less: hence eternal punishment is first [555] changed into temporal in the remission of punishment and guilt; secondly, temporal punishment, great and beyond one's strength, is changed into a punishment suited to one's strength in the priest's absolution; and thirdly the punishment which falls to the judgment of the [church]{g:ecclesia} is exchanged, by relaxation, into a lesser, in virtue of the residue of the merits of the church." And all these originate from the merit of Christ's passion and satisfaction, nowhere prejudicing justice.[^1][^2] And so he says that indulgences are made from the merits of supererogation of the [members]{g:membrum} of the church, which treasury it belongs to bishops alone to dispense, at least publicly, because they are vicars succeeding in the place of Christ, the elder brother who died, that they may raise up his seed by begetting spiritual sons for him.[^3] And each of these doctors replies to the [objections]{g:objectum} to the contrary.

## ¶2 · 555.16–556.4

> [p. 555] Et illam sentenciam sepe inculcant doctores decretorum ponentes quod indulgencia est liberalis et graciosa donacio de thesauro ecclesie, facta per vicarium Christi peccatoribus pro suis penis ad quas de iusticia obligantur. Thesaurus iste, inquiunt, est habundancia meritorum que multi sancti ultra mensuram debitorum erogaverunt et tribulaciones quas iniuste sustinuerunt; quorum meritorum tanta est copia quod excedit omnem penam debitam viventibus, et precipue passio et meritum Christi, cuius minima gutta sanguinis vel sudoris sufficeret ad expiacionem omnium peccatorum, hec et cetera bona supererogat, que omnia ponuntur in scrinio pectoris Christi tamquam thesaurus ecclesie. Ista sentencia patet per Willielmum in De Reliquiis et Veneracione Sanctorum, capitulo V°. Et patet [p. 556] quomodo a theologis decretiste sumpserunt istam sentenciam que, si sit infundabilis et principium erroris fidelium, patet gravedo peccati in auctoribus illius ficticie.

And the doctors of the decrees often inculcate that [teaching]{g:sentencia}, holding that an indulgence is a liberal and gracious gift from the treasury of the church, made by the [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius Christi} to sinners for their punishments, to which they are bound in justice. "This treasury," they say, "is the abundance of merits which many saints have paid out beyond the measure of their debts, and the tribulations which they unjustly bore; of which merits so great is the plenty that it exceeds all punishment owed to the living; and especially the passion and merit of Christ, of whom the least drop of blood or sweat would suffice for the expiation of all sins: these and other goods he paid out beyond what was owed, all of which are placed in the coffer of Christ's breast as the treasury of the church."[^4] This teaching is clear from William in *De reliquiis et veneracione sanctorum*, chapter 5. And it is clear [556] how the decretists took this teaching from the theologians; and if it is groundless and a principle of error for the faithful, the gravity of the sin in the authors of that [fiction]{g:ficticia} is clear.[^5]

## ¶3 · 556.5–556.8

> [p. 556] In ista materia sic procedam: Primo fundabo vel colorabo possibilitatem et probabilitatem illius sentencie, secundo obiciam multipliciter contra illam et tercio dicam quid videtur mihi probabiliter sustinendum.

In this matter I shall proceed thus: first I shall ground, or [give colour to]{g:colorare}, the possibility and probability of that teaching; secondly I shall object in many ways against it; and thirdly I shall say what seems to me probably to be maintained.[^6]

## ¶4 · 556.9–556.21

> [p. 556] Quantum ad primum sic arguo: Deus nedum potest de sua absoluta potestate quecunque peccata eciam sine satisfaccione remittere, sed unus potest de communi cursu pro alio satisfacere, et per consequens, Deus potest committere suo vicario potenciam contra quecunque peccata taliter dispensandi. Quo habito suadetur tripliciter quod sic facit, primo quia caput ecclesie pro quo Christus oravit Luce XXII°, 32, ut non deficiat fides sua, sic pretendit se facere de facto; sed foret error intollerabilis si in isto a fide exorbitet, sicut maxime dampnabilis fatuitas foret membris ecclesie hoc sibi imponere, ergo presumendum est factum istud esse catholicum.

As to the first, I argue thus: not only can God, of his [absolute power]{g:potencia absoluta}, remit any sins whatever even without satisfaction, but one can, in the common course, make satisfaction for another; and consequently God can commit to his vicar the power of dispensing in this way against any sins whatever. This granted, it is urged in three ways that he does so: first, because the [head]{g:caput} of the church, for whom Christ prayed (Luke 22:32) that his faith fail not, claims to do so in fact; but it would be an intolerable error if in this he strayed from the faith, just as it would be a most damnable folly for the members of the church to impose this on him; therefore it is to be presumed that this deed is catholic.[^7]

## ¶5 · 556.22–556.29

> [p. 556] Item, Matthei XVI°, 19 concessit Christus Petro et cuilibet eius vicario singulariter plenitudinem potestatis dicens: Quodcunque ligaveris super terram erit ligatum et in celis, et quodcunque solveris super terram erit solutum et in celis. Cum ergo dominus papa sic solvit et ligat in terris, videtur ex evangelio quod proporcionaliter fit in celis; aliter enim titubaret fides ecclesie.

Again, in Matthew 16:19 Christ granted to Peter and to each of his vicars singly the [plenitude of power]{g:plenitudo potestatis}, saying: "Whatsoever thou shalt bind upon earth, it shall be bound also in heaven: and whatsoever thou shalt loose on earth, it shall be loosed also in heaven." Since, then, the lord [pope]{g:papa} so looses and binds on earth, it seems from the gospel that proportionately it is done in heaven; for otherwise the faith of the church would totter.[^8]

## ¶6 · 556.30–557.2

> [p. 556] Item, Deus reliquit unum caput in ecclesia pro dubiis decidendis, quod non potest poni nisi Petri vicarius; ergo sibi restat difficultatem istam decidere; sed cum factum suum sit manifestissimus interpres sensus sui, videtur quod in facto docet hoc esse [p. 557] catholicum. Quis rogo iudicaret papam cum conventu suo errare?

Again, God left one head in the church for deciding doubts, who cannot be held to be anyone but Peter's vicar; therefore it remains for him to decide this difficulty; but since his deed is the most manifest interpreter of his meaning, it seems that by the deed he teaches this to be [557] catholic. Who, I ask, would judge the pope with his convent to err?[^9]

## ¶7 · 557.3–557.32

> [p. 557] Assumptum ergo primi argumenti sic docetur: Oportet ex fide credere quod Christus satisfecit pro omnibus membris suis, sicut ex vi communionis sanctorum quodlibet membrum ecclesie satisfecit pro altero, cum apostolus dicat Gallatis VI°, 2°: Alter alterius onera portate; ergo per idem contingit ecclesiam beatam satisfacere pro viante. Item, contingit unum condempnare alterum, ut patet de primis parentibus et multis aliis ex suis capitibus et consociis accusatis. Cum ergo Deus sit pronior ad premiandum quam puniendum, videtur quod contingit unum satisfacere pro alio. Sic enim brachium naturaliter se exponit pro capite; multo magis in corpore Christi mistico, ubi est ligatum forcius, videtur quod unum membrum debet satisfaciendo supportare pondus alterius iuxta istud Apostoli IIª Corinth. XI°, 29: Quis infirmatur et ego non infirmor? Item, Deus est liberior ad recipiendum satisfaccionem de suis debitoribus quam servus suus de debitoribus suis, sed homo sepe racionabiliter recipit debitum unius per puram satisfaccionem alterius, ergo contingit Deum liberrimum hoc facere. Si ergo creditor benigne accipit talentum debitum a Petro ex pura satisfaccione Pauli hoc solventis pro eo, multo magis Deus misericors. Si ergo unus in gracia impetrat alteri remissionem culpe, quod maius est, ipse evidencius potest impetrare sibi remissionem pene, quod minus est. Pia ergo consolativa fidelium et honorificativa cleri est ista posicio.

The [assumption]{g:assumptum} of the first argument, then, is shown thus. It must be believed, [as faith teaches]{g:ex fide}, that Christ made satisfaction for all his members, just as by virtue of the communion of saints each member of the church has made satisfaction for another, since the apostle says in Galatians 6:2: "Bear ye one another's burdens"; therefore by the same token it is possible for the blessed church to make satisfaction for [one on the road]{g:viantes}. Again, it is possible for one to bring condemnation on another, as is clear of the first parents and of many others, on account of their heads and associates who were accused. Since, then, God is more prone to reward than to punish, it seems that it is possible for one to make satisfaction for another.[^10] For thus the arm naturally exposes itself for the head; much more in the [mystical body of Christ]{g:corpus Christi misticum}, where it is bound more strongly, it seems that one member ought, by making satisfaction, to bear the weight of another, according to that of the Apostle, 2 Corinthians 11:29: "Who is weak, and I am not weak?" Again, God is freer to receive satisfaction from his debtors than his servant from his debtors; but a man often reasonably receives the debt of one by the pure satisfaction of another; therefore it is possible for God, most free, to do this. If, then, a creditor kindly accepts a talent owed by Peter from the pure satisfaction of Paul paying it for him, much more God the merciful. If, then, one in grace [obtains]{g:impetrare} for another the remission of guilt, which is greater, he can more evidently obtain for him the remission of punishment, which is less. This position, therefore, is pious, consoling to the faithful and honouring to the clergy.[^11]

## ¶8 · 557.33–558.21

> [p. 557] Primo ergo concedenda est ista conclusio quod contingit unum satisfacere pro alio. Sed notandum [p. 558] quod hoc intelligi potest tripliciter, primo participative satisfaciendo pro suo contento, secundo iuvative concausando totam satisfaccionem in omnino altero et tercio per se sine concursu alterius satisfaciendo pro toto debito. Exemplum primi est in assumpcione nature humane per Christum in attomo et complecione primiciarum tocius debiti humani generis pro peccato. Exemplum secundi est quomodo nedum Christus sed alia membra iuvant ad plenam satisfaccionem confratrum; et tercio modo unus satisfacit pro debito particulari alterius particulari creditori. In utraque primarum satisfaccionum oportet debitorem concurrere, ut quamvis Christus fecit se naturam humanam que debuit Deo satisfaccionem pro peccato primi hominis, tamen illa satisfaccio non potuit membris proficere, nisi ex adiutorio capitis se disponentibus active ad suscipiendum medicinam vel saltem ad consolvendum ministracionem debitam, in tantum quod infans unius diei decedens in gracia primo baptizatur flamine, secundo servit Deo meritorie et sic tercio participat fructum celestis medicine.

First, then, this [conclusion]{g:conclusio} is to be granted: that it is possible for one to make satisfaction for another. But it must be noted [558] that this can be understood in three ways: first participatively, by making satisfaction for one's own share; secondly helpingly, by co-causing the whole satisfaction in one wholly other; and thirdly of oneself, without the concurrence of the other, by making satisfaction for the whole debt.[^12] An example of the first is in the assumption of human nature by Christ in an individual, and the completion of the first-fruits of the whole debt of the human race for sin. An example of the second is how not only Christ but other members help toward the full satisfaction of their brethren; and in the third way one makes satisfaction for the particular debt of another to a particular creditor.[^13] In each of the first two satisfactions the debtor must concur; so that, although Christ made himself the human nature which owed God satisfaction for the sin of the first man, yet that satisfaction could not profit the members unless, by the help of the head, they disposed themselves actively to receive the medicine, or at least to pay together the service owed; so much so that an infant of one day, dying in grace, is first [baptized by the Spirit]{g:baptismus flaminis}, secondly serves God meritoriously, and so thirdly shares in the fruit of the heavenly medicine.[^14]

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** The quotation of Bonaventure continues from the previous part (*Obtinemus, inquit …*, 554.15) and is closed at *ecclesie*, where the print's italics end; *Et omnia ista … iusticie* is Wyclif's summary. *Secundo modo … tercio modo*: the merit of the head, then the merit of the church's unity, matching the three merits of obtaining good. *Condonacio*: "pardon", kept apart from *remissio* "remission" (cf. *condonare*, 549.27). *Ut sic currat misericordia cum iusticia*: so that mercy runs together with justice. *Commutacio maioris in minus*: an exchange of the greater into the less (row *commutacio*). **R.** Side-note 555.1.

[^2]: **T.** *Commutatur* is Loserth's correction of the codices' *commutacio*; he suggests reading *commutacio pene* (555.4). **C.** *Pena ad quam pertinet iudicio ecclesie*: the subject of *pertinet* is unexpressed; taken as the punishment that falls to the church's judgment, the enjoined penance. *In minorem relaxacionem*: literally "into a lesser relaxation"; taken as exchanged, by relaxation, into a lesser punishment. *Residui meritorum ecclesie*: the surplus of the church's merits.

[^3]: **S.** Bonaventure, *In IV Sent.* d.20 p.2 q.3 (Loserth, 555.8: *Opp.* IV, 558; LEDGER E23c-1). The figure is the law of the levirate, Deuteronomy 25:5, "his brother shall take her, and raise up seed for his brother" (Douay–Rheims). **C.** *Saltem publice*: at least publicly (cf. part 1: inferiors may indulge privately in the court of penance). *Semen suum … sibi*: taken as Christ's seed and sons for Christ; *sibi* may refer to the bishops. *Obiectus*: the objections (row *objectum*). **R.** Side-note 555.9.

[^4]: **C.** *Doctores decretorum*: the decretists. *Ad quas de iusticia obligantur*: to which they are bound in justice. *Habundancia*: "abundance", kept apart from *copia*, "plenty". *In scrinio pectoris Christi*: in the coffer of Christ's breast, the canonists' figure for the pope's *scrinium pectoris*, here given to Christ. The quotation is closed at *ecclesie*. **T.** *Supererogat* is Loserth's correction of the codices' *supererogata* (555.27); with the codices: "these and the other supererogated goods, all of which are placed …". The subject of *supererogat* is taken as Christ. **R.** Side-note 555.18; B in the margin at 555.16: *Indulgencia quid sit*; at 555.20: *Thesaurus ecclesie*.

[^5]: **S.** *Willielmum*: Loserth could not tell which William is meant: of Auxerre, of Montlauzun, Durand, or of Saint-Amour (555.29; LEDGER E23c-2). *De reliquiis et veneracione sanctorum* is the title X 3.45 of the Decretals, but that title has only two chapters, so *capitulo V°* cannot be X 3.45.5 (the form is ours; LEDGER E23c-3). **C.** *Que, si sit infundabilis …*: the relative is left without its own verb; the sentence turns to *patet gravedo peccati*. *Ficticie*: fiction (row *ficticia*).

[^6]: **C.** *Fundabo vel colorabo*: ground, or at least give colour to (row *colorare*): the arguments that follow are for the opinion, not Wyclif's own. *Possibilitatem et probabilitatem*: that it can be so, and that it may be argued to be so. *Probabiliter sustinendum*: to be maintained as probable. **R.** Side-note 556.5; A in the margin at 556.5: *Arguitur pro possibilitate indulgenciarum*.

[^7]: **C.** *De communi cursu*: in the common course of God's ordering, set against his absolute power. *Suadetur*: it is urged, argued persuasively. *Caput ecclesie*: in this argument for the opinion, the pope, whom Wyclif elsewhere denies to be head (row *caput*). *Pretendit*: claims, professes. *Hoc sibi imponere*: to charge him with such an error. *Dampnabilis*: untagged, beside row *dampnacio*. **S.** Luke 22:32, "I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not" (Douay–Rheims), given in the third person. **T.** B *absolutus* (556.10). Loserth's note at 556.17, *sic; sicut* (?), queries *sicut* for a *sic* in that line. **R.** Side-notes 556.11, 556.13; A in the margin at 556.9: *Racio*.

[^8]: **S.** Matthew 16:19 (Douay–Rheims). **C.** *Singulariter*: singly, each for himself. *Proporcionaliter*: in proportion, as much as is loosed on earth. **R.** Side-note 556.22.

[^9]: **C.** *Quod non potest poni nisi Petri vicarius*: which can be taken to be no one but Peter's vicar. *Factum suum … interpres sensus sui*: his deed interprets his mind; the pope's practice of granting indulgences shows what he holds. *Cum conventu suo*: with his assembly, the cardinals of the curia. **R.** Side-note 556.30.

[^10]: **C.** *Assumptum … primi argumenti*: the assumption (row) of the first argument, that one can make satisfaction for another (556.11). *Ecclesiam beatam*: the blessed church, the church triumphant. *Contingit unum condempnare alterum*: one can bring another to condemnation, as Adam and Eve their offspring. *Ex suis capitibus et consociis accusatis*: taken as condemned on account of their heads and associates, who were accused; *accusatis* may instead go with *multis aliis*, "many others accused on account of their heads". **S.** Galatians 6:2 (Douay–Rheims). **T.** B *contingir c.* (557.10); A *quam ad puniendum*, the *ad* struck out (557.13). **R.** Side-notes 557.3, 557.10.

[^11]: **C.** *Brachium … se exponit pro capite*: the arm exposes itself for the head, the common figure of natural self-defence. *Ubi est ligatum forcius*: where the body is bound more strongly. *Liberior … liberrimum*: freer, most free, that is, readier to release. *Talentum*: a talent, the sum of a debt (cf. Matthew 18:24). *Impetrat … impetrare sibi*: obtains by request (row); *sibi* taken as "for him", the other; it may be "for himself". *Pia … consolativa … honorificativa*: pious, consoling, honouring. **S.** 2 Corinthians 11:29 (Douay–Rheims). **R.** Side-note 557.21; A in the margin at 557.32: *Responsio conclusio*.

[^12]: **C.** *Concedenda est ista conclusio*: Wyclif grants the assumption of the first argument and then distinguishes. *Participative … iuvative … per se*: by sharing, by helping, of oneself. *Pro suo contento*: for his own share (side-note 558.1, "his separate share"). *In omnino altero*: in one wholly other. **T.** *Contento* is Loserth's reading; A has an abbreviation he queries as *cotitato* (cf. *partem cotam*, 510.6), B another (558.2). B *totalem satisfaccionem* (558.3). **R.** Side-note 558.1. A in the margin at 557.33: *Quomodo contingit unum satisfacere pro alio. Conclusio concedenda*; B at 558.4: *Satisfaccio pro alio tripliciter fit*.

[^13]: **C.** *In attomo*: in an individual, the logicians' *atomus*; B in the margin at 558.6: *Christus assumpsit naturam nostram in athomo*. *Complecione primiciarum tocius debiti*: the fulfilment of the first-fruits of the whole debt: Christ's nature, as first-fruits, pays the first share of the race's debt. **T.** B *consivium* (sic) for *confratrum* (558.10).

[^14]: **C.** *Fecit se naturam humanam*: made himself the human nature, by assuming it. *Ad consolvendum ministracionem debitam*: to pay, together with the head, the service owed. *Baptizatur flamine*: is baptized by the Spirit (row *baptismus flaminis*). **R.** B in the margin at 558.18 draws a consequence: if an infant dying without the baptism of the Spirit is condemned to eternal punishment for the first parents' guilt, without demerit of its own, then by the same token a blessed one could make satisfaction for a sinner who makes no satisfaction for his offence.

[^15]: **C.** *Ad primum*: to the first of the three arguments for the opinion (556.9). This paragraph and the next divide one sentence as the edition's paragraphs fall; it continues in the next part. **R.** Side-note 558.22.
