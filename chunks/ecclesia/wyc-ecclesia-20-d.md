# *De ecclesia*, chapter 20, part 4: the office of the valiant woman: her clothing, her husband in the gates, the fine linen and the girdle, wisdom on her tongue; and the beginning of her praise (Prov. 31:22–30)

## ¶1 · 484.29–490.4

> [p. 484] Unde pro declaracione ulteriori huius secte ecclesie undecimo subditur: Stragulatam vestem fecit sibi, byssus et purpura vestimentum eius. Non, inquit Augustinus, decebat matronam tanti viri nudam incedere vel pannosam, sed ornatam undique preciosis. Cum enim membra que Christus induit ex clero et [p. 485] laicis tamquam vestimento qualificato varie sint contexta et matrona ista vestimentum hoc varium in se contexuit, patet quod stragulatam vestem fecit sibi, et cum de utroque membro sint alii virginitate candidi sicut byssus, alii autem vernantes martirio tamquam purpura, Christus autem amictus sit utroque istorum luminum sicut vestimento, patet quod ipsa induens Christum habet byssum et purpuram vestimenta, sicut et habet de candida anima Christi et eius natura corporea rubricata. Ex istis sequitur duodecimo quod nobilis in portis vir eius, quando sederit cum senatoribus terre; oportet enim tam ex parte bonorum quam malorum quod iste cursus finem habeat, et per consequens, oportet (ut dicitur Isaie III°, 14) quod vir veniat ad iudicium cum senioribus populi sui. Unde et Matthei XXV°, 31 dicitur: Cum venerit filius hominis in maiestate sua et omnes angeli eius cum eo, tunc sedebit super sedem maiestatis sue et congregabuntur ante eum omnes gentes. Locus autem iudicii potest propter patenciam et introitum in civitatem duplicem dici porte. Tunc patebit Christi nobilitas primo in forma duplici quoad se ipsum, secundo in totali sua ecclesia sine macula vel ruga quoad suam familiam, et tercio in expulsione servi dampnabilis inhonesti, quia eius nobilitas non sinit cohabitacionem malefici talis perpetuam nec tantam iniuriam impunitam. Sed cognoscamus quod dictus paterfamilias posuit excrescenciam de corpore sue coniugis in cuiuslibet hominis libera potestate. Ideo terciodecimo subinfertur. Syndonem fecit et vendidit et cingulum tradidit Chananeo. Syndonem intelligo doctrinam evangelicam qua tamquam lineo vestimento purissimo et subtilissimo animus induatur. Vendidit autem spirituali vendicione, [p. 486] cum Luce XIX°, 23 dicitur: Huius coniugis pecuniam suam dedit ad usuram, et sic vendicio ista est satis gratuita, de qua dicitur Isaie LV°, 1: Venite et emite absque argento et absque ulla commutacione vinum et lac. In usura autem spirituali gratis precium est datum et donans querit fructum, et sic super excrescenciam, ut compossideat ad utilitatem datarii et honorem sui, ymmo (ut recitat Augustinus) cum evangelizans habeat temporalem mercedem pro sua evangelizacione, videtur quod sit racio dati mutui et accepti. Nam I Corinth. IX°, 11 dicit apostolus: Si nos vobis spiritualia seminavimus, non est magnum si carnalia vestra metamus. Sed non est contractus civilis, cum evangelizans principaliter querit salutem populi et non sua, ideo debentur stipendia evangelizantibus non pro commutacione civiliter possessorum. Oportet ergo quod recipientes hanc syndonem dent se ipsos, quia melius est ut pro frumento spirituali vendamus nos in servos perpetuos Joseph nostro quam pro fame spirituali sub dyabolo pereamus; cum enim das te carni, mundo sive dyabolo, tantum precium das pro voluptate transitoria et peccato quantum dares pro beatitudine et pro Deo. Ideo cum istis attento tedio patet quod in dampnatis est stulta mercacio. Cingulum autem tradidit, quando precepta evangelica gentibus predicavit; nam Cananei erant infideles ad literam et alienigene Israel et postquam sponsus dixit suis apostolis: Ite, docete omnes gentes, Luce ultimo, dicit apostolus Ephesiorum II°, 19: Iam non estis hospites et advene, sed estis cives sanctorum et domestici Dei, quod prefiguravit mulier Cananea de [p. 487] finibus suis egressa Matthei XV°. Nam ex predicacione fidei gentibus exeuntes de gentilitatis errore accincti sunt ad precepta domini adimplenda. Et quia hoc redundat ad profectum ecclesie, ideo decimo quarto subinfertur: Fortitudo et decor indumentum eius, et ridebit in die novissimo. Nam ex insercione gentilium induit ecclesia copiosius dominum Jesum Christum. Fortitudo secundum Augustinum signat constanciam passionis sanguinee et decor florem castitatis virginee iuxta dictum superius: Bissus et purpura indumentum eius. Nam hec due virtutes invaluerunt ultra Judeam in ecclesia gencium conversarum, et exhinc nimirum habebit hec matrona iubileum in die iudicii; nec miremur quid scriptura tam crebro precipit nos induere Isaac nostrum, quia notum est quod indumenta anime sunt virtutes, Christus autem Dei virtus et Dei sapiencia et forma exemplaris extrinseca efficiens et finis sine cuius illapsi informacione non est creatura aliqua virtuosa. Habitus autem qui sunt qualitates et forme intrinsece non sunt nisi consuetudines et promptitudines ad agendum. Unde Christus illapsus membro suo tamquam sibi accidente dicitur et ipsum induere et contexens virtutibus sapiencia perornare. Unde sequitur decimo quinto: Os suum aperuit sapiencie et lex clemencie in lingua eius. Virtus enim que est sapiencia patris illapsa ecclesie fecit eam ebullire sapiencia tam secundum actus quam habitus virtutis que sunt eius vestigia, et in lingua eius ordinate ponitur lex laudandi in suo ordine domini et quodlibet opus suum. Prestet nobis, inquit Augustinus, in illa constitutis, ipsam laudantibus, illi coherentibus, cum illa et in illa tamquam matre gloriam suam expectantibus, ut et nos in timore aperiamus os nostrum attente et caute et sollicite. Nam Iª Corinth. II°, 3 scribit Apostolus: Cum timore et tremore multo [p. 488] fui aput vos. Et sequitur: Os nostrum patet ad vos o Corinthi. Ordinem, inquit, posuit lingue sue, laudans creatorem et quamlibet creaturam in suo ordine, vel pulcrius hoc ordine quo homo amat quidlibet non ut aliud sed vere in ordine bonitatis quo Deus ipsum posuit. Tunc enim vere ipsum diligit ut se ipsum; unde signanter mater nostra Canticorum II°, 4 de sponso testatur: Ordinavit, inquit, in me caritatem. Sed quia ad illud requiritur noticia philosophica qua ordines bonitatum rerum secundum sapienciam cognoscuntur, ideo sexto decimo et ultimo ita subdit: Consideravit semitas domus sue et panem ociosum non comedit. Qui enim vigilanter considerat viam per quam sanctam matrem ecclesiam oportet in Deum ascendere, contemplando ipsum ordinate in suis effectibus, ocio culpabili non vacabit, cum oportet eum studiose vigilare ad fores sapiencie increate. Et in istis sedecim stat officium matris nostre. Quoad terciam partem Cantici qua declamatur huius matrone preconium, primo sic subditur: Surrexerunt filii eius et beatissimam predicaverunt et vir eius laudavit eam. Testimonium infallibile laudis huius domine capitur ascendendo a testibus expertis qui surrexerunt a miseriis huius vie usque ad testimonium viri sui. Ipse enim est veritas increata, Johannis XIV°; multi enim surrexerunt a purgatorio et a limbo qui communicarunt nobis noticiam de beatitudine et gloria ecclesie triumphantis, et veritas testatur in facto sue transfiguracionis et in verbis sue predicacionis laudem sponse Matthei XXV, 34: Venite benedicti patris mei, percipite paratum vobis regnum; et sequitur verbum laudis: Esurivi enim et dedistis mihi manducare; [p. 489] et modo, inquit Augustinus, in paupertate vivimus et cum morimur in paupertate dormimus, sed surgemus et vere ditabimur. Unde quia pars beatitudinis est verorum bonorum possessio, ideo secundo subditur in preconium huius matris: Multe filie congregaverunt divicias, tu supergressa es universas. Nam omnia temporalia transeunt et decipiunt quoscunque qui ea preponderanter diligunt. Ideo Matthei VI°, 19 dicitur: Nolite thesaurizare vobis thesauros in terra, ubi erugo et tinea demolitur et ubi fures effodiunt et furantur, sed thesaurizate vobis thesauros in celo; et sequitur: Ubi est enim thesaurus tuus, ibi et cor tuum. Filie autem vocantur secundum Augustinum heretici presciti, ut finaliter ab ecclesia dividantur. Inter has filias splendet ecclesia sicut lilium inter spinas: Tales, inquit, spine habent florem sine fructu; nam existentes ad tempus in ecclesia percipiunt fallaciter fidem et ecclesiastica sacramenta, eo quod eis deficit caritas consummata. Unde quia tales persepe habundant mundana gloria et fama seculi propter eius divicias, ideo tercio subditur: Fallax gracia et vana pulchritudo: mulier timens dominum ipsa laudabitur. Nam gracia laudis humane, mundane glorie vel carnalis concupiscencie declinant homines a Deo ad hostem triplicem. Ideo necesse est ut ista sit fallax gracia, quia abducit a sponso, qui est plenus gracie et veritatis. Vana eciam est pulchritudo quecunque sensibilis, cum non sit pulchritudo nisi propter proporcionalem numerum insensibilem increatum, in quo est primo per se et proprie pulchritudo (ut patet cap. Multe). Ideo signanter dicitur vanitas talis pulchritudo sensibilis et potissime temporalis. Unde rectus et per se ordo iniciandi materiam vere laudis est timor domini; ex hoc [p. 490] enim quod caste filialiter timetur sponsus, operatur mulier predicta totum bonum narratum secundum dictum ordinem sponso suo, et certum est quod talis bona operacio non potest finaliter a laude deficere.

Hence, for the further declaration of this [sect]{g:secta} of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, it is added eleventhly: "She hath made for herself clothing of tapestry: fine linen, and purple, is her covering."[^1] "It was not fitting," says Augustine, "for the matron of so great a husband to go about naked or in rags, but adorned on every side with precious things." For since the [members]{g:membrum} whom Christ clothes, from the [clergy]{g:clerus} and [485] the laity, are woven together variously, as a garment of varied quality, and this matron has woven this varied garment in herself, it is clear that "she hath made for herself clothing of tapestry"; and since of each [kind of] member some are white with virginity like fine linen, and others flourishing with martyrdom like purple, and Christ is clothed with both of these lights as with a garment, it is clear that she, putting on Christ, has fine linen and purple as garments, just as she also has [them] from the white soul of Christ and his reddened bodily nature.[^2] From these it follows twelfthly that "her husband is honourable in the gates, when he sitteth among the senators of the land"; for it is necessary, on the side of the good as much as of the wicked, that this course have an end, and consequently it is necessary (as is said in Isaiah 3:14) that the husband come to judgment with the ancients of his people. Hence also in Matthew 25:31 it is said: "When the Son of man shall come in his majesty, and all his angels with him, then shall he sit upon the seat of his majesty: and all nations shall be gathered together before him." And the place of judgment can be called "gates" because of its openness and the entry into the twofold city. Then the nobility of Christ will be manifest: first in his twofold form, as regards himself; secondly in his whole church without spot or wrinkle, as regards his [household]{g:familia}; and thirdly in the expulsion of the damnable, dishonest servant, since his nobility does not suffer the perpetual [joint dwelling]{g:cohabitacio} of such an evildoer, nor so great an injury unpunished.[^3] But let us recognize that the said father of the household has placed the increase from the body of his spouse in the free power of every man. Therefore it is inferred thirteenthly: "She made fine linen, and sold it, and delivered a girdle to the Chanaanite." By the fine linen I understand the evangelical [doctrine]{g:doctrina}, with which, as with a most pure and most fine linen garment, the mind is clothed.[^4] And she sold [it] by a spiritual sale, [486] since in Luke 19:23 it is said: [the husband] of this spouse gave his money at usury; and so this sale is quite gratuitous, of which it is said in Isaiah 55:1: "Come ye, buy wine and milk without money, and without any price."[^5] And in spiritual usury the price is given freely and the giver seeks the fruit, and so upon the increase, so that he may possess [it] jointly, to the profit of the receiver and to his own honour; indeed (as Augustine relates), since the one who preaches the gospel has a temporal wage for his preaching of the gospel, it seems that there is an account of mutual giving and receiving. For in 1 Corinthians 9:11 the apostle says: "If we have sown unto you spiritual things, it is no great matter if we reap your carnal things."[^6] But it is not a civil contract, since the one who preaches the gospel principally seeks the salvation of the people and not his own things; therefore wages are owed to those who preach the gospel, not as an [exchange]{g:commutacio} for things civilly possessed. Those who receive this fine linen must therefore give themselves, since it is better that for spiritual grain we sell ourselves as perpetual servants to our Joseph than that we perish under the devil for spiritual hunger; for when you give yourself to the flesh, the world or the devil, you give as great a price for transitory pleasure and sin as you would give for blessedness and for God. Therefore, when the weariness that goes with these things is weighed, it is clear that in the damned there is a foolish trade.[^7] And she delivered the girdle when she preached the gospel precepts to the gentiles; for the Chanaanites were, in the letter, [unbelievers]{g:infidelis} and aliens from Israel; and after the bridegroom said to his apostles, "Go, teach all nations," Luke, last [chapter], the apostle says in Ephesians 2:19: "Now you are no more strangers and foreigners: but you are fellow citizens with the saints and the domestics of God"; which the woman of Canaan, coming out [487] of her coasts, prefigured, Matthew 15.[^8] For by the preaching of the faith to the gentiles, those who went out from the error of heathendom were girded to fulfil the Lord's precepts.

And since this redounds to the profit of the church, therefore it is inferred fourteenthly: "Strength and beauty are her clothing, and she shall laugh in the latter day." For by the grafting in of the gentiles the church has put on the Lord Jesus Christ more abundantly.[^9] Strength, according to Augustine, signifies the constancy of bloody suffering, and beauty the flower of virginal chastity, according to what was said above: "Fine linen and purple is her covering." For these two virtues grew strong beyond Judea in the church of the converted gentiles, and hence, no wonder, this matron will have jubilee on the day of judgment.[^10] Nor let us wonder why scripture so often commands us to put on our Isaac; for it is known that the garments of the soul are the [virtues]{g:virtus}, and Christ is "the power of God and the wisdom of God", and the extrinsic exemplary form, the efficient [cause] and the end, without the informing of whose inflowing no creature is virtuous. And habits, which are qualities and intrinsic forms, are nothing but [customs]{g:consuetudo} and readinesses for acting. Hence Christ, having flowed into his member as into something accidental to him, is said both to clothe it and, weaving it with virtues, to adorn it with wisdom.[^11] Hence it follows fifteenthly: "She hath opened her mouth to wisdom, and the law of clemency is on her tongue." For the Power that is the wisdom of the Father, having flowed into the church, made her bubble over with wisdom, according to acts as much as to habits of virtue, which are its traces; and on her tongue is set in order the law of praising, each in its order, the Lord and each work of his.[^12] "May he grant us," says Augustine, "who are set in her, praising her, cleaving to her, awaiting his glory with her and in her as in a mother, that we too may open our mouth in fear, attentively and cautiously and solicitously." For in 1 Corinthians 2:3 the Apostle writes: "In fear and in much trembling [488] I was with you." And it follows: "Our mouth is open to you, O ye Corinthians."[^13] "She has set order," he says, "on her tongue, praising the Creator and each creature" in its order; or, more beautifully, in this order, by which a man loves each thing not as something else but truly in the order of goodness in which God has set it. For then he truly loves it as itself; hence [our mother]{g:mater-nostra}, in Canticles 2:4, testifies expressly of the [bridegroom]{g:sponsus}: "He set in order," she says, "[charity]{g:caritas} in me."[^14] But since for that is required the philosophical knowledge by which the orders of the goodnesses of things are known according to wisdom, therefore sixteenthly and lastly he adds thus: "She hath looked well on the paths of her house, and hath not eaten her bread idle." For one who watchfully considers the way by which holy mother church must ascend into God, contemplating him in order in his effects, will not be at leisure in culpable idleness, since he must studiously "watch at the doors" of uncreated wisdom. And in these sixteen stands the office of our mother.[^15]

As to the third part of the song, in which the commendation of this matron is proclaimed, first it is added thus: "Her children rose up, and called her blessed: her husband, and he praised her." The infallible testimony of the praise of this lady is taken by ascending from expert witnesses, who rose from the miseries of this [way]{g:via}, up to the testimony of her husband. For he is the uncreated truth, John 14; for many rose from purgatory and from limbo who communicated to us knowledge of the blessedness and glory of the [church triumphant]{g:ecclesia-triumphans}; and the Truth testifies, in the fact of his transfiguration and in the words of his preaching, to the praise of the [bride]{g:sponsa}, Matthew 25:34: "Come, ye blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom prepared for you"; and the word of praise follows: "For I was hungry, and you gave me to eat."[^16] [489] "And now," says Augustine, "we live in poverty, and when we die we sleep in poverty; but we shall rise and be truly enriched."[^17] Hence, since a part of blessedness is the possession of true goods, therefore secondly it is added in commendation of this mother: "Many daughters have gathered together riches: thou hast surpassed them all." For all [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} pass away and deceive whoever love them preponderantly. Therefore in Matthew 6:19 it is said: "Lay not up to yourselves treasures on earth: where the rust and moth consume, and where thieves break through and steal; but lay up to yourselves treasures in heaven"; and it follows: "For where thy treasure is, there is thy heart also."[^18] And the daughters, according to Augustine, are called the [foreknown]{g:prescitus} heretics, as those who are finally to be divided from the church. Among these daughters the church shines "as the lily among thorns": "Such thorns," he says, "have a flower without fruit"; for, being in the church for a time, they receive deceitfully the faith and the sacraments of the church, because consummated charity is lacking to them.[^19] Hence, since such men very often abound in worldly glory and the fame of the world on account of its riches, therefore thirdly it is added: "Favour is deceitful, and beauty is vain: the woman that feareth the Lord, she shall be praised." For the favour of human praise, of worldly glory or of carnal concupiscence turns men aside from God to the threefold enemy. Therefore it must be that this is deceitful favour, since it draws [men] away from the bridegroom, who is "full of grace and truth".[^20] Vain, too, is any [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} beauty whatever, since there is no beauty except on account of the proportional number, [imperceptible]{g:insensibilis} and uncreated, in which beauty is first, of itself and properly (as is clear in the chapter *Multe*). Therefore such perceptible, and above all temporal, beauty is expressly called vanity.[^21] Hence the right and essential order of beginning the matter of true praise is the fear of the Lord; for from the fact [490] that the bridegroom is feared chastely and filially, the aforesaid woman works for her bridegroom all the good narrated, in the said order; and it is certain that such good working cannot finally fail of praise.[^22]

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** Proverbs 31:22 (Wyclif *vestimentum* for the Vulgate's *indumentum*). **C.** *Huius secte ecclesie*: GLOSSARY's "sect" is kept; the word here is the church's following as a whole, and with the clothing of the verse it may carry the sense "suit, livery". **R.** Side-note 484.29.

[^2]: **S.** Augustine, *Sermo* 37 on Proverbs 31 (LEDGER E20d-1). *Amictus … sicut vestimento*: Psalm 103:2 ("clothed with light as with a garment"). **C.** *Membra que Christus induit*: Christ clothes his members (as at 487.22–23, *ipsum induere*); the church, conversely, puts on Christ. *De utroque membro*: of each of the two orders, clergy and laity. *Candida anima … natura corporea rubricata*: Christ's white soul and his body reddened in the passion answer to the linen and the purple. **T.** AA₁ lack *decebat* (484.32); AA₁ read *sunt* for *sint* (485.1, 485.4); B reads *amicus* (485.6).

[^3]: **S.** Proverbs 31:23. Isaiah 3:14 (the Vulgate: *Dominus ad judicium veniet cum senibus populi sui*; Wyclif has *vir* and *senioribus*). Matthew 25:31–32. *Sine macula vel ruga*: Ephesians 5:27. *Servi … inhonesti*: cf. Matthew 25:30. **C.** *Civitatem duplicem*: the two cities, heavenly and earthly (Augustine, *De civ. Dei* XIV.28, PL 41:436), entered from the judgment (LEDGER E20d-2). *Forma duplici*: the form of God and the form of a servant. **T.** B reads *quando dederit* (485.12). **R.** Side-note 485.11.

[^4]: **S.** Proverbs 31:24. **C.** *Excrescenciam*: the increase or surplus of the spouse's body, the fruit of her work, which the father of the household leaves to each man's free use; hence the sale and the girdle that follow. **T.** For *excrescenciam* (485.28–29) A has an abbreviation, B *existentibus*. B lacks *autem … ad* (485.34). **R.** Side-note 485.30.

[^5]: **S.** Luke 19:23 ("why then didst thou not give my money into the bank, that at my coming I might have exacted it with usury?"); Loserth prints Luke's words at the page foot. Wyclif gives the sense, not the words. Isaiah 55:1 (DR "without any price" for *absque ulla commutacione*). **C.** *Huius coniugis pecuniam suam dedit ad usuram*: as printed the genitive has no noun to hang on; it is taken as the husband "of this spouse", who gave his money at usury.

[^6]: **S.** "As Augustine relates": the same sermon (LEDGER E20d-3). *Racio dati … et accepti*: Philippians 4:15 ("as concerning giving and receiving"). 1 Corinthians 9:11: the Vulgate asks *magnum est si …?* ("is it a great matter if …?"); Wyclif's statement is translated. **C.** *Compossideat ad utilitatem datarii*: the giver shares in the possession of the increase, to the profit of the one who receives. *Mutui*: taken with both participles, "mutual giving and receiving".

[^7]: **C.** *Non pro commutacione civiliter possessorum*: the preacher's wage is not the price of goods held by civil title. *Joseph nostro*: Christ as Joseph, to whom the Egyptians sold themselves for grain (Genesis 47:19–25). *Cum istis attento tedio*: when the weariness accompanying these [pleasures] is considered. **T.** B lacks *pereamus … tantum* (486.20–21); AA₁ lack *et* (486.23).

[^8]: **S.** *Ite, docete omnes gentes* is Matthew 28:19 ("Going therefore, teach ye all nations"), not *Luce ultimo*; Luke 24:47 has preaching "unto all nations". Ephesians 2:19 (the Vulgate *Ergo jam*). Matthew 15:22. **C.** *Infideles ad literam*: the Chanaanites, unbelievers in the literal sense, stand for the gentiles. **T.** At 486.30 B reads *Iam non et advene sed*; AA₁ lack *et advene*. **R.** Side-note 486.26.

[^9]: **S.** Proverbs 31:25. *Induit … dominum Jesum Christum*: Romans 13:14. *Ex insercione gentilium*: the grafting of Romans 11:17–24. **R.** Side-note 487.5.

[^10]: **S.** Augustine, the same sermon, 37.22 (*decore, tanquam bysso; fortitudine, tanquam purpura*); virginity is Wyclif's (LEDGER E20d-4). **C.** Strength answers to the purple of martyrdom, beauty to the linen of virginity (484.30, 485.5–6). *Iubileum*: the laughter of the verse. **T.** The codices read *iubilium* (487.13).

[^11]: **S.** 1 Corinthians 1:24. **C.** *Virtus*: in "the power of God" *virtus* is power; Wyclif plays on the virtues as the soul's garments. *Isaac nostrum*: Christ, of whom Isaac is the figure. *Illapsi*: of Christ flowing into the soul (GLOSSARY *illabi*). *Tamquam sibi accidente*: the member is to Christ as an accident to its subject, so that he can be said to clothe it. *Consuetudines*: acquired habits are only customs and readinesses to act, against Christ the extrinsic form. **T.** The codices read *que sunt* (487.19).

[^12]: **S.** Proverbs 31:26. **C.** *Virtus … que est sapiencia patris*: the Son, as in 1 Corinthians 1:24. *Eius vestigia*: the acts and habits are traces of that wisdom. **R.** Side-note 487.24.

[^13]: **S.** Augustine, the same sermon (LEDGER E20d-5). 1 Corinthians 2:3 (the Vulgate *in infirmitate, et timore*; Wyclif omits "in weakness"). The words introduced by *Et sequitur* are 2 Corinthians 6:11. **T.** B reads *accincte et caute* for *attente et caute* (487.34); the codices read *apud* (488.1).
