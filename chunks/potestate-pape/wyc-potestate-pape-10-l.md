# *De potestate pape*, chapter 10, part 12: may the pope be reproved? three arguments answered

## ¶1 · 261.8–263.5

> [p. 261] Sed quomodocumque loquatur, fideli nichil insanius quam credere quod omnis fidelis debet credere quod omnis episcopus Romanus sit papa vel quod omnis subditus tenetur facere quicquid preceperit aut quod auctoritate et declaracione illius tenetur omnis christianus credere cuicumque scripture quam ipse limitat; nam mille sunt fideles, qui habent immediate a Deo fidem scripture independenter ab illo, eciam plus quam ipse; verumtamen cum multi pape sunt gloriosi post dotacionem ecclesie, quilibet christianus tenetur saltem confuse credere quod multi Romani episcopi sunt veri pape, et illi qui non fecerunt exaccionem talis fidei Antichristi; sed hoc non est generaliter ad laudem Romani pontificis, quia sic tenentur credere quod multi Romani pontifices non sunt pape, quia multi sunt dampnati, non membra sancte ecclesie, et sic de omni veritate tenetur omnis christianus credere quia est, etsi non sonat in pompam pape, quod omnis christianus tenetur ipsum credere esse papam, sicut non sonat in pompam cuiuscumque predestinati quod omnis homo tenetur credere ipsum esse salvandum, sicut omnem prescitum tenetur credere esse dampnandum, cum oportet Deum distincte illud cognoscere; ymo est michi evidens quod tam subtilis logicus atque theologus, sicut fuit Robertus Lyncolniensis [p. 262] non induceret hunc errorem fidei ad onus ecclesie, quod quemlibet Romanum pontificem tenetur credere esse papam, vel quod alius non habens ad hoc revelacionem de se ipso vel quocumque alio conversante secum tenetur expresse credere quod sit predestinatus vel apud Deum acceptus; de se ipso tamen tenetur hoc sperare et de aliis si moveat evidencia operis reputare. Nam ante dotacionem fuit privilegium et libertas ecclesie quod non tenebatur sic credere. Ideo non obstante dotacione debent ecclesie filii defendere pristinam libertatem; et sic intelliguntur multa dicta Roberti Grostest, que dicit condicionaliter aut reputative, quia aliter contradiceret sibi ipsi. Unde Epistola XXXVII. ut recitavi in fine secundi cap. libri V, dicit Lynconiensis quod iniustarum possessionum quas occupat ecclesia abscisio non est ecclesiarum imminucio, sed pocius earum pulcrificacio vel plurificacio. Et sic secundum eum et alios sanctos, qui iniuste occupat locum papalem, non sequendo Christum in moribus, non est papa vel clericus, sed unus dyabolicus antipapa. Ex istis patet consideranti quantum malum et onus irreligiosum ecclesie induxit dotacio; ad tantum enim insaniunt pape cesarei, quod tenent cum suis satrapis quod papa non debet corripi ab aliquo viatore. Quod probant tripliciter: primo per hoc gentile principium: par in parem non habet imperium, cum igitur papa excedit omnem personam viantem, videtur quod non interest alicuius viantis papam corripere. Sed quantum ad illud patet, quod tam deficit [p. 263] in forma quam in materia; in forma, quia necesse est antecedens esse verum, cum necessario pater in Spiritum Sanctum non habet imperium sed missionem et tamen necessario sunt pares.

But however one speaks, to a believer nothing is more insane than to believe that every believer ought to believe that every [Roman bishop]{g:romanus-episcopus} is [pope]{g:papa}, or that every subject is bound to do whatever he has commanded, or that on his [authority]{g:auctoritas} and declaration every Christian is bound to believe whatever scripture he [assigns]{g:limitare};[^1] for there are a thousand believers who have the faith of scripture immediately from God, independently of him, even more than he [has]. Nevertheless, since many popes after the [endowment]{g:dotacio} of the [church]{g:ecclesia} are glorious, every Christian is bound at least [indistinctly]{g:confuse} to believe that many Roman bishops are true popes, and [that] those are who did not make an exaction of such a faith, [the faith] of Antichrist;[^2] but this is not in general to the praise of the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex}, because in the same way they are bound to believe that many Roman pontiffs are not popes, because many are damned, not [members]{g:membrum} of holy church. And so every Christian is bound to believe of every truth that it is [true], although it does not sound to the pope's [boasting]{g:pompa} that every Christian is bound to believe him to be pope, just as it does not sound to the boasting of any [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} [man] that every man is bound to believe him to be saved, as [every man] is bound to believe every [foreknown]{g:prescitus} [man] to be damned, since God must know it distinctly.[^3] Indeed it is evident to me that so subtle a logician and theologian as Robert of Lincoln was [262] would not bring in this error of faith, to the burden of the church, that [a man] is bound to believe every Roman pontiff to be pope, or that another, not having a revelation to that effect about himself or about any other who lives with him, is bound to believe expressly that he is predestinate or accepted with God;[^4] yet of himself he is bound to hope this, and of others, if the [evidence]{g:evidencia} of works moves [him], to esteem it. For before the endowment it was the [privilege]{g:privilegium} and liberty of the church that it was not bound to believe so. Therefore, notwithstanding the endowment, the sons of the church ought to defend their former liberty; and so are to be understood many sayings of Robert Grosseteste, which he says conditionally or [by estimation]{g:reputative}, because otherwise he would contradict himself. Hence in Letter 37, as I recited at the end of the second chapter of book 5, Lincoln says that "the cutting off of the unjust possessions which the church occupies is not a diminishing of the churches, but rather their beautifying" or "multiplying".[^5] And so, according to him and other saints, he who unjustly occupies the papal place, not following Christ in his morals, is not pope or cleric, but a devilish antipope.[^6] From these things it is clear to one who considers how great an evil and irreligious burden the endowment brought upon the church; for the caesarean popes go so far in madness that they hold, with their [satraps]{g:satrapa}, that the pope ought not to be reproved by any [wayfarer]{g:viator}.[^7] This they prove in three ways: first, by this pagan principle, "an equal has no command over an equal"; since, therefore, the pope exceeds every person [on the road]{g:viantes}, it seems that it is not the business of anyone on the road to reprove the pope.[^8] But as to that, it is clear that it fails as much [263] in form as in matter: in form, because the antecedent is necessarily true, since necessarily the Father has no command over the Holy Spirit but a sending, and yet necessarily they are equals.[^9]

## ¶2 · 263.6–263.10

> [p. 263] Iterum, necessario imperium habet pater in filium secundum humanitatem assumptam, et tamen absolute necessario sunt pares; ideo si nullus par in parem habet imperium, periret fides catholica.

Again, the Father necessarily has command over the Son according to the assumed humanity, and yet they are equals with absolute necessity; therefore if no equal has command over an equal, the catholic faith would perish.

## ¶3 · 263.11–263.16

> [p. 263] Et iterum quoddam est imperium vicarium, cuiusmodi est omne humanum imperium, quomodo loquitur Apostolus prima Thym. VI, 17: Divitibus huius seculi precipe non sublime sapere. Quoddam autem imperium est per se vel originaliter autenticum super quamlibet creaturam.

And again, one command is vicarial, of which kind is every human command, in the way the Apostle speaks in 1 Tim. 6:17: "Charge the rich of this world not to be highminded." But another command is of itself, or originally, [authentic]{g:autenticus} over every creature.[^10]

## ¶4 · 263.17–264.11

> [p. 263] Et patet quam nuda sit ista argucia quoad materiam et formam. Intelligit enim per hoc principium quod par non habet imperium autoritativum in suum parem, sub racione qua par est taliter imperanti. Sed quid obest huic quod excellencior virtute corripiat inferiorem in moribus, licet sit superior quoad statum. Aliter enim periret lex Christi, que ordinat quemcumque christianum prelatum peccabilem, et cum peccaverit, ab alio corrigendum, ut patet Matth. XVIII, in tantum quod si per impossibile Christus peccaverit, foret ab ecclesia corrigendus; cum enim sit frater noster Hebre. II. 12 et lex sua dicit universaliter: si peccaverit in te frater tuus, corripe eum, patet quod omnis viator peccando debet corripi a confratre. Aliter enim deficeret lex [p. 264] Christi ordinando remedium contra maculam sponse sue, cui Paulus contradicit Gal. II. cum pro levi culpa in facie restitit Petro pape; ideo nichil infidelius quam negare quod statu superior et inferior quoad mores potest corripi a subiecto. Ideo secundo arguitur per hoc quod papa gerit in terris vicem domini Ihesu Christi, sed ipsum non licuit alicui corripere, ut patet Matth. XVI, 23 ubi propter correpcionem Christi Petrus vocatur sathanas, igitur nec eius vicem gerentem licet corripere.

And it is clear how bare this [quibble]{g:argucia} is as to matter and form. For by this principle he understands that an equal has no [authoritative]{g:auctoritativus} command over his equal, in the respect in which he is equal to the one so commanding.[^11] But what stands in the way of this, that one more excellent in [virtue]{g:virtus} should reprove one inferior in morals, although he is superior as to [state]{g:status}? For otherwise the law of Christ would perish, which ordains that any Christian [prelate]{g:prelatus}, being able to sin, and when he has sinned, is to be corrected by another, as is clear in Matt. 18:15; so much so that if, per impossibile, Christ had sinned, he would have to be corrected by the church.[^12] For since he is "our brother" (Heb. 2:12), and his law says universally: "If thy brother shall offend against thee, rebuke him," it is clear that every wayfarer, by sinning, ought to be reproved by a fellow brother.[^13] For otherwise the law [264] of Christ would fail in ordaining a remedy against the stain of his [bride]{g:sponsa}; which Paul contradicts in Gal. 2, when for a light fault he "withstood" Peter the pope "to the face";[^14] therefore nothing is more faithless than to deny that one superior in state and inferior as to morals can be reproved by a subject. Therefore, secondly, [it is argued by appeal to]{g:arguitur-per} this, that the pope bears on earth the place of the Lord Jesus Christ; now it was not lawful for anyone to reprove him, as is clear in Matt. 16:23, where, for reproving Christ, Peter is called Satan; therefore neither is it lawful to reprove one who bears his place.[^15]

## ¶5 · 264.12–264.16

> [p. 264] Sed ista argucia nimis claudicat, cum oporteret dicere quod omnis Christi vicarius sit impeccabilis sicut Christus: et sic vere concluditur quod nec papa nec alius debet corripi vel corrigi, in quantum sequitur caput Christum.

But this quibble limps badly, since one would have to say that every [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius-christi} is impeccable as Christ is; and so it is truly [inferred]{g:concludere} that neither the pope nor another ought to be reproved or corrected in so far as he follows Christ the [head]{g:caput}.[^16]

## ¶6 · 264.17–264.21

> [p. 264] Sed tercio arguitur ex lege XXI distinccione Nunc autem divina, ubi dicitur, quod nullus sanctorum episcoporum ausus est in Marcellinum papam inferre sentenciam, sed dixerunt: tuo ore iudica causam tuam, non nostro iudicio subiacebis.

But, thirdly, it is argued from the law, Distinction 21, *Nunc autem divina*, where it is said that "none of the holy [bishops]{g:episcopus} dared to pass [sentence]{g:sentencia} on Pope Marcellinus, but they said: 'Judge your cause with your own mouth; you will not be subject to our judgement.'"[^17]

## ¶7 · 264.22–265.5

> [p. 264] Sed constat quod illud verbum episcoporum non potest tollere legem Dei, qua in archiepiscopo Paulo corripuit papam Petrum, ut patet Gal. II. Ideo idem foret dicere quod non licet papam corripere et dicere quod est personaliter impeccabilis dominus Ihesus Christus vel ipseLucifer, qui per correpcionem fraternam non potest corrigi. Unde cum papa racione multiplici sit pronior et periculosior in peccando, oportet de correpcione et correccione sua prudencius precavere; et ad hoc ordinantur leges ecclesie, ut patet distinccione [p. 265] XL: Si papa, et distinccione LXXIX: Si quis pecunia; et aliter forent omnia facta in deposicione paparum iniusta et per consequens deponentes forent heretici, sicut et ecclesia approbans eius correpcionem et pape novi intronizacionem.

But it is evident that that word of the bishops cannot [dispose of]{g:tollere} the law of God, by which, in the archbishop Paul, it reproved Peter the pope, as is clear in Gal. 2.[^18] Therefore it would be the same to say that it is not lawful to reprove the pope and to say that he is personally the impeccable Lord Jesus Christ, or Lucifer himself, who cannot be corrected by [fraternal correction]{g:correpcio-fraterna}. Hence, since the pope for many reasons is more prone and more dangerous in sinning, it is necessary to take precaution more prudently for his reproof and correction; and to this [end] the laws of the church are ordained, as is clear in Distinction [265] 40, *Si papa*, and Distinction 79, *Si quis pecunia*;[^19] and otherwise all the things done in the deposition of popes would be unjust, and consequently those deposing would be heretics, as also the church that approves his reproof and the enthronement of a new pope.

## ¶8 · 265.7–265.16

> [p. 265] Et quantum ad dictum episcoporum de correpcione Marcellini, dicitur quod valde superfluum foret eos ipsum in tali casu corripere, quia perceperunt ex eius contricione quod fuit plene correptus a Domino, Ideo nimis levis evidencia foret ex isto facto concludere quod nullus papa debet in aliquo casu a fratribus reprehendi, ut patet ex libro beati Bernhardi ad papam Eugenium. Mille autem sunt blasfemie cum nonnulla intronizacione pape cesarei introducte.

And as to the saying of the bishops about the reproof of Marcellinus, [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that it would have been quite superfluous for them to reprove him in such a case, because they perceived from his contrition that he had been fully reproved by the Lord. Therefore it would be too light evidence to infer from this fact that no pope ought in any case to be rebuked by his brothers, as is clear from the book of blessed Bernard to Pope Eugenius.[^20] And there are a thousand blasphemies brought in with many an enthronement of a caesarean pope.[^21]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 261.8: We are not bound to believe that every Roman bishop is pope.
- 262.20: If he does not follow Christ he is a devil and antipope.
- 262.28: Popes even declare that no one may reprove them.
- 263.2: It is said falsely that between equals there is no authority.
- 264.24: Such a claim implies that the pope is impeccable.
- 265.7: Case of Marcellinus.

## Notes

[^1]: **T.** The manuscripts read *loquatur fidelis* for the print's *loquatur, fideli* (261.9): "however a believer speaks, nothing is more insane …". The print is translated. **C.** "He" throughout is the pope. **G.** *limitat*: "assigns", i.e. designates which scripture is to be believed.
[^2]: **C.** *et illi qui non fecerunt exaccionem talis fidei Antichristi* is read as a second object of *credere*, "[that] those are [true popes] who did not exact such a faith", with *Antichristi* naming the faith exacted; it could instead be predicate, "those who did not exact such faith [are not] of Antichrist". The construal is doubtful.
[^3]: **C.** *sonat in pompam*: "sounds to the boasting", i.e. redounds to his glory. Every truth is believed at least indistinctly, so a pope's being pope (where true) is believed only as every truth is, which no more flatters him than the like belief flatters a predestinate man. **G.** *prescitus*: "the foreknown" (glossed at its first occurrence in this work).
[^4]: **T.** B reads *christianum* for *Romanum* (262.2), "every Christian pontiff"; the print is translated.
[^5]: **S.** Grosseteste, Letter 37 (Luard, p. 226), which Loserth finds recited in *De civili dominio* III, p. 30 (LEDGER P10l-1). Wyclif's "book 5" counts that volume in his *Summa* (LEDGER P10l-2, to be checked). **T.** B has *vel plurificacio* in the margin (262.19).
[^6]: **R.** B notes in the margin at 262.26, in a later hand: *Moderni pontifices Romani sunt diabolici antipape*; both manuscripts at 262.15: *Papa malus si est papa*. **C.** *in moribus*: "in his morals", his way of living.
[^7]: **R.** Both manuscripts note in the margin at 262.25: *Papa non debet corripi, probat tripliciter*. **C.** *corripere* is "reprove" throughout, kept apart from *corrigere* "correct" (264.14). *Satrapa*: glossed at its first occurrence in this work.
[^8]: **S.** *Par in parem non habet imperium*, a maxim of Roman and canon law (LEDGER P10l-3, to be checked); not italic in the print, quoted here as a stated principle. **C.** *gentile*: "pagan", i.e. drawn from pagan law. *imperium* is "command" throughout the passage (Loserth's side-note says "authority").
[^9]: **T.** B omits *quam in materia; in forma* (263.1). **C.** The formal defect is put compressedly: the principle is necessarily true of some equals (the Father has over the Spirit a sending, *missio*, not command), so it cannot carry the inference. Translated as printed.
[^10]: **C.** Command "of itself" is God's; human command is exercised in another's place, as Paul charges Timothy to command the rich. **R.** Both manuscripts at 263.15: *Imperium duplex*.
[^11]: **C.** *sub racione qua*: "in the respect in which" (not the technical *racio* "aspect"). So read, the principle denies only command between equals as equals.
[^12]: **S.** The margin reference printed beside 263.30 (OCR "XVIII, 2 5") is taken as Matt. 18:15. **C.** *peccabilem*: "able to sin"; the print's semicolon after *peccaverit* is passed over in construing.
[^13]: **S.** Heb. 2:11–12, "he is not ashamed to call them brethren, saying: I will declare thy name to my brethren"; "our brother" is Wyclif's summary. Matt. 18:15, where the Vulgate has *vade, et corripe eum* (DR "go, and rebuke him"); Wyclif drops *vade*.
[^14]: **S.** Gal. 2:11, "I withstood him to the face, because he was to be blamed." **R.** Both manuscripts at 264.25 and 264.27: *Correpcio patris necessaria*; *Correpcio pape licita*.
[^15]: **S.** Matt. 16:22–23: Peter "began to rebuke him", and is told "Go behind me, Satan".
[^16]: **C.** *corripi vel corrigi*: "reproved or corrected", the two verbs kept apart. The concession is limited: in so far as anyone follows Christ, he needs no reproof.
[^17]: **S.** Decretum, D. 21 c. 7 (Loserth: *Decreti prima pars, dist. XXI, cap. VII*; the modern form is ours) (LEDGER P10l-4). Marcellinus, bishop of Rome (d. 304), was said to have offered incense in the persecution and to have been judged by a synod at Sinuessa (LEDGER P10l-5, to be checked). **C.** *sentenciam*: a judicial sentence, hence "sentence" (GLOSSARY *sentencia*).
[^18]: **C.** *qua … corripuit*: the subject is taken as the law of God, which reproved Peter through Paul; "archbishop" and "pope" are Wyclif's titles for the apostles. **G.** *correpcio fraterna* is "fraternal correction" (GLOSSARY); *correpcio* standing alone is "reproof".
[^19]: **S.** Decretum, D. 40 c. 6 and D. 79 c. 9 (Loserth; the modern form is ours) (LEDGER P10l-6). **T.** B reads *iri posicione* for *in deposicione* (265.2–3), a slip.
[^20]: **S.** Bernard, *De consideratione*, addressed to Eugenius III (LEDGER P10l-7, to be checked); chapter 11 quotes it at length.
[^21]: **C.** *cum nonnulla intronizacione*: singular in the Latin, "with many an enthronement".
