# *De potestate pape*, chapter 7, part 3: Peter on the waters: the dragon, the ship and the propertyless way

## ¶1 · 142.13–144.30

> [p. 142] Quantum ad tercium argumentum de adventu Petri ad dominum super aquas cum aliis exemplis audaciam Petri signantibus, patet quod docent ad sensum expositum vicarium Petri obligari diversissime ad faciendum similiter. Unde non video quomodo patencius, turpius aut convincibilius posset papa mendax allegare pro sua preeminencia quam afferre dictas autoritates ad confirmacionem sue preeminencie, faciendo ad sensum misticum eis contrarium; quia hoc esset afferre dampnacionem suam ex verbis propriis. Cum igitur ambulacio Petri super aquas signat suppeditacionem temporalium sub affeccione per quam viam vicarius Petri ut talis duceret ecclesiam plus vel minus, que maior confusio quam allegare illud iudicium ex lege Domini et cum hoc vivendo sub aquis mundi profundius in fastu et questu seculi, perplexius profundari? Deus enim ex ore proprio iudicat huiusmodi servum nequam. Si igitur sequimur Petrum moraliter in dictis audaciis, debemus postponere vecordiam omnium omissionum temporalium, omnium tribulacionum [p. 143] corporalium et omnium amissionum favorum mundialium, ut similius sequamur Christum et Petrum et induemus formalius corpus suum, cum secundum doctrinam prima Petri V, 3 debemus esse forma facti gregis non coacte sed ex animo. Quomodo igitur non proficit sed officit ad hoc seculare dominium; unde beatus Evangelista prenuncius Antichristi, qui statum tocius ecclesie previdit prophetice Apoc. XII, 3— 4 sic effatur: Ecce, inquit, draco magnus rufus habens capita septem et cornua decem et in capitibus eius septem dyademata, eciam cauda eius trahebat terciam partem stellarum celi, et misit eas in terram, et draco stetit ante mulierem, que erat paritura, ut cum peperisset, filium devoraret. Mulier ista amicta sole habens lunam sub pedibus est mater ecclesia, sponsa Christi, habens ipsum qui est sol iusticia amplexantem, cum de sponso Cantic. II, 5 dicit sponsa Christi: Leva eius sub capite meo, et dextra illius amplexabatur me. Ista mulier habet lunam sub pedibus, quando omnia temporalia, que sunt ut luna mutabilia, appetitui ecclesie substernuntur iuxta illud Apostoli Philipp. III. Omnia detrimentum esse et arbitror ut stercora, ut Christum lucrifaciam. Hec mulier habet capitaliter duodecim apostolos et eorum veros vicarios; hec mulier, ut dicit Johannes, parturiens cruciatur ut pariat, quia Christi vicarii signando Christum in animabus fidelium, utrimque sicut oportet varie tribulantur, cum Act. XIV, 21 dicitur per multas tribulaciones oportet nos intrare in regnum celorum et prima Cor. IV, 15 post multas tribulaciones expressas dicit Apostolus in Christo Jesu per evangelium, [p. 144] ego vos genui Et Gal. IV, 19 signanter dicit Apostolus: filioli mei quos iterum parturio, donec formetur Christus in vobis. Ideo dicitur quod mulier peperit filium masculum, qui redempturus est omnes gentes. Draco autem est quicumque capitalis in ecclesia Antichristi, draco propter venenum ypocrisis et voracitatem populi simplicis. Magnus autem propter pretensionem potencie, rufus propter effusionem sanguinis, quem consilio, auxilio et specialiter omissione causat in ecclesia ex consensu; necesse quidem est ut veniant bella inter gentes et regna pro inordinato amore temporalium, quando sacerdotes Christi, qui debent mundum relinquere, sunt maxime mundiales. Septem capita sunt universitas peccatorum vel evidenciarum, quibus suadet se esse caput ecclesie. Et decem cornua universitas potentatuum, qui militant sub vexillo huius principis Antichristi. Cauda eius est cleri communitas, que exemplo suo trahit stellas in terram, quando viros contemplativos inducit, ut deserentes splendorem virtutum et situm altum in firmamento ecclesie mundialiter conversantur. Oportet igitur quod Christi vicarius sit minime mundialis, ut patet Joh. XVII, et I. Joh. V, quia aliter submergit se in mari non cum Petro ambulans fideliter super aquas. Et iste sentencia evangelica movebat beatum Bernhardum libro III ad Eugenium, quando dixit: Nullum tibi venenum, nullum gladium tibi formido, quam libidinem dominandi.

As to the third argument, from Peter's coming to the Lord over the waters, with other examples that signify Peter's boldness, it is clear that, in the sense expounded, they teach that the [vicar of Peter]{g:vicarius-petri} is bound in the most diverse ways to do likewise.[^1] Hence I do not see how a lying [pope]{g:papa} could [allege]{g:allegare} for his [preeminence]{g:preeminencia} more openly, more shamefully or more to his own conviction than by bringing the said authorities to confirm his preeminence while acting, in their mystical sense, contrary to them; for this would be to bring his own [condemnation]{g:dampnacio} out of his own words. Since, therefore, Peter's walking on the waters signifies the treading underfoot of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} in affection, by which way the vicar of Peter as such would lead the [church]{g:ecclesia} more or less, what greater shame is there than to allege that judgement from the law of the Lord and, with this, living under the waters of the world more deeply in the pomp and gain of the world, to be sunk the more [perplexedly]{g:perplexus}? For God judges such a "wicked servant" out of his own mouth.[^2] If, therefore, we follow Peter morally in the said acts of boldness, we must put behind us [faint-heartedness]{g:vecordia} at all the giving up of temporal goods, at all bodily [143] tribulations and at all losses of worldly favours, so that we may follow Christ and Peter more closely and put on his body more formally, since according to the [doctrine]{g:doctrina} of 1 Peter 5:3 we must be "made a pattern of the flock", "not by constraint" but "from the heart".[^3] How, therefore, secular [lordship]{g:dominium} does not further this but hinders it [is clear]; hence the blessed Evangelist, who foretold Antichrist and prophetically foresaw the state of the whole church, speaks thus in Apocalypse 12:3–4: "Behold," he says, "a great red dragon, having seven heads and ten horns: and on his heads seven diadems. And his tail drew the third part of the stars of heaven and cast them to the earth. And the dragon stood before the woman who was ready to be delivered: that, when she should be delivered, he might devour her son."[^4] This woman clothed with the sun, having the moon under her feet, is mother church, the [bride]{g:sponsa} of Christ, who has him that is the sun of justice embracing her, since the bride of Christ says of the bridegroom in the Canticle 2:5: "His left hand is under my head, and his right hand embraced me." This woman has the moon under her feet when all temporal goods, which are changeable like the moon, are laid beneath the appetite of the church, according to that [word] of the Apostle, Philippians 3: "I count all things to be but loss, and count them but as dung, that I may gain Christ."[^5] This woman has [at her head]{g:capitaliter} the twelve apostles and their true vicars; this woman, as John says, travailing, is in pain to be delivered, because the [vicars of Christ]{g:vicarius-christi}, in imprinting Christ in the souls of the faithful, are troubled on every side in various ways, as they must be, since in Acts 14:21 it is said, "through many tribulations we must enter into the kingdom of heaven", and in 1 Corinthians 4:15, after many tribulations set out, the Apostle says, "in Christ Jesus, by the gospel, [144] I have begotten you". And in Galatians 4:19 the Apostle says significantly: "My little children, of whom I am in labour again, until Christ be formed in you."[^6] Therefore it is said that the woman "brought forth a man child", who is to redeem all nations.[^7] And the dragon is whoever is chief in the church of Antichrist: a dragon because of the venom of hypocrisy and his devouring of the simple people. And great because of the pretension of [power]{g:potencia}; red because of the shedding of blood which he causes in the church by counsel, by aid and especially by omission, through consent; for indeed it must be that wars come between nations and kingdoms for inordinate love of temporal goods when the priests of Christ, who ought to leave the world, are most worldly. The seven heads are the whole sum of the sins or [evidences]{g:evidencia} by which he persuades [men] that he is [head]{g:caput} of the church. And the ten horns are the whole sum of the [potentates]{g:potentatus} who serve as soldiers under the banner of this prince, Antichrist.[^8] His tail is the commonalty of the clergy, which by its example draws the stars to the earth when it induces contemplative men to forsake the splendour of the virtues and their high station in the firmament of the church and to live in worldly fashion. Therefore the vicar of Christ must be least worldly of all, as is clear from John 17 and 1 John 5, for otherwise he plunges himself into the sea, not walking faithfully with Peter upon the waters. And this gospel [teaching]{g:sentencia} moved blessed Bernard, in book 3 to Eugenius, when he said: "I fear no poison for you, no sword, [more] than the lust of lordship."[^9]

## ¶2 · 144.31–146.22

> [p. 144] Unde Augustinus sermone XIV De Verbis Domini notans navicellam Petri signare ecclesiam et mare seculum, cuius fluctus sunt persecuciones tyrannorum inflicte fidelibus, docet quomodo [p. 145] nauta huius navicelle debet artemonem arbori crucis infigere et ipsum lignum cum Christo suspenso ut velum erigere et sic eum efficaciter respiciens erit securus a turbine. Et hinc dicit Petrus nauta huius navicelle: Christus passus est pro nobis, nobis relinquens exemplum, ut sequamur vestigia eius. Et beatus Johannes: Sicut Christus pro nobis animam suam posuit et nos debemus pro fratribus animam ponere. Huic igitur velo simplex conversacio et vera confessio religentur et tuti esse possumus, quantumlibet mare seviat aut ventus incuciat, quod nostra navicella non mergetur; quantumlibet inter flatus et fluctus seculi conturbetur. Sed notet fidelis quod in ista nave non navigat nisi qui in humilitate et paupertate odit seculum, sequendo Christi vestigia. Ideo dicat fidelis prepositus: Si tu est Christus, non miror si solidam carnem super liquantes aquas suspenderis, sed fac quod mirer, iube me ex potestate divina venire ad te super aquas, quod non facit fidelis ad sensum misticum nisi calcata affeccione cunctorum temporalium imitetur Christum in moribus, et sic papa prepollens in talibus est verus Christi vicarius, ut tangitur in quarto argumento, quo Petrus Joh. ultimo misit se in mare ex calore fidei satis salvum. In quo, ut reor, datur intelligi quod stat christianum meritorie sequi Christi vestigia et sic esse in corpore Christi mistico, quod est Petri navicula, licet videatur in facie errantis ecclesie esse foris. Decet enim scripturam sacram esse gravidatam multiformi [p. 146] sentencia, ut preter literam factum illud signat iuxta quod martyrio cicius venitur ad Christum stantem in eternitatis littore, quam contemplacione vel officio confessorum. Et preter hec tercio signatur quod sicut apud Deum est possibile quod quis intromittat se cum mundo et adiutorio ac miraculo suo evadat periculum, ut dicit Christus Luce XVIII, 24 27 quam difficile qui pecunias habent regnum Dei introibunt; facilius est camelum per foramen acus transire, quam divitem in regnum Dei. Et dixerunt, qui hec audiebant, et quis potest salvus fieri? Et ait illis: que impossibilia sunt apud homines possibilia sunt apud Deum. Et videtur michi quod illam difficultatem signavit Petrus Matth. XIV, 30, quando veniens super aquas ad Dominum propter ventum validum cepit mergi; quamvis enim multi divites proni ad pompam, quos oportet finaliter mundum deserere, innitentes Christi adiutorio salvabuntur, via tamen veniendi navigio est clero securior, quia lignum salutis supponitur, ut medium impulsivum periculorum, que ex aquis mundi contigerint.

Hence Augustine, in sermon 14 *On the Words of the Lord*, noting that "the little ship of Peter signifies the church, and the sea the world", whose waves are the persecutions of tyrants inflicted on the faithful, teaches how [145] the sailor of this little ship must fix the sail to the mast of the cross and raise that wood, with Christ hanging on it, as a sail, and so, looking to him effectually, he will be safe from the storm.[^10] And hence Peter, the sailor of this little ship, says: "Christ suffered for us, leaving us an example, that we may follow his steps." And blessed John: "As Christ laid down his life for us, we also ought to lay down [our] life for the brethren." "To this sail, therefore, let a simple [manner of life]{g:conversacio} and true confession be bound, and we can be secure, however the sea may rage or the wind strike, that our little ship will not sink, however much it is tossed among the blasts and waves of the world."[^11] But let the faithful man note that in this ship no one sails except one who in humility and poverty hates the world, following Christ's footsteps. Therefore let the faithful [superior]{g:prepositi} say: "If thou art Christ, I do not wonder that thou holdest solid flesh up over the flowing waters; but do what I may wonder at: bid me, by divine [power]{g:potestas}, come to thee upon the waters." The faithful man does not do this in the mystical sense unless, having trampled on his affection for all temporal goods, he imitates Christ in conduct; and so a pope who excels in such things is a true vicar of Christ, as is touched on in the fourth argument, in which Peter (John 21 [the last chapter]) "cast himself into the sea" out of the warmth of his faith, safe enough.[^12] In this, as I think, we are given to understand that a Christian can meritoriously follow Christ's footsteps and so be in [the mystical body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi-misticum}, which is Peter's little ship, although to the face of the erring church he seems to be outside. For it befits holy scripture to be pregnant with manifold [146] teaching, so that, besides the letter, that deed signifies that one comes more quickly by martyrdom than by contemplation or the office of confessors to Christ, who stands on the shore of eternity.[^13] And besides these, thirdly, it is signified that, just as it is possible with God that someone should involve himself with the world and by his help and miracle escape the danger, as Christ says in Luke 18:24–27: "How hardly shall they that have riches enter into the kingdom of God! For it is easier for a camel to pass through the eye of a needle than for a rich man to enter into the kingdom of God. And they that heard it said: Who then can be saved? He said to them: The things that are impossible with men are possible with God." And it seems to me that Peter signified that difficulty in Matthew 14:30, when, coming over the waters to the Lord, he began to sink because of the strong wind. For although many rich men prone to pomp, who must finally forsake the world, will be saved by leaning on Christ's help, yet the way of coming by ship is safer for the clergy, because the wood of salvation is set beneath, as a means that drives off the dangers which may befall from the waters of the world.[^14]

## ¶3 · 146.23–146.33

> [p. 146] Ex quo videtur quod clerus sed papa precipue debet viare per illam semitam, in qua posset securius et expedicius sequi Christum; securius, quia status exproprietarius est elongacior a periculo temptatorum, et expedicius, quia minus intricatur cum mundo et hostibus, qui a legis Dei observancia retardarent. Unde propulsa vecordia ex temporalium pinguedine talis exproprietarius est ad martyrium pro Christo paracior; in cuius signum cronicantes notant quod post dotacionem fuerunt pauci vel nulli pape martyres.

From which it seems that the [clergy]{g:clerus}, but the pope above all, ought to [go as a wayfarer]{g:viator} along that path in which he could follow Christ more safely and more readily: more safely, because the [propertyless]{g:exproprietarii} [state]{g:status} is further removed from the danger of tempters; and more readily, because it is less entangled with the world and with enemies who would hold [him] back from the observance of God's law. Hence, with the faint-heartedness that comes of the fatness of temporal goods driven off, such a propertyless man is readier for martyrdom for Christ; in sign of which the chroniclers note that after the [endowment]{g:dotacio} there were few or no martyr popes.[^15]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 142.13: Peter's boldness in walking on the sea gives no authority to his vicar.
- 142.32: His courage should be an example,
- 143.1: teaching us not to fear worldly trouble,
- 143.14: In the Apocalypse the woman is the Church and the changing moon beneath her feet a symbol of things temporal.
- 144.6: The dragon signifies the chief Antichrist.
- 145.1: How the ship of the Church is to be navigated.
- 145.15: The navigator must follow Christ in humility and poverty.
- 146.5: The rich man may be saved by Christ's power, but the way of poverty is safer.

## Notes

[^1]: **S/C.** Matthew 14:28–29. *Vicarius Petri*: vicar of Peter, kept apart from vicar of Christ. *Diversissime* is construed "in the most diverse ways"; it may instead mean "very differently" (from what the pope claims these texts give him).

[^2]: **C/S.** *Convincibilius*: in a way that more plainly convicts him. *Dampnacio* is the judgement passed on him: "condemnation". *Suppeditacio temporalium sub affeccione* is construed "treading temporal goods underfoot in affection". *Confusio*: shame, the Vulgate sense. Luke 19:22 (DR "Out of thy own mouth I judge thee, thou wicked servant").

[^3]: **T/S.** Loserth prints *induemus* (sic) (143.3, with a manuscript reading his apparatus gives as *minemus*); construed with *sequamur* as part of the purpose clause. The manuscripts cite 1 Peter 4; Loserth corrects to 5 (143.4). Wyclif joins 5:2 (DR "not by constraint but willingly") and 5:3 (DR "being made a pattern of the flock from the heart").

[^4]: **C/S/R.** *Quomodo igitur … dominium* is an indicative clause with nothing to govern it (so the plate, 143.6: no word lost): "[is clear]" is supplied. It may instead be an exclamation: "How, therefore, secular lordship does not further this but hinders it!" Apocalypse 12:3–4 (DR); Wyclif has *eciam* for *et*, *eius* for *suis*, and no *eius* after *filium*: none changes the sense. B in marg. 143.10: "The woman [is] holy church, whose son the dragon sought to devour".

[^5]: **S/T.** Apocalypse 12:1; "the Sun of justice", Malachi 4:2. The print has *sol iusticia* (143.18, so the plate); we read *iusticie* (our emendation). The Canticle verse is 2:6 (DR "His left hand is under my head, and his right hand shall embrace me"); Wyclif has the past *amplexabatur* for the Vulgate's future *amplexabitur*, translated. Philippians 3:8, abridged; the manuscripts read *feci* where Loserth prints *esse* (143.24), and *omnia detrimentum feci* stands in the verse's second clause (DR "for whom I have suffered the loss of all things").

[^6]: **C/S.** *Capitaliter*, here "at her head": the crown of twelve stars on the woman's head (Apocalypse 12:1). *Parturiens cruciatur ut pariat*: Apocalypse 12:2 (DR "travailing in birth: and was in pain to be delivered"), paraphrased, so left unquoted. *Signando*: imprinting, as a seal. Acts 14:21: Wyclif has *regnum celorum* for the Vulgate's *regnum Dei* (DR "kingdom of God"). 1 Corinthians 4:15 and Galatians 4:19 (DR).

[^7]: **S/R.** Apocalypse 12:5: the Vulgate has *recturus* (DR "who was to rule all nations with an iron rod"); Wyclif's *redempturus* is translated. B in marg. 144.8: "The great red dragon".

[^8]: **C.** *Potencia* here is ordinary power or might. *Ex consensu*: the blood is shed with his consent, though by others' hands. *Universitas*: the whole number taken together. *Huius principis Antichristi* is construed "this prince, Antichrist"; "this prince of Antichrist" is possible.

[^9]: **S/T.** Bernard, *De consideratione* III.1.2 (PL 182, 759; Loserth *ubi supra*, 144.28; cf. 138.18; LEDGER P07c-1). In the same quotation at 138.18 Wyclif has *plus formido*; here *plus* is missing and *quam* has no comparative, so "[more]" is supplied. *Iste sentencia*: *iste* for *ista*. *Sentencia* (CONTEXTUAL): the teaching, not a judicial sentence.

[^10]: **S/T/R.** Augustine, *De verbis Domini* sermon 14: Loserth, *Opera* V, Appendix, p. 132 (144.31), that is, among the sermons the Maurists judged not Augustine's (LEDGER P07c-2): ps.-Augustine, *Sermo* 72.2 among the *Sermones suppositii* (PL 39, 1884–85), on Matt. 14:23–32: *erigatur antenna, ut suspensa arbori crucem Christi figuret* and *simplex conversatio et pura confessio … religentur*; *Si tu es … non miror … fac quod mirer* (145.15) is §4 of the same sermon (PL 39, 1886) (LEDGER P07c-3). Loserth calls the quotation loose (145.9). *Artemonem*: B in marg. *anlempnam* (for *antennam*, the yard), as Augustine reads (*erigatur antenna*). AB in marg. 145.5: "Peter's little ship".

[^11]: **S/T.** 1 Peter 2:21 (DR "Christ also suffered for us, leaving you an example that you should follow his steps"): Wyclif has *nobis* for *vobis* and the first person *sequamur* (the manuscripts *sequimur*, Loserth *sequamur*, 145.6). 1 John 3:16 (DR "he hath laid down his life for us: and we ought to lay down our lives for the brethren"), Wyclif's wording translated. The last sentence is Augustine's again, in Loserth's italics.

[^12]: **S/C/T.** The print has *Si tu est Christus* (145.18, so the plate); "thou art" follows *es* (our emendation). Matthew 14:28 (DR "Lord, if it be thou, bid me come to thee upon the waters"), in Augustine's words. *Prepositus*: a superior, one set over others. John 21:7 (DR "cast himself into the sea"; Loserth, 145.26). *Satis salvum* agrees with *se*: Peter himself was safe.

[^13]: **C.** *Stat christianum …*: the scholastic *stat*, "it can be that". *Sentencia* is the teaching, here the sense, that scripture carries. Christ "stood on the shore" in John 21:4.

[^14]: **S/C.** Luke 18:24–27 (DR); Wyclif's small departures (*introibunt*, no *intrare*) do not change the sense. *Sicut* opens a comparison whose second limb is never stated. Matthew 14:30 (DR "seeing the wind strong … when he began to sink"). *Medium impulsivum*: a means that drives away.

[^15]: **C/S.** *Viare*: the verb of *viator*. *Temptatorum*: of tempters. *Dotacio*: the endowment of the church, Constantine's. The chroniclers' claim has no reference from Loserth; Wyclif makes it again, also unsourced, at *De civili dominio* III, 217 (LEDGER P07c-4, untraced).
