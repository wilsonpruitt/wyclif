# *De ecclesia*, chapter I (a): the prologue and the definition of the church

## Headnote

**Occasion.** Written between early 1378 and early 1379 (SEP), in the months after Gregory XI's
five bulls of 1377 condemned nineteen articles drawn from *De civili dominio*, and as the Great
Schism opened. It is the first of the great ecclesiological treatises, and the others (*De officio
regis*, *De potestate pape*) build on its definition.

**The text.** Loserth's edition (1886) prints the treatise from Vienna, ÖNB Cod. Pal. 1294 (**A**),
with Vienna 3929 (**A₁**), which he judges "almost wholly valueless", and Prague, University Library
X.D.11 (**B**), whose early reader's glosses he prints in the apparatus. Dublin, TCD C.1.23
(**D**) has chapter 7 only. Our copy-text is Loserth's printed text, read from the plates. In the
printed scripture references the chapter numbers are Wyclif's; the verse numbers are Loserth's.

**The move.** Wyclif does not begin with the pope, the bishops or endowment. He begins with
Aristotle's order of knowing: one must know *what* a subject is before one can know its
attributes. The church's liberty, privileges and prosperity, which are what the bishops fight
about, are attributes. So the bishops cannot argue about them until the church's quiddity is
settled. The quiddity is then fixed in a single sentence (5.11–13), and every later question of
headship and membership is made to hang on it.

**Where it is answered.** In Hus's *De ecclesia* (1413), whose dependence on this treatise
Loserth argued in *Wiclif und Hus* (1884) `[verify]`. And at Constance (1415), whose first
condemned article of Hus is this definition `[verify]`. In English: extracts only (Penn 2019,
criticised on this very paragraph by Thakkar, *Vivarium* 58 (2020), 368).

## A · 1.1–2.24

> [p. 1] Quia nonnulli, eciam illi qui videntur esse aliquid discordant in materia de quiditate
> ecclesie, et fides est ecclesia que deberet in christianis omnibus esse una, decet christianos
> cognoscere matrem suam. Quomodo queso honoraret quis matrem illam primevam, sicut quilibet
> christianus tenetur sub pena dampnacionis ex primo mandato secunde tabule, nisi ipsam
> cognosceret? Aut quomodo ipsam perfecte cognosceret nisi cognoscendo eius quiditatem ac
> differencias sciretque a mulieribus alienis discernere? Unde inter omnes metaphisicas sub solis
> circulo non reputo quod sit alia christiano necessarior nec ad observanciam religionis
> christiane vel ad intelligenciam scripture sacre utilior. Christus enim ex fide est pater noster
> et dicta ecclesia sponsa sua est autonomatice mater nostra. Illos autem parentes debemus primo
> omnium honorare, quia recognoscendo eos in necessitatis articulo oportet eorum informacionem,
> castigacionem et suffragium invocare. Illis autem cultis ad plenum servatur plene christiana
> religio et de quanto deest eorum honoracio, deest preceptorum servacio, cum non sit possibile
> christianum delinquere, nisi de quanto subtrahit vel [p. 2] minuit honorem vel ministerium
> debitum istis parentibus. Et hec est racio, quare hoc precipitur in primo mandato secunde tabule
> tamquam metro et regula omnium aliorum. Quomodo ergo foret catholicus qui ignoraret partem
> simboli in qua fides matris sue inseritur? Nam *credo ecclesiam catholicam* dicit utrumque
> simbolum; nec credo dominis episcopis displicere quod tractetur fides christiana de quiditate
> ecclesie, tum quia ipsi maxime tenentur per se et per alios docere populum illam fidem, tum eciam
> quia contendunt de libertate et prosperitate, de edificacione et destruccione, de privilegiis et
> defensionibus ecclesie contra hereticos. Sed constat quod loqueretur ut pica qui tractaret de
> istis passionibus ignorando subiectum, cum secundo posteriorum in principio vere dicitur quod
> *oportet cognoscere questionem, quid est de subiecto, antequam noscatur eius passio.* Et error
> istius ordinis noscendi facit multos errare de fide ecclesie. Quomodo queso cognoscerem in quo
> staret eius prosperitas vel edificacio, nisi ipsam precognoscerem, cum sequatur: ego cognosco
> quod procedit prospere, ergo prius nosco illam esse? Adhibeamus ergo diligenciam, ut
> cognoscamus debite matrem nostram.

[1] Because some, even those who seem to be something,[^1] disagree on the question of the
[quiddity]{g:quiditas} of the church, and [the object of] faith is the church, which ought to be one in all
Christians,[^2] it befits Christians to know their mother. How, I ask, would anyone honour that
primeval mother, as every Christian is bound to do under pain of damnation by the first
commandment of the second table,[^3] unless he knew her? Or how would he know her perfectly
except by knowing her quiddity and differences, and knowing how to tell her apart from strange
women?[^4] Hence among all the metaphysics under the circle of the sun I do not reckon that there
is another more necessary to a Christian, nor more useful for the observance of the Christian
religion or for the understanding of holy scripture.[^5] For by faith Christ is our father, and the
said church, his bride, is by antonomasia our mother.[^6] Now those parents we ought to honour
first of all, because in acknowledging them we must, at the point of need, call upon their
instruction, correction and aid. And when they are honoured to the full, the Christian religion is
fully kept; and to the extent that honour to them is lacking, the keeping of the commandments is
lacking, since it is not possible for a Christian to transgress except to the extent that he
withdraws or [2] diminishes the honour or service owed to these parents. And this is the reason
why this is commanded in the first commandment of the second table, as the measure and rule of all
the others. How, then, would he be a catholic who did not know the part of the creed in which the
faith concerning his mother is set down? For both creeds say *I believe the catholic
church*;[^7] nor do I believe it displeases the lord bishops that the Christian faith concerning
the quiddity of the church should be treated, both because they themselves are bound above all,
by themselves and through others, to teach the people that faith, and also because they contend
about the liberty and prosperity, the building up and the destruction, the privileges and the
defences of the church against heretics.[^8] But it is evident that one who treated of these [attributes]{g:passio} while ignorant of the subject would be speaking like a magpie,[^9] since at the
beginning of the second book of the *Posterior Analytics* it is truly said that *one must know the
question "what is it?" about the subject before its attribute is known*.[^10] And error about this
order of knowing makes many err about the faith of the church. How, I ask, could I know in what
her prosperity or building up consisted unless I knew her beforehand, since it follows: "I know
that she is prospering; therefore I first know that she exists"?[^11] Let us, then, apply
diligence, so that we may duly know our mother.

## B · 2.25–3.13

> Quamvis autem ecclesia dicatur multipliciter in scriptura, suppono quod sumatur ad propositum
> pro famosiori, scilicet congregacione omnium predestinatorum. Illa autem est *sponsa* Christi, de
> qua est processus Cantici canticorum; de qua loquitur scriptura Isaie LXI°, 10: *Tamquam sponsam
> decoravit me corona.* Hec eciam est *mulier fortis,* de qua [p. 3] Proverb. XXXI°, 10, et *corpus
> Christi* misticum, de quo Iª Cor. 12°. Ista est Jerusalem, mater nostra, templum domini, regnum
> celorum et civitas regis magni, *que tota* inquit Augustinus Enchiridion XLVI°, *accipienda est
> non solum ex parte qua peregrinatur in terra a solis ortu usque ad occasum, laudans nomen domini,
> et post captivitatem vetustatis cantans canticum novum, verum eciam ex illa que in celis semper
> ex quo condita est cohesit deo, nec ullum malum sui casus est experta. Hec enim in sanctis
> angelis beata persistit et sue parti peregrinanti, sicut oportet, opitulatur, quia utraque una
> erit consorcio eternitatis et nunc est una vinculo caritatis.*

Now although "church" is [said in many ways]{g:dicitur-multipliciter} in scripture, I suppose that for the present
purpose it is taken for its best-known [sense], namely the congregation of all the [predestinate]{g:predestinatus}.[^12] Now that church is the *bride* of Christ, with whom the whole course of the Song
of Songs is concerned; of whom scripture speaks in Isaiah 61:10: *As a bride he has adorned me with
a crown*.[^13] She is also the *valiant woman* of [3] Proverbs 31:10, and the mystical *body of
Christ* of 1 Corinthians 12. She is Jerusalem our mother, the temple of the Lord, the kingdom of
heaven and the city of the great king, *which as a whole*, says Augustine in the *Enchiridion*, *is
to be taken not only in the part in which she is a pilgrim on earth, praising the name of the Lord
from the rising of the sun to its going down, and, after the captivity of her old state, singing a
new song, but also in that part which in heaven has always, since it was founded, cleaved to God,
and has experienced no evil of its own fall. For this part persists blessed in the holy angels,
and helps its pilgrim part, as it should; for both will be one in the fellowship of eternity, and
are now one in the bond of [charity]{g:caritas}.*[^14]

## B (cont.) · 3.14–3.22

> Hec est sancta ecclesia catholica quam confitentur christiani immediate post fidem in spiritum
> sanctum propter tria: primo quia secundum Augustinum est summa creatura. Ideo immediate ponitur
> post trinitatem increatam. Secundo quia amore spiritus sancti Christo in matrimonio perpetuo
> copulatur. Et tercio quia posita trinitate oportet ipsam habere templum vel domum quam
> inhabitet. Oportet dico ex necessitate supposicionis atque de congruo.

This is the holy catholic church which Christians confess immediately after faith in the Holy
Spirit, for three reasons.[^15] First, because according to Augustine she is the highest
creature;[^16] therefore she is placed immediately after the uncreated Trinity. Second, because by
the love of the Holy Spirit she is joined to Christ in perpetual marriage. And third, because, the
Trinity being posited, it must have a temple or house which it may inhabit: "must", I say, by
necessity of supposition and by congruity.[^17]

## C · 3.23–5.13

> Ista autem ecclesia secundum partem peregrinantem non habet aliquem prescitum partem sui, sicut
> non [p. 4] habet secundum partem triumphantem, dum est huiusmodi, aliquem miserum vel quomodolibet
> maculatum, ut ostendit Augustinus, III° de doctrina christiana XXXII°, ubi, postquam ostenderat
> quod *corpus Christi et caput eius Christus sunt una persona,* increpat Tyconium in secunda sui
> regula qua vocat ecclesiam corpus domini bipartitum. *Non* inquit *ita debuit appellare; non enim
> revera corpus domini est quod cum illo non manebit in eternum.* Ideo vocat fideles ad tempus sed
> prescitos ad dampnacionem ecclesiam similatam ex ypocritis. Potest autem ista sentencia
> demonstrari ex scriptura Johannis VI°, 54, ubi dicitur Judeis et per idem cuilibet viatori:
> *Nisi manducaveritis carnem filii hominis et biberitis eius sanguinem, non habebitis vitam in
> vobis.* Sed nemo potest esse membrum corporis Christi, nisi fuerit membrum vivum: ergo nemo
> potest esse de illo corpore, nisi manducaverit corpus Christi. Quomodo queso potest continuari
> cum nervis capitis, nisi fide formata in caput crediderit? Quod est ipsum manducare, ut exponit
> Augustinus omelia XXVª. Habet autem iste cibus contrarie ad cibos corporales virtutem assimilandi
> sibi manducantem secundum quatuor digestiones, de quibus Rom. VIII°, 30, scilicet
> *predestinacionem, vocacionem, iustificacionem et glorificacionem.* Quamvis enim sic manducans
> moritur temporaliter, non tamen eternaliter, cum *Christus, resurreccio et vita in finali
> iudicio vivificat omnia membra sua,* quia leo rugiens suscitat suam ecclesiam. *Caritas* autem
> que *nunquam excidit* est vinculum [p. 5] continuans membra illius corporis; foret ergo contra
> racionem, vel quod sponsus in persona propria, vel quod alius ex permissione sua laniaret sponsam
> suam divisione eterna. Ymmo claudit contradictorium, si bene inspicitur; cum Jeremie XXXI°, 3,
> sibi dicitur: *In caritate perpetua dilexi te.* Ista igitur et non lapides est mater nostra per
> orbem terrarum confitens caput suum. Quomodo queso tam potens, tam sciolus et tam benivolus,
> sicut est increata trinitas, eternaliter eligeret sibi sponsam quam finaliter repudiaret? Patet
> ergo ex fide et signacione *quid nominis ecclesie catholice* quod ipsa est omnes predestinati
> presentes, preteriti et futuri.

Now this church, in its pilgrim part, has no one [foreknown]{g:prescitus} as a part of itself,[^18] just as, in
its triumphant part, [4] it has no one wretched or in any way stained, while he is such,[^19] as
Augustine shows in *On Christian Teaching* III.32. There, after showing that *the body of Christ
and its head, Christ, are one person*, he rebukes Tyconius over his second rule, in which he calls
the church the Lord's bipartite body. *He ought not*, he says, *to have called it so; for what will
not remain with him for ever is not truly the Lord's body.* Therefore he calls those who are
faithful for a time but foreknown to damnation a simulated church, made of hypocrites.[^20] Now
this opinion can be demonstrated from the scripture of John 6:54, where it is said to the Jews,
and by the same token to every [wayfarer]{g:viator}:[^21] *Except you eat the flesh of the Son of man, and
drink his blood, you shall not have life in you.* But no one can be a member of the body of Christ
unless he is a living member; therefore no one can be of that body unless he has eaten the body of
Christ. How, I ask, can he be joined to the sinews of the head unless he has believed in the head
with [formed faith]{g:fides-formata}? And that is what eating is, as Augustine expounds in Homily 25.[^22] Now
this food, contrary to bodily foods, has the power of assimilating the eater to itself[^23]
according to four digestions, of which Romans 8:30 speaks: namely, *predestination, calling,
justification and glorification*.[^24] For although one who eats in this way dies in time, yet he
does not die eternally, since *Christ, the resurrection and the life, at the final judgement gives
life to all his members*, because the roaring lion raises up his church.[^25] Now *charity*, which
*never falleth away*, is the bond [5] joining the members of that body; it would therefore be
against reason that either the bridegroom in his own person, or another by his permission, should
tear his bride apart by an eternal division. Indeed, if it is well considered, it implies a
contradiction,[^26] since it is said to her in Jeremiah 31:3: *I have loved thee with an
everlasting love.* This, then, and not stones, is our mother, confessing her head throughout the
world.[^27] How, I ask, would one so powerful, so knowing and so benevolent as the uncreated
Trinity is,[^28] choose eternally for itself a bride whom it would finally repudiate? It is clear,
therefore, from faith and from the signification [of the term], what the name "catholic church" [means]{g:quid-nominis}: that she is all the predestinate, present, past and future.[^29]

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** Gal. 2:6 (Vg *ab his autem qui videbantur esse aliquid*): Paul's phrase for the
    Jerusalem leaders, turned here on the church's present authorities.

[^2]: **C.** *et fides est ecclesia que deberet in christianis omnibus esse una*. Literally "and
    faith is [the] church which ought to be one in all Christians". The construal is doubtful.
    (a) We take the clause to mean that the church is an object of faith, as the creed puts it
    below (2.6), and take *que* with *ecclesia* (*unam … ecclesiam*). (b) *Que* may instead go with
    *fides* ("one faith", Eph. 4:5), giving "and the faith that ought to be one in all Christians
    concerns the church". Loserth records no variant; the sentence may be disturbed. Our English keeps
    reading (a) and marks the supplied words.

[^3]: **S.** The second table of the Decalogue holds the commandments toward one's neighbour. Its
    first is "Honour thy father and thy mother" (Exod. 20:12). Wyclif applies it to the church as
    mother, and at 2.2–3 makes it the "measure and rule" of the rest.

[^4]: **G/S.** *Differencias*: differences in the logical sense (*differentiae*), the marks that
    set a species apart from others in its genus. *Mulieribus alienis*, "strange women", is the
    *mulier aliena* of Proverbs (2:16; 5:3, 20; 7:5). The false church is the woman who must not be
    mistaken for the mother.

[^5]: **C.** Penn construed *non reputo quod sit alia christiano necessarior* as "none is held to
    be more necessary than the Christian one". The syntax gives the reverse. No other metaphysics is
    more necessary *for a Christian*, and *alia* implies that the doctrine of the church's
    quiddity is itself a metaphysics (Thakkar, p. 368).

[^6]: **G.** *Autonomatice*: Loserth's printed form, while he reports the MSS as *autonomastice*.
    Both stand for *antonomastice*, "by antonomasia": she has the name "mother" pre-eminently,
    as the one to whom it belongs by right.

[^7]: **S.** Apostles' Creed: *Credo … sanctam ecclesiam catholicam*. Nicene–Constantinopolitan:
    *et unam sanctam catholicam et apostolicam ecclesiam*. The Latin has *credo ecclesiam*, not
    *credo in ecclesiam*: one believes *in* God, and believes the church. Hence "I believe the
    catholic church", not "believe in" `[verify: Rufinus, *Expositio symboli* 37, as the classic
    source of the distinction]`. Thakkar (p. 368) corrects Penn's "represent a symbol everywhere!"
    here.

[^8]: **C.** *tum … tum eciam*: two separate reasons why the bishops should welcome the treatise.
    First, they are bound to teach this faith. Second, they are themselves forever disputing the
    church's liberty, prosperity and privileges, which (the next sentence shows) are attributes
    of a subject they have not defined. *Quod tractetur fides christiana de quiditate ecclesie*
    means that the faith *about* the church's quiddity be treated. Not Penn's "that the Christian
    faith is drawn from the quiddity of the church" (Thakkar, p. 368).

[^9]: **G.** The magpie is the stock image of speech without understanding: it says the words and
    knows nothing of what they mean.

[^10]: **S/G.** *Passio* here is an *attribute* in the *Posterior Analytics* sense (a property
    demonstrated of a subject), not "passion". Loserth refers to *APo* I.1 and II.2. The maxim is a
    scholastic compression, not a quotation. It combines Aristotle's doctrine that learning
    presupposes knowledge of what a term means (I.1, 71a11–17) with the ordering of the questions
    "whether it is" and "what it is" before "that it holds" and "why" (II.1–2) `[verify Bekker
    lines]`.

[^11]: **G.** *Cum sequatur*: "since it follows". Wyclif states the inference (*consequencia*)
    he means, in quotation form: from "I know that she prospers" to "I first know that she
    exists". *Precognoscerem*: Loserth glosses it with Aristotle's *progignōskein* (*APo* I.1,
    71a1), pre-existent knowledge.

[^12]: **G.** *Sumatur … pro famosiori*: "is taken for the best-known [sense]", the language of
    supposition. *Congregacio omnium predestinatorum*: the definition on which the treatise rests.
    It returns as the conclusion at 5.11–13.

[^13]: **S.** A conflation. Vg Isa. 61:10 reads *quasi sponsum decoratum corona, et quasi sponsam
    ornatam monilibus suis* (DR "as a bridegroom decked with a crown, and as a bride adorned with
    her jewels"). Wyclif puts the bride where the Vulgate puts the bridegroom's crown. We translate
    his Latin (CONVENTIONS §10).

[^14]: **S.** Augustine, *Enchiridion* 56 (confirmed; augustinus.it). Loserth prints the chapter as
    XLVI, correcting the MSS' XLI, and refers to the Maurist edition, vol. VI, p. 217. Wyclif
    quotes closely.

[^15]: **S.** In the Apostles' Creed the article on the church immediately follows *credo in
    Spiritum Sanctum*. Wyclif gives three reasons for the order.

[^16]: **S.** *Summa creatura*: not identified by Loserth, and not yet traced (LEDGER V-06).

[^17]: **G.** *Ex necessitate supposicionis*: by conditional necessity. *Given* that the Trinity is
    posited as dwelling somewhere, it must have a house. *De congruo*: by fittingness, not by strict
    necessity. The double qualification limits the claim, so that the church does not become
    something God is absolutely compelled to have.

[^18]: **G.** ⭐ *Prescitus*, "foreknown": Wyclif's term for those whom God foreknows will be
    damned. It is the counterpart of *predestinatus*, and it is not the same word as *reprobus*
    (though Loserth's side-note at 3.23 says "reprobate"). The claim is that no one who will finally
    be damned is a *part* of this church, whatever his office or present standing. See GLOSSARY §A.

[^19]: **C/T.** *Dum est huiusmodi*: the subject of *est* is ambiguous. It could be the triumphant
    part ("while it is such") or the wretched person ("while he is such"). We follow the early
    reader of MS B, whose marginal gloss Loserth prints (apparatus to 3.24). He takes it of the
    person: the predestinate who are still wayfarers, "involved in miseries and stains", will later
    be parts of the church triumphant, but are not counted such *while* they are so involved, "and
    therefore it is said pointedly, *while he is such*".

[^20]: **S.** Augustine, *De doctrina christiana* III.32.45, on Tyconius's second rule, *de Domini
    corpore bipartito*. The first rule (III.31.44) is the one on Christ and his body as one person.
    Augustine proposes that Tyconius should rather have said "the Lord's true and *simulated*
    body", which is where Wyclif's *ecclesiam similatam* comes from. Wyclif's *manebit* stands for
    Augustine's *erit* `[verify wording against the Latin]`. Loserth: *Opp.* III.1, p. 58.

[^21]: **G.** *Per idem*, "by the same token": the saying to the Jews applies by parallel argument
    to every Christian (Thakkar, p. 365). *Viator*, "wayfarer": one still on the road and not yet
    in the homeland. Loserth: "a traveller on earth" (cf. *De civ. dom.* I, p. 4).

[^22]: **S.** Augustine, *Tractates on John* 25–26: *crede, et manducasti* (25.12) `[verify
    section]`. Loserth corrects the MSS' XXVI to XXV and refers to *Opp.* III.2, p. 489.

[^23]: **S.** The reversal of ordinary nourishment echoes Augustine, *Confessions* VII.10.16:
    *nec tu me in te mutabis sicut cibum carnis tuae, sed tu mutaberis in me* ("you will not change
    me into you like the food of your flesh; you will be changed into me") `[verify; our
    identification, not Loserth's]`.

[^24]: **G.** *Digestiones*: Wyclif keeps the metaphor of eating going. The chain of Rom. 8:30 is
    the four stages by which the food assimilates the eater. MS B's margin comments: *Christus
    (sic) digestio*.

[^25]: **S.** The bestiary lion, whose cubs are born dead and are raised on the third day by their
    father's roar: a figure of the resurrection. The *leo rugiens* of 1 Pet. 5:8 is the devil;
    Wyclif gives the phrase to Christ. The italicised clause combines John 11:25 ("I am the
    resurrection and the life") with 6:40.

[^26]: **G.** *Claudit contradictorium*: "implies a contradiction". It is stronger than *contra
    racionem* just before, and the step up is the argument. Not only would it be unreasonable for
    Christ to lose a member of his bride; given Jer. 31:3, it is impossible.

[^27]: **S/R.** *Non lapides*: the church is not the stone buildings. *Per orbem terrarum
    confitens* echoes the Te Deum, *Te per orbem terrarum sancta confitetur Ecclesia* ("the holy
    Church throughout all the world doth acknowledge thee").

[^28]: **G.** *Sciolus*: in classical Latin a "smatterer". Wyclif uses it without contempt: power,
    knowledge and goodness are the three attributes appropriated to Father, Son and Spirit. See
    GLOSSARY §D.

[^29]: **G/R.** *Quid nominis*: what the name means (Thakkar, p. 365). *Ipsa est omnes predestinati*:
    singular verb, plural predicate. The church *is* the predestinate, not a body in which the
    predestinate hold membership, and the English keeps the identity. The definition as Hus
    restated it heads the articles condemned at Constance `[verify: Hus art. 1, *Unica est sancta
    universalis ecclesia, quae est praedestinatorum universitas*]`.
