## Doubtful, left as they are

- **07-j, p. 227**: *Foret **autem** immemor sensus proprii, nisi appareret …*, rendered "And he would be unmindful of his own meaning". Read as (c): it extends the same point, the glossator's own doubt (he cites *Ego Berengarius* for the remaining and does not reprove it). The *Oportet ergo intelligere sanctos* that follows draws on the whole discussion of the glosses, not on this clause alone, so it is not a minor premiss. "Now" could be argued for. Left.
- **07-j, p. 230**: ***Sed** nichil plus hereticum, impium et insane*, rendered "But nothing [is] more heretical, impious and insane." This rejects the consequent of a reductio (God makeable by a baker, handleable by a beast), so it works like a minor premiss in modus tollens. No *ergo* follows, and the sentence is an exclamatory dismissal, not a premiss stated for a conclusion. "But" is defensible (same treatment as *Sed limitatum*, wave 18). Left.
- **07-k, p. 231**: *panis non sit essencialiter sed tropice corpus Christi, **sed** quod natura panis remanet*, rendered "but that the nature of bread remains". The second *sed* adds a second condition to the proviso and stands against "tropically the body of Christ": the bread is the body tropically, yet its nature remains. "And that" would read more smoothly, but "but" follows the Latin and does not invert the sense. Left.
