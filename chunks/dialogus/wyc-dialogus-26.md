# *Dialogus*, chapter 26: the universities and their colleges

## Falsehood · 53.15–53.21

> [p. 53] MENDACIUM. Adhuc videtur quod innuis destruccionem cleri in anglia et per consequens legis Christi. Quis, rogo, secularis sciret defendere fidem ecclesie contra hereticos si non essent in universitatibus collegia super temporalibus dominiis et appropriatis ecclesiis stabilita, ut patet de heresi in eucaristia et aliis similibus que in fine temporum diabolus spissius seminavit?

FALSEHOOD. It still seems that you hint at the destruction of [the clergy]{g:clerus} in England, and consequently of [the law of Christ]{g:lex-christi}. Who, I ask, among the [seculars]{g:secularis} would know how to defend the faith of the [church]{g:ecclesia} against heretics, if there were not colleges in the universities established upon temporal [lordships]{g:dominium} and [appropriated]{g:appropriare} churches,[^1] as is clear from the heresy on the eucharist and other like ones which at the end of time the devil has sown more thickly?[^2]

## Truth · 53.22–55.28

> [p. 53] VERITAS. Quondam movebat me ista sentencia, sed qui me segregavit ex utero affecionis proprietarie dixit mihi, quod oportet omnes istas affecciones relinquere, et in ordinacione Christi et dei fidem ponere. Cum ergo Christus non ordinavit istas universitates sive collegia, manifestum videtur quod ista, sicut graduaciones in illis, sunt vana gentilitas introducta in cujus signum tam collegiati quam alii graduati [p. 54] in universitatibus querunt que sua sunt, caritatis regulas deserentes; ex quo pullulant invidie, comparaciones personarum et patrie, et multa alia seminaria patris mendacii, sicut noverunt qui particulariter considerant istam scolam. Ideo objectus contra secularia dominia clericorum et appropriaciones ecclesiarum stant vivaciter contra istos. Et quantum ad fructum certum videtur quod unus idiota, mediante dei gracia, plus proficit ad edificandum Christi ecclesiam quam multi graduati in scolis sive collegiis, quia seminat humilius et copiosius legem Christi tam opere quam sermone. Ideo si omnes solicitudines superflue quantum ad edificacionem ecclesie forent abscise ab istis scolasticis, vel totum vel major pars ocupacionis hujus ab ecclesia raderetur. Conceditur tamen quod omnia ista tam studia quam collegia, sicut diaboli proposita, profuerunt, sed non directe, ymmo mediante dei gracia; et pure secundum legem et ordinacionem suam plus ecclesie profuissent. Et quantum ad destrucciones heresum, videtur multis quod talia studia sunt nidus heresis et fomentum, vel si quandoque prosunt per accidens, sicut sciencie que domini theologie graduacionem et confirmacionem suam aliquociens obsecuntur, hoc rarenter evenit, et peccata sua vel omissionis vel commissionis sepius eveniunt in hiis locis. Ut que securitas est in istis defendendo quod hostia consecrata non sit corpus domini sed accidens incognitum sine subjecto, ut reserant nove secte? Et sic est de aliis heresibus noviter pullulantibus contra fidem, nam inspiracio sacerdotum simplicium, tam in noticia quam in operacione voluntaria, plus proderit capacitati fidelium laicorum quam omnes dicte universitates cum studiis suis adjacentibus, vel privilegiis laycos deprimentibus ipocritice introductis. Nec ista asserimus propter invidiam sed propter amorem [p. 55] profectus ecclesie et cursum liberum legis Christi. Et idem dicimus de domibus fratrum et aliorum ordinum qui sunt in talibus universitatibus stabiliti. Paulus enim quondam phariseus, ut patet philip 3°, propter meliorem sectam Christi ex ejus licencia sectam istam tanquam providus dereliquit; quare ergo non debent sacerdotes hodie conformiter operari? Et hec racio quare claustrales, cujuscunque secte fuerint vel obligacionis aut juramenti stulti astricti, debent libere ex mandato Christi exire ista vincula et induere libere sectam Christi. Nam ex fide capimus quod secta illa in sua libertate est melior, facilior et undequaque utilior. Sed quis fidelis hesitat quin deus bonus dat servis suis licenciam ad istam partem libere eligendum et aliam condicionis contrarie dimittendum? Nam constitutiones papæ vel regule alie private non possunt infringere istam legem. Que, rogo, racio duceret fidelem ad habendum conscienciam super isto quod eligit melius, ordinacioni dei conformius, et ecclesie militanti utilius? Et cum super omissione istius voluntatis domini non habet conscienciam, sicut super peccatis multis gravibus que perpetrat tam patule quam occulte, numquid credimus sic prediligendo tradiciones humanas tanquam idolatria sibi constituit deos falsos, cum quicquid homo preponderanter diligit constituit deum suum, sed tales tradiciones humanas preponderanter diligendo ultra ordinacionem domini prediligit ordinantem. Et idem videtur de temporalibus dominis qui propter affeccionem proprietariam et mundani nominis constituunt vel consenciunt hujusmodi novitati. Omnes enim videntur ex quadam stulticia quodammodo blasfemare.

TRUTH. Once this [teaching]{g:sentencia} moved me, but he who separated me from the womb of [proprietary affection]{g:affeccio-proprietaria} told me that one must leave all these affections and put faith in the [ordinance]{g:ordinacio} of Christ and of God.[^3] Since, then, Christ did not ordain these universities or colleges, it seems manifest that they, like the [graduation]{g:graduacio}s in them, are a vain heathenism brought in; in token of which the collegiates as well as the other [graduates]{g:graduati} [54] in the universities "seek the things that are their own", forsaking the rules of [charity]{g:caritas}; from which sprout envies, comparisons of persons and of [homeland]{g:patria}, and many other seedbeds of the father of lies, as those know who consider this school closely.[^4] Therefore the objections against the secular lordships of [clerics]{g:clericus} and the [appropriation]{g:appropriacio}s of churches stand vigorously against these. And as to fruit, it seems certain that one unlearned man, by means of God's grace, profits more towards building up Christ's church than many graduates in the schools or colleges, because he sows the law of Christ more humbly and more abundantly, in deed as well as in word. Therefore if all cares superfluous to the building up of the church were cut away from these scholastics, either the whole or the greater part of this occupation would be scraped away from the church. Yet it is granted that all these, the studies as well as the colleges, have been of profit, as the devil's purposes are, but not directly; rather, by means of God's grace; and had they been purely according to his law and ordinance they would have profited the church more.[^5] And as to the destruction of heresies, it seems to many that such studies are the nest and tinder of heresy; or if they sometimes profit by accident, as the sciences do which sometimes serve the graduation and confirmation of a lord of theology,[^6] this rarely happens, and their sins, whether of omission or of commission, happen more often in these places. For example, what security is there in these [places] in defending that the consecrated host is not the Lord's body but an unknown accident without a subject, as the new [sects]{g:secta} disclose?[^7] And so it is with other heresies newly sprouting against the faith; for the inspiration of simple [priests]{g:sacerdos}, in knowledge as well as in voluntary working, will profit the capacity of the faithful laity more than all the said universities with their adjacent studies, or with the privileges, hypocritically brought in, that press down the laity. Nor do we assert these things out of envy, but for love of [55] the church's progress, and for the free course of the law of Christ.[^8] And we say the same of the houses of the [friars]{g:frater} and of the other orders that are established in such universities. For Paul, once a Pharisee, as is clear from Philippians 3, for the better sect of Christ, and by his leave, abandoned that sect, as a prudent man;[^9] why then ought not priests today to work in conformity with him? And this is the reason why [cloisterers]{g:claustrales}, of whatever sect they may be, or bound by whatever obligation or foolish oath, ought freely, by Christ's command, to go out of these bonds and freely put on the sect of Christ. For we take it, [as faith teaches]{g:ex-fide}, that that sect in its liberty is better, easier and in every way more useful. Now what faithful man doubts that the good God gives his servants leave freely to choose this part and to let go the other, of the contrary condition? For the constitutions of the [pope]{g:papa} or other private rules cannot break this law. What reason, I ask, would lead a faithful man [to be troubled in conscience over]{g:habere-conscienciam-super} this, that he chooses what is better, more conformed to God's ordinance, and more useful to the [church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans}? And since he is not troubled in conscience over the omission of this will of the Lord, as over many grave sins which he commits both openly and in secret, do we not believe that by so preferring human traditions he, as by idolatry, sets up false gods for himself, since whatever a man loves preponderantly he sets up as his god?[^10] Now by loving such human traditions preponderantly, beyond the Lord's ordinance, he prefers the one who ordained them.[^11] And the same seems [true] of the temporal [lords]{g:dominus} who, out of proprietary affection and [affection] for a worldly name, set up or consent to novelty of this kind. For all of them seem, out of a certain folly, in some manner to blaspheme.

## Notes

[^1]: **T/C.** *Secularis*: most likely "a secular [cleric]", as against the religious, since the colleges Falsehood defends were founded for seculars; it could also mean "a layman". For *fidem* (53.17) B, B¹, C read *legem*; for *universitatibus collegia* (53.18) B, B¹, C read *universalibus collegiis*; for *ecclesiis* (53.19) they read *elemosinis*, "alms", and *stabiliti* for *stabilita*.

[^2]: **T/R.** For *in fine temporum* (53.20) B, B¹, C read *fratrum temporibus*, "in the friars' times". The eucharistic "heresy" Falsehood cites: Pollard (intro p. vi) calls the phrase "very Wyclifite language" in Falsehood's mouth, and at 54.23–25 Truth names as heresy the doctrine of the host as an *accidens … sine subjecto* taught by the new sects. Read in character, Falsehood would mean Wyclif's own eucharistic teaching, already in dispute when the *Dialogus* was written (1379, Pollard pp. xix–xx); its condemnation at Oxford by Chancellor Berton's council came later, in 1380 or 1381 (Pollard p. xix n. 1 doubts the usual 1381) (LEDGER D26-1).

[^3]: **S/T.** Gal. 1:15 recast: the Vulgate has *qui me segregavit ex utero matris meae*, "who separated me from my mother's womb"; Wyclif puts *affecionis proprietarie*, "proprietary affection", in place of the mother. B, B¹, C omit *et dei* (53.25).

[^4]: **S/C.** *Querunt que sua sunt*: Phil. 2:21 (compare 1 Cor. 13:5), in the Douay–Rheims wording. *Patris mendacii*, "the father of lies": John 8:44 (*mendax est, et pater ejus*), with a play on the speaker *Mendacium*. *Patrie* here is one's native country, the ground of rivalry between regions in the school, not the heavenly homeland.

[^5]: **T.** For *proposita* (54.15) B, B¹, C read *peccata per accidens*: "as sins [profit] by accident". *Suam* refers to God, the nearest person named (*dei gracia*).

[^6]: **C/T.** Doubtful construal. The print is taken with *que* as subject and *domini theologie* as genitive singular: the sciences "serve" (*obsecuntur*) a master's degree in theology. Alternatively *domini theologie* is nominative plural, "which the lords of theology sometimes attend upon for their graduation and confirmation". B, B¹, C read *ecclesie quemadmodum theologie ad decorem* for *sciencie ... graduacionem*: "as [they] sometimes serve the church, as [they serve] theology, for adornment". For *omissionis ... commissionis* (54.22) B, B¹, C read *obmissius ... commissius*; for *istis* (54.23) *multis*.

[^7]: **C.** *Nove secte*: the religious orders (GLOSSARY *secta*). The position described, the host as an accident without a subject, is the one Wyclif rejects. *Reserant*: literally "unlock", i.e. set out openly.

[^8]: **C.** *Cursum* is accusative and so depends on *propter*, parallel to *amorem*, not on *amorem* ("love of ... the free course" would need *cursus*).

[^9]: **S.** Phil. 3:5–7: *secundum legem pharisaeus ... quae mihi fuerunt lucra, haec arbitratus sum propter Christum detrimenta*. *Ex ejus licencia*: by Christ's leave. B, B¹, C read *ad Phil.* (55.4).

[^10]: **C/T.** *Sicut super peccatis*: "as [he is not troubled] over many grave sins"; possibly "as [he is troubled] over", setting the omission beside sins he does feel. *Numquid credimus* is taken as expecting assent; B, B¹, C read *quin sic*, "do we believe otherwise than that so ...". For *perpetrat* (55.20) B, B¹ read *perpetrant*, C *perpetravit*; for *deos falsos* (55.22) B, B¹, C read *dominos plures*, "many lords".

[^11]: **C/T.** *Prediligit ordinantem*: he loves more the one who ordained the traditions, i.e. their human author, above the Lord. B, B¹, C read *non prediligit*, "he does not love the Ordainer [God] more". *Sed* marks the minor premiss of the argument begun at *cum quicquid*.
