# sed/autem extract, wave 11: chunks 11-e 11-f 11-g

### 11-e ¶1 · 279.16–281.17  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 279] Secunda vestis est zona, baltheus vel cingulum, latitudinis quasi quatuor digitorum, reticulatam, quasi pellis vipperea vel stricta tunica linea circa renes, per cuius fortem tinccionem notatur, ut castitas sacerdotis nullo incentivorum estu, cogitacione vel opere dissolvatur, iuxta illud Luce XII, 35: Sint lumbi vestri precincti. Et sic candor vestis signat mundiciam, zona vero continenciam, ut illic non sordescat, hic non effluat conversacio sacerdotis. De zona legitur Exod. XXVII: distincta quatuor coloribus, scilicet bisso et purpura, iacincto et cocco. Per que iuxta signacionem predictam duplicem duplex quaternarius designatur: Primo notantur quatuor virtutes cardinales, scilicet iusticia, fortitudo, temperancia et prudencia, per quas cinctus animus servat albedinem vestis, per quam sit digna, ut dicitur Apoc. XIV, 1 sequi agnum quocumque ierit. Et est correspondencia electorum in istis quatuor coloratis ad istas quatuor virtutes, [p. 280] cum bissus terre admodo, ut patet de combustis cineribus, ossibus et ceteris albis siccis notat iusticiam, que instar terre solide stat in omni temptacione, ut Aristoteles dicit I Ethicorum sicut tetragonus sine vituperio. Purpura vero ignem pretendens, racione ruboris signat fortitudinem purgatam, ut aurum in camino tribulacionis, ut loquitur scriptura, laccinctus **autem** celestis coloris, aquam induratam signans, notat temperanciam que massam corporis humani sic temperat, ut in via morum propter amorem celestium non effluat terminis alienis. Coccus vero bis tinctus est genus tincture medius inter rubeum et croceum, quem nos dicimus vermiculum vel subrubeum colorem ex silvestribus frondibus, vel (ut aliis placet) ex quodam verme decoctum; et signat prudenciam vigentem in sanguineis instar aeris temperatis et specialiter si tincti fuerint preter virtutes morales in prudencia conversacionis penalis domini Ihesu Christi. Secundo notantur quatuor humores, scilicet melancolica et colerica, flegma et sanguis, quibus nature sagacitate commixtis humana complexio denotatur. Sic inquam istis quatuor moribus continencia figuratur. Sicut enim electa in mixtis, sic humores in animalibus et proprietates spirituales in mentibus habent sua officia. Terra enim facit mixto constanciam, aqua continuacionem, aer capacitatem celestis influencie et ignis est via recipiendi peregrinas impressiones et quasi luna instrumentalis, mediante qua mitigantur frigiditas et humiditas inferioris duplicis elementi et confortatur qualitas aeris, ut puta raritas atque caliditas. Sic est de complexione hominis corporali [p. 281] et de continencia mentis spirituali, dum memoria proprie fragilitatis ut terra, continuacio dulcis devocionis ut aqua, tenuitas corporalis cibacionis ut aer et raritas muliebris conversacionis ut ignis, faciunt sacerdotem Domini continentem. Nichil enim tantum valet ad domandum carnis desideria, sicut pensare quomodo incinerabitur post mortem hominis facta terra; continuacio vero devocionis, extinguens ocium, precavet omne crimen et per consequens carnis luxum. Ieiunium vero cavens crapulam cum adiutorio celestis radii extinguit ignem libidinis, et caritativa fuga muliebris consorcii et pro fugienda luxuria supereminens medicina. Evanescas igitur et non expectes in cogitacione vel corpore muliebre consorcium instar ignis, quia nisi habueris ignem amoris, non calesces supernaturaliter.

ENGLISH: The second vestment is the girdle, belt or cincture, about four fingers broad, netted[^1] like a viper's skin, or "a close linen tunic about the loins, by whose firm binding[^2] it is signified that the chastity of the priest is not to be loosened by any heat of incitements, in thought or in deed, according to that of Luke 12:35: 'Let your loins be girt.' And so the whiteness of the vestment signifies cleanness, and the girdle continence, so that there the [manner of life]{g:conversacio} of the priest may not grow foul, and here may not flow away. Of the girdle we read in Exodus 27: distinguished with four colours, namely fine linen and purple, violet and scarlet."[^3] By these, according to the twofold signification already stated, a twofold [four]{g:quaternarius} is designated. First, the four cardinal virtues are signified, namely justice, fortitude, temperance and prudence; girt with these the mind keeps the whiteness of the vestment, by which it is worthy, as is said in Apocalypse 14:1, "to follow the Lamb whithersoever he goeth."[^4] And there is a correspondence of the elements[^5] in these four coloured things to these four virtues, [280] since fine linen, after the manner of earth[^6] (as is clear from burnt ashes, bones and other white, dry things), signifies justice, which like solid earth stands in every temptation, as Aristotle says in *Ethics* I, "like a square [man] without reproach."[^7] And purple, representing fire, by reason of its redness signifies fortitude purged, like "gold in the furnace of tribulation", as scripture says.[^8] And violet, of heavenly colour, signifying hardened water, denotes temperance, which so tempers the mass of the human body that on the way of morals, for love of heavenly things, it does not flow out into alien bounds. And scarlet twice dyed is a kind of dye midway between red and saffron, which we call vermilion or a reddish colour, boiled out of woodland leaves or (as others prefer) out of a certain worm; and it signifies prudence flourishing in the sanguine, who are tempered like air, and especially if they have been dyed, beyond the moral virtues, in the prudence of the hard manner of life of the Lord Jesus Christ.[^9] Second, the four humours are signified, namely the melancholic and the choleric, phlegm and blood, by whose mixture through the skill of nature the human complexion is denoted. So, I say, by these four habits[^10] continence is figured. For as the elements in mixed bodies, so the humours in animals and spiritual properties in minds have their offices. For earth gives the mixed body stability, water continuity, air a capacity for [celestial influence]{g:influencia}, and fire is the way of receiving foreign impressions and, as it were, an instrumental moon,[^11] by means of which the coldness and moisture of the lower twofold element are mitigated and the quality of air is strengthened, namely rarity and heat. So it is with the bodily complexion of man [281] and with the spiritual continence of the mind: memory of one's own frailty as earth, continuity of sweet devotion as water, slenderness of bodily feeding as air, and rareness of converse with women as fire,[^12] make the priest of the Lord continent. For nothing avails so much for taming the desires of the flesh as to ponder how, become earth after the man's death, [it] will be turned to ashes; and continuity of devotion, putting out idleness, guards against every crime and consequently against the wantonness of the flesh. And fasting, guarding against surfeit, with the help of the heavenly ray puts out the fire of lust; and charitable flight from the company of women [is], for the lechery that must be fled, the supereminent medicine.[^13] Vanish, therefore, and do not wait, in thought or in body, for the company of women, which is like fire; for unless you have the fire of love, you will not grow warm supernaturally.

### 11-e ¶2 · 281.18–282.2  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 281] Tercia vestis sunt femoralia, que preter braccas com munes sacerdos induet in signum castitatis eciam interioris, dum vacat divinis officiis. Non enim credi debet sacerdotes veteris testamenti alias continue discooperta carne pudoris incedere, ut solum intrando in sancta verenda tegere iubeantur, **sed** servando continue castitatem coniugalem voluit sermo Dei, ut sacerdotem fungentem officio sacri ministerii cooperiat castitas singularis; unde quia sacerdotes novi testamenti inconiugati servare debent castitatem virginalem continue, ideo tantum utuntur femoralibus communibus ad denotandum, quod continue non licet eis ut sacerdotibus legis veteris post completum officium, laxare ministerium carnalis copule; eis enim pro servanda castitate [p. 282] est cottidiana necessitas, quibus continue offerrendi summum sacrificium concessa est potestas.

ENGLISH: The third vestment is the breeches, which the [priest]{g:sacerdos} puts on besides the common drawers as a sign of chastity, inward chastity too, while he is free for the divine offices. "For it must not be believed that the priests of the old testament otherwise went about continually with the flesh of shame uncovered, so that only on entering the holy place were they bidden to cover their privy parts; but, by keeping conjugal chastity continually, the word of God willed that a singular chastity should cover the priest performing the office of the sacred ministry.[^14] Hence, because the unmarried priests of the new testament must keep virginal chastity continually, therefore they use only the common breeches, to denote that it is not lawful for them, as it was for the priests of the old law after their office was completed, to [loose]{g:laxare} themselves continually to the ministry of carnal union; for they have [282] a daily necessity of keeping chastity, to whom the [power]{g:potestas} of continually offering the highest sacrifice has been granted."[^15]

### 11-e ¶4 · 282.16–282.33  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 282] Quinta uestis est superhumerale, que hebrayce ephot dicitur quasi casula vel planeta; et contextum erat pilis de auro ac coloribus supradictis. In veteri vero testamento soli pontifices usi sunt hac veste quatuor distincta coloribus, ad sensum de zona expositum; tempore vere gracie utuntur ea quilibet sacerdotes. Signat **autem** superhumerale fortitudinem operum et pacienciam laborum, per duos humeros denotatas, unde altrinsecus super humeros fibulabantur duo lapides onichini, in quorum dextro inscripta erant sex nomina seniorum filiorum Ysrahel, et sex in alio, denotando quod sacerdos debet recolere de patriarcharum constancia tam in prosperis quam in adversis. Nostrum **autem** superhumerale non sic inscribitur, quia tempore gracie dimissis cerimoniis antiquis, veniente unico patriarcha, debemus plus inniti simplici veritati.

ENGLISH: The fifth vestment is the superhumeral,[^18] which in Hebrew is called ephod, like a chasuble or planeta; and it was woven of strands of gold[^19] and of the colours aforesaid. And in the old testament the pontiffs alone used this vestment, distinguished with four colours, in the sense expounded for the girdle; in the time of true grace[^20] any priests whatever use it. "And the superhumeral signifies the fortitude of works and the patience of labours, denoted by the two shoulders; hence on either side two onyx stones were clasped on the shoulders, on the right one of which were inscribed six names of the elder sons of Israel, and six on the other, denoting that the priest ought to recall the constancy of the patriarchs in prosperity as in adversity. But our superhumeral is not so inscribed, because in the time of grace, the ancient ceremonies having been dismissed and the one patriarch having come, we ought to lean more on the simple truth."

### 11-f ¶1 · 283.1–284.16  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 283] Sexta vestis est racionale vel logyon, quod pectori pontificis in foramine superhumeralis contexitur. Et fuit vestis conformis texture cum superhumerali, habens nomina duodecim filiorum Ysrahel inscripta secundum quatuor ordines in duodecim lapidibus, ut recolendo de sanctis patribus non degenerent ab eis in moribus. In medio **autem** fuit lapis sardonicus, cui inscribebantur hec duo nomina: iudicium vel doctrina et veritas. Unde secundum Yosephum, dum populus fuit domino placabilis sacerdosque daret populo secundum inspiracionem Dei responsum prosperum, lapis in signum placacionis divine in solito splendore emicuit. **Sed** ducentis annis antequam scripsit librum Antiquitatum, quod fuit tempore Tyti et Vaspasiani cessavit dictus splendor, quod fuit prenosticum reprobacionis populi ex peccato. Rasa dicit, quod urin et thyrin Hebreum, quod sonat doctrina et veritas, fuit nomen Domini tetragramaton, virtute cuius responsum et splendor populo innuebant et signat mistice, quod pontifex medians inter Deum et populum debet docere doctrinam inhaustam secundum Dei iudicium; et sicut non docebit aliam quam veritas suggerit, sic doctrinam suam opere et racione confirmare non desistit. Concathenatur **autem** racionale superhumerali [p. 284] in signum, quod habitus et opera sibi debeant coherere, sic quod totum corpus operum a caritate pectoris procederet. Que caritas licet sit modica in sua essencia, tamen extenditur in quadratum, quia secundum lineam protensam a summo in infimum a dextris amicorum ad sinistrum inimicorum, et ab antiquo ad patres preteritos ad retro quoad filios successores, latitudo vero in operibus debet effectualiter practisari correspondenter ad istam quadraturam, unde ad denotandum istum ordinem prius recitatur racionale in Exodo XXVIII ante superhumerale, quia oportet thesaurum habitus procedere, cum nemo doceat antequam sciat. **Sed** in ordini docendi oportet opus precedere iuxta illud Act. primo, 1: Cepit Ihesus facere et docere.

ENGLISH: "The sixth vestment is the rational or logion, which is woven onto the breast of the pontiff in the opening of the superhumeral."[^1] And it was a vestment conformed in weaving to the superhumeral, having the names of the twelve sons of Israel inscribed in four rows on twelve stones, so that, recalling the holy fathers, they might not degenerate from them in morals. And in the middle was a sardonyx stone, on which these two names were inscribed: judgment or [doctrine]{g:doctrina}, and truth.[^2] Hence, according to Josephus, while the people was pleasing to the Lord and the priest gave the people a favourable answer according to the inspiration of God, the stone shone out with its accustomed splendour as a sign that God was appeased. But two hundred years before he wrote the book of the *Antiquities*, which was in the time of Titus and Vespasian, the said splendour ceased, which was a presage of the people's rejection for sin.[^3] Rasa says that *urin* and *thyrin* in Hebrew, which sounds "doctrine and truth", was the name of the Lord, the tetragrammaton, by whose power they gave the people answer and splendour;[^4] and it signifies mystically that the pontiff, mediating between God and the people, must teach the doctrine he has drawn in, according to the judgment of God; and as he will teach no other than truth suggests, so he does not cease to confirm his doctrine by work and by reason. And "the rational is chained to the superhumeral [284] as a sign that habit and works ought to cohere with each other, so that the whole body of works should proceed from the [charity]{g:caritas} of the breast."[^5] And this charity, although it is small in its essence, is yet extended into a square: for along a line stretched from the highest to the lowest, from the right hand of friends to the left of enemies, and from of old to the fathers gone by, [and] backward as regards the sons who succeed, its breadth in works must be effectually [put into practice]{g:practizare} in correspondence with this square.[^6] Hence, to denote this order, the rational is recited in Exodus 28 before the superhumeral,[^7] because the treasure of habit must go first, "since no one teaches before he knows." But in the order of teaching the work must go first, according to that of Acts 1:1: "Jesus began to do and to teach."[^8]

### 11-f ¶2 · 284.17–285.3  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 284] Septimum ornamentum est mitra, cydaris vel thyara, que imposita capiti, in quo viget sensus, signat quod illis debet committi cura regendi alios, qui ad sensum preexpositum sciunt preeminenter se ipsos regere. Sic enim loquitur Apostolus prima Cor. XI 3 de capite ponens caput uxoris virum, caput **autem** viri Christum et caput Christi Deum; ex quo sequitur, quod Christus est creatura, cum secundum humanitatem regitur a se Deo. Dicitur **autem** mitra fuisse duplicata, cuius pars interior est bissina, pars vero exterior iacinctiva habens in parte inferiori semicirculum aureum ab auribus occupat amplectentem; per hanc igitur signatur vite mundicia, cum bissus supponitur, celestis conversacio, cum iacinctivus color eminet; donum **autem** caritatis et sapiencie connectens hec in capitaneo signatur per semicirculum [p. 285] aureum transeuntem per mitram capiti conectentem. Semicirculus **autem** signat, quod semiplene in via suscipimus ista dona.

ENGLISH: The seventh ornament is the mitre, cidaris or tiara, which, placed on the head, in which sense flourishes, signifies that the care of ruling others ought to be committed to those who, in the sense set out above, know how to rule themselves preeminently. For so the Apostle speaks of the head in 1 Corinthians 11:3, setting down: "the head of the wife [is] the man, and the head of the man Christ, and the head of Christ God";[^9] from which [it follows]{g:sequitur} that Christ is a creature, since according to his humanity he is ruled by himself as God. And the mitre is said to have been doubled, its inner part of fine linen and its outer part violet, having on its lower part a golden semicircle embracing the back of the head[^10] from the ears; by this, then, cleanness of life is signified, since the fine linen is placed beneath, [and] a heavenly [manner of life]{g:conversacio}, since the violet colour stands out; and the gift of charity and wisdom, which connects these in the [captain]{g:capitaneus},[^11] is signified by the golden semicircle [285] passing across the mitre and connecting it to the head. And the semicircle signifies that on the [way]{g:via} we receive these gifts half-fully.

### 11-f ¶3 · 285.4–285.19  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 285] Octavum ornamentum est lamina aurea, que transiens ab aure in aurem per frontem iuncta est; cum mitra filis iacinctivis, cui insculpebatur nomen Domini tetragramaton, quod in Hebreo ineffabile nominatur; per quod consignatur Deum omnium conditorem et rectorem secundum suam sapienciam esse precipue a pontificibus ostendendum tam opere quam sermone, ita quod nomen inscriptum significet essenciam, aurum sapienciam, semicirculus patens in fronte significet eius lucernam ad extra in fabrica quam creavit accione eius ad intra a nobis abscondita: ora **autem** huius lamine supposita signat opus creacionis, ora vero superior signat opus gubernacionis, et connexio huius lamine cum mitra signat, quod officio spiritualis prepositure coniungi debeat hec doctrina.

ENGLISH: The eighth ornament is the golden plate which, passing from ear to ear across the forehead, was joined to the mitre with violet threads; on it was engraved the name of the Lord, the tetragrammaton, which in Hebrew is called ineffable. By this it is signified that God, the maker and ruler of all things according to his wisdom, is to be shown forth chiefly by the pontiffs, as much by work as by word; so that the name inscribed signifies [his] essence, the gold [his] wisdom, and the semicircle open on the forehead signifies his lantern outwardly in the fabric which he created, his action inwardly being hidden from us; and the lower edge of this plate signifies the work of creation, the upper edge the work of governance, and the joining of this plate with the mitre signifies that this doctrine ought to be joined to the duty of the spiritual [office of superior]{g:prepositura}.[^12]

### 11-f ¶5 · 285.31–286.29  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 285] Alia **autem** confibulancia has vestes ad invicem signat cathenam auream, qua caritas connectit omnia opera grata Deo. Unde quatuor causas in [p. 286] genere ponunt doctores talis ornatus pontificis, prima ut excellencia Dei consideretur affectuosius dum eius abiectus vicarius sic ornatur. Secunda ut sacerdotes medii inter Deum et populum tractentur honorabilius. Tercia ut populus intendat cultui divino delectabilius. Et quarta ut divina excellencia consideretur in fabrica sua expressius. Per tunicam quidem lineam dicunt doctores offigurari terram cum suis ornamentis: Baltheus ligans hanc tunicam figuratur occeanum. Tunica iacinctina racione coloris signat aerem. Et tyntinabula cum malogranatis notant tonitrua cum aliis aeris impressionibus. Quidam **autem** ponunt baltheum cingentem hanc tunicam figurare speram ignis cingentem aerem, superhumerale cum racionali sibi iuncto signat celum ethereum, et multitudo colorum diversitatem stellarum. Duo lapides onichini signant duo luminaria magna celi, duodecim gemme in racionali signant duodecim signa zodiaci, in quibus sunt raciones istorum sublunarium; unde Yob XXXVIII: Numquid nosti ordinem celi et pones racionem eius in terra? Per mitram vero figuratur celum empireum et per laminam auream dominus presidens omnibus creaturis. Et hinc nomen Domini magnum sibi inscribitur. Et hinc creditur Alexandrum magnum adorare Deum pronum in terram, dum vidit formam pontificis sic vestiti, ut narrat Magister Historiarum in historia Machabeorum primo cap.

ENGLISH: And the other clasping pieces, which bind these vestments to one another, signify the golden chain by which charity connects all works pleasing to God. Hence the doctors set down four causes, [286] in general, of such adornment of the pontiff: first, that the excellence of God may be considered more affectionately while his lowly vicar is so adorned; second, that the priests, as mediators between God and the people, may be treated more honourably; third, that the people may attend to divine worship with more delight; and fourth, that the divine excellence may be considered more expressly in his fabric. By the linen tunic, the doctors say, the earth with its ornaments is figured; the belt binding this tunic figures the ocean; the violet tunic by reason of its colour signifies the air; and the bells with the pomegranates denote thunders with the other impressions of the air. But some hold that the belt girding this tunic figures the sphere of fire girding the air; the superhumeral with the rational joined to it signifies the ethereal heaven, and the multitude of colours the diversity of the stars; the two onyx stones signify the two great lights of heaven; the twelve gems in the rational signify the twelve signs of the zodiac, in which are the reasons of these sublunar things;[^14] hence Job 38: "Dost thou know the order of heaven, and canst thou set down the reason thereof on the earth?" And by the mitre the empyrean heaven is figured, and by the golden plate the Lord presiding over all creatures; and hence the great name of the Lord is inscribed on it. And hence it is believed that Alexander the Great worshipped God, prostrate on the ground, when he saw the form of the pontiff so vested, as the Master of the Histories relates in the history of the Maccabees, chapter 1.[^15]

### 11-g ¶1 · 286.30–288.6  (6 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 286] Notandum **autem** quod sacerdotibus inferioris ordinis non datur tunica iacinctinam nec superhumerale nec racionale nec lamina aurea, **sed** soli summo pontifici, sicut in nostris sacerdotibus [p. 287] est ordo vestium. Sex enim ornamenta habent simplices sacerdotes in missa; scilicet amictam, albam, zonam, manipulam, stolam et casulam. Per amictam notatur custodia sensuum sacerdotis, quia illud ornamentum tegit caput et collum, in quo sunt omnes sensus; sic clericus debet velare omnes sensus et specialiter tempore misse, ne adulterinis sensacionibus temptet mentem, nec collum, in quo est organum vocis, ministret vocem ab interioribus ad vicium lingue. **Sed** iuxta Petri monita: Si quis loquitur, quasi sermones Dei prima Petr. IV, 11. Verba enim sacerdotis debent esse evangelium. Alba camisia, que est stricta linea in veteri testamento signat omnia membra corporis sacerdotis divino servicio mancipanda. **Sed** tempore ecclesie Judeorum, qui spiritu servitutis erant astricti, erat hec tunica striccior, quam est modo, quando dilatamur libertate qua Christus nos liberavit. **Sed** quia surrepit ommissionis negligencia, cum inordinatis operibus quantumcumque divino cultui mancipemur, ideo quarto ponitur in sinistra manu mapula vel manipula, denotans quod crebro debemus ab oculis mentis sordes abstergere, et contra facta sinistre perpetrata ad opera penalia gratis obligacione perpetua nos parare. Unde quinto assumit stolam sive orarium, que collo sacerdotis imposita, signat, quod iuxta consilium Christi Matth. XI 29 sustulit super se iugum Domini longenimitatis sufferendum. Quod **autem** stola cancellata per anteriora descendit, signat quod sacerdos sustulit crucem suam sequendo Christum, per [p. 288] arma iusticie a dextris et a sinistris, ut loquitur Christus Luce IV et Apostolus II Cor. VI et debet stola huius perseverancie que sola coronabitur usque ad pedes affeccionum se extendere. Connexio vero cum zona signat concathenacionem perpetue castitatis cum ceteris virtutibus sacerdotis.

ENGLISH: And it is to be noted that to [priests]{g:sacerdos} of the lower order are given neither the violet tunic nor the superhumeral nor the rational nor the golden plate, but to the high priest alone,[^1] as among our priests [287] there is an order of vestments. For simple priests have six ornaments at mass, namely the amice, the alb, the girdle, the maniple, the stole and the chasuble. By the amice is signified the custody of the priest's senses, because that ornament covers the head and neck, in which are all the senses; so the cleric ought to veil all his senses, and especially at the time of mass, lest he tempt his mind with adulterous sensations, and lest the neck, in which is the organ of the voice, minister the voice from within to the vice of the tongue. But according to Peter's [admonitions]{g:monita}: "If any man speak, let him speak, as the words of God", 1 Peter 4:11. For the words of a priest ought to be gospel. The alb, a shirt, which in the old testament is the close linen [tunic], signifies that all the [members]{g:membrum} of the priest's body are to be given over to divine [service]{g:servicium}.[^2] But in the time of the [church]{g:ecclesia} of the Jews, who were bound by the spirit of bondage, this tunic was narrower than it is now, when we are enlarged by the freedom wherewith Christ has made us free.[^3] But since negligence of omission creeps in, with disordered works, however much we are given over to the [worship of God]{g:cultus-dei}, therefore, fourth, the mappula or maniple is put on the left hand, denoting that we must often wipe filth from the eyes of the mind, and, against deeds done amiss, prepare ourselves freely, by perpetual obligation, for works of hardship. Hence, fifth, he takes the stole or orarium, which, placed on the priest's neck, signifies that according to Christ's counsel, Matthew 11:29, he has taken upon himself the [yoke]{g:iugum} of the Lord, to be borne with long-suffering.[^4] And that the stole descends crossed over the front signifies that the priest has taken up his cross, following Christ, through [288] "the armour of justice on the right hand and on the left", as Christ speaks in Luke 4 and the Apostle in 2 Corinthians 6;[^5] and the stole of this perseverance, which alone will be crowned, must extend down to the feet of the affections. And its joining with the girdle signifies the chaining together of perpetual chastity with the other virtues of the priest.

### 11-g ¶2 · 288.8–289.6  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 288] **Sed** sexto induit casulam vel planetam ad denotandum quod eminenciori via incedit presbiter christianus, dum habet caritatem ad singulos. Unde ad denotandum perfeccionem rotunditatis et evacuacionem sordium angulorum declinat hec casula a quadratura superhumeralis in veteri testamento ad rotunditatem et simplicitatem. Non enim est lex Christi tam aspera in angulis ut lex vetus, cum nec apostoli nec patres eorum ipsam portare poterant, ut dicit Petrus Act. XV, 10 **sed** quibusdam circulis et catenulis fuerant a lateribus colligata. Nos **autem** sub tali concathenacione portamus suavem et levem casulam a dextris et sinistris. Caritas enim operatur magis, si est; et licet careat multitudine figurarum superhumeralis veteris testamenti propter unitatem simplicem capitis nostri Christi, debet tamen sacerdos esse planeta in populo, ut videant opera eius bona. Sic enim docet Christus Matth. V, quod luceat lux vestra coram hominibus. Et est aliud ornamentum in novo testamento, competens diacono vel levite, quod vocatur dalmatica, cuius nomen est sumptum a regione, ubi primo creditur institutum. Forma vero creditur capta a collobiis, quibus usi fuerunt apostoli. Quidam enim apostolorum successores adiectis [p. 289] manicis usi fuerunt huiusmodi indumentis; et eundem sensum misticum, quem habet casula in presbiteris, habet dalmatica in levitis; ipsi enim paracius et asperius debent caritative proficere tamquam alter Laurencius quam presbiteri etatis et corporis gravioris.

ENGLISH: But, sixth, he puts on the chasuble or planeta, to denote that the Christian [presbyter]{g:presbiter} walks a more eminent way, while he has [charity]{g:caritas} toward each one. Hence, to denote the perfection of roundness and the emptying out of the filth of corners, this chasuble departs from the squareness of the superhumeral in the old testament to roundness and simplicity. For the law of Christ is not so rough in its corners as the old law, since neither the apostles nor their fathers could bear that, as Peter says in Acts 15:10; but they[^6] had been bound together at the sides with certain rings and little chains. But we, under such a chaining together, carry a sweet and light chasuble on the right and on the left. For charity works more, if it is;[^7] and although it lacks the multitude of figures of the superhumeral of the old testament, because of the simple unity of our head, Christ, yet the priest ought to be a planet among the people, that they may see his good works.[^8] For so Christ teaches in Matthew 5, that "your light shine before men". And there is another ornament in the new testament, proper to a deacon or levite, which is called the dalmatic, whose name is taken from the region where it is believed first to have been instituted. And its form is believed to have been taken from the colobia which the apostles used. For certain successors of the apostles, adding [289] sleeves, used garments of this kind; and the dalmatic has in levites the same mystical sense that the chasuble has in presbyters; for they ought to advance in charity more readily and more roughly, like another Lawrence, than presbyters of graver age and body.[^9]

### 11-g ¶4 · 290.20–290.27  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 290] Unde baculus signat pastoris officium ad minandum et conprehendendum oves morbidas, ut sanentur, **sed** postquam corona baculi versa est in aurum preciosum et frigidum formasset in figuram artificiosam adinvencionis humane, dedignatur pastor cesareus refrigescente caritate curam apostolicam ovibus Christi impendere, **sed** in subtilitate secularis officii sigillatur.

ENGLISH: Hence the staff signifies the shepherd's office of driving and catching the diseased sheep, that they may be healed; but after the crown of the staff was turned into precious gold and, cold, had been formed into an artful figure of human invention,[^17] the caesarean shepherd, his charity growing cold, disdains to spend apostolic care on the sheep of Christ, but is sealed up in the subtlety of secular office.[^18]
