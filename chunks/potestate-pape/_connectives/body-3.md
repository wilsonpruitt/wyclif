### 04-a ¶1 · 62.10–63.8  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 62] Supposito quod beatus Petrus habuit primatum inter apostolos ad regendum, arbitror quod non solum accepit primatum pro se ipso **sed** pro eius vicariis succedentibus in ecclesia militante. Cum enim Christus non potest ecclesiam suam deserere et oporteat ipsam militantem post Christum exemplari ab ecclesia apostolica, mandatum est quod, sicut Christus fuit capitaneus cetus apostolici et post ipsum Petrus, sic oportet continue esse capitaneum in ecclesia militante. **Sed** antequam discuciatur utrum oporteat ipsum esse Romanum pontificem necessarium, arbitror precognoscere aliquas sentencias premittendas. Et primo, qualem oportet esse beati Petri archivicarium et per quid constituitur Petri vicarius. Nec debet fideli verti in dubium quin sicut Petrus ex hinc per se fuit Christi vicarius; quod magistrum suum quoad actus et mores secutus fuit similius, sic oportet Petri vicarium similius sequendo Petrum de corpore ecclesie derivari. [p. 63] Vicarius enim in quantum huiusmodi gerit vicem superioris a quo capit vicariam potestatem. Si igitur in principio quod debet regulare sequencia Petrus non fuisset sic Christi vicarius nisi quia secutus fuit eum propinquius, per locum a sufficienti similitudine oportet quod Petri archivicarius de propinquiori sequatur Christum et per consequens Petrum in moribus.

ENGLISH: Supposing that blessed Peter had the [primacy]{g:primatus} among the apostles for ruling, I think that he received the primacy not only for himself but for his vicars succeeding him in the [church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans}. For since Christ cannot desert his [church]{g:ecclesia}, and it must, militant after Christ, be modelled on the apostolic church, it is enjoined that, as Christ was [captain]{g:capitaneus} of the apostolic company, and after him Peter, so there must continually be a captain in the church militant.[^1] But before it is discussed whether he must necessarily be the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex}, I think [we must] know beforehand certain [teachings]{g:sentencia} to be set out first.[^2] And first, what sort of man the archvicar of blessed Peter must be, and by what a [vicar of Peter]{g:vicarius-petri} is constituted. Nor ought a believer to call into doubt that, just as Peter was in his own right [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius-christi} from this, that he followed his master more nearly in likeness as to acts and morals, so Peter's vicar must be derived from the body of the church by following Peter more nearly in likeness.[^3] [63] For a vicar, as such, bears the stead of the superior from whom he takes vicarial [power]{g:potestas}. If therefore in the beginning, which ought to regulate what follows, Peter would not have been thus vicar of Christ except because he followed him more nearly, then by the topic from sufficient likeness Peter's archvicar must follow Christ, and consequently Peter, more nearly in morals.[^4]

### 04-a ¶2 · 63.9–63.26  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 63] Item, illius est homo vicarius cuius vicem gerit et a quo procuratoriam potestatem legitime accepit, **sed** nemo gerit vicem Christi vel Petri nisi sequatur eum in moribus, cum nulla alia sequela sit pertinens nec aliter recipit a Deo procuratoriam potestatem, igitur ad illud officium vicarium requiritur et morum conformitas et instituentis auctoritas. Cum enim Deus necessario agit omnem accionem creature, patet quod impossibile est habere quemquam talem vicariam potestatem nisi Deus ipsam dederit, sicut dicit Apostolus Rom. XIII, ymo cum illa potestas sit tam excellens dominium supernaturale, videtur quod oportet ipsum singulariter et supernaturaliter dari a Deo. Deus **autem** non daret provide potestatem huiusmodi illi quem sciret esse proditurum suam ecclesiam, sicut nec dat graciam predestinacionis illi quem scit finaliter periturum.

ENGLISH: Again, a man is the vicar of him whose stead he bears and from whom he has lawfully received proctorial power; now no one bears the stead of Christ or of Peter unless he follows him in morals, since no other following is pertinent, nor does he otherwise receive proctorial power from God; therefore for that vicarial office there is required both conformity of morals and the authority of the one who institutes.[^5] For since God necessarily does every action of a creature, it is clear that it is impossible for anyone to have such vicarial power unless God has given it, as the Apostle says, Romans 13; indeed, since that power is so excellent a supernatural [lordship]{g:dominium}, it seems that it must be given by God singularly and supernaturally.[^6] Now God would not providently give power of this kind to one whom he knew would betray his church, just as he does not give the grace of predestination to one whom he knows will finally perish.

### 04-a ¶3 · 63.27–65.7  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 63] Ex quibus patet quod humana eleccio nichil valet in eligendo talem prepositum; nisi forte de quanto promulgaverit conformiter ad divinum iudicium. Et ista est secunda sentencia supponenda. Item suppono quod electores devient eligendo unum inhabilem propter affeccionem persone, pecunie vel in penam peccati populi, non concordando divine sentencie, tunc cum Deus non approbat nec confirmat quod peccator [p. 64] quem eligunt sit eius vicarius, patet quod eorum activa eleccio non dat electo iusticiam quoad Deum. Sic enim possent electores perverti quod eligunt in Christi vicarium pessimum Antichristum. Et hinc credo quod ritus ecclesie religiose nominant talem electum permissione divina episcopum; non presumunt dicere approbacione vel gracia divina; quod sepius mentiretur. Si dicatur quod eo ipso quis eligitur aput Deum autorizantem eleccionem, quo in eum maior pars consenserit electorum, eo ipso subditi debent sequi eum quomodocumque viaverit, patet quod nichil infidelius, quia non solum membra dyaboli cogerent Deum ad malum; **sed** fides consequentis subverteret statum ecclesie, ideo absit a fidelibus hoc porisma; tunc enim posset currus Dei ex principiis fidei duci in precipicium, descendendo per latitudinem itineris Antichristi. Ideo debet esse maxima fideli quod precise de tanto debet subditus sequi suum privatum prepositum vel sibi aut sue prepositure legitime consentire, de quanto imitatus est Christum vel docuerit legem suam, aut conversacione et opere ostendit signum vivum sui officii. Patet ex decreto sancti pape Cypriani distinccione VIII Si solus Christus, et docet apostolus prima Cor. XI, 1: Estote imitatores mei sicut ego Christi. Ecce quam breviter forma exprimitur imitandi. Et hec racio quare apostoli, ne eclipsarent, **sed** declararent visionem ad sequendum Christi vestigia, non ediderunt novas leges **sed** legem quam a Christo acceperant sedule docuerunt; dixerunt se esse vacuos et dyasones quasi nichil, ut patet prima Cor. XIII, 2, [p. 65] et Christum non se vigilancius predicabant, ut patet II. Cor. IV. Nichil enim periculosius in ecclesia quam obumbrare viam sequendi Christi vestigia, ducendo populum ab eius semita, quia error talis secundum imaginacionem Aristotelis primo De Celo et Mundo cresceret in declinacionem maximam Antichristi.

ENGLISH: From which it is clear that human election avails nothing in electing such a [superior]{g:prepositi}, unless perhaps in so far as it has promulgated in conformity with the divine judgement. And this is the second teaching to be supposed.[^7] Again, I suppose that electors go astray by electing an [unqualified]{g:inhabilis} man out of affection for a person or for money, or in punishment for the sin of the people, not agreeing with the divine sentence; then, since God neither approves nor confirms that the sinner [64] whom they elect is his vicar, it is clear that their active election does not give the one elected justice as regards God. For electors could be so perverted that they elect the worst Antichrist as vicar of Christ. And hence I believe that the [rites]{g:ritus} of the church religiously name such a one, once elected, "bishop by divine permission"; they do not presume to say "by divine approval" or "by divine grace", which would very often be a lie.[^8] If it is said that by the very fact that the greater part of the electors has consented to someone, he is elected before God, who [authorizes]{g:autorizare} the election, [and that] by that very fact the subjects must follow him however he has walked, it is clear that nothing is more faithless; for not only would [members]{g:membrum} of the devil compel God to evil, but faith in the [consequent]{g:consequens} would overturn the state of the church; therefore far be this [porism]{g:porisma} from the faithful; for then the chariot of God could, from principles of faith, be driven over a precipice, going down along the broadness of the road of Antichrist.[^9] Therefore it ought to be a maxim for the believer that a subject ought to follow his private superior, or lawfully consent to him or to his [office of superior]{g:prepositura}, precisely in so far as he has imitated Christ or taught his law, or by [manner of life]{g:conversacio} and work shows a living sign of his office.[^10] It is clear from the decree of the holy pope Cyprian, distinction 8, *Si solus Christus*, and the apostle teaches it, 1 Corinthians 11:1: "Be ye imitators of me, as I of Christ."[^11] See how briefly the form of imitating is expressed. And this is the reason why the apostles, so as not to eclipse but to make clear the vision for following Christ's footsteps, did not put out new laws, but diligently taught the law that they had received from Christ; they said that they were empty and *dyasones*, as it were "nothing", as is clear from 1 Corinthians 13:2, [65] and they preached Christ, not themselves, the more watchfully, as is clear from 2 Corinthians 4.[^12] For nothing is more dangerous in the church than to overshadow the way of following Christ's footsteps, leading the people away from his path, for such an error, according to Aristotle's image in the first book of *On the Heaven and the World*, would grow into the greatest deviation, [that] of Antichrist.[^13]

### 04-a ¶4 · 65.8–65.27  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 65] Et ex isto quarto colligitur quod, sive electores bene vel male elegerint, operibus bonis debemus credere et quicumque modus perficiendi prepositum fuerit iudicio Christi conformior est eo melior. Nam ex dictis secundo capitulo de potestate eo ipso quo quis copiosius operatur meritorie habet a Deo ad hoc copiosius potestatem. Et hinc dicit Christus Joh. X, 38: Operibus credite, et hinc dicit Crisostomus in De laude Pauli: Si, inquit, magisterii atque primatus adipiscendi gracia ad procuranda tanta negocia apostoli prosiluissent, consequenter essent de ambicione culpabiles. Sequitur: Sicut enim, qui princeps fuerit ordinatus, nisi digne sibi impositum ministret officium, meretur eciam maiora supplicia, sic alter etsi non ordinetur, suscipiat tamen et sicut oportet impleat non dico sacerdocium **sed** solicitudinem curamque multorum, tunc profecto honore est dignissimus. Iste sanctus non ponderavit elecciones nostras **sed** recta officia.

ENGLISH: And from this it is gathered, fourth, that whether electors have elected well or ill, we ought to believe good works, and whatever mode of making a superior is more in conformity with the judgement of Christ is by so much the better.[^14] For from what was said in the second chapter on power, by the very fact that someone works the more abundantly meritoriously, he has from God the more abundantly the power for it. And hence Christ says, John 10:38: "Believe the works"; and hence Chrysostom says in *De laude Pauli*: "If," he says, "the apostles had leapt forward to manage such great affairs for the sake of gaining [rule]{g:magisterium} and primacy, they would consequently be guilty of ambition." It continues: "For as he who has been ordained a prince, unless he administers worthily the office laid on him, deserves even greater punishments, so another, even if he is not ordained, should he yet take up and fulfil as he ought (I do not say the priesthood, but the solicitude and care of many), then he is surely most worthy of honour." This saint did not weigh our elections, but offices rightly done.[^15]

### 04-a ¶5 · 65.28–65.30  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 65] **Sed** contra illud arguitur per hoc, quod iuxta istam sentenciam perirent de eleccionibus multe leges et multa legum principia.

ENGLISH: But against that it is [argued by appeal to]{g:arguitur-per} this, that according to this teaching many laws on elections, and many principles of laws, would perish.

### 04-a ¶6 · 66.1–66.18  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 66] **Sed** negetur illud, nisi forte vocentur leges hereses, quibus intricatur ecclesia. Nichil enim est magis hereticum quam pertinaciter defendere quod eo ipso quo electores vel maior pars eorum consenserit viva voce secundum ritus hominum in personam aliquam, eo ipso illa persona legittime est electa; oportet enim subducere subduccionem, quia stat in culpatis electoribus quod electus sit pessimus antichristus et stat illos amore vel odio eligere personam in quam Deus non consenserit, sicut stat ipsos male eligere personam cuius eleccionem passivam Deus approbat. Ideo idem hoc foret credere et electores extollere super Deum; quia ratificare per eos eleccionem quoad personam apud Deum inhabilem, quod Trinitas non potest facere. Ideo illi qui religiosius eligunt subducta revelacione solum probabili reputacione pronuncciant pro electo.

ENGLISH: [66] But let that be denied, unless perhaps heresies, by which the church is entangled, are called laws. For nothing is more heretical than to defend obstinately that by the very fact that the electors, or the greater part of them, have consented by living voice, according to the rites of men, to some person, by that very fact that person is lawfully elected; for the exclusion must be made, since it can be, when the electors are culpable, that the one elected is the worst antichrist, and it can be that they elect, from love or hatred, a person to whom God has not consented, just as it can be that they elect wrongly a person whose passive election God approves. Therefore to believe this would be the same as to exalt the electors above God, since [it would be] to ratify through them an election as regards a person unqualified before God, which the Trinity cannot do. Therefore those who elect more religiously, revelation being absent, pronounce for the one elected only by a probable reckoning.[^16]

### 04-b ¶1 · 66.19–68.7  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 66] **Sed** secundo replicatur per hoc quod oportet pro salvando cursu et statu ecclesie incidere in tales errores absconditos, ne ecclesia destituatur regimine. **Sed** iste est communior lapsus in heresim, qua dogmatizatur peccandum esse ut bonum eveniat. Constat quidem ex fide quod pro toto mundo non consentiret fidelis mendacio electorum, sicut nec electores contra veritatem eligerent unum inhabilem quem Deus prius non elegerit et habilitaverit. Et patet quod sentencia electivarum legum debet supponere cetera paria et conformia in preeleccione divina, quia aliter est dicta maxima summe impossibilis et fidelium seductiva. Nec aliquis fidelis ad tantum infatuatur, ut credat Deum ex pacto electoribus assistere et suam eleccionem qualiscumque fuerit confirmare; [p. 67] tunc enim forent electores inpeccabiles, cum in eligendo facilius errare potuerunt. Nec tunc convinci poterit error eleccionis nec esset eius confirmacio ab homine expectanda. Ideo nichil fideli nocius quam quod in eleccionibus huiusmodi contingit errare crebrius; quod regulariter contingit, quando electores eligunt personam quam Deus non approbat vel habilitat ad tale officium. Et hec racio quare apostoli elegerunt Mathiam per sortem, ut patet Actuum Ubi sunt notanda misteria, primo quomodo beatus Petrus ex illo Psalmo CVIII, 8 episcopatum eius accipiat alter concludit quod oportet ex hiis viris, qui viderunt conversacionem apostolorum cum Christo; unum institui loco Jude et, quia nondum habuerunt revelacionem de persona; constituerunt duos plus habiles scilicet Barsabam et Mathiam, relinquentes divino iudicio quem illorum voluerit per sortem ostendere electum penes eum; cuius arbitrio oportet in eleccionibus dare fidem. Unde triginta electores sequentes dixerunt verba notabilia sepe dicte sentencie expressiva. Tu Domine, inquiunt, qui nosti corda hominum, ostende quem elegeris ex hiis duobus unum accipere locum ministerii huius, ubi patet quod oportet Deum eligere et secundum alios ritus eligentes valde ecquivoce eleccionem Dei fideliter promulgare, ideo dicit, ostende quem elegeris non ad dignitatem **sed** ministerium. **Sed** tercio obicitur, cum eleccio nostra debet exemplari et autorizari ab apostolis; a quibus trahimus ritus alios, videtur quod debemus in nostris eleccionibus sequi illos; nam magis autenticam [p. 68] formam eligendi non possumus cogitare; et obiectus eque moverent contra apostolos. Et iterum videtur quod non plus in isto quam in illis que sonant commodum et honorem licet a ritu apostolorum recedere, quia sic pertinenter imponeretur nobis quod deserentes fidem scripture exequimur infideliter ritus gentilium.

ENGLISH: But secondly it is rejoined, by appeal to this, that to save the course and state of the [church]{g:ecclesia} one must fall into such hidden errors, lest the church be left destitute of government.[^1] But this is the commoner lapse into the heresy by which it is taught as dogma that "one must sin, that good may come".[^2] It is evident indeed, [as faith teaches]{g:ex-fide}, that a believer would not, for the whole world, consent to the lie of the electors, just as electors would not, against the truth, elect an [unqualified]{g:inhabilis} man whom God has not first elected and [qualified]{g:habilitare}.[^3] And it is clear that the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of the laws on election ought to suppose all else equal and conformed to the divine pre-election, since otherwise the said maxim is supremely impossible and a seducer of the faithful. Nor is any believer so far infatuated as to believe that God by covenant assists electors and confirms their election, whatever it may be; [67] for then electors would be impeccable, although in electing they could the more easily err. Nor then could the error of an election be proved, nor would its confirmation have to be awaited from man. Therefore nothing is more harmful to the believer than that in elections of this kind error happens rather often; which happens regularly when electors elect a person whom God does not approve or qualify for such an office.[^4] And this is the reason why the apostles elected Matthias by lot, as is clear from Acts [1]; where mysteries are to be noted: first, how blessed Peter, from that [verse of] Psalm 108:8, "his bishopric let another take", concludes that one must be instituted in the place of Judas from these men who saw the [manner of life]{g:conversacio} of the apostles with Christ; and, because they did not yet have revelation about the person, they appointed two more [qualified]{g:habilis} [men], namely Barsabas and Matthias, leaving to the divine judgement which of them he would by lot show to be elected in his sight, to whose decision faith must be given in elections.[^5] Hence thirty electors, following, said notable words expressive of the teaching often stated: "Thou, Lord," they say, "who knowest the hearts of men, shew which one of these two thou hast chosen to take the place of this ministry"; where it is clear that God must elect, and that those electing according to other [rites]{g:ritus} must, very equivocally, faithfully promulgate God's election; therefore he says, "shew whom thou hast chosen", not to a dignity but to ministry.[^6] But thirdly it is objected: since our election ought to be modelled on and [authorized]{g:autorizare} by the apostles, from whom we draw other rites, it seems that we ought to follow them in our elections; for we cannot think of a more [authentic]{g:autenticus} [68] form of electing; and the objections would tell equally against the apostles.[^7] And again it seems that it is no more lawful in this than in those things that sound of advantage and honour to depart from the rite of the apostles, for so it would pertinently be charged against us that, deserting the faith of scripture, we faithlessly carry out the rites of the gentiles.

### 04-b ¶2 · 68.8–68.34  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 68] Quantum ad istam materiam diffuse tractat eam dominus Ardmacanus libro VII De questionibus Armenorum, recitans quatuor vias constituendi pontifices: una via est per sortem, secunda via per episcopi institucionem, tercia via per hereditariam successionem et quarta via per eleccionem: quod variatur secundum diversitatem variam electorum. Nec movet triplex obiectus istius materie. Primo quod sic temptaretur Deus, si non habentibus ad hec revelacionem liceret sortes mittere, quia ista argumenta procederent contra factum apostolorum et contra sortem veteris testamenti. Nec est Dei temptacio, ubi devotus Dei orator ignorat quid sit Dei voluntati conformius; qualis ignorancia communiter est in electoribus. Secundus obiectus est quod apostoli elegerunt duos scilicet Barsabam et Mathiam, ideo nos debemus operari conformiter. Quantum ad illud non contendo si ordinare multitudinem ut Deus de illa digniorem eligat vocetur eleccio, **sed** scriptura sic loquitur: Statuerunt duos etc. **Sed** rogo sic arguentem ut consenciat quod utrobique fiat conformiter nisi subsit racio diversitatis; statuant igitur de humilioribus, quantum adest illis certitudo, et de incerto residuo mittant sortem.

ENGLISH: As to this matter, the [lord of Armagh]{g:dominus-ardmacanus} treats it at length in book 7 of *De questionibus Armenorum*, reciting four ways of constituting pontiffs: one way is by lot, the second way by a bishop's institution, the third way by hereditary succession, and the fourth way by election, which varies according to the varied diversity of the electors.[^8] Nor does the threefold objection on this matter move [me]. First, that God would be tempted in this way if it were lawful for those who have no revelation for these things to cast lots; for these arguments would proceed against the deed of the apostles and against the lot of the old testament. Nor is it a tempting of God where a devout [suppliant]{g:orator} of God [is ignorant of]{g:ignorare} what is more conformed to God's will; and such ignorance is commonly in electors.[^9] The second objection is that the apostles chose two, namely Barsabas and Matthias; therefore we ought to act in conformity. As to that, I do not dispute whether ordering a multitude, so that God may choose the worthier from it, be called election; but scripture speaks thus: "They appointed two", etc. But I ask the one who argues thus to agree that it be done in conformity in both cases unless there is a ground for difference; let them therefore appoint from the humbler, so far as they have certainty, and on the uncertain remainder let them cast lots.[^10]

### 04-b ¶4 · 69.12–69.29  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 69] Secunda via est probabilis subducto errore instituentis, cum Petrus sic instituit Marcum et Clementem Antiochie et Rome, Paulus **autem** Thimoteum et Tytum. Ceteri vero apostoli ordinarunt episcopos super populos quos evangelizando converterunt, modo **autem** superadduntur sicut in ordinibus clericorum, de eleccionibus, de presentacionibus, de institucionibus; de induccionibus et confirmacionibus, que pharisei tantum ponderant ut sacramenta ecclesie; et certum est quod totum est tradicio gentilis superflua nisi forte sit medium ducens ad exequendum divinum arbitrium, sic quod rite exequens opus talis officii sine tali cerimonia introducta, sicut dicit Crisostomus, est tam autentice a Deo electus, sicut aliquis cum quotlibet tradicionibus phariseicis quantumlibet sollempniter celebratis.

ENGLISH: The second way is probable, error on the part of the one instituting being excluded, since Peter thus instituted Mark and Clement at Antioch and Rome, and Paul Timothy and Titus.[^12] And the other apostles ordained bishops over the peoples whom they converted by evangelizing; but now [ceremonies] are added, as in the orders of clerics: of elections, of presentations, of institutions, of inductions and confirmations, which the pharisees weigh as much as sacraments of the church; and it is certain that the whole is a superfluous gentile tradition, unless perhaps it is a means leading to the carrying out of the divine decision; so that one who duly carries out the work of such an office without such a ceremony introduced, as Chrysostom says, is as authentically elected by God as anyone with however many pharisaic traditions, however solemnly celebrated.[^13]

### 04-c ¶1 · 69.30–71.27  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 69] Et quantum ad omnes leges que videntur sonare contrarium, omnes intelliguntur de legibus humanis in quantum conferunt ut ordinetur conformiter ad divinum iudicium et de inobedientibus ecclesie triumphanti; ideo ne sit contencio de [p. 70] istis, ordinavit Spiritus Sanctus quod teneremus nos in limitibus legis sue. Unde istam materiam de eleccionibus tangit beatus Crisostomus super I. Thim. V: Dignitas, inquit, magisterii et sacerdocii magna est et mirabilis et vere sentencia Dei indigens ut eum qui dignus est in medium deducat. Nec audivi alios tam insanos, quod dissentirent isti sentencie. Unde quantum ad eleccionem sic loquitur iste sanctus: Ita antiquitus fiebat, ita et modo fit, cum sine passione humana elecciones facimus, cum ad nil vite huius respicimus, non ad amiciciam, non ad odium; et si in discrecione non tanto spiritu participamus, sufficit voluntas bona in Deo ponere intronizacionem, quia neque apostoli spiritu participabant, cum Mathiam elegerant, **sed** oracioni rem committentes in cathalogo apostolorum Mathiam annumeraverunt neque ad dileccionem humanam aspiciebant. Et ita in omni eleccione fieri oporteret, ubi electores sunt ambigui quis est habilior apud Deum. Videtur michi quod debent instar apostolorum sortes mittere; devote committendo sibi iudicium, sicut dicit iste sanctus, quia nemo diffiniret contra divinam sentenciam nec in materia ambigua, in qua non habuerit racionem naturalem, testimonium scripture vel revelacionem. Nec est factum apostolorum, a Deo tam crebro in lege veteri exemplatum; magis cerimoniale vel reprobandum, quam leges gentiles in ecclesia minori auctoritate vel evidencia introducte. Ideo secundum [p. 71] Crysostomum cum ista via sit secura et magis utilis propter multa et illam despicimus, qualiter Deus abscondita revelabit? Est enim intencio huius sancti quod apostoli in eligendo episcopos habuerunt revelacionem vel propheciam. Que, inquit, opera secundum nil humanum fiebant, a prophecia et spiritu sancto fiebant. Prophecia enim non est solum dicere futura **sed** presencia, cum Saul secundum propheciam monstratus est in vasis occultatus; Deus enim iustis revelat et prophecia fuit hec dicere: Separate, inquit, michi Paulum et Barnabam; ita et Thimoteus electus est a Paulo, qui multas prophecias habuit, ut quando circumcidit Thimoteum et intronizavit. Et ideo dicit, quod Deus eum elegit, non humanum iudicium, quod intronizatus recoleret custodiendo Dei depositum. Quando igitur electores spiritu ducuntur, alieni a vento calliditatis humane, tunc est simplex institucio vel multorum eleccio legitima; et aliter est infecta. Ideo debent electores postposito omni humano iudicio committere se divino regimini, sicut geromantici in punctuando suspendunt fantasiam propriam et committunt impulsum ad motum influencie celestis corporis. Et patet quod quilibet istorum trium modorum eligendi est laudibilis, quando fit conformiter ad eleccionem divinam.

ENGLISH: And as to all the laws that seem to sound the contrary, they are all understood of human laws in so far as they contribute to [an election's] being ordered in conformity with the divine judgement, and of those disobedient to the [church triumphant]{g:ecclesia-triumphans}; therefore, lest there be contention about [70] these, the Holy Spirit ordained that we should keep ourselves within the [limits]{g:limites} of his law.[^1] Hence blessed Chrysostom touches this matter of elections on 1 Timothy, [homily] 5: "The dignity," he says, "of the [teaching office]{g:magisterium} and of the priesthood is great and wonderful, and truly in need of God's [sentence]{g:sentencia}, that it may bring forth into the midst the one who is worthy."[^2] Nor have I heard of others so insane as to dissent from this teaching. Hence, as to election, this saint speaks thus: "So it was done of old, and so it is done now, when we make elections without human passion, when we look to nothing of this life, not to friendship, not to hatred; and if in discernment we do not share in so great a spirit, a good will suffices to place the enthronement in God; for neither did the apostles share in the spirit when they elected Matthias, but, committing the matter to prayer, they numbered Matthias in the catalogue of the apostles, nor did they look to human affection. And so it ought to be done in every election where the electors are in doubt who is more [qualified]{g:habilis} before God."[^3] It seems to me that they ought, after the pattern of the apostles, to cast lots, devoutly committing the judgement to him, as this saint says; for no one should define against the divine sentence, nor in a doubtful matter in which he has neither natural reason, nor the testimony of scripture, nor revelation. Nor is the deed of the apostles, so often exemplified by God in the old law, more ceremonial or more to be rejected than the gentile laws introduced into the [church]{g:ecclesia} with less authority or [evidence]{g:evidencia}.[^4] Therefore, according to [71] Chrysostom, "since this way is secure and more useful for many reasons, and we despise it, how will God reveal hidden things?"[^5] For this saint's meaning is that the apostles, in electing bishops, had revelation or prophecy. "Which works," he says, "were done according to nothing human; they were done by prophecy and the Holy Spirit. For prophecy is not only to tell things to come but things present, as when Saul, according to prophecy, was shown hidden among the vessels; for God reveals to the just; and it was prophecy to say these things: 'Separate me,' he says, 'Paul and Barnabas'; so also Timothy was elected by Paul, who had many prophecies, as when he circumcised Timothy and enthroned [him]."[^6] And therefore he says that "God elected him, not human judgement, so that, enthroned, he might call it to mind by guarding God's deposit".[^7] When, therefore, electors are led by the spirit, strangers to the wind of human craft, then a simple institution or an election by many is lawful; and otherwise it is infected. Therefore electors ought, setting aside all human judgement, to commit themselves to divine governance, as geomancers in pricking out their points suspend their own fancy and commit the impulse to the motion of the influence of a heavenly body.[^8] And it is clear that each of these three modes of electing is praiseworthy when it is done in conformity with the divine election.

### 04-c ¶2 · 71.28–72.7  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 71] Patet insuper quod eleccio adinventa humanitus est artificiosior **sed** callidior et postquam avari plus sunt terrenis dediti quam merito et labori officii est periculosior. Eleccio vero per sortem est securior, magis autentica et per consequens [p. 72] nunc eligibilior. **Sed** cupiditas et fastus obviant; dicitur enim quod nostri electores discernunt melius sicut apostoli; nec tales blasfemias credo cessare antequam status prelatorum sint tantum extenuati a commodo temporali quod officium capiatur propter meritum et non honorem vel commodum temporale.

ENGLISH: It is clear moreover that election devised humanly is more artful but craftier, and, since the avaricious are given more to earthly things than to the merit and labour of the office, it is more dangerous. But election by lot is more secure, more [authentic]{g:autenticus}, and consequently [72] now more to be chosen.[^9] But cupidity and pride stand in the way; for it is said that our electors discern better, as the apostles [did]; nor do I believe that such blasphemies will cease before the estates of [prelates]{g:prelatus} are so far thinned of temporal advantage that the office is taken for merit and not for honour or temporal advantage.[^10]

### 04-d ¶1 · 72.16–74.11  (8 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 72] **Sed** triplex ponitur causa quare tempore legis gracie sacerdocium non sic vadit iure hereditario. Prima quia sacerdotes nostri et specialiter summi pontifices debent caste vivere instar Christi et per consequens non relinquere sue carnali soboli ius ad quicquam. Secunda causa est quia Christus noluit eius vicarios habere seculare dominium vel honorem **sed** omnino specie hereditatis celestis, que solum datur patrissantibus in moribus spiritualibus filiis adoptivis. Tercia causa est ut nos tempore legis gracie spiritualius procedentes, discamus eligere meliores et amare duplicem virginitatem, et quod spiritualis prepositura vel dignitas sacerdotis non sit carnalis **sed** spiritualis ex filiacione in moribus; cum hereditas illa sit cuicumque promerenti indifferens. Necesse est igitur quod summus sacerdos noster sit in se totus collectus circa edificacionem ecclesie et non [p. 73] divisus ad placendum uxori sue; ut loquitur Apostolus I. Cor. VII. Et hec racio quare oportuit legem illam cessare et novam introduci, ut dicitur Heb. VII. **Sed** illud calliditate dyaboli est frustratum, cum episcopi nostri gravius fornicantur cum seculo. **Sed** quarto obicitur per hoc quod iuxta dictam sentenciam contingit duos episcopos super eodem populo legittime ordinari; quod concedo; cum Christus animarum necessario est episcopus cuiuslibet populi christiani et habet multos episcopos vicarios super eodem populo adiutores. Ipse tamen excellenter et differenter est episcopus ecclesie triumphantis et tocius ecclesie militantis. Similiter, eedem persone, quas Petrus, Paulus et ceteri apostoli ordinarunt episcopos, non excluserunt eos a superintendencia populi **sed** iuverunt, ut beatus Clemens quem beatus Petrus ordinavit prudenter ut oportet dicere episcopum Romanorum fuit episcopatui Petri nedum conpossibilis, **sed** adiutor, nam in quadam epistola beati Clementis directa Jacobo Jerusalimitano sic scribitur: Beatus Petrus morte sibi appropinquante in collegio fratrum positus ipsum Clementem per manum accipiens dixit, hunc Clementem episcopum vobis ordino, qui ab inicio mecum fuit. Nec dubium quin tanti capitis ordinacio quam oportuit ex Spiritu Sancto procedere non instituisset Clementem episcopum, nisi de facto foret episcopus; ymo videtur quod sicut actus episcopales non repugnant tam simul quam paulative super eodem populo, sic nec [p. 74] pontificum dignitates, sicut videtur de Christo et Petro, cum simul cum eo fuit summus pontifex. Sic enim beatus Johannes fuit episcopus continue ecclesie Asyane et tamen habuit septem episcopos adiutores. Actus ergo spirituales non repugnant et per consequens potestates vel habitus respectu eiusdem populi. **Sed** tota repugnancia videtur civilitas et dominium, quod haberent in populo. Hec enim fuit racio quare Abraham et Loth scilicet propter proprietates possessionum non potuerunt habitare simul, ut Gen. XIII.

ENGLISH: But a threefold cause is assigned why, in the time of [the law of grace]{g:lex-gracie}, the priesthood does not pass in this way by hereditary right. The first, because our [priests]{g:sacerdos}, and especially the [highest pontiffs]{g:summi-pontifices}, ought to live chastely after the pattern of Christ, and consequently not to leave to their carnal offspring a right to anything. The second cause is that Christ did not will his vicars to have secular [lordship]{g:dominium} or honour, but [to have] wholly the likeness of the heavenly inheritance, which is given only to spiritual adoptive sons who take after their father in conduct.[^1] The third cause is that we, proceeding more spiritually in the time of the law of grace, may learn to choose the better men and to love the twofold virginity, and [learn] that the spiritual [office of superior]{g:prepositura} or dignity of a priest is not carnal but spiritual, from sonship in conduct; since that inheritance lies open to whoever merits it, without distinction. It is necessary, therefore, that our high priest be wholly gathered in himself about the edification of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, and not [73] divided in order to please his wife, as the Apostle says, 1 Cor. 7. And this is the reason why that law had to cease and a new one be brought in, as is said in Heb. 7.[^2] But that has been frustrated by the cunning of the devil, since our bishops commit fornication more grievously with the world. But it is objected fourthly, [by appeal to]{g:obicitur-per} this: that according to the said [teaching]{g:sentencia} it comes about that two bishops are lawfully ordained over the same people. This I grant; since Christ is necessarily bishop of souls of every Christian people,[^3] and has many vicar bishops as helpers over the same people. Yet he is bishop, excellently and in a different way, of [the church triumphant]{g:ecclesia-triumphans} and of the whole church militant. Likewise, the same persons whom Peter, Paul and the other apostles ordained bishops did not exclude them from the oversight of the people, but helped them;[^4] as blessed Clement, whom blessed Peter ordained (prudently, as one must say) bishop of the Romans, was not only [compossible]{g:compossibilis} with Peter's episcopate but a helper to it. For in a certain letter of blessed Clement addressed to James of Jerusalem it is written thus: "Blessed Peter, when death was drawing near to him, placed in the company of the brethren, took Clement himself by the hand and said, This Clement I ordain bishop for you, who was with me from the beginning."[^5] Nor is there doubt that the ordination of so great a [head]{g:caput}, which had to proceed from the Holy Spirit, would not have instituted Clement bishop unless he were in fact a bishop; indeed, it seems that just as episcopal acts are not incompatible over the same people, whether at once or by turns, so neither are [74] the dignities of pontiffs, as appears of Christ and Peter, since he was highest pontiff together with him.[^6] For thus blessed John was continuously bishop of the church of Asia, and yet had seven bishops as helpers. Spiritual acts therefore are not incompatible, and consequently neither are [powers]{g:potestas} or habits with respect to the same people. But the whole incompatibility seems to be the [civil standing]{g:civilitas} and lordship which they would have among the people. For this was the reason why Abraham and Lot, namely because of the property of their possessions, could not dwell together, as Gen. 13.[^7]

### 04-d ¶2 · 74.12–76.11  (8 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 74] Tercio confirmatur de rectoribus et suis episcopis, qui sunt simul episcopi eiusdem parrochie, et Deus scit quis quoad Deum est suo adiutore superior, quia certum est quod ille qui magis spiritualiter prodest parrochie est episcopus superior quoad Deum; et certum est quod profectus suus est extensior. Nec valet dicere quod sacerdos talis non est episcopus, quia iuxta decretum Jeronimi positum 95 distinccione Olim; Olim, inquit, erat idem presbiter, qui et episcopus, et antequam dyaboli instinctu studia in religione fierent et diceretur in populis, ego sum Pauli, ego Apollo, ego sum Cephe, communi presbiterorum consilio ecclesie gubernabantur. Postquam **autem** unusquisque eos quos baptizaverat suos esse putabat, non Christi, decretum est ut unus de presbiteris superponeretur et scismatum semina tollerentur. Nec est fingendum quod propter adinventas preposituras secundum dignitates reputacionis humane fit novus ordo supra sacerdotum, **sed** manent septem ordines antiqui humanitus alterati. Non enim princeps secularis per viam dotacionis et maioritatis reputacionis mundane [p. 75] instituit novum ecclesiasticum sacramentum. Multi igitur sunt episcopi super eodem populo se iuvantes, quia aliter non liceret habere episcopum suffraganeum, cum ille sit episcopus non Nullatensis, **sed** illius loci quem incolit et illius populi, cui superintendit. Et idem est iudicium de Petro et Paulo qui simul erant Romani episcopi. Paulus enim erat episcopus sicut Scarioth et omnes apostoli, **sed** cuius populi si non gentium quas tantum edificando superintendit predicacionibus, miraculorum faccionibus et scriptura, et hec consumavit excellenter et finaliter cum Romanis. Ideo non beatus Petrus **sed** ipse fuit precipue episcopus Romanorum. Sic igitur non repugnat **sed** consonat multos angelos deputari ad unius custodiam, **sed** fides scripture testatur Deum, Paulum et ceteros apostolos habere in eodem officio spirituali plurimos adiutores; sic est de spirituali episcopatus officio senciendum; **sed** oportet illum excellere qui spiritualiter magis servit. Nec est credendum institucionibus civilibus vel ficticiis de repugnancia iurisdicionum, nisi de quanto fundata fuerint in scriptura, quomodo preeminencia Petri super ceteros apostolos stat in universali iurisdiccione super totam militantem ecclesiam, aut quod certa potestas iurisdicionalis debet esse in alios apostolos, per illam potestatem ad locum et populum limitata. Nam ceteri apostoli edificarunt ecclesiam in regionibus variis, non petita licencia a Petro, Paulus vero universalius quam Petrus convertit gentes, eciam antequam vidit Petrum, ut patet Actuum IX et infra, Galat. [p. 76] primo et infra; Joh. in Asya minori diu post Petri martirium. Nec oportuit vel decuit illum, qui tam crebras revelaciones Spiritus Sancti habuit; quomodo agendum fuerit, querere instruccionem a suo conservo et consocio Petro vel eius vicario qui instruccione eiusdem magistri indiguit. Et idem est iudicium de sancto Thoma, quando Christus in foro venalium precepit sibi, quod transiret in Yndiam, et sic de ceteris apostolis, ut patet ex fideli eorum cronica quam legit et sollempnizat ecclesia.

ENGLISH: Thirdly, it is confirmed from rectors and their bishops, who are at once bishops of the same parish; and God knows which of them, as regards God, is superior to his helper, for it is certain that he who profits the parish more spiritually is the superior bishop as regards God; and it is certain that his profit reaches further. Nor does it avail to say that such a priest is not a bishop, for according to Jerome's decree placed in the 95th distinction, *Olim*: "Formerly," he says, "the [presbyter]{g:presbiter} was the same as the bishop; and before, by the prompting of the devil, factions arose in religion and it was said among the peoples, I am of Paul, I of Apollo, I am of Cephas, the churches were governed by the common counsel of the presbyters. But after each one thought that those whom he had baptized were his own and not Christ's, it was decreed that one of the presbyters should be set over [the rest], and the seeds of schisms be removed."[^8] Nor is it to be feigned that, because of offices of superior devised according to the dignities of human reputation, a new order is made above that of priests; rather the seven ancient orders remain, altered in human fashion. For a secular prince does not, by way of [endowment]{g:dotacio} and the [greatness]{g:majoritas} of worldly reputation, [75] institute a new ecclesiastical sacrament. There are, therefore, many bishops over the same people helping one another, since otherwise it would not be lawful to have a suffragan bishop; for he is a bishop not ["of Nowhere"]{g:episcopus-nullatensis}, but of that place which he inhabits and of that people over which he has oversight.[^9] And the same judgement holds of Peter and Paul, who were at the same time bishops of Rome. For Paul was a bishop, as Iscariot was and all the apostles; but of what people, if not of the gentiles, over whom he had oversight, building them up so greatly by preachings, the working of miracles and scripture? And these he brought to completion, excellently and finally, with the Romans. Therefore not blessed Peter but he was chiefly bishop of the Romans.[^10] Thus, then, it is not incompatible but consonant that many angels be deputed to the guard of one [person]; and the faith of scripture testifies that God, Paul and the other apostles have very many helpers in the same spiritual office; so one must think of the spiritual office of the episcopate; but he must excel who serves more spiritually.[^11] Nor is credence to be given to civil or feigned institutions concerning the incompatibility of [jurisdictions]{g:iurisdiccio}, except in so far as they are founded in scripture: [such as] how the preeminence of Peter over the other apostles consists in a universal jurisdiction over the whole church militant, or that a definite jurisdictional power must extend to the other apostles, [limited]{g:limitare} to place and people by that power.[^12] For the other apostles built up the church in various regions without asking [licence]{g:licencia} from Peter; and Paul converted the gentiles more universally than Peter, even before he saw Peter, as is clear from Acts 9 and following, Gal. [76] 1 and following; [and] John in Asia Minor long after Peter's martyrdom. Nor was it needful or fitting for him who had such frequent revelations of the Holy Spirit as to how he should act to seek instruction from his fellow servant and companion Peter, or from his vicar, who himself needed the instruction of the same master. And the same judgement holds of Saint Thomas, when Christ in the market of the sellers commanded him to go over into India; and so of the other apostles, as is clear from their faithful chronicle, which the church reads and keeps solemnly.[^13]

### 04-e ¶1 · 76.12–78.3  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 76] Unde Januensis in suo Katholicon narrat ex quadam cronica conformiter ad cantum ecclesie, quomodo apostoli per orbem post ascensionem Domini sunt dispersi: Petrus Romam venit, Andreas Achaiam et uterque ibi crucifigitur, Johannes Ephesum vivus intrat sepulcrum, Jacobus maior Jerusolimis decollatur, Thomas in Yndia lancea transfigitur, Philippus in Frigia crucifigitur, Jacobus minor partica fullonis Jerusolimis occiditur, Bartolomeus in maiori Yndia excoriatur et decollatur, Matheus in Ethiopia in altari lancea perforatur, Symon et Yudas in Persyde a pontificibus ydolorum tumultu populi interimuntur, Mathias in Yndia pro Christo patitur, Lucas in Bythinia, obiit, Marcus Alexandrie martirium complevit, Barnabas in Salamia occisus est. Stultum **autem** et infidele mendacium foret fingere quod omnes alii apostoli habuerunt recursum ad Petrum vel eius vicarium; et ex eorum precepto ac auctoritate fecerunt quicquid fecerant, primo quia habuerunt magistrum omnium eis continue assistentem; iuxta [p. 77] promissum suum Math, ultimo: Ecce ego vobiscum sum, omnibus diebus usque ad consumacionem seculi. Quomodo igitur foret necessarium tam ambigue querere auctoritatem sive consilium, quando tanta copia fontis sapiencie continue fuit presens. Secundo quia Paulus testatur Gal. I, 17 quod post conversionem suam non venit statim Jerusolimam ad apostolos **sed** continuo predicavit in Arabya. Deinde post annos tres venit Jerusalem videre Petrum et mansit cum eo diebus quindecim, quia (ut dicit in principio) non fuit factus apostolus ab hominibus neque per hominem, eleccione vel instrumentali institucione, **sed** per Ihesum Christum secundum deitatem, per cuius revelacionem evangelium suum didicerat, deinde (ut narrat cap. II, I, 2 post annos quatuordecim iterum ascendit per revelacionem in Jerusalem cum Barnaba et Tito contulitque cum precipuis apostolis. Michi, inquit, qui videbantur esse aliquid nichil contulerunt, **sed** e contra cum vidissent, quod creditum est michi evangelium prepucii, sicut Petro circumcisionis, cum cognovissent graciam Dei, que data est michi, Jacobus et Cefas et Johannes, qui videbantur esse columpne, dextras dederunt michi et Barnabe societatis. Cum, inquit, venisset Cephas Antiochiam, in faciem ei restiti, quia reprehensibilis erat, Numquid credimus vas eleccionis et scribam Spiritus Sancti qui vidit quanta mala futura erant in ecclesia pro primatu usus fuit falsitate vel levitate in verbis fidei? quin pocius ad destruendum hereses quas sciverat postmodum pullulare premittens Jacobum dicit quod omnes illi tres [p. 78] nichil sibi contulerant? Cuius sensus repugnaret isti quod Paulus necessitatus fuit accipere autoritativam potestatem a Petro.

ENGLISH: Hence Januensis in his *Catholicon* relates from a certain chronicle, in agreement with the chant of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, how the apostles were dispersed through the world after the ascension of the Lord: "Peter came to Rome, Andrew to Achaia, and both are crucified there; John at Ephesus enters his tomb alive; James the greater is beheaded at Jerusalem; Thomas is pierced with a lance in India; Philip is crucified in Phrygia; James the less is killed at Jerusalem with a fuller's pole; Bartholomew is flayed and beheaded in greater India; Matthew is run through with a lance at the altar in Ethiopia; Simon and Jude are slain in Persia by the priests of the idols in a tumult of the people; Matthias suffers for Christ in India; Luke died in Bithynia; Mark completed his martyrdom at Alexandria; Barnabas was killed at Salamis."[^1] And it would be a foolish and faithless lie to feign that all the other apostles had recourse to Peter or his vicar, and did by his precept and authority whatever they did: first, because they had the master of all continually standing by them, according to [77] his promise, Matt. last: "Behold I am with you all days, even to the consummation of the world." How then would it be necessary to seek authority or counsel so uncertainly, when so great a [plenty]{g:copia} of the fount of wisdom was continually present? Secondly, because Paul testifies, Gal. 1:17, that after his conversion he did not come straightway to Jerusalem to the apostles, but at once preached in Arabia. Then after three years he came to Jerusalem to see Peter, and stayed with him fifteen days,[^2] because (as he says at the beginning) he was not made an apostle "of men, neither by man", by election or instrumental institution, "but by Jesus Christ" according to the [Godhead]{g:deitas}, by whose revelation he had learned his gospel. Then (as he relates in ch. 2:1–2) after fourteen years he went up again by revelation to Jerusalem with Barnabas and Titus, and conferred with the leading apostles. "To me," he says, "they who seemed to be some thing added nothing. But contrariwise, when they had seen that to me was committed the gospel of the uncircumcision, as to Peter [that] of the circumcision, when they had known the grace of God that was given to me, James and Cephas and John, who seemed to be pillars, gave to me and Barnabas the right hands of fellowship."[^3] "When," he says, "Cephas was come to Antioch, I withstood him to the face, because he was to be blamed." Do we believe that the vessel of election and scribe of the Holy Spirit, who saw how great evils were to come in the church for the sake of [primacy]{g:primatus}, made use of falsehood or levity in words of faith? Rather, to destroy the heresies which he knew would afterwards spring up, does he not, putting James first, say that all those three [78] had conferred nothing on him?[^4] The sense of which would be incompatible with this: that Paul was compelled to receive authoritative [power]{g:potestas} from Peter.

### 04-e ¶2 · 78.4–80.22  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 78] Et ad idem vadit secundum verbum quod illi apostoli gaudebant quod habuerunt tales socios in dextero brachio sancte ecclesie, ista (inquam) apostolorum societas excluderet eosdem esse subditos a paritate potestatis non auctoritative vel iurisdicionalis super consocios. Et idem patet per hoc quod Paulus reprobavit Petrum in sua parrochia; ideo absit fidelem credere hoc de Petro, cum ipse apostolorum humillimus fecit cuncta sua opera post ascensionem auctoritate et nomine Ihesu nichil auctoritati sue attribuens, quia aliter tota scriptura illud obmittens foret nimium defectiva. Tercio confirmatur de iurisdiccione data comuniter cunctis apostolis Matth. ultimo tam quoad locum quam populum. Data est, inquit, michi potestas in celo et in terra, euntes igitur docete omnes gentes servare omnia quecunque mandavi vobis. Et Mar, ultimo limitatur iurisdicionis locus: Euntes, inquit, in mundum universum, predicate evangelium omni creature; ubi patet primo quod alloquitur omnes apostolos pro se et suis vicariis. Apostoli enim iverunt in Asyam, Africam et Europam: et per omnem terram habitabilem exivit virtus sermonis eorum, quia (ut patet ex sepedictis) non est aliqua pars terre vel mundi, quin spiritualiter aleretur ex predicacione evangelii per potestatem datam Christo. Et sic docuerunt mediate vel immediate omnia genera gentium: Grecos, Latinos et barbaros. [p. 79] Et patet secundo quomodo Christus tempore quo apostoli magis recolerent; dedit eis preponderantem sentenciam; quomodo fideliter docerent populum ex integro servare mandata et consilia sua que ad hoc eos docuit. Omnis enim gens debet servare ipsa in una parte vel alia. Quidam tamen apostolorum peragrarunt plures regiones et quidam pauciores, ut Paulus; qui plus omnibus laboravit, visitavit corporaliter et convertit plures provincias. Et breviter cuilibet apostolo vel eius vicario fuit nedum licitum, **sed** debitum tantum de populo vel terra convertere vel confirmare in fide Christi quantum sufficeret, et non fuit restriccio iurisdicionis nisi ex insufficiencia. Non enim dicit singulariter Petro: vade tu et singulariter predica toti mundo limitaque ceteros apostolos quantum sub iurisdicione sua de terra vel populo subiacebit, quin pocius Spiritus Sanctus dixit Paulo quod predicaret lacius inter gentes; Petro vero quod striccius iret in Judeos, uterque tamen sicut omnes apostoli et eorum vicarii sunt debitores omni homini, ut sibi prosint meliori modo quo poterint. Absit quin rector vel episcopus debeat proficere populo de alia parrochia sive dyocesi, et in casu quo crederet plus prodesse ecclesie et placere Christo, debet parochiam suam dimittere, et aliis in quibus magis proficeret se gratis adiungere. **Sed** heu ista religiosa regula primeva temporalium cupiditate disrumpitur; et nostri satrape vertunt evangelizacionem in pecunie colleccionem et propter questum circa limites iurisdicionis contenditur, cuius civilitatis origo dicitur cepisse a capite Romane ecclesie. Dicitur enim in nobis [p. 80] occiduis, quod Romanus pontifex vendicat infideliter quod nemo salvabitur nisi subiaceat taxis suis; **sed** nichil plus horribile vel blasfemum; cum sine personarum accepcione, quecunque persona vel populus de Yndia, Ethiopia, Grecia vel quantumcunque remota insula crediderit in dominum Ihesum Christum salvabitur, per quemcunque apostolum vel Christi discipulum conversa fuerit nisi finaliter ad infidelitatem redierit; ideo omnes tales fideles, ubicunque terrarum fuerint, sunt imitatores Petri, Pauli, et cuiuscunque apostoli, ita quod sufficiat diligere caput Christum, cum diligendo ipsum universitas diligitur ordinate. Secunda stulticia est; qua plus credimus portentis quomodocumque factis, ut imponimus virtuti corporum mortuorum, quam signis vivacibus sanctorum evangelizancium, peccatores plurimos convertendo, in tantum quod si portentum tale sancto nostro ascribimus, cece arguimus quod quicquid fecerat non pecavit. **Sed** tercia stulticia est qua arguimus, si illi meritorie susceperunt elemosinas donatas ecclesie sub condicione, quam oportet intelligi, quod tunc nec licet ipsas auferre nostris, nec phas est negare, quin licet nobis ipsas expendere quomodolibet consumendo. Nec est color, si cerimonia sit in laycis meritoria, quod foret in clericis magis sancta. Sicut patet de homicidiis, de connubiis et multiplici meritorio ritu in laycis, qui foret in clericis venenosus.

LATIN: [p. 89] Notandum tamen quod temporalia non occasione ab eis data **sed** male accepta venenant ecclesiam. Si enim totus clerus diceret effectualiter quod omnia temporalia que habemus ut clerici forent pure elemosine secularium et bona communia pauperum, secundo effectualiter et indifferenter ministraremus de illis magis egentibus et tercio consumeremus precise de illis quantum est necessarium ad nostrum clericale officium, possemus proprietate usus et ministerii occupare licite omnia temporalia que habemus. Et sic occuparunt omnes sancti episcopi ex mundi diviciis commendandi. Quod si blasfemamus quod nude materia [p. 90] successionis Christi et apostolorum qui dominati sunt seculariter omnibus mundi diviciis habemus hec omnia, et secundo consumimus illa differenter seculariter et proprietarie, et tercio dimittimus vel remittimus exinde debitum officium clericale, manifestum est quod irreligiose, iniuste et proditorie consumimus bona Dei; et hoc totum inportaret in clerico monstruosa civilitas. **Sed** dubitatur supposito usu legitimo, quis modus vivendi foret perfeccior; nec dubium quin modus vivendi parcior et exproprietarius, quia est securior et vite Christi ac innocencie utrique conformior. Vix enim reperies mundi divitem quin vel nimis afficiatur et occupetur circa eius divicias vel inique ipsas distribuat vel tercio voluptuose atque superflue ipsas consumat, in tantum quod de divite evadente ista pericula scribitur Eccli. XXXI, 9: Quis est hic et laudabimus eum? fecit enim mirabilia in vita sua. Et hec fuit racio quare sapiencia Dei patris ob amorem nostri sic vixit exproprietarie et docuit suos cariores sic vivere. Ex quo invincibiliter colligitur quod status cuiuscumque cleri vel episcopi foret subducta appropriacione dotantis perfeccior, licet aliqui sancti in hoc evadant periculum, quia indubie predicta triplex occupacio temporalium foret ad onus cuiuscumque clerici qui intenderet perficere officium clericale; nam multitudo familie et mundani honoris nimis distraherent. Distribucio vero corporalis elemosine pertinet ad ministerium laycale, clerico intento ad distribuendum spirituales elemosinas pociores. Et sic ex dotacione sparguntur in clero macule, que ultra varietatem, quam Christus instituit, non pulcrificant matrem [p. 91] nostram. Et si dicatur quod alicui clerico foret melius sic dotari, revera hoc est valde remotum ad inferendum quod status dotati cleri sit statu exproprietarie viventis perfeccior. Sic enim expediret alicui clerico quod foret laycus, ymo ut dicit Christus Math. XVIII: Expedit scandalizanti ut suspendatur mola asinaria circa collum eius et demergatur in profundum maris. Hoc est secundum beatum Gregorium in De Cura Pastorali II expediret prelato prescito quod ob errorem laycalis officii sit dampnatus. Non igitur sequitur: Utilius foret Petro esse in statu tali; igitur status ille est alio statu perfeccior. Nam iuxta istam vesaniam quilibet gradus turpis status foret statu honesto perfeccior, quia status perfeccior, quem statuatus deturpat, facit per accidens quod deturpatori peius eveniat, sicut inferioritas status potest cum gracia Dei esse causa per accidens unde homo amplius mereatur: ymo necesse est ut status dyaboli ac membrorum suorum prosit per accidens ecclesie, sicut status peccandi Petri et aliorum sanctorum secundum Augustinum fuit multum expediens. Numquid propterea servabimus talem statum? Notato igitur illo clerico, quem decorat imperatoris dotacio, patet quod ipse posset eque perficere opus predicandi, orandi et contemplandi sine tanta copia terrenorum; cum omne quod per se superaddit sonat in mundi gloriam et retardat ab officio clericali, quomodo igitur non foret status signancius ex dotacione imperfeccio? Et idem est argumentum de quocumque clerico signato; ex quo colligitur quod si status tocius cleri dotati foret alteratus [p. 92] in statum exproprietarium similis statui apostolorum vel rectorum in communi vivencium pure de oblacionibus, decimis et privatis elemosinis, uniformitas faceret ad decorem ecclesie, quia pulchrius pulchriori additum, facit totum aggregatum pulchrius quam totum minus pulcrum additum minus pulchro. Unde igitur auctoritas membris ecclesie sic strangulare contra exemplum vel autoritatem Domini clerum suum, cum in veteri testamento non licuit; arare in bove et asino nec indui vestimento ex lana et lino, ut patet Numeri XII et Deutronomii XXII. Si igitur levite et sacerdotes legis veteris et Christi discipuli vixerunt conformiter, quare non sic viveret clerus sequens? quam uniformitatem Christi vicarius debet incipere, ut coraula; Christus enim fuit homo pauperrimus sic quod nemo potuit esse pauperior; ut hic supponitur, quomodo igitur non eius sequax in terris simillimus, cum paupertas illa sonat perfeccionem virtutis viancium. Sicut igitur oportuit Petrum in fide, in humilitate, in caritate evidencius sequi Christum ex dictis in fine tercii capituli, sic oportet Petri vicarium sequi ipsum. **Sed** que fides est regulam perfeccionis, quam Christus instituit, tam cito propter mundum deserere? Vel que humilitas est sine auctoritate Christi supra alios mortales, eciam supra elemosinantem seculariter se extollere? Nam libro IV dicit Bernhardus ad papam Eugenium, ut recitavi superius: In hiis, inquit, successisti Constantino non Petro. Et beatus Jeronimus, ut recitatum est XCII distinccione Olim, et XCV distinccione Absit, dicit quod tale sacramentum [p. 93] superioritas non est a Deo et tunc indubie est a dyabolo. Si igitur papa viveret recte exproprietarie cum ceteris paribus sicut Petrus, quomodo non omnes eius subditi conversarentur conformiter? Et istam doctrinam dat Petrus I Pet. IV precipiens quod seniores et prepositi non sint dominantes in clero **sed** forma gregis. Et illud verbum notaret Petri vicarius propter reverenciam dignitatis. Quantum ad caritatem non video quin repugnat perfectissime caritati viantis quod in Petri vicario sit tam dominativa et copiosa possessio, quia cum caritas non querit proprietatem huiusmodi, ut dicit apostolus I. Cum igitur iste gradus caritatis foret vicario Petri possibilis et ab illo deficit in magnum dampnum ecclesie, videtur quod propter dissimilitudinem non sit Petri vicarius et per consequens neque heres.

ENGLISH: It is to be noted, however, that [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} poison the [church]{g:ecclesia} not through an occasion given by them but through one wrongly taken.[^1] For if the whole [clergy]{g:clerus} said in effect that all the temporal goods we have as clerics were purely [alms]{g:elemosina} of seculars and the common goods of the poor; [if] secondly we ministered from them effectually and impartially to those in greater need; and [if] thirdly we consumed of them precisely as much as is necessary to our clerical office, we could lawfully occupy all the temporal goods we have with property of use and of ministry. And so all the holy bishops who are to be commended occupied [what they had] of the world's riches.[^2] But if we blaspheme [by saying] that we have all these things barely on the ground of [90] succession to Christ and the apostles, who [exercised lordship]{g:dominari} in a secular way over all the riches of the world, and secondly consume them with partiality, in a secular and proprietary way, and thirdly thereby abandon or [remit]{g:remittere} the clerical office that is due, it is manifest that we consume God's goods irreligiously, unjustly and treacherously; and monstrous [civil standing]{g:civilitas} in a cleric would bring all this with it.[^3] But it is doubted, lawful use being supposed, which manner of living would be more perfect; and there is no doubt [that it is] the sparer and [propertyless]{g:exproprietarii} manner of living, since it is safer and more conformed to both, the life of Christ and innocence.[^4] For you will scarcely find a rich man of the world who is not either too much attached to and occupied about his riches, or distributes them unjustly, or thirdly consumes them voluptuously and superfluously; so much so that of the rich man who escapes these dangers it is written, Ecclesiasticus 31:9: "Who is he, and we will praise him? for he hath done wonderful things in his life."
