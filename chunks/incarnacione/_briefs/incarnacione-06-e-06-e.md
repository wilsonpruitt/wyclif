## The volume

*Tractatus de benedicta incarnacione*, ed. Edward Harris (Wyclif Society, 1886), from the plates of
archive.org `latinworks03wycl` (**leaf = page + 39**; leaf images in `text/incarnacione/raw/leaves/`).
An **early** work: Harris (preface §II) puts it before 1367, in the first years of Wyclif's doctorate
(1363–67), with cross-references to *De universalibus*; the Oriel manuscript's medieval catalogue calls
it *super sententias*, and it sits in a volume of Sentences commentaries. It is the surviving text
closest to Wyclif's lost lectures on book III of the *Sentences*: **the theologian of the schools, not
yet the reformer**. No pope, no endowment, no friars: the whole treatise is the metaphysics of the
incarnation argued against the "modern doctors" (Ockham and the *moderni*), in the forms of the
disputation. A prologue and thirteen chapters; the prologue (pp. 1–2) describes each chapter in one
sentence, and Harris repeats that sentence at the chapter's head (below).

**Sigla** (Harris, preface §III): **A** Vienna 384 (the base of Beer's collation; *Prologus magistri
Johannis de Incarnacione* in red); **B** Vienna 404; **C** Vienna 387 (A, B, C one recension from a common
source, Beer); **O** Oriel College, Oxford, xv (c. 1400, English, two columns; Harris: "the best, as
probably the oldest", nearer C than A or B); **M** London, BL Royal 7 B. iii (15th c.; not a copy but
a **summary** made from a manuscript: the main course of the argument with the illustrations and
authorities left out; collated by F. D. Matthew). Cite variants with these sigla. Harris's text is
**eclectic**: "the best reading" chosen from all five, every rejected reading of A B C O at the foot
(the countless *ille/iste* swaps and trivial word-order variants excepted); the manuscripts' spelling
kept "even when manifestly incorrect" (*methaphisica*, *anthitesis*, *dyabolus*). The chapter breaks are
Wyclif's (the prologue); **the paragraphs are Harris's own** ("on my own responsibility, often a matter
of doubt": only chapters begin a new line in O).

**The page foot** (in your brief as "apparatus", by page) is keyed by **footnote figures**, which stand
in the Latin as superscripts on the word before them (`Prelibato¹ tractatu`): `1 Litera initialis (P) om.
A B C. 3 mesterium O.` From 01-b on it is **read from the plate** (`text/incarnacione/foot/`, 2026-10-22),
not OCR: trust its letters; a `[?]` or `CHECK:` line in it is a doubt to settle on the plate foot. The
figures are the editor's, not text: **never carry them into the English**; cite a variant by its page.line.
**Apparatus discipline** (first-chunk review, 2026-10-22: three of its four errors):
(i) **match a variant to its figure** in the Latin before citing it (a figure *n* belongs to the word
before ^n, not to a nearby word that resembles the variant); (ii) the foot's abbreviations are
**rejected readings, to be expanded** in the note (*ppcones* = *proposiciones*), never "beyond certain
reading"; (iii) where Harris prints **one manuscript (usually O, or M) against A B C** on a word the
argument turns on, write a **T** note giving both, even though the print is kept; (iv) a reading the
English follows that is not the print's is a departure: T note, always.

**Harris's margin and brackets.** Harris prints a running English abstract of the whole treatise down
the margin (≈21,500 words). It is **not reproduced** in this edition (Wilson, 2026-10-21): the headnote
says so once, and no chunk carries side-notes. At each chapter head he prints, in square brackets, the
prologue's Latin sentence for that chapter followed by his own English summary. The scaffold gives that
bracket as its own ¶ block (marked by a comment): **translate the Latin sentence only**, in square
brackets, worded as the prologue renders it, with **the chapter as subject ("it")**, not Wyclif ("he");
**drop Harris's English** (it is the editor's, like the margin). Its terms are tagged like any other
(a first occurrence in the bracket is the chunk's first occurrence). Write no note on the bracket.

   between briefs). Expect: *suppositum*, *ypostasis*, *res communis* (the common thing), *individuum*,

2. **Connectives carry the argument.** ⛔ Never omit one, never turn one into filler.

   | Latin | English | Never |
   |---|---|---|
   | enim, nam | for | "now", "indeed" |
   | ergo, igitur, ideo | therefore, so, then | "now", "and", "indeed" |
   | sed / autem, marking the **minor premiss or next step** | now | (omitted) |
   | sed / autem, adversative | but | (omitted) |
   | autem, continuative | and | (omitted), and **not "now"** |
   | cum (concessive / causal) | although / since | "whenever" |
   | tamen | yet, nevertheless | "therefore" |
   | item | again, also | "now" |
   | vero (continuative) | and; but (adversative) | "now", "truly" |
   | verumtamen | nevertheless | "but truly" |
   | unde | hence, so | "whence" (archaic), "where" |
   | per idem | by the same token | "in the same way" |
   | ymmo | indeed, rather (correcting upward) | (omitted) |
   | patet; constat | it is clear; it is evident | |

   "Now" is reserved for the minor premiss, or the step that brings a new premiss to bear. An *autem*
   that only extends the same step is "and". ⚠ The commonest error in *De ecclesia* (close-out, ~50 of
   69 connective fixes): a *sed* that brings the minor premiss of a syllogism closed by *ergo / ideo /
   igitur* rendered "but". Before writing "but" for a *sed*, look ahead for the *ergo*: if one follows,
   it is "now". This volume is syllogism on syllogism: the rule will be tested in every paragraph.
3. **Ordinary words with a medieval sense:** GLOSSARY §D; look up any word whose classical and
   Wyclifite senses may differ, and propose additions.
4. **Syntax:** construe before rendering (main verb, subject, what every relative and genitive
   hangs on). Keep quantifier order where it is meaning (*omnis natura*, *nulla creatura*). Keep
   Wyclif's long periods where English tolerates them. **A doubtful construal is never chosen
   silently:** translate the likelier reading and write a **C** note giving the alternative.
   ⚠ **Predication is the matter here, not just the form.** *Christus est creatura*, *humanitas est
   Christus*, *Verbum est homo*: Wyclif argues about what is predicated of what, with supposition and
   identity in play. Keep subject and predicate in his order, keep *est* as "is" (never "exists" unless
   the Latin is *existit* or the sense is plainly existential, with a C note), and keep *secundum* ("as
   to", "according to") visible, in its place: *secundum deitatem / humanitatem* "as to the Godhead / as
   to the humanity". Reduplicative ***secundum quod*** "insofar as", ⛔ never "in that" (heard as
   "because"). ⛔ **Never supply "as God / as man"** where the Latin has bare apposition (*ipse Deus*) or
   bare predication: the difference between the bare and the reduplicated form is the treatise's matter.
   **Propositions in indirect speech:** where an accusative + infinitive names a proposition whose sense
   is under discussion (*intelligentes Christum non esse creaturam*), render it as the quoted proposition
   ("who understand 'Christ is not a creature'"), not "understand that …", which asserts it.

# GLOSSARY — FROZEN 2026-09-28 (Wilson's pilot review)

*One Latin term, one English rendering, every time, across the whole corpus. A reader must be able
to recognise the same word coming back, because Wyclif's arguments turn on it. A rendering changes
only by a dated decision in CONVENTIONS' decision log, never silently in a chunk. Entries may be
*added* by recorded decision as new terms appear. **Tag keys:** a chunk tags a term as
`[English]{g:latin-form}` (CONVENTIONS §11), and the key must match a Latin form in one of the rows
below. These rows are also the source of the site's hover glossary. Where a term has two senses,
the entry says so and each chunk's note picks one. A term marked **CONTEXTUAL** holds together
senses English splits: its English is chosen by context, under the dikaiosune rule (CONVENTIONS §6).*

**Sources of the decisions.** "Pilot" = settled in `pilots/` this session. "T. p. N" = Mark
Thakkar, "Duces caecorum," *Vivarium* 58 (2020), page N (`reference/`), which documents the
errors of the two most recent translations. His corrections are adopted here unless an entry says
otherwise.

**Orthography.** Headwords are given in the Society editions' medieval spelling (*eciam*,
*racio*, *quiditas*, *ymmo*), because that is what the translator will meet on the page.

# *De benedicta incarnacione*, chapter 6, part 5: [TITLE]

## ¶1 · 100.19–101.30

> [p. 100] Quod si 2° obicitur quod ‘Christus’ dicens duas naturas dicit principaliter ac simpliciter naturam increatam, cum analogum¹⁰ per se sumptum secundum famosius¹¹ debet intelligi; dicitur, quod si illa evidencia procederet, negandum¹² esset omne predicabile de¹³ Christo humanitus,¹³ et per consequens totum misterium et fides incarnacionis. Ideo, cum scriptura et sancti doctores concedunt simpliciter utramque maneriem predicatorum de Christo, qui nos sumus ut audeamus¹⁴ tam consolatoriam fidem¹⁵ infringere? Unde Augustinus 2° De Visitacione Infirmorum: ‘Cum, inquit, credatur scripture sacre testimoniis sunt alia de Deo Filio nobis¹⁶¹⁷ propinqua et contigua, et¹⁶ ex ipsa propinquitate sui et contiguitate nobis familiaria, et ex ipsa familiaritate nobis delectabilia. Delectabile quippe est homini et salutare [p. 101] morienti loqui et saciari de humanitate Christi. Quod enim “Verbum caro factum est et habitavit in nobis,” hominis est; quod homo Deus factus est, hominis est: utrumque ergo hoc ineffabile sacramentum hominis est. Quoniam ergo infirmus hominis intellectus, ex sui ipsius humanitate ponderosa ebetatus, ‘videns nunc per speculum in enigmate,’ non potest Dei divinitatem, ut dignum est, comprehendere; ad illam, que sua est, Christi humanitatem oculum suum intellectualem reflectat, in illa delectetur, ex illa sacietur, ad illam faciem suam tanquam parietem cum Ezechia convertat.’ Et sequitur consilium bonum morbis pestilencialibus fatigatis. ‘De homine, inquit, tuo puro fac tibi participem,¹ quia prius fecit Deus de homine tuo puro sibi participem.¹ Ex divinitate processit salvacio et ex humanitate redempcio: utrumque tamen ex utroque:² sed,³ quod unum est michi quadam cognacione et secreta quadam affeccione [et] conglutinio quodam,³ proprio iure consanguinitatis vendico, tucius et iocundius loquor ad meum Iesum quam ad aliquem sanctorum spirituum⁴: plus debet Christus michi⁵ quam cuilibet celestium spirituum⁶ quia, quod tu es, fieri dignatus est Deus, non factus est angelus. Nollem, inquit, habere locum angeli, si possem habere locum debitum homini.’ Nec dubito quin omnes logici nesciunt offendendo impingere in illam sentenciam de vi vocis, quia cum Christus sit frater noster, ut patet Ioh. 20. ‘vade ad fratres meos et dic eis etc.’; et ad Hebre. 2° ‘non confunditur eos fratres vocare dicens: “nunciabo nomen tuum fratribus meis”’: consequens est quod sit eadem communis humanitas, que est quilibet frater suus; et per consequens debet lege proximitatis diligere fratres suos.

[ENGLISH]

## ¶2 · 101.31–102.4

> [p. 101] Sed 3° obicitur per hoc quod hereticum est credere Iesum Christum non fuisse ante concepcionem ex virgine, sicut⁷ patet de Heresi 44ᵃ. superius recitata. Ymo cum contradictoria [p. 102] non verificantur de eodem, et Christus Deus est eternaliter post et ante; videtur quod, concesso¹ Christum semper fuisse, negandum est, ut contradictorium, eundem Christum aliquando non fuisse.

## ¶3 · 102.5–103.3

> [p. 102] Hic dico quod, si essem pure gentilis philosophus indifferens omni secte coram capciosissimis logicis intendentibus² michi³ concludere; dum tamen admitterem gracia argumenti istam philosophicam⁴ supposicionem et necessariam fidei Christiane, quod eadem persona Verbi est trium naturarum quelibet; ego concederem conclusionem tanquam⁵ sine repugnancia⁵ consequentem. Pro cuius declaracione primo⁶ suppono quod in equivocis non sit contradiccio: 2° quod summa equivocacio sit inter deitatem et naturam creatam, cum in nullo universali univoco conveniunt: 3° quod persona Verbi sit corpus, anima, et deitas singillatim. Quibus suppositis patet ex posicione⁷ tam quod iste Christus fuit in mundi principio. Affirmativa patet ex hoc quod illa deitas tunc fuit et ipsa est Christus: negativa autem patet ex hoc quod illa humanitas tunc non fuit, et ipsa est Christus. Sicut ergo non est contradiccio quod homo currit et homo non currit, cum multa sunt quorum⁸ quodlibet est homo, pro quorum⁸ uno verificatur affirmativa, et pro alio negativa; sic in proposito, cum multa sunt,⁹ quorum quodlibet est Christus. Et hinc dicit beatus Ambrosius 3° libro De Spiritu Sancto quoad¹⁰ logicam subtiliter¹¹ et profunde. ‘Generalis, inquit, est fides ista¹² quod Christus est Dei filius¹² et¹³ natus ex virgine, quem quasi gigantem propheta describit, eo quod biformis gemine nature unus consors divinitatis et corporis. Idem ergo paciebatur et non paciebatur, moriebatur et non moriebatur, sepeliebatur¹⁴ et non sepeliebatur,¹⁴ resurgebat et non resurgebat; resurgebat secundum carnem, que mortua [p. 103] fuerat, non secundum Verbum, quod apud Deum semper manebat.’ Et quando ‘sapiebam ut¹ parvulus,’ putabam¹ istum sanctum multum ignarum logice.

[ENGLISH]

## ¶4 · 103.4–103.19

> [p. 103] Et patet quomodo intelligende sunt negative doctorum in materia de Incarnacione. Unde Ambrosius primo De Trinitate ca° 6 dicit quod Christus non est creatura: et tamen constat ipsum² expressissime dicere quod Christus suscepit predicaciones humanas univoce cum aliis, in tantum quod dicit eum ad litteram ‘profecisse sapiencia et etate,’ et ignorasse multa; ut probat, libro 3°· De Spiritu Sancto, auctoritate scripture. Et sic intelligendum est dictum Augustini in sermone De Fide: ‘si quis, inquit, dixerit Dei Filium passum, anathema sit.’ Exponit enim³ seipsum ibidem, quod hoc intelligendum est secundum divinitatem, cum affirmativa sit de substancia fidei, scilicet, quod Dei Filius passus est, ut sepe meminit Augustinus. Et ita concordanda sunt alia dicta doctorum, non concedendo contradictoria, sed veritates nedum simul esse veras, sed a quocunque fideli catholice concedendas.⁴

[ENGLISH]

## ¶5 · 103.20–103.28

> [p. 103] Quod si queritur quomodo dabimus contradiccionem in talibus cum negacio preposita toti non sufficit; dicitur⁵ iuxta regulam Aristotelis primo Elenchorum ca° 4°, quod enunciando contradictoria de aliquo attendendum est quod illud sit idem ultimum singulare⁶ et secundum idem simpliciter et in eodem tempore; ut, quod idem Christus in numero secundum⁷ eandem naturam in numero⁷ sit simul⁸ vivus et mortuus, passus et non passus. Et ita de aliis oppositis est repugnancia.

[ENGLISH]

## ¶6 · 103.29–104.13

> [p. 103] Unde concedentes quod Christus eciam⁹ secundum altissimam naturam in eo non habuit existenciam ante conceptum ex virgine senserunt,¹⁰ ut Augustinus recitat, nimis heretice. Non autem illi, qui concedunt ipsum non preextitisse subintelligendo secundum humanitatem ad sensum expositum. [p. 104] Contradiccio enim non est nominis tantum, sed et¹ rei et nominis. Et indubie causa quare scola discessit ab illa logica fuit inanis² apparencia² sophistica, qua mavult vitare apparenciam redargucionis vel puerilis inconsequencie, quam pausare³ in sensu reali scripture. Prima⁴ ad Cor. 4° scribitur: ‘nos stulti propter Christum.’ Nam apostoli, predicantes Deum⁵ crucifixum et mortuum, et⁶ simul parem Deo et minorem Deo, reputati sunt nimirum stulti tam a philosophis quam a plebeis. Sed⁷ ‘siquis videtur inter vos sapiens esse in hoc seculo, sic stultus fiat ut sit sapiens’ Deo:’ ut precipit Apostolus prima ad Cor. 3°·, quia indubie gentiles philosophi ex ignorancia logice et methaphisice reputant sensum illum stulticiam esse.

[ENGLISH]

## ¶7 · 104.14–105.9

> [p. 104] Et si queratur, utrum concedi debet quod Deus aliquando non fuit, sicut generabatur temporaliter vel incepit;⁸ videtur michi quod licet illud sane posset intelligi, tamen iste sensus non ministratur ex terminis. Ideo ego concedo negativas solum cum subiectis signantibus geminas substancias in Filio, ut Messias ille homo Iesus aliquando non fuit: quia in illis intelligo naturam que incepit⁹ esse et aliquando non fuit. Non¹⁰ sic autem in nominibus appropriate competentibus deitati. Conceditur tamen quod Deus fuit natus de virgine, passus et mortuus. Sed illud probabitur resolvendo predicacionem ad hominem sic formatum. Et ex hoc non sequitur quod Deus aliquando non fuit, sed¹¹ quod secundum hominem aliquando non fuit. Nec est incepcio vel generacio Deo conveniens, Deum esse et ipsum aliquando non fuisse: sed sic ex esse aliquando¹² humanitus et ex negacione, que est,¹³ antea humanitus non fuisse, cum instanti et aliis principiantibus illum motum. Et patet quod non sequitur—Christus aliquando non fuit; et ipse est Deus: ergo Deus¹⁴ aliquando non fuit—propter extraneacionem [p. 105] supposicionis. Unde si concedimus quod Deus genuit Deum et Deus non genuit Deum, licet tantum sit unus Deus propter distinccionem personarum; quanto magis concederemus quod Christus est factus et Christus non est factus, licet tantum unus sit Christus: cum sicut Deus est tres persone, sic Christus est tres nature, longe nobis manifestius disparate.¹ Et patet exemplariter quomodo respondebitur ad² questiones et communes² obiectus in ista materia seminatos.

[ENGLISH]
