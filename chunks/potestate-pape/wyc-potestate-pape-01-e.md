# *De potestate pape*, chapter 1, part 5: power given from above: the answers, and Christ before Pilate

## ¶1 · 16.22–17.17

> [p. 16] Ad secundum dicitur quod talis potestas non laxatur vel restringitur, augmentatur vel diminuitur quoad suam essenciam, sed quoad execucionem operis quod ab ipsa procederet, quod licet facere, sed non nisi affuerit causa rationalis, quoad Deum. Et illum sensum exponit XXIV canon, quest. prima, Miramur: Aliud, inquit, est potestas officii, aliud execucio, et ut plurimum in monachis et aliis retinetur potestas officii, velud in suspensis, quibus ministracio interdicitur, potencia non aufertur. Et ad conformem sensum conceditur quod naturalis potestas que est [p. 17] liberum arbitrium potest nunc laxari per graciam et nunc restringi, et sic per equivocacionem solvuntur apparentes discordie doctorum, quorum aliqui (ut Anshelmus) dicunt quod non potest perdi, augmentari vel minui liberum arbitrium. Alii autem (ut Augustinus Encheridion) dicunt quod perdi potest liberum arbitrium per peccatum et augeri per graciam. Intelligunt autem sufficienciam propinquam ad operam. quam oportet post primum peccatum dari ex gracia. sicut et ordinis potestatem, preter liberum arbitrium naturale. et sic potestas naturalis augetur vel restringitur per adventum potestatis adventicie et illa consistens in successione, potest instar etatis nunc esse amplior et nunc minor, licet sit indivisibilis quo ad molem. De potestate vero ordinis et iurisdictionis dicetur inferius.

To the second [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that such [power]{g:potestas} is not [loosed]{g:laxare} or restricted, increased or diminished as to its essence, but as to the execution of the work that would proceed from it, which it is lawful to do, though not unless a reasonable cause is present, as regards God.[^1] And the canon *Miramur*, [Causa] 24, question 1, expounds this sense: "The power of the office", it says, "is one thing, its execution another; and [for the most part]{g:ut-plurimum} the power of the office is retained in monks and others, as in the suspended, to whom the ministry is forbidden: the [power]{g:potencia} is not taken away."[^2] And in a conformable sense it is granted that natural power, which is [17] [free choice]{g:liberum-arbitrium}, can at one time be loosed by grace and at another restricted; and so by equivocation the apparent disagreements of the doctors are resolved, some of whom (as Anselm) say that "free choice cannot be lost, increased or diminished". But others (as Augustine, *Enchiridion*) say that "free choice can be lost by sin and increased by grace".[^3] And they mean the proximate sufficiency for the work, which after the first sin must be given by grace, as must also the power of order, besides natural free choice; and so natural power is increased or restricted by the coming of an adventitious power, and that [power], consisting in succession, can like age be at one time greater and at another less, although it is indivisible as to bulk.[^4] And of the [power of order]{g:potestas-ordinis} and of jurisdiction it will be said below.

## ¶2 · 17.18–18.9

> [p. 17] Ad tercium dicitur quod assumptum et consequencia illata videntur michi vera; quamvis enim creatura cooperatur cum Deo, faciendo signa sacramentalia et sic quodammodo faciendo, quia facit occasionaliter quod Deus dat graciam, imprimit karacterem. vel producit huiusmodi qualitatem supernaturalem, non tamen producit cum Deo huiusmodi qualitatem, ut dicetur proximo capitulo; sed Deus nunc antecedenter nunc consequenter et nunc concomitanter ad signum sensibile per se facit opus supernaturale, et potestas ordinis est quodammodo causa; quare Deus sic agit, cum statuit se concurrere cum quolibet sacerdote rite ministrante huiusmodi sacramenta. Sicut natura dat animam post disposicionem [p. 18] materie per qualitates elementares et virtutes corporales. quibus non est pars produccionis anime tribuenda sed disposicionis previe, ideo dicuntur coefficere animam quoad animacionem non nude quoad essenciam spiritus, quia tunc crearent; et sic homo non alterat spiritum imprimendo in ipso aliquod absolutum, sed subautentice facit nosci in signo sensibili, quod Deus dat huiusmodi potestatem.

To the third the answer is that the [assumption]{g:assumptum} and the [inference]{g:consequencia} drawn seem to me true; for although a creature cooperates with God by making the sacramental signs, and so in a way by making, since it brings it about [by occasion]{g:occasionaliter} that God gives grace, imprints a character, or produces a supernatural quality of this kind, yet it does not produce such a quality with God, as will be said in the next chapter; but God, sometimes antecedently, sometimes consequently and sometimes concomitantly to the [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} sign, of himself does the supernatural work, and the power of order is in a way the cause why God so acts, since he has determined to concur with every [priest]{g:sacerdos} duly ministering such sacraments.[^5] As nature gives the soul after the disposition [18] of matter through elementary qualities and bodily [powers]{g:virtus}, to which no part of the production of the soul is to be attributed, but [a part] of the prior disposition, and therefore they are said to co-effect the soul as to animation, not barely as to the essence of the spirit, since then they would create; and so man does not alter the spirit by imprinting in it something absolute, but [sub-authentically]{g:subautenticus} makes known in a perceptible sign that God gives power of this kind.[^6]

## ¶3 · 18.10–18.29

> [p. 18] Unde inter omnes locuciones blasfemas quibus christianismus illuditur, maximus cathaclismus mendacii est de collacione. subtraccione vel restriccione huiusmodi potestatum. Quando enim Deus et racio cogunt ad utilitatem ecclesie, ut sic fiat per hominem, tunc et aliter non Deus dat huiusmodi potestatem. Ideo in primitiva ecclesia non usi sunt apostoli tali locucione civili, quia tunc foret potestas symoniaca vendibilis et emibilis sicut officium, ideo propter bullas papales non attribuitur potestas subdito plus vel minus, cum Deus dat potestatem habilibus ad edificacionem ecclesie independenter a tali sacramento sensibili introducto, et rectitudo operis est evidencius signum talis potestatis quam littera vel sacramenta prelati; nec ex hoc infertur quod liceret civiliter assumere sibi potestatem ad taliter operandum; quia nemo assumeret potestatem sibi ipsi, sed acciperet ab alio quando Deus necessitat.

Hence, among all the blasphemous ways of speaking by which Christianity is mocked, the greatest cataclysm of lying is about the [conferral]{g:collacio}, withdrawal or restriction of such powers.[^7] For when God and reason compel, for the usefulness of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, that it be so done by a man, then and not otherwise God gives such power. Therefore in the primitive church the apostles did not use such civil speech, because then power would be simoniacal, saleable and purchasable like an office; therefore, because of papal bulls, power is not attributed to a subject more or less, since God gives power to the [qualified]{g:habilis} for the edification of the church independently of such a perceptible sacrament as has been introduced, and rightness of work is a more evident sign of such power than the letter or the sacraments of a [prelate]{g:prelatus}; nor is it inferred from this that it would be lawful to assume power to oneself in civil fashion to work in such a way, since no one would assume power to himself, but would receive it from another when God necessitates.

## ¶4 · 18.30–20.19

> [p. 18] Unde Symon Magus non presumpsit sibi assumere potestatem apostolicam, quia timeret tunc sibi, ne effectualis efficacia operis sequeretur. [p. 19] ideo voluit emisse potestatem pecunia, ut patet Actuum VIII. Sed nostri prepositi quietati in cortice signorum, solum querentes honorem et lucrum seculi, sunt odibilius Deo symoniaci; et sic conceditur, ut tangitur in confirmacione tercia, quod nimis multe sophisticaciones volant in ista materia, quia omnes que non sunt fundabiles in scriptura, cum ipsa contineat singulas veritates. Potestatem autem secularem regendi civiliter, videtur Apostolum fundare Rom. XIII 1 quando vocat temporales dominos potestates; et dicit, quod non sine causa portant gladium, id est potestatem civiliter coactivam, dicens quod ibidem foret resistere Deo et illis quoad execucionem huiusmodi potestatis, quia (ut inquit) non est potestas nisi a Deo; quod oportet omnino intelligere quod solus Deus dat huiusmodi potestatem, vel quod non datur a racionali creatura, nisi Deus prius ipsam dederit, ita quod hoc signum nisi sumatur pro si non, ad modum loquendi scripture. Et istum sensum dixit Christus Joh. XIX, 10 11 ubi legitur Christo tacente Pilatus dixisse: Nescis quia potestatem habeo crucifigere te et potestatem habeo dimittere te? Cui Jesus: Non haberes potestatem adversum me ullam, nisi tibi datum esset desuper. Super quo Augustinus Omelia CXVI notat, primo quomodo Dominus sepe tacuit et sepe respondit. Tacuit coram Pilato, coram sacerdotibus et Herode propter tria, primo ut impleretur scriptum Isaie LIII, 7: Sicut agnus coram tondente sine voce, sic non aperuit os suum; secundo [p. 20] ut ostendatur eius miticia Math. XII, 19 recitata: Neque aliquis in plateis audiet vocem eius, et tercio ut doceat quomodo verbalis contencio est sepe inutilis; et ideo quando expedit est silendum. Ideo ad tollendum instancias de huiusmodi negativis dicit Augustinus quod generaliter non sicut reus vel dolosus obmutuit, sed sicut agnus, hoc est simplex et innocens, non aperuit os suum. Nunquam enim loquebatur excusando se de suo crimine, sicut nunquam tacuit pro suo reatu; et sic utrobique tam eius silencium quam eius responsio propter latens misterium est notandum; ubi inquit, non respondit sicut ovis silebat, et ubi respondit sicut pastor docebat. Discamus ergo quatuor veritates; quas pastor in responsione sua docuerit, primo docet dicendo, quod non haberet potestatem nisi datum esset sibi desuper, quod per Apostolum suum docuit; quod non est potestas nisi a Deo.

Hence Simon Magus did not presume to assume apostolic power to himself, because he would then fear for himself that the effectual efficacy of the work would not follow;[^8] [19] therefore he wished to buy the power with money, as is clear in Acts 8. But our [superiors]{g:prepositi}, quieted in the rind of signs, seeking only honour and the gain of the world, are simoniacs more hateful to God;[^9] and so it is granted, as is touched on in the third confirmation, that too many sophistries fly about in this matter, since all are such that cannot be grounded in scripture, since it contains every single truth.[^10] And the Apostle seems to ground the secular power of ruling civilly in Romans 13:1, when he calls the [temporal lords]{g:temporales-domini} powers; and he says that they "bear not the sword in vain", that is, civilly [coactive]{g:coactivus} power, saying that here it "would be to resist God" and them as to the execution of such power, since (as he says) "there is no power but from God".[^11] This must be understood altogether [to mean] that God alone gives such power, or that it is not given by a rational creature unless God has given it first, so that this sign *nisi* is taken for *si non*, in scripture's manner of speaking.[^12] And Christ spoke this sense in John 19:10–11, where it is read that, Christ being silent, Pilate said: "Knowest thou not that I have power to crucify thee, and I have power to release thee?" To whom Jesus: "Thou shouldst not have any power against me, unless it were given thee from above." On which Augustine, Homily 116, notes first how the Lord often kept silent and often answered.[^13] He was silent before Pilate, before the priests and Herod, for three [reasons]: first, that the scripture of Isaiah 53:7 might be fulfilled: "like a lamb without voice before his shearer, so openeth he not his mouth";[^14] secondly, [20] that his gentleness might be shown, recited in Matthew 12:19: "neither shall any man hear his voice in the streets"; and thirdly, to teach how verbal contention is often useless, and therefore, when it is expedient, one must keep silence. Therefore, to [dispose of]{g:tollere} [objections]{g:instancia} from negatives of this kind, Augustine says that in general he did "not" keep dumb "as one guilty or deceitful, but as a lamb, that is, simple and innocent, he opened not his mouth". For he never spoke excusing himself of his crime, just as he never kept silent for his guilt; and so on both sides both his silence and his answer are to be noted for the hidden mystery: "where", he says, "he did not answer, as a sheep he kept silence, and where he answered, as a shepherd he taught. Let us learn, then", four truths which the shepherd taught in his answer. First he teaches, by saying "that he would not have power unless it were given him from above", what he taught through his Apostle: that "there is no power but from God".[^15]

## ¶5 · 20.20–20.24

> [p. 20] Secundo docet quod plus peccat qui potestati innocentem occidendum livore tradit quam potestas ipsa, cum eum timore alterius maioris potestatis occidit; propterea, inquit; qui tradidit me tibi maius peccatum habet.

Secondly he teaches that "he sins more who out of envy hands over an innocent man to the power to be killed than the power itself, when it kills him out of fear of another, greater power"; for this reason, he says, "he that hath delivered me to thee hath the greater sin".[^16]

## ¶6 · 20.25–20.27

> [p. 20] Et patet tercio, quomodo vanum est excusare Pilatum a crimine sed magis est vanum excusare maioritatem peccati in sacerdote.

And it is clear, thirdly, how vain it is to excuse Pilate from the crime, but it is more vain to excuse the [greatness]{g:majoritas} of the sin in the priest.[^17]

## ¶7 · 20.28–21.13

> [p. 20] Quarto vero innuitur quomodo quis est sub alterius potestate; et quod potentatus superior non est eo ipso culpandus quo inferior excedit [p. 21] limites potestatis talis: Talem, inquit, Deus Pilato dederat potestatem, ut esset eciam sub cesaris potestate. Unde quidam intelligunt Christum non solum intelligere dacionem potestatis Pilati desuper non solum a Deo sed cesare, quia cesarem plus timuit ut autorem potestatis sue quam occidere regem gentis aliene. Sed timor ille fuit servilis, cum postposita regula potestatis divine, non timuit occidere innocentem; excusacionem autem non habuit, cum excessit potestatem quam habebat a cesare, qui potuit pretendere se dare potestatem adversum Dominum, sed non Deus.

And fourthly it is intimated how one is under another's power, and that a superior potentate is not to be blamed by the very fact that an inferior exceeds [21] the [limits]{g:limites} of such power: "Such power", he says, "God had given Pilate, that he should be also under Caesar's power." Hence some understand Christ to mean not only the giving of Pilate's power from above not only by God but [by] Caesar,[^18] because he feared Caesar more, as the author of his power, than [he feared] to kill the king of a foreign nation. But that fear was servile, since, the rule of divine power being set aside, he did not fear to kill an innocent man; and he had no excuse, since he exceeded the power which he had from Caesar, who could claim to give power against the Lord, but God could not.[^19]

## ¶8 · 21.14–21.31

> [p. 21] Et videtur mihi quinto quod magister docet misterium metaphysice per hoc, quod dicit nisi datum esset tibi desuper, non solum quod cesar non dat nisi Deus prius dederit, verum eciam quod potestas non est quid absolutum, quod posset per se existere, sed potestas dicitur dari ab autore, quando datum est a Deo creature, ut ministro Dei, quod sic aut sic sufficiat facere ad populum in Dei nomine regulandum, et credere quod hoc sit res que posset per se esse foret nimis magna stulticia; et sic per hoc participium neutrum datum convenienter exprimitur res verbi exprimenda per complexum, ut concessum est creature, quod legittime possit in nomine Domini regulare, et illa licencia est potestas, quam Deus nunc dat immediate per se, et nunc per sacramentale ministerium ad notificandum populo Dei licenciam.

And it seems to me, fifthly, that the master teaches a mystery of [metaphysics]{g:metaphisica}[^20] by saying "unless it were given thee from above": not only that Caesar does not give unless God has given first, but also that power is not something absolute that could exist of itself; rather, power is said to be given by its author when it is given by God to a creature, as to a minister of God, that it be sufficient to do this or that for ruling the people in God's name; and to believe that this is a thing that could be of itself would be too great a folly. And so by this neuter participle *datum*, "given", there is fittingly expressed the thing of the verb, which is to be expressed by a complex: namely that it has been granted to the creature that it may lawfully rule in the name of the Lord; and that licence is power, which God gives sometimes immediately by himself, and sometimes through sacramental ministry, to notify the licence to the people of God.[^21]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *quod licet facere … quoad Deum*: the loosing or restricting of the execution is lawful, but before God only for a reasonable cause; *quoad Deum* may instead go with *causa rationalis* ("a cause reasonable in God's sight"). B in the margin (16.22): *ad 2m*.

[^2]: **S/C.** Decretum, C. 24 q. 1 c. 37, *Miramur* (Loserth 16.28; the modern form is ours) (LEDGER P01e-1, editor). The canon takes up *potestas officii* with *potencia*: the two words name one power here, and both are "power". Hus repeats 16.22–33 (Loserth: "Hussii Opp." 213b–214a) (LEDGER P01e-2, editor).

[^3]: **S/C.** Anselm, *De libertate arbitrii* (Loserth 17.4: "De Libero Arbitrio", chapter illegible in the scan) (LEDGER P01e-3, editor, to be checked). Augustine, *Enchiridion*: Loserth quotes *Libero arbitrio male utens homo et se perdidit et ipsum* (LEDGER P01e-4, editor; chapter to be checked). The "equivocation": the doctors use *liberum arbitrium* of the natural power (Anselm) and of the proximate sufficiency given by grace (Augustine).

[^4]: **C.** *illa consistens in successione*: the adventitious power, a successive thing, which grows and shrinks like age though it has no parts of bulk. At 17.12 Loserth notes that the codex has *stringitur*; he prints *restringitur*, translated.

[^5]: **C.** *quodammodo faciendo*: the creature "in a way" makes, only by occasion. *nunc … nunc … nunc*: "sometimes", not "now". "The next chapter" is ch. 2. AB in the margin (17.18): *ad 3m*; Loserth's side-note at 17.18 gives the sense.

[^6]: **C.** The comparison: the elementary qualities dispose matter but do not produce the soul, as the minister's sign disposes but does not produce the power. *absolutum*: an absolute entity, as against a relation (cf. 21.16).

[^7]: **R.** *cathaclismus*: B writes the Greek word otherwise (Loserth 18.10); AB in the margin: *Potestatum subtraccio*, with the three nouns bracketed; B (18.18): *Nota*. Hus repeats 18.10–14 (Loserth: cf. "Hussii Opp." 214a).

[^8]: **C.** *timeret … ne effectualis efficacia operis sequeretur*: literally "lest the effectual efficacy of the work should follow"; taken in the sense the argument wants, that Simon feared the work would not take effect. Acts 8:18–21 (Loserth 19.2).

[^9]: **C/T.** *quietati in cortice signorum*: resting in the husk of the signs (bulls, seals), not in the work. B reads *odibiles* (19.4) for *odibilius*; the print is translated.

[^10]: **C.** *in confirmacione tercia*: apparently the confirmation of the third objection (14.11–22). *quia omnes que non sunt fundabiles in scriptura*: every opinion that cannot be grounded in scripture is a sophistry, since scripture contains every truth. B lacks *secularem* (19.10); B in the margin (19.11): *Fundamentum potestatis civilis*.

[^11]: **S.** Romans 13:1 (Loserth 20.19); 13:4, "he beareth not the sword in vain" (singular in the Vulgate: Wyclif makes it plural of the lords); 13:2, "he that resisteth the power resisteth the ordinance of God", here paraphrased as "to resist God". Quotation marks follow Loserth's italics.

[^12]: **C.** *hoc signum nisi … pro si non*: the logical sign "except" (*nisi*) is read as "if not" (*si non*): no power is given if God has not first given it.

[^13]: **S.** Augustine, *Tractatus in Iohannem*: the text says "Homily 116"; Loserth (19.27) gives "in Ev. Joannis cap. XIX, tract. CXVII" (LEDGER P01e-5, editor; the number to be checked). John 19:10–11: Wyclif omits Pilate's "Speakest thou not to me?". AB in the margin (19.29): *Christus sepe tacuit*; A numbers the three reasons 1 (19.30), 2 (19.32), 3 (20.2).

[^14]: **S.** Wyclif cites Isaiah 53:7, but his words are those of Acts 8:32, which quotes it (Vulgate *sicut agnus coram tondente se sine voce sic non aperuit os suum*); the DR of Acts 8:32 is used. Isaiah 53:7 Vulgate: *quasi agnus coram tondente se obmutescet*. Matthew 12:19.

[^15]: **S.** Augustine, the same tractate (Loserth 20.13: *ibid.*), quoted within Loserth's italics; "Let us learn, then" opens Augustine's own list. Romans 13:1 (Loserth 20.19). B in the margin (20.14): *Quatuor veritates*, numbered 1 (20.16), 2 (20.19).

[^16]: **T/S.** 20.21: the manuscripts read *cum enim* and lack *potestatis*; Loserth supplies *potestatis* from Augustine's text and prints *cum* (translated). John 19:11 (Loserth 20.24). B in the margin (20.24): 3.

[^17]: **C.** *maioritatem peccati in sacerdote*: the "greater sin" of John 19:11, laid on the priest who handed Christ over; *excusare* here is "to explain away".

[^18]: **T/S.** *non solum … non solum* (21.3–4): the second *non solum* looks doubled; translated as printed. The words in quotation marks are Augustine's (Loserth 20.30: "Augustinus ut supra") (LEDGER P01e-6, to be checked). AB in the margin (20.30): *Pilatus habuit potestatem et peccavit*.

[^19]: **C.** *qui potuit pretendere se dare potestatem adversum Dominum, sed non Deus*: Caesar could claim to give power against the Lord, but God could not give it; alternatively "but [Caesar could] not [as] God".

[^20]: **C.** "The master" is Christ. The fifth truth is Wyclif's own, added to Augustine's four.

[^21]: **G/C.** *participium neutrum datum*: here the grammatical participle, not the GLOSSARY's *participium*, "participation". *res verbi exprimenda per complexum*: what the verb signifies is not a thing but what only a proposition can state ("that it has been granted …"). AB in the margin (21.7): *Potestas quomodo dicitur dari*.
