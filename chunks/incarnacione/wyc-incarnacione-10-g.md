# *De benedicta incarnacione*, chapter 10, part 7: the price of the redemption: the humanity adored with latria and hyperdulia

## ¶1 · 182.32–184.14

> [p. 182] Tercio confirmatur ex hoc quod nichil sufficit redimere genus humanum perfecte, ut modo redimitur, nisi ipsum sit [p. 183] Deus: humanitas Christi sic redimit: ergo est Deus. Ipsa¹ enim fuit passa omni passione, qua Christus dedit sanguinem in redempcionem ut Christus, et sic de posicione anime ac commercio quo mercatus est Christus. Cum ergo per² passionem et tales formas fiebat redempcio, sequitur quod ipsa fecit redempcionem; aliter enim³ non esset illa humanitas tante⁴ diligenda nec latria adoranda. Cum tamen indubie quilibet⁵ Christianus debet tantum diligere humanitatem Christi quantum⁵ et Christum sub racione qua homo: ymo hec idem sunt et colenda eadem pietate in numero, ut ostendit Magister 3°⁶ Sentenciarum Dist. 8. ex⁷ testimonio beati Augustini. Christus enim ex hoc quod est ista humanitas et per consequens creatura adorari debet yperdulia, et omnis alia creatura venerari debet tantum⁸ dulia. Nam excellens honor debet⁸ Christo attribui, qui ex unione ypostatica est creator ac eciam creatura; et illa vocatur significanter yperdulia; et cum hoc, cum idem Christus sit creator, debet latria soli deo debita adorari. Correspondenter humanitas Christi cum sit Verbum, debet adorari latria,⁹ et cum in se sit creatura debet adorari⁹ yperdulia sicut Christus. Et sic tam Christus quam ista humanitas, que est personaliter ipse Christus, debet secundum duplicem racionem eadem tam latria quam yperdulia adorari: sed absit hoc, nisi illa humanitas foret¹⁰ Christus! Ex quo patet quod non solum deitati, sed soli deo est latria exhibenda. Nec est putandum quod humanitas Christi adoranda sit latria solum secundum ordinem ad alterum, sicut crux, ymago, vel pars Christi quantitativa, sed¹¹ tanquam corpus animatum, sumptum de virgine, passum pro nobis, dolens et merens, et per consequens vere positum precium pro redempcione humani generis: ‘empti enim estis¹² precio [p. 184] magno,’ ut dicit Apostolus prima ad Cor. 6° et 7°: et ad id precium atque commercium explanat expressius: ‘Deus, inquit, proprio Filio non pepercit, sed pro nobis omnibus tradidit illum’: et indubie sic fecit tota Trinitas: unde ad Gal. 2°, ‘in fide vivo Filii Dei, qui dilexit me et tradidit semetipsum pro me’: et indubie precium traditum fuit Christi humanitas; unde¹ si per impossibile humanitas Christi dimissa¹ fuerit personalitati proprie conversans nobiscum ut proximis, ego diligerem eum ut salvatorem et redemptorem meum, adoraremque eum latria sicut prius: nulli enim alteri hominum adeo potero obligari. Nec video quin iste homo dimissus, sine peccato vel merito decedens, foret supra angelos et omnes alios homines merito exaltandus propter meritum quo meruit ypostatice copulatus.

Thirdly, this is confirmed from this: that nothing suffices to redeem the human race perfectly, as it is now redeemed, unless it is [183] God; Christ's [humanity]{g:humanitas} so redeems: therefore it is God. For it suffered every suffering by which Christ gave his blood for redemption, as Christ [did]; and so of the [laying-down of the soul]{g:posicio-anime} and the [trade]{g:commercium} by which Christ made his purchase.[^1] Since, therefore, the redemption was made through the passion and such forms, it follows that it made the redemption; for otherwise that humanity would not be so greatly to be loved, nor to be adored with [latria]{g:latria}; whereas, nevertheless, every Christian undoubtedly ought to love Christ's humanity as much as [he loves] Christ too, under the aspect by which he is a man: indeed these are the same, and to be worshipped with the same [piety]{g:pietas} in number, as the [Master]{g:magister} shows, [book] 3 of the *Sentences*, dist. 8, from the testimony of blessed Augustine. For Christ, from this, that he is this humanity, and in consequence a [creature]{g:creatura}, ought to be adored with [hyperdulia]{g:yperdulia}, and every other creature ought to be venerated only with [dulia]{g:dulia}.[^2] For an excelling honour ought to be ascribed to Christ, who from the [hypostatic]{g:ypostatice} [union]{g:unio} is creator and also creature; and that is called, significantly, hyperdulia; and together with this, since the same Christ is creator, he ought to be adored with latria, owed to God alone. Correspondingly Christ's humanity, since it is the [Word]{g:verbum}, ought to be adored with latria, and since in itself it is a creature it ought to be adored with hyperdulia, as Christ is. And so both Christ and this humanity, which is personally Christ himself, ought, according to a twofold aspect, to be adored with the same, both latria and hyperdulia: but far be this, unless that humanity were Christ! From which it is clear that latria is to be shown not only to the [Godhead]{g:deitas}, but to God alone.[^3] Nor is it to be thought that Christ's humanity is to be adored with latria only according to its order to another, as the cross, an image, or a [quantitative part]{g:pars-quantitativa} of Christ, but as a body animated, taken from the virgin, suffering for us, grieving and mourning, and in consequence truly laid down as the price for the redemption of the human race:[^4] "For you are bought with a great [184] price," as the Apostle says, 1 Corinthians 6:20 and 7:23; and he sets out that price and trade more expressly: "God," he says, "spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all";[^5] and undoubtedly the whole Trinity did so. Hence Galatians 2:20: "I live in the faith of the Son of God, who loved me and delivered himself for me"; and undoubtedly the price delivered was Christ's humanity. Hence if, by an impossible [supposition], Christ's humanity had been [laid aside]{g:dimittere} to a [personality]{g:personalitas} of its own, [living its life]{g:conversari} with us as neighbours, I would love him as my saviour and redeemer, and would adore him with latria as before: for to no other of men shall I be able to be so bound. Nor do I see but that this man, laid aside, dying without sin or merit, would deservedly be exalted above the angels and all other men, for the merit by which he merited when hypostatically joined.[^6]

## ¶2 · 184.15–184.23

> [p. 184] Tales sunt multe² evidencie² ex quibus credi debet—si illa humanitas sit aliquid, tunc est Christus—et sic intelligo illud ad Titum 3°; ‘cum³ autem benignitas et humanitas apparuit³ salvatoris nostri Dei’ etc. Illa quidem humanitas est mas de quo Baruch 3° ‘in terris visus est et cum hominibus conversatus est’; ipse enim est ‘vir quem femina circumdedit.’ Ierem. 31°. Et hinc dicit Augustinus in De Vera Religione quod ‘Verbum virum suscepit natus ex femina.’

Such are the many [evidences]{g:evidencia} from which it ought to be believed—"if that humanity is anything, then it is Christ"—and so I understand that [text] of Titus 3:4: "But when the goodness and humanity of our Saviour God appeared" etc.[^7] That humanity indeed is the male of whom Baruch 3:38 [speaks]: "he was seen upon earth, and conversed with men"; for he is "the [man (*vir*)]{g:vir} whom a woman compassed," Jeremiah 31:22. And hence Augustine says in *On True Religion* that "the Word [took up]{g:suscipere} a man (*vir*), born of a woman."[^8]

## Notes

[^1]: **C/T.** *Passio* (183.2) is here the suffering, the Passion, not the attribute of the logicians' *subiectum / passio*; "as Christ [did]": *ut Christus* may also be read "as being Christ". *Posicio anime*: the laying-down of the soul, i.e. of life (John 10:15, 17–18), not the logical "position". O reads *ipsum* for *Ipsa* (183.1), A B C *ipâ*; A B C omit *per* (183.4) and *enim* (183.6); A C read *tantum tante* (183.7).

[^2]: **G/S/T.** *Latria*: the worship due to God alone; *dulia*: the veneration due to a creature; *yperdulia* "hyperdulia": the higher veneration of a creature above the rest. The Master: Peter Lombard, *Sent.* III; the adoration of Christ's humanity with Augustine's testimony stands in modern editions at d. 9 c. 1, not d. 8 (LEDGER I10g-1, to be checked). B reads *Christus debet diligere humanitatem tantum quantum* (183.8–9); O omits *3°* and A adds *hoc* (183.11); O omits *tantum dulia … debet* (183.14–15).

[^3]: **C/T.** Latria goes to whatever is God, not to the abstract Godhead only: so the humanity, if it is the Word, is adored with latria in its own right. O omits *latria … adorari* (183.19–20); B reads *Christus sit* for *foret Christus* (183.24).

[^4]: **T.** C O read *sit* for *sed* (183.28), against A B and the print; with *sit* the clause has no construction ("nor is it to be thought … it is as a body animated"), and the print is kept.

[^5]: **S/T.** 1 Cor. 6:20 (DR); 7:23 has *pretio empti estis* without *magno*. Rom. 8:32, from the same Apostle: Wyclif supplies *Deus* and drops *Qui etiam* and *suo*; DR "He that spared not even his own Son, but delivered him up for us all". O reads *est* for *estis* (183.31).

[^6]: **C/T.** The counterfactual of the laying-aside: if the humanity were left to a personality of its own, the man would still be redeemer and owed latria. "Without sin or merit": dying with no sin and no merit of his own after the laying-aside; the second *merito* (184.13) is the adverb "deservedly". A B C read *que si dimissa fuerit*, omitting *per impossibile humanitas Christi* (184.7–8).

[^7]: **S/C/T.** Titus 3:4: DR "the goodness and kindness of God our Saviour"; Wyclif reads *humanitas* as the humanity, and the English keeps "humanity" because the argument turns on it. A B C omit *multe evidencie* (184.15); A C read *cum autem et humanitas apparuerit*, B *cum autem et apparuerit* (184.17–18), against the Vulgate.

[^8]: **S.** Baruch 3:38 (DR), without *Post haec*. Jer. 31:22: the Vulgate has *femina circumdabit virum*, DR "A woman shall compass a man"; Wyclif puts it in the past. Augustine, *De vera religione* (LEDGER I10g-2, to be checked). *Mas* "male" and *vir* name the sex, against the bare *homo*.
