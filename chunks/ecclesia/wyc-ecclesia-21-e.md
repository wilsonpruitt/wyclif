# *De ecclesia*, chapter 21, part 5: the third fabrication: Aquinas, and the senses of order

## ¶1 · 506.31–510.8

> [p. 506] Tercium commentum est ficcio testimonii sancti Thome quod in solo ordine sacerdocii vel suo subdito [p. 507] caracter ordinis est impressus, et limitatur locus super distinccione XXIVª quarti Sentenciarum; et indubie tractat istam sentenciam ibidem, questione secunda, articulo tercio, recitandoque tres opiniones, ut doctor Bonaventura; quarum prima dicit quod in solo ordine sacerdotali caracter inprimitur; secunda quod in solis sacris ordinibus, sed hoc, inquit, iterum nichil est. Unde, inquit, cum caracter sit signum distinctivum ab aliis, oportet quod in omnibus ordinibus imprimatur, cuius eciam signum est quod oportet quod caracteres perpetuo maneant et nunquam revertentur. Et respondet ad tria argumenta in oppositum, sicut supra. Et patet quod nec hoc est conveniens testimonium ad probandum solum in ordine sacerdotali caracterem esse impressum, cum militat directe in oppositum; et revera cum mendacium in castris armorum corporalium tamquam eis contrarium sit oditum, (ut patet Machabeorum VII°), multo magis in castris scolasticis in quibus militarent solum pugiles veritatis. Sed instatur tripliciter: Videtur enim primo quod uterque istorum doctorum ponat caracterem omnino indelebilem, ego autem dixi superius quod caracter aliquis est tantummodo temporalis. Hic dico quod neuter eorum est mihi autenticus. Ideo non allego eos nisi arguendo ad hominem. Dico tamen quod glossari possunt intelligendo per caracterem esse vel fuisse deputatum ad aliquod sacrum ministerium in sancta ecclesia, et hoc non potest desinere esse, et ideo dicit prior doctor quod caracter manet perpetuo in officio vel premio; nam in predestinatis manet post miliciam quod illi fuerunt deputati ad officium [p. 508] temporale propter cuius complecionem perpetuo premiantur, sicut in prescitis manet perpetuo quod ipsi perpetuo puniuntur propter abusum sui officii, et ad istum sensum omnis caracter manet perpetuo in officio vel mercede. Ego autem loquebar de caractere ut dicit simpliciter deputacionem ad officium, nec credo quod ex scriptura vel racione improbabitur sensus iste. Non enim est caracter talis una res que poterit per se esse, sed subiectum taliter se habere. Sed secundo videtur quod in omni homine, et per consequens in omni ordine caracter imprimitur, et sic reges et milites haberent gloriosum caracterem correspondenter ad suum ordinem. Hic dico quod extense loquendo de caractere (ut supra) est conclusio concedenda, nam omni homini deputat Deus officium ad profectum ecclesie. Ideo cum oportet omnem hominem esse servum Christi vel agendo vel paciendo, omnem hominem oportet portare de secta sua uno modo vel alio. Nec credo aliquem infringere quin, sicut milites spirituales Christi habent caracterem sui officii, sic et reges ac milites corporales (ut patet inferius). In tribus tamen sacramentis specialiter caracter imprimitur, scilicet baptismo, confirmacione et ordine, que tria sacramenta sunt initerabilia ut officia caracterum indelebilia quoad effectum vel fructum. Nam in baptismo recipit servus Christi distinccionem ab omnino infidelibus, in confirmacione roboracionem sue milicie, in ordine vero recipit singularem limitacionem supra laicos ad ministerium sacramenti. Et ita doctores palliant descripciones et applicant scripturam ad sensum quem asserunt, ut patet de caractere, de ordine et ceteris que vix habent corticem apparencie ex scriptura, ut ordinem militarem dicunt non habere caracterem, quia ille ordo non limitatur ad differenter a laicis ministrandum ecclesiastica sacramenta (de quo alibi). [p. 509] Sed tercio dubitatur quomodo illi caracteres ordinum unum constituunt. Et dicunt doctores predicti, quod nec sunt partes quantitative ordinis nec partes subiective sed partes potestative, ut patet prioris doctoris super d. XXIVª, ita quod ordines inferiores habent potestatem a sacerdocio ad quod finaliter ordinantur. Sicut ergo senarius constat ex partibus suis aliquotis que non sunt partes eius quantitative, sic sacramentum ordinis constat ex suis partibus, sed illud latinum videtur multis difficile. Nam signato Petro qui vicissim assumpsit septem ordines et maneat modo cum illis omnibus, patet quod si quilibet eorum fuit sacramentum quia ordo, per idem quilibet eorum est modo sacramentum quia ordo. Nam eiusdem racionis est quilibet eorum cuius foret quilibet huiusmodi per se sumptus, sed tunc foret ordo et sacramentum, ergo et modo. Si enim quilibet eorum sit ordo, ergo vel idem ordo in numero qui posterior, vel alius non idem in numero, quia sic foret ydemptificacio numeralis, et si alius, tunc forent septem ordines, et per consequens, cum quelibet multitudo priorum constituit unum ordinem, forent quatuordecim vel plures ordines distincti specifice. Nam sicut ordo inferior et ordo superior sunt disparis speciei, sic ordo sacerdocii constans ex sex ordinibus precedentibus et ordo sacerdocii simplex non habens partes vel causas huiusmodi forent disparis speciei, sicut simplex et compositum. In isto videtur mihi primo notandum quod ordo sumi potest tripliciter satis equivoce. Primo analogice pro gradu in quo Deus ordinat quicquam constitui ad pulchritudinem universi, et sic quelibet res habet suum ordinem in tantum quod necesse est [p. 510] peccatores ex hoc quod dissolvunt ordinem debitum ad quem ex institucione primaria ordinantur subire ordinem puniendi, sic quod (ut multis videtur) peccatum in quantum bonum habet racionem ordinis quoad penam, quia ordo et bonum quoad consequenciam convertuntur, unde congregaciones hominum in quibus ordinacior est unio vocantur ordines secundum quandam excellenciam inferioribus exemplarem (ut dixi alias).

The third [fabrication]{g:commentum} is the [fiction]{g:ficcio} of St Thomas's testimony, that in the order of priesthood alone, or in the one subject to it, [507] the character of order is imprinted; and the place is [assigned]{g:limitare} on distinction 24 of the fourth [book] of the *Sentences*. And without doubt he treats this [teaching]{g:sentencia} there, in the second question, third article, reciting three opinions, as the doctor Bonaventure [does]; of which the first says that a [character]{g:caracter} is imprinted in the priestly order alone; the second, that [it is imprinted] in the sacred orders alone; "but this too," he says, "is nothing. Hence," he says, "since a character is a sign that distinguishes from others, it must be imprinted in all orders; and a sign of this too is that the characters must remain perpetually and never revert."[^1] And he answers three arguments to the opposite, as above. And it is clear that this is no fitting testimony either for proving that a character is imprinted only in the priestly order, since it fights directly for the opposite;[^2] and truly, since a lie is hated in the camps of bodily arms as contrary to them (as is clear in Maccabees 7), much more [so] in the camps of the schools, in which only champions of truth should fight.[^3] But [it is objected]{g:instatur} in three ways. For it seems, first, that each of these doctors posits the character as altogether indelible, whereas I said above that some character is only temporal. Here I say that neither of them is [authentic]{g:autenticus} for me. Therefore I do not allege them except in [arguing to the man]{g:argumentum ad hominem}.[^4] Yet I say that they can be glossed by understanding by "character" being, or having been, deputed to some sacred ministry in holy [church]{g:ecclesia}; and this cannot cease to be; and therefore the earlier doctor says that the character remains perpetually in office or in reward. For in the [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} there remains, after their [soldiering]{g:milicia}, [the fact] that they were deputed to a temporal [508] office, for the fulfilment of which they are perpetually rewarded; just as in the [foreknown]{g:prescitus} there remains perpetually [the fact] that they are perpetually punished for the abuse of their office; and in this sense every character remains perpetually in office or in wage.[^5] But I was speaking of the character as it signifies, [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}, [deputation]{g:deputacio} to an office; nor do I believe that this sense will be disproved from scripture or reason. For such a character is not one thing that could exist by itself, but the subject's being disposed in such a way.[^6] But secondly it seems that a character is imprinted in every man, and consequently in every order; and so kings and knights would have a glorious character corresponding to their order. Here I say that, speaking of character broadly (as above), the [conclusion]{g:conclusio} is to be granted, for God deputes to every man an office for the profit of the church. Therefore, since every man must be a servant of Christ, either by doing or by suffering, every man must bear [the mark] of his [sect]{g:secta} in one way or another.[^7] Nor do I believe that anyone can break [this point], that just as the spiritual soldiers of Christ have the character of their office, so too do kings and bodily knights (as is clear below). Yet in three sacraments a character is imprinted specially, namely in baptism, confirmation and order; and these three sacraments are unrepeatable, as the offices of the characters are indelible as to effect or fruit. For in baptism the servant of Christ receives distinction from those who are altogether [unbelievers]{g:infidelis}; in confirmation, the strengthening of his soldiering; but in order he receives a singular [assignment]{g:limitacio} above the laity to the ministry of the sacrament.[^8] And so the doctors cloak their [definitions]{g:descripcio} and apply scripture to the sense they assert, as is clear of character, of order and of the rest, which have scarcely a rind of appearance from scripture; as they say that the order of knighthood has no character, because that order is not assigned to ministering the church's sacraments differently from the laity (of which elsewhere).[^9] [509] But thirdly it is doubted how those characters of the orders make up one. And the doctors aforesaid say that they are neither [quantitative parts]{g:pars quantitativa} of order nor subjective parts, but potestative parts, as is clear [from the words] of the earlier doctor on d. 24, so that the lower orders have power from the priesthood, to which they are finally ordered. Just as, then, six is made up of its aliquot parts, which are not its quantitative parts, so the sacrament of order is made up of its parts. But that Latin seems difficult to many.[^10] For, taking the case of Peter, who took the seven orders in turn, and let him now remain with all of them, it is clear that if each of them was a sacrament because [it was] an order, by the same token each of them is now a sacrament because [it is] an order. For each of them is of the same nature as each such would be if taken by itself; now it would then be an order and a sacrament; therefore now too. For if each of them is an order, then it is either the same order in number as the later one, or another; not the same in number, since so there would be a numerical identification; and if another, then there would be seven orders; and consequently, since any multitude of the earlier ones makes up one order, there would be fourteen or more orders distinct in species.[^11] For just as a lower order and a higher order are of unlike species, so the order of priesthood consisting of the six preceding orders, and the simple order of priesthood having no such parts or causes, would be of unlike species, as simple and compound [are]. In this it seems to me first to be noted that order can be taken in three ways, quite [equivocally]{g:equivoce}. First, analogically, for the grade in which God ordains anything to be set for the beauty of the universe; and so each thing has its order, to such an extent that it is necessary that [510] sinners, from the fact that they dissolve the due order to which they are ordered by their first institution, undergo the order of being punished; so that (as it seems to many) sin, insofar as it is good, has the nature of order as regards punishment, since order and good are convertible as to [inference]{g:consequencia}; whence the congregations of men in which the union is more ordered are called orders, by a certain excellence that is an exemplar to lower ones (as I have said elsewhere).[^12]

## ¶2 · 510.9–510.26

> [p. 510] Secundo modo accipitur ordo pro gradu a Deo limitato clerico ad differenter a laycis sacramentaliter ministrandum, et sic sunt septem ordines scilicet hostiarius, ceroferius, lector, exorcista, subdyaconus, dyaconus et sacerdos (de quibus dixi superius). Illi autem ordines quandoque sumuntur limitate pro caracteribus subiectatis in anima et quandoque respiciunt signacula sensibilia extrinsecus distincta, cuiusmodi sunt rasio capitis et barbe cum similibus signis humanitus institutis. Isti autem sensibiles caracteres non sunt ordo clericalis, quia sic continue variarentur ordines ad crementum, decrementum vel aliam variacionem istorum signorum, et barbitonsor vel alius laycus frequencius conferret ordines; quod foret ridiculum. Ideo necessitantur loquentes ponere ordines subiectari in anima. Ex quo patet quod anima est persona que est ordinatus, ut puta, diaconus vel sacerdos. Iste ergo ordo manet quietus in anima corrupto episcopo.

In the second way order is taken for a grade assigned by God to a cleric for ministering sacramentally, differently from the laity; and so there are seven orders, namely porter, taper-bearer, lector, exorcist, subdeacon, deacon and [priest]{g:sacerdos} (of which I have spoken above). And these orders are sometimes taken, in a restricted sense, for the characters subjected in the soul, and sometimes they regard [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} seals outwardly distinct, such as the shaving of head and beard, with similar signs instituted by men. And these perceptible characters are not the clerical order, since so the orders would continually vary with the increase, decrease or other variation of those signs, and a barber or other layman would confer orders more often [than a bishop]; which would be ridiculous. Therefore those who speak [of order] are compelled to posit that orders are subjected in the soul. From which it is clear that the soul is the person who is ordained, say a deacon or a priest. This order, then, remains at rest in the soul when the bishop has perished.[^13]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 507.1: Again my opponent quotes St. Thomas who is also adverse to him.
- 507.20: These doctors are alleged against me, but I do not admit their authority.
- 508.7: The "stamp" or character is not a thing, but a relation. Every rank has its character.
- 508.21: Yet three sacraments: baptism, confirmation and orders imprint the character in a special way.
- 509.1: Difficulties as to the grades of orders.
- 509.13: Either all orders are identical, or there are numerous orders specifically distinct.
- 509.25: I think orders may be taken in three senses; a) as the position which God assigns to each thing.
- 510.9: b) as a rank assigned by God to clerics for ministering sacramentally;
- 510.22: and such orders are in the soul.

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** Thomas Aquinas, *In IV Sent.* d. 24, which Wyclif cites as q. 2, a. 3 (Loserth, 506.32: *Thomae lib. IV Sent.*, Opp. X, p. 442; LEDGER E21e-1). **C.** *Commentum*: the opponent's third "fabrication" (row). *Vel suo subdito*: taken as "or in [the order] subject to it", the diaconate. *Limitatur locus*: the opponent assigns the passage. *Ut doctor Bonaventura*: Thomas, like Bonaventure (502.15–503.6), recites the opinions. *Revertentur*: so printed; the sense wanted is "are never repeated" (as if *reiterentur*), but the print is translated. **T.** AA₁ *quod careat; careat extinctum* (507.10); the OCR of the page foot does not show which printed words this answers. **R.** Side-note 507.1; A in the margin at 506.32: *S. Thomas*.

[^2]: **C.** *Sicut supra*: as Bonaventure answered his objections (504.2–505.17). *Militat directe in oppositum*: Thomas's text fights for the opposite; the military figure runs on into *castris* and *pugiles*. *Nec hoc*: no more than Bonaventure's.

[^3]: **S.** *Machabeorum VII°*: most likely 1 Maccabees 7, where the enemy twice speaks *verbis pacificis in dolo* (7:10, 27) and the deceit is detested (7:18, 30) (LEDGER E21e-2). **C.** *Militarent*: subjunctive, "should fight". *Pugiles veritatis*: champions of truth.

[^4]: **C.** *Instatur tripliciter*: three objections against Wyclif's own position, answered in turn (507.20–510.26). *Caracter aliquis … tantummodo temporalis*: Wyclif's earlier statement, 444 (the ecclesiastical character in the foreknown is delible, *solummodo temporale*) (LEDGER E21e-3). *Autenticus*: carrying authority for him. *Arguendo ad hominem*: arguing from what the opponent accepts. **R.** Side-note 507.20; A in the margin at 507.20: *Triplex obieccio. Responsio. Auctoris sentencia*.

[^5]: **C.** *Prior doctor*: Bonaventure, whose words *caracter … manet … vel ad gloriam … vel ad ignominiam* (502.6–9) and *in premium vel mercedem* (502.12) Wyclif here glosses. *Post miliciam*: after their earthly service, the Pauline figure of the soldier (row *milicia; milites*). *Prescitus*: the foreknown, those whom God foreknows will be damned.

[^6]: **C.** *Ut dicit simpliciter deputacionem*: as it signifies the bare deputation to office, not a quality added to it. *Subiectum taliter se habere*: the character is a manner in which the subject stands, not a thing; side-note 508.7 calls it "a relation". **R.** Side-note 508.7.

[^7]: **C.** *Extense*: in the broad sense. *Vel agendo vel paciendo*: by doing (as the just serve) or by suffering (as the punished serve). *Portare de secta sua*: to bear [something] of his sect, i.e. to wear Christ's following as a retainer wears his lord's (row *secta*, "sect"). **R.** A in the margin at 508.13: *Responsio*.

[^8]: **C.** *Infringere*: to break, refute. *Milites spirituales … milites corporales*: "soldiers" in the Pauline figure, "knights" for the estate (row *milicia; milites*). *Initerabilia ut officia caracterum indelebilia*: the three sacraments cannot be repeated, as the characters' offices cannot be blotted out as to effect or fruit. *Limitacio*: "assignment" to office. **T.** A *indelibilia* (508.24). **R.** Side-note 508.21; A in the margin at 508.21: *In tribus sacramentis caracter imponitur*.

[^9]: **C.** *Palliant descripciones*: they cloak their definitions. *Corticem apparencie*: a rind (bark) of plausibility. *Ordinem militarem*: the order of knighthood, the estate. *De quo alibi*: not identified.

[^10]: **S.** *Prioris doctoris super d. XXIVª*: Bonaventure on IV *Sent.* d. 24; Loserth gives no reference (LEDGER E21e-4); cf. Thomas, ibid. (Loserth, 509.5: "Thomae lib. IV. Sent. l. c. pag. 424"; LEDGER E21e-5). **C.** *Partes subiective*: the species under a genus; *partes potestative*: the parts of a power, the scholastic potential parts. *Partibus suis aliquotis*: the parts that divide six evenly (one, two, three). *Illud latinum*: that statement. **T.** As printed (nothing lost on the plate), *ut patet prioris doctoris* lacks a governing word (such as *ex dictis*); "[from the words]" is our supply. AA₁ lack *ut … potestatem* (509.4). **R.** Side-note 509.1; A in the margin at 509.7: *Hec est sentencia auctoris de potestate et caractere ordinis continue infra*.

[^11]: **C.** *Signato Petro*: Peter taken as the case (A in the margin at 509.10: *Casus*). *Vicissim*: in turn. *Vel alius non idem in numero*: punctuated after *alius*, taking *non idem in numero* as the rejection of the first limb; the print's comma could instead join *alius non idem in numero*. The argument: if each order was a sacrament when received, it still is; then either each lower order is numerically the priesthood (impossible), or there are seven orders and, with the priesthood made up of them, fourteen or more. **R.** Side-note 509.13.

[^12]: **C.** *Satis equivoce*: the three senses share little more than the name. *Ordinem puniendi*: the order of being punished. *Ordo et bonum … convertuntur*: order and good are convertible, each following from the other. *Exemplarem*: serving as an exemplar to lower congregations; the religious orders take their name from this. *Ut dixi alias*: not identified. **T.** B *obligantur* for *ordinantur* (510.1); B *habet ordinem* (509.31). **R.** Side-note 509.25; A and B in the margin at 509.29: *Nota quod ordo tripliciter accipitur*.

[^13]: **C.** *Ceroferius*: the taper-bearer, the acolyte. *Limitate*: in a restricted sense. *Subiectatis in anima*: having the soul as their subject. *Rasio capitis et barbe*: the tonsure and the shaving of the beard. *Frequencius conferret ordines*: "[than a bishop]" supplied for sense. *Corrupto episcopo*: taken as "when the bishop [who ordained him] has perished", so that the order does not depend on the ordaining bishop's continuing; it may instead mean "when the bishop's [body] has decayed", his own order remaining in his soul. **R.** Side-notes 510.9, 510.22.
