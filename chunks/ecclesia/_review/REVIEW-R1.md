## Verdicts

**02-b (28.8–32.8).** Sound. The construal of the dense expository-syllogism argument and the third reply is careful, and its doubts carry C notes. One minor-premiss *sed* is rendered "but" (#1). The rest is glossary drift since drafting: *laureola*, the *viacio* key, and untagged first occurrences. N. 5 still has an open `[verify]` (Gregory), so the chunk is not publishable until the ledger clears it.

**02-f (42.10–47.5).** One real text error (#6). The print's *plus* (46.24) was lost at a spurious paragraph split, and the English and n. 16 then "supply" it as a conjecture. N. 2 misnumbers Wyclif's senses of faith, and three notes present OCR scars as readings of the print. Four tag keys are malformed. The connectives, *formido*, *confuse* and the canonization argument are rendered well.

**04-b (72.11–76.24).** Sound in construal. The Richard of St Victor, Augustine, Chrysostom, John and Jeremiah quotations all open and close at the print's italics (checked on the plates). The problems are a dropped *in* in Rom. 8:9, the CONTEXTUAL *sentencia* without its C note, *capitales* against its new row, three malformed tag keys, and a stray "[?]".

**05-d (104.18–108.17).** The argument is construed accurately, with the quantifier order kept ("each with each") and the *sed*/minor-premiss "now"s correct. Its weakness is quotation extent. Measured against the plates, six quotations are mis-bounded: two italic runs split or truncated, two roman stretches quoted, and the "eight media" passage quoted on the wrong words. Two notes misstate what is settled on the plate.

**Pattern.** Class 2 is nearly clean (one slip in about 120 sentences). There are no class-3 errors. The serious items are textual (#6) and mechanical. First, tag keys with spaces or capitals pass `lint.py` unchecked and would not render. A grep finds about 278 single-line instances across `chunks/ecclesia/`, not counting keys broken across lines, so this needs one scripted fix plus a lint rule, not per-chunk edits. Second, quotation extents were set by sense rather than by the print's italics. That predates the brief-45 rule, so other early chunks probably share it.
