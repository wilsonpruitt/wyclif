# *De ecclesia*, chapter 19, part 1: whether the foreknown hold the orders and offices of the church; the character of orders

## ¶1 · 441.27–442.25

> [p. 441] Supposito ex fide scripture elaborata a sanctis doctoribus quod solum predestinati sint membra sancte matris ecclesie, restat dubitandum ulterius si [p. 442] presciti gerant ordines et officia illius ecclesie. Et videtur ex dictis quod non; nam Deus summe sapiens non daret potestatem inhabili. Sed nullus talis habet potestatem huiusmodi si non per Deum, ergo nullus talis habet huiusmodi potestatem. Est autem inhabilis, quia in mortali peccato necessario dampnabili continue, ut suppono ex dictis XXVI° capitulo libri Vⁱ. Confirmatur ex Decreto, XIª, q. III, ubi ex dicto beati Gregorii sic habetur: Ipse ligandi atque solvendi potestate se privat qui hanc pro suis voluntatibus et non subditorum moribus exercet. Si ergo pro leviori peccato habens potestatem ordinis ipsa privatur, multo magis pro graviori peccato ipse qui reciperet huiusmodi potestatem ab ea excluditur; culpandus itaque foret Christus, si ecclesie sue preficeret membrum diaboli tam inhabile et nocivum. Non ergo habet talis caracterem indelebilem, ut sit de exercitu christiano. Hic videtur mihi indubie quod nullus prescitus est pars vel gerens officium tamquam de sancta matre ecclesia; habet tamen intra illam ecclesiam ad sui dampnacionem et ecclesie utilitatem certa officia ministrandi, ut patet de Scarioth et multis eius vicariis, et ad tantum Deus eligit tales dans eis ad tale officium potestatem. Nam Johannis VI°, 71 scribitur: Nonne ego duodecim vos elegi et unus ex vobis diabolus est?

Supposing, [from the faith of scripture]{g:ex} as worked out by the holy doctors, that only the [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} are [members]{g:membrum} of holy [mother]{g:mater} [church]{g:ecclesia}, it remains to ask further whether [442] the [foreknown]{g:prescitus} hold the orders and offices of that church.[^1] And it seems from what has been said that they do not; for God, supremely wise, would not give power to one [unqualified]{g:inhabilis}. Now no such [man] has power of this kind if not through God; therefore no such [man] has power of this kind. And he is unqualified, because he is continually in mortal sin, necessarily damnable, as I suppose from what was said in the twenty-sixth chapter of book 5.[^2] It is confirmed from the Decretum, Causa 11, question 3, where, from a saying of blessed Gregory, it is had thus: "He deprives himself of the power of binding and loosing who exercises it according to his own wishes and not according to the conduct of his subjects." If therefore for a lighter sin one having the power of order is deprived of it, much more for a graver sin is he who would receive power of this kind excluded from it; Christ would therefore be to blame if he set over his church a member of the devil so unqualified and harmful. Such a man therefore does not have the indelible [character]{g:carecter}, so as to be of the Christian army.[^3] Here it seems to me without doubt that no foreknown [man] is a part, or bears an office as being of holy mother church; yet within that church he has, to his own damnation and the church's profit, certain offices of ministering, as is clear of Iscariot and his many vicars; and to that extent God chooses such men, giving them power for such an office.[^4] For in John 6:71 it is written: "Have not I chosen you twelve? And one of you is a devil."

## ¶2 · 442.26–444.5

> [p. 442] Super quo Augustinus, Omelia XXVª, dubitat quomodo Christus elegit dyabolum, et respondet quod electus est iste de quo nolente et nesciente magnum aliquid boni fieret. Hoc, inquit, est proprium Dei, iniquis contrarium quod, sicut iniqui male utuntur [p. 443] bonis Dei operibus, sic econtra Deus bene utitur malis operibus iniquorum. Et ponendo multa exempla conclusionaliter sic concludit: Omnibus ergo bonis Dei, ne per singula currere longum sit, male utitur malus. Et hinc sepe dixi quod existens in mortali continue abutitur bonis Dei, et per consequens, quicquid sic fecerit peccat mortaliter; econtra de malis: malis malorum, inquit Augustinus, bene utitur bonus. Et quid tam bonum, ut Deus, de quo Marci X°, 18 dicitur: Nemo bonus nisi solus Deus? Quanto ergo ille melior tanto melius utitur malis nostris. Quid Iuda peius inter apostolos cui loculi commissi sunt et dispensacio pauperum distributa? Sed quia ingratus tanto beneficio et honori accepit pecuniam, perdidit iusticiam: mortuus tradidit vitam; quem ut discipulus secutus, ut inimicus persecutus est; toto hoc malo lude bene usus est dominus, cum prodicione eius dampnabili nos redemit. Sic ergo habuit ludas officium in ecclesia, non de illa, sicut stercus et apostemata habent officia in humano corpore, non de illo. Modo, inquit Augustinus, corpus Christi mixtum est tamquam area; sed novit Deus qui sunt eius, sicut tu nosti quid trituras, quia ibi est latens massa, nec consumit trituracio quod purgatura est ventilacio. Sed difficultas est utrum tales presciti habent caracterem ordinis, utrum sint sacerdotes episcopi vel prelati. Quoad primum est dissensio inter multos de quiditate caracteris. Aliqui enim dicunt quod est qualitas de secunda specie qualitatis sicut naturalis potencia. Alii autem dicunt quod non est per se in genere sed per reduccionem, sicut alie veritates consimiles extra genus, ita quod caracter sit deputacio [p. 444] passiva creature racionalis ad serviendum Deo et ecclesie secundum gradum ordinis dignitatis; nec contendo circa quiditatem talis caracteris, cum certus sum quod non sit res ut serta regis vel aliquod signum inpressum quod poterit per se esse.

On which Augustine, in Homily 25, asks how Christ chose a devil, and answers that this one was chosen through whom, unwilling and unknowing, some great good would be done. "This," he says, "is proper to God, contrary to the wicked: that, as the wicked use the good works of God [443] badly, so conversely God uses well the bad works of the wicked."[^5] And setting out many examples, he [concludes]{g:concludere} by way of conclusion thus: "All the good things of God, therefore (lest it be long to run through them one by one), the bad man uses badly." And hence I have often said that one who is in mortal [sin] continually abuses the goods of God, and consequently whatever he so does, he sins mortally. Conversely of evils: "The evils of the evil," says Augustine, "the good man uses well. And what is so good as God", of whom it is said in Mark 10:18, "None is good but God alone"? "The better he is, therefore, the better he uses our evils. What was worse than Judas among the apostles, to whom the purse was committed and the dispensing for the poor assigned? But because, ungrateful for so great a benefit and honour, he took money, he lost justice; dead, he betrayed life; whom he followed as a disciple, him he persecuted as an enemy. All this evil of Judas the Lord used well", since by his damnable betrayal he redeemed us.[^6] So therefore Judas had an office in the church, not of it, as dung and [abscesses]{g:apostema} have offices in the human body, not of it. "At present," says Augustine, "the body of Christ is mixed, like a threshing-floor; but God knows who are his, as you know what you thresh, since there is a mass lying hidden there, and the threshing does not consume what the winnowing is to purge."[^7] But the difficulty is whether such foreknown [men] have the character of order, whether they are [priests]{g:sacerdos}, [bishops]{g:episcopus} or [prelates]{g:prelatus}. As to the first there is dissension among many about the [quiddity]{g:quiditas} of the character. For some say that it is a quality of the second species of quality, like a natural capacity. But others say that it is not of itself in a category but by reduction, like other similar truths outside a category, so that the character is a passive deputation [444] of a rational creature to serve God and the church according to the degree of the order of dignity.[^8] Nor do I contend about the quiddity of such a character, since I am certain that it is not a thing, like a king's crown, or some sign imprinted that could exist of itself.[^9]

## ¶3 · 444.6–445.29

> [p. 444] Tenendo ergo secundam viam de quiditate caracteris dico ulterius quod alius est necessario delebilis, ut caracter ecclesiasticus in prescitis, eo quod non ultra diem iudicii durabit in eis officium dignitatis. In predestinatis autem oportet esse caracterem omnino indelebilem, cum dignitas sequendi agnum mutuo illuminandi conbeatum et percipiendi fructum vivificum non posset deleri in eis, et de tanto habent caracterem ordinis vel caracterem baptismalem. Et patet quod predestinati et presciti secundum diversitatem officii equivoce habent caracterem. Et correspondenter dicitur quod equivoce habent ordinem, episcopatum vel aliam ecclesiasticam prelaciam, quia presciti ascribuntur ad tale officium solummodo temporale, non ut mereantur beatitudinem, sed ut mitigando penam per accidens prosint ecclesie. Et ita conceditur quod Scarioth et eius vicarii erant apostoli, presbiteri, episcopi et prelati, habentes proporcionaliter ad suum officium potestatem, et ad tantum aput Deum sunt habiles. Quod autem ludas fuit apostolus, patet Matthei X°, ubi inter duodecim quos Christus elegerat computatur, et beatus Petrus testatur quod sortitus est sortem ministerii apostolatus et quod de illo scriptum est Psalmo CVIII°,: 8: Fiant dies eius pauci et episcopatum eius accipiat alter; et sic cum singulum istorum sit nomen officii, patet quod non sequitur: ludas est apostolus, episcopus vel sacerdos, ergo est membrum vel de sancta ecclesia. [p. 445] Sufficit quod sit in illa tamquam palea temporaliter ministrans finaliter comburenda. Unde non dicimus quod ad serviendum Deo in inferno perpetuo paciendo oportet servum indelebiliter habere caracterem, nisi forte equivocetur de caractere bestie quam meminit Johannis Apoc. XIX°, 20, ubi dicitur: Seduxit eos qui acceperunt caracterem bestie et qui adoraverunt ymaginem eius. Et de isto caractere fit mencio Apocalypsis XIII° et XIV°. Caracterem autem transferimus in bonum pro scriptura predestinacionis in libro vite (ut loquitur Christus Luce X°, 20 et Apocalypsis XIII°, 8). Quandoque vero accipitur caracter pro aggregato ex predestinacione et limitacione per signum sacramentale, et sic secundum limitacionem signi caracteris est varie respondendum. Nam caracter predestinatorum et caracter prescitorum qui sunt de synagoga sathane (ut loquitur scriptura Apoc. II°) est indelebilis, quia primus est calculus candidus vel nomen novum, quod nemo scit nisi qui accipit (ut loquitur scriptura Apoc. II°, 9). Sed secundus est nomen finalis impenitencie vel comitans caracter bestie, caracter autem temporalis quando est limitacio ad officium temporale; et in talibus terminis magistralibus oportet responsalem quoad significacionem et quiditatem in principio stabiliri. Caracter enim communiter in malum sumitur in scriptura, sed aliter loquimur hic de caractere agni quoad dignitatem activam ministerii ad quam assignantur predestinati differenter a servis dyaboli in ordine vel baptismo.

Holding therefore the second way about the quiddity of the character, I say further that one [character] is necessarily delible, as the ecclesiastical character in the foreknown, because the office of dignity will not last in them beyond the day of judgement.[^10] But in the predestinate the character must be altogether indelible, since the dignity of following the Lamb, of mutually enlightening one's fellow-blessed, and of receiving the life-giving fruit could not be blotted out in them; and to that extent they have the character of order or the baptismal character. And it is clear that the predestinate and the foreknown, according to the diversity of office, have the character equivocally. And correspondingly it is said that they have order, the episcopate or another ecclesiastical prelacy equivocally, because the foreknown are enrolled for such an office as a merely temporal one, not that they may merit blessedness, but that, by mitigating their punishment, they may by accident profit the church.[^11] And so it is granted that Iscariot and his vicars were apostles, [presbyters]{g:presbiter}, bishops and prelates, having power in proportion to their office, and to that extent they are qualified before God. And that Judas was an apostle is clear from Matthew 10, where he is counted among the twelve whom Christ had chosen; and blessed Peter testifies that he "had obtained part of the ministry of the apostleship", and that of him it is written in Psalm 108:8: "May his days be few: and his bishopric let another take". And so, since each of these is the name of an office, it is clear that it does not [follow]{g:sequitur}: "Judas is an apostle, bishop or priest; therefore he is a member of holy church, or of it."[^12] [445] It suffices that he be in it as chaff, ministering for a time, to be burned in the end. Whence we do not say that, to serve God in hell by suffering perpetually, a servant must have a character indelibly, unless perhaps "character" is used equivocally of the character of the beast which John mentions in Apocalypse 19:20, where it is said: "He seduced them who received the character of the beast and who adored his image." And of this character mention is made in Apocalypse 13 and 14.[^13] But we transfer "character" to the good, for the writing of predestination in the book of life (as Christ speaks in Luke 10:20, and Apocalypse 13:8). And sometimes "character" is taken for the aggregate of predestination and assignment through a sacramental sign; and so, according to the assignment of the sign of the character, the answer must vary.[^14] For the character of the predestinate and the character of the foreknown who are of the synagogue of Satan (as scripture speaks, Apocalypse 2) is indelible: for the first is the white counter or new name, "which no man knoweth but he that receiveth it" (as scripture speaks, Apocalypse 2:9); but the second is the name of final impenitence, or the accompanying character of the beast; and the character is temporal when it is an assignment to a temporal office. And in such masters' terms the respondent must be fixed at the outset as to signification and quiddity. For "character" is commonly taken in a bad sense in scripture; but here we speak otherwise, of the character of the Lamb, as to the active dignity of ministry to which the predestinate are assigned, differently from the servants of the devil, in order or in baptism.[^15]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 441.27: Whether the foreknown hold orders and offices in the Church.
- 442.18: None such is a part or officer of the Church, but he has a duty of ministering within the Church.
- 442.26: God uses the wicked for good.
- 443.26: The difficulty is whether the foreknown have "stamp" (caracter) of orders.
- 444.6: Predestinate and foreknown have the stamp in different ways.
- 445.3: Various senses in which the "stamp" is spoken of.
- 445.16: The "stamp" of the predestinate is a white stone with a new name: of the foreknown the mark of the beast; and there is the temporal "stamp" marking for a temporal office.

[^1]: **C.** *Gerant ordines et officia*: "hold" orders and offices, as one bears an office. *Prescitus*: one whom God foreknows will be damned (GLOSSARY). **R.** A in the margin at 441.27: *Utrum presciti gerant ordines et officia ecclesie*.

[^2]: **C.** The argument: God gives no power to the unqualified; no one has such power except from God; therefore the unqualified have none. *Est autem inhabilis* supplies the premiss that the foreknown man is unqualified. **S.** *XXVI° capitulo libri Vⁱ*: Wyclif's own cross-reference, chapter 26 of "book 5", perhaps of his *Summa* (LEDGER E19a-1, to be checked). **R.** A in the margin at 442.4: *Responsio*.

LX*; the modern form is ours; LEDGER E19a-2, editor), from Gregory, *Homiliae in Evangelia* 26.5 (LEDGER E19a-3, to be checked). **T.** At 442.13–14 the manuscripts read *huius potestatem*; Loserth's *huiusmodi* is translated. **G.** *Caracter*: the "character" of orders (GLOSSARY *carecter*); Loserth's side-notes say "stamp".

[^4]: **C.** *Hic videtur mihi*: Wyclif's answer begins. *Tamquam de sancta matre ecclesia*: as one belonging to the church, against *intra illam ecclesiam*, "within" it. *Scarioth et … eius vicariis*: Judas Iscariot and those who succeed to his part. **R.** A in the margin at 442.18: *Responsio*.

[^5]: **S.** Augustine, *Tractatus in Iohannem* 27.10 (Loserth: *In Joh. Evang. cap. VI, tract. XXVII*, *Opp.* III, ii, p. 505; LEDGER E19a-4, editor); Wyclif cites it as "Homily 25". John 6:71 (DR, Wyclif's word order differs slightly). **R.** A in the margin at 442.26: *Augustinus*; at 442.29: *Sicut mali bonis abutuntur, sic boni malis bene utuntur*.

[^6]: **S.** Mark 10:18 as Wyclif gives it has *solus*, the wording of Luke 18:19 (DR "None is good but God alone"); Mark has *unus*. **C.** Augustine's words are taken to resume at *Quanto ergo* and to end at *usus est dominus*; *cum prodicione … redemit* is taken as Wyclif's. *In mortali*: in mortal [sin]. *Ingratus*: here a person ungrateful for a benefit (GLOSSARY *ingratus*). **T.** At 443.7–8 B reads *contra de bonis*; at 443.11 AA₁ omit *ille*; at 443.14 they read *sed quod*.

[^7]: **S.** Augustine, *Tractatus in Iohannem* 27.11, continuing (LEDGER E19a-5, editor); 2 Timothy 2:19, DR "the Lord knoweth who are his". **T.** Loserth notes that Augustine's text reads *tamquam in area* (443.22). **C.** *Modo*: Augustine's "at present", in this life.

[^8]: **C.** *Secunda species qualitatis*: in Aristotle's *Categories* 8 the second species of quality is natural capacity or incapacity (LEDGER E19a-6, to be checked). *Per reduccionem*: placed in a category only by reduction to something that is in it. *Deputacio passiva*: being deputed to a service. **T.** At 443.27 A reads *utrum sit*. **R.** A in the margin at 443.26: *Utrum presciti habent caracterem ordinis*; A and B in the margin at 443.33: *quid est caracter*.

[^9]: **C.** *Serta*: a crown or wreath; Loserth's note at 444.4: *corona ducalis, regalis sive imperialis*.

[^10]: **C.** *Secundam viam*: the second opinion, the character as a deputation. *Alius*: "one [character]", taken with *caracter*; the predestinate's character follows. **T.** At 444.6 B reads *secundam vitam*. **R.** A in the margin at 444.6: *Character ordinis est delebilis in prescitis, sed non sic in predestinatis*.

[^11]: **S.** Following the Lamb: Apocalypse 14:4. **C.** *Conbeatum*: one's fellow-blessed. *Equivoce*: the one word "character" names two different things. *Solummodo temporale*: taken with *officium*, an office that is only temporal. **T.** At 444.16 B omits *caracterem … habent*.

[^14]: **S.** Luke 10:20, DR "your names are written in heaven"; Apocalypse 13:8, "the book of life of the Lamb". **C.** *Limitacio*: assignment to an office (GLOSSARY *limitare*, "to assign"), here through a sacramental sign.
