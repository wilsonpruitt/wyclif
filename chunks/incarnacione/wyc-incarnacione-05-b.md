# *De benedicta incarnacione*, chapter 5, part 2: that the blessed cannot cease to be blessed, and the Word would sin with the humanity

## ¶1 · 68.5–70.3

> [p. 68] Item quilibet beatus in celo est tam insolubiliter Deo connexus quod repugnat illi statui miseriam peccati in anima succedere vel⁵ coesse: sed Christus ab instanti unionis fuit perfeccius beatus secundum animam quam alius, cum fuerit sanctus sanctorum: ergo multo magis repugnat anime sue peccatum succedere vel⁶ coesse: sed si⁶ dimissio foret possibilis,⁷ hoc potuit contigisse:⁸ ergo illa⁹ dimissio non fuit possibilis. Maior probatur ex hoc. Omnes beati sunt immortales et impeccabiles, sicut omnes rectiloqui¹⁰ confitentur: non quia non possunt peccare, quia multi prius tempore suo peccant; sed sunt in gracia confirmacionis et glorie, cui repugnat peccatum vel miseriam coexistere, vel post illam succedere: patet ergo quod beatitudo est qualitas implicans securitatem perpetuitatis pleni gaudii et exclusionis miserie. [Eciam¹¹¹² securitas perpetuitatis pleni gaudii¹² et exclusionis miserie est per seipsum securitas talis, cum sit deveniendum ad ultimum formale, quo formaliter et non alio est quis securus de perpetuitate pleni gaudii et exclusionis miserie. Sed contradiccionem implicat tali coexistere in subiecto suo vel post illam in ipso subiecto suo miseriam succedere: igitur etcetera.]¹¹ Unde omnes theologi concorditer confitentur, quod¹³ contradiccionem claudit aliquem esse¹³ vel fuisse beatum cum hoc quod fiat postmodum non beatus. Nec valet dicere quod beatitudo per accidens sit beatitudo, quia ipsa est ultima forma abstracta, qua quis est beatus formaliter. Ideo, si illa non sit per se beatitudo, non superest quid aliud per se foret. [p. 69] Sicut ergo iusticia non potest esse non iusticia et de formis similibus;¹ sic status beatitudinis non potest esse non huiusmodi² status. Unde notum est methaphisicis quod tales forme abstracte non suscipiunt sic³ predicacionem per accidens ut concreta: si enim beatitudo sit per accidens beatitudo—cum non sit processus in infinitum; sed, cum omne per accidens sit reducibile ad aliquid⁴ per se, est devenire ad ultimum formale, quo quis est beatus—et illud per se et non per accidens est beatitudo: et de tali est michi sermo. Confirmatur. Si A qualitas⁵ sit nunc beatitudo et potest manere et⁶ desinere esse beatitudo; per idem post esse potest reincipere esse beatitudo. Quo concesso, patet quod preter A, quod non sufficit denominare hominem beatum, requiritur forma superaddita; et sic non A, sed forma sibi adveniens,⁷ foret beatitudo. Patet ergo, quod cum beatitudo et confirmacio non possunt esse, nisi sint perpetue; nec possunt esse nisi huiusmodi quod, eo ipso quod insunt, reponunt hominem in statu cui repugnat miseriam succedere vel coesse; nec dependet beatitudo pocius a futuro, quam qualitates alie absolute;⁸ tunc enim non forent beati de sua beatitudine securati. Minor ergo argumenti patet generaliter omni professori fidei incarnacionis: cum anima Christi ab instanti sue creacionis fuit plena omni genere carismatum, habens clariorem⁹ visionem divine essencie quam alia creatura, et per consequens plenam fruicionem ad tantam noticiam naturaliter¹⁰ consequentem. Cum ergo ‘vita eterna sit¹¹ sic cognoscere Deum Patrem ac Dominum Iesum Christum,’ ut testatur Veritas Ioh. 17; patet quod anima Christi ab instanti sue creacionis perpetue est beata.¹² Nec audivi hoc negatum ab aliquo, cum palam sequitur ex incarnacione. Si enim homo solum¹³ intelligit quod intelligit mens¹³ [p. 70] ypostatice copulata; per locum a sufficienti similitudine spiritus Iesu cognoscit quidquid cognoverat¹ ipse Iesus specialiter cum sit personaliter Verbum Dei.

Again, every blessed one in heaven is so [inextricably]{g:insolubiliter} joined to God that it [is incompatible]{g:repugnare} with that [state]{g:status} that the misery of sin should succeed or coexist in the soul: now Christ from the instant of the [union]{g:unio} was more perfectly blessed as to the soul than any other, since he was the holy of holies: therefore much more is it incompatible with his soul that sin should succeed or coexist: now if the [laying-aside]{g:dimissio} were possible, this could have happened: therefore that laying-aside was not possible.[^1] The major is proved from this. All the blessed are immortal and impeccable, as all [who speak correctly]{g:rectiloqui} confess: not because they cannot sin, for many sin earlier, in their own time; but they are in the grace of confirmation and of glory, with which it is incompatible that sin or misery should coexist, or succeed after it: it is clear, therefore, that blessedness is a quality implying security of the perpetuity of full joy and of the exclusion of misery.[^2] Hence all theologians confess in concord that "someone is or was blessed, together with his afterwards being made not blessed" [implies a contradiction]{g:claudit-contradictionem}. Nor does it avail to say that blessedness is blessedness [by accident]{g:per-accidens}, for it is the ultimate abstract form by which someone is formally blessed. So, if it is not blessedness [of itself]{g:per-se}, nothing else remains that would be [so] of itself.[^3] [69] As, therefore, justice cannot be not-justice, and so of similar forms, so the state of blessedness cannot be not such a state. Hence it is known to metaphysicians that such abstract forms do not admit [predication]{g:predicacio} by accident in the way that concretes do: for if blessedness is blessedness by accident (since there is no process to infinity, but, since everything by accident is reducible to something of itself, one must come to an ultimate formal [principle] by which someone is blessed), that too is blessedness of itself and not by accident: and it is of such that I am speaking.[^4] This is confirmed. If the quality A is now blessedness, and can remain and cease to be blessedness, by the same token, after its being [so], it can begin again to be blessedness.[^5] Which being granted, it is clear that besides A, which does not suffice to [name]{g:denominare} a man blessed, a superadded form is required; and so not A, but a form coming to it, would be blessedness. It is clear, therefore, that since blessedness and confirmation cannot be unless they are perpetual, neither can they be except of such a kind that, by the very fact that they are present, they set a man in a state with which it is incompatible that misery should succeed or coexist; nor does blessedness depend on the future any more than other absolute qualities do; for then the blessed would not be made secure of their blessedness.[^6] The minor of the argument, therefore, is clear generally to every professor of the faith of the incarnation: since the soul of Christ from the instant of its creation was full of every kind of [charisms]{g:carismata}, having a clearer vision of the divine [essence]{g:essencia} than any other [creature]{g:creatura}, and consequently full enjoyment following naturally on so great a knowledge.[^7] Since, therefore, "eternal life is thus to know God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ", as the Truth testifies, John 17, it is clear that the soul of Christ from the instant of its creation is perpetually blessed.[^8] Nor have I heard this denied by anyone, since it follows openly from the incarnation. For if a man understands only what the mind [70] hypostatically joined understands, by the topic from sufficient likeness the spirit of Jesus knows whatever Jesus himself had known, especially since he is personally the Word of God.[^9]

## ¶2 · 70.4–70.32

> [p. 70] Ex istis colligitur talis racio. Tam determinatum et necessarium est ipsum hominem fuisse assumptum, quam necessarium est mundum vel aliquid aliud prefuisse: sed non potuit fuisse assumptus nisi fuisset exinde beatus: ergo tam necessarium est ipsum fuisse beatum quam necessarium est aliquid prefuisse: sed pretericiones huiusmodi non possunt solvi: ergo nec beatitudo vel assumpcio Domini nostri Iesu Christi. 2°. confirmatur ex hoc; quod anima Christi actu elicito vidit clare in Verbo omnia preterita vel futura; et per consequens clare vidit perpetuitatem sue beatitudinis; et ultra, cum illa visio² non potuit defuisse vel verti in ignoranciam aut³ aliud genus entis; sequitur quod illa anima non potuit desinere esse beata vel Verbo ypostatice copulata. 3°. confirmatur ex testimonio Leonis Pape in quodam sermone dicentis: ‘tanta fuit unicio⁴ Dei et hominis ut nec supplicio posset⁵ dirimi nec morte distingui.’ Nec obest isti sentencie divina dispensacio, qua voluit Verbum suum ad tempus esse corpus, non plene sed quasi arraliter tunc⁶ beatum, et per consequens esse animam secundum actuacionem corporis et racionem, qua⁷ ipsam respicit, quantumlibet dolorosam.⁸ Nam ille homo est multarum naturarum quelibet,⁹ secundum quarum unam¹⁰ fuit ad tempus abiectus viator, et simul secundum aliam perfectissimus comprehensor: et sic simul summe gaudens et summe tristis secundum vires et raciones dispares, ut¹¹ ostenditur consequenter: et dico¹¹ quod arraliter habuit in via dotes corporis, cum dote subtilitatis exivit sponsus de clauso utero,¹² dote agilitatis ambulavit super aquas, dote incorruptibilitatis et claritatis in transfiguracione emicuit.¹³

From these such an argument is gathered. It is as determined and necessary that "this man has been assumed" as it is necessary that "the world, or something else, has been before": now he could not have been assumed unless he had thereupon been blessed: therefore it is as necessary that "he has been blessed" as it is necessary that "something has been before": now pasts of this kind cannot be loosed: therefore neither [can] the blessedness or the [assumption]{g:assumpcio} of our Lord Jesus Christ.[^10] Second, this is confirmed from this, that the soul of Christ by an elicited act saw clearly in the Word all things past or future; and consequently saw clearly the perpetuity of its blessedness; and further, since that vision could not have been lacking, or be turned into ignorance or another genus of being, it follows that that soul could not cease to be blessed or [hypostatically]{g:ypostatice} joined to the Word. Third, this is confirmed from the testimony of Pope Leo in a certain sermon, saying: "So great was the uniting of God and man that it could neither be severed by punishment nor divided by death."[^11] Nor does the divine [dispensation]{g:dispensacio} stand against this [teaching]{g:sentencia}, by which he willed his Word for a time to be a body not fully but, as it were, [by way of earnest]{g:arraliter} then blessed, and consequently to be a soul, as to the actuation of the body and the aspect under which it regards it, however sorrowful.[^12] For that man is each of many natures, as to one of which he was for a time a cast-down [wayfarer]{g:viator}, and at the same time as to another a most perfect [comprehensor]{g:comprehensor}: and so at the same time supremely rejoicing and supremely sorrowful as to diverse powers and aspects, as is shown in what follows: and I say that he had by way of earnest on [the way]{g:via} the [dowries]{g:dotes} of the body: with the dowry of subtlety the bridegroom went out from the closed womb, with the dowry of agility he walked upon the waters, with the dowry of incorruptibility and of brightness he shone out in the transfiguration.[^13]

## ¶3 · 70.33–71.15

> [p. 70] 3°. per deducens ad impossibile patet idem.¹⁴ Nam si [p. 71] humanitas ypostatice copulata posset dimitti et sic dampnari pro peccato, et tam caro quam anima est personaliter ipsum Verbum; sequitur quod Verbum Dei peccare¹ poterit et dampnari; quod nephas² est dicere. Patet consequencia supposito fundamento, cum arguitur ab inferiori ad suum superius personale, cui per se competit accio personalis. Sequitur ergo—hec humanitas potest peccare: hec humanitas est persona Verbi: ergo persona Verbi potest peccare—. Nam persona Verbi nedum est hec³ humanitas, sed aliquid aliud³ quia divinitas; ideo est superior⁴ vel communior quam⁴ est hec⁵ humanitas. Unde, sicut sequitur quod Filius Dei fuit natus humanitus, conversatus,⁶ passus, mortuus, et sepultus, et ascendit, eo quod humanitas, que fuit dictus Filius, sic se habuit; ita eciam sequeretur quod Verbum peccaret, si humanitas, que fuit dictum Verbum, peccaret.

Third, the same is clear through [one] leading to the impossible.[^14] For if [71] the [humanity]{g:humanitas} hypostatically joined could be [laid aside]{g:dimittere} and so be damned for sin, and both the flesh and the soul are personally the Word himself, [it follows]{g:sequitur} that the Word of God could sin and be damned; which it is impious to say. The [inference]{g:consequencia} is clear, the foundation being supposed, since it is argued from an inferior to its personal superior, to which personal action belongs of itself. It follows, therefore: "This humanity can sin: this humanity is the [person]{g:persona} of the Word: therefore the person of the Word can sin." For the person of the Word is [not only]{g:nedum} this humanity but something else, since [it is] the [divinity]{g:divinitas}; so it is superior to, or more common than, this humanity.[^15] Hence, as it follows that the Son of God was born [humanly]{g:humanitus}, lived among [men], suffered, died, and was buried, and ascended, because the humanity, which was the said Son, was so, so also it would follow that the Word sinned, if the humanity, which was the said Word, sinned.

## ¶4 · 71.16–72.18

> [p. 71] Confirmatur ex testimonio moderniorum⁷ dicencium quod hec humanitas non posset⁸ ypostatice copulari peccando nisi Verbum exinde peccaret;⁹ et, quod plus est,¹⁰ racione unionis prioris Verbum denominatum est secundum moderniores jacuisse pro triduo in sepulcro, et ad sensum consimilem peccasse in casu, quo¹¹ natura dimissa pro tunc peccaverit. Que, rogo, causa diversitatis sensus Spiritus¹² Sancti in isto articulo fidei ‘Christus descendit ad inferos, fuit mortuus et sepultus;’ hoc est, non illa persona, sed pars eius pro tempore quo¹³ non fuit pars vel ypostatice copulata,¹⁴ sic se habuit; quin ad sensum simillimum concederet, quod natura dimissa et postmodum reassumpta, si peccaret interim, denominaret Filium sic peccare?¹⁵ Ponatur ergo quod in die parasceues anima Christi¹⁶ perfecerit liberacionem patrum a limbo,¹⁶ et pro tunc agitetur¹⁷ corpus a demone multos homines conquassando et quod in [p. 72] sancto¹ sabbato †reuniatur² anima corpori suspensa ypostatica unione, et peccet ante nonam adulterio, homicidio, blasfemia, et omni genere peccatorum; et post nonam peniteat, et procreet legitime, ac juste condempnet multos ad mortem, assumpta humanitate in prima sabbati in unitatem suppositi Verbi Dei: quem casum moderni admittunt et magis mirabiles tanquam necessario possibiles; in quo casu videtur juxta glosam quam dant³ articulis fidei quod⁴ omnes dicte operaciones in triduo redundarent in Christum vel aliter fingeret⁵ ex instinctu Spiritus Sancti sensum impossibilem et insolitum. Certum est itaque Christum non istos homines suffocasse,⁶ virginitatis laureolam⁷ ex procreacione seminis perdidisse, irregularitatem incurrisse, vel remansisse in eo vestigia peccatorum. Et conformi racione nec iacuit sepultus nec descendit ad inferos, nisi conformi racione denominaciones predicte in casu⁸ posito sibi insint,⁸ vel aliter dicatur sentencia supradicta, quod mansit pro dicto triduo unio ypostatica ad utrumque.

This is confirmed from the testimony of [the more modern]{g:moderniores-doctores} [doctors], who say that this humanity could not be hypostatically joined while sinning unless the Word thereupon sinned; and, what is more, by reason of the prior union the Word is named, according to the more modern, as having lain for [the three days]{g:triduum} in the tomb, and in a quite similar sense as having sinned, in the case in which the [nature]{g:natura} laid aside had sinned at that time.[^16] What, I ask, is the cause of the difference, that the sense of the Holy Spirit in this [article of faith]{g:articulus-fidei}, "Christ descended into hell, was dead and buried", is "not that person, but a part of him, for the time in which it was not a part or hypostatically joined, was so", and that one would not grant, in a most similar sense, that the nature laid aside and afterwards reassumed, if it sinned in the meantime, would name the Son as so sinning?[^17] Let it be posited, then, that on the day of the Parasceve the soul of Christ completed the freeing of the fathers from [limbo]{g:limbus}, and at that time the body is driven by a demon, battering many men, and that on [72] the holy sabbath the soul is reunited to the body, the hypostatic union being suspended, and sins before none by adultery, homicide, blasphemy, and every kind of sin; and after none repents, and begets lawfully, and justly condemns many to death, the humanity being [assumed]{g:assumere} on the first [day] of the week into the unity of the [supposit]{g:suppositum} of the Word of God:[^18] a case which [the modern]{g:moderni-doctores} [doctors] admit, and more marvellous ones, as necessarily possible; in which case it seems, according to the [gloss]{g:glosa} they give to the articles of faith, that all the said operations in the three days would redound upon Christ, or else one would be feigning, from the prompting of the Holy Spirit, an impossible and unaccustomed sense.[^19] It is certain, therefore, that Christ did not stifle these men, lose the [aureole]{g:laureola} of virginity by the begetting of seed, incur [irregularity]{g:irregularitas}, or that traces of sins remained in him. And by a conformable reason he neither lay buried nor descended into hell, unless by a conformable reason the aforesaid [namings]{g:denominacio} in the case posited belong to him; or else the teaching stated above must be said: that the hypostatic union remained for the said three days to both.[^20]

## Notes

[^1]: **T.** 68.10 *sed si*: A B C read *coesse. Secundo Christus si*, opening a new step ("Second: Christ ..."); the print, with O, keeps a single argument. A B C *alia* for *illa* (68.11); O *possibiliter*, *contingisse* (68.11), and *vel* added by a second hand (68.7).
[^2]: **T.** 68.19–25: the passage Harris prints in square brackets, *Eciam securitas … igitur etcetera*, is wanting in C and in O and stands only in A B (in B partly in the margin). C and O agree across the families against the print, and the sentence construes without it (*patet ergo … miserie. Unde omnes theologi …*), so it is left out of the English above. It runs: "Also, the security of the perpetuity of full joy and of the exclusion of misery is of itself such a security, since one must come to an ultimate formal [principle], by which formally and by no other someone is secure of the perpetuity of full joy and of the exclusion of misery. Now it implies a contradiction that [misery] should coexist with such [a security] in its subject, or that after it misery should succeed in that same subject: therefore etc." At 68.14 *non quia non possunt peccare*: the blessed are "impeccable" not in that they never could sin, for they sinned on the way. A B C *reliquie* for *rectiloqui* (68.13).
[^3]: **C.** 68.29–31 *per accidens / per se*: "by accident / of itself", the two kinds of predication (Harris italicises them as terms). An abstract form such as blessedness is what it is of itself; only a concrete subject is blessed by accident. At 68.26–27 C reads *contradiccionem anime esse*.
[^4]: **C.** 69.5–9: the conditional *si enim … beatitudo* is interrupted by the parenthesis, and *et illud … est beatitudo* is taken as its apodosis: whatever is blessedness by accident leads back to a form that is blessedness of itself. B *substancialibus* for *similibus* (69.2).
[^5]: **C.** 69.11 *post esse*: taken as "after its being [so]", i.e. after having ceased; the phrase is bare, and a word may be lost (*post [desinere] esse*?). O omits *manere et*. The argument: a quality that could stop and start being blessedness would need a further form to make it blessedness, and that form, not it, would be blessedness.
[^6]: **T.** 69.20 *absolute*: Harris suggests *abstracte* ("abstract", cf. *forme abstracte*, 69.4); the print is kept.
[^7]: **C.** 69.23 *carismatum*: the CONTEXTUAL row, here "charisms", gifts, not chrism. A C *generaliter* for *naturaliter* (69.26); O *cariorem* for *clariorem* (69.24).
[^8]: **S.** John 17:3 (DR "Now this is eternal life: That they may know thee, the only true God, and Jesus Christ, whom thou hast sent"): Wyclif gives the verse's sense in his own words. *Veritas*, "the Truth", is Christ. A B C *beatificata* for *beata* (69.29).
[^9]: **C.** 69.31–70.3: *homo* is taken as any man, whose understanding is his mind's; so, by likeness, the spirit of Jesus, a mind hypostatically joined, knows what Jesus knows, who is personally the Word. Alternatively *homo* is "the man" Jesus himself. *Per locum a sufficienti similitudine*: "by the topic from sufficient likeness", an argument from a like case. A *intelligit (rasura) mens*, C *intelligit quod mens*; O *cognoverit*.
[^10]: **C.** 70.4–11: what has been cannot not have been: if the assumption is a past as necessary as any past, so is the blessedness bound up with it. *Pretericiones*: "pasts", truths about the past. *Solvi*: "be loosed", the verb of the John text in 05-c.
[^11]: **S.** Leo, "in a certain sermon": a sermon on the passion, not located (to be checked, LEDGER I05b-1). A B C *unio* for *unicio*, *potest* for *posset* (70.18–19).
[^12]: **C.** 70.21 *arraliter*: "by way of earnest" (*arra*, the pledge paid ahead of the whole sum): the body's blessedness given in part beforehand. A B C add *esse* after *tunc*. *Racionem, qua ipsam respicit*: the aspect under which the soul regards the body; O *quam*, *dolosam*. *Sentencia* is the CONTEXTUAL row, here "teaching".
[^13]: **C.** 70.24–25 *multarum naturarum quelibet*: "each of many natures" (Godhead, body, soul; cf. the three natures of 01-a). *Comprehensor*: one who has attained the end, against *viator*. The dowries are the gifts of the glorified body, here shown beforehand in the virgin birth, the walking on the water, and the transfiguration. A B C *ut ostenditur ut dico* (70.28–29); O *enituit* for *emicuit*.
[^14]: **C.** 70.33 *per deducens ad impossibile*: *per* with a bare participle, as printed, "through [one] leading to the impossible", the *reductio ad impossibile*; a noun (*argumentum*?) may be lost. A B C *illud* for *idem*.
[^15]: **C.** 71.5–10 *ab inferiori ad suum superius personale*: from the lower term (this humanity) to the higher (the person of the Word): the person is both the humanity and the Godhead, so it is the wider, "more common" term. B omits *humanitas … aliud* (71.9) and reads *quam communior quam* (71.10); O *ista*, omitting *est* (71.11).
[^16]: **C.** 71.16–22: the more modern doctors grant that the Word is named from the prior union as having lain in the tomb; Wyclif turns the admission against them: the same naming would make the Word a sinner in the case put. O *modernorum* (71.16); B C *potest* (71.17); O *peccaverit* (71.18).
[^17]: **C.** 71.22–28 *Que … causa diversitatis … quin … concederet*: taken as one question: why read the article of the part (the body buried, the soul in hell), yet refuse the like reading for a sin of the nature laid aside. O *peccaret* for *peccare*; O *qui* (71.25), *copulativa* (71.26).
[^18]: **T.** 72.1 *†reuniatur*: Harris's dagger. A C *reniatur*, B *revivatur*, C in the margin *revinatur*, O *reunciatur*; Harris says read *revinciatur* ("be bound again") or *reuniatur*. The manuscripts do not agree, and the print is translated. The case runs from Good Friday (*parasceve*, the day of preparation) through the sabbath to *prima sabbati*, the first day of the week; *nonam*, "none", is the ninth hour. O *perfeceret libacionem patrum a libo* (71.29–30).
[^19]: **C.** 72.10 *fingeret*: its subject is not expressed, and is taken as "one"; C *finge (lacuna)*, O *fīgᵗ*. A B *dant autores*, C *dat autores*, "the authors give" (72.8); O omits *quod*.
[^20]: **C.** 72.18 *ad utrumque*: to both, body and soul. *Sentencia supradicta*: Wyclif's own teaching, argued in the chapters before, that the union remained in the three days. A B C *aureolam* for *laureolam* (72.12); O *suffocare* (72.12); A B *sibi posito insunt* (72.16).
