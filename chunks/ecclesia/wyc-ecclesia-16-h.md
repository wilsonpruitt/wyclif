# *De ecclesia*, chapter 16, part 8: the fictions that shield the clergy's alms from withdrawal

## ¶1 · 384.34–385.5

> [p. 384] Nec cadit excusacio quod nemo luet defectum alterius, quia oppositum est fides catholica, specialiter [p. 385] quando unus omissione vel commissione participat peccato alterius; si enim prepositus spiritualis exercitus vel capit stipendium pro militibus quos non habet vel pro indignis quos debet corrigere, quomodo non est dignum elemosinam proporcionalem ab illo subtrahere?

Nor does the excuse hold that no one will pay for another's defect, for the opposite is the catholic faith, especially [385] when one shares by omission or commission in another's sin; for if the [superior]{g:prepositi} of the spiritual army takes a wage either for [soldiers]{g:milites} whom he does not have, or for unworthy ones whom he ought to correct, how is it not fitting to withdraw a proportional [alms]{g:elemosina} from him?[^1]

## ¶2 · 385.7–386.14

> [p. 385] Nec valet infidelis ficcio qua dicitur quod absque condicione fuit facta collacio, quomodocunque religiosus voluerit donatum expendere, quia hoc accusaret patronos ex blasphemia et extingueret spem de suo spirituali suffragio; ideo non minus propter mortuorum suffragium quam vivorum subsidium debet fieri diligens inquisicio in hac parte; tunc enim haberemus pauciores milites, sed potenciores contra dyabolum. Nec valet infundabilis ficcio qua dicitur quod nemo aufert quicquam simpliciter ab alio nisi auferat a quolibet sibi simili in natura, quia tunc nemo posset quicquam auferre ab alio, cum omne ablatum ab impio tribuitur iusto. Sic enim iusti tulerunt spolia impiorum et omni habenti dabitur, ab eo autem qui non habet et quod videtur habere auferetur; et sic de modis loquendi scripture, sanctorum doctorum et legis ecclesie. Nam XXXVIIª epistola Augustini ad Macedonium dicitur quod non dimittitur peccatum nisi restituatur ablatum, et idem ponitur in canone, XIVª, q. VI. Si res. Ista tria nesciverunt sompniare quod non est ablacio nisi iusta, nec quod est iusta ablacio nisi fuerit auctorizata a pontifice Romano. Nam omnem ablacionem vel collacionem sicut et omnem [p. 386] operacionem quoad eius substanciam oportet auctorizari a Deo; et cum omnis talis operacio non est sic secundum quid sed operacio simpliciter, eo quod aliter nulla creatura foret simpliciter activa, sicut Commentator recitat quosdam dicere, quos dicit non habere cerebrum dispositum ad philosophandum, sequitur cum veris quod stat esse ablacionem simpliciter cum hoc quod non a papa fuerit approbata. Si ergo ablacio vel alia talis iniusta operacio non dependet ex approbacione divina quoad mores quin sit simpliciter ablacio et alterius generis operacio, quomodo blasphematur quod nulla est ablacio simpliciter nisi papa decreverit? Sic enim extolleretur super omne quod dicitur Deus.

Nor is the faithless fiction valid by which it is said that the conferral was made without condition, in whatever way the [religious]{g:religiosi} might wish to spend what was given; for this would charge the [patrons]{g:patronus} with blasphemy and would extinguish hope of their spiritual [suffrage]{g:suffragia}. Therefore, no less for the sake of the suffrage of the dead than for the support of the living, diligent inquiry ought to be made in this matter; for then we should have fewer soldiers, but more powerful against the devil.[^2] Nor is the groundless fiction valid by which it is said that no one takes anything away from another [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} unless he takes it away from every one like him in nature; for then no one could take anything away from another, since everything taken away from the impious is bestowed on the just. For so "the just took the spoils of the wicked", and "to every one that hath shall be given ... but from him that hath not, that also which he seemeth to have shall be taken away"; and so with the manners of speaking of scripture, of the holy doctors and of the law of the church.[^3] For in the thirty-seventh letter of Augustine, to Macedonius, it is said that the sin is not forgiven unless what was taken away is restored; and the same is laid down in the canon, [Causa] 14, q. 6, *Si res*. These three did not know how to dream that there is no [taking-away]{g:ablacio} except a just one, nor that there is a just taking-away unless it has been authorized by the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex}.[^4] For every taking-away or conferral, as also every [386] operation, must as to its substance be authorized by God; and since every such operation is not thus [an operation] [in a certain respect]{g:secundum quid} but an operation without qualification (because otherwise no creature would be active without qualification, as the Commentator reports certain men to say, who, he says, do not have a brain disposed to philosophizing), it follows, together with truths [already granted], that there can be a taking-away without qualification although it has not been approved by the [pope]{g:papa}.[^5] If, then, a taking-away or another such unjust operation does not depend on divine approval as to morals for its being without qualification a taking-away and an operation of another kind, how is it blasphemously said that there is no taking-away without qualification unless the pope has decreed it? For so he would be exalted above all that is called God.[^6]

## ¶3 · 386.15–386.33

> [p. 386] Nec minus blasphemum est dicere quod nulla est iusta ablacio, collacio vel alia operacio ecclesie nisi auctorizetur ab ipso, quia antequam ipse habuerit primatum huiusmodi erant multe operaciones ecclesie satis iuste, sicut operacio sua est iusta ex pura acceptacione divina. Ymmo pro tempore mortis sue, sicut et ipso superstite ignorante sunt multe ablaciones, collaciones et operaciones elemosinarie satis iuste, sicut patet de dotacione ecclesie Anglicane, que non emanavit ex eius auctoritate, sicut nec percepcio elemosine cesaree vel elemosina Anglie cesari non subiecte. Aliter enim non faceret Anglicus elemosinam clero suo nisi ostensa ad hoc auctoritate papali singularissima; quod non est fingendum, cum tempus et infinite alie circumstancie variarent operacionem elemosine. Et sic cum auctorizacio generalis non sufficit, frustraretur ex defectu auctoritatis papalis et ignorancia revocacionis licencie prioris omnis elemosina facienda egenis.

Nor is it less blasphemous to say that there is no just taking-away, conferral or other operation of the [church]{g:ecclesia} unless it is authorized by him; for before he had such primacy there were many operations of the church quite just, just as his own operation is just from God's pure acceptance. Indeed, for the time of his death, as also while he survives without his knowing, there are many takings-away, conferrals and almsgiving operations quite just, as is clear of the [endowment]{g:dotacio} of the English church, which did not issue from his authority, as neither did the receiving of the caesarean alms, or the alms of England, which is not subject to Caesar.[^7] For otherwise an Englishman would not give alms to his [clergy]{g:clerus} unless a most singular papal authority were shown for it; which is not to be feigned, since the time and infinite other circumstances would vary the work of alms. And so, since a general authorization does not suffice, every alms to be given to the needy would be frustrated for lack of papal authority and through ignorance of the revocation of a prior licence.

## ¶4 · 386.34–387.31

> [p. 386] Ex istis demum colligitur quod ficticia subdola recentissima qua maior pars nostrorum sacerdotum [p. 387] dotatorum fingit infideliter quod, cum papa dominatur civiliter et capitaliter super singulos reges terre, non licet eis auferre quicquam a clero, iudicare de cleri conversacione vel cleri officio, nec ipsum corripere vel iuvare nisi ex eius auctoritate previe requisita. Sic enim extolleretur super Petrum et omnem Christi vicarium, cum Petro ignorante et non approbante nisi forte confuse, sicut benevolus approbat omne bonum, Paulus nuda auctoritate Christi fecit magnum profectum ecclesie (sicut patet Act. IX° et infra). Secundo fingens hoc dogma nephandum daret occasionem populo laicali subtrahere beneficienciam suam egenis, tam clericis quam laicis, eo quod non expresse docetur satis singularis papalis auctoritas vel interim revocacio auctoritatis ostense, ut tangitur in destruccione proxime ficcionis. Tercio tolleretur omnis regalia et primatus secularis principum terrenorum, cum non liceret seculari iudicare de nequicia clerici sibi iniuriantis quantumcunque notorie nec exterrere clerum freneticum ab attemptato facinore, et multo magis non liceret seculari domino amerciare clericum vel eius familiam insolentem nisi prius consulto papa ad hoc licenciante, quia aliter liceret auferre a clero per viam multacionis temporalia sua mobilia et in foro civili iniuste occupata, papa qui est capitalis dominus inconsulto. Talia deliramenta nedum ex scriptura sacra infundabilia sed sibi contraria que hodie plus solito sunt emuncta dabunt finem, si Deus voluerit, ne post istum virum sterilem succedat alius tyrannice sic blasphemans, sed Danielis IX°, 27, dicitur quod abhominacio desolacionis perseverabit usque in finem.

From these things, finally, it is gathered that the most recent crafty [fiction]{g:ficticia}, by which the greater part of our [387] endowed [priests]{g:sacerdos} faithlessly feign that, since the pope [exercises lordship]{g:dominari} civilly and [as head]{g:capitaliter} over every king of the earth, it is not lawful for them to take anything away from the clergy, to judge of the clergy's [manner of life]{g:conversacio} or the clergy's office, nor to rebuke or help it except with his authority first sought, [is groundless].[^8] For so he would be exalted above Peter and every [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius Christi}, since, with Peter ignorant and not approving (unless perhaps [indistinctly]{g:confuse}, as a well-wisher approves every good), Paul by the bare authority of Christ made great progress for the church (as is clear in Acts 9 and following).[^9] Second, one who feigns this abominable dogma would give occasion to the lay people to withdraw their beneficence from the needy, clerics as well as lay, because a sufficiently singular papal authority is not expressly taught, or a revocation in the meantime of the authority shown, as is touched on in the demolition of the last fiction.[^10] Third, all the [regality]{g:regalia} and secular primacy of earthly princes would be taken away, since it would not be lawful for a [secular]{g:secularis} to judge of the wickedness of a [cleric]{g:clericus} injuring him, however notoriously, nor to frighten a frenzied clergy off from a [misdeed]{g:facinus} attempted; and much more it would not be lawful for a secular lord to amerce a cleric or his insolent [household]{g:familia} unless the pope had first been consulted and licensed it, because otherwise it would be lawful, without consulting the pope, who is [chief lord]{g:capitalis dominus}, to take away from the clergy by way of fine its movable [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, and those unjustly occupied, in the civil court.[^11] Such ravings, not only groundless in holy scripture but contrary to it, which today are put about more than usual, will make an end, if God wills, so that after this barren man another may not succeed blaspheming thus tyrannically; but in Daniel 9:27 it is said that "the abomination of desolation shall continue even to the end".[^12]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 385.7: It is vain to plead that the gifts were made without conditions.
- 385.15: Vain too is the fiction that all true taking away is from like by like;
- 386.15: or that every gift or withdrawal of gifts needs the Pope's sanction.
- 387.11: If this were true the laity might withdraw all gifts that had not been sanctioned by the Pope, while the clergy would be out of all control.

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** *Nemo luet defectum alterius*: cf. Ezekiel 18:20. **G.** *Prepositus*, "superior", is the head of the clerical army in the figure of 384; *milites*, "soldiers", as there (GLOSSARY *milicia; milites*, the Pauline figure).

[^2]: **R.** A and B in the margin at 385.7: *Tercia ficcio*. **T.** At 385.8 Loserth reports *quandocunque* ("whenever") in the manuscripts; his *quomodocunque* is translated. **C.** *Collacio*: the conferral of the gift by the founder. *Accusaret patronos ex blasphemia*: the patrons gave for the suffrages, and the fiction makes them give for nothing. *Mortuorum suffragium*: the suffrage owed to dead founders.

[^3]: **R.** A and B in the margin at 385.15: *Quarta ficcio*; A: *Nota ficticias pro dominacione cleri*. **C.** *Auferat quolibet sibi simili in natura*: on the fiction, a true taking-away removes the thing from every one of the same nature as the loser, so that what passes to another man is not taken away at all; Wyclif's reply turns on that. **S.** Wisdom 10:19; Matthew 25:29 (DR), where Wyclif omits *et abundabit* and *ab eo*.

[^4]: **S.** Augustine's letter to Macedonius, numbered 37 by Wyclif, is Ep. 153.6.20 in the modern numbering, identified by content (*non remittitur peccatum, nisi restituatur ablatum*); Wyclif's number, which he uses throughout, matches no known numbering, and Gratian has *LIV* (LEDGER E16h-1); Loserth refers back to his p. 304. The canon is Decretum C.14 q.6 c.1 (*Si res aliena*); Loserth's form is "Decr. II pars, causa XIV, q. VI, cap. I", the modern form ours (LEDGER E16h-2). **R.** A in the margin at 385.23: *Augustinus*; B: *Nota Augustinum quod non dimittitur peccatum nisi restituatur etc.* **C.** *Ista tria*: scripture, the holy doctors and the law of the church.

[^5]: **S.** *Commentator*: Averroes, reporting those who deny that creatures act (to be checked, LEDGER E16h-3). **C.** *Sequitur cum veris*: "it follows, together with truths [already granted]". *Stat esse ... cum hoc quod non*: "there can be ... although not". **R.** A in the margin at 385.29: *Stat esse ablacionem iustam preter hoc quod papa ipsam approbaret*.

[^6]: **S.** 2 Thessalonians 2:4, where the Vulgate has *extollitur supra omne* (DR "is lifted up above all that is called God"); Wyclif's conditional is translated. **C.** *Quin sit*: injustice does not keep a taking-away from being one. *Blasphematur quod*: an impersonal passive, "how is it blasphemously said that".

[^7]: **R.** A and B in the margin at 386.15: *Quinta ficcio*. **T.** At 386.17 B omits *ipse ... operacio sua*. **C.** *Pro tempore mortis sue*: the vacancy after a pope's death; *ipso superstite ignorante*: while he lives but knows nothing of the act. *Elemosina cesarea*: Constantine's endowment, received without papal authority; *elemosina Anglie cesari non subiecte*: England was never under the emperor, so its alms owe nothing to Caesar's.

[^8]: **T.** The print gives *ficticia* no verb: after the long *quod* clause the period passes to *Sic enim*. "[is groundless]" is supplied from the closing *infundabilia* (our supplement); a word may be lost, or the period is broken off. **R.** A in the margin at 386.34: *Nota*; A and B: *Sexta ficcio*.

[^9]: **S.** Acts 9:19–30, Paul's preaching after his conversion. **C.** *Confuse*: Peter approved, if at all, only as anyone approves all good in general, without knowledge of the act.

[^10]: **T.** At 387.15 A and A₁ omit *ostense*. **C.** *Proxime ficcionis*: the fifth fiction (386.15–33), where the want of a singular authority and the unknown revocation of a licence were argued. *Docetur*: is taught, i.e. made known to the giver.

[^11]: **C.** *Amerciare*: to amerce, fine at the court's mercy; *multacio*, "fine". *Temporalia sua mobilia et ... iniuste occupata* is taken as two objects, the clergy's movables and what it holds unjustly, with *in foro civili* naming the court; "goods unjustly occupied in the civil court" is also possible.

[^12]: **S.** Daniel 9:27, where the Vulgate has *et usque ad consummationem et finem perseverabit desolatio* (DR "and the desolation shall continue even to the consummation, and to the end"); Wyclif's words are translated. **C.** *Emuncta*: literally "wiped, blown out"; "put about" is a guess at the sense. *Dabunt finem*: the ravings, by their excess, will bring the matter to an end. *Istum virum sterilem* is not named.
