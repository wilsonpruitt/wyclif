## Doubtful, left as they are

- **05-b, p. 123**: ***Si autem** demonstratur pronomine panis aut vinum*, rendered "And if by the pronoun the bread or the wine is pointed to". Read as (c): it opens the run of cases (*Si autem … Si demonstretur … Si autem pronomen connotat*) on what the pronoun points to. A (b) reading, "But if", setting it against the sentence before (pointing to bread makes identical predication false), is possible. The second *Si autem pronomen* ("And if the pronoun connotes") is the same series and stays (c). Left.
- **05-c, p. 124**: ***Racio autem** videtur triplex*, rendered "And the reason seems threefold". The sentence before lists four grounds (sense, reason, testimony of the saints, scripture) and has just dealt with sense. This is the next item on that list, so (c) "and". It could also be read as (a) "now" (the next step), but it supplies no premiss to an inference. Left. The same goes for p. 125 *Triplex **autem** ponitur testificacio* ("And the testimony"), which is the third item.
- **05-d, p. 131**: ***Sed** tercii dicunt quod convertuntur*, rendered "But a third [party] say". This is the third of three views (*quidam … nusquam esse*, *decretiste vero … sed nescitur ubi*, *tercii*). The card's continuative class covers *autem* only. For *sed*, "but" holds, setting a third opinion against the two before it. Left.
- **05-d, p. 129**: *nec … corrumpitur secundum se totum **sed pocius** melioratus remanet*, rendered "but rather it remains". "rather" is *pocius*, and *sed* is "but". Correct, noted so that "rather" is not taken for the *sed*. Similarly, on p. 128 "Rather, were there not many grains" renders *ymmo*.
