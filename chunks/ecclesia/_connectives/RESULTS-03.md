## Doubtful, left as they are

- **07-b, p. 147**: *Supponitur autem quod habeat privilegium … adhuc totum istud est generalis concessio, sed secundum leges hominum …*, rendered "still all this is a general grant; but according to the laws of men". The *sed* brings in the rule (major) that closes the reply (*Cum ergo …*), so (a) "now" is possible; but it answers the concession *adhuc*, and "but" reads as (b). Left.
- **07-e, p. 156**: *Sed scrutetur fidelis evangelium Matthei XXV°*, rendered "But let the faithful man search". In effect the minor (the condition is that imprisonment be a work of alms; *sed* Matthew 25 does not list it), so (a) "Now" is possible; but it is an imperative turning on the princes' promise, and "But" is defensible.
- **08-c, p. 164**: *nunc autem dicitur non re, sed indole laudantur*, rendered "whereas in fact it says". Adversative (b); "whereas" carries the contrast, though "but in fact" would be the card rendering. Left as a construction that carries it.
- **08-c, p. 167**: *Sed tercio notandum … quod privilegium est privata lex*, rendered "But, thirdly". The third of three suppositions (next step, (a) "Now") or a turn back from the digression on lying (b). "But" is defensible.
- **08-e, p. 176**: *Illa autem privilegia que debemus magis attendere plus relucent in speculo scripture*, rendered "Now". Either (a), a premiss used in the next corollary (*Ex istis tercio colligitur … Patet ex duabus conclusionibus*; *notatur … expressius*), or (c), a remark extending "found the privilege on scripture" ("And those privileges"). "Now" is defensible.
- **08-e, p. 177**: *In hoc enim consistit excellencia … non autem in caractere ordinis …, sed in hoc quod deputati sunt*, rendered "(as is here supposed): not in the character of order, …, but in this". The *autem* is not rendered as a word; the colon plus "not … but" carries the correction.
- **09-b, p. 190**: *non haberet excusacionem de negligenti desidia, sed scitur demonstracione certissima quod omne spirituale privilegium superat …, et sic non est privilegium*, rendered "but it is known". Either (b), the reason John did not take the gold, or (a), the premiss of *et sic*. "But" is defensible.
