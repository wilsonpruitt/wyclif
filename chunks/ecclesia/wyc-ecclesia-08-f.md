# *De ecclesia*, chapter 8, part 6: Christ's privilege above Caesar's; the lords may relieve the clergy of temporal goods

## ¶1 · 178.1–178.16

> [p. 178] Item, proporcionaliter ut dominus est potencior, prudencior et plus affectus sue ecclesie, tribueret eis privilegia pociora, sed iste condiciones autonomatice reperte sunt in Christo in comparacione ad Cesarem, ergo et privilegiacio proporcionaliter correspondens. Nemo ergo dicet quod Cesar plus privilegiavit ecclesiam quam dominus Jesus Christus, nec licet fingere quin Christus fuit potencior ad donandum eis temporalia, sicut et apostoli potuerunt ea copiosius recepisse, ut patet in vitis eorum. Unde Symon magus voluit dedisse Petro multam pecuniam pro privilegio apostolico (ut patet Act. VIII°), et indubie foret melius quam quantalibet pecunia vel omnia temporalia huius mundi. Nos ergo non erimus magis degeneres dicto Symone, plus appreciantes privilegium Cesaris quam privilegium domini Jesu Christi.

Again, in proportion as a [lord]{g:dominus} is more powerful, more prudent and more affectioned to his [church]{g:ecclesia}, he would bestow on them more excellent privileges; now these conditions are found [by antonomasia]{g:autonomatice} in Christ in comparison with Caesar; therefore also a privileging proportionally corresponding.[^1] No one, therefore, will say that Caesar privileged the church more than the Lord Jesus Christ did; nor is it lawful to feign that Christ was not more powerful to give them [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, as the apostles also could have received them more copiously, as is clear in their lives. Hence Simon Magus wished to give Peter much money for the apostolic privilege (as is clear, Acts 8), and undoubtedly it would be better than any amount of money or all the temporal goods of this world.[^2] We, then, will not be more degenerate than the said Simon, valuing the privilege of Caesar more than the privilege of the Lord Jesus Christ.[^3]

## ¶2 · 178.17–178.23

> [p. 178] Nec valet dicere quod ista privilegia dicuntur equivoce voce et ideo non sunt comparabilia, nam Deus est melior diabolo, bona gracie sunt meliora bonis fortune, et iuxta sanctos melius est seculariter carere temporalibus quam habere. Si ergo ista tam disparia sunt conparabilia in bono analogo, a pari vel maiori bona minus disparia.

Nor does it avail to say that these privileges are so called equivocally, in name only, and therefore are not comparable; for God is better than the devil, the goods of grace are better than the goods of fortune, and according to the saints it is better to lack temporal goods in the secular manner than to have them. If, then, these things, so disparate, are comparable in a good said analogically, by equality or by the greater [argument] goods less disparate [are comparable].[^4]

## ¶3 · 178.24–178.30

> [p. 178] Et preter hoc aliqua temporalia donavit Deus suis que quoad quantitatem et modum habendi sunt comparabilia dono Cesaris vel alterius domini secularis; in hoc ergo quod dicta privilegia dicuntur tam equivoce, oportet concedere quod unum infinitum excedit reliquum in perfeccione, sicut spiritus excedit naturam corpoream.

And besides this, God gave his own some temporal goods which, as to quantity and manner of having, are comparable to the gift of Caesar or of another secular lord; in this, then, that the said privileges are called [privileges] so equivocally, one must grant that the one exceeds the other infinitely in perfection, as spirit exceeds bodily nature.[^5]

## ¶4 · 178.31–182.5

> [p. 178] Item, iuxta hic dicta in correlariis descripcionis privilegii, nemo potest dare privilegium, nisi Christus principaliter illud dederit, et cum non decet dominum dare per servum donum perfeccius quam ipse dat [p. 179] singulariter per se ipsum, sequitur quod privilegium datum appropriate per Christum excedit privilegium quod ipse dedit per Constantinum, potissime cum videtur privilegia illa fuisse contraria. Ergo uno existente bono aliud saperet in parte maliciam venenosam, et per consequens, non foret privilegium pocius quam fuisset, quod filiorum Zebedei unus sedisset ad dexteram Christi et alius ad sinistram in regno temporali. Confirmatur ex sepe dictis, privilegium est filium patrisfamilias domus poni in loco digniori ad premium et in loco tuciori ad evadendum periculum, nec fas est dubitare quin Christus vocavit apostolos suos ad talem locum, in quo eciam secundum quandam prerogativam fuit Baptista, dicente Christo Matthei XI°, 11: Inter natos mulierum qui ceciderant per peccatum non surrexit maior Johanne Baptista. Cum ergo hec diffinicio Christi necessario sit vera, patet quod nec Petrus nec alius citra Christum fuit maior Baptista quoad Deum, cuius maioritas non attenditur penes possessionem secularem, cum Baptista fuit citra Christum pauperrimus, sed penes gradum evangelice paupertatis que consistit in vera humiliacione et in meritoria penalitate et in ecclesie edificacione. In hoc ergo fuit Baptista positus securius ad cavendum periculum et alcius ad querendum premium. Cum ergo quicunque status sit alcior quem Deus plus acceptat, sequitur quod status in quo Christus posuit apostolos et docuit ecclesiam sit status alcior statu secularis glorie in quo positi sunt Christi vicarii et dotati per Cesarem. Nec valet dicere quod ille status fuit tunc perfeccior, nunc autem status iste est perfeccior, tum quia fructus status ostendit oppositum de intricacione et detrimento ecclesie, tum eciam quia racio fidei scripture contradicit, [p. 180] nam lex consiliorum est perpetua, non habens contrarium, cum ponit hominem in statu, statui utriusque innocencie propinquiori, statum autem magnitudinis secularis oportet deserere propter eius repugnanciam statui beatitudinis. Quomodo ergo non est alio statu inperfeccior, cum respicit magnitudinem temporalem quam oportet deserere, alius autem magnitudinem virtutis quam oportet semper habere? Ex istis colligitur quod domini temporales possent maxime zelando pro privilegiis ecclesie exonerare clerum suum ab occupacione temporalium que sunt preter necessaria ad faciendum opus evangelicum; nam possent sic faciendo reducere ecclesiam cleri ad perfecciorem statum ex dictis, sed cum impossibile sit hominem posse facere bonum de genere, nisi posset facere illud bene, sequitur quod domini temporales possent facere illud bene. Absit, inquam, credere quod status in quo Cesar posuit clerum non posset solvi licite propter habitudinem sacerdotum Christi ad globum temporalium. Et cum status in quo Christus posuit clerum suum dissolvi posset licite propter inperfeccionem habitudinis vite eorum ad paupertatem evangelicam et alia carismata in quibus Christus dotavit ecclesiam, nonne foret suspectum verbum, accusans proprietarios de affeccione inordinata temporalibus, dicendo quod lex Dei et eius consilium sunt temporalia sub dispensacione cadencia, sed non ordinacio Cesaris quoad donacionem temporalium? Numquid credimus quod excommunicacio vel malediccionis interpretacio plus ligat dissolventem ordinacionem dominorum temporalium, quam excommunicacio Christi, Johannis et Pauli ligat dissolventem Christi consilium? Nonne dicit Paulus Galat. 1°, 9: Si quis aliud evangelizaverit preter illud quod evangelizatum est, anathema sit, et cum ipse [p. 181] instruxit coapostolum Thymoteum Iª Thymotei VI°, 8: Habentes ergo alimenta et quibus tegamur hiis contenti simus? Numquid est contrarium isti contencioni aspirari inordinate ad habendum perpetuo et indissolubiliter quantumlibet seculariter dominium? Nonne dicit beatus Gregorius (ut recitavi superius, III° cap.) quod labor querendi et defendendi temporalia et dolor amittendi eadem, si superat laborem et dolorem circa bona spiritualia quesita vel perdita, indicat terrenorum affeccionem inordinate in animo presidere Nonne beatus Augustinus in libro De Vera Religione, XCII° cap., dicit idem? Ubi postquam ostenderat quid est adherere Deo et quibus profluit affluencia temporalium etc, respondet: Cum, inquit, mutabilium bonorum adest copia, non eis confidit, sed cum subtrahuntur, agnoscit utrum eum non ceperint, quia plerumque cum assunt nobis, putamus quod non ea diligimus, sed cum abesse ceperint, invenimus qui simus; hoc enim sine amore nostro aderat quod sine dolore discedit. Si ergo clerus aliquis sit mundo deditus quod plus dolet pro amissione temporalium quam virtutis, plus insistit circa adquisicionem vel defensionem eorum quam boni spiritualis, ut sic inhabilitat se ad habendum hec temporalia et per consequens dignificat se ut perdat illa. Nec est ille cui hec sunt donata qui in isto animo finaliter perseverat, quia est membrum diaboli et non pars ecclesie (ut patet in solutione VIⁱ argumenti de VI° cap. huius). Nam vera religio stat in recta dileccione beatorum, ut in libro De Vera [p. 182] Religione XCIII.: Et hec est perfecta iusticia qua pocius pociora et minus minora diligimus. Vana ergo est religio facientis oppositum, privilegium ciresorum iniectorum puris certantibus est medicinaliter removendum.

Again, according to what was said here in the corollaries of the [definition]{g:descripcio} of privilege, no one can give a privilege unless Christ has principally given it; and since it does not befit a lord to give through a servant a gift more perfect than he gives [179] singularly through himself, it follows that the privilege given by Christ as his own exceeds the privilege which he gave through Constantine, especially since those privileges seem to have been contrary.[^6] Therefore, the one being good, the other would savour in part of venomous malice; and consequently it would not be a privilege, any more than it would have been one that, of the sons of Zebedee, one should have sat at the right hand of Christ and the other at the left in a temporal kingdom.[^7] It is confirmed from what has often been said: a privilege is for a son of the father of the household to be placed in a worthier place for reward and in a safer place for escaping danger; nor is it right to doubt that Christ called his apostles to such a place, in which also, by a certain prerogative, the Baptist was, as Christ says, Matthew 11:11: "Among them that are born of women, who had fallen through sin, there hath not risen a greater than John the Baptist."[^8] Since, then, this definition of Christ is necessarily true, it is clear that neither Peter nor anyone else [short of Christ]{g:citra Christum} was greater than the Baptist with respect to God, whose [greatness]{g:majoritas} is not reckoned by secular possession, since the Baptist was, short of Christ, the poorest of all, but by the degree of evangelical poverty, which consists in true humbling, in meritorious [hardship]{g:penalitas} and in the building up of the church.[^9] In this, then, the Baptist was placed more securely for avoiding danger and more highly for seeking reward. Since, then, any state is the higher which God accepts the more, it follows that the state in which Christ placed the apostles and taught the church is a state higher than the state of secular glory in which the vicars of Christ have been placed and endowed by Caesar. Nor does it avail to say that that state was more perfect then, but now this state is more perfect: both because the fruit of the state shows the opposite, from the entanglement and detriment of the church, and also because the reasoning of the faith of scripture contradicts it; [180] for the law of the counsels is perpetual, having no contrary, since it places man in a state nearer to the state of both innocences, whereas the state of secular greatness must be deserted because of its repugnance to the state of blessedness.[^10] How then is it not more imperfect than the other state, since it regards temporal greatness, which must be deserted, while the other [regards] greatness of virtue, which must always be had?[^11] From these things it is gathered that the [temporal lords]{g:temporales domini}, most of all by zeal for the privileges of the church, could discharge their clergy of the occupation of temporal goods beyond what is necessary for doing the evangelical work; for by doing so they could bring the church of the clergy back to a more perfect state, from what has been said; now since it is impossible that a man can do a good in its kind unless he could do it well, it follows that the temporal lords could do it well.[^12] Far be it, I say, to believe that the state in which Caesar placed the clergy could not be lawfully dissolved because of the [condition]{g:habitudo} of the priests of Christ toward the mass of temporal goods. And since the state in which Christ placed his clergy could [on that view] be lawfully dissolved because of the imperfection of the condition of their life toward evangelical poverty and the other charisms with which Christ endowed the church, would not that word be suspect, accusing the proprietaries of inordinate affection for temporal goods, which says that [God's law]{g:lex dei} and his counsel are temporal things falling under dispensation, but not the [ordinance]{g:ordinacio} of Caesar as to the donation of temporal goods?[^13] Do we believe that excommunication, or the interpretation of a curse, binds the one who dissolves the ordinance of temporal lords more than the excommunication of Christ, of John and of Paul binds the one who dissolves the counsel of Christ? Does not Paul say, Galatians 1:9: "If any one preach besides that which hath been preached, let him be anathema"; and since he [181] instructed his fellow apostle Timothy, 1 Timothy 6:8: "Having therefore food and wherewith to be covered, with these we are content"?[^14] Is it not contrary to this contentment to aspire inordinately to have, perpetually and indissolubly, secular [lordship]{g:dominium} of whatever amount?[^15] Does not blessed Gregory say (as I recited above, chapter 3) that the labour of seeking and defending temporal goods, and the grief of losing them, if it exceeds the labour and grief about spiritual goods sought or lost, shows that affection for earthly things presides inordinately in the mind?[^16] Does not blessed Augustine, in the book De vera religione, chapter 92, say the same? Where, after he had shown what it is to cleave to God, and to whom the abundance of temporal goods flows, etc., he answers: "When," he says, "there is plenty of changeable goods, he does not trust in them; but when they are withdrawn, he learns whether they have not taken hold of him; because for the most part, when they are with us, we think that we do not love them, but when they begin to be absent, we find out who we are; for that was present without our love which departs without grief."[^17] If, then, any clergy is so given to the world that it grieves more for the loss of temporal goods than of virtue, and insists more on the acquisition or defence of them than of spiritual good, to that extent it makes itself unqualified to have these temporal goods, and consequently makes itself deserving to lose them. Nor is he who finally perseveres in this mind the one to whom these things were given, because he is a member of the devil and not part of the church (as is clear in the solution of the sixth argument of chapter 6 of this [work]).[^18] For true religion stands in the right love of blessed things, as in the book De vera [182] religione, 93: "And this is perfect justice, by which we love the more excellent more and the lesser less." Vain, therefore, is the religion of one who does the opposite; the privilege of [ciresorum] cast upon those who strive purely is to be removed by way of remedy.[^19]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 178.1: Christ, who gives poverty, is far greater than Caesar, who gives riches.
- 178.31: All privileges come from Christ directly or indirectly.
- 179.1: Those coming directly are the greatest.
- 179.15: Baptist, than whom none was greater, was very poor.
- 179.31: It is futile to say that the state of poverty was more perfect, but is so no longer.
- 180.1: Poverty is nearer to the state of innocence.
- 180.10: Hence rulers may relieve the clergy of useless wealth.
- 181.6: Opinions of Gregory
- 181.11: and Augustine.

## Notes

[^1]: **T/C.** Loserth's apparatus records *antonomastice* (178.3) beside the print's *autonomatice* (GLOSSARY: both forms). *Eis*: the members of the church, plural by sense after *ecclesie*. Side-note 178.1.

[^2]: **S.** Acts 8:18–19: Simon offered the apostles money for the power to give the Holy Ghost. "For the apostolic privilege" is Wyclif's description. *Vitis eorum*: the lives of the apostles.

[^3]: **C.** *Nos ergo non erimus*: we take the future as a resolve, "we will not be". It may also be read as a rhetorical question ("shall we not, then, be …?"), with the participle giving the condition: if we value Caesar's privilege more, we are worse than Simon, who at least valued the apostolic one above money.

[^4]: **T/C.** A margin *Responsio* (178.17). At 178.23 Loserth supplies *sc. sunt comparabilia*, which we translate. *Equivoce voce*: equivocally, in name only. *In bono analogo*: in good said analogically, which spans God and devil. *A pari vel maiori*: the topics from the equal and from the greater (cf. GLOSSARY *locus a maiori*). *Seculariter carere*: to lack civil possession. "The saints" are not identified.

[^5]: **C.** The argument concedes the equivocation and turns it: if Christ's privilege and Caesar's share only a name, Christ's exceeds Caesar's infinitely.

[^6]: **T/C.** A margin *Nota hoc argumentum* (178.31). "The corollaries of the definition" are those at 168.4–169.28 (08-d), especially the third (168.19). *Appropriate*: as his own, directly; not the canon-law sense of GLOSSARY *appropriare*. The privilege "through Constantine" is the endowment of the church under the Donation. Side-notes 178.31, 179.1.

[^7]: **R/S.** *Maliciam venenosam* may allude to the story that at Constantine's donation a voice was heard saying that poison had been poured into the church. Neither the allusion nor the story's wording has been checked (LEDGER E08f-1). Matt. 20:21.

[^8]: **S/C.** Matt. 11:11. Wyclif adds *qui ceciderant per peccatum*, which is not in the Vulgate. It excepts Christ, who is not counted among those fallen through sin. *Filium patrisfamilias domus*: a son of the household's father, that is, a member of Christ's household.

[^9]: **T.** A and A₁ lack *possessionem penes* (179.20–21). *Citra Christum*: Christ excepted. Side-note 179.15.

[^10]: **T/C.** A margin *Responsio* (179.31). *Utriusque innocencie*: we do not settle which two states of innocence are meant. Perhaps they are the first state and the state of glory, or the innocence of Adam and that of Christ. *Racio fidei scripture*: the reasoning of the faith of scripture (not the *ex fide* formula). Side-notes 179.31, 180.1.

[^11]: **C.** The subject is the state of secular greatness. *Alius* is the state of the counsels.

[^12]: **T/C.** Loserth's apparatus has an A margin *Correlarium. Ablacio temporalium nota supra* (179.33). Loserth thinks two words, *sicut patet* (or *sicut patet ex dictis*), are missing at 180.14. A and A₁ read *possunt facere* (180.16). *Bonum de genere*: a good in its kind (as at p. 164, 08-c). *Sed*, bringing the next premiss, is rendered "now". Side-note 180.10.

[^13]: **C/G.** *Habitudo … ad*: the condition of the priests in respect of temporal goods (GLOSSARY "condition"). *Globum temporalium*: "the mass of temporal goods". GLOSSARY *globus* "band" is a derogatory collective of persons and does not fit here, so the word is untagged. We take the second *cum* clause as the opponents' position, hence "[on that view]". *Proprietarios*: those who hold property (cf. *affeccio proprietaria*).

[^14]: **C/S/T.** *Malediccionis interpretacio*: as printed, "the interpretation of a curse". The sense expected is the curse clauses of charters of donation (perhaps an *imprecacio*). B reads *consilium Christi* (180.32). Gal. 1:9: Wyclif's *aliud … preter illud quod evangelizatum est* is not the Vulgate's *vobis … praeter id quod accepistis*. His Latin is translated. 1 Tim. 6:8 DR: Wyclif reads *ergo* for *autem*.

[^15]: **C.** *Isti contencioni*: we take it as "contentment", answering *contenti*. It could also be "contention", which construes less well.

[^16]: **T/S.** Loserth corrects the codices' *12° cap.* to *III° cap.* (181.7), translated. He refers to pp. 52–54 above (LEDGER E08f-2). The work of Gregory is not named here and is to be checked there. A margin *Gregorius; XII. cap. recte III* (181.6). Side-note 181.6.

[^17]: **S/T.** Augustine, *De vera religione* 92 (PL 34:163; Loserth: "cap. XCII … Opp. I, pag. 781"; LEDGER E08f-3). Loserth corrects two readings from Augustine's text, and his corrections are translated: the codices' *conficit* becomes *confidit* (181.15), and their *separent* is corrected at 181.16. A reads *ostenderit* (181.12). A has the margin notes *Amor temporalium occultus quomodo indicatur* (181.11), *Augustinus* (181.12) and *Nota* (181.15). A also has a Czech gloss, *Pomny*, "Memento" (181.12). Side-note 181.11.

[^18]: **T/S.** A margin *Conclusio* (181.25). The cross-reference is to the solution of the sixth argument in chapter 6 (Loserth: pp. 136–138 above; LEDGER E08f-4). *Inhabilitat se*: makes itself unqualified (cf. 08-c, p. 165).

[^19]: **S/T.** *De vera religione* 93 (PL 34:163–64, *Et haec est perfecta justitia*; LEDGER E08f-5). Loserth corrects the codices' *est hec* to *hec est* from Augustine (182.1), translated. B reads *religione quoque* (182.1). *Beatorum*: as printed, "blessed things". *Ciresorum*: so printed (*cire-|sorum*), with no note from Loserth. We could not construe it and leave it untranslated; Loserth's apparatus is silent. Conjectures, neither secure: *cesareorum* ("imperial grants", cf. *dotacio cesarea*) or *cirographorum* ("charters"). *Puris certantibus*: those who strive purely, the athletes of the spiritual contest.
