### 07-b ¶1 · 138.32–141.15  (9 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 138] Quantum ad secundum argumentum de illo Matth. X, ubi recitatis nominibus apostolorum Petrus preponitur, indubie notatur primatus Petri, [p. 139] quem Christus intenderat, ut scilicet Petrus sit capitaneus et pugil precipuus ad ducendum Christi exercitum sibi similius, scilicet fide, humilitate et passione, ideo Petrus querendo de vita evangeliste signantis vitam contemplativam recepit responsum a Deo: Quid ad te, tu me sequere Joh. ultimo, 19. Istum igitur sensum comportant argumenta duo facta, ut sepe repecii, et non dominacionem vel honorem secularem, aut, ut dicit Bernhardus, potestatem seculariter castigandi; sicut caput verbis et intuitu, non **autem** corporali invasione vel instrumentali defensione, pascit et regit residuum corporis, quod si pape deficiunt visus, auditus et omnis sensus ad regendum capitaliter corpus Christi, quid sibi et pape vel capiti Christi ecclesie? Revera nulla eleccio vel approbacio humana potest facere quod talis fit caput vel articulus Christi ecclesie, **sed** mendaciter presumendo sic nomen fit caput ecclesie malignancium et synagoge sathane. Homo enim habet ex se potenciam vadendi et esse membrum dyaboli, **sed** non habet nisi a Deo esse membrum ecclesie; oportet enim proximum Christi vicarium esse instar sui spiritualem, ut docet Luce IX, 55, 56 de tribus apostolis appetentibus humanam vindictam: Nescitis, inquit Christus, cuius spiritus estis, filius hominis non venit animas perdere **sed** salvare. Miticia namque et non guerra possidebunt terram vivencium, ut docet Veritas Matth. V. Et hinc notat Crysostomus super primam epistolam Thym. Omelie V: Non, inquit, ait apostolus auctorabiliter neque dominative neque cum potestate, **sed** fili, ait, Thymothee, et ideo [p. 140] dicit Bernhardus, quod pascendas non premendas suscepit Eugenius oves Christi; debet enim pugnare tamquam spiritualis miles cum gladio spiritus, ad spiritualem Dei iniuriam vindicandam. **Sed** postquam mixta est cum religione sacerdotum civilitas, surrepsit in clero superbia, civilis exaccio et monstruosum dominium, ita quod illud Psalmi IX de Antichristo loquentis verificatur hodie: Dum superbit impius, incenditur pauper. Et quantum ad illud tactum de clavibus singulariter datis Petro, ut dictum est, nulli fideli licet ambigere; quin Petrus tamquam generalis attornatus gerebat personam ecclesie, ubi recepit potestatem non singulariter pro se et quocumque Romano pontifice **sed** generaliter et supereminenter pro quocumque sacerdote similius sequenti, cui est quicumque locus vel eleccio humana impertinens, quia donacio istarum clavium est divinum misterium. Sunt enim dicte claves potestas et sciencia ad edificandum ecclesiam, que signanter debent esse in quolibet sacerdote, et (ut credo) erant in Paulo excellencius quam in Petro. Cum enim impium videtur sine racione ponere in Petro ocium et potestatis abusum, Paulus **autem** ex fide scripture prima Cor. XV, 10 plus omnibus laboravit, videtur quod habuit amplius potestatis; laboravit, inquam, evangelizando, scribendo et persecuciones amplius paciendo, et clavis potestatis regiminis quam dicimus auctoritatem singulariter Petro competere non fundatur. Potestas enim ad regendum ecclesiam [p. 141] datur unicuique episcopo, quantum potest matri sue proficere, sive evangelizando sive sacramenta ministrando sive oracione, contemplacione, exemplacione edificando. Unde propter excellenciam, quam debemus habere, claves iste dicuntur singulariter sacerdotibus Christi competere, sic quod potestas sit materiale spiritualis clavis presbiteri, et formale est sciencia Dei; non sciencia mathematica, non logica vel disputacio scolastica, **sed** sciencia scripture, quam episcopus debet excellenter cognoscere et docere. Unde sicut materia prima non potest esse sine forma materiali nec econtra, sic videtur michi quod potestas clavium sacerdotalium non potest esse sine predicta sciencia nec econtra.

ENGLISH: As to the second argument, from that [text] of Matthew 10, where in the listing of the names of the apostles Peter is put first, the [primacy]{g:primatus} of Peter is without doubt marked, [139] which Christ had intended: namely, that Peter should be [captain]{g:capitaneus} and chief champion, to lead Christ's army more closely like himself, that is, in faith, humility and suffering. So Peter, asking about the life of the evangelist, who signifies the contemplative life, received an answer from God: "What is it to thee? Follow thou me" (John 21 [the last chapter], 19).[^1] This sense, therefore, the two arguments put forward carry, as I have often repeated, and not [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio} or secular honour, or, as Bernard says, a power of chastising in secular fashion. Just as the [head]{g:caput} feeds and rules the rest of the body by words and by its gaze, but not by bodily assault or defence with weapons, so if the pope lacks sight, hearing and every sense for ruling the body of Christ [as head]{g:capitaliter}, what has he to do with "pope" or "head of Christ's [church]{g:ecclesia}"?[^2] In truth no human election or approval can make such a man become head or joint of Christ's church; but by lyingly presuming the name in this way he becomes head of "the assembly of the malignant" and of "the synagogue of Satan".[^3] For a man has of himself the [power]{g:potencia} of going off and of being a [member]{g:membrum} of the devil, but he has it only from God to be a member of the church; for the nearest [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius-christi} must be spiritual after his likeness, as Luke 9:55–56 teaches about the three apostles who desired human vengeance: "You know not," says Christ, "of what spirit you are. The Son of man came not to destroy souls, but to save." For meekness, and not war, shall possess the land of the living, as the Truth teaches in Matthew 5.[^4] And hence Chrysostom notes on the First Epistle to Timothy, homily 5: "The apostle does not speak," he says, "authoritatively, nor in lordly fashion, nor with power, but says 'son Timothy'"; and therefore [140] Bernard says that Eugenius received the sheep of Christ "to be fed, not to be oppressed".[^5] For he ought to fight as a spiritual [soldier]{g:milites} with the sword of the spirit, to avenge the spiritual injury done to God. But after [civil standing]{g:civilitas} was mingled with the religion of priests, pride crept into the clergy, civil exaction and monstrous [lordship]{g:dominium}, so that that [verse] of Psalm 9, which speaks of Antichrist, is verified today: "Whilst the wicked man is proud, the poor is set on fire."[^6] And as to the point touched on about the keys given singly to Peter, as has been said, it is not lawful for any of the faithful to doubt that Peter, as general attorney, bore the person of the church, where he received the [power]{g:potestas} not singly for himself and any [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex} whatever, but generally and preeminently for any [priest]{g:sacerdos} whatever who follows [Christ] more closely, to whom any place or human election whatever is irrelevant, because the giving of these keys is a divine mystery.[^7] For the said keys are power and knowledge for building up the church, which, significantly, ought to be in every priest, and (as I believe) were in Paul more excellently than in Peter. For since it seems impious to posit in Peter, without reason, idleness and abuse of power, but Paul, [from the faith of scripture]{g:ex-fide} (1 Corinthians 15:10), "laboured more than all", it seems that he had more of power: he laboured, I say, by preaching the gospel, by writing, and by suffering persecutions more abundantly. And that the key of the [power of rule]{g:potestas-regiminis}, which we call [authority]{g:auctoritas}, belongs singly to Peter has no foundation.[^8] For power to rule the church [141] is given to each [bishop]{g:episcopus}, as far as he can profit his mother, whether by preaching the gospel, or by ministering the sacraments, or by building up through prayer, contemplation and example. Hence, because of the excellence which we ought to have, these keys are said to belong singly to the priests of Christ, so that power is the material [element] of the [presbyter]{g:presbiter}'s spiritual key, and the formal [element] is the knowledge of God: not mathematical knowledge, not logic or scholastic disputation, but the knowledge of scripture, which a bishop ought to know and teach excellently. Hence, just as prime matter cannot be without a material form, nor the converse, so it seems to me that the power of the priestly keys cannot be without the aforesaid knowledge, nor the converse.[^9]

### 07-b ¶2 · 141.16–142.12  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 141] Unde Johannes XXII et alii seculum sapientes, quia non excesserant in istis clavibus et earum officiis, finxerunt claves potestatis et auctoritatis, quas congregando pecunias simplices sue tyrannidi et ypocrisi subiecerunt. Sicut enim sunt due vie, una descendendi in viciis ad infernum et alia ascendendi in virtutibus ad ecclesiam triumphantem, sic sunt due porte sive introitus. Sacerdos **autem** debet habere claves ad serandum portas inferi a simplicibus sibi subditis et ad aperiendum simplicibus portas celi, et sic debet aperire pascendis ovibus thesaurum virtutum, et ipse primo virtuosis operibus sequelam ovium exemplantibus et excitantibus introire. Unde quotquot potestates vel sciencias tales prelatus habuit, tot claves habet, que omnes solum duas constituunt. Quod **autem** claves iste specificantur ad excomunicaciones et absoluciones, Deus huius seculi per fastum et avariciam cecando simplices introduxit. [p. 142] Et sic intelligo decretum XXIV, q. I, Quodcumque cum eis similibus, que omnia oportet intelligi potestate militantis ecclesie non errante; quod solum est verum quando iudicio triumphantis ecclesie est conformis; non enim potest aliter aperire ianuam regni celorum eciam sibi ipsi. Si **autem** claves iste quoad materiam sint verse in affeccionem terrenam et quoad formam declinant monstruose a sciencia apostolorum ad artem luciferi, necesse est quod tales suis clavibus aperiant portas suas et dogmatizent discipulorum suorum precipicium ad infernum.

ENGLISH: Hence John XXII and others who savour the world, because they had not excelled in these keys and their offices, invented keys of power and authority, by which, in gathering money, they subjected the simple to their tyranny and hypocrisy.[^10] For just as there are two ways, one of descending by vices to hell and the other of ascending by virtues to the church triumphant, so there are two gates or entrances. And the priest ought to have keys to lock the gates of hell against the simple who are subject to him and to open the gates of heaven to the simple; and so he ought to open the treasure of virtues to the sheep that are to be fed, and himself to enter first, by virtuous works that give example to and stir up the [following]{g:sequela} of the sheep.[^11] Hence as many such powers or kinds of knowledge as a [prelate]{g:prelatus} has had, so many keys he has, and all of them make up only two. But that these keys are narrowed to excommunications and absolutions, the god of this world brought in, blinding the simple through pomp and avarice. [142] And so I understand the decree 24, q. 1, *Quodcumque*, with those like it: all of them must be understood [to hold] when the power of the church militant does not err, which is the case only when it is conformed to the judgement of the church triumphant; for otherwise it cannot open the door of the kingdom of heaven even to itself.[^12] But if these keys, as to their matter, have been turned into earthly affection and, as to their form, swerve monstrously from the knowledge of the apostles to the art of Lucifer, it must be that such men with their keys open their own gates and teach as dogma the headlong fall of their disciples into hell.[^13]

### 07-c ¶1 · 142.13–144.30  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 142] Quantum ad tercium argumentum de adventu Petri ad dominum super aquas cum aliis exemplis audaciam Petri signantibus, patet quod docent ad sensum expositum vicarium Petri obligari diversissime ad faciendum similiter. Unde non video quomodo patencius, turpius aut convincibilius posset papa mendax allegare pro sua preeminencia quam afferre dictas autoritates ad confirmacionem sue preeminencie, faciendo ad sensum misticum eis contrarium; quia hoc esset afferre dampnacionem suam ex verbis propriis. Cum igitur ambulacio Petri super aquas signat suppeditacionem temporalium sub affeccione per quam viam vicarius Petri ut talis duceret ecclesiam plus vel minus, que maior confusio quam allegare illud iudicium ex lege Domini et cum hoc vivendo sub aquis mundi profundius in fastu et questu seculi, perplexius profundari? Deus enim ex ore proprio iudicat huiusmodi servum nequam. Si igitur sequimur Petrum moraliter in dictis audaciis, debemus postponere vecordiam omnium omissionum temporalium, omnium tribulacionum [p. 143] corporalium et omnium amissionum favorum mundialium, ut similius sequamur Christum et Petrum et induemus formalius corpus suum, cum secundum doctrinam prima Petri V, 3 debemus esse forma facti gregis non coacte **sed** ex animo. Quomodo igitur non proficit **sed** officit ad hoc seculare dominium; unde beatus Evangelista prenuncius Antichristi, qui statum tocius ecclesie previdit prophetice Apoc. XII, 3— 4 sic effatur: Ecce, inquit, draco magnus rufus habens capita septem et cornua decem et in capitibus eius septem dyademata, eciam cauda eius trahebat terciam partem stellarum celi, et misit eas in terram, et draco stetit ante mulierem, que erat paritura, ut cum peperisset, filium devoraret. Mulier ista amicta sole habens lunam sub pedibus est mater ecclesia, sponsa Christi, habens ipsum qui est sol iusticia amplexantem, cum de sponso Cantic. II, 5 dicit sponsa Christi: Leva eius sub capite meo, et dextra illius amplexabatur me. Ista mulier habet lunam sub pedibus, quando omnia temporalia, que sunt ut luna mutabilia, appetitui ecclesie substernuntur iuxta illud Apostoli Philipp. III. Omnia detrimentum esse et arbitror ut stercora, ut Christum lucrifaciam. Hec mulier habet capitaliter duodecim apostolos et eorum veros vicarios; hec mulier, ut dicit Johannes, parturiens cruciatur ut pariat, quia Christi vicarii signando Christum in animabus fidelium, utrimque sicut oportet varie tribulantur, cum Act. XIV, 21 dicitur per multas tribulaciones oportet nos intrare in regnum celorum et prima Cor. IV, 15 post multas tribulaciones expressas dicit Apostolus in Christo Jesu per evangelium, [p. 144] ego vos genui Et Gal. IV, 19 signanter dicit Apostolus: filioli mei quos iterum parturio, donec formetur Christus in vobis. Ideo dicitur quod mulier peperit filium masculum, qui redempturus est omnes gentes. Draco **autem** est quicumque capitalis in ecclesia Antichristi, draco propter venenum ypocrisis et voracitatem populi simplicis. Magnus **autem** propter pretensionem potencie, rufus propter effusionem sanguinis, quem consilio, auxilio et specialiter omissione causat in ecclesia ex consensu; necesse quidem est ut veniant bella inter gentes et regna pro inordinato amore temporalium, quando sacerdotes Christi, qui debent mundum relinquere, sunt maxime mundiales. Septem capita sunt universitas peccatorum vel evidenciarum, quibus suadet se esse caput ecclesie. Et decem cornua universitas potentatuum, qui militant sub vexillo huius principis Antichristi. Cauda eius est cleri communitas, que exemplo suo trahit stellas in terram, quando viros contemplativos inducit, ut deserentes splendorem virtutum et situm altum in firmamento ecclesie mundialiter conversantur. Oportet igitur quod Christi vicarius sit minime mundialis, ut patet Joh. XVII, et I. Joh. V, quia aliter submergit se in mari non cum Petro ambulans fideliter super aquas. Et iste sentencia evangelica movebat beatum Bernhardum libro III ad Eugenium, quando dixit: Nullum tibi venenum, nullum gladium tibi formido, quam libidinem dominandi.

ENGLISH: As to the third argument, from Peter's coming to the Lord over the waters, with other examples that signify Peter's boldness, it is clear that, in the sense expounded, they teach that the [vicar of Peter]{g:vicarius-petri} is bound in the most diverse ways to do likewise.[^1] Hence I do not see how a lying [pope]{g:papa} could [allege]{g:allegare} for his [preeminence]{g:preeminencia} more openly, more shamefully or more to his own conviction than by bringing the said authorities to confirm his preeminence while acting, in their mystical sense, contrary to them; for this would be to bring his own [condemnation]{g:dampnacio} out of his own words. Since, therefore, Peter's walking on the waters signifies the treading underfoot of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} in affection, by which way the vicar of Peter as such would lead the [church]{g:ecclesia} more or less, what greater shame is there than to allege that judgement from the law of the Lord and, with this, living under the waters of the world more deeply in the pomp and gain of the world, to be sunk the more [perplexedly]{g:perplexus}? For God judges such a "wicked servant" out of his own mouth.[^2] If, therefore, we follow Peter morally in the said acts of boldness, we must put behind us [faint-heartedness]{g:vecordia} at all the giving up of temporal goods, at all bodily [143] tribulations and at all losses of worldly favours, so that we may follow Christ and Peter more closely and put on his body more formally, since according to the [doctrine]{g:doctrina} of 1 Peter 5:3 we must be "made a pattern of the flock", "not by constraint" but "from the heart".[^3] How, therefore, secular [lordship]{g:dominium} does not further this but hinders it [is clear]; hence the blessed Evangelist, who foretold Antichrist and prophetically foresaw the state of the whole church, speaks thus in Apocalypse 12:3–4: "Behold," he says, "a great red dragon, having seven heads and ten horns: and on his heads seven diadems. And his tail drew the third part of the stars of heaven and cast them to the earth. And the dragon stood before the woman who was ready to be delivered: that, when she should be delivered, he might devour her son."[^4] This woman clothed with the sun, having the moon under her feet, is mother church, the [bride]{g:sponsa} of Christ, who has him that is the sun of justice embracing her, since the bride of Christ says of the bridegroom in the Canticle 2:5: "His left hand is under my head, and his right hand embraced me." This woman has the moon under her feet when all temporal goods, which are changeable like the moon, are laid beneath the appetite of the church, according to that [word] of the Apostle, Philippians 3: "I count all things to be but loss, and count them but as dung, that I may gain Christ."[^5] This woman has at her head the twelve apostles and their true vicars; this woman, as John says, travailing, is in pain to be delivered, because the vicars of Christ, in imprinting Christ in the souls of the faithful, are troubled on every side in various ways, as they must be, since in Acts 14:21 it is said, "through many tribulations we must enter into the kingdom of heaven", and in 1 Corinthians 4:15, after many tribulations set out, the Apostle says, "in Christ Jesus, by the gospel, [144] I have begotten you". And in Galatians 4:19 the Apostle says significantly: "My little children, of whom I am in labour again, until Christ be formed in you."[^6] Therefore it is said that the woman "brought forth a man child", who is to redeem all nations.[^7] And the dragon is whoever is chief in the church of Antichrist: a dragon because of the venom of hypocrisy and his devouring of the simple people. And great because of the pretension of [power]{g:potencia}; red because of the shedding of blood which he causes in the church by counsel, by aid and especially by omission, through consent; for indeed it must be that wars come between nations and kingdoms for inordinate love of temporal goods when the priests of Christ, who ought to leave the world, are most worldly. The seven heads are the whole sum of the sins or [evidences]{g:evidencia} by which he persuades [men] that he is [head]{g:caput} of the church. And the ten horns are the whole sum of the [potentates]{g:potentatus} who serve as soldiers under the banner of this prince, Antichrist.[^8] His tail is the commonalty of the clergy, which by its example draws the stars to the earth when it induces contemplative men to forsake the splendour of the virtues and their high station in the firmament of the church and to live in worldly fashion. Therefore the [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius-christi} must be least worldly of all, as is clear from John 17 and 1 John 5, for otherwise he plunges himself into the sea, not walking faithfully with Peter upon the waters. And this gospel [teaching]{g:sentencia} moved blessed Bernard, in book 3 to Eugenius, when he said: "I fear no poison for you, no sword, [more] than the lust of lordship."[^9]

### 07-c ¶2 · 144.31–146.22  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 144] Unde Augustinus sermone XIV De Verbis Domini notans navicellam Petri signare ecclesiam et mare seculum, cuius fluctus sunt persecuciones tyrannorum inflicte fidelibus, docet quomodo [p. 145] nauta huius navicelle debet artemonem arbori crucis infigere et ipsum lignum cum Christo suspenso ut velum erigere et sic eum efficaciter respiciens erit securus a turbine. Et hinc dicit Petrus nauta huius navicelle: Christus passus est pro nobis, nobis relinquens exemplum, ut sequamur vestigia eius. Et beatus Johannes: Sicut Christus pro nobis animam suam posuit et nos debemus pro fratribus animam ponere. Huic igitur velo simplex conversacio et vera confessio religentur et tuti esse possumus, quantumlibet mare seviat aut ventus incuciat, quod nostra navicella non mergetur; quantumlibet inter flatus et fluctus seculi conturbetur. **Sed** notet fidelis quod in ista nave non navigat nisi qui in humilitate et paupertate odit seculum, sequendo Christi vestigia. Ideo dicat fidelis prepositus: Si tu est Christus, non miror si solidam carnem super liquantes aquas suspenderis, **sed** fac quod mirer, iube me ex potestate divina venire ad te super aquas, quod non facit fidelis ad sensum misticum nisi calcata affeccione cunctorum temporalium imitetur Christum in moribus, et sic papa prepollens in talibus est verus Christi vicarius, ut tangitur in quarto argumento, quo Petrus Joh. ultimo misit se in mare ex calore fidei satis salvum. In quo, ut reor, datur intelligi quod stat christianum meritorie sequi Christi vestigia et sic esse in corpore Christi mistico, quod est Petri navicula, licet videatur in facie errantis ecclesie esse foris. Decet enim scripturam sacram esse gravidatam multiformi [p. 146] sentencia, ut preter literam factum illud signat iuxta quod martyrio cicius venitur ad Christum stantem in eternitatis littore, quam contemplacione vel officio confessorum. Et preter hec tercio signatur quod sicut apud Deum est possibile quod quis intromittat se cum mundo et adiutorio ac miraculo suo evadat periculum, ut dicit Christus Luce XVIII, 24 27 quam difficile qui pecunias habent regnum Dei introibunt; facilius est camelum per foramen acus transire, quam divitem in regnum Dei. Et dixerunt, qui hec audiebant, et quis potest salvus fieri? Et ait illis: que impossibilia sunt apud homines possibilia sunt apud Deum. Et videtur michi quod illam difficultatem signavit Petrus Matth. XIV, 30, quando veniens super aquas ad Dominum propter ventum validum cepit mergi; quamvis enim multi divites proni ad pompam, quos oportet finaliter mundum deserere, innitentes Christi adiutorio salvabuntur, via tamen veniendi navigio est clero securior, quia lignum salutis supponitur, ut medium impulsivum periculorum, que ex aquis mundi contigerint.

ENGLISH: Hence Augustine, in sermon 14 *On the Words of the Lord*, noting that "the little ship of Peter signifies the church, and the sea the world", whose waves are the persecutions of tyrants inflicted on the faithful, teaches how [145] the sailor of this little ship must fix the sail to the mast of the cross and raise that wood, with Christ hanging on it, as a sail, and so, looking to him effectually, he will be safe from the storm.[^10] And hence Peter, the sailor of this little ship, says: "Christ suffered for us, leaving us an example, that we may follow his steps." And blessed John: "As Christ laid down his life for us, we also ought to lay down [our] life for the brethren." "To this sail, therefore, let a simple [manner of life]{g:conversacio} and true confession be bound, and we can be secure, however the sea may rage or the wind strike, that our little ship will not sink, however much it is tossed among the blasts and waves of the world."[^11] But let the faithful man note that in this ship no one sails except one who in humility and poverty hates the world, following Christ's footsteps. Therefore let the faithful [superior]{g:prepositi} say: "If thou art Christ, I do not wonder that thou holdest solid flesh up over the flowing waters; but do what I may wonder at: bid me, by divine [power]{g:potestas}, come to thee upon the waters." The faithful man does not do this in the mystical sense unless, having trampled on his affection for all temporal goods, he imitates Christ in conduct; and so a pope who excels in such things is a true vicar of Christ, as is touched on in the fourth argument, in which Peter (John 21 [the last chapter]) "cast himself into the sea", safe enough from the warmth of his faith.[^12] In this, as I think, we are given to understand that a Christian can meritoriously follow Christ's footsteps and so be in [the mystical body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi-misticum}, which is Peter's little ship, although to the face of the erring church he seems to be outside. For it befits holy scripture to be pregnant with manifold [146] teaching, so that, besides the letter, that deed signifies that one comes more quickly by martyrdom than by contemplation or the office of confessors to Christ, who stands on the shore of eternity.[^13] And besides these, thirdly, it is signified that, just as it is possible with God that someone should involve himself with the world and by his help and miracle escape the danger, as Christ says in Luke 18:24–27: "How hardly shall they that have riches enter into the kingdom of God! For it is easier for a camel to pass through the eye of a needle than for a rich man to enter into the kingdom of God. And they that heard it said: Who then can be saved? He said to them: The things that are impossible with men are possible with God." And it seems to me that Peter signified that difficulty in Matthew 14:30, when, coming over the waters to the Lord, he began to sink because of the strong wind. For although many rich men prone to pomp, who must finally forsake the world, will be saved by leaning on Christ's help, yet the way of coming by ship is safer for the clergy, because the wood of salvation is set beneath, as a means that drives off the dangers which may befall from the waters of the world.[^14]

### 07-c ¶3 · 146.23–146.33  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 146] Ex quo videtur quod clerus **sed** papa precipue debet viare per illam semitam, in qua posset securius et expedicius sequi Christum; securius, quia status exproprietarius est elongacior a periculo temptatorum, et expedicius, quia minus intricatur cum mundo et hostibus, qui a legis Dei observancia retardarent. ENGLISH: From which it seems that the [clergy]{g:clerus}, but the pope above all, ought to [go as a wayfarer]{g:viator} along that path in which he could follow Christ more safely and more readily: more safely, because the [propertyless]{g:exproprietarii} [state]{g:status} is further removed from the danger of tempters; and more readily, because it is less entangled with the world and with enemies who would hold [him] back from the observance of God's law. XIII, 5, 6 quando sic docuit: Sint mores sine avaricia, contenti presentibus; ipse enim dixit: Non te deseram neque derelinquam Ita ut confidenter dicamus: Dominus michi adiutor, et non timebo quid faciat michi homo. ENGLISH: Again, the pope is bound especially to stand for the [privileges]{g:privilegium} of his clergy; now the chief privilege by which clerics are distinguished from laymen consists in this, that clerics be kept in the higher propertyless state; therefore the pope ought to observe this privilege in himself and in his subjects. For so did the Apostle, in whom Christ spoke,[^11] Heb. 13:5–6, when he taught thus: "Let your manners be without covetousness, contented with such things as you have. ### 07-h ¶4 · 163.22–164.32  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 163] Item, papa non habet potestatem a Deo ad deteriorandum **sed** meliorandum ecclesiam, ut dicit Apostolus de se ipso II. Cor. Quomodo igitur autorizabit Christi vicarius [p. 164] ex fastu vel avaricia sic ducere gregem Domini per abrupta; cum posset securius in pascuis quas Christus instituit ducere oves suas? Sic enim prudens paterfamilias seminat semen bonum non inter spinas, ne ex earum cremento frugum contrario suffocetur. How then will the [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius-christi} authorize [himself], [164] out of pride or avarice, thus to lead the Lord's flock over precipices, when he could more securely lead his sheep in the pastures which Christ instituted? More securely, I say, because of the diet that preserves from putrid diseases, because of the ease of keeping to ever-green pastures that it provides, and because of the security that shuts out the assault of the robber and of the evil beast.[^14] For so a prudent householder sows good seed not among thorns, lest it be choked by their growth, which is contrary to the crops.[^15] For God knows the infirm state of his [household]{g:familia}, how prone it is to all kinds of disease; therefore, as the supreme physician, he enjoins a diet which, although it seems contrary to the Roman antichrist,[^16] is yet supremely consonant with our fragile state. For he enjoins us, as to the propertyless state, to leave all things; which we [curates]{g:curatus} ought to do, living purely from [tithes]{g:decime}, offerings and the private alms of laymen.[^17] And because an abuse of alms can stand together with that propertyless state, therefore, according to blessed Jerome, Peter adds what is perfect, namely the state of morals, when he says: "and have followed thee" (Matt. 19:27); for without this there can be no salvation of the faithful.[^18] But the state of him who has left all things is more perfect than that of one who remains in the world, because it is nearer the highest state. And it befits all the faithful to know this matter, because they will more warily beware of the wolves who come in the clothing of sheep;[^19] they will more securely follow the footsteps of Christ, leaving the ways of Antichrist;[^20] and [the way] will be more prudently prepared for disbelieving his blasphemies and for withholding from him undue worship and consent.

LATIN: [p. 171] Et patet quam horribile est fingere talia blasfema mendacia propter dominia temporalia pallianda, Patet eciam, quod nec seculares nec clerici iuvantur per assistenciam talium antichristianorum **sed** profundius peiorantur, tam quoad bona temporalia quam spiritualia. Quoad temporalia, ut prophetatum est Dan. II et exponitur inferius, hoc fecit scissionem imperii. Et quoad spiritualia, patet quod mutuo se inficiunt ex consensu. Assistencia **autem** cleri tenentis se in statu primevo decoraret miliciam et econtra, ut patet de beatis Sebastiano, Martino et ceteris **sed** apostatarum assistencia deturparet.

ENGLISH: And it is clear how horrible it is to feign such blasphemous lies for [cloaking]{g:palliare} temporal lordships. It is clear also that neither [seculars]{g:secularis} nor clerics are helped by the assistance of such antichristians, but are made deeply worse, as to temporal goods as well as spiritual. As to temporal goods, as was prophesied in Dan. 2 and is expounded below, this made the division of the empire.[^10] And as to spiritual goods, it is clear that they infect each other mutually by consent. But the assistance of a clergy keeping itself in the primitive [state]{g:status} would adorn the [knighthood]{g:milicia}, and conversely, as is clear of the blessed Sebastian, Martin and the rest;[^11] but the assistance of apostates would befoul [it].

### 08-b ¶3 · 171.29–172.2  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 171] Quinta causa ficta est loci sanctitas ex multiplici christianorum martyrio, ubi decet sanctissimum caput ecclesie conversari. **Sed** constat quod locus non sanctificat inhabitantem **sed** econtra, ut dicitur II. Machabeorum V. Locus enim non est de genere activorum, cum sit quantitas et [p. 172] influencia celestis vel est sanctitati impertinens vel loca sunt alia magis apta.

ENGLISH: The fifth cause feigned is the holiness of the place, from the manifold martyrdom of Christians, where it befits the most holy [head]{g:caput} of the [church]{g:ecclesia} to live.[^12] But it is evident that the place does not sanctify the inhabitant, but conversely, as is said in 2 Maccabees 5.[^13] For place is not of the genus of active things, since it is a quantity; and [172] celestial influence either is irrelevant to holiness, or other places are more apt.[^14]

### 08-b ¶4 · 172.3–172.13  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 172] Similiter, si locus capit sanctitatem, per idem malediccionem ab incolis facinus committentibus in eodem, ut patet II. Regum primo de monte Gelboe. **Sed** Rome ab imperatoribus et carnificibus sibi subiectis, qui permanencius incolebant locum post et ante quam martyres, fuit multiplicius facinus perpetratum, igitur locus ille foret evidencius maledictus. Et in signum huius multi pape fugiunt illum locum, sicut Balaam propter influenciarum varietatem fecit altaria variari, ut dicitur Numeri XXIII.

ENGLISH: Likewise, if a place takes on holiness, by the same token [it takes on] a curse from inhabitants who commit a [misdeed]{g:facinus} in it, as is clear from 2 Kings 1, of Mount Gelboe.[^15] Now at Rome, by the emperors and the executioners subject to them, who inhabited the place more permanently, after and before the martyrs, misdeeds were more manifoldly perpetrated; therefore that place would be more evidently cursed. And in sign of this many popes flee that place, as Balaam, because of the variety of influences, had the altars varied, as is said in Numbers 23.[^16]

### 08-b ¶5 · 172.14–172.30  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 172] Similiter, si sanctitas martyrum fugat facinus et locum sanctificat, Jerusalem foret locus sanctissimus, cum ibi predicavit et passum fuit caput martyrum, ibi occisus est uterque Jacobus et multi martyres, ymo in veteri testamento occisi sunt plurimi, ut testatur Christus Matth. XXIII, 37 Jerusalem que occidis prophetas et lapidas eos qui ad te missi sunt, et IV. Regum XXIV, 4 scribitur quomodo Manasses effudit sanguinem innoxium et implevit Jerusalem cruore innocencium, et ob hanc rem noluit dominus propiciari. Difficile **autem** est sompniare, in qua istarum civitatum occisi sunt plures martyrio. **Sed** certum est quod Christus predicans, conversans et occisus in Jerusalem, infinitum excedit totum collegium martyrum Rome vel alibi occisorum, et preter hec locus est sanctitate antiquior.

ENGLISH: Likewise, if the holiness of martyrs puts misdeed to flight and sanctifies the place, Jerusalem would be the most holy place, since there the head of martyrs preached and suffered; there each James was slain, and many martyrs; indeed in the old testament very many were slain there, as Christ testifies, Matt. 23:37: "Jerusalem, thou that killest the prophets and stonest them that are sent unto thee"; and in 2 Kings 24:4 it is written how Manasses shed innocent blood and filled Jerusalem with the blood of innocents, "and therefore the Lord would not be appeased."[^17] And it is difficult to dream in which of these cities more were slain in martyrdom. But it is certain that Christ, preaching, living and slain in Jerusalem, infinitely exceeds the whole college of martyrs slain at Rome or elsewhere; and besides this the place is older in holiness.

### 08-b ¶6 · 172.31–173.20  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 172] Ex istis colligitur quod nec iste quinque raciones nec alie fingende stabiliunt quod oportet realiter Romanum pontificem esse papam. Multi enim Romani pontifices sicut et imperatores erant pessimi antichristi, et civitas illa cum gente [p. 173] sua modo prevalet et modo subditur, cum multe sunt hodie civitates, gentes et comunitates plus divites, inclite et amplius populose. Quomodo igitur apostoli, quibus preceptum est indiferenter omnes gentes, limitarent se ad illum populum, cum possent plus lucrifacere Christo in alio populo eciam non converso. Unde ut credo hec fuit una racio, quare apostoli non receperunt dotacionem fixam in terris, ut ubicumque terrarum spiritus eos moveret ad plus prodessendum ecclesie illuc tenderent indilate. Iuxta quem sensum scribit Apostolus Hebr. XIII, 14: Non habemus hic manentem civitatem **sed** futuram inquirimus, aliter enim dominium affixum retraheret affeccionem convertendi. Et hec racio, quare Christus non fuit affectus templo, ut patet Mar. XIII, **sed** predicavit sub divo, nunc in deserto, nunc in montibus, nunc in mari et sub verbis indifferentibus et generalibus, ut patet Matth. ultimo, precepit apostolis predicare.

ENGLISH: From these things it is gathered that neither these five reasons nor others yet to be feigned establish that the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex} must really be pope. For many Roman pontiffs, like emperors too, were the worst antichrists; and that city with its [173] people at one time prevails and at another is subjected, since there are today many cities, peoples and communities richer, more renowned and more populous. How then would the apostles, who were commanded [to teach] all nations without distinction,[^18] [limit]{g:limitare} themselves to that people, when they could gain more for Christ in another people, even one not converted? Hence, as I believe, this was one reason why the apostles did not receive a fixed [endowment]{g:dotacio} on earth: so that wherever on earth the Spirit moved them, to profit the church more, they might go there without delay. In which sense the Apostle writes, Heb. 13:14: "We have not here a lasting city: but we seek one that is to come";[^19] for otherwise an affixed lordship would draw back the affection for converting. And this is the reason why Christ was not attached to the temple, as is clear from Mark 13,[^20] but preached [in the open air]{g:sub-divo}, at one time in the desert, at another on the mountains, at another on the sea; and in indifferent and general words, as is clear from the last [chapter] of Matthew, he commanded the apostles to preach.

### 08-b ¶7 · 173.21–173.32  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 173] Quantum ad secundam ficticiam ad colorandum connexionem predictam Romani et summi pontificis per institucionem Petri; patet quod innititur foliis sine fructu; non enim plus sequitur: Petrus ordinavit Clementem episcopum Romanum, igitur ordinavit eum summum pontificem quam sequitur, ordinavit Marcum Anthiochie sibi succedere, igitur ordinavit eum sibi succedere in summum pontificem; locus **autem** (ut dictum est) est indifferens ad papatum et eque plenus papa fuit Petrus Anthiochie sicut Rome, et preterea Anthiochie accepit a populo insignia et non Rome.

ENGLISH: As to the second [fiction]{g:ficticia}, for colouring the aforesaid connexion of the Roman and the [highest pontiff]{g:summi-pontifices} through Peter's institution: it is clear that it leans on leaves without fruit;[^21] for it no more [follows]{g:sequitur}, "Peter ordained Clement Roman bishop, therefore he ordained him highest pontiff", than it follows, "He ordained Mark to succeed him at Antioch, therefore he ordained him to succeed him as highest pontiff";[^22] and the place (as has been said) is indifferent to the papacy, and Peter was pope as fully at Antioch as at Rome; and moreover at Antioch he received the insignia from the people, and not at Rome.[^23]
