# *De eucharistia*, chapter 9, part 8: the co-multiplied accidents pressed, and the sophist's argument from extension

## ¶1 · 300.6–301.3

> [p. 300] Item, omne accidens substancie multiplicate independens ab extrinseco, cuiusmodi sunt quantitas et qualitas, commultiplicatur cum ea, sed extensio, locacio et multa accidencia respectiva non dependent in corpore Christi ab extrinseco; ergo omnia talia commultiplicantur cum illo; nam staret de Deo possibili quod omne corpus vel quantitas foret per se sine maiori substancia quod concedunt ut fidem. Ex quo videtur quod extensio quantitatis non dependet ab aliquo extrinseco, sicut nec figura ad quam requiritur extensio. Et ex istis videtur quod corpus multiplicatum commultiplicat locum secum, quia Deo annichilante totum residuum creature foret corpus tantum in loco suo; et sequitur locorum confusio, et de posicione que est sessio, stacio vel iacere videtur quod inest ab intrinseco (sicut dicit Gilbertus Porretanus) quia stante Petro, sedente Paulo et iacente Lino annichilatoque residuo mundi usque ad illos continue servatos inmotos foret fingere grave, quomodo in illis variaretur posicio, cum conservatur continue moto subiecto in curru vel annichilatis polis mundi vel centro. Et idem videtur de distancia, de inmediacione et aliis respectibus quos foret maxime evidens non servari; nam stat corpora maxime distancia esse in infinitum propinqua, cum infinitum longa linea recta multiplicata posset intercipi inter quantumcunque [p. 301] propinqua, sicut patet de longitudine corporis Christi intercepta inter quamcunque partem eukaristie et aliam.

Again, every [accident]{g:accidens} of a multiplied [substance]{g:substancia} that is independent of anything extrinsic, of which kind are [quantity]{g:quantitas} and [quality]{g:qualitas}, is co-multiplied with it; now extension, location and many [relational]{g:respectivus} accidents do not depend, in the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}, on anything extrinsic; therefore all such are co-multiplied with it. For it would stand, as possible to God, that every body or quantity should be [by itself]{g:per-se} without a greater substance, which they grant as faith.[^1] From which it seems that the extension of a quantity does not depend on anything extrinsic, as neither does [figure]{g:figura}, for which extension is required. And from these it seems that a multiplied body co-multiplies its place with it; for if God annihilated the whole remainder of [creature]{g:creatura}, the body would be only in its own place; and there follows a confusion of places. And of [position]{g:posicio}, which is sitting, standing or lying, it seems that it is in [its subject] from within (as Gilbert of Poitiers says);[^2] for, Peter standing, Paul sitting and Linus lying, and the rest of the world annihilated down to them, they being continuously preserved unmoved, it would be hard to [feign]{g:fingere} how position would vary in them, since it is continuously preserved when the subject is moved in a cart, or when the poles of the world or its centre are annihilated. And the same seems [to hold] of distance, of immediacy and of the other [relations]{g:respectus}, which it would be most evident are not preserved; for it may be that bodies most distant are infinitely near, since an infinitely long straight line, multiplied, could be intercepted between [things] however [301] near, as is clear of the length of the body of Christ intercepted between any part of [the Eucharist]{g:eukaristia} and another.

## ¶2 · 301.4–301.27

> [p. 301] Item, ubicunque est quantitas vel qualitas corpori Christi, ibi est quod ipsum est sic quantum et quale et per consequens per totam hostiam est quod corpus Christi est septipedale et gloriose qualificatum ut in celo. Et hic mirantur philosophi, cum qualitas sine subiecto habet operacionem suam naturalem eque efficacem sicut substancia sic qualificata, quod celestis qualitas in nobili subiecto corpore Christi ab accione suspenditur, et unio qua uniretur qualitas celestis in hostia fortificaret accionem, quia secundum philosophos omnis virtus unita est forcior se dispersa, ymmo cum secundum perspectivorum sentenciam forma collacionis iuvat ad luminis accionem ac in corpore Christi si est ibidem est claritas sole splendidior et ad omnem punctum figura Mukephi que secundum Vitilonem est figura efficacissima possibilis ad lumina congreganda, videtur quod valde sensibiliter ageret, et hoc forte movet eos qui dicunt quod vident in hostia corpus Christi oculo corporali, sed evangelium dicit quod fuit singulariter concessum privatis apostolis videre scilicet gloriam transfiguracionis corporis Domini ante mortem; ideo cum non dicunt cum Petro: bonum est nos hic esse, creditur quod de visione quatuor dotium corporis Christi in hostia menciuntur.

Again, wherever there is quantity or quality to the body of Christ, there it is [the case] that it is so [quantified]{g:quantus} and qualified; and consequently, throughout the whole [host]{g:hostia} it is [the case] that the body of Christ is seven feet long and gloriously qualified, as in heaven. And here the philosophers wonder (since a quality without a subject has its natural operation as efficacious as a substance so qualified) that the heavenly quality in the noble subject, the body of Christ, is [suspended]{g:suspendere} from action; and the [union]{g:unio} by which the heavenly quality would be united in the host would strengthen the action, for according to the philosophers every [power]{g:virtus} united is stronger than itself dispersed;[^3] indeed, since according to the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of the [perspectivists]{g:perspectivi} the form of the [collection]{g:collacio} helps toward the action of light,[^4] and in the body of Christ, if it is there, there is a [clarity]{g:claritas} more splendid than the sun, and at every point the figure *Mukephi*, which according to Witelo is the most efficacious figure possible for gathering lights,[^5] it seems that it would act very [perceptibly]{g:sensibiliter}. And this perhaps moves those who say that they see in the host the body of Christ with the bodily eye; now the gospel says that it was granted [alone]{g:singulariter} to apostles taken apart to see, namely, the glory of the transfiguration of the [body of the Lord]{g:corpus-domini} before [his] death; therefore, since they do not say with Peter, "It is good for us to be here", it is believed that they lie about the vision of the four dowries of the body of Christ in the host.[^6]

## ¶3 · 301.28–302.6

> [p. 301] Et quoad quantitatem fidelis theologus eciam laicus non crederet predicanti quod corpus Christi est septipedale in quacunque parte hostie. Nec dubium quin [p. 302] si ibi sit illa septipedalitas corporis Christi que est in celo, ibi est quod corpus Christi est septipedale, nam tantum incredibile foret fidelibus quod ibi sit illa septipedalis corporeitas cum dimensione panis per eundem locum, sicut quod ibidem sit corpus Christi.

And as to quantity, a faithful theologian, even a lay one, would not believe a preacher [saying] that the body of Christ is seven feet long in any part of the host whatever.[^7] Nor is there doubt that, [302] if that seven-foot length of the body of Christ which is in heaven is there, it is there [the case] that the body of Christ is seven feet long; for it would be as incredible to the faithful that that seven-foot corporeity is there with the dimension of the [bread]{g:panis} through the same place, as that the body of Christ is there.

## ¶4 · 302.7–302.23

> [p. 302] Item, non negabunt quin corpus Christi sit ibi divisibile aut magnum; habet enim ibi caput, pedes et omnia cetera organa, figuras et posiciones parcium, ne sit monstrum et per consequens pars est ibi minor suo toto; nam satis inconsonum est concedere quod Christus habet ibi pedes supra caput omnia interiora sua extra cutem et omnia organa sua eversa, licet non ponatur maius inpossibile quam quod quantitas corporis Christi est ibi cum subiecto suo, et cum ibi non informat ut accidencia panis carent subiecto et informant se ipsa, per idem omnis pars corporis Christi careret ibi racione partis corporis Christi, et sic corpus Christi foret ibi exanime atque acefalum, ymmo os Christi et omnes partes eius forent ibi indivisibiles quante, et tamen hoc non oportet, cum tota quantitas corporis Christi sit per singulas eius partes.

Again, they will not deny that the body of Christ is there divisible or great; for it has there head, feet and all the other organs, figures and positions of parts, lest it be a monster; and consequently a part is there less than its whole. For it is quite discordant to grant that Christ has there his feet above his head, all his inward parts outside his skin and all his organs turned inside out, although no greater impossibility is posited than that the quantity of the body of Christ is there with its subject; and since it does not [inform]{g:informare} there, as the accidents of the bread lack a subject and inform themselves, by the same token every part of the body of Christ would lack there the account of a part of the body of Christ, and so the body of Christ would be there lifeless and headless; indeed, the mouth of Christ and all his parts would be there indivisible [yet] quantified;[^8] and yet this need not be, since the whole quantity of the body of Christ is through each of its parts.

## ¶5 · 302.24–302.32

> [p. 302] Sed absit fidelem de Deo suo concedere inconveniencia ad que heresis ista ducit; cum enim corpus Christi habet ibi partes quantitativas distinctas a partibus qualitativis, oportet quod utrumque genus parcium, sicut habet ibi nomen, sic habeat ibi racionem nominis. Et confirmacio est quod corpus Christi foret ibi, solummodo (quod est adversariis summe possibile) foret magnum, quantum ergo foret ibi, si non tantum quanta est sua quantitas.

But far be it from a faithful man to grant of his God the [absurdities]{g:inconveniens} to which this heresy leads.[^9] For since the body of Christ has there [quantitative parts]{g:pars-quantitativa} distinct from [qualitative parts]{g:pars-qualitativa}, it must be that each kind of parts, as it has the name there, so has there the account of the name. And the confirmation is that the body of Christ would be there; merely (which is to the adversaries supremely possible) it would be great; how great, then, would it be there, if not as great as its quantity is?[^10]

## ¶6 · 303.1–303.7

> [p. 303] Similiter, eque spiritualis res est quantificacio sicut quantitas; cum ergo ibi sit quantitas cum figura, quod sua informacio sit ibidem, aliter enim oportet blasphemare in Deum, dicendo quod potest alicubi servare quantitatem in subiecto cuius informacionem bene suspendit, sed non potest suspensionem illam dissolvere.

Likewise, quantification is as spiritual a thing as quantity; since, therefore, quantity is there with figure, [it must be] that its [informing]{g:informacio} is there too; for otherwise one must blaspheme against God, saying that he can somewhere preserve quantity in a subject whose informing he indeed suspends, but cannot undo that suspension.[^11]

## ¶7 · 303.8–303.15

> [p. 303] In istis autem suppono ex loyca quod, si alicubi erit quantificacio, tunc est res quanta ibi, quia aliter motus et omnis respectus multiplicarentur per totum mundum. Infinita sunt talia argumenta propter que dicit katholicus quod corpus Christi est ibi virtualiter et in signo, non corpus Christi ut est in celo, sed signum eius vicarium, ut dictum est in fine proximi capituli.

And in these I suppose from [logic]{g:loyca} that, if quantification will be anywhere, then there is a quantified thing there; for otherwise motion and every relation would be multiplied through the whole world. Infinite are such arguments, on account of which the catholic says that the body of Christ is there [virtually]{g:virtualiter} and in [sign]{g:signum}: not the body of Christ as it is in heaven, but its vicarial sign, as was said at the end of the preceding chapter.[^12]

## ¶8 · 303.16–303.21

> [p. 303] Sed contra istud instatur loyce: Videtur enim primo quod corpus sit in hostia extensum, quia est ibi panis extensus et sic extensum, quia res extensa et sic sensibile sacramentum foret res sacramenti, quod est contra leges ecclesie, et sequeretur magna confusio et repugnancia predictorum.

But against this [it is objected]{g:instatur} logically: for it seems, first, that the body is extended in the host, because the bread is there extended, and so [something] extended, because a thing extended; and so the [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} would be the [reality of the sacrament]{g:res-sacramenti}, which is against [the laws of the church]{g:leges-ecclesie}; and there would follow great confusion and [incompatibility]{g:repugnancia} of the things said before.

## ¶9 · 303.22–304.7

> [p. 303] Quantum ad dictum, apparet quomodo oportet in ista materia notare equivocaciones que excludunt contradiccionem et elucidant responsionis variacionem et specialiter de equivocacione predicacionis et corporis Christi; nam secundum predicacionem tropicam concedunt sancti cum scriptura quod corpus Christi est panis vel signum sacramentaliter ipsum significans; et ad conformem sensum concedunt quod corpus Christi est in altari et mistice ille panis et sic corpus Christi quod est in celo natum de virgine et crucifixum est in altari non ipsum corpus nec aliquid [p. 304] ydemptice sed est tropice signum sacramentaliter ipsum significans; et ita intelligunt leges et dicta sanctorum quod sacramentum sensibile non est ydemptice corpus Christi; et sicut sophista replicat corpus Christi est in altari extensum equivocando predicacione, ut accipiendo predicatum in neutro genere substanciato, et tamen corpus Christi non ibi extenditur.

As to what has been said, it appears how one must in this matter note the [equivocations]{g:equivocacio} which exclude contradiction and throw light on the variation of the answer, and especially the equivocation of [predication]{g:predicacio} and of "the body of Christ". For according to [tropical predication]{g:predicacio-tropica} [the saints]{g:sancti}, with scripture, grant "the body of Christ is bread", or "a sign [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} signifying it"; and in a conforming [meaning]{g:sensus} they grant "the body of Christ is on the altar", and "[mystically]{g:mistice}, that bread", and so "the body of Christ which is in heaven, born of the virgin and crucified, is on the altar": not [as] that body itself, nor anything [304] [identically]{g:ydemptice}, but it is [tropically]{g:tropice} a sign sacramentally signifying it. And so they understand the laws and the sayings of the saints: that the perceptible sacrament is not identically the body of Christ.[^13] And just as the [sophist]{g:sophista} [rejoins]{g:replicatur}, "the body of Christ is on the altar extended", by equivocating in the predication, as by taking the predicate in the neuter gender made a substantive, and yet the body of Christ is not extended there.[^14]

## ¶10 · 304.8–304.14

> [p. 304] Sed secundo replicat sophista si hoc ibi extenditur et hoc est corpus Christi, tunc corpus Christi ibi extenditur; et per idem sequitur quod corpus in altari sit corpus Christi, quia est ibidem panis qui ubicunque fuerit est corpus Christi. Aliter enim sequeretur confusa multiplicacio inconveniencium et nimia intricacio, sophismatum.

But secondly the sophist rejoins: "If this is extended there and this is the body of Christ, then the body of Christ is extended there"; and by the same token [it follows]{g:sequitur} that the body on the altar is the body of Christ, since the bread is there which, wherever it is, is the body of Christ. For otherwise there would follow a confused multiplication of absurdities and an [exceeding]{g:nimis} entanglement of [sophisms]{g:sophisma}.

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 301.28: Christ is not present in the host dimensionally.
- 303.8: Christ's body is present in power and symbolically; not as in heaven.
- 303.15: Our opponents make logical quibbles about the extension of the bread.
- 303.21: They succeed in showing how needful it is to beware of equivocation.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Staret de Deo possibili* is taken as "it would stand, as possible to God" (consistent with God's power). *Per se* is "by itself", a quantity with no subject; *sine maiori substancia*, "without a greater substance", is rendered as printed. "Which they grant as faith": the adversaries hold the accident without a subject as of faith.

[^2]: **S.** Gilbert of Poitiers, *Liber sex principiorum*, tr. II c. 5, *De posicione* (Loserth at 300.21, citing the 1512 Venice *Organon*: *Sedere namque non magis est posicio quam accumbere neque minus*); that it says position is intrinsic is not confirmed: the text was not read, and Albert’s commentary appears to discuss whether position inheres or only assists (LEDGER K09h-1). Linus is Peter's successor in the lists of Roman bishops; the three are an example.

[^3]: **S.** "Every power united is stronger than itself dispersed" is a philosophers’ maxim, the school form of *Liber de causis* prop. 17 (16): *Omnis virtus unita plus est infinita quam virtus multiplicata*, which Thomas illustrates by many men hauling a ship together (LEDGER K09h-2). *Virtus* here is "power", a faculty of acting, not moral virtue.

[^4]: **C.** *Sentencia* is the position the perspectivists teach ("teaching"), not a sentence. *Collacio* is taken in its sense "collection", the gathering of rays, not "conferral".

[^5]: **S.** Witelo, *Perspectiva*; Loserth (301.19) refers to Wyclif's *Sermones* II, 385 for Witelo. The figure *Mukephi* is left as printed: Witelo’s Arabic name for the parabola (*Perspectiva* I.98: *parabola, et est illa quam Arabes dicunt mukefi*); IX.43 shows that a concave mirror of parabolic section gathers all the sun’s parallel rays to one point, where fire may be kindled (LEDGER K09h-3).

[^6]: **S/C.** Matt. 17:1 (DR "bringeth them up into a high mountain apart") and 17:4, where Wyclif omits *Domine* (DR "Lord, it is good for us to be here"). The "four dowries" of the glorified body are clarity, agility, subtlety, impassibility. *Sed evangelium dicit* is rendered "now": it brings the premiss closed by *ideo*. ABCD read *quod bonum*; ABC *nobis*.

[^7]: **R.** Loserth (301.30) compares Jan Příbram, who quotes this sentence verbatim among the articles of Wyclif he rejects (*quod fidelis Theologus etiam Laicus non crederet praedicanti, quod corpus Christi est septipedale*), *Professio fidei antiquae*, in Cochlaeus, *Historia Hussitarum*, p. 540 (LEDGER K09h-4).

[^8]: **C.** *Os Christi* is taken as *os, oris* "the mouth", with head and feet before it; it may be *os, ossis* "the bone". *Indivisibiles quante* is construed "indivisible [yet] quantified": parts that are quantified yet have no parts, as with an accident that informs no subject.

[^9]: **C.** *Inconveniencia* is "absurdities", the unfitting conclusions of an argument, not troubles in affairs.

[^10]: **C/T.** The sentence does not construe cleanly as printed; it is rendered as it stands, taking *solummodo* with *foret magnum* ("merely … it would be great"). A conditional may be meant: "if the body of Christ were there merely [as] great …"; a lost *si* is possible (302.29–31). D reads *quod scilicet* and *quod ergo*.

[^11]: **T.** *Quod sua informacio sit ibidem* lacks a governing verb; "[it must be]" is supplied (303.2). D reads *aliter servare*; C *bene dissolvere*.

[^12]: **C.** *Signum eius vicarium*, "its vicarial sign": the sign that stands in the body's place, as *sensus vicarius* at 296.12. The reference is to the end of ch. 8.

[^13]: **C.** *Intelligunt leges et dicta sanctorum* is taken with the saints (or those who grant these propositions) as subject. Another construal makes the laws and the sayings the subject: "so the laws and the sayings of the saints mean that …". The propositions granted are given in quotation marks, since their mode of truth is the matter.

[^14]: **C.** *Extensum* taken "in the neuter gender made a substantive" means "an extended thing" (the bread), so that "the body of Christ is extended" is granted only in that equivocal sense. *Et sicut … et tamen* has no closing correlative; the English keeps the break. In C *et sicut* stands on an erasure.
