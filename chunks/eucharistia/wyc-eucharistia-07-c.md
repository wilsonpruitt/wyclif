# *De eucharistia*, chapter 7, part 3: the sacrament is not whiteness nor any accident

## ¶1 · 197.23–198.13

> [p. 197] Item, nulla alia qualitas coexistens informatur albedine, quia hoc foret potissime qualitas prima, ex qua resultat albedo. Sed quis dicit quod qualitates elementares sunt sic colorate? Tunc enim quilibet sensus perciperet qualitates tangibiles et homo videret potissime gravitatem, cum illa vel sit forma substancialis (ut placet Averroi) vel forma propinquissime principians colorem; ymmo cum color secundum philosophos non sit nisi lux incorporata dyaphano, videtur quod ad variacionem lucis varietur et color. Cum ergo coexistencia qualitatum non sufficit ut una [p. 198] informet reliquam, necesse habet hec via dicere, quomodo causatur informacio qualitatum. Non enim est color concludere quod, si qualitas resultat ex elemento, ex materia et forma, quod propterea qualificat suum principium, quia sic omnis qualitas qualificaret Deum. Videtur ergo cum hoc sacramentum non sit album quod sit albedo et per idem qualiscunque, qualitas aut quantitas, et nullomodo aggregatum ex omnibus accidentibus in hostia consecrata, cum illud non potest informari albedine, tum quia continue variatur, tum eciam quia multa sunt ibi accidencia distincta in genere que non possunt albari; sed pocius albedo denominatur ab illis.

Again, no other coexisting [quality]{g:qualitas} is [informed]{g:informare} by whiteness, because this would be above all the [first quality]{g:qualitas-prima}, from which whiteness results.[^1] But who says that the elemental qualities are thus coloured? For then every sense would perceive tangible qualities, and a man would see heaviness above all, since that is either a [substantial form]{g:forma-substancialis} (as Averroes holds) or the form that most proximately [is the principle of]{g:principiare} colour;[^2] indeed, since colour, according to the philosophers, is nothing but light incorporated in the diaphanous, it seems that colour too varies with the variation of light.[^3] Since, then, the coexistence of qualities does not suffice for one [198] to inform the other, this way must say how the [informing]{g:informacio} of qualities is caused. For it is no [colour]{g:color}[^4] to [infer]{g:concludere} that, if a quality results from an element, from matter and form, it therefore qualifies its principle; because so every quality would qualify God. It seems, then, since this [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} is not white, that it is whiteness, and by the same token any quality or [quantity]{g:quantitas} whatever, and in no way the aggregate of all the [accidents]{g:accidens} in the [consecrated host]{g:hostia-consecrata}; since that cannot be informed by whiteness, both because it continually varies and also because there are many accidents there distinct in genus which cannot be whitened; but rather whiteness is [named]{g:denominare} from them.

## ¶2 · 198.14–199.5

> [p. 198] Sicut ergo glossa ordinaria decretorum (ut supra exposui) dicit quod hoc sacramentum non est ponderosum sed ipsa ponderositas vel sibi subiectum, sic debet dicere de albedine, duricie, sapore et eis similibus; et per consequens sunt in eadem hostia infinita sacramenta distincta in genere. Non ergo foret unitas in hostia vel denominacio accidentalis; quod manifeste repugnat sensui; videmus enim quod hostia est continua, frangitur et nutrit, odorat et sapit, sonat et descendit. Numquid credimus illud quod sic demonstratur esse nichil vel unum aggregatum ex sono, odore, sapore, descensu et alia accione? Talia enim continue variantur et accidunt sacramento, sicut conficere in fermento vel azimo. Numquid credimus quod accidens illud sit fermentatum vel azimum, extensum vel fractum et cum hoc multiplicatum et non huius, quia secundum alias partes quarum quelibet est sacramentum invisibile; alias multiplicavi argumenta que [p. 199] darent fidem fidelibus quod nulla qualitas poterit per se esse et multo minus alia genera accidentis. Non enim est figura, pulcritudo, febris vel potencia anime res talis quam debemus orare, pro se colere vel timere.

As, then, the ordinary gloss on the [decrees]{g:decretum} (as I set out above) says that this sacrament is not [heavy]{g:ponderosum} but [heaviness]{g:ponderositas} itself, or what is subject to it,[^5] so it must say of whiteness, hardness, taste and the like; and consequently there are in the same [host]{g:hostia} infinite sacraments distinct in genus. There would therefore be no unity in the host, nor accidental [naming]{g:denominacio}; which is manifestly [incompatible]{g:repugnare} with sense; for we see that the host is continuous, is broken and nourishes, smells and tastes, sounds and descends. Do we believe that that which is thus pointed out is nothing, or one aggregate of sound, smell, taste, descent and other action? For such things continually vary and befall the sacrament, as does [consecrating]{g:conficere} in leavened or unleavened [bread].[^6] Do we believe that that accident is leavened or unleavened, extended or broken, and with this multiplied, and not such, because according to other parts, each of which is an invisible sacrament?[^7] Elsewhere I have multiplied arguments that [199] would give the faithful faith that no quality can be [of itself]{g:per-se}, and much less the other genera of accident. For figure, beauty, fever or a [power]{g:potencia} of the soul is not such a thing as we ought to pray to, worship for itself or fear.[^8]

## ¶3 · 199.6–199.18

> [p. 199] Ideo quantum ad istud laudo Deum qui me liberavit ab isto scandaloso et derisibili errore de quidditate huius sacramenti. In qua materia philosophi et pagani derident nimirum nostram ficticiam de incertitudine et variacione circa quidditatem hostie consecrate. Est enim panis aut vinum quod subiectat omnia illa accidencia. Et illud sentitur sensacione multiplici cui nova accidencia non consecrata possunt accidere, que non sint pars sacramenti sicut accidencia hostie, que tamen omnia reducuntur cum tota universitate creata ad quandam unitatem in Deo cui omnia instituuntur, ut ipsum significent et omnia modo suo ipsum dicunt atque aliqua magis sacra et alia minus sacra.

Therefore, as to this, I praise God, who has freed me from this scandalous and laughable error about the quiddity of this sacrament. In which matter philosophers and pagans deride, no wonder, our [fiction]{g:ficticia} of uncertainty and variation about the quiddity of the consecrated host. For it is [bread]{g:panis} or [wine]{g:vinum} that [is the subject of]{g:subiectare} all those accidents. And that is [perceived]{g:sentire} by manifold [sensation]{g:sensacio}, and to it new accidents, not consecrated, can befall, which are not part of the sacrament as the accidents of the host are; all of which, nevertheless, are brought back, with the whole [created totality]{g:universitas-creata}, to a certain unity in God, for whom all things are instituted, that they may signify him; and all things in their manner speak him, and some are more [sacred]{g:sacer} and others less sacred.[^9]

## ¶4 · 199.19–199.33

> [p. 199] Sacramentum autem altaris potest vocari, ut sepe dixi, corpus sic rite benedictum a presbitero, quelibet eciam eius pars; et quodlibet eius sensibile accidens potest vocari secundarie vel per accidens sacramentum, sicut et corpus aggregatum ex corpore consecrato et alio sibi commixto; ymmo quandoque corpus permanens vocatur sacramentum et quandoque corpus successivum. Sed inpossibile est sacrum accidens, potissime quantitatem et qualitatem, esse per se sacramentum, cum secundum Augustinum non poterit per se esse. Et patet quod nullum istorum sacramentorum est ydemptice Deus vel pars eius, sed signum efficax quod ad sensum equivocum et tropicum suscipit predicacionem partis Christi in modis loquendi sanctorum et Romane ecclesie.

And the [sacrament of the altar]{g:sacramentum-altaris} can be called, as I have often said, the body thus duly blessed by the [presbyter]{g:presbiter}, and also any part of it;[^10] and any [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} accident of it can be called a sacrament secondarily or [by accident]{g:per-accidens}, as also the body aggregated from the consecrated body and another mixed with it; indeed, sometimes a [permanent]{g:permanens} body is called the sacrament, and sometimes a [successive]{g:successivum} body. But it is impossible that a sacred accident, above all quantity and quality, should be of itself a sacrament, since according to Augustine it cannot be of itself.[^11] And it is clear that none of these sacraments is [identically]{g:ydemptice} God or a part of him, but an efficacious [sign]{g:signum} which, in an equivocal and tropical [meaning]{g:sensus}, receives the [predication]{g:predicacio} of a part of Christ in the manners of speaking of [the saints]{g:sancti} and of the Roman [church]{g:ecclesia}.[^12]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 197.23: Whiteness does not inform another quality.
- 198.14: Following this line of argument we find that the sacrament is all kinds of accidents. Consequently in one host there are many sacraments. Thus we are led into endless follies.
- 199.5: Wyclif thanks God for freeing him from all this error.
- 199.9: The quiddity of the host is bread and wine.
- 199.11: In this substance the accidents unite to be all alike symbols of God.
- 199.18: The sensible accidents of the host may in a way be called the sacrament, but having no separate existence it cannot be the sacrament. A sacrament is not identically but symbolically God.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Qualitas prima*: a primary quality of the elements (hot, cold, wet, dry), from which colour was held to result; if anything were informed by whiteness, it would be that.
[^2]: **S/T.** Averroes on heaviness as substantial form or the form nearest to it; place not given (to be checked, LEDGER K07c-1). ABCD *Averrois*; D *principans*; E *ut homo* for *et homo*. *Colorate* here is literal "coloured", not the GLOSSARY *colorate* "with colour enough".
[^3]: **S.** Colour as light incorporated in the diaphanous: the Aristotelian definition (*De sensu* 3, 439b; to be checked, LEDGER K07c-2).
[^4]: **C.** *Non est color*: "it is no colour", no plausible ground (GLOSSARY *color*), here in a passage on colour proper.
[^5]: **S/T.** The ordinary gloss on Gratian, *De cons.* D. 2; Loserth refers back to p. 184 (LEDGER K07c-3, editor; the canon to be checked). C reads *neque ponderositas*, "nor heaviness" (198.15–16), which would deny both; the print is translated.
[^6]: **C.** *Conficere* "consecrate" (GLOSSARY): whether the sacrament was consecrated in leavened or unleavened bread is one more of its varying accidents.
[^7]: **C/T.** *Et non huius*: Loserth notes *huiusmodi* is better, "and not such"; the clause *quia secundum alias partes …* has no verb of its own and is taken as "because [it is so] according to other parts". Words may be lost (see the Latin doubts).
[^8]: **C.** *Figura* is shape here, not the sacramental figure of the GLOSSARY row; *potencia anime* a power of the soul (*potencia*, CONTEXTUAL row: "power"). The point is that an accident cannot be the object of worship.
[^9]: **C/T.** *Omnia … ipsum dicunt*: all things "speak" God, as signs of him (cf. *dicere* "signify", GLOSSARY, of a term). ABCD read *alia magis* (C has *aliqua* in the margin); D *sacramenta* for *sacra*.
[^10]: **C.** *Corpus* here is the body of the bread that the presbyter blesses, not the body of Christ. ABD *vocare*; ABC *ritu* for *rite*.
[^11]: **S.** Augustine on an accident's not being able to be of itself; place not given (to be checked, LEDGER K07c-4).
[^12]: **C/G.** *Ad sensum equivocum et tropicum*: the predication *panis est corpus Christi* holds in an equivocal and tropical meaning (cf. *predicacio tropica* "tropical predication"), not identically. D reads *dominus* for *Deus* (199.30).
