# *De benedicta incarnacione*, chapter 2, part 1: how this position differs from Arius: John 10 and Matthew 22

## ¶1 · 13.0–13.15

> [p. 13] [Declarat quomodo ista sentencia discrepat ab heresi Arriana. The assertion of the reality of Christ's humanity, by maintaining that as man he is a creature, gives no support to the errors of Arius, Sabellius, or Nestorius.] Ex istis per modum corollarii elucescit quomodo ista posicio⁵ differt ab opinionibus⁶ hereticorum de Christo heretice senciencium.

[It [makes clear]{g:declarare} how this [teaching]{g:sentencia} is at variance with the Arian heresy.]

From these [points] it shines out, by way of corollary, how this [position]{g:posicio} differs from the [opinions]{g:opinio} of the heretics who thought heretically about Christ.[^1]

## ¶2 · 13.16–14.7

> [p. 13] Et primo de opinione⁷ Arrii de quo narrat Eusebius in Ecclesiastica Historia li° 10° ca. 13. 'Arrius, inquit, pergens ad⁸ ecclesiam episcoporum et populorum frequencia constipatus, humane necessitatis causa⁹ ad locum publicum¹⁰ declinavit; ubi cum sederet, intestina eius atque omnia viscera in secessu¹¹ cumulorum¹² defluxerunt, et ita in apto [p. 14] loco dignam mortem blasfemie¹ et² fetide mentis² exsolvit.' Et quia secundum Magistrum Sentenciarum in 3°· Dist. 2ᵃ. Arrii hec fuisse perfidia³ legitur ut Christum esse⁴ creaturam fateretur, ut capit a beato Ambrosio primo libro de Trinitate cap. 9°· 'Ideo, inquit Ambrosius, diffusa sunt Arrii viscera et crepuit medius prostratus in faciem ea, quibus Christum⁵ negaverat, feda ora⁶ pollutus.'

And first, of the opinion of Arius, of whom Eusebius tells in the *Ecclesiastical History*, book 10, chapter 13: "Arius," he says, "going to the [church]{g:ecclesia} thronged by a crowd of bishops and peoples, turned aside to a public place for the sake of human necessity; where, as he sat, his intestines and all his bowels flowed down into the channels of the privy, and so in a fitting [14] place he paid the death his blasphemy and fetid mind deserved."[^2] And because, according to [the Master]{g:magister} of the Sentences in III, distinction 2, the perfidy of Arius is read to have been this, that he confessed "Christ is a [creature]{g:creatura}", as he takes it from blessed Ambrose, *On the Trinity*, book 1, chapter 9: "Therefore," says Ambrose, "the bowels of Arius were poured out, and he burst asunder in the midst, fallen headlong on his face, befouled in that foul mouth with which he had denied Christ."[^3]

## ¶3 · 14.8–14.17

> [p. 14] Quando⁷ ergo illa⁸ opinio videtur Arrio in hoc consentire bonum videtur differenciam⁹ declarare. Nam propterea videtur Magistro et ipsum sequentibus potissime quod non debet concedi simpliciter Christum esse creaturam vel factum sed cum determinacione¹⁰ quod fuit factus secundum hominem. Primo ergo notandum quod nunquam fuit hereticus quin in aliquo dixit verum: omnes enim concesserunt Deum esse: et ideo valde inconsonum est doctori imponere alteri¹¹ heresim Arrianam, nisi prius diligenter notaverit in quibus et quomodo talis heresis est fundata.

Since, therefore, that opinion seems to agree with Arius in this, it seems good to make clear the [difference]{g:differencia}.[^4] For it is above all for this reason that it seems to the Master and those who follow him that "Christ is a creature" or "made" ought not to be granted [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}, but with the [determination]{g:determinacio} that he was made as to the man. First, then, it must be noted that there was never a heretic who did not say something true in some point: for all granted "God is"; and so it is very discordant for a doctor to impute the Arian heresy to another unless he has first diligently noted in what and how such a heresy is founded.

## ¶4 · 14.18–14.22

> [p. 14] Stetit ergo heresis Arriana in his tribus precipue. Primo in hoc quod negavit Christum esse Deum deorum et magnum Deum, cum posuit eum¹² esse non eiusdem substancie cum Patre, sed ipsos differre ab invicem eciam secundum deitatem substancialiter sicut¹³ differunt personaliter.

The Arian heresy, then, stood chiefly in these three [points]. First, in this: that [Arius] denied "Christ is the God of gods and a great God", since he held that he is not of the same substance with the Father, but that they differ from one another even [as to the Godhead]{g:secundum-deitatem} substantially, as they differ personally.

## ¶5 · 14.23–15.31

> [p. 14] Unde Augustinus Epa. 33 ad Bonifacium, 'Ut, inquit, breviter¹⁴ insinuem dilectioni tue quid inter Arrianorum¹⁵ et Donatistarum intersit errorem: Arriani Patris et Filii et Spiritus Sancti diversas esse substancias dicunt; Donatiste autem non hoc¹⁶ dicunt, sed unam Trinitatis substanciam confitentur.' Dixit itaque¹⁷ Arrius quod summa essencia que est Christus sit citra essenciam Dei Patris, ut est essencia angeli vel hominis quos scriptura sacra vocat deos pluraliter, ut allegat Salvator Iudeis Ioh. 10. 'Nonne scriptum est in lege vestra, 'Ego dixi dii estis?' Si igitur eos vocat deos, [p. 15] ad quos sermo Dei factus est, et non potest solvi scriptura; quem Pater sanctificavit et misit in mundum, quomodo et vos dicitis 'blasfemas,'¹ quia dixi 'Filius Dei sum¹?' Et forte ex isto dicto capta est heresis Arriana. Unde dicit Augustinus Ome 48.² quod Iudei audientes illud Ioh. 10. 'Ego et Pater unum sumus,' et intelligentes Christum intendere quod sit eadem substancia cum Patre, 'Iudei, inquit, intellexerunt quod Arriani non intellexerunt; quoniam senserunt non posse dici ''ego et Pater unum sumus,'' nisi ubi equalitas est Patris et Filii. Sed Dominus videns Judeos pre ira non ferre splendorem veritatis, eum temperavit in verbis eis allegans illud Psⁱ· 81.³ ''Ego dixi dii estis etc.''' Nec est putandum quod Veritas illuserit⁴ Iudeis⁵ sophistice in hoc dicto, sed dulciter manuduxit mitigando eorum rancorem ex⁶ scriptura patriarche David plus eis autentica⁷ inter omnes. Cum⁸ enim non fuit in Christo EST et NON, et dixit⁸ eis superius Ioh. 10. quod ipse est omnipotens et eadem substancia cum Patre: patet quod illam sentenciam postmodum non retractat. Minor patet notato textu illius capⁱ. Nam ibi dicitur: 'oves mee vocem meam audiunt et ego vitam eternam do eis, et non rapiet eas⁹ quisquam de manu mea' et cetera que secuntur. Probatur illa trimembris conclusio ex ibi positis. Nam ibi¹⁰ dicitur, 'Pater meus quod dedit michi maius omnibus est:' ergo Pater meus¹¹ est omnipotens, et per consequens 'nemo potest rapere de manu Patris mei,' et ultra sequitur¹² 'nemo potest rapere de manu Patris mei;¹³ et 'Ego et Pater unum sumus;' ergo 'nemo potest rapere oves de manu mea.' Istam itaque connexionem subtilissimam Iudei conceperant quam ignorabant Arriani. Ex quo patet quod in deduccione sequenti eandem conclusionem intenderat sed medio plus occulto.

Hence Augustine, letter 33, to Boniface: "That I may briefly," he says, "convey to your Love what difference there is between the error of the Arians and of the Donatists: the Arians say that the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit are diverse substances; but the Donatists do not say this, but confess one substance of the Trinity."[^5] So Arius said that the highest [essence]{g:essencia} which is Christ falls short of the essence of God the Father, as is the essence of an angel or of a man, whom holy scripture calls gods in the plural, as the Saviour [alleges]{g:allegare} to the Jews, John 10: "Is it not written in your law: I said, you are gods? If therefore he calls them gods, [15] to whom the word of God was spoken, and the scripture cannot be broken; how do you also say of him whom the Father hath sanctified and sent into the world: Thou blasphemest; because I said: I am the Son of God?"[^6] And perhaps the Arian heresy was taken from this saying. Hence Augustine says, Homily 48, that the Jews, hearing that [text] of John 10, "I and the Father are one", and understanding Christ to [maintain]{g:intendere} that he is the same substance with the Father: "the Jews," he says, "understood what the Arians did not understand; for they perceived that 'I and the Father are one' could not be said except where there is equality of the Father and the Son. But the Lord, seeing that the Jews for anger did not bear the splendour of the truth, tempered it in words, alleging to them that [text] of Psalm 81, 'I have said: You are gods, etc.'"[^7] Nor is it to be thought that the Truth mocked the Jews [sophistically]{g:sophistice} in this saying; but he gently led them by the hand, mitigating their rancour from the scripture of the patriarch David, the most [authentic]{g:autenticus} to them of all. For since there was not in Christ "It is" and "It is not", and he said to them above, in John 10, that he is omnipotent and the same substance with the Father, it is clear that he does not afterwards retract that teaching.[^8] The minor is clear when the text of that chapter is noted. For there it is said: "My sheep hear my voice; and I give them life everlasting, and no man shall pluck them out of my hand", and the rest that follows. That three-membered [conclusion]{g:conclusio} is proved from what is laid down there. For there it is said, "That which my Father hath given me is greater than all": therefore my Father is omnipotent, and consequently "no one can snatch them out of the hand of my Father"; and further it [follows]{g:sequitur}: "no one can snatch them out of the hand of my Father; and 'I and the Father are one'; therefore 'no one can snatch the sheep out of my hand.'" So the Jews had grasped this most subtle [conditional]{g:connexio}, of which the Arians [were ignorant]{g:ignorare}.[^9] From which it is clear that in the deduction that follows he maintained the same conclusion, but by a more hidden [middle term]{g:media}.

## ¶6 · 16.1–16.33

> [p. 16] Pro quo notandum quod sicut Judei multum appreciati¹ sunt prophetam² David, sic³ ex eius textu cognoverant quod Messias venturus ex eis foret tam Deus quam homo. Hoc videtur evidens ex argumento Salvatoris facto Phariseis, Mt 22. ubi sic congregatis Phariseis interrogat ab eis⁴ Jesus dicens,⁵ 'Quid vobis videtur de Christo? cuius filius est? dicunt ei, 'David:'⁶ ait illis, 'quomodo ergo David⁷ in spiritu vocat eum dominum dicens: 'dixit Dominus Domino meo, 'sede a dextris, donec ponam inimicos tuos scabellum pedum tuorum.' Si ergo David vocat eum Dominum quomodo filius eius est? Et nemo poterat ei respondere verbum, nec ausus est quisquam ex illa die eum amplius interrogare.' Non enim contendebant⁶ illi legis periti in ampliacione⁸ verbi de presenti scientes ex sensu scripture quod apud Deum sunt cuncta⁹ presencia. Ideo sciverunt Christum vel Messiam esse filium David pro quodam tempore. Sciverunt 2^do illud Psalmi 109 quod Veritas allegavit dictum esse de Christo. Sciverunt 3° quod propheta tam eximius non vocaret filium suum tam pauperem et abiectum Dominum suum secundum naturam ab¹⁰ eo assumptam sed secundum naturam¹⁰ superiorem. Ideo non affuit eis vel ullum¹¹ coloratum subterfugium evadendi. Unde pro 2° argumento Salvatoris supponitur quod Verbum vel Sermo Dei quod idem est, sit eternum idem substancialiter Deo Patri. Patet hoc per illud Psalmi 32¹² 'Verbo Domini celi firmati sunt.' 2^do supponitur quod dictum verbum fuit incarnandum. Patet hoc per illud Psalmi 106¹³ 'misit verbum suum et sanavit eos.' Unde et dictum Verbum vocaverunt prophete concorditer Messiam vel Christum eis promissum. 3° supponitur quod dicti Verbi participacione sit multitudo hominum in¹⁴ Christum directe¹⁴ credencium deificanda participative, sicut carbo ex ignis participacione est ignitus. Patet hoc per illud Ps. 81.¹⁵ 'Ego dixi dii estis.'

On which it must be noted that, as the Jews greatly prized the prophet David, so from his text they had come to know that the Messiah who was to come from them would be both God and man. This seems evident from the Saviour's argument made to the Pharisees, Matthew 22, where, the Pharisees being thus gathered together, Jesus asks of them, saying: "What think you of Christ? Whose son is he? They say to him: David's. He saith to them: How then doth David in spirit call him Lord, saying: The Lord said to my Lord: Sit on the right hand, until I make thy enemies thy footstool? If David then call him Lord, how is he his son? And no man was able to answer him a word: neither durst any man from that day forth ask him any more questions." For those learned in the law did not dispute over the [ampliation]{g:ampliacio} of the verb in the present [tense], knowing from the sense of scripture that with God all things are present.[^10] Therefore they knew that Christ or the Messiah is the son of David for a certain time. Second, they knew that [the text] of Psalm 109 which the Truth alleged was said of Christ. Third, they knew that so eminent a prophet would not call his son, so poor and abject, his Lord as to the [nature]{g:natura} [assumed]{g:assumere} from him, but as to a higher nature. Therefore there was not available to them even any subterfuge [given colour]{g:colorare} for escaping.[^11] Hence for the Saviour's second argument it is supposed that the [Word]{g:verbum} or [Speech]{g:sermo} of God, which is the same, is eternal, substantially the same as God the Father.[^12] This is clear from that [text] of Psalm 32: "By the word of the Lord the heavens were established." Second, it is supposed that the said Word was to be incarnate. This is clear from that [text] of Psalm 106: "He sent his word, and healed them." Hence also the prophets with one accord called the said Word the Messiah or Christ promised to them. Third, it is supposed that by participation in the said Word a multitude of men who believe directly in Christ is to be deified [participatively]{g:participative}, as a coal is set on fire by participation in fire. This is clear from that [text] of Psalm 81: "I have said: You are gods."

## ¶7 · 17.1–17.19

> [p. 17] Istis suppositis formetur racio sic ex sentencia Salvatoris: Ille Sermo vel Verbum, in quo concordant omnes prophete de operibus miraculosis, de tempore et aliis circumstanciis, et illis quibus mittitur salvandis et deificandis, est verus Messias, et per consequens Deus et homo iuxta textus propheticos: sed in me omnia ista conveniunt: ergo sum Deus¹ et homo: et per consequens non solum¹ participacione deificatus.² Ideo hic³ dicit Augustinus; 'Si Sermone⁴ Dei facto ad⁵ homines fiunt ipsi dii, tunc⁶ participando fiunt ipsi dii⁶; et per consequens Sermo ille, quo participant, erit Deus. Si lumine. luminati dii sunt, lumen quod illuminat non est Deus? si calefacti igne salutari⁷ dii efficiuntur, ignis unde calefiunt non est Deus?' Oportet ergo quod⁸ Sermo, quo⁸ fontaliter deificantur homines ex unitate substancie cum suo eternaliter adintra dicente, sit eadem deitas cum dicente; et per consequens non blasfemat⁹ dicendo se naturalem filium Dei esse. Et ex ignorancia huius sensus literalis scripture exciderant¹⁰ Arriani, sicut et Sabelliani, ut recitat Augustinus Ome. 47.

These being supposed, let the argument be formed thus from the Saviour's teaching: That Speech or Word, in whom all the prophets agree concerning the miraculous works, concerning the time and the other circumstances, and concerning those to whom he is sent, who are to be saved and deified, is the true Messiah, and consequently God and man according to the prophetic texts; now in me all these things come together; therefore I am God and man; and consequently not deified only by participation.[^13] So Augustine says here: "If by the Speech of God made to men they themselves become gods, then by participating they themselves become gods; and consequently that Speech in which they participate will be God. If those lit by light are gods, is the light that lights them not God? If those warmed by the saving fire are made gods, is the fire by which they are warmed not God?"[^14] It must be, therefore, that the Speech by which men are [fontally]{g:fontaliter} deified, from unity of substance with the one who speaks him eternally within, is the same Godhead as the speaker;[^15] and consequently he does not blaspheme in saying that he is the natural son of God. And from ignorance of this literal sense of scripture the Arians had fallen away, as also the Sabellians, as Augustine recounts, Homily 47.[^16]

## Notes

[^1]: **T.** *posicio* (13.15): A B C read *proposicio*; *opinionibus*: B *posicionibus*. The GLOSSARY keeps the three terms apart; the print's *posicio* is the position this treatise defends.
[^2]: **S.** Rufinus's continuation of Eusebius, *Historia ecclesiastica* X (here book 10, chapter 13), to be checked (LEDGER I02a-1). The print's *dignam mortem blasfemie et fetide mentis*: M has *debitam* for *et fetide mentis*. **T** *in secessu cumulorum* (13.22), "into the privy of heaps", does not construe; the English follows Harris's suggested *cuniculorum* "channels" (O *cuñulorum*, B *tumulorum*; A B C *secessus*).
[^3]: **S/C.** Peter Lombard, *Sent.* III d. 2 as printed (the Master's chapter on whether Christ is a creature is d. 11), and Ambrose, *De fide* I.19 (called *De Trinitate*), with *crepuit medius* from Acts 1:18 (Judas): both to be checked (LEDGER I02a-2, I02a-3). **C** The *quia* clause has no main clause of its own; its consequence is carried by Ambrose's *Ideo* inside the quotation.
[^4]: **C.** *illa opinio*: "that opinion" is the position defended here, which seems to agree with Arius in calling Christ a creature; O reads *ista* "this" (14.8). *Quando* is causal (A B C *Cum*).
[^5]: **S.** Augustine, letter to Boniface, *Ep.* 185.1 (here "letter 33"), to be checked (LEDGER I02a-4). *Dilectioni tue*: "your Love", a form of address.
[^6]: **S.** John 10:34–36, with Wyclif's departures from the Vulgate translated: *Si igitur eos vocat deos* for *Si illos dixit deos*, and *quomodo et vos dicitis* for *vos dicitis*.
[^7]: **C.** *dicit Augustinus … quod Iudei audientes … et intelligentes …*: the *quod* clause breaks off and Augustine's own words take over. **S** Augustine, *In Iohannis evangelium tractatus* 48 (here "Homily 48"; A B C *ep. 49*), on John 10:30–36, to be checked (LEDGER I02a-5).
[^8]: **S.** 2 Cor. 1:19, "was not: It is and It is not". The argument is a syllogism whose minor ("he said above that he is omnipotent and the same substance") is proved next.
[^9]: **C.** *connexionem*: the GLOSSARY's "conditional" (*si … tunc*); here the whole chain just set out, which has that form: if the Father is omnipotent and he and the Son are one, no one snatches the sheep from the Son. John 10:27–28 is quoted with a cut (*et ego cognosco eas, et sequuntur me … et non peribunt in aeternum*).
[^10]: **G.** *ampliacio verbi de presenti*: the logicians' extending of a present-tense verb to other times. *Cuius filius est?* is in the present; the lawyers did not quibble over it, since with God all times are present.
[^11]: **C.** *coloratum subterfugium*: a subterfuge with a plausible ground (*color*, the period sense, not "pretext").
[^12]: **C.** *Verbum vel Sermo*: *sermo* is "Speech", to keep it apart from *Verbum* "the Word", which Wyclif says is the same; in the scripture quoted (John 10:35) the Douay-Rheims "word" renders *sermo*. The pronoun is "he", as for the Word.
[^13]: **T.** *deificatus*: all the manuscripts read *deificus*; Harris prints *deificatus*, against them, and the English follows the print. A B C have *Deus et per consequens homo non solum*.
[^14]: **S.** Augustine, *Tract. in Ioh.* 48.9, quoted freely, to be checked (LEDGER I02a-6). The print has a full stop after *lumine*.
[^15]: **C.** *ex unitate substancie cum suo … dicente* is read with *sit eadem deitas*: the Speech is the same Godhead as its speaker by unity of substance with him. It could instead go with *deificantur*: men are deified by the Speech because of its unity of substance with the speaker; the sense is close.
[^16]: **S.** Augustine "Homily 47", on the Arians and Sabellians: to be checked (LEDGER I02a-7).
