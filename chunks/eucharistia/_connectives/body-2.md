# sed/autem extract, wave 2: chunks 01-b 01-c 01-d

### 01-b ¶1 · 15.27–16.29  (8 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 15] Hic dicitur quod primum non sequitur, **sed** destructo errore blasphemie servaretur et comprehenderetur sacerdotalis potestas in suis limitibus. Nichil enim horribilius quam quod quilibet sacerdos celebrans [p. 16] facit vel consecrat cotidie corpus Christi; nam Deus noster non est Deus recens nec corpus suum cum sit summe sacrum atque perpetuum est sic sacramentale vel noviter faciendum, **sed** nos sacerdotes facimus et benedicimus hostiam consecratam que non est corpus dominicum **sed** efficax eius signum. Ista **autem** benediccio est maior quam benediccio calicis vel alterius ornamenti strictissime episcopo reservata, cuius causam Deus novit. Satis ergo est ad honorem et potenciam sacerdotis quod ipse benedicit, consecrat et conficit sacramentum; quod est hostia consecrata et non corpus Christi **sed** eius signum vel tegumentum; unde quia sacerdos non habet potestatem faciendi hoc sacramentum nisi ministeriale Deo principaliter faciente, ideo dicitur conficere non corpus Christi **sed** nudum sacramentum, et sic propter sanctitatem vite et non propter illud adeo est laudandus. Et sic missa sacerdotis sancti est devocius audienda et missa peccatoris notorii est fugienda. Nec emimus suffragia sacerdotum pecunia **sed** sine convencione civili retribuimus eis vite necessaria, sicut Apostolus docet esse faciendum cum predicatoribus I Cor. IX. Nota tamen quod sicut laudative non effective benedicimus tam Deo quam domino, sic et benedicimus corpori Christi et sanguini, non faciendo ipsum esse beatum vel sanctum, **sed** laudando et promulgando sanctitatem et beatitudinem quam Deus in corpore suo instituit, et sic ymmolamus Christum et ipsum offerimus Deo patri.

ENGLISH: To this [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that the first does not [follow]{g:sequitur};[^1] but, once the error of blasphemy were destroyed, the priestly power would be kept and contained within its [limits]{g:limites}. For nothing is more horrible than that any [priest]{g:sacerdos} who celebrates [16] makes or consecrates [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} daily; for our God is not a new God,[^2] nor is his body, since it is supremely holy and everlasting, thus sacramental or to be made anew; but we priests make and bless the consecrated [host]{g:hostia}, which is not the Lord's body but its effective [sign]{g:signum}. And this blessing is greater than the blessing of a chalice or of another ornament, which is most strictly reserved to the [bishop]{g:episcopus}, the cause of which God knows. It is enough, therefore, for the honour and [power]{g:potencia} of the priest that he blesses, consecrates and [confects]{g:conficere} the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum};[^3] which is the consecrated host and not the body of Christ but its sign or [covering]{g:tegumentum}; hence, because the priest does not have power of making this sacrament except [ministerially]{g:ministerialiter}, God making it principally,[^4] therefore he is said to consecrate not the body of Christ but the bare sacrament; and so it is for the holiness of his life, and not for that, that he is so greatly to be praised. And so the mass of a holy priest is to be heard more devoutly, and the mass of a notorious sinner is to be shunned. Nor do we buy the [suffrages]{g:suffragia} of priests with money, but without a civil contract we pay back to them the necessaries of life, as the Apostle teaches is to be done with preachers, 1 Cor. 9.[^5] Note, however, that just as we bless God as well as a lord [by way of praise]{g:laudative}, not [by way of effect]{g:effective},[^6] so too we bless the body of Christ and the blood, not by making it to be blessed or holy, but by praising and proclaiming the holiness and blessedness which God instituted in his body; and so we immolate Christ and offer him to God the Father.

### 01-b ¶2 · 16.30–17.22  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 16] Nota eciam ulterius ad accepcionem spiritualem corporis Christi quod non consistit in corporali accepcione, [p. 17] masticacione vel taccione hostie consecrate, **sed** in pastione anime ex fructuosa fide secundum quam nutritur spiritus noster in Domino. Et propter ignoranciam istius manducacionis corporis Christi et pocionis sui sanguinis multi discipuli abierunt retrorsum, ut patet Joh. VI°, 61: Durus, inquiunt, est iste sermo et quis potest eum audire? Nichil enim horribilius quam necessario manducare carnaliter carnem et bibere carnaliter sanguinem hominis tam tenere predilecti; ideo dicit Christus apostolis qui ultra indignos discipulos qui retrorsum abierant digni erant informari in sensu vivifico, quod sensus carnalis istius non proderit, **sed** spiritus est, hoc est spiritualis sensus, qui vivificat, ubi occasione male accepta sensus alius scandalizat. Oportet ergo fidelem credere quod illud quo anima pascitur obiective est spiritualis cibus anime; et sic oportet carnem Christi et sanguinem tam varie pro peccatoribus tribulatis accendere in nostris spiritibus amorem qui est cibus anime ut grate faciamus quodammodo recompensam. Et sic intelligit Veritas de manducacione et potacione spiritualibus quas oportet esse in quolibet salvandorum.

ENGLISH: Note also, further, as to the spiritual reception of the body of Christ, that it does not consist in bodily reception, [17] chewing or touching of the consecrated host, but in the feeding of the soul out of fruitful faith, according to which our spirit is nourished in the Lord. And through ignorance of this [eating]{g:manducacio} of the body of Christ and drinking of his blood many disciples went back, as is clear in John 6:61: "This saying," they say, "is hard, and who can hear it?"[^7] For nothing is more horrible than of necessity to eat carnally the flesh and to drink carnally the blood of a man so tenderly loved; therefore Christ says to the apostles, who, beyond the unworthy disciples who had gone back, were worthy to be instructed in the life-giving sense, that the carnal sense of this will not profit, but "it is the spirit", that is, the spiritual sense, "that quickeneth", where, the occasion being ill taken, the other sense gives scandal. Therefore the faithful man must believe that that by which the soul is fed, as its object, is the spiritual food of the soul; and so the flesh of Christ and the blood must, for sinners so variously in tribulation,[^8] kindle in our spirits love, which is the food of the soul, so that we may gratefully make some sort of recompense. And so the Truth understands [this] of the spiritual eating and drinking which must be in every one of those who are to be saved.

### 01-b ¶3 · 17.23–17.30  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 17] Unde Augustinus in Omelia: Hoc, inquit, est manducare illam escam et illum bibere potum, in Christo manere et illum manentem in se habere. Ac per hoc qui non manet in Christo et in quo non manet Christus, procul dubio non manducat spiritualiter eius carnem, licet carnaliter et visibiliter premat sacramenta corporis et sanguinis Christi, **sed** magis tante rei sacramentum ad iudicium sibi manducat et bibit.

ENGLISH: Hence Augustine in a homily: "This," he says, "is to eat that meat and to drink that drink: to abide in Christ and to have him abiding in oneself. And by this, he who does not abide in Christ, and in whom Christ does not abide, without doubt does not eat his flesh [spiritually]{g:spiritualiter}, although he carnally and visibly presses the sacraments of the body and blood of Christ; but rather he eats and drinks the sacrament of so great a thing to judgment for himself."[^9]

### 01-b ¶4 · 18.1–18.13  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 18] Et sic ex verbis Christi et sui discipuli Augustini tria notabilia sunt notanda, primo quod caro Christi et sanguis solum spiritualiter solent comedi; nam Deus ordinavit eternaliter quod sanctus suus non videret corrupcionem in aliquo membro suo. Patet secundo quod nec bestia nec prescitus manducat sic corpus Christi, licet manducet corporaliter sacramentum. Et patet tercio mira subtilitas verborum doctoris qua dicit non quod indignus visibiliter premit dentibus corpus Christi **sed** quod visibiliter premit dentibus sacramentum corporis Christi et sanguinis. Illud enim sacramentum valde distinguitur a corpore Christi quod est res huius sacramenti.

ENGLISH: And so from the words of Christ and of his disciple Augustine three notable points are to be noted: first, that the flesh and blood of Christ are wont to be eaten only spiritually; for God ordained eternally that "his holy one should not see corruption" in any [member]{g:membrum} of his.[^10] It is clear secondly that neither a beast nor one of [the foreknown]{g:prescitus}[^11] eats the body of Christ in this way, although he eats the sacrament [bodily]{g:corporaliter}. And there is clear thirdly the wonderful subtlety of the doctor's words, by which he says not that the unworthy man visibly presses the body of Christ with his teeth, but that he visibly presses with his teeth the sacrament of the body of Christ and blood.[^12] For that sacrament is greatly distinguished from the body of Christ, which is the [reality of this sacrament]{g:res-sacramenti}.

### 01-b ¶5 · 18.14–18.20  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 18] Unde notanda est differencia quam ponit Augustinus inter esum carnalem et spiritualem. Nam in esu carnali comestum cedit in nutrimentum edentis, cum inbibitur eius membris. In comestione vero spirituali est econtra, cum comedens corpus Christi spiritualiter exhinc incorporatur membris ecclesie et sic Christo; comestus **autem** spiritualiter superat comedentem.

ENGLISH: Hence the [difference]{g:differencia} is to be noted which Augustine sets between carnal and spiritual [eating]{g:esus}. For in carnal eating what is eaten passes into the nourishment of the eater, since it is imbibed by his members. But in spiritual eating it is the reverse, since he who eats the body of Christ spiritually is thereby incorporated into the members of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, and so into Christ; and the one eaten spiritually overcomes the one who eats.[^13]

### 01-b ¶6 · 18.21–18.29  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 18] Nec valent argucie et ficticie sompniate contra istam sentenciam. Arguunt enim quidam quod, si homo sentit hominem, dum sentit eius indumenta ex motu vite latentis intrinsecus, quod per idem sentit corpus Christi, dum sentit sacramentum ex eius mocione. **Sed** ista argucia procedens per locum a simili argueret quod homo videt Deum et animam et sic de qualibet quinque sensacionum, quando sentit subiectum ex eorum mocione vitali intrinseca.

ENGLISH: Nor do the [quibbles]{g:argucie} and dreamed-up [fictions]{g:ficticia} against this [teaching]{g:sentencia}[^14] avail. For some argue that, if a man [perceives]{g:sentire} a man while he perceives his clothes through the motion of the life hidden within, then by the same token he perceives the body of Christ while he perceives the sacrament through its motion. But this quibble, proceeding by the topic [from likeness]{g:a-simili}, would argue that a man sees God and the soul, and so of each of the five [sensations]{g:sensacio}, when he perceives a [subject]{g:subiectum} through their vital inner motion.

### 01-b ¶7 · 18.30–19.8  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 18] Conceditur ergo quod contingit sentire subiectum per accidens modo quo dicitur, **sed** tunc oportet sensum communem vel aliquem sensum intrinsecum [p. 19] comprehendere subiectum ex mocione per se sensibilis adiacentis. **Sed** modo nullus sensus hominis **sed** pure intellectus per fidem percipit corpus Christi; quod patet ex hoc quod commixta hostia consecrata cum aliis homo non plus discernit eam ab aliis subducta noticia fidei quam facit bestia. Sicut ergo bestia non sufficit apprehendere hanc rem sacramenti sub tali habitudine, ita nec sensus hominis.

ENGLISH: It is granted, therefore, that a subject can be perceived [by accident]{g:per-accidens} in the way described; but then [the common sense]{g:sensus-communis} or some inner sense must [19] apprehend the subject from the motion of the adjacent thing that is [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} [of itself]{g:per-se}.[^15] Now, as it is, no sense of man, but the understanding purely, through faith, perceives the body of Christ; which is clear from this, that when a consecrated host is mixed with others, a man, with the knowledge of faith withdrawn, no more distinguishes it from the others than a beast does. Just as, therefore, a beast does not suffice to apprehend this reality of the sacrament under such a [respect]{g:habitudo}, so neither does the sense of man.

### 01-c ¶1 · 19.19–20.17  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 19] Quantum ad istud patet quod fingitur assumptum, sicut nunc finguntur miracula et nunc ex veris miraculis propter ignoranciam commune falsum creditur, ut audivi quendam fingere quomodo hostia de altari paulatim descendit ad ventrem ecclesie et ingressum est cor cuiusdam infirmi qui devote et publice professus est sic inquiens: Tu Deus nosti quod reverenter subducta infirmitate te sumerem, **sed** non infirmitas mentis, ymmo infirmitas corporis me retardat. Hostia vero fisso pectore cum corde infirmi surrepsit in cordis ventriculum, et sic infirmus constitutus est [p. 20] subito totus sanus. Et postquam narrator iste ex narracione et populi devocione fuit ad partem a quodam familiari socio commendatus, confessus est mendacium hoc turpe: Os finxit, inquit, hoc pulchrum mendacium. **Sed** esto quod fiant miracula circa hostiam consecratam (ut narrat beatus Gregorius), adhuc foret nimis extraneum noscenti consequencias ex istis inferre quod corpus Domini sit illa hostia consecrata. Correspondenter dico quod, si sompniator iste noverit fidem infirmi credentis quod sentit modo dicto corpus dominicum quod non potest ab experimento vel scriptura convincere, adhuc dico quod iste credens non tunc vel propterea fuit sanctus **sed**, sicut sunt nimis multi, fuit ex cultu signorum Antichristi ydolatra manifestus. Revera sic posset ex signis seduci populus quod coleret talpas et vespertiliones et creaturas magis abhominabiles tanquam Deum.

ENGLISH: As to this, it is clear that the [assumption]{g:assumptum} is [feigned]{g:fingere},[^1] just as miracles are now feigned, and now, out of true miracles, a common falsehood is believed through ignorance; as I heard a certain man feign how the [host]{g:hostia} came down little by little from the altar to the body of the [church]{g:ecclesia}[^2] and entered the heart of a certain sick man, who had devoutly and publicly professed, saying thus: "Thou, God, knowest that I would receive thee reverently, were my infirmity withdrawn; but not infirmity of mind, rather infirmity of body holds me back." And the host, the breast being split open along with the sick man's heart, crept into the ventricle of the heart, and so the sick man was made [20] suddenly wholly sound.[^3] And after this narrator, for his narration and the people's devotion, had been commended aside by a certain familiar companion, he confessed this foul lie: "The mouth," he said, "feigned this fine lie." But granted that miracles are done about the consecrated host (as blessed Gregory [relates]{g:narrare}),[^4] still it would be [exceedingly]{g:nimis} strange to one who knows [inferences]{g:consequencia} to infer from these that [the body of the Lord]{g:corpus-domini} is that consecrated host. Correspondingly I say that, if this dreamer knew the faith of the sick man, believing that he [perceived]{g:sentire} the Lord's body in the way described, which he cannot prove from experience or from scripture, still I say that this believer was not then, or on that account, holy, but, as exceedingly many are, was by the worship of the signs of Antichrist a manifest idolater.[^5] In truth the people could thus be seduced by signs so as to worship moles and bats, and creatures more abominable, as God.[^6]

### 01-c ¶2 · 20.18–20.28  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 20] Quantum ad quinque sensaciones predictas patet quod moderniores doctores negant hoc de visu, quia (ut inquiunt) nichil videtur ab oculo nisi secundum formam trianguli cuius conus est in oculo et basis in ipso visibili; **sed** corpus Domini cum sit multiplicatum ad omnem punctum hostie, non terminat talem basem, ideo secundum vera perspectivorum principia non sic videtur oculo corporali, in tantum quod sancti tenent nec oculum corporis glorificati nec eciam oculum Christi posse carnaliter videre corpus Christi vel sanguinem in hostia consecrata.

ENGLISH: As to the five [sensations]{g:sensacio} aforesaid, it is clear that [the more modern doctors]{g:moderniores-doctores} deny this of sight, because (as they say) nothing is seen by the eye except according to the form of a triangle whose [apex]{g:conus} is in the eye and its base in the visible thing itself; but the body of the Lord, since it is multiplied to every point of the host, does not terminate such a base; therefore, according to the true principles of [the perspectivists]{g:perspectivi}, it is not seen thus by the bodily eye, so much so that [the saints]{g:sancti} hold that neither the eye of a glorified body nor even the eye of Christ can carnally see [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} or the blood in the consecrated host.[^7]

### 01-c ¶4 · 21.12–21.29  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 21] Item, si tam clare intuentur corpus Christi in sacramento altaris (ut inquiunt), queratur cuius coloris aut quantitatis et figure est corpus Christi ibidem; et oportet eos dicere quod in hostia una sit albius, in alia **autem** fuscius, in una maius, in alia minus, secundum proprietates accidentales hostie consecrate; et sic Deus noster foret in una hostia plene rotundus et integer, et in alia hostia oblongus et fractus, et cum corpus non videtur sic accidentatum nisi per accidens quod in eo fuerit subiective, oportet dicere quod corpus Christi subiacet cuilibet huiusmodi accidentalitati, et sic Deus noster foret cotidie quantumcunque varie contrarie et inhonorifice transmutatus, quia sacerdos alteraret eum a colore celesti, a figura humana et ceteris accidentibus que decent Christum in gloria ad turpia accidencia absoluta. **Sed** quid magis paganicum quam quod nos possumus figurare ad libitum Deum nostrum?

ENGLISH: Again, if they behold the body of Christ so clearly in [the sacrament of the altar]{g:sacramentum-altaris} (as they say), let it be asked of what colour, or [quantity]{g:quantitas} and figure, the body of Christ is there; and they must say that in one host it is whiter, but in another darker, in one larger, in another smaller, according to the accidental properties of the consecrated host; and so our God would be in one host fully round and whole, and in another host oblong and broken. And since a body is not seen so [subject to accidents]{g:accidentatus} except by an [accident]{g:accidens} that was in it as in a [subject]{g:subiectum}, it must be said that the body of Christ underlies every accidentality of this kind; and so our God would be daily transmuted in whatever way, variously, contrarily and dishonourably, because the [priest]{g:sacerdos} would alter him from the heavenly colour, from the human figure and the other accidents which befit Christ in glory, to foul [absolute accidents]{g:accidens-absolutum}. But what is more pagan than that we can [figure]{g:figurare} our God at our pleasure?[^9]

### 01-c ¶5 · 21.30–22.20  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 21] Et conformiter obicitur de auditu: Si enim audimus Dominum in fractura hostie, cum ipsa non sit nisi fraccio corporis quod auditur, oportet concedere quod [p. 22] Christus frangitur; quod est horribilius quam opus militum, quia ut dicitur Joh. XIXº, 33: Ad Jesum **autem** cum venissent et viderent eum iam mortuum, non fregerunt eius crura, quia Exod. XIIº, 46 de agno pascali figurante Christum precipitur: Os non comminuetis ex eo; multo magis ergo de corpore Christi vivo et glorificato christiani non frangerent eius membra. Sicut ergo facta collisione membrorum vel lapidum exhinc non auditur Deus vel anima, sic senciendum est de corpore Christi fracta hostia. Aliter enim sonus articulatus per organa dignissima foret nimis raucus et illiteratus. Oportet ergo dare duriciem in hostia cum aliis accidentibus non subiectatis in corpore Christi, ex quibus cum aere reverberante efficitur talis sonus; et sic senciendum est de tribus aliis sensibus, scilicet olfactu, gustu et tactu. Nichil enim paganius quam quod putrefacta hostia olet Deus, acefacto sacramento calicis acesceret Deus, et sic Deus noster in ecclesiis ruralibus fetet et acet, **sed** in civitatibus est sapidus atque recens.

ENGLISH: And in like manner [it is objected]{g:obicitur} about hearing: for if we hear the Lord in the [breaking]{g:fractio} of the host, since that is nothing but a breaking of the body that is heard, one must grant that [22] Christ is broken; which is more horrible than the work of the soldiers, because, as is said in John 19:33: "But after they were come to Jesus, when they saw that he was already dead, they did not break his legs", because in Exod. 12:46 it is commanded of the paschal lamb that [figured]{g:figura} Christ: "You shall not break a bone of him";[^10] much more, therefore, would Christians not break the members of the body of Christ, living and glorified. As, therefore, when a collision of members or of stones takes place, God or the soul is not thereby heard, so must one judge of the body of Christ when the host is broken.[^11] For otherwise a sound articulated through the most worthy organs would be exceedingly hoarse and unlettered. One must therefore posit hardness in the host, with the other accidents not subjected in the body of Christ, from which, with the reverberating air, such a sound is produced; and so must one judge of the three other senses, namely smell, taste and touch. For nothing is more pagan than that when the host is putrefied God smells, when the sacrament of the chalice turns sour God would turn sour; and so our God in country churches stinks and is sour, but in cities is savoury and fresh.

### 01-d ¶1 · 22.21–24.9  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 22] Nec docetur racio quare corpus Christi in mutando visum videtur eadem visione qua hostia, quin per idem in mutando alios sensus sentitur conformiter sicut hostia. Et revera tam fidelis quam infidelis horreret de concessione conclusionum quas oportet sic errantes concedere in ista materia; ideo beati qui non viderunt et crediderunt eorum opposita. Quid enim horribilius quam quod sacerdos post celebracionem portat corporaliter Deum in extremitatibus digitorum et quidquid tetigerit cum illis digitis facit [p. 23] corpus dominicum illud tangere? Non enim tollit locio quin sacramentum imbibitum remaneat secundum partes subtiles in corpore continente. Cum ergo remanent odor et sapor, patet naturalibus quod oportet subiectum corporeum remanere. Et patet ista sentencia secundum leges ecclesie; nam in De Consecracione, distinccione II, sub verbis Augustini sic scribitur: Nos **autem** sub specie panis et vini quam videmus res invisibiles, id est, carnem Christi et sanguinem honoramus. Ex quibus patenter patet quod illa species sensibilis quam sacramentum dicimus non est corpus Christi vel sanguis. Et idem patet eadem distinccione sub auctoritate Ambrosii Forte dicis: Similitudinem, inquit, non video carnis, non video sanguinis veritatem. Verumptamen, si viderem sanguinis Christi essenciam, viderem per idem omnem quantitatem vel qualitatem sensibilem, que informat corpus Christi in toto; quod est tercio expresse contra eundem Augustinum eadem distinccione Prima quidem et sepe inferius recitatum, quomodo sanctus doctor exponit dictum Christi Joh. VIº, 67, propter quod multi discipuli abierunt retrorsum; quod debet intelligi spiritualiter, non quod morsibus corporalibus comesturi erant corpus quod oportet esse sursum in eodem loco et figura inmobile, donec seculum finiatur, **sed** spiritualiter per idem non videret corporaliter corpus Christi. Unde sequitur huius sancti sentencia sub hiis verbis, De Consecracione, distinccione II: Non hoc corpus quod videtis manducaturi [p. 24] estis et bibituri illum sanguinem quem effusuri sunt illi qui me crucifigent, et post detegens equivocacionem sic loquitur: ipsum quidem et non ipsum, ipsum invisibiliter, non ipsum visibiliter; necesse est, inquit, celebrari illud visibiliter, necesse est tamen invisibiliter intelligi. Multa sunt similia dicta sanctorum et legum ecclesie que oportet fidelem credere et per consequens in eis propter carnales hereticos informari.

ENGLISH: Nor is any reason taught why [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}, in acting on the sight, is seen with the same vision as the [host]{g:hostia}, without its being by the same token [perceived]{g:sentire} in like manner as the host in acting on the other senses.[^1] And in truth the faithful as much as the [unbeliever]{g:infidelis} would shudder at granting the [conclusions]{g:conclusio} which those who err thus must grant in this matter; therefore "blessed are they that have not seen and have believed" their opposites.[^2] For what is more horrible than that the [priest]{g:sacerdos} after the celebration carries God [bodily]{g:corporaliter} in the tips of his fingers, and whatever he touches with those fingers he makes [23] the Lord's body touch? For washing does not take away that the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}, once imbibed, remains according to its subtle parts in the containing body. Since, then, the smell and the savour remain, it is clear to natural philosophers that a bodily [subject]{g:subiectum} must remain. And this [teaching]{g:sentencia}[^3] is clear according to [the laws of the church]{g:leges-ecclesie}; for in *De consecratione*, distinction 2, under the words of Augustine it is written thus: "We, under the [species]{g:species} of [bread]{g:panis} and [wine]{g:vinum} which we see, honour invisible things, that is, the flesh of Christ and the blood."[^4] From which it is openly clear that that [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} species which we call the sacrament is not the body or blood of Christ. And the same is clear in the same distinction under the authority of Ambrose: "Perhaps you say," he says, "'I do not see the likeness of flesh, I do not see the truth of blood.'"[^5] Nevertheless, if I saw the essence of the blood of Christ, I would by the same token see every perceptible [quantity]{g:quantitas} or [quality]{g:qualitas} which informs the body of Christ in the whole; which is, thirdly, expressly against the same Augustine in the same distinction, [the canon] *Prima quidem*, and [against what is] often recited below: how the holy doctor expounds the saying of Christ in John 6:67, on account of which many disciples went back; that it ought to be understood [spiritually]{g:spiritualiter}, not that they were going to eat with bodily bites the body which must be above, in the same place and figure, immovable, until the world is ended; but spiritually; by the same token one would not see the body of Christ bodily.[^6] Hence there follows the teaching of this saint in these words, *De consecratione*, distinction 2: "Not this body which you see are you going to eat, [24] and drink that blood which they who will crucify me are going to shed"; and afterwards, laying bare the [equivocation]{g:equivocacio}, he speaks thus: "the same and not the same: the same [invisibly]{g:invisibiliter}, not the same visibly"; "it is necessary," he says, "that it be celebrated visibly, yet it is necessary that it be understood invisibly."[^7] There are many like sayings of [the saints]{g:sancti} and of the laws of the [church]{g:ecclesia} which the faithful man must believe, and consequently be instructed in them on account of carnal heretics.

### 01-d ¶2 · 24.10–24.18  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 24] **Sed** contra ista arguitur per easdem leges ecclesie; nam in De Consecracione, distinccione II, dicit Pius papa: Si per negligenciam aliquid de sanguine stillaverit super terram, lingua lambetur; si super tabulam, tabula radetur, sicut terra vel aliud ipsum recipiens, et igne consumetur et cinis iuxta altare condatur. Et multe sunt leges talis sentencie que omnes sonant quod illud sensibile liquidum et fluxibile contentum in calice est realiter sanguis Christi.

ENGLISH: But against this [it is argued by appeal to]{g:arguitur-per} the same laws of the church; for in *De consecratione*, distinction 2, [Pope]{g:papa} Pius says: "If through negligence anything of the blood drips upon the ground, it shall be licked up with the tongue; if upon a board, the board shall be scraped, as shall the ground or anything else that receives it, and it shall be consumed by fire and the ash buried beside the altar."[^8] And there are many laws of this sense, which all sound to the effect that that perceptible liquid and flowing thing contained in the chalice is [really]{g:realiter} the blood of Christ.

### 01-d ¶3 · 24.19–24.29  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 24] Hic dicitur quod multa dicta decretorum oportet sane intelligere ad sensum quem debent facere; unde figurative vel mistice sepe in locucionibus istius materie figuratum intelligitur per figuram, ut sicut grammaticus dicit quod dominus est nomen, et loycus quod hoc signum homo est species, sic dicit theologus quod panis est corpus Christi et vinum eius sanguis, quod oportet figurative intelligi signo supposito pro signato. Si **autem** doctores signorum intelligant predicacionem secundum essenciam vel non figurativam **sed** formalem, est error in ianuis.

ENGLISH: To this [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that many sayings of [the decrees]{g:decreta} must be understood soundly, in the sense they ought to make; hence [figuratively]{g:figurative} or mystically, in expressions on this matter, the [thing figured]{g:figuratum} is often understood by the figure; so that, as the grammarian says that "lord" is a noun, and the logician that this [sign]{g:signum} "man" is a species, so the theologian says that the bread is the body of Christ and the wine his blood, which must be understood figuratively, the sign standing for the [thing signified]{g:signatum}.[^9] But if [the doctors of signs]{g:doctores-signorum} understand a [predication by essence]{g:predicacio-secundum-essenciam}, or one not figurative but [formal]{g:formalis}, error is at the doors.[^10]

### 01-d ¶4 · 24.30–25.18  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 24] Et in ista equivocacione laborant multi. Unde absit katholicum credere quod propter effusionem huiusmodi [p. 25] sacramenti in calicem exhinc sanguis Domini effundatur. Tunc enim non preciperet ecclesia sanguinem illum comburere et cineres combusti Domini in ecclesia in detestacionem tam horrendi facinoris tamquam reliquias remanere; **sed** loquitur de nudo sacramento quod figurative nominat rem signatam modo quo posset vocare quamlibet creaturam Deum suum, cum sit signum efficax Dei sui. Tales enim locuciones tropicas non debemus nos admittere generaliter, quia sic diceremus quod Christus cotidie effundit per multos cados plus de sanguine quam umquam habuit hic in terris, et totidem racione multitudinis sui sanguinis foret complexione intemperatus, cum acquireret cotidie humores grossos inglorificatos cum aliis inconvenientibus reducendis; et sic glosari debet cantus ecclesie, dum dicit Quod fit vinum sanguis Christi, Etsi sensus deficit.

ENGLISH: And many labour under this equivocation. Hence God forbid that a catholic believe that through the pouring out of such [25] a sacrament into the chalice the blood of the Lord is thereby poured out.[^11] For then the church would not command that that blood be burned, and the ashes of the burned Lord remain in the church as relics, in detestation of so horrible a [misdeed]{g:facinus}; but it speaks of the bare sacrament, which figuratively names the thing signified, in the way one could call any [creature]{g:creatura} its God, since it is an effective sign of its God.[^12] For we ought not to admit such [tropical expressions]{g:locucio-tropica} generally, because so we should say that Christ daily pours out through many casks more blood than he ever had here on earth, and by reason of the [multitude]{g:multitudo} of his blood he would be just as often intemperate in [complexion]{g:complexio}, since he would daily acquire gross unglorified humours, with other [absurdities]{g:inconveniens}[^13] to be brought in; and so the chant of the church must be [glossed]{g:glosare} where it says: "That wine becomes the blood of Christ, Though sense fail."[^14]
