# *Dialogus*, chapter 14: prayers to saints and implicit faith

## Falsehood and Truth · 26.28–28.30

> [p. 26] MENDACIUM. Iterum manifeste innuis non esse ut fidem [p. 27] credendum illos esse sanctos qui in missis, in letaniis, et aliis ecclesie serviciis inseruntur; ad quid ergo diceremus de illis nisi crederemus quod in patria sunt beati? cum spes meriti super fide stabili in credentibus est fundanda. VERITAS. Hic dictum est sepius quod de sanctis quos canonizat ecclesia supponimus, sed non ut fidem credimus, quod sunt beati in patria. Undecim tamen apostolos cum paulo, magdalene et ceteris quos fides scripture implicat esse sanctos, credendum est ut fidem esse beatos, et alios, quantumcunque magna sanctitate in celis prepolleant, credimus esse in beatitudine, verumtamen citra fidem. Et ideo ordinavit ecclesia quod quecumque oracio porrecta ad sanctum ad deum principaliter dirigatur. Et sic quelibet talis oracio implicat tacite condicionem, si et quatenus placet deo per talis sancti meritum nobis miseris suffragari. Et ut videtur multis nulla oracio porrecta specialiter sancto Christi est laudabilis, nisi de quanto acuit devocionem in Christum, sic quod si omnes intenciones et oraciones uniri possent in Christum, sincerius directe unite in Christum, esset utilius Christiano omnes oraciones specialiter Christo porrigere. Nec valet false credere quod Christus propter celsitudinem sui dominii non potest a nobis miseris faciliter exorari; ideo est necessarium habere alias personas mediantes; Christus enim assistens pontifex immediate illabitur viatori et cum hoc immediate conjungitur deitati. Ideo nullus sanctus est misericordius aut facilius auditivus miseri quam est Christus. Nec valent oraciones aliorum sanctorum, nisi de quanto Christus prius ex sua gracia has acceptat. Ideo dicunt quidam quod porrecciones oracionum ad istos sanctos sepe [p. 28] sunt superflue vel nocive. Conceditur ergo quod non est articulus fidei appropriare cuilibet sancto de quo dicimus specialem articulum fidei sed beatitudinem illorum supponimus secundum majorem evidenciam vel minorem. Unde absit quod omnes legende sanctorum in ecclesiam introducte sint fides catholica, cum in festo corporis Christi de sancto sanctorum scribitur quod accidencia sunt in eucaristia sine subjecto, quod non fuit fides tempore augustini. Ideo dicit in de sermone Domini in monte quod multa vera, licet in legendis talibus inserantur non sunt fides catholica; sicut exemplificat de ministro convivii cujus unum brachium a cane rapido est abstractum. Sufficit ergo ad tales oraciones secundarias habere probabilem supposicionem, et de Christo qui fundamentaliter oratur habere cum aliis virtutibus firmam fidem; et patet solucio. In ista tamen materia dicunt scolastici quod aliqua est fides explicita vel expressa et aliqua est fides implicita sive tacita. Fidem autem implicitam dicunt esse quamcunque veritatem juxta illud apostoli prima cor. 13° Caritas omnia credit, et tamen infinite sunt veritates de quibus non oportet nos hic habere fidem explicitam; cum sufficit fideli quod credat quamcunque veritatem secundum gradum et formam qua deus vult ipsum hanc credere. Ut carnis resurreccionis est articulus fidei cuilibet Christiano, et tamen non oportet de qualibet persona hominis habere expressum articulum fidei quod resurget. Sed deus vult quod per istam fidem generalem sua militans ecclesia excusetur. Non enim vult quod sua ecclesia cum veritatibus inutiliter oneretur. Communis autem carnis resurreccio erit tempore sempiterno adhuc futura, et postmodum tempore suo presens; sed post diem judicii est elapsa.

FALSEHOOD. Again, you plainly hint that it is not to be [27] believed as faith that those are saints who are put into masses, into litanies, and into the other services of the [church]{g:ecclesia}. To what end, then, should we speak of them, unless we believed that they are blessed in the [homeland]{g:patria}? For the hope of merit must be founded, in believers, upon a stable faith.

TRUTH. It has been said here more than once that of the saints whom the church [canonizes]{g:canonizare} we suppose, but do not believe as faith, that they are blessed in the homeland.[^1] Yet the eleven apostles, with Paul, Magdalene, and the others whom the faith of scripture implies to be saints, are to be believed as faith to be blessed. The others, however great the sanctity in which they excel in heaven, we believe to be in blessedness, yet [short of faith]{g:citra-fidem}. And therefore the church has ordained that any prayer offered to a saint should be directed principally to God. And so every such prayer tacitly implies a condition: if, and so far as, it pleases God through the merit of such a saint to [aid]{g:suffragari} us wretches by intercession. And, as it seems to many, no prayer offered specially to a saint of Christ is praiseworthy, except in so far as it sharpens devotion toward Christ. So, if all intentions and prayers could be united in Christ, being more sincerely united directly in Christ, it would be more useful for a Christian to offer all prayers specially to Christ.[^2] Nor does it avail to believe falsely that Christ, because of the loftiness of his [lordship]{g:dominium}, cannot easily be entreated by us wretches, and that therefore it is necessary to have other persons as mediators.[^3] For Christ, "being come an high priest,"[^4] [flows into]{g:illabi} the [wayfarer]{g:viator} without mediation, and with this is joined without mediation to [the Godhead]{g:deitas}. Therefore no saint hears the wretched more mercifully or more readily than Christ does. Nor do the prayers of the other saints avail, except in so far as Christ first, of his grace, accepts them. Therefore some say that the offering of prayers to these saints is often [28] superfluous or harmful. It is granted, then, that it is not an [article of faith]{g:articulus-fidei} to [appropriate]{g:appropriare} to each saint of whom we speak a special article of faith; but we suppose their blessedness according to greater or lesser [evidence]{g:evidencia}.[^5] Hence God forbid that all the legends of the saints brought into the church should be the catholic faith. For in the feast of Corpus Christi it is written of the holy of holies that the accidents are in the Eucharist without a [subject]{g:subiectum}, which was not faith in Augustine's time.[^6] So he says in *On the Lord's Sermon on the Mount* that many true things, although they are put into such legends, are not the catholic faith, as he shows by the example of the servant at a feast whose one arm was carried off by a fierce dog.[^7] It suffices, then, for such secondary prayers to have a probable supposition, and concerning Christ, who is prayed to fundamentally, to have firm faith together with the other virtues. And the solution is clear.

Yet in this matter the schoolmen say that some faith is [explicit]{g:fides-explicita} or express, and some faith is [implicit]{g:fides-implicita} or tacit. And implicit faith, they say, is any truth whatever,[^8] according to that saying of the apostle in 1 Corinthians 13 (1 Cor. 13:7): "[Charity]{g:caritas} believeth all things." Yet there are infinitely many truths of which we need not have explicit faith here, since it suffices for the faithful man that he believe any truth in the degree and form in which God wills him to believe it. Thus [the article] of the resurrection of the flesh is an article of faith for every Christian,[^9] and yet it is not necessary to have, about each single person of mankind, an express article of faith that he will rise again. But God wills that by this general faith his [church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans} should be excused. For he does not will that his church should be uselessly burdened with truths. And the common resurrection of the flesh is still to come in everlasting time, and afterwards, in its own time, present; but after the day of judgement it has gone by.[^10]

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Ut fidem credere*, "to believe as faith", is set against *supponere*, "to suppose", and against *citra fidem*, "short of faith" (27.10). Plain *credimus* at 27.10 is belief that falls short of faith. At 27.2, B and c add *horarum officia*: "to what end should we say the offices of the hours of them".

[^2]: **T/C.** 27.19: above *Xtum* a later hand in B writes *scilicet sanctum*; B1 corrects *Xtum* to *ipsum*; c has *ipsum*, with *sanctum* interlined. With that reading, one *Christum* becomes "the saint": "even if all prayers could be united in the saint, being more sincerely united directly in Christ…". The apparatus does not show which *Christum* is meant, so the print is translated. B1 and c read *est* for *esset*; B, B1 and c read *ad Christum* for *Christo*.

[^3]: **C.** *Ideo est necessarium habere alias personas mediantes* is taken as the second half of the false belief. Read as Truth's own inference, it would contradict the next sentence.

[^4]: **S.** Heb. 9:11, *Christus autem assistens pontifex futurorum bonorum*. Only the participial phrase is echoed. *Immediate* is rendered "without mediation" to keep it against *personas mediantes*.

[^5]: **T/G.** 28.1–3: B, B1 and c read *appropriate* (adverb) and *oras* (= *horas*?) for *de quo*, and add *adaptare quod in patria sit beatus* after *fidei*. This gives "to fit appropriately to each saint whose hours we say a special article of faith that he is blessed in the homeland". The print is construable and is translated. *Appropriare* here has its general sense, not the canon-law annexation of a church.

[^6]: **S/C.** Pollard's margin gives the Corpus Christi office, first nocturn, lesson II (LEDGER D14-1). That lesson says the accidents subsist without a subject (LEDGER D14-2). *De sancto sanctorum*, "of the holy of holies", is taken to mean the sacrament. B and c read *facto* for *festo*.

[^7]: **S/T.** Pollard's margin gives Augustine, *De sermone Domini in monte* i. 65 (LEDGER D14-3). Augustine there reports, as apocryphal, the story of a servant who struck the apostle Thomas at a feast, and of his hand being carried in by a dog (LEDGER D14-4). B, B1 and c read *rabido*, "mad", for *rapido*, "fierce".

[^8]: **C/S.** *Fidem implicitam dicunt esse quamcunque veritatem*: literally "they say implicit faith is any truth whatever". The sense is that implicit faith reaches to every truth. B, B1 and c omit *esse*. At 1 Cor. 13:7 the Vulgate has only *omnia credit*: Wyclif supplies *caritas* from 13:4.

[^9]: **T.** The print has the genitive *carnis resurreccionis*, so "[the article]" is supplied. B and B1 read *resurreccionem et alios articulos*, which does not construe here either.

[^10]: **T.** 28.28: B, b1 and c read *est in* for the print's *erit*. This is translated, because it fits the sequence of future, present and past. The print reads "will be, through everlasting time, still to come".
