# *De eucharistia*, chapter 4, part 9: the natural and the moral goodness of the host

## ¶1 · 113.29–115.4

> [p. 113] Distinguat, inquam, katholicus inter bonitatem naturalem hostie que est par in alio pane non sacrato ac bonitatem eius moralem, et videbit quod illam potest suscipere magis et minus secundum meritum et sanctitatem vite presbiteri; non enim per se capit [p. 114] sanctitatem ex presencia humanitatis Christi, quia sic Scarioth cum Christo comedens et presbiter iniquus sacramentaliter Christum in hostia accipiens foret sanctus; ipse enim est capax maioris sanctitatis quam res inanimata que habet in se equivoce sanctitatem, sicut aqua baptismi habet in se graciam tamquam signum vel occasionaliter efficiens graciam in persona que communicat sacramento, sic quod sanctitas quam habet ex presencia corporis Christi videtur esse par in cunctis eukaristiis, sed sanctitas orta ex benediccione presbiteri videtur correspondenter ad suum meritum variari, et per distinccionem intelligantur leges que videntur contrarie. Sicut enim oracio dominica quoad eius naturam est eque bona, a quocunque dicatur, sic est de sacramento, sed ista habet efficaciam et bonitatem. Bona enim inanimata quibus homo utitur ex virtuoso usu contrahunt bonitatem; quomodo ergo sacramentum quo iustus utitur ut instrumento merendi super aliis non habet bonitatem respectivam ultra sacramentum quo dampnabiliter iniustus abutitur? Sicut enim tota universitas prodest iusto et iniustus facit iniuriam toti mundo, quia debet communicare sibi perfeccionem secundam a qua deficit. Dicunt enim quod denarius est melior in manu prudentis quam fatui, et tamen negant sacramentum esse melius in ministerio iusti qui facit cum illo fructum centuplum quam in ministerio reprobi qui cum illo dampnificat se ipsum et populum. Unde ad tantum clerus illudit populo quod credit et docet quod sacramentum non sit nisi accidens abiectum, et tamen offenditur illi qui vulgum docet credere quod illud sacramentum eukaristie non sit Deus. Et tamen secundum Apostolum Ephes. IVᵒ, 5 debet esse [p. 115] unus dominus et una fides cuilibet christiano. Ille ergo est invidus qui bibit dulcem potum fidei orthodoxe et consentit quod populus cuius debet habere curam intoxicetur veneno.

Let the catholic, I say, distinguish between the natural goodness of the [host]{g:hostia}, which is equal in another bread not [hallowed]{g:sacrare}, and its moral goodness, and he will see that it can admit the latter more and less according to the merit and holiness of life of the [presbyter]{g:presbiter};[^1] for it does not [of itself]{g:per-se} take [114] holiness from the presence of the [humanity]{g:humanitas} of Christ, because then Iscariot eating with Christ, and a wicked presbyter receiving Christ [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} in the host, would be holy;[^2] for he is capable of greater holiness than an inanimate thing, which has holiness in it [equivocally]{g:equivoce}, as the water of baptism has grace in it as a sign, or as [by occasion]{g:occasionaliter} effecting grace in the person who partakes of the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}; so that the holiness which it has from the presence of the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} seems to be equal in all eucharists, but the holiness arising from the blessing of the presbyter seems to vary correspondingly to his merit; and by this distinction let the laws that seem contrary be understood. For as the Lord's prayer, as to its nature, is equally good by whomever it is said, so it is with the sacrament; but this has efficacy and goodness.[^3] For inanimate goods which a man uses gain goodness from virtuous use; how then does the sacrament which a just man uses as an instrument of meriting above others not have a [relational]{g:respectivus} goodness beyond the sacrament which an unjust man damnably abuses? For as the whole totality profits the just man, and the unjust man does injury to the whole world, because he ought to communicate to it the [second perfection]{g:perfeccio-secunda} from which he falls short.[^4] For they say that a [penny]{g:denarius} is better in the hand of a prudent man than of a fool, and yet they deny that the sacrament is better in the ministry of a just man, who brings forth with it a hundredfold fruit, than in the ministry of a reprobate, who with it [damages]{g:dampnificare} himself and the people. Hence the [clergy]{g:clerus} deludes the people to such a point that it believes and teaches that the sacrament is nothing but an abject [accident]{g:accidens}, and yet is offended at the one who teaches the common people to believe that that sacrament of the eucharist is not God. And yet according to the Apostle, Ephesians 4:5, there must be [115] one Lord and one faith for every Christian. He therefore is envious who drinks the sweet drink of orthodox faith and consents that the people whose care he ought to have be poisoned with venom.[^5]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 113.29: The sanctity which comes of Christ's presence is always the same;
- 114.1: but there is a sanctity which depends on the profit of the priest's prayers, and this varies.

## Notes

[^1]: **T/C.** *Illam* (the moral goodness) is Loserth's reading; the manuscripts have *illa*. *Suscipere magis et minus*: to admit of more and less, of degrees. D reads *uno pane* for *alio pane* (113.30); C *et bonitatem*, E *ad bonitatem* (113.31).

[^2]: **C.** *Per se* (CONTEXTUAL): "of itself", the logical sense, not the spatial "apart". *Ipse* in the next clause is the man (Iscariot, the wicked presbyter), set against the inanimate thing.

[^3]: **T/C/S.** The manuscripts read *super ista* for *sed ista* (114.15), "beyond these [it] has efficacy and goodness"; Loserth's *sed ista* is translated, with *ista* the sacrament's goodness beyond its nature. At 114.19 *super aliis* is C's correction; ABE read *sed aliis*, D *sed et aliis*. The "laws that seem contrary" are not named (LEDGER K04i-1).

[^4]: **C.** *Sicut enim … et …* has no main clause; it is taken as "as … so too", the unjust man's injury answering the just man's profit. *Sibi* is the world. ABCD read *a quo* for *a qua* (114.23).

[^5]: **S/T.** *Fructum centuplum* recalls Matt. 13:8 ("an hundred fold"). Eph. 4:5 (DR "One Lord, one faith, one baptism"), given in Wyclif's construction. At 115.2 E reads *inmundus* for *invidus* and *mundum potum* for *dulcem potum*; ABC *orthodochie*.
