# *De ecclesia*, chapter 10, part 1: the Westminster privilege: the charters of Edgar and Edward

## ¶1 · 204.28–206.10

> [p. 204] Istis premissis descendendum est specialiter ad privilegium Westmonasterii super quo versatur contencio. Supponatur itaque in primis (ut patet ex vera copia ministrata) quod dictum Westmonasterium habeat [p. 205] privilegium concessum ab Edgaro et sancto Edwardo confessore, concessum (inquam) sibi et ecclesie sub hac forma: Cognoscat, inquit Edgarus, magnitudo seu utilitas vestra quam discernimus et in perpetuum mansurum iubemus atque constituimus ut pro reverencia reliquiarum gloriosissimi apostoli Petri et quiete monachorum ibidem Deo famulancium, honor et laus eidem ecclesie habeatur, ut quisquis fugitivorum pro quolibet scelere ad prefatam basilicam sancti Petri fugiens precinctum eius intraverit sive pedes vel pedester, sive eques vel equester, sive de curia regali sive de civitate seu villa, seu cuiuscunque condicionis sit, quocunque delicto facinoris contra nos vel succesores reges Anglie vel contra alium quemlibet fidelem sancte ecclesie foris factus sit, relaxetur et liberetur et vitam atque membra absque ulla contradiccione optineat. Preterea interminamur divina auctoritate et nostra, ut neque nos neque successores nostri neque ullus episcopus nec quicunque alius de iudiciaria potestate in ipsam sanctam basilicam vel in manentes in ipsa vel in homines qui cum sua substancia vel rebus ad ipsam tradere vel devovere se voluerint, nisi per voluntatem abbatis et suorum monachorum, ullam unquam habeant potestatem, sed sit hec mater ecclesia libera et absoluta ab omni invasione vel inquietudine omnium hominum cuiuscunque ordinis vel potestatis. Obsecramus eciam omnes successores nostros reges et [p. 206] principes per sanctam et individuam trinitatem et per adventum iusti iudicis, ut quoniam ex munificencia antecessorum nostrorum ipse locus videtur esse dicatus, nullus episcoporum vel abbatum aut eorum ordinatores vel quelibet persona possit quoquo ordine de loco ipso aliquid auferre aut aliquam potestatem sibi in ipso monasterio usurpare vel aliquid quasi per commutacionis titulum absque voluntate ipsius congregacionis vel nostrum permissum minuere presumat.

These things premised, we must come down specifically to the [privilege]{g:privilegium} of Westminster, over which the contention turns.[^1] Let it be supposed, then, in the first place (as is clear from a true copy supplied [to me]) that the said Westminster has a [205] privilege granted by Edgar and by Saint Edward the Confessor, granted (I say) to it and to the [church]{g:ecclesia} in this form:[^2] "Let your greatness or usefulness know," says Edgar, "what we decide, and command and establish to abide for ever: that, for reverence of the relics of the most glorious apostle Peter and for the quiet of the monks serving God there, honour and praise be paid to that church; so that whoever of the fugitives, for any crime whatever, fleeing to the aforesaid basilica of Saint Peter, has entered its precinct, whether a foot-soldier or on foot, whether a horseman or on horseback, whether from the royal court or from a city or township, or of whatever condition he be, by whatever offence of crime he has [committed forfeiture]{g:forisfacere} against us or the kings of England our successors, or against any other faithful man of holy church, let him be released and set free and keep life and limbs without any gainsaying.[^3] Moreover we forbid under threat, by divine authority and our own, that neither we nor our successors nor any bishop nor anyone else of judicial power should ever have any power over that holy basilica, or over those dwelling in it, or over the men who wish to deliver or devote themselves to it with their substance or goods, except by the will of the abbot and his monks; but let this mother church be free and quit of all invasion or disturbance by all men of whatever order or power. We also beseech all our successors, kings and [206] princes, by the holy and undivided Trinity and by the coming of the just judge, that, since that place is seen to have been dedicated by the munificence of our predecessors, none of the bishops or abbots or their ordainers, or any person whatever, may in any manner take anything away from that place, or usurp any power for himself in that monastery, or presume to diminish anything as if by [title]{g:titulus} of [exchange]{g:commutacio}, without the will of the congregation itself or our permission."[^4]

## ¶3 · 206.12–206.26

> [p. 206] Sit hic sancta mater ecclesia peculiaris patroni nostri et domini et magni apostoliter beati Petri libera et absoluta ab omni invasione et inquietudine omnium hominum cuiuscunque ordinis vel potestatis esse videantur. Et in alia carta eiusdem sancti sic scribitur: Preterea in perpetuum confirmo ut quisquis fugitivorum de quocunque loco pro quacunque causa cuiuscunque condicionis sit ipsum sanctum locum vel precinctum eius fugiens intraverit membrorum suorum ac vite inpunitatem consequatur cum omnimoda libertate, nec aliquis aliud quidlibet ab ecclesia illa vel a terris ad eam pertinentibus per vim aut consuetudinem exigat. Hec sunt verba magis pregnancia pro libertate dicti monasterii que omnia sunt per bullas Nicolai III. postmodum confirmata.

"Let this holy mother church of our special patron and lord, the great apostle blessed Peter, be free and quit of all invasion and disturbance by all men, of whatever order or power they may appear to be." And in another charter of the same saint it is written thus: "Moreover I confirm in perpetuity that whoever of the fugitives, from whatever place, for whatever cause, of whatever condition he be, fleeing, has entered that holy place or its precinct, shall obtain impunity of his limbs and life with every kind of liberty; and let no one exact anything else whatever from that church or from the lands belonging to it by force or by [custom]{g:consuetudo}."[^5] These are the most telling words for the liberty of the said monastery, all of which were afterwards confirmed by bulls of Nicholas III.[^6]

## Notes

[^1]: **R.** *Super quo versatur contencio*: the dispute is the dispute over the abbey's privilege of sanctuary after its violation on 11 August 1378 (Loserth, Introduction, pp. x–xi). A has in the margin *Nota de privilegio Westermonasterii* (204.28). *Vera copia ministrata*: a true copy supplied [to Wyclif]. Side-notes 205.3, 206.12.

[^2]: **S/T.** Edgar's charter: Loserth refers to Kemble, *Codex diplomaticus aevi Saxonici* II, 366, which "gives the exact words of the greater part of this charter", and corrects the codices from it (LEDGER E10a-1). His corrections are translated: *villa* (codd. *villa cuiuscunque*, 205.12–13), *facinoris* (codd. *facinoriter*, 205.13), *ad ipsam* (wanting in the codices, 205.22). Charter readings he records without adopting them: *decernimus* (205.4), *et pro quiete* (205.7), *eiusdem ecclesie habeatur id est ut* (205.8), *beati apostoli Petri* (205.10), *esse videantur* after *vel potestatis* (205.27), *quemadmodum ex munificencia* (206.1–2). The print is translated.

[^3]: **C/G.** *Cognoscat … quam discernimus*: *quam* agrees with nothing; we take it as introducing the object, "what we decide" (the charter has *decernimus*, Loserth). *Magnitudo seu utilitas vestra*: forms of address. *Pedes vel pedester … eques vel equester*: the charter's doubling, noun and adjective (foot-soldier / on foot), is kept. *Foris factus sit*: the participle of *forisfacere* (GLOSSARY): has made himself liable to forfeiture by crime.

[^4]: **C.** *Mater ecclesia*: Westminster as a mother church; neither the *matrix ecclesia* of GLOSSARY nor the church as *mater nostra*, and so untagged. *Eorum ordinatores*: literally "their ordainers"; perhaps those who appoint them. *Absque voluntate … vel nostrum permissum*: the accusative after *absque* is as printed; the sense is "without … our permission". *Quoquo ordine*: in any manner (not "order").

[^5]: **S/T.** Edward's charter: Loserth refers to Kemble, *Cod. dipl.* IV, no. 825, p. 181 (compare pp. 173 ff.); the "other charter" to IV, p. 186 (LEDGER E10a-2, E10a-3). Loserth's corrections are translated: *magni apostoli … beati Petri* from Edward's charter (codd. *etc. beati*, 206.13), *videantur* from Kemble p. 186 (codd. *videatur*, 206.16), *consuetudinem* (codd. *consuetum*). B lacks *sit* (206.12). At 206.20 Loserth notes that Edward's diploma reads here *immunis sit omnino ac plenam libertatem consequatur*. *Esse videantur* hangs loosely on *omnium hominum*: "of whatever order or power they may appear to be".

[^6]: **S.** Loserth points to a bull of Nicholas III inserted in this diploma (Kemble, *Cod. dipl.* IV, p. 183) and to the *Monasticon Anglicanum* (Dodsworth and Dugdale, London 1655); he says he has not seen bulls confirming the diploma. The bull inserted there is addressed by *Nicholaus episcopus* to King Edward (Kemble IV, 183–84; its *nulli omnino personae nisi regi subdantur* is quoted at 230), so the pope is Nicholas II (1059–61); the text's *Nicolai III* is an error (LEDGER E10a-4). *Verba magis pregnancia*: the most telling (literally "pregnant") words.
