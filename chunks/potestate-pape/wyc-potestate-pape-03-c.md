# *De potestate pape*, chapter 3, part 3: Peter's primacy: the mystical sense, Peter's acts after the Ascension, and the witness of the saints

## ¶1 · 51.11–52.2

> [p. 51] Quamvis enim sensus misticus scripture non sit de se evidens ignaris ad probandum scolastice figuratum, tamen si probari potest ex racione vel scriptura alia quod sensus iste misticus intentus fuerat ab autore; tunc sensus ille est equivalens literali; et sic sepe probat Apostolus ex figuris veteris testamenti figuratum allegorice in novo, ut patet I. Cor. X. Gal. IV et Heb. VII. Cum igitur non dicetur quid pertinencius figurarunt ista singulariter facta Petro quam prerogativam Petri super alios; videtur quod iste suasiones quietarent fidelem theologum ad fidem questioni proposite adhibendam. Dicat igitur adversarius istam sentenciam, vel quod aliquid est a Christo inpertinenter factum; aut ex indifferencia positum in scriptura; vel aliter dicat quid ordo et singularitas prenarratorum operum quoad Petrum pertinencius quam eius preeminenciam super alios figuraret. Cum enim scriptura sacra exprimit et racio necessitat oportere aliquem apostolum esse talem, ecclesia autem generaliter credat hoc de Petro, Deus notabiliter permitteret [p. 52] ecclesiam suam a principio oberasse, si isti fidei falsitas sit substrata.

For although the mystical sense of scripture is not of itself evident to the unlearned for proving scholastically the thing figured, yet if it can be proved from reason or from another scripture that this mystical sense was intended by the author, then that sense is equivalent to the literal; and so the Apostle often proves from figures of the old testament what is figured allegorically in the new, as is clear from 1 Corinthians 10, Galatians 4 and Hebrews 7.[^1] Since therefore it will not be said what these things done singularly to Peter figured more fittingly than Peter's prerogative over the others, it seems that these persuasions would set a faithful theologian at rest so as to lend faith to the question proposed.[^2] Let the adversary therefore either affirm this [teaching]{g:sentencia}, or say that something was done by Christ unfittingly, or put in scripture indifferently; or else let him say what the order and singularity of the works narrated above, as regards Peter, would figure more fittingly than his preeminence over the others.[^3] For since holy scripture expresses, and reason necessitates, that some apostle must be such, and the [church]{g:ecclesia} generally believes this of Peter, God would notably have permitted [52] his church to have erred from the beginning, if falsehood underlay this faith.

## ¶2 · 52.3–55.26

> [p. 52] Item, secundo ex opinionibus Petri post ascensionem eadem veritas probabiliter convinci poterit, nam soli Petro ostensum est lintheum submissum de celo, in quo erant omnia quadrupedia et repencia terre et volatilia celi, ut patet Act. X, 13 et XI, 7 et sibi soli dictum est de omnibus illis animalibus: Surge Petre, occide et manduca. Lintheum autem quatuor iniciis submissum signat superficiem terre habitabilis hominum, cuius planicies est contexta et trita ut lintheum descendente influencia celi ab oriente, occidente, borea et austro ad temperandum soli planiciem ad usum hominum. Illa autem tria genera animalium submissa in lintheo signant per ordinem seculares dominos ut quadrupedia, vulgus ut terre repencia, et clerum ut volatilia in consideracionem contemplativam temporum volancia. Ista quidem tria genera hominum integrant incolas huius mundi. Petrus autem surrexit armatus virtutibus et occidit de istis tribus generibus, cum extinxit in eis mundialitatem et fecit eos non homines quoad mundum. Sic enim accipitur homo sepius in scriptura, ut patet I. Corinth. III, 3: Cum sint inter vos zelus et contencio, nonne carnales estis et secundum hominem ambulatis? Unde beatus Petrus verbo et opere interpretatus fuit visionem ymaginativam ad hunc sensum, quod manducare suum erat perversos mores in eis prescindere et eos personaliter incorporare ecclesie; sic enim dicit Christus Joh. IV, 32: Ego cibum habeo manducare quem vos nescitis. Quando enim predicator, qui Canticorum IV, 2 dicitur dentes sponse, gladio spiritus occidit carnalitatem et ore ecclesie conterit ad penitenciam convertendos: tunc spiritualiter comedit conversos assimilando eos in [p. 53] membra matris ecclesie, quod officium pertinuit Petro primarie. Unde Actuum I, 15 legitur quod exsurgens Petrus in medio fratrum ostendit per scripturam veteris testamenti quod oportet loco Yude proditoris alium episcopum eligere, qui suppleat vices suas; et sic secundum prioritatem dignitatis celebravit eleccionem Mathie. Item, Act. II, 14 legitur quomodo Petrus cum undecim elevavit vocem suam loquens pro omnibus contra imponentes eis ebrietatem, ostendens ex scriptura quod sit spiritualis replecio per Spiritum Sanctum Yohelis III prelocuta. Item, Act. II, 31 consequenter predicavit Judeis de resurreccione et predixit eis pre omnibus apostolis quid esset eis necessarium ad salutem, et infra capitulo tercio, 1, 2, Petro et Johanne ascendentibus in templum Petrus habuit preposituram locucionis in claudi sanacione sicut et prenominacionem in serie scripture, Petrus eciam pro omnibus respondit magistratibus templi, ut patet Act. capitulo IV, et pro omnibus protulit mortis sentenciam in Ananiam et Saphiram, ut patet V capitulo, et sic in Symonem magum, Act. VIII. Item, in consilio generali, de quo Act. XV, Petrus protulit pro omnibus sentenciam quod conversi ex gentibus non debeant circumcidi nec legis cerimonias observare. Talem autem preposituram usurpare non presumeret tam sanctus apostolus post missionem Sancti Spiritus docentis ipsum omnem veritatem et confirmantis in ipso omnem a Christo instructam humilitatem, nisi sciret pertinere suo officio accipere magnanimiter huiusmodi dignitatem, que videtur specificata a Christo Math. [p. 54] XVII, 23, 26, quando exactores didragmatis in presencia apostolorum singulariter dicunt Petro tamquam eorum maximo: Magister vester non solvit didragma? quod Salvator docuit in effectu, quando Petrum solum in solucione census sibi coniunxerat: Vade, inquit, ad mare et mitte hamum, et piscem qui primo ascenderit tolle et aperto ore eius invenies staterem, illum sumens da illis pro me et pro te. Tercio confirmatur ista sentencia ex testimonio sanctorum. Nam beatus Dionysius post autores scripture testis precipuus in libro suo De Divinis Nominibus capitulo tercio vocat beatum Petrum verticalem, id est, capitalem seu capitaneum, motus ex dicto Christi Joh. I, ubi in prima vocacione Petri nominat eum Cephas, quod sonat caput. Et libro suo De Ecclesiastica Yerarchia V capitulo vocat eciam beatum Petrum discipulorum verticalem, quod ostendit ex hoc quod ipse principaliter celebravit eleccionem Mathie loco Yude, ut patet Act. I. Unde iste sanctus beati Pauli precipuus discipulus et in morte assistens in libello quem scripsit De Morte Apostolorum sic alloquitur Thimoteum: Cum separarentur ab invicem ducti ad loca supplicii, Paulus Petrum alloquitur in hec verba: Pax tecum, fundamentum ecclesiarum et pastor ovium et agnorum Christi. Hec testimonia non testaretur tam sanctus et tam subtilis theologus se sensibiliter audivisse et tam diligenter notasse, nisi crederet beatum Petrum fuisse caput collegii Apostolorum post [p. 55] mortem Christi, sicut ecclesia sepe inculcat in servicio huius sancti. Et eandem sentenciam tenent leges ecclesie, nam XXIV, q. I Rogamus Marcellus papa sic loquitur: Rogamus vos fratres, non aliud doceatis quam quod a beato Petro et a reliquis apostolis accepistis, ipse est enim caput tocius ecclesie, cui ait Dominus: Tu es Petrus. Et idem patet XXI dist. capitulo. In novo, et XXII dist. capitulo. Omnes et Sacrosancta. Item, XXIV, q. III De illicita. Item beatus Augustinus in De questionibus veteris et nove legis, LXXIX capitulo, dicit, quod Petrus erat primus inter apostolos. Ideo dicit Christus quod Petrus solveret pro se didragma, ipsum, inquit Augustinus, constituit caput eorum, ut pastor esset gregis dominici. Et idem dicit cap. finali, et in libello Ad Spiritum Sanctum dicit: flere fecisti apostatam et ordinasti in summum pontificem. Nec oporteret tantum immorari in declaracione istius sentencie, nisi fuerit propter tria: primo pro declaracione autoritatum scripture per quas questio ista ostenditur, secundo ut cognoscatur sensus ecclesie, ad quem concedit Petrum habere super alios apostolos preeminenciam dignitatis, et tercio ut Petri vicarii cognoscant formam, secundum quam potestas Petri est ad pastores derivata.

Again, secondly, from Peter's opinions after the Ascension the same truth could probably be established; for to Peter alone was shown the sheet let down from heaven, in which were all four-footed beasts and creeping things of the earth and fowls of the air, as is clear from Acts 10:13 and 11:7, and to him alone was it said of all those animals: "Arise, Peter. Kill and eat."[^4] Now the sheet let down by four corners signifies the surface of the earth habitable by men, whose plane is woven together and worn like a sheet, the influence of heaven descending from east, west, north and south to temper the plane of the soil for the use of men. And those three kinds of animals let down in the sheet signify in order the secular lords as the four-footed beasts, the common people as the creeping things of the earth, and the [clergy]{g:clerus} as the fowls, flying up into the contemplative consideration of the times.[^5] These three kinds of men indeed make up the inhabitants of this world. Now Peter arose armed with [virtues]{g:virtus} and killed of these three kinds, when he extinguished the [mundanity]{g:mundialitas} in them and made them not men as regards the world. For so "man" is often taken in scripture, as is clear from 1 Corinthians 3:3: "Whereas there is among you envying and contention, are you not carnal and walk you not according to man?"[^6] Hence blessed Peter interpreted the imaginative vision by word and deed to this sense: that his eating was to cut away the perverse morals in them and to incorporate them personally into the church; for so Christ says, John 4:32: "I have meat to eat which you know not." For when the preacher, who in Canticles 4:2 is called the "teeth" of the bride, kills carnality with the sword of the spirit and with the mouth of the church grinds those who are to be converted to penance, then he spiritually eats the converted, assimilating them into [53] members of mother church; and this office belonged to Peter primarily.[^7] Hence in Acts 1:15 it is read that Peter, "rising up in the midst of the brethren", showed by the scripture of the old testament that in the place of Judas the traitor another bishop must be chosen, to fill his place; and so, according to [priority]{g:prioritas} of dignity, he conducted the election of Matthias.[^8] Again, in Acts 2:14 it is read how Peter with the eleven "lifted up his voice", speaking for all against those who charged them with drunkenness, showing from scripture that this is the spiritual filling by the Holy Spirit foretold in Joel 3.[^9] Again, in Acts 2:31 he next preached to the Jews "of the resurrection", and foretold to them, before all the apostles, what was necessary for them for salvation; and below, in chapter 3:1–2, when Peter and John went up into the temple, Peter held the [office of superior]{g:prepositura} in speaking at the healing of the lame man, as he holds also the first naming in the sequence of scripture; Peter also answered for all to the magistrates of the temple, as is clear from Acts chapter 4, and for all pronounced the sentence of death on Ananias and Sapphira, as is clear from chapter 5, and so on Simon Magus, Acts 8.[^10] Again, in the general council of which Acts 15 speaks, Peter pronounced for all the sentence that converts from the gentiles ought not to be circumcised nor to observe the ceremonies of the law.[^11] Now so holy an apostle would not presume to usurp such an office of superior after the sending of the Holy Spirit, who taught him all truth and confirmed in him all the humility instructed by Christ, unless he knew that it belonged to his office to accept such a dignity magnanimously; and this dignity seems to be specified by Christ in Matthew [54] 17:23, 26, when the collectors of the didrachma, in the presence of the apostles, say singularly to Peter, as to the greatest of them: "Doth not your master pay the didrachma?" This the Saviour taught in effect when he joined Peter alone to himself in the payment of the tribute: "Go," he says, "to the sea, and cast in a hook: and that fish which shall first come up, take: and when thou hast opened its mouth, thou shalt find a stater: take that, and give it to them for me and for thee."[^12] Thirdly, this teaching is confirmed from the testimony of the saints. For blessed Dionysius, after the authors of scripture the chief witness, in his book *On the Divine Names*, chapter 3, calls blessed Peter the summit (*verticalis*), that is, chief or [captain]{g:capitaneus}, moved by Christ's saying in John 1, where at Peter's first calling he names him Cephas, which means "head".[^13] And in his book *On the Ecclesiastical Hierarchy*, chapter 5, he also calls blessed Peter "the summit of the disciples", which he shows from this, that he principally conducted the election of Matthias in the place of Judas, as is clear from Acts 1.[^14] Hence this saint, blessed Paul's chief disciple, who stood by him at his death, in the little book he wrote *On the Death of the Apostles* addresses Timothy thus: "When they were being separated from each other, led to the places of execution, Paul addresses Peter in these words: Peace be with thee, foundation of the churches and shepherd of the sheep and lambs of Christ."[^15] So holy and so subtle a theologian would not testify that he had heard these testimonies with his senses and noted them so diligently, unless he believed that blessed Peter was [head]{g:caput} of the college of the Apostles after [55] Christ's death, as the church often inculcates in the service of this saint. And the laws of the church hold the same teaching; for in *Causa* 24, question 1, *Rogamus*, Pope Marcellus speaks thus: "We ask you, brethren, teach nothing other than what you received from blessed Peter and from the other apostles; for he is head of the whole church, to whom the Lord said: Thou art Peter."[^16] And the same is clear from Distinction 21, chapter *In novo*, and Distinction 22, chapters *Omnes* and *Sacrosancta*. Again, *Causa* 24, question 3, *De illicita*.[^17] Again, blessed Augustine, in *On the Questions of the Old and New Law*, chapter 79, says that "Peter was first among the apostles". Therefore Christ says that Peter should pay the didrachma for himself: "him", says Augustine, "he appointed their head, that he might be shepherd of the Lord's flock".[^18] And he says the same in the last chapter, and in the little book *To the Holy Spirit* he says: "Thou madest the apostate weep and didst ordain him highest pontiff."[^19] Nor would it be needful to dwell so long on the declaration of this teaching, were it not for three things: first, for the declaration of the authorities of scripture by which this question is shown; second, that the sense of the church may be known, according to which it grants that Peter has a preeminence of dignity over the other apostles; and third, that the vicars of Peter may know the form according to which the [power]{g:potestas} of Peter is derived to the pastors.[^20]

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** 1 Cor. 10:1–11; Gal. 4:22–31; Heb. 7:1–17. *Tamen si*: the manuscripts read *tamen sic* (51.13); Loserth's print is translated.

[^2]: **T/C.** *Dicetur*, future where a subjunctive is expected, is the reading of both manuscripts (51.19), kept by Loserth. *Ad fidem … adhibendam*: "so as to lend faith", i.e. to assent to Peter's primacy, the question of the chapter.

[^3]: **C.** *Dicat … istam sentenciam*: literally "let him state this teaching", i.e. concede it, against the two alternatives that follow.

[^4]: **T/S/C.** *Ex opinionibus Petri* is the print (read on the plate); what follows is Peter's acts, and the sense may want *operacionibus*, "works"; the print is translated. Acts 10:11–13 (DR "a great linen sheet let down by the four corners from heaven", "all manner of four-footed beasts and creeping things of the earth and fowls of the air", "Arise, Peter. Kill and eat"); 11:7. Wyclif's *repencia* for the Vulgate's *serpentia* is translated "creeping things" as in DR.

[^5]: **C.** *Influencia* is the heavens' influence on the earth, not the *influxus* of grace. *In consideracionem contemplativam temporum*: "of the times", perhaps "of the seasons"; the clergy as birds rise to contemplate the course of time.

[^6]: **S.** 1 Cor. 3:3 (DR); Wyclif omits *enim* and has *sint*. "Not men as regards the world": the figure takes *homo* in Paul's sense of the carnal man.

[^7]: **S/C.** Cant. 4:2 (DR "Thy teeth as flocks of sheep"); "the teeth of the bride" is the interpretive tradition's reading of the verse, not its words. The sword of the spirit: Eph. 6:17. *Matris ecclesie*, "mother church", is the church as mother, not the *matrix ecclesia* of a diocese.

[^8]: **S.** Acts 1:15 (DR "Peter rising up in the midst of the brethren"); the rest is Wyclif's summary of Acts 1:16–26 (1:20, "his bishopric let another take"). Loserth's italics run on over the summary to *eligere*; only the scriptural words are quoted. B omits *ostendit* (53.3); the print *oslendit* is translated as *ostendit*. A margin note "Matthias" stands at 53.5.

[^9]: **S.** Acts 2:14 (DR "But Peter standing up with the eleven, lifted up his voice"); the charge of drunkenness, Acts 2:13–15. "Joel 3" as printed; Loserth: rightly Joel 2:28 (Acts 2:17).

[^10]: **S/C.** Acts 2:31; "of the resurrection", of Christ, as Loserth notes (53.13). Acts 3:1–12; 4:8–12; 5:1–11; 8:18–24. *Prepositura locucionis* is the lead in speaking, the precedence a superior holds; "the first naming" is Peter named before John in Acts 3:1. *Sentencia* here is a judicial sentence.

[^11]: **T/S/C.** Acts 15:7–11; the "sentence" is Peter's decision for the council (*sentencia* as a judgement given, not a teaching). B reads *exigentibus* for *ex gentibus* (53.25).

[^12]: **S/C.** Matt. 17:23, 26 (DR); Wyclif reads *pro me et pro te* (Vulgate *pro me et te*). *Quod Salvator docuit* takes up the collectors' treating Peter as "greatest of them": Christ taught the same "in effect" by joining Peter alone to himself.

[^13]: **S.** Pseudo-Dionysius, *De divinis nominibus* 3: Loserth notes that the chapter only reads that Peter was present, "the supreme and most venerable summit of theologians" (*summa et porrectissima theologorum summitas*), ed. Cologne 1536 (54.12); the Greek is *hē koryphaia kai presbytatē tōn theologōn akrotēs*, *De div. nom.* 3.2 (PG 3, 681D), and *koryphaia* lies behind Wyclif's *verticalem* (LEDGER P03c-1). John 1:42 (DR "Cephas, which is interpreted Peter"); Wyclif takes *Cephas* as "head", the medieval etymology from Greek *kephalē*.

[^14]: **S.** Pseudo-Dionysius, *De ecclesiastica hierarchia* 5: Loserth gives the more correct form *discipulorum summum* (54.18); the Greek is *ho tōn mathētōn koryphaios*, *De eccl. hier.* 5.3.5 (PG 3, 512C), of Peter at the election of Matthias (LEDGER P03c-2). Acts 1:15–26.

[^15]: **S.** The letter of Dionysius to Timothy on the deaths of Peter and Paul is apocryphal; Loserth refers to Pitra, *Analecta sacra* IV (54.24), which prints it, with a Latin version from the Syriac, as the letter to Timothy *de morte apostolorum Petri et Pauli*; Paul's parting greeting there is *Pax tecum, fundamentum ecclesiarum et pastor ovium et agnorum Christi* (LEDGER P03c-3).

[^16]: **S.** *Decretum* C. 24 q. 1 c. 15 (*Rogamus*), under the name of Pope Marcellus; Loserth gives "cap. XV" (55.3); the modern form is ours (LEDGER P03c-4, editor). Matt. 16:18.

[^17]: **T/S.** Loserth: D. 21 c. 2 (*In novo*); D. 22 c. 1 (*Omnes*; the manuscript reads *Omnis*, 55.9) and c. 2 (*Sacrosancta*); C. 24 q. 3 c. 6 (*De illicita*) (55.9–10; LEDGER P03c-5, editor; modern forms ours). *In* of *In novo* is lost from our Latin at 55.9 (the plate reading shows *In novo*); it is translated.

[^18]: **S.** Ps.-Augustine (Ambrosiaster), *Quaestiones veteris et novi testamenti*: Loserth corrects the chapter to 75 (55.11): q. 75, PL 35, 2269–70, *qui primus inter Apostolos erat … Ipsum enim constituit esse caput eorum, ut pastor esset gregis dominici* (LEDGER P03c-6). Matt. 17:26.

[^19]: **S.** "The last chapter" of the same work, and the prayer *Ad Spiritum Sanctum*: the last question, q. 127 (PL 35, 2384), says that Peter received the primacy among the apostles; the prayer has not been found, and Loserth's references (*Opera* III, App., and *Tract. in Ioh.*, *Opera* III, 2, 724) belong to the *Quaestiones* citation at 55.10, not to this line (LEDGER P03c-7). The apostate is Peter after his denial.

[^20]: **T/C.** *Ad quem concedit*: the subject of *concedit* is the church. The manuscripts read *propterea* at 55.20, probably at *propter tria*; Loserth's print is translated.
