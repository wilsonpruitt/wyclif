# *De benedicta incarnacione*, chapter 9, part 5: three examples that lead the faithful to the mystery

## ¶1 · 158.24–160.9

> [p. 158] Sed dimissis arguciis queritur communiter racio diversitatis, quam est difficile placide assignare, cum sit de fundamento nature; ut quare Deus agens libere contradictorie adextra potuit illud tempus eternum, et non aliud nisi eius partem producere et sic de similibus. Prima ergo pars questionum huiusmodi vel non habet causam,¹¹ vel consequitur essencialiter ad Deum, sicut passio ad subiectum. Causa autem negative, que est secunda pars questionis, absolute necessaria est per se divina essencia. Cum enim¹² [p. 159] non potest fieri quod due persone fiant eadem persona in numero, aut¹ due nature eadem natura in numero,¹ vel quod Verbum aliam naturam assumpserat;² patet quod solum Deus est causa huius. Quod si dicatur adversarium eque faciliter sustinere oppositum sicut illud a nobis fingitur, dicitur quod tales contenciones sophistice secundum Apostolum sunt nimis inutiles, et ideo debet theologus ipsas abicere³ tenendo illud quod fides Christiana, scriptura sacra, et sanctorum doctorum consors sentencia ab antiquo nos edocet. Et in omni secta sunt multa credita⁴ que nec sufficiunt nec expedit protervo deducere. In⁵ hoc tamen⁵ excedit secta Christiana quascunque alias, quod maxime archana sue fidei copiose probari possunt, miracula explanari in naturali lumine evidenciis et exemplis philosophicis, necnon omnes impugnatorum argucie evidenter tolli eciam ex naturalibus et propriis eorum principiis. Ut triplex exemplum⁶ ponitur quod fidelis poterit manuduci ad credendum incarnacionis misterium. Primum quomodo duo accidencia diversorum generum, quorum nullum secundum genus potest esse reliquum, sunt idem subiecto ut quantitas et qualitas; non quod sint⁷ nature, res, sive substancie⁷ que possunt a subiecto et⁸ a se invicem separari, sed quod eidem subiecto inest esse quantum et quale; sicut eidem supposito Verbi inest esse tam Deum quam hominem. 2ᵐ exemplum propius est, quod eidem⁹ essencie corporee inest posse igniri, quod est materia prima, et accidentaliter esse ignem quod est forma; et sic materia et forma tam dispares sunt eadem essencia, que¹⁰ adventu forme sit aliud quam prefuit; sicut humanitas et deitas sunt eadem persona,¹⁰ que est aliud quam prefuit. 3ᵐ exemplum propinquissimum creatorum est de unione ypostatica corporis humani ad animam. Nam eadem persona est tam corpus [p. 160] quam anima, aliud propter corpus et aliud propter spiritum, sed non alius. Proporcionaliter est de Christo, qui est duarum naturarum utraque. Nam sicut eadem persona hominis nunc est tantum spiritus, nunc cum hoc aliud quia corpus; sic et Christus necessario est deitas sed contingenter humanitas. Raciones autem alias diversitatis et alias conveniencias scit peritus satis elicere et raciones oppositas plane dissolvere.

But, [quibbles]{g:argucie} laid aside, the reason of the difference is commonly asked, which it is difficult to assign calmly, since it belongs to the foundation of nature: as, why God, acting [freely, as between contradictories]{g:libere-contradictorie}, [outward]{g:adextra}, could produce that eternal time, and no other but its part, and so of like things.[^1] The first part of questions of this kind, therefore, either has no cause, or follows essentially upon God, as an [attribute]{g:passio} upon its [subject]{g:subiectum}. And the cause of the negative, which is the second part of the question, is, absolutely necessary, the divine [essence]{g:essencia} of itself.[^2] For since [159] it cannot be that two [persons]{g:persona} become the same person in number, or two [natures]{g:natura} the same nature in number, or that [the Word]{g:verbum} had assumed another nature, it is clear that God alone is the cause of this. And if it is said that an adversary sustains the opposite as easily as that [position] is devised by us, the answer is that such [sophistical]{g:sophistice} contentions, according to the Apostle, are [exceedingly]{g:nimis} unprofitable,[^3] and therefore the theologian ought to reject them, holding that which the Christian faith, holy scripture and the concordant [teaching]{g:sentencia} of the holy doctors teach us from of old. And in every [sect]{g:secta} there are many things believed which neither suffice, nor is it expedient, to be deduced for an [obstinate]{g:protervus} man.[^4] Yet in this the Christian sect exceeds all others whatever, that the secrets of its faith can most of all be copiously proved, its miracles explained in natural light by evidences and philosophical examples, and also all the quibbles of its assailants evidently removed, even from their own natural and proper principles. So a threefold example is set down by which a faithful man could be led by the hand to believing the [mystery]{g:misterium} of the incarnation.[^5] The first: how two [accidents]{g:accidens} of different genera, of which neither can, as to its genus, be the other, are the same in subject, as quantity and quality; not that they are natures, things or substances that can be separated from the subject and from each other, but that to be [quantified]{g:quantus} and qualified is in the same subject; as to be both God and man is in the same [supposit]{g:suppositum} of the Word.[^6] The second example is nearer: that in the same bodily essence there is being able to be set on fire, which is [prime matter]{g:materia-prima}, and accidentally being fire, which is form; and so matter and form, so unlike, are the same essence, which at the coming of the form is other than it was before; as [the humanity]{g:humanitas} and [the Godhead]{g:deitas} are the same person, which is other than it was before.[^7] The third example, the nearest of created things, is of the hypostatic [union]{g:unio} of the human body to the soul. For the same person is both body [160] and soul, one thing by reason of the body and [another thing]{g:aliud} by reason of the spirit, but not another one.[^8] Proportionally it is with Christ, who is each of two natures. For as the same person of a man is now only spirit, now with this something else, because body, so Christ too is necessarily the Godhead but contingently the humanity. And other reasons of difference and other [agreements]{g:conveniencia} the skilled man knows well enough how to draw out, and to dissolve plainly the opposite arguments.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** Obscure as printed: *potuit illud tempus eternum, et non aliud nisi eius partem producere* is construed literally, the question why God produced this time and not another, nor more than a part of it. *Adextra* "outward", the works of God outside himself, against *adintra*.

[^2]: **C/T.** *Causa … negative*: the cause why the contrary cannot be (the second part of the question) is the divine essence itself, of necessity. C omits *causam* (158.29); A B C omit *enim* (158.32).

[^3]: **S/T.** Tit 3:9 (Harris's margin; DR "contentions … For they are unprofitable and vain"). A reads *ipsas abicere*, corrected by another hand to *ipsis*; B *ipsis obicere*; C *ipsis abicere* (159.7). B omits *aut … numero* (159.2); O reads *assumat* (159.3).

[^4]: **C/T.** *Que nec sufficiunt nec expedit protervo deducere*: the plural *sufficiunt* has the things believed as subject; construed "which neither suffice to be deduced, nor is it expedient to deduce them, for the obstinate". A B C read *tradita* "handed down" for *credita* (159.10), and *et in hoc tantum* for *in hoc tamen* (159.11).

[^5]: **T.** O omits *exemplum* (159.16).

[^6]: **T.** *Sint nature, res, sive substancie* (159.20–21), with B C; A *sint vere res sive substancie*; O abbreviates, perhaps *sicut nature limitate substancie*. A B C omit *et* (159.21).

[^7]: **T.** *Que adventu … persona* (159.27–29) is wanting in A B C; O has it, abbreviated. O reads *idem* for *eidem* (159.24).

[^8]: **C.** *Aliud … sed non alius*: the neuter (another thing) against the masculine (another one, a person), the patristic formula of the two natures in one person.
