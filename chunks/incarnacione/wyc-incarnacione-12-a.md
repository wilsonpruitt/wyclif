# *De benedicta incarnacione*, chapter 12, part 1: the moderns' three opinions, Thomas's many men, and the monstrous Christ

## ¶1 · 201.0–201.0

<!-- the editor's bracketed argument at the chapter head: a Latin summary, then his own English
     summary in the same bracket (see the volume CARD for what is translated) -->

> [p. 201] [Recitando opiniones varias de assumpcione creature declarat quod, si assumeret multas humanitates, foret multi homines, sicut dictat sentencia sancti Thome. Against the several opinions that any divine Person can assume (a) any created nature; (b) every substance existing by itself and only such a substance; (c) every rational creature capable of bliss and only such a creature: it is contended that only the Word could assume human nature, and that only in the man Christ Jesus: and it is argued according to the dictum of St. Thomas that, if the Word assumed many humanities, he would be many men.]

[It recites various opinions on the [assumption]{g:assumpcio} of a [creature]{g:creatura}, and [makes clear]{g:declarare} that, if he assumed many [humanities]{g:humanitas}, he would be many men, as the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of St Thomas dictates.]

## ¶2 · 201.18–202.27

> [p. 201] Sed descendendum est ad trimembrem posicionem modernorum qui inani sollicitudine venantur⁷ quid contingeret si Christus multas humanitates assumeret. Falsitas enim [p. 202] dissonat sibi ipsi, teste Gregorio 12 Moralium ca° 14. ‘Alius, inquit, relicta veritate mentiri deliberat ut audiencium animum fallat. Sed quantus labor est custodire sollicite, ne ipsa eius fallacia deprehendi queat! Ponit quippe ante oculos quid aliis veritatem scientibus responderi possit; et cum magno cogitatu pertractat quomodo per argumenta falsitatis documenta¹ veritatis exsuperet; hinc inde se circumtegit; et contra hoc, ubi deprehendi potuerit, veritati similem responsionem querit; qui si utrobique verum diceret, hucusque² sine labore potuisset. Plana quippe est veritatis via et grave est iter mendacii.’³ Et indubie, sicut in agilibus, sic in speculabilibus verificatur ista sentencia; et patet tam exemplariter quam eciam racione. Si, inquam, consideremus pictacias quibus negantes universalia, extensionem temporis, et veritates de anima cum sibi similibus pregravantur, in quot errores incidunt, quot subterfugia querunt, et quam onerose locuntur, ut sub falsitatis pallio simulent veritatem; patesceret in speculabilibus sentencia Gregorii supradicta. Sed et racio illud idem exigit. Veritas enim obiective movet⁴ et instruit ad ipsam vere cognoscendum in animo et proporcionaliter proferendum: sed⁵ ad exprimendum⁵ falsitatem deficit obiectum huiusmodi in natura. Ideo difficultatur falsidicus pallians non existens, ac si absque fundamento vel exemplari aliquis⁶ intendit unum non ens vel vacuum fabricare. Unde et illud potest patescere in varietate sentenciarum de incarnacione hodie ventilata.

But we must descend to the three-membered [position]{g:posicio} of the [moderns]{g:moderni}, who with empty anxiety hunt for what would happen if Christ assumed many humanities.[^1] For falsehood [202] is discordant with itself, as Gregory witnesses, *Morals* 12, chapter 14: "Another," he says, "having abandoned the truth, resolves to lie so as to deceive the mind of his hearers. But how great a labour it is to guard anxiously lest his very deceit be detected! For he sets before his eyes what could be answered by others who know the truth; and with great thought he works out how, by the arguments of falsehood, he may overcome the proofs of truth; he covers himself on this side and that; and against this, where he could be detected, he seeks an answer like the truth; who, if he spoke the truth on both sides, could surely have [done it] without labour. For plain is the way of truth, and heavy is the road of lying."[^2] And without doubt, as in matters of action, so in speculative matters this teaching [holds true]{g:verificari}; and it is clear both by example and also by reason. If, I say, we consider the patches with which those who deny [universals]{g:universale}, the extension of time, and the truths about the soul, with their like, are weighed down; into how many errors they fall, how many subterfuges they seek, and how burdensomely they speak, so as to simulate truth under the cloak of falsehood: Gregory's teaching above would become plain in speculative matters.[^3] But reason too demands the same. For truth moves objectively, and instructs toward knowing it truly in the mind and uttering it proportionately; now for expressing falsehood such an object is lacking in nature. Therefore the false speaker, [cloaking]{g:palliare} what does not exist, is put in difficulty, as if someone, without foundation or exemplar, intended to build a non-being or a void.[^4] Hence this too can become plain in the variety of teachings on the incarnation aired today.

## ¶3 · 202.28–203.6

> [p. 202] Ponunt enim hi quod quamlibet naturam creatam quelibet persona divina indifferenter potest assumere: hi quod omnem et solam substanciam per se entem: et hi quod omnem et solam creaturam racionalem beatificabilem: et sic de quotlibet subdicionibus et evidenciis, quas nobis, qui ponimus solum Verbum pure Dominum Iesum Christum posse assumere, [p. 203] non expedit recitare; sed in difficultate, si Verbum multas humanitates assumeret, moderni doctores senciunt satis opposite. Sed pro ulteriori declaracione huius materie iuxta¹ ponam istorum sentencias, ut videatur quomodo exorbitantes a seipsis dissonant, et veritas rectissime in omnibus consonat sibi ipsi.

For these hold that any divine [person]{g:persona} can indifferently [assume]{g:assumere} any created [nature]{g:natura}; these, every and only substance that is [of itself]{g:per-se}; and these, every and only rational creature that can be beatified: and so of any number of subdivisions and [evidences]{g:evidencia}, which it is not expedient for us, who hold that only the [Word]{g:verbum} can assume, [and] purely the Lord Jesus Christ, [203] to recite; but on the difficulty, if the Word assumed many humanities, the [modern doctors]{g:moderni-doctores} feel quite oppositely.[^5] But for the further making clear of this matter I shall set their teachings side by side, so that it may be seen how those who leave the track are discordant with themselves, and truth is most rightly in harmony with itself in all things.[^6]

## ¶4 · 203.7–203.23

> [p. 203] Ponit enim sanctus Thomas super 3ᵐ Sentenciarum Distinccione prima Questione 8ᵃ et 9ᵃ quod, si Verbum multas humanitates assumeret, foret² multi homines; sicut si tres persone eandem humanitatem assumerent, forent omnes et singule idem homo. Et inter omnes veritates, quas laudabiliter de Christo³ scripserat, non estimo aliquam veriorem. Illa enim veritas connexionis est absolute necessaria, quomodolibet fuerit de extremis. Patet sic. Si aliqua sit humanitas, ipsa est homo; ergo si insint multe humanitates, ille sunt multi homines; et per consequens, si Verbum assumit ypostatice⁴ multas humanitates, tunc assumendo facit se multos homines. Antecedens⁵ patet quodammodo ex predictis.⁵ Ymo ex dictis De Anima palam sequitur: quia cum quilibet spiritus hominis per se sit homo, patet quod, si sint multi spiritus tales assumpti, assumuntur et multi homines. Sed⁶ et hoc⁶ patet ex eo quod omnis humanitas est personam esse hominem.

For St Thomas holds, on the third [book] of the *Sentences*, distinction 1, questions 8 and 9, that if the Word assumed many humanities he would be many men; just as, if three persons assumed the same humanity, they would all and each be the same man.[^7] And among all the truths which he wrote laudably about Christ, I reckon none truer. For that truth of the [conditional]{g:connexio} is absolutely necessary, however it may be with the extremes. It is clear thus. If there is some humanity, it is a man; therefore if many humanities are there, they are many men; and consequently, if the Word assumes [hypostatically]{g:ypostatice} many humanities, then by assuming he makes himself many men.[^8] The [antecedent]{g:antecedens} is clear in a way from what has been said. Indeed, from what was said in *De anima* it plainly follows: for since each spirit of a man is of itself a man, it is clear that, if many such spirits are assumed, many men are also assumed. And this too is clear from this: that every humanity is a person's being a man.[^9]

## ¶5 · 203.24–204.10

> [p. 203] Sed quia sanctus Thomas sicut et alii moderni doctores discordant a principiis iam assumptis, ideo oportet arguere contra possibilitatem suppositi aliis evidenciis. Videtur enim quod Christus posset esse quantumlibet monstruosus, quod esse non potest, cum minimum inconveniens sit Deo impossibile; et consequencia videtur ex hoc; quod, mille capitibus incommunicantibus existentibus in eodem corpore⁷ humano et⁷ proporcionaliter de aliis membris organicis, foret ille homo evidentissime monstrum magnum; sed hoc potest esse de Christo, cum omnem humanitatem potest [p. 204] simul assumere: ergo conclusio. Si enim Verbum totam unam cognacionem assumpserit tam maris quam femine, adhuc foret per adversarios solummodo unus homo, et per consequens tantum unum animal ac unum corpus; et super isto forent omnia dicta membra¹ monstruosa² ut estimo. Unde credo quod sanctus Thomas negans multiplicacionem dimensionalem eiusdem corporis, negaret in casu isto quod Christus est solummodo unus homo; nedum quod tunc foret hermofrodita,³ pater et filius, et tota cognacio; verum quia idem⁴ corpus foret dimensionaliter per loca distancia.

But because St Thomas, like the other modern doctors too, departs from the principles already assumed, it is therefore necessary to argue against the possibility of the [case] supposed with other evidences.[^10] For it seems that Christ could be as monstrous as you please, which cannot be, since the least [absurdity]{g:inconveniens} is impossible for God; and the [inference]{g:consequencia} appears from this: that, with a thousand heads [that do not communicate]{g:incommunicans} existing in the same human body, and proportionately of the other organic members, that man would most evidently be a great monster; now this can be [so] of Christ, since he can assume every humanity [204] at once: therefore the [conclusion]{g:conclusio}. For if the Word assumed the whole of one kindred, male as well as female, he would still, according to the adversaries, be only one man, and consequently only one animal and one body; and on this [body] all the said members would be monstrous, as I reckon.[^11] Hence I believe that St Thomas, denying the [dimensional multiplication]{g:multiplicacio-dimensionalis} of the same body, would deny in this case "Christ is only one man"; [not only]{g:nedum} because he would then be a hermaphrodite, father and son, and the whole kindred, but because the same body would be dimensionally in distant places.[^12]

## ¶6 · 204.11–204.14

> [p. 204] Sed hic dicitur quod monstruositas et impossibilitas multiplicacionis dimensionalis intelligitur secundum idem corpus; sed in casu posito foret secundum corpora multum disparia habitus talium situum et membrorum.

But here [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that monstrosity and the impossibility of dimensional multiplication are understood as to the same body; but in the case supposed [the having]{g:habitus} of such positions and members would be as to very disparate bodies.[^13]

## ¶7 · 204.15–205.14

> [p. 204] Contra illud tripliciter arguitur.⁵ Primo probatur quod secundum idem corpus habeat Christus istas denominaciones; quia secundum eandem naturam creatam et eandem humanitatem, que est Verbum esse unicum hominem; ergo secundum idem corpus. Si enim secundum seipsum habet ista, et ipse sit idem corpus, ubicunque est homo; sequitur quod secundum idem corpus habet ista, ubicunque sit homo.⁶ Similiter, si omnia ista corpora essent localiter copulata et adunata stante unione⁷ sine generacione vel corrupcione alicuius partis eorum, foret monstrum horribilissimum; quia corpus compositum ex corpore vetule et infantis, viri et uxoris, et sic de tota cognacione. Illud ergo cum fuerit totale et adequatum corpus Christi idem quod prefuit, sicut est modo, sic fuit ante monstruosum. Non enim adquiritur Christo aliqua pars vel partis posicio propter motum localem vel continuacionem⁸; ergo nec aliqua monstruositas secundum se totam. Et hoc est evidencius illis qui ponunt quod omnes partes integri⁹ sunt simul ipsum totum. Similiter non est evidencia contra multiplicacionem dimensionalem [p. 205] eiusdem corporis quin consimile contingit reducere contra hoc quod eadem persona sit dimensionaliter multiplicata secundum diversa corpora, ut quod hic sit senex, hic iuvenis, hic mas, hic femina, hic pater et uxor procreans¹ seipsum infantem, et hic adolescens sepeliens seipsum vetulam,² et ita de aliis denominacionibus aliorum generum. Unde, cum acciones et passiones personales individuantur primo a supposito non³ natura, videtur quod multiplicantur ad multiplicacionem suppositi; et sic idem homo foret quantumlibet dissimilis et contrarius sibi ipsi tam secundum accidencia corporis quam anime, ut sedulus excogitator potest calculare.⁴ Et sic contra principia sancti Thome idem corpus in numero foret quantumlibet multis et disparibus formis substancialibus accidentatum.⁵

Against this it is argued in three ways. First it is proved that Christ has these [namings]{g:denominacio} as to the same body; because [he has them] as to the same created nature and the same humanity, which is the Word's being a single man; therefore as to the same body. For if he has these as to himself, and he is the same body wherever he is a man, it follows that he has these as to the same body, wherever he is a man.[^14] Likewise, if all these bodies were locally joined and united, the union remaining, without the generation or corruption of any part of them, there would be a most horrible monster; for [it would be] a body composed of the body of an [old woman]{g:vetula} and of an infant, of husband and wife, and so of the whole kindred. Since, then, that would be the total and adequate body of Christ, the same as it was before, as it is now so was it before monstrous. For no part, or position of a part, is acquired by Christ through local motion or [continuation]{g:continuacio}; therefore no monstrosity either, as to the whole of it. And this is more evident to those who hold that all the parts of an integral [whole] together are the whole itself.[^15] Likewise there is no evidence against the dimensional multiplication [205] of the same body without a like [argument] being brought back against this: that the same person is dimensionally multiplied as to diverse bodies, so that here he is old, here young, here male, here female, here father and wife procreating himself an infant, and here a youth burying himself an old woman, and so of the other namings of other genera.[^16] Hence, since personal actions and [beings-acted-on]{g:passio} are individuated first by the [supposit]{g:suppositum}, not the nature, it seems that they are multiplied at the multiplication of the supposit; and so the same man would be as dissimilar and contrary to himself as you please, as to the [accidents]{g:accidens} of the body as well as of the soul, as a diligent deviser can calculate. And so, against the principles of St Thomas, the same body in number would be [subject to accidents]{g:accidentatus} by as many and as disparate [substantial forms]{g:forma-substancialis} as you please.[^17]

## ¶8 · 205.15–205.24

> [p. 205] Item, si Christus potest simul habere⁶ humanitates tam dispariter accidentatas cum hoc quod maneat idem homo, tunc posset pro diversis sitibus habere denominaciones contrarias; et per consequens, coextensis illis humanitatibus, forent accidencia contraria coextensa; et sic idem simul et semel contrariis accidentibus informatum. Sed hoc modicum videtur modernis, si secundum diversas naturas eidem supposito ista insint. Unde concedunt quod qualescunque forme contrarie per eundem situm et idem suppositum, sed secundum diversas naturas, coextendi poterunt.

Again, if Christ can at once have humanities so disparately subject to accidents, with this: that he remains the same man, then he could, for diverse positions, have contrary namings; and consequently, those humanities being coextended, contrary accidents would be coextended; and so the same [thing] would at once and together be informed by contrary accidents. But this seems a small matter to the moderns, if these things are in the same supposit as to diverse natures. Hence they grant that any contrary forms whatever could be coextended through the same position and the same supposit, but as to diverse natures.[^18]

## Notes

[^1]: **C/T.** "Three-membered position": the three opinions set out at 202.28–203.1 on which creatures a divine person can assume; *posicio* is the moderns' position, not a proposition. O reads *variantur* for *venantur* "hunt" (201.19).

[^2]: **S/T.** Gregory, *Moralia in Iob* XII.42.47 (Wyclif's c. 14; PL 75, 1008C; LEDGER I12a-1, verified). "Surely": *utique*, with O and Gregory, against the print's *hucusque* "thus far" (202.10); it is Gregory's word (*si vellet verum dicere, utique sine labore potuisset*). A B C read *argumenta* for *documenta* "proofs" (202.7); O *menda[ciu]m* (202.11).

[^3]: **C.** *Sentencia* (CONTEXTUAL) is "teaching" throughout this chunk: Gregory's saying, Thomas's position, the opinions on the incarnation. "Patches": *pictacias* (*pittacium*), the patches on the "cloak of falsehood" (*pallio*) in the same sentence. Those weighed down by them are the moderns who deny real universals, the extension of time, and truths about the soul.

[^4]: **C/T.** "Now for expressing falsehood": *sed* brings the minor premiss, closed by *ideo*. Truth is the object that moves the mind; falsehood has none in nature, so its speaker must build on nothing. O omits *sed ad exprimendum* (202.21–22); A B C read *monet* "admonishes" for *movet* (202.20); B *aliquid* for *aliquis* (202.24).

[^5]: **C.** "Only the Word can assume, [and] purely the Lord Jesus Christ": Wyclif's own position, that only the Word can assume and he only the humanity of Christ; Harris's summary has "only in the man Christ Jesus". The three opinions: any person any created nature; every and only substance existing of itself; every and only beatifiable rational creature.

[^6]: **T.** "Side by side": *iuxta ponam*; O reads *antequam* for *iuxta* (203.4).

[^7]: **S/T.** Thomas, *Scriptum super Sententiis* III d. 1 q. 2 a. 5, which grants "many men" (ad 3: *plures homines, et non unus homo*); not *Summa theologiae* III q. 3 a. 7, which concludes "one man" (LEDGER I12a-2, verified; cf. Iprol-3). Wyclif's "questions 8 and 9" count the articles of the distinction straight through: q. 1 has four, so 8 and 9 are q. 2 a. 4 and a. 5. The three persons and one humanity: the same distinction, q. 2 a. 4 ad 6, *dicerentur tres unus homo* (Wyclif's "8"; cf. *Summa theologiae* III q. 3 a. 6; LEDGER I12a-3, verified). *Foret* "he would be" is singular; A C read *forent* (203.9). A B C omit *de Christo* "about Christ" (203.12).

[^8]: **C/T.** "Truth of the conditional": *veritas connexionis*; the "if … then" holds necessarily whatever the truth of its antecedent and consequent, "the extremes". B omits *ypostatice* (203.17).

[^9]: **S/C/T.** *De anima*: Wyclif's own *De anima*, which the first sentence of this treatise names as its introduction; Harris notes it as unprinted and cited here (Notes on p. 1 l. 3) (LEDGER I12a-4, editor). "A person's being a man": *personam esse hominem*, the humanity as that by which a person is a man. B C omit *Antecedens … predictis* (203.18–19); A B C read *sicut et modo* "as also now" for *Sed et hoc* (203.22).

[^10]: **C.** "Principles already assumed": taken up in the argument, the logician's sense of *assumere*, not the assumption of a nature. "The possibility of the [case] supposed": *suppositi*, what is supposed (that the Word assumes many humanities), not the supposit. Thomas is counted among the modern doctors. *Inconveniens* (CONTEXTUAL) below is "absurdity", the logical sense.

[^11]: **C/T.** "On this [body]": *super isto*, the one body the adversaries must grant. A B C read *corpore ut* and omit *humano* (203.30–31); B omits *membra*, and O reads *monstruose* (204.5).

[^12]: **S/T.** Thomas's denial that one body can be dimensionally in several places (*Quodlibet* III q. 1 a. 2: *idem corpus simul localiter … in duobus locis*, which *a Deo fieri non potest*; LEDGER I12a-5, verified). A B C read *illud* "that" for *idem* "the same" (204.10). O spells *hermifodrita* (204.9).

[^13]: **C.** "The having": *habitus*, the possession of positions and members, not the Lombard's *habitus*. The reply: the members are spread over many bodies, each normal, so no one body is monstrous.

[^14]: **C/T.** "The Word's being a single man": the humanity is the Word's being one man (cf. 203.22–23, "a person's being a man"); the first proof runs from one humanity to one body. A C omit *homo* (204.21).

[^15]: **C/T.** "Continuation": *continuacionem*, the making of bodies into one continuum, not "carrying on"; Harris suggests *contiguacionem* "contiguity", citing *Trialogus* p. 87 l. 23 (204.30; LEDGER I12a-6, editor). A B C omit *unione* (204.23); A B read *integre* for *integri* (204.32): *integri*, genitive, the integral whole whose parts together are it.

[^16]: **C/T.** "Without a like [argument] being brought back": whatever is urged against one body in many places can equally be urged against one person in many bodies, which the adversaries grant. O reads *procreatus* for *procreans* (205.4); A B C omit *vetulam* (205.5–6).

[^17]: **C/S/T.** "Subject to accidents by … substantial forms": *accidentatum*, with O and B; A C read *actuatum* "actuated" (205.14), the plainer sense against Thomas's unity of substantial form (*Summa theologiae* I q. 76 a. 4: *nulla alia forma substantialis est in homine, nisi sola anima intellectiva*; LEDGER I12a-7, verified), but the print stands. *Passiones*, against *acciones*: being-acted-on. O reads *nam* for *non* (205.8) and *coniungere* for *calculare* (205.12).

[^18]: **C/T.** "As to diverse natures": the moderns' rule, by which contraries are said of Christ as to the Godhead and as to the humanity, here stretched to many humanities. B reads *summe* (perhaps *sumere*) for *habere* (205.15).
