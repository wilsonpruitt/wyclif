# *De potestate pape*, chapter 2, part 3: how the apostles were made priests, and the order of powers

## ¶1 · 32.9–33.5

> [p. 32] Sed tercio principaliter arguitur de modo committendi potestates nec non de ordine et perdicione potestatum ecclesie; et que potestas sit alia prestancior? Quantum ad primum patet, cum Deus dat omnem potestatem vicariam, qualem solum Deus communicat creature, non refert sive in signo sensibili, sive inspiracione interna Deus potestatem talem communicaverit creature quia oportet omnino quod insensibiliter communicet sive sacramentum sensibile affuerit sive non. Unde vana est concencio, quando et quomodo apostoli facti fuerant sacerdotes, ut Ardmacanus libro II De Questionibus Armenorum recitat septem opiniones in ista materia. Prima dicit quod, quando Christus dixit eis Math. XVIII, 18: Quodcunque ligaveritis super terram erit ligatum et in celis, secunda dicit, quando in cena dixit apostolis de confeccione eukaristie, hoc facite in meam commemoracionem, de quo Luce XXII, 19; tercia opinio dicit quod, quando Christus inflavit in eos dicens: Accipite Spiritum Sanctum, quorum remiseritis peccata, remittentur eis, Joh. XX, 22, 23. Quarta opinio dicit quod die Penthecostes, quando Spiritus Sanctus apparuit eis in linguis [p. 33] igneis Act. II; quinta opinio dicit quod in ascensione, quando dixit: Ite, docete omnes gentes, baptizantes eos Matth, ult.; sexta opinio dicit quod, quando primo misit apostolos ad predicandum Matth. X, 8 dicit: Infirmos curate.

But in the third principal place it is argued about the manner of committing powers, and also about the order and the loss of the [powers]{g:potestas} of the [church]{g:ecclesia}; and which power is more excellent than another. As to the first it is clear: since God gives every vicarial power, such as God alone communicates to a creature, it makes no difference whether God has communicated such power to a creature in a [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} sign or by inward inspiration, since he must altogether communicate it [imperceptibly]{g:insensibilis}, whether a perceptible sacrament is present or not. Hence the contention is vain over when and how the apostles were made [priests]{g:sacerdos}, as Armachanus, in book 2 *On the Questions of the Armenians*, recites seven opinions on this matter.[^1] The first says [it was] when Christ said to them, Matthew 18:18: "Whatsoever you shall bind upon earth, shall be bound also in heaven"; the second says, when at the supper he said to the apostles, of the confection of the eucharist, "Do this for a commemoration of me", of which Luke 22:19; the third opinion says [it was] when Christ breathed on them, saying: "Receive ye the Holy Ghost. Whose sins you shall forgive, they shall be forgiven them", John 20:22–23.[^2] The fourth opinion says [it was] on the day of Pentecost, when the Holy Spirit appeared to them in tongues [33] of fire, Acts 2; the fifth opinion says [it was] at the ascension, when he said: "Go, teach ye all nations: baptizing them", Matthew, the last [chapter]; the sixth opinion says [it was] when he first sent the apostles to preach, Matthew 10:8, [where] he says: "Heal the sick."[^3]

## ¶2 · 33.6–33.14

> [p. 33] Sed doctor reprobat omnes istas sex opiniones per hoc quod, quando fecit eos apostolos dedit eis potestatem sui officii, que est potestas sacerdocii; nec sequitur quod primo, quando precipit eis hec facere, habuerunt huiusmodi potestatem. Nam Joh. IV, legitur quod vivente Baptista apostoli baptizarunt et sic predicarunt et multa sacerdotis officia ante mortem Christi exercuerant, cum Yudas fuit episcopus, ut dicitur Actuum primo.

But the doctor rejects all these six opinions on the ground that, when he made them apostles, he gave them the power of their office, which is the power of the priesthood; nor does [it follow]{g:sequitur} that they first had power of this kind when he commanded them to do these things. For in John 4 it is read that while the Baptist was living the apostles baptized, and so they preached and had exercised many offices of a priest before Christ's death, since Judas was a [bishop]{g:episcopus}, as is said in Acts 1.[^4]

## ¶3 · 33.15–34.6

> [p. 33] In ista materia videntur mihi tria dicenda: Primo quod sentencia ista doctoris sit satis probabilis, secundo videtur mihi vanum nimis discutere quando apostoli baptizabantur, ordinabantur in sacerdotes vel sacramenta alia ministraverant sive receperant ultra quod est in evangelio expressatum. Scivit enim Christus quod infideles christiani signa querentes ad sacramentalia sensibilia, dimissa re sacramenti, attenderent; ideo de ministerio sacramentorum huiusmodi facit modicam mencionem, sed ut ego credo insensibiliter sine sacramento sensibili, tamquam primus magister ordinavit suos apostolos sacerdotes. Sic enim insensibiliter ordinavit Moysen in veteri testamento; ipse autem fuit sacerdos, licet non Aaronita sensibiliter ordinatus; et ita ignoramus locum; tempus et formam, quibus apostoli ordinati fuerant sacerdotes; debemus tamen credere quod si fuisset plus necessarium ad fidem [p. 34] ecclesie, Spiritus Sanctus in evangelio expressisset. Unde vana curiositas videtur, plus quam evidenter ex scriptura elicitur, tractare huiusmodi questiones, sed ignoranciam nostram confitendo humiliter debemus gracias agere Deo; qui noticiam nostram sic salubriter moderavit.

On this matter it seems to me that three things are to be said. First, that this [teaching]{g:sentencia} of the doctor is quite probable; second, it seems to me too vain to discuss when the apostles were baptized, were ordained priests, or ministered or received other sacraments, beyond what is expressed in the gospel.[^5] For Christ knew that [faithless]{g:infidelis} Christians, seeking signs, would attend to perceptible [sacramentals]{g:sacramentalia}, letting go the [reality of the sacrament]{g:res-sacramenti}; therefore he makes little mention of the ministry of sacraments of this kind; but, as I believe, he ordained his apostles priests imperceptibly, without a perceptible sacrament, as the first master. For so he imperceptibly ordained Moses in the old testament; and he was a priest, although not an Aaronite perceptibly ordained; and so we [are ignorant of]{g:ignorare} the place, time and form in which the apostles were ordained priests; yet we ought to believe that, if it had been more necessary to the faith [34] of the church, the Holy Spirit would have expressed it in the gospel.[^6] Hence it seems a vain curiosity to treat questions of this kind beyond what is evidently drawn from scripture; but, confessing our ignorance humbly, we ought to give thanks to God, who has so wholesomely moderated our knowledge.

## ¶4 · 34.7–34.11

> [p. 34] Sed tercio videtur doctorem arguere ex inconveniencia quod oportuit simul et plene fieri sacerdotes, quia aliter (ut inquit) sacramentum sacerdocii foret partibile et non indivisibile ut reliqua sacramenta.

But thirdly the doctor seems to argue from an [absurdity]{g:inconveniencia} that they had to be made priests at once and fully, since otherwise (as he says) "the sacrament of priesthood would be divisible and not indivisible, like the other sacraments".[^7]

## ¶5 · 34.12–35.16

> [p. 34] Sed hic videtur mihi iuxta dicta de potestate ordinis quod, quotquot sunt genera officiorum presbyteri, tot sunt genera potestatum, ymo respectu cuiuscunque actus quem debet facere ex potestate huiusmodi est dare unam insensibilem; et omnes ille tamquam partes integrales vel aggregata eidem subiecto unam potestatem constituunt. Nec videtur illud insensibile sed signum sensibile quod facit ordinans esse ecclesiasticum sacramentum. Nec est inconveniens sed veritati consonum quod quotlibet parcialia constituunt unum sacramentum, licet sint disparium specierum, nam per se notum videtur fidelibus quod Deus posset dare homini potestatem ad unum et subtrahere potestatem ad aliud. Ex quibus sequitur cum dictis de quiditate potestatis, quod oportet dare particulares potestates secundum numerum obiectorum terminancium, quia diceretur aliter quod preter liberum arbitrium nemo acquireret vel perderet potestatem. Ulterius videtur incertum quantam potestatem oporteret viantem habere, ad hoc quod sit sacerdos, vel si omnes sacerdotes sunt paris [p. 35] potestatis ordinis, quia certum est quod Deus dat vel non dat, ut sibi placuerit, ordinis potestatem. Unde caracterem sacerdotalis ordinis supponimus communiter consequi par officium sacerdotis. In qua materia vellem nostros solempnizantes constituciones humanas ad fidem scripture et puram racionem attendere et non amplius fingere ad onus ecclesie, nisi quod exinde scirent clare deducere, quia potissimum in talibus quod summus sacerdos potest facere, est mereri vel preconizare quod tale sit Dei iudicium, a quo sensu sepe exorbitat; ideo sepe creditur quod absolvit vel excommunicat, et tamen totum contrarium evenit aput Deum, et dum nos viantes sumus ecclesie triumphanti contrarii, necesse est quod sit controversia in universali ecclesia.

But here it seems to me, in keeping with what was said about the [power of order]{g:potestas-ordinis}, that as many as are the kinds of offices of a [presbyter]{g:presbiter}, so many are the kinds of powers; indeed, with respect to each act which he ought to do from power of this kind, there is one imperceptible [power] to be posited; and all of them, as integral parts, or as aggregated to the same subject, constitute one power.[^8] And it seems that not that imperceptible [power] but the perceptible sign which the one ordaining makes is the ecclesiastical sacrament. Nor is it an absurdity, but consonant with truth, that any number of partial [powers] constitute one sacrament, although they are of disparate species; for it seems self-evident to the faithful that God could give a man power for one thing and withdraw power for another. From these, together with what has been said about the [quiddity]{g:quiditas} of power, it follows that one must posit particular powers according to the number of the terminating objects; for otherwise it would be said that apart from [free choice]{g:liberum-arbitrium} no one would acquire or lose power.[^9] Further, it seems uncertain how much power [one on the road]{g:viantes} would need to have in order to be a priest, or whether all priests are of equal [35] power of order, since it is certain that God gives or does not give the power of order as it pleases him. Hence we commonly suppose that the [character]{g:caracter} of priestly order follows upon an equal office of priest.[^10] In this matter I would wish our men who solemnize human constitutions to attend to the faith of scripture and to pure reason, and not to feign more, to the burden of the church, than they could clearly deduce from there; for the most that the highest priest can do in such things is to merit, or to proclaim, that such is God's judgment, from which sense he often strays; therefore it is often believed that he absolves or excommunicates, and yet the whole contrary comes about with God; and while we who are on the road are contrary to the [church triumphant]{g:ecclesia-triumphans}, there must be controversy in the universal church.[^11]

## ¶6 · 35.17–36.27

> [p. 35] Quantum ad tercium, scilicet ordinem potestatum, dicitur communiter quod sicut sunt novem ordines angelorum, sic sunt novem ordines potestatum; et hoc elicitur ex Apostolo I. Corinth. XII, 6 ubi declarans unitatem corporis ecclesie ostendit, primo quod divisio graciarum, ministracionum et operacionum procedit ab uno spirituali Domino, qui operatur in omnibus; oportet enim graciam precedere, que sit principium ministrandi in clericis et operandi in laycis. Spiritus autem dat graciam, Dominus recipit ministracionem, Deus exigit ministracionem. Alii, inquit, per Spiritum datur sermo sapiencie, alii vero sermo sciencie secundum eundem Spiritum. Alteri fides in eodem Spiritu, alii gracia sanitatum in uno Spiritu, alii [p. 36] operacio virtutum, alii prophecia, alii discrecio Spirituum, alii genera linguarum, alii interpretacio sermonum. Et hec novem videtur Apostolus consequenter ordine suo in subiectis suis exprimere. Quosdam, inquit, Deus posuit in ecclesia, primo apostolos, secundo prophetas, tercio doctores, demum virtutes, exinde gracias curacionum, opitulacioues, gubernaciones, genera linguarum, interpretaciones sermonum. Que novem videntur aptanda novem prioribus. Apostoli autem sunt primi post Christum, quorum officium est preeminenter secundum sapienciam et missionem capitis ministrare, et eorum officium gerunt episcopi, sicut dicitur papam gerere vicem Christi. Nec oportet contendere circa gradum disposicionis requisite ad esse apostoli, quia certum est quod est latitudo supereminentis disposicionis ad statum apostolicum requisita, qua deficiente non est apostolus sive episcopus; nec requiritur quod Christus viando ipsum elegerit, ut patet de Paulo, Barnaba, Luca, Marco et Matthia; nec obest quod habeat multa officia, cum debeat propinquius sequi Christum assumentem summe ministratorie officium septem ordinum, ut patet libro IV capitulo VIII. Ideo officia cleri debent esse supereminenter in apostolis aggregata, sicut sapiencia excedit alia bona.

As to the third, namely the order of powers, it is commonly said that, just as there are nine orders of angels, so there are nine orders of powers; and this is drawn from the Apostle, 1 Corinthians 12:6, where, declaring the unity of the body of the church, he shows first that the division of graces, of ministries and of operations proceeds from one spiritual Lord, "who worketh all in all"; for grace must come first, which is the principle of ministering in clerics and of working in laymen.[^12] And the Spirit gives grace, the Lord receives ministry, God demands ministry.[^13] "To one indeed," he says, "by the Spirit, is given the word of wisdom: and to another, the word of knowledge, according to the same Spirit: to another, faith in the same spirit: to another, the grace of healing in one Spirit: to another [36] the working of miracles: to another, prophecy: to another, the discerning of spirits: to another, diverse kinds of tongues: to another, interpretation of speeches." And these nine the Apostle seems to express in sequence, in their order, in his subjects. "God," he says, "hath set some in the church; first apostles, secondly prophets, thirdly doctors: after that miracles: then the graces of healings, helps, governments, kinds of tongues, interpretations of speeches."[^14] Which nine seem to be fitted to the nine before. And the apostles are first after Christ; their office is to minister pre-eminently according to the wisdom and the mission of the head; and the bishops bear their office, as the [pope]{g:papa} is said to bear the place of Christ.[^15] Nor need one contend about the degree of disposition required for being an apostle, since it is certain that there is a latitude of surpassing disposition required for the apostolic state, and where it is lacking there is no apostle or bishop; nor is it required that Christ chose him while on the way, as is clear of Paul, Barnabas, Luke, Mark and Matthias; nor does it stand in the way that he has many offices, since he ought to follow more nearly Christ, who took on himself, in the highest and most ministering way, the office of the seven orders, as is clear in book IV, chapter 8.[^16] Therefore the offices of [the clergy]{g:clerus} ought to be aggregated in the apostles in a surpassing way, as wisdom exceeds the other goods.

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** Richard FitzRalph (Armachanus, archbishop of Armagh), *Summa in quaestionibus Armenorum* II; Loserth gives no chapter; the place is not in book II of the Paris 1512 print, which concerns the Messiah (LEDGER P02c-1). He lists six opinions to reject them; the seventh is his own (¶2). A and B in marg.: *Apostoli quando facti sunt sacerdotes*; B reads *dicit quod* (32.26).

[^2]: **S.** Matt. 18:18 (Wyclif's *Quodcunque ligaveritis … erit ligatum*, singular, for the Vulgate's *quaecumque alligaveritis … erunt ligata*); Luke 22:19; John 20:22–23, where the Vulgate has *remittuntur* (DR "they are forgiven"); Wyclif's future *remittentur* is translated.

[^3]: **S.** Acts 2:3. Matt. 28:19 (editor's reference): the Vulgate has *euntes ergo docete* (DR "Going therefore, teach ye"); Wyclif's *Ite, docete* is translated. Matt. 10:8.

[^4]: **S.** John 4:1–2 ("though Jesus himself did not baptize, but his disciples"); Acts 1:20, "his bishopric (*episcopatum*) let another take", of Judas. **T.** The print's stop after *facere* (33.10) falls inside the sentence and is disregarded.

[^5]: **C.** Three things are announced, but only *Primo* and *secundo* are marked; the third is taken to be the conclusion *debemus tamen credere …* (33.32), that what scripture does not express was not necessary to the church's faith.

[^6]: **S.** Moses as priest: Ps. 98:6, "Moses and Aaron among his priests". A and B in marg.: *Moises fuit sacerdos, non Aaronita*. **C.** *Infideles christiani* renders *infideles* with "faithless" (GLOSSARY *infidelis*): Christians who lack faith, not unbelievers outside the church.

[^7]: **C.** *Inconveniencia* here is an absurdity in argument (GLOSSARY CONTEXTUAL): FitzRalph's reductio, that otherwise priesthood would be divisible. The quotation follows Loserth's italics. B in marg.: *de unitate sacerdotalis ordinis*.

[^8]: **C.** *Est dare unam insensibilem*: the scholastic *est dare*, "there is one to be posited"; the feminine agrees with *potestatem* understood. *Partes integrales*: integral parts, which make up a whole by aggregation, as against subjective parts.

[^9]: **C.** *Obiectorum terminancium*: the objects in which each power terminates; a power is counted by its objects (cf. 24.8, 02-a). B reads *ex quiditate* (34.27) and *quoniam preter* (34.30); A *oportet* for *oporteret* (34.33).

[^10]: **C.** *Consequi par officium*: the character of order is supposed to follow an equal office, so that all priests are equal in the power of order; Wyclif doubts that this is certain.

[^11]: **C.** *Solempnizantes constituciones humanas*: those who make human constitutions solemn, the canonists and legislators (Loserth's side-note: "our legislators"). *Summus sacerdos*, "the highest priest", is the pope (side-note 35.12).

[^12]: **S.** 1 Cor. 12:4–6: "there are diversities of graces, but the same Spirit; … diversities of ministries, but the same Lord; … diversities of operations, but the same God, who worketh all in all" (Loserth refers to 12:5). A and B in marg.: *Novem genera potestatum*. B reads *spiritu alio dominio* (35.23–24).

[^13]: **T.** *Deus exigit ministracionem* (35.27) repeats the noun of the clause before; the Pauline triad (12:4–6) assigns operations (*operaciones*) to God, and *operacionem* may be the true reading. Translated as printed. B reads *recepit* for *recipit*.

[^14]: **S.** 1 Cor. 12:8–10; 12:28, where the Vulgate has *exinde doctores, deinde virtutes*; Wyclif's *tercio … demum* is followed by DR's "thirdly … after that". *Virtutes* is DR's "miracles". A and B in marg. at 36.14: *Prima ierarchia*.

[^15]: **C.** *Capitis*: of the head, Christ. *Gerere vicem Christi*, "to bear the place of Christ", the formula of the vicar.

[^16]: **T.** Loserth prints *habeat* (36.22); the codices read *habeant*, as the plural *apostoli*. **S.** Loserth says the place is not found in book IV (*De civili dominio* II), but it is there: ch. 8 (ed. Loserth, p. 73), where Wyclif names the seven orders and says that Christ gathered every mode of correction in himself, exercising it *ministratorie* (LEDGER P02c-2). Barnabas, Acts 14:13; Matthias, Acts 1:26. "The seven orders": the seven orders of the church, which Christ is said to have fulfilled in his own person (so Lombard, *Sent.* IV d.24, of each order: *hoc officio usus est Christus*) (LEDGER P02c-3).
