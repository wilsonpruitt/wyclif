# *De ecclesia*, chapter 5, part 5: Bernard's eight unities; one form of membership, predestination

## ¶1 · 108.18–110.8

> [p. 108] Hec autem fecit abbas noster, ut doceat nos tenere medium virtutis in nostris operibus. Et ex II° Ethicorum extrema ab illo medio in quo virtus consistit deviancia corrumpunt virtutem; nec video quin, si quantitas mathematica continuat elementa, si humor aqueus continuat iuxta singenia, si armonia corporis animalis faciat membra adnata esse continua, quasi longe plus latitudo, longitudo, sublimitas et profundum (de quibus loquitur apostolus Eph. III° 18) facit unanimes vere unum. Illa eciam aqua sapiencie (de qua Ecclesiastici XV, 3) continuabit unius moris in domo, et demum armonia in trinitate fundata illapsa universali ecclesie ipsam ex membris compaginatam faciet unam sponsam. [p. 109] Unde Bernhardus, De Consideracione, libro V°, ad Eugenium papam, ponit octo modos unitatum. Est, inquit, unitas que collectiva potest dici, ut multi lapides faciunt acervum unum. Est unitas constitutiva, cum multa membra unum corpus vel multe partes unum quodque totum constituunt. Est et coniugativa qua fit, ut duo iam non duo sint sed una caro. Est et nativa qua anima et caro unus nascitur homo. Est unitas potestativa qua homo virtutis non instabilis non dissimilis sed unus sibimet semper nititur inveniri. Est consentanea, cum per caritatem multorum hominum est cor unum et anima una. Est et votiva, cum anima votis omnibus adherens Deo unus spiritus est. Et est dignativa unitas qua limus noster a Dei verbo in unam assumptus est personam. Et super istas octo unitates dicit in quodam sermone quod est unitas principalis qua tres persone divine sunt una indivisibilis essencia. Et sic omnis unitas potest dici supernaturalis, naturalis vel virtualis. Duo posteriora membra recipiunt divisiones multiplices et cum in equivocis non sit contradiccio, patet quod non repugnat corpus ecclesie non continuari, loquendo de continuacione naturali sensibili, cum quelibet pars eius quantitativa completa in actu non sic continuatur cum alia naturaliter incommunicante, et tamen continuatur alia continuacione spirituali plus forti per spiritum Dei diffusum in cordibus omnium membrorum ecclesie habencium omnia in communi. Quarto principaliter arguitur per hoc quod ad univocam racionem ecclesie requiritur univoca racio suarum [p. 110] parcium, sed non est talis univocacio in peregrinantibus, dormientibus et beatis. Ergo non univoce constituunt Christi sanctam matrem ecclesiam que non potest dici etherogena, nam si fides ponatur forma constituens membrum ecclesie, videtur quod beati quibus inest clara visio, evacuata fide non sint de membris ecclesie, si caritas adhuc caritas patrie et caritas vie sunt disparium racionum.

And these things our abbot did to teach us to hold the mean of virtue in our works. And from
*Ethics* II, the extremes that deviate from that mean in which virtue consists corrupt virtue;[^1] nor
do I see why, if mathematical quantity makes the elements continuous, if watery humour makes things
continuous with their kin, if the harmony of an animal body makes the members grown on it to be
continuous, the breadth, length, height and depth (of which the apostle speaks, Ephesians 3:18) should
not, as it were far more, make those of one mind truly one.[^2] That "water of wisdom" too (of which
Ecclesiasticus 15:3 [speaks]) will make continuous [those] "of one manner in a house"; and finally the
harmony founded in the Trinity, flowing into the universal [church]{g:ecclesia}, will make it, joined
together of members, one [bride]{g:sponsa}.[^3] [109] Hence Bernard, *On Consideration*, book 5, to
[Pope]{g:papa} Eugenius, sets out eight modes of unity. "There is," he says, "a unity that can be
called collective, as many stones make one heap. There is a constitutive unity, when many members
constitute one body, or many parts any one whole. There is also a conjugal [unity], by which it comes
about that two are no longer two, but one flesh. There is also a native [unity], by which soul and
flesh are born one man.[^4] There is a potestative unity, by which a man of virtue strives always to
be found not unstable, not unlike himself, but one with himself. There is a consentient [unity], when
through [charity]{g:caritas} many men have one heart and one soul. There is also a votive [unity], when
the soul, cleaving to God with all its desires, is one spirit [with him]. And there is a condescending
unity, by which our clay was assumed by the Word of God into one person."[^5] And above these eight
unities he says in a certain sermon that there is a principal unity, by which the three divine persons
are one indivisible essence.[^6] And so every unity can be called supernatural, natural or virtual. The
two latter members admit manifold divisions; and since there is no contradiction in equivocal [terms],
it is clear that it is not repugnant that the body of the church is not continuous, speaking of
natural, [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} continuity, since each quantitative part of it, complete in act,
is not so continuous with another that naturally does not communicate [with it]; and yet it is
continuous by another, spiritual continuity, stronger, through the Spirit of God poured forth in the
hearts of all the members of the church, who have all things in common.[^7] Fourthly and principally,
[it is argued, by appeal to]{g:arguitur-per} this: that for a univocal account of the church there is
required a univocal account of its [110] parts; now there is no such univocity in those who are
[pilgrims]{g:peregrinari}, the sleeping and the blessed. Therefore they do not univocally constitute
Christ's holy mother church, which cannot be called heterogeneous; for if faith is posited as the form
constituting a member of the church, it seems that the blessed, in whom is clear vision, faith being
done away, are not of the members of the church; if charity, still the charity of the
[homeland]{g:patria} and the charity of the [way]{g:via} are of disparate accounts.[^8]

## ¶2 · 110.9–110.17

> [p. 110] Sed hic dicitur (ut supra) quod forma exemplaris eternaliter formans ab extra quodcunque membrum ecclesie est predestinacio Dei eterna. Forma autem per se et ab intrinseco constituens membrum ecclesie est predestinacio passiva. Ipsa enim non potest inesse multiplicative, nisi assit ecclesia. Et sic habet quilibet predestinatus fidem formatam caritate predestinacionis secundum quam constituitur ecclesia vel eius membrum. Et ita racio est univoca in quocunque salvando.

But here [the answer is]{g:dicitur} (as above) that the exemplar form, eternally forming from without
any member of the church, is God's eternal predestination. And the form constituting a member of the
church of itself and from within is passive predestination. For it cannot be present
multiplicatively unless the church is there. And so every [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} [person]
has [faith formed]{g:fides-formata} by the charity of predestination, according to which the church,
or its member, is constituted. And so the account is univocal in anyone who is to be saved.[^9]

## ¶3 · 110.18–112.9

> [p. 110] Unde sicut crementum et profectus persone non variat eius speciem, quia sic homo secundum variaciones etatum transiret de specie ad speciem, sic etas persone sancte matris ecclesie non variat eius speciem, sed corroborat per accidens racionem ecclesie. Ymmo manet eadem fides in mundo, in purgatorio et in celo. Sed imperfeccio que ex parte est hic evacuabitur in beatis; manebit, inquam, eadem fides que creditur, cum sit veritas eterna, et manebit eadem fides qua et secundum quam creditur, quia manebunt iidem actus et habitus. Est enim prima adhesio quam creatura habet de veritate credenda actus fidei cui accidit obscuritas enigmatica pro via que evacuatur in patria veniente intuicione clara, caritas autem eadem habens in patria confirmacionem loco incertitudinis. [p. 111] Dissensio autem est aput doctores nostros, eo quod aliqui intelligunt fidem aggregative pro adhesione prima cum imperfeccione sequente. Aliqui autem intelligunt fidem personaliter pro illa adhesione cui inest per accidens intuicio vel enigma. Illa autem prima adhesio precedens racionem est eiusdem racionis hic et in patria, sed ibi validior (sicut alibi diffuse exposui). Et sic omnes predestinati habent continue fidem gracia predestinacionis formatam, et sic non cadit a membris ecclesie per mortale, ut patet III°, De Doctrina Christiana XXXII°, et epistola XXXIII ad Bonifacium. Explicat hoc de David et Petro; de David autem explanat hoc in libro De Octo Questionibus ad Dulcicium amicum suum; quorum omnium racio est quia Deus non potest quemquam noviter diligere vel odire, sed sub quocumque gradu amat vel odit aliquem, sub eodem gradu eternaliter sic amat vel odit. Nec est detestabile quod Deus semper odit beatos in celo, nam semper odit peccata eorum preterita, non pro semper, ideo semper amat eos pro sempiterna glorificacione. Unde ecclesia excommunicat tales cum, ipsis existentibus in mortali, notificat eos privari communione sanctorum secundum presentem iusticiam, cum humidum fluens ad tempus eis deficiat, nunquam autem anathematizat tales vel omnino dividit a corpore Christi nec potest. Et ista est plerumque sentencia Augustini, si bene inspicitur, non in dolo. Et patet ex solucione proximi argumenti et huius, quomodo intelliguntur sancti Ysidorus, De Summo Bono. [p. 112] cap. XIX° et Augustinus, epistola XXXIIIª ad Bonifacium, quomodo ecclesia fidelium est ubique diffusa, ecclesia autem hereticorum est in parvo angulo coartata. Predestinati nempe dilatant more celestium fructus suos, alii autem licet sint hinc plures in numero, more terrestrium constringuntur. Christus enim caput prioris ecclesie cui oportet ut secunda subserviat, est potencior patre mendacii quem sic ligat, hoc est, ipsum in membris suis penaliter sic constringit.

Hence, just as the growth and progress of a person does not vary his species (for so a man, according
to the variations of his ages, would pass from species to species), so the age of the person of holy
mother church does not vary its species, but strengthens by accident the account of the church.
Indeed, the same faith remains in the world, in purgatory and in heaven. But the imperfection that is
"in part" here will be done away in the blessed; there will remain, I say, the same faith which is
believed, since it is eternal truth; and there will remain the same faith by which and according to
which [one] believes, since the same acts and habits will remain. For the first adhesion which the
creature has to the truth to be believed is the act of faith, to which, for the way, there befalls an
enigmatic obscurity, which is done away in the homeland when clear intuition comes; and charity
[remains] the same, having in the homeland confirmation in place of uncertainty.[^10] [111] And there
is disagreement among our doctors, in that some understand faith [aggregatively]{g:aggregative}, for
the first adhesion together with the imperfection that follows; but others understand faith
personally, for that adhesion in which intuition or enigma is present by accident. Now that first
adhesion, preceding reason, is of the same account here and in the homeland, but stronger there (as I
have expounded at length elsewhere).[^11] And so all the predestinate continually have faith formed by
the grace of predestination, and so [such a one] does not fall from the members of the church through
mortal [sin], as is clear from *On Christian Doctrine* III, 32, and the letter 33 to Boniface. He
explains this of David and Peter; and of David he explains it in the book *On Eight Questions* to his
friend Dulcitius;[^12] and the reason of all these is that God cannot love or hate anyone newly, but
under whatever degree he loves or hates someone, under the same degree he eternally so loves or hates.
Nor is it detestable that God always hates the blessed in heaven, for he always hates their past sins,
[though] not for always; therefore he always loves them for everlasting glorification.[^13] Hence the
church excommunicates such [persons] when, while they are in mortal [sin], it makes known that they are
deprived of the communion of saints according to present justice, since the [flowing
moisture]{g:humidum-fluens} fails them for a time; but it never anathematizes such, or wholly divides
[them] from the body of Christ, nor can it. And this is for the most part the
[teaching]{g:sentencia} of Augustine, if it is looked at well, not with guile.[^14] And it is clear,
from the solution of the last argument and of this one, how the saints are to be understood, Isidore,
*On the Highest Good*, [112] chapter 19, and Augustine, letter 33 to Boniface, [when they say] that
the church of the faithful is spread everywhere, but the church of the heretics is confined in a small
corner. For the predestinate spread their fruits after the manner of heavenly things; but the others,
although they are on this side more in number, are confined after the manner of earthly things. For
Christ, [head]{g:caput} of the former church, which the second must serve, is mightier than the father
of lies, whom he thus binds; that is, he thus confines him [in hardship]{g:penaliter} in his
members.[^15]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 108.18: which Aristotle has shown to be the seat of virtue.
- 109.18: Unity may be supernatural, natural or virtual.
- 110.1: 4th objection: If there is but one Church there should be but one way of membership, but we on earth have not the same form as the blessed.
- 110.9: Answer: We have the same form: God's predestination.
- 110.18: Moreover faith and charity are the same, only that in heaven they lose their imperfection.
- 111.1: Difference of opinion as to what Faith is.
- 111.8: All the predestinate have faith and cannot lose iteven by mortal sin.
- 111.21: The Church may rightly excommunicate them for sin, but cannot cut them off from the body of Christ.

## Notes

[^1]: **S/T.** *Abbas noster*: Christ, the "abbot" of 108.16. Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics* II
    (Loserth: II.7, Bekker II, 1107) (LEDGER E05e-1). A A₁ *Et secundo* (108.19).

[^2]: **S/T/C.** *Nec video quin … facit*: "nor do I see but that … makes"; we turn it as a question
    of "why not". *Singenia*: Loserth's *syngeneia*, things of the same kind; A A₁ *mixta singenia*
    (108.23); B *quin longe*, without *quasi* (108.24). Eph. 3:18,
    DR "what is the breadth and length and height and depth".

[^3]: **S.** Ecclus. 15:3, DR "the water of wholesome wisdom"; *unius moris in domo* is Ps. 67:7, DR
    "God who maketh men of one manner to dwell in a house", taken into Wyclif's sentence.

[^4]: **T/S.** *143°* at the head of 109 is a manuscript folio mark, not Wyclif's, and is
    not translated. A margin *Bernhardus*; A B margin *Unitatis octo modi*. Bernard, *De consideratione*
    V.8.18 (PL 182:799C–D; Loserth: *Opp.*, p. 1048), the same eight unities, near verbatim; *inquit*
    is Wyclif's (LEDGER E05e-2). "Two … one
    flesh": Matt. 19:6.

[^5]: **C/T/S.** The adjectives are Bernard's terms, kept close: *potestativa*, the unity of a man
    master of himself; *consentanea*, of agreement (Acts 4:32); *votiva*, of the will's desires (1 Cor.
    6:17, DR "he who is joined to the Lord is one spirit"); *dignativa*, of God's condescension in the
    incarnation. B *non instabit* (109.10); B *una assumptus* (109.15).

[^6]: **S.** Loserth (109.16): Bernard, *Sermones de diversis* 80, *De multiplici unitate* (PL 183:698D–699A;
    *Opp.*, p. 450): "the principal unity, which is also
    substantial, is in the Trinity" (LEDGER E05e-3).

[^7]: **C/S.** *Virtualis*: of power or virtue, as against substance and nature. *Duo posteriora
    membra*: the natural and the virtual. *In equivocis*: between terms taken in different senses there
    is no contradiction, so the church can be continuous in one sense and not in another. The Spirit
    "poured forth in the hearts": Rom. 5:5; "all things in common": Acts 4:32.

[^8]: **T/C/R.** The stray *q* before *Quarto* (109.20) is an alinea or margin letter, not Wyclif's,
    and is not translated. A margin *Quarta obieccio. Hic nota. Nota materiam de univoca racione parcium
    ecclesie*; B margin, another hand, *Predestinacio …* (garbled in the OCR). *Racio*: the account or
    definition. The sleeping are the church in purgatory. A A₁ *etheromogena* (110.4). *Si caritas
    adhuc …*: we construe "if [charity is posited as the form], still …"; the faith of the way is done
    away in the blessed (1 Cor. 13:10).

[^9]: **T/R/C.** A margin *Responsio* (110.9). Active predestination is God's act (the exemplar form);
    passive predestination is the being-predestined of the member. *Assit* (plate): *adsit*.
    *Multiplicative*: in many subjects at once. *Ecclesia vel eius membrum*: A *membra*, B *ut eius* (110.16); B margin *Quid hominem constituit membrum
    ecclesie*. The closing *faith* (110.17) is a fragment of Loserth's English side-note at 110.18, not
    translated.

[^10]: **S/C/R.** 1 Cor. 13:10, DR "that which is in part shall be done away"; *obscuritas enigmatica*:
    1 Cor. 13:12, DR "through a glass in a dark manner" (Vulgate *in aenigmate*). *Fides que creditur*
    and *fides qua creditur*: the faith believed and the faith by which one believes. A margin (110.32):
    *Nota bene quomodo manet eadem fides in celis que est hic*.

[^11]: **T/C/R.** B *prima imperfeccione* (111.2–3); A margin *Dupliciter dicitur fides*. *Personaliter*
    is set against *aggregative*: faith taken by itself, apart from the accidents it has in each state;
    the word is doubtful (Latin doubts). *Alibi diffuse exposui*: the place is not identified here.

[^12]: **S/T.** A margin *Augustinus* (111.10). *De doctrina christiana* III.32, as at 103.16. Codd.
    *epistola XXXII*, Loserth *XXXIII* (*Opp. Aug.* II, p. 263) (LEDGER E05e-4). Codd. A A₁ *de decem
    questionibus*, B corrupt; Loserth corrected to *De octo Dulcitii quaestionibus* (*Opp. Aug.* VI, p.
    121) (LEDGER E05e-5). David and Peter: *Ep.* 185.45–46 (PL 33:812–813), the letter to Boniface; David:
    *De octo Dulcitii quaestionibus* q. 5 (PL 40:167–169).

[^13]: **C/R.** A margin *Deus odit ab eterno et diligit quos diligit et odit*; B *sic amat* (111.16).
    *Diligere* and *amare* are both "love". *Non pro semper*: he hates their sins always, but not as
    lasting for all time; his love is for their everlasting glory.

[^14]: **C.** *Excommunicat … anathematizat*: excommunication declares a present loss of communion;
    anathema would cut off from the body of Christ, which the church cannot do to the predestinate.
    *Humidum fluens*: the flowing moisture that feeds a living body (GLOSSARY), here the grace that
    fails them for a time. *Non in dolo*: without guile, read honestly.

[^15]: **S/T/R.** Isidore, *Sententiae* (*De summo bono*) I, chapter 19 (codd. *cap. XVI*), quoted by
    Loserth: the church is called catholic because spread through all the world, "but the churches of
    the heretics are confined in parts of the world" (LEDGER E05e-6). Wyclif's *in parvo angulo* is not
    Isidore's (cf. Grosseteste at 99.17). Codd. again *epistola XXXII* (112.1). A margin *Ubi est
    ecclesia*. "The father of lies": John 8:44; binding the strong man: cf. Matt. 12:29.
