# *De ecclesia*, chapter 6, part 4: the second and third difficulties; honour due to prelates is judged by their works

## ¶1 · 126.14–128.9

> [p. 126] Secunda difficultas logica est, utrum debet concedi, posito quod essemus ante incarnacionem, quod verbum est homo Christus Jesus. Et videtur quod sic, nam secundum rectam philosophiam et logicam creatura nedum habet existenciam in suo singulari sed tam esse ydeale in verbo quam esse potenciale in causis secundis. Cum ergo ante incarnacionem fuit illa sancta massa materialis in patribus, quam postmodum assumpsit de virgine, et spiritus habuit esse in humana specie, videtur quod totus Christus habuit humanitus esse potenciale, et sic esse. Confirmatur secundo ex hoc quod sacerdos conficiens in sancto triduo vere conficeret corpus Christi, sicut dicunt doctores concorditer, in tantum quod hoc foret tunc verum hoc est corpus Christi (ut recitavi in tractatu De Incarnacione); quare ergo non foret verum ante incarnacionem quod verbum est Christus, cum tunc habet corpus quod post unit sibi, sicut est de corpore suo pro triduo? [p. 127] Tercio confirmatur ex hoc quod ecclesia foret aliter acephala, cum tunc non haberet caput intrinsecum sicut post, et cum non potest habere taliter nisi unum caput, videtur quod Christus ante incarnacionem fuit caput sponse sue intrinsecum, quia aliter vel haberet duo capita vel idem foret caput extrinsecum et caput intrinsecum. Et ad illud sonant quotlibet scripture (ut allegavi alias de beato Jeronymo in quadam epistola ad Paulum et Eustochium). Hic dicitur quod patribus veteris testamenti fuit hec vera: verbum est homo Christus Jesus. Ipsi enim secundum sanctos habuerunt eandem fidem quam nos habemus. Ideo sicut nos credimus creacionem mundi, carnis resurreccionem et vitam eternam, hoc est quod Deus tempore suo creat mundum, quod mortui resurgent, et quod beati vivunt eternaliter post diem iudicii, ita utraque ecclesia credit incarnacionem, scilicet quod Christus pro suo tempore incarnatur. Et sic proposicio nostra: Christus est homo, foret in signo utrobique vera, non quia tunc est quod Christus est homo, sed quia verum est quod Christus est homo pro suo tempore. Nec video quod Christus est homo pro illo tempore, cum tunc componeretur ex corpore et anima quam assumeret pro illo tempore, et potissime foret primus Adam secundum materiam et non secundum raciones seminales, qualiter apostolus dicit Levi fuisse in lumbis Abrahe, et qualiter Anselmus dicit: Omnes nos fuisse Adam, sed limitatum est ad existenciam individuam humanitatis Christi. Et ad primam instanciam dicitur quod non sequitur: Hoc eternaliter fuit et hoc est iste homo, ergo iste homo eternaliter fuit, deducendo pure creaturam. [p. 128] Conceditur tamen de Christo quod ipse eternaliter fuit Deus; nec sequitur: Si iste homo habet esse potenciale in causis secundis, ergo iste homo est pro eadem mensura, quia tunc omne individuum quod posset esse foret, et sic esse in potencia est distrahens. Vel concesso quod Christus habet esse creatum pro mensura data, quod est, esse cause secunde, tamen tunc non habet esse Christi, quia tunc haberet existenciam humanitus singularem.

The second difficulty is a logical one: whether, supposing that we were before the incarnation, it
ought to be granted that the Word is the man Christ Jesus.[^1] And it seems that it ought: for
according to right philosophy and logic a creature not only has existence in its singular, but both
ideal [being]{g:esse} in the Word and potential being in secondary causes. Since, therefore, before
the incarnation that holy material mass which he afterwards assumed from the virgin was in the
fathers, and the spirit had being in the human species, it seems that the whole Christ had potential
being as man, and so being.[^2] It is confirmed, secondly, from this: that a priest consecrating in
the holy three days would truly consecrate the body of Christ, as the doctors say with one accord, so
much so that this would then be true, "This is the body of Christ" (as I have recounted in the
treatise *On the Incarnation*); why, then, would it not be true before the incarnation that the Word
is Christ, since he then has the body which he afterwards unites to himself, as is the case with his
body for the three days?[^3] [127] Thirdly, it is confirmed from this: that otherwise the
[church]{g:ecclesia} would be headless, since it would not then have an intrinsic [head]{g:caput} as
afterwards; and since it cannot have more than one head in this way, it seems that Christ before the
incarnation was the intrinsic head of his [bride]{g:sponsa}; for otherwise either it would have two
heads, or the same [one] would be extrinsic head and intrinsic head. And any number of scriptures
sound to that effect (as I have alleged elsewhere from blessed Jerome, in a certain letter to Paula
and Eustochium).[^4] Here [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that for the fathers of the old testament this
was true: "The Word is the man Christ Jesus." For they, according to the saints, had the same faith
that we have. Therefore, just as we believe the creation of the world, the resurrection of the flesh
and life everlasting, that is, that God in his time creates the world, that the dead will rise, and
that the blessed live eternally after the day of judgment, so each church believes the incarnation,
namely that Christ for his time is incarnate.[^5] And so our proposition, "Christ is man", would be
true in the sign on both sides, not because it is then [the case] that Christ is man, but because it
is true that Christ is man for his time.[^6] Nor do I see that Christ is man for that time, since then
he would be composed of a body and a soul which he would assume for that time; and above all he would
be the first Adam according to matter and not according to seminal reasons, in the way in which the
apostle says Levi was in the loins of Abraham, and in which Anselm says "that we all were Adam"; but
it is limited to the individual existence of Christ's humanity.[^7] And to the first
[objection]{g:instancia} the answer is that [it does not follow]{g:sequitur}, "This was eternally,
and this is this man, therefore this man was eternally", when it is drawn out purely of the creature.
[128] Yet it is granted of Christ that he was eternally God; nor does it follow, "If this man has
potential being in secondary causes, therefore this man is for the same measure", for then every
individual that could be would be; and so "being in potency" is a diminishing qualifier. Or, granted
that Christ has created being for the given measure, that is, the being of the secondary cause, yet
he does not then have the being of Christ, for then he would have singular existence as man.[^8]

## ¶2 · 128.10–128.17

> [p. 128] Et sic respondetur ad secundam instanciam con- cedendo assumptum, cum Christus fuit in triduo tam corpus quam anima, sed cum Christus non assumit corpus ypostatice nisi mediante anima, patet, cum nondum creata est anima Christi pro tempore veteris testamenti, non pro illo tempore est Christus, nec sufficit ad esse individuum esse specificum, quia tunc omnes effectus absolute necessario evenirent.

And so [the reply is]{g:respondetur} to the second objection by granting the
[assumption]{g:assumptum}, since Christ was in the three days both body and soul; but since Christ
does not assume a body hypostatically except by means of the soul, it is clear, since the soul of
Christ was not yet created for the time of the old testament, that Christ is not for that time; nor
does specific being suffice for individual being, for then all effects would come about with absolute
necessity.[^9]

## ¶3 · 128.18–128.28

> [p. 128] Ad tercium negatur assumptum, cum ecclesia semper fuit capitata per verbum quod mansit perpetue eadem persona et sic idem caput post et ante. Conceditur tamen quod post illas nupcias celebratas in utero virginis, quando Christus fuit murus et antemurale sponse sue, fuit caput racionis duplicis et duplicis nature et sic simul caput intrinsecum et caput extrinsecum eiusdem ecclesie. Et sic ad auctoritates potest dici (ut alias) quod Christus est pro tempore veteris testamenti nedum secundum deitatem sed secundum causas secundas, et sic possunt ipsi dici christiani.

To the third, the assumption is denied, since the church was always [headed]{g:capitatus} by the
Word, which remained perpetually the same person, and so the same head after and before. Yet it is
granted that after those nuptials celebrated in the virgin's womb, when Christ was the wall and the
bulwark of his bride, he was head of a twofold aspect and of a twofold nature, and so at once
intrinsic head and extrinsic head of the same church. And so to the authorities it can be said (as
elsewhere) that Christ is, for the time of the old testament, not only according to the
[Godhead]{g:deitas} but according to secondary causes; and so they can be called christians.[^10]

## ¶4 · 128.29–129.11

> [p. 128] Tercia difficultas naturalis est, quomodo manet idem corpus inanimatum aliis partibus corruptis continue generatis. Sed ad illud dictum est alias quod est dare corpus successivum tam continue quam interpolate, et ita est de domo materiali, sive ecclesia, sic quod existente secundum totam aliquam fuit illa que permansit [p. 129] in principio, adhuc manet; et est eadem domus successiva que fuit a principio usque in finem; sicut posito quod ignis incipiat partibiliter generari et cum hoc corrumpi, tunc ignis successivus mensurat secundum partes utrumque motum, et sic corrumpitur inanimatum ad corrupcionem tocius et manet continue, dum corrumpitur. Et si queratur, quando consecrande forent ecclesie, dicitur quod ille est ritus humanitus introductus, ideo non est vis de tali consecracione assistente sanctitate persone incolentis. Istam autem materiam relinquo aliis.

The third difficulty is a natural one: how the same inanimate body remains when some parts are
corrupted and [others] continually generated.[^11] But to that it has been said elsewhere that there
is such a thing as a [successive]{g:successivum} body, both continuous and interrupted; and so it is
with a material house, or church, so that, while some [part] exists as a whole, that which has
remained [129] from the beginning still remains; and it is the same successive house that was from
the beginning to the end;[^12] as, supposing that a fire begins to be generated part by part and at
the same time to be corrupted, then the successive fire measures each motion according to its parts;
and so the inanimate thing is corrupted to the corruption of the whole, and remains continuously
while it is being corrupted. And if it is asked when churches would have to be consecrated, the
answer is that that is a rite introduced by men; therefore there is no weight in such consecration,
so long as the holiness of the person who dwells there is present. But this matter I leave to
others.[^13]

## ¶5 · 129.12–129.17

> [p. 129] Sed quinto arguitur, probando sequi ex dictis omnem honorem et obedienciam inpendendam prelato confundi et omnem exaccionem corporalis debiti, cum deficeret probacio predestinacionis vel sanctitatis, et illud secundum racionem foret fundamentum talia vendicandi.

But fifthly it is argued, by proving that it follows from what has been said that all honour and
obedience to be paid to a [prelate]{g:prelatus} are thrown into confusion, and every exaction of a
bodily debt, since proof of predestination or holiness would be lacking; and that, according to
reason, would be the foundation for claiming such things.[^14]

## ¶6 · 129.18–130.17

> [p. 129] Hic negatur assumptum, quia (ut patet alibi) operibus debet credi, nec licet prelato aspirare ad lucrum vel honores illis dimissis, quia ut sic redderet se indignum illis lucris atque honoribus. Unde oraremus omnes, ut ecclesia sicut in suis primitivis attendat pure fructuoso operi sine ambicione temporalis commodi vel honoris. Modo vero ad tantum invalescebat oppositum quod de clero nostro potest dici illud Ysaie III°, 12: Populum meum exactores sui spoliaverunt et mulieres dominate sunt eius. Et sequitur: Popule meus qui beatum te dicunt, ipsi te decipiunt. Nam qui olim erant rectores anime, iam perversi sunt in exactores pecunie; qui olim zelabant pro honore divino contempto honore mundano, modo econtra lege Dei et per consequens honore eius postposito, preponderantes tradiciones [p. 130] humanas nimis dediti sunt mundo, qui olim dediti sunt ministerio apostolico, contempto temporali lucro, modo econtra totaliter inhiant temporali comodo, insolencia mundana et ocio introducto. Et tum extorta sunt nomina: Nam qui olim nominarunt se a Deo vocatos apostolos, hodie contendunt se permissione divina ab homine nude electos; qui olim nominarunt se servos domini Jesu Christi, hodie retorquent, ut nominentur domini reverendissimi et patres beatissimi, et (quod est summus gradus ypocrisis) vocant se servos servorum Dei, licet sint in opere, et per consequens, in nomine reali servi precipui patris mendacii et mendacissima membra diaboli. Et sic necesse est iuxta propheciam scripture ut pervertantur mendacio et ypocrisi singuli non electi. Tales autem, ut docet Augustinus in libro De Vita Christiana, non sunt veraciter christiani.

Here the assumption is denied, for (as is clear elsewhere) works ought to be believed; nor is it
lawful for a prelate to aspire to gain or honours with those [works] set aside, for as such he would
render himself unworthy of those gains and honours.[^15] Hence we should all pray that the church, as
in its first [days], may attend purely to fruitful work without ambition of temporal advantage or
honour. But now the opposite has grown so strong that of our clergy can be said that of Isaiah 3:12:
"As for my people, their oppressors have stripped them, and women have ruled over them." And it
follows: "O my people, they that call thee blessed, the same deceive thee."[^16] For those who were
once rulers of the soul are now perverted into exactors of money; those who were once zealous for
God's honour, worldly honour despised, now on the contrary, God's law and consequently his honour
being put after, and weighting the scale with [130] human traditions, are too much given over to the
world; those who were once given to the apostolic ministry, temporal gain despised, now on the
contrary gape wholly after temporal advantage, worldly insolence and idleness being brought in.[^17]
And then the names were wrested: for those who once named themselves apostles called by God today
contend that they are elected barely by man, by divine permission; those who once named themselves
servants of the Lord Jesus Christ today twist it round, so that they are named most reverend
[lords]{g:dominus} and most blessed fathers; and (what is the highest degree of hypocrisy) they call
themselves servants of the servants of God, although in work, and consequently in real name, they are
chief servants of the father of lies and most lying members of the devil.[^18] And so it is necessary,
according to the prophecy of scripture, that every one of the non-elect be perverted by lying and
hypocrisy. And such men, as Augustine teaches in the book *On the Christian Life*, are not truly
christians.[^19]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 126.14: A difficulty: Whether before the incarnation the Word was the man Christ Jesus. Yes; for he was so both ideally and potentially.
- 126.24: Confirmation: In the three days the body to which he was afterwards united was his.
- 127.1: Confirmation: Otherwise the Church must have been headless.
- 127.10: Reply. The fathers of the O. T. believed in the incarnation,
- 127.19: so far that Christ would be man in due time.
- 128.1: We must be careful to distinguish between potential and actual being.
- 128.10: Christ assumed the human body with the soul. He had the soul in the three days but not in O. T. times.
- 128.18: The Church would not be headless, for the word was always its head, but in another way since the incarnation.
- 129.12: Objection: We should not know to whom to pay honour or even debt.
- 129.18: Answer: As to honouring prelates we must judge them by their works.
- 129.25: Prevailing abuses.

## Notes

[^1]: **R/C.** A margin *Secunda difficultas. Utrum verbum est homo Christus Jesus ante
    incarnacionem*; B margin (later hand) *Ante incarnacionem verbum est homo Christus Jesus* (126.14).
    The second of the three difficulties announced at 123.24 (06-b). *Posito quod essemus*: a
    supposition in the manner of the schools, testing the proposition's truth at that time.

[^2]: **T/C.**  *Esse ydeale … esse potenciale*: ideal being in
    the divine Word, potential being in secondary causes (his ancestors). *Massa materialis in
    patribus*: the matter of Christ's body, in his forefathers. We take *spiritus habuit esse in humana
    specie* of the human soul, having being in the species; doubtful. *Humanitus*: as man.

[^3]: **T/S/R.**  The page foot gives
    "B: missa" (126.21) without the word it replaces; B margin (later hand) *In triduo* (126.32).
    *Sanctum triduum*: the three days of Christ's death; *conficere*: to consecrate the sacrament. The
    doctors' common teaching is to be checked (e.g. Aquinas, *ST* III q.81 a.4; LEDGER E06d-1), and so
    is Wyclif's place in *De incarnacione* (LEDGER E06d-2).

[^4]: **C/T/S.** *Intrinsecum … extrinsecum*: a head within the body (as man) and a head flowing in
    from without (as God), as at 123.26 (06-b); *taliter*: as intrinsic head. Print *Paulum* (127.9, plate);
    Jerome's addressees are Paula and Eustochium, and we give "Paula". The letter is not identified (Ep. 46, PL 22:483, does not have the
    passage) (LEDGER E06d-3).

[^5]: **R/T/C.** A margin *Responsio* (127.10). A A₁ lack *Deus*, B *resurgunt* (127.15).  The presents (*creat*, *vivunt*, *incarnatur*) are
    Wyclif's: each article is true "for its time", past or to come. *Utraque ecclesia*: the church of
    the old and of the new testament.

[^6]: **T/C.**  *In signo*: in the proposition taken as a
    sign, spoken or written; *utrobique*: in both churches (or "at both times"). The proposition is true
    not because the thing then is, but because it is true of its time.

[^7]: **C/S.** *Nec video quod*: we take it, "I do not see [how it could be granted] that Christ is man
    for that time". *Primus Adam secundum materiam et non secundum raciones seminales*: Christ was in
    Adam by his body's matter, not by seminal reasons; the *qualiter* clauses illustrate the seminal
    way, in which Levi "was yet in the loins of his father" (Heb. 7:10, DR; Loserth, 127.24, cites Heb.
    7:5) and all men were Adam. The distinction recalls Augustine on Christ's not paying tithes in
    Abraham (*De Gen. ad litt.* X.19.34, PL 34:423: *non est decimatus Christus, quia non erat in lumbis
    Abrahae … secundum carnem ibi fuisse*; LEDGER E06d-4). Anselm, *De conceptu virginali* c. 11
    (Loserth 127.27; LEDGER E06d-5): the words are not in c. 11 (PL 158:445A–446C); the nearest
    is *Omnis autem homo aut Adam est, aut de Adam* (446C). *Sed limitatum est*: we take the subject as
    "man" said of Christ, restricted to his humanity's individual existence; doubtful.

[^8]: **T/C.** *Primam instanciam*: the argument from ideal and potential being (126.15–23).
    *Deducendo pure creaturam*: taking the terms of the creature alone; "this" ranges over the eternal
    Word, so the inference fails. B *Concedit* (128.1); B *esse in persona* (128.5, its place unclear).
    *Mensura*: the measure of time. *Distrahens*: a qualifier that takes away from what it qualifies
    (as "dead" in "a dead man"), so that "being in potency" does not yield "being" (Glossary
    proposals). *Esse cause secunde*: the being Christ has in his causes, which is not his own.

[^9]: **C/T.** *Concedendo assumptum*: that a priest consecrating in the three days would consecrate
    Christ's body; for then his body and soul, though parted, were each Christ's. *Non pro illo tempore
    est Christus*: Christ does not exist for that time. The page foot (128.16) reads "In cod. A₁
    spec"ra; A B: speculum" (OCR rough): the manuscripts appear corrupt where the print has
    *specificum*; we translate the print.

[^10]: **S/C.** Isa. 26:1, "a wall and a bulwark shall be set therein" (DR). *Caput racionis duplicis*:
    head under two aspects, as God and as man. *Ad auctoritates*: the scriptures and Jerome of 127.8–9.
    *Ipsi*: the fathers of the old testament.

[^11]: **R/C.** A margin *Tercia difficultas* (128.29). *Aliis partibus corruptis continue generatis*:
    printed with one *aliis*; we read "some parts corrupted, [others] continually generated". The
    difficulty is the house of 123.17–22 (06-b).

[^12]: **C/T.** *Est dare*: there is such a thing as; *interpolate*: with interruption. *Existente
    secundum totam aliquam fuit illa que permansit* does not construe as printed (no noun for *existente
    … aliquam*, and *fuit* stands unconnected); we give the likeliest sense, that the house remains
    while some part persists through the replacements (Latin doubts). The *146b* folio mark at 129.1 is
    not Wyclif's.

[^13]: **T/C.** A *ad corrumpcionem*; A margin *Nota* (129.6). *Mensurat … utrumque motum*: the fire,
    itself successive, measures both its generation and its corruption. *Quando consecrande forent
    ecclesie*: when a church renewed part by part would need consecrating again. *Non est vis de*: it
    is of no weight. *Persone incolentis*: the person who dwells in the church, whether minister or
    faithful; doubtful.

[^14]: **R/C.** A margin *Quinta obieccio* (129.12). *Confundi*: since no one could know to whom honour
    and dues are owed. *Corporalis debiti*: dues in temporal goods. *Illud*: the proof of predestination
    or holiness.

[^15]: **R/S.** A margin *Responsio* (129.18), *Nota processum* (129.20). *Operibus debet credi*: John
    10:38, "believe the works" (DR). *Illis dimissis*: the works set aside; *ut sic*: as such, without
    the works.

[^16]: **T/S/C.** B lacks *attendat* (129.22). *In suis primitivis*: in its first days, the primitive
    church (or "its first members"). Isa. 3:12 agrees with the Vulgate except *eius* for *eis*; DR's
    "oppressors" renders *exactores*, the word Wyclif takes up below ("exactors of money"). A margin
    *Is. Illa* (129.25).

[^17]: **T/C.** A A₁ lack *sunt* (129.29); A margin *Avaricia cleri* (129.31). At 130.1 the codd. read
    *sumus*, which Loserth corrected (presumably *dediti sunt mundo*); we translate his text.
    *Preponderantes tradiciones humanas*: making human traditions outweigh God's law. *Dediti sunt*
    with *olim* (130.1–2): "were given", as the series requires.

[^18]: **T/S/R.** B *extorquent ut vocentur* (130.8) for *retorquent, ut nominentur*; A margin *papa*
    (130.9) at *servos servorum Dei*, the papal style. *Permissione divina*: the style of a bishop's
    title; against Gal. 1:1, "not of men, neither by man" (DR). *Patris mendacii*: John 8:44, the devil
    "is a liar, and the father thereof" (DR). We take *in opere* and *in nomine reali* both with *servi
    precipui*.

[^19]: **S/C.** *Propheciam scripture*: not identified; cf. 1 Tim. 4:2, "Speaking lies in hypocrisy"
    (DR). *Non electi*: *electi*, "the elect", not *predestinati*. Loserth (130.16): *De vita
    christiana* c. 6, in the appendix to Augustine's works (*Opp.* VI, 186; LEDGER E06d-6); PL 40:1036–37; it
    is not Augustine's (PL: *auctor incertus*) (LEDGER E06d-7).
