# *De eucharistia*, chapter 3, part 2: the modern doctors' straits over the conversion

## ¶1 · 57.28–58.7

> [p. 57] Unde hic angustiantur moderni doctores, cum quibus signis vere exprimerent veritatem huius sacramenti; ut negant quod panis est, fuit vel erit corpus [p. 58] Christi, negant eciam quod panis poterit esse corpus Christi, et tercio negant quod ex pane fiet corpus Christi, cum hoc implicat panem cedere in corpus Christi et ipsum noviter generari; ideo dicunt quod panis transsubstanciabitur in corpus Christi, panis convertetur vel mutabitur in corpus Christi et (ut quibusdam placet) ex pane fiet corpus Christi.

Hence here the [modern doctors]{g:moderni-doctores}[^1] are in straits as to the signs with which they might [truly]{g:vere} express the truth of this [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}: as they deny "[bread]{g:panis} is, was or will be the [body [58] of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}," they deny also "bread will be able to be the body of Christ," and thirdly they deny "from bread the body of Christ will be made," since this implies that bread [gives way]{g:cedere} into the body of Christ and that this is newly generated; so they say "bread will be [transubstantiated]{g:transsubstanciacio} into the body of Christ," "bread will be [converted]{g:conversio} or changed into the body of Christ," and (as it pleases some) "from bread the body of Christ will be made."[^2]

## ¶2 · 58.8–59.3

> [p. 58] Unde sanctus Thomas super IVᵒ, distinccione XIᵃ, articulo IIᵒ glosat beatum Ambrosium in libro suo de sacramentis et ponitur in De Consecracione, distinccione IIᵃ, Panis est in altari: Hoc est, dicit Thomas, quod est sub speciebus panis, primo fuit panis et postmodum corpus Christi. Sed Magister IVᵒ Sentenciarum, distinccione Xᵃ, capitulo IIIᵒ glosat melius talia dicta sanctorum quod utuntur tropo quodam, quo solent signa suorum signatorum sortiri vocabula. Nam ante consecracionem non fuit panis ille sic corpus Christi, sed virtute verborum Christi fit signum sic efficax; et sic intelligitur quod panis convertitur in corpus Christi, transsubstanciatur vel quocunque termino mutacionis exprimatur. Aliter tamen intelligunt moderni doctores sed non docent, ymmo se ipsos intricantes sibimet contradicunt. In omnibus enim istis angustiantur de subiecto mutacionis, cum repugnat panem et vinum expectare motum talem, ut inquiunt. Angustiantur eciam in declaracione triplicis termini supradicti et quid ponit illa conversio ultra hoc quod poneret panis anichilacio [p. 59] et corporis Christi sub illis accidentibus sacramentaliter successio. Ad tantum enim peioratur universitas corporum sensibilium utrobique.

Hence Saint Thomas, on [Book] IV, distinction 11, article 2, [glosses]{g:glosare} blessed Ambrose in his book *On the Sacraments*, and it is placed in *De consecratione*, distinction 2, *Panis est in altari*: "That is," says Thomas, "what is under the [species]{g:species} of bread was first bread and afterwards the body of Christ."[^3] But the [Master]{g:magister}, [Book] IV of the *Sentences*, distinction 10, chapter 3, glosses such sayings of the [saints]{g:sancti} better: that they use a certain trope, by which [signs]{g:signum} are wont to take the names of the things they signify.[^4] For before the consecration that bread was not in this way the body of Christ, but by the [power]{g:virtus} of Christ's words it becomes a sign thus efficacious; and in this way it is understood that bread is converted into the body of Christ, is transubstantiated, or by whatever term of change it be expressed. Yet the modern doctors understand otherwise, but they do not teach [it]; indeed, entangling themselves, they contradict themselves. For in all these they are in straits about the [subject]{g:subiectum} of the change, since it is [incompatible]{g:repugnare} that bread and [wine]{g:vinum} should await such a motion, as they say.[^5] They are in straits also in [making clear]{g:declarare} the threefold term mentioned above, and what that conversion posits beyond what the [annihilation]{g:anichilacio} of the bread [59] and the succession of the body of Christ [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} under those [accidents]{g:accidens} would posit. For the totality of [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} bodies is [made worse]{g:peiorare} to the same extent on both sides.[^6]

## ¶3 · 59.4–60.14

> [p. 59] Item, manifeste videtur secundum istos doctores quod in conversione hostie est dare duos motus natura priores, scilicet desicionem panis secundum se totam et presencie corporis Christi inchoacionem secundum se totam; et illa conversio vel est illa duo vel ad illa formaliter consequens ut passio ad subiectum; cum ergo deficit subiectum illius conversionis nec illa sit de numero accidencium que potuerunt per se esse sed naturam corruptam mutari, videtur quod illa conversio sit aggregatum ex illis duobus motibus; cum ergo prior illorum sit idem motus qui foret, si de possibili careret concomitancia aliorum, videtur quod sicut tunc panis anichilaretur sic et modo. Conversio enim cum sit mocio positiva, requirit existenciam essencie subiecti, quia aliter ipsum non plus converteretur quam anichilata essencia, cui succederet corpus novum. Sic autem (ut inquiunt) posset esse de Dei potencia absoluta, nec sompnium nec racio docet adversarios oppositum, quare ergo sic non ponunt? et cum anichilacio non sit per accidens anichilacio, videtur quod talis desicio foret anichilacio quolibet motu alio comitante; nam conversio vel presencie corporis Christi acquisicio, cum foret accidentalis illi desicioni, videtur quod non tollit sibi speciem aut quidditatem; et hoc videntur sic opinantes qui negant composicionem continui ex non quantis admittere de possibili illam desicionem panis pro uno instanti et illam conversionem immediate post succedere, quia aliter videtur quod pro [p. 60] instanti quo foret uterque motus remaneret panis nondum conversus, et per consequens conversio foret futura; sed hoc implicat duo instancia esse inmediata, sicut via que dicit quod est dare primum instans in quo angeli ceciderunt et ultimum instans in quo steterunt, sed maior pars difficultatis foret intelligibiliter declarare quod panis transit, convertitur vel cedit corpori Christi, cum iuxta hoc concedentes possibile est substanciam panis anichilari sive extra sive intra dicta accidencia; ideo preter desicionem panis et incepcionem presencie corporis Christi ponunt conversionem omnimode separatam; sed illam nesciunt omnes loquentes exponere; nec docetur in fide scripture vel simbolo ecclesie.

Again, it plainly seems according to these doctors that in the conversion of the [host]{g:hostia} [one may posit]{g:est-dare} two motions prior by nature, namely the [ceasing]{g:desicio} of the bread according to its whole self and the beginning of the presence of the body of Christ according to its whole self; and that conversion either is those two or is formally consequent on them, as an [attribute]{g:passio} on its subject. Since therefore the subject of that conversion fails, and it is not among the number of accidents that could exist [of themselves]{g:per-se} but be changed with the nature corrupted,[^7] it seems that that conversion is an aggregate of those two motions; since therefore the prior of them is the same motion that it would be if, as is possible, the [concomitance]{g:concomitancia} of the others were lacking, it seems that, just as the bread would then be annihilated, so too now. For conversion, since it is a positive motion, requires the existence of the essence of the subject, because otherwise this would no more be converted than an annihilated essence to which a new body succeeded. Now thus (as they say) it could be by God's [absolute power]{g:potencia-absoluta}, nor does dream or reason teach the adversaries the opposite: why therefore do they not posit it so? And since annihilation is not annihilation [by accident]{g:per-accidens}, it seems that such a ceasing would be annihilation whatever other motion accompanied it; for conversion, or the acquiring of the presence of the body of Christ, since it would be accidental to that ceasing, seems not to take from it its species or quiddity. And those who opine so and deny the composition of the continuum from non-[quantified]{g:quantus} [parts] seem to admit this: that it is possible for that ceasing of the bread to be at one instant and that conversion to succeed [without intermediary]{g:immediate} after,[^8] because otherwise it seems that at the [60] instant at which both motions would be, the bread would remain not yet converted, and consequently the conversion would be future; but this implies that two instants are without intermediary [to each other], as [does] the way that says one may posit a first instant in which the angels fell and a last instant in which they stood. But the greater part of the difficulty would be to make clear intelligibly that bread passes, is converted or gives way to the body of Christ, since according to those who grant this it is possible for the [substance]{g:substancia} of bread to be annihilated either outside or inside the said accidents; so, besides the ceasing of the bread and the [beginning]{g:incepcio} of the presence of the body of Christ, they posit a conversion separate in every way; but this all the speakers do not know how to expound;[^9] nor is it taught in the faith of scripture or in the creed of the [church]{g:ecclesia}.

## ¶4 · 60.15–61.2

> [p. 60] In natura autem, quando unum corpus convertitur in aliud corpus in quod essencia prioris corporis convertitur, manet essencialiter corpus conversum; ideo corpus sic desinens non convertitur in sua accidencia nec accidencia noviter creata, quia sic posset omnis substancia creata converti in punctum sine anichilacione, cum conversio non dicit melioritatem conversi, et tunc illo puncto anichilato totus mundus converteretur in nichilum et tamen nec ante priorem conversionem nec post; ymmo subtiliores doctores istius secte asserunt quod foret in ista transsubstanciacione anichilacio, nisi panis aliquitas remaneret in carne Christi in quam fit conversio. In cuius testimonium dicunt quod Deus potest de hostia corpus suum sacramentaliter subtrahere et ipsam in naturam eiusdem panis revertere; sed certum est quod non remaneret sic panis aliquitas, nisi remaneret quod [p. 61] panis est aliquid et sic panis, cui accidit quod corpus Christi est sacramentaliter ibi presens.

Now in [nature]{g:natura}, when one body is converted into another body, into which the essence of the prior body is converted, the converted body remains essentially; so a body ceasing in this way is not converted into its accidents nor into newly created accidents, because thus every created substance could be converted into a point without annihilation, since conversion does not imply the bettering of the thing converted; and then, that point being annihilated, the whole world would be converted into nothing, and yet [it would be annihilated] neither before the prior conversion nor after.[^10] Indeed, the subtler doctors of this sect assert that there would be annihilation in this transubstantiation, unless some [somethingness]{g:aliquitas} of the bread remained in the flesh of Christ into which the conversion is made. In witness of this they say that God can sacramentally withdraw his body from the host and turn it back into the nature of the same bread; but it is certain that a somethingness of the bread would not remain in this way unless "[61] bread is something" remained [true], and so bread, to which it [befalls]{g:accidens} that the body of Christ is sacramentally present there.[^11]

## ¶5 · 61.3–61.13

> [p. 61] Alii autem stulte ponunt quod omne desinens convertitur in omne remanens; et sic affirmacio posset converti in negacionem et tolleretur possibilitas anichilacionis; quod negant, sicut et illi qui dicunt quod omne anichilatum convertitur in Deum secundum suum esse supremum, et omne corruptum convertitur in non esse panis aut convertitur in non esse secundum se totum, ut dicitur; nec docetur quod convertitur in corpus Christi, ex hoc quod corpus Christi succedit sub illis accidentibus ibi presens; nec verba sacramentalia illud docent, ut patebit inferius.

But others foolishly posit "everything ceasing is converted into everything remaining"; and so an affirmation could be converted into a negation, and the possibility of annihilation would be taken away; which they deny,[^12] as do those also who say "everything annihilated is converted into God according to its supreme [being]{g:esse}," and "everything corrupted is converted into the not-being of bread, or is converted into not-being according to its whole self," as is said; nor is it taught that it is converted into the body of Christ from the fact that the body of Christ succeeds under those accidents, present there; nor do the sacramental words teach that, as will be clear below.

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 57.28: Difficulty of finding language to express the modern doctrine.
- 59.4: The modern doctors require two movements: the cessation of the bread, the beginning of the presence of the Body.
- 59.10: These are separate movements, independent of each other, whereas conversion, as a positive movement, requires a subject in which it can exist continuously.
- 60.15: Some say that something of the bread (aliquitas panis) remains, so that God may withdraw His body and the host return to bread.
- 61.3: Various fancies as to what becomes of that which is annihilated.

## Notes

[^1]: **T.** *Moderni doctores* is Loserth's reading after C's correction; the manuscripts have *modernorum* (57.28). BE lack *vere* (57.29), and A has *vere exprimerent* in the margin by another hand.

[^2]: **C.** The propositions are given as quoted forms, since their sense is under discussion. The third form, denied in the first list (*ex pane fiet corpus Christi*), is the one "some" admit in the second: the Latin sets the two against each other. *Ipsum noviter generari*: *ipsum* is the body of Christ.

[^3]: **S.** Thomas, *In IV Sent.* d. 11 q. 1 a. 4 qc. 1 ad 1, which Wyclif quotes verbatim (*quod est sub speciebus panis, primo fuit panis, et postea corpus Christi*); his *articulo II°* is wrong (Loserth: Opp. X, 167) (LEDGER K03b-1), glossing Ambrose, *De sacramentis* (IV.4.14–16, PL 16, 439B–441A: *Panis iste panis est ante verba sacramentorum; ubi accesserit consecratio, de pane fit caro Christi*) (LEDGER K03b-2), as received in Gratian, *De cons.* D. 2 c. 55 *Panis est in altari* (Loserth: *cap. LV*; the modern form is ours). The canon ends with the words Thomas expounds, *quod erat panis ante consecrationem iam corpus Christi est post consecrationem* (LEDGER K03b-3).

[^4]: **S.** Peter Lombard, *Sent.* IV d. 10 (Loserth: Lib. IV dist. X, ed. Col. 1676, fol. 35; c. 3, as Wyclif says, LEDGER K03b-4). Wyclif follows its wording closely: *tropo quodam utitur hic Aug., quo solent res significantes rerum sortiri vocabula quas significant* (PL 192, 860). So the rule that signs take the names of the things signified is the Lombard's reading of Augustine.

[^5]: **C.** *Expectare motum talem*: "await", that is, stand as the subject that undergoes such a motion; bread and wine cannot, on the modern view, since they do not survive the change.

[^6]: **C.** *Ad tantum … utrobique*: the conversion as they describe it costs the world of bodies exactly as much as plain annihilation followed by succession; *utrobique* refers to these two accounts.

[^7]: **C.** *Nec illa sit de numero accidencium que potuerunt per se esse sed naturam corruptam mutari*: the clause after *sed* is compressed. We take *mutari* as passive, "be changed", with the corrupted nature as its occasion. ABC read *naturam corpoream* (59.12). See Latin doubts.

[^8]: **C.** *Immediate post*: with no instant between; the argument that follows turns on whether two instants can be *inmediata*, which those who deny that the continuum is made of indivisibles cannot allow. DE read *succedere post* (59.32).

[^9]: **C.** *Illam nesciunt omnes loquentes exponere*: the quantifier stands before the negative verb in the Latin. The sense is that no one who speaks of this conversion can expound it, not that only some fail.

[^10]: **C.** *Et tamen nec ante priorem conversionem nec post*: the verb is to be supplied; we supply "it would be annihilated", from *sine anichilacione* just before. The world would come to nothing without ever being annihilated.

[^11]: **C.** *Aliquitas* "somethingness": the abstract of *aliquid*, which the next clause explains (*panis est aliquid*). *Cui accidit*: the body's presence is an accident befalling the bread that remains; the verb is that of *accidens*.

[^12]: **C.** *Quod negant*: "which" is the removal of the possibility of annihilation; the absurdity follows from their premiss, and they deny it.
