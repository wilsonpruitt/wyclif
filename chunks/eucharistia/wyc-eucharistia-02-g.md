# *De eucharistia*, chapter 2, part 7: the definition of transubstantiation challenged, and the danger of idolatry

## ¶1 · 51.18–53.4

> [p. 51] Ulterius quantum ad descripcionem transsubstanciacionis, videtur quod capit calumpniam, supponendo (ut adversarii fatentur) quod illa conversio non ponit anichilacionem nec ydemptificacionem panis cum corpore Christi; nam omne accidens oportet habere subiectum, omnis talis transubstanciacio est accidens, ergo omnem talem oportet habere subiectum; licet autem fateantur accidens esse sine subiecto, tamen dicunt quod ipsum oportet habere subiectum in principio, ymmo (ut infecte loquendo dicunt) quod ipsum est subiectum ad quemlibet sui punctum, non ergo est sine subiecto, ymmo omne accidens respectivum cuiusmodi est omnis mutacio, concedunt non [p. 52] posse esse sine subiecto suo, ergo oportet dari aliquid quod subicitur transsubstanciacioni passive; illud non est accidens, quia ipsum manet non transsubstanciatum, nec est panis vel vinum, quia, cum oportet transsubstanciacionem illam esse fundatam, ne cogamur concedere corpus Christi vel hostiam successive confici, patet quod pro instanti transsubstanciacionis nec panis transsubstanciatus nec vinum est secundum aliquam sui partem, et per consequens nullum accidens tunc subiectat; illud deductum est in tractatu De Anichilacione movisse philosophos ad ponendum materiam primam generacioni subiectam, quia fingere unam rem prius inauditam sine fundamento foret illusio; ponens ergo transsubstanciacionem diceret quid est, ubi est et quomodo sustentatur; cum enim sit forma, oportet quod aliquod subiectum informet et pulcrificet, cum tam miraculose producitur. Unde licet quondam laboraverim ad describendum transsubstanciacionem concorditer ad sensum prioris ecclesie, tamen modo videtur michi quod contrariantur, posteriori ecclesia oberrante. Si enim transsubstanciacio sit cessio unius substancie alteri quoad locum, sic quod una substancia transsubstanciata remanet per eundem locum quo prius, et substancia dignior sit per illum locum sacramentaliter cui subordinatur substancia prior ut signum, tunc contingit quod panis transsubstancietur pro instanti quo non mutetur, sed remanet panis subiectus accidentibus post consecracionem; quod dicitur repugnare sentencie decretalis; ideo dimitto nunc concordancias legum istarum et voco transsubstanciacionem conversionem [p. 53] unius substancie in aliam, ut semen convertitur in corpus vivum, homo convertitur in terram, et sic generaliter quando unum corpus ex alio generatur.

Further, as to the [definition]{g:descripcio} of [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio}, it seems that it is [open to challenge]{g:calumpnia}, supposing (as the adversaries admit) that that [conversion]{g:conversio} posits neither [annihilation]{g:anichilacio} nor [identification]{g:ydemptificacio} of the [bread]{g:panis} with the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi};[^1] for every [accident]{g:accidens} must have a [subject]{g:subiectum}; every such transubstantiation is an accident; therefore every such [transubstantiation] must have a subject. And although they admit that an accident is without a subject, yet they say that it must have a subject at the beginning; indeed (as they say, speaking imperfectly) that it is subjected at every point of itself; therefore it is not without a subject.[^2] Indeed, every [relational]{g:respectivus} accident, of which kind is every change, they grant cannot [52] be without its subject; therefore something must be given which is subjected to transubstantiation passively. That is not an accident, because it remains not transubstantiated; nor is it the bread or the [wine]{g:vinum}, because, since that transubstantiation must be founded, lest we be forced to grant that the body of Christ or the [host]{g:hostia} is [consecrated]{g:conficere} successively, it is clear that at the instant of transubstantiation neither the transubstantiated bread nor the wine is, according to any part of itself, and consequently it [is then the subject of]{g:subiectare} no accident.[^3] This, as was deduced in the treatise *On Annihilation*, moved the philosophers to posit [prime matter]{g:materia-prima} as subject to generation, because to [feign]{g:fingere} one thing previously unheard of, without foundation, would be [illusion]{g:illusio}. So whoever posits transubstantiation should say what it is, where it is and how it is sustained; for since it is a form, it must [inform]{g:informare} and beautify some subject, since it is produced so miraculously.[^4] Hence, although I once laboured to [define]{g:describere} transubstantiation in accord with the [meaning]{g:sensus} of the earlier [church]{g:ecclesia}, yet now it seems to me that they are contrary, the later church having strayed.[^5] For if transubstantiation is a giving-way of one [substance]{g:substancia} to another as to place, such that the one substance, transubstantiated, remains through the same place as before, and the worthier substance is through that place [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter}, to which the prior substance is subordinated as a [sign]{g:signum}, then it happens that the bread is transubstantiated at an instant at which it is not changed, but bread remains subject to the accidents after the consecration; which is said to [be incompatible]{g:repugnare} with the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of the [decretal]{g:decretalis}. Therefore I now let go the concordances of these laws, and I call transubstantiation the conversion [53] of one substance into another, as seed is converted into a living body, man is converted into earth, and so generally when one body is generated from another.[^6]

## ¶2 · 53.5–53.24

> [p. 53] Ulterius quoad ydolatriam patet quod populus est instruendus ne credat illud accidentatum quod sentit esse ydemptice corpus Christi; et hoc est necessarium secundum omnem opinionem istius materie, quia ecclesia posterior fatetur quod remanet unum album, rotundum et durum, quod non potest esse corpus Christi; et revera cum illud sit per se sensibile et per consequens populus est ita pronus vel pronior ad credendum illud esse corpus Christi ut panem, patet quod non remanet illis punctus coloris ad putandum panem desinere secundum quamlibet eius partem, ne adorando panem ut corpus Christi ydolatria committatur; per idem enim tolleretur quodcunque accidens sensibile, ymmo modo superadditur illusio de subiecto et accidente suo; et maior pars populi credit infideliter et irreverencius quod illud remanens quod ipsi ponunt accidens sit ydemptice corpus Christi. Constat quidem quod accidens est magis extraneum a natura corporis Christi vel sanguinis, quam natura panis aut vini. De transsubstanciacione autem patet diffuse posterius.

Further, as to [idolatry]{g:ydolatria}, it is clear that the people must be instructed not to believe that that [thing subject to accidents]{g:accidentatus} which it [perceives]{g:sentire} is [identically]{g:ydemptice} the body of Christ;[^7] and this is necessary according to every [opinion]{g:opinio} on this matter, because the later church admits that there remains a white, round and hard thing, which cannot be the body of Christ. And [in truth]{g:revera}, since that is [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} [of itself]{g:per-se}, and consequently the people is as prone or more prone to believe that it is the body of Christ as to believe the bread is, it is clear that there remains to them no point of [colour]{g:color} for thinking that the bread ceases according to every part of it, lest by adoring the bread as the body of Christ idolatry be committed;[^8] for by the same token any perceptible accident would be taken away; indeed, now an illusion about the subject and its accident is added over and above; and the greater part of the people believes [faithlessly]{g:infideliter} and more irreverently that that remaining thing, which they posit [as] an accident, is identically the body of Christ. It is evident indeed that an accident is more foreign to the [nature]{g:natura} of the body or blood of Christ than the nature of bread or wine. And about transubstantiation it is clear at length later.[^9]

## Notes

[^1]: **G/T.** *Descripcio*: "definition", the decretists' definition set out at 48.8–22. *Capit calumpniam*: "is open to challenge", the disputant's formal objection. *Ydemptificacionem panis cum corpore Christi*: the bread made one and the same thing with the body. D reads *substaciacio* (51.23).
[^2]: **C/T.** *Infecte loquendo*: "speaking imperfectly" (*infectus*, unfinished; it can also mean tainted); D reads *infecte loco*, and C has *logice*, "logically", in another hand. *Ipsum est subiectum ad quemlibet sui punctum*: the accident is in a subject at every point of its duration, so it is not without one; E reads *esse subiectum*. C has *sine* and *est* in another hand; D lacks *est*; D reads *respicit tantum eiusmodi est*; C *contendunt* for *concedunt*.
[^3]: **C/T.** The argument: what transubstantiation inheres in is neither an accident (the accidents remain untransubstantiated) nor the bread or wine, which on the adversaries' view is not, in any part, at the instant of change; *est* here is "is" in the sense of existing. *Fundatam*: grounded in a subject. *Ne cogamur … successive confici*: the change must be instantaneous. ABCD read *dare* for *dari*; D *quod manet*.
[^4]: **S/T.** *Tractatus De Anichilacione*: Wyclif's own treatise on annihilation, where this argument for prime matter as the subject of generation is said to be deduced: *De ente* II tr. 6 (*De potentia productiva Dei ad extra*) c. 12, ed. Dziewicki (1909), p. 289.12–15, *istud argumentum induxit philosophos ad racione convincendum materiam primam, quia antecedenter non esset dare subiectum generacionis et corrupcionis* (LEDGER K02g-1). *Pulcrificet*: "beautify", as a form adorns its subject. D reads *inauditam prius*.
[^5]: **R/C.** Side-note 52.18: Wyclif records that he has changed his definition. Loserth refers to Lechler, *Johann von Wiclif* I, 614, and to F. D. Matthew, "The date of Wyclif's attack on transubstantiation", *English Historical Review*, April 1890 (LEDGER K02g-2). *Contrariantur*: the subject is unexpressed, taken as the definition and the earlier church's meaning. *Sensum*: "meaning", the earlier church's understanding.
[^6]: **C/T.** *Cessio … quoad locum*: transubstantiation as one substance giving way to another in place (cf. *cedere*, "give way"); on this account the bread is transubstantiated without being changed, which is said to be incompatible with the decretal's teaching (*Una vero*, 48.1–4). *Sentencie*: "teaching" (CONTEXTUAL *sentencia*). *Concordancias legum istarum*: the attempts to make the decree and the decretal agree, which Wyclif now lets go. C has *per eundem — cui* in the margin in another hand; D reads *cum subordinatur*, *contingat*, and *remaneret* twice.
[^7]: **T/C.** ABC lack *ydemptice*, which leaves the prohibition without its mode. *Illud accidentatum quod sentit*: the thing subject to accidents that the people perceives. D reads *remaneret* (53.9); C has *esse* in another hand.
[^8]: **G/C.** *Punctus coloris*: "a point of colour", not the least plausible ground (*color* in the period sense). The adversaries' reason for the bread's ceasing, that otherwise it would be adored (48.20–22), gives them none, since the white, round, hard thing that remains is as open to being adored. ABCE lack *et revera*.
[^9]: **T.** ABCE read *ut irreverencius*; ABC lack *sit ydemptice — accidens*; D reads *quod tantum*; DE *et vini*. D reads *vero patet*, AB lack *autem*, C has *autem patet* in another hand; ABC add *etc.* after *posterius*.
