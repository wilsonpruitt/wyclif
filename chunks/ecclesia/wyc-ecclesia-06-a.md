# *De ecclesia*, chapter 6, part 1: objections answered: many heads, virgin and bride, the church as nothing

## ¶1 · 116.2–118.5

> [p. 116] Sed adhuc instatur finaliter deducendo ad conformia inconveniencia ad qualia adversarii deducuntur. Primo enim videtur quod ecclesia tamquam monstrum habeat mille capita, et sic de aliis membris monstruose fabricatis, et sic foret simul virgo et corruptissima synagoga et Christi ecclesia. Hic dicitur (ut sepe) negando assumptum; nam non sequitur: Ista capita sunt istius, demonstrando sanctam matrem ecclesiam, ergo sunt capita istius, nec sequitur: Ista sunt capita istorum qui sunt ista ecclesia, ergo sunt capita huius ecclesie, quia sunt capita istorum singillatim. Et tamen nullum eorum est caput istorum. Ad hoc ergo quod sint capita ecclesie, oportet quod ecclesia habeat illa omnia et singula tamquam eius capita. Cum enim caput iuxta Aristotelem dicatur ad aliquid, patet sophistis quod non sequitur: Hoc caput, hic pater vel hoc membrum est huiusmodi, ergo est caput, pater vel membrum illius, nec sequitur: Hoc est caput naturale huius capitanei, existentis caput date ecclesie, ergo hoc est caput illius ecclesie. Non enim oportet equivocando in capite, si hoc sit caput capitis, tunc hoc est caput cuiuscunque capitati ab illo capite, et correspondenter de aliis membris. Quelibet enim pars predestinati potest dici pars corporis ecclesie, nullum tamen est membrum ecclesie nisi persona predestinata, et omnis talis est membrum ecclesie sicut proporcionaliter dicendum est in humano corpore. Et patet quod ad noticiam illius materie de sancta matre ecclesia nedum oportet scire theologiam et iura canonica, sed omnino logicam, phisicam et ethicam que omnes subserviunt [p. 117] matri nostre. Sicut enim non sequitur: Pater in divinis generat, et ipse est natura divina, ergo ipsa generat, ita non sequitur: Ista persona capitatur istis capitibus, et ista persona est hec ecclesia, ergo hec ecclesia sic capitatur. Nam ecclesia dicit abstractive multitudinem fidelium secundum racionem qua uniunturin capite; ad quem sensum ecclesia non potest esse non ecclesia vel dampnari. Ecclesia ergo non est monstruose composita. Sed quoad secundum, conceditur quod ecclesia est virgo, cum sit sponsa virginis Jesu Christi ex qua ut vera matre spiritualiter procreamur, et sic est castissima secundum se totam in patria; hic tamen, fornicando cum adulterante diabolo et sic membris eius, multis criminibus parcialiter est corrupta (ut patet ex fide scripture Ezechielis XVIII°). Verumptamen nunquam recipitur ut sponsa beatifice amplexanda in dextera in lecto sponsi, antequam fuerit pura virgo omnimode sine ruga; Christus enim est sponsus virginitatis. Et quantum ad tercium punctum, dicitur quod omnis sinagoga que est pars sancte matris ecclesie est ecclesia, non econtra. Et ideo tam crebro vocatur synagoga in veteri testamento ecclesia, sed non memini quod in novo testamento aliqua particularis ecclesia dicitur synagoga (cuius racio dicta est alibi). Secundo obicitur per hoc quod iuxta dicta sequitur eandem personam verbi esse filium, patrem et sponsum ecclesie; quod est contra leges tam nature quam ecclesie, cum nunquam desponsavit quis licite matrem suam. Hic dicitur concedendo conclusionem assumptam, cum Christus nedum desponsat universalem ecclesiam sed desponsacione perpetua quodlibet membrum eius. Et quantum ad leges allegandas in contrarium, patet quod [p. 118] loquuntur de contractu, de cognacione et desponsacione carnali, non autem spirituali, sicut debemus loqui in proposito et eo debemus eam magis attendere, quo est preciosior, proprior et in scriptura sacra expressior.

But it is still [objected]{g:instatur}, finally, by leading [us] to [absurdities]{g:inconveniencia}
of the same form as those to which [our] adversaries are led.[^1] For first it seems that the
[church]{g:ecclesia}, like a monster, would have a thousand heads, and so of the other members
monstrously fashioned; and so she would be at once a virgin and a most corrupt synagogue and the
church of Christ. Here [the answer is]{g:dicitur} (as often) by denying the
[assumption]{g:assumptum}; for it does not follow: "These heads are this one's" (pointing to holy
mother church), "therefore they are heads of this one"; nor does it follow: "These are the heads of
those who are this church, therefore they are heads of this church"; for they are heads of these
severally. And yet none of them is head of these [together].[^2] For them to be heads of the church,
therefore, the church must have them all and each as its heads. For since "head", according to
Aristotle, is said relatively, it is clear to sophists that it does not follow: "This head, this
father or this member is such a one's, therefore it is the head, father or member of that one"; nor
does it follow: "This is the natural head of this [captain]{g:capitaneus}, who is head of a given
church, therefore this is the head of that church."[^3] For it is not necessary, equivocating on
"head", that if this is the head of a head, then this is the head of whatever is [headed]{g:capitatus} by that head;
and correspondingly of the other members. For any part of a [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} [person]
can be called a part of the body of the church; yet nothing is a member of the church except a
predestinate person, and every such [person] is a member of the church, as is to be said
proportionally in the human body.[^4] And it is clear that for knowledge of this matter of holy
mother church one must know not only theology and the canon laws but altogether logic, physics and
ethics, which all serve [117] [our mother]{g:mater-nostra}. For just as it does not follow: "The
Father in divine things generates, and he is the divine nature, therefore it generates", so it does
not follow: "This person is headed by these heads, and this person is this church, therefore this
church is so headed."[^5] For "church" signifies abstractly the multitude of the faithful in the
respect in which they are united in the [head]{g:caput}; in which sense the church cannot be
not-church or be damned. The church therefore is not monstrously composed. But as to the second
[point], it is granted that the church is a virgin, since she is the [bride]{g:sponsa} of the virgin
Jesus Christ, and from her, as from a true mother, we are spiritually begotten; and so she is most
chaste as a whole in the [homeland]{g:patria}; here, however, committing fornication with the
adulterous devil, and so with his members, she is partly corrupted by many crimes (as is clear
[from the faith of scripture]{g:ex-fide}, Ezekiel 18).[^6] Nevertheless she is never received as
bride, to be embraced in blessedness at the right hand in the bridegroom's bed, before she is a pure
virgin in every way, without wrinkle; for Christ is the bridegroom of virginity.[^7] And as to the
third point, the answer is that every synagogue that is part of holy mother church is church, not
conversely. And therefore a synagogue is so often called church in the old testament; but I do not
remember that in the new testament any particular church is called a synagogue (the reason for which
has been given elsewhere).[^8] Secondly, [it is objected, by appeal to]{g:obicitur-per} this: that
according to what has been said it follows that the same person of the Word is son, father and
bridegroom of the church; which is against the laws both of nature and of the church, since no one
ever lawfully espoused his own mother. Here the answer is by granting the [conclusion]{g:conclusio}
assumed, since Christ espouses not only the universal church but, with a perpetual espousal, each
member of it. And as to the laws to be alleged to the contrary, it is clear that [118] they speak of
contract, of kinship and of espousal that is carnal, but not of spiritual, as we must speak in the
present matter; and we must attend to it the more, the more precious, the more proper and the more
explicit in holy scripture it is.[^9]

## ¶2 · 118.6–118.32

> [p. 118] Nam secundum illam omnes salvandi sunt filii Dei et veri fratres ex utroque parente, ymo secundum logicam Christi rectissimam Matthei XII°, 50: Quicunque filius regni voluntatem patris sui fecerit qui in celis est, ipse suus frater, soror et mater est. Et sic sine distinccione sexus spiritualis omnis christianus predestinatus, sive masculus sive femina, est frater, soror, mater et sponsa Christi. Ideo Canticorum V°, 1, dicitur ecclesie Veni in ortum meum soror mea sponsa. Ymo ecclesia est vere domus Dei, civitas regis iusticie, regnum celorum, ortus conclusus, fons signatus et alia que scriptura ad sensum misticum sibi attribuit. Nam in spiritualibus eadem substancia habet in se sine repugnancia quotlibet raciones vere earum denominaciones, ut exemplariter videre possumus quomodo contrariorum species et intenciones sunt simul in medio et in mente. Et ille qui ignorat hoc est valde indispositus ad cognoscendum catholice illud celeste coniugium. Nam Ysaie XXVI°, 1, dicitur de Christo et sancta ecclesia: Salvator ponetur in ea murus et antemurale. Christus enim secundum divinitatem inmediate complectitur sponsam suam, et secundum humanitatem qua minutus est paulominus ab angelis est antemurale, quia dux belli diro certamine superans hostes ecclesie, et sic de quotlibet racionibus quas omnes apostolus Iª Corinth. XV°, 28 compendiose complectitur, dicens quod Christus est omnia in omnibus.

For according to that [spiritual kinship] all who are to be saved are sons of God and true brothers on
both parents' side; indeed, according to Christ's most correct logic, Matthew 12:50: "Whatever son of
the kingdom shall do the will of his Father who is in heaven, he is his brother, sister and
mother."[^10] And so, without distinction of spiritual sex, every predestinate Christian, whether
male or female, is brother, sister, mother and bride of Christ. Therefore in Canticles 5:1 it is said
to the church: "I am come into my garden, O my sister, my spouse." Indeed the church is truly the
house of God, the city of the king of justice, the kingdom of heaven, a garden enclosed, a fountain
sealed up, and the other things that scripture attributes to her in the mystical sense.[^11] For in
spiritual things the same substance has in itself without repugnance any number of
[aspects]{g:racio}, truly their namings, as by way of example we can see how the species and
intentions of contraries are together in the medium and in the mind. And he who [is ignorant
of]{g:ignorare} this is very [unfit]{g:indispositus} to know in a catholic way that heavenly
marriage.[^12] For Isaiah 26:1 says of Christ and holy church: "A saviour, a wall and a bulwark shall
be set therein." For Christ according to his divinity embraces his bride immediately, and according
to his humanity, by which he was made a little less than the angels, he is the bulwark, because as
leader in war he overcomes the enemies of the church in dire combat; and so of any number of aspects,
all of which the apostle, 1 Corinthians 15:28, comprehends in brief, saying that "Christ is all in
all".[^13]

## ¶3 · 118.33–119.11

> [p. 118] Unde ignari scripture divertunt ad tales pueriles instancias, quando aliunde deficiunt argumenta et dicunt quod scriptura nostra est impossibilis in tantum [p. 119] quod quidam dicunt nullam esse ecclesiam catholicam. quia non est dare universalia ex parte rei. Quidam vero dicunt quod ecclesia est ecclesia, sed non est aliquid sed aliqua, nec aliquod est membrum ecclesie, et sic scriptura sacra de capite ecclesie, de anima eius et de aliis eius membris est manifeste impossibilis. Et sic videtur quod habent dicere communiter quod, sicut ecclesia nichil est, ita nichil valet, et sic Christus foret mercator stultissimus qui tradidit semet ipsum pro dicta ecclesia et nimis illuderet christianis de sponsalibus, de privilegiis et de attributis ecclesie.

Hence those ignorant of scripture turn aside to such childish [objections]{g:instancia} when arguments
fail them from elsewhere, and they say that our scripture is impossible, so much [119] that some say
there is no catholic church, because one cannot grant [universals]{g:universale} on the side of the
thing. But others say that the church is the church, but is not something but some things, nor is
anything a member of the church; and so holy scripture concerning the head of the church, its soul
and its other members is manifestly impossible.[^14] And so it seems they have to say commonly that,
as the church is nothing, so it is worth nothing; and so Christ would be a most foolish merchant, who
delivered himself up for the said church, and he would mock Christians too much with the espousals,
the privileges and the attributes of the church.[^15]

## ¶4 · 119.12–120.2

> [p. 119] Nam si ecclesia non sit ens vel aliquid, tunc non est verum nec bonum, et cum nichil valet nisi proporcionaliter ut est bonum, sequitur quod ecclesia nichil valet. Sequitur enim secundum rudimenta discipline: Utrumque istorum est bonum, deducendo duas particulares ecclesias, ergo utrumque istorum est res aliqua, et sic mille res forent una res que, licet raciocinetur, vivat bene, male, exercens actus humanos, subiectans accidencia velut substancia, tamen nichil est. Unde habet partes et est satis sensibilis, divisibilis et sic de aliis denominacionibus, sed nullum est caput vel membrum eius, nec aliquid est intra, extra vel pars ecclesie, cum nunquam incepit, incipit vel desinet. Et sic nedum domus materiales non manebunt post ablacionem minime partis materialis per auram vel avem, et sic oporteret indies de novo consecrare ecclesias, sed et communitates, ut conventus, abbathie, atque religio deficerent continue et innovarentur, ut quevis persona de talibus deficit, vel quomodolibet innovatur, et periret appropriacio temporalium, eo quod non superesset collegium cui temporalia sunt donata. Non enim iure hereditario carnali sed imitacione [p. 120] spirituali a qua forte degenerantur, debent tales elemosinas vendicare.

For if the church is not [a being]{g:ens} or something, then it is not true nor good; and since
nothing is worth anything except in proportion as it is good, [it follows]{g:sequitur} that the
church is worth nothing. For it follows according to the rudiments of the discipline: "Each of these
is good" (adducing two particular churches), "therefore each of these is some thing"; and so a
thousand things would be one thing which, although it reasons, lives well [or] ill, performs human
acts [and] underlies accidents as a substance does, yet is nothing.[^16] Hence it has parts and is
quite [perceptible]{g:sensibilis}, divisible, and so of the other namings; but nothing is its
head or member, nor is anything within, outside, or a part of the church, since it never began,
begins, or will cease. And so not only will material houses not remain after the removal of the least
material part by the wind or a bird, so that churches would have to be consecrated anew daily, but
communities too, such as convents, abbeys and a religious order, would continually fail and be
renewed as any person of them fails or is in any way renewed; and the
[appropriation]{g:appropriacio} of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} would perish, in that the college to
which the temporal goods were given would not survive.[^17] For not by [right]{g:ius} of fleshly
inheritance but by spiritual [120] imitation, from which perhaps they degenerate, ought such [men] to
claim [alms]{g:elemosina}.[^18]

## ¶5 · 120.3–120.13

> [p. 120] Item, iuxta dicta totum genus humanum est unus homo, divisus in duos homines, et uterque eorum habet Deum et dominum Jesum Christum pro capite, ergo uterque eorum est corpus domini, et per consequens, ille totus homo foret corpus domini et sic sine racione reprobaret Augustinus Ticonium de corpore domini bipertito. Nec est evidencia quod exercitus vel populus facit quidquid facit pars eius, quin per idem ille magnus homo foret simul corpus Christi et corpus diaboli, salvatus et dampnatus, quia secundum partes, ergo etc.

Again, according to what has been said, the whole human race is one man, divided into two men, and
each of them has God and the Lord Jesus Christ for head; therefore each of them is the body of the
Lord, and consequently that whole man would be the body of the Lord; and so Augustine would without
reason reprove Tyconius concerning the bipartite body of the Lord.[^19] Nor is there
[evidence]{g:evidencia} that an army or a people does whatever a part of it does, without that great
man, by the same token, being at once the body of Christ and the body of the devil, saved and damned,
since [he is so] according to [his] parts; therefore etc.[^20]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 116.2: 1. We have to solve the difficulties that we have raised against our adversaries
- 116.7: that the Church is a monster, having a thousand heads, and that she is a virgin and a corrupt synagogue, and Christ's Church.
- 116.19: The difficulty as to heads rests on an equivocation. A head may belong to the Church without being the head of the Church.
- 117.9: 2. The Church is a virgin, polluted now by sins of her members, but to be purified for final glory.
- 117.19: 3. Every synagogue that is a part of Holy Church is a Church.
- 117.26: It is objected that Christ would be at once son, father ind husband of the Church.
- 118.1: There is no reason why such relations should not exist spiritually;
- 118.13: as the Church is truly all the things that she is called in Scripture.
- 119.1: Some say that the Catholic Church (as an universal) has no real existence.
- 119.12: If the Church is nothing it cannot be good.
- 119.17: Any local church may be something and so a thousand somethings may make up one which, in spite of all it does and is, we are to call nothing.
- 119.26: By similar reasoning we might conclude a house or a corporation has no continuous existence, since the parts vary.
- 120.3: Objection: If a whole is all that its parts are, man is at once the body of Christ and the body of the devil.

## Notes

[^1]: **T/R/C.** The *to* at the head of 116.2 is not Wyclif's (a line number or the side-note's
    numeral) and is not translated. A margin *Prima obieccio* (116.3). *Inconveniencia*: absurdities in
    argument (CONTEXTUAL, GLOSSARY). The objector turns on Wyclif the method of 112–115 (05-f): he
    reduces Wyclif's account to absurdities like those Wyclif drew from the doctor's definition.

[^2]: **C/T/R.** A margin *Responsio* (116.7). *Istius … istius*: the same genitive twice, first of
    belonging (heads found in the church, the heads of its members), then of headship over it.
    *Singillatim* A B, A₁ *sigillatim* (116.12). *Istorum* in the last clause: of these taken
    together.

[^3]: **T/S/C.** B *de
    sophistis* (116.16); B *membrum est huius* (116.17–18); the print has *huiusmodi* ("of this sort"),
    which gives the inference no correlate; "such a one's" follows B's sense (*huius*), a departure from
    the print (Latin doubts). Head as a relative term: Aristotle, *Categories* 7 (to
    be checked; LEDGER E06a-1). *Capitaneus*: a captain of a church, not its head (GLOSSARY).

[^4]: **C/R.** *Equivocando in capite*: "head" as the bodily head and as the ruling person. *Pars* and
    *membrum*: any part of a predestinate person is part of the church's body, but only a whole
    predestinate person is a member of it. A margin *Correlarium* (116.28).

[^5]: **C/T/R.** The Father generates and is the divine nature, yet the nature does not generate: the
    scholastic rule, as in Lateran IV, *Damnamus* (*X* 1.1.2: *illa res non est generans, neque genita, nec procedens; sed est Pater, qui generat*; Friedberg II, 7; LEDGER E06a-2). *Capitatur*: "is
    headed" (GLOSSARY). A margin, printed below 144c without mark (117.9): *Nota argumenta
    logica de materia capitis ecclesie*. B lacks *sic* (117.13). Loserth (117.9): cf. Hus, *De
    ecclesia*, *Opp.* I, fol. 198 (the OCR of the folio is unclear) (LEDGER E06a-3).

[^6]: **C/S/R.** *Secundum racionem qua*: "in the respect in which". *Ex qua*: from the church, not
    from Christ. A margin *Nota* (117.15). Ezekiel 18 as printed; the figure of the unfaithful bride
    is Ezek. 16 (16:15, DR "thou playedst the harlot") and 23, so the chapter is to be checked.

[^7]: **S.** Cant. 2:6, DR "his right hand shall embrace me"; "without wrinkle": Eph. 5:27, DR "not
    having spot or wrinkle".

[^8]: **C.** *Est ecclesia, non econtra*: every synagogue that is part of the church is a church, not
    every church a synagogue. *Alibi*: the place is not identified here.

[^9]: **R/C.** A margin *Secunda obieccio. Item est filius pater et sponsus ecclesie, puta Christus
    etc.* (117.24); *Responsio* (117.29). *Conclusionem assumptam*: the conclusion the objector drew,
    which Wyclif grants. *Eam* (118.4): spiritual kinship and espousal.

[^10]: **S/C.** Matt. 12:50, Vulgate *Quicumque enim fecerit voluntatem Patris mei … ipse meus
    frater*, DR "whosoever shall do the will of my Father … he is my brother"; Wyclif adds *filius
    regni* (roman in the print) and puts Christ's words in the third person (*patris sui … suus*). *Ex utroque parente*:
    God as father, the church as mother.

[^11]: **C/S.** *Sexus spiritualis*: we take *spiritualis* with *sexus*; it could go with
    *christianus* ("every spiritual predestinate Christian"). Cant. 5:1 and 4:12 agree with the
    Vulgate; DR.

[^12]: **C.** *Raciones vere earum denominaciones*: we construe "aspects, [which are] truly their
    namings", the aspects giving the names; the construal is doubtful. *Species et intenciones*:
    the likenesses of contraries (white and black) present together in the medium and in the mind.

[^13]: **T/S/R.** Codd. *Ysidorus*; Loserth corrected *Ysaie* (118.24). Isa. 26:1 agrees with the
    Vulgate; DR. Ps. 8:6, DR "Thou hast made him a little less than the angels". 1 Cor. 15:28 reads
    "that God may be all in all"; "Christ is all and in all" is Col. 3:11. A margin *Christi respectus
    ad ecclesiam* (118.26).

[^14]: **T/R/C.** A margin *Duplex opinio* (118.33). A A₁ *quidam diceret* (119.1). The *145a* folio
    mark and the alinea *D* at 119.1 are not Wyclif's. Loserth notes codd. *scriptura sacra* at 119.5.
    *Universalia ex parte rei*: universals in the things themselves, which the first group denies.
    *Non aliquid sed aliqua*: not one something but several.

[^15]: **S.** Eph. 5:25, DR "delivered himself up for it"; the merchant: cf. Matt. 13:45–46.

[^16]: **R/C.** A margin *Obieccio* (119.12). *Ens … verum … bonum*: what is not a being is neither
    true nor good. *Deducendo*: adducing, pointing to. *Raciocinetur … nichil est*: Wyclif's irony, a
    thing that reasons and acts and is nothing.

[^17]: **T/C.** B lacks *sed* (119.22); B *vel partes* (119.23–24); B *vel delinet* (119.24); B
    *innovearuntur* (119.29). *Religio*: a religious order. *Deficit*: fails, by death or departure.
    *Appropriacio*: the canon-law annexation of revenues to a college or house (GLOSSARY).

[^18]: **T/C.** B *elemosina* (120.2). *Imitacione spirituali*: their title is by imitating their
    founders (cf. *filii imitacionis*, GLOSSARY), not by fleshly descent.

[^19]: **T/R/S.** The *E* before *homo* (120.3) is an alinea letter, not translated. A margin *Tercia
    obieccio* (120.3). Tyconius's second rule, on the bipartite body of the Lord: Augustine, *De
    doctrina christiana* III.32 (Loserth, 120.8; LEDGER E06a-4).

[^20]: **C.** *Nec est evidencia … quin*: we construe "nor is there evidence that … without its
    following by the same token that …": the argument from what a part does to what the whole army or
    people does would prove too much.
