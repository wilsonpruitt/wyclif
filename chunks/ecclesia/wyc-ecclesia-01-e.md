# *De ecclesia*, chapter 1, part 5: subjection to the roman pontiff; why the church is called roman

## ¶1 · 14.17–15.16

> [p. 14] Quinta conclusio est ista: Subesse Romano pontifici omni humane nature est de necessitate salutis. Patet ex hoc quod nemo potest salvari, nisi meritorie subsit Christo; sed ipse est Romanus pontifex sicut est caput universalis ac cuiuslibet particularis ecclesie: ergo conclusio. Nam columba dicitur modo viatrix, quia secundum partem, igitur per idem: ipsa est Romana ecclesia, quia secundum partem. Non enim est racio, quare ipsa modo peregrinatur vel tribulatur nisi quod secundum partem. Et per idem ipsa inhabitat Romam, Angliam et quemcunque locum fidelem Nam si ubicunque papa fuerit, ibi est Romana ecclesia, quia eadem ecclesia que Rome edificatur [p. 15] per apostolos succedens ex quibuscunque fidelibus, per idem ecclesia Romana sic est in Anglia et ubicunque fuerit fidelis christianus, quia sancta ecclesia catholica; que non potest stringi secundum se totam in Roma vel cetu pape et cardinalium; sed distenditur per tria loca sancta, scilicet celum, locum peregrinacionis et purgatorium. Unde nimis erronee concipiunt qui fingunt quod ubicunque fuerit dominus papa, ibi est Roma, quia superflue equivocaretur in nomine civitatis, sicut pueri vocant Oxonie Romam monticulum Belli Montis; sed ubicunque dominus papa fuerit, ibi est ecclesia Romana, sicut fuit Jerusalem tempore Christi, in Constantinopoli tempore sinodi, ut patet XXII dist. Sacrosancta, in Antiochia tempore cathedracionis sancti Petri et post in Roma tempore predicacionis et passionis Petri et Pauli.

The fifth conclusion is this: to be subject to the Roman pontiff is, for every human nature, of
necessity of salvation.[^1] This is clear from the fact that no one can be saved unless he is
meritoriously subject to Christ; now he is the Roman pontiff, as he is head of the universal
[church]{g:ecclesia} and of every particular church: therefore the conclusion.[^2] For the dove is now
called a [wayfarer]{g:viator} because [she is so] in part; therefore, by the same token, she is the
Roman church because [she is so] in part. For there is no reason why she is now [a
pilgrim]{g:peregrinari} or in tribulation except that [she is so] in part.[^3] And by the same token
she dwells in Rome, in England and in any faithful place. For if wherever the [pope]{g:papa} is, there
is the Roman church, because [it is] the same church which is built at Rome [15] by the apostles,
continuing out of any faithful whatever, then by the same token the Roman church is thus in England and
wherever there is a faithful Christian, because [it is] the holy catholic church; which cannot be
confined, in its whole self, to Rome or to the company of the pope and cardinals, but is spread out
through three holy places, namely heaven, the place of pilgrimage, and purgatory.[^4] Hence those
conceive too erroneously who imagine that wherever the lord pope is, there is Rome; for the name of the
city would be made equivocal to no purpose, as boys at Oxford call the little hill of Beaumont
"Rome".[^5] But wherever the lord pope is, there is the Roman church, as it was at Jerusalem in the time
of Christ, in Constantinople at the time of the synod, as is clear in [Distinction] 22,
*Sacrosancta*, in Antioch at the time of St Peter's taking his chair, and afterwards in Rome at the time
of the preaching and passion of Peter and Paul.[^6]

## ¶2 · 15.17–16.34

> [p. 15] Et sic intelligitur dictum pape post Luce XI°, 20. Profecto pervenit in vos regnum dei et illud Luce XVII°, 22: Regnum dei intra vos est; ubicunque enim fuerit corpus, illic congregabuntur aquile. Quamvis enim ecclesia cepit a Judea et Christus caput ecclesie fuit martirizatus in Jerusalem, tamen racionabiliter vocata est ecclesia Christi secundum quandam preeminenciam propter tria: Primo quia Christus scivit gentes sub Romano imperio loco Judeorum infideliter discredencium inserendas, sicut dixit apostolus Rom. XI°, 23. Secunda causa est, quia maior multitudo martirum ibi triumphavit quam in alia civitate; sic enim [p. 16] ubi homo nascitur ex utero et triumphat gloriose, nomen abhinc contrahit. Cum ergo persona universalis ecclesie secundum multas partes suas ibi nascebatur, segregata ex utero synagoge, et ibi triumphavit crescens in gentibus: ideo fuit consonum quod caperet nomen a civitate metropoli gencium que est Roma. Tercia causa est, ut notetur quod non locus sive antiquitas sed fides formata fundet Christi ecclesiam. Nam quoad personam et quoad tempus prius fuit ecclesia Christi in sedibus prioribus et ad sensum istum dicitur II° Mach. V°, 19, quod non locus gentem sed gens locum sanctificat. Et hinc credo derelictum licere vocare Christi ecclesiam nomine cuiuscunque loci quem fideles inhabitant, sicut Christus vocatur Nazarenus propter concepcionem, et potest catholice vocari David Bethlehemiticus propter nativitatem, et sic de aliis civitatibus que possent licite dici sue propter inhabitacionem vel propter factum aliud notabile vel passionem. Notando tamen quod vocando ecclesiam quamcunque nomine loci hoc potest intelligi dupliciter, vel appropriate quoad habitacionem illius loci vel secundum partem, et in hoc sunt quotlibet gradus secundum amplitudinem parcium ecclesie. Non enim est bona causa vocare matrem nostram Romanam ecclesiam propter fastum aut complacenciam imperatoris dotantis ecclesiam nec propter extollenciam domini pape pompantis de parte imperii ex suo primatu sive dominio nec tercio, ut credatur quod ad ipsum oportet omnem christianum recurrere et de necessitate salutis ipsum capitalem recognoscere, sed propter causas topicas supradictas. Cum enim Romana ecclesia sit terminus institutus preter fundacionem in scriptura sacra, satis est habere racionem probabilem cavendo semper de deduccione perfida.

And so the pope's saying is understood, after Luke 11:20, "doubtless the kingdom of God is come upon
you", and that of Luke 17:21, "the kingdom of God is within you"; for "wheresoever the body shall be,
there shall the eagles be gathered together".[^7] For although the church began from Judea, and Christ,
the head of the church, was martyred in Jerusalem, yet the church of Christ has reasonably been called
[Roman] by a certain pre-eminence, for three [reasons].[^8] First, because Christ knew that the
gentiles under the Roman empire were to be grafted in, in place of the Jews who faithlessly
disbelieved, as the apostle said, Romans 11:23.[^9] The second cause is that a greater multitude of
martyrs triumphed there than in any other city; for so, [16] where a man is born from the womb and
triumphs gloriously, he takes his name from there. Since, therefore, the person of the universal
church, in many of her parts, was born there, separated from the womb of the synagogue, and triumphed
there, growing among the gentiles, it was therefore fitting that she should take her name from the city
that is the metropolis of the gentiles, which is Rome.[^10] The third cause is that it may be noted
that not place or antiquity but [formed faith]{g:fides formata} founds the church of Christ. For as to
person and as to time the church of Christ was earlier in earlier sees; and in this sense it is said in
2 Maccabees 5:19 that not the place sanctifies the people, but the people the place.[^11] And hence I
believe it has been left [open] that it is lawful to call the church of Christ by the name of any place
which the faithful inhabit,[^12] as Christ is called a Nazarene by reason of [his] conception, and can
catholicly be called "David the Bethlehemite" by reason of [his] birth; and so of other cities which
could lawfully be called his by reason of dwelling, or of some other notable deed, or of [his]
passion.[^13]

Yet it is to be noted that, in calling any church by the name of a place, this can be understood in
two ways: either appropriately, as to dwelling in that place, or according to part; and in this there
are any number of degrees according to the extent of the parts of the church.[^14] For it is not a good
cause to call [our mother]{g:mater nostra} the Roman church on account of the pride or the complaisance
of the emperor who endowed the church, nor on account of the exaltation of the lord pope, who makes a
display of his share in the empire by his primacy or [lordship]{g:dominium}, nor, third, so that it
should be believed that every Christian must have recourse to him and, of necessity of salvation,
acknowledge him as chief; but for the topical causes stated above. For since "the Roman church" is a
term instituted beyond [any] foundation in holy scripture, it is enough to have a probable reason,
always guarding against a perfidious deduction.[^15]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 14.17: Fifth conclusion. Submission to the Pope is necessary to Salvation.
- 14.26: As, wherever the Pope is, there is the Roman Church: so wherever is one of the faithful, there too is the Roman Church.
- 15.11: Local name at Oxford.
- 15.23: For three reasons the Church of Christ is called the Romish Church.
- 16.19: We must be careful how we use local names for the Church.

## Notes

[^1]: **S/T.** The fifth conclusion is the closing sentence of *Unam sanctam* (Loserth, at 14.18;
    *Extrav. comm.* 1.8.1, our modern form; LEDGER E01e-1), which reads *humanae creaturae*
    (Friedberg II, 1245–46); Wyclif's *nature* is translated as printed.

[^2]: **C.** *Ipse … sicut est caput*: Christ is the Roman pontiff inasmuch as he is head of every
    church; the conclusion is granted only by moving its subject from the pope to Christ. *Sed* brings
    the minor premiss ("now").

[^3]: **C/T.** The dove is the church (Song 6:8, *una est columba mea*). *Quia secundum partem*: the
    church is a wayfarer, a pilgrim and in tribulation only in her earthly part, and by the same token
    "Roman" only in part. AA₁ lack the words from *igitur* to *partem* (14.22); the print follows B.

[^4]: **C/T.** *Nam si …, per idem …*: the inference runs from the curial maxim (wherever the pope is,
    there is the Roman church) to Wyclif's own (wherever a faithful Christian is). B lacks *quia —
    ecclesia* (14.29). The three places are
    the church triumphant, militant and sleeping.

[^5]: **S.** *Bellus Mons*: Beaumont, a royal palace, later destroyed, in the north suburb of Oxford
    (Loserth, after Wood's *History of the City of Oxford*; LEDGER E01e-2). *Equivocaretur*: "Rome"
    would become an equivocal name for wherever the pope stands.

[^6]: **S.** Gratian, *Decretum* D.22 c.2 *Sacrosancta* (Loserth; our modern form; PL 187:123A; LEDGER E01e-3): the canon names
    Rome, Antioch and Alexandria; Constantinople is in Gratian's dictum and cc. 3–4; B's
    margin: *22ª distinccione*. *Cathedracio*: Peter's taking the chair at Antioch.

[^7]: **T/S/C.** *Dictum pape post*: we take it as the curial saying just corrected (wherever the
    pope is, there is the Roman church), understood "after" these texts; the phrase is doubtful
    (Latin doubt reported). The MSS have Matt. 12 (12:28 has the same words); Loserth corrected to
    Luke 11:20. The print's verse "22" for Luke 17:21 is Loserth's; B lacks *Luce 17*. The last clause
    is Matt. 24:28 (cf. Luke 17:37); Wyclif lacks Vg *et*, "also", and we omit it.

[^8]: **T/R.** The print supplies no predicate name; "[Roman]" is from the side-note and A's margin
    (*Propter tres causas ecclesia Christi vocata est Romana ecclesia*). *I3o"* (15.21) is the folio
    mark (fol. 130) left in the Latin. B's reader objects to *a Judea*: the church began from Abel,
    "as the blessed Gregory thinks".

[^9]: **S.** Rom. 11:23 (MSS *Rom. IX*; Loserth's correction), DR "shall be grafted in": a reference
    to the grafting of 11:17–24, not a quotation.

[^10]: **C.** The comparison joins birth and
    triumph: a martyr is named from the place of his triumph, and the church, "born" at Rome out of
    the synagogue and triumphing there, is named from Rome. B *ad nomen* (16.2).

[^11]: **S.** 2 Macc. 5:19, DR "God did not choose the people for the place's sake, but the place for
    the people's sake"; Wyclif's *non locus gentem sed gens locum sanctificat* is the proverbial form,
    and we translate his Latin. *Sedibus prioribus*: Jerusalem and Antioch, above.

[^12]: **T/R.** *Derelictum*: "sic in codd." and in Hus, *De ecclesia* (Loserth; LEDGER E01e-4). We
    construe "left [open]", not settled by scripture; the word is doubtful (Latin doubt reported).

[^13]: **C.** We keep Christ as subject: he "can be called 'David the Bethlehemite'", as David's son
    born in Bethlehem. The Latin could also mean "David can be called the Bethlehemite", but *sue*
    ("his" cities) follows Christ throughout.

[^14]: **C.** *Appropriate*: "properly", of a
    church named from where it dwells; not the canonists' *appropriacio*. *Secundum partem*: named
    from one of its parts.

[^15]: **C/T.** *Dotantis*: the emperor of the endowment (Constantine, unnamed). B *excellenciam*
    (16.25) for *extollenciam*; B *nec estimo ut*, "nor do I think that" (16.28), for *nec tercio,
    ut*. *Capitalem*: "as chief", the word of *capitalis dominus*. *Causas topicas*: dialectical,
    probable grounds, hence "a probable reason" next. B *de seduccione perfida* (16.34).
