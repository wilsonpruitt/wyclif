# *De eucharistia*, prologue, part 2: chapters five to eight, from the older doctors to the multiplying of a body

## ¶1 · 4.26–6.2

> [p. 4] Capitulum quintum replicat de locucione tropica secundum modernos doctores et solvit. Secundo replicat per doctores antiquiores et solvit, ita quod tangit primo Scotum, Henricum et dominum Armachanum, ubi docet tripliciter quod hoc decretum Ego Beringarius non est abrogatum. Sed post tanguntur [p. 5] alii doctores provecciores, primo Petrus Lombardus, ubi ostenditur quod ipse non repugnat huic sentencie, et tollit evidenciam Bonaventure et solvit, declarans quid concluditur ex dictis multis sanctorum et decreti quod Eukaristia est panis et vinum sacramentaliter consecrata. Secundo replicat per Anshelmum et declarat ex testimonio suo et racionibus quod accidens non potest esse sine subiecto, et ostendendo quid Augustinus sentit de quantitate declarat quomodo ista via magnificaret ulterius Dei potenciam ponendo hostiam infinitum magis gloriosam quam modo sompniat, et quomodo error istius materie induxit in multos alios errores. Quarto obicit per hoc quod multi sancti canonizati moriebantur in ista fide de Eukaristia. Et allegat sanctum Thomam probantem quod via dicens panem remanere post consecracionem est incompetens, inpossibilis et heretica, et primo tollit evidenciam ex testimonio triplici quod non probatur multos sanctos in ista fide de Eukaristia decessisse. Secundo dicitur probabiliter, quod multe sancti Thome sentencie sunt corrupte. Tercio declaratur tripliciter quod non est de substancia fidei credere quod post benediccionem non remanet substancia sacramenti et colorat concordancias decreti Nicholai IIⁱ et decretalis Innocencii IIIⁱⁱ in hac parte. Quarto infringitur trimembre argumentum per quod sanctus Thomas videtur probare incompetenciam huius sentencie. Quinto probat sextupliciter falsitatem porismatis, per quod nititur Thomas probare inpossibilitatem huius sentencie. Sexto obicitur tripliciter contra evidenciam hereticantem sentenciam [p. 6] istam et declaratur quam irreligiose procedit ista secta in condempnando sentenciam quam ignorat.

The fifth chapter [rejoins]{g:replicatur} on [tropical speech]{g:locucio-tropica} according to [the modern doctors]{g:moderni-doctores}, and [resolves]{g:solvere} [the rejoinder]. Second, it rejoins by appeal to the older doctors and resolves [the rejoinder], so that it touches first on Scotus, Henry and [the lord of Armagh]{g:dominus-ardmacanus}, where it teaches in three ways that this [decree]{g:decretum} *Ego Berengarius* has not been abrogated.[^1] Now afterwards [5] other, more advanced doctors are touched on:[^2] first Peter Lombard, where it is [shown]{g:ostendere} that he [is not incompatible]{g:repugnare} with this [teaching]{g:sentencia};[^3] and it [disposes of]{g:tollere} the [evidence]{g:evidencia} of Bonaventure and resolves [it], [making clear]{g:declarare} what [is inferred]{g:concludere} from many sayings of [the saints]{g:sancti} and of the decree: that the Eucharist is [bread]{g:panis} and [wine]{g:vinum}, [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} consecrated.[^4] Second, it rejoins by appeal to Anselm, and makes clear from his testimony and arguments that an [accident]{g:accidens} cannot be without a [subject]{g:subiectum}; and, showing what Augustine thinks about [quantity]{g:quantitas}, it makes clear how this way would magnify God's [power]{g:potencia} further by positing a [host]{g:hostia} infinitely more glorious than it now dreams of, and how the error of this matter has led into many other errors.[^5] Fourth, it objects on the ground that many [canonized]{g:canonizare} saints died in this faith concerning the Eucharist.[^6] And it [alleges]{g:allegare} Saint Thomas, who proves that the way which says that the bread remains after the consecration is [unfitting]{g:incompetens}, impossible and heretical; and first it disposes of the evidence by a threefold testimony that it is not proved that many saints departed in this faith concerning the Eucharist.[^7] Second, it is said [with probability]{g:probabiliter} that many of Saint Thomas's teachings are corrupted.[^8] Third, it is made clear in three ways that it is not of the [substance]{g:substancia} of the faith to believe that after the blessing the substance of the sacrament does not remain; and it [gives colour to]{g:colorare} the concordances of the decree of Nicholas II and the decretal of Innocent III on this point.[^9] Fourth, the three-membered argument by which Saint Thomas seems to prove the unfittingness of this teaching is broken. Fifth, it proves in six ways the falsity of the [porism]{g:porisma} by which Thomas strives to prove the impossibility of this teaching. Sixth, it is objected in three ways against the evidence that [pronounces]{g:hereticare} [6] this teaching heretical, and it is made clear how irreligiously this [sect]{g:secta} proceeds in condemning a teaching of which it [is ignorant]{g:ignorare}.

## ¶2 · 6.3–6.29

> [p. 6] Capitulum sextum declarat quomodo homo dignificabit se ut communicet sacramento; et primo declarat quomodo oportet quod habeat habitus et opera virtuosa. Secundo descendit ad tres virtutes theologicas quas oportet omnis habere, et narrat quomodo ipse verse sunt in vicia. Tercio descendit ad actus, narrando quod quatuordecim secundum exposicionem beati Gregorii in esu agni paschalis fuerant; quod mirabiliter prodesset fidelibus sacramentum Eukaristie memorando. Quarto obicit tripliciter et dissolvit, primo per hoc quod in legendis et lege ecclesie canitur quod sacramentum sit accidens sine subiecto; et solvendo declarat ex testimonio Augustini quomodo se homo habebit ad talia docens triplici medio, quomodo cultores accidencium reduci poterint ad ecclesiam primitivam. Secundo obicit per hoc, quod sacerdotes sunt nimis fragiles ad celebrandum nec habet hostia virtutem ad peccata tollendum ac graciam acquirendum; et solvit declarans quomodo in ista materia populus est delusus. Tercio obicit per hoc quod errantes in ista materia forent puniendi tanquam heretici, quod tamen declarative concedit, dicens quod variacio opinionum in ista materia prenosticat opiniones plurimorum criminum et virtutum, et descendit ad opinionem triplicem et usum ecclesie Anglicane et invehit contra illas ex testimonio Rabani et aliorum, eliciens quid cultores talis accidentis prenosticant.

The sixth chapter makes clear how a man will make himself worthy to communicate in the sacrament; and first it makes clear how he must have virtuous [habits]{g:habitus} and works. Second, it comes down to the three theological [virtues]{g:virtus} which everyone must have, and [relates]{g:narrare} how they have been turned into vices. Third, it comes down to acts, relating that, according to the exposition of blessed Gregory, there were fourteen in the [eating]{g:esus} of the paschal lamb; which would marvellously profit the faithful in recalling the sacrament of the Eucharist.[^10] Fourth, it objects in three ways and [resolves]{g:dissolvere} [the objections]: first, on the ground that in the legends and the [law]{g:leges-ecclesie} of the [church]{g:ecclesia} it is sung "the sacrament is [an accident without a subject]{g:accidens-sine-subiecto}";[^11] and in resolving it makes clear from the testimony of Augustine how a man will conduct himself toward such things, teaching by a threefold means how [the worshippers of accidents]{g:cultores-accidencium} could be brought back to the primitive church. Second, it objects on the ground that [priests]{g:sacerdos} are [too]{g:nimis} frail for celebrating, and the host does not have power to take away sins and to acquire grace; and it resolves [this], making clear how the people has been deluded in this matter.[^12] Third, it objects on the ground that those who err in this matter would have to be punished as heretics; which it nevertheless grants by way of explanation, saying that the variation of [opinions]{g:opinio} in this matter foretokens opinions of very many crimes and virtues;[^13] and it comes down to the threefold opinion and the use of the English church, and inveighs against them from the testimony of Rabanus and others, drawing out what the worshippers of such an accident foretoken.[^14]

## ¶3 · 7.1–8.3

> [p. 7] Capitulum septimum introducit materiam de ydemptificacione; contra quam arguit septempliciter, et arguit pro ydemptificacione, primo pro idemptificacione individuorum in specie et genere, et dissolvit. Secundo replicatur per hoc quod hoc sacramentum est uni una qualitas et alii alia; et dissolvit negans illud, declarans quod qualitates non poterunt per se esse ex parte qualitatum anime. Et solvit tria argumenta, declarans multipliciter quomodo accidens varie denominat. Et tercio ostendendo quomodo Deus videtur declarat novem genera visionum. Et solvit quatuor obiectus quibus suadetur Deum videri oculo corporali. Quarto solvit tercium obiectum factum pro ydemptificacione per hoc quod panis fit corpus Christi, ubi declarando sensum istius ostendit qualis est virtus sacramenti et quomodo non sufficit facere panem esse idem corpori Christi. Et ultimo solvit quartum obiectum de inpanacione, exponendo primo et colorando posicionem; secundo narrat originem huius vie et quid sibi contingit ac quam sinistre glosantur dicta sanctorum et leges ecclesie ex ista infecta radice. Et tercio probat tripliciter quod sentencia ista de inpanacione est heretica, primo quia scriptura sacra et sancti doctores in verbis asserunt, ut allegant beatum Augustinum dicentem quod panis est corpus Christi, secundo per hoc quod quidditas panis non potest esse ydemptice quidditas carnis, et tercio per hoc quod ecclesia non est difficultata in fide sine magna racione; sed non est racio, ymmo inconvenientis multiplicis implicacio ponendo inpanacionem. Et exponit decretum ecclesie quod videtur [p. 8] contrarium narrans tres logicas in ista materia et quomodo usus earum est secundum epikeyam pro loco et tempore adaptandus.

The seventh chapter introduces the matter of [identification]{g:ydemptificacio}; against which it argues in seven ways, and it argues for identification: first, for the identification of [individuals]{g:individuum} in species and genus, and it resolves [this].[^15] Second, it is rejoined on the ground that "this sacrament is one [quality]{g:qualitas} to one man and another to another"; and it resolves [this] by denying it, making clear that qualities could not be [of themselves]{g:per-se} on the side of the qualities of the soul.[^16] And it resolves three arguments, making clear in many ways how an accident [names]{g:denominare} variously. And third, in showing how God is seen, it makes clear nine kinds of vision. And it resolves four [objections]{g:objectum} by which [the case is made]{g:suadere} that God is seen with the bodily eye. Fourth, it resolves the third objection made for identification, on the ground that "the bread becomes the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}"; where, in making clear the sense of this, it shows what the power of the sacrament is, and how it does not suffice to make the bread be the same as the body of Christ.[^17] And last it resolves the fourth objection, concerning [impanation]{g:inpanacio}, first expounding the [position]{g:posicio} and giving it colour; second, it relates the origin of this way, and what befell it, and how perversely the sayings of the saints and the laws of the church are [glossed]{g:glosare} from this infected root. And third, it proves in three ways that this teaching of impanation is heretical: first, because holy scripture and the holy doctors assert [it] in words, as they allege blessed Augustine saying "the bread is the body of Christ";[^18] second, on the ground that the quiddity of bread cannot be [identically]{g:ydemptice} the quiddity of flesh; and third, on the ground that the church is not burdened in faith without great reason; now there is no reason, but rather the implication of manifold [absurdity]{g:inconveniens} in positing impanation.[^19] And it expounds the decree of the church which seems [8] contrary, relating three logics in this matter, and how their use is to be adapted according to [epikeia]{g:epikeya} for place and time.[^20]

## ¶4 · 8.4–8.29

> [p. 8] Capitulum octavum exponendo et limitando sensum multitudinis probat sextupliciter quod nullum corpus idem in numero potest multiplicari simul et semel dimensionaliter per diversa loca. Secundo obicit tripliciter et dissolvit, primo per hoc quod multa corpora possunt esse per eundem locum, ubi docet quod corpora appropriate replent loca, secundo ex miraculo de beato Ambrosio quod dissolvit tripliciter; et tercio ex scriptura sacra triplici de multitudine corporum et de augmentacione; que omnia dissolvit exponendo illud Matth. XVᵒ: Omne quod intrat in os etc. Tercio principaliter resumendo sentenciam Doctoris Communis tractat si tempus componitur ex instantibus, arguit quadrupliciter contra ipsum in materia de tempore discreto ex vicissitudine affeccionis angelice resultante, recitando in ista materia responsivam distinccionem de duplici tempore, et arguit sextupliciter contra illam; et sic descendit ponendo quiete sentenciam triplicem, scilicet Dei, angeli et nature moralis temporalis. Quarto principaliter describendo multitudinem et distinguendo de esse in loco et esse per locum declarat quomodo corpus Christi multiplicatur in Eukaristia, et quale esse habet ibi; et concludit in quibus et qualiter credi debet ecclesie, quia in materia fidei precise de quanto se fundat efficaciter ex scriptura.

The eighth chapter, expounding and [limiting]{g:limitare} the sense of [multitude]{g:multitudo}, proves in six ways that no body that is the same in number can be multiplied at one and the same time [dimensionally]{g:dimensionaliter} through diverse places.[^21] Second, it objects in three ways and resolves [the objections]: first, on the ground that many bodies can be through the same place, where it teaches that bodies fill places in their proper manner; second, from the miracle of blessed Ambrose, which it resolves in three ways;[^22] and third, from holy scripture in three [places], on the multitude of bodies and on [augmentation]{g:augmentacio}; all of which it resolves by expounding that [text] of Matt. 15: "Whatsoever entereth into the mouth" etc.[^23] Third, [as a main head]{g:principaliter}, taking up again the teaching of [the Common Doctor]{g:doctor-communis}, it treats whether time is composed of instants; it argues in four ways against him in the matter of discrete time resulting from the alternation of angelic affection, [reciting]{g:recitare} in this matter a distinction given in reply, of a twofold time, and it argues in six ways against that; and so it comes down, laying down calmly a threefold teaching, namely of God, of the angel, and of moral temporal nature.[^24] Fourth, as a main head, defining multitude and distinguishing between being in a place and being through a place, it makes clear how the body of Christ is multiplied in the Eucharist, and what [being]{g:esse} it has there; and it infers in what things and in what manner the church ought to be believed: for in a matter of faith [it is to be believed] precisely so far as it grounds itself effectively from scripture.[^25]

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** The Latin names Scotus, "Henry" and the lord of Armagh (Richard FitzRalph) as teaching that *Ego Berengarius* is not abrogated; "Henry" is presumably Henry of Ghent; the passages are in ch. 5, to be checked (LEDGER Kprolb-1). The decree is Gratian, *De cons.* D. 2 c. 42 (modern form ours), to be checked (LEDGER Kprolb-2).

[^2]: **C.** *Provecciores*, "more advanced", is set against *antiquiores* "older": either further advanced in years (earlier) or more eminent. The Lombard and Bonaventure are earlier than Scotus, Henry and FitzRalph, which favours the first.

[^3]: **C.** *Sentencia* (CONTEXTUAL) is here and throughout the chunk the position taught, "teaching": the view the compendium defends (the bread remains, sacramentally the body).

[^4]: **T/S.** Loserth's apparatus (5.4, 5.9) says *quid* would better be *quod*; we translate the print *quid* ("what is inferred … : that"), which construes. D reads *sacramentaliter confectum* (5.5–6). The Lombard is *Sent.* IV d. 10 c. 1 (*Sunt item alii precedencium insaniam transcendentes*, quoted at 119.30–120.11); Bonaventure is named at 120.14 with Thomas as recording that view as heresy in commenting the Lombard (i.e. *In IV Sent.* d. 10), and at 135.3; his locus is not checked (LEDGER Kprolb-3).

[^5]: **C/S.** *Ista via … quam modo sompniat*: the subject of *sompniat* is unexpressed ("it"): either the opponents' way (pressed to its consequence, a host more glorious than they now dream) or the way opened by Augustine on quantity set against what the sect dreams. We keep the Latin's ambiguity. *Potencia* (CONTEXTUAL): of God, "power". Anselm: the *De corpore Christi* letter (130.4–19; Loserth: *Ep.* IV.107) and *De veritate* 13 (131.23–26); Augustine on quantity is *De Trinitate* V.10.11 (133.1–11) (LEDGER Kprolb-4, Kprolb-5).

[^6]: **T.** *Quarto*: no *tercio* precedes in the print; ABC read *Tercio obicitur* (5.13). We translate the print.

[^7]: **S.** Thomas, *ST* III q. 75 a. 2, where the remaining of the bread's substance is rejected on three grounds and "to be avoided as heretical"; the "three-membered argument" and the "porism" below are presumably in the same place; to be checked (LEDGER Kprolb-6). *Incompetens*, "unfitting", not "incompetent".

[^8]: **C.** *Corrupte*: "corrupted" may mean that Thomas's text has been tampered with or that the teachings are in error; the Latin does not decide. *Probabiliter*: argued with probability, not demonstrated.

[^9]: **C/S.** *Substancia sacramenti*: the substance of the sacrament, i.e. of the sign (the bread); *benediccio*, "blessing", is the consecration. *Colorat*: gives a plausible construction to the agreement of the two texts with this teaching. Nicholas II's decree is *Ego Berengarius*; Innocent III's decretal is *Firmiter* (Lateran IV c. 1; X 1.1.1, modern form ours), the decretal *loquens de transsubstanciacione* of 141.11–29 (LEDGER Kprolb-7).

[^10]: **S/C.** Gregory's exposition of the paschal lamb (Exod. 12) is *Hom. in Evang.* 22 (Wyclif *Omelia XII*, quoted at 164.8–20) (LEDGER Kprolb-8). *Quod … prodesset*: the relative most likely takes up the fourteen acts; *memorando* may instead mean that the faithful profit by recalling them at the sacrament. AB read *monstrabiliter*, A in the margin *mirabiliter* (6.10–11).

[^11]: **C.** *Legendis*: the legends, the lessons read in the office. The proposition is kept as quoted; ABDE read *canetur* "will be sung" (6.14).

[^12]: **C.** *Nimis fragiles ad celebrandum*: "too frail for celebrating", since excess is the point here (frail beyond fitness). *Virtus* is here "power", the faculty. For *hostia*, DE and C in the margin read *Eukaristia* (6.20).

[^13]: **C.** *Declarative concedit*: grants it by way of explanation, i.e. with a qualification. *Prenosticat opiniones … criminum et virtutum*: as printed, the variation "foretokens opinions of" crimes and virtues, i.e. of what men will hold about them; E reads *et criminum* and omits *virtutum* (6.26).

[^14]: **S/C.** Rabanus (Maurus), *De universo* V.11 (Wyclif *De naturis rerum* V.11), quoted at 187.5–11 (LEDGER Kprolb-9). *Illas*: feminine, the opinions. *Usus ecclesie Anglicane*: the use (liturgical custom) of the English church.

[^15]: **T/C.** The MSS read *septempliciter secundo arguit*; *et arguit* is Loserth's correction ("Correxi", 7.2–3), which we translate. *Arguit pro ydemptificacione*: it sets out arguments for identification, which it then resolves. B omits *primo — ydemptificacione* (7.3–4).

[^16]: **C.** *Per se esse*: to be of themselves, i.e. without a subject; *ex parte qualitatum anime*: on the side of the soul's qualities, the perceiver's. The phrase is compressed and the construal is ours. The proposition under discussion is kept as quoted.

[^17]: **C.** The fourth head resolves the third objection for identification (the first being of individuals, the second of quality). The unexpressed subject of *non sufficit* is likeliest *virtus sacramenti*. *Fit* is "becomes", the Latin's verb. AB omit *facere* (7.16).

[^18]: **C/S/T.** *Ut allegant*: the subject is scripture and the holy doctors; ABCD read *allegat*, "as it [the chapter] alleges" (7.24). Augustine's saying "the bread is the body of Christ" is the chapter's *Non omnis panis sed accipiens benediccionem Christi fit corpus Christi* (227.10–17), from his Easter sermon (Loserth: *Sermo* 234), as in Gratian, *De cons.* D. 2 c. 61 (LEDGER Kprolb-10).

[^19]: **C.** *Inconveniens* (CONTEXTUAL): here an absurdity in argument. *Sed* brings the minor premiss of the third proof ("now"). *Difficultata*: burdened, made difficult. D reads *ydemptifice* (7.27) and *inconveniens* (7.29–30).

[^20]: **C.** *Epikeya*: epikeia (Greek *epieikeia*), equity, the adapting of a rule to its circumstances. *Tres logicas*: three logics, three ways of speaking on the matter. ABC read *usus eorum*; B *aptandus* (8.2–3).

[^21]: **C.** *Multitudo*: "multitude", a body's being many, in many places, which the chapter's question concerns; kept as one word through ¶4. *Limitando*: restricting the sense. *Idem in numero*: numerically one.

[^22]: **S.** The Latin says only "the miracle of blessed Ambrose"; the identification is to be checked (LEDGER Kprolb-11). *Appropriate*: in the manner proper to each.

[^23]: **S.** Matt. 15:17. Wyclif's *Omne quod intrat in os* has the Vulgate's words in another order (*omne quod in os intrat*); the Douay–Rheims wording is given.

[^24]: **T/S/C.** *Moralis* (8.23): ABCD read *mobilis*, "of mobile (changeable) temporal nature", which gives the three measures (God's, the angel's, the time of changeable nature); we translate the print and flag it. *Doctor Communis*: Thomas; his discrete angelic time is quoted from *In II Sent.* d. 2 q. 1 a. 1 ad 4 (255.4–18) and from his commentary on *II Sent.* d. 3 (250.28–251.10); *ST* I q. 53 a. 3 is a parallel (LEDGER Kprolb-12). *Quiete*: "calmly", as printed.

[^25]: **C.** *Describendo*: "defining" (cf. *descripcio* "definition"). *De quanto*: "so far as"; the subject of *se fundat* is the church, and "[it is to be believed]" is supplied from *credi debet*. ABC read *ex scriptura efficaciter*, D *efficaciter de scriptura* (8.29).
