# Connective check: *sed* / *autem*, De ecclesia, extract 08 (chunks 19-h – 22-d)

Classes: (a) minor premiss / next step, "now" · (b) adversative, "but" · (c) continuative *autem*, "and";
*non … sed* (incl. *nedum / non solum … sed*), "but".

## Doubtful, left as they are

- **20-a, p. 472**: *Primo autem supponitur esse unum primum coniugium*, rendered "Now first it is supposed". (a), the first supposition brought to bear, or (c), opening the first part of the division just announced (cf. Dialogus 05, *Tercia autem lex*, changed to "And").
- **20-d, p. 487**: *notum est quod indumenta anime sunt virtutes, Christus autem Dei virtus …*, rendered ", and Christ is 'the power of God'". (c), or (a), the minor of an unstated syllogism (garments of the soul are virtues; *autem* Christ is the virtue of God; so we put on Christ), embedded in a *quia* clause with no *ergo*.
- **20-b p. 479, 20-c pp. 480 and 482, 20-d pp. 485 and 488**: *Sed quia nec navis …*, *Sed quia matrona …*, *Sed quantum ad sensum … de fuso*, *Sed cognoscamus …*, *Sed quia ad illud requiritur …*, all rendered "But". Each moves to the next verse of the song; (b) as rendered, or (c)/(a), the next step.
- **21-b, p. 497**: *Sed ulterius querit doctor*, rendered "But further". (b), or (c), moving on to the next question.
- **21-c, p. 501**: *baptismus configuratur morienti … Confirmacio vero … Sed ordo configuratur ministranti*, rendered "But order". Third member of a list; (b) as rendered, or (c) "And order".
- **21-e, p. 510**: *Isti autem sensibiles caracteres non sunt ordo clericalis*, rendered "And these perceptible characters". (c), or (a), the premiss that the *Ideo necessitantur … ponere ordines subiectari in anima* needs.
- **21-g, p. 516**: *Debet autem supponi in istis prepositorum discrecio*, rendered "Now in these matters". (a), or (c), a qualification added to the superiors' power.
- **22-a, p. 519**: *si licet mentiri, sic licitum est peccare, sed dare illam licenciam non subest potestati divine*, rendered "; but to give that licence". (b), or (a), the minor of a modus tollens whose conclusion comes only in the rhetorical *Quis ergo blasphemus …*; left by the Dialogus 12 precedent (*Sed hoc nimis perturbaret rempublicam*).
- **22-b, p. 521**: Hostiensis, *Boni vero sunt qui salvabuntur, mali autem sunt qui mala sustinent*, rendered "good indeed, in that they will be saved; bad, in that they suffer evils". *autem* has no word of its own; the balanced pair carries the contrast. Could be "but bad, in that …".
