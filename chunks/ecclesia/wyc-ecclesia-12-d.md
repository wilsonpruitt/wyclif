# *De ecclesia*, chapter 12, part 4: occasion given and occasion taken; fourth objection: holy men approved the privilege; the duke's stand

## ¶1 · 263.27–264.32

> [p. 263] quod hoc est contra fidem catholicam ut patet Rom. III° et VII°, ubi docet apostolus quod non sunt faciendo mala culpe, ut bona eveniant, nam cum tales casuum circumstancie sint casuales, patet quod tunc contingeret ut per maiorem partem duracionis date legis iudicaretur secundum ipsam iniustum iudicium, et tunc foret indubie lex iniusta et nunquam permittenda. Quoad terciam difficultatem super qua clamant scolastici, oportet distinguere (sicut facit Apostolus) [p. 264] inter occasionem datam et occasionem acceptam ex lege ad committendum iniuriam: datur autem occasio a lege iniusta ad iniuriam perpetrandum, quando secundum sensum eius cum veris infertur iniuria, ut sensus vendicatur competere dicto privilegio quod omnis fugitivus ad illum locum, sive fuerit dignus sive indignus, habebit ibi omnimodam libertatem; quod declaratum est repugnare divine iusticie. Si autem dicatur quod solum pro dignis vendicatur libertas, tunc vel oportet docere quod fugitivi libertandi sunt digni, vel dicere quod eo ipso quo rex sic concedit, quicunque illuc fugiens erit dignus; dato primo, tunc docto per consilium regis quod datus fugitivus non sit dignus habere refugium, nichil sibi et privilegio libertanti. Si secundum detur, tunc blaspheme extollitur princeps privilegians supra Deum. Deus enim non sic privilegiat locum vel naturam peccabilem, quin ponit in libertate sui arbitrii quod possit reddere se indignum ad percipiendum fructum privilegii concessi. Nec valet deduccio quod tunc iudicium secundum leges humanas foret sepe iniustum cum non attendunt iusticiam quoad Deum: verum concluditur, cum solum lex domini est immaculata convertens animas. Et ita si secundo obicitur quod in Anglia sunt multa privilegia tam iniqua, patet quod improbatio huius privilegii non solvitur sed gravatur, cum multiplicare inconveniens non sit solvere; ymmo manifestior, generalior et contrarior iniusticia verbis, et intencioni privilegii plus dissonaret illi loco et ordini quam alibi in Anglia in manu secularium, ubi communiter non tanta iniuria est commissa.

that this is against the catholic faith, as is clear from Romans 3:8 and 7:8, where the Apostle teaches that evils of fault are not to be done that good may come; for since such circumstances of cases are matters of chance, it is clear that it would then happen that for the greater part of the duration of a given law an unjust judgment would be given according to it; and then the law would undoubtedly be unjust and never to be permitted.[^1] As to the third difficulty, over which the schoolmen cry out, one must distinguish (as the Apostle does) [264] between occasion given and occasion taken from the law for committing injury. Now occasion for perpetrating injury is given by an unjust law when, according to its sense, together with truths, injury is inferred: as [when] the sense claimed to belong to the said [privilege]{g:privilegium} is that every fugitive to that place, whether he be worthy or unworthy, will have there liberty of every kind; which has been shown to conflict with divine justice.[^2] But if it is said that liberty is claimed only for the worthy, then either it must be shown that the fugitives to be set free are worthy, or it must be said that, by the very fact that the king so grants, whoever flees there will be worthy. Granted the first: then, once it has been shown through the king's council that a given fugitive is not worthy to have refuge, he has nothing to do with the freeing privilege.[^3] If the second is granted, then the privileging prince is blasphemously exalted above God. For God does not so privilege a place or a nature capable of sin without placing it in the [freedom of its choice]{g:libertas arbitrii} that it can render itself unworthy to receive the fruit of the privilege granted. Nor does the deduction avail, that then judgment according to human laws would often be unjust, since they do not attend to justice as regards God: what is [inferred]{g:concludere} is true, since only "the law of the Lord is unspotted, converting souls" (Psalm 19:7).[^4] And so, if, second, [it is objected]{g:obicitur} that in England there are many privileges just as wicked, it is clear that the disproof of this privilege is not dissolved but made heavier, since to multiply an [absurdity]{g:inconveniens} is not to resolve it; indeed, a more manifest, more general injustice, and one more contrary to the words and to the intention of the privilege, would be more out of keeping with that place and order than elsewhere in England in the hands of [seculars]{g:secularis}, where commonly not so great an injury is committed.[^5]

## ¶3 · 265.1–265.8

> [p. 265] vulgares approbarunt hoc privilegium, quando regnum processit prospere; qua ergo fronte nos minus sancti, minus scioli vel potentes usurpare audemus violacionem sancte matris ecclesie, cum domini et partes quibus iniuriatum est possunt (quantum in eis est) remittere satisfaccionem et penam recommendando Deo vindictam? Sic enim alique civitates in Anglia sunt servis in perpetuum refugium.

[and] common people approved this privilege when the [kingdom]{g:regnum} went forward prosperously; with what face, then, do we, less holy, less [wise]{g:sciolus} or powerful, dare to usurp the violation of holy mother [church]{g:ecclesia}, since the lords and the parties to whom injury has been done can (as far as in them lies) remit satisfaction and penalty, commending vengeance to God? For in this way certain cities in England are a refuge in perpetuity for serfs."[^6]

## ¶4 · 265.9–266.31

> [p. 265] Hic dicitur quod falsum assumitur ad mentem obiectus, nam nec potest esse sanctitas neque prudencia defendere hoc privilegium ad nudum sensum expositum, unde quicunque hunc sensum approbaverant vel fructuose penituerant vel aliter sunt dampnati; nec docetur qui sancti vel approbati viri approbaverant istum sensum. Sed sinistre extortum est ut privilegium ultra fines iusticie contra iuramentum regis et prosperitatem regni, ideo tam excessivo abusui necesse est apponi novum remedium. Et hec racio quare regnum non potens ulterius tollerare crementum iniurie apposuit nunc remedium pocius quam perante. Nam semistultus percipit et impugnat manifestam iniuriam, postquam sciolus insensibilem iniuriam pretermittit. Nec est verbum nisi stultorum dicere quod basilica ut lignum et lapides sit sancta mater ecclesia. Nam spiritus quos Deus vocat ad coronam desponsans in via, sunt sancta mater ecclesia; cuius domus est talis basilica cui in nomine sponsi et sponse est reverencia ymaginis tribuenda; quod fit potissime observata pro illo loco iusticia. Nam sanctitas vite et observacio iusticie plus honoraret locum ecclesie, quam totum aurum vel omnia iocalia corporalia huius mundi, sicut minimum peccatum plus ipsum inficeret in oculis sponsi, [p. 266] quam omnes turpitudines vel polluciones corporales possibiles. Nam Deus cuilibet creature quantumlibet turpi illabitur, ipsam causans, quem amorem non potest habere ad peccatum, unde (quantum ego concipio) domini temporales insteterunt prudenter pro loci reverencia et honore ecclesie. Nam sentencia domini mei, domini ducis, fuit quod conservaretur in eis quodcunque privilegium quod in honestatem loci sonuerit, quod utilitatem monachorum intulerit, quod ad veram misericordiam et pietatem fugitivis pertinuerit, quod honor regis ac iuramentum suum de conservando cuicunque legio suo iusticiam permiserit, in tantum quod audivimus eum dicere quod tollerabile foret ut profugi haberent ibi refugium in causa alicuius speciei criminis lese regie maiestatis. Defloracio, inquit, filie regis est una species criminis lese regie maiestatis, sed longe hoc a prodicione regni, a defensione iniurie Dei et proximi, et eciam a dampnacione anime incolentis locum illum ex defensione sceleris fugitivi. Ipse autem animose stetit singulariter pro istis iusticiis in tantum quod docuit ubi, per quam et quando oblate sunt sibi mille libre ad procurandum vel promittendum dictos fugitivos cum suo captivo ad terras inimicorum accedere, quod quia visum fuit sibi et regis consilio ipsis confederatis cum hostibus ad periculum regni vergere, noluit assentire. Unde omnino est irreligiositatis condicio invidorum auribus prurientibus de homine ficta scandalosa mendacia et graviter ferre de eo commenta laudabilia que per multos centenarios hominum sunt audita.

To this [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that something false is assumed in the mind of the objection; for there can be neither holiness nor prudence in defending this privilege expounded in its bare sense; hence whoever had approved this sense either had fruitfully repented or else are damned; nor is it shown which holy or approved men had approved that sense.[^7] But it has been perversely wrested, as a privilege, beyond the bounds of justice, against the king's oath and the prosperity of the kingdom; therefore a new remedy must be applied to so excessive an abuse. And this is the reason why the kingdom, no longer able to tolerate the growth of injury, has now applied a remedy rather than before.[^8] For a half-fool perceives and attacks a manifest injury after the wise man has passed over an [imperceptible]{g:insensibilis} injury. Nor is it the word of any but fools to say that the basilica, as wood and stones, is holy mother church. For the spirits whom God calls to the crown, espousing [them] in the [way]{g:via}, are holy mother church; whose house is such a basilica, to which, in the name of the [bridegroom]{g:sponsus} and the bride, the reverence of an image is to be paid; which is done above all when justice is observed for that place.[^9] For holiness of life and the observance of justice would honour the place of the church more than all the gold or all the bodily jewels of this world; just as the least sin would infect it more in the eyes of the bridegroom [266] than all possible bodily foulnesses or pollutions. For God [flows into]{g:illabi} every creature, however foul, causing it, a love which he cannot have toward sin; hence (as far as I understand) the [temporal lords]{g:temporales domini} pressed prudently for the reverence of the place and the honour of the church.[^10] For the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of my lord, the lord duke, was that there should be preserved in them whatever privilege tended to the honour of the place, brought benefit to the monks, pertained to true mercy and piety toward fugitives, [and] was permitted by the honour of the king and his oath to preserve justice for every liege man of his; so much so that we heard him say that it would be tolerable that fugitives should have refuge there in a case of some species of the crime of lèse-majesté. "The deflowering of the king's daughter," he said, "is one species of the crime of lèse-majesté"; but this is far from the betrayal of the kingdom, from the defence of injury to God and neighbour, and also from the damnation of the soul of one dwelling in that place, by the defence of the fugitive's crime.[^11] And he stood spiritedly, singularly, for these just claims, so much so that he showed where, through whom and when a thousand pounds were offered him to procure, or to promise, that the said fugitives should go with their captive to the lands of the enemies; to which, because it seemed to him and to the king's council, since they were confederate with the enemies, to tend to the danger of the kingdom, he would not assent.[^12] Hence it is wholly the mark of irreligion in the envious, with itching ears, [to take in] fictitious scandalous lies about a man, and to take hard the praiseworthy accounts of him that have been heard by many hundreds of men.[^13]

## ¶5 · 266.32–267.34

> [p. 266] Pars autem adversa visa est nobis omnino stare pro contrario; cum enim domum Dei decet sanctitudo tempore synagoge, quanto magis tempore legis gracie [p. 267] Christo eiciente vendentes et ementes de templo et dicente Matthei XXI°, 13: Domus mea domus oracionis vocabitur, vos autem fecistis illam speluncam latronum? Qualis ergo religio est intra ecclesiam defendere sceleratos, eciam licet evidencia probabili fuerint indurati? Aut qualis utilitas est monacho parare domum conductivam eciam induratis latronibus fugitivis, quamvis in fine vite eorum abbathia habuerit omnia latrocinia que apportant? Aut qualis religio est tercio tueri sceleratos sub pallio false pietatis ad dampnacionem anime eorum et infeccionem ecclesie Anglicane, ubi extra illam speluncam positi parendo iuri communi forent verisimiliter salubriter castigati? Vel quomodo staret quarto cum iuramento regis in recipiendo regni gubernacula, quod stat ut patet ex iuramento regis observacione iusticie cuicunque, ut veneno privilegii foveat eciam membra diaboli ad intoxicacionem et iniuriam regni sui? Nonne Ecclesiastici X°, 8, scribitur: Regnum de gente in gentem transfertur propter iniusticias atque iniurias? Sicut enim nichil plus facit ad stabilimentum regni quam observare cuicunque iusticiam et per punicionem prevaricatorum severam, sic nichil plus minatur ruinam regno quam constanter fovere iniurias. Et si obicitur quod iuxta istam sentenciam rex puniret homicidia inibi perpetrata, fateor, sed rectus ordo exigit, ut gravior iniusticia cicius corrigatur: modo vero ex inhabitacione sceleratorum in sanctuario, ex occisione religiosi intra ecclesiam, ex fraccione capitis religiosi eciam graduati intra ecclesiam per unum de suis confratribus, cum similibus que sonant cum habitantibus ecclesiam maioris peccati gravedinem, plus polluunt locum quam effusio sanguinis vel seminis in qua non tanta gravedo criminis est fundata.

But the opposing party seemed to us to stand wholly for the contrary; for since holiness becometh the house of God in the time of the synagogue, how much more in the time of the [law of grace]{g:lex gracie}, [267] when Christ cast out those who sold and bought from the temple and said (Matthew 21:13): "My house shall be called the house of prayer; but you have made it a den of thieves." What kind of religion, then, is it to defend criminals within the church, even though by probable [evidence]{g:evidencia} they have been hardened?[^14] Or what benefit is it to a monk to prepare a hired house even for hardened fugitive robbers, although at the end of their life the abbey should have all the plunder they bring? Or what kind of religion is it, third, to protect criminals under the cloak of false piety, to the damnation of their soul and the infection of the English church, when, placed outside that den, by obeying the common law they would probably have been wholesomely chastised? Or how, fourth, would it stand with the king's oath on receiving the government of the kingdom (which stands, as is clear from the king's oath, in the observance of justice to everyone) that by the poison of the privilege he should foster even members of the devil, to the poisoning and injury of his kingdom?[^15] Is it not written in Ecclesiasticus 10:8: "A kingdom is translated from one people to another, because of injustices, and wrongs"? For just as nothing does more for the establishment of a kingdom than to observe justice to everyone, and [that] through the severe punishment of [transgressors]{g:prevaricator}, so nothing more threatens ruin to a kingdom than constantly to foster injuries.[^16] And if it is objected that, according to this teaching, the king would punish the homicides perpetrated there, I confess it; but right order demands that the graver injustice be corrected sooner: but as things now are, [the deeds] arising from the dwelling of criminals in the sanctuary, from the killing of a [religious]{g:religiosi} within the church, from the breaking of the head of a religious, even a [graduate]{g:graduati}, within the church by one of his own brethren, together with like things which, among those who inhabit the church, sound of the greater weight of sin, pollute the place more than the shedding of blood or of seed, on which no such weight of crime is founded.[^17]

## Notes

[^1]: **S/C.** The sentence begins at 263.24 (12-c); the "second difficulty" is the jurist's claim (258.17–20, 12-b). Romans 3:8 ("let us do evil that there may come good", DR) and 7:8 ("sin, taking occasion by the commandment", DR), the latter looking forward to the distinction of occasions; Loserth prints *Rom. III° et VII°*. *Mala culpe*: evils of fault (sin), as against evils of punishment. *Casuales*: matters of chance, which may therefore fall at any time.

[^2]: **C/T.** Side-note 264.1. A and A₁ read *et accionem* (264.1). *Cum veris*: together with truths (admitted premisses), as at 253.29 (12-a). *Sensus vendicatur competere*: the sense is claimed to belong [to the privilege]. A and A₁ read *sive fuerit dignus sive fuerit* (264.6). *Declaratum est*: in 12-b and 12-c (e.g. 259.13–17).

[^3]: **C/T.** *Libertandi*: to be set free (given sanctuary). A and A₁ read *Dato premio* for *dato primo* (264.13). *Nichil sibi et privilegio libertanti*: "nothing [is there] between him and the freeing privilege", the idiom of *quid mihi et tibi*.

[^4]: **S/C.** *Peccabilem*: agrees with *naturam*; the nature that can sin keeps its freedom of choice. *Nec valet deduccio*: the deduction is no objection, since Wyclif grants its conclusion (*verum concluditur*). Psalm 19:7 (Vulgate 18:8; DR).

[^5]: **G/C/T.** Side-note 264.24. A reads *pravilegia … pravilegii* (264.25–26). *Inconveniens* here in its logical sense, "absurdity" (CONTEXTUAL, GLOSSARY §C), where 12-b and 12-c had "ill consequence". *Tam iniqua*: "just as wicked". *Contrarior iniusticia verbis, et intencioni privilegii*: the comma is the print's; *verbis* and *intencioni* are both taken with *contrarior*. *Illi loco et ordini*: the abbey and its order. *Secularium*: of seculars (GLOSSARY §B: the word keeps its range).

[^6]: **C/R.** A in the margin *Contra. Quarta obieccio* (264.33); side-notes 264.33, 265.1. *Vulgares* (265.1) closes the list of approvers, after kings, bishops and clerics. *Usurpare … violacionem*: to take upon ourselves the violation. *Servis … refugium*: presumably the custom by which a serf who lived a year and a day in a privileged town became free (to be checked, LEDGER E12d-1).

[^7]: **G/C.** A in the margin *Responsio* (265.9); side-note 265.9. *Hic dicitur*: "To this the answer is" (GLOSSARY §C). *Ad mentem obiectus*: in the sense the objection intends (*obiectus*, genitive of the fourth-declension noun). *Ad nudum sensum expositum*: expounded in its bare (unqualified) sense. *Quicunque … approbaverant … sunt dampnati*: the plural verbs are the print's.

[^8]: **C/R.** Side-note 265.19. *Sinistre extortum est ut privilegium …*: there is no verb after *ut*; taken as "wrested, as a privilege, beyond the bounds of justice"; it could be "wrested so that the privilege [reaches] beyond …". *Iuramentum regis*: the coronation oath (cf. 267.14). *Apposuit nunc remedium*: presumably the measure taken on sanctuary in the parliament of 1378 at Gloucester (context to be checked, LEDGER E12d-2). *Perante*: before.

[^9]: **C/T.** Side-note 265.24. B reads *et fit* (265.29). A and A₁ read *sanctitas vita* (265.30); A in the margin, without mark, *Conclusio. Contra pretensam immunitatem ecclesiarum* (265.30). *Semistultus … sciolus*: the half-fool sees the manifest injury that the knowing man had let pass as imperceptible. *Basilica*: the church building, the house (*domus*) of the church that is the company of spirits. *Reverencia ymaginis*: the building is honoured as an image of the church. *Desponsans in via*: espousing them in the way (GLOSSARY §A, *via / patria*).

[^10]: **G/C/T.** A in the margin *Nota* (266.2). *Illabitur*: flows into (GLOSSARY §D). *Quem amorem*: the relative draws *amorem* into its clause, "a love which"; God's flowing into and causing every creature is the love he cannot have toward sin. B reads *quam amorem* (266.3). *Insteterunt*: pressed, urged; not the disputation formula.

[^11]: **R/C/T.** Side-notes 266.7, 266.16. *Dominus dux*: John of Gaunt, duke of Lancaster (identification to be checked, LEDGER E12d-3). *Sentencia*: his position (CONTEXTUAL, "teaching"). *In eis*: in the churches. *Quod honor regis … permiserit*: the last of four *quod* clauses, with the king's honour and oath as subject. *Legio*: liege man. *Lese regie maiestatis*: treason against the king's majesty; A and A₁ lack *Defloracio … maiestatis* (266.16–17). *Incolentis locum illum*: of one dwelling in that place, presumably the monk who harbours the fugitive.

[^12]: **R/C.** The fugitives and their captive: presumably Robert Hauley and John Shakell, who fled to Westminster with the hostage they refused to surrender, the occasion of the killing of 1378 (context to be checked, LEDGER E12d-4). *Mille libre*: a thousand pounds. *Ipsis confederatis cum hostibus*: ablative absolute, "since they [the fugitives] were confederate with the enemies".

[^13]: **S/C.** Side-note 266.27. *Auribus prurientibus*: cf. 2 Timothy 4:3 ("having itching ears", DR). The first clause has no infinitive; "[to take in]" is supplied, parallel to *graviter ferre*. *Commenta laudabilia*: praiseworthy accounts, without the sense "fictions" that *commenta mendacii* had at 250.20. *Centenarios*: hundreds.

[^14]: **S.** A in the margin *Nota* (267.2); side-note 267.4. *Pars … adversa*: those who defended the privilege. "Holiness becometh thy house": Psalm 93:5 (Vulgate 92:5, DR), here of *domum Dei*. Matthew 21:13 (DR). *Evidencia probabili*: probable evidence, short of proof.

[^15]: **C/R/T.** A and A₁ read *conducticiam* for *conductivam* (267.6–7); side-notes 267.9, 267.14. *Domum conductivam*: a hired house, a lodging for hire; *speluncam*, "den", is taken up from Matthew 21:13. *Iuri communi*: the common law. *Quod stat … observacione iusticie cuicunque*: the oath consists in the observance of justice to everyone (the coronation oath's promise, to be checked, LEDGER E12d-5). A reads *pravilegii* (267.16).

[^16]: **S.** Ecclesiasticus (Sirach) 10:8 (DR), which Wyclif breaks off after *iniurias*: the Vulgate continues *et contumelias, et diversos dolos* ("and injuries, and divers deceits"); he writes *de gente* for the Vulgate's *a gente*.

[^17]: **C/R/T.** A in the margin *Obieccio* (267.25); side-note 267.25. *Homicidia inibi perpetrata*: the killings at Westminster in 1378 (12-a, 252.26). *Modo vero ex … plus polluunt*: no subject is expressed for *polluunt*; taken as the deeds arising from (*ex*) the things named, with *similibus*. *Occisione religiosi*: the killing of a monk in the violation of 1378; *fraccione capitis religiosi eciam graduati … per unum de suis confratribus*: an incident not identified (both to be checked, LEDGER E12d-6). *Cum habitantibus ecclesiam*: "among those who inhabit the church". The apparatus records a B reading for *intra … maioris* (267.29), illegible in the OCR. *Effusio sanguinis vel seminis*: the grounds on which canon law holds a church polluted.
