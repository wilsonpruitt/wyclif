# *De potestate pape*, chapter 4, part 3: chrysostom on the lot; the hereditary priesthood

## ¶1 · 69.30–71.27

> [p. 69] Et quantum ad omnes leges que videntur sonare contrarium, omnes intelliguntur de legibus humanis in quantum conferunt ut ordinetur conformiter ad divinum iudicium et de inobedientibus ecclesie triumphanti; ideo ne sit contencio de [p. 70] istis, ordinavit Spiritus Sanctus quod teneremus nos in limitibus legis sue. Unde istam materiam de eleccionibus tangit beatus Crisostomus super I. Thim. V: Dignitas, inquit, magisterii et sacerdocii magna est et mirabilis et vere sentencia Dei indigens ut eum qui dignus est in medium deducat. Nec audivi alios tam insanos, quod dissentirent isti sentencie. Unde quantum ad eleccionem sic loquitur iste sanctus: Ita antiquitus fiebat, ita et modo fit, cum sine passione humana elecciones facimus, cum ad nil vite huius respicimus, non ad amiciciam, non ad odium; et si in discrecione non tanto spiritu participamus, sufficit voluntas bona in Deo ponere intronizacionem, quia neque apostoli spiritu participabant, cum Mathiam elegerant, sed oracioni rem committentes in cathalogo apostolorum Mathiam annumeraverunt neque ad dileccionem humanam aspiciebant. Et ita in omni eleccione fieri oporteret, ubi electores sunt ambigui quis est habilior apud Deum. Videtur michi quod debent instar apostolorum sortes mittere; devote committendo sibi iudicium, sicut dicit iste sanctus, quia nemo diffiniret contra divinam sentenciam nec in materia ambigua, in qua non habuerit racionem naturalem, testimonium scripture vel revelacionem. Nec est factum apostolorum, a Deo tam crebro in lege veteri exemplatum; magis cerimoniale vel reprobandum, quam leges gentiles in ecclesia minori auctoritate vel evidencia introducte. Ideo secundum [p. 71] Crysostomum cum ista via sit secura et magis utilis propter multa et illam despicimus, qualiter Deus abscondita revelabit? Est enim intencio huius sancti quod apostoli in eligendo episcopos habuerunt revelacionem vel propheciam. Que, inquit, opera secundum nil humanum fiebant, a prophecia et spiritu sancto fiebant. Prophecia enim non est solum dicere futura sed presencia, cum Saul secundum propheciam monstratus est in vasis occultatus; Deus enim iustis revelat et prophecia fuit hec dicere: Separate, inquit, michi Paulum et Barnabam; ita et Thimoteus electus est a Paulo, qui multas prophecias habuit, ut quando circumcidit Thimoteum et intronizavit. Et ideo dicit, quod Deus eum elegit, non humanum iudicium, quod intronizatus recoleret custodiendo Dei depositum. Quando igitur electores spiritu ducuntur, alieni a vento calliditatis humane, tunc est simplex institucio vel multorum eleccio legitima; et aliter est infecta. Ideo debent electores postposito omni humano iudicio committere se divino regimini, sicut geromantici in punctuando suspendunt fantasiam propriam et committunt impulsum ad motum influencie celestis corporis. Et patet quod quilibet istorum trium modorum eligendi est laudibilis, quando fit conformiter ad eleccionem divinam.

And as to all the laws that seem to sound the contrary, they are all understood of human laws in so far as they contribute to [an election's] being ordered in conformity with the divine judgement, and of those disobedient to the [church triumphant]{g:ecclesia-triumphans}; therefore, lest there be contention about [70] these, the Holy Spirit ordained that we should keep ourselves within the [limits]{g:limites} of his law.[^1] Hence blessed Chrysostom touches this matter of elections on 1 Timothy, [homily] 5: "The dignity," he says, "of the [teaching office]{g:magisterium} and of the priesthood is great and wonderful, and truly in need of God's [sentence]{g:sentencia}, that it may bring forth into the midst the one who is worthy."[^2] Nor have I heard of others so insane as to dissent from this teaching. Hence, as to election, this saint speaks thus: "So it was done of old, and so it is done now, when we make elections without human passion, when we look to nothing of this life, not to friendship, not to hatred; and if in discernment we do not share in so great a spirit, a good will suffices to place the enthronement in God; for neither did the apostles share in the spirit when they elected Matthias, but, committing the matter to prayer, they numbered Matthias in the catalogue of the apostles, nor did they look to human affection. And so it ought to be done in every election where the electors are in doubt who is more [qualified]{g:habilis} before God."[^3] It seems to me that they ought, after the pattern of the apostles, to cast lots, devoutly committing the judgement to him, as this saint says; for no one should define against the divine sentence, nor in a doubtful matter in which he has neither natural reason, nor the testimony of scripture, nor revelation. Nor is the deed of the apostles, so often exemplified by God in the old law, more ceremonial or more to be rejected than the gentile laws introduced into the [church]{g:ecclesia} with less authority or [evidence]{g:evidencia}.[^4] Therefore, according to [71] Chrysostom, "since this way is secure and more useful for many reasons, and we despise it, how will God reveal hidden things?"[^5] For this saint's meaning is that the apostles, in electing bishops, had revelation or prophecy. "Which works," he says, "were done according to nothing human; they were done by prophecy and the Holy Spirit. For prophecy is not only to tell things to come but things present, as when Saul, according to prophecy, was shown hidden among the vessels; for God reveals to the just; and it was prophecy to say these things: 'Separate me,' he says, 'Paul and Barnabas'; so also Timothy was elected by Paul, who had many prophecies, as when he circumcised Timothy and enthroned [him]."[^6] And therefore he says that "God elected him, not human judgement, so that, enthroned, he might call it to mind by guarding God's deposit".[^7] When, therefore, electors are led by the spirit, strangers to the wind of human craft, then a simple institution or an election by many is lawful; and otherwise it is infected. Therefore electors ought, setting aside all human judgement, to commit themselves to divine governance, as geomancers in pricking out their points suspend their own fancy and commit the impulse to the motion of the influence of a heavenly body.[^8] And it is clear that each of these three modes of electing is praiseworthy when it is done in conformity with the divine election.

## ¶2 · 71.28–72.7

> [p. 71] Patet insuper quod eleccio adinventa humanitus est artificiosior sed callidior et postquam avari plus sunt terrenis dediti quam merito et labori officii est periculosior. Eleccio vero per sortem est securior, magis autentica et per consequens [p. 72] nunc eligibilior. Sed cupiditas et fastus obviant; dicitur enim quod nostri electores discernunt melius sicut apostoli; nec tales blasfemias credo cessare antequam status prelatorum sint tantum extenuati a commodo temporali quod officium capiatur propter meritum et non honorem vel commodum temporale.

It is clear moreover that election devised humanly is more artful but craftier, and, since the avaricious are given more to earthly things than to the merit and labour of the office, it is more dangerous. But election by lot is more secure, more [authentic]{g:autenticus}, and consequently [72] now more to be chosen.[^9] But cupidity and pride stand in the way; for it is said that our electors discern better, as the apostles [did]; nor do I believe that such blasphemies will cease before the estates of [prelates]{g:prelatus} are so far thinned of temporal advantage that the office is taken for merit and not for honour or temporal advantage.[^10]

## ¶3 · 72.8–72.15

> [p. 72] Quantum ad quartum modum instituendi summum sacerdotem iure hereditario patet quod hoc fuit ex ordinacione Dei olim autenticum, quia Exod. XXVIII et XXIX docetur quomodo filii Aaron forent sacerdotes iure perpetuo, et idem patet Numeri XXV de Finees, filio Eleazari, filii Aaron cum genere suo; quod fungeretur sacerdocio iure perpetuo.

As to the fourth mode of instituting the high priest, by hereditary [right]{g:ius}, it is clear that this was once authentic by God's [ordinance]{g:ordinacio}, since Exodus 28 and 29 teaches how the sons of Aaron should be priests by perpetual right; and the same is clear in Numbers 25 of Phinees, son of Eleazar, son of Aaron, with his race, that he should exercise the priesthood by perpetual right.[^11]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 70.4: Chrysostom is in favour of lot where choice is doubtful.
- 71.5: The apostles had divine guidance in their choice.
- 72.8: Hereditary succession was allowed in the old law.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Intelliguntur de legibus humanis*: the contrary laws are to be read as human laws, binding only so far as they serve conformity with God's judgement, and as aimed at those disobedient to the church triumphant. The construal is uncertain: *de legibus humanis* may instead mean "[as] concerning human [elections]".

[^2]: **S/G/R.** Chrysostom, *Homilies on 1 Timothy*, hom. 5; Loserth: "In Epist. I ad Timoth. cap. I, Hom. V (Migne), tom. XI" (70.4): Wyclif's "V" is the homily, not the chapter (LEDGER P04c-1, to be checked). *Magisterium* is the teaching office here, beside the priesthood (GLOSSARY §D); *sentencia Dei* is God's decision, "sentence" (CONTEXTUAL), and *isti sentencie* in the next sentence is "teaching". B in marg.: *Nota* (70.7).

[^3]: **T.** The manuscripts read *respicientes* (70.11) and B *participant* (70.15); at 70.17 the manuscripts read *cum Mathiam* (the note is unclear in the scan). They lack *fieri oporteret*, which Loserth supplied from Chrysostom's text (70.19). The quotation runs to *apud Deum* by Loserth's italics.

[^4]: **C.** *Committendo sibi*: committing the judgement to God. *Diffiniret*, "should define": determine the case.

[^5]: **S.** Loserth gives Chrysostom's words: *si vero manifesta pretermittimus, quomodo nobis Deus obscura et incerta revelabit?* (71.1; LEDGER P04c-2, to be checked). Wyclif paraphrases; we follow Loserth's italics in quoting.

[^6]: **T/S.** 1 Kings (1 Sam.) 10:22: the Vulgate reads *ecce absconditus est domi* (DR "Behold he is hidden at home"); "among the vessels" is Chrysostom's (LEDGER P04c-3, to be checked). *Iustis* is Loserth's correction; the manuscripts read *in istis* (71.10). Acts 13:2: Wyclif reads *Paulum* for the Vulgate's *Saulum* (DR "Separate me Saul and Barnabas"). Acts 16:3 (Timothy circumcised).

[^7]: **S.** 1 Tim. 6:20 (DR "keep that which is committed to thy trust"). *Recoleret*, "might call it to mind": that God, not man, chose him.

[^8]: **C/R.** *Simplex institucio*: the second way, institution by one man; *multorum eleccio*: election, the fourth way of 68.12. *Geromantici*: geomancers, who mark points at random and read figures from them (the print's spelling). B in marg.: *Nota* (71.20).

[^9]: **C.** *Eligibilior*, "more to be chosen", plays on *eleccio*. *Postquam* is causal, "since". *Vero* is adversative here: "but".

[^10]: **C.** *Discernunt melius sicut apostoli*: either "discern better [than the lot], as the apostles [did]" or "discern as well as the apostles"; we translate the first. *Status prelatorum*, "the estates of prelates".

[^11]: **S/C/R.** Exod. 29:9 (DR "they shall be priests to me by a perpetual ordinance"); Num. 25:13 (DR "the covenant of the priesthood for ever"). *Ius* here is a right of succession. "Fourth": after the three ways just discussed (lot, institution, election); in Armachanus's list (68.10–12) hereditary succession is third. A in marg.: *Summi pontificis institucio iure hereditario* (72.8).
