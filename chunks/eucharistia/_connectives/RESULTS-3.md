## Doubtful, left as they are

- **01-e, p. 25, and 02-a, p. 31**: *Consencio **autem** sancte Romane ecclesie* (the oath *Ego Berengarius*, quoted twice), rendered "And I consent" both times. This is judged in the decree's own sentence. There it follows the anathema on the old heresy (*anathematizo omnem heresim …*), so it turns from the abjuration to the positive profession: either (c), the next clause of the oath, or (b), the turn from rejecting to consenting. "And" is defensible, and the two places agree. If one changes, change both.
- **01-e, p. 27**: *Videtur **autem** sanctum intelligere figurative per Christum hostiam consecratam*, rendered "And the saint seems". This is Wyclif's reading of the Jerome text just quoted, so it could be (b), "But", against the literal sense of "consecrates Christ". It does not answer an objection, though; it explains the authority it brought in. That makes it continuative (c). Left.
- **02-a, p. 30**: *quia cum oportet ipsum esse accidens, quantitas **autem** est primum accidens post genus substancie, oportet …*, rendered "since it must be an accident, and quantity is the first accident". This is logically a minor premiss: the sacrament must be an accident; now quantity is the first accident; so it is quantity. But both premisses sit inside the one *cum* clause, and "now" cannot stand inside an English subordinate clause. "And" carries the coordination. A recast ("… an accident; now quantity is the first accident …, so …") is possible but is a syntax change, not a connective swap. Left.
- **02-a, p. 33**: *Quod **autem** plus consonat sensui et racioni patet inferius*, rendered "And that it is more consonant". Unlike the p. 31 change above, this premiss was already stated and used at the end of ¶6 (*prior sentencia plus consonat sensui, racioni, sanctis doctoribus …, igitur*). The *autem* starts the proof of that premiss, which extends the step already taken: (c), "And". "Now" would also be defensible.
- **02-b, p. 35**: ***Sed** quis tunc crederet verbis nostris …? Oportet **ergo** glosare*, rendered "Now who then would believe". The card's look-ahead rule applies: an *ergo* follows, so (a). Note that the same rhetorical-question *sed* was left as "But" in waves 1–2 (01-a p. 15, 01-c p. 21), where no *ergo* followed. Consistent with the rule, noted only.
- **02-b, p. 35**: *Ydemptica est … **Sed** predicacio tropica … est*, rendered "But tropical". This sets the second member of a distinction against the first, so (b) "But" is fine. "And" would also do. Left.
