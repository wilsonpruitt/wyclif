# *De eucharistia*, De eucharistia et penitencia, chapter 3: who has power to make the law of yearly confession

## ¶1 · 334.2–335.11

> [p. 334] Videtur rimandum ulterius quis habet potestatem condere tales leges. Et videtur multis probabile quod solus dominus temporis qui est Deus, quia solus iste habet potestatem super longitudinem terre, super racionem legis et super genus hominum obligandum. Ideo papa non habet potestatem condendi hanc legem, sicut non Deus voluit ipsam condere, cum concessit libertatem homini penitencie huiusmodi repugnantem. Hortari autem posset papa vel alius ad taliter penitendum. Sed quod omnis homo qui discrecionem attigerit et proprium sacerdotem paratum habuerit, confiteatur semel in anno omnia peccata sua proprio sacerdoti sub pena dampnacionis, non subiacet potestati papali, cum nec potest dampnare hominem nec salvare, licet filii diaboli et filii Dei sunt occasio dampnacionis multorum et quare alii sunt salvati. Nec habet papa potestatem ordinandi quod perpetuo sint tales sacerdotes ydonei aut parati; nec habet potestatem obligandi racionem quod semel in anno sit sibi sufficiens, quin per idem semel in biennio vel quolibet alio tempore et de multis preservatis ex Dei gracia pro nullo tempore hoc oportet. Ideo dicunt aliqui quod verificatum in isto loco est dictum Apostoli II Thess. II quod Antichristus extollitur super omne quod dicitur Deus, eo quod Christus istam legem ordinare non potuit, si volebat. Sic ergo in quatuor religionibus privatis sepedictis licet quedam facere que regule istarum religionum precipiunt stante [p. 335] semper Domini libertate et non licet istis tradicionibus ut legi Domini taliter obligari: sic senciendum est de hac tradicione pape qui est patronus prime secte. Observet ergo fidelis christianus hanc libertatem taliter confitendi quod confiteatur taliter sacerdoti, si et quatinus prosit sibi et ecclesie. Et istam libertatem habet concessam a Domino quam papa non potest destruere neque dare. Et per hoc patet solucio ad dicta Augustini et sanctorum similium quod homo debet presbytero confiteri, quando Deus movet peccantem humiliter confiteri de peccatis propriis que commisit, tunc debet taliter confiteri.

It seems we must search out further who has the [power]{g:potestas} to make such laws. And it seems probable to many that [it is] only the [lord]{g:dominus} of time, who is God; for he alone has power over the length of the earth, over the reason of the law, and over the race of men that is to be bound.[^1] Therefore the [pope]{g:papa} does not have the power to make this law, just as God did not will to make it, since he granted to man a freedom [incompatible]{g:repugnare} with penance of this kind. But the pope or another could exhort [men] to do penance in this way. But that "every man who has reached discretion and has his own [priest]{g:sacerdos} ready should confess all his sins once a year to his own priest", on pain of [damnation]{g:dampnacio}, is not subject to papal power, since he can neither damn a man nor save him, although sons of the devil and sons of God are the occasion of the damnation of many and [the reason] why others are saved.[^2] Nor has the pope power to ordain that there always be such priests, fit or ready; nor has he power to bind reason [to hold] that once a year is sufficient for him, without its being needed, by the same token, once in two years or at any other interval; and for many who are preserved by the grace of God this is needed at no time at all.[^3] Therefore some say that in this place the saying of the Apostle, 2 Thess. 2, [is true]{g:verificari}, that "Antichrist is lifted up above all that is called God", in that Christ could not ordain this law, if he willed it.[^4] Therefore, as in the four [private religions]{g:religio-privata} so often mentioned it is lawful to do certain things which the rules of those religions command, the freedom of the Lord [335] always standing, and it is not lawful to be bound to these traditions in this way as to the law of the Lord: so we must think of this tradition of the pope, who is the [patron]{g:patronus} of the first [sect]{g:secta}.[^5] Let the faithful Christian therefore keep this freedom of confessing in such a way that he confesses so to a priest if and insofar as it profits him and the [church]{g:ecclesia}. And this freedom he has as granted by the Lord, which the pope can neither destroy nor give. And by this the [resolution]{g:solucio} is clear to the sayings of Augustine and of [saints]{g:sancti} like him, that a man ought to confess to a [presbyter]{g:presbiter}: when God moves the sinner to confess humbly the sins of his own that he has committed, then he ought to confess in this way.[^6]

## ¶2 · 335.12–336.17

> [p. 335] Sed videtur multis quod generalis confessio et publica est melior quam confessio hec privata, utraque tamen est bona, licet in lege veteri et lege gracie prima sit magis fundabilis quam secunda; nec solum presbiteris est illa confessio facienda sed discretis tam viris quam feminis et omnimode Deo nostro. Et de illa generali confessione videtur loqui Jacobus, quando dicit: Confitemini alterutrum peccata vestra; et aliter usus ecclesie quod sacerdos confitetur peccata sua tam viris quam feminis ipsis existentibus quandoque solum in missarum suarum iniciis foret culpabiliter introductus. Et sic quidam sophisticant cum lege papali audientes missas proprii sacerdotis quandoque solitarii, dicentes quod fuerunt tunc confessi omnia peccata sua proprio sacerdoti. Sed videtur multis quod sophisticacio ista non sufficit cuicunque, cum multi necessitantur ostendere peccata sua discreto presbytero et recipere ab ipso salubre consilium quomodo [p. 336] de peccato preterito satisfaciant et pro peccato futuro de possibili precavebunt. Et tales fuerunt multi presbyteri tempore Augustini. Et sic concedo dictum Augustini quod homo debet, ymmo necessitatur confiteri peccata sua secundum formam quam Deus limitat. Sed absit ex isto concludere quod racionabile est omnem hominem confiteri semel in anno omnia peccata sua proprio sacerdoti, ut salubre est homini audire tempore congruo verbum Dei, sicut salubre est homini quoad corpus pasci cibario corporali. Sed insanum foret homini tempus statuere in quo sub pena mortis taliter sermonem audiat sive edat. Deus enim dedit homini liberum arbitrium et multis specialem instinctum, quando et qualiter debent taliter operari. Ideo nimia extollencia super Christum foret sic generaliter condere talem legem.

But it seems to many that general and public [confession]{g:confessio} is better than this private confession, though each is good, although in the old law and [the law of grace]{g:lex-gracie} the first is better founded than the second; nor is that confession to be made only to presbyters, but to discreet persons, men as well as women, and in every way to our God.[^7] And of that general confession James seems to speak when he says: "Confess your sins one to another";[^8] and otherwise the [use]{g:usus} of the church, by which the priest confesses his sins to men as well as women when they are present, sometimes only at the beginnings of his masses, would have been culpably introduced.[^9] And so some [play the sophist]{g:sophisticari} with the papal law: hearing the masses of their own priest, sometimes alone, they say that they had then confessed all their sins to their own priest. But it seems to many that this [sophistry]{g:sophisticacio} does not suffice for everyone, since many are necessitated to show their sins to a discreet presbyter and to receive from him wholesome counsel on how [336] they may [make satisfaction]{g:satisfacere} for past sin and, as far as possible, guard against future sin. And such were many presbyters in the time of Augustine. And so I grant the saying of Augustine that a man ought, indeed is necessitated, to confess his sins according to the form that God [assigns]{g:limitare}. But far be it to [infer]{g:concludere} from this that it is reasonable for every man to confess all his sins once a year to his own priest, as it is wholesome for a man to hear the word of God at a fitting time, just as it is wholesome for a man, as to the body, to be fed with bodily food.[^10] Now it would be insane to fix for a man a time in which, on pain of death, he must so hear a sermon or eat. For God has given man [free choice]{g:liberum-arbitrium}, and to many a special prompting, when and how they ought to act so. Therefore it would be excessive [exaltation]{g:extollencia} above Christ to make such a law so generally.[^11]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 334.2: God only has the right to make such laws.
- 334.7: The Pope may not take away the freedom God has given.
- 334.16: He cannot damn nor save; nor can he provide fit priests everywhere.
- 335.3: Let the Christian use his freedom and confess if it profits him and the Church.
- 335.12: General and public confession is better than private, though both are good.
- 335.18: Confession need not be made only to priests.
- 335.25: How some evade the decree.
- 336.6: A man should judge when he will confess, as he judges when he will hear a sermon or take tood. So doing he uses rightly the free will God gives him.

## Notes

[^1]: **C/T.** *Dominus temporis*: the law binds for a time (*semel in anno*), so only the lord of time can make it; D reads *dominus talis*, E *talis* corrected to *temporis*. *Super longitudinem terre*: power over the earth's extent, i.e. over every place the law would bind (cf. 334.19, priests ready everywhere); the phrase could also mean the earth's duration, with *temporis*.

[^2]: **S/C.** The quoted clause takes up the wording of *Omnis utriusque sexus* (X 5.38.12: *postquam ad annos discretionis pervenerit … saltem semel in anno proprio sacerdoti*; LEDGER Kpen03-1). *Dampnacio*: here "damnation", the eternal sense. *Occasio … et quare*: sons of the devil are the occasion of others' damnation, sons of God the reason others are saved; neither damns or saves.

[^3]: **C.** *Obligandi racionem quod …*: to bind reason to the claim that a year suffices; *quin per idem*: he cannot fix the year without the same argument serving for two years or any other term, and for some no term at all. *Sibi* is the penitent. B reads *sint sibi*; D *pro nomine tempore*.

[^4]: **S/C.** 2 Thess. 2:4, Vulgate *extollitur supra omne quod dicitur Deus* (DR "is lifted up above all that is called God"); Wyclif has *super* and names *Antichristus* as subject, where the Vulgate has the relative *qui adversatur*. *Eo quod … si volebat*: the pope makes a law that Christ could not make even if he willed it, since it would take away the freedom he granted.

[^5]: **C.** *Patronus prime secte*: the pope as head of the first of the sects. The four private religions *sepedicte* and their order are not named here; in Wyclif's fixed order they are the caesarean clergy, monks, canons and friars (*De quatuor sectis novellis* c. 1, Polemical Works I p. 242: *sacerdos cesareus, monachus, canonicus atque frater*), and the caesarean priesthood *a papa et cardinalibus capitaliter capit originem* (p. 243), so the pope is patron of the first (LEDGER Kpen03-2). DEF read *in quatuor et religionibus*; D *sepedictis hec quedam facere*; AC are reported as *extinxit*, which does not construe here.

[^6]: **C.** *Solucio ad dicta Augustini*: the sayings answered are those cited at 332.17 (chapter 2). The *quod*-clause gives the sayings; the *quando … tunc* clause is Wyclif's reading of them. E's margin: *Solucio ad dicta*.

[^7]: **C.** *Prima … secunda*: the general and public confession is better grounded in scripture than the private. *Omnimode Deo nostro*: confession is to be made to God in every case.

[^8]: **S.** Jas. 5:16; Wyclif omits the Vulgate's *ergo* (DR "Confess therefore your sins one to another").

[^9]: **S/C.** The *Confiteor* said by the priest at the beginning of mass, in the people's hearing (Loserth, citing the *Lay Folks Mass Book*, ed. Simmons, p. 6, ll. 41–44 (the line quoted is l. 43): "til alle tho folk he shryves him thare"; LEDGER Kpen03-3). *Ipsis existentibus quandoque solum*: read with *in missarum suarum iniciis*; BCE read *solis*, "to them alone".

[^10]: **C.** *Ut salubre est … sicut salubre est*: the comparison grants that confession, like hearing the word and eating, is wholesome; the next sentence denies that a fixed time under pain may be imposed for any of them.

[^11]: **T/C.** *Condere* is Loserth's emendation (cf. 334.3, *condere tales leges*); the manuscripts read *concedere*. *Extollencia* takes up *extollitur* of 2 Thess. 2:4 above.
