# *De eucharistia*, chapter 4, part 4: the blessing before the words, why the evangelists vary, and truth and figure together

## ¶1 · 94.29–95.7

> [p. 94] In isto autem exemplatur infirmo vel in alia necessitate posito quod licet sibi prandenti accipere corpus Christi; cum devotus audiens missam post gentaculum melius sepe accipit quam sacerdos suus [p. 95] qui celebrat: Melius eciam celebraret in casu sacerdos eadem die artificiali post nonam et prandium quam institutis humanitus quantumcunque ordinata apparencia celebrando, sicut melius in casu comederet tam clericus quam laycus corpus Christi, predicando vel opus meritorium faciendo quam conficiendo, quia copiosius haberet Christum in anima.

And in this an example is given for the sick man, or one placed in some other necessity: that it is lawful for him, having dined, to receive the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi};[^1] since a devout man hearing mass after breakfast often receives better than his [priest]{g:sacerdos} [95] who celebrates. Also, in a given case, a priest would celebrate better on the same [artificial day]{g:dies-artificialis} after nones and dinner than by celebrating with institutions made [humanly]{g:humanitus}, however ordered their appearance;[^2] just as in a given case both a cleric and a layman would eat the body of Christ better by preaching or doing a meritorious work than by [consecrating]{g:conficere}, because he would have Christ more abundantly in the soul.

## ¶2 · 95.8–95.14

> [p. 95] Secundo obicitur per hoc quod Mattheus et Marcus narrant quomodo Christus accepit panem, benedixit fregit et dixit: Accipite et comedite, hoc est corpus meum, ubi videtur benediccio Christi nobis abscondita precedere verba que nos ponimus consecracionis; aliter enim non sequeremur Christum frangendo post confeccionem hostiam consecratam.

Second, [it is objected, by appeal to]{g:obicitur-per} this, that Matthew and Mark [relate]{g:narrare} how Christ "took bread, blessed, broke and said: Take ye and eat. This is my body",[^3] where a blessing of Christ hidden from us seems to precede the words which we lay down as those of consecration; for otherwise we should not follow Christ in breaking the [consecrated host]{g:hostia} after the [consecration]{g:confeccio}.[^4]

## ¶3 · 95.15–97.28

> [p. 95] Hic dicitur quod benedicere accipitur in proposito pro gracias agere, modo quo omnis creatura sed precipue racionalis benedicit Deum. Unde in confirmacionem istius sentencie, ubi Mattheus et Marcus dicunt benedixit Lucas et Paulus dicunt: Gracias egit vel gracias agens fregit, et in isto nos sacerdotes Christum sequimur modo nostro; debemus enim in omnibus operibus nostris sequi Christum; vel quadam propinqua similitudine, ut quoad opera humanitatis sue vel quadam analoga proporcione ut quoad opera divinitatis et primogeniture sue, et diversi debent sequi eum diversimode secundum quod Christus debet esse regula status sui, ut posito duce exercitus qui errare non poterit et quem severe iniunctum est quodlibet membrum exercitus sequi modo suo, patet quod aliqui sequerentur uno incessu et aliqui alio, [p. 96] secundum differenciam qua post ipsum sunt positi. Aliqui eciam sequerentur ipsum propinquius et alii distancius secundum quod in calore et virtute secundum locum motiva et organis variantur; sic dispari incessu secuntur fideles Christum secundum disparitatem statuum et gracie quibus capitaneus illos vocat. Omnes tamen salvandos oportet sequi hunc ducem (ut docet Crisostomus In Imperfecto Omelia VII°), quia ipse est via sine qua nemo potest celestem civitatem attingere. Unde ait supra: Triplex ponitur causa, quare quatuor evangeliste qui sunt organa Spiritus Sancti tantum in istis verbis sacramentalibus variabant; potuit enim facillime facere omnes illos quatuor similime concordare; sed ista variacio fuit magis conveniens, primo ut doceret per hoc predictam veritatem katholicam quomodo totus christianus exercitus debet dispariter sequi Christum; sicut istorum quatuor evangelistarum discipuli quadam varietate decenti sub variis formis conficiunt sacramentum, et omnes sequuntur Christum; secundo ut iste subtilis et realis philosophus ostendat se habere curam parvam de signis sed principaliter attendere ad sentenciam designatam, et tercio ut lex sua in varietate verborum que sunt nobis exercicia sit gravidata multiformi sentencia. Non enim est aliqua istorum evangelistarum quantumcunque parva varietas, quin trahit secum notandam subtilitatem sentencie, ut in sacramento sanguinis singulariter fit mencio de testamento, quamvis tam consecracio panis quam calicis ad novum testamentum pertineat; cuius misterium credo esse quod [p. 97] liquor solet effundi in lege veteri in testimonium contracti federis, in signum quod sanguis eius effundi debeat qui fedus illud diruperit, ut patet; Christus autem authonomatice effudit sanguinem suum preciosum in testimonium federis inter sponsum et ecclesiam et pene dissipancium legem suam, et sic effusione sanguinis Christi testamentum suum confirmatum est. Cui sentencie videtur Zachariam alludere, dum IX° capitulo sic alloquitur Jesum nostrum: Tu quoque in sanguine testamenti tui eduxisti vinctos tuos de lacu in quo non est aqua. Istud autem misterium non sic pertinenter exprimeretur faciendo cum verbis sacramenti panis memoriam testamenti. Sed notandum ulterius quod evangelium Luc. XXII°, 20 habet sic: Hic est calix novum testamentum in meo sanguine. Evangelium autem Apostoli I. Cor. XI°, 25 habet simpliciter: Hic calix novum testamentum est in meo sanguine, ubi patet primo quod accipitur methonomatice contentum pro continente. Et patet secundo quod loquitur figurative et non ydemptice, quando dicit quod calix est novum testamentum, ut vel sic manuducamur ad intelligendum sensum verborum quibus eukaristia est confecta; unde Math. XXVI°, 28 sic loquitur: Hic est sanguis meus novi testamenti. Et eandem seriem habet Marc. XIV°, 24, ubi utrobique demonstratur vinum in calice; et sic verborum istorum quatuor evangelistarum diversitas plena est misterio.

Here [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that "to bless" is taken in the present case for "to give thanks", in the way in which every [creature]{g:creatura}, but especially the rational, blesses God. Hence, in confirmation of this [teaching]{g:sentencia},[^5] where Matthew and Mark say "he blessed", Luke and Paul say: "he gave thanks", or "giving thanks, broke"; and in this we priests follow Christ in our manner. For we ought in all our works to follow Christ: either by a certain near likeness, as with regard to the works of his [humanity]{g:humanitas}, or by a certain analogous proportion, as with regard to the works of his [divinity]{g:divinitas} and his [primogeniture]{g:primogenitura};[^6] and different men ought to follow him differently, according as Christ ought to be the rule of each one's [state]{g:status}. As, supposing a leader of an army who cannot err, and whom every [member]{g:membrum} of the army has been strictly enjoined to follow in his own manner, it is clear that some would follow with one gait and some with another, [96] according to the difference by which they are placed after him. Some also would follow him more nearly and others more distantly, according as they vary in heat, and in the power of moving with respect to place, and in their organs; so with unequal gait the faithful follow Christ according to the disparity of states and of grace in which the [captain]{g:capitaneus} calls them. Yet all who are to be saved must follow this leader (as Chrysostom teaches in the *Imperfect Work*, Homily 7), for he is the [way]{g:via} without which no one can reach the heavenly city.[^7] Hence he says above: A threefold cause is laid down why the four evangelists, who are the organs of the Holy Spirit, varied so much in these sacramental words; for he could very easily have made all those four agree most closely; but this variation was more suitable:[^8] first, that he might teach by it the aforesaid catholic truth, how the whole Christian army ought to follow Christ unequally, just as the disciples of these four evangelists consecrate the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} under various forms with a certain becoming variety, and all follow Christ; second, that this subtle and real philosopher might show that he has little care for [signs]{g:signum} but principally attends to the sense designated; and third, that his law, in the variety of words which are exercises for us, might be made pregnant with multiform sense.[^9] For there is no variety of these evangelists, however small, that does not draw with it a subtlety of sense worth noting: as mention of the testament is made in the sacrament of the blood [alone]{g:singulariter}, although the consecration both of the [bread]{g:panis} and of the [chalice]{g:calix} pertains to the new testament.[^10] The [mystery]{g:misterium} of this I believe to be that [97] in the old law a liquid used to be poured out in witness of a covenant contracted, in sign that the blood of him who should break that covenant ought to be shed, as is clear; and Christ, [by antonomasia]{g:autonomatice}, shed his precious blood in witness of the covenant between the [bridegroom]{g:sponsus} and the [church]{g:ecclesia}, and of the penalty of those who dissipate his law; and so by the shedding of the blood of Christ his testament was confirmed.[^11] To this teaching Zechariah seems to allude, when in chapter 9 he thus addresses our Jesus: "Thou also by the blood of thy testament hast brought forth thy prisoners out of the pit, wherein is no water."[^12] And this mystery would not be so pertinently expressed by making mention of the testament with the words of the sacrament of the bread. But it is to be noted further that the gospel, Luke 22:20, has thus: "This is the chalice, the new testament in my blood." And the gospel of the Apostle, 1 Cor. 11:25, has [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}: "This chalice is the new testament in my blood";[^13] where it is clear, first, that the contained is taken [metonymically]{g:methonomatice} for the container.[^14] And it is clear, second, that he speaks [figuratively]{g:figurative} and not [identically]{g:ydemptice} when he says that the chalice is the new testament, so that at least in this way we may be led by the hand to understand the [meaning]{g:sensus} of the words by which the Eucharist is consecrated; hence Matt. 26:28 speaks thus: "This is my blood of the new testament." And Mark 14:24 has the same sequence, where in both places the [wine]{g:vinum} in the chalice is demonstrated; and so the diversity of the words of these four evangelists is full of mystery.

## ¶4 · 97.29–98.14

> [p. 97] Et patet tercio quod non obest sed consonat, ut dicit decretalis Cum Marthe, quod in hoc sacramento [p. 98] sint simul veritas et figura; sacramentum enim figurat Christum et unionem cum ipso atque ecclesia; et vere facit atque figurat presenciam Christi sacramentalem ultra figuras alias. Unde sicut vane convincitur heresis super hoc quod Christus est creatura, quia Arrius illud dixit, sic nude et stulte arguitur sentenciam illam esse hereticam que dicit locucionem istam esse tropicam: Hoc est corpus meum, et quod illa hostia est figura ex hoc quod heretici illud dicunt. Illi enim non attendunt ad modum ac intencionem dicendi cum aliis superadditis, sed ad veritatem quam fatetur omnis hereticus quam diffamant, et tamen scitur quod in moribus est modus operis maxime attendendus.

And it is clear, third, that it is no obstacle but is consonant, as the [decretal]{g:decretalis} *Cum Marthe* says, that in this sacrament [98] there are at once truth and [figure]{g:figura}; for the sacrament [figures]{g:figurare} Christ and the union with him and with the church; and it [truly]{g:vere} makes and figures the sacramental presence of Christ beyond other figures.[^15] Hence, just as heresy is vainly [proved conclusively]{g:convincere} in this, that Christ is a creature, because Arius said it, so it is barely and foolishly argued that the teaching is heretical which says "this speech, *This is my body*, is [tropical speech]{g:locucio-tropica}" and "that host is a figure", from this, that heretics say it.[^16] For they do not attend to the manner and intention of the saying, with the other things added on top, but to the truth which every heretic confesses, which they defame; and yet it is known that in morals the manner of the work is most to be attended to.

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 94.29: In case of weakness or other need we may communicate after eating.
- 95.8: Objection: Christ blessed the bread before He used the words of consecration.
- 95.15: This blessing was (as Luke and Paul say) a giving of thanks.
- 96.9: Why the evangelists vary in reporting Christ's words.
- 97.29: In the sacrament are both truth and figure.
- 98.4: It does not follow that language is heretical, because it has been used by a heretic.

## Notes

[^1]: **T/C.** *Exemplatur*: "an example is given"; D reads *excipiatur*, "an exception is made" (94.29), which gives the same rule. The print construes and is translated.

[^2]: **C.** *Dies artificialis*: the day from sunrise to sunset, as against the natural day; *nonam*, the ninth hour. *Institutis humanitus quantumcunque ordinata apparencia*: two ablatives, the humanly made institutions and their appearance, however ordered. C *prandendo* (95.5).

[^3]: **S.** Matt. 26:26 (Loserth's reference) and its parallel in Mark 14, in the Douay–Rheims wording, abridged as Wyclif abridges it ("and gave to his disciples" is not quoted). Quoted to the editor's italics.

[^4]: **T.** The clause *aliter enim … hostiam consecratam* (95.13–14) is in D alone; ABCE omit it. Loserth prints it, and it is translated.

[^5]: **C/S.** *Sentencia* (CONTEXTUAL): of an interpretation held, "teaching" (also 97.5, 98.5); of the meaning words carry (*sentenciam designatam*, *multiformi sentencia*, *subtilitatem sentencie*), "sense", under the separate row. Luke 22:19 and 1 Cor. 11:24 (Douay–Rheims) for "he gave thanks" and "giving thanks, broke".

[^6]: **T.** *Primogeniture* is Loserth's: the codices read *progeniture* (95.25). Cf. *primogenitus* at 94.24.

[^7]: **S/T.** Chrysostom (so the Latin; now pseudo-Chrysostom), *Opus imperfectum in Matthaeum*, hom. 7 on Matt. 4:18–22 (PG 56, 673–676: following Christ *ad civitatem caelestem*), paraphrased; the army image opens hom. 8, which Migne notes some copies number seventh (Loserth: *Opp.* VI, ed. Paris. altera, 785; LEDGER K04d-1); ABCD read *Omelia XXVIII* (96.8). *Via* echoes John 14:6.

[^8]: **C.** *Unde ait supra*: the speaker is not expressed, and *supra* points to no passage in this chapter that gives these three causes; the nearest named author is Chrysostom. Translated as printed and flagged. The subject of *potuit* and *doceret* is the Holy Spirit.

[^9]: **C/T.** *Subtilis et realis philosophus*: the Holy Spirit, who cares little for signs and attends to the sense they designate. *Gravidata*: D *ad exercicium*, C *sit guida* (96.24).

[^10]: **C.** *Singulariter* (CONTEXTUAL): here "alone": only the words over the chalice mention the testament.

[^11]: **C/S.** *Authonomatice*: by antonomasia, that is, pre-eminently the one who shed blood in witness of a covenant. *Dissipancium legem*: the phrase of Ps. 118:126 (DR "they have dissipated thy law"). *Pene* is read with *dissipancium*: the penalty of those who dissipate his law.

[^12]: **S.** Zech. 9:11 (Loserth's reference). Wyclif's *eduxisti*, "hast brought forth", for the Vulgate's *emisisti* (DR "hast sent forth") is translated as his; otherwise Douay–Rheims.

[^13]: **T/S.** *Calix* in 1 Cor. 11:25 is Loserth's addition: the codices omit it (97.17, "Codd.: *calix* deest. Addidi"), and E reads *Hoc novum*; the Vulgate has *calix*. Luke 22:20 and 1 Cor. 11:25 in the Douay–Rheims. *Simpliciter*: the Apostle's form, without Luke's apposition.

[^14]: **T/C.** *Methonomatice*, "metonymically": AB *methavorice*, C *methaforice* in the text with *metanomatice* in the margin by another hand (97.19). *Contentum pro continente* is translated as printed, "the contained taken for the container"; the figure described, *calix* naming what it holds, is commonly put the other way round (*continens pro contento*). ABC read *patet tercio* for *patet secundo* (97.19–20).

[^15]: **S.** *Cum Marthe*, X 3.41.6 (Loserth at 97.30; the modern form is ours; LEDGER K04d-2). *Unionem cum ipso atque ecclesia*: *ecclesia* is ablative with *cum*, "with him and with the church".

[^16]: **C.** The two propositions held heretical are given as quoted (*locucionem istam esse tropicam: Hoc est corpus meum*; *illa hostia est figura*). *Convincitur heresis super hoc*: the parallel case is that "Christ is a creature" is not proved heretical by Arius's having said it. *Quam diffamant*: the feminine takes up *veritatem*.
