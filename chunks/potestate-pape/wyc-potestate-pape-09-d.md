# *De potestate pape*, chapter 9, part 4: the blind quibble of the keys; pride of order and the narrow way

## ¶1 · 204.30–205.26

> [p. 204] Sed ceca argucia, unde cap. IX ex auctoritate beati Augustini magister sic loquitur: Quodcumque ligaveris super terram etc, hoc est ego Deus [p. 205] et omnes celestis milicie ordines et omnes sancti in gloria mea laudant vobiscum et confirmant, quos ligatis et solvitis. Non dixit, quos putatis ligare et solvere, sed in quos exercetis opus iusticie vel misericordie. Aliter autem opera vestra in peccatores non cognosco. Quis igitur non horreret, quod sacerdos cogit Deum et totam triumphantem ecclesiam consentire culpabiliter sue stulticie vel quicquid in isto fecerit ad ratam prudenciam imputare, vel quod populus sine iudicio debet credere esse confirmatum a Deo, quicquid sacerdos fecerit in hac parte. Tales blasfemias et mille permittit Deus in peccatum ecclesie, confitens vero erit optimus iudex peccati proprii, considerans si perfecte dolet de commisso in Deum, si humiliter proponit et non vult sic peccare amplius. Sacerdos autem qui apponit materiam in confitentem ignorat hoc; verumtamen multum valet apud utramque ecclesiam, quod quis humiliter confiteatur presbitero, quod ipse secundum clavem sciencie aptet sanificam medicinam, et quod oret ac supportet cum ceteris membris Christi pro scelere confitentis. Sic enim secundum Augustinum super Johanne Sermone CXXI, ecclesia sanctorum in racione meriti dimittit peccata.

But [this is] a blind [quibble]{g:argucia}; hence in chapter 9 the Master, on the authority of blessed Augustine, speaks thus:[^1] "'Whatsoever thou shalt bind upon earth,' etc., that is, I God [205] and all the orders of the heavenly [soldiery]{g:milicia} and all the saints in my glory praise with you and confirm those whom you bind and loose. He did not say, those whom you think you bind and loose, but those upon whom you exercise the work of justice or of mercy. But otherwise I do not acknowledge your works upon sinners." Who then would not shudder [at the claim] that a [priest]{g:sacerdos} compels God and the whole [church]{g:ecclesia} triumphant to consent culpably to his folly, or to set down whatever he has done in this to ratified prudence, or that the people must believe without judgement that whatever the priest has done in this matter has been confirmed by God? Such blasphemies, and a thousand [more], God permits for the sin of the church;[^2] but the one confessing will be the best judge of his own sin, considering whether he grieves perfectly for what he committed against God, whether he humbly purposes and wills not to sin so any more. But the priest who applies the matter to the one confessing is ignorant of this;[^3] nevertheless it avails much with both churches that one humbly confess to a [presbyter]{g:presbiter}, that he according to the key of knowledge fit a healing medicine, and that he pray and bear [him] up with the other [members]{g:membrum} of Christ for the crime of the one confessing. For thus, according to Augustine on John, Sermon 121, the church of the saints forgives sins by reason of merit.[^4]

## ¶2 · 205.27–207.22

> [p. 205] Quantum ad secundum pomposum, quod est ordinis vel status dignitas, consideraret sic pompans dignitatem naturalem et statum dyaboli, et in quantam indignitatem incidit per peccatum, ut pulchre declarat Augustinus in De Verbis Domini Sermone LIII: Melior est, inquit, virginalis [p. 206] sanctitas quam pudicicia coniugata, sed attende superbiam et humilitatem, iungeque virginitati tue humilitatem et eris matre tua melior, quod si tu virgo admittas superbiam et mater tua servet humilitatem, ipsa est longe melior te superba. Nam sicut bonum bono adiunctum facit integrum magis bonum, sic hoc malum facit totum malum sive bono adiunctum fuerit sive malo; privat enim te racione membri humilis capitis, quod est Christus, quod si est, ut patet in lucifero, qui est melior te natura, ubi est potestas vel dignitas, de qua pompas? Nichil, inquam, foret clero utilius et specialiter Christi vicario quam sentire humiliter de seipso, quod Augustinus declarat subtiliter et devote, dicens quod genus humanum post lapsum omnino indiguit celesti medico, quod quia non suffecit ad ipsum ascendere, Deus ineffabiliter descendit ad ipsum, docens vita, opere et sermone, quomodo oportet genus humanum si sanabitur humilitate contraria vie superbie qua adeo recesserat sanum redire ad dominum. Et hec racio, quare Christus servavit in vita et doctrina summam pauperiem et humilitatem; Antichristus autem querit mundanam gloriam Christo contrarie, ut dicitur Joh. V; et ideo licet papa sic fecerit (ut dicit Augustinus) non est de Christi ecclesia, quia si sit verus papa est homo humillimus, mundanos honores et lucrum seculi parvipendens. Et sic Christus est humanitus via, per quam oportet omnem salvandum incedere, que via cum sit arta impossibile est tumentem superbia per illam semitam penetrare. Sed de quanto collisus stante illo tumore plus illi vie facietenus innititur, de tanto comportando prohibens [p. 207] plus offendit. Nec est talis tumorositas vera magnitudo, sed innaturalis mundana aquositas, que repugnat omnino Christi vicario, quia aliter est mendacissimus Antichristus. Et sicut dicitur Johannis XIV. 6: Viator habet, qua vult ire, cum Christus est via, quo vult ire, Christus est veritas et ubi vult manere, Christus est cursiv. Quamvis autem in via non sunt insidie, cum sit Christus, tamen iuxta illam sunt multa pericula, iuxta illud Psalmi CXXXIX, 6: Iuxta iter scandalum posuerunt michi. Vis secure incedere, tene te in via regia et omnino desere mundum ac eius gloriam, quia aliter es indubie Antichristus et non de Christi ovibus. Christus enim non potest superbum talem ad se trahere, cum oportet exaltacionem ad Deum per humilitatem procedere. Ideo quomodocumque pompaveris, exaltando te ipsum et querendo mundi gloriam, ille qui sentenciavit, quod qui se exaltat humiliabitur, et qui se humiliat exaltabitur, non potest mutare suam sentenciam, quin talis sit magis alienus a Christo et per consequens Antichristus.

As to the second object of [boasting]{g:pompare}, which is the dignity of order or state, one who boasts so should consider the natural dignity and state of the devil, and into how great an indignity he fell through sin, as Augustine finely declares in *On the Words of the Lord*, Sermon 53: "Virginal holiness," he says, "is better [206] than wedded chastity; but attend to pride and humility, and join humility to your virginity, and you will be better than your mother; but if you, a virgin, admit pride, and your mother keeps humility, she is far better than you in your pride."[^5] For as a good joined to a good makes the whole more good, so this evil makes the whole evil, whether it has been joined to good or to evil; for it deprives you of the standing of a member of the humble head, which is Christ. And if this is so, as is clear in Lucifer, who is better than you by nature, where is the [power]{g:potestas} or dignity of which you boast?[^6] Nothing, I say, would be more useful to the clergy, and especially to the [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius-christi}, than to think humbly of himself; which Augustine declares subtly and devoutly, saying that the human race after the fall wholly needed a heavenly physician; and because it was not able to ascend to him, God ineffably descended to it, teaching by life, work and word how the human race, if it is to be healed, must return whole to the Lord by a humility contrary to the way of pride by which it had gone so far away.[^7] And this is the reason why Christ kept, in life and teaching, the highest poverty and humility; but Antichrist seeks worldly glory contrary to Christ, as is said in John 5; and therefore, although a pope do so (as Augustine says), he is not of Christ's church; for if he is a true pope he is a most humble man, setting little store by worldly honours and the gain of the world.[^8] And so Christ in his humanity is the way by which everyone who is to be saved must walk; and since that way is narrow, it is impossible for one swollen with pride to pass through by that path. But the more one who is bruised presses outwardly upon that way while that swelling remains, the more, obstructing [it] by what he carries, does he [207] stumble.[^9] Nor is such swollenness true greatness, but an unnatural worldly wateriness, which is wholly [incompatible]{g:repugnare} with the vicar of Christ, since otherwise he is a most lying Antichrist. And as is said, John 14:6: the [wayfarer]{g:viator} has that by which he wishes to go, since Christ is "the way"; whither he wishes to go, Christ is "the truth"; and where he wishes to abide, Christ is the life.[^10] And although there are no snares in the way, since it is Christ, yet beside it there are many dangers, according to that of Psalm 139:6: "They have laid for me a stumblingblock by the wayside." Do you wish to walk safely? Keep yourself on the king's highway and wholly forsake the world and its glory, for otherwise you are without doubt Antichrist and not of Christ's sheep. For Christ cannot draw such a proud man to himself, since exaltation to God must proceed through humility. Therefore, howsoever you boast, exalting yourself and seeking the glory of the world, he who [pronounced]{g:sentenciare} that "he that exalteth himself shall be humbled: and he that humbleth himself shall be exalted" cannot change his [sentence]{g:sentencia}, so that such a man should not be the more estranged from Christ, and consequently Antichrist.[^11]

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** The Master is Peter Lombard, *Sentences* IV d. 18; Loserth gives "l.c. pag. 887", i.e. PL 192, 887. Lombard (d. 18 c. 5, PL 192, 887) ascribes the gloss to Augustine, *De vera et falsa poenitentia*. It stands in that pseudo-Augustinian work, c. 10.25 (PL 40, 1122), on the plural *Quodcumque solveritis* (Matt. 18:18), and Lombard gives it under Matt. 16. Wyclif's "chapter 9" matches neither Lombard's chapter (5) nor the treatise's (10) (LEDGER P09d-1). The quotation runs to "upon sinners" as Loserth's italics mark it.
[^2]: **C.** *ad ratam prudenciam imputare* depends on *cogit* like *consentire*: the priest compels God to count his act as ratified prudence. *in peccatum ecclesie*: "for the sin of the church", that is, in punishment of it; possibly "to the church's sinning".
[^3]: **C/R.** *apponit materiam*: the priest lays the matter (the penance enjoined) upon the penitent; "this" is whether the penitent's sorrow is perfect. "Both churches": militant and triumphant. "The key of knowledge": Luke 11:52. A and B in the margin (205.14): *Confessio que fit sacerdoti*, "confession made to a priest".
[^4]: **S.** Augustine, *Tractatus in Iohannem* 121 (Wyclif's "Sermon 121"); Tract. 121.4, PL 35, 1958: *Ecclesiae charitas quae per Spiritum sanctum diffunditur in cordibus nostris, participum suorum peccata dimittit*. Lombard cites tract. 121 a few lines before he quotes the same sentence (*Sent.* IV d. 18, PL 192, 886–87). Loserth's "Opp. tom. V, 595" points to the Maurist *Sermo* 121, which does not contain it (LEDGER P09d-2).
[^5]: **S/R.** Augustine, cited as *De verbis Domini* Sermon 53; Loserth: "St. Aug. Serm. CCCLV, Opp. tom. V, 1379. Loosely quoted." The passage is *Sermo* 354.9 (*Ad continentes*), PL 39, 1567: *melior est virginalis sanctitas, quam pudicitia conjugalis … Si enim tu tenueris superbiam, et tua mater humilitatem, melior erit mater quam filia*. Loserth's CCCLV is a slip for CCCLIV (LEDGER P09d-3). A and B in the margin (205.32): *Dignitas unde causatur*, "whence dignity is caused".
[^6]: **C/T.** *racione membri* is "the standing of a member", not the technical *racio* "aspect". *quod si est*: if pride so deprives even Lucifer, better than you by nature, of his dignity. B reads *pompos* for *pompas* (206.12).
[^7]: **S.** Augustine on Christ the humble physician: Loserth gives no reference. The passage is *Sermo* 142.2 (PL 38, 778): *haec res fecit ut veniret ad te tantus medicus … Sed quia tu ire non potuisti ad eum, ille venit ad te. Venit docens humilitatem, qua redeamus: quia superbia nos redire non sinebat* (LEDGER P09d-4). *sanum* agrees with *genus humanum*: the race is to return "whole", healed.
[^8]: **S/C/R.** "As is said in John 5": John 5:43–44 (another coming "in his own name", "receive glory one from another"). *licet papa sic fecerit*: "although a pope do so"; possibly "though he be pope, if he has done so". The Augustine is probably *Tract. in Ioh.* 29.8 (PL 35, 1631–32), on John 5:43: Antichrist *gloriam nominis sui quaesiturus est, inflatus, non solidus … Omnes ergo pertinentes ad corpus Christi … non quaeramus gloriam nostram*; cf. *Sermo* 129.6 (PL 38, 723) (LEDGER P09d-5). The manuscripts in the margin (206.23): *Antichristus quis est. Via est Christus.*
[^9]: **C/S.** A hard sentence. *collisus* "one who is bruised"; *facietenus* "outwardly" (literally "as far as the face"); *comportando prohibens* "obstructing [it] by what he carries": the swollen man jams the narrow way of Matt. 7:14 (DR "strait is the way"). *aquositas*, "wateriness", continues the figure of a dropsical swelling. The image comes from Augustine, *Sermo* 142.5 (PL 38, 780–81): *Tumuerat autem superbia, et ipso tumore per angustum redire non poterat … Magnitudo enim soliditatem habet, tumor inflationem* (see LEDGER P09d-4, P09d-6).
[^10]: **T/S/R.** "the life": the print reads *cursiv* (207.7, confirmed on the plate), which does not construe; John 14:6 (*Ego sum via, et veritas, et vita*) requires *vita* (our emendation). The triple *qua … quo … ubi* is Augustine, *Sermo* 142.1 (PL 38, 778): *Qua vis ire? Ego sum via. Quo vis ire? Ego sum veritas. Ubi vis permanere? Ego sum vita*. Augustine goes on, as Wyclif does, to the snares beside the way (Ps. 139:6) (LEDGER P09d-6). The manuscripts in the margin (207.5): *Viator attendat Christum*, "let the wayfarer attend to Christ".
[^11]: **S/C.** Psalm 139:6 in DR. For "he that exalteth himself …" Loserth refers to Matt. 23:12 (page foot of p. 207, keyed to line 6); Wyclif's present tenses are those of Luke 14:11 and 18:14, given in DR without "every one". *sentenciam* is here the judge's "sentence" (GLOSSARY *sentencia*, second sense). The manuscripts in the margin (207.17): *Gloriam fugere debet prelatus*.
