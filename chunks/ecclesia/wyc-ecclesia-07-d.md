# *De ecclesia*, chapter 7, part 4: unjust excommunication, and the three wonders of the laity

## ¶1 · 152.14–154.25

> [p. 152] Ex quibus videtur quod publice excomunicantes ministros regis sine examinacione diligenti delicti, et specialiter si iurati sunt legem ipsam defendere, offendunt nimium. Nam Gen. XVIII°, 20, 21, dicit omnisciens ad dandum exemplum iudicibus, quomodo diligenter examinent causam, antequam ferant iudicium: Clamor, inquit, Sodomorum multiplicatus est; descendam et videbo utrum clamorem opere compleverint an non est ita ut sciam. Nec sufficit notorietas substancie facti, sed oportet quod sit notorium qualitate; quia notum est quod excommunicans propter operacionem legitimam iniuriatur excommunicato excommunicando se ipsum. Nec est color excommunicare capitaneos operacionis legitime propter hoc quod preter eorum consensum, auctorisacionem vel occasionem datam aliquis de comitiva fecit opus indebitum, quia sic Christus foret excommunicandus pro facto Scarioth, et quilibet dux exercitus pro quocunque facinore commisso a persona sui exercitus et (quod evidencius est) quilibet prelatus [p. 153] pro quocunque scelere subditi sui gregis; que si essent vera tolleretur a prelatis potestas excommunicandi subiectos, cum non licet excommunicato excommunicare quemquam, ymmo cum ut sic sit hereticus, non licet sibi ministrare aliqua sacramenta. Nec cadit excusacio quod prelatus non excommunicat, sed denunciat tales excommunicatos de iure, quia talis denunciacio est defamacio minimis; et ve illi qui taliter scandalizat fratrem suum. Est autem excommunicacio iniusta tripliciter: Primo quando sacerdos ex rancore et zelo vindicte excommunicat subditum licet reum. Secundo quando prelatus pocius pro temporalibus quam pro amore et correccione subditi eum excommunicat, licet excomunicandus fuerit bono modo. Et tercio quando indiscrete excommunicat subditum propter factum legitimum. Talis autem excommunicator videtur plus polluere sanctam ecclesiam quam corporaliter occidens hominem ad altare; pro quo notandum quod corporalis pollucio videtur esse excommunicacioni inpertinens, nisi de quanto sapit peccatum, tum quia pollucio per sputum vel alia pondera nature foret turpior quam pollucio per semen vel sanguinem, tum eciam quia illa pollucio deletur per locionem corpoream. Ex istis videtur, cum iniuste excommunicans fratrem suum (quantum in eo est) occidit animam et virtutes, sed corporalis homicida non occidit ut sic nisi corpus solummodo, sequitur quod, sicut anima excedit corpus, sic occisio in animam perpetrata excedit in malicia occisionem corporis. Ex quo videtur, cum peccatum non potest subiectari in lapide vel edificio corporali, quod locus per se non polluit suos incolas, sed [p. 154] econtra, cum II° Machab. V°, 19, scribitur: Non propter locum gentem, sed propter gentem locum dominus elegit. Et patet quod pollucio loci non impediret racionabiliter cultum Dei, nisi de quanto horrorem incuteret ex peccato, et cum maius peccatum maiorem horrorem incuteret, sequitur quod peccatum gravius commissum in sanctuario pocius retraheret a divino servitio. Nec aliud volunt leges ecclesie debite intellecte. Non enim licet clerico Christi cecari in stulticia phariseica (de qua Matthei XV°), plus appreciando locionem corporalem ex pluri horrore sordis corporee quam locionem spiritualem a sorde anime, cum secundum diffinicionem Christi, que exeunt a corde interioris hominis, hec sunt que preponderanter coinquinant hominem. Et hic miratur tota ecclesia laycalis quare clerus excommunicat cum tanta instancia et fervore pro suo debito corporali, vel pro sensibili privilegio ex tradicionibus hominum introducto; clericos autem non sic excomunicant pro rependendo debito spirituali, cum tamen peccatum sit infinitum gravius et debitum illud quoad Deum sit obligacius. Plus eciam foret ad privilegium et iustificacionem ecclesie quod prevaricatores mandati Dei per censuras ecclesie sint correcti quam quod omnes ritus humani legi Christi superadditi sint quantumcunque multipliciter regulariter ac solempniter observati.

From which it seems that those who publicly excommunicate the king's ministers without diligent examination of the offence, and especially if they are sworn to defend that very law, offend exceedingly. For in Genesis 18:20–21 the all-knowing [God], to give an example to judges how diligently they should examine a cause before they give judgment, says: "The cry," he says, "of the Sodomites is multiplied; I will go down and see whether they have fulfilled the cry in deed, or whether it be not so, that I may know."[^1] Nor is the notoriety of the substance of the deed enough, but it must be notorious in its quality; for it is known that one who excommunicates for a lawful act wrongs the excommunicate, excommunicating himself. Nor is there [colour]{g:color} for excommunicating the [captains]{g:capitaneus} of a lawful operation because, beyond their consent, authorization or occasion given, someone of the company did an undue deed; for so Christ would have to be excommunicated for the deed of [Is]cariot, and every leader of an army for any crime committed by a person of his army, and (what is more evident) every [prelate]{g:prelatus} [153] for any crime of a subject of his flock; and if these things were true, the power of excommunicating subjects would be taken away from prelates, since it is not lawful for one excommunicated to excommunicate anyone; indeed, since as such he is a heretic, it is not lawful for him to administer any sacraments.[^2] Nor does the excuse hold that the prelate does not excommunicate, but declares such men excommunicated by law; for such a declaration is a defamation to the least; and woe to him who so scandalizes his brother.[^3] And excommunication is unjust in three ways. First, when a [priest]{g:sacerdos} out of rancour and zeal for vengeance excommunicates a subject, though guilty. Secondly, when a prelate excommunicates a subject rather for [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} than for the love and correction of the subject, though he deserved excommunication in a good manner. And thirdly, when he indiscreetly excommunicates a subject for a lawful deed.[^4] And such an excommunicator seems to pollute holy [church]{g:ecclesia} more than one who bodily kills a man at the altar. For which it is to be noted that bodily pollution seems to be irrelevant to excommunication, except in so far as it savours of sin: both because pollution by spittle or other burdens of nature would be fouler than pollution by semen or blood, and also because that pollution is wiped out by a bodily washing.[^5] From these things it seems, since one who unjustly excommunicates his brother (as far as in him lies) kills the soul and the virtues, but the bodily homicide as such kills only the body, [that] it follows that, as the soul exceeds the body, so the killing perpetrated on the soul exceeds in malice the killing of the body. From which it seems, since sin cannot have its subject in a stone or a bodily building, that the place of itself does not pollute its inhabitants, but [154] the reverse; since in 2 Maccabees 5:19 it is written: "The Lord did not choose the people for the place's sake, but the place for the people's sake."[^6] And it is clear that the pollution of a place would not reasonably hinder the worship of God except in so far as it struck horror from the sin; and since a greater sin would strike a greater horror, it follows that a graver sin committed in the sanctuary would rather draw [men] back from divine service. Nor do the laws of the church, rightly understood, mean anything else. For it is not lawful for a cleric of Christ to be blinded in the Pharisaic folly (of which Matthew 15), valuing a bodily washing, from greater horror of bodily filth, more than a spiritual washing from the filth of the soul; since, according to Christ's definition, the things that come forth from the heart of the inner man, these are what preponderantly defile a man.[^7] And here the whole lay church wonders why the [clergy]{g:clerus} excommunicate with such urgency and fervour for their bodily debt, or for a [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} privilege brought in by the traditions of men; but they do not so excommunicate clerics for the repaying of a spiritual debt, although the sin is infinitely graver and that debt as regards God more binding.[^8] It would also be more to the privilege and justification of the church that [transgressors]{g:prevaricator} of God's command be corrected by the [censures]{g:censura} of the church than that all human rites added on to the law of Christ be observed, however manifoldly, regularly and solemnly.[^9]

## ¶2 · 154.26–156.2

> [p. 154] Recoleremus siquidem quod propter preponderan- ciam ritus phariseici legi Dei destructe sunt Jerusalem et Judea (ut patet Matthei XV° et Luce XIX°). Recoleremus eciam quomodo privilegium est lex privata ad commodum privilegiati concessa a principe, [p. 155] et per consequens foret clero magnum privilegium quod adiutorio dominorum temporalium foret exoneratus ab istis cerimoniis humanitus introductis, propinquius sequendo Christum pure secundum regulas primevas a Christo traditas. In hoc enim habemus supra laycos privilegium singulare. Mirantur insuper seculares quomodo non licet eis communicare cum excommunicato a sacerdote pro questu suo vel excellencia seculari, et hoc sub pena excommunicacionis maioris, licet autem communicare cum gravius excommunicatis a Deo sine inferencia tante pene, ut patet de simoniacis, de sacerdote fornicario et eius amasia. Ymmo licet cardinales tamquam heretici vel scismatici sint divisi a capite, tamen dicimus seculares excommunicatissimos, si ab eis subtrahant elemosinas suas que sunt bona pauperum, licet iniustissime peius predonibus occupent aliena. Licet autem (ut inquiunt in Westmonasterio) communicare cum excomunicato quoad Deum racione iniuste detencionis debiti vel iniuste occisionis proximi, eciam sustinendo eum in sua malicia, sed laicis non licet communicare cum minus excommunicatis ab eis pro bonis ecclesie. Talia autem multa deliramenta finguntur per que layci mendaciter illuduntur; licet enim falsitates ficte quandoque prosunt per accidens, subditos a facinore deterrendo, absit tamen christianum contra fidem scripture uti istis fallaciis pro hoc fine. Notum est enim ex fide scripture quod nemo ad sui dampnum excommunicatur, nisi racione delicti proprii excommunicatus fuerit quoad Deum. Notum est iterum quod nemo propter corporalem communicacionem cum excommunicato incurrit periculum, nisi sit particeps criminis cum eodem. Et notum est tercio quod infinitum periculosius est communicare culpabiliter cum excommunicato a Deo, qui solum excommunicat [p. 156] pro mortali, quam communicare nude corporaliter cum excommunicato solum ab homine.

Let us recall, indeed, that for the preponderance of the Pharisaic rite over [God's law]{g:lex dei} Jerusalem and Judea were destroyed (as is clear in Matthew 15 and Luke 19). Let us recall also how a privilege is a private law granted by a prince for the advantage of the privileged, [155] and consequently it would be a great privilege for the clergy to be relieved, with the help of the [temporal lords]{g:temporales domini}, of these ceremonies brought in by men, following Christ more nearly, purely, according to the first rules handed down by Christ. For in this we have a singular privilege above the laity.[^10] The [seculars]{g:secularis} wonder besides how it is not lawful for them to communicate with one excommunicated by a priest for his own gain or secular eminence, and this under pain of the greater excommunication; but it is lawful to communicate with those more gravely excommunicated by God without the infliction of so great a penalty, as is clear of [simoniacs]{g:simonia}, of a fornicating priest and his mistress. Indeed, although the cardinals, as heretics or schismatics, are divided from the head, yet we declare seculars most excommunicate if they withdraw from them their [alms]{g:elemosina}, which are the goods of the poor, although they, most unjustly and worse than robbers, occupy what is another's.[^11] And it is lawful (as they say at Westminster) to communicate with one excommunicated as regards God by reason of the unjust detention of a debt or the unjust killing of a neighbour, even maintaining him in his malice; but it is not lawful for the laity to communicate with those less excommunicated by them for the goods of the church. And many such ravings are feigned, by which the laity are deceived with lies; for although feigned falsehoods are sometimes of use by accident, deterring subjects from crime, yet God forbid that a Christian should use these [fallacies]{g:fallacia} for this end against the faith of scripture.[^12] For it is known [from the faith]{g:ex fide} of scripture that no one is excommunicated to his own harm unless by reason of his own offence he has been excommunicated as regards God. It is known again that no one incurs danger through bodily communication with one excommunicated unless he is a sharer in the crime with him. And it is known, thirdly, that it is infinitely more dangerous to communicate culpably with one excommunicated by God, who excommunicates only [156] for mortal [sin], than to communicate merely bodily with one excommunicated only by man.[^13]

## ¶3 · 156.3–156.14

> [p. 156] Tercio miratur secularis ecclesia quod clerus instat erga brachium seculare, ut omnes excommunicati sui per quadraginta dies, remanentes a prelatis quos ipsi signaverint non soluti, mancipentur carceri seculari, quousque secundum eorum arbitrium satisfecerint, quantumcunque sophistice sint ligati. Non enim meretur eorum ingratitudo tale subsidium, cum regula iuris divini sentenciat quod privilegium indultum tenetur amittere qui sibi concessa abutitur potestate. Abusus ergo tendens ad enervacionem regni et bonum privatum bono communi preponderans meretur ablacionem privilegii sic abusi.

Thirdly, the secular church wonders that the clergy press upon [the secular arm]{g:brachium seculare} that all their excommunicates who remain for forty days unabsolved by the prelates, [being those] whom they themselves have signified, be committed to secular prison until they have made satisfaction at their discretion, however sophistically they may have been bound. For their ingratitude does not merit such assistance, since a rule of divine law [pronounces]{g:sentenciare} that he who abuses the power granted to him is bound to lose the privilege bestowed. Therefore an abuse tending to the enervation of the [kingdom]{g:regnum}, and putting a private good above the common good, deserves the taking away of the privilege so abused.[^14]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 152.14: It is an offence to excommunicate the King's servants without careful enquiry.
- 152.27: Persons in authority are not to be condemned for wrong done by their subordinates without their consent.
- 153.10: Excommunication is unjust: 1. if inflicted from spite, 2. from avarice;
- 153.15: 3. if undeserved.
- 153.25: Unjust excommunication is worse than murder.
- 154.15: Men want to know why the wicked clergy are not excommunicated.
- 155.6: The rules as to relations with excommunicated persons are bad.
- 155.28: No such relation is dangerous except a share in the crime.
- 156.3: The Prelates' privilege of imprisoning excommunicated persons should be taken away,

## Notes

[^1]: **T/C/S.** A margin *Conclusio* (152.14). *Si iurati sunt*: we take the excommunicators as the subject (those sworn to the king's laws, 152.11–13); it could be the king's ministers. The excommunications are those pronounced after the Westminster killing by the archbishop and four suffragans on its instigators and perpetrators, with the king, his mother and the duke of Lancaster excepted, and published by the bishop of London at St Paul's (Loserth, Introduction, p. x). Gen. 18:20–21: Wyclif shortens the Vulgate (*Clamor Sodomorum et Gomorrhae … utrum clamorem qui venit ad me opere compleverint*); DR "The cry of Sodom and Gomorrha is multiplied … whether they have done according to the cry that is come to me"; his Latin translated, DR kept where it agrees.

[^2]: **T/G.** D *notandum* (152.24); B *excommunicatus propter* (152.24). *Color*: a plausible ground (GLOSSARY), not "pretext". D *facit* (152.30). A A₁ B lack *quocunque* (152.32). *Scarioth*: Judas Iscariot. *Ut sic sit hereticus*: the excommunicator, as one himself excommunicate.

[^3]: **C/S.** *Defamacio minimis*: we take *minimis* as dative, "to the least ones" (cf. Matt. 18:6, cited, not quoted); a reading *nimis*, "an excessive defamation", would construe more easily, but no witness gives it; the print is translated. *Ve illi …*: cf. Matt. 18:7 (alluded).

[^4]: **T/R.** A margin *Tripliciter facit excommunicacio iniusta* (153.10); B margin, another hand, *Excommunicacio tripliciter*. A A₁ lack *fuerit* (153.15). *Licet reum*: "though guilty". *Bono modo*: in a right manner.

[^5]: **T/C/S.** A margin *Nota; De excommunicacione persone vel loci* (153.15). *Excommunicacioni inpertinens*: having no bearing on excommunication. *Pondera nature*: bodily evacuations. Canon law treats blood or semen as polluting a church (*X* 3.40.10, Friedberg II, 636; cf. *X* 3.39); Wyclif's point is a hypothetical: spittle would be fouler still, so bodily pollution cannot be what excommunication is about. No canon on spittle was found (LEDGER E07d-2).

[^6]: **T/S.** D *corporalem. Ex istis videtur, quod* (153.24); A B *solo modo* (153.28); D *p'* = *persone*, rightly *per se* (153.32, Loserth's note). *Subiectari*: to have as its subject, to inhere in. 2 Macc. 5:19: A B *I. Mach.*, D *Mach. VI* (154.1); Wyclif has *dominus* for the Vulgate's *Deus*, so DR with "the Lord".

[^7]: **T/S.** B *honorem* for *horrorem* (154.4, 5); D *errorem*, D *cum magis* (154.5); A *quam* corrected from *quando* (154.11); B *exemit* for *exeunt* (154.13). Matt. 15:1–20 (the unwashed hands); Loserth refers to 15:18–20, Vulgate *quae … de corde exeunt, et ea coinquinant hominem … haec sunt, quae coinquinant hominem*; Wyclif's paraphrase translated, "defile" as DR.

[^8]: **T/R/G.** *Eta* (154.15) is *Et* with Loserth's alinea letter *a*, keyed by the A margin *Nota tres miraculose questiones laicales a, b, c* (154.15); the letter is not translated. The three wonders: here, 155.6 (*Mirantur insuper*) and 156.3 (*Tercio miratur*). D *sensuali* (154.17); D *non excommunicant*, with *non* added in the margin by another hand (154.18). *Instancia*: urgency, not GLOSSARY "objection". *Pro rependendo debito spirituali*: to have a spiritual debt repaid, i.e. clerics' sins.

[^9]: **T.** D *censure*, corrected in the margin *censuras* (154.23). *Regulariter*: by rule.

[^10]: **T/S.** D *recoleremus propter*; A lacks *propter* (154.26); D *distincte* for *destructe* (154.27). Matt. 15 and Luke 19:41–44 cited. *Privilegium est lex privata*: Isidore, *Etym.* V.18.1 (PL 82:202A–B), taken into Gratian D.3 c.3 (PL 187:34B) (LEDGER E07d-3). B *sequendo exemplum* (155.4).

[^11]: **T/C.** *Pro questu suo*: for the priest's gain. *Excommunicacionis maioris*: the greater excommunication. D *in capite* (155.14). *Cardinales … divisi a capite*: the Schism of 1378; Wyclif does not say which cardinals. *Dicimus*: "we", Wyclif speaking in the person of the clergy. *Occupent*: the cardinals.

[^12]: **C.** *Ut inquiunt in Westmonasterio*: the Westminster party's teaching. *Minus excommunicatis ab eis*: those excommunicated by the clergy, less gravely than God's excommunicates; or "under the lesser excommunication". *Contra fidem scripture*: "against the faith of scripture", as GLOSSARY *ex fide scripture*.

[^13]: **T.** *Nude* (156.1), "merely". D *pro mortalibus* (156.1).

[^14]: **T/C/S.** D *quomodo clerus* (156.3); A margin *Brachium seculare* (156.4); D *satisfecerunt* (156.7). *Quos ipsi signaverint*: grammatically the prelates, but in sense the excommunicates whom the prelates have "signified" to the king; the procedure is the writ *de excommunicato capiendo*: after forty days' obstinacy, on the bishop's letters patent (*significavit*), the sheriff takes the excommunicate until he satisfies (Bracton f. 426b–427, ed. Thorne IV, 327) (LEDGER E07d-4). *Instat erga*: presses upon, not the disputation's *instatur*. *Ingratitudo*: ingratitude toward the king who lends his aid, not GLOSSARY *ingratus* "displeasing [to God]". The rule that one who abuses a granted power loses the privilege is Gregory in Gratian C.11 q.3 c.63, *Privilegium omnino meretur amittere qui permissa sibi abutitur potestate* (PL 187:862; also D.74) (LEDGER E07d-5).
