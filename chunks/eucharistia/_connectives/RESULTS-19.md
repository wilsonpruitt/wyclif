## Doubtful, left as they are

- **07-g, p. 216**: *hoc **autem** servabitur pro theoreumate quod scriptura debet undique concedi*, rendered "and this will be kept as a theorem". Read as (c): it carries on from *Ideo oportet attendere ad sensum scripture katholicum*. "Now" (a) could be argued, because the theorem is the premiss the reply then rests on (*Hoc est corpus meum*). But no *ergo* closes a syllogism on it here. Left.
- **07-h, p. 220**: *illud **autem** foret plus quam transsubstanciare*, rendered "and that would be more than to transubstantiate". Read as (c), extending the *enim* clause about the pope's enthronement. "Now" (a) for the step that makes the point (the superiors already accept a change stronger than this transubstantiation, so they should accept this sense) is defensible. What follows is *Unde*, not *ergo*. Left.
- **07-i, p. 225**: *cum hereticum sit quod panis et vinum remaneant post consecracionem sacramentum, **sed** sunt res aliene nature*, rendered "since it is heretical that bread and wine remain a sacrament after consecration; but they are things of an alien nature". This is a correction after an implied negation ("it is heretical that X ... but [they are] Y"), so it works like *non … sed*. "But" is right. The semicolon loosens the link a little, but the sense is not inverted. Left.
