# *De potestate pape*, chapter 5, part 1: the chief vicar must live without property

## ¶1 · 80.24–83.27

> [p. 80] Supersunt alie tres sentencie premittende: prima sive Romanus pontifex sive quicumque alius fuerit Christi archivicarius, debet vivere maxime exproprietarie et esse a mundo maxime elongatus; patet ex hoc, quod debet esse inter viantes maxime virtuosus et in conversacione Christo similior. Ipse autem sic vixit, ut ex fide [p. 81] scripture sepe declarata supponitur; quomodo igitur non oportet eius proximum vicarium conversari conformiter. Hoc enim movebat Petrum, Johannem et ceteros apostolos, qui potuerunt facillime, copiosissime et splendidissime fuisse dotati et tamen ex religione et debito imitandi magistrum illud omiserant. Johannes autem, qui post ascensionem Domini septuaginta annis et amplius in maiori parte vixit fixe in pace, potuit dotasse suam ecclesiam, si fuisset expediens, cum silvestres frondes mutavit in aurum et fragmina saxorum in gemmas, sed in detestacionem amoris pecunie. Et revera ista sentencia de verbis et factis apostolorum daret fidem fidelibus, quod proximi Christi et Petri vicarii debent exire de mundo et conversari similiter et quod conversacio illa perficeret statum cleri. Christus enim ipsam elegerat in se et suis apostolis: quod omnipotens et omnisciens non faceret, nisi quia scivit quod perficeret statum suorum militum. Et idem confirmatur ex decretali ecclesie, quam papa Nicolaus tercius in sexto incorporat, Exiit qui seminat, ubi declarat quod status fratrum Minorum est ideo celestis et perfectissimus, quia statui Christi et apostolorum simillimus. Et ex istis credo quod ecclesia Romana, decretiste et fratres, sed specialiter fratres Minores, defenderent istam sentenciam. Aliter enim non crederetur alicui decretali, cum illam que evidencius fundatur ex scriptura ipsimet parvipendunt. Et conformiter dicitur ad obiectum quo dicitur Johannem XXII ordinasse decretales multas [p. 82] contra istam sentenciam. Quis, inquam, crederet talibus prepositis, quorum unus sine fundacione alteri contradicit? Scolares igitur legis papalis defenderent decretalem predictam, quia aliter non crederetur alicui ipsorum textui, sed eo ipso quo textus eorum sic asserit foret sentencia minus credibilis et de mendacio plus suspecta; nec dat fidem quod papa subsequens approbat hoc vel illud, quia sequentes sunt communiter minus religiosi et plus ignari; ideo debet eis minor fides in talibus adhiberi. Et istam sentenciam credo fratres et specialiter Minores velle defendere, quia aliter indubie ipsi tamquam mendaces ypocrite multipliciter perturbarent et molestarent ecclesiam. Item, quantum ad scolasticos in fide ecclesie credo quod ipsi consentirent eidem sentencie; nam bonum quod afferunt seculares divicie, non est nisi mundi prosperitas. Sed illam tam sancti doctores quam oraciones ecclesie docent despicere, igitur cum pauperies affert bonum spirituale, videtur quod superhabundans dotacio magis imperficeret clerum Christi. Confirmatur primo per hoc quod, cum imperatores sint pontifices, ut patet distinccione De Capitulis, videtur quod ante dotacionem ecclesie ipsi erant summi pontifices, quia tunc episcopi exproprietarie viventes erant miseri et egeni, cum caruerunt perfeccione quam modo ecclesia habet ex mundi diviciis. Illam autem perfeccionem diviciarum pertinencius tunc habuerunt cesares, igitur undequaque ipsi erant perfecciores pontifices, et sic summi dupliciter tam quoad seculum quam ad Deum. Et ex isto videtur dotacionis imperfeccio quod recipientes illam inperfecerant dominos qui dotarunt. Sed dominium [p. 83] Christi, quia spirituale fuit, nulli perfeccioni incompossibile, fuit perfeccius. Cum igitur secundum legem ecclesie XIV q. V Denique si non potest sacerdos non debet illi officio vel arti intendere, per quam sequitur deterioracio proximi, videtur quod non debent intendere proprietati civili. Si enim renunciacio temporalium per viam elemosine sit perfecta in laico, multo magis in clerico. Illi igitur qui tantum imperfecerunt laicos, remitterent illis sua dominia ex causa multiplici. Omnis enim, qui se regem facit, per quod diminueretur imperium, contradicit cesari. Oportet igitur quod temporale dominium ponat plus imperfeccionis quam perfeccionis in clerico. Similiter, ultra vite necessaria non est perfeccio in mundi diviciis, nisi ad eas debite regulandum, sed hoc officium est alienum clerico, igitur et superhabundans possessio, sicut docet Apostolus Thim. VI. Regimen enim spirituale et regimen corporale, sicut et occupacio eorum, mutuo se retardant. Et hinc dicunt sancti quod divicie sunt pondus anime. Et quantum ad securitatem habendam et clerum multiplicandum, patet quod illa mundi insecuritas est meritoria et pertinens Christi discipulo, ut patet Math. VI. Mundi autem securitas est fallax et irreligiosa et solum affectabilis imperfectis.

There remain three other [teachings]{g:sentencia} to be set out first. The first: whether the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex} or whoever else is Christ's archvicar, he ought to live most fully [without property]{g:exproprietarie} and be most removed from the world.[^1] It is clear from this, that he ought to be the most virtuous among [those on the road]{g:viantes} and the most like to Christ in [manner of life]{g:conversacio}. Now he lived thus, as is supposed [from the faith]{g:ex-fide} [81] of scripture, often set out; how then must not his nearest vicar live in conformity [with him]? For this moved Peter, John and the other apostles, who could most easily, most copiously and most splendidly have been endowed, and yet, out of religion and the duty of imitating the master, had forgone it. And John, who after the ascension of the Lord lived seventy years and more, for the greater part settled in peace, could have endowed his [church]{g:ecclesia} if it had been expedient, since he changed the leaves of the woods into gold and fragments of stones into gems; but [he did so] in detestation of the love of money.[^2] And indeed this teaching, from the words and deeds of the apostles, would give faith to the faithful that the nearest vicars of Christ and of Peter ought to go out of the world and live likewise, and that that manner of life would perfect the state of the [clergy]{g:clerus}. For Christ had chosen it in himself and his apostles: which the almighty and all-knowing would not have done, except because he knew that it would perfect the state of his [soldiers]{g:milites}.[^3] And the same is confirmed from the decretal of the church which Pope Nicholas III incorporates in the Sext, *Exiit qui seminat*, where he declares that the state of the Friars Minor is heavenly and most perfect for this reason, that it is most like the state of Christ and the apostles.[^4] And from these [premisses] I believe that the Roman church, the decretists and the friars, but especially the Friars Minor, would defend this teaching. For otherwise no decretal would be believed, since they themselves make light of the one which is more evidently founded on scripture. And in conformity [with this] the answer is given to the [objection]{g:objectum} by which it is said that John XXII ordained many decretals [82] against this teaching.[^5] Who, I say, would believe such [superiors]{g:prepositi}, one of whom contradicts another without foundation? The scholars of papal law, therefore, would defend the aforesaid decretal, since otherwise no text of theirs would be believed; but by the very fact that their text so asserts, the teaching would be less credible and more suspect of falsehood. Nor does it give faith that a later pope approves this or that, for those who come after are commonly less religious and more ignorant; therefore less faith ought to be given them in such matters. And I believe that the friars, and especially the Minors, wish to defend this teaching, since otherwise they would without doubt, as lying hypocrites, disturb and trouble the church in many ways. Again, as to those schooled in the faith of the church, I believe that they would consent to the same teaching; for the good that secular riches bring is nothing but the prosperity of the world. Now both the holy doctors and the prayers of the church teach [us] to despise that; therefore, since poverty brings a spiritual good, it seems that superabundant [endowment]{g:dotacio} would rather make the clergy of Christ imperfect.[^6] It is confirmed, first, by this: since emperors are pontiffs, as is clear in the distinction *De capitulis*, it seems that before the endowment of the church they were the highest pontiffs, because then the bishops, living without property, were wretched and needy, since they lacked the perfection which the church now has from the riches of the world. Now the caesars at that time had that perfection of riches more fittingly; therefore in every respect they were the more perfect pontiffs, and so highest in two ways, as regards the world as well as as regards God.[^7] And from this the imperfection of endowment appears, that those who received it made imperfect the lords who endowed [them]. But the [lordship]{g:dominium} [83] of Christ, because it was spiritual, [and so] [incompossible]{g:incompossibilis} with no perfection, was the more perfect. Since, therefore, according to the law of the church, 14 q. 5 *Denique si non potest*, a [priest]{g:sacerdos} ought not to give himself to that office or craft through which the deterioration of his neighbour follows, it seems that they ought not to give themselves to civil property.[^8] For if the renunciation of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} by way of [alms]{g:elemosina} is perfect in a layman, much more so in a cleric. Those therefore who have made the laity so imperfect should [remit]{g:remittere} their lordships to them, for many reasons. For "whosoever maketh himself a king", whereby the empire would be diminished, "speaketh against Caesar."[^9] It must be, therefore, that temporal lordship sets more imperfection than perfection in a cleric. Likewise, beyond the necessaries of life there is no perfection in the riches of the world, except for ruling them duly; now this office is foreign to a cleric; therefore superabundant possession is too, as the Apostle teaches, 1 Tim. 6.[^10] For spiritual rule and bodily rule, like their occupations, hinder each other. And hence the saints say that riches are a weight on the soul.[^11] And as for having security and multiplying the clergy, it is clear that that insecurity of the world is meritorious and befits a disciple of Christ, as is clear in Matt. 6. But the security of the world is deceptive and irreligious, and desirable only to the imperfect.

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 80.26: Christ's chief-vicar should be free from all worldly cares.
- 81.5: The apostles did not endow churches, though St. John could easily have done so.
- 81.19: Christ chose a poor lite for himself and his apostles. Nicholas III. praised the Minorites for their poverty.
- 82.1: The utmost we can infer from the contradiction of John XXII is that neither pope speaks with authority.
- 82.24: The emperors are pontiffs and if endowment of pontiffs is good, they should keep it for themselves,
- 83.4: The clergy should not accept a gift which injures the giver.
- 83.15: There is no perfection in wealth save in ordering it properly, but this is not the duty of the clergy

## Notes

[^1]: **G.** *archivicarius*: "chief vicar", as Loserth's side-note has it; a coinage beside *vicarius Christi* that leaves open whether the pope holds the office. **R.** A in the margin: *Exproprietarie vivere debet vicarius Christi*.

[^2]: **S.** The legend of John turning branches into gold and pebbles into gems; Loserth cites the first lesson for St John's day in the Sarum Missal and compares *De ecclesia* p. 189 (LEDGER P05a-1). **C.** *Sed in detestacionem amoris pecunie* is elliptical: the miracle was done to shame the love of money, not to enrich the church.

[^3]: **G.** *militum*: Christ's "soldiers" in the Pauline figure, not the knightly estate (GLOSSARY *milicia; milites*).

[^4]: **S.** Nicholas III, *Exiit qui seminat* (1279), in the *Liber Sextus*, *De verborum significatione*, Sext 5.12.3 (Loserth "De Verborum Signif. V, 12, 3"; the modern form is ours) (LEDGER P05a-2). **R.** A in the margin: *Decretales invicem contradicunt scilicet Nicolai III et Johannis XXII*.

[^5]: **S.** John XXII's decretals on Franciscan poverty (e.g. *Ad conditorem canonum*, *Cum inter nonnullos*), which Loserth does not name (LEDGER P05a-3); to be checked.

[^6]: **G.** *imperficeret*, "would make imperfect": Wyclif's coinage against *perficere*; it returns as *inperfecerant* and *imperfecerunt*.

[^7]: **S.** *Decretum* D.10 c.9 (Loserth "Dist. X, cap. IX: Leges imperatorum custodiri oportet"; the modern form is ours) (LEDGER P05a-4). **C.** The argument runs on the opponent's premiss that riches perfect a pontiff: if so, the endowed caesars were the better pontiffs.

[^8]: **S.** *Decretum* C.14 q.5 c.10 *Denique* (Loserth "Decreti Secunda pars, causa XIV, q. V, cap. X"; the modern form is ours) (LEDGER P05a-5). **C.** *si non potest* is read with the canon's incipit, as Loserth's italic run takes it; *sacerdos* begins the rule Wyclif draws from it.

[^9]: **S.** John 19:12 (DR "whosoever maketh himself a king speaketh against Caesar"), with Wyclif's clause *per quod diminueretur imperium* inserted: the endowing lord diminishes his own lordship.

[^10]: **S.** 1 Tim. 6:8–9 (DR "having food, and wherewith to be covered, with these we are content. For they that will become rich fall into temptation …"), given by sense.

[^11]: **S.** "Riches are a weight on the soul": not identified by Loserth; a commonplace of the fathers (Gregory the Great?) (LEDGER P05a-6); to be checked. Matt. 6 is the sermon on solicitude, 6:25–34.
