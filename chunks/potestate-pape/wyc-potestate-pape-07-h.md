# *De potestate pape*, chapter 7, part 8: the eighth confirmation: the propertyless state of the clergy

## ¶1 · 160.5–161.31

> [p. 160] Ad octavam confirmacionem Johannis ultimo ubi Petrus singulariter recepit pastum ovium propter prerogativam amoris quo Christum et corpus suum dilexerat, vere concluditur primatus Petri predictus; quod si papa fuerit expers predicti amoris, quid sibi et papatui? Nunquid credimus Christum acceptare personam Romani pontificis propter elecciones aut invenciones humanas, cum hoc quod sit in vita et moribus Christo contrarius? Deliramentum blasfemum foret sic credere. Unde videtur quod papa timens mentiri Domino, servaret se in limitibus paupertatis et apostolice a mundo alienacionis, in quibus Petrum Christus constituit; certum quidem est, quod si mentitur de Christo et Petro, dicens quod virtute successionis vicarie Petri habet ex ordinacione divina tantam excellenciam mundanam super ecclesiam, oportet quod finaliter super verecundo mendacio sit convictus, quia cum peccatum sit mentiri super discolum in materia impertinente fidei; quanto maius peccatum est mentiri super beatos et caput ecclesie in materia fidei, in qua tota ecclesia est seducta? Si igitur papa sit Petri vicarius procuret pro ecclesia instar Petri. Petrus autem procuravit assidue quod clerus Christi poneretur in statu exproprietario plus perfecto, quod si papa viis et modis nitatur degradare se et clerum ab illo statu evangelico ad statum secularem proprietarium, quis dubitat, quin sit infidelissimus proditor, preparans viam dyabolo? Quomodo superabit [p. 161] ecclesiam Christi cum mammona? Ille, inquam, vivit exproprietarie qui ob amorem Dei et securitatem a seculi seduccione relinquit omnem titulum humane securitatis de dotacione vel elemosina perpetua, spe suspensus in Deo, cui in superiori ecclesie officio inturpate et assidue famuletur; qualiter vixerunt prophete legis veteris. Christus cum suis apostolis et multi posteriores docti ab illis. Unde paulative recepit iste status gradus et sophisticabatur mille versuciis; nam tempore apostolorum dividebantur exproprietarii, alii ministrando mensis et alii pure intendentes evangelizacioni et aliis spiritualibus operibus sacerdotis, ut patet Act. VI. Secundo recepit clerus glebam et paulative dotaciones ulteriores ex titulo elemosine sine affeccione vel securitate humana, ymo rapinam et oblacionem talium cum gaudio susceperunt. Et sic fuit tempore Augustini et aliorum sanctorum, qui leviter peccaverunt. Sed cum istis surrepsit venenum dyaboli, quo cum expropriacione de facto propriacionem immiscuit in affectum, ut patet de Scarioth et multis sequentibus; secundo vero subintroducte sunt lacius cautele dyaboli, quibus elemosine occupate a notorie indignis sunt ut hereditas avidissime vendicata. Et ad defendendum istam cleri avariciam sunt multe leges condite; in tantum quod oblito tali officio summe pro lucro temporali contenditur, et illud venenum est periculosius et extensius: ideo ve illis, qui fovent ipsum propter animum dominandi.

To the eighth confirmation, from the last chapter of John, where Peter alone received the feeding of the sheep because of the prerogative of the love with which he had loved Christ and his body:[^1] the aforesaid [primacy]{g:primatus} of Peter is truly inferred; but if the [pope]{g:papa} should be without the aforesaid love, what has he to do with the papacy?[^2] Do we believe that Christ accepts the person of the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex} because of human elections or devisings, when all the while he is contrary to Christ in life and manners? It would be blasphemous raving to believe so. Hence it seems that the pope, fearing to lie to the Lord, would keep himself within the limits of poverty and of the apostolic estrangement from the world in which Christ established Peter; and it is certain that if he lies about Christ and Peter, saying that by virtue of succession to the vicarship of Peter[^3] he has by divine [ordinance]{g:ordinacio} so great a worldly excellence over the [church]{g:ecclesia}, he must finally be convicted of a shameful lie; for if it is a sin to lie about a [froward]{g:discoli} man in a matter that does not pertain to faith, how much greater a sin is it to lie about the blessed and the [head]{g:caput} of the church in a matter of faith, in which the whole church is led astray? If therefore the pope is the [vicar of Peter]{g:vicarius-petri}, let him provide for the church after the manner of Peter. Now Peter assiduously provided that the [clergy]{g:clerus} of Christ should be placed in the more perfect [propertyless]{g:exproprietarii} [state]{g:status}; but if the pope by ways and means strives to degrade himself and the clergy from that evangelical state to the secular [proprietary]{g:proprietarius} state, who doubts that he is a most faithless traitor, preparing the way for the devil? How [else] will [the devil] overcome [161] the church of Christ [but] with mammon?[^4] That man, I say, lives without property who, for the love of God and for security from the seduction of the world, leaves every [title]{g:titulus} of human security from [endowment]{g:dotacio} or [perpetual alms]{g:elemosina-perpetua}, hanging in hope upon God, whom he is to serve unstainedly and assiduously in the higher office of the church: as the prophets of the old law lived, Christ with his apostles, and many later men taught by them. Hence this state gradually passed through stages[^5] and was [counterfeited]{g:sophisticare} with a thousand tricks; for in the time of the apostles the propertyless were divided, some serving tables and others attending purely to preaching the gospel and to the other spiritual works of a [priest]{g:sacerdos}, as is clear in Acts 6. Secondly, the clergy received the [glebe]{g:gleba} and gradually further endowments by title of [alms]{g:elemosina}, without affection for them or human security; indeed, they took with joy the being stripped of such things and the offering of them.[^6] And so it was in the time of Augustine and the other saints, who sinned lightly. But with these the devil's poison crept in, by which, together with [renunciation of property]{g:expropriacio} in fact, he mixed owning into the affection,[^7] as is clear of Iscariot and many who followed; and secondly the devil's [wiles]{g:cautela} were brought in more widely, by which alms occupied by the notoriously unworthy are claimed most greedily as an inheritance. And to defend this avarice of the clergy many laws have been made, so much so that, that office being forgotten, men contend to the utmost for temporal gain;[^8] and that poison is the more dangerous and the more widespread: therefore woe to those who foster it out of a will to [lord it]{g:dominari}.

## ¶2 · 161.32–163.8

> [p. 161] Sunt autem multi fuci, quibus discipuli Antichristi perturbant ecclesiam: querunt enim descripcionem vite exproprietarie et arguunt quod [p. 162] omnis homo vivit tam proprietarie quam exproprietarie, et sic inponunt magnam stulticiam pape Nicolao III. et sanctis doctoribus, ymo nostris religiosis, cum dicunt quod non sit statuum istorum distinccio. Notum est, inquam, ex modo vivendi tam preterito quam presenti quod in alio statu est clerus postquam recepit dotacionem cesaream et in alio antequam fuerit sic dotatus, sicut in alio statu vivunt religiosi possessionati, eciam in alio fratres et alii, quibus non sunt elemosine perpetue humanitus sic affixe. Status igitur exproprietarius cleri est status cleri conversandi sine dotacione et elemosinacione perpetua secularium dominorum. Et status cleri proprietarius est status cleri conversando ex dotacione vel elemosinacione perpetua secularium dominorum, ut contingit personis vel ecclesiis cleri quibus appropriatur ecclesia vel annuus redditus ex pacto elemosinacionis perpetue; que omnia oportet originari ex auctoritate dominorum secularium, licet alii de clero ad hoc adiuverint et autorizare false finxerint. Et patet quod talis appropriacio sapit ingratam diffidenciam de Christo et suis legibus, qua homo superflue exponit se spinis et temptacionibus huius mundi. Status igitur exproprietarius non est non habere temporalia, sed habere ex puro titulo Dei sine autorizacione humana vel sollicitudine preter necessaria ad cleri officium. Nec obest perfeccioni illius status quod sint in illo statu pessimi, quia distinguitur a statu morum. Supposito igitur quod status ille sit alcior atque perfeccior, patet quod ille papa ingrate et infideliter ducit ecclesiam, [p. 163] quia nititur statum illum deprimere et alium exaltare. Non enim licet degradare clerum Christi a statu quem ipse instituit, sed status proprietarius in quo per papam confirmatur ecclesia est inferior statui quem Christus instituit, igitur non licet pape sine auctoritate divina a statu illo deicere clerum Christi. Petrus enim vel Paulus non habuit huiusmodi potestatem.

And there are many disguises with which the disciples of Antichrist trouble the church: for they ask for a [definition]{g:descripcio} of the propertyless life, and argue that [162] every man lives both proprietarily and without property; and so they impute great folly to Pope Nicholas III[^9] and to the holy doctors, indeed to our [religious]{g:religiosi}, when they say that there is no distinction between these states. It is known, I say, from the manner of living both past and present, that the clergy is in one state after it received the caesarean endowment and in another before it was so endowed, just as the [possessioned]{g:possessionatus} religious live in one state, and in another the friars and others to whom perpetual alms are not thus humanly fixed. Therefore the propertyless state of the clergy is the state of the clergy living without endowment and perpetual [almsgiving]{g:elemosinacio} from [secular lords]{g:seculares-domini}. And the proprietary state of the clergy is the state of the clergy living from endowment or perpetual almsgiving from secular lords, as happens to persons or churches of the clergy to which a church or an annual [revenue]{g:reditus} is [appropriated]{g:appropriare} by a compact of perpetual almsgiving; all of which must originate from the [authority]{g:auctoritas} of secular lords, although others of the clergy have helped toward it and have falsely feigned to [authorize]{g:autorizare} it. And it is clear that such appropriation savours of an [ungrateful]{g:ingratus} distrust of Christ and his laws, by which a man needlessly exposes himself to the thorns and temptations of this world.[^10] Therefore the propertyless state is not to have no [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, but to have them by the pure title of God, without human authorization or anxiety beyond what is necessary for the office of the clergy. Nor does it hurt the perfection of that state that there are very bad men in that state, because it is distinguished from the state of morals.[^11] Supposing therefore that that state is higher and more perfect, it is clear that that pope leads the church ungratefully and faithlessly, [163] since he strives to depress that state and exalt the other. For it is not lawful to degrade the clergy of Christ from the state which he himself instituted; now the proprietary state in which the church is confirmed by the pope is inferior to the state which Christ instituted; therefore it is not lawful for the pope, without divine authority, to cast the clergy of Christ down from that state. For Peter or Paul had no such [power]{g:potestas}.

## ¶3 · 163.9–163.21

> [p. 163] Item, papa tenetur specialiter stare pro privilegiis cleri sui, sed precipuum privilegium per quod distinguuntur cleri a laycis stat in hoc quod cleri serventur in statu exproprietario alciori, igitur hoc privilegium debet papa in se et subditis observare. Sic enim fecit Apostolus in quo locutus est Christus Heb. XIII, 5, 6 quando sic docuit: Sint mores sine avaricia, contenti presentibus; ipse enim dixit: Non te deseram neque derelinquam Ita ut confidenter dicamus: Dominus michi adiutor, et non timebo quid faciat michi homo. Que doctrina videtur expresse facere pro expropriacione contra propriacionem.

Again, the pope is bound especially to stand for the [privileges]{g:privilegium} of his clergy; now the chief privilege by which clerics are distinguished from laymen consists in this, that clerics be kept in the higher propertyless state; therefore the pope ought to observe this privilege in himself and in his subjects. For so did the Apostle, in whom Christ spoke,[^12] Heb. 13:5–6, when he taught thus: "Let your manners be without covetousness, contented with such things as you have. For he hath said: I will not leave thee: neither will I forsake thee. So that we may confidently say: The Lord is my helper: and I will not fear what man shall do to me."[^13] Which [doctrine]{g:doctrina} seems expressly to make for renunciation of property against owning.

## ¶4 · 163.22–164.32

> [p. 163] Item, papa non habet potestatem a Deo ad deteriorandum sed meliorandum ecclesiam, ut dicit Apostolus de se ipso II. Cor. X, 8: Si aliquid amplius gloriatus fuero de potestate nostra, quam nobis dedit Dominus in edificacionem et non in destruccionem. Et patet de racione; nam bonus dominus cesar semper Augustus non dat potestatem homini, ut dampnificet sponsam suam, magna autem dampnificacio foret statum tam altum et securum perdere sine equivalencia recompense; omnis enim mundana ditacio non potest attingere illum statum, cum maior ditacio plus abducit. Quomodo igitur autorizabit Christi vicarius [p. 164] ex fastu vel avaricia sic ducere gregem Domini per abrupta; cum posset securius in pascuis quas Christus instituit ducere oves suas? Securius dico propter dietam a morbis putridis preservantem, propter facilitatem tenendi ad pascua semper virencia preparantem, et propter securitatem insultus latronis et male bestie excludentem. Sic enim prudens paterfamilias seminat semen bonum non inter spinas, ne ex earum cremento frugum contrario suffocetur. Scit enim Deus infirmum statum sue familie quomodo prona est ad omnia morborum genera; ideo ut summus medicus iniungit dietam, que licet videatur antichristo Romano contraria, est tamen statui nostro fragili summe consona. Nam iniungit nobis quoad statum exproprietarium relinquere omnia; quod debemus nos curati facere; viventes pure de decimis, oblacionibus et privatis elemosinis laycorum. Et quia cum illo statu exproprietario stat elemosinarum abusio, ideo secundum beatum Jeronimum Petrus adiungit, quod perfectum est scilicet statum morum; dum dicit: Et secuti sumus te, sine hoc enim non potest esse salus fidelium. Sed illius; qui reliquit omnia, est status perfeccior quam remanentis in seculo, quia statui summo propinquior. Istam autem materiam decet cunctos fideles cognoscere, quia caucius cavebunt a lupis qui veniunt in vestimentis ovium; securius sequentur Christi vestigia dimissis viis Antichristi, et prudencius preparabitur ad discredendum suis blasfemiis et ab adhibendo sibi cultus indebitos et consensum.

Again, the pope does not have power from God to make the church worse but to make it better, as the Apostle says of himself, 2 Cor. 10:8: "If I should boast somewhat more of our power, which the Lord hath given us unto edification and not for destruction."[^14] And it is clear by reason; for the good Lord, Caesar ever Augustus, does not give a man power to harm his bride;[^15] now it would be a great harm to lose a state so high and secure without an equivalent recompense; for no worldly enriching can reach that state, since greater enriching leads further away. How then will the [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius-christi} authorize [himself], [164] out of pride or avarice, thus to lead the Lord's flock over precipices, when he could more securely lead his sheep in the pastures which Christ instituted? More securely, I say, because of the diet that preserves from putrid diseases, because of the ease of keeping to ever-green pastures that it provides, and because of the security that shuts out the assault of the robber and of the evil beast.[^16] For so a prudent householder sows good seed not among thorns, lest it be choked by their growth, which is contrary to the crops.[^17] For God knows the infirm state of his [household]{g:familia}, how prone it is to all kinds of disease; therefore, as the supreme physician, he enjoins a diet which, although it seems contrary to the Roman antichrist,[^18] is yet supremely consonant with our fragile state. For he enjoins us, as to the propertyless state, to leave all things; which we [curates]{g:curatus} ought to do, living purely from [tithes]{g:decime}, offerings and the private alms of laymen.[^19] And because an abuse of alms can stand together with that propertyless state, therefore, according to blessed Jerome, Peter adds what is perfect, namely the state of morals, when he says: "and have followed thee" (Matt. 19:27); for without this there can be no salvation of the faithful.[^20] But the state of him who has left all things is more perfect than that of one who remains in the world, because it is nearer the highest state. And it befits all the faithful to know this matter, because they will more warily beware of the wolves who come in the clothing of sheep;[^21] they will more securely follow the footsteps of Christ, leaving the ways of Antichrist;[^22] and [the way] will be more prudently prepared for disbelieving his blasphemies and for withholding from him undue worship and consent.

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** John 21:15–17, the threefold "Feed my sheep" after "lovest thou me more than these?". A marks the place in the margin "Ad 8." (the eighth confirmation of the chapter's thesis).
[^2]: **C.** *Quid sibi et papatui?*, the idiom of *quid mihi et tibi* (John 2:4): "what is there between him and the papacy?" The print's "?," after *amoris* is kept in the Latin and not rendered.
[^3]: **C.** *Successionis vicarie Petri*: *vicarie* read as the genitive of *vicaria*, "vicarship"; it could also be the adjective, "the vicarial succession of Peter". The sense is the same.
[^4]: **C.** The subject of *superabit* is unexpressed: taken as the devil, for whom the pope prepares the way; it may be the pope himself (*superare*, "surpass": "How will he surpass the church of Christ with mammon?").
[^5]: **C.** *Recepit iste status gradus*, literally "this state received degrees": it came down step by step, as the stages that follow (*nam … Secundo … secundo vero*) set out.
[^6]: **S.** *Rapinam … cum gaudio susceperunt* echoes Heb. 10:34, "took with joy the being stripped of your own goods"; the DR phrase is kept and *oblacionem* ("offering") added. **C.** The early clergy took gifts and losses alike without affection.
[^7]: **G.** *Propriacio* here and at 163.21 is the opposite of *expropriacio* "renunciation of property": "owning", making a thing one's own. It is not *appropriacio* (the annexing of a church, 162.21). **C.** *In affectum*: into the will, while the renunciation stood "in fact".
[^8]: **C.** *Summe* may go with *oblito* ("that office being utterly forgotten") rather than with *contenditur*; we take it with the verb.
[^9]: **S.** Nicholas III's decretal *Exiit qui seminat* (1279), which defined the Franciscans' use of goods without lordship (*Sext.* 5.12.3, Friedberg II; the form is ours; LEDGER P07h-1).
[^10]: **T.** A reads *spernis* for *spinis* (Loserth). **S.** The thorns of the parable, Matt. 13:7, 22. **G.** *Ingratus*: ungrateful toward Christ, who provides (GLOSSARY: "ungrateful" where a person is ungrateful to someone).
[^11]: **C.** The *status morum* is a man's moral condition; the propertyless state is a state of life and can hold bad men. The same pair returns at 164.24.
[^12]: **S.** *In quo locutus est Christus* echoes 2 Cor. 13:3, "of Christ that speaketh in me".
[^13]: **T.** The print has *contenti presentibus*, with the Vulgate; Loserth reports that the manuscripts read *contemptis presentibus* ("the present things despised"). **S.** Wyclif adds *et* before *non timebo*, rendered "and".
[^14]: **S.** Wyclif's citation omits the Vulgate's *Nam*, *vestram* ("your") and the close *non erubescam*; DR is adjusted to his Latin. **T.** Loserth reports that in the manuscripts the quotation (*Si aliquid … destruccionem*) stands in the margin, and that B lacks *et* before *patet*.
[^15]: **C.** Christ, given the imperial style *semper Augustus*; the bride is the church.
[^16]: **C.** The three participles agree with their nouns: the diet preserves, the ease is "providing" (*preparantem*), the security shuts out. An alternative takes all three with *dietam*.
[^17]: **C.** Loserth notes that the subject of *suffocetur* is *semen*, the seed. *Frugum contrario*: the thorns' growth is contrary to the crops. **S.** Matt. 13:7, 22.
[^18]: **T.** The print reads *antichristo Romano*; Loserth reports *Romani* in the manuscripts ("the antichrist of the Roman").
[^19]: **R.** *Nos curati*: Wyclif counts himself among the curates (the Crown presented him to the rectory of Lutterworth on 7 April 1374; LEDGER P07h-2).
[^20]: **S.** Jerome, *Commentary on Matthew* 3, on Matt. 19:27: leaving is not enough, so Peter adds what is perfect, "and have followed thee" (PL 26, 138: *Et quia non sufficit tantum relinquere, jungit quod perfectum est: et secuti sumus te*; LEDGER P07h-3). Loserth places the reference to Matt. 19:27 in the margin.
[^21]: **S.** Matt. 7:15, "who come to you in the clothing of sheep".
[^22]: **T.** The print reads *viis*; Loserth reports that the manuscripts read *aviis* ("the trackless ways"). For *preparabitur* he proposes supplying *via*, which we give in brackets.
