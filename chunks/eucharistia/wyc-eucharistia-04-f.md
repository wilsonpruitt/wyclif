# *De eucharistia*, chapter 4, part 6: the mixed chalice and the decretal's three teachings on the water

## ¶1 · 102.29–104.12

> [p. 102] Quoad ritum magis pertinenter conficimus et sic pertinenter inmiscemus aquam, cum sanguis et aqua figurati per hoc sacramentum fluxerunt de latere [p. 103] crucifixi Joh. XX°, 20. Et ideo (ut dicit Alexander papa) sic pertinenter quoad utrumque signatum recolitur memoria passionis. Nec oportet nos sollicitari de necessitate fidei ad distincte discernendum differenciam inter vinum et acetum, quia nullus nostrum in confinio conversionis sufficit illud facere; ymmo in liquore reputato et vere existente vinum secundum maiorem partem sui quecunque pars notabilis est acetum; nam aqua, terra etc. tam elementa quam mixta, licet sepe nobis insensibilia, sunt partes vini, et ideo mussitat decretalis libro III De Celebracione missarum capitulo Cum Marthe: Si aqua in vino confecto convertitur in partem Christi vel cedit in nihilum et recitando multas opiniones stolidas non diffinit. Prima sentencia est quod licet oportet aquam misceri vino in calice, tamen non debet fieri verbis sacramenti confeccio, antequam aqua assimiletur vino et sic aqua convertitur in aquam que est in Christo et vinum in sanguinem ut in hoc sacramento plene sit veritas et figura. Secunda est quod sit assimilacio aque in vinum et post totum transsubstanciatur in sanguinem, sic quod quelibet pars quantitativa consecrandi huiusmodi sit vinum, cum nulla pars quantitativa mixti sit elementum, licet phisici aquam a vino asserunt posse per artificium separari. Sed tercia est innaturalis sentencia cui videtur decretalis annuere: Potest, inquit, dici, quod aqua non transit in sanguinem sed remanet prioris vini accidentibus circumfusa ita quod vini saporem assumit; quod inde cognosci potest quod si post calicis consecracionem aliud vinum [p. 104] inmittatur in calicem, illud non transit in sanguinem, nec sanguini commiscetur, sed accidentibus prioris vini commixtum corpori quod sub eis latet undique circumfunditur non madidans circumfusum, ipsa tamen accidencia vinum appositum videntur afficere, quia si aqua fuerit apposita, vini saporem assumit. Contingit igitur accidencia mutare subiectum, sicut contingit subiectum accidencia permutare: cedit quippe natura miraculo et virtus supra consuetudinem operatur. Et finaliter videtur decretali illam sentenciam esse magis probabilem que asserit tam aquam quam vinum in Christi sanguinem transmutari.

As to the rite, we [consecrate]{g:conficere} more [fittingly]{g:pertinenter}, and so fittingly mingle in water, since the blood and water figured by this [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} flowed from the side [103] of the crucified, John 20:20.[^1] And therefore (as [Pope]{g:papa} Alexander says) the memory of the passion is thus fittingly recalled as to both [things signified]{g:signatum}.[^2] Nor need we be anxious, as of necessity of faith, to discern distinctly the difference between [wine]{g:vinum} and vinegar, since none of us is able to do that at the border of the conversion; indeed, in a liquid reputed to be, and [truly]{g:vere} being, wine according to its greater part, some notable part or other is vinegar.[^3] For water, earth, etc., both elements and [mixed bodies]{g:mixtum}, though often [imperceptible]{g:insensibilis} to us, are parts of wine; and therefore the [decretal]{g:decretalis} mutters, in book 3, *On the celebration of masses*, chapter *Cum Marthe*, "whether the water in the consecrated wine is converted into a part of Christ or [gives way]{g:cedere} into nothing", and, [reciting]{g:recitare} many stolid [opinions]{g:opinio}, does not define.[^4] The first [teaching]{g:sentencia} is that, although water must be mixed with the wine in the chalice, yet the [consecration]{g:confeccio} ought not to be made with the words of the sacrament before the water is assimilated to the wine; and so the water is converted into the water that is in Christ and the wine into the blood, so that in this sacrament there may be fully both truth and [figure]{g:figura}.[^5] The second is that there is an assimilation of the water into the wine, and afterwards the whole is [transubstantiated]{g:transsubstanciacio} into the blood, so that every [quantitative part]{g:pars-quantitativa} of what is to be consecrated in this way is wine, since no quantitative part of a mixed body is an element; although natural philosophers assert that water can be separated from wine by art. But the third is an unnatural teaching, to which the decretal seems to nod: "It can be said," it says, "that the water does not pass into the blood but remains surrounded by the [accidents]{g:accidens} of the former wine, so that it takes on the taste of the wine; which can be known from this, that if after the [consecration]{g:consecracio} of the chalice other wine [104] is put into the chalice, that does not pass into the blood, nor is it mixed with the blood, but, mingled with the accidents of the former wine, it is poured all round the body that lies hidden under them, without wetting what it surrounds; yet the accidents themselves seem to affect the wine added, since if water has been added it takes on the taste of the wine. It happens, then, that accidents change the [subject]{g:subiectum}, just as it happens that the subject changes the accidents: for [nature]{g:natura} gives way to miracle, and [power]{g:virtus} works above [custom]{g:consuetudo}."[^6] And finally the decretal seems to hold that teaching the more probable which asserts that both the water and the wine are transmuted into the blood of Christ.[^7]

## ¶2 · 104.13–104.28

> [p. 104] Quantum ad istam sentenciam patet philosophis et theologis quod ista est sentencia indigesta, nam quoad primam sentenciam patet philosophis quod sacerdos nescit quando aqua assimilatur vino, ergo sepe contingit quod aqua maneat seorsum per se quousque sacerdos insorbeat sacramentum, sicut sensualiter patet in hieme ubi aqua per se congelatur, et vinum ex calore intrinseco manet seorsum fluxibile, sicut primo, nec est color quod aqua solum vertatur in aquam lateris Christi et vinum in sanguinem, quia per idem quodlibet elementum in vino converteretur appropriate in elementum in Christo, cum tamen totus Christus sit in hoc sacramento ad quemlibet eius punctum, quia aliter non foret conversio aque in aquam sed oportet aquam infusam aque in Christi latere coequari vel ydemptificari aque alii.

As to this teaching, it is clear to philosophers and theologians that it is an undigested teaching.[^8] For as to the first teaching, it is clear to philosophers that the [priest]{g:sacerdos} does not know when the water is assimilated to the wine; therefore it often happens that the water remains apart, by itself, until the priest swallows the sacrament, as is [sensually]{g:sensualiter} clear in winter, when the water freezes by itself and the wine, from its intrinsic heat, remains apart, fluid, as at first.[^9] Nor is there [colour]{g:color} [for saying] that the water is turned only into the water of Christ's side and the wine into the blood, since [by the same token]{g:per-idem} every element in the wine would be [converted]{g:conversio} correspondingly into an element in Christ; although the whole Christ is in this sacrament at every point of it; since otherwise there would not be a conversion of water into water, but the water poured in would have to be made equal to the water in Christ's side, or [identified]{g:ydemptificare} with that other water.[^10]

## ¶3 · 104.29–105.16

> [p. 104] Quoad secundam sentenciam patet ut supra, quod non oportet aquam ante consecracionem assimilari vino nec facta assimilacione oportet quod sit ad omnem [p. 105] punctum commixtio, ut noverunt philosophi qui considerant quomodo elementa sunt in mixtis. Si enim post mixtionem sit vinum ad omnem punctum sine aliquo elemento, videtur pro tunc illud consecrandum esse alienum a figurato corporis Christi et sanguinis, ymmo superinfusio aque in vinum olim facta per laycum foret sufficiens. Non enim est sacramentum benediccionis aque a sacerdote qui debet celebrare tam necessarium, aquam tamen benedicimus et non vinum, vel quia primordialis materia ante benediccionem Dei vocata est aqua, ut patet Genes. primo, vel quia vinum expressius et proprius figurat illum sacrum sanguinem quem actuat anima Christi et non sic aqua, ideo eget benediccione; vel tercio quia baptismus figuratus per aquam est in re non sic sacra ut generis humani redempcio.

As to the second teaching, it is clear, as above, that the water need not be assimilated to the wine before the consecration, nor, when the assimilation is made, need there be mixture at every [105] point, as philosophers know who consider how the elements are in mixed bodies. For if after the mixing there is wine at every point without any element, then that which is to be consecrated seems to be foreign to the [thing figured]{g:figuratum}, the body and blood of Christ; indeed a pouring of water into the wine, made formerly by a layman, would suffice.[^11] For the sacrament of the blessing of the water by the priest who is to celebrate is not so necessary; yet we bless the water and not the wine, either because the primordial matter before God's blessing was called water, as is clear from Genesis 1; or because the wine more expressly and more properly [figures]{g:figurare} that [sacred]{g:sacer} blood which the soul of Christ actuates, and the water not so, and so it needs blessing; or, third, because baptism, figured by water, is in a thing not so sacred as the redemption of the human race.[^12]

## ¶4 · 105.17–105.23

> [p. 105] Quantum ad terciam sentenciam videtur primo quod aqua non sit pars huius sacramenti et per idem nec forma vini. Videtur secundo quod innaturaliter ponit aquam esse superinfusam accidentibus que quondam erant vini; tunc enim accidens naturaliter posset migrare de subiecto in subiectum et naturaliter per se unire se ut principium formale aque.

As to the third teaching, it seems, first, that the water would not be part of this sacrament, and by the same token not the form of the wine either.[^13] It seems, second, that it unnaturally posits the water poured over the accidents which were once the wine's; for then an accident could naturally migrate from subject to subject and naturally, [of itself]{g:per-se}, unite itself [to the water] as the formal principle of the water.[^14]

## ¶5 · 105.24–106.20

> [p. 105] Ex quo videtur quod illa aqua sit sacramentum, quia forma eius est sacramentum, et sic coleret homo illam aquam ut sanguinem, ydolatrando (ut adversarii probant) quod panis non remanet ob hanc causam; ymmo videtur, cum accidens potest esse ad omnem punctum corporis cum hoc quod non informet, sine evidencia ponitur accidens taliter informare, ymmo inconsequenter dicitur quod illud sacramentum est ponderositas et non ponderosum, cum quantitas et qualitas alterius speciei possunt recipere predicaciones [p. 106] huiusmodi in concreto. Nec videtur ex evidenciis quod accidencia prioris vini circumfunduntur vino post consecracionem iniecto, quia eque vinum infusum utribus vel alii vasi recipit saporem ab eis quod non potest fingi tingi a sapore per se latente in utribus, sed corpora subtilia latencia consumat, odorem et saporem, ex quorum commixtione liquor infusus intingitur; et forte sic intelligunt hii accidencia que per se existunt in hostia esse corpora subtilia modo quo pictores vocant corpora quibus liniendo pingunt colores pictorum; nam (ut fatentur) sacramentum calicis potest misceri cum vino, et sic totum aggregatum foret sacramentum sine ydolatria, et tam quantitas quam qualitas rarefacta composita ex suis partibus intensivis, posito quod unum aliud sacramentum, tercium et quartum commisceantur in calice que constituant unum sacramentum, et sic nec quantitas nec qualitas foret sacramentum eucharistie, sed vel vinum vel ex vino et sanctitate aut alio aggregatum. Veritas itaque irridet tales vanas ficticias.

From which it seems that that water is the sacrament, since its form is the sacrament; and so a man would worship that water as the blood, committing idolatry, which is the very ground on which the adversaries prove that the [bread]{g:panis} does not remain.[^15] Indeed it seems that, since an accident can be at every point of a body without [informing]{g:informare} it, it is posited without [evidence]{g:evidencia} that the accident informs in such a way. Indeed, it is said [inconsistently]{g:inconsequens} that that sacrament is heaviness and not heavy, since [quantity]{g:quantitas} and [quality]{g:qualitas} of another kind can receive predications [106] of this sort [in the concrete]{g:concretum}.[^16] Nor does it seem, from the evidences, that the accidents of the former wine are poured round the wine thrown in after the consecration; for equally wine poured into wineskins or another vessel receives taste from them, and it cannot be feigned to be tinged by a taste lying hidden by itself in the skins, but it takes up subtle bodies lying hidden, odour and taste, from whose mixture the liquid poured in is tinged.[^17] And perhaps in this way these men understand the accidents that exist of themselves in the [host]{g:hostia} to be subtle bodies, in the way in which painters call "bodies" the things with which, by smearing, they paint the painters' colours.[^18] For (as they admit) the sacrament of the chalice can be mixed with wine, and so the whole aggregate would be the sacrament without idolatry; and [so would] both the quantity and the quality, rarefied, composed of their intensive parts, supposing that one, another, a third and a fourth sacrament are mixed together in the chalice so as to make up one sacrament; and so neither quantity nor quality would be the sacrament of the eucharist, but either wine or an aggregate of wine and holiness or of something else.[^19] Truth therefore mocks such vain [fictions]{g:ficticia}.

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 102.29: The mixed chalice.
- 103.2: Needless cares on this subject, as in the decretal where three different theories as to what becomes of the water.
- 104.13: The first implies a knowledge which the priest does not possess, as to when the water is assimilated.
- 104.29: The second lays stress on assimilation which is unimportant.
- 105.17: The third involves a false view as to the relation of accidents.

## Notes

[^1]: **S/T.** The blood and water from the side are John 19:34 (DR "immediately there came out blood and water"); the Latin's reference, John 20:20, is the side shown to the disciples (DR "he shewed them his hands and his side"). The English keeps the Latin's reference. At 102.30 AB read *inmiscimus*, D *miscemus*; B *cum sanguine*, A *sanguine*: *sanguis* is Loserth's correction, translated.
[^2]: **S.** Pope Alexander (I), *De cons.* D. 2 c. 1 (Loserth 103.1, "cf."; form ours) (LEDGER K04f-1). **C.** *Utrumque signatum*: the blood and the water, both signified by the mixed chalice.
[^3]: **C.** *In confinio conversionis*: at the border where wine turns into vinegar; the *conversio* here is not the eucharistic one. *Quecunque pars notabilis est acetum* is construed as "some notable part or other is vinegar" (*quicunque* for *aliquis*); the alternative, "any notable part whatever", would make the whole vinegar.
[^4]: **S/T.** Innocent III, *X* 3.41.6 *Cum Marthe* (Loserth 103.12, "Decreti Greg. lib. III, tit. XLI, cap. VI"; form ours) (LEDGER K04f-2). Loserth (103.13): for *in partem* the decretal reads *in sanguinem*; the print is translated. At 103.11 ABD read *musita*; at 103.12 ABCD *misse*.
[^5]: **C.** *Sentencia* (CONTEXTUAL): "teaching", the position taught, for each of the three. The decretal's first opinion (Loserth 103.20, "cf. … *Cum Marthe* §1") (LEDGER K04f-3; *antequam aqua assimiletur vino* is Wyclif's addition, not in the decretal). At 103.20 D reads *fit veritas*.
[^6]: **S/T.** The decretal *Cum Marthe* (Loserth 103.24, 103.27, "ib.") (LEDGER K04f-4); the quotation runs from *Potest* to *operatur*, exactly the decretal's third opinion; the decretal's verdict (*illa probabilior iudicatur*) follows in paraphrase. At 103.26 C reads *naturalis*, corrected in the margin to the print's *innaturalis*; at 104.9 *et* (before *virtus*) is Loserth's addition (*Codd.: et deest. Addidi*), translated, and AB lack *natura*.
[^7]: **T.** At 104.11 ABCD read *probabile*; the print's *probabilem* (E) is translated.
[^8]: **T/C.** Loserth (104.13): *istam*, *rectius primam*. The print is translated: *istam sentenciam* is construed as the decretal's position taken as a whole, which the next clause (*nam quoad primam*) then takes up part by part. At 104.13–15 C writes *patet philosophis … sentenciam* twice, with *va … cat* in the margin.
[^9]: **G.** *Per se* here is spatial, "by itself", of the water standing apart, not the metaphysical "of itself" (105.23, 106.7). *Insorbeat*: "swallows" (consumes the sacrament in communion).
[^10]: **C.** *Color* (GLOSSARY: a plausible ground, the period sense). *Appropriate*: each element into its corresponding element in Christ (not *appropriacio* of canon law). The *cum tamen* clause gives the reason the correspondence is idle: the whole Christ is at every point. At 104.21 B reads *est calor*; at 104.27 D *oporteret*.
[^11]: **C.** *A figurato corporis Christi et sanguinis*: "the thing figured, the body and blood of Christ", construing the genitive as appositive. At 105.5 D reads *figuracione*.
[^12]: **S.** Genesis 1:2 (DR "the spirit of God moved over the waters"): the primordial matter called water before God's blessing; reference only. **T.** At 105.15 ABCE read *in se* for the print's *in re*; *sacra* agrees with *re*, and the print is translated. At 105.11 C has *Dei* in the margin.
[^13]: **C.** *Forma vini*: the form of the wine, i.e. the accidents that the third teaching says remain; if the water is not part of the sacrament, neither are those accidents, by the same token. At 105.18–19 D reads *ut forma*.
[^14]: **C.** *Unire se ut principium formale aque*: the accident would join itself to the water as its formal principle; "to the water" is supplied from the sense. At 105.22 D reads *migrari*.
[^15]: **C.** *Ydolatrando (ut adversarii probant) quod panis non remanet ob hanc causam*: construed as "committing idolatry, by which (*ob hanc causam*) the adversaries prove that the bread does not remain": they argue that if the bread remained, worship of the host would be idolatry. At 105.25 D reads *forma cuius*; at 105.27 C *remaneat*.
[^16]: **C.** *Ponderositas … ponderosum*: the abstract "heaviness" against the concrete "heavy". *Alterius speciei*: quantity and quality in another kind of thing; they take concrete predication there, so denying it here is inconsistent.
[^17]: **C.** The sentence is compressed and reported as a doubt: *fingi tingi* (D lacks *fingi*) is translated as printed, "be feigned to be tinged", and *consumat*, without an expressed subject, is taken of the wine poured in. At 106.1 ABC read *Hoc videtur*; at 106.2 the print's *vino* (ABC *ymmo*; C corrects to *vino*; E lacks it).
[^18]: **C.** *Corpora … quibus liniendo pingunt colores pictorum*: the painters' "bodies" are the media with which colours are laid on. At 106.8 C has *que* in the margin; at 106.9 D reads *corporata*.
[^19]: **C.** *Et tam quantitas quam qualitas rarefacta …*: elliptical; construed as a further member of what "would be the sacrament", with "[so would]" supplied. At 106.19 E reads *aggregato*; at 106.20 B *venas* (for *vanas*).
