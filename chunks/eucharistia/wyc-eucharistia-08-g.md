# *De eucharistia*, chapter 8, part 7: against the fiction of a discrete angelic time

## ¶1 · 255.24–256.5

> [p. 255] Contra istam ficticiam arguitur primo per hoc, quod nichil est ponendum sine racione philosophica vel testimonio ex scriptura; sed neutrum istorum docet hanc duplicitatem temporis, ergo non est ponenda. Assumptum patet ex hoc quod aliter positum foret sompnium infundabile, irracionabile positum, cum pari evidencia infinita alia forent fingenda contra regulas nature que compendiosiori modo sine superfluitate [p. 256] semper agit, et Deus irracionabiliter oneraret ecclesiam fidelium ad credendum, et veritas oneraret falsitatem atque mendacium; et minor patet per consequens, hoc autem est commune argumentum temptativum, quo opinans cogitur dare evidenciam vie sue.

Against this [fiction]{g:ficticia} it is argued, first, through this: that nothing is to be posited without philosophical reason or testimony from scripture; now neither of these teaches this doubleness of time; therefore it is not to be posited. [The assumption]{g:assumptum} is clear from this, that otherwise what is posited would be a dream [without foundation]{g:infundabilis}, a thing posited without reason, since by the same [evidence]{g:evidencia} infinitely many other things would have to be [feigned]{g:fingere} against the rules of nature, which [256] always acts in the more compendious way, without superfluity; and God would unreasonably burden the [church]{g:ecclesia} of the faithful with believing, and truth would burden falsehood and lying;[^1] and the minor is clear in consequence. And this is a common [tentative argument]{g:argumentum-temptativum},[^2] by which one who holds an opinion is forced to give evidence for his way.

## ¶2 · 256.6–256.21

> [p. 256] Item, cum tempus sit communis mensura omnium temporaliter se habencium, et omnis angelus (in quantum sic) mutat vicissitudinem temporalem se habet temporaliter, videtur quod tempus commune sufficit mensurare omnem vicissitudinem angeli temporalem; non enim oportet ponere ad hoc novum genus temporis, cum antiquum tempus sufficit numerare illam vicissitudinem, sicut mensurat motum angeli et alias vicissitudines insensibiles; aliter enim oporteret ponere in Deo tempus propter variacionem sue accionis extrinsece quod tempus ipsum mensuret limitacius quam eternitas. Si ergo tempus nostrum sufficeret mensurare eciam Dei creanciam successivam, a pari vel maiori sufficeret mensurare affeccionem angeli successivam; nam tempus meriti mensurat motus intellectus hominis vicissitudinem inplicantes.

Again, since time is the common measure of all things that stand temporally, and every angel ([insofar as]{g:in-quantum} he thus) changes in temporal alternation, [and] stands temporally,[^3] it seems that common time suffices to measure every temporal alternation of an angel; for there is no need to posit a new genus of time for this, since the ancient time suffices to number that alternation, as it measures the motion of an angel and other [imperceptible]{g:insensibilis} alternations. For otherwise it would be necessary to posit time in God on account of the variation of his extrinsic action, a time that would itself measure more limitedly than eternity. If therefore our time would suffice to measure even God's successive [creating]{g:creancia}, [from the equal or from the greater]{g:a-maiori-vel-pari} it would suffice to measure the angel's successive affection; for the time of merit measures motions of the intellect of man that involve alternation.

## ¶3 · 256.22–257.14

> [p. 256] Item, Deus de potencia sua absoluta posset permittere variacionem affeccionis angeli sine hoc quod produceret novum genus temporis, sed mensuraret affecciones sibi succedentes tempore communi; non ergo sequitur ex variacione talium affeccionum novum genus temporis; alias sic: Una affeccio potest esse permanens quantumcunque diuturna mensurata evo, post quam potest alia affeccio permanens succedere, et Deus non foret necessitatus pro mensura unius affeccionis nec pro mensura alterius nec pro instanti temporis medio novum genus temporis procreare, ergo hoc foret Deo possibile; nam commune evangelium [p. 257] sufficeret mensurare sequentem, nec pro instanti temporis potest tempus discretum subito secundum se totum produci, ymmo sicut dicit super distinccione XIᵃ, IVᵗⁱ Sentenciarum quod est dare ultimum tempus et non ultimum instans in quo manebit forma panis et primum instans, in quo erit corpus Christi sub illis accidentibus, ita videtur consequenter dicendum quod est de affeccione angelica variata sine creacione novi generis temporis, cum nec evum potest esse pars temporis nec duo vel finita instancia temporis possunt constituere tempus quantumcunque longum, quia sic foret tempus discretum, per accidens longum tempore nostro continue mensuratum; et patet quod ista ficticia est irracionabilis sine causa.

Again, God by his [absolute power]{g:potencia-absoluta} could permit a variation of an angel's affection without producing a new genus of time, but would measure the affections succeeding one another by common time; therefore a new genus of time does not [follow]{g:sequitur} from the variation of such affections. Otherwise, thus: one affection can be permanent, however long-lasting, measured by [aeviternity]{g:evum}, after which another permanent affection can succeed; and God would not be necessitated, for the measure of the one affection, nor for the measure of the other, nor for the instant of time between, to procreate a new genus of time; therefore this would be possible to God. For the common aeviternity[^4] [257] would suffice to measure the following [affection]; nor at an instant of time can a discrete time be produced suddenly, whole according to itself. Indeed, as he says on distinction 11 of the fourth [book] of the *Sentences*, "that [one may posit]{g:est-dare} a last time and not a last instant in which the form of [bread]{g:panis} will remain, and a first instant in which the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} will be under those [accidents]{g:accidens},"[^5] so it seems one must consequently say that it is [so][^6] with the angelic affection varied without the creation of a new genus of time; since neither can aeviternity be a part of time, nor can two or finitely many instants of time constitute a time however long, because so it would be a discrete time, long [by accident]{g:per-accidens}, measured continuously by our time. And it is clear that this fiction is unreasonable, without cause.

## ¶4 · 257.15–258.21

> [p. 257] Item, pari evidencia qua crearetur unum novum genus temporis ex vicissitudine affeccionum in uno angelo, et in alio, et sic forent quotlibet tempora simul, quorum nullum foret pars alicuius temporis, quod est inpossibile; et consequencia videtur ex hoc quod non est fingenda racio quare propter unum angelum malum variatum in affeccione variari debet genus temporis quin per idem ad vicissitudinem cuiuscunque, ymmo propter motum localem angeli vel variacionem affeccionis anime, cum motus ille non ordinatur ad motum primi mobilis; non enim scitur vel requiritur quod vicissitudo affeccionis angelice mensuretur per variacionem affeccionis primi angeli tamquam per primam mensuram, quia Deus potest libere variare affeccionem cuiuscunque angeli stante affeccione in quocunque reliquo. Si ergo ista sit mora [p. 258] affeccionis angelice que potest esse tempus et nullum tempus potest per accidens esse tempus, cum tempus sit per se species quantitatis, sequitur quod ista mora est tempus; et iuxta hoc sequitur quod omne evum angeli sit instans temporis vel tempus, quia potest esse tempus vel instans temporis et est quidquid potest esse. Si enim angelus variaret affeccionem suam in futurum, tunc mora istarum duarum affeccionum foret tempus quod haberet unam partem mensurantem primam affeccionem et aliam mensurantem secundam; et cum omne evum non potest esse longius vel brevius, sicut nec instans temporis discreti, sequitur quod omne tale sit equale tempori sempiterno; et ita videtur omne tempus discretum componi solum ex duobus instantibus vel saltem tanto tempori coequari. Ad quid ergo poneretur tale discretum esse tempus in cuius diuturnitate non ponitur certitudo? Nam sicut idem foret angelus, sive duraret perpetuo sive annichilaret cicius vel tardius, sic est de suo evo: ideo philosophi habent dicere quod evitas per accidens est eternitas, sicut accidenter respicit moram temporis.

Again, by the same evidence by which one new genus of time would be created from the alternation of affections in one angel, [it would be created] in another too, and so there would be any number of times at once, none of which would be part of any time, which is impossible. And the [inference]{g:consequencia} appears from this, that no reason is to be feigned why, on account of one bad angel varied in affection, the genus of time ought to be varied, without [its varying], by the same token, for the alternation of anyone whatever; indeed, on account of the local motion of an angel or the variation of a soul's affection, since that motion is not ordered to the motion of [the first movable]{g:primum-mobile}. For it is not known, or required, that the alternation of angelic affection be measured by the variation of the affection of the first angel as by the first measure, because God can freely vary the affection of any angel whatever while the affection in any other one stands. If, then, this be an abiding [258] of angelic affection which can be time, and no time can be time [by accident]{g:per-accidens}, since time is [of itself]{g:per-se} a species of [quantity]{g:quantitas}, it follows that this abiding is time; and according to this it follows that every aeviternity of an angel is an instant of time or time, because it can be time or an instant of time, and is whatever it can be.[^7] For if an angel varied his affection in the future, then the abiding of these two affections would be a time that would have one part measuring the first affection and another measuring the second; and since no aeviternity can be longer or shorter, as neither can an instant of discrete time, it follows that every such [aeviternity] is equal to everlasting time; and so it seems that every discrete time is composed only of two instants, or at least is made equal to so much time. To what end, then, would such a discrete [thing] be posited to be time, in whose length no certainty is posited? For as the angel would be the same whether he endured perpetually or were annihilated sooner or later,[^8] so it is with his aeviternity: therefore the philosophers have to say that aeveity is eternity by accident, as it accidentally regards the abiding of time.[^9]

## ¶5 · 258.22–259.18

> [p. 258] Item, tempus discretum ex affeccione angelorum est prius ac dignius quam tempus nostrum continuum et eque sufficiens ad mensurandum omnem motum vel vicissitudinem sensibilem, ergo superfluit ponere aliud; per hoc enim quod duracio motus primi mobilis sufficit mensurare omnes motus alios ponit secta ista ipsam specialiter esse tempus. Si ergo duracio angelica sufficit prius mensurare ipsum motum, sequitur quod foret tempus ad ipsum et eius duracio solum quando. Nec valet dicere quod vicissitudo angelica non est continua, quia utraque eius medietas que est instans illius temporis est vere continua; cur ergo non totum, cum una pars sequitur immediate ad [p. 259] reliquam, et hoc sufficit ad continuacionem temporis; ymmo videtur quod in quolibet instanti temporis nostri est mutacio vicissitudinis affeccionis angelice; nam angelus videt nedum creaturas in verbo sed in genere proprio; cum ergo angelus videt continue noviter presenciam ac novitatem creature genite pro quolibet instanti temporis nostri, videtur quod continue habet novam noticiam vel modum sciendi. Sicut enim tempus discretum consequitur ad affeccionem vel volicionem angeli, ita sequitur ad intuicionem et quamcunque variacionem ex parte angeli, et ita (ut arguit Doctor Subtilis contra hanc viam) nedum simul forent multa tempora sed numeri angelorum variancium affecciones suas, que tempora ut subiecta et obiecta forent distincta in specie, sed idem angelus in numero secundum variaciones intencionum et affeccionum circa diversa obiecta in specie causaret simul diversa tempora in specie.

Again, discrete time from the affection of angels is prior to and worthier than our continuous time, and equally sufficient to measure every [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} motion or alternation; therefore it is superfluous to posit another. For through this, that the duration of the motion of the first movable suffices to measure all other motions, this [sect]{g:secta} posits it specially to be time. If then the angelic duration suffices, prior [to it], to measure that motion, it follows that it would be time to it, and its duration only a "when".[^10] Nor is it sound to say that the angelic alternation is not continuous, because each half of it, which is an instant of that time, is [truly]{g:vere} continuous;[^11] why then not the whole, since one part follows [without intermediary]{g:immediate} upon [259] the other, and this suffices for the [continuation]{g:continuacio} of time? Indeed, it seems that in every instant of our time there is a change of alternation of angelic affection; for the angel sees creatures [not only]{g:nedum} in [the Word]{g:verbum} but in their proper genus; since, then, the angel continually sees anew the presence and newness of the creature begotten for every instant of our time, it seems that he continually has a new knowledge or mode of knowing. For as discrete time follows upon the affection or volition of an angel, so it follows upon intuition and any variation on the angel's side; and so (as [the Subtle Doctor]{g:doctor-subtilis} argues against this way)[^12] not only would there be many times at once, but [as many as] the numbers of angels varying their affections,[^13] which times would be distinct in species as [their] subjects and objects [are]; but the same angel in number, according to the variations of intentions and affections about objects diverse in species, would cause at once times diverse in species.

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Veritas oneraret falsitatem*: construed as it stands, truth (perhaps God, the Truth) burdening falsehood, i.e. made to carry it; the sense may be "truth would be charged with falsehood". The doubt is noted, not resolved.
[^2]: **G.** *Argumentum temptativum*: the testing argument of the dialecticians (Aristotle's peirastic), which makes the respondent show grounds for his position. It has no row. D reads *patet per posterius*, E *patet posterius* for *patet per consequens* (256.3–4).
[^3]: **C.** *Omnis angelus (in quantum sic) mutat … se habet temporaliter*: two finite verbs with no connective; construed as "every angel, insofar as he thus changes in temporal alternation, stands temporally". A word (*qui* or *et*) may have been lost (256.8).
[^4]: **T.** The print has *commune evangelium*, "the common gospel", which does not construe in an argument about measures; we read *evum* "aeviternity" (our emendation), the same confusion as *evangelice* for *angelice* at 255.3, where Loserth prefers C's *angelico*. The apparatus gives no variant (256.33).
[^5]: **S.** Thomas, *In IV Sent.* d. 11; Loserth's foot quotes it: *Et ideo dicendum quod non est designare ultimum instans, sed ultimum tempus in quo est panis …* (d. 11 q. 1 a. 3, Solutio). The same reply ends *in toto tempore praecedenti erat panis, praeter ultimum instans, in quo est corpus Christi*, Wyclif's first instant of the body; his *forma panis* is his own for Thomas's *panis* (LEDGER K08g-1).
[^6]: **T.** *Dicendum quod est de affeccione*: a *sic* ("that it is so") seems lost before or after *est*; supplied in brackets (257.5–6).
[^7]: **S.** *Est quidquid potest esse*: the principle that in everlasting things to be possible and to be do not differ (Aristotle, *Phys.* III.4, 203b30: "in the case of eternal things what may be must be") (LEDGER K08g-2).
[^8]: **C.** *Annichilaret* is active in form ("annihilated"); taken as "were annihilated", the angel being the one whose duration is in question.
[^9]: **C.** *Evum* is rendered "aeviternity" (the angel's measure) and *evitas* "aeveity", to keep the two words apart; neither has a row. *Per accidens* "by accident" is kept apart from *accidenter* "accidentally".
[^10]: **C.** *Quando*: the category "when". If angelic duration were the first measure, the duration of the first movable's motion would be a mere "when" of it, not time.
[^11]: **C.** *Utraque eius medietas que est instans illius temporis*: each half is an instant of discrete time, yet within itself continuous; the argument is that two continuous parts that follow without intermediary make a continuous whole.
[^12]: **S.** Scotus against the discrete time of the angels (*Ordinatio* II d. 2 p. 1 q. 4, nn. 158–159, against Henry of Ghent: *magnam multitudinem temporum*, a discrete time for every angel and two in each, with "nows" differing in species) (LEDGER K08g-3).
[^13]: **C.** *Sed numeri angelorum variancium*: construed as "[as many times as] the numbers of angels varying"; the phrase does not construe without a supplied word. At 259.14 D reads *que ita ut* for *que tempora ut*, and ABCD *substancia et* for *subiecta et*; the print is translated.
