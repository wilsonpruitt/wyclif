## Doubtful, left as they are

- **09-k ¶1, p. 312**: *sic sophisticant …; sed sicut sunt in signis … contradictorii sibi ipsis, sic alternatim contradicunt …; ideo non est conveniens in hoc testimonium eorum*, rendered "but just as they are contradictory". An *ideo* follows, so (a) "now" is possible: the premiss that grounds rejecting their testimony. But the first clause is not a major premiss; it is set against this one (they agree when cloaking the caesarean life, but contradict themselves on the accidents). So (b) "but" stands.
- **09-k ¶2, p. 313**: *Sed et nos qui ex obligacione fidelium vivimus* (inside the Gregory quotation, Hom. 17), rendered "But we too". *Sed et* may only be intensive ("and we too"), but "But" carries the connective and keeps Gregory's turn from those who foster sins to "we" ourselves.
- **09-l ¶3, p. 317**: *Sed tercio modo adoramus ymagines nude ut signa*, rendered "But in the third way". This could be (c), the third item of the enumeration ("And in the third way"). It is also set against the two excellences that ground adoration, so (b) is defensible.
- **09-k ¶4, p. 314**: *Cultum autem rectum non reprobo*, rendered "And right worship". This could be (b), turning from the "conficere … facere corpus Christi" complaint to what he approves. (c) is defensible.
