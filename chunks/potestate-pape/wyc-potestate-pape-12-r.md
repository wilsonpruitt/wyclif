# *De potestate pape*, chapter 12, part 18: dispensations: the pope only promulgates what God allows

## ¶1 · 381.3–383.1

> [p. 381] Et quantum ad alia privilegia, ut licenciam varie celebrandi dispensacionem cum irregularibus, bigamis et coniugatis ad sacerdocium vel matrimonium cum infinitis huiusmodi adinventis, videtur michi quod Deus sepe dispensat cum talibus. Sed hoc ex fide capio quod non licet pape vel homini dispensare in quocumque cum aliquo, nisi Deus dispenset ante ea in eodem, sic quod vicarius Christi dispensans in talibus est nudus divine sentencie promulgator, quia cum non dependent sacramentaliter a suo officio, non restat sibi medium dispensandi nisi per modum meriti vel discrecionis promulgandi non esse periculum. Unde quia solet esse in papa maior sanctitas atque discrecio, ideo solet laycus in talibus ad papam recurrere, et modo reputatum est ut sacramentum divinissimum quod ad eum spectat singulariter ista concedere; videtur enim quod ista primo discerneret, si sint racionabilia et edificativa ecclesie, quia stante contrario non obstante sua concessione, tam ipse quam privilegiatus deceptorie est in culpa. Quod si sint racionabilia et edificatoria, quicumque promulgaverit eorum licenciam, ymo sola racione movente est licitum secundum illa procedere, cum Deus per se faciat hec licere, nec potest fundari ex humanis tradicionibus adinventis quod oportet capere generaliter sic a prelatis licenciam; vel quod licet eis sic restringendo potestatem populi vel presbiteri in neutris vel licitis ex fide scripture; servare sibi licenciam ad procedendum in talibus singulariter vel quod aliter obedienciam istam non faciens; eciam plus quam propter prevaricacionem decalogi puniatur; quia cuiuslibet christiani et specialiter prelati est ampliare atque defendere [p. 382] privilegia et libertates, quas Christus concessit ecclesie, ut patet Act. XV, sic quod peccatum antichristianum foret libertatem illam tollere vel limites in altero extremo excedere. Ideo oportet fidelem videre secundum legem scripture, exprimendo vel tacendo sic licitum, et procedere secundum doctrinam huius exemplaris generaliter et licenter. Unde pene et dispensacionum reservaciones sive licencie nimis excecant populum, sicut dicam, si Deus voluerit, in materia de sacramentis. Dicitur enim quod sacerdos benedicens in secundis nupciis nedum suspenditur, sed eo ipso irregularitatem incurrit, sed faciendo mille enormitates Deo odibiles nedum reputatur immunis, sed pro pecunia collaudatur; et fundamentum in hiis omnibus est ficta obediencia, sic quod non foret tam grave benedicendo implorare super prius nuptis divinam graciam, nisi quia leges papales precipiunt quod non fiat. Inobediencia, inquiunt, facta statutis papalibus, est gravissime punienda, et sic Christus cum lege sua postponitur. Sic enim papa posset in tollendo vel difficultando legem Christi decernere quod nullus christianus licite faciat aliquod opus neutrum, nisi quod ipse posterius approbando ratificat, et consequenter ordinare satrapas suos ad citandum quoscumque comparere responsuros in suo iudicio, cum legem consimilem ordinat Ihesus noster, ut dicunt parificatur Christo humanitus; sic enim possent ad votum vexare et mulctare populum, ut faciunt in absolucionibus, in reservacionibus et in dispensacionibus, et, ut creditur, practizarent hec amplius, nisi quia timent sibi [p. 383] quod populus percipiens suam versuciam rebellaret.

And as to other [privileges]{g:privilegium}, such as [licence]{g:licencia} to celebrate in various ways, dispensation with the irregular, [bigamists]{g:bigamia} and the married for the priesthood or for matrimony,[^1] with countless invented things of this kind, it seems to me that God often dispenses with such persons. But this I hold [as faith teaches]{g:ex-fide}: that it is not lawful for the [pope]{g:papa} or any man to dispense anyone in anything unless God dispenses beforehand in the same thing,[^2] so that the [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius-christi} dispensing in such things is the bare promulgator of the divine [sentence]{g:sentencia};[^3] for since these things do not depend sacramentally on his office, no means of dispensing remains to him except by way of merit, or of discretion in promulgating that there is no danger. Hence, because there is usually greater holiness and discretion in the pope, the layman usually has recourse to the pope in such things; and nowadays it is reckoned as a most divine sacrament that it belongs to him alone to grant these things. For it seems that he should first discern these things, whether they are reasonable and edifying to the church; for if the contrary holds, notwithstanding his grant, both he and the one [privileged]{g:privilegiare} are deceptively in fault. But if they are reasonable and edifying, whoever has promulgated their licence, indeed with reason alone moving [him], it is lawful to proceed according to them, since God of himself makes these things lawful; nor can it be grounded from invented human traditions that one must thus generally take licence from [prelates]{g:prelatus}; or that it is lawful for them, so restricting the [power]{g:potestas} of the people or of a [presbyter]{g:presbiter} in [neutral things]{g:neutra} or in things lawful from the faith of scripture, to reserve to themselves the licence to proceed in such things alone; or that one who otherwise does not perform this obedience should be punished even more than for [transgression]{g:prevaricacio} of the decalogue.[^4] For it belongs to every Christian, and especially to a prelate, to enlarge and defend [382] the privileges and liberties which Christ granted to the church, as is clear from Acts 15,[^5] so that it would be an antichristian sin to take that liberty away or to exceed the [limits]{g:limites} at the other extreme. Therefore the faithful man must look to the law of scripture, which by expressing or by keeping silence [shows a thing] thus lawful, and proceed according to the [doctrine]{g:doctrina} of this exemplar, generally and freely.[^6] Hence the reservations of penalties and of dispensations, or licences, blind the people too much, as I shall say, God willing, in the matter of the sacraments.[^7] For it is said that a priest who gives the blessing at second nuptials is not only [suspended]{g:suspendere} but by that very fact incurs [irregularity]{g:irregularitas}; but by doing a thousand enormities hateful to God he is not only reckoned immune but is praised for money;[^8] and the foundation in all these things is a feigned obedience, so that it would not be so grave, in blessing, to implore divine grace upon those previously married, except that papal laws command that it not be done. "Disobedience", they say, "done to papal statutes is to be punished most gravely"; and so Christ with his law is put second. For so the pope could, in taking away or making hard the law of Christ, decree that no Christian may lawfully do any neutral work except what he himself afterwards ratifies by approving it, and consequently ordain his [satraps]{g:satrapa} to cite whomever to appear and answer in his court, since "our Jesus" ordains a like law: as they say, he is made equal to Christ in human fashion.[^9] For so they could harass and fine the people at will, as they do in absolutions, in reservations and in dispensations; and, it is believed, they would [put these things into practice]{g:practizare} more widely, except that they fear for themselves [383] that the people, perceiving their cunning, would rebel.

## ¶2 · 383.3–384.14

> [p. 383] Unde logici multiplicant in ista materia argumenta, ut posito quod papa vel alius prelatus iniungat cuicumque clerico sibi subiecto virtute sacre obediencie et sub obtentu absolucionis a pena et culpa vel alterius suffragii spiritualis quod obviet primo plene pape obedienti et iniungat cuicumque layco sub forma consimili, quod obviet primo pape inobedienti, et fiat ista iniunccio sub gravissimo anathemate, et posito cum hoc quod omnis clericus vel laycus pape isti subiectus fuit primo plene sibi obediens, et quod omnis clericus obviet primo singulariter layco et econtra, et videtur sequi contradiccio. Quia da quod non, de Petro clerico et de Paulo layco sibi primo obviantibus, et quero utrum Petrus pro instanti obvie sit pape obediens; si sic, tunc in casu oportet dicere quod Paulus pro illo instanti sit pape inobediens, quia cum primo obviat Petro, qui est plene pape obediens, et iniunctum est sibi quod primo obviet pape inobedienti, sequitur quod Paulus incurrit notam inobediencie et sic Petrus, si in casu isto Petrus sit pro instanti obvie pape inobediens, tunc oportet concedere Paulum esse obedientem et consequenter Petrum, quia ante obviam fuit uterque obediens, et Petrus ex obvia non fit inobediens, sed confirmatur eius obediencia iuxta dicta. Nec valet negare casum propter tria: primo quia solum neutrum vel possibile est preceptum, secundo [p. 384] quia prelatus potest precipere simpliciter impossibile et omnino irracionale, igitur per idem vel a maiori potest istud precipere, ideo non restat responsio nisi veritas quod propter suum precipere nec magis nec minus incurrit clerus vel laycus penam vel premium, quia oportet preceptum cui obedietur esse prius racionabile apud Deum: et tunc fieret, licet nemo sub obediencia humana illud preciperet, cum aliter foret homo inobediens racioni. Et patet quod sicut in isto casu non restat expectare plenam remissionem vel anathema, sic generaliter in papali iudicio, nisi de quanto quis meruerit apud Deum; oportet enim quod obiectus sophisticus sit solutus.

Hence logicians multiply arguments in this matter.[^10] For example, suppose that the pope or another prelate enjoins on every cleric subject to him, by virtue of holy obedience and on the inducement of absolution from penalty and fault or of another spiritual [suffrage]{g:suffragia}, that he first meet one fully obedient to the pope; and that he enjoin on every layman in like form that he first meet one disobedient to the pope; and let this injunction be made under the gravest anathema; and suppose with this that every cleric or layman subject to this pope was at first fully obedient to him, and that every cleric first meets a single layman, and conversely: then a contradiction seems to follow. For grant that it does not, and take Peter, a cleric, and Paul, a layman, first meeting each other; and I ask whether Peter at the instant of the meeting is obedient to the pope. If so, then in the case one must say that Paul at that instant is disobedient to the pope; for since he first meets Peter, who is fully obedient to the pope, and it was enjoined on him that he first meet one disobedient to the pope, [it follows]{g:sequitur} that Paul incurs the mark of disobedience, and so [does] Peter.[^11] If in this case Peter is at the instant of the meeting disobedient to the pope, then one must grant that Paul is obedient, and consequently Peter; for before the meeting each was obedient, and Peter does not become disobedient by the meeting, but his obedience is confirmed, according to what has been said. Nor is it any use to deny the case, for three reasons:[^12] first, because only a neutral or possible thing is commanded; second, [384] because a prelate can command what is impossible [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} and altogether irrational; therefore by the same token, or a fortiori, he can command this. Therefore no reply remains but the truth: that on account of his commanding, a cleric or a layman incurs neither more nor less penalty or reward; for the command which is obeyed must first be reasonable before God, and then it would be done even though no one commanded it under human obedience, since otherwise a man would be disobedient to reason. And it is clear that, as in this case there is no room to expect full [remission]{g:remissio} or anathema, so generally in papal judgement, except in so far as one has merited before God; for the sophistical [objection]{g:objectum} must be solved.

## ¶3 · 384.15–384.27

> [p. 384] Quotlibet sunt tales casus longe coloraciores quam obligaciones obediencie humanitus adinvente, ut posito quod Petrus prior habeat duas duodenas claustrales, omnes sibi obedientes, et precipiat stabiliori duodene, quod non colloquatur cum reliqua, nisi forte inobediente Petro ad obedienciam reducendum; et quod secunda duodena non colloquatur cum reliqua, nisi obediente Petro ad obedienciam confirmandum, et incipiat Paulus prioris duodene loqui cum Lino secunde duodene, utroque prestante in hoc obedienciam observare, sic quod continue ante illud colloquium uterque fuit rite Petro obediens.

There are any number of such cases, far more colourable than the obligations of obedience devised by men:[^13] as, suppose that Peter the prior has two dozens of [cloisterers]{g:claustrales}, all obedient to him, and commands the steadier dozen not to converse with the other except, it may be, with one disobedient to Peter, to bring him back to obedience; and that the second dozen not converse with the other except with one obedient to Peter, to confirm him in obedience; and let Paul of the first dozen begin to speak with Linus of the second dozen, each undertaking in this to keep obedience, so that continuously before that conversation each was duly obedient to Peter.[^14]

## ¶4 · 384.28–385.31

> [p. 384] Ulterius quantum ad dispensacionem affinitatis vel consanguineitatis patet quod fabricatur ut plurimum zophistarum illusio, nam si contractus sit racionalis et consentaneus legi Dei, non obstante [p. 385] prohibicione papali est validus; nec licet pape illum dissolvere, quia Matth. XIV, 6 dicitur: Quos Deus coniunxit, homo non separet. Si autem huiusmodi coniugium foret dissolutivum legi Dei, papalis dispensacio utramque partem seduceret. Videtur itaque mihi quod coniugium parentis ad prolem proximam contrariaretur legi nature et sic Dei, quia honor et subieccio debetur parentibus, sed coniugibus amicicia socialis. Fratres autem et sorores oportuit in principio mundi contrahere, ut patet de patriarchis; quibus limitatum est de cognacione propria coniugari; tempore autem legis gracie toto genere gencium converso ad Dominum, racionaliter ordinatum est quod extra cognacionem matrimonium contrahatur, ut liga federis cognacionis habeat aliam ligam affinitatis ex desponsacione carnali, et sic forcius atque extensius sit triplex vinculum pro humani generis federe colligando, et sic dimissa liga fraternitatis spiritualis in Domino multiplicantur tradiciones hominum de gradu contractus liciti et alias revocantur; et dispensacio pro pecunia et de cognacione potencie incurrit. Certum est tamen quod non valet dispensacio papalis nisi subsit racio scripture, sed ad tantum valet diffinicio papalis, de quanto ipse racionem prudenter dilucidat et non ultra. Mille conclusiones ex istis sequuntur, quas omnes oportet concedere; unde stulticia facit quod homines plus appreciantur tradiciones hominum de coniugio cognatorum quam mandata decalogi.

Further, as to dispensation in affinity or consanguinity, it is clear that [for the most part]{g:ut-plurimum} a delusion of [sophists]{g:sophista} is fabricated. For if the contract is rational and agreeable to God's law, it is valid notwithstanding [385] papal prohibition; nor is it lawful for the pope to dissolve it, for it is said in Matthew 19:6: "Whom God hath joined together, let no man put asunder."[^15] But if a marriage of this kind were destructive of God's law, papal dispensation would lead both parties astray. It seems to me therefore that the marriage of a parent to his nearest offspring would be contrary to the law of nature and so of God; for honour and subjection are owed to parents, but to spouses companionable friendship. And brothers and sisters had to contract [marriage] at the beginning of the world, as is clear of the patriarchs, who were [limited]{g:limitare} to marrying within their own kindred;[^16] but in the time of the [law of grace]{g:lex-gracie}, the whole race of the gentiles having been converted to the Lord, it was reasonably ordained that marriage be contracted outside the kindred, so that the [league]{g:liga} of the covenant of kinship might have another league, of affinity, from carnal espousal, and so there might be, more strongly and more widely, a threefold bond for binding together the covenant of the human race.[^17] And so, the league of spiritual brotherhood in the Lord being let go, the traditions of men about the degree of lawful contract are multiplied and at other times revoked; and dispensation for money and on account of kinship with power comes in.[^18] Yet it is certain that papal dispensation is of no avail unless a reason from scripture underlies it; but papal [definition]{g:diffinicio} avails just so far as he prudently makes the reason clear, and no further. A thousand conclusions follow from these, all of which must be granted; hence folly makes men value the traditions of men about the marriage of kin more than the commandments of the decalogue.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Bigamis*: in canon law a bigamist is a man married twice in succession, or to a widow, which bars him from orders; not one with two wives at once. *Varie celebrandi* stands without a conjunction before *dispensacionem*; we take them as items of a list.
[^2]: **C.** *Ante ea*: we read it as *antea*, "beforehand" (perhaps a word broken in the print); "before these [men]" is the alternative, with the same sense.
[^3]: **C.** *Sentencia* is here the judicial sense: God's sentence, which the pope only promulgates; not "teaching".
[^4]: **C.** The three clauses after *nec potest fundari* (*quod oportet …; vel quod licet eis …; vel quod … puniatur*) all depend on it; the semicolons are the print's. *Servare sibi licenciam* we take as governed by *licet eis*, and *ex fide scripture* with *licitis*.
[^5]: **S.** Acts 15:28 (DR "to lay no further burden upon you than these necessary things"); Wyclif gives the chapter only.
[^6]: **C.** *Exprimendo vel tacendo sic licitum*: we take the gerunds with *legem scripture*, which shows a thing lawful either by saying so or by silence; *huius exemplaris* is scripture as exemplar.
[^7]: **R.** A promise of a treatment of the sacraments; no particular later work has been identified (LEDGER P12r-1); the complaint that follows returns in *Opus evangelicum* III, c. 13 (ed. Loserth, p. 48).
[^8]: **S.** The bar on the nuptial blessing at second marriages is in the *Decretales* (X 4.21.1 *Capellanum*, where the priest who blesses is suspended and sent to the apostolic see, and X 4.21.3 *Vir autem*; Friedberg II, 730–732; LEDGER P12r-2); Loserth gives no reference. The manuscripts' margin at 383 (*Spoliat clerus populum suum mirabiliter*) belongs to this complaint.
[^9]: **R/C.** Loserth: *Citacio auctoris alluditur*, an allusion to the summons of the author himself (LEDGER P12r-3). *Ihesus noster*, "our Jesus", we take as an ironic name for the pope, who "is made equal to Christ" (*parificatur*); the alternative, that Christ ordains a like law, does not fit *ut dicunt*.
[^10]: **R.** Hus copies the case word for word, from *ut posito* to *sit solutus* (384), in his *De ecclesia*, ch. 21 (Loserth; LEDGER P12r-4). A in marg.: *Nota casum pulchrum*; the manuscripts: *Casus*. B reads *post* for the second *posito*.
[^11]: **C.** *Et sic Petrus*: "and so [does] Peter", who has met Paul, now disobedient. The next sentence, *si in casu isto …*, is the other horn; an *autem* or *si non* may have been lost before it. B in marg.: *Obediencia pape in neutris*.
[^12]: **C.** Three reasons are announced and two given; the inference *igitur per idem … potest istud precipere* may count as the third. *Sub obtentu absolucionis* above: "on the inducement of absolution", the absolution offered as reward.
[^13]: **C.** *Obligaciones obediencie* may play on the logicians' *obligationes* (the art of obligation), the genre in which such cases are posed. The manuscripts' margin: *Nota casum*.
[^14]: **R/C.** Hus copies this case too, from *quod Petrus prior* to *obediens* (*De ecclesia*, ch. 21, next to the first case; Loserth: p. 246; LEDGER P12r-5). Wyclif leaves it unsolved. *Nisi forte inobediente Petro* we take as the ablative after *cum* understood, "with one disobedient to Peter".
[^15]: **T/S.** The text reads *Matth. XIV, 6*; Loserth's foot gives *Cod.: Matth. XIX*, the true place, and we give Matt. 19:6. DR "What therefore God hath joined together, let no man put asunder"; Wyclif reads *Quos* ("whom") for the Vulgate *Quod*.
[^16]: **T/C.** Loserth: after *contrahere*, "Adde: matrimonia"; hence "[marriage]". *Limitatum est … coniugari*: they were restricted to their own kindred, so "limited", not "assigned".
[^17]: **S.** The threefold bond recalls Eccles. 4:12 (DR "a threefold cord is not easily broken"): kinship, affinity and spiritual brotherhood.
[^18]: **C.** *Et dispensacio pro pecunia et de cognacione potencie incurrit* hardly construes: *incurrit* has no object, and *de cognacione potencie* is obscure. We take *incurrit* as "comes in" and *cognacio potencie* as kinship with the powerful; doubtful. The "revocations" are such as Lateran IV (1215) c. 50 (X 4.14.8 *Non debet*), which revoked earlier constitutions on affinity and limited the prohibition to the fourth degree (LEDGER P12r-6).
