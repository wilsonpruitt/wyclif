# *De eucharistia*, chapter 3, part 1: transubstantiation as the destruction of God's work

## ¶1 · 53.26–54.4

> [p. 53] Restat superaddere alia argumenta contra doctores novellos pro antique ecclesie sentencia et primo per hoc quod non est Dei destruere suam fabricam in auctorisando ecclesiastica sacramenta; sed sic faceret iuxta sentenciam istam noviter adinventam, ergo ipsa [p. 54] est irracionabilis. Minor patet ex hoc quod iuxta hanc viam quantum de pane et vino in toto mundo est transsubstanciatum, tantum desinit atque destruitur tam quoad materiam quam quoad formam.

It remains to add further arguments against the [newer]{g:novelli} doctors for the [teaching]{g:sentencia}[^1] of the [ancient church]{g:antiqua-ecclesia}; and first by this, that it is not God's part to destroy his own fabric in authorising the church's [sacraments]{g:sacramentum}; now he would do so according to this newly invented teaching; therefore it [54] is unreasonable. The minor is clear from this: that on this way, as much of [bread]{g:panis} and [wine]{g:vinum} in the whole world as is [transubstantiated]{g:transsubstanciacio}, so much ceases and is destroyed, as to the matter as well as to the form.

## ¶2 · 54.5–54.8

> [p. 54] Et videtur nocivum esse cambium, cum tanta substancia plus prodesset mundo quam eius horrenda transsubstanciacio; Deus ergo inepte et infundabiliter sic prodesset ecclesie.

And the [barter]{g:cambium} seems to be harmful, since so great a [substance]{g:substancia} would profit the world more than its horrendous transubstantiation; God therefore would profit the [church]{g:ecclesia} in this way unfittingly and [without foundation]{g:infundabiliter}.

## ¶3 · 54.9–54.23

> [p. 54] Item, cum nec remanet materia nec forma transsubstanciati, ne corpus Christi foret nimis gravidatum novella materia, videtur iuxta concedentes possibilitatem anichilacionis quod simpliciter anichiletur natura transsubstanciata; nam possibile est secundum illos quod anichiletur tam panis quam vinum et succedant corpus Christi et sanguis sacramentaliter sub istis accidentibus, ut modo videmus, sed quomodocunque foret tunc ex parte panis aut vini est materia; ergo nunc est irrevocabiliter anichilata in consecracione. Assumptum est famosum aput illos, cum substancia potest anichilari servato accidente et econtra, ut inquiunt, nec obest quin Christus potest pro eodem instanti multiplicare corpus suum sub illis accidentibus, cum sint motus omnimode separati.

Again, since neither the matter nor the form of the transubstantiated thing remains, lest the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} be [exceedingly]{g:nimis} burdened[^2] with new matter, it seems, according to those who grant the possibility of [annihilation]{g:anichilacio}, that the transubstantiated [nature]{g:natura} is annihilated [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}; for it is possible according to them that both the bread and the wine be annihilated and the body of Christ and the blood succeed [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} under these [accidents]{g:accidens}, as we now see; now, however it would then be on the part of the bread or the wine, so it is with the matter;[^3] therefore it is now irrevocably annihilated in the consecration. The [assumption]{g:assumptum} is well known among them, since a substance can be annihilated while the accident is preserved, and conversely, as they say; nor does it stand in the way that Christ can at the same instant multiply his body under those accidents, since the motions are separate in every way.

## ¶4 · 54.24–55.8

> [p. 54] Item, anichilacio secundum sic loquentes specificatur ab esse parcium anichilati et terminatur ad simpliciter non esse; quod est in omni transsubstanciacione secundum ipsos, ergo etc. Que rogo diversitas inter annichilacionem A panis, succedente corpore Christi sub eisdem accidentibus pro instanti [p. 55] anichilacionis, et transsubstanciacionem B panis in corpus Christi sub suis accidentibus sacramentaliter succedens pro eodem instanti? Nam par est desicio atque destruccio substancie utrobique, ideo videtur quod quoad motum destruccionis sit in toto conformitas. Conformis est enim destruccio panis, sive cicius redibit materia sive maneat perpetuo corpus Christi sub sensibili subiecto.

Again, annihilation, according to those who speak so, is specified by the [being]{g:esse} of the parts of the annihilated thing and is terminated at not-being without qualification; and this is so in every transubstantiation according to them; therefore etc. What, I ask, is the difference between the annihilation of bread A, the body of Christ succeeding under the same accidents at the instant [55] of annihilation, and the transubstantiation of bread B into the body of Christ under its accidents, succeeding sacramentally at the same instant?[^4] For the [ceasing]{g:desicio} and destruction of the substance is equal on both sides; so it seems that as to the motion of destruction there is conformity in everything. For the destruction of the bread is conformable, whether the matter will return sooner or the body of Christ remain perpetually under the [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} subject.

## ¶5 · 55.9–56.14

> [p. 55] Nec valet ficticia de transsubstanciacione vel conversione panis in corpus Christi, cum corpus Christi nec accipit materiam nec formam substancialem vel accidentalem a pane corrupto; ideo videtur non pocius converti in corpus Christi quam dies convertitur in noctem vel econtra; ideo vel irregulariter panis non convertitur in corpus Christi in confeccione eukaristie vel est ambiguum quando est talis conversio; cuius descripcio non fingetur. Et patet, licet canat ecclesia: Dogma datur christianis Quod in carnem transit panis Et vinum in sanguinem, quod natura illius transitus panis in corpus Christi et vini in sanguinem videtur esse ignorata eciam theologis, quia quilibet eorum ponit desicionem substancie secundum se totam et nescit ubi aliquid panis remaneat vel quo vadat; ideo separando illum motum ab aliis videtur secundum eos quod sit anichilacio. Aliter enim posset fingi de quolibet motu quod non sit anichilacio propter motum positum [p. 56] alium comitantem; videtur enim quod panis vertitur in nichilum, sicut est de gracia que non vertitur in peccatum, sicut nec anichilatum vertitur in corpus quod occupat locum suum; nam nichil vertitur vel transit in aliud nisi subiectet versionem vel transitum usque ad terminum ad quem est motus, non vocando illam mutacionem mirabilem ab esse panis ad eius non esse, cum non sit materia substrata, sicut nec in anichilacione, videtur illis dicendum quod sit anichilacio, cum non sciunt conversionem impedientem detegere; nam (ut inquiunt) postmodum producetur materia prima secundum se et quamlibet sui partem; de nichilo ergo in desicione sua anichilata est.

Nor does the [fiction]{g:ficticia} of the transubstantiation or [conversion]{g:conversio} of the bread into the body of Christ avail, since the body of Christ receives neither matter nor form, substantial or accidental, from the corrupted bread; so it seems to be no more converted into the body of Christ than day is converted into night, or conversely; so either the bread is [irregularly]{g:irregulariter} not converted into the body of Christ in the [consecration]{g:confeccio} of the eucharist,[^5] or it is ambiguous when there is such a conversion; of which no [definition]{g:descripcio} will be [feigned]{g:fingere}. And it is clear, though the church sings, "Dogma is given to Christians, that bread passes into flesh and wine into blood,"[^6] that the nature of that passing of the bread into the body of Christ and of the wine into the blood seems to be a thing of which even theologians are ignorant, because each of them posits the ceasing of the substance according to its whole self, and does not know where anything of the bread remains or whither it goes; so, separating that motion from the others, it seems according to them that it is annihilation. For otherwise one could feign of any motion that it is not annihilation, on account of another motion posited [56] accompanying it; for it seems that the bread is turned into nothing, as is the case with grace, which is not turned into sin, as neither is the annihilated thing turned into the body that occupies its place; for nothing is turned or passes into another unless it is the subject of the turning or passing up to the term to which the motion runs. Not calling that wonderful change from the being of the bread to its not-being [a conversion],[^7] since there is no matter underlying, as neither in annihilation, it seems they must say that it is annihilation, since they do not know how to [expose]{g:detegere} a conversion that would prevent it; for (as they say) [prime matter]{g:materia-prima} will afterwards be produced according to itself and every part of it; it is therefore [produced] from nothing, and in its ceasing it is annihilated.[^8]

## ¶6 · 56.15–56.25

> [p. 56] Et mirantur hic naturales quomodo irregulariter eadem materia prima in numero redit secundum se totam, cum talia individuantur ab instanti in quo producuntur; ideo sicut non redit primum instans mundi, sic nec redit eadem materia in numero. Cum ergo sit eque facile Deo producere in toto novam materiam, nec habent revelacionem, racionem vel sompnium quando sic fecit, ergo non est credendum narracioni istorum, quin creatur nova materia annichilata antiqua. In tales perplexitates involvunt ecclesiam qui extraneant ab antiquis sanctis.

And the natural philosophers wonder here how, irregularly, the same prime matter in number returns according to its whole self, since such things are individuated by the instant in which they are produced; so, just as the first instant of the world does not return, so neither does the same matter in number return. Since therefore it is equally easy for God to produce new matter wholly, and they have no revelation, reason or dream of when he did so, therefore their account is not to be believed against [the view] that new matter is created, the old having been annihilated.[^9] Into such [perplexities]{g:perplexitas} do they entangle the church who [estrange]{g:extraneare} themselves from the ancient [saints]{g:sancti}.

## ¶7 · 56.26–57.27

> [p. 56] Item, secundum Augustinum super Joh., Omelia XXVIᵃ, super isto Joh. VIᵒ, 56: Caro mea vere est cibus et sanguis meus vere est potus: In illis, inquit, rebus Deus commendavit nobis hoc sacramentum, [p. 57] que ad unum aliquid reducuntur ex multis. Namque ex multis granis panis conficitur, et ex multis racemis vinum confluit; nunc secundum adversarios istud foret hereticum, cum plane implicat panem et vinum post consecracionem hostie remanere; ideo non in illis rebus sed in accidentibus que ignorant dicunt quod accipimus corpus Christi. Cum ergo Deus decrevit nobis dare donum tam magnum, videtur convenire sue veritati magnifice quod dedit nobis ipsum in velamento honorifico illusionem hominis excludente. Omnis enim talis decepcio est mala, cum homo naturaliter innititur cognoscere veritatem, ideo cum sciencia veritatis sit bona et utilis, ignorancia veritatis est mala, beatitudini repugnans, ideo dicimus accidencia remanere ut sensus a decepcione immunes reddantur; sensus autem interiores iudicant de substanciis materialibus, cum sint sensibilia per accidens; ideo magis malum foret in illis decipi. Cum ergo sensus hominis tam exteriores quam interiores iudicant illud remanens esse panem et vinum rei non consecrate simillimum, videtur quod non convenit domino veritatis tantam illusionem inducere in graciose communicando donum tam dignum; non enim punit nisi citra condignum propter culpam que meruit sic puniri. Unde ergo puniret decepcione tam ignobili eciam beatum qui sacramentaliter assumeret corpus suum?

Again, according to Augustine on John, Homily 26, on this text of John 6[:56], "My flesh is [truly]{g:vere} meat and my blood is truly drink":[^10] "In those things," he says, "God has commended this sacrament to us, [57] which are brought back to some one thing out of many. For out of many grains bread is made, and out of many grapes wine flows together."[^11] Now according to the adversaries this would be heretical, since it plainly implies that the bread and the wine remain after the consecration of the [host]{g:hostia}; so they say that we receive the body of Christ not in those things but in accidents of which they are ignorant. Since therefore God decreed to give us a gift so great, it seems to befit his magnificent truth that he gave it to us in an honourable veil that excludes man's [illusion]{g:illusio}. For every such deception is evil, since man naturally strives to know the truth; so, since knowledge of the truth is good and useful, ignorance of the truth is evil, [incompatible]{g:repugnare} with blessedness; so we say that the accidents remain, so that the senses may be rendered immune from deception. Now the inner senses judge of material substances, since these are perceptible [by accident]{g:per-accidens};[^12] so it would be a greater evil to be deceived in them. Since therefore the senses of man, outer as well as inner, judge that remaining thing to be bread and wine, most like an unconsecrated thing, it seems that it does not befit the Lord of truth to bring in so great an illusion in graciously communicating a gift so worthy; for he does not punish except short of the condign, on account of a fault that has deserved to be so punished. On what ground, then, would he punish with so ignoble a deception even a blessed man who sacramentally took his body?

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 53.26: The modern doctrine makes God destroy His own work.
- 54.24: Cessation to exist is equivalent to annihilation.
- 55.9: The bread is not converted since Christ's body takes neither matter nor form from it.
- 56.15: It is a mere fancy to pretend that the primal matter which was in the bread returns in the Body.
- 57.7: God does not mingle deception with His great gift in the Sacrament.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Sentencia* here is the position taught ("teaching"), as in the next clause (*sentenciam istam noviter adinventam*), not a sentence of words.

[^2]: **C.** *Gravidatum*: literally "made heavy, made pregnant": the body of Christ would be loaded with the new matter if the bread's matter passed into it.

[^3]: **C.** *Sed quomodocunque foret tunc ex parte panis aut vini est materia*: elliptical. We take it "as it would then stand with the bread or wine, so it stands with the matter now", which the *ergo nunc* that follows requires. The *sed* brings the minor premiss to the *ergo*, hence "now". ABCE read *quocunque*; D has *est modo* (54.17).

[^4]: **C.** *Succedens* is nominative and agrees with nothing in the clause: the sense requires the body of Christ succeeding (as *succedente corpore Christi* in the first member).

[^5]: **C.** *Irregulariter*: "against the rule" of conversion as nature knows it; the same adverb returns at 56.15 of prime matter returning. The negation goes with *convertitur*: the bread is not converted at all.

[^6]: **S.** The sequence *Lauda Sion* for Corpus Christi; Loserth: "sequentia que vulgo tribuitur Thomae Aquinati", citing Daniel, [*Thesaurus hymnologicus*] II, 97 (LEDGER K03a-1).

[^7]: **C.** *Non vocando*: the name withheld is not expressed; we supply "a conversion" from the argument (they cannot name it a conversion, so must call it annihilation). ABCD read *notando*, "not noting" (56.7).

[^8]: **C.** *De nichilo ergo in desicione sua anichilata est*: punctuated by us after *nichilo*. If the matter is produced afterwards from nothing, it was annihilated when the bread ceased. The alternative, "from nothing, in its ceasing", would make *de nichilo* go with *anichilata*.

[^9]: **C.** *Non est credendum narracioni istorum, quin creatur*: the *quin* clause is the rival view, which their account cannot exclude. ABD read *qua* (56.25); C at 56.23 *racioni* for *narracioni*.

[^10]: **S.** John 6:56. Wyclif omits the Vulgate's *enim* (DR: "For my flesh is meat indeed: and my blood is drink indeed"); we translate his Latin, keeping *vere* "truly". The chapter is Wyclif's, the verse Loserth's.

[^11]: **S/T.** Augustine, *In Iohannis evangelium tractatus* 26 (Loserth: Opp. III/2, p. 500; tr. 26.17 is our form, LEDGER K03a-2). Augustine's text, per Loserth: *in eis rebus … quae ad unum aliquid rediguntur ex multis. Namque aliud in unum ex multis granis conficitur, aliud in unum ex multis acinis confluit*. *Ex multis* (57.2) is lacking in all the manuscripts; Loserth added it ("Addidi").

[^12]: **C.** *Sensibilia per accidens*: substances are perceived not of themselves but through their accidents; the *autem* brings the minor premiss to *ideo*, hence "now".
