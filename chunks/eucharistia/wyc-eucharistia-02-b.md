# *De eucharistia*, chapter 2, part 2: the witness of scripture, and identical and tropical predication

## ¶1 · 33.28–34.11

> [p. 33] Quantum ad testimonium scripture patet Math. XXVIº, Marc. XIVº, Luc. XXIIº et Iª Cor. XIº, et Act. IIº [p. 34] ac Joh. VIº, ubi fit mencio de hoc sacramento et datur forma conficiendi; utrobique narratur quod Jesus accepit panem et de illo dixit: Hoc est corpus meum. Nam Luc. XXIIº, 19 sic scribitur: Et accepto pane gracias egit et fregit et dedit eis dicens: Hoc est corpus meum; ubi patet non protervo quod illum panem quem accepit postea demonstravit. Et constat quod illo manente pane non est realiter corpus Christi sed signum efficax eius. Ex istis colligitur quod confessio Beringarii et antiquum decretum ecclesie sunt undique plus consona veritati.

As to the testimony of scripture, it is clear in Matthew 26, Mark 14, Luke 22 and 1 Corinthians 11, and Acts 2[^1] [34] and John 6, where mention is made of this [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} and the form of [consecrating]{g:conficere} is given; in both places it is [related]{g:narrare} that Jesus took [bread]{g:panis} and said of it: "This is my body."[^2] For in Luke 22:19 it is written thus: "And taking bread, he gave thanks and brake and gave to them, saying: This is my body"; where it is clear to one not [obstinate]{g:protervus} that he afterwards demonstrated the bread which he took.[^3] And it is evident that, while that bread remains, it is not [really]{g:realiter} the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} but an efficacious [sign]{g:signum} of it. From these it is gathered that the confession of Berengar and the ancient [decree]{g:decretum} of the [church]{g:ecclesia} are on every side more consonant with the truth.[^4]

## ¶2 · 34.12–34.17

> [p. 34] Sed obicitur per hoc quod secunda pars decreti repugnat priori et katholice veritati et per consequens tota confessio est infecta; ponit enim quod panis et vinum nedum sunt sacramentum post consecracionem, sed verum corpus Christi et sanguis quod frangitur et sentitur ut panis, quod est contra dicta.

But [it is objected, by appeal to]{g:obicitur-per} this, that the second part of the decree [is incompatible with]{g:repugnare} the first and with catholic truth, and consequently the whole confession is infected; for it posits that the bread and wine are [not only]{g:nedum} a sacrament after the consecration, but the true body and blood of Christ, which is broken and [perceived]{g:sentire} as bread, which is against what has been said.[^5]

## ¶3 · 34.18–35.14

> [p. 34] Hic dico (ut supra) quod oportet omnino equivocare in isto secundo dicto, cum nec substancia panis nec illud quod fuit eius forma accidentalis sit realiter corpus Christi, sicut oportet tam antiquos quam novellos concedere; ideo dicit glosa ordinaria quod oportet confessionem Beringarii sane intelligi, quia aliter foret novissimus error peior priore ab ecclesia dampnato tanquam heretico. Hodie namque approbaretur prior Beringarii opinio secundum tres partes quas ecclesia tunc dampnavit tanquam hereticas, scilicet quod illud album manet post consecracionem solummodo sacramentum, secundo quod illud non est corpus Christi, et tercio quod per sensacionem illius [p. 35] corpus Christi non frangitur vel sentitur. In istis tribus se correxit iuxta decretum ecclesie; et revera nunc defendere quod ecclesia illa tunc erravit in fide tanquam ignara loyce que per sapienciorem ecclesiam est correcta foret inponere toti determinacioni nostre quoad materiam fidei suspectam calumpniam; per idem est dicere quod scriptura cum sanctis doctoribus defecit in istis. Sed quis tunc crederet verbis nostris, cum possumus ad nutum fidem pro pecunia variare? Oportet ergo glosare dictum utriusque ecclesie secundum regulam fidei scripture, et hoc est plus consonum et honorabilius utrobique omnino ad intelligendum scripturam, sanctos doctores et confessionem Beringarii, que fuit decretum ecclesie.

Here I say (as above) that one must altogether equivocate in this second saying, since neither the [substance]{g:substancia} of the bread nor that which was its accidental form is really the body of Christ, as both the ancients and the [newer]{g:novelli} [doctors] must grant; therefore the ordinary [gloss]{g:glosa} says that the confession of Berengar must be understood soundly, because otherwise the last error would be worse than the first, which was condemned by the church as heretical.[^6] For today Berengar's earlier [opinion]{g:opinio} would be approved in the three parts which the church then condemned as heretical: namely, that that white [thing] remains after the consecration only a sacrament; second, that it is not the body of Christ; and third, that by the [sensation]{g:sensacio} of it [35] the body of Christ is not broken or perceived. In these three he corrected himself according to the decree of the church; and in truth, to defend now that that church then erred in faith, as ignorant of [logic]{g:loyca}, and has been corrected by a wiser church, would be to lay a suspect [challenge]{g:calumpnia} against our whole determination in the matter of faith;[^7] by the same token it is to say that scripture with the holy doctors failed in these things. Now who then would believe our words, since we can at a nod vary the faith for money?[^8] We must therefore [gloss]{g:glosare} the saying of each church according to the rule of the faith of scripture; and this is more consonant, and more honourable on both sides, altogether, for understanding scripture, the holy doctors and the confession of Berengar, which was the decree of the church.[^9]

## ¶4 · 35.15–36.23

> [p. 35] In ista materia oportet (ut supra) signanter notare distinccionem inter predicacionem ydempticam et predicacionem tropicam. Ydemptica est (ut dixi), quando predicatum ydemptice asseritur de subiecto, ut Christus est homo. Sed predicacio tropica vel figurativa est, quando unum extremum notatur figurari per reliquum, ut prius exposui: Petra autem erat Christus. Unde triplex exemplum adduco de omnibus pro hoc sensu; primum est dictum Apostoli I. Cor. Xº, 1, 4: Nolo autem vos ignorare fratres, quoniam patres nostri omnes sub nube fuerunt, et omnes mare transierunt, et omnes eundem potum spiritualem biberunt. Bibebant autem de spirituali consequente eos petra: petra autem erat Christus. Et patet quoad intellectum istius scripture et sibi similium quod oportet notare sensum figurativum vel tropicum; ad quem sensum est hoc dictum eque verum sicut alia pars scripture; omnes namque patriarche fuerunt sub proteccione [p. 36] divina, secundum quam fuit ardor vindicte Domini effugatus; omnes transierunt mundi et omnium adversancium populorum periculum; et cum fides non variatur propter fluxum temporis, patet quod nutriebantur eadem fide qua christiani postmodum sunt nutriti. Et totum hoc figuratum est per ambulacionem quadraginta annorum ad terram promissionis, sic quod nichil contigit itinerantibus de Egypto quin fuit figura futura succedentibus in peregrinacione sua ad patriam. Sicut enim populus bibebat de aqua fluente de petra quam Moyses bis percussit (ut patet Exodi XVIIº, 6), sic populus christianus bibit spiritualiter subtilitatem fidei Jesu Christi postquam populus iudaicus Christum infamiter sic percussit; et sicut secutum fuit obiurgacionis obprobrium de percussione predicta, ita consequitur iudeos obprobrium, quod Christum sic in patibulo percusserunt. Petra, inquit, erat Christus, hoc est, illa petra Christum mistice figuravit. Notandum tamen quod tales locuciones possunt dupliciter sane intelligi, scilicet quod talis figura figuravit huius figuratum, vel dimittendo litteralem sensum figure intelligere equivoce figuratum, et utrobique erit sensus katholicus absque scrupulo falsitatis.

In this matter one must (as above) expressly note the distinction between [identical predication]{g:predicacio-ydemptica} and tropical predication. Identical [predication] is (as I said) when the predicate is asserted [identically]{g:ydemptice} of the subject, as "Christ is man". But tropical or [figurative predication]{g:predicacio-figurativa} is when one extreme is marked as being [figured]{g:figurare} by the other, as I explained before: "And the rock was Christ."[^10] Hence I adduce a threefold example, out of all [of them], for this sense; the first is the saying of the Apostle, 1 Cor. 10:1, 4: "And I would not have you ignorant, brethren, that our fathers were all under the cloud: and all passed through the sea, and all drank the same spiritual drink. And they drank of the spiritual rock that followed them: and the rock was Christ."[^11] And it is clear, as to the understanding of this scripture and those like it, that one must note the [figurative]{g:figurative} or tropical sense; in which sense this saying is as true as another part of scripture; for all the patriarchs were under the [36] divine protection, by which the burning of the Lord's vengeance was put to flight; all passed through the danger of the world and of all hostile peoples; and since faith is not varied by the flux of time, it is clear that they were nourished by the same faith by which Christians were afterwards nourished.[^12] And all this is figured by the walking of forty years to the land of promise, so that nothing befell those journeying from Egypt but it was a figure to come for those who followed in their [pilgrimage]{g:peregrinacio} to the [homeland]{g:patria}. For just as the people drank of the water flowing from the rock which Moses struck twice (as is clear in Exodus 17:6), so the Christian people drinks [spiritually]{g:spiritualiter} the subtlety of the faith of Jesus Christ, after the Jewish people so infamously struck Christ; and just as the reproach of chiding followed from the aforesaid striking, so the reproach follows the Jews, that they so struck Christ on the gibbet.[^13] "The rock," he says, "was Christ"; that is, that rock mystically figured Christ. Yet it is to be noted that such locutions can be soundly understood in two ways: namely, that such a [figure]{g:figura} figured the thing figured of it; or, leaving aside the literal sense of the figure, to understand [equivocally]{g:equivoce} the thing figured; and in both ways the sense will be catholic, without scruple of falsehood.[^14]

## Notes

[^1]: **T.** ABCD read *Act. XX* (33.29), the breaking of bread at Troas (Acts 20:7), for the print's *Act. II* (Acts 2:42, 46). The print is translated. The chapter numbers are Wyclif's, the verse numbers Loserth's.

[^2]: **C.** *Utrobique* "in both places": with six places named, read as the gospels on the one side and Paul on the other; it may mean "in each place". *Conficere* is "consecrate" here, the verb standing alone (cf. 01-e, where it stands beside *consecrare*).

[^3]: **C.** *Demonstravit*: the logician's sense, pointed out by the pronoun *hoc*: the bread he took is what *hoc* demonstrates in *Hoc est corpus meum*. Luke 22:19 agrees with the Vulgate as far as Wyclif quotes it.

[^4]: **S.** Loserth compares Wyclif, *De apostasia* p. 68 (LEDGER K02b-1).

[^5]: **C.** *Contra dicta*: against what has been said in 33.28–34.11, that the bread, while it remains, is not really the body of Christ. *Quod frangitur et sentitur* is singular after *corpus … et sanguis*, the body taken as subject.

[^6]: **S.** The ordinary gloss on *De cons.* D. 2 c. 42 *ad vocem* "Dentibus": "Nisi sane intelligas verba Berengarii, in maiorem incides haeresim quam ipse habuit"; Loserth's reference (LEDGER K02b-2). *Novissimus error peior priore* echoes Matt. 27:64 (DR "the last error shall be worse than the first"), unmarked.

[^7]: **C.** *Que per sapienciorem ecclesiam est correcta*: the relative is read of *ecclesia illa* (the church that erred, corrected by a wiser one), not of *loyce*. *Determinacio*: the church's ruling, as *determinaciones ecclesie*, not the logician's qualifying addition; untagged.

[^8]: **T.** *Ad nutum* is Loserth's emendation ("Correxi"): A reads *ad meritum*, BCE *ad ritum*, D *ad votum* (35.9).

[^9]: **C.** *Utrobique*: "on both sides", the ancient and the present church, both of whose sayings are glossed by the one rule.

[^10]: **S.** 1 Cor. 10:4 (DR), as at 26.9.

[^11]: **S.** 1 Cor. 10:1, 4 (DR, with Wyclif's omissions). Wyclif reads *Nolo autem*, the Vulgate *Nolo enim* (DR "For I would not"), and passes from the middle of verse 1 to verse 4, leaving out verses 2–3 (the baptism in the cloud and the sea, the spiritual food). ABCD read *vestri* for *nostri*.

[^12]: **C.** The figurative sense is applied clause by clause: "under the cloud" as under God's protection, "passed through the sea" as through the world's danger, "the same spiritual drink" as the same faith.

[^13]: **S.** Moses struck the rock twice at Num. 20:11 (DR "struck the rock twice with the rod"); Exod. 17:6, which Wyclif cites, has one striking. The "reproach of chiding" is Exod. 17:7 (DR "because of the chiding of the children of Israel"). D reads *Exod. XXIV*.

[^14]: **C.** Two sound readings of "the rock was Christ": the figure figured the thing figured (a tropical predication), or the word "rock", its literal sense laid aside, is taken equivocally for the thing figured. *Dimittendo* is untagged: the row *dimittere* is of laying aside the assumed nature.
