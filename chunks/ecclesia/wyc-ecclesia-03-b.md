# *De ecclesia*, chapter 3, part 2: the hypocrite's three replies: counsels, care for the church, love of the robber

## ¶1 · 51.8–52.9

> [p. 51] Tercio colligitur quod adulterantes scripturam sacram in ista sentencia sunt ypocrite, quos prophetavit apostolus Timotheo periculosis temporibus in ecclesiam successuros. Pro cuius declaracione suppono factum, quomodo moderni ecclesiastici fingunt secundum tradiciones humanas quod nedum licet eis, sed peccarent mortaliter, omittendo prosequi pro defensione matris ecclesie. Illud meminit beatus Gregorius (ubi supra). Istis, inquit, si forte dicatur: Cur ista agitis qui seculum reliquistis? respondent illico Deum se metuere et idcirco tanto studio in defendendis patrimoniis Christi insudare. Hodie autem superadditur quod bona pro quorum acquisicione, conservacione et repeticione tantum insistimus sunt bona ecclesie et patrimonium Christi: ideo licet pro bono universalis ecclesie conservacioni eorum insistere. Iste autem fucus ex hoc tollitur quod deserendo ipsa pro Christo tam relinquens quam ecclesia ipsa habet, ut dicit Augustinus super Joh. Omelia VIª. Ideo, ut dicit beatus Gregorius: Frustra laboravit nullo timore cogente, et ut dicitur Psalmo XIII°, 5: Illic trepidaverunt timore, ubi non [p. 52] erat timor. Ecce, inquit, divina voce precipitur: Si quis tulerit tibi tunicam et voluerit tecum in iudicio contendere, dimitte illi pallium. Et rursum: Si quis quod tuum est tulerit, ne repetas. Paulus quoque apostolus discipulos suos cupiens exteriora despicere, ut valeant interna servare, admonet eos dicens: Omnino delictum est in vobis quod iudicia habetis inter vos, quare non magis iniuriam accipitis quare non magis fraudem patimini?

Third, it is gathered that those who adulterate holy scripture in [the matter of] this
[teaching]{g:sentencia} are hypocrites, whom the apostle prophesied to Timothy would come into the
[church]{g:ecclesia} in dangerous times.[^1] For the explanation of this I suppose as a fact how modern
churchmen pretend, according to human traditions, that not only is it lawful for them [to sue], but
they would sin mortally by omitting to sue for the defence of mother church. Blessed Gregory recalls
this (in the place cited): "If perhaps it is said to these men: Why do you do these things, who have
left the world? they answer at once that they fear God, and therefore sweat with such zeal in defending
the patrimonies of Christ."[^2] And today it is added besides that the goods for whose acquisition,
preservation and recovery we press so hard are the goods of the church and the patrimony of Christ;
therefore it is lawful, for the good of the universal church, to press for their preservation. But
this disguise is taken away by this: that by deserting them for Christ both the one who leaves them
and the church itself has them, as Augustine says on John, homily 6.[^3] Therefore, as blessed Gregory
says: "She hath laboured in vain, no fear constraining her"; and, as is said in Psalm 13:5: "There
have they trembled for fear, where there [52] was no fear." "Behold," he says, "it is commanded by the
divine voice: If any man take away thy coat and will contend with thee in judgment, let go thy cloak
unto him. And again: If any man take away what is thine, ask it not again. Paul the apostle also,
desiring his disciples to despise outward things so that they may be able to keep inward ones,
admonishes them, saying: There is plainly a fault among you, that you have law suits one with another.
Why do you not rather take wrong? Why do you not rather suffer yourselves to be defrauded?"[^4]

## ¶2 · 52.10–52.15

> [p. 52] Nec valet glosa ypocrite quod hec sunt consilia, non precepta. Si enim sunt Christi consilia et tu in habitu pretendis te super alios Christum diligere, adhereres eius leni consilio, preceptis seculi pretermissis; aliter enim fores ypocrita, offendens in dileccione Dei super omnia.

Nor does the hypocrite's gloss avail, that these are counsels, not precepts. For if they are Christ's
counsels, and you in your habit make a show of loving Christ above others, you would adhere to his
gentle counsel, the precepts of the world being passed over; for otherwise you would be a hypocrite,
offending in the love of God above all things.[^5]

## ¶3 · 52.16–54.22

> [p. 52] Nec valet secundo dicere quod oportet aliquem solicitari circa hec bona, ne ecclesia defraudetur, quia illis relictis pro Christo tu verius haberes hec bona, et sic ecclesia. Ideo vis exponere te periculo dimittendo perfeccionem divini consilii quam acceptando, Deus non posset de promissione temporalium deficere; nam cum istud officium incumbit laicis, patet quod indigencia ecclesie stat in paucitate procurancium pro spiritualibus bonis ecclesie, sicut fuit de contemplativis, quando crevit ecclesia. Cum ergo istud officium sit alcius, salubrius et ecclesie necessarius, manifestum est quod amor culpabilis temporalium est in causa huius cece eleccionis. Unde Gregorius ibidem capitulo X°: Plus Deo sese amare convincitur qui neglectis hiis que Dei sunt propria tuetur; perdere namque animas hominum ypocrite non timent, et [p. 53] amittere res cum mundo transeuntes quasi districto iudici posituri racionem timent; ac si placatum inveniant, cui desideratis rebus, id est, racionabilibus perditis insensibilia et non quesita conservent. Certum est quod non. Possidere, inquit, in hoc mundo aliquid volumus et ecce veritas clamat: Nisi quis renunciaverit omnibus que possidet, non potest meus esse discipulus. Perfectus ergo christianus quomodo debet res terrenas iurgando defendere quas precipitur non possidere? Itaque cum res proprias amittimus, si perfecte Deum sequimur, in huius vite itinere a magno onere levigamur. Non ergo debemus, periculo nos exponendo, perfecciorem statum relinquere et statum plus periculosum, plus a Christo elongatum et plus tardativum eligere. Nec valet tercia responsio ypocrite qua dicit se propter amorem iniuste occupantis contendere, ne ipse peccet iniusta detencione, sed ut ipse habeat instrumenta virtutum quibus possit ad honorem Dei plus matri sue proficere. Unde pro prima parte dicit beatus Gregorius (ubi supra): Quidam cum nostra rapiunt, solummodo tolerandi sunt; quidam vero servata caritate prohibendi; non tamen sola cura ne nostra subtrahant, sed ne rapientes non sua semetipsos perdant. iste fucus est magis palliatus quam ceteri: nam (sicut alias ostendi) licet inferioribus ecclesie pro bonis fortune contendere, servata caritate utrimque et sancta intencione cum forma procedendi legitima. Ideo debet homo illius responsionis cavere, ne propinquitas ad voraginem periculi preparet ad ruinam. Nam ut dicit beatus Gregorius ibidem: Plus ipsis raptoribus debemus metuere quam inhiare ipsis rebus irracionabilibus [p. 54] defendendis, quia illa non rapta morientes amittimus, cum illis vero et nunc condicionis ordine, etsi corrigi studeant, post percepcionem finalis premii unum sumus. Quis autem nesciat, quia minus ea quibus utimur et plus debemus amare quod sumus? Si ergo pro sua utilitate raptoribus loquimur, iam non nobis que temporalia, sed eciam illis que sunt eterna vindicamus. Qua in re illud est solercius intuendum, ne per necessitatis metum cupiditas surrepat rerum et zelo succensa prohibicio, impetu immoderancie distensa, usque ad odiose turpitudinem contencionis erumpat. Dumque pro terrena re pax a corde cum proximo scinditur, liquido apparet quod plus res quam proximus amatur. Si enim caritatis viscera eciam circa raptorem proximum non habemus, nosmet ipsos peius ipso raptore persequimur graviusque nos quam alter poterat devastamus, quia dileccionis bonum sponte deserentes a nobis ipsis intus est quod amittimus, qui ab illo sola exteriora perdebamus. Sed hanc ypocrita formam caritatis ignorat, quia plus terrena quam celestia diligens contra eum qui temporalia diripit sese in intimis immani odio inflammat.

Nor, second, does it avail to say that someone must be solicitous about these goods, lest the church
be defrauded; for, these being left for Christ, you would more truly have these goods, and so would the
church.[^6] So you choose to expose yourself to danger by abandoning the perfection of the divine
counsel rather than by accepting [it], [though] God could not fail of his promise of
[temporal goods]{g:temporalia}; for since this office falls to laymen, it is clear that the church's
want consists in the fewness of those who take care for the spiritual goods of the church, as was the
case with the contemplatives when the church grew.[^7] Since, therefore, this office is higher, more
wholesome and more necessary to the church, it is manifest that a culpable love of temporal goods is
the cause of this blind choice. Hence Gregory in the same place, chapter 10: "He is convicted of loving
himself more than God who, neglecting the things that are God's, protects his own; for hypocrites do
not fear to destroy the souls of men, and [53] they fear to lose things that pass away with the world,
as men who will render account to a strict judge; as if they would find him appeased, for whom, the
things he desired, that is, rational things, being lost, they preserve things
[without sensation]{g:insensibilis} and not sought." It is certain that they will not. "We want," he
says, "to possess something in this world, and behold, the Truth cries out: Unless a man renounce all
that he possesseth, he cannot be my disciple. How, then, ought a perfect Christian to defend by
quarrelling earthly things which he is commanded not to possess? And so, when we lose our own things,
if we follow God perfectly, we are relieved of a great burden on the journey of this life." We ought
not, therefore, by exposing ourselves to danger, to leave the more perfect state and choose a state
more dangerous, further removed from Christ and more apt to delay.[^8] Nor does the hypocrite's third
reply avail, by which he says that he contends out of love for the one who unjustly occupies [the
goods], lest he sin by unjust detention, but so that he himself may have the instruments of the virtues
by which he may profit his mother more, to the honour of God.[^9] Hence, for the first part, blessed
Gregory says (in the place cited): "Some, when they seize our things, are only to be borne with; but
some are to be restrained, [charity]{g:caritas} being kept; yet not with the sole care that they not
take away what is ours, but that, seizing what is not theirs, they not destroy themselves." This
disguise is more cloaked than the others: for (as I have shown elsewhere) it is lawful for the lower
[members] of the church to contend for goods of fortune, charity being kept on both sides, and with
holy intention, with a lawful form of procedure.[^10] Therefore the man of that reply ought to beware,
lest nearness to the whirlpool of danger prepare [him] for ruin. For, as blessed Gregory says in the
same place: "We ought to fear more for the robbers themselves than to gape after the irrational things
themselves, [54] to defend them; for those things, even unseized, we lose in dying, but with these men,
both now by the order of our condition and, if they strive to be corrected, after the receiving of the
final reward, we are one. And who does not know that we ought to love less the things we use, and more
what we are? If, therefore, we speak to robbers for their own advantage, we claim now not for ourselves
the things that are temporal, but also for them the things that are eternal.[^11] In which matter this
must be looked to the more shrewdly: lest, through fear of necessity, the desire of things creep in,
and the restraint, kindled by zeal, distended by the impetus of immoderation, break out even into the
foulness of hateful contention. And while peace with a neighbour is cut off from the heart for an
earthly thing, it appears plainly that the thing is loved more than the neighbour. For if we do not
have the bowels of charity even towards the neighbour who robs us, we persecute ourselves worse than
the robber himself, and lay ourselves waste more heavily than the other could; because, freely
deserting the good of love, what we lose within is from ourselves, we who were losing from him only
outward things. But the hypocrite does not know this form of charity, because, loving earthly things
more than heavenly, he inflames himself in his inmost parts with monstrous hatred against him who
plunders temporal goods."[^12]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 51.8: Hypocrites apply Scripture falsely in their own support.
- 52.10: The hypocrite says: a. These are counsels, not commands; as if the counsels of Christ were not to be followed.
- 52.16: b. He says that but for his care the Church would be defrauded;
- 52.24: but the care would be better bestowed on spiritual things.
- 53.16: c. He declares that he acts for the good of the wrong doer:
- 53.25: This is a better excuse but it is dangerously near to evil.
- 53.31: St. Gregory teaches
- 54.5: that we must care only for the eternal interests of the wrong doer;
- 54.15: otherwise we hurt ourselves more than him.

## Notes

[^1]: **S/C.** The apostle's prophecy: 2 Tim. 3:1, DR "in the last days shall come dangerous times"
    (Loserth's reference, 51.10); Wyclif paraphrases. *Tercio colligitur*: the third thing gathered from
    Gregory's conclusions (03-a). *In ista sentencia*: we take it as "in [support of] this teaching", the
    churchmen's claim that follows; it could be "in this sense". AB margin: *Tercia conclusio*; A:
    *Contra defensores iurium et consuetudinis humane gracia proprie avaricie et victorie* (51.8).

[^2]: **S/T.** Gregory, *Moralia in Iob* 31.12 (§20), PL 76:584D, read this session (LEDGER E03b-1).
    Gregory has *Cur ista vos agitis* and *defendendis patrimoniis insudare*; Wyclif adds *in* and
    *Christi*. The MSS read *ita agitis*; Loserth prints Gregory's *ista* (51.16); B *dicitur, cur
    certa*. *Prosequi*: to proceed at law, sue. AB margin: *Gregorius* (51.15); B: *Contra eos qui se
    dicunt contendere debere pro acquirendo bona ecclesie* (51.22); A: *Nota fucum* (51.23).

[^3]: **S/C.** Augustine, *In Iohannis evangelium tractatus* 6 (Loserth: Opp. III/2, 330 ff.). We read
    tract. 6.25–26 (PL 35): Augustine there argues that possessions are held by human law, which the
    Donatists renounce; the claim Wyclif draws, that by leaving goods for Christ one has them, is not
    there in those words (LEDGER E03b-2). Construal: *tam relinquens quam ecclesia ipsa habet* has a
    singular verb for the pair; *ipsa* is the goods. *Fucus*: "disguise", paint laid over the truth.
    B margin: *Augustinus* (51.25).

[^4]: **S.** Job 39:16 (DR), the verse on the ostrich, Gregory's figure of the hypocrite, which he
    expounds here (PL 76:584D; LEDGER E03b-1); Loserth also cites Augustine's *Annotationes in Iob*
    (51.26). Ps. 13:5, DR. Gregory goes on (PL 76:585A) with Matt. 5:40 and Luke 6:30 in his own wording,
    which Wyclif keeps: Vulgate *et ei qui vult tecum iudicio contendere et tunicam tuam tollere, dimitte
    ei et pallium* (DR "And if a man will contend with thee in judgment, and take away thy coat, let go
    thy cloak also unto him"); *qui aufert quae tua sunt, ne repetas* (DR "of him that taketh away thy
    goods, ask them not again"). 1 Cor. 6:7, DR, without the opening *Iam quidem* ("Already indeed"),
    which Gregory has. B margin: *Contra contencionem sacerdotum* (52.3).

[^5]: **C/R.** *Consilia … precepta*: the evangelical counsels against the commandments. *In habitu*:
    in the religious habit. *Adhereres*, *fores*: past subjunctives, "you would". *Leni*: "gentle" (cf.
    Matt. 11:30). AB margin: *Triplex responsio* (52.16, the three replies Loserth's side-notes letter a,
    b, c); A: *Contra illos qui loquuntur contra consiliorum implecionem*.

[^6]: **T.** *Verius* (52.18), "more truly": the plate's reading (the OCR had *venus*). The thought answers *ne ecclesia defraudetur*: goods left for Christ are
    held more truly, by the one who leaves them and by the church (51.25, n. 3).

[^7]: **C/T.** Construal: *vis exponere te periculo dimittendo … quam acceptando*: "you choose to expose
    yourself to danger by abandoning [the counsel] rather than by accepting it", the comparative carried
    by *quam*; the clause *Deus non posset … deficere* is taken as concessive (cf. Matt. 6:33). It could
    be read as a question: "So would you rather expose yourself …?" *Istud officium* (52.22) is the care
    of temporal goods, which falls to laymen; at 52.25 *istud officium* is the spiritual office.
    *Sicut fuit de contemplativis*: the church grew when those who cared for spiritual goods were many.
    A *provisione* for *promissione* (52.21). AB margin: *Gregorius*; A: *Paupertas cleri iuxta Christi
    concilia* (52.28).

[^8]: **S/T.** Gregory, *Moralia* 31.13 (§21–22), PL 76:585C–D (LEDGER E03b-1). Wyclif's "chapter 10" is
    the old division (Migne's *Vet. X*, at §20). Gregory: *Plus ergo Deo se amare*; *quae ejus sunt*;
    *Perdere namque hypocritae ea quae Dei sunt, id est animas hominum*; *conservant*; *quas non
    praecipitur possidere* (Wyclif *precipitur non possidere*); *levigati sumus*. Loserth corrects the
    MSS' *invenient* to *inveniant* (53.2–3). *Certum est quod non* and the last sentence are Wyclif's.
    *Racionabilibus … insensibilia*: rational souls against goods that cannot feel. Luke 14:33 in
    Gregory's form (Vulgate *qui non renuntiat omnibus quae possidet*, DR "every one of you that doth not
    renounce all that he possesseth cannot be my disciple"). *Tardativum*: slowing the journey.

[^9]: **C.** *Ne ipse peccet … sed ut ipse habeat*: the first *ipse* is the unjust occupier, the second
    the hypocrite; *sed* adds his further motive, to have goods as the instruments of the virtues for
    the profit of "his mother", the church. *Pro prima parte* (53.21): Gregory's words answer the first
    part, love of the robber. AB margin: *Gregorius* (53.21).

[^10]: **S/C/T.** Gregory, PL 76:586A (LEDGER E03b-1), who has *dum eas rapiunt* and *conservata
    charitate*. *Sicut alias ostendi*: unidentified (LEDGER E03b-3). *Inferioribus ecclesie*: we take
    "the lower members", the laity, to whom the care of temporal goods falls (52.22); B's margin reads
    *Licet inferioribus ecclesie contendere pro bonis ecclesie*. B *utrumque* for *utrimque* (53.27).
    AB margin: *Conclusio* (53.25).

[^11]: **S/C.** Gregory, PL 76:586A (LEDGER E03b-1): *Ista namque etiam non rapta*; *post perceptionem
    muneris* (Wyclif *finalis premii*); *non jam solum nobis … sed ipsis etiam*: Wyclif lacks *solum*,
    so his *non … sed eciam* reads "not for ourselves … but also for them". *Et nunc … etsi corrigi
    studeant*: *etsi*, as printed and as in Migne, is construed *et si*, "and, if", answering *et nunc*.
    *Metuere* with a dative: to fear for. A margin: *Raptor est a nobis plus amandus quam res arrepta per
    raptorem* (53.31).

[^12]: **S/T.** Gregory, PL 76:586B (LEDGER E03b-1), who has *solerter* and *impetus immoderatione*.
    The MSS read *pro habito* at 54.10; Loserth's *prohibicio* is Gregory's *prohibitio*, the restraint
    of robbers just named (*prohibendi*). AB *in inani* at the same line (its lemma is not clear from the
    apparatus). At 54.11 the MSS have *turpitudinis contencionem*; Loserth prints Gregory's order. A
    *inflammant* (54.22).
