# *De potestate pape*, chapter 8, part 4: two elections: how the name of pope arose

## ¶1 · 176.5–178.16

> [p. 176] Unde post dotacionem sunt elecciones hominum introducte et cadunt super officio, dignitate vel dominacione humanitus instituta et non super carismata Deo propria. Ut communitas eligit hominem in pontificem cesareum vel nuncupative dictum, et sic cadit eleccio humana super episcopum, ut dicit communiter episcopum secundum reputacionem vel approbacionem humanam, quod fit sepius cum errore, vel secundum autorizacionem divinam, quod semper sit iuste, unde quando contingit quod eleccio humana concordat cum eleccione divina, tunc homo eligit verum episcopum et summum pontificem, sicut sepe contigit de Romanis pontificibus veris papis. Et quando discordat ab eleccione divina, tunc eligit false reputatum summum pontificem et non summum pontificem, licet errando illud crediderit. Unde cum iste terminus papa et summum dicunt summitatem in moribus, quam mirabilitatem solius Dei est facere, videtur quod solus Deus eligit hominem in papam vel summum pontificem, quia non videtur fundabile quod isti termini extendantur ad false nuncupatum huius, quia sic dyabolus foret summus pontifex. Episcopus autem secundum apostolum est nomen officii et sic aliud est eligere papam et aliud eligere hominem in papam, quia secunda proposicio implicat quod cadat eleccio in papatum, sed prima proposicio verificaretur de eleccione persone ad quodcumque officium vel preposituram, ut populus eligit [p. 177] papam, quando instituit vel acceptat personam ad officium vel dignitatem humanam, quam personam Deus eligit ad preeminenciam sanctitatis, unde, cum scriptura Deutro. XXVI, 17 signanter dicit: Dominum elegisti hodie, patet quod quedam eleccio est institucio vel quedam faccio, et quedam eleccio est acceptacio; et per consequens oportet preter eleccionem instituencium esse eleccionem equivocam, que sit approbacio subditorum. Sic enim populus eligit dominum, quando effectualiter acceptat, quod ille sit dominus suus et spondet fideliter se esse sibi ut domino serviturum. Unde quando successione hereditaria vadit dominium, adhuc est alia eleccio subditorum; ad quod oportet movere vitam et mores in spirituali preposito, et aliter est intrusoris monstruosa extorsio. Et patet quod debet esse pape duplex eleccio, una humana in Romanum episcopum et alia pure divina in papam vel summum pontificem; qua deficiente Romanus pontifex non est papa sed unus alius mundo absconditus. Sed cum tres ficciones non sufficiunt, remanet dubium, quomodo dicta duo nomina sunt connexa. In qua materia credendum est cronicis. Narrat enim Cestrensis libro IV, cap. XXXVI, quomodo excellencia Romani imperii adinvenit papatum sui pontificis super alios: Nycena, inquit, synodus hoc contulit privilegium Romano pontifici, ut sicut Augustus pre ceteris regibus, ita Romanus pre ceteris pontifex haberetur episcopis et papa velut principalis pater vocaretur. Origo autem istius nominis et excellencie fuit in dotacione ecclesie, [p. 178] ut specificat Decretum XLVI distinccione Constantinus. Ideo superfluum et labor inutilis foret fingere mendacia, quod sic oportuit esse ex racionibus ad hoc urgentibus vel scriptura. Unde quia ista videtur levis fundacio et ecclesia Romana tunc sibi cadere a papatu, narrat idem Cestrensis libro V cap. X, quomodo Bonifacius IV impetravit a Foca cesare, ut ecclesia beati Petri Rome Caput esset ecclesiarum, quia caput ecclesie Constantinopolitane se scripsit papam. Quotlibet tales fideles cronice et decreta testantur primatum Romani pontificis fuisse humanitus adinventum. Nam si papa fieret vagus ut olim et nulli loco affixus sed in mari mortuo vel deserto, et sic post eum papa secundus, et sic ad finem viacionis ecclesie, patet quod non obstante isto vere foret papa.

Hence after the [endowment]{g:dotacio} the elections of men were brought in, and they fall upon an office, dignity or [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio} humanly instituted, and not upon the charismata proper to God. So a community elects a man as caesarean pontiff, or pontiff so called in name, and in this way human election falls upon a bishop, as one commonly speaks of a bishop either according to human reputation or approval, which is often done with error, or according to divine [authorization]{g:autorizare}, which is always done justly;[^1] hence when it happens that human election agrees with divine election, then a man elects a true bishop and highest pontiff, as has often happened with Roman pontiffs who were true popes. And when it is at odds with divine election, then he elects one falsely reputed highest pontiff and not a highest pontiff, though in erring he believed it. Hence, since the terms "pope" and "highest" signify highness in morals, a marvel which God alone can work, it seems that God alone elects a man pope or highest pontiff, since it does not seem to be groundable that these terms be extended to one falsely so named; for in that way the devil would be highest pontiff.[^2] Now "bishop", according to the apostle, is the name of an office;[^3] and so it is one thing to elect a pope and another to elect a man pope, since the second proposition implies that the election falls upon the papacy, but the first proposition would be verified of the election of a person to any office or [office of superior]{g:prepositura} whatever, as the people elect [177] a pope when they institute or accept a person for an office or human dignity, which person God elects to [preeminence]{g:preeminencia} of holiness.[^4] Hence, since scripture, Deut. 26:17, says pointedly, "Thou hast chosen the Lord this day", it is clear that one kind of election is an institution or a kind of making, and another kind of election is an acceptance; and consequently, besides the election of those who institute, there must be an equivocal election, which is the approval of the subjects. For in this way a people elects a lord, when it effectually accepts that he be its lord and pledges faithfully to serve him as its lord. Hence when [lordship]{g:dominium} passes by hereditary succession, there is still another election, that of the subjects; and to this the life and morals in a spiritual [superior]{g:prepositi} must move [them], and otherwise it is the monstrous extortion of an intruder.[^5] And it is clear that there must be a twofold election of the pope, one human, to Roman bishop, and the other purely divine, to pope or highest pontiff; and if this is lacking, the Roman pontiff is not pope, but some other man, hidden from the world, is.[^6] But since three [fictions]{g:ficcio} do not suffice,[^7] a doubt remains how the two said names are connected. In this matter the chronicles are to be believed. For the Chester [chronicler] relates in book 4, chapter 36, how the excellence of the Roman empire devised the papacy of its pontiff over others: "The synod of Nicaea," he says, "conferred this [privilege]{g:privilegium} on the Roman pontiff, that as Augustus [was held] before the other kings, so the Roman pontiff should be held before the other bishops, and be called pope, as being the principal father."[^8] And the origin of this name and excellence was in the endowment of the church, [178] as the Decretum specifies, distinction 96, *Constantinus*.[^9] Therefore it would be superfluous and useless labour to feign lies, that it had to be so from reasons urging it or from scripture. Hence, since this seems a light foundation, and the Roman church then [seemed] to be falling from the papacy, the same Chester [chronicler] relates in book 5, chapter 10, how "Boniface IV [obtained]{g:impetrare} from Caesar Phocas that the church of blessed Peter at Rome should be Head of the churches, since the head of the church of Constantinople wrote himself pope."[^10] Any number of such faithful chronicles and decrees testify that the [primacy]{g:primatus} of the Roman pontiff was humanly devised. For if the pope became a wanderer, as of old, and fixed to no place, but on the Dead Sea or in the desert, and so the second pope after him, and so to the end of the church's [wayfaring]{g:viacio}, it is clear that notwithstanding this he would truly be pope.

## ¶2 · 178.17–179.7

> [p. 178] Ideo oportet quod aliqua sit racio pape ante notacionem loci huius qualiscumque. Ideo vere dicit dominus Ardmacanus libro VII cap. XXI De Questionibus Armenorum: Quod Petrus ideo fuit Christi vicarius, quia fuit sibi similior in virtute. Et foret inanis ficticia quod, ubicumque fuerit talis electus humanis ritibus, consequitur quod sit Roma; non enim est racio, quare pocius fieret ibi Roma quam Anthiochia, Jerusalem vel Constantinopolis. Tales enim locorum illorum metropolitani dixerunt se esse papas propter disparem racionem: Anthiochia, quia ibi vocati sunt primo discipuli christiani, ut patet Act. XI, et beatus Petrus ibi resedit episcopus multis annis, et tercio quia prius recepit fidem Christi quam Roma. Episcopus autem Jerosolimitanus [p. 179] vendicat papatum propter Christum, propter primitatem fidei, propter mortem Jacobi, qui ibi primo secutus est mortem Domini. Constantinopolis vero propter nomen cesaris, qui dotando tantum decoravit ecclesiam. Et Roma, quia ibi sancti apostoli sunt occisi et realiter, quia impetrarunt a cesare hoc privilegium.

Therefore there must be some ground of [being] pope prior to any designation of this place whatever. Therefore the lord of Armagh says truly, in book 7, chapter 21 *Of the Questions of the Armenians*: "That Peter was [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius-christi} for this reason, that he was more like him in [virtue]{g:virtus}."[^11] And it would be an empty [fiction]{g:ficticia} that wherever such a man has been elected by human rites, it follows that it is Rome; for there is no reason why Rome should come to be there rather than Antioch, Jerusalem or Constantinople. For the metropolitans of those places said that they were popes, each for a different reason:[^12] Antioch, because there the disciples were first called Christians, as is clear from Acts 11, and blessed Peter sat there as bishop for many years, and third because it received the faith of Christ before Rome. And the bishop of Jerusalem [179] claims the papacy on account of Christ, on account of the [firstness]{g:primitas} of the faith, on account of the death of James, who there first followed the death of the Lord.[^13] And Constantinople on account of the name of Caesar, who by endowing so greatly adorned the [church]{g:ecclesia}. And Rome, because the holy apostles were slain there, and, in reality, because they obtained this privilege from Caesar.

## ¶3 · 179.8–180.20

> [p. 179] Et revera omnes et singulas istarum causarum propter impertinenciam ad papatum est turpe fingere, sed turpissimum quod papa aliquis impetrat privilegium illud concedere vel donare, quam potest concedere, quod datus episcopus vivat Christo similius. Sed sicut sophista vel maniacus concedit quod Deus regnat in angelis et tamen non sequitur propter suum concedere sive imperium plus vel minus, ita est de concessione privilegii pape vel cesaris, ut quis sequatur Christum similius in virtute. Sed cum locus sit huic dignitati impertinens, oportet rogare trinitatem obnixius, implorare suffragium ecclesie utriusque humilius et attendere devote bonis operibus, ut Deus eligat quem voluerit, et ostendat oraculis vel sanctis operibus, que precedant eleccionem humanam, quia eleccio humana est istis impertinens. Concedo igitur cum domino Ardmacano historiam de beato Petro quomodo timore cesaris proposuit sub colore Christi consilii a Roma aufugere, et quomodo obvianti Christo in porta civitatis et querenti: Domine, quo vadis, responsum est ab eo venio Romam iterum crucifigi. Per quod Petrus expectans concepit, quod dominus crucifigendus fuit ibi in ipso, quia iuxta rectam logicam Christi Matth. XXV [p. 180] Christo faciunt vel non faciunt homines quicquid fecerint vel non fecerint membris suis, et sic nedum fuit ex divina ordinacione, quod Petrus et Paulus inibi morerentur, sed omne quod fit in mundo fit ex ordinacione divina accessoria vel directa. Accessoria in viciosis operibus, de quibus bonum ordinat, et directa in virtuosis operibus vel operibus naturalibus. Causam autem quare Christus ordinavit gloriosos apostolos ibi pati martyrium est exemplar et speculum aliis martyribus, quod ordinavit postmodum ibi pati, cum aliis bonis exemplum sequentibus. Sed credo quod nullus fidelis tantum insaniet, quod fingat dominum ordinasse ibi illorum martyrium, ut doceat fore papam quemcumque Romanum episcopum successurum, quia (ut dictum est) nomen tale inolevit ex institucione humana et sepe opinioni hominum in istis falsitas est substrata; locus enim ille est impertinens, ut eius episcopus sit precipuus sequax Christi.

And in truth it is shameful to feign each and every one of these causes, for their irrelevance to the papacy; but most shameful [to feign] that some pope obtains [the power] to grant or bestow that privilege, [any more] than he can grant that a given bishop should live more like Christ.[^14] But as a [sophist]{g:sophista} or a madman grants that God reigns in the angels, and yet nothing more or less follows on account of his granting or command, so it is with the [grant]{g:concessio} of a privilege by pope or Caesar that someone should follow Christ more closely in virtue.[^15] But since the place is irrelevant to this dignity, one must pray the Trinity more earnestly, implore more humbly the [suffrage]{g:suffragia} of the church of both [states], and attend devoutly to good works, that God may choose whom he will and show [him] by oracles or holy works which precede the human election, since human election is irrelevant to these.[^16] I grant, therefore, with the lord of Armagh, the story of blessed Peter: how for fear of Caesar he proposed, under [colour]{g:color} of Christ's counsel, to flee from Rome, and how, as he met Christ at the gate of the city and asked, "Lord, whither goest thou?", he was answered by him: "I come to Rome to be crucified again."[^17] By which Peter, considering, conceived that the Lord was to be crucified there in himself; for according to Christ's right logic, Matt. 25, [180] men do or do not do to Christ whatever they have done or not done to his [members]{g:membrum}.[^18] And so not only was it from divine [ordinance]{g:ordinacio} that Peter and Paul died there, but everything that is done in the world is done by divine ordinance, accessory or direct: accessory in vicious works, from which he ordains good, and direct in virtuous works or natural works.[^19] And the cause why Christ ordained the glorious apostles to suffer martyrdom there is [that it is] an exemplar and mirror to other martyrs, whom he ordained afterwards to suffer there, with other good men following the example.[^20] But I believe that no believer will be so mad as to feign that the Lord ordained their martyrdom there in order to teach that whatever Roman bishop should succeed would be pope; for (as has been said) such a name grew up from human institution, and falsehood often underlies men's [opinion]{g:opinio} in these matters; for that place is irrelevant to its bishop's being a principal follower of Christ.[^21]

## ¶4 · 180.21–180.32

> [p. 180] Unde videtur non esse de substancia fidei, quod Clemens, Linus, Cletus vel alius post Petrum Romanus episcopus fuit papa, intelligendo papam, ut dicit primitatem alienum excludentem. Intelligendo autem papam communiter, modo quo Ambrosius, Augustinus et ceteri sancti episcopi erant pape, manifestum est quod Paulus, Clemens et ceteri sancti martyres et confessores Romani episcopi erant pape simul cum aliis, quod si aliquis eorum in regimine ovium Christi omnes conversantes secum excesserat, tunc fuit iste papa ad sensum superius limitatum.

Hence it seems not to be of the substance of faith that Clement, Linus, Cletus or another Roman bishop after Peter was pope, understanding "pope" as it signifies a firstness that excludes another. But understanding "pope" commonly, in the way in which Ambrose, Augustine and the other holy bishops were popes, it is manifest that Paul, Clement and the other holy martyrs and confessors who were Roman bishops were popes together with others;[^22] and if any of them had excelled all who lived with him in the rule of Christ's sheep, then he was pope in the sense limited above.[^23]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 176.5: Man's election does not make a pope unless God too has chosen him.
- 177.5: An election requires acceptance by the subjects.
- 177.21: The true origin of the name 'Pope'.
- 178.7: The right to be sole pope was first granted by the Emperor Phocas.
- 178.17: Local claims as strong in other cities as in Rome.
- 179.8: All these claims are groundless and wrong; the worst is a privilege begged from the Emperor.
- 180.21: It is not a matter of faith that Clement and others were popes in the modern use.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *ut dicit communiter episcopum*: the subject of *dicit* is unexpressed; taken impersonally, "as one commonly speaks of a bishop", in two ways, by human reputation or by divine authorization. **T.** *quod semper sit iuste*: the print reads *sit* (plate checked); B reads *fit iuste* (apparatus 176.14), "is done justly". We follow B's *fit* because it answers *quod fit sepius cum errore* in the line before ("which happens often with error … which happens always justly"); this is our choice of the manuscript reading. *pontificem … nuncupative dictum*: a pontiff "so called in name" only (cf. *ecclesia nuncupativa*, GLOSSARY §D).

[^2]: **C.** *false nuncupatum huius*: "one falsely named [by] this [term]". The argument: *papa* and *summus* name height in holiness, which only God confers; if the words could be stretched to one falsely so named, the devil, the highest in false reputation, would be highest pontiff.

[^3]: **S.** The apostle: 1 Tim. 3:1, "If a man desire the office of a bishop" (*episcopatum*); Wyclif takes *episcopus* as the name of an office.

[^4]: **C.** "To elect a pope" is said of the people's instituting or accepting for a human office a person whom God [has already] elected to preeminence of holiness; "to elect a man pope" implies that the election confers the papacy. **R.** AB in the margin (176.28): *Aliud est eligere hominem in papam et aliud eligere papam*.

[^5]: **C.** *ad quod oportet movere vitam et mores*: the subjects' acceptance must be moved by the life and morals of the spiritual superior; without them the office is "extorted" by an intruder. **R.** AB in the margin (177.6): *Eleccio duplex est*.

[^6]: **C.** *unus alius mundo absconditus*: if divine election is lacking in the Roman bishop, the true pope is some other man, unknown to the world.

[^7]: **C.** *Tres* as printed (plate checked). The chapter does not number them; the three are not identified.

[^8]: **S.** Ranulf Higden, *Polychronicon* ("Cestrensis", the Chester monk), ed. Rolls Series, vol. 5, p. 140: *Postquam vero Nicena synodus hoc contulit Romano pontifici privilegium, ut sicut Augustus prae ceteris regibus, ita Romanus pontifex prae ceteris praeemineat episcopis, et papa tanquam principalis pater vocaretur*. The passage stands in book 4, chapter 26. The codices read *cap. XIV*, which Loserth corrected to XXXVI, the reading translated; both are wrong (LEDGER P08d-1). **T.** Loserth's apparatus at 177.29–30 records that words of the clause *ita Romanus pre ceteris pontifex* are wanting in the codices (*Romanus pontifex pre ceteris deest*); the print, his supplement, is translated. **R.** Loserth notes Hus's reuse, *Opera* fol. 220b (apparatus 177.25). AB in the margin (177.24): *Papa illud nomen unde habet ortum*.

[^9]: **S/T.** The *Constitutum Constantini* in Gratian is *Decretum* D. 96 c. 14 *Constantinus* (form ours); Loserth's reference at 177.33 gives "Cap. XIV". The print has *XLVI distinccione* (178.1, so the plate); the canon stands in distinction 96, and we read *XCVI* (our emendation; PL 187, 460; Wyclif writes *XCVI distinccione Constantinus* himself at 170.13; LEDGER P08d-2).

[^10]: **S.** Higden, *Polychronicon* 5.10; Loserth: ed. Rolls Series, vol. 5, p. 417 (the Latin is on p. 416: *Hic impetravit ab Augusto Phoca ut ecclesia beati Petri Romae caput foret omnium ecclesiarum*); the codices read *cap. LVII* (LEDGER P08d-3). The quotation bounds follow Loserth's italics. **T.** At 178.5 Loserth proposes to add *videbatur* after *tunc*; the bracketed "[seemed]" supplies it.

[^11]: **S.** FitzRalph, *Summa in quaestionibus Armenorum* 7.21; no reference at the page foot. Paris 1512, fol. 51r: *premisso ab ore dominico Christi Petro tanquam sibi similiori in virtute: Beatus es Simon Bariona …*, after which Christ names him successor (LEDGER P08d-4). **R.** AB in the margin (178.18): *Vicarius Christi quare dicitur Petrus*.

[^12]: **C.** *propter disparem racionem*: each see on its own ground, as the list that follows shows. **T.** *metropolitani*: the codices read *metropoli* (apparatus 178.27); the print is translated. A in the margin names each claimant: *Papa Antiochie*, *Papa Jerosolimitanus*, *Papa Constantinopolitanus*, *Papa Romanus*.

[^13]: **S.** Acts 11:26, "at Antioch the disciples were first named Christians". **C.** *mortem Jacobi, qui ibi primo secutus est mortem Domini*: James, the first to follow the Lord's death there; Wyclif does not say which James (cf. Acts 12:2 for the son of Zebedee; tradition gives James the bishop a martyr's death at Jerusalem).

[^14]: **C.** *quam potest concedere*: the feminine *quam* cannot be a relative to *privilegium*; it is taken as "than", with the comparison elliptical: the pope can grant the privilege no more than he can grant that a bishop live more like Christ. The bracketed words supply the ellipse.

[^15]: **T.** At 179.15 (*propter suum concedere*) Loserth notes *Hic aliquot verba desunt*, "some words are missing here"; the print is translated as it stands, with "nothing more or less follows" for *non sequitur … plus vel minus*.

[^16]: **C.** *ecclesie utriusque*: probably the church triumphant and the church militant, whose intercession is sought. *suffragium* is taken here in the sense of the church's prayers (*suffragia*), not "aid".

[^17]: **S.** The *Quo vadis* story; its common Latin source is Ambrose, *Sermo contra Auxentium* 13 (PL 16, 1011: *videns sibi in porta Christum occurrere … ait: Domine, quo vadis? Respondit Christus: Venio iterum crucifigi*) (LEDGER P08d-5). FitzRalph tells it at *Summa* 7.18 (Paris 1512, fol. 50r), where Peter would leave Rome under Christ's precept in Matt. 10:23, Christ answers *Vado Romam iterum crucifigi*, and Peter understands *seipsum tanquam alterum Christum … ibi crucifigi debere* (LEDGER P08d-6). **C.** *sub colore*, "under colour": a plausible ground (here a pretext in all but name) Peter took from Christ's counsel to flee persecution (Matt. 10:23). *obvianti … et querenti*: dative participles agreeing with an understood *ei* (Peter), to whom *responsum est*; *Christo* is the object of *obvianti*, and *ab eo* is Christ. **R.** AB in the margin (179.25): *Fuga Petri de Roma*.

[^18]: **S.** Matt. 25:40, 45 (Loserth: Matt. 25:34 ff.), "as long as you did it to one of these my least brethren, you did it to me." **C.** *expectans*: "considering", or "waiting" (for the sense of the answer).

[^19]: **R.** AB in the margin (180.6): *Ordinacio divina est duplex*.

[^20]: **C.** *Causam … est exemplar*: the accusative *causam* stands for the nominative subject. *quod ordinavit postmodum ibi pati* is read as *quos*, "whom he ordained afterwards to suffer there", referring to *aliis martyribus*.

[^21]: **R.** AB in the margin (180.12): *Roma est impertinens ut eius episcopus sit precipuus sequax Christi*. **T.** *nomen tale*: the codices read *nomine* (apparatus 180.16); the print is translated.

[^22]: **C.** *Paulus* among the Roman bishops: Wyclif may mean Paul as joint founder of the Roman church with Peter (179.4, "the holy apostles"), or a later pope Paul; the print is translated. **R.** AB in the margin (180.25): *Papa dicitur dupliciter*.

[^23]: **C.** *ad sensum superius limitatum*: the strict sense of "pope", the one who most closely follows Christ (175.21 ff.).
