# *De eucharistia*, chapter 9, part 14: the error of the affection and the threefold peace

## ¶1 · 324.29–326.3

> [p. 324] Quod autem digressus sum tractando difficultates huius materie scolastice, ad hoc valet ut sciatur errorem affectus errorem consequi intellectus. Sicut enim cultores signorum errant falsissime in accidentibus sacramenti, sic errant affectus circa temporalia [p. 325] clero accidencia, ut acquirat sibi merendo et populo veras divicias sempiternas, et sicut blasphemi dicunt quod virtute benediccionis Christi panis secundum se totum destruitur ad non esse, sic quod nulla pars eius remaneat, ita affectus in nostris benediccionibus est perversus, sic quod iuxta prophetam Deus prelatis contrariis maledicit suis benediccionibus et econtra. Et illud in facto ostenditur, cum confessio et usus sacramenti altaris debet precipue fieri propter pacem membrorum ecclesie constituendam et confirmandam, quam sacerdotes tam committendo quam permittendo magis dilacerant. Taceo autem bella et pugnas quas prelati in persona sua procurant et faciunt et voco temporalia que monstruose sibi accumulant et consumunt. Cum autem bonum hoc putridum sit causa bellorum, patet quod presbiteri et specialiter prelati sunt causa dissensionis et bellorum fidelium, et sic omissione dampnabili nec hortantur nec consulunt ad pacem regnorum sed bella, et sic degenerantes a vita et doctrina apostolorum celebrant falsissime missas suas. Procurarent enim verbo et opere quod omnes christiani eciam seculares domini, cum sint unus panis, et de uno pane edant tamquam membra unius ecclesie, quod superant omnia mundi temporalia et honores, antequam edant, lacerent vel turbent minimum membrum matris ecclesie. Quam leccionem si ex sacramento altaris perfecte didicerint, tunc attingendo finem sacramenti tamquam filii pacis vere celebrant missas suas. Et circa hoc intenti dimissis aliis sollicite studeremus; ergo per se finem huius sacramenti que [p. 326] est pax triplex concedat nobis Deus in nobis acquirere in matre nostra adiuvando Deum conficere et in Christo Jesu finaliter observare. Amen.

And that I have digressed in treating the difficulties of this scholastic matter serves for this: that it may be known that the error of the affection follows the error of the understanding.[^1] For just as the [worshippers of signs]{g:cultores-signorum} err most falsely about the [accidents]{g:accidens} of the sacrament, so the affections err about the [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} [325] that befall the clergy, [given] so that it may acquire, by meriting, true and everlasting riches for itself and the people;[^2] and just as the blasphemers say "by virtue of Christ's blessing the [bread]{g:panis} is destroyed in its whole self to not-being, so that no part of it remains", so the affection in our blessings is perverse, so that, according to the prophet, God, to contrary [prelates]{g:prelatus}, "curses their blessings, and conversely".[^3] And that is shown in the fact: since confession and the use of [the sacrament of the altar]{g:sacramentum-altaris} ought chiefly to take place for establishing and strengthening the peace of the [members]{g:membrum} of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, which the [priests]{g:sacerdos} rather tear apart, as much by committing as by permitting. And I pass over the wars and battles that prelates procure and make in their own person, and I name the temporal goods that they monstrously heap up for themselves and consume.[^4] Now since this rotten good is the cause of wars, it is clear that [presbyters]{g:presbiter}, and especially prelates, are the cause of dissension and of the wars of the faithful; and so, by a damnable omission, they neither exhort nor counsel to the peace of kingdoms but to wars; and so, degenerating from the life and [doctrine]{g:doctrina} of the apostles, they celebrate their masses most falsely. For they should procure by word and deed that all Christians, even [secular lords]{g:seculares-domini}, since they "are one bread, and eat of one bread" as members of one church, rise above all the temporal goods and honours of the world before they eat, sooner than tear or trouble the least member of mother church.[^5] If they have learned this lesson perfectly from the sacrament of the altar, then, attaining the end of the sacrament, they [truly]{g:vere} celebrate their masses as sons of peace.[^6] And intent on this, with other things set aside, we should study diligently; therefore may God grant us to acquire the end [of itself]{g:per-se} of this sacrament, which [326] is a threefold peace: in ourselves to acquire it, in [our mother]{g:mater-nostra} by helping God to [make]{g:conficere} it, and in Christ Jesus finally to keep it.[^7] Amen.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Materie scolastice*: taken as a genitive adjective, "this scholastic matter"; as an adverb (*scolastice*, "in the scholastic manner") it would qualify *tractando*. *Affectus* (the will's affection) is set against *intellectus*: the error about temporal goods follows the error about the sacrament.

[^2]: **C/G.** *Temporalia clero accidencia*: Wyclif plays on *accidens*, the accidents of the sacrament and the goods that "befall" (*accidunt*) the clergy. *Ut acquirat*: the purpose for which temporal goods come to the clergy; [given] is supplied for the construal.

[^3]: **S/C.** *Iuxta prophetam*: Mal. 2:2, "I will curse your blessings" (Vulgate *maledicam benedictionibus vestris*); Wyclif turns it into the third person, so his Latin is translated, within Loserth's italic bounds. *Prelatis contrariis* is a dative of disadvantage: prelates contrary [to Christ]. The blasphemers' proposition is quoted as the proposition under discussion.

[^4]: **C.** *Et voco temporalia que …*: "I name", "I call to mind", against *taceo*, "I pass over". The phrase could also be read "and I call 'temporal goods' what they heap up", a gibe at the name; the construal is doubtful.

[^5]: **S/C/T.** *Cum sint unus panis, et de uno pane edant*: 1 Cor. 10:17, "we, being many, are one bread, one body: all that partake of one bread"; Wyclif's wording differs, so his Latin is translated. The rest of the period is hard: *superant* is indicative in a *quod*-clause, and *antequam* is taken twice, of time with *edant* ("before they eat") and of preference with *lacerent vel turbent* ("sooner than tear"). The manuscripts read *edent* (Loserth prints *edant*); E has *lacerant vel turbant*, D *trudent*; ABC read *procurent* for *Procurarent*, and lack *cum*.

[^6]: **S.** *Filii pacis*: Luke 10:6, "if the son of peace be there".

[^7]: **C/G.** *Per se finem*: the end that is the sacrament's of itself, its proper end. *Conficere* has the peace as its object, so "make", against "consecrate", the row's eucharistic sense; the play on the priest's act is Wyclif's. The three infinitives (*acquirere*, *conficere*, *observare*) give the three places of the threefold peace. ABC lack *Deus in nobis* (326.1) and *et* (326.2).
