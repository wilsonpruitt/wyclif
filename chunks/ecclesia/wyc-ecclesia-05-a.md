# *De ecclesia*, chapter 5, part 1: the universal church: equivocations, and why Christ alone is its head

## ¶1 · 92.7–92.18

> [p. 92] Sed adhuc obicitur contra dicta per hoc quod communitas intelligit per Romanam ecclesiam papam et cardinales quibus est necessarium omnibus aliis obedire. Et sic intelligit maior pars ecclesie in tantum quod dominus Ardmacanus scribit Innocencio pape librum De Pauperie Christi tamquam capiti universalis ecclesie. Et talem stilum a maioribus et minoribus recipiunt pape communiter, ubi utrinque est verisimile quod nec papa nec suus fidelis subditus audiret talem blasfemiam, si stilus ille foret scripture sacre contrarius. Quare ergo non potest universalis ecclesia vocari quecunque universitas fidelium viatorum?

But it is still [objected, by appeal to]{g:obicitur-per} this, against what has been said: that the
community understands by the Roman [church]{g:ecclesia} the [pope]{g:papa} and cardinals, whom all
others must of necessity obey. And so the greater part of the church understands [it], so far that
the lord Armachanus writes to Pope Innocent the book *On the Poverty of Christ* as to the
[head]{g:caput} of the universal church. And popes commonly receive such a style from greater and
lesser [men], where on both sides it is likely that neither the pope nor his faithful subject would
hear such blasphemy, if that style were contrary to sacred scripture.[^1] Why, then, cannot any whole
body whatever of faithful [wayfarers]{g:viator} be called the universal church?

## ¶2 · 92.19–94.9

> [p. 92] Hic dicitur (ut supra) quod multi laborant in equivocis, loquendo de ecclesia, unde communiter universitas predestinatorum viancium vocatur universalis ecclesia tam in legibus quam in doctoribus. Et racio potest esse in hoc quod ipsi habent equivoce racionem inperfeccionis in obscuritate fidei qua filii superioris ecclesie sunt purgati. Et quod plus est: Aliqui descendunt [p. 93] particularius ad gradus universalitatum, et hii vocant ecclesiam Latinorum universalem ecclesiam, hii ecclesiam Grecorum vel Hebreorum, et sic de aliis linguis aut gentibus de quarum universitate solum considerant. Et hoc contingit vel ex radice superbie vel ignorancie. Nam quidam ex fastu aspirant quod sint magnarum universitatum capita. Quod non laudo, et hinc credo quod scole que olim vocate sunt studia generalia propter doctrinam septem arcium, nunc dicuntur universitates, cum sint quasi punctus in comparacione ad primam universitatem creatam. Quandoque autem ex ignorancia quidam vocant datam particularem congregacionem universalem ecclesiam, non putantes esse ecclesiam ampliorem, ut III Reg. XIX, 18, alloquitur Helyas Deum, quoniam ipse solus relictus est fidelis in populo, et tamen audivit divinum responsum quod Deus reliquit sibi septem millia qui non curvaverunt genua ante Baal (ut meminit apostolus). Sic quidam maniaci putant quod non sit fidelis in terris nisi sit eis seculariter subiectus, cum tamen stat quod sint multe gentes ignote linguis priores illis in ecclesia sancta Dei. Quando ergo doctores sic loquuntur ex circumstanciis, potest perpendi equivocacionis sue deteccio, ut vocando episcopum Romanum caput universalis ecclesie intelligitur quod sit caput tante Latine ecclesie quantam regit. Quod reputative vel suppositive potest dici de quocunque Romano pontifice cuius opera papalia ad hoc movent. Sed ne laboraverim in istis equivocis, limitavi ad universalem ecclesiam, distinctam contra particularem, que quidem universalis est unica, habens omnes alias eius partes. Nam (ut ostendi superius, cap. II) sunt multe [p. 94] tales particulares ecclesie: ideo decreta ecclesie et sancti doctores non debent vocare illam particularem et pauperem ecclesiam sic artatam in angulo ecclesiam sanctam catholicam. Verumptamen si papa ut olim regat illam secundum legem Christi principaliter et capitaliter tam in moribus quam in dubiis decidendis, tunc cum suo collegio potest dici virtualiter universalis ecclesia in effectu. Sed hec est solum per accidens dependens ab opere et non a nudo nomine.

To this the answer is (as above) that many labour in equivocations in speaking of the church; hence
the whole body of the [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} [on the road]{g:viantes} is commonly called the
universal church, both in the laws and in the doctors. And the reason can lie in this, that they
have, equivocally, the character of imperfection in the obscurity of faith, from which the sons of
the higher church have been purged.[^2] And what is more, some descend [93] more particularly to
grades of universality, and these call the church of the Latins the universal church, those the
church of the Greeks or of the Hebrews, and so of other tongues or nations, of whose whole body alone
they take account. And this happens from the root either of pride or of ignorance. For some out of
haughtiness aspire to be heads of great universities. This I do not praise; and hence I believe that
the schools which were once called general studies, on account of the [doctrine]{g:doctrina} of the
seven arts, are now called universities, although they are as a point in comparison with the first
created universe.[^3] And sometimes out of ignorance some call a given particular congregation the
universal church, not thinking that there is a wider church; as in 1 Kings 19:18 Elias addresses God
[saying] that he alone was left faithful among the people, and yet he heard the divine answer that
God had left himself seven thousand who had not bowed their knees before Baal (as the apostle
recalls). So some madmen think that there is no faithful [man] on earth unless he is subject to them
in secular fashion, although it may well be that there are many nations, unknown in tongue, before
them in the holy church of God.[^4] When, therefore, the doctors speak thus, the detection of their
equivocation can be weighed from the circumstances; as, in calling the Roman bishop head of the
universal church, it is understood that he is head of as much of the Latin church as he rules. Which
can be said by reckoning or by supposition of any Roman pontiff whatever whose papal works move [us]
to this. But, lest I labour in these equivocations, I have [assigned]{g:limitare} [the name] to the
universal church, distinguished against the particular, which universal [church] indeed is single,
having all the others as its parts.[^5] For (as I showed above, chapter 2) there are many [94] such
particular churches; therefore the decrees of the church and the holy doctors ought not to call that
particular and poor church, so confined in a corner, the holy catholic church. Nevertheless, if the
pope, as once, rules it according to the law of Christ, principally and [as head]{g:capitaliter},
both in morals and in deciding doubts, then with his college he can be called virtually the universal
church in effect. But this is only by accident, depending on the work and not on the bare name.[^6]

## ¶3 · 94.10–94.15

> [p. 94] Unde (ut dixi superius) solus Christus potest esse caput ecclesie sancte catholice. Ipsa enim (ut dicit Augustinus in epistola ad Dardanum) habet solummodo unum caput; nec movet quod respublica habet imperatorem et papam capita unum subordinatum alteri (ut exemplificat dominus Ardmacanus).

Hence (as I said above) Christ alone can be head of the holy catholic church. For it (as Augustine
says in the letter to Dardanus) has only one head; nor does it move [me] that the commonwealth has an
emperor and a pope as heads, one subordinated to the other (as the lord Armachanus exemplifies).[^7]

## ¶4 · 94.16–95.1

> [p. 94] Nam caput tocius ecclesie oportet simul in unum exemplare tam spirituale quam seculare regnum, et cum hoc regnum ierarchie celestis ad quod officium nulla alia creatura potest attingere. Unde bene est pape, si habet a Christo virtutem ad regendum se ipsum et pauculas oves in spiritualibus, licet non blasphemet quod sit caput universalis ecclesie paris potestatis cum Christo. Nec debet eius vicarius sentire heretice quod regat parcialem ecclesiam viantem tam seculariter quam spiritualiter, dum Christus solum spiritualiter rexit utramque ecclesiam. Ideo sufficit eius vicario quod tam in temporalibus quam in spiritualibus regat spiritualiter eius iuvenculam. Nam vivendo seculariter Christus non potuit exemplare utrumque officium. For the head of the whole church must at once, in one, be the pattern of the spiritual as well as of
the secular [kingdom]{g:regnum}, and with this of the kingdom of the heavenly hierarchy; to which
office no other creature can attain. Hence it is well with the pope if he has from Christ the power
to rule himself and a very few sheep in spiritual things, even though he does not blaspheme [by
saying] that he is head of the universal church, of equal power with Christ. Nor ought his vicar to
think heretically that he rules the partial church on the road both secularly and spiritually, while
Christ ruled both churches only spiritually. Therefore it suffices for his vicar that he rule his

office of superior is the worthier. But the rule of Christ regards, by essential causation, the rule

their food is turned into a poisonous substance, so some grow so used to false
[opinions]{g:opinio} that they are rendered [unqualified]{g:inhabilis} for instruction in the

does not do to call a [superior]{g:prepositi} anything but most reverend, most holy and most blessed,
since in any lower degree there is dulia, but in the superlative degree there is latria, than which
no ampler honour is to be looked for. Correspondingly to these things, the superior is not content to
be called head of the [church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans}, but demands to be called head of the
universal church and of the whole catholic church. In both cases, nevertheless, the aforesaid can be
soundly conceived: as, that God is to be adored in such a superior as in an image; or, secondly, that
among the superiors to be designated he is the most reverend, most holy and, in his time, most
blessed, provided however that the name and the meritorious ministry correspond; which ought to be
supposed in the superior by reckoning, not asserted definitively, provided however that neither
idleness nor [a deed] committed notoriously contradict [it].[^11]

## ¶6 · 96.1–96.19

> [p. 96] Et sic Potest intelligi papam esse caput universalis ecclesie vel in quantum ministratorie propinquius gerit ministerium Christi quem induit, vel secundo equivocando in universali, universaliori et universalissimo intelligatur per universalem ecclesiam tota ecclesia militans quam hic regit; que debet supponi extencior quam alia militans cui vicarius eius preest. Melius tamen foret inniti modo loquendi scripture et instar apostolorum vehemenciori ac humiliori ministerio totalis ecclesie propter blasphemam ydolatriam precavendam. Nam dum papa non supereminet quoad Deum, sed appreciatur honorem secularem plus opere servili ecclesie, tunc facit populum non adorare eum, sed statum communem in alio ad sui dedecus et ypocrite detrimentum. Et ita ecclesia Romana potest sumi tripliciter: vel pro ecclesia catholica (ut in fine proximi capituli), vel pro papa, vel coniunctim et divisim pro suo collegio. Sed hec duo membra postrema sunt extorta.

And so it can be understood that the pope is head of the universal church: either inasmuch as, by
way of ministry, he bears more nearly the ministry of Christ, whom he has put on; or, secondly, by
equivocating among universal, more universal and most universal, [so that] by the universal church
is understood the whole church militant which he rules here, which ought to be supposed more
extensive than another [church] militant over which his vicar presides. Yet it would be better to
rely on scripture's manner of speaking and, after the pattern of the apostles, on a more vehement and
humbler ministry of the total church, so as to guard against blasphemous idolatry.[^12] For when the
pope does not stand pre-eminent with respect to God, but prizes secular honour more than the servile
work of the church, then he makes the people adore not him but the common state in another, to his
own dishonour and the hypocrite's detriment. And so the Roman church [can be taken]{g:sumitur-pro}
in three ways: either for the catholic church (as at the end of the preceding chapter), or for the
pope, or, jointly and severally, for his college. But these two last members are forced.[^13]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 92.7: Objection: The Roman Church is commonly understood of the Pope and Cardinals.
- 92.15: Why may not "the whole Church" mean the whole of any body of the faithful.
- 92.19: Answer: Whole is used in various senses
- 93.1: and commonly in too low a sense
- 93.10: as of Universities.
- 93.14: Many do not know how great the Church is.
- 93.20: And some madmen think that only their subjects are in the faith.
- 93.29: We mean the Church truly universal
- 94.4: which Pope and Cardinals may represent if they rule rightly.
- 94.10: But only Christ can be its head;
- 94.16: to offer a model, of spiritual and secular rule.
- 94.22: The Pope has to rule only spiritually.
- 95.2: The emperor's rule is independent of the pope.
- 95.15: Prelates demand undue honours.
- 96.1: The Pope may be head of the Church, as meaning the militant Church that is under his rule.

## Notes

[^1]: **S/T.** *Dominus Ardmacanus*: Richard FitzRalph, archbishop of Armagh; the book is his *De
    pauperie Salvatoris*, which Wyclif calls *De pauperie Christi* and says was written to Pope
    Innocent [VI] (Loserth's foot identifies the work; the dedication to Innocent is not
    independently checked, LEDGER E05a-1). Loserth's foot cites Vienna, ÖNB Cod. Pal. 1430, *Libri
    septem de paupertate Salvatoris*, whose fol. 1 note says Ricardus composed it in the Roman curia
    against the mendicant friars "at the instance of Pope Clement VI" (LEDGER E05a-1). A
    *verissimile* (92.15). A₁ *auderet* (92.16), "would dare [to use]", is easier; but A₁ alone does
    not outweigh the print, which construes: neither would listen to such blasphemy. *Stilus*: the
    style of address.

[^2]: **C/R.** A margin *Ecclesie equivocacio* (92.19). *Universitas* runs through ¶1–2 as "whole
    body", "university" and "universe": the play is Wyclif's. *Ipsi habent equivoce racionem
    inperfeccionis*: our construal, doubtful: the predestinate on the road are "universal church" only
    equivocally, since they share the imperfection of faith's obscurity, from which the church above has
    been purged. *Qua*: ablative with *purgati*.

[^3]: **T/C.** B
    *ut bene vocant* (93.2). *De quarum*: Loserth's emendation; the MSS *de quorum* (93.4). B *quod
    sicut* (93.6–7). *Studia generalia*: the medieval name of a university. *Doctrina* (GLOSSARY §B):
    here the teaching of the arts course. *Cum*: concessive. *Prima universitas creata*: the created
    universe.

[^4]: **S/T/C.** 1 Kings 19:10–18 (Wyclif *III Reg.*, the Vulgate numbering), recalled through Rom.
    11:3–4 (Loserth's foot); the indirect speech follows Rom. 11:4, DR "But what saith the divine answer
    to him? I have left me seven thousand men that have not bowed their knees to Baal"; Wyclif *ante
    Baal*, as the Vulgate. B *quod sicut*; B *ignote lingue* (93.21–22). *Ignote linguis*: we take the
    ablative of respect (nations whose tongues they do not know); a dative, "unknown to [their]
    tongues", is possible. *Cum tamen stat quod*: "although it stands that", the logicians' "it is
    consistent that".

[^5]: **C/T.** *Ex circumstanciis*: taken with *potest perpendi*; it may go with *loquuntur* ("speak
    thus from the circumstances"). A margin *Papa quomodo est caput ecclesie* (93.28). *Reputative vel
    suppositive*: by reckoning or supposition, not as known (cf. 90.29). *Limitavi* (93.29): the print
    has an inkless gap (*limi avi*), corrected in the Latin. *Limitare*: to assign (GLOSSARY
    §D): the name "universal church" is kept for the whole church as against the particular.

[^6]: **C.** *Cap. II*: chapter 2 of this work. *Illam particularem et pauperem ecclesiam*: the Roman
    church in the narrow sense, as against the catholic church. *Ut olim*: as [popes did] once. *Hec*:
    this naming of pope and college "universal church".

[^7]: **S/T.** Augustine, *Ep.* 187 to Dardanus §§20, 37, PL 33 (*Hujus corporis caput est Christus*; *unum caput est
    Christus*), in sense (Loserth: *Opp. Aug.* II, p. 678) (LEDGER E05a-2);
    cf. 04-e ¶1 (85.25–29). A margin (94.10):
    *Solucio obiectus. Solus Christus est caput ecclesie sancte catholice*. B *unum scribo animatum
    alteri* (94.14), a garble of *unum subordinatum alteri*; B *dominus Dardinandus* (94.15).
    FitzRalph's example of the two heads: untraced (not in *De pauperie Salvatoris* I–IV; Loserth gives no reference) (LEDGER E05a-3). *Respublica*: the commonwealth
    of Christendom.

[^8]: **C/R.** *Simul in unum exemplare*: to be in one person the pattern of both kingdoms and of the
    heavenly hierarchy; only Christ is. *Licet non blasphemet*: concessive; the alternative, "provided
    he does not blaspheme", gives the same point. A margin (94.24): *Solucio obiectus. Papa debet
    spiritualiter regere utramque partem ecclesie et non seculariter*, which takes *utramque ecclesiam*
    as both parts of the church. *Eius vicarius*: Christ's vicar, the pope. *Iuvencula*: a girl, a
    young woman (also a heifer); here Christ's young bride, the church on the road. A margin *Conclusio*
    (94.28).

[^9]: **T/G.** B lacks *tamen* (95.2); B *Regnum autem* for *Regimen autem* (95.4). *Regimine pape*:
    ablative after *independens*. *Prepositura*: the office of a superior (GLOSSARY §B, *prepositi*).

    humanity (GLOSSARY §B).

[^10]: **S/T.** A margin *Contra adulatores ad papam* (95.7), *Commentator* (95.10). Gregory's wine

[^11]: **T/C/R.** *Cap. …*: Loserth, *Num. capituli deest* (95.15). B, a later hand, in the margin

[^12]: **C/T.** *Potest* (96.1) is printed with a capital. *Quam hic regit*: *hic* may be "here" or
    "this [man]", the pope. If the pope rules, *eius vicarius* is the pope's deputy; if Christ rules

    idolatry"; we translate the print *blasphemam*.

[^13]: **C/R.** *Eum*: the pope, or God (*quoad Deum*); *statum communem in alio*: the common state of
    man in another man: the people adore a man for his rank. The construal is doubtful. A margin
    *Equivocacio ecclesie Romane ad tria* (96.16). *Proximi capituli*: chapter 4 (04-e ¶2). *Membra*:
