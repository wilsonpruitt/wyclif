# *De potestate pape*, chapter 10, part 5: the argument from what the community believes, and a third rejoinder for the endowment

## ¶1 · 236.1–237.14

> [p. 236] De indulgenciis vero quas ipsi distribuebant per bullas non recolo, sed committebant illas sine bullis divino iudicio. Nec credo quod omnes falsigraphi fundabunt oppositum. Arguitur igitur communiter: quod Deus posset concessisse Petro et cuicumque Romano pontifici, ut communitas credit, preeminenciam potestatis, nec probari poterit quod non fecit; igitur cum ista fides multum intererat devocionis fidelium, videtur quod theologus debet ipsam defendere et non infundabiliter impugnare. Ista argucia est plena mendacio, nam communitas credit multa impossibilia et heretica de potestate vicaria, ut puta quod quilibet papa habet tantam potestatem sicut Christus habebat humanitus, quod quilibet Romanus pontifex in quantum talis habet infinitum maiorem potestatem quam habet alius in concedendo indulgencias, cum terminum potestatis ponunt in aliis non in illo, sic quod eo ipso quo pretendit se quovismodo solvere vel ligare, eo ipso sic solvit vel ligat; et sic de infinitis summe impossibilibus atque hereticis. De veritatibus autem, quas credere debet communitas de pape preeminencia, non dubium quin probari potest ex scripture testistimonio vel ex sensu. Loquendo autem de excessu ficte potestatis, patet quod minor est falsa. Sed si loquamur de gradu potestatis possibili, patet quod non est color in hoc concludere quod debemus illum concedere, quia sic concederemus in papa et quocumque presbitero omnem potestatem sibi possibilem. Ex ista blasfema evidencia potest habere istam potenciam, igitur habet. Sed quis tunc resisteret Antichristo, cum iuxta hoc debemus mendaciter credere falsissimam extollenciam [p. 237] potestatis? Est igitur argumentum sufficiens, si quis extollit se de preeminencia potestatis ecclesiastice, ultra quod scitur fundari ex scriptura verbo vel opere fecisse sanctos apostolos, tunc non debemus credere huiusmodi potestatem. Et sic aliquam potestatem vendicatam debemus non credere, ut ego non credo, non obstante concessione Cesaris quod quilibet papa habet potestatem iurisdicionis sive regiminis super totam Yndiam, Yudeam et alias naciones, ymo credo quod non habet, sicut nec credo quod Petrus superstes habuit tam ample singulariter tantam potenciam, quia tunc nimis graviter ex negligencia peccavisset.

[236] And as for indulgences which they distributed by bulls, I do not recall [any]; rather, they committed those matters without bulls to the divine judgement.[^1] Nor do I believe that all the [false writers]{g:falsigraphus} will ground the opposite. It is argued, therefore, commonly: that God could have granted to Peter and to every [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex}, as the community believes, [preeminence]{g:preeminencia} of [power]{g:potestas}, and it cannot be proved that he did not; therefore, since this faith mattered greatly to the devotion of the faithful, it seems that a theologian ought to defend it and not attack it [without foundation]{g:infundabiliter}. This [quibble]{g:argucia} is full of lying, for the community believes many impossible and heretical things about the vicarial power: for instance, that every [pope]{g:papa} has as much power as Christ had in his humanity; that every Roman pontiff, in so far as he is such, has infinitely greater power than another has in granting indulgences, since they set the limit of power in others, not in him,[^2] so that by the very fact that he claims to loose or bind in any way whatever, by that very fact he so looses or binds; and so on for infinitely many things supremely impossible and heretical. But as for the truths which the community ought to believe about the pope's preeminence, there is no doubt that they can be proved from the testimony of scripture or from sense.[^3] And speaking of the excess of a feigned power, it is clear that the minor is false.[^4] But if we speak of a possible degree of power, it is clear that there is no [colour]{g:color} in [inferring]{g:concludere} from this that we ought to grant it,[^5] since so we would grant, in the pope and in any [presbyter]{g:presbiter}, every power possible to him. By this blasphemous [evidence]{g:evidencia}: "He can have this power, therefore he has it." But who then would resist Antichrist, since according to this we ought lyingly to believe a most false [exaltation]{g:extollencia} [237] of power?[^6] It is therefore a sufficient argument: if anyone exalts himself on the preeminence of ecclesiastical power beyond what the holy apostles are known, on the ground of scripture, to have done by word or deed, then we ought not to believe in power of this kind.[^7] And so some claimed power we ought to disbelieve, as I do not believe, notwithstanding the [grant]{g:concessio} of Caesar,[^8] that every pope has [power of jurisdiction]{g:potestas-iurisdiccionis} or [of rule]{g:potestas-regiminis} over all India, Judaea and other nations; indeed I believe that he does not have it, just as I do not believe either that Peter, while he lived, had singly so great a power so amply, for then he would have sinned too gravely by negligence.

## ¶2 · 237.15–237.27

> [p. 237] Tercio replicatur contra dicta per hoc quod multi sancti pape et episcopi, multi beati seculares domini et populi dotacionem ecclesie approbarunt, ymo aliter non haberemus papam vel ordinem episcoporum, et per consequens nec causarum ecclesiasticarum decisiones nec privilegiaciones nec sacramentorum aut ministrorum ecclesiasticorum ministraciones, quod est contra racionem naturalem, ut ecclesia sit ita acephala. Sicut igitur oportet esse principes seculares ita et principes spirituales, sicut exemplatum est in veteri testamento; nimis magna itaque foret presumpcio cessare dotaciones, elecciones et leges ecclesie super istis.

[237] Thirdly, it is rejoined against what has been said,[^9] on the ground that many holy popes and [bishops]{g:episcopus}, many blessed [secular lords]{g:seculares-domini} and peoples approved the [endowment]{g:dotacio} of the [church]{g:ecclesia}; indeed, otherwise we would not have a pope or the order of bishops, and consequently neither decisions of ecclesiastical cases, nor [privilegings]{g:privilegiacio}, nor ministrations of sacraments or of ecclesiastical ministers, which is against natural reason: that the church should be so [acephalous]{g:acephala}. Just as, therefore, there must be secular princes, so also spiritual princes, as is exemplified in the old testament; so it would be too great a presumption to put an end to the endowments, elections and laws of the church concerning these things.

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 236.1: Some argue that the powers ascribed to the pope tend to piety, though they cannot be proved,
- 236.27: Then we should credit every priest with every possible power and admit the pretensions of Antichrist.
- 237.1: It is said that withdrawing endowment would ruin the Church.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *De indulgenciis vero … non recolo*: *vero* continues the preceding account (in part 4) of the early popes, who are the *ipsi*; *non recolo* "I do not recall" is Wyclif's dry way of saying there were none. *Illas* (fem. pl.) most naturally takes up *indulgencias*: they left the remission of sins to God's judgement rather than issuing bulls for it.

[^2]: **C.** *Cum terminum potestatis ponunt in aliis non in illo*: the subject of *ponunt* is the community of believers. They grant that every other prelate's power has a limit (*terminus*), but set none to the pope's, hence the copyists' marginal note at 236.4, *Pape potestas infinita* ("the pope's power infinite").

[^3]: **T.** 236.22–23: the manuscripts read *quas de papa credere debet communitas de pape preeminencia*; Loserth's print drops the redundant *de papa*, and we follow the print. *Testistimonio* (236.23) is translated as *testimonio*.

[^4]: **C.** *Minor*: the minor premiss of the argument stated at 236.3–5, "it cannot be proved that he did not [grant it]". Of a feigned excess of power the denial can be proved, so the minor is false; of a merely possible degree (the next sentence) the inference to "we ought to grant it" fails.

[^5]: **C.** *Non est color*: "colour" in the period sense of a plausible ground (as in "colour of title"), not a pretext.

[^6]: **T.** 237.1 *extollenciam*: so Loserth's print; the manuscripts read *excellenciam* "excellence". The print is kept; it echoes *extollit se* in the next sentence.

[^7]: **T/C.** 237.4: the manuscripts read *vel nostro opere vel fecisse*; Loserth prints *verbo vel opere fecisse* (cf. 283.3), which we translate. The clause is construed with *sanctos apostolos* as subject of *fecisse*: what the apostles are known from scripture to have done "by word or deed". The alternative, "known to be grounded in scripture, by word or deed", leaves *fecisse* without a construction.

[^8]: **S/C.** *Concessione Cesaris*: the Donation of Constantine, the grant by which Wyclif holds the caesarean papacy was made (LEDGER P10e-1). *Debemus non credere* is stronger than "need not believe": "we ought to disbelieve".

[^9]: **R.** 237.15: A in the margin, *Tercio arguitur* ("thirdly it is argued"); both manuscripts, *Pape necessitas, nota bene* ("the necessity of the pope, note well"). The rejoinder is answered in part 6.
