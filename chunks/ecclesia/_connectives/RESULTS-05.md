# Connective check: *sed* / *autem*, De ecclesia, extract 05 (chunks 13-f – 15-g)

Classes: (a) minor premiss / next step, "now" · (b) adversative, "but" · (c) continuative *autem*, "and";
*non … sed* (incl. *nedum / non solum … sed*), "but".

## Doubtful, left as they are

- **13-f, p. 295**: *non autem spirituales et periculosissimos inimicos*, rendered "not spiritual and most dangerous enemies". *autem* has no word of its own, but the English "friends …, not enemies" carries the adversative; "but not" would be the stricter form.
- **13-f, p. 295**: *Sed Matthei X, 36, dicit Christus: Inimici eius, domestici eius*, rendered "But". Either (b), against the command to make friends, or (a), the premiss that the clergy (*domestici*) are the enemies meant.
- **14-a, p. 301**: *Hoc autem est contra evangelium Luce XXII°*, rendered "But". Either (b), or (a), the premiss that overturns the hypothesis *si Christus … habuissent ius* (a reductio without an explicit *ergo*).
- **15-e, p. 348**: *Illud vero caritatis, hoc autem est impietatis officium*, rendered "The former is the office of charity, the latter of impiety". *vero/autem* are carried by the former/latter construction.
- **15-f, p. 350**: *Servus autem civilis est omnino expers libertatis civilis*, rendered "Now". Either (a), the premiss behind *et sic serviunt clerici regi libere*, or (c), a definition added in passing ("And a civil serf").
- **15-f, p. 351**: *posito … quod omnes episcopi … deficiant …, regum autem est … clerum suum corripere*, rendered "it is then for kings". *autem* resumes the main clause after a long absolute; "then" carries it as the apodosis. "Now" or "and" would read badly here.
- **15-f, p. 351** (changed above): *Econtra autem* could also be (c), "And conversely"; "But" chosen because the two subjections are set against each other.
