# *De eucharistia*, chapter 9, part 3: teachers as witnesses, the glossators, and Lanfranc

## ¶1 · 280.17–281.17

> [p. 280] Quod si obicitur necessario aliquos esse qui autentice doceant sensum scripture et alias veritates katholicas quas scriptura non exprimit, ut patet de beato Augustino contra Donatistas et multis aliis articulis fidei adiectis: hic dicitur quod necesse est aliquos esse preeminentes in ecclesia qui sint simul pastores et doctores (ut concludit Apostolus) et illis oportet credere non tanquam auctoribus sed tanquam doctoribus. Quod si prelati errant in pastu et circa mundana [p. 281] solliciti christiane fidei sunt ignari: quomodo curremus in isto ad ipsos ut capita ydolorum? Quod si desunt tales, melius est quiescere in fide explicita quam habemus quam suscitare novam sentenciam; ymmo ignoto sensu mistico vel litterali secundario melius est pausare in sensu rudi, relinquendo Spiritui Sancto cum sanctificacione vite sensum alium quam infundabiliter sculpere novum sensum. Unde quia secure possumus vocare eucaristiam panem, ut notat fides scripture cum sanctis doctoribus, debemus ad imitacionem eorum illud eligere et ambiguum de corpore isto vel alio accidente ignoto respuere nisi forte declaretur revelacione. Sed Deus non tardaret revelare fidem istam carioribus eius discipulis; nec credo Augustinum vel alios sanctos innovare quidquam credendum ecclesie nisi elaboratum ex fide scripture.

But if [it is objected]{g:obicitur} that there must necessarily be some who teach [authentically]{g:autenticus} the [meaning]{g:sensus} of scripture and other catholic truths which scripture does not express, as is clear of blessed Augustine against the Donatists and of many other [articles of faith]{g:articulus-fidei} that have been added: here the answer is that it is necessary that there be some preeminent in the [church]{g:ecclesia} who are at once pastors and doctors (as the Apostle [infers]{g:concludere}),[^1] and these one must believe not as authors but as doctors. But if prelates err in feeding [the flock] and, busy [281] about worldly things, are ignorant of the Christian faith, how shall we run in this to them as to heads of idols?[^2] And if such are lacking, it is better to rest in the [explicit faith]{g:fides-explicita} which we have than to raise up a new [teaching]{g:sentencia};[^3] indeed, when the mystical or the secondary literal sense is unknown, it is better to pause in the rough sense, leaving the other sense to the Holy Spirit, with the [sanctification]{g:sanctificacio} of life, than [without foundation]{g:infundabilis} to carve out a new sense.[^4] Hence, since we can safely call the [Eucharist]{g:eukaristia} [bread]{g:panis}, as the faith of scripture with the holy doctors notes, we ought in imitation of them to choose that, and to spit out the ambiguity concerning this body or another unknown [accident]{g:accidens}, unless perhaps it is [made clear]{g:declarare} by revelation.[^5] But God would not delay to reveal this faith to his dearer disciples; nor do I believe that Augustine or other saints introduced anything new to be believed by the church unless worked out [from the faith of]{g:ex-fide} scripture.

## ¶2 · 281.18–281.30

> [p. 281] Unde utinam nostri considerarent fundamentum Augustini contra Donatistas. Nam evangelium precipit homines baptisari, non precipiendo baptismi iteracionem. Sic nec lex Christi nec eius cerimonie debent gravari sine auctoritate scripture: ergo iugum difficultacionis ecclesie non est sibi inponendum multiplicando baptismum. Tota ista sentencia patet ex fide scripture et sic occasione male data ab emulis sollicitabant se sancti ultra expressionem fidei ecclesie primitive. Et observata perfecte ista sentencia nunquam oneraretur ecclesia ad credendum quod eukaristia sit quantitas sacra. Et ita dicitur generaliter de omni fide a sanctis doctoribus noviter expressata.

Hence, would that our [men] considered Augustine's foundation against the Donatists! For the gospel commands men to be baptized, without commanding the repetition of baptism. So neither the law of Christ nor his ceremonies ought to be burdened without the [authority]{g:auctoritas} of scripture: therefore the [yoke]{g:iugum} of making things difficult for the church is not to be laid on it by multiplying baptism.[^6] This whole teaching is clear from the faith of scripture; and so, on an occasion wrongly given by rivals, the saints exerted themselves beyond the expression of the faith of the primitive church.[^7] And were this teaching perfectly observed, the church would never be burdened with believing that the Eucharist is a [sacred]{g:sacer} [quantity]{g:quantitas}. And so it is said generally of all faith newly expressed by the holy doctors.

## ¶3 · 281.31–281.33

> [p. 281] Tercio arguitur contra hoc per glossas decretorum et famosos doctores qui scripserant contrarium super illa materia: illis ergo oportet credere plus quam nobis.

Thirdly, against this, [it is argued by appeal to]{g:arguitur-per} the [glosses]{g:glossa} of the [decrees]{g:decretum} and famous doctors who wrote the contrary on that matter: these, therefore, one must believe more than us.[^8]

## ¶4 · 282.1–283.17

> [p. 282] Hic dicitur quod nulli pure creature credendum est nisi ut testi, non ut domino veritatis, in tantum quod Christus venit in mundum humanitus, ut testimonium perhibeat veritati et cum omnis veritas sit in scriptura (ut dicit Augustinus), patet quod nulli creature credendum est nisi de quanto fundaverit se in scriptura; quanto magis nos testes abiecti debemus dolere de errore in falsitate et testificare quantum ex fide scripture sufficimus veritatem et docto errore nostro humiliter revocare. In testem autem veritatis non debet accipi nisi qui habuerit eius noticiam, in tantum quod in veritate facti humani non debet secundum poleticos capi in testem nisi qui de ipso noticiam habuerit, quia aliter testis foret necessitatus mentiri. Et in isto inpugnatur quedam lex Anglie, que necessitare videtur testem asserere quod vel distincte scit vel non scit quis commisit facinus perpetratum. Et hec est racio quare leges ecclesie quoad fidem scripture plus credunt quatuor sanctis doctoribus et specialiter Augustino quam aliis quos credunt nec ex dono Dei nec ex studio humano tantum de veritate fidei cognovisse. In ambiguis tamen credendum est illis evidencia topica, exspectando inspiracionem auctoris fidei a quo est omnis noticia nostra veritatis; de veritate autem et sufficiencia scripture non licet ambigere. Ideo cum nullibi docet, vel quod eukaristia sit aliquid de novem generibus accidentis vel quod potest ipsum permittere per se esse, sed periciores sancti dixerunt oppositum, videtur stultum recentes diffinire sic esse de facto et cum illo ut fide onerare [p. 283] ecclesiam. Stetit enim sponsa Christi in floribus (ut creditur) circiter mille annos, antequam fuerit onerata ista perfidia, quia usque ad indebitos cultores. Et patet quod doctores legum hominum sunt valde suspecti in ista materia, cum spissim ex ignorancia seminant hereticos sensus. In ambiguis autem extra fidem scripture solent periti silere vel ipsa abicere curiosis, concedentes quod expedit nobis nedum multa ignorare sed nec cogitare, cuiusmodi sunt impertinencia fidei. Itaque quantum ad glossatores, patet ex dictis quod non debet credi illis et specialiter in ista materia nisi de quanto se fundaverint in racione vel scriptura autentica. Unde propter perplexitatem illius materie datam a nostris novellis doctoribus glossatores decretorum titubant et discordant; ideo dictum eorum in ista materia non est argumentum nisi ad hominem.

Here the answer is that no pure [creature]{g:creatura} is to be believed except as a witness, not as lord of truth, so much so that Christ came into the world [humanly]{g:humanitus} to give testimony to the truth;[^9] and since all truth is in scripture (as Augustine says),[^10] it is clear that no creature is to be believed except so far as it has founded itself on scripture. How much more ought we, outcast witnesses, to grieve over error in falsehood, and to testify the truth as far as we are able from the faith of scripture, and, our error being shown, humbly to revoke [it]. And none ought to be accepted as a witness of truth except one who has knowledge of it, so much so that in the truth of a human deed none ought, according to the political [writers], to be taken as witness except one who has knowledge of it, for otherwise the witness would be necessitated to lie.[^11] And in this a certain law of England is impugned, which seems to necessitate a witness to assert that he either distinctly knows or does not know who committed the [misdeed]{g:facinus} perpetrated.[^12] And this is the reason why the [laws of the church]{g:leges-ecclesie}, as to the faith of scripture, believe the four holy doctors, and especially Augustine, more than others whom they believe to have known so much of the truth of faith neither by the gift of God nor by human study. In doubtful things, however, they are to be believed with [topical evidence]{g:evidencia-topica},[^13] awaiting the inspiration of the author of faith, from whom is all our knowledge of truth; but of the truth and sufficiency of scripture it is not lawful to doubt. Therefore, since it nowhere teaches either that the Eucharist is something of the nine genera of accident or that [God] can permit it to be [of itself]{g:per-se}, but the more expert saints said the opposite, it seems foolish for recent [men] to define that it is so in fact, and with it to burden [283] the church as with faith.[^14] For the [bride]{g:sponsa} of Christ stood among flowers (as is believed) about a thousand years before she was burdened with this perfidy, [that is,] until the undue [worshippers]{g:cultor} [came].[^15] And it is clear that the doctors of men's laws are highly suspect in this matter, since they frequently, out of ignorance, sow heretical meanings. And in doubtful things outside the faith of scripture the expert are wont to be silent, or to cast them away to the curious, granting that it is expedient for us [not only]{g:nedum} to be [ignorant of]{g:ignorare} many things but not even to think of them; of which kind are things [irrelevant]{g:impertinens} to faith. So, as to the [glossators]{g:glossator}, it is clear from what has been said that they are not to be believed, especially in this matter, except so far as they have founded themselves on reason or on authentic scripture. Hence, because of the [perplexity]{g:perplexitas} given to that matter by our [newer]{g:novelli} doctors, the glossators of the decrees stagger and disagree; therefore their saying in this matter is no argument except [to the man]{g:argumentum-ad-hominem}.[^16]

## ¶5 · 283.18–284.4

> [p. 283] Quantum ad doctores legis, dicit primus, et precipuus videtur esse Lanfrancus abbas Beccensis in libello suo contra Beringarium De Sacramento Altaris, ubi videtur inpugnare quod panis et vinum remanent post consecracionem sed in consecracione desinunt. Iste autem Lanfrancus, licet fuerit sanctus, aliunde tamen in sua inveccione inartificiose procedit, quia sine evidencia nunc contendit modo quo abbas solet alloqui suos novicios et nunc simulat falsa contra que arguit. Non enim invenit homo in toto scripto suo (si non fallor) nisi evidenciam triplicem: Prima si panis remanet, tunc sacramentum parvi panis indignius est quam manna. Secunda racio est quod prophecia veteris testamenti figurans corpus Christi de futuro foret sollempnius quam nostrum sacramentum quod habet [p. 284] nudam efficaciam ad signandum corpus Christi de preterito; quod cum non sit prophecia sed veritas de preterito, ydiota sciret ostendere. Et tercia racio stat super decretis ecclesie que allegat.

As to the doctors of law, the first who speaks, and the chief, seems to be Lanfranc, abbot of Bec, in his little book against Berengar, *On the Sacrament of the Altar*,[^17] where he seems to impugn [the view] that the bread and [wine]{g:vinum} remain after [consecration]{g:consecracio}, but [holding] that in consecration they cease. But this Lanfranc, though he was a saint, nevertheless proceeds in his invective unskilfully on another side, for now he contends without [evidence]{g:evidencia}, in the way an abbot is wont to address his novices, and now he feigns falsehoods against which he argues. For one finds in his whole writing (if I am not mistaken) only a threefold evidence. The first: if bread remains, then the sacrament of a little bread is less worthy than the manna. The second reason is that the prophecy of the Old Testament figuring the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} as future would be more eminent than our sacrament, which has [284] the bare efficacy of signifying the body of Christ as past;[^18] which, since it is not a prophecy but a truth about the past, an [unlettered man]{g:ydiota} would know how to [show]{g:ostendere}. And the third reason rests on the decrees of the church which he [alleges]{g:allegare}.

## ¶6 · 284.5–284.14

> [p. 284] Quantum ad primum, patet quod racio militat (ut dixi) sepius contra ipsum, cum secta ista ponit quoad essenciam sacramentum illud vel unum nichil vel unum abiectissimum in natura quia accidens infinitum inperfeccius quam est panis et per consequens infinitum abieccius quam est manna quod de celo descendit; ideo nemo inveniet colorem efficacem ad illud nisi loquendo yronice dicatur quod est irreligiose propter devocionem populi menciendum (de qua materia dixi diffuse alibi).

As to the first, it is clear that the reason militates (as I have said) more often against him, since this sect lays down that [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}, as to its [essence]{g:essencia}, as either a nothing or a most abject thing in nature, because an accident [is] infinitely more imperfect than bread is, and consequently infinitely more abject than the manna which came down from heaven;[^19] therefore no one will find an effective [colour]{g:color} for that, unless, speaking [ironically]{g:yronice}, it be said that one must irreligiously lie for the devotion of the people (on which matter I have spoken at length elsewhere).[^20]

## ¶7 · 284.15–284.31

> [p. 284] Quoad secundum patet quod false assumitur professio Beringarii, cum decretum de ipso recitat quod confessus fuit secundum informacionem ecclesie, quod panis est post consecracionem nedum sacramentum, ut in errore suo confessus est, sed verum corpus Christi, hoc est figurat efficaciter, ut sacramentum nove legis, verum corpus Christi sacramentaliter ibi presens. Nec plus dicit adversarius; unde creditur Lanfrancum arguere contra eum pro dictis suis hereticis ante suam correccionem. Sed quid hoc ad nos? vel quod metu et non fide Beringarius ista dixit in sinodo? et sic sacramentum eukaristie non solum dicit veritatem corporis Christi de preterito sed facit quod verum corpus Christi sit vere et realiter ibi presens; quod non fecerunt sacramenta legis veteris. Nec ad hoc pertinet desicio nature panis vel eius destruccio.

As to the second, it is clear that the profession of Berengar is falsely taken up, since the decree about him [recites]{g:recitare} that he confessed, according to the instruction of the church, that "the bread is after consecration not only the sacrament", as he had confessed in his error, "but the true body of Christ", that is, it [figures]{g:figurare} effectively, as a sacrament of the new law, the true body of Christ [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} present there.[^21] Nor does the adversary say more; hence Lanfranc is believed to argue against him for his heretical sayings before his correction.[^22] But what is this to us? Or that Berengar said these things in the synod out of fear and not faith? And so the sacrament of the Eucharist not only [signifies]{g:dicere} the truth of the body of Christ as past, but makes the true body of Christ to be [truly]{g:vere} and [really]{g:realiter} present there; which the sacraments of the old law did not do.[^23] Nor does the [ceasing]{g:desicio} of the nature of the bread, or its destruction, pertain to this.

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 280.17: It is said there must be authority somewhere to expound Scripture.
- 281.2: If we have no competent prelates we had best keep to the literal sense of Scripture.
- 281.18: Augustine's ground against the Donatists was that rites ought not to be imposed without scriptural authority.
- 281.31: 3. We are confronted with doctors of more weight than we.
- 282.1: Creatures can be but witnesses, not lords of truth. They must rest on Scripture and must know what they witness.
- 283.5: There are many doubtful things which we need not care about.
- 283.10: The glosses contradict each other so much that they count for nothing.
- 283.17: Of doctors of law the chief is Lanfranc, but he never really meets the question.
- 284.5: He says, we honour the host insufficiently, but our opponents degrade it to a mere accident.
- 284.15: On the point of Berengarius' confession, he does not touch us, for his attack is against the error renounced.

## Notes

[^1]: **S/T.** Eph. 4:11, "pastors and doctors" (DR), alluded to, not quoted (Loserth's reference at 280.22). The print's *concludit* is the reading A has in the margin in another hand; A's text and BDE read *coniungit*, "joins" (the Apostle joins pastors and doctors in one office), which fits the sense better. The print is translated and the reading flagged.
[^2]: **C.** *In pastu*: in feeding, the pastor's work (*pastores*). *Ut capita ydolorum*: taken as "as to heads of idols", heads who are idols themselves; the phrase could also be "as to the heads of idols", heads of an idolatrous people. DE read *non fide* (281.3).
[^3]: **C.** *Sentencia*: the position taught, "teaching", the first sense of the CONTEXTUAL row; so throughout the chunk.
[^4]: **C.** *Sensus misticus / litteralis secundarius / rudis*: the senses of scripture keep "sense" (GLOSSARY *sensus*); the meaning of words is "meaning".
[^5]: **C/T.** *De corpore isto vel alio accidente ignoto*: "this body" is taken as the quantified body the opponents posit in the host, which *alio accidente* counts among accidents; if *alio* is loose, it is the body of Christ. AB lack *eucaristiam* (281.9); C adds it in another hand.
[^6]: **S/T.** Augustine against rebaptism; the argument is not found in these words in *De baptismo contra Donatistas*, which at V.23.31 grants that the apostles commanded nothing on it and rests on custom (LEDGER K09c-1). DE read *precepit* (281.19); ABCD *sed nec* (281.21).
[^7]: **C/T.** *Occasione male data ab emulis*: the rivals (the Donatists) gave the occasion; the saints went beyond the expressed faith of the primitive church only under that pressure. AC read *sollicitarent*; E lacks *sic* (281.25).
[^8]: **T.** ABC read *contraria* (281.32); ABD *supra* for *super*; B lacks *plus* (281.33).
[^9]: **S.** John 18:37 (DR "for this came I into the world; that I should give testimony to the truth"), in Wyclif's own form, not quoted. *In tantum quod*: "so much so that": even Christ came as man to bear witness.
[^10]: **S.** Loserth: Augustine, *Contra Faustum* XIII.5, and *De baptismo contra Donatistas* II.3; both set canonical scripture above all later writing (PL 42, 282–283; PL 43, 128), neither in the words *omnis veritas sit in scriptura* (LEDGER K09c-2).
[^11]: **C/T.** *Poleticos*: the writers on politics (ADE *polites*, "citizens"); whom Wyclif means is not said. AB read *facti hic materia*, C *humani* over an erasure (282.12); ABDE lack *eius* (282.11).
[^12]: **T/R.** The print's *distincte*: ABDE read *disiuncte*, "disjunctively", C *distincte* over an erasure; ABC *vel quod*. Which law of England is meant the Latin does not say (LEDGER K09c-3).
[^13]: **C/T.** *Evidencia topica*: probable, not demonstrative, grounds (GLOSSARY, with *argumentum topicum*): in doubtful matters the doctors are believed as on probable evidence. ABC read *tropica*; E *revelacionem* for *inspiracionem*.
[^14]: **C/T.** The subject of *docet* is scripture. *Potest ipsum permittere per se esse*: the subject is unexpressed, taken as God; *per se esse*, "to be of itself", that is, without a subject (GLOSSARY *per se*). AB lack *potest* (282.28); C adds it in another hand. *Novem generibus accidentis*: the nine categories besides substance.
[^15]: **C.** *Quia usque ad indebitos cultores*: *quia* does not construe as "because"; taken as "that is", with a verb of time understood, and flagged. *Indebiti cultores*: those who worship [the sacrament] unduly (GLOSSARY *cultor*).
[^16]: **C/T.** *Perplexitas … datam a nostris novellis doctoribus*: the tangle the newer doctors brought into the matter. *Argumentum ad hominem*: binding only on one who grants the glossators. D reads *sensum hereticos*, E *sensus hereticos* (283.6); E *soluciones periti* (283.7).
[^17]: **S/C.** Lanfranc, *De corpore et sanguine Domini adversus Berengarium*; Loserth: "Lanfranci Opp. ed. Giles II, 163" (LEDGER K09c-4). *Dicit primus*: "the first who speaks"; the Latin is abrupt (perhaps for *dicitur*, "is said to be the first"), and flagged. D reads *desinit* (283.22), *invocacione* (283.24).
[^18]: **C.** *Sollempnius*: "more eminent", the medieval sense of *sollempnis* (GLOSSARY *sollempnior*). *De futuro / de preterito*: "as future / as past", the time of the thing figured.
[^19]: **C/T.** *Contra ipsum*: Lanfranc's first argument is turned on those who use it: it is their accident that is less worthy than the manna (side-note 284.5). D reads *Quia racio* (284.5); B *unum adiectum* (284.8).
[^20]: **C.** *Color* (GLOSSARY): colour, a plausible ground. *Irreligiose … menciendum*: the ironic conclusion Wyclif imputes; "elsewhere" is not specified.
[^21]: **S/C.** *Ego Berengarius* (*De cons.* D. 2 c. 42), in Wyclif's short form; the canon reads *non solum sacramentum, sed etiam uerum corpus et sanguinem Domini nostri Iesu Christi*, which Wyclif's *nedum … sed verum corpus Christi* paraphrases (LEDGER K09c-5). *Informacio*: "instruction", the church's teaching of Berengar, not GLOSSARY's *informacio* of a form in its subject. *Hoc est figurat efficaciter* is Wyclif's exposition of *verum corpus*.
[^22]: **C/S.** *Nec plus dicit adversarius*: taken as "the adversary" of the moderns, Wyclif's side, which says no more than the corrected confession; it may instead be the opponent Wyclif answers. Lanfranc wrote against Berengar's sayings before the correction (side-note 284.15). ABC read *ut plus* (284.22). Loserth compares Wyclif, *De apostasia*, p. 164 (LEDGER K09c-6).
[^23]: **C/T.** *Dicit*: "signifies" (GLOSSARY *dicere*, logical use). *Vere et realiter*: "truly and really", two mode adverbs kept apart. E lacks *sic* (284.26); AB *non solum non*; C has *fecerunt* in another hand (284.29).
