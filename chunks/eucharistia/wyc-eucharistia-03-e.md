# *De eucharistia*, chapter 3, part 5: the plea of miracle, and the witness of scripture, reason and the saints

## ¶1 · 69.22–71.3

> [p. 69] Nec valet allegare in isto miracula ad colorandum quamcunque opinionem contrariam veritati; non enim fundatur fictum miraculum. Secundo quia sic accidens factum subiectum transsubstanciacionis crearet in casu et anichilaret et tamen cum sit res inanimata, non habens substanciam que sustentaret eius mutacionem, nulla una permanens qualitas posset pati vel agere. Assumptum sic arguitur: Posito quod calor per se agat in sacramento, quousque fiat alimentum [p. 70] et redeat eadem materia, patet quod diu erit antequam redibit materia panis (ut concedunt adversarii), et tota qualitas que iterum corrumperetur anichilabitur, sicut qualitas que interim producetur creabitur, cum non sit materia que suscipiat illam transmutacionem naturalem, sic quod qualitas non corrumpetur in potencia materie, sed corrumpetur in nichilum vel totaliter producitur ex nichilo, sicut agens naturale suscitat priorem materiam panis ex nichilo. Talis enim substancia non generatur ex accidente, ut patet quod, licet ecclesia sollempnizet hec signa supra substancias, sicut transfert ad contraria nomina ministrorum ecclesie, manente tamen disposicione quam Deus instituit vel variancia, non oportet quod natura rei proporcionaliter consequatur, ut posito quod anima vel quecunque forma materialis inmediate actuet huiusmodi accidens absolutum per se servatum foret substancia composita ut est modo et vel nulla creatura potest per se esse sine miraculo vel vocatum accidens potest per se esse sine novo miraculo vel fingi posset per idem miraculum. Unde tolleretur omnis descripcio creature. Sic enim fingunt sollempnes doctores quod generato verme de hostia anima sensitiva induceretur in accidencia sine materia; quare ergo non consumeretur ex tibi dubio tota materialis substancia facta generacione et motu inter talia accidencia, ymmo cum materia individuatur ab instanti sue creacionis, videtur quod nova creatur et non sic inevitabiliter antiqua sustentatur et sic quot substancie convertuntur in corpus Christi tot materie prime sunt perdite [p. 71] et nemo nostrum cognosceret huiusmodi materialem substanciam. Quomodo ergo auderet quisquam conficere vel iurare de actu materialis substancie.

Nor does it avail to [allege]{g:allegare} miracles in this matter to [give colour to]{g:colorare} any [opinion]{g:opinio} contrary to the truth; for a feigned miracle is not grounded. Second, because in this way an [accident]{g:accidens} made the subject of [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio} would, in a given case, create and [annihilate]{g:anichilare}; and yet, since it is an inanimate thing, not having a [substance]{g:substancia} to sustain its change, no one permanent [quality]{g:qualitas} could be acted on or act. [The assumption]{g:assumptum} is argued thus. Suppose that heat acts [of itself]{g:per-se} in the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} until it becomes nourishment[^1] [70] and the same matter returns: it is clear that it will be long before the matter of the [bread]{g:panis} returns (as the adversaries grant), and the whole quality that would in the meantime be corrupted will be annihilated, just as the quality that will be produced in the meantime will be created, since there is no matter to receive that natural transmutation; so that the quality will not be corrupted into the potency of matter, but will be corrupted into nothing, or is produced wholly from nothing, just as the natural agent raises up the former matter of the bread from nothing. For such a substance is not generated from an accident; as is clear, because although the [church]{g:ecclesia} solemnizes these signs over substances, just as it transfers the names of the ministers of the church to their contraries,[^2] yet, whether the disposition which God instituted remains or varies,[^3] the nature of the thing need not follow in proportion. So, supposing that a soul or any material form immediately actuated such an [absolute accident]{g:accidens-absolutum} kept of itself, it would be a composite substance as it is now; and either no [creature]{g:creatura} can be of itself without a miracle, or the [so-called]{g:vocatus} accident can be of itself without a new miracle, or [anything] could be feigned by the same miracle. Hence every [definition]{g:descripcio} of a creature would be taken away. For in this way solemn doctors [feign]{g:fingere} that, when a worm is generated from the [host]{g:hostia}, a sensitive soul would be induced into the accidents without matter.[^4] Why then, [for all you know]{g:ex-tibi-dubio},[^5] would not the whole material substance be consumed, generation and motion having taken place among such accidents? Indeed, since matter is individuated from the instant of its creation, it seems that a new [matter] is created and the old is not so inevitably sustained; and so, as many substances as are converted into [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}, so many [prime matters]{g:materia-prima} are lost, [71] and none of us would know such material substance. How then would anyone dare to [consecrate]{g:conficere} or to swear about the act of material substance?

## ¶2 · 71.4–71.14

> [p. 71] Item, ecclesia non est oneranda novitate tali insolita nisi vel fides scripture vel vivax racio vel efficax sanctorum testimonium ad hoc cogat, sed quodlibet istorum trium deficit predicto errori de sacramento altaris, ergo et racio sic onerandi ecclesiam. Assumptum patet ex multis testimoniis sanctorum et veritate ac sufficiencia legis Christi. Sic enim coleretur abhominacio desolacionis, quia accidens entitatis minime, tamquam Deus, vel saltem coleretur tamquam sacrum signum mendacissime accidens loco substancie et imponeretur Deo quod sit auctor mendacii.

Again, the church is not to be burdened with such an unaccustomed novelty unless either the faith of scripture, or living reason, or the effective testimony of [the saints]{g:sancti} compels it to this; now each of these three is lacking to the aforesaid error about [the sacrament of the altar]{g:sacramentum-altaris}; therefore so is the reason for so burdening the church. The assumption is clear from many testimonies of the saints, and from the truth and sufficiency of [the law of Christ]{g:lex-christi}. For in this way "the abomination of desolation" would be worshipped,[^6] since an accident of the least entity [would be worshipped] as God; or at least an accident would be worshipped, most mendaciously, as a [sacred]{g:sacer} sign in place of substance, and it would be imputed to God that he is the author of a lie.

## ¶3 · 71.15–71.19

> [p. 71] Quantum ad scripturam, patet in sex locis predictis de hoc sacramento tangentibus quod nec ponunt accidencia sic esse sine subiecto, nec antecedens ad hoc. Et idem est iudicium de qualibet parte scripture in qua est omnis veritas.

As to scripture, it is clear in the six places mentioned before that touch on this sacrament[^7] that they neither posit accidents to be so without a subject, nor an [antecedent]{g:antecedens} to this. And the same judgement holds of every part of scripture, in which is all truth.

## ¶4 · 71.20–71.25

> [p. 71] Quantum ad racionem, patet luce clarius quod aliena est a fundacione existencie huiusmodi accidencium sine subiecto, ymmo ut loyci instant, inpossibile est accidens esse nisi unum accidens subiectet aliud; et cum quelibet creatura sit accidens deitati, patet quod oportet esse accidens sine subiecto suo cui inhereat.

As to reason, it is clear, clearer than light, that it is foreign to founding the existence of such accidents without a subject; indeed, as the logicians object, it is impossible for an accident to be unless one accident is the subject of another; and since every creature is an accident to the [Godhead]{g:deitas}, it is clear that there must be an accident without its subject in which it inheres.[^8]

## ¶5 · 71.26–71.30

> [p. 71] Et quantum ad testimonium doctorum, patet quod antiqui sancti testati sunt concorditer quod accidens non potest esse sine subiecto cui inhereat; tamen suffecit eis sicut philosophis ponere accidens quod est substanciam esse accidentatam.

And as to the testimony of the doctors, it is clear that the ancient saints testified with one accord that an accident cannot be without a subject in which it inheres; yet it sufficed them, as it did the philosophers, to posit an accident which is the substance's being [subject to accidents]{g:accidentatus}.

## ¶6 · 71.31–72.2

> [p. 71] Et quantum ad decretalem, patet quod non est forcior quam decretum pape et CXIV episcoporum [p. 72] qui diffinierunt panem et vinum remanere post consecracionem (ut sepe recitatum est).

And as to the [decretal]{g:decretalis}, it is clear that it is not stronger than the [decree]{g:decretum} of the pope and 114 bishops [72] who defined that bread and wine remain after the consecration (as has often been [recited]{g:recitare}).[^9]

## ¶7 · 72.3–72.10

> [p. 72] Unde quantum ad testimonia sanctorum, reduco ex multis triplex testimonium Augustini;. primo illud dictum de Quantitate Anime capitulo IVº, ubi dicit quod corpus non potest esse sine altitudine; prorsus, inquit, non dubito corpora omnia longitudine, latitudine et altitudine carere non posse, sed numquid potest cogitari ista tria non esse nisi in corporibus? Non intelligo, inquit, quomodo alibi esse possint.

Hence, as to the testimonies of the saints, I bring forward, out of many, a threefold testimony of Augustine. First, that saying in *De quantitate animae*, chapter 4, where he says that a body cannot be without height: "I do not at all doubt," he says, "that all bodies cannot lack length, breadth and height; but can it be thought that these three are not, except in bodies? I do not understand," he says, "how they could be elsewhere."[^10]

## ¶8 · 72.11–72.27

> [p. 72] Et quoad qualitatem scribit IIº Soliloquiorum XIIº capitulo: Esse aliquid in aliquo nos non fugit duobus modis dici. Uno modo localiter ut corpus seiungi atque alibi esse possit, ut hoc lignum in hoc loco et sol in oriente. Altero autem quo ita est aliquid in subiecto, ut ab eo nequeat separari, ut in hoc ligno forma et species quam videmus, ut in sole lux, ut in igne calor et in anima disciplina et si qua sunt alia. Ista, inquit, vetustissima nobis sunt et ab ineunte adolescencia studiosissime percepta et cognita, quia non possum de hiis interrogatus, quin ea sine ulla deliberacione concedam. Et sequitur: Illud vero quod interrogasti, quis concesserit aut cui posse fieri, videatur ut id quod est in subiecto maneat ipso intereunte subiecto? Monstruosum enim et a veritate alienissimum ut id quod non esset nisi in ipso esset, eciam cum ipsum non fuerit possit esse.

And as to quality, he writes in *Soliloquies* II, chapter 12:[^11] "It does not escape us that something is said to be in something in two ways. In one way locally, so that it can be separated and be elsewhere, as this wood in this place, and the sun in the east. In the other, by which something is so in a subject that it cannot be separated from it, as in this wood the form and appearance which we see, as in the sun light, as in fire heat, and in the soul learning, and whatever others there are. These things," he says, "are very old to us, and from early youth most studiously grasped and known, because I cannot, being asked about them, fail to grant them without any deliberation."[^12] And [it follows]{g:sequitur}: "But as to what you asked, who would grant, or to whom would it seem possible, that what is in a subject should remain when the subject itself perishes? For it is monstrous and most foreign to the truth that that which would not be unless it were in it could be even when it itself is not."

## ¶9 · 73.1–73.18

> [p. 73] Et tercio Epistola XL ad Dardanum sic loquitur Augustinus: Tolle ergo ipsa corpora qualitatibus, qualitates corporum nec erit ubi sint, et ideo necesse est ut non sint. Et idem sine retractacione sepe sentenciat, ut patet De Trinitate IXº et XIIIº, VIº De Trinitate VIº et IXº, De Trinitate IIIº et in De Moribus Ecclesie capitulo VIº. Nec valet glosare dicta sancti quod intelligit sic esse nisi fiat miraculum, tum quia nullo existente miraculo non foret aliqua creatura, tum eciam quia nemo potest mereri vel beatificari sine miraculo, ymmo maneret ubique incertitudo, quando et ubi foret miraculum, et per consequens periret certitudo de quacunque materiali substancia et sic naturalis philosophia, tum tercio quia non magis probabiliter fingitur miraculum in hoc venerabili sacramento, quam fingi posset quidquid volueris; et per istam glosam tolleretur consequenter quodlibet sanctorum testimonium vel scripture.

[73] And third, in Epistle 40, to Dardanus, Augustine speaks thus: "Take away, then, the bodies themselves from the qualities: the qualities of bodies will have nowhere to be, and therefore it is necessary that they not be."[^13] And he often [pronounces]{g:sentenciare} the same without retraction, as is clear in *De Trinitate* 9 and 13, *De Trinitate* 6, [chapters] 6 and 9, *De Trinitate* 3, and in *De moribus ecclesiae*, chapter 6.[^14] Nor does it avail to [gloss]{g:glosare} the saint's sayings [to mean] that he understands it to be so unless a miracle happens: first, because if no miracle existed there would not be any creature; second, also because no one can merit or be beatified without a miracle; indeed uncertainty would remain everywhere as to when and where there would be a miracle, and consequently certainty about any material substance would perish, and so would natural philosophy; third, because a miracle is not feigned [with more probability]{g:probabiliter} in this venerable sacrament than whatever you like could be feigned; and by this [gloss]{g:glosa} every testimony of the saints, or of scripture, would consequently be taken away.

## Notes

[^1]: **T.** *Alimentum* (69.30) is the print, after A; BCDE read *alienatum*, "until it is altered". Heat acting on the species until they nourish is the case of the host digested.
[^2]: **C.** *Transfert ad contraria nomina ministrorum ecclesie*: the church gives the names of its ministers to men who are their contraries; a name given in solemn use does not make the thing so. Loserth's side-note reads the passage as a matter of language, not of change in things.
[^3]: **C.** *Manente ... disposicione ... vel variancia* (70.13–14) is taken as "whether the disposition remains or varies"; ABCE read *variari*, which gives the same sense. C corrected *diffinicione* (D, A, B) to *disposicione*.
[^4]: **S.** The worm generated from the consecrated species is a case put in Thomas, *ST* III q. 77 a. 5 (LEDGER K03e-1, to be checked). The Latin names only "solemn doctors".
[^5]: **C.** *Ex tibi dubio*: "for all you know", as *ex sibi dubio* (66.15). D has *ex tali* in the margin.
[^6]: **S.** "The abomination of desolation": Matt. 24:15, Dan. 9:27 (Douay). Wyclif uses the phrase without the rest of the verse.
[^7]: **C.** The six scriptural places on the sacrament are those set out earlier in the treatise (LEDGER K03e-2, to be checked: which six).
[^8]: **C.** The construal of the last clause is doubtful: it is taken as the logicians' own consequence, which turns their objection against them (every creature is an accident to God, and God is no subject in which it inheres). It could also be Wyclif's reduction of their position.
[^9]: **S.** The decretal is probably *Firmiter credimus* (Lateran IV, *X* 1.1.1, our modern form), and the decree of the pope with the bishops *Ego Berengarius* (*De cons.* D. 2 c. 42), where Gratian has Nicholas II with 113 bishops (LEDGER K03e-3, K03e-4, to be checked). Wyclif's count is 114.
[^10]: **S.** Augustine, *De quantitate animae* c. 4, Loserth's reference *Opp.* I, p. 404 (LEDGER K03e-5).
[^11]: **S/T.** Augustine, *Soliloquia* II c. 12, Loserth's reference *Opp.* I, p. 378; the manuscripts read *XIII*, corrected by Loserth to *XII*. The second quotation (*Et sequitur*) he places in c. 13 (LEDGER K03e-6, K03e-7).
[^12]: **T.** *Quia non possum* (72.21) is the print, translated; Loserth notes *recte: quare non possum*, "wherefore I cannot", which is Augustine's text as he gives it.
[^13]: **S.** Augustine to Dardanus, Wyclif's Epistle 40; Loserth's reference *Opp.* II, p. 682. It is Ep. 187 in the modern numbering (LEDGER K03e-8, to be checked).
[^14]: **S/T.** The *De Trinitate* references are as printed and partly garbled (ABC *De Trinitate et IX*; E *et VI Trinitate*); Loserth refers to *Opp.* VIII, p. 833 ff., and for *De moribus ecclesiae* c. 6 to *Opp.* I, pp. 690–691 (LEDGER K03e-9, K03e-10).
