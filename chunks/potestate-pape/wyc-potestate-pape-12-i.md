# *De potestate pape*, chapter 12, part 9: causes, benefices and the dangers of papal provision

## ¶1 · 345.22–347.25

> [p. 345] Ex quo patet ulterius, quod sit a fidelibus tamquam hereticum abrogandum, et per consequens papa non debet pro toto mundo secundum ipsum generaliter causas ecclesiasticas terminare. Et idem est argumentum de generalibus legum paparum principiis, et specialiter de maximis Bonifacii VIII in Sexto, que manifeste obviant legi Dei. Non enim preferunt nec sciunt preferre quem Deus preelegit, sed communiter preeligunt maximum Antichristum, quia cum Judas non dormit avaricia debriatus sepe contingit quod membrum dyaboli preferatur; calliditas [p. 346] enim vulpina sepe preficit minus dignos. Unde apostoli non audebant nec potuerunt sicut nec Christus talem legem statuere. Ideo ista vocata lex est Antichristi, qui extollitur super omne quod dicitur Deus. Christus enim non potest ex nuda prelacione momenti temporis, ex callida presentacione priori collacionis papalis nec ex iterata eleccione sepe frivola capituli acceptantis, ineptum virtute preponere, sed oportet omnino, quod ille quem Christus vel lex sua preeligit, sit quoad Deum virtute prestancior. Et hinc apostoli non presumpserunt vel legem eleccionis statuere vel Mathiam eligere, sed commiserunt hec divino iudicio, ut patet Act. I. Sic autem faceret papa, cum pro toto mundo non statueret legem iniustam vel committeret peccatum eciam veniale. Est autem maior blasfemia constanter asserrere quod eo ipso quo Papa hoc statuit eciam contra decretum Domini, tunc est iustum, vel quod oportet quod Deus condescendat pape et legem quamcumque pape iustificet. Vel tercio quod levem iniuriam homo debet committere propter maius commodum inde sequens. Cum igitur quoad omnes causas terminandas per se sufficit lex scripture, quomodo presumeret Christi vicarius legem assumere tam iniquam quoad iniurias, commoveatur utraque pars pati iniurias et propter periculum tempus sibi redimere. Hoc autem ex fide est melius quam iudicium quod sentenciat lex humana, quod si neutra pars voluerit decreto Domini in talibus humiliter consentire, compromittant ponendo se in arbitrio boni viri vel ad minimum iudicentur secundum leges civiles intra ecclesiam, vel si omnia ista renuerint, fugiantur tamquam ethnici et publicani [p. 347] et discipuli Antichristi. Et nullomodo trahat Christi vicarius huiusmodi causas sibi, sed effugiat ut venenum iuxta exemplaria legum civilium condere novas leges, sed abnuentem finaliter hanc viam triplicem, excommunicet ut membrum dyaboli. Excommunicanten im quandoque simplices; quia dissentire renuunt legi. Et patet quomodo preter papam oportet esse, sicut fuit in primitiva ecclesia, unum caput ad causas ecclesiasticas terminandum. Quoad secundum patet quod oportet esse unum caput ad beneficia ecclesiastica parciendum, nam lex Christi est ad illud sufficiens, et persona populi, cui preficeretur talis prepositus, foret optimus iudex ad discernendum talem prepositum episcopo presentandum. Sic enim fuit in primitiva ecclesia; nec cessat racio, quare non sic foret hodie. Nam illa persona tenerrime provideret de mediis ad salutem anime necessariis cuiusmodi est pastor discretus; si enim providet de mediis corporis nutritivis, fides necessitat quod infinitum plus provideat de mediis anime nutritivis. Ideo Ecclesiastici XXX, 24 dicitur: Miserere anime tue placens Deo. Istam igitur providenciam non est Christi et per consequens nullius creature vetare.

From this it is further clear that it is to be abrogated by the faithful as heretical, and consequently the [pope]{g:papa} ought not to determine ecclesiastical causes generally for the whole world according to it. And the argument is the same about the general principles of the popes' laws, and especially about the maxims of Boniface VIII in the Sext, which plainly run counter to God's law. For they do not prefer, nor know how to prefer, the one whom God has chosen beforehand, but commonly choose beforehand the greatest Antichrist; for since Judas, drunk with avarice, does not sleep, it often happens that a [member]{g:membrum} of the devil is preferred; for foxlike cunning [346] often sets the less worthy over [others]. Hence the apostles did not dare and were not able, just as Christ was not, to establish such a law. Therefore this [so-called]{g:vocatus} law is Antichrist's, who "is lifted up above all that is called God".[^1] For Christ cannot, on the bare priority of a moment of time, on a crafty earlier presentation of the papal [conferral]{g:collacio}, or on the repeated and often frivolous election of the accepting chapter, set over [others] one unfit in [virtue]{g:virtus}; but it must altogether be that the one whom Christ or his law chooses beforehand is more excellent in virtue as regards God. And hence the apostles did not presume either to establish a law of election or to elect Matthias, but committed these things to divine judgment, as is clear from Acts 1.[^2] And so the pope would do, since for the whole world he would not establish an unjust law or commit even a venial sin.[^3] And it is a greater blasphemy to assert steadfastly that by the very fact that the pope has established this, even against the decree of the Lord, it is then just; or that God must condescend to the pope and justify any law of the pope whatever; or, third, that a man ought to commit a slight injury for the sake of a greater advantage following from it. Since, therefore, the law of scripture by itself suffices for determining all causes, how would the [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius-christi} presume to take up a law so wicked? As to injuries, let each party be moved to suffer injuries and, because of the danger, to redeem the time for itself.[^4] And this, [as faith teaches]{g:ex-fide}, is better than the judgment which human law [pronounces]{g:sentenciare}; but if neither party is willing humbly to consent to the Lord's decree in such matters, let them compromise by putting themselves in the arbitration of a good man, or at least be judged according to the civil laws within the [church]{g:ecclesia}; or if they refuse all these, let them be shunned as heathens and publicans [347] and disciples of Antichrist.[^5] And let the vicar of Christ in no way draw causes of this kind to himself, but let him flee like poison the making of new laws after the patterns of the civil laws; but let him excommunicate, as a member of the devil, the one who finally refuses this threefold way. For they sometimes excommunicate simple people, because they refuse to dissent from the law.[^6] And it is clear how, apart from the pope, there must be, as there was in the primitive church, one [head]{g:caput} for determining ecclesiastical causes.[^7] As to the second, it is clear that there must be one head for distributing [ecclesiastical benefices]{g:beneficium-ecclesiasticum}, for the law of Christ is sufficient for that; and the person of the people over whom such a [superior]{g:prepositi} would be set would be the best judge for discerning such a superior to be presented to the [bishop]{g:episcopus}.[^8] For so it was in the primitive church; nor has the reason ceased why it should not be so today.[^9] For that person would provide most tenderly for the means necessary to the salvation of the soul, of which kind is a discerning shepherd; for if it provides for the means that nourish the body, faith requires that it provide infinitely more for the means that nourish the soul. Therefore it is said in Ecclesiasticus 30:24: "Have pity on thy own soul, pleasing God." It belongs, therefore, neither to Christ, nor consequently to any creature, to forbid this providing.

## ¶2 · 347.26–347.31

> [p. 347] Quantum ad legem, patet quod sola lex Christi perfecte docet pastoris officium, quomodo introducendus est, quomodo nutriendus et quomodo castigandus; illam igitur legem oportet communitatem christianorum defendere et tradiciones contrarias impugnare.

As to the law, it is clear that the law of Christ alone teaches perfectly the office of a shepherd: how he is to be brought in, how nourished and how corrected; therefore the community of Christians must defend that law and attack the traditions contrary to it.

## ¶3 · 347.32–348.26

> [p. 347] Sunt igitur tam ex parte electorum quam electi pericula. Ex parte electoris in hoc, quod posset blasfeme presumere quod sufficit voluntas [p. 348] sua, cum signo ab eo accepto ad rite constituendum prelatum ecclesie; et sic foret personaliter Ihesus Christus. Oportet enim Deum prius eligendo et habilitando constituere, cuius ordinacioni concordans sagax instrumentum Dei potest esse nudus promulgator divine sentencie, et ignorando divinam sentenciam reticeret, sicut fecerunt apostoli in eleccione Matthie. Si enim contrariatur divine sentencie, tunc peccat; nec debet sibi in hoc credi nec a fidelibus consentiri, quia aliter posset papa esse cum sibi adherentibus, infra modicum tempus maximus Antichristus, quia proditorie contra Christum dissipans oves suas; hoc autem est signum potissimum Antichristi. Item, est valde possibile quod papa ignoret vel negligat personam ydoneam gregi fideli postponere, sed nimis periculosum et stultum foret cum hoc preficere prelatum ecclesie, igitur in illo officio adiacet pape magnum periculum. Cum igitur in omni tali pars securior est tenenda videtur quod securius et optacius foret illud dimittere et per consequens papa peccaret in eligendo partem tam periculosam atque ambiguam. Nam iuxta legem pape in De Consecracione distinccione V: Non mediocriter peccat, qui maiori bono postposito eligit minus bonum.

There are therefore dangers both on the side of the electors and on that of the one elected. On the side of the elector in this, that he could blasphemously presume that his will suffices, [348] with a sign received from him, for duly constituting a [prelate]{g:prelatus} of the church; and so he would be Jesus Christ in person.[^10] For God must first constitute [him] by electing and [qualifying]{g:habilitare} [him]; and, agreeing with his [ordinance]{g:ordinacio}, a shrewd instrument of God can be the bare promulgator of the divine [sentence]{g:sentencia}, and, not knowing the divine sentence, would keep silent, as the apostles did in the election of Matthias.[^11] For if he goes against the divine sentence, then he sins; and he ought not to be believed in this, nor consented to by the faithful, since otherwise the pope, with those who adhere to him, could within a short time be the greatest Antichrist, as treacherously scattering against Christ his sheep; and this is the [foremost]{g:potissimus} sign of Antichrist. Again, it is very possible that the pope, not knowing or neglecting [him], passes over a person suitable for the faithful flock; now it would be [exceedingly]{g:nimis} dangerous and foolish, with this [possibility], to set a prelate over the church; therefore in that office great danger lies upon the pope.[^12] Since, therefore, in every such case the safer part is to be held, it seems that it would be safer and more desirable to give that up, and consequently the pope would sin in choosing a part so dangerous and doubtful. For according to the pope's law in *De consecratione*, distinction 5: "He sins not a little who, setting aside the greater good, chooses the less good."[^13]

## ¶4 · 348.27–349.6

> [p. 348] Assumptum patet in facto de distancia personarum inexpertarum quas ex nudo verbo testium eorum sepe preficit, et ex hoc quod ipse ac examinatores sui ignorant communiter mores persone extranee, et quoad scienciam sepe preponderant artem litigandi civiliter arti curandi animas; quod est condicio Antichristi. Recoleret [p. 349] igitur quomodo omni facinori perversi prepositi quem stulte sic preficit est non solum consenciens sed ipsum capitaliter auctorizans; pape igitur sanctissimi istam dominativam formam preficiendi prepositos non temptarunt. Que igitur prudencia, quod infirmiores pape hoc facerent?

[The assumption]{g:assumptum} is clear in fact from the distance of untried persons whom he often sets over [others] on the bare word of their witnesses, and from this, that he and his examiners are commonly [ignorant of]{g:ignorare} the character of a stranger, and, as to knowledge, often weigh the art of litigating in civil law above the art of the cure of souls; which is the mark of Antichrist.[^14] Let him therefore recall [349] how to every [misdeed]{g:facinus} of a perverse superior whom he so foolishly sets over [them] he is not only consenting but authorizing it [as head]{g:capitaliter}; the most holy popes, therefore, did not attempt this lordly form of setting superiors over [others]. What prudence is it, then, that weaker popes should do this?[^15]

## ¶5 · 349.7–349.26

> [p. 349] Item, grave peccatum est propositis e duabus partibus minus bona eligere, sed hoc sepe contingit in preposituris papalibus, igitur sepius sunt inique. Quis ergo color excommunicandi omnes, qui resistunt pagine tam inique. Iste itaque error sepe devolvit profundius quod excommunicetur persona, quia facit ut debet conformiter legi Dei. Assumptum patet ex hoc, quod Deus vult et precipit quod homo eligat pociora, quia aliter excusaretur stulticia, ne sapiat peccatum et liceret voluntati Dei quamlibet deformari. Principium igitur nature est, quod homo eligat sibi utilius, ideo plane sequitur ex primo principio quod homo debet sic facere. Et hec racio quare fides scripture docet orare et petere condicionaliter media, de quibus ignoratur utrum sint magis utilia. Nec dubium quin Deo bono sit beneplacitum, quod homo laboret continue circa sibi utilius, quia aliter foret invidus.

Again, it is a grave sin, when two sides are put forward, to choose the less good; now this often happens in the papal [offices of superior]{g:prepositura}; therefore they are very often wicked.[^16] What [colour]{g:color}, then, is there for excommunicating all who resist so wicked a page?[^17] This error, then, often rolls down deeper, so that a person is excommunicated because he does as he ought, in conformity with God's law. The assumption is clear from this, that God wills and commands that a man choose the better things; since otherwise folly would be excused, so as not to savour of sin, and it would be lawful [for a man] to be deformed in any way whatever from the will of God.[^18] It is therefore a principle of nature that a man choose what is more useful to himself; so it plainly [follows]{g:sequitur} from the first principle that a man ought so to act. And this is the reason why the faith of scripture teaches us to pray and to ask conditionally for means of which it is not known whether they are more useful.[^19] Nor is there doubt that it is well pleasing to the good God that a man labour continually about what is more useful to him, since otherwise he would be envious.

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** 2 Thessalonians 2:4 (*super* for the Vulgate's *supra*). The "maxims" are the *regulae* of the rule on rival provisions quoted in 12-h (*Liber Sextus* 1.3.14).
[^2]: **S.** Acts 1:23–26: the apostles put forward two and committed the choice to the lot.
[^3]: **C.** *Sic autem faceret papa*: "so the pope would do", i.e. he would commit such matters to God's judgment; *cum … non statueret* gives the reason: the pope would not for the whole world make an unjust law.
[^4]: **C.** We begin a new sentence at *quoad iniurias*; the print runs it on from the question, which leaves *commoveatur* without a link. **S.** Ephesians 5:16, "redeeming the time".
[^5]: **S.** Matthew 18:17, "as the heathen and publican". **C.** *Quod si*, "but if": the second course, for parties unwilling to suffer injury, is arbitration or the civil laws.
[^6]: **C.** *Excommunicant enim*, as the sense requires (the print's *Excommuni-canten im* is a misprint). *Dissentire renuunt legi*: the simple refuse to dissent from God's law, and are excommunicated for it.
[^7]: **C.** The one head is taken as Christ: *preter papam*, "apart from the pope", as in the primitive church, which had no such pope. The next sentence supports this: the head for benefices needs no pope, "for the law of Christ is sufficient". Read without that sense, the sentence would assert a single human head, against the argument; a lost negative is possible (Latin doubt). **R.** Both manuscripts note in the margin (347.10): *Caput unum oportet esse ad distribuendum beneficia*.
[^8]: **C.** *Persona populi*: the people taken as one person (the community over whom the superior is set), the subject of *provideret* and *providet* below. *Prepositus*: here the parish superior, the pastor to be presented to the bishop.
[^9]: **C.** *Nec cessat racio, quare non sic foret hodie*: literally "nor does the reason cease why it would not be so today"; taken as "the reason for it still holds".
[^10]: **C.** *Cum signo ab eo accepto*: the sign (the letter or seal) received from the elector; the elector would act as though his will alone made the prelate, as only Christ can. **T.** B omits *Christus* (348.3).
[^11]: **G.** *Sentencia*: God's sentence, his decision on the person, not a position taught. **C.** *Reticeret*: if the instrument does not know God's sentence, it would keep silent rather than choose, as the apostles did over Matthias (Acts 1:26).
[^12]: **C.** *Quod papa ignoret vel negligat personam ydoneam gregi fideli postponere*: *ydoneam gregi fideli*, "suitable for the faithful flock"; *postponere*, "to put after, pass over", depends loosely on *ignoret vel negligat*. Literally "that the pope does not know or neglects to pass over a suitable person", which inverts the sense; we take *ignoret vel negligat* as giving the cause. *Preponere* would give "does not know how, or neglects, to set a suitable person over the faithful flock" (Latin doubt).
[^13]: **S.** *Decretum*, *De consecratione* D.5 c.24: Loserth gives "Cap. XXIV" (348.24); the modern form is ours (LEDGER P12i-1). He notes (348.25) that the canon's correct reading is *errant*, not *peccat*. **R.** Both manuscripts note in the margin (347.32): *Eleccio prelati habet pericula*.
[^14]: **C.** *In facto de distancia personarum inexpertarum*: the pope provides to persons far off and untried. **T.** The apparatus at 348.29 gives a manuscript reading for *testium* that cannot be made out in the OCR.
[^15]: **C.** *Recoleret*, imperfect subjunctive: "let him recall", of the pope. *Dominativam formam*: compare *potestas dominativa*, "lordly power".
[^16]: **C.** *Propositis e duabus partibus*: "when two sides are put forward" (ablative absolute). *Sepius*: "very often". **R.** Both manuscripts note in the margin (349.15): *Distribucio beneficiorum quid confert pape*.
[^17]: **G.** *Color*: a plausible ground (the period sense, as in "colour of title"). *Pagina*: the written law.
[^18]: **C.** *Ne sapiat peccatum*: folly would be excused "so that it would not savour of sin". *Liceret voluntati Dei quamlibet deformari*: *voluntati Dei* is taken as dative with *deformari*, "to be out of form with God's will". **R.** Both manuscripts note (349.31): *Eligere pociora homo vult Deus*.
[^19]: **G.** Asking *condicionaliter*: the conditional prayer, *oracio condicionata*.
