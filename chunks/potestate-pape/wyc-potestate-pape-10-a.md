# *De potestate pape*, chapter 10, part 1: the objection that the pope must be the mightiest lord

## ¶1 · 222.20–222.28

> [p. 222] Contra dicta arguitur per multa media tam raciones quam testimonia, et primo per deducens ad inconsequens probatur quod oportet papam esse dominum terre potissimum, quia aliter non sufficeret pro illo ministerio, sed subditi, eciam clerici, forent sibi inproporcionales quoad mundanam potenciam; ymo in possessione mundana vel securitate eius est occasio virtutis non vicii, et per consequens est racionabiliter appetenda.

Against what has been said [it is argued by appeal to]{g:arguitur-per} many middle terms, reasons as well as testimonies.[^1] And first, by an argument leading to what does not follow, it is proved that the [pope]{g:papa} must be the mightiest [lord]{g:dominus} of the earth: because otherwise he would not be sufficient for that ministry, but his subjects, even clerics, would be out of proportion to him as regards worldly [power]{g:potencia};[^2] indeed, in worldly possession, or in the security of it, there is occasion of virtue, not of vice, and consequently it is reasonably to be sought.

## ¶2 · 222.29–223.12

> [p. 222] In isto oportet ex fide credere summo medico Ihesu Christo, cuius est palatum oris mentis sanissimum, omnes autem infecti febribus, et [p. 223] cum alio genere morborum spiritualium, patimur discrasiam. Ideo sibi debemus credere, postposito nostro iudicio. Ipse autem verbis docuit et facto ac vita exposuit quod in diviciarum mundi affluencia est periculum et in earum parcitate ac remocione a seculo medicina, ideo reservavit illud regimen carioribus eius discipulis, ut patet Luce XVII et testatur Augustinus in De Ecclesiasticis Dogmatibus: Bonum est facultates cum dispensacione erogare pauperibus, sed melius est pro intencione sequendi Dominum semel donare, et absolutum sollicitudine egere cum Christo.

In this one must, [as faith teaches]{g:ex-fide}, believe the supreme physician Jesus Christ, whose palate of the mouth of the mind is most healthy; but all of us, infected with fevers and [223] with another kind of spiritual diseases, suffer distemper.[^3] Therefore we must believe him, setting our own judgement aside. Now he taught by words, and set forth by deed and life, that in the abundance of the world's riches there is danger, and in their scantiness and in withdrawal from the world a medicine; therefore he reserved that regimen for his dearer disciples, as is clear from Luke 17,[^4] and as Augustine testifies in *On Ecclesiastical Dogmas*: "It is good to pay out one's means to the poor with careful distribution; but it is better, with the intention of following the Lord, to give them all at once and, freed from care, to be in want with Christ."[^5]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 222.20: It is argued that the pope's secular greatness is needed for the protection of the Church.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Media*: the middle terms (premisses) of the arguments to come, divided into *raciones* (arguments from reason) and *testimonia* (authorities). Chapter 10 now gives the objections to chapter 9's account.
[^2]: **T/C.** *Per deducens ad inconsequens*: literally "by [an argument] leading to what does not follow", i.e. leading the opposite view to an untenable result; the usual form is *ad inconveniens* ("to an absurdity"), and *inconsequens* may be a slip, but the print stands. **C.** *Potencia* here is "power" (worldly might), not Aristotle's potency.
[^3]: **C.** *Discrasia* (dyscrasia) is the physicians' term for a bad mixture of the humours, "distemper"; the image is the healthy palate that alone judges taste rightly. *Autem* here sets "all of us" against Christ: "but".
[^4]: **S.** "Luke 17" is the print; the counsel to sell all and follow is Luke 18:22 (cf. Luke 14:33). We keep Wyclif's reference.
[^5]: **S.** Gennadius of Marseilles, *Liber de ecclesiasticis dogmatibus* 38 (71 in some editions), transmitted under Augustine's name; Loserth refers to the Maurist Augustine, vol. 8, appendix, p. 79, c. 38 (PL 58, 997, cap. 71: *Bonum est facultates cum dispensatione pauperibus erogare. Melius est pro intentione sequendi Dominum insimul donare, et absolutum sollicitudine cum Christo egere*; LEDGER P10a-1).
