## The thread

- **Name without a revival.** In English, Wyclif survives as a metonym: the "Morning Star of the
  Reformation" (Bale, Foxe), the man behind the English Bible. There was never a Wycliffite
  revival of his *ideas*: dominion, the Church, the Eucharist, realist metaphysics, logic.
  (Nuance: the Wycliffite Bible is largely his followers' work.)
- **The ideas were taken up in German.** Two motives:
  - the Protestant search for the "Vorgeschichte der Reformation" (Lechler, 1873);
  - the Bohemian question: Loserth, *Hus und Wiclif* (1884), showing Hus copied Wyclif, with a
    German-vs-Czech charge. Czech editors of Hus (Sedlák, Flajšhans, Novotný) answered.
    → link to the Hus companion, `reference/hus/`.
- **The Society came out of that.** The Wyclif Society (Furnivall, 1882) was spurred by the
  embarrassment that Wyclif's Latin was unprinted while a German-speaking scholar was proving
  Hus depended on it. Much of the editing was Central European: Loserth, Buddensieg, Beer,
  Dziewicki, beside Poole, Matthew, Pollard.
- **And the German scholarship was mostly not translated either.** Lechler only in Lorimer's
  adaptation (1878); Buddensieg, Loserth's *Studien* and the dissertation literature not at all
  (to check). Exception: *Hus und Wiclif* → Evans, *Wiclif and Hus* (1884).
- **Honest coda.** Twentieth-century English interest returned (Workman 1926, Leff, Kenny,
  Hudson, Thakkar), but the corpus itself stayed untranslated. That is the gap this edition fills.
