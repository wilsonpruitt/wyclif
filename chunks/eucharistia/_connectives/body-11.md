### 05-b ¶1 · 119.27–120.28  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 119] **Sed** super istos recentes sunt antiquiores et graviores persone que videntur inpugnare sensum scripture de locucione figurativa in verbis Domini, per que sacramentum istud conficitur. Scribit enim Magister Sentenciarum IVᵒ, distinccione Xᵃ in principio: Sunt [p. 120] item alii precedencium insaniam transcendentes, qui Dei virtutem iuxta modum naturalium rerum mecientes audacius et periculosius veritati contradicunt, asserentes in altari non esse corpus Christi vel sanguinem nec substanciam panis et vini in substanciam carnis et sanguinis converti, **sed** ita Christum dixisse: Hoc est corpus meum, sicut Apostolus dixit: Petra **autem** erat Christus. Dicunt enim corpus Christi ibi esse tantum in sacramento, id est in signo, et tantum in signo manducari a nobis. Qui errandi occasionem sumunt a verbis Veritatis Joh. VIᵒ. Et illud recitant ut heresim posteriores doctores, scribentes super Lumbardi sentencias et specialiter Thomas et Bonaventura. Cum ergo nos dogmatisamus eandem sentenciam, videtur quod incidimus in caribdim, et quia modicam evidenciam facit assercio istorum doctorum, attendendum est ad eorum evidenciam pro qua asserunt quod faciunt tam racio quam scriptura; decet enim Christum dominum nostrum ex maiori miraculo, pietate et gracia procedere in novo testamento quoad sacramentum finale in quo sit memoriale suorum mirabilium consumatum. **Sed** in veteri testamento fuit corpus Christi in manna et ceteris figuris ut signo; oportet ergo quod in isto sacramento sit humanitas Christi efficacius quam in signo, quia aliter laycus vel avis faceret in voce corpus Christi esse eque efficaciter in signo, ut est in hostia consecrata.

ENGLISH: But above these recent [doctors] there are older and weightier persons who seem to attack the [meaning]{g:sensus} of scripture concerning figurative speech in the words of the Lord, by which this [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} is [consecrated]{g:conficere}.[^1] For [the Master]{g:magister} of the Sentences writes in the fourth [book], distinction 10, at the beginning: "There are [120] also others, going beyond the madness of those before them, who, measuring the power of God after the manner of natural things, contradict the truth more boldly and more dangerously, asserting that [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} or the blood is not on the altar, nor is the [substance]{g:substancia} of [bread]{g:panis} and [wine]{g:vinum} converted into the substance of flesh and blood, but that Christ said 'This is my body' in the way the Apostle said 'and the rock was Christ'. For they say that the body of Christ is there only in the sacrament, that is, in the [sign]{g:signum}, and is eaten by us only in the sign. They take the occasion of erring from the words of the Truth in John 6."[^2] And the later doctors, writing on the Lombard's Sentences, and especially Thomas and Bonaventure, [recite]{g:recitare} that as a heresy.[^3] Since, therefore, we [teach as dogma]{g:dogmatisare} the same [teaching]{g:sentencia}, it seems that we fall into Charybdis; and since the assertion of these doctors makes little [evidence]{g:evidencia}, attention must be paid to their evidence, in favour of which, they assert, both reason and scripture tell:[^4] for it befits Christ our Lord to proceed in the new testament with greater miracle, [piety]{g:pietas} and grace as to the final sacrament, in which the [memorial]{g:memoriale} of his marvels is to be consummated. Now in the old testament the body of Christ was in the manna and the other [figures]{g:figura} as in a sign; therefore [the humanity]{g:humanitas} of Christ must be in this sacrament more efficaciously than in a sign, because otherwise a layman or a bird would, by its voice, make the body of Christ be in a sign equally efficaciously, as it is in the [consecrated host]{g:hostia}.[^5]

### 05-b ¶3 · 121.3–121.8  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 121] **Sed** patet ex dictis proximo capitulo quod ista evidencia non obviat nostre sentencie; omnis enim katholicus concordat cum heretico in quibusdam. Primo itaque dicendum est de veritate et diversitate nostre sentencie ab ista sentencia reprobata. Et secundo respondendum est obiectibus.

ENGLISH: But it is clear from what was said in the last chapter that this evidence does not [meet]{g:obviare} our teaching; for every catholic agrees with a heretic in some things. First, then, we must speak of the truth of our teaching and of its difference from this condemned teaching; and secondly the objections must be answered.[^7]

### 05-b ¶4 · 121.9–121.24  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 121] Quantum ad primum, videtur quod Christus sit in hostia ut in signo, quia aliter non foret hostia sacramentum, non tamen est sic in signis quin sit ibi realiter et vere secundum totam humanitatem; et sic conceditur quod Christus non est ibi solummodo ut in signo, est tamen ibi efficacius quam in signo; nec repugnat **sed** consonat quod idem sit veritas et figura vel signum, cum secundum Augustinum Enchiridion capitulo LII mors Christi, resurreccio et ascensio erant figure mortis nostre, resurreccionis et ascensionis spiritualis, et tamen erant veritas rei geste. Quamdiu itaque sumus viantes, oportet nos uti signis et vivere in velamento fidei et figuris, et sic cum in signis sint gradus multiplices, patet quod nos tempore legis gracie habemus signa efficaciora quam habebant patres veteris testamenti.

ENGLISH: As to the first, it seems that Christ is in the host as in a sign, because otherwise the host would not be a sacrament; yet he is not so in signs that he is not there [really]{g:realiter} and [truly]{g:vere} according to his whole humanity; and so it is granted that Christ is not there only as in a sign, yet he is there more efficaciously than in a sign. Nor is it [incompatible]{g:repugnare}, but consonant, that the same thing be truth and figure or sign, since according to Augustine, *Enchiridion* chapter 52, the death, resurrection and ascension of Christ were figures of our death, resurrection and spiritual ascension, and yet were the truth of a thing done.[^8] So long, then, as we are [those on the road]{g:viantes}, we must use signs and live in the veil of faith and in figures; and so, since there are manifold degrees in signs, it is clear that we, in the time of [the law of grace]{g:lex-gracie}, have signs more efficacious than the fathers of the old testament had.[^9]

### 05-b ¶6 · 122.9–123.28  (6 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 122] Ulterius quantum ad verba sacramentalia, videtur probabile domino Armachano libro nono capitulo 5ᵗᵒ De Questionibus Armenorum, quod Christus confecit hoc sacramentum pura voluntate, antequam protulit illa verba, quia ut narrant Mathaeus et Marcus Jesus accepit panem, benedixit et fregit deditque discipulis suis et ait illis: Accipite et manducate, hoc est corpus meum; ubi notata serie verborum, cum uniformiter demonstravit panem quem consecratum precepit eis comedere erantque ante ista verba benediccio, fraccio et manducandi precepcio, videtur quod erat ante ista verba hoc est corpus meum panis consecracio, videtur secundo sibi probabile iuxta fidem scripture quod verba consecratoria intelligunt de pane et vino, cum omnes predicti quatuor apostoli notant quod Jesus accepit panem materialem in manus suas, quem fregit, manducari precepit et esse corpus suum asseruit; et idem iudicium est de vino. Quid enim planius quam textus Marci XIVº, 23, 24, ubi dicitur: Accepto calice gracias agens dedit eis et biberunt ex eo omnes et ait illis: Hic est sanguis meus, ubi patet quod vocat vinum contentum in [p. 123] calice sanguinem suum; et idem patet Math. XXVIº, 26. Ex quibus videtur consonancius quam dicit magister quod talis locucio sit tropica et quodammodo **sed** non essencialiter conversiva vini in sanguinem, cum sensus tropicus sit verus **sed** demonstrando panem et vinum foret falsum quoad predicacionem ydempticam quod hoc est corpus Christi vel sanguis. Si **autem** demonstratur pronomine panis aut vinum in principio et in fine fit conversio, tunc proposicio in sua successione foret falsa vel conversioni inpertinens, nisi sompnietur quod Deus instituit proposicionem talem ad habendum efficaciam taliter convertendi; quod non fundabitur. Si demonstretur pronomine corpus Christi, tunc nichil novi constituitur, ut sit Christi carnis effectivum. Si **autem** pronomen connotat quod hoc sub istis accidentibus non subiectatis est corpus Christi, petitur infundabiliter contra scripture ordinem sompniatum; et sensum istius scripture vellem illos attendere qui fantasiant ex isto textu quod est dare conversionem sine essencia differente; videtur ergo utrobique in verbis sacramentalibus esse locucionem tropicam efficaciorem quam in ista: Petra **autem** erat Christus, cum illa verba faciunt occasionaliter quod humanitas Christi sit realiter **sed** sacramentaliter ad omnem punctum hostie consecrate, et sic est illa hostia signum efficacius quam panis benedictus a layco et secundum formam aliam a presbitero benedictus.

ENGLISH: Further, as to the sacramental words, it seems probable to [the lord of Armagh]{g:dominus-ardmacanus}, in book nine, chapter 5, *Of the Questions of the Armenians*, that Christ consecrated this sacrament by pure will before he uttered those words;[^13] because, as Matthew and Mark relate, "Jesus took bread and blessed and broke and gave to his disciples" and said to them: "Take ye and eat: this is my body";[^14] where, once the order of the words is noted, since he uniformly pointed to the bread which, consecrated, he commanded them to eat, and the blessing, the breaking and the command to eat came before these words, it seems that the [consecration]{g:consecracio} of the bread was before these words "this is my body".[^15] It seems to him, secondly, probable according to the faith of scripture that the consecrating words understand [their subject] of the bread and wine, since all the four aforesaid apostles note that Jesus took material bread into his hands, which he broke, commanded to be eaten, and asserted to be his body; and the same judgement holds of the wine.[^16] For what is plainer than the text of Mark 14:23, 24, where it is said: "And having taken [the chalice]{g:calix}, giving thanks, he gave it to them. And they all drank of it. And he said to them: This is my blood"; where it is clear that he calls the wine contained in the [123] chalice his blood; and the same is clear in Matt. 26:26.[^17] From which it seems more consonant than what the master says that such speech is [tropical]{g:locucio-tropica}, and in a certain way, but not [essentially]{g:essencialiter}, converting the wine into blood; since the tropical sense is true, but, when the bread and wine are pointed to, it would be false as to [identical predication]{g:predicacio-ydemptica} that "this is the body of Christ" or "the blood".[^18] And if by the pronoun the bread or the wine is pointed to at the beginning, and at the end the conversion takes place, then the [proposition]{g:proposicio} in its succession would be false, or impertinent to the conversion, unless it is dreamed that God instituted such a proposition to have the efficacy of so converting; which will not be grounded. If the body of Christ is pointed to by the pronoun, then nothing new is constituted, so as to be productive of the flesh of Christ. And if the pronoun connotes that "this, under these unsubjected [accidents]{g:accidens}, is the body of Christ", a dream against the order of scripture [is begged]{g:petitur} [without foundation]{g:infundabiliter};[^19] and I would wish those who imagine from this text that [one may posit]{g:est-dare} a conversion without a differing essence to attend to the meaning of this scripture.[^20] It seems, therefore, that in both places in the sacramental words there is tropical speech more efficacious than in this: "and the rock was Christ"; since those words make [by occasion]{g:occasionaliter} that the humanity of Christ is really but [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} at every point of the consecrated host; and so that host is a sign more efficacious than bread blessed by a layman, and [than bread] blessed by a [presbyter]{g:presbiter} according to another form.[^21]

### 05-c ¶2 · 124.20–125.2  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 124] Et si queratur quomodo fundabitur quod panis et vinum remanent hostia consecrata, dicitur (ut sepe superius) quod ad hoc movent sensus et racio sanctorum, doctorum testificacio et fidei scripture informacio. Nam eque certe movet sensus ad credendum ibi esse panem et vinum, sicut movet ad credendum ibi esse quantitatem aut qualitatem, sicut prior est illorum sensitiva noticia. Racio **autem** videtur triplex: prima quod omnes disposiciones previe requisite ad formas elementi vel mixti sunt posite, sicut et omnes potencie agendi ex quibus naturalis concluderet formas tales, et tercio quod Deus non sine causa necessaria [p. 125] dampnificaret suam universitatem subtrahendo naturam substancie.

ENGLISH: And if it is asked how it will be grounded that [bread]{g:panis} and [wine]{g:vinum} remain, the host consecrated, [the answer is]{g:dicitur} (as often above) that sense and reason move [us] to this, the testimony of the saints [and] doctors, and the [informing]{g:informacio} of the faith of scripture.[^4] For sense moves [us] to believe that bread and wine are there just as certainly as it moves [us] to believe that quantity or quality is there, as the sense-knowledge of those is prior.[^5] And the reason seems threefold: first, that all the dispositions [required beforehand]{g:previe-requisitum} for the forms of an element or of a mixed [body] are posited, as are all the [powers]{g:potencia} of acting from which a natural [philosopher] would [infer]{g:concludere} such forms; and thirdly, that God would not without necessary cause [125] [damage]{g:dampnificare} [his universe]{g:universitas} by withdrawing the nature of [substance]{g:substancia}.[^6]

### 05-c ¶3 · 125.3–125.9  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 125] Triplex **autem** ponitur testificacio sanctorum secundum dicta superius. Primum est dictum Augustini positum in De Consecracione, distinccione IIª, Qui manducant, ubi sic dicitur: quod videtur panis est, et calix quod oculi renunciant, quod **autem** fides postulat instruenda, panis est corpus Christi et calix sanguis.

ENGLISH: And the testimony of the saints is laid down as threefold, according to what was said above. The first is the saying of Augustine set in *De consecratione*, distinction 2, *Qui manducant*, where it is said thus: "What is seen is bread, and the [chalice]{g:calix}, as the eyes report; but as faith asks to be instructed, the bread is [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} and the chalice the blood."[^7]

### 05-c ¶4 · 125.10–125.14  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 125] Item, eadem distinccione ex dicto Hilarii sic habetur: Corpus Christi quod sumitur de altari figura est, dum panis et vini natura extra videtur: veritas **autem**, dum corpus et sanguis Christi in veritate interius creditur.

ENGLISH: Again, in the same distinction, from a saying of Hilary, it is had thus: "The body of Christ which is taken from the altar is a [figure]{g:figura}, while the [nature]{g:natura} of bread and wine is seen outwardly; but a truth, while the body and blood of Christ is believed inwardly in truth."[^8]

### 05-c ¶5 · 125.15–127.14  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 125] Item, sanctus papa Nicholaus cum multis episcopis declarat ut fidem panem et vinum que in altari ponuntur remanere post consecracionem tam sacramentum quam carnem Christi et sanguinem, ut patet distinccione IIª Ego Berengarius. Et ex istis credo diffinicionem Urbani nostri cum suis episcopis tenere antiquam fidem Romane ecclesie, licet Robertus cum suis teneat ficticiam Avinone de transsubstanciacione; nec videtur alter eorum dignus in papam recipi, nisi declarare sciverit istam fidem, cum docere fidem katholicam sit precipuum illorum officium. Unde quidam credunt quod non concordabunt caritative antequam per alterum eorum hoc efficaciter sit edoctum, quia propter huius defectum et ipsi et eorum subditi sunt ydolatre et blasphemi, sic quod de sic errantibus in hiis signis potest dici illud [p. 126] Ysaie Iº, 11: Quo mihi multitudinem victimarum vestrarum? Plenus sum. Et sequitur: Cum extenderitis manus vestras, avertam oculos meos a vobis, et cum multiplicaveritis, oracionem non exaudiam: Quomodo ergo excusabuntur in isto peccato, qui ex officio tenentur in isto docere ecclesiam? Et ex fide scripture videtur quod panis et vinum post benediccionem remaneant sacramentum; nam (ut sepe repecii) quatuor evangeliste narrant quomodo Christus accepit panem et vinum et illa demonstrando dixit: Hoc est corpus meum vel sanguis. Secundo confirmatur ex evangelio Apostoli Iº, Cor. Xº, 14, 16, 17, ubi primo precipit: Fugite ab ydolorum cultura. Et adiungit: Calix benediccionis cui benedicimus nonne communicacio sanguinis Christi est? et panis quem frangimus nonne participacio corporis Domini est? quoniam unus panis et unum corpus multi sumus; ubi manifeste patet quod loquitur de pane et vino materialibus, que post benediccionem sunt hoc sacramentum; et iterum manifestum est quod hic utitur predicacione tropica et non ydemptica, dum intelligit panem et vinum figurare unionem ecclesie cum Christo qui est res huius sacramenti. Et patet quanta cecitate percutitur ecclesia que dimisso evangelio et sanctis doctoribus regulatur per stultam heresim decretistarum, qui tradunt pro regula quod generaliter in hiis dictis per panem et vinum intelliguntur accidencia non substancia vel natura. Numquid credimus sanctum Apostolum intelligere appropriate per panem quem frangimus illa accidencia que nescimus? Nonne beatus Hilarius explicat sensum quod sunt natura panis et [p. 127] vini? Nonne secundum decretum beati Jeronimi illi qui intelligunt scripturam sacram aliter quam Spiritus Sanctus flagitat sunt heretici? Et tam detestanda et iners est glossa quam dant dictis sanctorum; et quando Augustinus dicit superius quod videtur panis est, **sed** quod creditur panis est corpus Christi, glossant per oppositum; quod videtur non est panis **sed** accidens per se quod fuit in pane; **sed** certum est quod tam subtilis logicus, tam profundus methaphisicus et tam prudens theologus non sic oneraret verba fidei tam aliena sentencia, specialiter cum expresse tam crebro asserit quod accidens non potest esse sine subiecto, ut exemplificat de quantitate et qualitate.

ENGLISH: Again, the holy [pope]{g:papa} Nicholas with many bishops [makes clear]{g:declarare} as faith that the bread and wine which are placed on the altar remain after the [consecration]{g:consecracio} both a sacrament and the flesh and blood of Christ, as is clear in distinction 2, *Ego Berengarius*.[^9] And from these I believe that the [definition]{g:diffinicio} of our Urban with his bishops holds the ancient faith of the Roman [church]{g:ecclesia}, although Robert with his [men] holds the fiction at Avignon about [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio}; nor does either of them seem worthy to be received as pope unless he knows how to make this faith clear, since to teach the catholic faith is their chief office.[^10] Hence some believe that they will not agree in [charity]{g:caritas} before this has been effectually taught by one of them, because for lack of this both they and their subjects are idolaters and blasphemers; so that of those who so err in these signs that [word] [126] of Isaiah 1:11 can be said: "To what purpose do you offer me the multitude of your victims? I am full." And it follows: "And when you stretch forth your hands, I will turn away my eyes from you: and when you multiply prayer, I will not hear."[^11] How then will they be excused in this sin, who are bound by their office to teach the church in this? And [from the faith of scripture]{g:ex-fide} it seems that the bread and wine remain the sacrament after the blessing; for (as I have often repeated) the four evangelists relate how Christ took bread and wine and, pointing to them, said: "This is my body" or "blood".[^12] Secondly, this is confirmed from the gospel of the Apostle, 1 Cor. 10:14, 16, 17, where he first commands: "Fly from the service of idols." And he adds: "The chalice of benediction which we bless, is it not the communion of the blood of Christ? And the bread which we break, is it not the partaking of [the body of the Lord]{g:corpus-domini}? For we, being many, are one bread and one body"; where it is plainly clear that he speaks of material bread and wine, which after the blessing are this sacrament; and again it is plain that here he uses [tropical predication]{g:predicacio-tropica} and not identical, while he understands the bread and wine to [figure]{g:figurare} the [union]{g:unio} of the church with Christ, who is the [reality of this sacrament]{g:res-sacramenti}.[^13] And it is clear with what blindness the church is struck which, having set aside the gospel and the holy doctors, is ruled by the foolish heresy of the [decretists]{g:decretiste}, who hand down as a rule that generally in these sayings by bread and wine are understood the [accidents]{g:accidens}, not the substance or nature.[^14] Do we believe that the holy Apostle means properly, by "the bread which we break", those accidents which we do not know? Does not blessed Hilary unfold the [meaning]{g:sensus}, that they are the nature of bread and [127] of wine? Are not, according to the [decree]{g:decretum} of blessed Jerome, those who understand holy scripture otherwise than the Holy Spirit demands heretics?[^15] And so detestable and inert is the gloss which they give to the sayings of the saints; and when Augustine says above, "What is seen is bread, but what is believed, the bread is the body of Christ", they gloss by the opposite: "what is seen is not bread but an accident by itself which was in the bread". But it is certain that so subtle a logician, so profound a metaphysician and so prudent a theologian would not so burden the words of the faith with so alien a [sense]{g:sentencia-verborum}, especially since he expressly and so often asserts that an accident cannot be without a subject, as he exemplifies of quantity and quality.[^16]

### 05-d ¶1 · 127.15–128.4  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 127] Consolacio itaque contra istos doctores est quod iuxta propria principia non debet credi alicui eorum et specialiter in materia theologica, nisi de quanto se fundaverit (quod non poterit) in hac glossa. Et sic patet quod panis et vinum sunt hoc venerabile sacramentum; verumptamen protestor publice quod, si Robertus Gilbonensis vel quicunque de suis fautoribus doceat contrarium, paratus sum ut fidelis filius ecclesie ad credendum. **Sed** credo quod Robertus cum omnibus suis complicibus non faciet ita probabiles evidencias ad credendum quod hoc sacramentum non est essencialiter panis aut vinum, **sed** unum accidens ignotum. Necesse est tamen has hereses esse ad puniendum eos in penam peccati qui phariseice ponderant nimis signa. Exhinc, inquam, occupatur scola circa falsas et vanas ficticias, dimissa doctrina [p. 128] veritatis fidei necessaria ad salutem; et oportet omnino ex hoc consequi contemptum cultus Dei et animum attentum ad cultum dyaboli ad profundacionem infinitam in vanitate signorum seculi.

ENGLISH: The consolation, then, against these doctors is that, by their own principles, none of them ought to be believed, especially in theological matter, except so far as he has grounded himself (which he will not be able to do) in this gloss.[^1] And so it is clear that [bread]{g:panis} and [wine]{g:vinum} are this venerable [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}; nevertheless I [declare solemnly]{g:protestari}, in public, that if Robert of Geneva or any of his supporters teaches the contrary, I am ready, as a faithful son of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, to believe [it].[^2] But I believe that Robert with all his accomplices will not make such probable [evidence]{g:evidencia} for believing that this sacrament is not [essentially]{g:essencialiter} bread or wine, but one unknown [accident]{g:accidens}. Yet these heresies must be, to punish, as a penalty of sin, those who in Pharisee fashion weigh signs [exceedingly]{g:nimis}.[^3] From this, I say, the school is occupied about false and vain [fictions]{g:ficticia}, the [doctrine]{g:doctrina} [128] of the truth of faith that is necessary to salvation being set aside; and from this there must wholly follow contempt of the [worship of God]{g:cultus-dei}, and a mind attentive to the worship of the devil, to an infinite sinking in the vanity of the signs of the world.

### 05-d ¶2 · 128.5–129.11  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 128] Unde Augustinus libro Sermonum, Sermone LV, promittit narrare populo quid sit hoc sacramentum quod fideles accipiunt: Panis, inquit, iste quem videtis in altari sanctificatus per verbum Dei corpus Christi est. Quod habet calix sanctificatum per verbum Dei sanguis Christi est. Per ista voluit Dominus commendare corpus suum et sanguinem. Si bene accipitis, unus panis, unum corpus multi sumus, sicut exponit Apostolus. Et sic commendatur vobis in isto pane quomodo unitatem diligere debeatis. Numquid enim panis ille de uno grano factus est? ymmo multa erant tritici grana? **Sed** antequam ad panem venirent, separata erant, per aquam coniuncta sunt et post quandam contricionem. Nisi enim molatur triticum, per aquam conspergatur et per ignem pinsatur, ad formam minime venit que panis vocatur. Sic et vos ante ieiunii humiliacionem exorcismi sacramento quasi molebamini. Accessit baptismum; quasi aqua conspersi estis et igne karitatis constantes, ut ad formam panis veniretis. Nec dubium quin illum panem sic artificiatum quem sanctus doctor dicit esse essencialiter sacramentum mistice manducandum non vocat accidens ignotum **sed** realiter panem communem verbo Domini consecratum; nec umquam concepit iste vel aliquis ex hoc sanctus quod benediccione et [p. 129] virtute verborum Domini panis consumitur usque ad fundamenta in eo vel corrumpitur secundum se totum **sed** pocius melioratus remanet figurans nobis secundum proprietates predictas sensum misticum, scilicet allegoricum, anagogicum et moralem. Non enim pinsavit Deus unam pastam monstruosam accidencium per se existencium, ut tam varia in naturis figurent veritatem summe unam, nec epistolatus est aliquis secundum illam fidem in azimis veritatis, **sed** contra deitatem ac humanitatem Christi in fermento erroris nimis veteri.

ENGLISH: Hence Augustine, in the book of Sermons, sermon 55, promises to [relate]{g:narrare} to the people what this sacrament is which the faithful receive: "This bread," he says, "which you see on the altar, sanctified by the word of God, is [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}. What the [chalice]{g:calix} holds, sanctified by the word of God, is the blood of Christ. Through these the Lord willed to commend his body and blood. If you receive well, 'we, being many, are one bread, one body', as the Apostle expounds. And so it is commended to you in this bread how you ought to love unity. For was that bread made of one grain? Rather, were there not many grains of wheat? But before they came to the bread they were separate; they were joined through water, and after a certain crushing. For unless the wheat is ground, sprinkled with water and baked by fire, it by no means comes to the form which is called bread. So you too, before the [humbling]{g:humiliacio} of fasting, were as it were ground by the sacrament of exorcism. Baptism came: you were as it were sprinkled with water, and standing firm by the fire of [charity]{g:caritas}, so that you might come to the form of bread."[^4] Nor is there doubt that that bread so crafted, which the holy doctor says is essentially the sacrament to be eaten [mystically]{g:mistice}, he does not call an unknown accident but [really]{g:realiter} [calls] common bread consecrated by the word of the Lord;[^5] nor did he, or any saint from this [teaching], ever conceive that by the blessing and [129] [power]{g:virtus} of the words of the Lord the bread is consumed down to the foundations in it, or is corrupted according to its whole self; but rather it remains, bettered, figuring to us, according to the aforesaid properties, the mystical sense, namely the allegorical, the anagogical and the moral.[^6] For God did not bake one monstrous paste of accidents existing [of themselves]{g:per-se}, so that things so varied in their [natures]{g:natura} should [figure]{g:figurare} the truth that is supremely one; nor did anyone write epistles according to that faith "in the unleavened bread of truth", but against [the Godhead]{g:deitas} and [the humanity]{g:humanitas} of Christ, in the exceedingly old leaven of error.[^7]

### 05-d ¶3 · 129.12–130.2  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 129] Deus enim non posset destruere tantam substanciam partem mundi sine recompensacione facta aliunde, nisi iniuste dampnificet quamlibet partem mundi. **Sed** constat quod illa accidentalis existencia humanitatis Christi in hostia non equivalet bonificacioni quam creatura haberet ex substancia desinente, tum quia Christus tantum benefacit universitati sue, pausando limitate in celo et essendo virtualiter in creatura quam perficit, tum eciam quia blasphema ficcio falsitatis de Deo qui est veritas effugiens ficcionem, perfeccionem accidentalem abstrahit a creatis. Quomodo ergo consonat Deo bono quod sine demerito dampnificet eciam bonos ex subtraccione perfeccionis mundi, de quo antea congaudebat? Sic ergo potest fidelis colligere quod sacramentum eukaristie sit realiter panis et vinum, vel (ut alii intelligunt) sacramentum est unum aggregatum ex illa substancia et corpore Christi (ut innuit Augustinus De Consecracione, distinccione secunda, Hoc est); quod **autem** aggregatum [p. 130] ex benediccione Domini vel virtute, sic quod nec panis nec vinum in hoc sacramento destruitur.

ENGLISH: For God could not destroy so great a [substance]{g:substancia}, a part of the world, without compensation made from elsewhere, unless he unjustly [damages]{g:dampnificare} every part of the world. Now it is evident that that accidental existence of the humanity of Christ in the [host]{g:hostia} does not equal the [bettering]{g:bonificacio} which the [creature]{g:creatura} would have from the substance that ceases: first, because Christ benefits [his universe]{g:universitas} only by resting, limited, in heaven, and by being [virtually]{g:virtualiter} in the creature which he perfects; and also because the blasphemous [fiction]{g:ficcio} of falsity about God, who is truth that shuns fiction, abstracts accidental perfection from created things.[^8] How then does it accord with a good God that, without demerit, he should damage even the good by withdrawing a perfection of the world, in which he formerly rejoiced? So, then, a faithful man can gather that the sacrament of the eucharist is really bread and wine; or (as others understand) the sacrament is one aggregate of that substance and the body of Christ (as Augustine intimates, *De consecratione*, distinction 2, *Hoc est*); and that aggregate [130] [is] from the blessing or power of the Lord, so that neither the bread nor the wine is destroyed in this sacrament.[^9]

### 05-d ¶4 · 130.3–130.21  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 130] Tercio replicatur per provecciores doctores contra predictam sentenciam. Scribit enim Anshelmus in libro suo De Corpore Christi, quomodo sub forma panis et vini datur nobis tota humanitas Christi, sic quod panis fieret corpus Christi et vinum eius anima, cum sanguis generatus ex vino sit secundum philosophos sedes anime, ymmo cum corpus Christi sit inviolabile et incorruptibile; et videtur quandoque hostiam violare et a soricibus comedi et corrodi. Tollendo istam difficultatem sic loquitur secundum diffiniciones sanctorum patrum: Intelligendum est panem super altare positum per illa sollempnia verba in corpus mutari, nec remanere substanciam panis et vini, speciem tamen intelligendum est remanere, scilicet formam, colorem et saporem, et secundum illa fit attricio, soricis corrosio et multiplex localis mocio, ut in ventrem trahicio, non **autem** secundum corpus Christi. Es istis videtur hunc sanctum sentire nudum accidens per se remanere, ut dicunt moderni.

ENGLISH: Thirdly, [it is rejoined]{g:replicatur} by [more advanced doctors]{g:provecciores-doctores} against the aforesaid [teaching]{g:sentencia}.[^10] For Anselm writes in his book *On the Body of Christ* how under the form of bread and wine the whole humanity of Christ is given to us, so that the bread would become the body of Christ and the wine his soul, since the blood generated from wine is, according to the philosophers, the seat of the soul; indeed, [he writes] since the body of Christ is inviolable and incorruptible, and the host is sometimes seen to be violated and to be eaten and gnawed by mice.[^11] [Disposing of]{g:tollere} this difficulty, he speaks thus, according to the [definitions]{g:diffinicio} of the holy fathers: "It is to be understood that the bread placed on the altar is changed into the body through those solemn words, and that the substance of bread and wine does not remain; yet the [species]{g:species} is to be understood to remain, namely the form, colour and savour; and according to these there takes place the wearing away, the gnawing of the mouse and manifold local [motion]{g:mocio}, as the drawing into the belly, but not according to the body of Christ."[^12] From these it seems that this saint [holds]{g:sentire} that a bare accident remains by itself, as [the moderns]{g:moderni} say.

### 05-d ¶5 · 130.22–131.13  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 130] **Sed** pro isto notandum quod Anshelmus et alii sancti laboraverant non ad honorem illius signi **sed** ad veneracionem corporis Christi signati et sic Christi; et sic nitebantur quod quidditas substancie signantis sopiatur quoad consideracionem hominis, et tota devota intencio coiligatur in Christum, ad quod per se movent signa per se sensibilia et non sic materialis substancia. Sic enim in presencia maioris vel preponderacioris obiecti sopitur appreciacio inferioris, ut patet de elementis inmixtis que multi propter latenciam [p. 131] ponunt non esse; de frigore latente in ferro candente et omnino de litteris quarum essencias et figuram clericus non attendit, cum hoc sit officium idiote, avis vel bestie; **sed** quantum potest consideracione tali suspensa respicit ad signatum. Sic facit fidelis quoad signum sacramentale et suum signatum; et sic conceduntur ille materiales essencie post consecracionem a quibusdam minus bene intelligentibus simpliciter nusquam esse, decretiste vero dicunt quod panis et vinum remanent, **sed** nescitur ubi sunt. **Sed** tercii dicunt quod convertuntur in corpus Christi et sanguinem; quod potest tam bene quam male intelligi.

ENGLISH: But on this it is to be noted that Anselm and the other [saints]{g:sancti} laboured not for the honour of that [sign]{g:signum} but for the veneration of the body of Christ signified, and so of Christ; and so they strove that the [quiddity]{g:quiditas} of the signifying substance should be lulled to sleep as to man's consideration, and the whole devout intention be gathered into Christ, to which the signs [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} of themselves move of themselves, and not so the material substance.[^13] For so, in the presence of a greater or more preponderant object, the appreciation of the lower is lulled to sleep: as is clear of the elements mingled [in a compound], which many, on account of their latency, [131] hold not to be [there]; of the cold latent in red-hot iron; and altogether of letters, whose essences and shape the cleric does not attend to, since this is the office of an [unlearned man]{g:idiota}, a bird or a beast; but, as far as he can, with such consideration suspended, he looks to [the thing signified]{g:signatum}.[^14] So does the faithful man as to the sacramental sign and its thing signified; and so it is granted by some who understand less well that those material essences are after the [consecration]{g:consecracio} [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} nowhere; but the [decretists]{g:decretiste} say that the bread and wine remain, but it is not known where they are. But a third [party] say that they are converted into the body and blood of Christ; which can be understood well as well as badly.[^15]
