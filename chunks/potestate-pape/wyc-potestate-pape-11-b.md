# *De potestate pape*, chapter 11, part 2: the caesarean priest and the disciple of Christ; Christ the one prince of priests

## ¶1 · 268.28–271.16

> [p. 268] Ex istis plane colligitur sensus discernendi sacerdotem cesareum a Christi discipulo, nam cesareus innititur titulo cesaris, dominacionem exigens et observans, ac intentus mundialibus fit exhinc in officio quod Christus instituit negligens [p. 269] vel remissus. Sed presbiter christianus innititur titulo elemosine ex gracia nec affidatur cum temporalium perpetuitate, sed reputans hoc ad onus instanter, inturbate et humiliter sine dominio complet ministerium sibi creditum, paratus sustinere cum gaudio ablacionem ymo rapinam temporalium si contingat. Nec videtur quod ista nomina sint directe opposita, cum sacerdos christianus sit sacerdos cesareus vel remisse simul vel per vices; oportet tamen omnem sacerdotem cesareum salvandum titulum imperialem deserere et in predicto titulo finaliter consumare. Unde multi decipiuntur in hoc, quod ignorant sensum scripture et doctorum, qui sepe simpliciter et in sensu composito intelligunt huiusmodi aggregata, ut sicut Apostolus dicit, quod nichil potest eos separare a caritate Dei, subintelligendo simpliciter predestinatos. Sic Aristoteles concedit de eodem, quod album desinit esse et musicum manet, et ignorancia logice facit stultos credere quod nullus papa potest peccare, eo quod peccare repugnat racioni papali vel saltem ipsam denigrat, et sic faceto scemate dicta doctorum de papa intelligunt condicionaliter, reputative, vel in una persona generalis et debiti officii descripcionem, ut Christus sepe alloquitur Petrum et ceteros omnes apostolos. Et istomodo loquitur Jeronymus cum Lyncolniensi de papa, ut patet ex dictis. Sed et Bernhardus libro II de papa sic loquitur: Indagemus ad hoc diligencius, quis sis, quam geris pro tempore personam in ecclesia Dei, quis es, sacerdos magnus, summus pontifex, tu princeps [p. 270] episcoporum, tu heres apostolorum, tu primatu Abel, gubernatu Noe, patriarchatu Abraham, ordine Melchisedech, dignitate Aaron, auctoritate Moyses, iudicatu Samuel, potestate Petrus, unccione Christus. Et sequitur: Sunt quidam et alii Celi ianitores et gregum pastores, sed tu tanto gloriosius, quanto differencius utrumque pre ceteris nomen hereditasti, habent illi sibi assignatos greges singuli singulos, tibi universi crediti, unus uni nec modo omnium, sed pastorum cum unus omnium pastor. In istis tredecim et aliis descripcionibus foret beatus Bernhardus stulto stulcior, si sine revelacione diffinitive assereret quod papa sibi contemporaneus huiusmodi sit de facto; sed talis debet esse ex veritate assumpti nominis, sicut erant alique persone eiusdem nominis, cum prophetetur se gerere personam Christi in terris, in quo congregantur omnium sanctorum patrum laudum principia, quod si mentitur ex obliquitate vite in nomine: Ve mendaci illi cum suis complicibus. Ex istis colligo tercio, concedendo quod oportet in ordine sacerdotali tam quoad potestatem quam ministerium esse gradus, ut prepositi innitantur, quis paracius, humilius aut fructuosius ministret ecclesie, et sic conceditur quod sicut in lege veteri figuratum est tam per Moysen quam Aaron et ceteros succedentes, oportet esse in lege nova unum omnium aliorum sacerdotum principem dominum Ihesum Christum, qui non posset patencius ostendere suam stulticiam aut huius dignitatis carenciam quam promulgando de se ipso in populo illud nomen; licet enim Christus sepe expressit et sepius tacuit unigeniti dignitatem, alia tamen racio subest in aliis, cum necesse fuit illam fidem de Christo detegere. In nobis autem oportet cum timore sperare quod erimus Christi membra, quod est valde continens. [p. 271] In ipso autem necesse fuit verbum Dei esse unigenitum Dei patris, unde videtur quod cogens ad credendum ut fidem, quod ipse sit caput sancte matris ecclesie, extollitur super Christum, quia Christus docuit illud abscondite in tantum quod elicere ex scriptura unionem ypostaticam humanitatis et divinitatis Christi, sine qua non foret caput universalis ecclesie est multis difficile. Illud autem, quod Christus docuit latenter et abscondite de se ipso, illud docmatizat Antichristus inordinate, publice et infideliter de se ipso. Inordinate dico, quia sine evidencia que fidem gigneret quoscumque qui illud negaverint conatur extinguere; et hoc non est medium apostolicum sed apostaticum antichristive fidei inductivum.

From these things the sense in which a [caesarean priest]{g:sacerdos-cesarius} is to be discerned from a disciple of Christ is plainly gathered.[^1] For the caesarean rests on the [title]{g:titulus} of Caesar, demanding and maintaining the [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio}, and, intent on worldly things, he becomes thereby negligent [269] or slack in the office that Christ instituted. But the Christian [presbyter]{g:presbiter} rests on the title of [alms]{g:elemosina}, by grace, and is not [affianced]{g:affidacio} to the perpetuity of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, but, reckoning this a burden, he fulfils the ministry entrusted to him earnestly, untroubled and humbly, without [lordship]{g:dominium}, ready to bear with joy the [taking-away]{g:ablacio}, indeed the plunder, of temporal goods if it happens.[^2] Nor does it seem that these names are directly opposed, since a Christian priest may be a caesarean priest, either slackly at the same time or by turns;[^3] yet every caesarean priest who is to be saved must abandon the imperial title and end at last in the aforesaid title. Hence many are deceived in this, that they are ignorant of the sense of scripture and of the doctors, who often understand such aggregates [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} and in the composite sense:[^4] as when the Apostle says that "nothing can separate them from the [charity]{g:caritas} of God", [understanding as implied]{g:subintelligere}, without qualification, [the predestinate]{g:predestinatus}.[^5] So Aristotle grants of the same thing that the white ceases to be and the musical remains;[^6] and ignorance of logic makes fools believe that no [pope]{g:papa} can sin, because sinning [is incompatible]{g:repugnare} with the papal [aspect]{g:racio}, or at least blackens it; and so, by a witty figure, the sayings of the doctors about the pope are understood conditionally, [by estimation]{g:reputative}, or as the [definition]{g:descripcio} of a general and due office in a single person, as Christ often addresses Peter and all the other apostles.[^7] And in this way Jerome, with Lincoln, speaks of the pope, as is clear from what has been said. But Bernard too, in book 2, speaks thus of the pope: "Let us inquire into this more diligently: who you are, what person you bear for the time in the church of God. Who are you? A great priest, the [highest pontiff]{g:summi-pontifices}. You are the prince [270] of bishops, you the heir of the apostles; you are Abel in [primacy]{g:primatus}, Noah in governance, Abraham in patriarchate, Melchisedech in order, Aaron in dignity, Moses in [authority]{g:auctoritas}, Samuel in judgeship, Peter in [power]{g:potestas}, Christ in anointing."[^8] And it follows: "There are some others too, door-keepers of heaven and shepherds of flocks; but you have inherited both names the more gloriously, the more distinctively, beyond the rest. They have flocks assigned to them, each one his own; to you all are entrusted, one to one; and not only of all, but of the shepherds, since [you are] the one shepherd of all."[^9] In these thirteen definitions and others blessed Bernard would be more foolish than a fool if, without revelation, he asserted definitively that the pope contemporary with him was such in fact; but such he ought to be from the truth of the name he has assumed, as there were certain persons of the same name, since he declares that he bears the person of Christ on earth,[^10] in whom are gathered the first principles of the praises of all the holy fathers; and if he lies in the name by the crookedness of his life: woe to that liar, with his accomplices. From these things I gather, thirdly, granting that in the priestly order there must be degrees as regards both power and ministry, so that [superiors]{g:prepositi} may strive over who ministers to the church more readily, more humbly or more fruitfully,[^11] and so it is granted that, as was figured in the old law both by Moses and by Aaron and the others who succeeded, there must be in the new law one prince of all the other priests, the Lord Jesus Christ; [and any other] could not show his folly, or his lack of this dignity, more plainly than by proclaiming that name of himself among the people.[^12] For although Christ often expressed, and more often kept silent about, the dignity of the only-begotten, yet another reason underlies [the case] in others, since it was necessary [in him] [to expose]{g:detegere} that faith about Christ. But in us we must hope with fear that we shall be [members]{g:membrum} of Christ, which is a strong restraint.[^13] [271] But in him it was necessary that the Word of God be the only-begotten of God the Father; hence it seems that one who compels [men] to believe, as faith, that he himself is [head]{g:caput} of holy mother church is lifted up above Christ,[^14] because Christ taught that so covertly that to draw out of scripture the hypostatic union of the humanity and divinity of Christ, without which he would not be head of the universal church, is difficult for many. And what Christ taught of himself hiddenly and covertly,[^15] that Antichrist teaches as dogma of himself, disorderly, publicly and faithlessly. "Disorderly", I say, because without [evidence]{g:evidencia} that would beget faith he tries to extinguish whoever may have denied it;[^16] and this is not an apostolic middle term but an apostate one, [leading on]{g:medium-inductivum} to antichristian faith.[^17]

## ¶2 · 271.17–271.21

> [p. 271] Sed contra illud multi arguunt quod stat feminam vel laycum esse papam, cum verisimile sit beatam virginem post ascensionem inter ceteros propinquius sequi Christum, et stat laycum esse superiorem in moribus quocumque presbitero.

But against this many argue that it is consistent for a woman or a layman to be pope, since it is likely that the blessed Virgin, after the ascension, followed Christ more closely than the rest, and it is consistent for a layman to be superior in morals to any presbyter whatever.[^18]

## Notes

[^1]: **R.** B in marg. at 268.28 (later hand): *Omnes pape post dotacionem e. v.* [probably *ecclesie vocantur*] *dicuntur cesarei sacerdotes*, "all popes after the endowment of the church are called caesarean priests". *Sacerdos cesareus*: GLOSSARY "caesarean priest" (the priest who holds by Caesar's title).
[^2]: **C/S.** *Affidatur cum*: the betrothal figure (GLOSSARY *affidacio*): the Christian presbyter is not wedded to perpetual temporal goods. *Sustinere cum gaudio … rapinam* echoes Heb. 10:34 (DR "took with joy the being stripped of your own goods"; Vulg. *rapinam bonorum vestrorum cum gaudio suscepistis*).
[^3]: **C.** *Vel remisse simul vel per vices*: one man may be both at once, with the caesarean side held slackly, or each in turn; the side-note (269.7) reads it so.
[^4]: **C.** *Aggregata*: compound terms such as "Christian priest" or "pope" taken with their qualification. *In sensu composito*: the logicians' composite sense, in which the predicate is said of the subject as qualified (the predestinate, as predestinate, cannot be separated), as against the divided sense.
[^5]: **S.** Rom. 8:39, in indirect speech and of "them": Vulg. *neque creatura alia poterit nos separare a caritate Dei* (DR "the love of God"); *caritas* is "charity" here as throughout.
[^6]: **S.** Aristotle's stock example of one accident ceasing in a subject while another remains (cf. *Physics* 1.7, 190a; *De generatione* 1.4, 319b); no reference at the page foot; to be checked (LEDGER P11b-1).
[^7]: **C.** *Intelligunt* ("they understand") has no expressed subject; rendered as a passive, since the adverbs (*condicionaliter, reputative*) name the right way of reading the doctors. It could be taken of the "fools" just named, but they read the doctors as fact, not conditionally. The B and A margins at 269.21 carry the heading *Papam non posse peccare*.
[^8]: **S.** Bernard, *De consideratione* 2.8.15; Loserth's foot gives "l. c. pag. 751" (a page in the edition cited earlier). To be checked (LEDGER P11b-2). Thirteen titles, from "great priest" to "Christ", hence "these thirteen" below.
[^9]: **T.** The print reads *sed pastorum cum unus omnium pastor* (270.10, so the plate; the OCR had lost *unus omnium*). *Nec modo omnium*: Bernard's text has *nec modo ovium* ("not only of the sheep"); translated as printed. Bernard also reads *quidem* for *quidam*. To be checked against Bernard (LEDGER P11b-3).
[^10]: **C.** *Prophetetur se gerere*: with the reflexive *se* the verb must be active in sense ("declares, proclaims that he bears"); *profitetur*, "professes", may lie behind it. Translated as "declares".
[^11]: **C.** *Ut prepositi innitantur, quis …*: taken as "strive over who …", i.e. the degrees exist so that superiors may compete in service; *inniti* elsewhere in this paragraph is "rest on".
[^12]: **T/C.** *Qui non posset …*: the relative has no fitting antecedent (grammatically Christ, which cannot be the sense); the side-note (270.21, "no other should claim the office") reads it of any other claimant, and "[and any other]" is supplied to that effect. A word may have been lost before *qui*.
[^13]: **C.** *Quod est valde continens*: the hope with fear "holds [us] in check"; *continens* taken as restraining, not "self-controlled".
[^14]: **S.** *Extollitur super Christum* echoes 2 Thess. 2:4 (DR "is lifted up above all that is called God"). Codd. in marg. at 271.1: *Caput universalis ecclesie non est papa*.
[^15]: **T.** *Latenter* is Loserth's reading; both manuscripts read *rarenter*, "rarely".
[^16]: **T.** *Gigneret* is the print; the manuscripts are reported (in a rough OCR of the foot) as *digueret*, probably *digneret*. Translated as printed.
[^17]: **C.** *Medium … inductivum*: the logicians' middle term that leads on to a conclusion (GLOSSARY); *medium* could also be taken as "means". The pun *apostolicum / apostaticum* is Wyclif's.
[^18]: **C/R.** *Contra illud*: against the definition of the pope as the wayfarer who follows Christ most closely (answered at 271.24). *Stat* is "it is consistent", i.e. possible. Codd. in marg. at 271.18: *Papa fuit virgo Maria*.
