# *De eucharistia*, chapter 8, part 1: the multiplication of a body: the two ways, and the absurdities drawn against the first

## ¶1 · 232.5–232.17

> [p. 232] Restat ulterius videre de corporis multiplicacione. Non enim queritur hic quomodo species vel commune multiplicatur in suis suppositis, cum sit singula eius singularia essencialiter et numeraliter condistincta. Sed quomodo idem suppositum in numero potest multiplicari per distincta loca et distancia, sicut dicimus animam eandem hominis secundum se totam esse in quolibet membro suo, oportet eciam fidelem concedere quod tam anima Christi quam totum corpus eius sit simul tempore per diversa loca in multis ecclesiis atque altaribus, et illud vocamus multiplicacionem corporis, sicut dicitur totum corpus esse ad omnem punctum hostie consecrate.

It remains further to see about the [multiplication of a body]{g:multiplicacio-corporis}. For it is not asked here how a species or a common [thing] is multiplied in its [supposits]{g:suppositum}, since its singulars are severally distinct from one another, [essentially]{g:essencialiter} and numerically.[^1] But how the same supposit in number can be multiplied through distinct and distant places: just as we say that the same soul of a man is, according to its whole self, in each [member]{g:membrum} of him, the faithful man must also grant that both the soul of Christ and his whole body are at the same time through diverse places, in many [churches]{g:ecclesia} and altars; and this we call the multiplication of a body, as the whole body is said to be at every point of the [consecrated host]{g:hostia-consecrata}.[^2]

## ¶2 · 232.18–233.8

> [p. 232] In ista autem materia sunt due vie: prima que ascribitur Doctori Subtili et suis sequacibus, ponens quod stat idem corpus in numero multiplicari dimensionaliter simul tempore per quotlibet loca non communicancia. Et secunda que ascribitur Doctori Communi cum suis sequacibus quod idem corpus potest simul multiplicari per quotlibet loca, sic quod per unicum sit dimensionaliter et per alia incommunicancia virtualiter vel sacramentaliter sicut est de corpore Christi, quod dimensionaliter est in celo et sacramentaliter in quotlibet locis aliis distantibus, [p. 233] quia ubicunque est hostia consecrata; non tamen est dimensionaliter in aliqua earum, quia tunc extenderetur per locum hostie coequatum eidem, sic quod quelibet pars loci eiusdem hostie haberet appropriate partem corporis Christi singulariter occupatum, sicut est de aliis corporibus que per loca eis equalia diffunduntur; tunc enim foret omnino equale loco suo totali et omnino conformiter figuratum.

And in this matter there are two ways. The first, which is ascribed to [the Subtle Doctor]{g:doctor-subtilis} and his followers, posits that it is consistent for the same body in number to be multiplied [dimensionally]{g:dimensionaliter} at the same time through any number of places that do not communicate.[^3] And the second, which is ascribed to [the Common Doctor]{g:doctor-communis} with his followers, [holds] that the same body can be multiplied at the same time through any number of places, so that through one only it is dimensionally, and through the others, [that do not communicate]{g:incommunicans}, [virtually]{g:virtualiter} or [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter}: as is the case with [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}, which is dimensionally in heaven and sacramentally in any number of other distant places, [233] because [it is] wherever the consecrated host is.[^4] Yet it is not dimensionally in any of them, because then it would be extended through the place of the host, made equal to it, so that each part of the place of that host would have appropriated to it a part of the body of Christ, occupied by it alone, as is the case with other bodies that are spread through places equal to them; for then it would be altogether equal to its total place and altogether conformably [figured]{g:figurare}.[^5]

## ¶3 · 233.9–233.30

> [p. 233] Contra primam arguitur, primo per hoc quod omne corpus sit potencie infinite; nam Petrus foret eiusdem potencie cuius foret multiplicatus dimensionaliter per mille miliaria aut quotlibet loca; sed tunc foret correspondenter ad multiplicacionem maioris potencie: ergo modo est tante potencie. Assumptum patet ex hoc quod potencia est una qualitas absoluta que est locacioni inpertinens, quia aliter progrediens continue variaret potenciam. Et patet possibilitas assumpti cuius veritas supponatur; et minor probatur ex hoc quod in quocunque loco est Petrus tante potencie sufficit cum paribus tantum efficere et per consequens penes mensuram penes quam attendi debet magnitudo potencie est proporcionaliter maioris potencie; si enim sufficit ex potencia quam nunc habet facere tantum quantum mille homines, quare non est nunc tante potencie quante illi? Et assumptum patet, posito quod Petrus fortissimus multiplicaretur dimensionaliter per mille loca, foret tam potens sicut mille homines eque fortes, cum ubicunque fuerit manet tanta potencia non suspensa. Sufficeret ergo Petrus sic multiplicatus pugnare cum mille hominibus et sic in infinitum.

Against the first it is argued, first by this: that every body would be of infinite [power]{g:potencia}; for Peter would be of the same power as he would be of, multiplied dimensionally through a thousand miles or any number of places; now then he would be, correspondingly to the multiplication, of greater power: therefore he is now of so great power.[^6] [The assumption]{g:assumptum} is clear from this, that power is one absolute [quality]{g:qualitas}, which has no bearing on location, because otherwise one walking forward would continually vary his power. And the possibility of the assumption is clear, whose truth let be supposed; and the minor is proved from this, that in whatever place Peter is, of so great power, he suffices with his equals to effect so much, and consequently, by the measure by which the magnitude of power ought to be gauged, he is of proportionally greater power; for if from the power that he now has he suffices to do as much as a thousand men, why is he not now of as great power as they?[^7] And the assumption is clear: supposing that Peter, being very strong, were multiplied dimensionally through a thousand places, he would be as powerful as a thousand men equally strong, since wherever he were, so great a power remains, not [suspended]{g:suspendere}. Peter so multiplied would therefore suffice to fight with a thousand men; and so to infinity.

## ¶4 · 234.1–234.26

> [p. 234] Et hic eliciunt quidam conclusiones mirabiles, ut puta quod Petrus solus constituit coream vel exercitum quantumcunque et vulnerat et occidit se ipsum quantuncunque atrociter et in diversis locis recipit simul denominaciones quantumcunque contrarias; nam Petrus multiplicatus per mille loca constitueret coream vel exercitum plus unicordem et tantum quam tot homines; conformius ergo moveretur et constancius iuvaret se ipsum ex vi naturalis amoris quam multitudo cuius non est cor unum; et cum posset ignorare se ipsum ex varietate sensacionis, videtur quod stat in isto casu Petrum vulnerare se ipsum quantumcunque atrociter; nam unus homo non multiplicatus in casu voluntarie occidit se ipsum; quare ergo hoc non foret possibile de homine multiplicato ignorante se ipsum; nam secundum leges perspective alicubi videret sic multiplicatus quod alicubi est ab oculis suis absconditum, quia aliter foret infinitum visivus, et videret in casu ad intra et extra se indifferenter secundum omnem differenciam posicionis, ut eliciunt illi qui particulariter sollicitantur in ista materia ad eliciendum conclusiones mirabiles, ut sophiste arguunt. Et hoc tollit regulas de velocitate motus cum subito vel quantumcunque celeriter posset Petrus per viam multitudinis quantumcunque tarde acquirere quantumlibet longum situm, et sic de aliis inconvenienciis reductis.

And here some elicit wondrous conclusions: for instance, that Peter alone constitutes a [ring-dance]{g:corea} or an army however great, and wounds and kills himself however atrociously, and in diverse places receives at once [namings]{g:denominacio} however contrary.[^8] For Peter multiplied through a thousand places would constitute a ring-dance or army more of one heart and as great as so many men; he would therefore move more conformably, and help himself more constantly by force of natural love, than a multitude which has not one heart; and since he could be ignorant of himself through the variety of [sensation]{g:sensacio}, it seems that it is consistent, in this case, that Peter wounds himself however atrociously; for one man, not multiplied, in a case voluntarily kills himself; why then would this not be possible of a man multiplied and ignorant of himself? For according to the laws of perspective one so multiplied would see in some place what in some place is hidden from his eyes, because otherwise he would be infinitely visive; and he would see, in a case, within and outside himself indifferently according to every difference of position,[^9] as those infer who are particularly busy in this matter to elicit wondrous conclusions, as [sophists]{g:sophista} argue. And this takes away the rules about the speed of motion, since Peter could, suddenly or however quickly, by way of [multitude]{g:multitudo}, [moving] however slowly, acquire a site however long; and so of the other [absurdities]{g:inconveniencia} brought in by reduction.[^10]

## ¶5 · 234.27–235.24

> [p. 234] Hoc ergo habito queritur in secunda questione utrum Petrus mereretur in casu paciendo mortem a se ipso ipsam iniuste et irracionabiliter inferente; et quare non cessat, cum utrobique sentit dolorem et remanet compos mentis, nam actus sensibiles individuantur a loco et tempore et per consequens propter diversitatem eorum forent actus illi diversi. Et Deus, [p. 235] qui ex omnipotencia sua potest sic concomitanter multiplicari miraculose, potest demultiplicare, coartare sive restringere, ut accidens spirituale in uno loco multiplicetur et alibi supernaturaliter impediatur a multitudine; sed superinducatur eius contrarium, cum contraria non repugnant nisi pro simul et semel; ymmo omnia contraria Deus potest ponere simul; nec lex nature potest inpedire Deum quin potest facere quod informent; quod si Deus potest facere maius, potest facere et minus, ut communiter assumitur in ista materia. Forma ergo humanitatis et forma deitatis forent secundum istos magis contraria et plus distancia quam sunt ista, et ita virtus et vicium, meritum et demeritum possent simul tempore sed in locis diversis inesse eidem persone simplici, que foret et predestinata et prescita; quomodo ergo Petrus beatus videns in verbo suam dampnacionem non compateretur sibi ipsi quem tantum caritative diligit? nam habet quidquid vult et nichil mali vult; nec denominaciones contrarie destruerentur, ut sancti ipsas describunt, ymmo consequenter loquendo mille forent forme contrarie in natura; Deus enim posset coextendere quodcunque calidum cum quocunque frigido et facere ut forme simul informent.

This therefore had, it is asked in the second question whether Peter would merit, in the case, by suffering death from himself, inflicting it unjustly and irrationally; and why he does not cease, since [in both places]{g:utrobique} he feels pain and remains in possession of his mind; for [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} acts are individuated by place and time, and consequently, because of their diversity, those acts would be diverse.[^11] And God, [235] who of his omnipotence can [make a thing] be thus [concomitantly]{g:concomitanter} multiplied by miracle, can [demultiply]{g:demultiplicare}, compress or restrict [it], so that a spiritual [accident]{g:accidens} is multiplied in one place and elsewhere is supernaturally hindered from multitude; but [let] its contrary be brought in over it,[^12] since contraries are not [incompatible]{g:repugnare} except for together and at once; indeed God can posit all contraries together; nor can the law of nature hinder God from being able to make them [inform]{g:informare}; for if God can do the greater, he can do the lesser too, as is commonly assumed in this matter. On these men's account, then, the form of [the humanity]{g:humanitas} and the form of [the Godhead]{g:deitas} would be more contrary and further apart than these are; and so [virtue]{g:virtus} and vice, merit and demerit, could at the same time, but in diverse places, be in the same simple [person]{g:persona}, who would be both [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} and [foreknown]{g:prescitus}. How then would blessed Peter, seeing in [the Word]{g:verbum} his [damnation]{g:dampnacio}, not suffer with himself, whom he loves so charitably? For he has whatever he wills, and wills nothing evil.[^13] Nor would contrary namings be destroyed, as the saints [define]{g:describere} them; indeed, speaking consistently, there would be a thousand contrary forms in nature; for God could coextend any hot thing with any cold thing and make the forms inform together.

## ¶6 · 235.25–236.4

> [p. 235] In tercia vero questione queritur quare non sequitur: Petrus hic movetur localiter, alteratur, augmentatur vel est huiusmodi, ergo simpliciter est huiusmodi, et sic de contraria denominacione quoad locum alium, et quare proposicio de predicato contrario non infert affirmativam de predicato infinito et contradictorium concessi de predicato finito. Aliter enim possibile foret [p. 236] idem simul et semel esse per totum calidum et frigidum, salvatum et dampnatum, et omnino carens perfeccione quam habet esse excellencius aut diminucius se ipso in denominacionibus satis multis.

And in the third question it is asked why it does not follow, "Peter here is moved [locally]{g:localiter}, is altered, is augmented, or is of this kind; therefore [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} he is of this kind", and so of the contrary naming as to another place; and why a proposition with a contrary predicate does not infer the affirmative with an [infinite predicate]{g:predicatum-infinitum}, and the contradictory of what is granted with a finite predicate.[^14] For otherwise it would be possible [236] for the same thing together and at once to be throughout hot and cold, saved and damned, and altogether lacking a perfection which it has, [and] to be more excellent or more diminished than itself in very many namings.[^15]

## ¶7 · 236.5–236.30

> [p. 236] Item, posito quod Petrus sit infinitis modis contrarie accidentatus et in quacunque latitudine denominacionis humane contrariis et remissis gradibus eiusdem latitudinis in locis distantibus, esset Deo possibile quod Petrus moveretur ad se et coextenderetur se cum constituendo quantumcunque intensam qualitatem ex quantumcunque remissa et omnes species qualitatis secunde resultarent ex eadem qualitate coextensa cum se, et sic (ut arguunt) idem corpus posset infinicies esse per eundem locum; et evidenciam istius multitudinis capiunt ex factis Christi qui ex quinque panibus et duobus piscibus saciavit quinque milia hominum et adhuc remanserunt duodecim cophini fragmentorum (ut patet Marc. VIᵗᵒ cap.); et idem confirmant de septem panibus et pisciculis paucis ex quibus saciabantur quatuor milia hominum et superfuerunt septem sporte fragmentorum (de quibus Marc. VIIIᵒ); et tercio confirmatur idem de formacione Eve ex costa viri (de quo Genes. tercio); ymmo dicitur communiter quod omnis essencialis materia hominis post Adam fuit in ipso, nec aliquid augmentatur per incorporacionem huiusmodi cibalis, sicut videtur Christum diffinire Math. XV, 17: Omne, inquit, quod intrat in os, in ventrem vadit et in secessum mittitur, nec aliter posset salvari in corporibus animatis et specialiter in arboribus ydemptitas numeralis.

Again, supposing that Peter is in infinitely many ways contrarily [subject to accidents]{g:accidentatus}, and in any [latitude]{g:latitudo} of human naming, with contrary and [remiss]{g:remitti} degrees of the same latitude in distant places, it would be possible to God that Peter be moved to himself and coextended with himself, constituting a quality however intense out of one however remiss, and all the species of the [second quality]{g:qualitas-secunda} would result from the same quality coextended with itself; and so (as they argue) the same body could be infinitely many times through the same place.[^16] And they take the [evidence]{g:evidencia} of this multitude from the deeds of Christ, who from five loaves and two fishes filled five thousand men, and there still remained twelve baskets of fragments (as is clear in Mark, chapter 6); and they confirm the same from the seven loaves and a few little fishes, from which four thousand men were filled, and seven baskets of fragments were left over (of which Mark 8); and thirdly the same is confirmed from the forming of Eve from the rib of the man (of which Genesis, third).[^17] Indeed it is commonly said that all the essential matter of man after Adam was in him, nor is anything augmented by the incorporation of such food, as Christ seems to define, Matt. 15:17: "Whatsoever," he says, "entereth into the mouth, goeth into the belly, and is cast out into the privy"; nor otherwise could [numerical identity]{g:ydemptitas-numeralis} be saved in animate bodies, and especially in trees.[^18]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 232.5: Of the multiplication of the body.
- 232.18: Two theories: 1. That of Scotus, who says the body may be indefinitely multiplied in dimension. 2. That of Aquinas, that the body is dimensionally in heaven, but sacramentally with the host.
- 233.9: The objection to the first view is that it implies infinite powers in every body.
- 234.1: Absurd results that would follow from multiplication.
- 234.27: It would lead to contradiction of all kinds.
- 236.5: Argument for multiplication from the miracles of the loaves and from the creation of Eve.
