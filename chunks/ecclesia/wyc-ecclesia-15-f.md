# *De ecclesia*, chapter 15, part 6: may the king correct the clergy? the second and third objections

## ¶1 · 348.24–349.16

> [p. 348] Secundo obicitur per hoc quod oportet partes ecclesie habere suam iurisdiccionem et ordinem impermixtum; sed clerus est seculari parte superior, ideo supposito delicto debet corrigi per se ipsum. Confirmatur per hoc quod sic est in celesti ierarchia in qua oportet exemplari omne officium ecclesiastice ierarchie. [p. 349] Ad illud sepe dixi quod falsum assumitur; oportet enim utrumque brachium corporis ecclesie iuvare reliquum, nec est in isto confusio, sed secundum Apostolum Iª Cor. XII° caritativa connexio. Ulterius sepe dictum est quod, sicut clerus stans in suo ordine est seculari superior, sic nominetenus clericus degenerans est inter omnes viatores abieccior. Unde (ut sepe dixi) nobis stantibus in nostro ordine non debemus contemptibiliter esse subiecti principibus, unde glosa ordinaria super XCVIª dist. dicitur quod episcopus est super imperatorem, non tamen si facit contra religionem, nam in illo casu posset a quocunque redargui, ut patet XLª dist., Si papa, vel eciam si scisma faceret, XXIIIª, q. V, De Liguribus. Et plures sunt casus in quibus layci habent iurisdiccionem super clericos ut dixi; inquit XXIII, q. Vª, Principes.

Secondly [it is objected, by appeal to]{g:obicitur per} this: that the parts of the [church]{g:ecclesia} must have their own jurisdiction and order unmixed; now the [clergy]{g:clerus} is superior to the secular part; therefore, supposing an offence, it ought to be corrected by itself. This is confirmed by the fact that so it is in the celestial hierarchy, on which every office of the ecclesiastical hierarchy must be modelled. [349] To that I have often said that a falsehood is assumed; for each arm of the body of the church must help the other, nor is there confusion in this, but, according to the Apostle, 1 Corinthians 12, a connexion in [charity]{g:caritas}.[^1] Further, it has often been said that, just as the clergy standing in its order is superior to the secular [part], so a [cleric in name only]{g:nominetenus clerici} who degenerates is the most abject among all [wayfarers]{g:viator}. Hence (as I have often said) while we stand in our order we ought not to be contemptibly subject to princes; hence in the ordinary [gloss]{g:glosa} on distinction 96 it is said that the bishop is above the emperor,[^2] yet not if he acts against religion, for in that case he could be [refuted]{g:redarguere} by anyone, as is clear in distinction 40, *Si papa*, or also if he should make a schism, Cause 23, question 5, *De Liguribus*.[^3] And there are many cases in which lay people have jurisdiction over [clerics]{g:clericus}, as I have said; so says Cause 23, question 5, *Principes*.[^4]

## ¶2 · 349.17–350.11

> [p. 349] Unde miror quomodo quidam innituntur exemplo de celesti ierarchia, putando quod clerici non debent corrigi a laycis, nam stabilito ordine militantis ierarchie, sicut est in ierarchia celesti, procederet racio a similitudine quod clerici non forent subiecti laicis, sed si deficit eis virtus illuminandi et expurgandi, sic quod sint ceciores et criminosiores quam layci, quomodo staret in illis perturbantibus ordinem ecclesie exemplum ierarchie celestis? Ideo in defectu illorum stat quod pervertitur ordo ecclesiastice ierarchie; ordo dico non simpliciter sed ordo individuus qui foret in illis. Non enim est fas fideli credere quod datus clerus servabit ordinem superiorem in ecclesia quantumcunque peccaverit, cum primus angelus cum suis complicibus propter apostasiam ab ordine deiectus [p. 350] est de ecclesia triumphante (ut notatur Is. XIV°). Viator ergo extra ordinem debet habere subsidium a fratre inferioris ordinis, cum pes iuvat oculum, eciam utroque servante integritatem corporis. Et ita videtur Lincolniensi quod angelus superioris ordinis illuminatur ab angelo inferioris ordinis, cum omnes beati spiritus sese iuvant reciproce, ymmo (ut alias dixi): Michael et omnes angeli inferioris ordinis confirmati pugnarunt contra draconem Luciferum et contra complices suos apostatantes, quare ergo non debet esse proporcionaliter in ecclesiastica ierarchia?

Hence I wonder how some rely on the example of the celestial hierarchy, thinking that clerics ought not to be corrected by lay people; for, once the order of the militant hierarchy is established as it is in the celestial hierarchy, the argument from likeness would proceed that clerics would not be subject to lay people; but if the [power]{g:virtus} of illuminating and purging fails in them,[^5] so that they are blinder and more criminal than lay people, how would the example of the celestial hierarchy hold in those who disturb the order of the church? Therefore, in their failure, it holds that the order of the ecclesiastical hierarchy is perverted: the order, I say, not [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}, but the individual order that would be in them. For it is not right for a believer to believe that a given clergy will keep the superior order in the church however much it has sinned, since the first angel with his accomplices was thrown down from his order for apostasy [350] from the church triumphant (as is noted in Isaiah 14).[^6] Therefore a wayfarer outside his order ought to have help from a brother of a lower order, since the foot helps the eye, each also preserving the integrity of the body. And so it seems to [the bishop of] Lincoln that an angel of a higher order is illuminated by an angel of a lower order,[^7] since all the blessed spirits help one another reciprocally; indeed (as I have said elsewhere) Michael and all the angels of the lower order, confirmed [in grace], fought against the dragon Lucifer and against his apostate accomplices:[^8] why then should it not be so, proportionally, in the ecclesiastical hierarchy?

## ¶3 · 350.12–351.19

> [p. 350] Sed alias insteti per hoc quod nulla potestas punitiva civilis se extendit ad amplius quam potestas civiliter dominandi, sed non licet civili domino dominari civiliter super clerico, cum clericus cui foret licitum servire civiliter posset coloracius civiliter dominari. Sed hic sepe dixi quod de lege nature et de lege evangelii qua astringimur ad correpcionem fraternam debet rex Anglie corripere clerum suum, nec video quin stat clericum servire civiliter eo quod dominari civiliter dicit superbiam. Sed servire civiliter potest esse sine peccato. Nec oportet quod sic homo servit civiliter quod sit servus civilis, cum omnis dominus Anglie sub rege servit sibi civiliter, cum sit eius servitor tenens de rege in capite per servicia suo domino debita et consueta. Servus autem civilis est omnino expers libertatis civilis, et sic serviunt clerici regi libere, qui tenent de illo in capite; et sic videtur mihi quod rex noster habet super clerum suum legium quoad bona nature et bona fortune secundum quid [p. 351] civile dominium. Si autem viverent vitam omnino exproprietariam, sicut vixerunt Christus et Baptista, non forent sic subiecti, et sic racione bonorum fortune et bonorum nature in casu quo contingat in eis abusus vel contemptus notabilis licet regi eos civiliter cohercere, non in quantum sacerdotes, sed in quantum regis elemosinarii vel homines eius legii contempnentes. Et sic regi suo in corpore et bonis multipliciter sunt subiecti. Econtra autem rex debet esse eis subiectus in hiis que sunt ad Deum, ut sit sic mutua connexio caritatis et ordinis utrobique. Tercio arguitur per hoc quod clerus nunquam debet castigari per laicum nisi in defectu spiritualis prepositi, et per consequens nunquam antequam papa defecerit, oportet ergo subditum corripi per diocesanum, secundo per metropolitanum, et si illi defecerint, tunc per papam. Et non est presumendum quod in hiis tribus signis ecclesia Christi deficiet.

But elsewhere I raised the objection,[^9] by appeal to this: that no civil punitive power extends further than the power of [exercising lordship]{g:dominari} civilly; now it is not lawful for a civil [lord]{g:dominus} to exercise lordship civilly over a cleric, since a cleric for whom it were lawful to serve civilly could with more [colour]{g:color} exercise civil lordship.[^10] But here I have often said that by the law of nature and by the law of the gospel, by which we are bound to fraternal correction, the king of England ought to correct his clergy; nor do I see why it is not consistent that a cleric serve civilly, because to exercise lordship civilly implies pride. But to serve civilly can be without sin. Nor need a man serve civilly in such a way that he is a civil serf, since every lord of England under the king serves him civilly, being his servant [holding of]{g:tenere de} the king [in chief]{g:in capite} by the [services]{g:servicium} owed and customary to his lord. Now a civil serf is wholly without civil liberty; and so the clerics who hold of him in chief serve the king freely; and so it seems to me that our king has over his [liege]{g:legii} clergy, as to the goods of nature and the goods of fortune, [in a certain respect]{g:secundum quid} [351] [civil lordship]{g:dominium civile}.[^11] But if they lived a wholly [propertyless]{g:exproprietarii} life, as Christ and the Baptist lived, they would not be so subject; and so, by reason of the goods of fortune and the goods of nature, in a case where notable abuse or contempt occurs in them, it is lawful for the king to [coerce]{g:cohercere} them civilly, not inasmuch as they are priests, but inasmuch as they are the king's [almsmen]{g:elemosinarius} or his liege men who hold him in contempt. And so they are subject to their king in body and goods in many ways. But conversely, the king ought to be subject to them in the things that pertain to God, so that there is thus a mutual connexion of charity and of order on both sides. Thirdly [it is argued by appeal to]{g:arguitur per} this: that the clergy ought never to be chastised by a layman except in default of the spiritual [superior]{g:prepositi}, and consequently never before the [pope]{g:papa} has failed; therefore the subject must be corrected [first] by the diocesan, secondly by the metropolitan, and, if they have failed, then by the pope. And it is not to be presumed that at these three stages the church of Christ will fail.[^12]

## ¶4 · 351.20–352.16

> [p. 351] Hic dicitur quod assumptum est verum, quia (ut sepe dixi) clericus nunquam castigari debet per laicum nisi auctoritate ecclesie in defectu spiritualis prepositi et in casu quo clericus fuerit a fide devius. Unde posito de possibili quod omnes episcopi regni nostri deficiant in suis correccionibus post vel ante, racione cuius defectus invalescat peccatum ad regni periculum, regum autem est sub gravi periculo clerum suum corripere quoad corpus vel substanciam cleri, in quibus potestati regie est subiectus; quod si clerus devenit ad tantam profunditatem malicie, spiritualis prepositus omnimode est in causa. Nec est prudentis asserere quod rex expectaret correccionem cleri, quousque papa [p. 352] super isto decreverit, propter multa. Primo quia stat quod in curia redempcio sit venalis, sic quod cum thesauris regni proditor defendat ibidem peccatum suum contra Deum, regem et regnum. Secundo quia stat quod papa et sui cardinales sint acceptores personarum coniuncti hostibus regni nostri. Tercio quia stat eos esse ignaros legis scripture cecatosque tradicionibus hominum propter questum. Quarto quia stat longitudinem et discrimina itineris esse tanta quod nec persone regni nostri haberent illuc securum accessum, nec rex et regnum expectarent tantundem propter regni periculum. Quinto quia Romanus pontifex non habet illam iurisdiccionem in Anglia, nisi vel ex lege Christi, quod limitatur de quanto dicit eis humilius et sanccius legem Christi, vel ex decreto cesaris, ut notatur XCVIª dist. Constantinus.

[To this the answer is]{g:dicitur} that the [assumption]{g:assumptum} is true, because (as I have often said) a cleric ought never to be chastised by a layman except by the authority of the church, in default of the spiritual superior, and in case the cleric has strayed from the faith. Hence, supposing as possible that all the bishops of our [kingdom]{g:regnum} fail in their corrections, after or before, by reason of which failure sin grows strong to the peril of the kingdom, it is then for kings, under grave peril, to correct their clergy as to the body or substance of the clergy, in which it is subject to the royal power;[^13] and if the clergy has come to such a depth of malice, the spiritual superior is in every way the cause.[^14] Nor is it the part of a prudent man to assert that the king should wait for the correction of the clergy until the pope [352] has decreed on it, for many reasons. First, because it may be that in the curia redemption is for sale,[^15] so that a traitor, with the treasures of the kingdom, may there defend his sin against God, king and kingdom.[^16] Second, because it may be that the pope and his cardinals are respecters of persons, joined to the enemies of our kingdom. Third, because it may be that they are ignorant of the law of scripture and blinded by the traditions of men for gain. Fourth, because it may be that the length and dangers of the journey are so great that neither would the persons of our kingdom have safe access there, nor would king and kingdom wait so long, because of the peril of the kingdom. Fifth, because the Roman pontiff does not have that jurisdiction in England except either from [the law of Christ]{g:lex Christi}, which is [limited]{g:limitare} by how far he tells them the law of Christ more humbly and more holily,[^17] or from the decree of Caesar, as is noted in distinction 96, *Constantinus*.[^18]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 348.24: Objection: 2. The clergy, as superior to the laity, should judge itself.
- 349.1: Answer: The assumption is false; all parts of the Church owe mutual help.
- 349.17: As to the pattern of the celestial hierarchy, it is already forsaken when the clergy are wicked.
- 350.12: Objection: If the clergy cannot have dominion, neither can they serve Civilly.
- 350.18: This does not follow.
- 351.4: They are subject not as clergy, but as almsmen and liegemen.
- 351.12: 3. The laity ought to act only in default of the spiritual authorities.
- 351.20: Answer: True; but the King is not to wait for the Pope's action, for many reasons;
- 352.1: 1. because the curia is venal;
- 352.4: 2, Pope and Cardinals are in league with our enemies; 3. they are blinded by avarice;
- 352.8: 4. they are too far away;
- 352.12: 5. the Roman pontiff has no such jurisdiction in England.

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** 1 Cor. 12:12–27, the members of one body, which need one another (12:21 DR "the eye cannot say to the hand: I need not thy help"). *Caritativa connexio* is Wyclif's phrase, not a quotation.
[^2]: **S.** The ordinary gloss on D. 96 c. 10 (*Duo sunt*), as Loserth refers it (modern form ours; LEDGER E15f-1). **C.** *Glosa ordinaria … dicitur* is printed without *in*: *glosa* is read as an ablative of place ("in the gloss"); see the Latin doubts.
[^3]: **S.** D. 40 c. 6 (*Si papa*): Loserth gives no reference, ours (LEDGER E15f-2); C. 23 q. 5 c. 43 (*De Liguribus*), editor (foot-note 13, *Cap. XLIII*; modern form ours; LEDGER E15f-3).
[^4]: **S.** C. 23 q. 5 c. 20 (*Principes*), editor (foot-note 15, *Cap. XX*; modern form ours; LEDGER E15f-4). **R.** B in marg. at 349.11: *Quod prelati delinquentes a regibus sunt corrigendi, ut est dignum* ("that delinquent prelates are to be corrected by kings, as is fitting").
[^5]: **S.** The hierarchic acts of purging and illuminating (and perfecting) of Pseudo-Dionysius, *Celestial Hierarchy* 3: to be checked (LEDGER E15f-5). **G.** *Virtus* here is the power (faculty), not the moral virtue.
[^6]: **S.** Isa. 14:12–15, the fall of Lucifer.
[^7]: **S.** *Lincolniensis* is Robert Grosseteste, bishop of Lincoln; Loserth gives no place, and the passage is to be checked (LEDGER E15f-6).
[^8]: **S.** Apoc. 12:7 (DR "Michael and his angels fought with the dragon"). Wyclif puts Michael among the angels "of the lower order" (the archangels); *confirmati* is "confirmed" in grace.
[^9]: **C.** *Insteti* is first-person perfect of *instare*: Wyclif reports an objection he once raised himself.
[^10]: **C.** *Coloracius*, the comparative adverb beside *color* (a plausible ground, as in "colour of title"): the cleric's claim to lordship would rest on a better colour than his claim to serve.
[^11]: **C.** *Secundum quid* qualifies *civile dominium* across the page break: the king's civil lordship over the clergy is only in a certain respect, in their goods of nature and fortune.
[^12]: **C.** *Signis*: the scholastic "stages" (*signa*, as of an order of priority), here the three grades of diocesan, metropolitan and pope.
[^13]: **C.** After the long *posito … periculum*, *regum autem est* gives the apodosis: *autem* resumes, rendered "then".
[^14]: **T.** A A₁ lack *quod si clerus … omnimode est* (351.28); Loserth prints it, and it is translated.
[^15]: **C.** *Redempcio*: the buying off of a sentence for money; not the *redempcio temporalium* of the glossary. **C.** *Stat quod*, "it is consistent that", is rendered "it may be that" in all four reasons.
[^16]: **T.** At 352.4 B reads *Deum et legem* (as it seems, "against God and the law"); the print is translated.
[^17]: **C.** *Limitatur de quanto*: the pope's jurisdiction from the law of Christ reaches only so far as he teaches that law; "limit" in the restricting sense.
[^18]: **S.** D. 96 c. 14 (*Constantinus*), the Donation of Constantine, a *palea*; c. 13, also a *palea*, begins *Constantinus imperator* too, so the incipit fits either. Loserth gives no reference here (modern form ours; LEDGER E15f-7).
