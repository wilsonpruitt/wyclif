## The volume

*Tractatus de eucharistia* and *Tractatus de penitencia sive de confessione*, ed. Johann Loserth (Wyclif
Society, 1892), from the plates of archive.org `latinworks13wycl` (**leaf = page + 71**; leaf images in
`text/eucharistia/raw/leaves/`). Three texts:

Chunk ids: `prol-a … prol-c` the compendium, `01-a … 09-n` the nine chapters, `pen01 … pen06` *De
penitencia*, `quest` the *Questio*.

- **pp. 1–10, the compendium** (*Sentencia tractatus de Eukaristia in compendio*, under "Prologus"): a
  medieval summary of the nine chapters that came down with the work. Translate it like the text.
- **pp. 11–326, the *Tractatus de eucharistia* (maior)**, nine long chapters. Written in Wyclif's last
  years, before 1382–83 (Loserth, Introd. §4: it shares passages word for word with *Sermones* III, and
  it avoids the words *substancia* and *materialis* of the bread, which Wyclif says in *Sermo* III.54 he
  has stopped using). Wyclif's case against the "modern" doctrine: that the consecrated host is in its
  nature bread and wine and sacramentally, figuratively, the body and blood of Christ; that an accident
  without a subject is impossible; that the decree *Ego Berengarius* (*De cons.* D. 2 c. 42) is the
  faith of the ancient Roman church; against the friars, Thomas, Scotus and "the sect of the signs".
  A **treatise**: Wyclif speaks in the first person, states conclusions, raises objections and answers
  them. The tractatus minor (the *Confessio*) is NOT in this volume (Shirley, *Fasc. Ziz.*).
- **pp. 327–348, *De eucharistia et penitencia sive de confessione*** in six chapters (only ch. 1 is on
  the eucharist; the rest is penance and auricular confession), then the short ***Questio ad fratres de
  sacramento altaris*** (pp. 347–348, its first part only: Loserth notes that the rest of the
  manuscript's text is not on the sacrament, and is Hus's).

**Sigla. ⚠ They differ between the two works.** *De eucharistia* (Introd. §5): **A** Vienna 3927;
**B** Vienna 3932; **C** Vienna 1387 ("the best of the three" Vienna MSS, correcting A and B); **D** Prague
Univ. IV D 22 (1404–05, Andreas of Kouřim); **E** Prague Univ. XI E 3 (1460; Loserth: "the best and most
reliable text of all the five"). Loserth's lettering "does not in any way refer to their respective
value". The Vienna MSS are one group, the Prague MSS another. *De penitencia* (Introd. §6): **A** Vienna
3927; **B** Bautzen, Gersdorf Library M. St. 80.7; **C** Vienna 1337; **D** Prague Univ. III G 11; **E**
Vienna 3980; **F** Vienna 4527. Cite variants with the sigla of the work you are in. Marginal notes in the
manuscripts that Loserth prints ("F in marg.", "DB in marg.") are reception: an **R** note where they
show how a reader took the text.

**The page foot** (in your brief as "apparatus", by page) has two blocks, both keyed to the **line**:
the variants (`19. C: ecclesie in marg. alia manu.`) and Loserth's source references. The OCR of it is
rough: read it for sense, not letter by letter, and read the plate foot where a variant matters.

2. **Connectives carry the argument.** ⛔ Never omit one, never turn one into filler.

   | Latin | English | Never |
   |---|---|---|
   | enim, nam | for | "now", "indeed" |
   | ergo, igitur, ideo | therefore, so, then | "now", "and", "indeed" |
   | sed / autem, marking the **minor premiss or next step** | now | (omitted) |
   | sed / autem, adversative | but | (omitted) |
   | autem, continuative | and | (omitted), and **not "now"** |
   | cum (concessive / causal) | although / since | "whenever" |
   | tamen | yet, nevertheless | "therefore" |
   | item | again, also | "now" |
   | vero (continuative) | and; but (adversative) | "now", "truly" |
   | verumtamen | nevertheless | "but truly" |
   | unde | hence, so | "whence" (archaic), "where" |
   | per idem | by the same token | "in the same way" |
   | ymmo | indeed, rather (correcting upward) | (omitted) |
   | patet; constat | it is clear; it is evident | |

   "Now" is reserved for the minor premiss, or the step that brings a new premiss to bear. An *autem*
   that only extends the same step is "and". ⚠ The commonest error in *De ecclesia* (close-out, ~50 of
   69 connective fixes): a *sed* that brings the minor premiss of a syllogism closed by *ergo / ideo /
   igitur* rendered "but". Before writing "but" for a *sed*, look ahead for the *ergo*: if one follows,
   it is "now".
3. **Ordinary words with a medieval sense:** GLOSSARY §D; look up any word whose classical and
   Wyclifite senses may differ, and propose additions.
4. **Syntax:** construe before rendering (main verb, subject, what every relative and genitive hangs
   on). Keep quantifier order where it is meaning. Keep Wyclif's long periods where English tolerates
   them. **A doubtful construal is never chosen silently:** translate the likelier reading and write a
   **C** note giving the alternative.
   ⚠ **Predication is the matter here, as in *De incarnacione*.** *Hoc est corpus meum*, *panis est
   corpus Christi*, *hoc sacramentum est panis*: Wyclif argues about what the pronoun demonstrates and
   in what mode the predicate holds. Keep subject and predicate in his order, keep *est* as "is" (never
   "exists", "becomes" or "represents" unless the Latin says so), and keep the mode adverb where he puts
   it (*est sacramentaliter corpus Christi* "is sacramentally the body of Christ"). ⛔ Never soften "is"
   into "signifies" or harden "figuratively" into "really": the difference is the treatise's matter.
   Demonstrative pronouns (*hoc*, *istud*) under discussion: render "this", and keep their gender
   visible in a C note where the argument turns on it (*hoc* neuter against *hic*, 348).
   **Propositions in indirect speech** whose sense is under discussion: render as the quoted proposition
   ("who say 'this is an accident without a subject'"), not "who say that …", which asserts it.

## Glossary (the 435 rows these chunks use; the full file is GLOSSARY.md)

# GLOSSARY — FROZEN 2026-09-28 (Wilson's pilot review)

*One Latin term, one English rendering, every time, across the whole corpus. A reader must be able
to recognise the same word coming back, because Wyclif's arguments turn on it. A rendering changes
only by a dated decision in CONVENTIONS' decision log, never silently in a chunk. Entries may be
*added* by recorded decision as new terms appear. **Tag keys:** a chunk tags a term as
`[English]{g:latin-form}` (CONVENTIONS §11), and the key must match a Latin form in one of the rows
below. These rows are also the source of the site's hover glossary. Where a term has two senses,
the entry says so and each chunk's note picks one. A term marked **CONTEXTUAL** holds together
senses English splits: its English is chosen by context, under the dikaiosune rule (CONVENTIONS §6).*

**Sources of the decisions.** "Pilot" = settled in `pilots/` this session. "T. p. N" = Mark
Thakkar, "Duces caecorum," *Vivarium* 58 (2020), page N (`reference/`), which documents the
errors of the two most recent translations. His corrections are adopted here unless an entry says
otherwise.

**Orthography.** Headwords are given in the Society editions' medieval spelling (*eciam*,
*racio*, *quiditas*, *ymmo*), because that is what the translator will meet on the page.

# *De eucharistia*, chapter 2, part 6: [TITLE]

## ¶1 · 48.23–50.27

> [p. 48] Hic videtur michi quod ecclesia primitiva illud non posuit, sed ecclesia novella, ut quidam infideliter et infundabiliter sompniantes baptizarunt terminum et fantasiarunt multa false ad onus ecclesie. Primo ergo videtur quod non pocius credendum est dicte decretali vel suis autoribus quam decreto ecclesie in persona Beringarii, quia (ut supra dictum est) in isto supereminebant sanctitate et sciencia papa cum sinodo magis credibili, qui locuti sunt conformius ad scripturas, ad sanctorum doctorum sentencias et plus consone racioni et preter hec expressius et [p. 49] facilius detexerunt credendum. Cum enim transsubstanciari sit terminus magistralis, licet fideli trahere eius significacionem ad quemcunque sensum katholicum particularem et sic videtur ambigue et incomplete innuere fidem ecclesie de Eukaristia. Aliud autem decretum explicat quomodo papa et sinodus decreverunt in Beringario quomodo panis et vinum que in altari ponuntur remanent post consecracionem non solum sacramentum sed eciam verum corpus Christi et sanguis. Unde illud credendum ut fidem misit papa iocunde diversis ecclesiis. Cum ergo signum materiale decretalium vel decretorum sit inpertinens fidei quam sentit ecclesia et tanta vel maior relucebat in ecclesia priori auctoritas, videtur quod tollitur evidencia capta ex decretali ecclesie de transsubstanciacione que tolleret naturam panis et vini post consecracionem hostie remanere. Quod si dicatur doctores glosantes ut glosam communem et Archidiaconum cum multis theologis intelligere per panem solummodo formam panis, patet quod coloracius diceret expositor prioris decreti quod isti expositores intelligunt per formam panis et vini realiter essenciam vel naturam panis et vini que est realiter ipsum corpus; et sic est locucio decreti expressior glosa sua. Confirmatur primo per hoc quod inevitabiliter sequitur quantumcunque rudi loico: Sub forma panis et vini est corpus Christi sacramentaliter, ergo ille panis et illud vinum sunt substancia illi forme et per consequens illa existente forma accidentali panis et vinum remanent maioris permanencie illa forma. Secundo per hoc quod CXIVᶜⁱᵐ episcopi non congregarentur ad discernendum quod panis et vinum que in altari ponuntur manerent post consecracionem, si intelligerent [p. 50] eius contradiccionem quod non panis et vinum remanent post consecracionem illud sacramentum altaris sed accidencia que quondam fuerunt panis et vinum; non enim illuderent populo, dicentes insolite quod accidencia ponuntur intendentes contradictorium dicti sui. Sic enim facile foret glosare quoscunque testes fidei per contradictorium, fidem ecclesie subvertendo; videat ergo primo glosator quomodo nullus diceret panem et vinum poni in altari ante consecracionem, hoc est, illa accidencia non illam substanciam; videat secundo quod eque falsum est illa accidencia esse corpus Christi et sanguinem sicut illas substancias, et eque vere figurant ille substancie corpus Christi ut accidencia; videat tercio quomodo scriptura sacra a qua exemplatur ista locucio non intelligit per panem et vinum illa accidencia sed ipsas essencias. Quare ergo non sic intelligeret decretum cum sanctis doctoribus? Tercio confirmatur ut sepe quod verba decreti magis concordant cum scriptura et sanctis doctoribus, cum racione et sensu; ideo tam maturi et sancti episcopi vel intelligerent famose per verba fidei vel exprimerent sensum suum. Sic enim explanat decretum multociens quomodo debet intelligi quod panis est corpus Christi, hoc est, signum sacramentale eius; qui modus loquendi fuit famosus in scriptura et tunc acceptus a populo, sed post declinavit ad predicacionem ydempticam.

[ENGLISH]

## ¶2 · 50.28–51.17

> [p. 50] Unde hodie loquendum foret conformiter sensui exposito et scandalo in fide subiecto, ymmo Christus non omisit sic loqui propter scandalum, ut dicitur Joh. VIᵒ; sed subtiliter exponit se ipsum, ut docet decretum Augustini super Psalmo IVᵒ, quod ponitur [p. 51] in De Consecracione, distinccione IIᵃ, Prima quidem: Spiritualiter intelligite que locutus sum: Non hoc corpus quod videtis manducaturi estis nec bibituri sanguinem quem fusuri sunt qui me crucifigent. Sacramentum aliquod vobis commendavi, spiritualiter intellectum vivificabit vos. Unde ad intelligendum quod non illo modo quo ipsi putant erogat corpus suum dicit ipsum ascensurum in celum integrum. Cum videritis, inquit, filium hominis ascendentem, ubi erat prius, spiritus est qui vivificat. Antiquo ergo decreto patrum pocius credendum est, cum quinquagesima distinccione, Domino sancto, sub auctoritate Isidori sic habetur: Quocienscunque in gestis conciliorum discors sentencia invenitur, illius concilii magis teneatur sentencia cuius antiquior et pocior extat auctoritas. Cum ergo sentencia decreti sit propinquior fonti scripture, videtur quod illa in materia fidei pocius est tenenda.

[ENGLISH]

# *De eucharistia*, chapter 2, part 7: [TITLE]

## ¶1 · 51.18–53.4

> [p. 51] Ulterius quantum ad descripcionem transsubstanciacionis, videtur quod capit calumpniam, supponendo (ut adversarii fatentur) quod illa conversio non ponit anichilacionem nec ydemptificacionem panis cum corpore Christi; nam omne accidens oportet habere subiectum, omnis talis transubstanciacio est accidens, ergo omnem talem oportet habere subiectum; licet autem fateantur accidens esse sine subiecto, tamen dicunt quod ipsum oportet habere subiectum in principio, ymmo (ut infecte loquendo dicunt) quod ipsum est subiectum ad quemlibet sui punctum, non ergo est sine subiecto, ymmo omne accidens respectivum cuiusmodi est omnis mutacio, concedunt non [p. 52] posse esse sine subiecto suo, ergo oportet dari aliquid quod subicitur transsubstanciacioni passive; illud non est accidens, quia ipsum manet non transsubstanciatum, nec est panis vel vinum, quia, cum oportet transsubstanciacionem illam esse fundatam, ne cogamur concedere corpus Christi vel hostiam successive confici, patet quod pro instanti transsubstanciacionis nec panis transsubstanciatus nec vinum est secundum aliquam sui partem, et per consequens nullum accidens tunc subiectat; illud deductum est in tractatu De Anichilacione movisse philosophos ad ponendum materiam primam generacioni subiectam, quia fingere unam rem prius inauditam sine fundamento foret illusio; ponens ergo transsubstanciacionem diceret quid est, ubi est et quomodo sustentatur; cum enim sit forma, oportet quod aliquod subiectum informet et pulcrificet, cum tam miraculose producitur. Unde licet quondam laboraverim ad describendum transsubstanciacionem concorditer ad sensum prioris ecclesie, tamen modo videtur michi quod contrariantur, posteriori ecclesia oberrante. Si enim transsubstanciacio sit cessio unius substancie alteri quoad locum, sic quod una substancia transsubstanciata remanet per eundem locum quo prius, et substancia dignior sit per illum locum sacramentaliter cui subordinatur substancia prior ut signum, tunc contingit quod panis transsubstancietur pro instanti quo non mutetur, sed remanet panis subiectus accidentibus post consecracionem; quod dicitur repugnare sentencie decretalis; ideo dimitto nunc concordancias legum istarum et voco transsubstanciacionem conversionem [p. 53] unius substancie in aliam, ut semen convertitur in corpus vivum, homo convertitur in terram, et sic generaliter quando unum corpus ex alio generatur.

[ENGLISH]

## ¶2 · 53.5–53.24

> [p. 53] Ulterius quoad ydolatriam patet quod populus est instruendus ne credat illud accidentatum quod sentit esse ydemptice corpus Christi; et hoc est necessarium secundum omnem opinionem istius materie, quia ecclesia posterior fatetur quod remanet unum album, rotundum et durum, quod non potest esse corpus Christi; et revera cum illud sit per se sensibile et per consequens populus est ita pronus vel pronior ad credendum illud esse corpus Christi ut panem, patet quod non remanet illis punctus coloris ad putandum panem desinere secundum quamlibet eius partem, ne adorando panem ut corpus Christi ydolatria committatur; per idem enim tolleretur quodcunque accidens sensibile, ymmo modo superadditur illusio de subiecto et accidente suo; et maior pars populi credit infideliter et irreverencius quod illud remanens quod ipsi ponunt accidens sit ydemptice corpus Christi. Constat quidem quod accidens est magis extraneum a natura corporis Christi vel sanguinis, quam natura panis aut vini. De transsubstanciacione autem patet diffuse posterius.
