# *De benedicta incarnacione*, chapter 9, part 4: the assuming is the assumed; Christ's twofold sonship and twofold assumption

## ¶1 · 154.22–156.16

> [p. 154] Ad illud conceditur quod sicut natura divina est generans et generatum, et natura specifica humana cum sit homo pater et eius filius, et per consequens efficiens⁹ et effectum; sic Verbum commune¹⁰ ad hec tria deitas, corpus, et anima est simul assumens et assumptum; et racione communicacionis personalis, que distinguitur a duabus prioribus, in quibus est communicacio naturarum, suppositis¹¹ istis, conceditur quod assumens et assumptum, quamvis in Trinitate non generans, sed illud quod est gignens est genitum; et sic non communis humanitas, sed persona, gignit et gignitur. Ideo aliter [p. 155] loquendum est in communicacione personarum quam communicacione¹ naturarum eidem supposito. Verumptamen licet Verbum sit assumens et assumptum, tamen non assumitur, sed est natura que assumitur. Et conformiter conceditur quod causans est suum causatum, et² creans est suum creatum.² Nec ex illo sequitur quod idem secundum³ idem³ causat se, cum Verbum solum secundum naturam divinam causat se secundum naturam humanam. Unde non sequitur, quod Deus assumitur, quia predicatum assumpcionis limitat ad naturam creatam excludendo actum persone vel nature divine assumpto competere: et aliter non esset concedendum quod Deus assumitur, nisi quia vel⁴ natura vel persona divina assumitur, quorum⁵ utrumque cum sit impossibile docet negandum esse quod Deus assumitur.⁵ Et patet quod non est color concedendi quod natura divina creatur, assumitur, patitur, moritur etc. licet Christus, qui est natura divina, predicaciones tales recipiat; cum non secundum deitatem sed secundum humanitatem illas suscipiat. Et in isto sophismate⁶ cecatus est ille Clericus in fide devius,⁷ contra quem Anselmus fecit librum De Incarnacione; credidit enim si natura divina sit homo, tunc et omne, quod est natura divina, est homo, et⁸ per consequens confuse quelibet persona divina est quelibet, vel tres persone⁸ divine sunt tres homines; ignoravit enim naturam rerum communium. Licet autem negetur quod Deus assumitur, conceditur tamen quod Deus subicitur, humiliatur, et sic de similibus predicatis formalibus; generari quidem vel nasci non solum competit supposito sed nature. Ideo conceditur quod Christus, sicut est duplex essencia vel natura, sic habet duplex esse et duplicem nativitatem: et⁹ iuxta illos qui restringunt racionem filii ad suppositum vel personam, tunc tantum una est filiacio in Christo, sicut tantum inest sibi una personalitas, ita quod filiacio [p. 156] humana accidit¹ filiacioni divine, sicut singularitas hominis accidit personalitati² divine. Et sic filiacio humana in Christo simpliciter³ non est filiacio; cum, multiplicata filiacione que est racio filii, multiplicarentur et filii; sed est genitura temporalis. Aliis autem plus⁴ placet, cum quibus et michi, quod est dare in Christo⁵ duplicem filiacionem, sicut et duplicem nativitatem, non autem duplicem personalitatem, cuius racio est nasci, sicut et mori competit persone Christi⁶ racione humanitatis et non per se⁶ primo illi nature. Ergo⁷ per idem vel evidencius filiacio competit supposito primo racione nature, et non primo illi nature.⁷ Et hoc sentenciant moderni, qui dicunt, quod natura assumpta non est filius, sed per eius dimissionem⁸ Maria potuit⁹ duos filios habuisse sine actu gignicionis duplici, vel sine hoc quod illa natura fuit filius Marie pro instanti aliquo vite sue, cum senex inciperet esse filius hominis, quando inciperet dimitti.

To that it is granted that, as the divine [nature]{g:natura} is generating and generated, and the human [nature of the species]{g:natura-specifica}, since it is a man [who is] father and his son, is consequently efficient and effect; so the [Word]{g:verbum}, common to these three, [the Godhead]{g:deitas}, body and soul, is at once assuming and assumed; and by reason of the personal communication, which is distinguished from the two prior ones, in which there is communication of natures to [supposits]{g:suppositum},[^1] it is granted that the assuming [is] the assumed, although in the Trinity [it is] not [granted that] the generating [is the generated], but that that which is begetting is begotten; and so not the common [humanity]{g:humanitas} but the [person]{g:persona} begets and is begotten.[^2] Therefore one must [155] speak otherwise in the communication of persons than in the communication of natures to the same supposit. Nevertheless, although the Word is the assuming and the assumed, yet he is not assumed, but it is a nature that is assumed.[^3] And conformably it is granted that the causing is its caused, and the creating is its [created]{g:creatus}. Nor does it follow from that that the same thing causes itself according to the same, since the Word causes himself as to the human nature only as to the divine nature. Hence it does not follow that God is assumed, because the predicate of [assumption]{g:assumpcio} limits to the created nature, excluding that an act of the divine person or nature belongs to what is assumed: and otherwise "God is assumed" would not be to be granted except because either the divine nature or the divine person is assumed; and since each of these is impossible, it teaches that "God is assumed" must be denied.[^4] And it is clear that there is no [colour]{g:color} for granting that the divine nature is created, is assumed, suffers, dies, etc., although Christ, who is the divine nature, receives such predications; since he takes them up not [as to the Godhead]{g:secundum-deitatem} but as to the humanity.[^5] And in this [sophism]{g:sophisma} that cleric astray in the faith was blinded, against whom Anselm made the book *De incarnatione*; for he believed that if the divine nature is man, then everything too that is the divine nature is man, and consequently, [indistinctly]{g:confuse}, any divine person is any [other], or the three divine persons are three men; for he was ignorant of the nature of [common things]{g:res-communes}.[^6] And although it is denied that God is assumed, it is nevertheless granted that God is subjected, is [humbled]{g:humiliacio}, and so of like formal predicates; to be generated, indeed, or to be born belongs not only to the supposit but to the nature. Therefore it is granted that Christ, as he is a twofold essence or nature, so has a twofold [being]{g:esse} and a twofold [nativity]{g:nativitas}: and according to those who restrict the account of a son to the supposit or person, there is then only one [sonship]{g:filiacio} in Christ, as only one [personality]{g:personalitas} is in him, so that the human [156] sonship befalls the divine sonship, as the singularity of a man befalls the divine personality. And so the human sonship in Christ is not, [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}, a sonship; since, if sonship, which is the account of a son, were multiplied, sons too would be multiplied; but it is a temporal begetting. But it pleases others more, and with them me too, that [one may posit]{g:est-dare} in Christ a twofold sonship, as also a twofold nativity, but not a twofold personality; the account of which [sonship] is to be born, as also to die belongs to the person of Christ by reason of the humanity, and not [of itself]{g:per-se} first to that nature.[^7] Therefore, by the same token or more evidently, sonship belongs to the supposit first by reason of the nature, and not first to that nature.[^8] And this [the moderns]{g:moderni} [pronounce]{g:sentenciare}, who say that the assumed nature is not a son, but that by [its laying-aside]{g:dimissio} Mary could have had two sons without a twofold act of begetting, or without that nature's having been the son of Mary for any instant of its life, since an old man would begin to be the son of a man when it began to be laid aside.[^9]

## ¶2 · 156.17–157.11

> [p. 156] Licet autem talia ludicria sint impossibilia, tamen videtur probabile quod Christus racione humanitatis habeat filiacionem humanam, sicut racione eiusdem nature habet nativitatem¹⁰ temporalem; potissime cum filiacio sit realis relacio persone¹¹ in genere sicut alia accidencia Christo humanitus dependent a parente temporali: non sic autem personalitas absoluta, cum persona dicitur ethimologice per se una: illa ergo non dependet relative ab extremo, nec ab actu temporali, ut nativitas, concepcio, sive gignicio. Unde vel oportet dici¹² quod filiacio eterna accidentaliter sit filiacio hominis, vel quod filiacio hominis non sit filiacio. Secundum non est dandum cum iste terminus hominis vel humanus non distrahit. Primum autem ex hoc videtur deficere quod filiacio eterna non potest fieri filiacio temporalis. Christus ergo habet in se tot filiaciones quot parentes, sicut Petrus in se tot proporciones duplas et similitudines [p. 157] quot rebus ipse¹ duplus vel similis est. Et sic¹ cum Ioseph fuit pater eius, ut patet² testimonio beate virginis Luce 2°; ‘ecce pater tuus et ego dolentes querebamus te;’ patet quod Christus habuit ad istum parentem vel nutricium filiacionem legitimam putativam; non autem fuit filius adoptivus, cum non potuit esse filius perdicionis, et illa potencia vel imperfeccio presupponitur ad filium adoptivum. Verumptamen ‘predestinatus est Filius Dei,’ ut dicit Apostolus ad Rom. 1°. illa³ quidem creatura ordinata est eternaliter esse Dei Filius naturalis ut³ sepe dicit Augustinus, et recitat eum Magister 3°⁴ Sentenciarum Dist. 6ᵃ.

But although such playthings are impossible, yet it seems probable that Christ by reason of the humanity has a human sonship, as by reason of the same nature he has a temporal nativity; most of all since sonship is a real relation of the person in its category, as the other accidents depend in Christ [humanly]{g:humanitus} on a temporal parent: but not so the absolute personality, since a person is called, by etymology, "one by itself": that, therefore, does not depend relatively on a term, nor on a temporal act, such as nativity, conception or begetting.[^10] Hence it must be said either that the eternal sonship is accidentally the sonship of a man, or that the sonship of a man is not a sonship. The second is not to be posited, since this term "of a man" or "human" is not [a diminishing qualifier]{g:distrahens}. And the first seems to fail from this, that the eternal sonship cannot become a temporal sonship. Christ, therefore, has in himself as many sonships as parents, as Peter has in himself as many double proportions and likenesses [157] as there are things to which he is double or like. And so, since Joseph was his father, as is clear from the testimony of the blessed virgin, Luke 2[:48], "behold thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing", it is clear that Christ had toward this parent or foster-father a lawful reputed sonship; but he was not an adoptive son, since he could not be a son of perdition, and that [potency]{g:potencia} or imperfection is presupposed to an adoptive son.[^11] Nevertheless, "he was predestinated the Son of God", as the Apostle says, Romans 1[:4]: that [creature]{g:creatura}, indeed, was ordained eternally to be the natural Son of God, as Augustine often says, and [the Master]{g:magister} recites him in book 3 of the *Sentences*, distinction 6.[^12]

## ¶3 · 157.12–157.18

> [p. 157] Redeundo ergo ad propositum conceditur quod Christus assumitur, sicut incepit esse et aliquando non fuit; et⁵ negatur⁶ quod Deus assumitur vel aliquando non fuit,⁵ licet ille sit Christus. Et movet me inter alia anathema, quod Augustinus in Exposicione Fidei illud negantibus imprecatur: ‘si quis, inquit, dixerit atque crediderit hominem⁷ Iesum Christum a Filio Dei assumptum non fuisse, anathema sit.’

Returning, therefore, to the purpose, it is granted that Christ is assumed, as he began to be and at some time was not; and it is denied that God is assumed or at some time was not, although he is Christ.[^13] And among other things there moves me the anathema which Augustine, in the *Exposition of the Faith*, calls down on those who deny it: "if anyone," he says, "says and believes that the man Jesus Christ was not assumed by the Son of God, let him be anathema."[^14]

## ¶4 · 157.19–158.9

> [p. 157] Quod si obicitur quod alius sit sensus quem⁸ verba pretendunt, dico quod ad sensum catholicum, quem et⁹ ipse conceperat, loquor. Ego puto quidem me in recta logica et philosophia huius sancti dimissis sophismatibus scripture¹⁰ sacre contrariis educari.¹⁰ Christus ergo assumpsit hominem et non Deum, licet ille homo sit Deus. Nec est color negandi quod Deus sit exinanitus,¹¹ passus, humiliatus, et sic de predicatis creatis. Et¹² sic negatur,¹² quod Deus assumptus est; predicatum enim assumi limitat ad supposicionem simplicem pro¹³ natura, ut—si Deus assumptus est, tunc est deitas—sicut¹³ homo assumitur ex hoc quod humanitas est assumpta. Ulterius cum¹⁴ assumpcio respicit [p. 158] naturam, conceditur quod, sicut duplex natura a Christo assumitur, scilicet corpus et anima, sic¹ duplex fuit¹ in Christo simplex assumpcio et una completa ad humanitatem principaliter terminata. Nec² exinde sequitur quod aliqua illarum posset esse non existente reliqua; sed³ ordo nature extitit inter³ illas, ut assumpcio anime fuit prima; et mediante illa assumptum est corpus; et tercio ex his unita est completa humanitas, et totum in eodem instanti temporis, quo iste nature incipiunt esse.⁴

And if [it is objected]{g:obicitur} that the sense is other than the words purport, I say that I speak to the catholic sense, which he too had conceived.[^15] I think, indeed, that I am trained in the right logic and philosophy of this saint, sophisms contrary to holy scripture being laid aside. Christ, therefore, [assumed]{g:assumere} man and not God, although that man is God. Nor is there colour for denying that God was [emptied]{g:exinanire}, suffered, was humbled, and so of the created predicates. And so it is denied that God was assumed; for the predicate "to be assumed" limits to [simple supposition]{g:supposicio-simplex} for the nature, so that, if God was assumed, then it is the Godhead [that was assumed], as man is assumed from the fact that the humanity is assumed.[^16] Further, since assumption regards [158] the nature, it is granted that, as a twofold nature is assumed by Christ, namely body and soul, so there was in Christ a twofold simple assumption, and one complete [assumption] terminated principally at the humanity. Nor does it follow thence that either of them could be while the other did not exist; but an order of nature stood between them, so that the assumption of the soul was first, and by means of it the body was assumed, and thirdly from these the complete humanity was united; and the whole in the same instant of time in which these natures begin to be.[^17]

## ¶5 · 158.10–158.23

> [p. 158] Quoad ultimum de ydemptitate constat michi quod numerose evidencie volant hodie⁵ ad probandum per locum a simili quod quidlibet⁶ potest ydemptificari cuilibet, si natura⁷ prope nichil potest fieri⁷ Deus: exinde enim concluditur quod Deus quamlibet naturam creatam potest assumere, et sic de multis inaniter geminatis. Utrum autem ydemptitas personalis naturarum disparium in Christo sit maior quam ydemptitas personarum⁸ in eadem natura simplici, est difficultas. Certum tamen est quod iste ydemptitates sunt disparium racionum et ydemptitas Trinitatis in prima essencia, cum presupponitur ad omnem aliam, est maior quam reliqua, licet non in proporcione racionali.⁹ Quoad instancias factas per locum a simili, patet ex dictis quod similitudo non est probabilis.

As to the last, about identity, it is evident to me that numerous [evidences]{g:evidencia} fly about today to prove, by the topic from likeness, that anything whatever can be [identified]{g:ydemptificare} with anything whatever, if a nature next to nothing can become God: for thence it is [inferred]{g:concludere} that God can assume any created nature whatever, and so of many things idly doubled.[^18] But whether the personal identity of unlike natures in Christ is greater than the identity of persons in the same simple nature is a difficulty. Yet it is certain that these identities are of unlike kinds, and the identity of the Trinity in the first essence, since it is presupposed to every other, is greater than the other, though not in a natural proportion.[^19] As to the [objections]{g:instancia} made by the topic from likeness, it is clear from what has been said that the likeness is not probable.
