# *De eucharistia*, chapter 4, part 7: the Avignon decretal against *Ego Berengarius*, and why the quiddity of the host comes first

## ¶1 · 106.21–107.4

> [p. 106] Sed adhuc prodest curiam Avinonicam intromittere se circa tales materias quas ignorat et ignoranter testetur in aliquibus veritatem, ut exemplet nobis quomodo de talibus licitum est tractare, et ut videamus quam frivolum fundamentum est: si clerus Avinonicus sic diffinit in fide, tunc oportet omnes christianos sic credere. Nam predictum decretum ecclesie Romane in persona Beringarii et predicte decretales cleri Avinonici videntur expresse contradicere, ideo foret opus magistrale specialiter theologi aut decretiste si poterit harum ecclesiarum sentencias concordare, vel si non potest cui istarum sentenciarum credendum [p. 107] est dimissa reliqua tanquam heretica katholice declarare. Ego autem primum abnuens tanquam inutile non dubito de secundo quin decreti sentencia sit satis katholica ad sensum expositum.

But it is still of profit that [the Avignon curia]{g:curia-avinonica} meddles in such matters, of which it is ignorant, and ignorantly testifies to the truth in some things: so that it may give us an example of how it is lawful to treat such things, and so that we may see how frivolous a foundation this is, "if the Avignon [clergy]{g:clerus} defines thus in faith, then all Christians must believe thus."[^1] For the aforesaid [decree]{g:decretum} of the Roman [church]{g:ecclesia} in the person of Berengar and the aforesaid [decretals]{g:decretalis} of the Avignon clergy seem expressly to contradict each other; so it would be a masterly work, especially for a theologian or a [decretist]{g:decretiste}, if he could bring the [teachings]{g:sentencia} of these churches into agreement; or, if he cannot, to [make clear]{g:declarare} in a catholic way which of these teachings is to be believed, [107] the other being laid aside as heretical.[^2] But I, refusing the first as useless, do not doubt as to the second that the teaching of the decree is catholic enough in the [meaning]{g:sensus} set out.[^3]

## ¶2 · 107.5–107.22

> [p. 107] Ad quod preter evidencias predictas noto cronicam quam dicit Cistrensis libro VI°, capitulo XXXV°: Nicolaus, inquit, secundus Benedicto eiecto, sedit in papatu annis pene duobus, cuius diebus Gallicana ecclesia plurimum perturbatur per Beringarium Turonensem qui asserebat eukaristiam non verum corpus Christi esse sed figuram. Contra quem Nicholaus papa concilium celebravit in quo Beringarius errorem suum retractavit, sicut habetur in De Consecracione distinccione IIᵃ, capitulo Ego Beringarius. Lamfrancus eciam Beccensis abbas tonantem librum contra eundem edidit quem Scintillarium intitulavit; post hec Beringarius sancte vixit et obitum suum predixit. Ex istis videtur quod ecclesia quando plus habuit sollicitudinem de fide quam de seculari dominio, ponderavit errorem et fidelem revocacionem Beringarii que ex vite sue sanctitate sequente et dacione prophecie de suo obitu declaratur.

For this, besides the [evidences]{g:evidencia} already given, I note the chronicle which the Chester monk tells, book 6, chapter 35: "Nicholas the second," he says, "after Benedict was cast out, sat in the [papacy]{g:papatus} almost two years; in whose days the Gallican church was greatly troubled by Berengar of Tours, who asserted that the eucharist was not the true [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} but a [figure]{g:figura}. Against him [Pope]{g:papa} Nicholas held a council, in which Berengar retracted his error, as is found in *De consecratione*, distinction 2, chapter *Ego Berengarius*. Lanfranc also, abbot of Bec, published a thundering book against him, which he entitled *Scintillarius*; after this Berengar lived a holy life and foretold his death."[^4] From these things it seems that the church, when it had more care for the faith than for secular [lordship]{g:dominium}, weighed the error and the faithful recantation of Berengar, which is made clear by the holiness of the life that followed and by the gift of prophecy about his death.[^5]

## ¶3 · 107.23–108.22

> [p. 107] Unde glosa ordinaria supra 3° decretalium recitat tres opiniones de quidditate hostie consecrate, et idem recitat glosa ordinaria super primo capitulo De Consecracione, distinccione IIᵃ, quarum tercia opinio dicit quod post consecracionem remanent panis et [p. 108] vinum hostia consecrata et allegat utraque glosa ad hoc illud capitulum: Ego Beringarius, quia ista confessio indubie fuit sua et confirmata ex decreto sinodi Romane ecclesie, quod est longe coloracius quam decretalis ecclesie Avinonice in hac parte. Et ex istis videtur, cum textus legis ecclesie dicit quod remanent post consecracionem panis et vinum, et idem recitant opinative et non reprobant doctores precipui huius legis, quod doctores novelli eciam episcopi non presumerent istam antiquam sinodi sentenciam condempnare, potissime cum post questionem si est de hostia velit homo nolit, oportet volentem procedere in eius noticiam ‘quid est’, quia aliter balbuciendo ignarus vocis proprie procedet in sequenti secunda questione; ideo solebam querere ab eis qui volunt in ista materia determinare et impetere alios super heretica pravitate quid est sacramentum altaris, quod cum ignorant, patet quod necesse est eos sequencia ignorare. Vix enim alter papa cum suis complicibus, omnes episcopi Anglie cum suis doctoribus sciunt dicere quid est hostia consecrata; quomodo ergo diffinirent ipsi in heresi de tercia hostie questione?

Hence the ordinary [gloss]{g:glosa} on book 3 of the Decretals [recites]{g:recitare} three [opinions]{g:opinio} on the quiddity of the [consecrated host]{g:hostia}, and the ordinary gloss on the first chapter of *De consecratione*, distinction 2, recites the same; of which the third opinion says that after the [consecration]{g:consecracio} the [bread]{g:panis} and [108] [wine]{g:vinum} remain, the consecrated host;[^6] and each gloss [alleges]{g:allegare} for this that chapter *Ego Berengarius*, since that confession was without doubt his and confirmed by decree of a synod of the Roman church, which is far [more colourable]{g:coloracior} than the decretal of the Avignon church in this matter.[^7] And from these things it seems (since the text of the law of the church says that bread and wine remain after the consecration, and the leading doctors of this law recite the same [as an opinion]{g:opinative} and do not reprove it) that [the newer doctors]{g:novelli}, bishops too, should not presume to condemn this ancient teaching of the synod;[^8] most of all since, after the question "whether it is" about the host, a man must, willing or not, if he wishes to proceed, come to the knowledge of it, "what it is", since otherwise, stammering, ignorant of the proper word, he will proceed into the second question that follows.[^9] So I used to ask those who wish to determine in this matter and to impeach others for [heretical depravity]{g:heretica-pravitas} what [the sacrament of the altar]{g:sacramentum-altaris} is; and since they [are ignorant of]{g:ignorare} that, it is clear that they must be ignorant of what follows. For hardly does the other pope with his accomplices, [or] all the [bishops]{g:episcopus} of England with their doctors, know how to say what the consecrated host is; how then would they define, as of heresy, the third question of the host?[^10]

## ¶4 · 108.23–108.27

> [p. 108] Sed redeundo querit ydiota si sacerdos potest conficere in calice ligneo, vitreo vel cupreo aut secundum ritum a nostris observanciis alienum, ut puta, dimittendo ornamenta sacerdotum et in maiori parte misse ac verbis sacramentalibus variando.

But, to return, an [unlettered man]{g:ydiota} asks whether a [priest]{g:sacerdos} can [consecrate]{g:conficere} in a chalice of wood, glass or copper, or according to a rite foreign to our observances: for instance, by laying aside the vestments of priests and varying in the greater part of the mass and in the sacramental words.[^11]

## ¶5 · 109.1–110.21

> [p. 109] Sed hic dicitur (ut supra) de duplici potencia; non est enim nostrum querere usque ad quos limites homo potest agere de Dei potencia absoluta; sed quid liceat homini et quid debeat facere de lege iam posita, sic quod expedit primo saltem confuse cognoscere quid homo potest facere in hoc ministerio. Expedit secundo magis distincte cognoscere quid de lege currente debet facere in hoc ministerio, et expedit tercio quod sine data occasione presumpcionis vel scandali nec excedat limites nec ocietur in illis que debet facere vel dicere in concernentibus fidem hostie consecrate; et hoc movit me dicere, primo quod sacramentum istud sensibile non est pars Christi, secundo quod nemo videt aliquam partem Christi in hoc sacramento oculo corporali, et tercio quod iuxta decretum Romane ecclesie hoc sacramentum est essencialiter panis et vinum sub quo debet credi Christum esse humanitus secundum quamlibet sui partem; et tam necessaria est noticia de quidditate huius sacramenti quod nemo potest cognoscere terciam questionem de illo, scilicet cuiusmodi est, nisi prius cognoverit eius quidditatem, quia, si scio qualis homo est, tunc scio hominem esse talem et per consequens scio quod ipse est homo et sic animal racionale, et per consequens scio quid est; et sic arguitur de quolibet cuius debeo cognoscere passionem. Sicut enim questio ‘quid est’, presupponit questionem ‘si est’, sic presupponitur ad terciam questionem ‘quia est’, que presupponitur ad questionem ‘propter quid est’, ut patet II° Posteriorum in principio; ideo cum [p. 110] unumquodque sic se habet ad esse sicut ad cognosci, patet quod oportet cognoscere questionem ‘quid est’ ante duas alias consequentes; noticia tamen quedam est confusa et communiter dicta; et quedam est distincta et propria; et priorem noticiam oportet eciam laicum prehabere de hostia antequam cognoverit duas alias questiones; ut si cognovero quod hoc est album et si ignorem eius immediatam speciem, adhuc confuse cognosco quod hoc est citra generalissimum, et aliter ignoro cuiusmodi hoc est, ut secundum tres opiniones recitatas in principio secundi capituli assignata ista proposicione hoc est sacramentum, cum cuiuslibet harum trium virtus intellectiva limitat singulariter intellectum iuxta suam opinionem esse hostiam, patet quod equivocant in pronomine, cum primus singulariter demonstrat quantitatem, secundus singulariter qualitatem sed nescit quam, et tercius singulariter substanciam panis; et patet quod nimis equivocant, cum quilibet horum negaret de demonstrato per reliquum, quid sit sacramentum.

But here [the answer is]{g:dicitur} (as above) from the twofold [power]{g:potencia}: for it is not ours to ask up to what [limits]{g:limites} a man can act by God's [absolute power]{g:potencia-absoluta}, but what is lawful for a man and what he ought to do by the law already laid down; so that it is expedient, first, to know at least [indistinctly]{g:confuse} what a man can do in this ministry.[^12] It is expedient, second, to know more distinctly what he ought to do by the current law in this ministry; and it is expedient, third, that, without giving occasion of presumption or scandal, he neither exceed the limits nor be idle in the things he ought to do or say in what concerns the faith of the consecrated host.[^13] And this moved me to say, first, that this [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} is not a part of Christ; second, that no one sees any part of Christ in this sacrament with the bodily eye; and, third, that according to the decree of the Roman church this sacrament is [essentially]{g:essencialiter} bread and wine, under which Christ must be believed to be [humanly]{g:humanitus} as to every part of himself.[^14] And knowledge of the quiddity of this sacrament is so necessary that no one can know the third question about it, namely of what kind it is, unless he has first known its quiddity; since, if I know of what quality a man is, then I know that the man is such, and consequently I know that he is a man and so a rational animal, and consequently I know what he is; and so it is argued of anything whose [attribute]{g:passio} I am to know.[^15] For as the question "what it is" presupposes the question "whether it is", so it is presupposed to the third question, "that it is", which is presupposed to the question "why it is", as is clear in *Posterior Analytics* 2 at the beginning.[^16] Therefore, since [110] each thing stands to [being]{g:esse} as it stands to being known, it is clear that one must know the question "what it is" before the two others that follow.[^17] Yet one knowledge is indistinct and commonly so called, and another is distinct and proper; and the former knowledge even a layman must have of the host beforehand, before he has known the two other questions:[^18] as, if I have known that this is white and am ignorant of its [immediate]{g:immediate} species, I still know indistinctly that this is short of the most general [genus]; and otherwise I am ignorant of what kind this is.[^19] So, according to the three opinions recited at the beginning of the second chapter, when this [proposition]{g:proposicio} is assigned, "this is the sacrament", since the intellective [power]{g:virtus} of each of these three limits the understanding [alone]{g:singulariter} to what, by his opinion, is the host, it is clear that they equivocate in the pronoun: since the first demonstrates quantity alone; the second quality alone, but he does not know which; and the third the [substance]{g:substancia} of bread alone.[^20] And it is clear that they equivocate [exceedingly]{g:nimis}, since each of these would deny, of what is demonstrated by the other, that it is what the sacrament is.[^21]

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Curia Avinonica*: the papal court at Avignon (cf. *curia romana*, "the Roman curia"). The proposition Wyclif calls a frivolous foundation is given as quoted, since its sense is under discussion. The "such matters" are those of 04-f, the decretal *Cum Marthe* on the water in the chalice. At 106.22 D reads *ut ignoranter*; at 106.24 D lacks *quomodo*.
[^2]: **C.** *Sentencia* (CONTEXTUAL): "teaching", the position each church teaches. *Decretum … in persona Beringarii*: the decree *Ego Berengarius* (*De cons.* D. 2 c. 42), spoken in Berengar's person. *Opus magistrale*: a master's work, said with irony. At 106.25 B reads *clericus*; at 106.28 E *predicti*.
[^3]: **T.** At 107.2–3 AB read *inutile dubitabo*, corrected in C's margin by a second hand; at 107.4 D *exponi*. *Ad sensum expositum*: in the meaning Wyclif has set out (the bread remains and is sacramentally the body of Christ).
[^4]: **S.** Ranulf Higden, *Polychronicon* VI.35 (*Cistrensis*, the monk of Chester; Loserth 107.6, with Wyclif, *De apostasia* p. 194) (LEDGER K04g-1); *Ego Berengarius*, *De cons.* D. 2 c. 42 (Loserth 107.14; form ours) (LEDGER K04g-2); Lanfranc, *Liber de corpore et sanguine Domini* (Loserth 107.15, ed. Giles II, 147–199) (LEDGER K04g-3). The quotation is taken to run from *Nicolaus* to *predixit*; whether the cross-reference to *De consecratione* is Higden's or Wyclif's is to be checked with the chronicle (LEDGER K04g-4). *Perturbatur*, present in the Latin, is rendered as past. At 107.12 ABE read *celebrat*; at 107.14 ABE lack *Ego*; at 107.16 D *scintillurium*.
[^5]: **C.** *Revocacionem*: Berengar's recantation. Wyclif's inference: the church of that time weighed both the error and the recantation, and the recantation is shown sincere by the holy life and the prophecy that followed.
[^6]: **S.** The ordinary gloss on *X* 3.41 (Loserth 107.23, "cf. Glossam ord. (ed. Jenson, Ven. 1477) l. c.") and on *De cons.* D. 2 c. 1 (LEDGERs K04g-5, K04g-6). Loserth quotes the gloss on the third opinion (108.1): *Tertia tenet quia remanet ibi substantia panis et vini et sub eadem specie est corpus Christi. Quelibet tamen opinio fatetur ibi esse corpus Christi.* **C.** *Remanent panis et vinum hostia consecrata*: "the consecrated host" in apposition to "bread and wine". At 107.23 ABCD read *sup. a.*; at 107.26 D lacks *quarum tercia*; at 107.27 E *panem*.
[^7]: **C.** *Quod est longe coloracius*: the relative is taken of the confession confirmed by synodal decree, which has far more colour (a plausible ground) than the Avignon decretal. At 108.2 ABC lack *ista*; at 108.3 D *indubitative*.
[^8]: **C.** *Opinative*: they recite it as an opinion. *Non presumerent*: "should not presume", the potential subjunctive of what is fitting. At 108.5 AB lack *Et*; at 108.8 E *sed non*.
[^9]: **S/C.** The four questions of *Posterior Analytics* II.1 (see note 16). The "second question" is *quid est*, "what it is", after *si est*, "whether it is"; whoever lacks the proper word stumbles in it. *Velit homo nolit*: "willing or not". At 108.12 E reads *nolentem*; at 108.14 B *procedit*, C *procederet*, D *in secunda*.
[^10]: **C.** *Alter papa*: one of the two popes of the Schism (from 1378). *Diffinirent … in heresi*: construed as defining a point under pain of heresy. At 108.15 ABD read *soleo*, corrected in C's margin to the print's *solebam*; at 108.19 C *doctoribus vel complicibus*. At 108.22 C adds in the margin *scilicet cuiusmodi est*, "namely of what kind it is", identifying the third question (cf. 109.20).
[^11]: **C.** *Ornamenta sacerdotum*: the priests' vestments. At 108.24 C has *vitreo* in the margin; at 108.26 B *in niti parte*.
[^12]: **C.** *Potencia* (CONTEXTUAL): "power", the twofold power of 98.21–23: what God can do by absolute power against what is lawful by the law laid down. *De lege iam posita*: "by the law already laid down". At 109.1 D reads *secunda potencia*; C has *ut supra* in the margin.
[^13]: **T.** At 109.9 ABE read *data dicione*; at 109.11 DE *et dicere*, ABE lack *in*; at 109.11–12 E *finem hostie*. The print is translated.
[^14]: **C.** The three claims Wyclif says this moved him to make; the third gives the decree *Ego Berengarius* as his authority. *Humanitus*: in his humanity, as to every part. At 109.12 ABDE read *monuit me*; at 109.17 ABD *aut vinum*, which C corrects.
[^15]: **G.** *Passio*: the proper attribute (*Posterior Analytics*), not "passion". The argument: knowing of what quality a thing is presupposes knowing what it is. At 109.22 ABC lack *eius*; at 109.26 C reads *cuius decet*, D *cuius dicte*.
[^16]: **S.** Aristotle, *Posterior Analytics* II.1, 89b23–35 (Loserth 109.30) (LEDGER K04g-7). Wyclif's ordering is his own: *si est*, *quid est*, *quia est* (= *cuiusmodi est*), *propter quid est*. At 109.29 D reads *supponitur*.
[^17]: **S.** The axiom *unumquodque sicut se habet ad esse, sic ad cognosci* is commonly taken from Aristotle, *Metaph.* II.1, 993b30–31; not identified at the page foot (LEDGER K04g-8).
[^18]: **T.** At 110.6 AB read *loycum*, "a logician", for the print's *laicum*. *Confusa* and *distincta*: the scholastic pair, "indistinct" (known in the whole) and "distinct".
[^19]: **C.** *Citra generalissimum*: "short of the most general [genus]", i.e. below the highest genus of its category; *citra* as in the row *citra fidem*. *Et aliter ignoro cuiusmodi hoc est*: construed as "and beyond that I do not know of what kind it is". At 110.8 AB lack *eius*.
[^20]: **C.** The pronoun *hoc* in *hoc est sacramentum* demonstrates, for each of the three opinions of chapter 2, a different thing: quantity, an unknown quality, the substance of bread (LEDGER K04g-9). *Singulariter* (CONTEXTUAL): "alone", in all four places. At 110.16 ABDE read *prius*.
[^21]: **T.** At 110.20 ABDE read *quod sit sacramentum*, "that it is the sacrament" (C has *sit* in the margin); the print's *quid sit* is translated, "that it is what the sacrament is". At 110.19 E reads *eorum*.
