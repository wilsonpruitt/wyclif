# *De potestate pape*, chapter 10, part 8: the schism and the oaths of obedience

## ¶1 · 246.22–247.3

> [p. 246] Feci autem ad hoc raciones multiplices ex sufficiencia legis Christi et ex vertibilitate Romani pontificis; ymo patet ex dictis quomodo corpus Christi militaret securius atque perfeccius subducto tali ordine cesareo; nam vivendo omnino exproprietarie, sicut ante dotacionem tempore quo crevit ecclesia, quando pure regulabatur per legem Christi et regebatur per sacerdotes socios sine preeminencia humanitus instituta, melius et perfeccius vixit quam modo. Nec est racio quin per idem hodie viaret sic perfeccius quam nunc viat, igitur cum hoc sit possibile, patet conclusio. Tolle igitur dotacionem et tradiciones humanas, quibus preter scripturam [p. 247] sacram occupatur ecclesia et oriretur ecclesie prior status, qui foret indubie similior et disposicior ad ecclesiam triumphantem.

And I have made many arguments to this end, from the sufficiency of [the law of Christ]{g:lex-christi} and from the changeableness of the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex}; indeed, it is clear from what has been said how the body of Christ would fight more securely and more perfectly if such a [caesarean order]{g:ordo-cesareus} were withdrawn.[^1] For by living altogether [without property]{g:exproprietarie}, as before the [endowment]{g:dotacio}, in the time when the [church]{g:ecclesia} grew, when it was ruled purely by the law of Christ and governed by [priests]{g:sacerdos} who were fellows, without a [preeminence]{g:preeminencia} instituted by men, it lived better and more perfectly than now. Nor is there any reason why, by the same token, it would not today [be a wayfarer]{g:viator} in this way more perfectly than it now is; therefore, since this is possible, the [conclusion]{g:conclusio} is clear. Take away, then, the endowment and the human traditions with which, beyond holy [247] scripture, the church is occupied, and the church's former [state]{g:status} would arise, which would without doubt be more like the [church triumphant]{g:ecclesia-triumphans} and more disposed toward it.

## ¶2 · 247.4–247.7

> [p. 247] Sed obicitur contra illud tripliciter, primo quod tunc perirent iuramenta et lige tam personarum quam ordinum quibus obediencie pape et Romane curie obligantur.

But [it is objected]{g:obicitur} against this in three ways: first, that then the oaths and [leagues]{g:liga}, of persons as much as of orders, by which they are bound to the obedience of the [pope]{g:papa} and of the [Roman curia]{g:curia-romana}, would perish.[^2]

## ¶3 · 247.8–248.5

> [p. 247] Sed constat quod lige tales forent infidelissime, nisi subintelligantur condicionaliter, de quanto sedes illa et persone compensant in facto imitando Christum similius et tenendo religionem suam perfeccius, ut verbaliter profitentur. Aliter quidem contingeret quod persone et ordines obligate forent tenere cum Antichristo et eius discipulis contra Christum, nam (ut patet ex dictis) est magna disposicio, ut ista comitiva sit realiter Antichristus. Unde ad tantum dicitur hodie perplexa ecclesia, quod fratres necessitantur Parisius iurare Roberto obedienciam tamquam capiti, quod sciunt vel credunt firmiter verum papam; in Anglia autem necessitantur iurare Urbano nostro forma consimili, licet illi sint directe contrarii, ubi oportet quod altera pars infideliter incurrat periurium sive perfidiam. Et cum ex famosis principiis legistarum oportet facere sub pena peccati mortalis quicquid papa mandaverit, oportet credere ut fidem, quicquid in excomunicacione et infidelitate papa decreverit. Et sic de iudicio pape ad propositum. Manifeste sequitur cum paribus quod oportet utramque partem imponere infidelitatem secte Antichristi alteri et relinquere comunicacionem cum reliqua tamquam excomunicata, heretica et omni possessione ac iure indigna; et sic de infinitis inconvenientibus, [p. 248] que ad nimis magnam equivocacionem fidei sequerentur. Ideo tam prelati qui onerarunt homines sic iurare quam illi qui sic sollempnizarunt hanc obedienciam, discuterent et declararent suam sentenciam pro instruccione ecclesie.

But it is evident that such leagues would be most faithless unless they are [understood as implied]{g:subintelligere} conditionally: to the extent that that see and its persons make return in deed by imitating Christ more closely and keeping their religion more perfectly, as they profess in words. Otherwise, indeed, it would come about that the persons and orders so bound would be obliged to hold with Antichrist and his disciples against Christ; for (as is clear from what has been said) there is a great disposition for this company to be in reality Antichrist. Hence the church is said today to be [perplexed]{g:perplexus} to such a degree that the friars are compelled at Paris to swear obedience to Robert as [head]{g:caput}, whom they know or firmly believe [to be] the true pope; and in England they are compelled to swear to our Urban in a like form, although those two are directly contrary, where one party or the other must faithlessly incur perjury or perfidy.[^3] And since, by the well-known principles of the [legists]{g:legiste}, one must do on pain of mortal sin whatever the pope has commanded, one must believe as faith whatever the pope has decreed in [matters of] excommunication and [unbelief]{g:infidelitas}. And so with the pope's judgement on the matter in hand.[^4] It follows plainly, with like [premisses], that each party must impute the unbelief of the sect of Antichrist to the other, and abandon communion with the other as excommunicate, heretical, and unworthy of every possession and [right]{g:ius}; and so on with infinite [absurdities]{g:inconveniencia}[^5] [248] that would follow upon too great an equivocation of faith.[^6] Therefore both the [prelates]{g:prelatus} who laid it on men to swear thus and those who so solemnized this obedience should discuss and declare their [teaching]{g:sentencia}[^7] for the instruction of the church.

## ¶4 · 248.6–249.12

> [p. 248] Sentencia autem catholica quam sepe repecii stat in isto quod nec est credendum nec obediendum pape, episcopo, abbati vel cuicumque spirituali preposito, nisi de quanto dixerit vel mandaverit legem Christi, et per consequens nec Urbano nec Roberto tenetur fidelis credere, nisi de quanto fidem scripture dixerit; nec est de substancia fidei explicite credere alterum eorum esse papam. Benedictus Deus veritatis, qui ordinavit istam dissensionem, ut veritas huius fidei elucescat. Nam ego fidelis philosophus extra utramque istarum parcium sic opinancium, unam istarum parcium hec negantem de reliqua sic arguo; nec credo quod evadet iuxta sua principia: Dicit enim quod subditus tenetur sub pena mortalis obedire papa suo in quibuscumque neutris que ipse mandaverit. Cum igitur iuxta sua principia omnis verus papa habens iurisdicionem super totam ecclesiam precipit vel debet precipere cuilibet membro eius, ut declinet reliquum ut excomunicandum Antichristum, patet quod utraque secta tenetur credere consequenter de reliqua, quod sit a fide Christi scismatica. Dicit enim utraque secta quod omnis subiectus pape sub pena peccati mortalis debet facere omne possibile sibi quod papa suus virtute sacre obediencie sibi mandaverit, et per consequens ipso mandante ut veniat sibi, ut det sibi medietatem [p. 249] sue substancie, et sic de neutris similibus tenetur sub pena mortalis illud perficere; et sic uno precipiente suis quod non veniant ad concilium generale, et alio precipiente suis quod veniant, diu foret antequam esset utrumque concilium congregatum. Unde medium empericum foret ad temptandum religiosos qui sic magnificant suam obedienciam, si loquuntur ex animo, quod papa suus precipiat ista neutra. Et revera ego verecundarer in Oxonia caput erigere, si istam sentenciam de obediencia in scolis publice seminassem.

And the catholic teaching, which I have often repeated, consists in this: that one is neither to believe nor to obey pope, [bishop]{g:episcopus}, abbot or any spiritual [superior]{g:prepositi}, except to the extent that he has spoken or commanded the law of Christ; and consequently the faithful man is not bound to believe either Urban or Robert except to the extent that he has spoken the faith of scripture; nor is it of the substance of faith to believe explicitly that either of them is pope. Blessed be the God of truth, who ordained this dissension, that the truth of this faith might shine forth. For I, a faithful philosopher outside both of these parties that so [opine]{g:opinio}, argue thus against one of these parties, which denies these things of the other; and I do not think it will escape, by its own principles.[^8] For it says that a subject is bound, on pain of mortal [sin], to obey his pope in any neutral things whatever that he has commanded.[^9] Since, therefore, by its principles every true pope having [jurisdiction]{g:iurisdiccio} over the whole church commands, or ought to command, every [member]{g:membrum} of it to shun the other as an Antichrist to be excommunicated,[^10] it is clear that each sect is bound to believe, consequently, of the other that it is schismatic from the faith of Christ. For each sect says that every subject of the pope, on pain of mortal sin, must do everything possible to him that his pope has commanded him in virtue of holy obedience; and consequently, if he commands him to come to him, or to give him half [249] of his substance, and so with like neutral things, he is bound on pain of mortal [sin] to carry it out; and so, with one commanding his own not to come to the general council and the other commanding his own to come, it would be long before either council was assembled. Hence it would be an empirical means of testing [the religious]{g:religiosi} who so magnify their obedience, whether they speak from the heart, that their pope should command these neutral things. And in truth I would be ashamed to raise my head in Oxford if I had sown this teaching on obedience publicly in the schools.

## ¶5 · 249.13–249.35

> [p. 249] Est igitur tractatus istius articuli cuicumque fideli necessarius et specialiter isti opinioni, cum dicit se esse membrum dyaboli et nisi resipiscat dampnandum, nisi adhereat vero pape; et revera pro querendo minori bono quam est regnum celorum, et profugiendo minori malo quam est dampnacio gehenne, daret operam ad scrutinium veritatis, ymo antequam scandalizaret tantum numerum de exercitu christiano, tum oportet eos dicere, quod maior pars vel quasi medietas christianismi relicta sit per unum Antichristum perdita. Noticiam igitur veri pape discuterent et post noticiam verum papam eligerent ac pro salvacione sui et populi veritatem catholicam publice predicarent; ideo vel sunt segnes infidelibus nequiores vel credunt sentenciam suam de obediencia esse ficticiam. Ego autem credo ex necessitate divina sentenciam quam dixi superius esse fidelibus instancius detegendam, ut si Deus voluerit, fideles moveantur dotacionem destruere, que est radix istius magne versucie vel saltem primatum cesareum minus attendere.

The treatment of this article, then, is necessary to every faithful man, and especially to this opinion, since it says that [a man] is a member of the devil, and to be damned unless he comes to his senses, unless he adheres to the true pope; and in truth, for seeking a lesser good than the [kingdom]{g:regnum} of heaven, and for fleeing a lesser evil than the [damnation]{g:dampnacio} of gehenna, it would give its effort to the scrutiny of the truth, indeed before it scandalized so great a number of the Christian army; for then they must say that the greater part, or almost half, of Christendom has been left lost through one Antichrist.[^11] They should therefore discuss the knowledge of the true pope, and after the knowledge elect the true pope, and for the [salvation]{g:salvacio} of themselves and of the people preach the catholic truth publicly; therefore either they are sluggish, more wicked than unbelievers, or they believe their teaching on obedience to be a [fiction]{g:ficticia}. But I believe, from divine necessity, that the teaching I stated above must be [exposed]{g:detegere} to the faithful more urgently, so that, if God wills, the faithful may be moved to destroy the endowment, which is the root of this great craftiness, or at least to pay less heed to the caesarean [primacy]{g:primatus}.

## ¶6 · 249.36–250.6

> [p. 249] Oportet igitur studere, ex quo signo debemus cognoscere verum papam, cum eleccio, opinio [p. 250] aut reprobacio humana sit huic impertinens. Ista enim non auctoritative constituunt vel faciunt verum papam, quia tunc recurreretur ad hoc fundamentum fidei, ut salus exinde consequens habeatur, sed omittendo vel incurrimus notam infidelitatis vel subsannamus istam ficticiam.

We must therefore study by what sign we ought to know the true pope, since human election, opinion [250] or reprobation is [irrelevant]{g:impertinens} to this. For these do not [authoritatively]{g:auctoritativus} constitute or make a true pope, since then recourse would be had to this as a foundation of faith, so that salvation would be held to follow from it; but in passing [it] over we either incur the mark of unbelief or mock this fiction.[^12]

## ¶7 · 250.7–250.21

> [p. 250] Secundo replicatur per decretum beati Jeronymi positum XXIV distinccione prima, ubi sic scribitur: Hec est fides, papa beatissime, quam in ecclesia catholica didicimus quamque semper tenuimus, in qua si quid minus perite aut parum caute aliquid positum est, emendari cupimus a te, qui Petri sedem tenes et fidem. Si autem hec nostra confessio apostolatus tui iudicio comprobatur, quicumque me culpare voluerit, se imperitum aut malivolum vel eciam non catholicum sed hereticum comprobabit. Et sequitur: Sancta Romana ecclesia, que semper immaculata permansit, Domino concedente et beato Petro apostolo opem ferente in futuro manebit sine ulla hereticorum insultacione firmaque et immobilis persistet omni tempore.

Secondly, [it is rejoined]{g:replicatur}, by appeal to the decree of blessed Jerome set down in XXIV, first distinction, where it is written thus:[^13] "This is the faith, most blessed pope, which we have learned in the catholic church and have always held; in which, if anything has been set down less skilfully or with too little caution, we desire to be corrected by you, who hold the see and the faith of Peter. But if this our confession is approved by the judgement of your apostleship, whoever wishes to blame me will prove himself unskilled or malevolent, or even not catholic but heretical." And [it follows]{g:sequitur}: "The holy Roman church, which has always remained immaculate, with the Lord granting it and blessed Peter the apostle bringing help, will remain in future without any assault of heretics, and will persist firm and immovable at all times."[^14]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 246.22: The Church would be better without such prelates and without endowments.
- 247.4: It is objected that disendowment would destroy the oaths and services by which men are bound to the pope.
- 247.15: Such bonds must be conditional or men would find themselves bound to Antichrist e. g. in swearing to Robert or Urban.
- 248.6: We should obey the pope and prelates only so far as they accord with Christ.
- 248.17: Christ has ordained the Schism to make this clear,
- 249.6: I should be ashamed to shew miself at Oxford, if I had upheld such obedience in the schools.
- 249.18: Half Christendom would be lost eternally.
- 250.10: St. Jerome's reliance on the Roman Church.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *vertibilitate*: "changeableness", the mutability of whoever holds the see (a good pope may be followed by a heretic), set against the sufficiency of Christ's law. *subducto tali ordine cesareo* is an ablative absolute, read as a condition.
[^2]: **R.** Both manuscripts mark the objection in the margin: *Obicitur*. The second and third objections follow at 250.7 and 256.3 (*Secundo replicatur*, *Tercio replicatur*).
[^3]: **R.** Robert of Geneva, elected at Fondi in September 1378 as Clement VII, and Urban VI, elected at Rome in April 1378: the two obediences of the Great Schism, Paris holding to Clement and England to Urban (LEDGER P10h-1). **C.** *quod sciunt vel credunt firmiter verum papam*: *quod* is taken as a relative ("whom") with the head *capiti*; it could also be "that", with an ellipse ("[swear] that they know or firmly believe [him] the true pope").
[^4]: **C.** *Et sic de iudicio pape ad propositum* has no verb: "and the same holds of the pope's judgement, as bears on the point", i.e. what holds of his commands holds of his judgements in matters of faith.
[^5]: **C.** *inconvenientibus*: the absurd consequences of a premiss (the argument's sense), not "troubles"; GLOSSARY's CONTEXTUAL row.
[^6]: **T.** Loserth suggests at 248.1 *an enervacionem?* ("or weakening?") for *equivocacionem*. The print's *equivocacionem* construes ("equivocation", one word "faith" made to name contrary things) and is translated.
[^7]: **C.** *sentenciam*: "teaching", the position taught (GLOSSARY's CONTEXTUAL row), not a judicial sentence; so throughout this chunk.
[^8]: **T.** Both manuscripts write *unam istarum parcium … opinancium* twice (Loserth on 248.18); the print gives it once. **C.** Loserth italicises *ego fidelis philosophus*; no source is identified and it is rendered as Wyclif's own phrase, without quotation marks.
[^9]: **C.** *neutra*: "neutral things", acts neither commanded nor forbidden by God's law (things indifferent). Both manuscripts note in the margin at 248.19: *Obediencia in neutris*.
[^10]: **T.** *excomunicandum* is Loserth's; both manuscripts read *excommunicativum* ("excommunicative", 248.27). The print governs.
[^11]: **C.** The period is loose: *daret operam* ("it would give effort") argues from the lesser to the greater (men labour even for lesser goods; the more should this opinion search out the truth when heaven and hell are at stake). *tum oportet eos dicere* is taken as "for then they must say"; the plural *eos* passes from the opinion to those who hold it.
[^12]: **C.** The sense of the last clause is compressed: if human election made a pope authoritatively, it would become a foundation of faith on which salvation depends; since it is not, to pass it over is either (on their view) unbelief or (on ours) a mocking of their fiction. The alternative, *omittendo* as "by leaving [the true sign] aside", gives the same result.
[^13]: **S.** Decretum, Part II, C. 24 q. 1 c. 14 (Loserth; modern form ours); Wyclif calls the *quaestio* a *distinccio* (LEDGER P10h-2). The text is the profession of faith that circulated under Jerome's name (the *Libellus fidei* of Pelagius) (LEDGER P10h-3, to be checked). **R.** B notes in the margin at 250.7: *Replicacio notabilis*; A, the Catholic copyist, at 250.8: *Nota argumentum optimum pro papam esse et pro eius regimine, sed cave extortam evasionem istius*, "note a very good argument for the pope's existence and his rule, but beware this man's forced evasion".
[^14]: **S.** The second quotation (*Sancta Romana ecclesia …*) is given by Wyclif as following in the same place; its canon is not given by Loserth (LEDGER P10h-4, to be checked).
