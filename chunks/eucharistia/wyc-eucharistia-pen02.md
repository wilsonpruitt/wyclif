# *De eucharistia*, De eucharistia et penitencia, chapter 2: whether confession to a priest is necessary

## ¶1 · 331.23–331.28

> [p. 331] Sed quia locucio in ista materia est valde periculosa ex consuetudine antiquata et multi ac magni venerantur hoc sacramentum, ac si sine illo non poterit homini esse salus, ideo procedendo securius dicendum in principio quid sit de hoc sacramento invento ex tradicione hominum concedendum.

But since speech on this matter is very dangerous because of long-established [custom]{g:consuetudo}, and many great men venerate this [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} as if without it there could be no salvation for a man, therefore, to proceed more securely, we must say at the outset what is to be granted about this sacrament, devised out of the tradition of men.[^1]

## ¶2 · 332.1–332.27

> [p. 332] Et videtur in principio quod sit spirituale frenum contra peccata tam commissa delenda quam eciam ut homo caveat committenda. Multi enim in confitendo taliter habent dolorem et ruborem propter que volunt posterius precavere sic peccare alias, cum necessitati fuerint illud peccatum ostendere proprio sacerdoti; et sic videtur hoc sacramentum esse medicinam thyriacam contra peccatum commissum hominis abluendum et specialem preservativam ne homo iterum incidat in peccatum; et nisi hoc sacramentum haberet efficaciam, videtur quod Christus superflue dedisset apostolis et succedentibus sacerdotibus clavium potestatem. Ad quid rogo concessit Christus suis apostolis solvendi ligandique potestatem, nisi ostensio eorum sacerdoti solventi aut liganti faceret ipsa crimina esse nota? Quis, inquam, dubitat quod nemo abluit hominem a crimine quod ignorat? Et ad hoc sunt dicta Augustini et specialiter in De Visitacione infirmorum et scripta aliorum doctorum et dictorum papalium infinita. Quomodo eciam confiteretur quis peccata sua commissa suo proprio sacerdoti nisi auriculariter et sibi abscondite ipsis solis noscentibus, ut docet decretalis Omnis utriusque sexus? Aut quomodo foret peccatum deletum nisi per penitenciam iniunctam fiat satisfaccio pro eodem? Tales multas evidencias faciunt cupidi quidam ignari, qui nituntur libertatem legis Domini impugnare.

And at the outset it seems that it is a spiritual bridle against sins: both those committed, that they may be blotted out, and also those yet to be committed, that a man may beware of them. For many in confessing have such sorrow and shame that on account of them they wish afterwards to guard against sinning so another time, since they will have been compelled to show that sin to their own [priest]{g:sacerdos}; and so this sacrament seems to be a theriac medicine against a man's committed sin, to wash it away, and a special preservative, so that a man does not fall into sin again;[^2] and unless this sacrament had efficacy, it seems that Christ would have given the [power]{g:potestas} of the keys to the apostles and to the priests succeeding them to no purpose. To what end, I ask, did Christ grant to his apostles the power of loosing and binding, unless the showing of them to the priest who looses or binds made the crimes themselves known?[^3] Who, I say, doubts that no one washes a man from a crime that he [is ignorant of]{g:ignorare}? And to this purpose are the sayings of Augustine, and especially in *On the Visitation of the Sick*, and numberless writings of other doctors and of papal sayings.[^4] How, too, would anyone confess the sins he has committed to his own priest except auricularly and in secret to him, they alone knowing [them], as the [decretal]{g:decretalis} *Omnis utriusque sexus* teaches?[^5] Or how would sin be blotted out unless [satisfaction]{g:satisfaccio} is made for it through an enjoined [penance]{g:penitencia}? Many such [evidences]{g:evidencia} are produced by certain covetous and ignorant men, who strive to attack the freedom of the law of the Lord.

## ¶3 · 332.28–333.9

> [p. 332] Ideo respondendo ad istos obiectus dicitur primo quod necesse est peccatori cuius peccata delenda sunt contricione animi penitere; sed ex hoc non infertur [p. 333] quod sit tam necessaria verbalis confessio sacerdoti. Concedo tamen quod necessaria est verbalis confessio sacerdoti conversanti cum peccatore sed non est simpliciter necessaria cuicunque, cum multis martyribus deest copia vel presencia sacerdotis; et multi tempore utriusque legis sine confessione tali in patria sunt salvati. Cum autem omnia que eveniunt de necessitate evenient et talis confessio prodest pluribus, patet quod talis confessio est necessario facienda.

Therefore, in reply to these objections, the answer is, first, that it is necessary for a sinner whose sins are to be blotted out to repent with [contrition]{g:contricio} of mind; but it is not inferred from this [333] that verbal [confession]{g:confessio} to a priest is so necessary. I grant, nevertheless, that verbal confession is necessary to a priest who [lives his life]{g:conversari} with the sinner; but it is not necessary [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} for anyone whatever, since many martyrs lack the [access]{g:copia-sacerdotis} or the presence of a priest;[^6] and many in the time of both laws have been saved in the [homeland]{g:patria} without such confession. And since all things that happen will happen of necessity, and such confession profits many, it is clear that such confession is necessarily to be made.[^7]

## ¶4 · 333.10–333.32

> [p. 333] Et iterum, cum auricularis et ista sacramentalis confessio ex institucione hominum prosit multis, patet quod illa confessio est necessario facienda proprio sacerdoti. Non autem dico quod est necessario facienda a quolibet viatore semel in anno, si salvabitur, cum Baptista et multi alii sancti salvantur, qui nunquam confessione huiusmodi erant usi. Nec subiacet potestati hominum huiusmodi sacramentum; Deus enim libertavit suam ecclesiam quod ex sua gracia et cordis contricione fidelium sine ista penitencia sint salvati, ut patet de Petro, Paulo et Magdalena cum multis ceteris. Nam isti tres sunt salvati ex fide scripture, et tamen quod ista sacra confessione mundati fuerant, non docetur. Et certum videtur quod Christus ecclesie sue foret negligens si hoc sacramentum foret sibi tam necessarium et tamen non foret usque ad solucionem sathane ecclesie declaratum. Nam blasphemum videtur asserere quod sancti usque ad edicionem istius decretalis in obmittendo istam penitenciam peccaverunt. Et iterum certum videtur quod si huius legis edicio fuit ita racionabilis tempore Innocencii IIIⁱⁱ, quod illa racio stetit prius et sic homines ex obmissione talis penitencie antea peccaverunt.

And again, since this auricular and sacramental confession, by the institution of men, profits many, it is clear that that confession is necessarily to be made to one's own priest. But I do not say that it must necessarily be made by every [wayfarer]{g:viator} once a year if he is to be saved, since the Baptist and many other saints are saved who never made use of confession of this kind. Nor is a sacrament of this kind subject to the power of men; for God has so freed his [church]{g:ecclesia} that by his grace and by the contrition of heart of the faithful they are saved without this penance, as is clear of Peter, Paul and the Magdalene, with many others. For these three are saved [from the faith of scripture]{g:ex-fide}, and yet it is not taught that they had been cleansed by this [sacred]{g:sacer} confession. And it seems certain that Christ would be negligent of his church if this sacrament were so necessary to it and yet were not [made clear]{g:declarare} to the church until the loosing of Satan.[^8] For it seems blasphemous to assert that the [saints]{g:sancti}, until the issuing of this decretal, sinned in omitting this penance. And again it seems certain that, if the issuing of this law was so reasonable in the time of Innocent III, that reason held before, and so men sinned before by omitting such penance.[^9]

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Hoc sacramentum* is penance, the subject of chapters 2–6, not the eucharist; "sacrament" in its plain sense. *Consuetudine antiquata*: custom grown old, i.e. long-established (not "abrogated"). *Invento ex tradicione hominum*: the sacrament as found out, i.e. devised, by human tradition; this is the chapter's thesis, stated before the objections.

[^2]: **T/C.** *Thyriacam*: theriac, the physicians' antidote; ABDEF read *theoricam*, and one witness *preservanciam* for *preservativam* (the siglum is lost in the OCR); the print is translated. *Cum necessitati fuerint*: "since they will have been compelled"; BEF read *tamen necessitati*.

[^3]: **C/T.** *Ostensio eorum*: the showing of the crimes, the noun that follows (*ipsa crimina*). *Liganti* read on the plate. BDEF lack *et succedentibus sacerdotibus* (A has *succedentibus clavium*, without *sacerdotibus*); A lacks *solvendi*; B reads *ligandique peccata*. The keys and the power of binding and loosing: Matt. 16:19, "whatsoever thou shalt bind upon earth … whatsoever thou shalt loose on earth".

[^4]: **S.** *De visitacione infirmorum*, cited as Augustine's: a work transmitted under his name, not Augustine's: an anonymous work printed as *incerti auctoris* in PL 40, 1147–1158, and assigned in a Lambeth manuscript to Baldric of Dol (preface to the PL 166 reprint); the objector's point is II.4, *Quod non sufficiat soli Deo confiteri* (PL 40, 1154), against those who will not *ostendere se sacerdotibus* (LEDGER Kpen02-1). *Scripta … dictorum papalium*: the genitive hangs on *scripta*, as the print stands; A reads *doctorum et doctorum*.

[^5]: **S/T.** *Omnis utriusque sexus*: X 5.38.12 (Loserth, at 331.20: *Decret. Greg.* lib. V, tit. XXXVIII, cap. XII; the modern form is ours), the canon requiring confession *proprio sacerdoti* (LEDGER Kpen02-2). *Ipsis solis noscentibus*: the penitent and the priest alone. ABCEF lack *et* before *sibi*; D reads *auricularitate*; F *Decretum* for *decretalis*.

[^6]: **C/G.** *Verbalis confessio sacerdoti conversanti cum peccatore*: the dative read as the one confessed to, the priest who keeps company with the sinner; it could be read as necessary *for* such a priest, which the next clause (*cuicunque*, of the sinner) does not favour. *Copia (sacerdotis)*: "access" to a priest, his availability; not "plenty" here.

[^7]: **C/T.** The concession rests on Wyclif's thesis that whatever happens happens of necessity: confession that in fact profits is in that sense "necessarily to be made", which is not the necessity of salvation denied above. *Evenient* (future) as printed; A *que evenient*, C *que de evenient*, BC *necessitate evenient*.

[^8]: **S/T.** *Usque ad solucionem sathane*: Apoc. 20:7, "Satan shall be loosed out of his prison" when the thousand years are finished. At 333.23 ABCE are reported as *quod episcopus*, and at 333.25 *Codd.: et cum*; the words these replace are not clear from the OCR of the apparatus, and the print is translated.

[^9]: **S.** Innocent III: the Fourth Lateran Council (1215), whose canon 21 is *Omnis utriusque sexus* (LEDGER Kpen02-3). B reads *huiusmodo edicio* and *Ante ea peccaverant*.
