# *De eucharistia*, chapter 5, part 1: Scotus on figurative speech, and the decree *Ego Berengarius*

## ¶1 · 115.6–115.22

> [p. 115] Quantum ad duas difficultates in parte expeditum est, scilicet de virtute et veritate verborum sacramentalium et quid de hostia est credendum. Sed antequam tangam terciam difficultatem obmissam, restat replicare contra sentenciam istam. Nam graviter ferunt doctores et cultores signorum quod locucio effectiva sacramenti est tropica. Ponit enim Scotus super quartum in principio distinccione Xᵃ quod hec non est figurativa locucio: Hoc est corpus meum; quod probat ex hoc quod secundum Augustinum LXXXIIIᵘᵐ questionum questione LXIXᵃ ex parte scripture precedenti vel sequenti aut alibi posita potest cognosci que sit figurativa. Cum ergo Christus dicit Luce XXIIᵒ, 19: Hoc est corpus meum, statim subiungit quod pro vobis tradetur, et Joh. VIᵒ, 56 dicit: Caro mea vere est cibus. Ex quibus concludit quod omnino est contra intencionem Salvatoris loqui hic figurative.

As to two difficulties, the matter has in part been settled, namely about the power and truth of the sacramental words, and what is to be believed about the [host]{g:hostia}. But before I touch the third difficulty, which was left aside, it remains to [rejoin]{g:replicatur} against this [teaching]{g:sentencia}.[^1] For the doctors and [worshippers of signs]{g:cultores-signorum} take it hard that the [effective speech]{g:locucio-effectiva} of the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} is [tropical]{g:locucio-tropica}.[^2] For Scotus lays down on the fourth [book], at the beginning of distinction 10, that this is not figurative speech: "This is my body"; which he proves from this, that according to Augustine, *Eighty-three Questions*, question 69, it can be known from a part of scripture preceding or following, or set elsewhere, which [speech] is figurative.[^3] Since therefore Christ says in Luke 22:19, "This is my body", he at once adds "which shall be delivered for you"; and in John 6:56 he says: "My flesh is meat indeed."[^4] From which he [infers]{g:concludere} that it is wholly against the intention of the Saviour to speak [figuratively]{g:figurative} here.

## ¶2 · 115.23–117.8

> [p. 115] Sed non satis subtiliter, ymmo nimis superficialiter videtur doctor in isto fuisse locutus, tum quia ex verbis Christi tam de sacramento panis quam calicis patet ipsum locutum fuisse figurative. Nam non dubium quin panem materialem accepit, benedixit et fregit et ex illo manducare precepit, quem demonstravit dicens: Hoc est corpus meum, quod oportet omnino figurative intelligi sicut et verba de calice. [p. 116] Nec dubium quin, sicut panem et vinum materiale assumpsit, sic ipsum sumi tamquam sacramentum mandavit; aliter enim illusorie equivocasset cum ecclesia. Et sic indubie figurative locutus est Joh. VIᵒ (ut patet per Augustinum); unde miror quomodo aliqua subtilitas potest ex relacione ydemptitatis quod pro vobis tradetur excludere locucionem figurativam, cum antecedens locucio foret ad hoc efficacior; ut in isto dicto Joh. XVᵒ, 1: Ego sum vitis vera foret evidencius quod excludit figuram loquendo ydemptice quam dicendo, sicut dixit implicite quod apostoli sunt palmites que inseruntur in hac vite. Idem enim est dicere: Hoc est corpus meum quod pro vobis tradetur, et: Hoc efficaciter et sacramentaliter figurat corpus meum quod pro vobis tradetur. Et tales sunt multe locuciones sanctorum (ut patet in De Consecracione, distinccione IIᵃ). Istud autem est quod doctor abhorret tamquam hereticum quod corpus Christi et sanguis non sint veraciter et realiter in sacramento altaris, et sic intelligitur Henricus Gaunt quodlibeto IXᵒ, questione IXᵃ cum multis aliis testibus. Et evidenter colligitur quod non in toto est conveniens illorum testimonium, eo quod quilibet eorum alteri in aliquo contradicit. Cum ergo nec testificacioni Augustini nec Jeronimi nec alicuius sancti debet credi nisi de quanto se fundaverint in scriptura, patet quod oportet istos doctores transcendere et commenta scripture vel racionem vivacem proponere, et multo magis de doctoribus decretorum. Convenio autem cum illis quantum sumunt testimonium illud superius, cui testimonio ipsis contrariantibus ego illis intrepide contradico et specialiter in isto quod panis et vinum [p. 117] pro instanti confeccionis desinunt esse secundum se tota et remanet solum accidens sacramentum; hoc enim repugnat decreto Romane ecclesie, De Consecracione, distinccione IIᵃ, capitulo: Ego Beringarius et sentencie sancti Augustini cum aliis sanctis et racionibus. Nec scit tota pars adversa facere scintillam evidencie que istud vel loyco suaderet. Quare ergo onerarentur discipuli Veritatis hoc credere?

But the doctor seems to have spoken in this not subtly enough, indeed [exceedingly]{g:nimis} superficially: first, because from the words of Christ, both about the sacrament of the [bread]{g:panis} and about that of the [chalice]{g:calix}, it is clear that he spoke figuratively. For there is no doubt that he took material bread, blessed and broke [it], and commanded [them] to eat of it, which he pointed out, saying: "This is my body"; which must wholly be understood figuratively, as must the words about the chalice.[^5] [116] Nor is there doubt that, as he took material bread and [wine]{g:vinum}, so he commanded it to be [received]{g:sumpcio} as a sacrament; for otherwise he would have equivocated deceptively with the [church]{g:ecclesia}. And so without doubt he spoke figuratively in John 6 (as is clear through Augustine); hence I wonder how any subtlety can, from the [relation of identity]{g:relacio-ydemptitatis} "which shall be delivered for you", exclude figurative speech, since the antecedent speech would be more efficacious for this; as in this saying, John 15:1, "I am the true vine", it would be more evident that it excludes a figure, speaking [identically]{g:ydemptice}, than by saying, as he said implicitly, that the apostles are branches which are grafted into this vine.[^6] For it is the same to say "This is my body which shall be delivered for you" and "This efficaciously and [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} [figures]{g:figurare} my body which shall be delivered for you." And such are many speeches of [the saints]{g:sancti} (as is clear in *De consecratione*, distinction 2). And this is what the doctor abhors as heretical: that the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} and blood should not be [verily]{g:veraciter} and [really]{g:realiter} in the sacrament of the altar; and so Henry of Ghent is understood, *Quodlibet* 9, question 9, with many other witnesses.[^7] And it is evidently gathered that their testimony is not in agreement throughout, because each of them contradicts the other in something. Since therefore neither the testimony of Augustine nor of Jerome nor of any saint ought to be believed except so far as they have founded themselves in scripture, it is clear that one must go beyond these doctors and put forward the [commentaries on scripture]{g:commenta-scripture} or living reason, and much more so with the doctors of the [decrees]{g:decretum}. And I [agree]{g:convenire} with them so far as they take up that testimony above; but where they contradict that testimony I fearlessly contradict them, and especially in this, that bread and wine [117] at the instant of [consecration]{g:confeccio} cease to be in their whole selves, and only an [accident]{g:accidens} remains, the sacrament;[^8] for this [is incompatible with]{g:repugnare} the decree of the Roman church, *De consecratione*, distinction 2, chapter *Ego Berengarius*, and with the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of Saint Augustine, with other saints and with reasons.[^9] Nor can the whole opposing party make a spark of [evidence]{g:evidencia} which would [make the case]{g:suadere} for it even to a logician. Why then should the disciples of the Truth be burdened with believing it?

## ¶3 · 117.9–118.12

> [p. 117] Nec valet dicere quod abrogatur illud decretum, sicut communiter dicitur, quando aliqua lex ecclesie non placet satrapis, primo quia tam glossa Beringarii quam glossa ordinaria decretorum allegat illud capitulum, ut patet alibi, secundo quia Vᵒ Decretalium De Penitencia, capitulo primo, docetur per hoc capitulum, quod qui publice peccat debet publice penitere. Et tercio quia cum tangit fidem ecclesie et fulcitur scriptura sacra, sanctis doctoribus et evidencia racionis, oportet docere, quando et qualiter abrogatur. Aliter enim non pocius crederetur hoc dicenti quam cuicunque ydiote omnem legem ecclesie per istum fucum sophisticum deneganti. Nam editor decretalis non potest sentenciam istam destruere nisi docto quod sit contraria legi Christi; sed cum hoc non potest, remanet secura, et quecunque decretalis de quanto fuerit illi contraria abroganda. Penset, rogo, fidelis quomodo fides de eukaristia debet servari conformius scripture sacre, racioni et sanctis doctoribus, ponendo quod panis et vinum post benediccionem remanent sacramenta et videat quare hodie sentencia ista Romane ecclesie est neganda. Numquid ascribendum [p. 118] est miraculo Christi magnifico quod sine causa destruit creatas substancias, dampnificat suam universitatem, tollit figuras quibus in pane et vino (ut dicunt doctores) edificaretur ecclesia, inducit decepcionem sensus hominum, facit accidens abiectum coli ut Deum et cum dubiis inutilibus fatigat ecclesiam? Omnes enim in isto conveniunt quod eque honorifice, eque religiose et pie posset Christus coli sub specie panis, sicut sub illo globo accidencium ignotorum quibus diffamatur ecclesia, ymmo religiosius, quia in signo plus honorabili, plus fundabili et plus defensibili contra infideles presumpcionis nostre fatue irrisores.

Nor does it avail to say that that decree is abrogated, as is commonly said when some law of the church does not please the [satraps]{g:satrapa}: first, because both the gloss of Bernard and the ordinary gloss of the decrees [allege]{g:allegare} that chapter, as is clear elsewhere; secondly, because in the fifth [book] of the [Decretals]{g:decretalis}, *De penitencia*, chapter one, it is taught through this chapter that he who sins publicly must do penance publicly.[^10] And thirdly because, since it touches the faith of the church and is propped up by holy scripture, by the holy doctors and by the evidence of reason, they must teach when and how it is abrogated. For otherwise one would not believe the man who says this more than any [unlettered man]{g:ydiota} who denies every law of the church by this sophistical disguise. For the maker of a decretal cannot destroy this teaching unless it is shown that it is contrary to the law of Christ; now since this cannot [be done], it remains secure, and any decretal whatever, so far as it is contrary to it, is to be abrogated. Let the faithful man weigh, I ask, how the faith concerning the eucharist ought to be kept more in conformity with holy scripture, with reason and with the holy doctors, by laying down that bread and wine remain sacraments after the blessing; and let him see why today this teaching of the Roman church is to be denied.[^11] Is it to be ascribed [118] to a magnificent miracle of Christ that without cause he destroys created [substances]{g:substancia}, [damages]{g:dampnificare} his totality, takes away the [figures]{g:figura} by which (as the doctors say) the church would be edified in the bread and wine, brings in deception of men's senses, makes an abject accident to be worshipped as God, and wearies the church with useless doubts? For all agree in this, that Christ could be worshipped equally honourably, equally religiously and piously under the [species]{g:species} of bread as under that heap of unknown accidents by which the church is defamed; indeed more religiously, because in a sign more honourable, more able to be grounded and more defensible against unbelievers, the mockers of our foolish presumption.

## ¶4 · 118.13–118.18

> [p. 118] Quando enim opponunt nobis in ista materia, vix duos invenies quin discordant. Et quando movetur difficultas ultra materiam de trinitate, recurritur ad fugam ignorancie cum Machometi discipulis; credi, inquiunt, salubriter potest, investigari salubriter non potest.

For when they oppose us in this matter, you will scarcely find two who do not disagree. And when a difficulty is raised beyond the matter of the Trinity, there is recourse to the flight of ignorance, with the disciples of Mahomet: it can, they say, be believed salutarily; it cannot be investigated salutarily.[^12]

## ¶5 · 118.19–118.22

> [p. 118] Et quando queritur quid de isto sacramento et qualiter est credendum, hii dicunt quod hoc non debet queri, et hii quod debet credi sicut ecclesia sancta credit.

And when it is asked what is to be believed of this sacrament, and how, some say that this ought not to be asked, and others that it must be believed as holy church believes.

## ¶6 · 118.23–119.9

> [p. 118] Quoad primum quare nunc seminatur ultra fidem antiquam quod accidencia manent sine subiecto et sic sacramentum illud est accidens, ecce quod respondetur per duplex mendacium et nimis confuse per analogum vulgo incognitum; nam vulgus et multi qui ista balbuciunt ignorant quid est accidens, sicut ignorant quod accidens est eukaristia de novem generibus accidentis. Fideles autem dicunt quod eukaristia est panis aut vinum verbo Domini consecratum; ecce hic modo antique ecclesie descenditur [p. 119] ad speciem vulgo satis noscibilem; voluit enim Deus iugum legis sue esse leve et aput quemcunque fidelem noscibile, ne in angulis latebre mendacium abscondatur. Sic enim discipuli cognoverunt Christum in fraccione panis, quem Augustinus dicit esse sacramentum, sic eciam fideles fregerunt panem circa domos in primitiva ecclesia, ut dicitur Act. IIᵒ; quod dominus Armachanus libro IX capitulo... declarat dici de hostia consecrata.

As to the first, why it is now sown beyond the ancient faith that accidents remain without a subject, and so that that sacrament is an accident: see, the reply is made by a twofold lie, and exceedingly [indistinctly]{g:confuse}, by an [analogous term]{g:analogum} unknown to the common people; for the common people, and many who stammer these things, do not know what an accident is, just as they do not know which accident, of the nine genera of accident, the eucharist is.[^13] But the faithful say that the eucharist is bread or wine consecrated by the word of the Lord; see, here, in the manner of the [ancient church]{g:antiqua-ecclesia}, one descends [119] to a species well enough knowable to the common people; for God willed the yoke of his law to be light and knowable to any faithful man, lest a lie should be hidden in the corners of a hiding-place.[^14] For so the disciples knew Christ in the breaking of bread, which [bread] Augustine says is the sacrament; so also the faithful broke bread from house to house in the primitive church, as is said in Acts 2[:46]; which the lord of Armagh, book 9, chapter …, makes clear is said of the [consecrated host]{g:hostia-consecrata}.[^15]

## ¶7 · 119.10–119.26

> [p. 119] Quantum ad aliam sentenciam, patet quod est communis cuilibet fideli; quilibet enim fidelis dicit sectam suam esse katholicam et communitatem suam esse sanctam ecclesiam et sic credere sicut ecclesia sancta credit. Et iterum si fides ista sufficiat, quare oneratur ecclesia ad concedendum insolite quod eukaristia non est substancia sed res infinitum abieccior de genere accidentis? Et tercio suppono quod fiat interrogacio a periciori huius scole vel a layco subtilis ingenii qui ultra questionem ‘si est’ de sacramento languet circa questionem ‘quid est’ de sacramento altaris. Dictum, inquam, est sibi quod sunt septem ecclesie sacramenta et specificantur aliqua; quare ergo non specificaretur et distincte cognosceretur sacramentum altaris communissimum, necessariissimum atque dignissimum? oportet enim cognoscere per quid distinguitur ab aliis sacramentis.

As to the other teaching, it is clear that it is common to every faithful man; for every faithful man says that his [sect]{g:secta} is catholic and his community holy church, and that he believes as holy church believes. And again, if this faith suffices, why is the church burdened to grant, in an unwonted way, that the eucharist is not a substance but a thing infinitely more abject, of the genus of accident? And thirdly I suppose that the question is put by the more skilled of this school, or by a layman of subtle wit, who, beyond the question "whether it is" about the sacrament, languishes over the question "what it is" about the sacrament of the altar. He has been told, I say, that there are seven sacraments of the church, and some of them are specified; why then should not the sacrament of the altar, the most common, most necessary and most worthy, be specified and distinctly known? For one must know by what it is distinguished from the other sacraments.[^16]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 115.6: The words of institution are figurative, in spite of what Scotus says.
- 116.25: Saints and doctors to be followed only so far as they agree with Scripture.
- 117.9: The decretal Ego Berengarius is still in force.
- 118.1: Its opponents dishonour Christ.
- 118.13: They shun investigation.
- 118.23: They say that the sacrament is an accident; but cannot say which of the nine accidents it is.
- 119.10: We claim the sanction of the Church by as good a right as they.

## Notes

[^1]: **C/T.** *Sentencia* (CONTEXTUAL): the teaching set out in the chapters before, that the words are figurative; "teaching", not a judicial sentence. *Replicare*: to bring rejoinders against it. D reads *ammissam*, A *emissam* (corrected by another hand) for *obmissam*; AB omit *contra*.

[^2]: **C.** *Locucio effectiva sacramenti*: the speech that effects the sacrament, the words of institution. *Cultores signorum*: Wyclif's name for his opponents.

[^3]: **S.** Scotus, *Reportata* IV d. 10 q. 1, scholium 1 (Loserth: *Opera* XI.1, p. 632) (LEDGER K05a-1). Augustine, *De diversis quaestionibus 83* q. 69 (Loserth gives only *Opera* VI, pp. 55–60) (LEDGER K05a-2).

[^4]: **S.** Luke 22:19: the Vulgate has *quod pro vobis datur* (DR "which is given for you"); Wyclif's *tradetur* is translated. John 6:56 agrees with the Vulgate (without *enim*); DR's "indeed" renders *vere*, elsewhere "truly". E reads *dicitur* for *dicit* (115.20); E *hic loqui* (115.22).

[^5]: **C/T.** *Quem demonstravit*: *quem* is the bread, which the pronoun *hoc* points out; *quod oportet … intelligi* takes the whole saying. ABDE read *precepit illum* (115.28); ABC *quod hoc est* (115.29); ABCD *materialem* with *vinum* (116.1).

[^6]: **C/T.** *Relacio ydemptitatis*: the relative of identity, the clause *quod pro vobis tradetur* referring back to *corpus*. ABCD read *ex religione* for *ex relacione* (116.6). The *cum* clause is construed: a qualifier in the antecedent itself (as *vera* in "the true vine") would exclude figure more strongly than a following relative; the argument holds that even this would not exclude it. The construal is doubtful. "Branches" recalls John 15:5 ("I am the vine: you the branches"; Loserth's reference).

[^7]: **S.** The sayings of the saints in *De cons.* D. 2 are not specified (LEDGER K05a-3). Henry of Ghent, *Quodlibet* IX q. 9 (Loserth: Paris 1580; cf. *De ecclesia* p. 317) (LEDGER K05a-4). Augustine on John 6 as figurative speech is not specified (LEDGER K05a-5). E reads *sunt veraciter* (116.19); D *Gamet* for *Gaunt* (116.20).

[^8]: **C.** *Commenta scripture*: the expositions of scripture, not the *commentum (mendacii)* "fabrication" of the glossary. *Testimonium illud superius* is taken as the testimony cited above (the scriptural one); it may mean "that higher testimony". *Remanet solum accidens sacramentum*: the accident in apposition, "only an accident remains, [as] the sacrament".

[^9]: **S/T.** *Ego Berengarius* is *De cons.* D. 2 c. 42 (modern form ours) (LEDGER K05a-6). ABCD give the order *ecclesie distinccione II De Consecracione* (117.3–4). D reads *accidentaliter* (117.2).

[^10]: **T/S.** The print reads *glossa Beringarii*; we translate ABCD *glossa Bernhardi* (117.11). Wyclif pairs it with *glossa ordinaria decretorum*: the two ordinary glosses of the canon law, Bernard of Parma's on the Decretals and that on Gratian's *Decretum*. Berengar wrote no gloss, so the print gives no sense (LEDGER K05a-7). The decretal is X 5.38.1, *Manifesta peccata non sunt occulta correctione purganda* (Loserth: *Decr. Greg.* V.38.1; modern form ours) (LEDGER K05a-8). ABD read *obrogatur* (117.9).

[^11]: **T.** ABCD read *posset* for *debet* (117.26), "how the faith … could be kept". AC have *hodie quare* (117.29); AB omit *istam* (117.22, C adds it in the margin); DE *non posset* (117.21–22).

[^12]: **S/T.** The saying given to "the disciples of Mahomet" is not identified (LEDGER K05a-9). *Ignorancie* stands in C's margin in another hand and is not in C's text (118.16). D reads *duo* (118.14).

[^13]: **C.** *Quoad primum*: the first of the two answers of ¶5, with the question it dodges ("why …") in apposition. *Respondetur* "the reply is". *Analogum*: "accident" is said analogically of the nine genera, so names none of them; *quod accidens* is interrogative, "which accident". D reads *accidenter*; E *quod eukaristia est accidens* (118.29).

[^14]: **C/S/T.** *Species* here is the logical species (bread, wine) under which the eucharist falls, set against the analogous "accident"; not the sacramental species. *Iugum … leve* recalls Matt. 11:30 ("my yoke is sweet and my burden light"). ABCD read *noscibile* for *noscibilem* (119.1); ABE *latebris*, C over an erasure *latebre* (119.3).

[^15]: **S/T.** Luke 24:35 ("how they knew him in the breaking of bread"); Acts 2:46 ("breaking bread from house to house"). *Quem* agrees with *panis*, not *fraccione*. Augustine's saying is not specified (LEDGER K05a-10). Armachanus (FitzRalph), *De quaestionibus Armenorum* IX (LEDGER K05a-11); the chapter number is blank in the print. BC read *ignoraverunt* for *cognoverunt* (119.4); DE *Ardmachanus* (119.8).

[^16]: **C/S/T.** *Aliam sentenciam*: the second answer of ¶5; D reads *aliam responsionem* (119.10). The questions *si est* and *quid est* are the order of inquiry of Aristotle, *Posterior Analytics* II.1 (LEDGER K05a-12). AB read *apericior* for *periciori* (119.18); ABCD *alia* (119.22); C *et sacramentum*, *necessarium* (119.24).
