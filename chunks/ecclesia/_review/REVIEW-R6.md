## State of each chunk

- **18-c**: Sound construal of a dense argument about the church's place and soul, with every connective accounted for. Its quotation marks need setting from the italics: Acts 4:32 and *nati pro patribus* are roman, and Augustine's closing sentence runs one clause too far. The frozen Latin silently has *nonam* where the print has *novam* (424.4). N. 13 attaches *minata* to a genitive it cannot agree with.
- **21-d**: The English is accurate, but this is the chunk the italics rule bites hardest. Bonaventure's text and Wyclif's lead-ins (*Tercio sic arguitur*, *Quarto arguitur per hoc quod*, *Ideo dicit … quod*, *Ergo et posicio*) run together inside one set of quotation marks, and the objection at 502.10 is not quoted at all. Two minor-premiss *sed/autem* are "but"/"and". N. 2 calls Wyclif's what the print italicises as Bonaventure's.
- **21-f**: Careful notes on a hard passage about characters. One misleading rendering: "ordination of a bishop" for the bishop's act of ordaining. Two quotation bounds are off, one in Romans 8:28 where *predestinatis* is Wyclif's own. A *sentencia* C note is missing. Two notes report the OCR of the page foot rather than the plate (*pem*, line 20).
- **23-h**: The cleanest of the five on construal. The calculators' cases are well followed and the doubtful points are all noted. What remains is tagging, a missing *sentencia* C note, and one *sed* that should be "now".
- **23-k**: Accurate, with the connective "now" already handled at 583.25–29. One more minor-premiss *autem* (582.5) is "and". N. 15 reports an OCR "1" as the print's verse number. The rest is tagging.
