# sed/autem extract, wave 14: chunks 05-k 06-a 06-b

### 05-k ¶1 · 155.16–157.14  (11 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 155] Hic dicitur quod multi sunt heretici, aliqui cogniti et aliqui ignoti, omnes enim presciti sunt heretici, **sed** aliqui sunt heretici in actu et alii in habitu, licet sint in gracia secundum presentem iusticiam. Aliqui eciam sunt heretici ad tempus solum secundum presentem iusticiam ut predestinati in mortali, et sic de multis divisionibus cum suis declaracionibus quas hic suppono, **sed** notum est quod stat multos homines tenere sentenciam hereticam sine hoc quod ipsi sint tunc heretici, quia multi opinative vel condicionaliter tenent multas sentencias parati ad revocandum docto opposito; et certum est quod nulli tales sunt ut sic heretici, quia tunc Doctor Communis fuisset nimis crebro hereticus. Unde videtur quod illi dogmatisantes qui deficientibus argumentis defamant universitatem in vulgari propter dicta scolastica et sanctorum sentencias sint publice puniendi; hoc **autem** optarem, [p. 156] cum ipsi sint manifeste heretici nisi probaverint quod divulgant aut quod non accipiant dignitatem in Oxonia antequam docuerint heresim quam imponunt; nam de natura accusacionis heretice est quod accusator obligat se ut sic ad penam talionis, cum credit katholicum eius contradictorium quod inpugnat; et idem est iudicium de toto capitulo quod consentit. Sic ergo multi sancti preterierunt istum articulum de sacris accidentibus tamquam fidei contrarium a nullis sciolis sui temporis attemptatum, ut patet de antiquis sanctis doctoribus. Et videtur quod ecclesia Avinonica ponens transsubstanciacionem non exhinc implicat substanciam panis destrui vel corrumpi. **Sed** conformiter ad antiquum decretum Romane ecclesie permittit panis substanciam remanere; unum tamen credo quod quicunque pertinaciter crediderit quod non remanent panis et vinum in hostia consecrata, **sed** quod ipsa est globus sacrorum accidencium sine subiecto, est manifeste hereticus a qualibet universitate fidelium expellendus. Contradicit eciam fidei quam magnus Augustinus confessus est cum Romana ecclesia. Nec usque hodie scivit fidelis vel infidelis inpugnare istam sentenciam Augustini. Sentencia **autem** ista que sancto Thome ascribitur est tam infirma et debilis quod nemo scit illam defendere vel probare; et credo quod non fient efficaciores argucie ad hereticandum predictam Augustini sentenciam quam in nomine Communis Doctoris recitate et patule sunt solute. Non enim consonat fidei Christi, quod vel sine causa aut ordine accusetur persona de heresi, vel quod eatur ad curiam mendaciter procurando katholicacionem [p. 157] omnium que scripsit sanctus Thomas. Primum non quia in hoc differt secta Machometi a secta Christi quod hic pseudo legem suam iubet defendi non racione **sed** gladio; Christus **autem** mandat in Petro quod de omni fide sua debent discipuli eius discrete, modeste et pacifice reddere racionem. Scarioth **autem** excitatus a dyabolo accepit cohortem et ministros pontificum, **sed** caute in tenebra, ut deprimeret veritatem, ut patet Joh. XVIIIᵒ, 3, **sed** pseudo Scariotis discipuli agitati a demonio meridiano publice in sermonibus incitant populum in vulgari, ut destruant tamquam hereticos simplices discipulos veritatis. Nec mirum quia eodem spiritu in fratres proprios tanquam Roberti discipuli magis callide machinantur.

ENGLISH: Here [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that many are heretics, some known and some unknown, for all [the foreknown]{g:prescitus} are heretics;[^1] but some are heretics in act and others in habit, although they may be in grace [according to present justice]{g:secundum-presentem-iusticiam}. Some also are heretics for a time only, according to present justice, as [the predestinate]{g:predestinatus} in mortal [sin]; and so of many divisions with their explanations, which I here suppose. But it is known that it can stand that many men hold a heretical [teaching]{g:sentencia}[^2] without their being heretics then, for many hold many teachings [as an opinion]{g:opinative} or conditionally, ready to retract when the opposite is taught; and it is certain that none such are heretics as such, for then [the Common Doctor]{g:doctor-communis} would have been a heretic [exceedingly]{g:nimis} often. Hence it seems that those who [teach as dogma]{g:dogmatisare}, who, when arguments fail them, defame the university[^3] in the vernacular for scholastic sayings and the teachings of [the saints]{g:sancti}, should be publicly punished; and this I would wish, [156] since they are manifestly heretics unless they prove what they spread abroad, or [I would wish] that they not receive a dignity at Oxford before they have shown the heresy which they impute;[^4] for it is of the nature of an accusation of heresy that the accuser, as such, binds himself to the [penalty of retaliation]{g:pena-talionis}, since he believes the contradictory of what he attacks to be catholic;[^5] and the same judgement holds of the whole chapter that consents.[^6] So therefore many saints passed over this article of the sacred [accidents]{g:accidens} as contrary to faith, an article ventured by none of the [wise]{g:sciolus} of their time, as is clear of the ancient holy doctors.[^7] And it seems that the Avignon [church]{g:ecclesia}, positing [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio}, does not thereby imply that the [substance]{g:substancia} of the [bread]{g:panis} is destroyed or corrupted. But in conformity with the ancient [decree]{g:decretum} of the Roman church it allows the substance of the bread to remain;[^8] yet one thing I believe: that whoever obstinately believes "bread and [wine]{g:vinum} do not remain in the [consecrated host]{g:hostia}, but it is a mass of sacred accidents [without a subject]{g:accidens-sine-subiecto}" is manifestly a heretic, to be expelled from any [whole body of the faithful]{g:universitas-fidelium}.[^9] He also contradicts the faith which the great Augustine confessed with the Roman church. Nor to this day has believer or [unbeliever]{g:infidelis} known how to attack this teaching of Augustine. But this teaching which is ascribed to St Thomas is so weak and feeble that no one knows how to defend or prove it; and I believe that no more effective [quibbles]{g:argucie} will be made for [pronouncing heretical]{g:hereticare} the aforesaid teaching of Augustine than those which have been [recited]{g:recitare} in the name of the Common Doctor and openly [resolved]{g:solvere}. For it does not accord with the faith of Christ either that a person be accused of heresy without cause or order, or that one go to the curia, lyingly procuring the [declaration as catholic]{g:katholicacio} [157] of all that St Thomas wrote.[^10] Not the first, because the [sect]{g:secta} of Mahomet differs from the sect of Christ in this, that this false one orders his law to be defended not by reason but by the sword; but Christ commands in Peter that his disciples ought to render a reason of all their faith discreetly, modestly and peaceably.[^11] And Iscariot, stirred up by the devil, took a band and servants of the [pontiffs]{g:pontifex}, but warily, in the dark, to suppress the truth, as is clear from John 18:3; but the false ones, disciples of Iscariot,[^12] driven by the noonday devil, publicly in sermons incite the people in the vernacular, to destroy as heretics the simple disciples of the truth.[^13] Nor is it a wonder, for by the same spirit they plot more cunningly against their own brethren as disciples of Robert.[^14]

### 05-k ¶2 · 157.15–158.10  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 157] Quantum ad secundum patet quod multe sentencie heretice sunt scriptis sancti Thome imposite; patet quod idem foret in tali involucro approbare totam sentenciam sancti Thome et in equivocacionem tocius evangelii et fidei katholice inducere dogmata Antichristi. Inponitur enim sibi Secunda Secunde, questione LXVII, articulo IIᵒ, quod asserit iudicem debere sentenciare secundum allegata et probata, licet sciverit illa esse falsa et iudicio divino contraria, quod est manifeste hereticum, pallians Pylati iudicium contra Christum. Ideo idem foret defendere istam sentenciam et defendere quod propter tradiciones humanas licet peccando facere contra Deum. Cum ergo beatus Augustinus et alii sancti doctores qui plus sciverunt quam mille tales doctores nunquam vendicarunt **sed** [p. 158] reprobarunt quod credatur de eis si hoc asserunt, ergo verum, qua fronte vendicarent fratres viciantes sic scripta Thome si ipse sic asserit, ergo verum? Nam Doctor Subtilis cui plus credendum est in speculacionibus quam sibi in pluribus plane detegit errores quos seminat. Cum ergo plane infirmate sunt raciones predicte, facile est tollere alias fiendas contra sentenciam Augustini et Romane ecclesie. Non ergo sequitur: Si Thomas hoc asserit, ergo hoc est populo predicandum.

ENGLISH: As to the second, it is clear that many heretical teachings have been imputed to the writings of St Thomas;[^15] it is clear that, in such a [wrapping]{g:involucrum}, to approve the whole teaching of St Thomas would be the same as to bring in the dogmas of Antichrist, to the [equivocation]{g:equivocacio} of the whole gospel and of the catholic faith.[^16] For it is imputed to him, in the *Secunda Secundae*, question 67, article 2, that he asserts that a judge ought to [pronounce]{g:sentenciare} according to what is [alleged]{g:allegare} and proved, although he knows it to be false and contrary to divine judgement;[^17] which is manifestly heretical, [cloaking]{g:palliare} Pilate's judgement against Christ. Therefore it would be the same to defend this teaching and to defend "for the sake of human traditions it is lawful, by sinning, to act against God". Since, then, blessed Augustine and other holy doctors, who knew more than a thousand such doctors, never claimed, but [158] rejected, that it be believed of them "if they assert this, therefore it is true", with what face would the friars, who thus corrupt the writings of Thomas, claim "if he so asserts, therefore it is true"?[^18] For [the Subtle Doctor]{g:doctor-subtilis}, who is to be believed more than he in speculative matters, plainly [exposes]{g:detegere} in many places the errors which he sows.[^19] Since, then, the aforesaid arguments have been plainly weakened, it is easy to [demolish]{g:tollere} others that may be made against the teaching of Augustine and of the Roman church.[^20] Therefore [it does not follow]{g:sequitur}: "If Thomas asserts this, therefore this is to be preached to the people."

### 06-a ¶1 · 158.12–158.31  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 158] **Sed** dimissa ista contenciosa materia superest videre (ut tactum est superius) quomodo christianus debet disponi ad ministrandum et recipiendum hoc sacramentum. Iste enim foret fructus tractatus scolastici istius materie quoad dignitatem conficiencium et recipiencium hoc sacramentum; patet quod oportet digne sumentem ipsum habere actus et habitus virtuosos, cum oportet quod Dei virtus et Dei sapiencia sit munde secundum habitus et opera quoad humanitatem suam undique ministrata; oportet, inquam, digne sumentem hoc sacramentum habere tres virtutes theologicas et conversacionem virtuosam imitatoriam capitis sui Christi; quod quidam intelligunt ex verbis Apostoli prima I. Cor. Vᵒ, quando docet quod non debemus epulari in fermento veteri quoad maculam peccati **sed** in azimis sinceritatis quoad virtutes que sunt disposicio ad recipiendum lumen gracie et operis virtuosi et veritatis vite qua fidelis servus Christi ostenditur in opere. Et hec est plena disposicio ad digne accipiendum corpus dominicum.

ENGLISH: But, this contentious matter set aside, it remains to see (as was touched on above) how a Christian ought to be disposed for ministering and receiving this [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}. For this would be the fruit of a scholastic treatise on this matter, as regards the worthiness of those who [consecrate]{g:conficere} and those who receive this sacrament: it is clear that one who takes it worthily must have virtuous acts and habits, since the power of God and the wisdom of God must be ministered cleanly on every side, in habits and works, as regards his [humanity]{g:humanitas};[^1] one who takes this sacrament worthily must, I say, have the three theological virtues and a virtuous [manner of life]{g:conversacio} imitative of his head, Christ. This some understand from the words of the Apostle in 1 Cor. 5, where he teaches that we ought not to feast with the old leaven, as to the stain of sin, but with the unleavened bread of sincerity, as to the virtues, which are the disposition for receiving the light of grace and of virtuous work, and of truth of life, by which the faithful servant of Christ is shown in work.[^2] And this is the full disposition for worthily receiving [the Lord's body]{g:corpus-dominicum}.[^3]

### 06-a ¶3 · 159.12–160.14  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 159] Habita **autem** dignitate ex fundamento fidei necesse est quod erigatur spes in Domino omnipotente, omnisciente et benivolente, qui tantam gratitudinem nobis sibi ingratis ostendit humanitus paciendo, moriendo, ascendendo et sacramentum illud in memoriam istorum et aliorum mirabilium tribuendo. Unde Augustinus Enchiridion LIIIᵒ: Quidquid **autem** gestum est in cruce Christi, in sepultura, in resurreccione tercia die, in ascensione in celum et in eo quod sedet ad dextram patris, ita gestum est ut hiis rebus non mistice tantum dictis **sed** eciam gestis configuraretur vita christiana que hic geritur. Nam propter eius crucem dictum est: Qui **autem** Christi sunt carnem suam crucifixerunt cum viciis et concupiscenciis. Propter sepulturam: Consepulti enim sumus Christo per baptismum in mortem. Propter resurreccionem: Ut quemadmodum Christus resurrexit a mortuis per gloriam patris, ita et nos in novitate vite ambulemus. Propter ascensionem in celum sedemque ad [p. 160] dextram Patris: Si consurrexistis cum Christo, que sursum sunt sapite, non que super terram: Mortui enim estis et vita vestra abscondita est cum Christo in Deo. Ecce quomodo iste sanctus connectit ex fide scripture istam figuram nostram quintuplicem et rem gestam ad nostram doctrinam in moribus. Et totum istud debemus secundum usum ecclesie in sacramento eukaristie specialiter memorari, cum Christus precipiat pro istis Luc. XXᵒ, 19: Hoc facite in meam commemoracionem; et addit ecclesia: Hec quocienscunque feceritis in mei memoriam facietis. Et patet quomodo dignitas manducacionis huius sacramenti requirit quod ipsum digne accipiens per spem ad Dominum erigatur.

ENGLISH: And, worthiness had from the foundation of faith, it is necessary that hope be raised up in the Lord, almighty, all-knowing and benevolent, who showed such great [kindness]{g:gratitudo} to us who are [ungrateful]{g:ingratus} to him,[^6] [humanly]{g:humanitus} suffering, dying, ascending, and granting that sacrament in memory of these and other wonders. Hence Augustine, *Enchiridion* 53: "Whatever was done in the cross of Christ, in his burial, in his resurrection on the third day, in his ascension into heaven and in his sitting at the right hand of the Father, was so done that by these things, not only said [mystically]{g:mistice} but also done, the Christian life which is lived here might be configured. For on account of his cross it is said: 'And they that are Christ's have crucified their flesh, with the vices and concupiscences.' On account of his burial: 'For we are buried together with Christ by baptism into death.' On account of his resurrection: 'That, as Christ is risen from the dead by the glory of the Father, so we also may walk in newness of life.' On account of his ascension into heaven and his seat at the [160] right hand of the Father: 'If you be risen with Christ, mind the things that are above, not the things that are upon the earth. For you are dead: and your life is hid with Christ in God.'"[^7] See how this saint connects, [from the faith of scripture]{g:ex-fide}, this fivefold [figure]{g:figura} of ours and the thing done, for our [doctrine]{g:doctrina} in morals. And all this we ought, according to the use of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, to remember specially in the sacrament of the eucharist, since Christ commands for these things, Luke 22:19: "Do this for a commemoration of me"; and the church adds: "As often as you do these things, you shall do them in memory of me."[^8] And it is clear how the worthiness of [eating]{g:manducacio} this sacrament requires that one who receives it worthily be raised up to the Lord through hope.

### 06-b ¶1 · 161.14–163.12  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 161] Et oportet quarto quod persona salvanda recipiat formam panis que imposita furno tribulacionis recipiat ex fervore caritatis quam habet ad Deum duriciem gracie consumate, et sic per ignem caritatis incorporabitur finaliter in circulo pani vivo et verificabitur illud Apostoli 1. Cor. Xº, 17: Quoniam unus panis et unum corpus multi sumus. Nam omnes de uno pane et de uno calice participamus. Et sic intelligunt de triplici gracia, scilicet preveniente quantum ad meritoriam passionem; nam pati et recipere precedit in nobis naturaliter meritoriam accionem, ad quam requiritur gracia cooperans. Et tercio post graciam predestinacionis requiritur ad generanciam et continuacionem gracia consumans. Oportet enim vitam veram dare nobis graciam calidam confuse informantem; unde signum infallibile est quod manducamus hunc panem indigne quod inter omnes gentes pro temporalibus magis contendimus et refrigescente caritate multorum ex superhabundante frigiditate temporalium preoccupancium mentem egeni in spiritualibus [p. 162] sequelam Christi magis contempnimus, dum tercio tempus et studium circa avidam conquisicionem, improvidam dispensacionem et avaram retencionem temporalium occupamus, sic quod ubi nobis sacerdotibus secundum regulam Christi omnia debent esse communia, unus manducans hunc panem esurit et alter est ebrius. Et inter omnes sacerdotes illi superiores qui efficacius imitarentur Christi vestigia in faciendo istud officium magis contemptibiliter quia plus Christo dissimiliter celebrant vel audiunt missas suas, in tantum quod ut periuri ad sui dispendium memorantur et nominant nomen Dei, sic et isti memorantur in sacramento eukaristie vite Christi; ideo tollerabilius et minus nocivum foret eis et populo quod non facerent talem falsam memoriam Salvatoris. Et sic ubi domini temporales care conducunt huiusmodi, ut eos habeant dignos et assiduos oratores, habent eos precipuos proditores. Nec mirum, quia contra legem Christi et racionem sacerdotalis ministerii dissolvendo statum primevum, onerant eos ac inhabilitant ad implendum suum ministerium. Ideo oportet quod racio militet contra utrumque membrum ecclesie sic protervum. Hoc enim sacramentum significaret quoscunque qui ipsum digne suscipiunt paratos ut pro Christo et causa legis Christi moriantur cum gaudio **sed** signata obmittimus. Ad signa **autem** que ritum et fastum sapiunt tamquam generacio adultera nimis attendimus; in tantum quod superiores persone ecclesie signis istis sensibilibus sic cecantur quod Samaritanis et paganis dampnabilius adorant (ut Christus dicit Joh. IVº capitulo) quod ignorant. Numquid credimus [p. 163] quod congregatis omnibus prelatis cesariis omnes sciunt quid est hostia consecrata? et errante intellectu necesse est quod erret affectus, cum cecitas oculi precipitat ambulantem. Cum igitur accipimus hoc sacramentum gracia gratitudinis Domini memorande, patet quod ad iudicium nobis manducamus et bibimus, quia remanemus post tam sollempnem memoriam Domino tam ingrati. Unde decretum Augustini positum in de Consecracione distinccione II sic loquitur: Quia morte Christi liberati sumus, huius rei memores in edendo carnem et potando sanguinem eius, que pro nobis oblata sunt nos obligaciores signamus.

ENGLISH: And fourthly the person to be saved must receive the form of [bread]{g:panis}, which, placed in the oven of tribulation, receives from the fervour of [charity]{g:caritas} which he has towards God the hardness of consummate grace; and so through the fire of charity he will finally be incorporated, [in a round]{g:in-circulo}, into the living bread,[^1] and that word of the Apostle [will be true]{g:verificari}, 1 Cor. 10:17: "For we, being many, are one bread, one body: for all partake of one bread and of one [chalice]{g:calix}."[^2] And so they understand [it] of a threefold grace, namely [prevenient]{g:gracia-preveniens} as to meritorious [being-acted-on]{g:passio};[^3] for to be acted on and to receive precedes in us by nature meritorious action, for which cooperating grace is required. And thirdly, after the grace of predestination, [consummating grace]{g:gracia-consumans} is required for [begetting]{g:generancia} and [continuity]{g:continuacio}.[^4] For the true life must give us warm grace, [informing]{g:informare} [us] [indistinctly]{g:confuse};[^5] hence it is an infallible sign that we eat this bread unworthily, that among all peoples we contend most for [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, and, the charity of many growing cold from the superabounding coldness of temporal goods preoccupying the mind, we, needy in spiritual things, [162] most despise the [following]{g:sequela} of Christ, while, thirdly, we occupy time and study about the greedy acquisition, improvident [dispensation]{g:dispensacio} and avaricious retention of temporal goods;[^6] so that where, for us priests, according to the rule of Christ all things ought to be common, "one" eating this bread "is hungry and another is drunk".[^7] And among all [priests]{g:sacerdos} those superiors who would more effectively imitate the footsteps of Christ in performing this office celebrate or hear their masses more contemptibly, because more unlike Christ,[^8] so much so that, as perjurers make mention of and name the name of God to their own loss, so these too make mention, in the sacrament of the eucharist, of the life of Christ; therefore it would be more tolerable and less harmful for them and for the people that they did not make such a false memorial of the Saviour. And so, where [temporal lords]{g:temporales-domini} hire such men dearly, to have them as worthy and assiduous [bedesmen]{g:oratores}, they have them as chief [traitors]{g:proditor}.[^9] Nor is it a wonder, for, against the law of Christ and the nature of the priestly ministry, dissolving the primitive [state]{g:status}, they burden them and [disqualify]{g:inhabilitare} them from fulfilling their ministry. Therefore reason must fight against both [members]{g:membrum} of the [church]{g:ecclesia} so [obstinate]{g:protervus}.[^10] For this [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} would signify that whoever receive it worthily are ready to die with joy for Christ and the cause of the law of Christ; but we omit the [things signified]{g:signatum}. And to the [signs]{g:signum} that savour of rite and pomp we attend [too much]{g:nimis}, as an adulterous generation;[^11] so much so that the superior persons of the church are so blinded by these [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} signs that, more damnably than Samaritans and pagans, they adore (as Christ says, John 4) what they know not.[^12] Do we believe [163] that, were all the [caesarean prelates]{g:prelatus} gathered, all of them know what the consecrated [host]{g:hostia} is? And when the understanding errs, the affection must err, since blindness of the eye throws the walker headlong. Since, therefore, we receive this sacrament for the sake of the [kindness]{g:gratitudo} of the Lord, which is to be remembered, it is clear that we eat and drink to our judgement, because after so solemn a memorial we remain so [ungrateful]{g:ingratus} to the Lord.[^13] Hence the [decree]{g:decretum} of Augustine placed in *De consecratione*, distinction 2, speaks thus: "Because we have been freed by the death of Christ, mindful of this thing in eating the flesh and drinking the blood, which were offered for us, we signify ourselves the more bound."[^14]

### 06-b ¶2 · 163.13–164.3  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 163] Et patet quomodo fides, spes, caritas sunt necessaria celebranti; **sed** hodie est nobis nimis sinistra perversio, dum fides infigitur in signo, spes in mundo et caritas in infimo. Ponimus enim plus quam generacio phariseorum adultera fidem huius sacramenti in signo et in perfidis circumstanciis signorum, quia circa illorum observancias est nostra sollicitudo, nostra disputacio et nostra religio. Quoad spem patet quod ipsam ponimus in temporalium possessione, in amicorum confederacione et in prosperitatis mundane perseveracione. Et quoad caritatem patet quod ipsa sequitur in transformacione duas sorores priores, dum illos fratres quos exaltamus ad fimum mundi et honorem seculi, fingimus nos caritative diligere; illum sexum quem appetimus illecebris carnis confundere fingimus nos amare. Et quoad caritatem Dei ille fingitur plus [p. 164] Deum diligere qui superius aspirat vivere, quia (ut inquiunt) Deo similius. Et certum est quod quodlibet istorum est odium.

ENGLISH: And it is clear how faith, hope and charity are necessary to the one who celebrates; but today there is among us an exceedingly sinister perversion, while faith is fixed in the sign, hope in the world and charity in the lowest.[^15] For, more than the adulterous generation of the Pharisees, we place the faith of this sacrament in the sign and in the faithless circumstances of the signs, for about their observances is our solicitude, our disputation and our religion. As to hope, it is clear that we place it in the possession of temporal goods, in alliance with friends and in the continuance of worldly prosperity. And as to charity, it is clear that it follows its two prior sisters in transformation, while we pretend that we love charitably those brethren whom we exalt to the dung of the world and the honour of the age;[^16] we pretend that we love that sex which we desire to confound with the allurements of the flesh. And as to the charity of God, he is feigned to love [164] God more who aspires to live higher, because (as they say) more like God. And it is certain that each of these is hatred.

### 06-b ¶3 · 164.4–165.16  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 164] Quantum ad secundum, scilicet conversacionem virtuosam unitatoriam Jesu Christi patet ex dictis quod gracia huius constituitur hoc sacramentum. Unde inaniter celebrant qui pretereunt hoc figuratum. Figuratur **autem** nobis hec imitacio conversandi Exod XIIº, 7, ubi precipitur quod in esu agni paschalis ista 14ᶜⁱᵐ observentur, ut exponit Gregorius Super Evangelia Omelia XII: Quis, inquit, sit sanguis agni, non iam audiendo **sed** bibendo didicistis, qui sanguis super utrumque postem ponitur, quando non solum ore corporis **sed** eciam ore cordis accipitur aut hauritur. In utroque etenim poste sanguis agni positus est, quando sacramentum passionis illius cum ore ad redempcionem sumitur, ad imitacionem quoque intenta mente cogitatur. Nam qui sic redemptoris sui sanguinem accipit, ut imitari passionem illius necdum velit, in uno poste sanguinem posuit, qui eciam in superliminaribus domorum ponendus est. Quid enim spiritualiter domos nisi mentes nostras accipimus, in quibus per cogitacionem habitamus? Cuius domus superliminare est ipsa intencio que preeminet accioni. Qui igitur intencionem cogitacionis sue ad imitacionem dominice passionis dirigit in superliminari domus agni sanguinem ponit. Vel certe domus nostre sunt ipsa corda in quibus quousque vivimus, habitamus et in superliminari domus agni sanguinem ponimus, quia crucem passionis illius in fronte portamus. De quo [p. 165] adhuc agno subditur: Et edent carnes nocte illa assas igni. In nocte quippe agnum comedimus, quia in sacramento modo corpus dominicum accipimus, quando adhuc adinvicem consciencias nostras non videmus. Que tamen carnes igne assande sunt, quia nimirum dissolvit carnes quas aqua coxerit, quas vero ignis sine aqua excoquit roborat. Carnes itaque agni nostri coxit ignis, quia eum ipsa vis passionis illius ad resurreccionem valenciorem reddidit atque incorrupcionem roboravit. Qui enim ex morte convaluerit, videlicet carnes illius ab igne duruerunt. Unde per Psalmistam eciam dicit: Exaruit velud testa virtus mea quid namque est testa ante ignem; nisi molle lutum? **Sed** ei ex igne agitur ut solidetur, virtus igitur humanitatis eius velut testa exaruit, quia ab igne passionis ad virtutem incorrupcionis crevit.

ENGLISH: As to the second, namely the virtuous [manner of life]{g:conversacio} imitating Jesus Christ,[^17] it is clear from what has been said that this sacrament is constituted for the sake of this. Hence they celebrate emptily who pass over this [thing figured]{g:figuratum}.[^18] And this imitation in [living one's life]{g:conversari} is figured for us in Exod. 12:7, where it is commanded that in the [eating]{g:esus} of the paschal lamb these fourteen [things] be observed,[^19] as Gregory expounds, *On the Gospels*, Homily 12: "What the blood of the lamb is," he says, "you have learned not now by hearing but by drinking; which blood is put upon both posts when it is received or drawn not only with the mouth of the body but also with the mouth of the heart. For the blood of the lamb is put on both posts when the sacrament of his passion is taken with the mouth for redemption and is also thought on with an attentive mind for imitation. For he who so receives the blood of his Redeemer that he does not yet wish to imitate his passion has put the blood on one post, which is also to be put on the lintels of the houses. For what do we take the houses to be, [spiritually]{g:spiritualiter}, but our minds, in which we dwell by thought? The lintel of this house is the intention itself, which stands above action. He therefore who directs the intention of his thought to the imitation of the Lord's passion puts the blood of the lamb on the lintel of the house. Or certainly our houses are the hearts themselves, in which we dwell as long as we live, and we put the blood of the lamb on the lintel of the house because we bear the cross of his passion on the forehead. Of which [165] lamb it is further added: 'And they shall eat the flesh that night roasted at the fire.' For we eat the lamb at night, because in the sacrament we now receive [the Lord's body]{g:corpus-dominicum} while we do not yet see one another's consciences.[^20] Yet this flesh is to be roasted with fire, because the flesh which water has boiled it indeed dissolves, but what fire cooks without water it hardens.[^21] Fire therefore cooked the flesh of our lamb, because the very force of his passion rendered him stronger for the resurrection and hardened him to incorruption. For he who grew strong out of death: that is, his flesh was hardened by the fire.[^22] Hence through the Psalmist he also says: 'My strength is dried up like a potsherd.' For what is a potsherd before the fire but soft clay? But it is acted on by fire so that it is made solid. The strength of his [humanity]{g:humanitas} therefore dried up like a potsherd, because by the fire of the passion it grew to the strength of incorruption."[^23]
