## Doubtful, left as they are

- **08-i, p. 267**: *Deus autem … presens cuilibet instanti …; tempus **autem** a mundi inicio usque ad finem mensurat ipsum*, rendered "and time, from the beginning of the world to the end, measures it". The sentence returns from God to time, so the *autem* could be read as setting time against God's eternity ("but time"). It reads equally well as continuing the account of the three measures: ¶5 is about time, and the God clause is the contrast already marked by the preceding "But God". "And" stands.
- **08-j, p. 269**: *Nam secundum sanctos non est in eo formaliter multitudo, alia **autem** licet sint ubique … ubique tamen dispariter a Deo*, rendered "and other things, although they are everywhere …, are yet everywhere otherwise than God". God and other things are set against each other, so "but other things" (b) is defensible. The contrast, though, is carried by *tamen* ("yet … otherwise than God"), and the *autem* continues the *nam* explanation. A real toss-up, so it is left.
- **08-j, p. 270**: *Cum **autem** forma substancialis aut accidentalis non sit nisi talem essenciam informari, patet quod non exinde multiplicatur situs replecio*, rendered "And since a substantial form … it is clear that the filling of the site is not multiplied thereby". This brings a further premiss to bear on a conclusion (*patet*), so "Now since" (a) could be argued. No *ergo / ideo / igitur* closes it, and it reads as a corollary added to the preceding step (the site's capacity is filled), so "and" is defensible. Left.
