### 02-c ¶1 · 36.24–38.21  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 36] Secundum exemplum est illud Apoc. XIXº, 8: Byssinum enim iustificaciones sunt sanctorum, ut alias diffuse exposui; et tercium exemplum est illud I. Cor. XIº, 25: Hic calix novum testamentum est in meo sanguine; figuravit enim vinum in calice novum testamentum, sicut sanguis vituli fuit signum confirmacionis veteris testamenti, ut patet Exod. XXXIVº. Sic igitur videtur sanctos ecclesie primitive et suos filios post eos intelligere per panem et vinum figurative corpus Christi et sanguinem. Unde Iª Cor. XIº, 21 narrans Apostolus quomodo Corinthi conveniebant ad manducandum cenam dominicam [p. 37] unoquoque portante secum proprium panem et vinum, sic quod unus fuit ebrius et alius esuriens post hanc cenam, increpat eos leniter in hoc facto et addit instruccionem quoad hoc sacramentum, que tunc suffecit ecclesie; videlicet quod conveniant manducantes in ecclesia ordinate vel aliter domi infirmi manducent nec alii darent scandalum fratribus in copiosis, in sumptuosis et in deliciosis preparacionibus panis et vini. Unde pro plena instruccione quoad hoc sacramentum sic loquitur: Ego enim accepi a Domino quod et tradidi vobis; quoniam dominus Jesus in qua nocte tradebatur accepit panem et gracias agens fregit et dixit: Accipite et manducate, hoc est corpus meum quod pro vobis tradetur, hoc facite in meam commemoracionem. Quocienscunque, inquit, manducabitis panem hunc et calicem bibetis, mortem Domini annunciabitis donec veniat; ubi patet quod loquitur quadrupliciter de pane et vino materiali et per consequens dicit hoc esse corpus eius et sanguinem in figura, sicut dicit hunc calicem esse novum testamentum et hoc fieri in eius commemoracionem. Quod **autem** alii dicuntur in medio prandii accipere instar Christi hostiam a sacerdotibus consecratam, videtur esse sompnium, **sed** sacramenta et ritus adiecti videntur michi esse racionabiles, sic tamen quod non sint nimium ponderati. Aliqui enim stulte plus ponderant ritus adiectos quam sentenciam Apostoli, dum sic dicit: Itaque quicunque manducaverit panem hunc vel biberit calicem Domini indigne, reus erit corporis [p. 38] et sanguinis Domini. Intelligit enim quod panis et vinum non debent sumi in memoriam corporis Christi et sanguinis nisi digne, hoc est, nisi mundo corde et pura intencione; et sic videtur michi quod nulli ritus honesti de Eukaristia debent contempni nec plus virtutibus ponderari; et utrobique videtur quod scriptura vocat hoc sacramentum panem et vinum, quomodo quidam intelligunt illud Act. IIº, 46 quod dicitur de conversis: Cotidie **autem** perdurantes unanimiter in templo et frangentes circa domos panem sumebant cibum cum exultacione et simplicitate cordis. Panis itaque vocatur utrobique hoc venerabile sacramentum; quod si sit verum, patet cum paribus quod graviter peccaverunt qui onerarunt ecclesiam tam speculative quam practice tot perplexitatibus quibus difficultatur circa hoc sacramentum. Manet ergo iuxta sentenciam ecclesie in Beringario, iuxta sanctos doctores et formam verborum scripture tam panis quam vinum post benediccionem et sacramentum et corpus Christi in signo et non in essencia quod sic frangitur et tractatur.

ENGLISH: The second example is that of Apoc. 19:8: "For the fine linen are the justifications of saints", as I have set out at length elsewhere;[^1] and the third example is that of 1 Cor. 11:25: "This [chalice]{g:calix} is the new testament in my blood"; for the [wine]{g:vinum} in the chalice [figured]{g:figurare} the new testament, as the blood of the calf was the sign of the confirmation of the old testament, as is clear from Exod. 34.[^2] So therefore it seems that [the saints]{g:sancti} of the primitive [church]{g:ecclesia} and their sons after them understood by the [bread]{g:panis} and wine [figuratively]{g:figurative} [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} and [his] blood. Hence in 1 Cor. 11:21 the Apostle, relating how the Corinthians came together to eat the Lord's supper, [37] each one bringing with him his own bread and wine, so that one was drunk and another hungry after this supper, rebukes them gently in this deed and adds an instruction concerning this [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}, which then sufficed the church: namely, that they come together to eat in the church in order, or else that the weak eat at home, and that the others should not give scandal to the brethren in plentiful, sumptuous and delicious preparations of bread and wine. Hence for full instruction concerning this sacrament he speaks thus: "For I have received of the Lord that which also I delivered unto you, that the Lord Jesus, the same night in which he was betrayed, took bread, and giving thanks, broke, and said: Take ye and eat: This is my body, which shall be delivered for you. This do for the commemoration of me." "As often", he says, "as you shall eat this bread and drink the chalice, you shall shew the death of the Lord, until he come."[^3] Where it is clear that he speaks four times of material bread and wine, and consequently says this to be his body and blood in figure, as he says this chalice to be the new testament and this to be done in his commemoration.[^4] But that others are said, in the middle of a dinner, to receive after the manner of Christ a [host]{g:hostia} consecrated by priests, seems to be a dream;[^5] but the sacraments and the [rites]{g:ritus} added seem to me to be reasonable, yet so that they are not weighed too much. For some foolishly weigh the added rites more than the [sense]{g:sentencia-verborum} of the Apostle, when he says thus: "Therefore, whosoever shall eat this bread,[^6] or drink the chalice of the Lord unworthily, shall be guilty of the body [38] and of the blood of the Lord." For he understands that the bread and wine ought not to be taken in memory of the body and blood of Christ except worthily, that is, except with a clean heart and pure intention; and so it seems to me that no honest rites of the Eucharist ought to be despised, nor weighed more than the virtues; and in both places it seems that scripture calls this sacrament bread and wine,[^7] as some understand that of Acts 2:46 which is said of the converts: "And continuing daily with one accord in the temple and breaking bread from house to house, they took their meat with gladness and simplicity of heart." So this venerable sacrament is called bread in both places; and if this is true, it is clear, with like cases, that those sinned gravely who burdened the church, both speculatively and practically, with so many [perplexities]{g:perplexitas} by which it is made difficult concerning this sacrament. There remains therefore, according to the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of the church in Berengar, according to the holy doctors and the form of the words of scripture, both the bread and the wine after the blessing, both sacrament and body of Christ, in sign and not in essence, which is thus broken and handled.[^8]

Quare ergo non teneremus nos eandem loycam, cum non licet in materia fidei secundum loycam variare?

ENGLISH: And the [resolution]{g:solvere} of the objection is clear, by which it is said that the teaching of the church in Berengar contradicts the teaching stated above. But [it is rejoined]{g:replicatur} secondly, on the ground that sacred scripture in the six places aforesaid calls the sacrament of the Eucharist bread, and the holy doctors say the same, together with the [decree]{g:decretum} of the church in Berengar. Why therefore should we not hold the same [logic]{g:loyca}, since it is not lawful in a matter of faith to vary as to logic?[^9]

### 02-d ¶2 · 41.17–42.7  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 41] **Sed** tercio replicatur contra dicta per hoc quod non docetur que scriptura debet admitti et que abnui, nec in quo doctor est tenendus et in quo negandus. Ymmo videtur quod ecclesia tempore Beringarii fuit heretica vel aliter infidelis fuit ecclesia sequens illi contraria. Hic dico quod tota scriptura sacra debet admitti ut fides et detestari eius falsitas, cum ex integro sit vera et in nulla parte falsa, ut hic supponitur. Figurativas **autem** locuciones scripture debemus exponere et modo discipulorum dimissis figuris nostris auctoribus loqui plane. Unde solebam exponere predictam scripturam Genes. VIᵒ, ubi notato textu solebam dicere, quomodo minus bene intelligentibus textus iste videtur pretendere quod Deus prius ignorans peccatum hominum futurum, postquam in facto [p. 42] didicit hominem ex pronitate sic peccare, doluit et displicuit sibi vel quod unquam fecit hominem, vel quod fecit hominem tam fragilem; **sed** quod fecisset eum constantem et confirmatum ut angelum, quia tunc caruisset occupacione penali in castigando eum pro delicto; **sed** longe alium sensum pretendit bene intelligentibus.

ENGLISH: But thirdly [it is rejoined]{g:replicatur} against what has been said, on the ground that it is not taught which scripture ought to be admitted and which refused, nor in what a doctor is to be held and in what denied. Indeed, it seems that the church in the time of Berengar was heretical, or else the church that followed, contrary to it, was unbelieving. Here I say that the whole of sacred scripture ought to be admitted as faith, and falsity in it detested, since it is entirely true and in no part false, as is supposed here.[^5] But the figurative locutions of scripture we ought to expound, and, after the manner of disciples, leaving the figures to our authors, speak plainly.[^6] Hence I used to expound the aforesaid scripture of Gen. 6, where, having marked the text, I used to say how to those who understand less well this text seems to put forward that God, previously ignorant of the future sin of men, after he [42] learned in fact that man sinned thus from proneness, grieved, and it displeased him either that he ever made man, or that he made man so frail; but [he wished] that he had made him steadfast and confirmed like an angel, because then he would have been without the [hard]{g:penalis} occupation of chastising him for his offence; but it puts forward a far other sense to those who understand well.[^7]

### 02-d ¶4 · 42.13–42.25  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 42] Secundo secundum historiographos est notandum quod mortuo Adam Seth ex mandato patris separavit cognacionem suam a Kaym, rediens ad solum natale in quendam montem propinquum paradiso, **sed** post septimam generacionem fertur filios eius qui propter religiositatem vocantur filii Dei miscuisse cum filiabus Kaym que propter petulanciam vocantur filie hominum; et potuit cum hoc esse quod demones incubi qui numerantur inter filios Dei detulerunt semina hominum in matrices mulierum procreando gigantes ex industria fovendi fetus per virtutes mixtorum, et ista brutalitas cum aliis que narrat Methodius martyr fuit causa diluvii.

ENGLISH: Secondly, it is to be noted, according to the historiographers, that when Adam was dead Seth, by his father's command, separated his [kindred]{g:cognacio} from Cain, returning to his native soil, onto a certain mountain near paradise; but after the seventh generation his sons, who on account of their [religiousness]{g:religiositas} are called sons of God, are said to have mingled with the daughters of Cain, who on account of their wantonness are called daughters of men; and it could be, along with this, that incubus demons, who are numbered among the sons of God, carried the seeds of men into the wombs of women, procreating giants by their skill in fostering the fetuses through the [powers]{g:virtus} of [mixed bodies]{g:mixtum}; and this brutishness, with other things that Methodius the martyr [relates]{g:narrare}, was the cause of the flood.[^9]

### 02-d ¶6 · 43.13–43.29  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 43] Secundo principaliter premittitur prima noticia malicie commisse, cum dicitur: Videns **autem** Deus etc. Secundo subditur pronitas ad recidivandum, dum adiungitur: Et cuncta cogitacio hominis etc. Et tercio ostenditur medicinalis satisfaccio, dum dicitur: Penituit eum quod hominem fecisset in terra. Ex faccione namque hominis terreni et peccato eius contra summum bonum oportuit Christum pati et intrare cum redemptis in gloriam suam; et hoc sonat penitere, id est, penam tenere. Nec moveat quod dicitur de presenti, quia Deus semper scit se pati. Tercio principaliter tangit in generali remedium ad abscindendum peccatum hominis in futurum, dum dicitur: Precavens in futurum, nam potestas gygancium qui fuerant super terram et sterilitas terre nascencium cum serenitate et salubritate aeris fuit illis occasio bestialiter lasciviendi.

ENGLISH: Secondly, [as a main head]{g:principaliter}, there is set first the first knowledge of the malice committed, when it is said: "And God seeing" etc. Secondly is added the proneness to relapse, when there is joined: "And all the thought of man" etc. And thirdly is shown the medicinal [satisfaction]{g:satisfaccio}, when it is said: "It repented him that he had made man on the earth."[^13] For from the [making]{g:faccio} of earthly man and his sin against the highest good it behoved Christ to suffer and to enter with the redeemed into his glory; and this is what "repent" sounds: that is, *penam tenere*, "to hold the penalty". Nor let it trouble [anyone] that it is said of the present, since God always knows himself to suffer.[^14] Thirdly, as a main head, he touches in general on the remedy for cutting off the sin of man in the future, when it is said: "Taking precaution for the future";[^15] for the [power]{g:potestas} of the giants who had been upon the earth, and the barrenness of the earth's growths, with the serenity and healthiness of the air, was for them an occasion of wantoning like beasts.[^16]

### 02-e ¶1 · 44.1–45.29  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 44] Secundo premittit eternam displicenciam, dum dicitur: Factus dolore cordis intrinsecus; non enim credendum est quod Deus habet penam secundum naturam divinam, etsi sibi mala displiceant; nec est credendum quod noviter habuit vel eternaliter elicuit displicenciam, cum fuit intrinsecus in corde; per hoc **autem** notatur quod finaliter specificat modum delendi occasionem ab inusu hominis ad terrestria, cum finaliter dicit: Delebo, inquit, hominem quem creavi a facie terre etc. Consumpsit enim mixta ornancia terram et aerem ac indisposuit raciones seminales, sicut pater misericors precavens petulanciam filii aufert ab eo occasionem peccandi; non enim punit Deus predestinatum ut prescitum, nisi misericorditer ad puniti demeritum; unde punicio aque preternaturaliter inundantis correspondet eorum inordinate luxurie. Corrupte vero exalaciones causantes salsedinem et indisponentes terram et aerem ut influencia celestis sit proporcionalis cremento et aure temporanee, sicut ante correspondet eorum affeccioni inordinate circa terrena violenter rapiendo. Unde pro istis duobus peccatis subditur: Corrupta est **autem** terra coram Deo quoad primum peccatum carnale, et repleta est iniquitate quoad secundum peccatum avaricie. Si **autem** placet intelligere per punicionem iustam execucionem vindicte que est finis penitudinis in potente, excludendo mocionem, passionem, inprovisionem etc., que dicunt inperfeccionem in penitente, non contradico sentencie. Sic enim intelligitur illud Psalmi VI, 2 et Psalmi XXXI: Domine ne in furore tuo arguas me, [p. 45] neque in ira tua corripias me. Furor enim sonat in vehemenciam et acceleracionem vindicte pro peccato irremissibili continuando usque ad finem vite pro quo Deus arguit insolubiliter concludens, ut patet Matth. XXVᵒ, 31: Cum venerit filius hominis. Talium enim terrenorum inventa est iniquitas ad odium perpetuum; secundo quia horrendum est incidere in manus Dei viventis ad Hebr. Xᵒ, 31; ideo petit quod non corripiatur propter lignum, fenum et stipulam in purgatorio expurgandum. Quamvis enim ira talis sit micior iuxta illud Abakuk IIIᵒ, 2: Cum iratus fueris, misericordie recordaberis, tamen utrumque est timendum, et pro supplecione tocius penitencie in via humiliter est petendum. Absit **autem** quod in talibus locucionibus credamus passionem vel perturbacionem in Deo accidere, cum Sap. XIIᵒ, 18 scribitur: Tu **autem** Domine virtutum cum tranquillitate iudicas. Et conformiter iudicandum est de austeritate et usura Dei in parabola Salvatoris Luc. XIXᵒ. Et eodem modo intelligendum est de Dei serenitate et quibuscunque aliis sibi attributis, que omnia ex equo concurrunt; ad Rom. IIᵒ scribit Apostolus: Vide ergo bonitatem et serenitatem Dei; et illud Apok. XVI, 15: Ecce venio sicut fur; beatus qui vigilat et custodit vestimenta sua. Et sic intelligendum est illud Psalmi LXXVIIᵒ, 65: Et excitatus est tanquam dormiens Dominus et tanquam potens crapulatus a vino cum quotlibet similibus, quorum particulares exposiciones continent indubie profunda misteria.

ENGLISH: Secondly, he sets first the eternal displeasure, when it is said: "[Touched] inwardly with sorrow of heart";[^1] for it is not to be believed that God has pain according to the divine [nature]{g:natura}, even though evils displease him; nor is it to be believed that he had the displeasure newly, or [elicited]{g:elicere} it eternally, since it was inward in the heart.[^2] And by this it is marked that he finally specifies the manner of destroying the occasion [arising] from man's use of earthly things,[^3] when he says finally: "I will destroy", he says, "man, whom I have created, from the face of the earth" etc.[^4] For he consumed the [mixed bodies]{g:mixtum} adorning the earth and the air, and [made unfit]{g:indisponere} the [seminal reasons]{g:raciones-seminales}, as a merciful father, guarding against the wantonness of his son, takes away from him the occasion of sinning; for God does not punish [the predestinate]{g:predestinatus} as [the foreknown]{g:prescitus}, except mercifully, in proportion to the demerit of the one punished;[^5] hence the punishment of water flooding beyond nature corresponds to their inordinate lust. And the corrupt exhalations, causing saltness and making the earth and air unfit, so that the celestial [influence]{g:influencia} should be proportioned to growth and to a seasonable air, correspond, as before, to their inordinate affection towards earthly things, in seizing [them] violently.[^6] Hence for these two sins it is added: "And the earth was corrupted before God", as to the first sin, the carnal, "and was filled with iniquity", as to the second, the sin of avarice.[^7] But if it pleases [anyone] to understand by punishment the just execution of vengeance, which is the end of repentance in one who is powerful, excluding motion, passion, want of foresight and the like, which [signify]{g:dicere} imperfection in one who repents, I do not contradict the [teaching]{g:sentencia}.[^8] For so is that of Psalm 6:2 and of Psalm 31 understood: "Rebuke me not, O Lord, in thy indignation; [45] nor chastise me in thy wrath."[^9] For indignation sounds the vehemence and speeding of vengeance for an unremittable sin continued until the end of life, for which God rebukes, concluding [inextricably]{g:insolubiliter},[^10] as is clear from Matt. 25:31: "When the Son of man shall come". For the iniquity of such earthly men is found unto perpetual hatred; secondly, because "it is a fearful thing to fall into the hands of the living God", Heb. 10:31; therefore he asks that he be not chastised for wood, hay and stubble, to be purged in purgatory.[^11] For although such wrath is milder, according to that of Habakkuk 3:2, "When thou art angry, thou wilt remember mercy", yet both are to be feared, and for the supplying of the whole of penance one must humbly pray in [the way]{g:via}. But far be it that in such locutions we should believe passion or disturbance to befall God, since it is written in Wisd. 12:18: "But thou, Lord of hosts, judgest with tranquillity."[^12] And in conformity one must judge of the austerity and usury of God in the Saviour's parable, Luke 19. And in the same way one must understand the serenity of God and whatever other things are attributed to him, all of which concur equally; the Apostle writes in Rom. 2: "See then the goodness and the serenity of God";[^13] and that of Apoc. 16:15: "Behold, I come as a thief. Blessed is he that watcheth and keepeth his garments." And so must that of Psalm 77:65 be understood: "And the Lord was awaked as one out of sleep, and like a mighty man that hath been surfeited with wine", with any number of like [texts], whose particular expositions without doubt contain deep [mysteries]{g:misterium}.[^14]

### 02-e ¶3 · 46.13–47.9  (6 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 46] Quantum ad secundum allegatum de autenticacione doctoris, patet quod quilibet doctor est concedendus, **sed** nullius posterioris doctoris sentencia nisi de quanto est fundabilis ex scriptura. Credo **autem** quod sentencia beati Bernhardi fuit katholica **sed** logica eius est hodie postponenda. Sic enim facimus cum loyca ecclesie de Eukaristia, sic eciam sepe prohibet Augustinus quod defendantur vel teneantur dicta sua nisi de quanto consonant cum scriptura. Intellexit **autem** Bernhardus per manus, oculos et aures personaliter verbum Dei et sic posuit figurative illa organa crucifigi; hoc est signum signatum per illa organa vel secundum alium sensum nobis absconditum. **Sed** nos non debemus predicare taliter et specialiter in vulgari sine explanacione sensus katholici, cum sentencia heretica sic de facile surriperet in auditorio ex negligencia predicantis; [p. 47] debemus enim loqui palam populo sentencias scripture necessarias ad salutem. Nec sufficit quod nos habeamus sensum katholicum, quia sic possemus seminare quotcunque hereses adversando toti scripture, dum tamen debemus niti apciori modo quo sufficimus imprimere in auditorio katholicas et edificatorias veritates, quia aliter non sumus adiutores Dei **sed** dyaboli, nostrum honorem vel seculare commodum proditorie procurantes.

ENGLISH: As to the second [allegation]{g:allegare}, concerning the [authentication]{g:autenticacio} of a doctor, it is clear that any doctor is to be granted, but the teaching of no later doctor except insofar as it can be founded on scripture.[^18] And I believe that the teaching of blessed Bernard was catholic, but his [logic]{g:loyca} is today to be [set aside]{g:postponere}. For so we do with the logic of the [church]{g:ecclesia} concerning the Eucharist; so also Augustine often forbids that his sayings be defended or held except insofar as they accord with scripture.[^19] Now Bernard understood by hands, eyes and ears, personally, [the Word]{g:verbum} of God, and so posited [figuratively]{g:figurative} that those organs were crucified; that is, the sign signified by those organs, or according to another sense hidden from us.[^20] But we ought not to preach thus, and especially in the vernacular, without explanation of the catholic sense, since a heretical teaching would thus easily creep in among the audience through the negligence of the preacher; [47] for we ought to speak openly to the people the teachings of scripture necessary to salvation. Nor is it enough that we have the catholic sense, since thus we could sow any number of heresies, opposing the whole of scripture, whereas we ought to strive, in the aptest manner we are able, to imprint on the audience catholic and edifying truths; since otherwise we are not helpers of God but of the devil, traitorously procuring our own honour or secular advantage.

### 02-e ¶5 · 47.28–48.7  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 47] **Sed** tercio principaliter replicatur per hoc quod novella ecclesia ponit transsubstanciacionem panis et vini in corpus Christi et sanguinem; hoc **autem** non posuit ecclesia primitiva, ergo in sentencia contradicunt. Assumptum patet Iᵒ Decretalium De summa [p. 48] Trinitate et Fide katholica, Una vero: Corpus Christi, inquit, et sanguis sub speciebus panis et vini in sacramento altaris veraciter continentur transsubstanciatis pane in corpus et vino in sanguinem potestate divina. Si **autem** panis et vinum manent integra et· non alterata post consecracionem, quomodo sunt transsubstanciata per verba sacerdotis?

ENGLISH: But thirdly, [as a main head]{g:principaliter}, [it is rejoined]{g:replicatur} on the ground that [the newer church]{g:novella-ecclesia} posits the [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio} of the bread and [wine]{g:vinum} into the body and blood of Christ; now the primitive church did not posit this; therefore they contradict each other in teaching. [The assumption]{g:assumptum} is clear from Decretals I, *De summa* [48] *Trinitate et fide catholica*, *Una vero*: "The body of Christ", it says, "and the blood are [verily]{g:veraciter} contained in the [sacrament of the altar]{g:sacramentum-altaris} under the [species]{g:species} of bread and wine, the bread being transubstantiated into the body and the wine into the blood by divine [power]{g:potestas}."[^24] But if the bread and wine remain whole and unaltered after the consecration, how are they transubstantiated by the words of the priest?

### 02-e ¶6 · 48.8–48.22  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 48] In ista materia nostri katholici, eciam decretiste, didicerunt sic describere terminum supradictum: Transsubstanciacio est transitus unius substancie secundum se totam in aliam tota accidencium multitudine remanente, ut nec materia nec forma substancialis que fuerunt in pane et vino remanent post consecracionem, **sed** omnis substancia materialis vel formalis que fuit in eis corrumpitur, et succedit per conversionem sub eisdem accidentibus corpus Christi, et sic non est alicuius substancie annichilacio, tum quia conversio tocius substancie in melius, tum eciam quia accidencia remanent que prius erant in pane et vino, quia ipsis remanentibus committeretur ydolatria adorando panem et vinum ut corpus dominicum et sic Deum.

ENGLISH: In this matter our catholics, the [decretists]{g:decretiste} too, have learned to [define]{g:describere} the aforesaid term thus: transubstantiation is the passing of one [substance]{g:substancia}, according to its whole self, into another, the whole multitude of [accidents]{g:accidens} remaining; so that neither the matter nor the [substantial form]{g:forma-substancialis} which were in the bread and wine remain after consecration, but every substance, material or formal, that was in them is corrupted, and the body of Christ succeeds by [conversion]{g:conversio} under the same accidents; and so there is no annihilation of any substance, both because [it is] a conversion of the whole substance into something better, and also because the accidents remain which were before in the bread and wine; since, if they remained, idolatry would be committed in adoring the bread and wine as the Lord's body, and so as God.[^25]
