# *Dialogus*, chapter 14: prayers to saints and implicit faith

## Falsehood and Truth · 26.28–28.30

> [p. 26] MENDACIUM. Iterum manifeste innuis non esse ut fidem [p. 27] credendum illos esse sanctos qui in missis, in letaniis, et aliis ecclesie serviciis inseruntur; ad quid ergo diceremus de illis nisi crederemus quod in patria sunt beati? cum spes meriti super fide stabili in credentibus est fundanda. VERITAS. Hic dictum est sepius quod de sanctis quos canonizat ecclesia supponimus, sed non ut fidem credimus, quod sunt beati in patria. Undecim tamen apostolos cum paulo, magdalene et ceteris quos fides scripture implicat esse sanctos, credendum est ut fidem esse beatos, et alios, quantumcunque magna sanctitate in celis prepolleant, credimus esse in beatitudine, verumtamen citra fidem. Et ideo ordinavit ecclesia quod quecumque oracio porrecta ad sanctum ad deum principaliter dirigatur. Et sic quelibet talis oracio implicat tacite condicionem, si et quatenus placet deo per talis sancti meritum nobis miseris suffragari. Et ut videtur multis nulla oracio porrecta specialiter sancto Christi est laudabilis, nisi de quanto acuit devocionem in Christum, sic quod si omnes intenciones et oraciones uniri possent in Christum, sincerius directe unite in Christum, esset utilius Christiano omnes oraciones specialiter Christo porrigere. Nec valet false credere quod Christus propter celsitudinem sui dominii non potest a nobis miseris faciliter exorari; ideo est necessarium habere alias personas mediantes; Christus enim assistens pontifex immediate illabitur viatori et cum hoc immediate conjungitur deitati. Ideo nullus sanctus est misericordius aut facilius auditivus miseri quam est Christus. Nec valent oraciones aliorum sanctorum, nisi de quanto Christus prius ex sua gracia has acceptat. Ideo dicunt quidam quod porrecciones oracionum ad istos sanctos sepe [p. 28] sunt superflue vel nocive. Conceditur ergo quod non est articulus fidei appropriare cuilibet sancto de quo dicimus specialem articulum fidei sed beatitudinem illorum supponimus secundum majorem evidenciam vel minorem. Unde absit quod omnes legende sanctorum in ecclesiam introducte sint fides catholica, cum in festo corporis Christi de sancto sanctorum scribitur quod accidencia sunt in eucaristia sine subjecto, quod non fuit fides tempore augustini. Ideo dicit in de sermone Domini in monte quod multa vera, licet in legendis talibus inserantur non sunt fides catholica; sicut exemplificat de ministro convivii cujus unum brachium a cane rapido est abstractum. Sufficit ergo ad tales oraciones secundarias habere probabilem supposicionem, et de Christo qui fundamentaliter oratur habere cum aliis virtutibus firmam fidem; et patet solucio. In ista tamen materia dicunt scolastici quod aliqua est fides explicita vel expressa et aliqua est fides implicita sive tacita. Fidem autem implicitam dicunt esse quamcunque veritatem juxta illud apostoli prima cor. 13° Caritas omnia credit, et tamen infinite sunt veritates de quibus non oportet nos hic habere fidem explicitam; cum sufficit fideli quod credat quamcunque veritatem secundum gradum et formam qua deus vult ipsum hanc credere. Ut carnis resurreccionis est articulus fidei cuilibet Christiano, et tamen non oportet de qualibet persona hominis habere expressum articulum fidei quod resurget. Sed deus vult quod per istam fidem generalem sua militans ecclesia excusetur. Non enim vult quod sua ecclesia cum veritatibus inutiliter oneretur. Communis autem carnis resurreccio erit tempore sempiterno adhuc futura, et postmodum tempore suo presens; sed post diem judicii est elapsa.

FALSEHOOD. Again you plainly intimate that it is not to be [27] believed as faith that those are saints who are put into masses, into litanies and into the other services of the church. To what end, then, should we say [offices] of them,[^1] unless we believed that they are blessed in the [homeland]{g:patria}? For hope of merit must be founded, in those who believe, upon a stable faith.[^2]

TRUTH. Here it has often been said that of the saints whom the [church]{g:ecclesia} [canonizes]{g:canonizare} we suppose, but do not believe as faith, that they are blessed in the homeland. Yet it is to be believed as faith that the eleven apostles, with Paul, Magdalene and the others whom the faith of scripture implies to be saints, are blessed; and the others, however great the holiness in which they excel in heaven, we believe to be in blessedness, but [short of faith]{g:citra-fidem}. And therefore the church has ordained that whatever prayer is offered to a saint should be directed principally to God. And so every such prayer tacitly implies a condition: if and so far as it pleases God, through the merit of such a saint, [to aid]{g:suffragari} us wretches by intercession. And, as it seems to many, no prayer offered specially to a saint of Christ is praiseworthy except in so far as it sharpens devotion toward Christ; so that, if all intentions and prayers could be united in Christ, being more sincerely directed when united in Christ,[^3] it would be more profitable for a Christian to offer all prayers specially to Christ. Nor does it avail to believe falsely that Christ, because of the height of his [lordship]{g:dominium}, cannot readily be entreated by us wretches, and that therefore it is necessary to have other persons mediating. For Christ, "being come an high priest", [flows into]{g:illabi} the [wayfarer]{g:viator} without intermediary, and together with this is joined without intermediary to the [Godhead]{g:deitas}.[^4] Therefore no saint is more mercifully or more readily disposed to hear a wretch than Christ is. Nor do the prayers of other saints avail, except in so far as Christ first accepts them of his grace. Therefore some say that the offerings of prayers to these saints are [28] often superfluous or harmful. It is granted, therefore, that it is not an [article of faith]{g:articulus-fidei} to appropriate to each saint of whom we speak a special article of faith; but we suppose their blessedness according to greater or lesser [evidence]{g:evidencia}.[^5] Hence God forbid that all the legends of the saints brought into the church should be the catholic faith, since at the feast of Corpus Christi it is written of the holy of holies that accidents are in the Eucharist without a [subject]{g:subiectum}, which was not faith in Augustine's time.[^6] Therefore he says in *On the Lord's Sermon on the Mount* that many true things, although they are inserted in such legends, are not the catholic faith; as he gives the example of the servant at a banquet, one of whose arms was torn away by a ravening dog.[^7] It suffices, then, for such secondary prayers to have a probable supposition, and concerning Christ, who is prayed to fundamentally, to have firm faith together with the other virtues; and the solution is clear. Yet in this matter the schoolmen say that there is some [explicit faith]{g:fides-explicita}, or express, and some implicit faith, or tacit. And implicit faith they say is any truth whatever, according to that [saying] of the apostle, 1 Cor. 13[:7], "[Charity]{g:caritas} believeth all things";[^8] and yet there are infinitely many truths of which we need not here have explicit faith, since it suffices for the faithful man that he believe any truth according to the degree and form in which God wills him to believe it. As the resurrection of the flesh is an article of faith for every Christian,[^9] and yet one need not have, of every human person, an express article of faith that he will rise again. But God wills that by this general faith his [church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans} be excused. For he does not will that his church be uselessly burdened with truths. And the common resurrection of the flesh is, in everlasting time, still to come, and afterwards, in its own time, present; but after the day of judgement it is past.[^10]

## Pollard's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 26.28: *Mend.* objects:
- 27.2: You thus deny that the sanctity of saints to whom we address prayers is of faith.
- 27.5: Ver. answers :
- 27.6: We do not hold as of faith the sanctity of any but Scripture saints.
- 27.11: Prayers are addressed mediately to saints, ultimately to God.
- 27.17: And are only good, if at all, in so far as they quicken devotion to Christ.
- 27.25: Who is more ready to hear than any saint.
- 27.28: Prayers to saints are often harmful.
- 28.3: The sanctity of saints is to be believed or not according to the evidence.
- 28.5: Their legends are not of faith.
- 28.7: Just as the existence of accidents without a substance in the Eucharist is a doctrinal accretion.
- 28.11: So are many of these legends.
- 28.13: It is enough to believe tentatively in prayers to saints, absolutely in Christ.
- 28.16: Explicit (or specific) differs from implicit (or indefinite) faith.
- 28.22: We must believe in the resurrection of the body, but not separately in that of this or that man's body.

## Notes

[^1]: **T/C.** *Dicere de*: to say [a mass or office] of a saint; the bracket supplies the object. B and c add *horarum officia* (in B in the margin, by another hand): "why then should we say the offices of the hours of them".

[^2]: **C.** *Cum ... est fundanda* is causal ("since"), completing the question; rendered as a separate sentence opening "For". *Spes meriti*, "hope of merit": most likely the saints' merit on which the petitioner relies (cf. 27.14 *per talis sancti meritum*); it could be the petitioner's own.

[^3]: **T/C.** Translated as printed, with *in Christum* twice. At 27.19 Pollard records: over *Christum*, B has *scilicet sanctum* in another hand; B1 has *ipsum* corrected from *Christum*; c has *ipsum* with *sanctum* interlined by another hand. On that reading the clause runs "more sincerely directed when united in him [the saint]". The apparatus does not show which *Christum* is meant. B1 and c read *est* for *esset*.

[^4]: **S/C.** *Christus ... assistens pontifex*: Heb. 9:11 (Vulgate *Christus autem assistens pontifex futurorum bonorum*), in DR's wording. *Immediate* (twice) is "without intermediary", answering *personas mediantes*; it is not temporal.

[^5]: **T.** *Appropriare* is used in the general sense "attach to"; it is not the canon-law term, so it is left untagged. B, B1 and c read *appropriate*. Pollard's apparatus (OCR "oras dicimus") seems to give *oramus* for *dicimus* ("to whom we pray"). After *fidei* they add *adaptare quod in patria sit beatus*: "to fit [the article] that he is blessed in the homeland".

[^6]: **R/S.** *In festo corporis Christi*: the office for the feast. Pollard's margin gives *In primo nocturno, Lectio II* ; that lesson's words on accidents without a subject are unverified . The lessons read at an office are the *legende* of 28.5. *De sancto sanctorum*: "of the holy of holies", that is, the sacrament.

[^7]: **S/T.** Pollard's margin: Aug. *De serm. Dom. in monte* i. 65 . Augustine there cites an apocryphal writing on the apostle Thomas and a servant at a feast, whose hand a dog carried off, and says it is not received in the catholic canon . Wyclif has *brachium*, "arm". B, B1 and C read *rabido*, "mad", for *rapido*.

[^8]: **C/T.** Faith here names what is believed: implicit faith "is" any truth whatever. B, B1 and C omit *esse*. The verse is 1 Cor. 13:7, with *Caritas* supplied from 13:4.

[^9]: **T.** The print has the genitive *carnis resurreccionis* where the construction wants a nominative (*resurreccio*). It is translated as a nominative and flagged. B and B1 read *resurreccionem et alios articulos*, which does not mend the construction.

[^10]: **T.** Translated from *est in* (B, b1, c) in place of the print's *erit*. The print's *erit tempore sempiterno adhuc futura*, "will be still to come in everlasting time", conflicts with the clause that follows (*post diem judicii est elapsa*).
