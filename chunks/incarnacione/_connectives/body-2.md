### 02-b ¶1 · 17.20–18.5  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 17] Secundo stetit¹¹ error Arrianorum in hoc quod dicunt Christum sine anima solam carnem suscepisse¹²; ut meminit Augustinus libro suo De Heresibus Heresi 49. 'Arriani, inquit, dicti ab Arrio in eo sunt notissimi¹³ errore, quo Patrem et Filium et Spiritum Sanctum nolunt esse eiusdem substancie vel nature; **sed** Filium dicunt creaturam, Spiritum vero creaturam creature; hoc est, ab ipso Filio creatum¹⁴ volunt. In eo **autem** quod dicunt Christum sine anima solam carnem sumpsisse minus noti sunt. **Sed** hoc verum esse¹⁵ Epifanius¹⁶ non tacuit, et ego ex eorum scriptis et collocucionibus certissime comperi.' Et in hoc conveniunt cum Appollinaristis¹⁷; de quibus scribit Augustinus Heresi 55 [p. 18] quod de anima Christi ab ecclesia catholica¹ differunt² dicentes, sicut Arriani, Dominum³ Jesum Christum sine anima carnem suscepisse,³ et Verbum fuisse pro anima: et sic glosant scripturas loquentes de anima Christi et Verbum in carnem⁴ fuisse conversum, non carnem sumpsisse de virgine.

ENGLISH: Second, the error of the Arians stood in this, that they say "Christ took up flesh alone, without a soul"; as Augustine recalls in his book *On Heresies*, heresy 49: "The Arians," he says, "named from Arius, are best known for that error by which they will not have the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit be of the same substance or [nature]{g:natura}; but they say the Son is a [creature]{g:creatura}, and the Spirit a creature of a creature; that is, they will have him created by the Son himself. But in this, that they say Christ took flesh alone without a soul, they are less known. But Epiphanius did not keep silent that this is true, and I have learned it most certainly from their writings and conversations."[^1] And in this they agree with the Apollinarists, of whom Augustine writes, heresy 55, [18] that they differ from the catholic [church]{g:ecclesia} concerning the soul of Christ, saying, like the Arians, that the Lord Jesus Christ took up flesh without a soul, and that [the Word]{g:verbum} was in place of a soul: and so they [gloss]{g:glosare} the scriptures that speak of the soul of Christ, and [say] that the Word was converted into flesh, not that he took flesh from the virgin.[^2]

### 02-b ¶3 · 18.21–19.4  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 18] Et ponit ad hoc consequenter duplicia exempla quibus Deus hucusque non¹⁹ dignatus est ostendere hominibus meliora, per que tam in noticiam Trinitatis quam eciam incarnacionis posset²⁰ hereticus manuduci. Primum sic. 'Cum corpus, inquit, et anima sunt unus homo, quamvis corpus et anima non sunt²¹ unum; cur non multo magis Pater et Filius sunt unus Deus? cum Pater et Filius sunt unum secundum illam Veritatis vocem ''ego et Pater unum sumus.'' Cum, inquit, homo exterior et interior homo non sunt unum, neque eiusdem nature est exterior cuius [p. 19] est interior¹; quia exterior cum corpore nuncupato¹ dicitur homo; interior **autem** sola anima racionali² intelligitur; utrumque tamen illorum simul non³ duo homines **sed** unus dicitur: quanto magis Pater et Filius unus Deus est?'

ENGLISH: And to this he next lays down two examples, than which God has not hitherto deigned to show men better, by which the heretic might be led by the hand into knowledge as much of the Trinity as of the incarnation.[^6] The first is thus. "Since," he says, "body and soul are one man, although body and soul are not one, why are not the Father and the Son much more one God, since the Father and the Son are one, according to that voice of the Truth, 'I and the Father are one'?" "Since," he says, "the outer man and the inner man are not one, nor is the outer of the same nature as [19] the inner; for the outer, with the body so named, is called man, but the inner is understood as the rational soul alone; yet both of them together are called not two men but one: how much more are the Father and the Son one God?"[^7]

### 02-b ¶4 · 19.5–19.25  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 19] Secundum exemplum est de natura universali. 'Homines, inquit, per consorcium et communionem⁴ unius eiusdem nature qua omnes homines unum erant, et si aliquando secundum diversitates voluntatum et sentenciarum opinionum et morum dissimilitudines⁵ non erant unum, erunt⁶ tamen perfecte pleneque unum⁶ cum perventum fuerit ad eum finem ut sit ''Deus omnia in omnibus.'' Et sic non est possibile homines esse unum in caritate vel alio accidente, nisi prius sint unum in substancia vel natura: ut declarat libro primo et⁷ 3° contra Maximinianum 22° capitulo. Quod **autem** multi homines sint unum in substancia vel natura⁸ probat⁹ per illud Apostoli ad Cor. 3° dictum¹⁰ de Paulo et Appollo ''qui plantat et qui rigat unum sunt.'' Unde Veritas Joh. 17.¹¹ ''Non¹² (dixit) rogo ut ipsi et nos unum simus;''¹² **sed** ''ut ipsi unum sint, sicut et nos unum sumus¹³:'' non solum natura quod iam erat verum, **sed** et perfecte¹³ caritatis atque iusticie¹⁴ pro sue¹⁵ capacitate nature, quantum¹⁶ in Dei regno esse poterit,¹⁷ ut sic ipsi summe unum sint in natura sua, sicut Pater et Filius summe unum sunt incomparabiliter¹⁸ meliori natura. Nunquam, inquit, de aliquibus rebus legimus quod unum sunt, nisi sint eiusdem substancie vel nature.'

ENGLISH: The second example is about [universal]{g:universale} nature. "Men," he says, "through the fellowship and communion of one and the same nature, by which all men were one, even if at some time, according to diversities of wills and of [teachings]{g:sentencia}, opinions and dissimilarities of manners, they were not one, will yet be perfectly and fully one when the end is reached, that 'God may be all in all.'"[^8] And so it is not possible for men to be one in [charity]{g:caritas} or in another accident unless they are first one in substance or nature: as he [makes clear]{g:declarare} in the first and third books against Maximinus, chapter 22. And that many men are one in substance or nature he proves by that [text] of the Apostle, 1 Corinthians 3, said of Paul and Apollo: "he that planteth and he that watereth, are one." Hence the Truth, John 17: "I do not pray (he said) that they and we may be one," but "that they may be one, as we also are one": not only in nature, which was already true, but also [one] of perfect charity and justice, according to the capacity of their nature, as far as can be in the kingdom of God; so that thus they may be supremely one in their nature, as the Father and the Son are supremely one in an incomparably better nature. "Never," he says, "do we read of any things that they are one, unless they are of the same substance or nature."[^9]

### 02-b ¶7 · 20.22–21.22  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 20] Multi enim¹² cupiunt autorizare novam logicam extraneam a scriptura et hinc dicunt¹³ se ipsam corrigere tamquam impossibilem¹⁴ de vi vocis. Et illud meminit Augustinus contra dictum Pascencium. Nam¹⁵ contra¹⁵ negantes scripturas de Christo sic loquitur: 'Homines, inquit, minus intelligentes quid propter quid dicatur precipites volunt habere sentencias: et scripturis non diligenter scrutatis arripiunt defensionem¹⁶¹⁷ cuiuscumque¹⁸ opinionis¹⁶ et ab ea vel¹⁹ nunquam vel de difficili deflectuntur, dum docti atque sapientes magis putari quam esse cupiunt. Ea quippe, que propter formam²⁰ servi [p. 21] dicta sunt, volunt transferre ad formam Dei. 'Hoc, inquit, nomen Jesus Christus ex dispensacione misericordie¹ suscepteque² humanitatis assumptum est, quia ipse Filius Dei factus est filius hominis, non mutando quod erat, sed³ assumendo quod non erat.' Et probat diffuse ex scripturis⁴ quod tam Christus quam Spiritus Sanctus⁵ est idem Deus cum Patre et specialiter per illud Apostoli ad Cor. 8. 'Scimus quoniam nullus Deus nisi unus: nam etsi sunt qui dicantur dii⁶ multi et domini multi, nobis tamen unus Deus Pater ex quo omnia et nos in illo: et unus Dominus⁷ Jesus Christus per quem omnia et nos per illum.' Ex isto plane sequitur non protervo quod Christus sit idem Deus cum Patre. Unde dicunt periti scripture quod deus et dominus per se sumptus⁸ dicitur authonomatice de Deo et Domino dominorum. Scio tamen quod protervus potest,⁹ ut hodie, negare quamlibet partem scripture de virtute sermonis, et dicere quod Deus erat verbum nuncupative, et quod per Christum non sunt omnia facta **sed** omnia citra ipsum, et¹⁰ sic est Deus ac animatus,¹⁰ **sed** nec Deus deorum nec animatus¹¹ nobiscum univoce. **Sed** non sic sophisticati sunt eciam heretici, non sic negantes¹² scripturam ut hodie, quia melius fundati in logica et methaphisica quam nostrates.

ENGLISH: For many desire to [authorize]{g:autorizare} a new logic foreign to scripture, and hence they say that they correct it as impossible [by the force of the words]{g:de-vi-vocis}.[^13] And Augustine recalls that against the said Pascentius. For against those who deny the scriptures about Christ he speaks thus: "Men," he says, "who understand too little what is said on account of what, headlong want to have their views; and, the scriptures not having been diligently searched, they seize on the defence of whatever [opinion]{g:opinio}, and from it they are turned aside either never or with difficulty, while they desire to be thought learned and wise rather than to be so. For the things that were said on account of the form of a servant [21] they want to transfer to the form of God."[^14] "This name," he says, "Jesus Christ, was [assumed]{g:assumere} from the dispensation of mercy and of the humanity taken up, because the very Son of God was made son of man, not by changing what he was, but by assuming what he was not." And he proves at length from the scriptures that both Christ and the Holy Spirit are the same God with the Father, and specially by that [text] of the Apostle, 1 Corinthians 8: "We know that there is no God but one. For although there be that are called gods many and lords many, yet to us there is but one God, the Father, of whom are all things, and we in him: and one Lord Jesus Christ, by whom are all things, and we by him."[^15] From this it plainly [follows]{g:sequitur}, for one who is not [obstinate]{g:protervus}, that Christ is the same God with the Father. Hence those skilled in scripture say that "god" and "lord", taken by itself, is said [by antonomasia]{g:autonomatice} of God and of the Lord of lords.[^16] Yet I know that the obstinate man can, as today, deny any part of scripture in the proper sense of the words, and say that "the Word was God" [nuncupatively]{g:nuncupative}, and that all things were not made by Christ, but all things short of him; and so he is God and ensouled, but neither God of gods nor ensouled [univocally]{g:univoce} with us.[^17] But even the heretics did not so [play the sophist]{g:sophisticari}, not so denying scripture as today, since they were better grounded in logic and metaphysics than our own people.

### 02-c ¶1 · 21.23–22.2  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 21] Tercio antem stat opinio Arrii in his que ex predictis erroribus sequuntur¹³: ut puta¹⁴ quod Christus nedum¹⁵ est univoce homo cum aliis **sed** non est homo simpliciter, cum non potest esse homo si non sit anima intellectiva; et per consequens non est passus¹⁶ mortuus aut sepultus, et sic de quibuscunque predicatis humanitatem Christi concernentibus.¹⁷ Quamvis enim dici possit quod angelus, coniunctus nature corporee tanquam motrix, sit homo; verumtamen est alterius speciei quam totus vel completus homo, id est, [p. 22] natura integra ex corpore et anima composita: homo enim est racione spiritus eiusdem speciei cum angelis et non racione nature corporee.

ENGLISH: And third, the [opinion]{g:opinio} of Arius stands in the things that follow from the aforesaid errors:[^1] as, for example, that Christ, let alone being [univocally]{g:univoce} a man with others, is not a man [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}, since he cannot be a man if he is not an intellective soul; and consequently he did not suffer, die, or was buried, and so of whatever predicates concern [the humanity]{g:humanitas} of Christ.[^2] For although it could be said that an angel, joined to a bodily [nature]{g:natura} as its mover, is a man, nevertheless it is of another species than the whole or complete man, that is, [22] the integral nature composed of body and soul: for [such a] man is, by reason of the spirit, of the same species with the angels, and not by reason of the bodily nature.[^3]

### 02-c ¶2 · 22.3–24.3  (6 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 22] Et per hoc patet quod quidam minus intelligentes Augustinum putant ipsum contradicere sibi ipsi, quando dicit quod anima non completus aut totus¹ homo: nam² verum dicit ad sensum³ expositum. Unde Epistola 40 ad Dardanum solvendo questionem de dicto Salvatoris⁴ Luc. 23 quo⁵ promisit latroni quod foret secum illo die in paradiso; 'antequam, inquit, sciatur utrum secundum deitatem⁶ vel⁷ secundum humanitatem intelligatur promissio Veritatis, oportet videre quomodo sit loquendum de homine. Non, inquit Augustinus, sicut quidam heretici dicunt, Verbum Dei⁸ et carnem sine anima humana⁹ suscepit, sic quod Verbum esset carni pro anima; vel Verbum Dei carnem et animam sine mente, ut Verbum Dei esset anime¹⁰ pro mente. **Sed** accipis, inquit ad Dardanum, hominem Christum; sicut¹¹ superius aisti quod Christum Deum non crederes nisi perfectum hominem credidisses. Profecto, inquit, cum dicis hominem, perfectam totam illic naturam humanam vis intelligere. Non est¹² **autem** homo perfectus si vel anima carni vel anime ipsi mens¹³ humana defuerit.'¹⁴ Et illam naturam complete ex anima et corpore compositam vocat super Joh. Ome. 47¹⁵ totum hominem: quam¹⁶ notum est ex dictis De Anima distingui tam a corpore quam anima¹⁷ licet omnia ista tria sint eadem persona hominis. Sic ergo¹⁸ intelligendus est Augustinus quandocunque videtur dicere quod corpus vel anima non est homo; id est, non est¹⁹ tota vel completa natura ex corpore et anima composita. **Sed** pro solucione Dardani questionis videtur Augustino catholicum quod promissio facta latroni intelligatur secundum [p. 23] humanitatem Christi:¹ **sed** cum corpus Christi¹ sepultum sit solum in orto, ut patet Ioh. 19,² relinquitur quod sue anime in parasceue erant simul; quia modicum felicitatis fuisset animam latronis fuisse cum solo corpore Christi mortuo in sepulcro. Et cum vera mors fuit in Christi corpore, cum aliter non fuisset vera resurreccio; querendum est—quo devenit anima³ Christi in illo triduo? Quod cum evangelium non reserat, supplet⁴ beatus Petrus Actuum 2° probans ex illo Psalmi 15 dicto de Christo ‘quoniam non⁵ derelinques animam meam in inferno, nec dabis sanctum tuum videre corrupcionem,’ quod anima Christi descendit in illo triduo ad limbum patrum, quem Veritas Luc. 16. vocat ‘sinum Abrahe,’ ubi patres veteris testamenti feliciter quieverunt.⁶ Quamvis enim dictum psalmiste sit negativum, impertinenter tamen menti prophetice diceretur, nisi intenderet Messiam secundum mentem visitare inferos et statim sine media incarceracione corpori reuniri.⁶ Et hinc fundatus est de Christo articulus fidei, quod ‘descendit ad inferos.’ Cum ergo paradisus generale nomen est ubicunque feliciter⁷ vivitur, quid obest animam latronis fuisse cum anima Christi in regione quiescencium congaudendo et non in regione inferiori dolencium,⁸ inter quas est ‘magnum chaos’ secundum veritatem evangelicam interceptum?⁹ Redeundo¹⁰ ergo ad propositum patet ex dictis quantum differt ista posicio ab heresi Arriana. Ideo est michi verisimile quod doctores moderni¹¹ cum non ydemptificarent que minus intelligunt,¹² locuntur michi temptative, yronice, vel iocose. Yronice quidem, quia Arrius negavit Verbum Dei esse idem substancialiter Deo Patri, **sed** creatum esse secundum supremam substanciam, que est Christus. Ego **autem** dico [p. 24] quod Christus absolute necessario¹ est eadem deitas que est Pater,² nec posse secundum supremam naturam, que est Christus, creari vel fieri.

ENGLISH: And by this it is clear that certain [people], understanding Augustine too little, think that he contradicts himself when he says that the soul is not the complete or whole man: for he says what is true in the sense expounded.[^4] Hence in letter 40, to Dardanus, solving the question about the saying of the Saviour, Luke 23, by which he promised the thief that he would be with him that day in paradise: "Before," he says, "it is known whether the promise of the Truth is understood [as to the Godhead]{g:secundum-deitatem} or [as to the humanity]{g:secundum-humanitatem}, it is necessary to see how one is to speak of man. Not," says Augustine, "as certain heretics say, [that] [the Word]{g:verbum} of God took up flesh without a human soul, so that the Word was to the flesh in place of a soul; or [that] the Word of God [took up] flesh and soul without mind, so that the Word of God was to the soul in place of mind. But you accept," he says to Dardanus, "the man Christ; as you said above that you would not believe Christ God unless you had believed him perfect man. Truly," he says, "when you say man, you wish the whole perfect human nature to be understood there. And a man is not perfect if either soul is lacking to the flesh or a human mind to the soul itself."[^5] And that nature, composed completely of soul and body, he calls, on John, homily 47, "the whole man": which, as is known from what is said in *On the Soul*, is distinguished both from the body and from the soul, although all these three are the same [person]{g:persona} of the man.[^6] So, then, Augustine is to be understood whenever he seems to say that body or soul is not man: that is, it is not the whole or complete nature composed of body and soul. But for the solution of Dardanus's question it seems catholic to Augustine that the promise made to the thief be understood as to [23] the humanity of Christ; now since the body of Christ was buried only in the garden, as is clear from John 19, it remains that their souls were together on the Parasceve; for it would have been little felicity for the soul of the thief to have been with the dead body of Christ alone in the sepulchre.[^7] And since there was true death in the body of Christ, since otherwise there would not have been a true resurrection, it must be asked: where did the soul of Christ go in [the three days]{g:triduum}? Since the gospel does not disclose this, blessed Peter supplies it, Acts 2, proving from that [text] of Psalm 15, said of Christ, "Because thou wilt not leave my soul in hell; nor wilt thou give thy holy one to see corruption," that the soul of Christ descended in the three days to the limbo of the fathers, which the Truth, Luke 16, calls "Abraham's bosom", where the fathers of the old testament happily rested.[^8] For although the psalmist's saying is negative, yet it would be said [irrelevantly]{g:impertinens} to the prophetic mind unless he meant that the Messiah, as to the mind, visits those below and at once, without intervening incarceration, is reunited to the body.[^9] And hence the [article of faith]{g:articulus-fidei} was founded concerning Christ, that "he descended into hell". Since, therefore, paradise is a general name for wherever there is happy living, what prevents the soul of the thief from having been with the soul of Christ in the region of those at rest, rejoicing with him, and not in the lower region of those in pain, between which there is "a great chaos" fixed, according to the gospel truth?[^10] Returning, then, to the point, it is clear from what has been said how much this [position]{g:posicio} differs from the Arian heresy. Therefore it seems likely to me that [the modern doctors]{g:moderni-doctores}, since they would not identify the things they understand too little, speak to me tentatively, ironically, or jokingly.[^11] Ironically indeed, because Arius denied that the Word of God is substantially the same as God the Father, but [said] that he was created as to the supreme substance, which is Christ. But I say [24] that Christ is, absolutely of necessity, the same [Godhead]{g:deitas} that is the Father, and that he cannot be created or made as to the supreme nature, which is Christ.

### 02-c ¶3 · 24.4–24.8  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 24] 2° **autem** dixit Arrius quod Christus non habet animam **sed** pure carnem; spiritu illo creato³ supplente vicem anime non ypostatice, **sed** ut motrice, corpori copulato. ### 02-c ¶5 · 24.14–24.21  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 24] Et sic, ubi Arrius peccavit in deitate Christi negando eum verum Deum⁹ eundem cum Patre; ubi eciam peccavit in humanitate Christi negando ipsum esse verum hominem; ego utrumque¹⁰ pleno corde confiteor non confundendo naturas nec¹¹ distinguendo personas in Christo. Et patet quod non, quia dixit Christum esse creaturam, censetur hereticus¹²; **sed**, quia dixit Christum esse puram¹³ creaturam meliorem homine; et non hominem passum abiectum et ab hominibus reprobatum.

ENGLISH: And so, where Arius sinned in the Godhead of Christ, denying him [to be] true God, the same with the Father, and where he also sinned in the humanity of Christ, denying him to be true man, I confess both with a full heart, not confounding the natures nor dividing the persons in Christ.[^14] And it is clear that it is not because he said "Christ is a [creature]{g:creatura}" that he is judged a heretic, but because he said "Christ is a pure creature better than man", and not a man who suffered, cast down and rejected by men.[^15]

### 02-c ¶6 · 24.22–24.31  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 24] Et utinam nostri loquentes attenderent ad regulam Augustini ‘quid propter quid de Christo dicitur,’ allegando complete scripturam vel determinaciones ecclesie non truncate. Ut primo libro contra Maximinianum ca° 11. ‘Audi, inquit, Maximiniane, auctoritatem synodice¹⁴ locucionis: Si quis ex nichilo Filium dicit non ex Deo Patre, anathema sit.’ Quis ergo color, si dico—Christus est creatura—**sed** et Arrius dixit Christum esse creaturam, quod coincido in heresim Arrianam? Vere non plus quam sequitur, si uterque nostrum dicat Christum esse aliquid¹⁵ sive Deum.

ENGLISH: And would that our speakers attended to Augustine's rule, "what is said of Christ on account of what", [alleging]{g:allegare} scripture or the determinations of the [church]{g:ecclesia} completely, not truncated.[^16] As in the first book against Maximinus, chapter 11: "Hear," he says, "Maximinus, the [authority]{g:auctoritas} of a synodal utterance: If anyone says the Son is from nothing, not from God the Father, let him be anathema."[^17] What [colour]{g:color} is there, then, if I say "Christ is a creature", and Arius too said "Christ is a creature", [for saying] that I fall into the Arian heresy?[^18] Truly, no more than it would follow if each of us said "Christ is something" or "God".

### 02-d ¶1 · 25.1–26.3  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 25] **Sed** obicitur quod dico horribilius Arrianis, quia ipsi dixerunt Christum esse primam et optimam creaturam; ego **autem** dico Christum esse materiam primam, et sic¹ abiectissimam creaturam. Hic dicitur quod non sequitur. Nam Christus est optimus homo possibilis, inter omnes creaturas particulares optima, in tantum quod est melior angelo²; et per consequens melior² illa creatura, si esset, quam³ finxit Arrius nimis stolide esse⁴ primam creaturam Dei, mediante qua⁵ Deus creavit Spiritum Sanctum et consequenter alias creaturas. Nam illa creatura non foret melior creato angelo: de Christo **autem** dicit Apostolus ad Hebr. 1° quod ‘est tanto melior angelis effectus quanto differencius pre illis nomen hereditavit.’ Et si obicitur per illud Ps. 8⁶ dictum de Christo ad litteram⁷ ‘minuisti eum paulo minus ab angelis’; dicitur quod Christus, sicut est natura increata et natura creata, sic secundum naturam creatam est simul⁸ tam natura corporea quam natura incorporea;⁹ secundum **autem** naturam corpoream⁹ est ‘paululum minoratus ab angelis,’ sicut¹⁰ sentenciat satis¹¹ psalmus; et secundum creatum spiritum per graciam unionis angelos¹² antecellit. Conceditur tamen¹³ quod abiectissima creatura que est Christus Deus, licet sit natura inferior quam angelus vel anima humana, est tamen optima et suprema persona possibilis: cum omnis natura que est filius¹⁴ Marie virginis hominis¹⁵ est et¹⁶ Filius Dei; et e contra omnis natura, que est Filius¹⁴ Dei, est filius hominis; quia natura divina est filius hominis, ut patet ex confessione beati Petri Mat. 16. querente Veritate, ‘Quem dicunt¹⁷ homines esse filium hominis?’ et respondente Petro de eadem humanitate que est Christus, de qua fit questio. ‘Tu es, inquit, Filius Dei vivi,’ intelligens indubie filium naturalem. Unde Veritas [p. 26] approbando responsionem¹ adiunxit premium, et monet eum:² ‘Beatus es, inquit, Symon Bariona, quia caro et sanguis non revelavit tibi, **sed** Pater meus.’

ENGLISH: But [it is objected]{g:obicitur} that I say something more horrible than the Arians, since they said "Christ is the first and best [creature]{g:creatura}", but I say "Christ is [prime matter]{g:materia-prima}, and so the most abject creature". [The answer]{g:dicitur} here is that it does not [follow]{g:sequitur}. For Christ is the best man possible, the best among all particular creatures, so much so that he is better than an angel; and consequently better than that creature, if there were one, which Arius [exceedingly]{g:nimis} stupidly feigned to be the first creature of God, by means of which God created the Holy Spirit and consequently the other creatures.[^1] For that creature would not be better than a created angel; but of Christ the Apostle says, Hebrews 1, that he "is made so much better than the angels as he hath inherited a more excellent name than they." And if [it is objected, by appeal to]{g:obicitur-per} that [text] of Psalm 8, said of Christ literally, "Thou hast made him a little less than the angels", the answer is that Christ, as he is uncreated [nature]{g:natura} and created nature, so as to the created nature he is at once both bodily nature and incorporeal nature; but as to the bodily nature he is "made a little lower than the angels", as the psalm sufficiently [pronounces]{g:sentenciare}; and as to the created spirit, by the [grace of union]{g:gracia-unionis}, he excels the angels.[^2] Yet it is granted that the most abject creature which is Christ God, although it is a nature lower than an angel or a human soul, is yet the best and supreme [person]{g:persona} possible: since every nature that is the son of the virgin Mary, of man, is also the Son of God; and conversely every nature that is the Son of God is the son of man; for the divine nature is the son of man,[^3] as is clear from the confession of blessed Peter, Matthew 16, when the Truth asked, "Whom do men say that the Son of man is?" and Peter answered about the same [humanity]{g:humanitas} which is Christ, about which the question is put: "Thou art," he says, "the Son of the living God," understanding without doubt the natural son. Hence the Truth, [26] approving the answer, added a reward, and admonishes him: "Blessed art thou," he says, "Simon Bar-Jona: because flesh and blood hath not revealed it to thee, but my Father."[^4]

ENGLISH: And by this the Nestorian heresy is destroyed, which argues from the diversity of natures in Christ that there is a diversity of persons. For they do not know how the assumed humanity and the divine nature, so unlike, are each the same person with [the Word]{g:verbum} of God, as Anselm [shows]{g:ostendere}, *On the Incarnation of the Word*, chapter 6; and so [Nestorius] held that the son of man is one [person] and the Son of God another, as the son of man is one thing and the Son of God another, as Augustine says above.[^5] And it is clear how much this [position]{g:posicio} differs from the Arian and the Nestorian heresy, with which to those who understand too little it would seem most to agree. And if it is objected that this does not rule out that Christ is a creature, and the most abject, which Arius did not hold, I grant the [conclusion]{g:conclusio}.[^6] Hence among the other things that would kindle the theological [virtues]{g:virtus} in us [who are on the road]{g:viantes}, it seems to me that what ought most to engender them is that supreme [lowering]{g:minoracio}, that supreme [emptying]{g:exinanicio}, that supreme obedience or [humbling]{g:humiliacio}, by which the Word, remaining substantially, completely and eternally what he was, [assumed]{g:assumere} [hypostatically]{g:ypostatice} what before he was not. "For nowhere doth he take hold of the angels", according to the Apostle, Hebrews 2, as the Arians fictitiously prattle; but [he takes hold of] "the seed of Abraham", as much according to the corruptible nature as according to the immortal nature, "that he might become in all things like to his brethren"; the Apostle saying in the place cited above, "he is not ashamed to call them brethren, saying: I will declare thy name to my brethren"; and there follows: "For nowhere doth he take hold of the angels: but of the seed of Abraham he taketh hold. Wherefore it behoved him in all things to be made like unto his brethren, that he might become a merciful and faithful high priest before God."[^7] From which text it is clear that our faith would grow green from this, that he deigns to take up our fragile nature, not a purely angelic one. [27] Second, since he cannot be frustrated, it is clear that from the [assumption]{g:assumpcio} of our nature, whom he calls his brethren, having suffered and been tempted in order to help us, human hope would grow strong.[^8] And third, seeing the humbling by which the natural firstborn of God the Father deigned to assume our fragile and abject nature, [charity]{g:caritas} would be strengthened towards loving him, and his brethren in him, more dearly. Therefore it does no injury to us, nor ought it to grow cheap, that he so united us, not angels, to himself; nor does he fall away from the honour of the Father, nor is a slacker reason for loving us founded in him; but in all things the reverse.[^9] Hence, John 14, he truly says: "If you loved me, you would indeed be glad, because I go to the Father." For the [making clear]{g:declarare} of this I suppose, first, that Christ, saying this as to the assumed humanity, is of the same nature with us. Second, I suppose that the apostles to whom he spoke, and others keeping his words, are not only servants of the Lord but friends. This is clear by that [text] of John 15: "You are my friends, if you do the things that I command you. I will not now call you servants, etc." Third, I suppose that it belongs to the nature of friendship, since a friend is another self, to rejoice with a [fellow-friend]{g:coamicus} in his prosperity, especially if a richer fruit of friendship is hoped for from it. This is clear by Aristotle, *Ethics* 8.5.[^10] From these it is argued thus. Every friend ought to rejoice at a prosperous event of a fellow-friend that confirms their friendship; now to Christ, the friend of the apostles, ascending into heaven and set at the right hand of God the Father, supreme felicity came; therefore the apostles ought to have rejoiced together at his passing. And hence the Apostle says, Hebrews 7, "that Jesus goes by himself to God, always living to make intercession for us."[^11] In conformity with which it is written, 1 John 2: "But if any man sin, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ." Hence Augustine on that text, homily 78, writes thus: "Human nature is to be congratulated [28] in this, that it was assumed by the Word so as to be established immortal in heaven, and so earth became sublime, so that incorruptible dust sits at the right hand of the Father; and so for Christ to go to the Father and withdraw from us is to raise into heaven the mortal [thing] which he took from us, and to make it immortal. Who then would not rejoice at this, so as both to congratulate his own nature, already immortal in Christ, and to hope that he himself will be so through Christ?"[^12] Nor do I doubt that all the modern scholastics would not know how to impugn these [pieces of] logic [by the force of the words]{g:de-vi-vocis}. And so Jerome and the other [those who speak correctly]{g:rectiloqui}, understand that all our hope and consolation is in the supreme humbling of Christ, by which, by assuming our nature and giving us an example of [manner of life]{g:conversacio}, he made himself not an angel or a nature foreign to man, as the heretics fable, but most truly and univocally our brother, as will be made clear later.[^13]
