### 10-a ¶2 · 160.10–161.2  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 160] Quia maior pars variacionis in ista materia stat in methaphisica de humanitate assumpta; ideo expedit iuxta ponere sentencias contrarias supradictis. Dicunt enim moderni concorditer¹ quod humanitas Christi nec est homo, nec suppositum, vel persona, licet sit substancia racionalis composita² ex corpore² et anima, que sunt partes hominis Iesu Christi; **sed** dimissa illa natura suppositacioni proprie,³ ut⁴ possit per possibile⁴ quod maneat non assumpta, tunc foret homo et modo non est homo. Ideo dicunt quod diffinicio hominis negacionem includit formaliter, ita quod descriptive homo sit substancia ex corpore et anima intellectiva composita⁵ non suppositata suppositacione⁶ aliena. Et quia ultima differencia deficit nature assumpte cum [p. 161] suppositatur suppositacione Verbi; ideo¹ stante assumpcione ypostatica non est homo.

ENGLISH: Because the greater part of the variation in this matter lies in the metaphysics of the assumed humanity, it is therefore expedient to set beside [them] the [teachings]{g:sentencia} contrary to those stated above. For [the moderns]{g:moderni} say with one accord[^1] that the humanity of Christ is neither man, nor [supposit]{g:suppositum}, nor [person]{g:persona}, although it is a rational substance composed of body and soul, which are parts of the man Jesus Christ; but, that [nature]{g:natura} [laid aside]{g:dimittere} to its own [suppositation]{g:suppositacio}, as it can be, by a possible case, that it remain unassumed, then it would be a man; and now it is not a man.[^2] Therefore they say that the [definition]{g:diffinicio} of man includes a [negation]{g:negacio} formally, so that, by way of definition, man is a substance composed of body and intellective soul, not [supposited]{g:suppositari} by an alien suppositation.[^3] And because the last [difference]{g:differencia} is lacking to the assumed nature when [161] it is supposited by the suppositation of the [Word]{g:verbum}, therefore, while the hypostatic [assumption]{g:assumpcio} stands, it is not a man.

### 10-a ¶4 · 161.20–162.11  (6 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 161] **Sed** hic dicit Doctor Subtilis super 3° Sentenciarum Dist. 1ᵃ Questione articuli 3ⁱ quod est dare triplicem⁵ negacionem, sicut et triplicem dependenciam pertinentem. Aliquid enim⁶ actualiter dependet a suppositacione aliena, quando est natura completa suppositi nature superioris; ut humanitas Christi⁷ non est suppositum, **sed** natura suppositi Verbi; et si Verbum faceret ipsam esse non naturam persone⁸ superioris, tunc esset suppositum et persona. Nichil⁹ ergo deficit ad hoc quod sit persona nisi ista negacio quod non est natura suppositi superioris nature. Ideo suppositacio, quam habet in potencia terminatur suppositacione Verbi, que non actu **sed** potencia est aliena a suppositacione nature humane. 2ᵃ dependencia vocatur potencialis;¹⁰ quando scilicet [p. 162] natura racionalis dependet a supposito increato a quo potest assumi, **sed** nondum assumitur in unitatem persone; **sed** et talem negacionem oportet naturam creatam habere, ut sit persona¹ hominis. 3ᵃ est dependencia obediencialis,² qua omne suppositum substancie essencialis dependet a qualibet persona increata, **sed** eciam³ potest assumi in unitatem suppositi increati, ut sit communicacio⁴ ydiomatum, et ut ipso facto natura persone increate vere Deus sit lapis vel lignum vel cuiuscunque speciei substancie⁵ individuum; sicut Deus iam est individuum speciei⁵ humane, licet natura talis non possit esse⁶ persona divina.

ENGLISH: But here [the Subtle Doctor]{g:doctor-subtilis}, on the third [book] of the *Sentences*, dist. 1, question, article 3, says that [one may posit]{g:est-dare} a threefold negation, as also a threefold pertinent [dependence]{g:dependencia}.[^6] For something actually depends on an alien suppositation when it is the complete nature of a supposit of a higher nature; as the humanity of Christ is not a supposit but the nature of the supposit of the Word; and if the Word made it not to be the nature of a higher person, then it would be a supposit and a person. Nothing, therefore, is lacking for it to be a person but this negation, that it is not the nature of a supposit of a higher nature. Therefore the suppositation which it has in [potency]{g:potencia} is terminated by the suppositation of the Word, which is alien from the suppositation of the human nature not in act but in potency.[^7] The second dependence is called potential: namely when [162] a rational nature depends on an uncreated supposit by which it can be assumed, but is not yet assumed into the unity of person; but a created nature too must have such a negation, that it may be a person of a man.[^8] The third is obediential dependence, by which every supposit of an essential substance depends on any uncreated person, but also can be assumed into the unity of an uncreated supposit, so that there is a [communication of idioms]{g:communicacio-ydiomatum}, and so that by that very fact the nature of an uncreated person, truly God, is a stone or wood or an [individual]{g:individuum} of any species of substance; as God now is an individual of the human species, although such a nature cannot be a divine person.[^9]

### 10-a ¶5 · 162.12–162.25  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 162] Dimissa **autem** contencione verbali, quod suppositacio Verbi non est aliena a suppositacione nature assumpte, cum illa non sit; arguitur primo contra illud⁷ per hoc quod omnis homo-non-Deus per se est homo: omnis natura humana completa non assumpta est homo: ergo omnis talis natura per se est homo, et per consequens non potest vicissim nunc esse homo et nunc non-homo. Maior licet sit famosa apud philosophos potest⁸ ex hoc patescere, quia aliter humanitas non esset species vel quiditas cuiuscunque persone⁹ hominis; cum potest manere idem quod est modo¹⁰ et non homo. Non ergo predicatur homo in quid de aliquo supposito¹¹ humano; et per idem nulla potest esse per se species in genere substancie, cum omnis substancia potest esse idem quod est, et non esse individuum huius speciei.

ENGLISH: But, setting aside the verbal contention that the suppositation of the Word is not alien from the suppositation of the assumed nature, since the latter is not,[^10] [it is argued]{g:arguitur-per} first against it by appeal to this: every man-not-God is [of itself]{g:per-se} a man: every complete human nature not assumed is a man: therefore every such nature is of itself a man, and consequently it cannot by turns be now man and now not-man. The major, although it is well known among the philosophers, can be made plain from this: that otherwise the humanity would not be the species or [quiddity]{g:quiditas} of any person of a man, since it can remain the same as it is now and [be] not a man. Therefore "man" is not predicated [in quid]{g:in-quid} of any human supposit; and by the same token no species can be of itself in the genus of substance, since every substance can be the same as it is and not be an individual of this species.[^11]

### 10-a ¶6 · 162.26–163.13  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 162] Item impossibile est aliquid esse accidentaliter alicuiusmodi¹² nisi sibi insit accidens a quo est huiusmodi formaliter sicut patet ponenti respectus et accidencia distincta: **sed** omnis homo iuxta hanc viam accidentaliter est homo: ergo est dare formam accidentalem qua est homo formaliter. **Sed** et hoc concedunt plurimi dicentes quod in assumpcione ypostatica negacio supradicta corrumpitur et in dimissione [p. 163] ypostatica generatur: et per illam est homo formaliter aliquid positivum, ut nichil, in quantum caret visu vel non habet hoc vel illud, est aliquid. Ex quo sequitur quod nichil in quantum homo vel persona hominis est aliquid vel alicuiusmodi¹ positivum; cum quicquid aut cuiusmodicunque² homo nunc est, assistente negacione quod non suppositatur² suppositacione aliena, potest esse adveniente suppositacione aliena ipso facto non³ homine vel persona. Quod est contra Augustinum 7° De Trinitate 9° volente, quod persona in quantum huiusmodi, est aliquid, non ad aliquid. Si ergo nemo potest fieri qualis aut quantus nisi moveatur, multo magis nemo potest fieri homo cum hoc quod non exinde moveatur; aliter enim non esset generacio.

ENGLISH: Again, it is impossible for something to be accidentally of some kind unless an accident is in it by which it is formally of this kind, as is clear to one who posits [relations]{g:respectus} and accidents distinct [from their subjects]: now every man, according to this way, is accidentally a man: therefore one may posit an accidental form by which he is formally a man. But most concede this too, saying that in the hypostatic assumption the negation stated above is corrupted, and in the hypostatic laying-aside [163] it is generated: and by it man is formally something positive, as nothing, [insofar as]{g:in-quantum} it lacks sight or does not have this or that, is something.[^12] From which [it follows]{g:sequitur} that nothing, insofar as [it is] man or the person of a man, is something or of some positive kind; since whatever, or of whatever kind, a man now is, with the negation present that he is not supposited by an alien suppositation, he can be, with an alien suppositation supervening, he being by that very fact not a man or person.[^13] Which is against Augustine, *De Trinitate* 7, [chapter] 9, who holds that a person, insofar as [it is] such, is something, not relative to something.[^14] If, then, no one can become qualified or [quantified]{g:quantus} unless he is moved, much more can no one become a man without being moved thereby; for otherwise there would not be generation.

### 10-a ¶7 · 163.14–163.33  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 163] Item eo ipso quod forma inseparabilis inest⁴ mere formaliter alicui, ipsum denominat⁵: **sed** humanitas est forma inseparabiliter existens cuicumque nature humane: ergo si inest alicui singulari nature ipsam denominat⁵: **sed** non potest denominare quicquam primo formaliter nisi hominem: ergo, si inest nature create formaliter, ipsa est homo. Cum⁶ ergo humanitas manet continue in natura humana assumpta, videtur quod stante assumpcione erit homo continue sicut prius. Minor videtur ex hoc quod omnis homo est formaliter humanitate homo, sicut mobile movetur motu formaliter, album denominatur albedine, et paciens passione. Humanitas ergo manens post unionem denominat⁵ formaliter Christum esse hominem et non dicta negacio: ymo cum ipse sit homo⁷ nobiscum univoce et in⁷ sua racione, non oportet negacionem ponere, **sed** ipse humanitate est homo formaliter, videlicet⁸ quod eadem sit racio cuiuslibet fratris sui. Ipse enim non potest suppositari suppositacione aliena, nec negacione; **sed** humanitate oportet ipsum esse singulare speciei humane formaliter: ergo per idem et alios univoce consimilis speciei.

ENGLISH: Again, by the very fact that an inseparable form is merely formally in something, it [names]{g:denominare} it: now the humanity is a form existing inseparably in any human nature: therefore, if it is in any singular nature, it names it: now it cannot name anything primarily [and] formally except a man: therefore, if it is formally in a created nature, that [nature] is a man. Since, then, the humanity remains continually in the assumed human nature, it seems that, while the assumption stands, it will be a man continually, as before. The minor appears from this: every man is formally a man by humanity, as a [movable]{g:mobile} is moved formally by motion, a white thing is named by whiteness, and what is acted on by [being-acted-on]{g:passio}.[^15] The humanity, therefore, remaining after the [union]{g:unio}, formally names Christ to be a man, and not the said negation: indeed, since he is a man [univocally]{g:univoce} with us and in his own account, it is not necessary to posit a negation, but he is formally a man by humanity, namely so that the account of each of his brothers is the same.[^16] For he cannot be supposited by an alien suppositation, nor by a negation; but by humanity he must be formally a singular of the human species: therefore by the same token others too, univocally of a like species.

### 10-a ¶8 · 164.1–164.27  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 164] Item iuxta dicta De Universalibus omnis humanitas est suppositum humanum esse hominem: **sed**, posito quod Verbum assumat ypostatice naturam Petri, manebit ista assumpta humanitas: ergo manebit quod idem suppositum est homo; et sic personam manentem personam Verbum assumeret. Pro¹ assumpto non est facile fingere, quid sit humanitas, quam Apostolus vocat ‘formam servi,’² nisi esset³ hominis vel humana essencia. Et ex isto⁴ videtur quod alia nova humanitas incepit⁵ esse homo, quando Christus incepit esse homo, humanitate⁵ Petri extincta per assumpcionem; quia quod Verbum est homo, tunc incipit⁶ esse et quod Petrus est homo tunc desinit. Ex quo patet quod non assumpsit naturam Petri, cum illa natura manet modo⁷ tantum distans a Verbo ut prius. Nam illa res, que prius⁸ fuit suppositum Petri, nec est natura nec persona divina, nec est homo modo sicut nec Christus est natura humana: per antecedens⁹ vero ista res et Christus conveniebant in specie sic quod uterque fuit homo ut suppono¹⁰: ergo maior fuit conveniencia tunc quam modo. Nec valet dicere quod Christus est homo formaliter non negacione¹¹ nec humanitate, **sed** ypostatica unione; quia sic foret racio qua¹² Christus est homo multum equivoca a racione qua quicumque alius est homo¹³; quod indubie sequitur¹³ multiplici racione. Nam quilibet alius homo dicitur formaliter homo negative¹⁴ et per consequens racione increata; Christus **autem** dicitur homo per unionem ypostaticam, que nulli alii potest competere.

ENGLISH: Again, according to what is said in *De universalibus*, every humanity is "a human supposit's being a man": now, supposing that the Word hypostatically assumes the nature of Peter, this assumed humanity will remain: therefore it will remain that the same supposit is a man; and so the Word would assume a person remaining a person.[^17] For [the assumption]{g:assumptum} it is not easy to feign what the humanity is, which the Apostle calls "the form of a servant", unless it were the essence of a man, or a human essence.[^18] And from this it seems that another, new humanity began to be a man when Christ began to be a man, the humanity of Peter being extinguished through the assumption; since "the Word is a man" then began to be, and "Peter is a man" then ceases. From which it is clear that he did not assume the nature of Peter, since that nature now remains as far distant from the Word as before.[^19] For that thing which before was the supposit of Peter is neither a nature nor a divine person, nor is it now a man, just as Christ is not a human nature either: and beforehand this thing and Christ [agreed]{g:convenire} in species, so that each was a man, as I suppose: therefore there was greater [agreement]{g:conveniencia} then than now.[^20] Nor does it avail to say that Christ is formally a man not by negation nor by humanity, but by hypostatic union; since thus the account by which Christ is a man would be very equivocal from the account by which anyone else is a man; which undoubtedly follows for manifold reasons. For any other man is called formally a man negatively, and consequently by an uncreated account; but Christ is called a man through the hypostatic union, which can belong to no other.[^21]

### 10-b ¶1 · 164.28–166.7  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 164] Item quilibet alius homo a Christo¹⁵ est per se humanitas; Christus secundum istam viam non potest esse humanitas: ergo non est univoce homo cum alio. Maior patet ex hoc quod licet omnis homo per accidens sit homo, tamen eo ipso [p. 165] quod est¹ humanitas, cum iuxta hanc viam post assumpcionem manebit non homo **sed** humanitas: **sed** cum illa humanitas non inceperit² esse per assumpcionem, sequitur quod prius manserat iste homo. Hoc 2° confirmatur ex eo quod omne per accidens oportet³ reduci ad aliquid per se³: **sed** Petrus est per accidens homo: ergo est prius per se aliquid, quod potissime foret humanitas. 3° per istud⁴ quod ista humanitas adequate individuatur ex eisdem principiis ex quibus iste homo: ergo sunt idem: non ergo potest esse ordo causandi inter eos. Et consequencia principalis ex hoc videtur quod, dato antecedente, Christus et quicumque alius homo non forent eiusdem nature univoce. Nam quilibet alius homo foret natura humana in recto: Christus non potest esse natura humana **sed** nature humane: igitur sicut Deus non predicatur univoce de creatura, de qua solum dicitur in obliquo, et de persona divina, de qua dicitur in recto; sic nec humanitas dicitur univoce de Christo et aliis: et per consequens non sunt univoce eiusdem nature: ymo cum humanitas sit nomen competentissimum speciei per se predicatum de suis suppositis⁵ et non homo, humanitas **autem** non potest predicari in recto de Christo, videtur quod Christus non sit eiusdem speciei cum homine. Confirmatur ex hoc quod Christus non potest esse alicuius⁶ speciei: ergo Christus non est singulare⁷ speciei humane. Nam⁸ per adversarios non potest esse⁶ aliquid nisi deitas, sed⁹ ipsa¹⁰ non potest esse aliquid speciei humane⁸: ergo Christus non potest esse aliquid speciei humane. Si enim deitas foret illius speciei, tunc foret causabilis passibilis et activa, sicut alia individua speciei: quod est impossibile. Ideo, si non fallor, repugnat isti vie,¹¹ quod Christus sit eiusdem speciei cum aliis. Et in ista methaphisica vellem me studuisse, quando sollicitabam¹² me, si quantitas, motus, [p. 166] et cetera accidencia distinguantur. Subtilior enim est distinccio humanitatis a Verbo quam accidentis huiusmodi a subiecto; cum¹ secundum moderniores² Petrus inficians³ in ista hora per assumpcionem et dimissionem potest esse homo et post non homo⁴ sine⁵ generacione vel corrupcione alicuius rei vel motu aliquo pertinente; quod non contingit eciam de relacione.

ENGLISH: Again, every man other than Christ is [of itself]{g:per-se} a [humanity]{g:humanitas}; Christ, according to this way, cannot be a humanity: therefore he is not [univocally]{g:univoce} a man with another.[^1] The major is clear from this: that, although every man is a man [by accident]{g:per-accidens}, yet by the very fact [165] that he is a humanity [he is a man], since according to this way, after the [assumption]{g:assumpcio}, he will remain not a man but a humanity: now since that humanity did not begin to be through the assumption, it follows that before, it had remained this man.[^2] This is confirmed, second, from this: everything by accident must be reduced to something of itself: now Peter is a man by accident: therefore he is first of himself something, which would most of all be the humanity. Third, by this: this humanity is adequately individuated from the same principles from which this man [is]: therefore they are the same: therefore there cannot be an order of causing between them.[^3] And the principal [inference]{g:consequencia} appears from this: that, given the [antecedent]{g:antecedens}, Christ and any other man would not be univocally of the same [nature]{g:natura}. For any other man would be human nature [in the direct case]{g:in-recto}: Christ cannot be human nature, but [is] of human nature: therefore, just as "God" is not predicated univocally of a [creature]{g:creatura}, of which it is said only [in an oblique case]{g:in-obliquo}, and of a divine [person]{g:persona}, of which it is said in the direct case; so neither is "humanity" said univocally of Christ and of others: and consequently they are not univocally of the same nature: indeed, since "humanity" is the most fitting name of the species, predicated of itself of its [supposits]{g:suppositum}, and not "man", but "humanity" cannot be predicated in the direct case of Christ, it seems that Christ is not of the same species as man.[^4] This is confirmed from this: Christ cannot be of any species: therefore Christ is not a singular of the human species. For according to the adversaries he cannot be anything but [the Godhead]{g:deitas}, now it cannot be anything of the human species: therefore Christ cannot be anything of the human species. For if the Godhead were of that species, then it would be causable, [passible]{g:passibilis} and active, like the other individuals of the species: which is impossible. Therefore, if I am not mistaken, it [is incompatible]{g:repugnare} with this way that Christ should be of the same species as the others.[^5] And I would wish to have studied in this metaphysics when I was troubling myself whether quantity, motion [166] and the other [accidents]{g:accidens} are distinguished [from their subjects]. For the distinction of the humanity from the [Word]{g:verbum} is subtler than that of an accident of this kind from its [subject]{g:subiectum}; since, according to [the more modern]{g:moderniores}, Peter can infinitely many times in this hour, by assumption and [laying-aside]{g:dimissio}, be a man and afterwards not a man, without generation or corruption of any thing, or any pertinent motion; which does not happen even with relation.[^6]

### 10-b ¶2 · 166.8–167.15  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 166] Item iuxta nunc tacta, si illa humanitas non sit Christus, **sed** Christus tam extranee differt et essencialiter et personaliter⁶ ab illa; tunc Christus per incarnacionem non est aliquid⁷ nisi quod fuit eternaliter, cum sit solum deitas sicut ante: **sed** in deitate non univocatur cum aliqua creatura: ergo nichil vel non aliquid⁸ est Christus, in quo univocatur cum aliqua creatura.⁹ Sequitur enim—Christus est univoce homo cum aliis: ergo est univoce aliquid cum alio—sicut a simili,¹⁰ si Christus sit univoce albus cum Petro, tunc est univoce coloratus, et qualis cum eo, et ita de aliis generibus quibuscumque. Et sequitur—Christus est univoce aliquid cum aliqua creatura; ergo¹¹ aliquid est Christus in quo ipse univocatur cum aliqua creatura—quod non esset possibile,¹² si solum deitas foret Christus: oportet ergo speciem humanam, que est communis humanitas, esse Christum; et per consequens non solum naturam divinam, cum illa sit natura plurimum separata a natura humana: ymo cum¹³ nulla substancia secunda inest supposito nisi mediante substancia prima, patet quod non communis humanitas foret Christus, nisi singularis¹⁴ humanitas, que natura assumpta foret¹⁴ Christus: patet igitur,¹⁵ si Christus sit eiusdem speciei nobiscum, quod ista species in athomo¹⁶ vel individuo foret Christus; et sic aliquid, quod non eternaliter fuit [p. 167] Christus. Et sic intelligit Augustinus Enchiridion 29°,¹ ‘Utraque, inquit, substancia, divina scilicet atque humana, Filius est unicus Dei Patris omnipotentis, de quo procedit Spiritus² Sanctus; utraque³ unus, **sed** aliud propter Verbum, et aliud propter hominem.’ Unde prius 28°⁴ miratur quomodo ille homo fit⁵ una persona cum Deo. Et in libro De Predestinacione Sanctorum bene post medium: ‘Ille, inquit, homo ut a Verbo Patri coeterno in unitate persone assumptus Filius Dei unigenitus esset! Unde⁶ hoc meruit? Faciente, inquit, et suscipiente Deo Verbo,⁷ ipse homo, ex quo esse cepit, Filius Dei unigenitus⁸ esse cepit.’ Ideo ut exponit 13 De Trinitate 37°. ‘Gracia Dei in hoc commendatur quod Christus tanta unitate Deo sit coniunctus,⁹ ut una cum illo persona Filius Dei fieret, nullis eius precedentibus meritis.’

ENGLISH: Again, according to what has now been touched on, if that humanity is not Christ, but Christ differs from it so foreignly, both essentially and personally, then Christ through the incarnation is not anything but what he was eternally, since he is only the Godhead, as before: now in the Godhead he [is univocal]{g:univocari} with no creature: therefore nothing, or not anything, is Christ in which he is univocal with any creature.[^7] For [it follows]{g:sequitur}, "Christ is univocally a man with others: therefore he is univocally something with another", as, [by likeness]{g:a-simili}, if Christ is univocally white with Peter, then he is univocally coloured, and qualified, with him, and so of any other genera whatever. And it follows, "Christ is univocally something with some creature; therefore something is Christ in which he is univocal with some creature"; which would not be possible if only the Godhead were Christ: therefore the human species, which is the common humanity, must be Christ; and consequently not only the divine nature, since that is a nature most separated from the human nature: indeed, since no [second substance]{g:substancia-secunda} is in a supposit except by means of a first substance, it is clear that the common humanity would not be Christ unless the singular humanity, which [is] the [assumed]{g:assumere} nature, were Christ: it is clear, therefore, that if Christ is of the same species with us, that species [in an individual]{g:in-athomo}, or individual, would be Christ; and so something that was not eternally [167] Christ.[^8] And so Augustine understands [it], *Enchiridion* 29: "Each substance," he says, "the divine, namely, and the human, is the only Son of God the Father almighty, from whom the Holy Spirit proceeds; both one, but [it is] [another thing]{g:aliud} on account of the Word, and another thing on account of the man."[^9] Hence earlier, [in chapter] 28, he marvels how that man becomes one person with God.[^10] And in the book *De praedestinatione sanctorum*, well past the middle: "That man," he says, "that he, assumed by the Word coeternal with the Father into the unity of person, should be the only-begotten Son of God! Whence did he merit this? By God the Word making and [taking up]{g:suscipere} [him]," he says, "this man, from when he began to be, began to be the only Son of God."[^11] Therefore, as he expounds [it] in *De Trinitate* 13, [chapter] 37: "The grace of God is commended in this, that Christ was joined to God with so great a unity that he would become, with him, one person, the Son of God, with no merits of his preceding."[^12]

### 10-b ¶3 · 167.16–167.22  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 167] **Sed** hic datur duplex responsio. Prima quod non est equivocacio vel univocacio nisi in signis solummodo, et sic nulla res est quicquam univoce sive equivoce, **sed** signa dicuntur quedam equivoce et quedam univoce. 2ª responsio modernorum doctorum stat in glosa Augustini et aliorum sic loquencium, quod ipsi non intendunt¹⁰ naturam assumptam esse Verbum **sed** naturam¹¹ Verbi.

ENGLISH: But here a twofold reply is given. The first, that there is no [equivocation]{g:equivocacio} or [univocation]{g:univocacio} except in [signs]{g:signum} only, and so no thing is anything univocally or [equivocally]{g:equivoce}, but signs are said, some equivocally and some univocally. The second reply, of [the modern doctors]{g:moderni-doctores}, consists in a [gloss]{g:glosa} on Augustine and the others who speak so: that they do not [maintain]{g:intendere} "the assumed nature is the Word", but "[it is] the nature of the Word".[^13]

### 10-c ¶1 · 167.23–169.5  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 167] Contra primum verbale sophisticum non delector pro¹² nunc arguere, quia intencio loquencium de rebus univocis est cognoscere, si res similes in racione vel quiditate communicant, vel si de creata essencia Christi est quod ipse sit homo, sicut est de essencia cuiuscunque alterius singularis hominis quod sit homo. ### 10-e ¶1 · 175.17–176.2  (1 sed/autem)

### 10-e ¶2 · 176.3–176.26  (1 sed/autem)

ENGLISH: And from this is taken, third, that every substantial or accidental [difference]{g:differencia} by which the [Word]{g:verbum} [as to the humanity]{g:secundum-humanitatem} differs from another [supposit]{g:suppositum} or [nature]{g:natura} is a difference by which Christ's humanity differs from the same. ### 10-e ¶3 · 176.27–177.22  (2 sed/autem)

ENGLISH: Again, Christ's humanity has complete causes and positive [namings]{g:denominacio} which can belong only to a man: therefore it is a man. So, if it were not a substance, it would not be known what it was, or what other thing would be a substance; and further it seems, since every substance is [first substance]{g:substancia-prima} or second, that that humanity is a first substance, and consequently an [individual]{g:individuum} of a species or genus. And since it is not only in the genus of substance or of body but lower, in the genus of animal, because it is sensitive and rational and consequently a [sentient]{g:sensibilis} animate substance, it follows that it is an animal. For there is no reason why the negation should fall in the [definition]{g:diffinicio} of animal rather than in the definition of any substance whatever, since every singular substance is a supposit.[^8]

Hence also that [verse] of Psalm 44, said literally of Christ, "Blessed is he whom thou hast chosen and taken to thee", they say is false [in the proper sense of the words]{g:de-virtute-sermonis}: but it is understood thus, "blessed [is he] whose is the nature which [179] thou hast chosen and taken to thee"; and this nature is not blessed, since it is not a supposit, because by the same token it [would have] merited the redemption of the human race, for which a creature does not suffice, and it is neither to be worshipped nor adored with [latria]{g:latria}: so that which never exercised the acts of a supposit is not blessed.[^12] But I ask the reader to attend and judge which of these ways is the more pious to the ears of the faithful: either to say that that nature through the assumption is the most blessed of creatures (so that it does not lack the being of a man or of a person, the ground of acting and being acted on [of itself]{g:per-se} and of receiving formally happiness and perfection of every kind; so that the assumption raises it to every kind of perfection and does not bind it by excluding it from perfection of this sort), or to say its opposite? For there is no doubt that to be a man meriting supremely from a passion supremely acceptable to the Trinity, and afterwards to be blessed with the greatest blessedness possible to a creature, is a naming of perfection. And the opposing [position]{g:posicio} takes it away, and our position lays it down expressly, in accord with the ancient doctors and scripture.[^13]

Quomodo, queso, aliter adorarem eam latria, si non tantum benefecit michi ut crux Christi? aut quomodo dimissa recoleret de operibus Christi sui, vel maneret inter passionem et mortem dimissa a Deo corpore fatigata⁸ si nichil antea⁹ fuit passa. Dimissa ergo in uno loco et Christo assumente eam in alio, foret plus quam Christi germanus, ut patet de convenienciis substancialium [p. 182] et accidentalium: et non est conveniencia amplior nisi fuerit personalis; in nullo itaque casu Verbum assumeret personam, que esset natura Verbi passa et quomodolibet temporaliter¹ formata; ut patet de comitancia absolutorum conmultiplicatorum cum taliter multiplicatis; et sic facta revelacione illi homini quod adnichilabitur, patet quod dolor redundaret in Christum, nulli **autem** alteri homini posset sic affici.

ENGLISH: And undoubtedly the same judgement seems [to hold] of a spiritual naming as to the soul. For if the souls of the [saints]{g:sancti} are blessed in heaven, indeed the bodies too, how much more the complete humanity of Christ, since it has in itself first, as subject, all the created blessedness by which Christ humanly is blessed. So Augustine, who holds that the soul [not only]{g:nedum} understands but even senses before the whole composite, would say that Christ's soul in heaven would have the beatific vision and [fruition]{g:fruicio} beyond any other soul.[^7] Nor is it to be feigned that the assumption binds the spirit of Jesus so that it may not delight beatifically. And by the same token, having the four [dowries]{g:dotes} of the body, it would be perfectly and completely blessed with the same blessedness with which Christ is; and the same is the judgement of all the created accidents that are in Christ humanly.[^8] How, I ask, should I otherwise adore it with [latria]{g:latria}, if it has not done me as much good as the cross of Christ? Or how, laid aside, would it recall the works of its Christ, or remain between the passion and death, laid aside by God, wearied in body, if it had suffered nothing before?[^9] If, then, it were laid aside in one place and Christ assumed it in another, it would be more than Christ's brother, as is clear from the [agreements]{g:conveniencia} of substantial [182] and accidental [things]: and there is no fuller agreement unless it is personal; so in no case would the Word assume a person which was a nature of the Word that had suffered and been formed in any way in time; as is clear from the concomitance of absolutes multiplied together with the things so multiplied; and so, if it were revealed to that man that he will be [annihilated]{g:adnichilare}, it is clear that the grief would redound to Christ; but he could be so affected toward no other man.[^10]

### 10-f ¶3 · 182.9–182.31  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 182] Quo dato, videtur multipliciter quod humanitas Christi sit homo, et sic persona Verbi. Nam nichil est risibile nisi homo; humanitas Christi est risibilis, cum sufficiat predicare: ergo est homo. Maior patet ex hoc quod risibilitas est propria passio hominis convertibilis cum specie. Et idem est iudicium de passione mortalitatis capta a causa materiali, de productivitate sui similis in specie, que competit sibi passionaliter a causa efficiente, et de beatificabilitate² secundum corpus et animam que competit sibi a³ causa finali.³ Cum ergo predicaciones huiusmodi solum possunt formaliter inesse suppositis, videtur, cum humanitati Christi insunt, quod ipsa sit suppositum Verbi. Secundo confirmatur idem ex hoc quod omnis forma⁴ accidentalis non est aliud⁵ nisi subiectum accidentaliter se habere; ut mocio est mobile⁶ moveri, locucio est ipsum loqui, et ita de ceteris, ut hic supponitur. Cum ergo eodem accidente informatur Christus et ista humanitas, sequitur quod idem est Christus et illa humanitas: vel⁷ si Christus non sit illa humanitas **sed** movetur secundum illam, alia est mocio Christi et alius motus eius: et ita numerarentur omnes forme complete accidentales in Christo secundum numerum subiectorum: quod⁸ est impossibile, cum nullum tale accidens foret pars alterius, **sed** quelibet pars unius pars alterius et e contra.

ENGLISH: This granted, it seems in many ways that Christ's humanity is a man, and so the person of the Word. For nothing is [risible]{g:risibilis} but a man; Christ's humanity is risible, since it is able to preach: therefore it is a man. The major is clear from this, that risibility is the proper [attribute]{g:passio} of man, [convertible]{g:convertibilis} with the species.[^11] And the same is the judgement of the attribute of mortality, taken from the material cause; of the productivity of its like in species, which belongs to it [as an attribute]{g:passionaliter} from the efficient cause; and of [beatifiability]{g:beatificabilitas} as to body and soul, which belongs to it from the final cause. Since, then, predications of this sort can be formally only in supposits, it seems, since they are in Christ's humanity, that it is a supposit of the Word.[^12] Second, the same is confirmed by this, that every accidental form is nothing but the subject's being accidentally disposed; as motion is the [movable]{g:mobile}'s being moved, speech is the speaking itself, and so of the rest, as is supposed here.[^13] Since, then, Christ and this humanity are informed by the same accident, it follows that Christ and that humanity are the same: or if Christ is not that humanity but is moved according to it, the motion (*mocio*) of Christ is one, and his movement (*motus*) another; and so all complete accidental forms in Christ would be numbered according to the number of subjects: which is impossible, since no such accident would be a part of the other, but every part of the one [is] a part of the other and conversely.[^14]
