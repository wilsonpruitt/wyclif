# *De potestate pape*, chapter 4, part 5: the apostles had no recourse to Peter

## ¶1 · 76.12–78.3

> [p. 76] Unde Januensis in suo Katholicon narrat ex quadam cronica conformiter ad cantum ecclesie, quomodo apostoli per orbem post ascensionem Domini sunt dispersi: Petrus Romam venit, Andreas Achaiam et uterque ibi crucifigitur, Johannes Ephesum vivus intrat sepulcrum, Jacobus maior Jerusolimis decollatur, Thomas in Yndia lancea transfigitur, Philippus in Frigia crucifigitur, Jacobus minor partica fullonis Jerusolimis occiditur, Bartolomeus in maiori Yndia excoriatur et decollatur, Matheus in Ethiopia in altari lancea perforatur, Symon et Yudas in Persyde a pontificibus ydolorum tumultu populi interimuntur, Mathias in Yndia pro Christo patitur, Lucas in Bythinia, obiit, Marcus Alexandrie martirium complevit, Barnabas in Salamia occisus est. Stultum autem et infidele mendacium foret fingere quod omnes alii apostoli habuerunt recursum ad Petrum vel eius vicarium; et ex eorum precepto ac auctoritate fecerunt quicquid fecerant, primo quia habuerunt magistrum omnium eis continue assistentem; iuxta [p. 77] promissum suum Math, ultimo: Ecce ego vobiscum sum, omnibus diebus usque ad consumacionem seculi. Quomodo igitur foret necessarium tam ambigue querere auctoritatem sive consilium, quando tanta copia fontis sapiencie continue fuit presens. Secundo quia Paulus testatur Gal. I, 17 quod post conversionem suam non venit statim Jerusolimam ad apostolos sed continuo predicavit in Arabya. Deinde post annos tres venit Jerusalem videre Petrum et mansit cum eo diebus quindecim, quia (ut dicit in principio) non fuit factus apostolus ab hominibus neque per hominem, eleccione vel instrumentali institucione, sed per Ihesum Christum secundum deitatem, per cuius revelacionem evangelium suum didicerat, deinde (ut narrat cap. II, I, 2 post annos quatuordecim iterum ascendit per revelacionem in Jerusalem cum Barnaba et Tito contulitque cum precipuis apostolis. Michi, inquit, qui videbantur esse aliquid nichil contulerunt, sed e contra cum vidissent, quod creditum est michi evangelium prepucii, sicut Petro circumcisionis, cum cognovissent graciam Dei, que data est michi, Jacobus et Cefas et Johannes, qui videbantur esse columpne, dextras dederunt michi et Barnabe societatis. Cum, inquit, venisset Cephas Antiochiam, in faciem ei restiti, quia reprehensibilis erat, Numquid credimus vas eleccionis et scribam Spiritus Sancti qui vidit quanta mala futura erant in ecclesia pro primatu usus fuit falsitate vel levitate in verbis fidei? quin pocius ad destruendum hereses quas sciverat postmodum pullulare premittens Jacobum dicit quod omnes illi tres [p. 78] nichil sibi contulerant? Cuius sensus repugnaret isti quod Paulus necessitatus fuit accipere autoritativam potestatem a Petro.

Hence Januensis in his *Catholicon* relates from a certain chronicle, in agreement with the chant of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, how the apostles were dispersed through the world after the ascension of the Lord: "Peter came to Rome, Andrew to Achaia, and both are crucified there; John at Ephesus enters his tomb alive; James the greater is beheaded at Jerusalem; Thomas is pierced with a lance in India; Philip is crucified in Phrygia; James the less is killed at Jerusalem with a fuller's pole; Bartholomew is flayed and beheaded in greater India; Matthew is run through with a lance at the altar in Ethiopia; Simon and Jude are slain in Persia by the priests of the idols in a tumult of the people; Matthias suffers for Christ in India; Luke died in Bithynia; Mark completed his martyrdom at Alexandria; Barnabas was killed at Salamis."[^1] And it would be a foolish and faithless lie to feign that all the other apostles had recourse to Peter or his vicar, and did by his precept and authority whatever they did: first, because they had the master of all continually standing by them, according to [77] his promise, Matt. last: "Behold I am with you all days, even to the consummation of the world." How then would it be necessary to seek authority or counsel so uncertainly, when so great a [plenty]{g:copia} of the fount of wisdom was continually present? Secondly, because Paul testifies, Gal. 1:17, that after his conversion he did not come straightway to Jerusalem to the apostles, but at once preached in Arabia. Then after three years he came to Jerusalem to see Peter, and stayed with him fifteen days,[^2] because (as he says at the beginning) he was not made an apostle "of men, neither by man", by election or instrumental institution, "but by Jesus Christ" according to the [Godhead]{g:deitas}, by whose revelation he had learned his gospel. Then (as he relates in ch. 2:1–2) after fourteen years he went up again by revelation to Jerusalem with Barnabas and Titus, and conferred with the leading apostles. "To me," he says, "they who seemed to be some thing added nothing. But contrariwise, when they had seen that to me was committed the gospel of the uncircumcision, as to Peter [that] of the circumcision, when they had known the grace of God that was given to me, James and Cephas and John, who seemed to be pillars, gave to me and Barnabas the right hands of fellowship."[^3] "When," he says, "Cephas was come to Antioch, I withstood him to the face, because he was to be blamed." Do we believe that the vessel of election and scribe of the Holy Spirit, who saw how great evils were to come in the church for the sake of [primacy]{g:primatus}, made use of falsehood or levity in words of faith? Rather, to destroy the heresies which he knew would afterwards spring up, does he not, putting James first, say that all those three [78] had conferred nothing on him?[^4] The sense of which would be incompatible with this: that Paul was compelled to receive authoritative [power]{g:potestas} from Peter.

## ¶2 · 78.4–80.22

> [p. 78] Et ad idem vadit secundum verbum quod illi apostoli gaudebant quod habuerunt tales socios in dextero brachio sancte ecclesie, ista (inquam) apostolorum societas excluderet eosdem esse subditos a paritate potestatis non auctoritative vel iurisdicionalis super consocios. Et idem patet per hoc quod Paulus reprobavit Petrum in sua parrochia; ideo absit fidelem credere hoc de Petro, cum ipse apostolorum humillimus fecit cuncta sua opera post ascensionem auctoritate et nomine Ihesu nichil auctoritati sue attribuens, quia aliter tota scriptura illud obmittens foret nimium defectiva. Tercio confirmatur de iurisdiccione data comuniter cunctis apostolis Matth. ultimo tam quoad locum quam populum. Data est, inquit, michi potestas in celo et in terra, euntes igitur docete omnes gentes servare omnia quecunque mandavi vobis. Et Mar, ultimo limitatur iurisdicionis locus: Euntes, inquit, in mundum universum, predicate evangelium omni creature; ubi patet primo quod alloquitur omnes apostolos pro se et suis vicariis. Apostoli enim iverunt in Asyam, Africam et Europam: et per omnem terram habitabilem exivit virtus sermonis eorum, quia (ut patet ex sepedictis) non est aliqua pars terre vel mundi, quin spiritualiter aleretur ex predicacione evangelii per potestatem datam Christo. Et sic docuerunt mediate vel immediate omnia genera gentium: Grecos, Latinos et barbaros. [p. 79] Et patet secundo quomodo Christus tempore quo apostoli magis recolerent; dedit eis preponderantem sentenciam; quomodo fideliter docerent populum ex integro servare mandata et consilia sua que ad hoc eos docuit. Omnis enim gens debet servare ipsa in una parte vel alia. Quidam tamen apostolorum peragrarunt plures regiones et quidam pauciores, ut Paulus; qui plus omnibus laboravit, visitavit corporaliter et convertit plures provincias. Et breviter cuilibet apostolo vel eius vicario fuit nedum licitum, sed debitum tantum de populo vel terra convertere vel confirmare in fide Christi quantum sufficeret, et non fuit restriccio iurisdicionis nisi ex insufficiencia. Non enim dicit singulariter Petro: vade tu et singulariter predica toti mundo limitaque ceteros apostolos quantum sub iurisdicione sua de terra vel populo subiacebit, quin pocius Spiritus Sanctus dixit Paulo quod predicaret lacius inter gentes; Petro vero quod striccius iret in Judeos, uterque tamen sicut omnes apostoli et eorum vicarii sunt debitores omni homini, ut sibi prosint meliori modo quo poterint. Absit quin rector vel episcopus debeat proficere populo de alia parrochia sive dyocesi, et in casu quo crederet plus prodesse ecclesie et placere Christo, debet parochiam suam dimittere, et aliis in quibus magis proficeret se gratis adiungere. Sed heu ista religiosa regula primeva temporalium cupiditate disrumpitur; et nostri satrape vertunt evangelizacionem in pecunie colleccionem et propter questum circa limites iurisdicionis contenditur, cuius civilitatis origo dicitur cepisse a capite Romane ecclesie. Dicitur enim in nobis [p. 80] occiduis, quod Romanus pontifex vendicat infideliter quod nemo salvabitur nisi subiaceat taxis suis; sed nichil plus horribile vel blasfemum; cum sine personarum accepcione, quecunque persona vel populus de Yndia, Ethiopia, Grecia vel quantumcunque remota insula crediderit in dominum Ihesum Christum salvabitur, per quemcunque apostolum vel Christi discipulum conversa fuerit nisi finaliter ad infidelitatem redierit; ideo omnes tales fideles, ubicunque terrarum fuerint, sunt imitatores Petri, Pauli, et cuiuscunque apostoli, ita quod sufficiat diligere caput Christum, cum diligendo ipsum universitas diligitur ordinate. Nec valet alterius obediencia vel subieccio, nisi de quanto docuerit legem et dileccionem Christi servare, in tantum quod sive dyabolus sive paganus sive inmediate Christus ipsam anuncciaverit subducto quocumque apostolo vel eius vicario sufficit ad salutem, cum amor Christi sit per se sufficiens. Et ista est concors apostolorum sentencia qui se ipsos parvipenderant et magnificaverant nomen Ihesu.

And to the same point goes the second saying, that those apostles rejoiced that they had such companions on the right arm of holy church: this fellowship of the apostles (I say) would rule out the same men's being subjects, on account of the parity of a power that is not authoritative or jurisdictional over their fellows.[^5] And the same is clear from this, that Paul reproved Peter in his own parish; therefore far be it from a believer to believe this of Peter, since he, the humblest of the apostles, did all his works after the ascension by the authority and name of Jesus, attributing nothing to his own authority; for otherwise the whole of scripture, in leaving that out, would be excessively defective.[^6] Thirdly, it is confirmed from the [jurisdiction]{g:iurisdiccio} given in common to all the apostles, Matt. last, as much as regards place as people. "Power," he says, "is given to me in heaven and in earth: going therefore, teach ye all nations to observe all things whatsoever I have commanded you."[^7] And in Mark last the place of the jurisdiction is [assigned]{g:limitare}: "Go ye," he says, "into the whole world and preach the gospel to every creature."[^8] Here it is clear, first, that he addresses all the apostles, for themselves and their vicars. For the apostles went into Asia, Africa and Europe, and through all the habitable earth the [power]{g:virtus} of their word went forth;[^9] for (as is clear from what has often been said) there is no part of the earth or of the world that was not spiritually nourished from the preaching of the gospel, through the power given to Christ. And so, mediately or immediately, they taught all kinds of nations: Greeks, Latins and barbarians. [79] And it is clear, secondly, how Christ, at the time which the apostles would best remember, gave them a [teaching]{g:sentencia} of preponderant weight: how they should faithfully teach the people to keep, in their entirety, his commandments and counsels, which he taught them for this purpose.[^10] For every nation ought to keep them in one part or another. Yet some of the apostles travelled through more regions and some fewer; as Paul, who "laboured more than all", visited in the body and converted more provinces.[^11] And in brief, for each apostle or his vicar it was not only lawful but owed to convert or confirm in the faith of Christ as much of the people or the land as he could manage; and there was no restriction of jurisdiction except from insufficiency. For he does not say to Peter alone: "Go thou and preach alone to the whole world, and limit the other apostles to as much of land or people as will lie under thy jurisdiction"; rather, the Holy Spirit told Paul to preach more widely among the gentiles, and Peter to go more narrowly to the Jews; yet each of them, like all the apostles and their vicars, is a debtor to every man, to profit them in the best way they can.[^12] Far be it that a rector or bishop should not be bound to profit the people of another parish or diocese; and in case he believed he would profit the church more and please Christ more, he ought to leave his parish and join himself freely to others in whom he would profit more. But alas, this first religious rule is broken by greed for [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}; and our [satraps]{g:satrapa} turn the preaching of the gospel into the collecting of money, and for gain there is strife about the limits of jurisdiction; and the origin of this [civil standing]{g:civilitas} is said to have begun from the [head]{g:caput} of the Roman church.[^13] For it is said among us [80] westerners that the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex} faithlessly claims that no one will be saved unless he is subject to his taxes. But nothing is more horrible or blasphemous; since, without respect of persons, whatever person or people of India, Ethiopia, Greece, or however remote an island, shall have believed in the Lord Jesus Christ will be saved, by whatever apostle or disciple of Christ it was converted, unless it finally returns to [unbelief]{g:infidelitas}. Therefore all such believers, wherever on earth they are, are imitators of Peter, of Paul and of any apostle whatever, so that it suffices to love Christ the head, since in loving him the whole is loved in due order.[^14] Nor does the obedience or subjection of another avail, except in so far as he has taught [men] to keep the law and the love of Christ; so much so that, whether the devil or a pagan or Christ immediately has announced it, with any apostle or his vicar set aside, it suffices for salvation, since the love of Christ is of itself sufficient.[^15] And this is the concordant teaching of the apostles, who had made little of themselves and had magnified the name of Jesus.

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 77.1: They acted on Christ's command given with promise.
- 77.9: Paul's rejection of human authority,
- 78.16: Christ's commission to the apostles extended to the whole world.
- 79.14: He gave no charge to Peter to control the other apostles.
- 79.28: Our prelates quarrel over limits of jurisdiction for the sake of gain.
- 80.1: It is said that none can be saved who do not pay the Papal dues.
- 80.12: He who loves Christ, and only he, will be saved.

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** Johannes Balbus of Genoa (*Januensis*), *Catholicon*; Loserth compares *De civili dominio* III, 425 (LEDGER P04e-1). The list is the italic run of the print, so Loserth takes it as quotation; the *Catholicon* has not been checked. *Partica* is *pertica*, the fuller's pole; *Salamia* is Salamis in Cyprus. **R.** B in the margin: *Natalicia apostolorum*; A: *Apostolorum mortes*.

[^2]: **S.** Gal. 1:17–18, retold in the third person (DR "Neither went I to Jerusalem, to the apostles who were before me: but I went into Arabia … Then, after three years, I went to Jerusalem to see Peter: and I tarried with him fifteen days"). Wyclif's *predicavit* ("preached") for the Vulgate's *abii* ("went") is his own.

[^3]: **S.** Gal. 1:1 (the quoted phrases), Gal. 2:1–2, then Gal. 2:6–7, 9 with v. 8 left out; Wyclif's *graciam Dei* adds "of God" to the Vulgate's *gratiam*. Gal. 2:11 follows. Loserth's references: Gal. I, 1; II, 6–8; II, 11. **T.** The print's parenthesis "(ut narrat cap. II, 1, 2" is not closed.

[^4]: **S.** "Vessel of election", Acts 9:15. **C.** *Premittens Jacobum*: in Gal. 2:9 James is named before Cephas, which Wyclif reads as a deliberate check on Petrine claims. The *quin pocius* clause is read as a second question expecting "yes".

[^5]: **C.** A doubtful period. *Excluderet eosdem esse subditos* is "would rule out their being subjects"; *a paritate potestatis* is taken causally ("on account of the parity"), with *non auctoritative vel iurisdicionalis* agreeing with *potestatis*. A word may have been lost (e.g. *sed ponit eos* before *a paritate*); the sense is the same: equal apostles, none with jurisdiction over the others.

[^6]: **T.** *humillimus* is Loserth's correction; the manuscripts read *humilius* ("more humbly", 78.12). **C.** *hoc* is the claim just refuted: that Peter held authority over his fellows.

[^7]: **S.** Matt. 28:18–20, abridged: Wyclif drops *omnis* ("All power"), the baptismal clause and *docentes eos*, so that *docete … servare* runs together. Loserth: Matt. XXVIII, 18–20.

[^8]: **S.** Mark 16:15 (Loserth's "Marc. XV, 15" in the OCR); Wyclif adds only *inquit*. **G.** *limitatur*: the place is assigned, not restricted: it is the whole world.

[^9]: **S.** After Ps. 18:5 (Vulg.; cf. Rom. 10:18), DR "Their sound hath gone forth into all the earth"; Wyclif's *per omnem terram habitabilem exivit virtus sermonis eorum* is his own wording.

[^10]: **C.** *tempore quo apostoli magis recolerent*: the last words before the ascension, which they would best remember. B lacks *tempore* (79.1). *Sentencia* is taken as "teaching", qualified as weightier than any other.

[^11]: **S.** 1 Cor. 15:10, DR "I have laboured more abundantly than all they"; Wyclif's *plus omnibus laboravit*. **C.** The print's semicolon after *ut Paulus* would set Paul among those who travelled less; the relative clause shows he is the example of *plures*.

[^12]: **S.** Rom. 1:14, "I am a debtor"; the division between Paul and Peter is Gal. 2:7–9. **G.** *limitaque* here restricts: "limit".

[^13]: **G.** *satrape*: Wyclif's derogatory word for church-court officials with delegated power (GLOSSARY).

[^14]: **T.** Loserth's apparatus at 80.12 has the lemma *ita quod*, with B *quod si*; we follow the print, *ita quod*. **C.** *universitas* is the whole (of the faithful) loved in Christ.

[^15]: **T.** For *ipsam* (80.17) B reads *iam* ("already"); the print is followed, *ipsam* taken as the law of Christ. **R.** A in the margin: *Romano pontifici subesse non est de necessitate salutis* ("To be subject to the Roman pontiff is not of necessity for salvation").
