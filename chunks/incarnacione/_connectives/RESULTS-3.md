## Doubtful, left as they are

- **03-a ¶8, p. 32**: *Sed si hoc posset in virtute Dei creatura ab eo ypostatice separata; per idem posset passio …*, rendered "But if". The clause opens a reductio that supports the conclusion just drawn (*sic nichil quod non est Deus suffecit …*). That makes it either (a), "Now if", the next step, or (b), the opposing supposition brought in to be refuted. No *ergo* closes it, and "But if" reads naturally as "but [suppose]". Kept.
- **03-b ¶1, p. 33**: Eph. 4:9 *quod autem ascendit, quid est …*, rendered "Now that he ascended". Either (a), the Apostle's next step drawn from the verse he has just quoted (the Douay reading too), or (c), "And that he ascended". It is a step, not a mere extension, so "Now" stands.
- **03-b ¶5, p. 34**: *Sed 3°. post privatam instruccionem apostolorum … ascendit*, rendered "But third". Either (b), the ascension set against the descents of ¶3, or (a), the next item of the series. "But" is defensible. (*Sed* has no continuative class, so "and" is not the alternative.)
- **03-c ¶3, p. 39**: *Sed quelibet illarum particularum est impossibilis de vi vocis*, rendered "But". Either (b), the opponents' reading set against the Apostle's and Augustine's exposition just given, or (a), the step that closes the reductio. But no *ergo* follows (the next sentence is *Deliramenta sunt ista*), so "But" stands.
