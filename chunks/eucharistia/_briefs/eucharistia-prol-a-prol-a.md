## The volume

*Tractatus de eucharistia* and *Tractatus de penitencia sive de confessione*, ed. Johann Loserth (Wyclif
Society, 1892), from the plates of archive.org `latinworks13wycl` (**leaf = page + 71**; leaf images in
`text/eucharistia/raw/leaves/`). Three texts:

Chunk ids: `prol-a … prol-c` the compendium, `01-a … 09-n` the nine chapters, `pen01 … pen06` *De
penitencia*, `quest` the *Questio*.

- **pp. 1–10, the compendium** (*Sentencia tractatus de Eukaristia in compendio*, under "Prologus"): a
  medieval summary of the nine chapters that came down with the work. Translate it like the text.
- **pp. 11–326, the *Tractatus de eucharistia* (maior)**, nine long chapters. Written in Wyclif's last
  years, before 1382–83 (Loserth, Introd. §4: it shares passages word for word with *Sermones* III, and
  it avoids the words *substancia* and *materialis* of the bread, which Wyclif says in *Sermo* III.54 he
  has stopped using). Wyclif's case against the "modern" doctrine: that the consecrated host is in its
  nature bread and wine and sacramentally, figuratively, the body and blood of Christ; that an accident
  without a subject is impossible; that the decree *Ego Berengarius* (*De cons.* D. 2 c. 42) is the
  faith of the ancient Roman church; against the friars, Thomas, Scotus and "the sect of the signs".
  A **treatise**: Wyclif speaks in the first person, states conclusions, raises objections and answers
  them. The tractatus minor (the *Confessio*) is NOT in this volume (Shirley, *Fasc. Ziz.*).
- **pp. 327–348, *De eucharistia et penitencia sive de confessione*** in six chapters (only ch. 1 is on
  the eucharist; the rest is penance and auricular confession), then the short ***Questio ad fratres de
  sacramento altaris*** (pp. 347–348, its first part only: Loserth notes that the rest of the
  manuscript's text is not on the sacrament, and is Hus's).

**Sigla. ⚠ They differ between the two works.** *De eucharistia* (Introd. §5): **A** Vienna 3927;
**B** Vienna 3932; **C** Vienna 1387 ("the best of the three" Vienna MSS, correcting A and B); **D** Prague
Univ. IV D 22 (1404–05, Andreas of Kouřim); **E** Prague Univ. XI E 3 (1460; Loserth: "the best and most
reliable text of all the five"). Loserth's lettering "does not in any way refer to their respective
value". The Vienna MSS are one group, the Prague MSS another. *De penitencia* (Introd. §6): **A** Vienna
3927; **B** Bautzen, Gersdorf Library M. St. 80.7; **C** Vienna 1337; **D** Prague Univ. III G 11; **E**
Vienna 3980; **F** Vienna 4527. Cite variants with the sigla of the work you are in. Marginal notes in the
manuscripts that Loserth prints ("F in marg.", "DB in marg.") are reception: an **R** note where they
show how a reader took the text.

**The page foot** (in your brief as "apparatus", by page) has two blocks, both keyed to the **line**:
the variants (`19. C: ecclesie in marg. alia manu.`) and Loserth's source references. The OCR of it is
rough: read it for sense, not letter by letter, and read the plate foot where a variant matters.

## The four error classes (Thakkar) — check every sentence

1. **Terms:** GLOSSARY.md governs, one term one rendering. Tag the **first occurrence in the chunk** of
   every §A–C term: `[accident]{g:accidens}`. The key must exist in GLOSSARY.md. A term that looks
   technical but has no row: render it sensibly, untagged, and propose it in your report.
   **CONTEXTUAL** rows hold two senses English splits: choose by context and write a **C** note at the
   first occurrence in the chunk and wherever it leans to the other sense (the dikaiosune rule,
   CONVENTIONS §6).
   Rows that already exist and bear on this volume: *accidens* "accident" · *accidens absolutum*
   "absolute accident" · *transsubstanciacio* "transubstantiation" · *conficere* "consecrate (the
   eucharist)" · *figura / figuratum* "figure / thing figured" · *res sacramenti* "reality of the
   sacrament" · *sacramentalia* "sacramentals" · *quantus* "quantified" · *per se / per accidens* "of
   itself / by accident" · *sensibile commune* "common sensible" · *ecclesia* church (**lower-case**) ·
   *caritas* charity · *sentencia* teaching · *ex fide* as faith teaches.
   ⚠ **This volume's own terms are the vocabulary of the eucharistic controversy**, and most have no row
   yet. Render consistently within your brief and PROPOSE the row (the coordinator rules between briefs).
   Expect: *sacramentum* (the sign) and *corpus Christi*; *hostia* (consecrated) "host"; *panis* "bread";
   *remanencia* (of the bread) "remaining"; *accidens sine subiecto* "accident without a subject";
   *quantitas / qualitas*; *substancia*; *materialis*; the modes of being — *sacramentaliter, realiter,
   vere, spiritualiter, virtualiter, figurative, tropice, ydemptice, dimensionaliter, substancialiter*
   (⛔ keep each adverb distinct and in its place; never merge two in one English word); *esse sacramentale*;
   *consecracio*; *ydemptificacio*; *signum* (the "sect of the signs", *secta signorum*); *conversio*;
   *fractio* (of the host); *manducacio* (*corporalis / spiritualis*); *confessio auricularis*,
   *penitencia*, *absolucio*, *claves*. *Ego Berengarius* stays as the decree's title (Wyclif's spelling
   *Beringarius* in the Latin; "Berengar" for the man in the English).
2. **Connectives carry the argument.** ⛔ Never omit one, never turn one into filler.

   | Latin | English | Never |
   |---|---|---|
   | enim, nam | for | "now", "indeed" |
   | ergo, igitur, ideo | therefore, so, then | "now", "and", "indeed" |
   | sed / autem, marking the **minor premiss or next step** | now | (omitted) |
   | sed / autem, adversative | but | (omitted) |
   | autem, continuative | and | (omitted), and **not "now"** |
   | cum (concessive / causal) | although / since | "whenever" |
   | tamen | yet, nevertheless | "therefore" |
   | item | again, also | "now" |
   | vero (continuative) | and; but (adversative) | "now", "truly" |
   | verumtamen | nevertheless | "but truly" |
   | unde | hence, so | "whence" (archaic), "where" |
   | per idem | by the same token | "in the same way" |
   | ymmo | indeed, rather (correcting upward) | (omitted) |
   | patet; constat | it is clear; it is evident | |

   "Now" is reserved for the minor premiss, or the step that brings a new premiss to bear. An *autem*
   that only extends the same step is "and". ⚠ The commonest error in *De ecclesia* (close-out, ~50 of
   69 connective fixes): a *sed* that brings the minor premiss of a syllogism closed by *ergo / ideo /
   igitur* rendered "but". Before writing "but" for a *sed*, look ahead for the *ergo*: if one follows,
   it is "now".
3. **Ordinary words with a medieval sense:** GLOSSARY §D; look up any word whose classical and
   Wyclifite senses may differ, and propose additions.
4. **Syntax:** construe before rendering (main verb, subject, what every relative and genitive hangs
   on). Keep quantifier order where it is meaning. Keep Wyclif's long periods where English tolerates
   them. **A doubtful construal is never chosen silently:** translate the likelier reading and write a
   **C** note giving the alternative.
   ⚠ **Predication is the matter here, as in *De incarnacione*.** *Hoc est corpus meum*, *panis est
   corpus Christi*, *hoc sacramentum est panis*: Wyclif argues about what the pronoun demonstrates and
   in what mode the predicate holds. Keep subject and predicate in his order, keep *est* as "is" (never
   "exists", "becomes" or "represents" unless the Latin says so), and keep the mode adverb where he puts
   it (*est sacramentaliter corpus Christi* "is sacramentally the body of Christ"). ⛔ Never soften "is"
   into "signifies" or harden "figuratively" into "really": the difference is the treatise's matter.
   Demonstrative pronouns (*hoc*, *istud*) under discussion: render "this", and keep their gender
   visible in a C note where the argument turns on it (*hoc* neuter against *hic*, 348).
   **Propositions in indirect speech** whose sense is under discussion: render as the quoted proposition
   ("who say 'this is an accident without a subject'"), not "who say that …", which asserts it.

## Glossary (the 257 rows these chunks use; the full file is GLOSSARY.md)

# GLOSSARY — FROZEN 2026-09-28 (Wilson's pilot review)

*One Latin term, one English rendering, every time, across the whole corpus. A reader must be able
to recognise the same word coming back, because Wyclif's arguments turn on it. A rendering changes
only by a dated decision in CONVENTIONS' decision log, never silently in a chunk. Entries may be
*added* by recorded decision as new terms appear. **Tag keys:** a chunk tags a term as
`[English]{g:latin-form}` (CONVENTIONS §11), and the key must match a Latin form in one of the rows
below. These rows are also the source of the site's hover glossary. Where a term has two senses,
the entry says so and each chunk's note picks one. A term marked **CONTEXTUAL** holds together
senses English splits: its English is chosen by context, under the dikaiosune rule (CONVENTIONS §6).*

**Sources of the decisions.** "Pilot" = settled in `pilots/` this session. "T. p. N" = Mark
Thakkar, "Duces caecorum," *Vivarium* 58 (2020), page N (`reference/`), which documents the
errors of the two most recent translations. His corrections are adopted here unless an entry says
otherwise.

**Orthography.** Headwords are given in the Society editions' medieval spelling (*eciam*,
*racio*, *quiditas*, *ymmo*), because that is what the translator will meet on the page.

# *De eucharistia*, prologue, part 1: [TITLE]

## Headnote

[HEADNOTE: see pilots/wyc-ecclesia-01a.md]

## ¶1 · 1.2–2.5

> [p. 1] Sentencia tractatus de Eukaristia in compendio sic habetur: Capitulum primum premittens distinccionem trimembrem de rebus huius sacramenti solvit tria argumenta paganica. Secundo recitat tria argumenta fidelium et dissolvit. Et ponendo distinccionem inter esum corporalem et spiritualem obicit quod homo videt corpus Christi in sacramento oculo corporali, et solvit. Secundo obicitur per hoc quod multi sancti doctores crediderant quod sensierunt corpus Domini in Eukaristia; et solvendo narrat quomodo finguntur miracula et quomodo populus ex ignorancia consequencie facte de miraculis est seductus. Tercio obiciendo per hoc miraculum narratum a beato Gregorio dicit quod per hoc non docetur quod hostia sit corpus Christi nec quod ille de quo fingitur miraculum propterea fuit sanctus. Et probat tripliciter quod homo non videt corpus Christi in Eukaristia oculo corporali et discurrit per alios sensus deducendo ad inconveniens, concesso quod corpus Domini sacramentaliter senciatur. Ulterius probat quadrupliciter ex testimonio sanctorum et legum ecclesie quomodo corpus Domini manet invisibiliter in hostia consecrata. Finaliter obicit contra hoc tripliciter per leges ecclesie quod corpus [p. 2] Domini est ibi sensibile. Et dissolvit, primo per decretum pape quomodo agendum est effuso sanguine de calice, secundo per confessionem .Beringarii, et tercio per decretum .Jeronimi; et solvit per duplicem maneriem predicandi.

[ENGLISH]

## ¶2 · 2.6–3.5

> [p. 2] Capitulum secundum narrat triplicem sentenciam de quidditate hostie consecrate, quarum prima dicit quod est quantitas, secunda quod est qualitas, et tercia quod est panis et vinum. Et obiciendo contra duas priores quomodo se ipsas destruunt declarat tripliciter quomodo tercia fuit sentencia Romane ecclesie. Quam confirmat tripliciter supra priorem sentenciam in hoc quod plus concordat racioni, sanctis doctoribus et scripture; de sanctis doctoribus allegat triplex testimonium positum in decretis. Quantum ad testimonium scripture notat sex loca in quibus fit mencio de hoc sacramento. In quorum quatuor fit mencio de eius institucione; et sic concludit quod decretum ecclesie in Beringario est tenendum. Sed obicit per hoc, primo quod videtur dictum decretum contradicere sibi ipsi; et dissolvit notans distinccionem inter predicacionem ydempticam et tropicam, in quo exponit textum Apostoli I. Cor. Xᵒ, 16, declarans quod ipse vocat hoc sacramentum panem et calicem; secundo obicit per hoc quod videtur corpus Christi debere nunc vocari panem et vinum, cum scriptura sacra sic loquitur. Et solvit ponens primo exemplum de logica beati Bernhardi concedentis quod manus quibus Deus formavit celum et terram fuerunt crucifixe. Secundo ostendit ex scriptura Genes. VIᵒ, quod non licet nobis tales conclusiones elicere; et exponendo scripturam docet secundum quam mensuram debent concedi dicta doctorum et quomodo in periculosa [p. 3] materia de Eukaristia est loquendum. Tercio et ultimo obicit quod novella ecclesia ponit transsubstanciacionem, et antiqua ecclesia eque vel magis autentica ipsam negat. Et diffuse prosequitur de nomine transsubstanciacionis et declarat cui ecclesie pocius est credendum.

[ENGLISH]

## ¶3 · 3.7–3.24

> [p. 3] Capitulum tercium declarat per sex argumenta quod transsubstanciacio non est fundabilis in scriptura. Posterius declarat per octo argumenta quod accidencia non possunt per se esse. Et tercio recitando doctores dat viam ad eliciendum conclusiones mirabiles. Primum argumentum prime partis fundatur super isto quod Deus inprovide sic mutaret. Secundum argumentum fundatur super hoc quod tunc annichilaretur substancia panis et vini; quod probat multipliciter consequenter. Et quantum ad secundum punctum deducit per multa media philosophica per deduccionem ad inconveniens et per multiplex testimonium Augustini quod nullum accidens potest esse sine subiecto; et arguendo doctores novellos obicit per Hugonem de Sancto Victore et solvit. Et ex ista varietate elicit tres conclusiones mirabiles et finaliter dat sensum ad quem doctores sancti et decreta debent intelligi.

[ENGLISH]

## ¶4 · 3.25–4.25

> [p. 3] Capitulum quartum declarat, primo quomodo verba Christi quibus hoc sacramentum conficitur figurative debent intelligi, secundo quomodo Christus habens nobiscum multiplex esse humanitus quietabit beatos visione corporali post diem iudicii, et tercio quomodo est in sacramento altaris dispariter ab esse quod habet in aliis sacramentis, et quomodo hoc sacramentum tripliciter differenter alia antecellit. Secundo principaliter movendo tria dubia prosequitur primum, [p. 4] quod querit de veritate verborum sacramentalium, quando proposicio est vera et quare non debet concedi quod quelibet creatura est Christus, et quomodo nulla forma verborum est ad confeccionem Eukaristie necessario requisita, et quomodo quatuor evangeliste variant, et quomodo ecclesia in multis locis dat quintum modum variandi, et quomodo postpositis ritibus est ad sentenciam attendendum. Tercio principaliter obicit tripliciter et dissolvit, primo quomodo non sequimur Christum conficiendo et sumendo hostiam in prandendo, secundo obicit per hoc quod Christus fregit panem antequam consecravit. Et dissolvit narrans causam triplicem quare Spiritus Sanctus sic variavit in suis evangelistis et quare singulariter in benediccione calicis fit mencio de testamento. Et tercio obicit per hoc quod laycus videtur posse conficere; in qua materia distinguit de posse, explanat sentenciam beati Ambrosii, et descendendo ad tres opiniones quas recitat ecclesia IIIᵒ Decretalium invehit contra illas et preponit sentenciam Romane ecclesie ex sanctitate et facto Beringarii, et redeundo declarat quomodo oportet precognoscere questionem quid est de hostia antequam noscantur due alie questiones, et sic ostendit quod non tantum valet missa vel hostia sacerdotis reprobi ut devoti.

[ENGLISH]
