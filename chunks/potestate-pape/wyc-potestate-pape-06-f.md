# *De potestate pape*, chapter 6, part 6: the seventh to eleventh conditions of the pseudo-pope

## ¶1 · 122.30–123.8

> [p. 122] Septimo, ubi Christus prohibet suis ferire cum gladio sed pati ut ipse, cum potuit hostes suos faciliter superare; ut patet Joh. XVIII, iste Pseudo docet et conducit milites de bonis pauperum [p. 123] ad cumulandum sibi superfluitates stercorum et discipulos suos, qui severius exequuntur illud officium, plus commendat et promovet ad seculi dignitatem, sic quod Christo conversante nobiscum non foret suis cum illis societas vel amicicia nisi inter homines prope pares, Et secundum Apostolum II Cor. VI, 15 non est communicacio Christi ad Belial, ut tangebam superius.

Seventh: whereas Christ forbids his own to strike with the sword, but [bids them] suffer as he did, although he could easily have overcome his enemies, as is clear in John 18 (18:11), this Pseudo teaches and hires [soldiers]{g:milites} from the goods of the poor [123] to heap up for himself superfluities of dung; and those of his disciples who carry out that office the more severely he commends the more and promotes to the world's dignity; so that, were Christ living among us, his own would have no fellowship or friendship with them, [for there is none] except between men nearly equal.[^1] And according to the Apostle, 2 Corinthians 6:15, there is no communion of Christ with Belial, as I touched on above.[^2]

## ¶2 · 123.9–123.20

> [p. 123] Octavo, ubi Christus prohibuit cariores eius discipulos Jacobum et Johannem inprecari ignem de celo descendere instar Helye, IV Reg primo ad vindicandum de Samaritanis negantibus eis necessaria, cibum et tegumina, ut patet Luce IX, 56, iste Pseudo deficiente materiali gladio fingit fulmen anathematis in eos, qui nolunt sue tyrannidi obedire, sic quod ubi Christus dicit suos discipulos esse spirituales nulli obnoxios, cum ipse non venit animas perdere sed salvare, ut ibi dicitur, iste Pseudo pro nichilo perdit utramque vitam multiplicium animarum.

Eighth: whereas Christ forbade his dearer disciples, James and John, to call down fire from heaven after the manner of Elijah (4 Kings 1) to take vengeance on the Samaritans who denied them necessaries, food and shelter, as is clear in Luke 9:56, this Pseudo, when the material sword fails him, forges the thunderbolt of anathema against those who will not obey his tyranny; so that, whereas Christ says that his disciples are spiritual, hurtful to no one, since he "came not to destroy souls, but to save", as is said there, this Pseudo for nothing destroys both lives of many souls.[^3]

## ¶3 · 123.21–124.7

> [p. 123] Nono, ubi Christus dicit in suo Apostolo prima Cor. VI, 7 quod omnino est delictum in gentibus, quod iudicia secularia sic exercent, cum ipse renuit tale iudicium, ut patet Luce XII, iste Pseudo hortatur viis et modis scolares suos contendere, dicens quod condidit multiplices leges sacras ecclesie et ordinavit satrapas ad complementum talis iusticie; ideo sicut excomunicantur qui impediunt pugiles ecclesie, qui volunt exequi eius iura, sic excomunicantur iniuriati in paciendo iniurias, cum ob torporem eorum leges et possessiones ecclesie sunt extincte. Unde Apostolus dicens: Quare non magis iniuriam patimini? per [p. 124] antiphrasim locutus est yronice, sicut quando dicit quod talia debent per contemptibiles ecclesie iudicari, et sic ubi Christus cum sua familia suspensus fuit a talibus intendens pure saluti anime, iste Pseudo cum sua familia trahit sibi omnia secularia negocia, gracia congregandi pecunie.

Ninth: whereas Christ says in his Apostle, 1 Corinthians 6:7, that "there is altogether a fault" among the gentiles in that they so "exercise secular judgments", since he himself refused such judgment, as is clear in Luke 12 (12:14), this Pseudo exhorts his scholars by all ways and means to go to law, saying that he has made many sacred laws of the [church]{g:ecclesia} and ordained [satraps]{g:satrapa} for the fulfilment of such justice; therefore, as those are excommunicated who hinder the champions of the church who wish to execute its [rights]{g:ius}, so those are excommunicated who, being wronged, [persist] in suffering the wrongs, since through their sluggishness the laws and possessions of the church are extinguished.[^4] Hence the Apostle, in saying "Why do you not rather suffer wrong?", spoke ironically, [124] by antiphrasis, as when he says that such matters ought to be judged by the contemptible of the church.[^5] And so, whereas Christ with his [household]{g:familia} was [suspended]{g:suspendere} from such things, attending purely to the salvation of the soul, this Pseudo with his household draws to himself all secular business, for the sake of gathering money.[^6]

## ¶4 · 124.8–124.34

> [p. 124] Decimo, ubi Christus ostendit humiliter suam inferioritatem celans sub testa humanitatis suam divinitatem, ut patet Joh. XIV et alibi sepe in evangelio, iste Pseudo proclamat pompatice et blasfeme quod est Deus mixtus vel Deus in terris, quem nemo debet corripere, cum habens omnes leges in scrinio pectoris, debet ad votum scripturam autorizare, exponere vel quantum libuerit abrogare, et sic propter iniustam pene gravedinem periculosius est in mandatum suum quam in mandatum Christi offendere, cum potest declarare quoscumque sibi contradicentes, licet pure asserentes fidem scripture, esse hereticos, cum pure ex bullis suis ad votum habilitat et inhabilitat quemcumque voluerit, ymo eo ipso quo pretendit se quovis modo solvere vel ligare, eo ipso sic solvit vel ligat. Et ipse supra Christum habet plenam potestatem civiliter dominandi, et sic de infinitis blasfemiis, ex quibus infertur prophecia Apostoli II. Tess. II, 3 de Antichristo dicta quem hominem peccati nominant, ita ut in templo Dei sedeat, ostendens se tamquam sit Deus, ymo ut dicit Apostolus, extollitur super omne quod dicitur Deus. Deus enim non potest taliter dominari nec nude per bullas sine interiori illapsu quemquam habilitare, nec potestatem preter Dei licenciam ab imperatore capere.

Tenth: whereas Christ humbly showed his inferiority, hiding his [divinity]{g:divinitas} under the shell of his humanity, as is clear in John 14 (14:28) and often elsewhere in the gospel, this Pseudo proclaims pompously and blasphemously that he is a mixed God, or God on earth, whom no one ought to rebuke, since, having all laws in the shrine of his breast, he is at will to [authorize]{g:autorizare} scripture, to expound it, or to abrogate it as far as he pleases;[^7] and so, because of the unjust gravity of the penalty, it is more dangerous to offend against his command than against the command of Christ, since he can declare any who contradict him, though they purely assert the faith of scripture, to be heretics, since purely by his bulls he [qualifies]{g:habilitare} and disqualifies at will whomever he will; indeed, by the very fact that he claims in any way to loose or bind, by that very fact he so looses or binds. And he has, above Christ, full [power]{g:potestas} of exercising civil lordship; and so of infinite blasphemies, from which the Apostle's prophecy is drawn, 2 Thessalonians 2:3, spoken of Antichrist, whom they name "the man of sin": "so that he sitteth in the temple of God, shewing himself as if he were God"; indeed, as the Apostle says, he "is lifted up above all that is called God" (2:4).[^8] For God cannot exercise lordship in that way, nor qualify anyone barely by bulls without an inward inflowing, nor take power from the emperor apart from God's [licence]{g:licencia}.[^9]

## ¶5 · 125.1–125.31

> [p. 125] Undecimo et ultimo patet quod Christus abnuit omnem sentenciam, in qua necesse foret facere alicui iniuriam, ideo Luce XII, 14 dicit cuidam petenti eius iudicium: Quis me constituit iudicem aut diuisorem super vos? Unde et eius apostoli abalienati sunt a divisione cuiuscumque prepositure, que lucrum temporale sapiunt, quia didicerant a magistro Matth. X, 8: Gratis accepistis gratis date. Iste autem Pseudo cum discipulis suis palliat, quod ad eum solum spectat in solidum, omnes preposituras sapientes pinguedinem temporalem tribuere; nec potest propter plenitudinem potestatis symoniam committere, sed propter plenitudinem dominii oportet quemcumque secundum pinguedinem sui beneficii de thesauro Pseudo afferre; et de plenitudine potestatis facit reservaciones mentales absconditas, et virtute earum plus offerens preponetur, cum potest anteriorare vel posteriorare tempus ad libitum, ymo non obstante quod Christus sit cum suis apostolis omnibus diebus usque ad consumacionem seculi, ut dicit Matth. ultimo, iste tamen Pseudo potest dispensando tempus redimere et mutare, sic quod quando est maxime necessarium Christo et sue ecclesie consilia Christi servare, liceat sibi consilia ipsa suspendere, privilegia quecumque propter questum concedere, et quantumcumque Christo inhabiles tamquam habiles approbare, et quicquid fecerit debet tota militans ecclesia, quam oportet esse sub dicione ut legem Christi ratum credere.

Eleventh and last: it is clear that Christ refused every [sentence]{g:sentencia} in which it would be necessary to do wrong to anyone; therefore, Luke 12:14, he says to one who asked for his judgment: "Who hath appointed me judge or divider over you?"[^10] Hence his apostles too were estranged from the distribution of any [office of superior]{g:prepositura} that savours of temporal gain, for they had learned from the master, Matthew 10:8: "Freely have you received, freely give." But this Pseudo with his disciples [cloaks]{g:palliare} [the matter, saying] that it belongs to him alone, in full, to bestow all offices of superior that savour of temporal fatness; and that he cannot commit simony, because of the [plenitude of power]{g:plenitudo-potestatis}, but because of the plenitude of [lordship]{g:dominium} everyone must bring [payment] out of his treasure to the Pseudo, according to the fatness of his [benefice]{g:beneficium-ecclesiasticum};[^11] and out of the plenitude of power he makes hidden mental reservations, and by virtue of them the one who offers more will be preferred, since he can put the time earlier or later at pleasure.[^12] Indeed, notwithstanding that Christ is with his apostles "all days, even to the consummation of the world", as he says in the last [chapter] of Matthew (28:20), yet this Pseudo can by dispensing redeem and change the time, so that, when it is most necessary for Christ and his church to keep the counsels of Christ, it is lawful for him to suspend those very counsels, to grant any [privileges]{g:privilegium} whatever for gain, and to approve as [qualified]{g:habilis} men however [unqualified]{g:inhabilis} for Christ; and whatever he has done, the whole [church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans}, which must be under [his] sway, ought to believe ratified, as [it believes] the law of Christ.[^13]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 122.30: 7. Christ forbade his disciples to take the sword;
- 123.1: the pope uses the spoils of the poor to hire soldiers,
- 123.9: Christ would not call down fire on the Samaritans; the pope curses all who do not obey him.
- 123.21: 9. Christ refused to be a judge; the pope is always grasping at jurisdiction.
- 124.8: 10. Christ humbly hid his deity under cover of his manhood; the pope claims to be God in earth,
- 124.28: and fulfils St. Paul's prophecy of the man of sin.
- 125.1: Christ's disciples made no money by prelacies, the pope makes all appointments a matter of gain.

## Notes

[^1]: **T/G/C.** Apparatus 123.5: codd. *sui*; the print's *suis* (Loserth's) is translated: "there would be for his own no fellowship". *Milites* hired for pay are "soldiers" (GLOSSARY *milicia; milites*, the estate is "knights"). The last clause gives the reason: fellowship needs near-equals. John 18:11, "Put up thy sword"; cf. 18:36. A in marg. at 122.30: *7*.
[^2]: **S.** 2 Cor. 6:15, where the Vulgate asks *quae autem conventio Christi ad Belial?* (DR "And what concord hath Christ with Belial?"); Wyclif states it, with *communicacio*, and the English follows his Latin. "Above": perhaps 06-e, the fourth condition, on the pot and the kettle (our suggestion).
[^3]: **S/C.** Luke 9:54–56, with 4 Kings 1:10–12. "His disciples are spiritual" answers Luke 9:55, "you know not of what spirit you are". *Nulli obnoxios* may also be read "liable to no one". "Both lives" are the bodily and the eternal. The print's *patetLuce* is *patet Luce*. AB in marg. at 123.9: *8*.
[^4]: **S/C.** 1 Cor. 6:7, paraphrased: the Vulgate has *in vobis* and *quod iudicia habetis inter vos*; Wyclif's *in gentibus* and *iudicia secularia* (from 6:4) are his, and the quotation marks follow Loserth's italics. Luke 12:14. *Contendere*: to contend at law. *Iniuriati in paciendo iniurias*: the wronged who go on bearing their wrongs, "[persist]" supplied. *Ius* is subjective here, "rights". AB in marg. at 123.21: *9*.
[^5]: **S/C.** 1 Cor. 6:7 again; Wyclif's *patimini* (Vulgate *accipitis*, DR "take wrong") is translated "suffer". "The contemptible of the church": 1 Cor. 6:4 (DR "who are the most despised in the church"). This reading of Paul as ironic is the Pseudo's, reported in Wyclif's voice; the *unde* continues the Pseudo's case.
[^6]: **G.** *Suspensus fuit a talibus*: held aloof from such business; GLOSSARY "suspend" is kept, with no pun on hanging here.
[^7]: **C/S.** *Deus mixtus* is unexplained (perhaps God mingled with man, a mock of the two natures). *Omnes leges in scrinio pectoris* is the canonists' maxim (Boniface VIII, *Sext* 1.2.1, *Licet Romanus pontifex*: *qui iura omnia in scrinio pectoris sui censetur habere*; Wyclif has *omnes leges*; LEDGER P06f-1). *Debet … autorizare* is read as the Pseudo's claimed right ("is to authorize"), not a duty.
[^8]: **S.** 2 Thess. 2:3–4, quoted in DR; *nominant*, "they name", has the doctors as subject. *Infertur*: the prophecy is brought in, i.e. it applies.
[^9]: **C.** *Illapsus*: God's inflowing into the soul, without which no bull qualifies anyone. The last clause (*nec potestatem preter Dei licenciam … capere*) cannot have God as subject in sense; Wyclif seems to slip back to the Pseudo, who takes power from the emperor (the Donation) apart from God's licence. Translated as printed.
[^10]: **C.** *Sentenciam* here is the judicial sentence, not a teaching (GLOSSARY *sentencia*, CONTEXTUAL). AB in marg. at 125.1: *11*.
[^11]: **C.** *Palliat quod* is read "pleads, as a cloak, that". *De thesauro Pseudo afferre*: *Pseudo* is taken as dative, "to the Pseudo"; it may also be genitive, "into the Pseudo's treasury". The plenitude of power is set against the plenitude of lordship: the first clears him of simony, the second obliges every beneficed man to pay (the annates and services, our gloss).
[^12]: **C.** *Reservaciones mentales*: papal reservations of benefices, kept unpublished (in the pope's mind) so that the date of reservation can be moved, *anteriorare vel posteriorare*, to suit the higher bidder.
[^13]: **S/C.** Matt. 28:20 in DR. *Tempus redimere* turns Eph. 5:16 ("redeeming the time") to the sale of dispensations. *Ut legem Christi ratum credere* is read "believe ratified, as the law of Christ"; it may also mean "believe it ratified as [being] the law of Christ".
