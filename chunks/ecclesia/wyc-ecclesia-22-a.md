# *De ecclesia*, chapter 22, part 1: the fourth lie: Hostiensis on suffrages for the damned

## ¶1 · 517.28–519.13

> [p. 517] Quartum mendacium est ficcio testimonii Hostiensis in Summa de oracionis suffragio pro dampnatis. Scribit enim in libro V°, De Penitenciis et Remissionibus, [p. 518] capitulo Quibus mortuis suffragia ecclesia prosunt, quod quatuor beneficiis specialiter adiuvantur, scilicet missarum celebracionibus, sanctis oracionibus, amicorum elemosinis et ieiuniis cognatorum. Sed quibus mortuis prosunt hec suffragia scolastici varie opinantur. Quidam enim dicunt quod nullis qui sunt in inferno prosunt penitus vivorum suffragia, sed tantum hiis qui sunt in purgatorio. Secundi dicunt quod prosunt omnibus regeneratis qui dum viverent meruerunt ut eis mortuis prodessent suffragia; et quia non sufficimus discernere qui meruerunt et qui non, qui decesserunt in mortali et qui non, ideo oramus generaliter pro omnibus regeneratis. Et valet eciam pro dampnatis quodammodo, cum pro valde bonis sunt graciarum acciones, pro mediocriter bonis et malis sunt propiciaciones, pro valde malis prosunt ut tollerabilior sit eis dampnacio. Prima autem est opinio theologorum et secunda doctorum canonum; que licet sit magis pia, prima tamen est magis catholica. Hugucius tamen dicit quod opinio theologorum parum distat ab heresi, cum de quatuor generibus defunctorum eundem constituat simul et semel bonum et malum. Ideo dicunt decretiste quod Augustinus intellegit in illa divisione quadrimembri per excellenter bonos eos qui sunt in celo, per mediocriter bonos salvandos ad tempus spectantes in purgatorio, per mediocriter malos dampnatos leviter in inferno, et per excellenter malos dampnatos gravius. Et duobus [p. 519] generibus prosunt suffragia in tantum quod Judas in die dominico quievit a pena, licet non sit valde autenticum. Et sic nunquam dimittitur dampnatis pena simpliciter, sed proporcionaliter secundum partem cotam non quantam, et sic glossant dicta sanctorum quod dampnatis non prosunt suffragia, scilicet excellenter malis. Et post sequitur Hostiensis sentencia in hec verba: Non est mihi tanta copia ut ista diffiniam quid debeat teneri precise. Nec Innocencius III. qui tantus erat voluit diffinire sed reliquit disputacioni et discrete investigacioni (ut patet in De Celebracione misse, Cum Marthe). Tamen sentencia theologorum est celebrior et verior videtur.

The fourth lie is the [fiction]{g:ficcio} of a testimony of Hostiensis in the *Summa*, on the [suffrage]{g:suffragia} of prayer for the damned.[^1] For he writes in book 5, *On Penances and Remissions*, [518] in the chapter "To which of the dead the suffrages of the church profit", that they are helped especially by four benefits, namely by the celebrations of masses, by holy prayers, by the alms of friends and by the fasts of kinsfolk.[^2] But as to which of the dead these suffrages profit, the schoolmen hold various [opinions]{g:opinio}. For some say that the suffrages of the living profit none at all of those who are in hell, but only those who are in purgatory. Others say that they profit all the regenerate who, while they lived, merited that suffrages should profit them when dead; and because we are not able to discern who merited and who did not, who died in mortal sin and who did not, therefore we pray generally for all the regenerate. And it avails even for the damned in a certain way, since for the very good they are thanksgivings, for the moderately good and bad they are propitiations, for the very bad they profit so that their [damnation]{g:dampnacio} may be more tolerable.[^3] And the first is the opinion of the theologians, and the second that of the doctors of the canons; and though this is more pious, yet the first is more catholic. Yet Huguccio says that the opinion of the theologians is little distant from heresy, since of the four kinds of the dead it makes the same one at once and together good and bad.[^4] Therefore the decretists say that Augustine, in that fourfold division, understands by the excellently good those who are in heaven, by the moderately good those to be saved, waiting for a time in purgatory, by the moderately bad those lightly damned in hell, and by the excellently bad those more heavily damned. And to two [519] kinds suffrages profit, to such a degree that Judas rested from punishment on the Lord's day, though this is not very [authentic]{g:autenticus}.[^5] And so punishment is never remitted to the damned [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}, but proportionally, according to a proportional part, not a quantity; and so they gloss the sayings of the saints that suffrages do not profit the damned, namely the excellently bad.[^6] And afterwards follows the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of Hostiensis in these words: "I have not so great a store as to define these things, what ought to be held precisely." Nor did Innocent III, who was so great, wish to define it, but left it to disputation and discreet investigation (as is clear in *On the Celebration of Mass*, *Cum Marthe*). Yet the teaching of the theologians is more renowned, and seems truer.[^7]

## ¶2 · 519.14–520.14

> [p. 519] Volve itaque et revolve sentenciam huius doctoris magni in iure canonico et vide si sit testis ydoneus ad confirmandum quod sunt facienda suffragia pro dampnatis ad penam perpetuam. Unde videtur mihi quod totum genus humanum debet concorditer detestari huiusmodi mendacia et dampnare istam blasphemiam quod licitum est mentiri. Cum enim mentiri sit ex equo peccare (ut dicunt sancti), si licet mentiri, sic licitum est peccare, sed dare illam licenciam non subest potestati divine. Quis ergo blasphemus extolleret se super omne quod dicitur Deus, quod ipse habet privilegium vel licenciam menciendi? Unde graviter fert doctor cum suis quod irridebam istam sentenciam dicendo me nescire quam laudem talis mentitor egregius reportaret, nisi forte ut pre omnibus aliis ferat cotem. Cum enim Deus non potest per se vel alium facere tam solempne mendacium, videtur quod omnes discipuli veritatis debent acui [p. 520] contra illos, et cum ille qui habitat in celis irridebit eos omnes, sui debent irrisione, exprobracione vel racione, ut Deus donaverit confundere istam scolam. Sic enim legitur III° Regum XVIII° quod Helias canus irrisit quadringentos sacerdotes Baal qui seduxerunt populum suis mendaciis; nam illis paciscentibus cum consensu populi quod credatur illi Deo qui fecerit ignem descendere super bovem divinitus comburendum, et cum prophete Baal invocassent nomen Baal de mane usque ad meridiem, et non erat vox que eis responderet, post meridiem illudebat eis Helias dicens: Clamate voce maiori: Deus enim vester est et forsitan cum alio loquitur, aut in diversorio est, aut in itinere, aut certe dormit, ut excitetur.

Turn over, then, and turn over again the teaching of this doctor, great in canon law, and see whether he is a fit witness to confirm that suffrages are to be made for those damned to perpetual punishment. Hence it seems to me that the whole human race ought with one accord to detest lies of this kind and to condemn this blasphemy, that it is lawful to lie. For since to lie is equally to sin (as the saints say), if it is lawful to lie, then it is lawful to sin; but to give that licence does not lie within the divine power.[^8] What blasphemer, then, would lift himself up above all that is called God, holding that he has a [privilege]{g:privilegium} or licence of lying? Hence the doctor with his men takes it hard that I mocked this teaching, saying that I did not know what praise so egregious a liar would carry off, unless perhaps that he should bear the whetstone before all others. For since God cannot, by himself or by another, make so solemn a lie, it seems that all disciples of truth ought to be whetted [520] against them; and since he that dwelleth in heaven shall laugh at them all, his own ought, by mockery, reproach or reason, as God shall grant, to confound this school.[^9] For so it is read in 3 Kings 18 that the grey-haired Elijah mocked the four hundred priests of Baal who had seduced the people by their lies; for when they had covenanted, with the consent of the people, that that God should be believed who should make fire come down upon the bullock, to be burned by divine power, and when the prophets of Baal had called on the name of Baal from morning even until noon, and there was no voice to answer them, after noon Elijah jested at them, saying: "Cry with a louder voice: for he is your god, and perhaps he is talking with another, or is in an inn, or on a journey; or perhaps he is asleep, and must be awaked."[^10]

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Quartum mendacium*: the fourth of the opponent's lies, numbered from chapter 21. *Ficcio testimonii*: a feigned testimony, Hostiensis being cited for what he does not teach (row *ficcio*). *De oracionis suffragio*: on the suffrage of prayer. **S.** Hostiensis (Henry of Segusio), *Summa aurea* V, *De poenitentiis et remissionibus*, § *Quibus mortuis prosint suffragia*; to be checked (LEDGER E22a-1). **R.** Side-note 517.28.

[^2]: **S.** The four benefits: Decretum C.13 q.2 c.22 *Animae* (Loserth, 517.30; the modern form is ours; LEDGER E22a-2). **T.** *Suffragia ecclesia*: so printed; taken as *ecclesie*, "of the church". B *sacris oracionibus* (518.3). **R.** Side-note 518.1; A in the margin at 518.5: *Oracionum suffragia an dampnatis prosunt*.

[^3]: **C.** *Secundi*: the second party. *Regeneratis*: the baptized. *Graciarum acciones … propiciaciones*: Augustine's terms for what the church's offerings are for each kind of the dead. **S.** Augustine, *Enchiridion* 29.110; Loserth gives "cap. CIX" (Opp. VI, p. 238) and Decretum C.13 q.2 c.23 *Tempus* at 518.23 (the modern form is ours; LEDGER E22a-3, E22a-4). **R.** Side-note 518.10.

[^4]: **T.** The codices read *prima inquit*; *prima tamen* is the print's (518.17). **C.** *Que licet sit magis pia*: the second, the canonists' view. *Eundem constituat … bonum et malum*: the same person, the soul in purgatory, is counted at once among the moderately good and the moderately bad. **S.** Huguccio of Pisa, the decretist; Loserth refers to p. 225 above, to Fabricius, *Bibliotheca latina* VI, p. 283, and to Schulte, *Geschichte der Quellen* I, pp. 156–170, and for Hostiensis ibid. II, pp. 123–129 (518.20; LEDGER E22a-5). **R.** A in the margin at 518.20: *Hugucius*.

[^5]: **C.** *Decretiste*: the commentators on the Decretum. *Duobus generibus*: the two middle kinds, the moderately good and the moderately bad. **S.** Judas's Sunday respite from hell is told in the *Navigatio sancti Brendani*; to be checked (LEDGER E22a-6).

[^6]: **C.** *Secundum partem cotam non quantam*: Loserth notes "*cotam*, id est *quotam*". The remission is by a proportional part, a fraction of what remains, not by a fixed quantity, so that it never brings the punishment to an end. *Glossant*: the decretists gloss. **T.** A₁ *totam* (519.5). **R.** Side-note 519.6.

[^7]: **S.** Innocent III, *Cum Marthe*, X 3.41.6 (Loserth, 519.10; the modern form is ours; LEDGER E22a-7). **C.** *Copia*: store of learning. The print's italics end the verbatim quotation of Hostiensis at *precise*; the rest is reported. **R.** Side-note 519.11; A in the margin at 519.7: *Hostiensis*.

[^8]: **T.** A and A₁ *Volve et revolve itaque* (519.14). **C.** *Ex equo*: equally, on the same footing. *Ut dicunt sancti*: not located. *Non subest potestati divine*: God himself cannot license sin.

[^9]: **S.** 2 Thessalonians 2:4: *extolleret se super omne quod dicitur Deus*, after the Vulgate *extollitur supra omne quod dicitur Deus* (Douay "is lifted up above all that is called God"). Psalm 2:4, Douay–Rheims. **C.** *Quod ipse habet*: "holding that he has", giving the content of the self-exaltation. *Cum suis*: with his followers. *Ferat cotem*: bear off the whetstone, the mock prize of the greatest liar; *acui*, "whetted", takes up the figure, and it returns at 520.17. *Sui*: God's own, the disciples of truth. **R.** Side-note 519.26; A in the margin at 519.23: *Nota quomodo mendaces sunt irridendi*.

[^10]: **S.** 3 Kings 18 (1 Kings 18 in modern numbering): the prophets of Baal were four hundred and fifty, with four hundred prophets of the groves (v. 19); Wyclif has four hundred. Verses 26–27 are paraphrased; in v. 27 he has *Deus enim vester est et forsitan cum alio loquitur* for the Vulgate *deus enim est, et forsitan loquitur*, translated as his. **C.** *Canus*: grey-haired; doubtful, since scripture says nothing of Elijah's age here. *Paciscentibus*: covenanting (v. 24). **R.** Side-note 520.4; A in the margin at 520.5: *Quomodo irrisit Elias quadringentos sacerdotes Baal*.
