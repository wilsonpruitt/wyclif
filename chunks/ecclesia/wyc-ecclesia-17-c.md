# *De ecclesia*, chapter 17, part 3: the seven women of Isaiah, and three ways of being Christian

## ¶1 · 394.24–398.1

> [p. 394] Unde in De Questionibus veteris et nove legis cap. XLVII°, Augustinus exponens illud Isaie IV°, 1: Apprehendent septem mulieres virum unum in die illa dicentes: Panem nostrum comedemus et vestimentis nostris operiemur: tantummodo invocetur nomen tuum super nos, aufer obprobrium nostrum, primo dicit quod propheta loquitur de die gracie et muliere septemplici, scilicet ecclesia que est mulier [p. 395] fortis, de qua Prov. XXXI°: Per septem mulieres, inquit, septem ecclesias dico esse significatas que quamvis una sit tamen septiformis dicitur, ut unum corpus septemplici numero constat membrorum: Non solum, inquit, nos sed eciam superni cives ecclesie membra sunt cuius caput est Christus. Sive ergo una sive septem dicantur ecclesie, non discrepat, quia tota universitas ecclesiarum que est septem ecclesie propter septem dona spiritus sancti et simul omnes unum corpus sunt Christi ex quo omnia in celis et in terra habent originem. Unde in hac militante ecclesia omnium ecclesiarum facta est mencio, quia cum huic sacramentum creatoris predicatur, eciam celestibus insinuatur doctrina. Inferioribus enim cum insinuatur necesse est ut audiant et superni. Unde Apostolus Ephesiorum III°: Michi autem omnium sanctorum minimo data est gracia in gentibus evangelizare investigabiles divicias gracie Christi et illuminare omnes, que sit dispensacio sacramenti absconditi a seculis in Deo qui omnia creavit, ut innotescat principibus et potestatibus in celestibus per ecclesiam multiformis sapiencia Dei. Ecce quod iste sanctus apostolus in thabernaculo hoc mortali positus illuminavit cives ierarchie celestis et indubie expurgavit Petrum ac perfecit sanctam matrem ecclesiam, sicut quilibet christianus debet facere et specialiter ierarchia ecclesie militantis. Non enim est distinccio primatuum quoad illud, sed quodcunque membrum ecclesie secundum datum sibi lumen intelligencie tenetur communicare tres actus ierarchicos membris singulis matris sue; sicut enim lux corporalis illuminat intellectum, sic membra Christi viancia [p. 396] illuminant beatos in patria, dum faciunt eos cognoscere noticia in proprio genere fructus lucis. Unde non est credendum quod in triumphante ecclesia remanent sordes peccati purgandi (ut docetur Ephesiorum VI°), sed purgacio est expulsionis sordium continuacio, sic quod intellectus et affectus civium illuminantur et purgantur per obiectivam presentacionem docium suorum concivium. Et ex istis sequitur quod multitudo civium beatorum sicut et plena eorum beatitudo per actus suos coalternos perficitur, ymmo cum verbum sit in medio omnium membrorum suorum omnibus diebus usque ad consummacionem seculi, non dubium quin cives celestes vident in ipso et audiunt quicquid conservi sui faciunt ad edificacionem ecclesie. Sic enim unum membrum corporis sentit iuvamentum et nocumentum alterius virtute sensus capitis. Hec ergo septiplex ecclesia alloquitur sponsum Christum qui est vir, de quo Jeremie XXXI°, 22, scribitur quod femina circumdabit virum; ipsum autem apprehendit modo novo in sponsum, quando accepit sacramenta tamquam cartas sue redempcionis in effluxu sanguinis et aque in sui latere dormientis in cruce (ut notat Apostolus Ephes. VI°). Et sic hec septiplex ecclesia ante incarnacionem rogat salvatorem, factum hominem, ut auferat obprobrium sue sterilitatis. Panem, inquiunt, nostrum comedemus, quia in verbis legis que in fidem unius Dei instruunt convivemus, nam Hebreorum X°, 38, scribitur: Iustus ex fide vivit, et vestimentis nostris operiemur, quia ex fide creacionis et spe recreacionis cognoscimur quales sumus. Et isto modo loquitur Apostolus, Galat. III°, 27: Omnes qui in Christo baptizati estis Christum induistis. Membra enim Christi non sunt nuda virtutibus, sed induunt armaturam suam saltem in spe, imitando ipsum imperfecte, si [p. 397] sunt de suo exercitu. Sic ergo panem et vestimenta habuerunt patres veteris testamenti, sed sicut non in solo pane vivit homo (ut dicitur Matthei IV°, 4), sic spes confunderet relinquereturque tota vita obprobriosa et sterilis nisi caritas consummans ut potus et edulium data fuerit per dominum Jesum Christum; aliter enim non deleretur peccatum humani generis, nec procrearetur operacio vitalis digna beatitudine, sed remaneret in singulis viatoribus sempiternum obprobrium ut dampnatis. Aufer, inquiunt, obprobrium nostrum, quia remanebimus perpetuo tamquam abortivi vel colobon, nisi tu dans vestem nupcialem et domicilium fueris incarnatus. Nam victus et vestitus non sufficiunt homini sine ultimo stabili tegumento quod est caritas patrie non excidens, data per dominum Jesum Christum. Invocetur ergo super nos nomen Christi, ut dicamur actualiter christiani. Unde istam deprecacionem dicit Augustinus redundare in ecclesiam triumphantem; quamquam, inquit, spirituales sint et celestes potencie, obprobrio tamen subiacent, si a creatore suo fuerint aliene et nec membra erunt corporis que caput suum non cognoscentes oberrant. Vult dicere quod toti ecclesie beatitudo deficeret nisi Christus merito sue passionis redempcionem hominis consummasset. Sed hic videtur dubium: Si patres veteris testamenti fuerant christiani, et videtur Augustinum in ista sentencia contradicere sibi ipsi. Sed notandum quod homines possunt dici tripliciter christiani, scilicet in figura, in effectu et in fructu. In figura vero dicti sunt omnes sancti legis veteris christiani. Si enim dieta dicitur sana propter sanitatem futuram cuius [p. 398] est effectiva, quanto magis patriarche dicuntur pro suo

Hence in *Of Questions of the Old and New Law*, chapter 47, Augustine, expounding that of Isaiah 4:1, "And in that day seven women shall take hold of one man, saying: We will eat our own bread, and wear our own apparel: only let us be called by thy name, take away our reproach," says first that the prophet speaks of the day of grace and of the sevenfold woman, namely [the church]{g:ecclesia}, which is the [valiant woman]{g:mulier-fortis} [395] of whom Proverbs 31 [speaks]: "By the seven women," he says, "I say that seven churches are signified, which, though it is one, is yet called sevenfold, as one body consists of a sevenfold number of members."[^1] "Not only we," he says, "but also the citizens above are [members]{g:membrum} of the church, whose [head]{g:caput} is Christ. Whether therefore the churches are called one or seven, it makes no difference, because the whole totality of the churches, which is seven churches on account of the seven gifts of the Holy Spirit, and all together are one body of Christ, from whom all things in heaven and on earth have their origin. Hence in this militant church mention is made of all the churches, because when the mystery of the creator is preached to it, the teaching is conveyed to the heavenly ones too. For when it is conveyed to the lower, it is necessary that those above hear it too."[^2] Hence the Apostle, Ephesians 3: "To me, the least of all the saints, is given grace to preach among the Gentiles the unsearchable riches of the grace of Christ, and to enlighten all men, what is the dispensation of the mystery which hath been hidden from eternity in God who created all things, that the manifold wisdom of God may be made known to the princes and powers in heavenly places through the church."[^3] See that this holy apostle, placed in this mortal tabernacle, enlightened the citizens of the heavenly hierarchy, and without doubt purged Peter and perfected holy mother church, as every Christian ought to do, and especially the hierarchy of the [church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans}. For there is no distinction of primacies as regards that; but every member of the church, according to the light of understanding given to it, is bound to communicate the three hierarchical acts to the several members of its mother; for as bodily light illuminates the understanding, so the members of Christ on the way [396] illuminate the blessed in [the homeland]{g:patria}, while they make them know, by a knowledge in its own kind, the fruits of light.[^4] Hence it is not to be believed that in the triumphant church there remain stains of sin to be purged (as is taught in Ephesians 6); but purgation is the continuation of the expulsion of stains, such that the intellect and affection of the citizens are illuminated and purged by the objective presentation of the dowries of their fellow citizens.[^5] And from these it follows that the multitude of the blessed citizens, as also their full blessedness, is perfected by their mutual acts; indeed, since the Word is in the midst of all his members all days, even to the consummation of the world, there is no doubt that the heavenly citizens see in him and hear whatever their fellow servants do for the edification of the church. For thus one member of the body feels the help and the harm of another by virtue of the sense of the head.[^6] This sevenfold church therefore addresses Christ the [bridegroom]{g:sponsus}, who is the man of whom it is written in Jeremiah 31:22 that "a woman shall compass a man"; and she took hold of him in a new way as bridegroom when she received the sacraments as the charters of her redemption, in the outflow of blood and water in the side of him sleeping on the cross (as the Apostle notes, Ephesians 6).[^7] And so this sevenfold church before the incarnation asks the saviour, made man, to take away the reproach of her barrenness. "We will eat our own bread," they say, because we shall live together on the words of the law which instruct us in the faith of one God; for in Hebrews 10:38 it is written: "The just liveth by faith"; "and wear our own apparel," because by faith in the creation and hope of the re-creation we are known for what we are. And in this way the Apostle speaks, Galatians 3:27: "All you who have been baptized in Christ have put on Christ." For the members of Christ are not naked of virtues, but put on his armour, at least in hope, imitating him imperfectly, if [397] they are of his army.[^8] Thus therefore the fathers of the old testament had bread and clothing; but as "not in bread alone doth man live" (as is said in Matthew 4:4), so hope would be confounded, and the whole of life would be left reproachful and barren, unless consummating [charity]{g:caritas}, as drink and food, had been given through the Lord Jesus Christ; for otherwise the sin of the human race would not be blotted out, nor would a vital activity worthy of blessedness be brought forth, but an everlasting reproach would remain in each [wayfarer]{g:viator}, as in the damned. "Take away," they say, "our reproach," because we shall remain forever as abortive or maimed, unless you, giving the wedding garment and dwelling, have become incarnate. For food and clothing do not suffice for man without the last stable covering, which is the charity of the homeland that does not fall away, given through the Lord Jesus Christ. Let the name of Christ therefore be called upon us, that we may be called Christians in act.[^9] Hence Augustine says that this petition redounds to the church triumphant: "although," he says, "they are spiritual and heavenly powers, they are yet subject to reproach if they are estranged from their creator; and they will not be members of the body who wander astray, not knowing their head." He means that blessedness would be lacking to the whole church unless Christ, by the merit of his passion, had consummated the redemption of man.[^10] But here a doubt appears: whether the fathers of the old testament were Christians; and Augustine seems in this [teaching]{g:sentencia} to contradict himself. But it must be noted that men can be called Christians in three ways, namely in figure, in effect and in fruit. In figure, indeed, all the saints of the old law were called Christians. For if a diet is called healthy on account of the future health of which [398] it is productive, how much more are the patriarchs called[^11]

## ¶2 · 398.2–398.12

> [p. 398] tempore christiani, cum Christus eos denominat membra sua? Christus enim manet in eternum secundum naturam divinam et creatus est a principio mundi nedum in causis secundis communibus, sed in materiali essencia singulari; non tamen assumpsit illam ypostatice, antequam assumens animam fuit homo. In actu autem secundum nomen expressum Christi dicebantur tempore gracie populi christiani et talem nominis denominacionem invocant patres legis veteris super illos; quod et habuerunt, antequam ducti sunt cum Christo ad celum. Sed post diem iudicii habebunt

Christians for their time, since Christ names them his members? For Christ abides forever according to the divine nature, and was created from the beginning of the world, not only in the common secondary causes but in the singular material essence; yet he did not assume it hypostatically before he was man, assuming a soul.[^12] But in act, by the express name of Christ, the Christian peoples were so called in the time of grace; and the fathers of the old law invoke such a naming upon themselves; which they also had before they were led with Christ to heaven. ## ¶4 · 398.17–398.26

> [p. 398] Secunda responsio ad secundam raciunculam negat consequenciam; sed videtur, si nulla sit ecclesia nisi multitudo christianorum, sed Christus non est christianus, tunc Christus non est pars vel membrum ecclesie. Concedit itaque quod Christus Deus noster est christianus, nec sequitur, inquit, si a Christo dicitur quilibet christianus, quod Christus ille non sit christianus: sic a creatore dicitur quelibet creatura, Christus est creator et sic ab eo dicitur quelibet creatura et tamen ipsemet est creatura, ut ego assero.

The second reply, to the second little argument, denies [the inference]{g:consequencia}; but it seems [to hold]: if there is no church except a multitude of Christians, now Christ is not a Christian, then Christ is not a part or member of the church. He therefore grants that Christ our God is a Christian; nor does it follow, he says, if every Christian is named from Christ, that that Christ is not a Christian: so every creature is named from the creator; Christ is the creator, and so every creature is named from him, and yet he himself is a creature, as I assert.[^15]

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** *De questionibus veteris et novi testamenti*, ascribed to Augustine (Loserth: *Quaestiones ex veteri test.* cap. 47, *Opp.* III, Appendix, p. 60; LEDGER E17c-1, to be checked). Isaiah 4:1 as the Vulgate; Proverbs 31:10, the valiant woman. **R.** A and B in the margin at 394.25–26: *Apprehendent septem mulieres virum de ecclesia intelligitur*. **T.** At 395.2 the manuscripts read *signa...as*; Loserth corrected to *significatas* from Augustine's text; his correction is translated.

[^2]: **C.** We take the quotation to run from *Non solum* to *superni*; *Unde Apostolus* is Wyclif's own. *Tota universitas ... et simul omnes unum corpus sunt*: the sentence changes construction mid-way (the singular subject takes a plural verb with *omnes*); translated as it stands. **R.** A in the margin: *Que sunt septem ecclesie designate per septem mulieres et que sunt septem membra quibus corpus constituitur*.

[^3]: **S.** Ephesians 3:8–10. Wyclif omits *hec* ("this grace"), adds *gracie* ("of the grace of Christ") and reads *principibus*, "princes", for the Vulgate's *principatibus*, "principalities"; his Latin is translated.

[^4]: **C.** *Expurgavit Petrum*: Paul's rebuke of Peter at Antioch (Galatians 2:11–14). The three hierarchical acts are purging, illuminating and perfecting, the Dionysian triad of *The Celestial Hierarchy* 3.2 (PL 122:1045B) (LEDGER E17c-2). *Fructus lucis*: Ephesians 5:9, "the fruit of the light". **R.** A in the margin at 395.31: *Sicut enim lux corporalis illuminat intellectum, sic membra Christi viancia illuminant beatos in patria*; B: *Viatores illuminant beatos in patria*. **G.** *Viancia*: "on the way" (GLOSSARY *viare; vians*).

[^5]: **S.** "Ephesians 6" is Wyclif's reference; the church "not having spot or wrinkle" is Ephesians 5:27 in the Clementine numbering. **C.** *Docium* is taken as *dotium*, the "dowries" (*dotes*) of the blessed, the gifts of glory; "of the learned" (*doctium*) would also construe, less well.

[^6]: **T.** At 396.9 B omits *Et*. **S.** *Omnibus diebus usque ad consummacionem seculi*: Matthew 28:20, joined to the Word "in the midst" of his members. **C.** *Coalternos*: "mutual", acts given and received among the citizens.

[^7]: **T.** At 396.19 the manuscripts read *Jer. XXI*; Loserth corrected to *XXXI*; his correction is translated. **S.** The blood and water: John 19:34. "Ephesians 6" is Wyclif's reference; the church as bride is Ephesians 5:25–32. **C.** *Cartas*: "charters", the legal deeds of her redemption; the sleeping Christ answers to Adam, from whose side Eve was taken.

[^8]: **S.** Hebrews 10:38: Wyclif reads *Iustus ex fide vivit* for the Vulgate's *Iustus autem meus ex fide vivit* (DR "But my just man liveth by faith"); Galatians 3:27: *Omnes qui* for *Quicumque enim* (DR "For as many of you as have been baptized in Christ have put on Christ"). His Latin is translated in both. **G.** *Ex fide* is here governed by the verb ("liveth by faith"; "by faith in the creation"), not the formula. **C.** *Convivemus*: "we shall live together on", from *convivere*; *in fidem*: "in the faith".

[^9]: **R.** A in the margin at 397.11: *Passio Christi perfecit nos et omnia*. **C.** *Colobon*: Greek κολοβόν, "maimed, cut short". *Caritas patrie non excidens*: 1 Corinthians 13:8, "Charity never falleth away". *Actualiter*: "in act", the second of the three ways named below.

[^10]: **S.** The same work of Pseudo-Augustine, cap. 47 (Loserth: *l. c.*; LEDGER E17c-1). **R.** A in the margin at 397.18: *Augustinus*. **C.** *Que ... oberrant*: the feminine relative agrees with *potencie*.

[^11]: **R.** A and B in the margin at 397.26: *Dubium*; A at 397.29: *Nota quod homines dicuntur tripliciter christiani*. **C.** *Si ... fuerant*: indirect question, "whether". The diet is the stock example of an analogical name: called healthy as productive of health.

[^12]: **R.** B in the margin at 398.4: *Christus fuit creatus a principio mundi*. **C.** Christ's body was "created" from the beginning in its matter, but united hypostatically only at the incarnation.

[^13]: **T.** At 398.9 B reads *docebantur*. **C.** *Super illos*: literally "upon them"; taken as reflexive, after Isaiah's *super nos*. *Ducti sunt cum Christo ad celum*: at the ascension. **R.** A in the margin: *Utrum patres veteris testamenti fuerant christiani*.

[^14]: **C.** *Prima raciuncula*: the first of the opponent's arguments set out at the head of the chapter (17-a); Wyclif judges it unanswered by the doctor's own glosses.

[^15]: **R.** A in the margin at 398.17: *Secunda responsio*; at 398.31: *Cristus non est cristianus*. **S.** Loserth compares p. 112.30 (LEDGER E17c-3). **C.** *Sed videtur ... sed Christus non est christianus*: the first *sed* introduces the argument the reply must meet, the second its minor premiss. *Ut ego assero* is the doctor's own first person.
