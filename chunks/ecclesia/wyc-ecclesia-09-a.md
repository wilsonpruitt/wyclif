# *De ecclesia*, chapter 9, part 1: privilege is chiefly in spiritual gifts; exemptions and earthly privileges

## ¶1 · 182.7–182.10

> [p. 182] Sed contra dicta arguitur sic: Videtur enim primo quod equivocatur in nomine privilegii, cum communitas non vocat privilegium nisi excellenciam quam habet una persona super aliam quoad mundum.

But against what has been said it is argued thus. For it seems, first, that there is an equivocation in the name "[privilege]{g:privilegium}", since the community calls nothing a privilege except an excellence which one person has over another as regards the world.[^1]

## ¶2 · 182.11–183.33

> [p. 182] Hic dicitur quod illud assumptum indicat sic vocantes terrena diligere, et despicere vel odire celestia, quia voluntas trahit intellectum ad affectuosius cogitandum et nominandum rem cui magis infigitur: ad quem sensum dicitur Joh. III°, 31: Qui de terra est de terra loquitur. Et hinc apostoli et sancti priores reputarunt ista terrena ut stercora, sicut patet in dictis et factis eorum et exprimit Apostolus Philipp. III° cap. Ergo quod communitas sic loquitur et attendit, non nisi accusat ipsam perversam a Deo et verificat dictum Evangeliste Ia Johannis V°, 19: Totus mundus in maligno positus est et dictum Christi Matthei XII°, 34: Ex habundancia cordis os loquitur. Ideo debemus attendere qualiter secundum regulas fidei est loquendum: cum autem privilegium sonat in maioritatem prerogativam vel preeminenciam creature, manifestum est quod partes ecclesie tam militantis quam triumphantis sunt privilegiate a Deo. Ideo dicunt sancti doctores communiter quod Christus et mater sua habuerunt singularia privilegia super alios, et ita de apostolis et aliis beatis, quilibet habet dispariter [p. 183] donum suum, ut manifestavit Apostolus. Iª Cor. XII°, 4, dicens quod divisiones graciarum sunt, Notet ergo fidelis racionem privilegii et videbit quod principaliter sit in donis spiritualibus a Deo appropriate collatis, unde, si argumentum factum moveret, diceret consequenter quod nulla est lex, possessio vel dominium nisi de bonis temporalibus, quia de illis principaliter vel precise loquitur communitas, in tantum quod statim audito de bonis, applicant ad mundi divicias; et locuto de episcopatu, rectoria vel alio nomine prelacie, statim applicant ad dotem temporalem dimissa consideracione de officio; et ita de aliis. Non sic autem in primitiva ecclesia vel scriptura sacra; ideo postponendo viciosam consuetudinem debemus loqui conformiter sanctis doctoribus. Non enim reperi rem in scriptura sacra vocatam sub nomine privilegii, sed doctores et usus ecclesie vocant eam sic communiter, ut patet de sancto Evangelista, de sancto Petro et Romana ecclesia. Et patet quod innitendo pure modo loquendi vulgari totus conventus Christi non foret privilegiatus sub aliquo gradu, nisi forte fingatur quod Scarioth fuit privilegiatus in hoc quod Deus permisit eum portare loculos, et sic introductum esset privilegium in ecclesiam cum superbia ex contencione circa maioritatem et secularem obedienciam quam Christus nitebatur in suis destruere. Non ergo concedendum est quod solus dominus temporalis privilegiavit, edificavit vel dotavit ecclesiam, cum ecclesia canit quod Christus privilegio amoris Johannem dilexit apostolum; et idem est de quolibet singulari dono Dei, ut patet ex descripcione privilegii. Unde non expressius posset quis ostendere se iuste perditurum privilegium per abusum quam negando Christi privilegia vel preponderando privilegia terrena.

To this the answer is that that [assumption]{g:assumptum} shows that those who speak so love earthly things and despise or hate heavenly ones; for the will draws the intellect to think more affectionately of, and to name, the thing in which it is more deeply fixed. In this sense it is said, John 3:31: "He that is of the earth, of the earth he speaketh." And hence the apostles and the earlier saints reckoned these earthly things "as dung", as is clear in their words and deeds, and as the Apostle expresses, Philippians 3.[^2] Therefore, that the community speaks and attends in this way only accuses it of being turned away from God, and verifies the saying of the Evangelist, 1 John 5:19: "The whole world is seated in wickedness," and the saying of Christ, Matthew 12:34: "Out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh." So we ought to attend to how one is to speak according to the rules of faith. Now since "privilege" sounds of the [greatness]{g:majoritas}, prerogative or pre-eminence of a creature, it is manifest that the parts of the church, both of the [church militant]{g:ecclesia militans} and of the triumphant, are privileged by God.[^3] So the holy doctors commonly say that Christ and his mother had singular privileges above others, and so of the apostles and the other blessed: each has, unequally, [183] his own gift, as the Apostle showed, 1 Corinthians 12:4, saying that "there are diversities of graces." Let the faithful man therefore note the meaning of privilege, and he will see that it lies principally in spiritual gifts conferred by God as proper [to each];[^4] hence, if the argument made had force, it would say consequently that there is no law, possession or [lordship]{g:dominium} except of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, because the community speaks principally or exclusively of those, so much so that as soon as "goods" is heard they apply it to the riches of the world; and when a bishopric, a rectory or another name of prelacy is spoken of, they at once apply it to the temporal endowment, setting aside consideration of the office; and so of other things.[^5] But it is not so in the primitive [church]{g:ecclesia} or in holy scripture; so, setting the vicious [custom]{g:consuetudo} behind us, we ought to speak in conformity with the holy doctors. For I have not found a thing called by the name "privilege" in holy scripture; but the doctors and the usage of the church commonly call it so, as is clear of the holy Evangelist, of St Peter and of the Roman church. And it is clear that, relying purely on the common way of speaking, the whole company of Christ would not be privileged in any degree, unless perhaps it is feigned that Iscariot was privileged in this, that God permitted him to carry the purse; and so privilege would have been brought into the church with pride, out of strife about greatness and secular obedience, which Christ strove to destroy in his own.[^6] It is not to be granted, therefore, that the [temporal lord]{g:temporales domini} alone privileged, built or endowed the church, since the church sings that Christ loved John the apostle with the privilege of love; and the same holds of any singular gift of God, as is clear from the [definition]{g:descripcio} of privilege.[^7] Hence one could not show more expressly that one will justly lose privilege through abuse than by denying the privileges of Christ or by preferring earthly privileges.

## ¶3 · 184.1–186.13

> [p. 184] Secundo videtur quod non licet clero insistere circa privilegia terrena cuiusmodi videntur esse exempciones, prerogative, proprietates et seculares dominaciones, cum videntur repugnare privilegiis Christi que non licet amittere. Hic dicitur, primo quantum ad exempciones quod, licet videantur esse male de genere, tamen contingit subditum bene uti illis sicut et aliis actibus malis de genere; est enim contra regulas ecclesie triumphantis quod quis sit exemptus ne superior illuminet inferiorem, et ita contra regulas nature est quod subditus sit exemptus, ne ducatur vel corrigatur per superiorem in casu quo deviet vel delinquat. Unde videtur idem inpetrare oppositum et luciferine querere licenciam ad peccandum; diaboli enim exempti sunt a tali regimine et (ut ego credo) a privilegiacione, quia nulla excellencia quam habent cedit dampnatis ad bonum utile, sed econtra beati sunt summe privilegiati, in tantum quod nullum privilegium vie valet homini, nisi de quanto preparat ad querendum privilegium regni. Quia tamen contingit prepositos ecclesie esse ceciores ducendis vel inniti in visitacione fastui vel questui plus quam correccioni, ideo expedit per accidens a talibus temporalis exempcio. Non autem debet talis esse perpetua nisi cognito quod perpetuo erit prepositus deses, inscius vel avarus, quia aliter (ut recitat Lincolniensis) hoc inconveniens sequeretur quod quantumlibet bonus pastor videns exemptum strangulare oves, pro quibus reddet racionem in die iudicii, non audebit propter exempcionem pape exagitare vel exterrere lupos mordentes tam nequiter oves suas. Hoc autem non potest dici privilegium sed rete diabolicum. Unde privilegium foret eximi a iurisdiccione talis pape, si fuerit qui [p. 185] propter pecuniam et casum particularem, in minori parte eciam ob defectum sui contingentem, statuerit exempcionem talem ut legem perpetuam. Nam diabolus et membra sua seducendo populum ab observancia legis Christi videntur inducere exempcionem huiusmodi, ne inferior fruatur suffragio sui prepositi, ut innuit Augustinus libro secundo De Ordine. Et quantum ad alia insignia perquisita de portando mitram, crucem et baculum ac ordinando clericos extensius vel striccius, cum aliis vocatis privilegiis istis similibus, cum ut plurimum sapiunt fastum et non commodum, sunt sepe non privilegia sed diabolica sagena quibus pisces natantes in mari huius seculi capiuntur; et sic dicendum videtur de privilegiis terrenis seculariter impetratis. Unde Augustinus in libro De Agone Christiano, cap. IV: Gaudet piscis quando hamum non videns escam devorat, sed piscator cum eum ceperit, torquentur eius viscera primo, deinde ab omni leticia sua per ipsam escam de qua letatus est trahitur ad consumacionem. Sic sunt omnes qui de bonis temporalibus beatos se putant unde bonis nichil nocet ablacio temporalium, cum ipsa non diligunt. Unde inter omnes cautelas diaboli mundana preeminencia est prima sagena diaboli; de illa enim primo temptavit Christum. Nam Matthei IV°, 8, 9, dicitur quod ostendit sibi omnia regna mundi dicens: Hec omnia tibi dabo, si cadens adoraveris me. Et revera propter minus privilegium adoramus sepius membra sua, cum tamen profitemur in baptismo quod abrenunciamus sathane et omnibus pompis eius. Talis itaque finalis preponderancia secularis privilegii est signum infallibile membri diaboli, et sic [p. 186] videtur mihi quod contendentes superbe circa talia privilegia nisi redeant humiliter ad unitatem subiecti invicem, non sunt de sancta matre ecclesia, dicente decreto Pelagii pape XXIV, q. I. Pudenda: Ecclesia, inquit, una est, ut patet in canticis, et illa est que fide formata per orbem diffunditur; quod beati Augustini testimonio comprobemus. Audite quid in quodam opere suo preclarissimus doctor dicat ecclesie: Ait enim quod, cum nullo modo recte potest dici ecclesia in qua scisma est, restat ut, quoniam nulla esse ecclesia non potest, ea sit quam in sedis apostolice per successiones episcoporum radice constitutam nullorum hominum malicia eciamsi nota excludi non possit.

[184] Secondly, it seems that it is not lawful for the [clergy]{g:clerus} to insist on earthly privileges, of which kind seem to be [exemptions]{g:exempcio}, prerogatives, properties and secular [exercises of lordship]{g:dominacio}, since they seem to be repugnant to the privileges of Christ, which it is not lawful to lose. To this the answer is, first, as to exemptions, that although they seem to be bad in kind, yet it happens that a subject uses them well, as also other acts bad in kind; for it is against the rules of the church triumphant that anyone be exempt so that a superior may not enlighten an inferior; and so it is against the rules of nature that a subject be exempt, so as not to be led or corrected by a superior in case he goes astray or offends.[^8] Hence it seems the same thing to obtain the opposite and, like Lucifer, to seek licence to sin; for the devils are exempt from such rule and (as I believe) from privileging, since no excellence which they have turns to a useful good for the damned; but on the contrary the blessed are supremely privileged, so much so that no privilege of the [way]{g:via} avails a man except in so far as it prepares him to seek the privilege of the kingdom.[^9] Since, nevertheless, it happens that the [superiors]{g:prepositi} of the church are blinder than those they are to lead, or in visitation rely on pomp or gain more than on correction, therefore a temporary exemption from such men is expedient by accident. But such [an exemption] ought not to be perpetual, unless it is known that the superior will perpetually be slothful, ignorant or avaricious; because otherwise (as Lincoln relates) this [ill consequence]{g:inconveniens} would follow: that a pastor, however good, seeing the exempt [party] strangling the sheep for which he will render account on the day of judgement, will not dare, because of the exemption of the [pope]{g:papa}, to harry or frighten off the wolves that bite his sheep so wickedly.[^10] And this cannot be called a privilege, but a devilish net. Hence it would be a privilege to be exempted from the jurisdiction of such a pope, if there should be one who, [185] for money and for a particular case, in the lesser part also on account of a defect of his own that befalls, should establish such an exemption as a perpetual law.[^11] For the devil and his [members]{g:membrum}, by seducing the people from the observance of [the law of Christ]{g:lex Christi}, seem to bring in an exemption of this kind, so that the inferior may not enjoy the [aid]{g:suffragium} of his superior, as Augustine hints in the second book De ordine.[^12] And as to the other insignia acquired, of carrying mitre, cross and staff, and of ordaining clerics more widely or more narrowly, with other so-called privileges like these: since [for the most part]{g:ut plurimum} they savour of pomp and not of profit, they are often not privileges but a devilish [dragnet]{g:sagena}, in which the fish swimming in the sea of this world are caught; and so it seems one must say of earthly privileges secularly obtained.[^13] Hence Augustine in the book De agone christiano, chapter 4: "The fish rejoices when, not seeing the hook, it devours the bait; but when the fisherman has caught it, first its entrails are wrenched, then from all its gladness it is drawn to its destruction by the very bait in which it rejoiced." So are all who think themselves blessed from temporal goods. Hence the taking away of temporal goods does the good no harm at all, since they do not love them.[^14] Hence among all the [wiles]{g:cautela} of the devil, worldly pre-eminence is the devil's first dragnet; for with it he first tempted Christ. For in Matthew 4:8–9 it is said that "he shewed him all the kingdoms of the world, saying: 'All these will I give thee, if falling down thou wilt adore me.'" And truly, for a lesser privilege we often adore his members, although we profess in baptism that we renounce Satan and all his pomps.[^15] Such a final preponderance of secular privilege is therefore an infallible sign of a member of the devil; and so [186] it seems to me that those who contend proudly about such privileges, unless they return humbly to unity, subject to one another, are not of holy mother church, as the decree of Pope Pelagius says, C. 24 q. 1 [c. 33] Pudenda: "The church," he says, "is one, as is clear in the Canticles, and it is that which is spread throughout the world by [formed faith]{g:fides formata}; which let us prove by the testimony of blessed Augustine. Hear what the most illustrious doctor says of the church in a certain work of his. For he says that, since that in which there is schism can in no way rightly be called a church, it remains, since it cannot be that there is no church, that it is that which is established in the root of the apostolic see through the successions of [bishops]{g:episcopus}, [and which] the malice of no men [can extinguish], even if [that malice] is notorious and cannot be shut out."[^16]

## ¶4 · 186.14–186.31

> [p. 186] Ex isto notandum, primo quomodo iste papa allegat venerabiliter Augustinum: quod divisi a Christo per invidiam extrudentem caritatem non sunt membra ecclesie. Patet secundo quod, si collegium Romanum sit sic divisum scismate, tunc ipsum non est caput vel pars ecclesie, quia deficit unio apostolica. Unde dicit quod, si talis episcopus consecrat alium, non consecracio sed execracio debet dici. Et tercio notari potest quomodo crescente superbia et cupiditate primatus de loco ecclesie, potest fieri synagoga sathane, cum nemo potest esse Christi vel Petri vicarius, nisi sequatur eos in moribus, ut patet XLª distinccione: Non est facile. Et patet quod licet clero insistere circa privilegia terrena, de quanto promovent ad eterna; quod oportet exemplo sanctorum et fide scripture pensari, nullomodo amittendo privilegia que Christus dedit propter aliquod bonum mundi.

From this one must note, first, how reverently this pope [alleges]{g:allegare} Augustine: that those divided from Christ by envy, which thrusts out [charity]{g:caritas}, are not members of the church. It is clear, secondly, that if the Roman college is so divided by schism, then it is not [head]{g:caput} or part of the church, because apostolic union is lacking. Hence he says that if such a bishop consecrates another, it ought to be called not a consecration but an execration.[^17] And thirdly it can be noted how, as pride and greed for primacy grow, a synagogue of Satan can be made out of the place of the church, since no one can be vicar of Christ or of Peter unless he follows them in manners, as is clear in Distinction 40, Non est facile.[^18] And it is clear that it is lawful for the clergy to insist on earthly privileges in so far as they promote [men] to eternal things; which must be weighed by the example of the saints and by the faith of scripture, in no way losing, for any good of the world, the privileges which Christ gave.[^19]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 182.7: Objection: Privilege is generally understood of worldly things.
- 182.14: If so, it is the fault of the people who use the word.
- 183.5: It might as well be said that law and possession apply only to earthly goods.
- 183.12: Such is not the language of the primitive Church.
- 184.1: The Church ought not to insist on earthly privileges such as exemptions and endowments.
- 184.6: Exemptions are bad in kind, although they may sometimes serve a good purpose.
- 184.27: Good prelates ought not to be hampered by them.
- 185.9: Privileges of wearing mitre etc. are often a snare of the devil.
- 186.15: The Roman college may be the synagogue of Satan and not the head of the Church.

## Notes

[^1]: **T/C.** A margin *Prima obieccio*; A lacks *sic*, B *primo* (182.7). *Communitas*: common usage, the community of speakers (again at 182.17, 183.7). The objection: "privilege" in Wyclif's argument (cap. 8) means something other than what people mean by it, so the comparison of Christ's privilege with Caesar's is equivocal. Side-note 182.7.

[^2]: **S/C.** A margin *Responsio* (182.11). John 3:31: Wyclif quotes only *Qui de terra est de terra loquitur*, leaving out the Vulgate's middle clause *de terra est*; the DR is adapted to his Latin. Philippians 3 (v. 8, *arbitror ut stercora*): Wyclif gives the chapter only. *Rem cui magis infigitur*: the thing in which the will is more deeply fixed. Side-note 182.14.

[^3]: **C/G.** *Accusat ipsam perversam a Deo*: accuses the community as turned away from God. *Sonat in*: "sounds of", i.e. signifies. *Maioritatem*: greatness (GLOSSARY *majoritas*), as in the strife "about greatness" at 183.24. B reads *attendat* (182.19).

[^4]: **T/C.** A margin *Privilegium debet esse in donis spiritualibus* (183.5). *Appropriate collatis*: conferred as proper to each (cf. 08-f, *appropriate* "as his own"), not the canon-law sense of GLOSSARY *appropriare*. *Racionem privilegii*: the meaning (account) of privilege.

[^5]: **C.** *Si argumentum factum moveret*: if the argument made had force; the argument from common usage proves too much, since the same usage restricts "law", "possession" and "lordship" to temporal goods. *Precise*: exclusively. *Rectoria*: a rectory, the benefice. Side-note 183.5.

[^6]: **S/T.** A margin *Conclusio* (183.29) belongs to the next sentence. B lacks *quod* (183.19) and *sub aliquo — privilegiatus* (183.20–22), and reads *suis defervere* (183.26). John 12:6 (Judas "having the purse"). *Ut patet de sancto Evangelista …*: John, Peter and the Roman church are those commonly said to be privileged; John's "privilege of love" follows at 183.30. *Secularem obedienciam*: obedience paid to worldly rank. Side-note 183.12.

[^7]: **S.** "The church sings": the invitatory of Matins of St John the Evangelist, *Adoremus regem apostolorum qui privilegio amoris Johannem dilexit apostolum* (CAO 1013) (LEDGER E09a-1). *Descripcione privilegii*: the definition of privilege given in cap. 8 (08-d).

[^8]: **T/C.** A margins *Secunda obieccio* (184.1) and *Responsio: Nota contra exempciones continue infra* (184.6). *Male de genere*: bad in kind (cf. *bonum de genere*, 08-f). *Ne superior illuminet inferiorem*: the order of the church triumphant by which the higher enlightens the lower; Wyclif names no source. *Proprietates*: properties, holdings owned. Side-notes 184.1, 184.6.

[^9]: **C.** *Idem inpetrare oppositum*: we take *oppositum* as the opposite of being led and corrected, i.e. the exemption itself: to obtain it is the same as to seek licence to sin. *Luciferine*: like Lucifer, who would not be subject. *Privilegiacione*: the act of privileging (as 08-f *privilegiacio*). *Privilegium vie / regni*: the privilege of the way against that of the kingdom (cf. GLOSSARY *via / patria*).

[^10]: **T/S/C.** B lacks *Quia tamen — exempcio* (184.22–24). *Temporalis exempcio*: a temporary exemption, against *perpetua*, not a "temporal" one. "Lincoln" (A margin *Lincolniensis*, 184.26) is Robert Grosseteste, bishop of Lincoln; Loserth gives no reference: Grosseteste's *Sermo* at Lyon, 1250 (Brown, *Fasciculus rerum expetendarum* II, 1690, p. 255: pastors see their sheep *a lupis … strangulari*, and *per exemptorum privilegia sic obstruuntur pastorum ora*), recited in sense (LEDGER E09a-2). *Inconveniens*: GLOSSARY CONTEXTUAL (an absurdity in argument; ill consequences in affairs). Here it is the harm that follows in the church's government, hence 'ill consequence'. *Exemptum*: the exempt person or house. B lacks *potest* (184.31). Side-note 184.27.

[^11]: **C.** *In minori parte eciam ob defectum sui contingentem*: obscure. We take it as a further aggravation of the pope's act (made for money, for one case, and in part because of a failing of his own); *sui* may instead refer to the case. *Talis pape*: a pope who sells perpetual exemptions.

[^12]: **S/G.** Augustine, *De ordine* II; Loserth's reference reads "cap. XVIII (?) vid. Opp. I", with the page uncertain (LEDGER E09a-3). *Suffragio*: aid (GLOSSARY *suffragium*), not the church's suffrages.

[^13]: **T.** A margin *Augustinus* (185.15). *Ordinando clericos extensius vel striccius*: privileges of conferring orders beyond or short of the ordinary bounds. *Sagena*: a dragnet. Side-note 185.9.

[^14]: **S/T/C.** Augustine, *De agone christiano* 4 (Loserth: "Opp. VI, 245"; LEDGER E09a-4). A has an erasure at *de agone* (185.16); A₁ is corrected to *torquet* (185.18). The passage is cap. 7 (PL 40:295), not 4: Augustine has *Sic sunt omnes qui de bonis temporalibus beatos se putant* (roman in the print), and *unde … diligunt* paraphrases his next sentence, *Et ideo bonis nihil nocent; quia hoc eis auferunt quod non diligunt* (*bonis*: the good).

[^15]: **S/T.** A margin *Mundana preeminencia est sagena diaboli* (185.21). Matthew 4:8–9: v. 8 is abridged and v. 9 quoted; the print italicises both. The baptismal renunciation of Satan "and all his pomps": the rite asks *Abrenuntias Satanae? … Et omnibus operibus ejus? … Et omnibus pompis ejus?* (Gelasian Sacramentary, PL 74:1106B); Wyclif leaves out the works (LEDGER E09a-5). *Membra sua*: the devil's members.

[^16]: **S/C/T.** A and A₁ margin *Membrum diaboli* (185.32); A margin *Conclusio* (186.2). Decretum C. 24 q. 1 c. 33 *Pudenda* (Pelagius; the modern form is ours; Loserth: "a few words having been altered"; LEDGER E09a-6). "The Canticles": cf. Song 6:8 "One is my dove". The canon is Friedberg I, 979–80 (*Pudenda … non consecratus, sed execratus episcopus*); the work of Augustine quoted inside it is unnamed and untraced (LEDGER E09a-7). *Fide formata*: Wyclif's compression; the canon has *in apostolica … radice fundata … fidem universo orbe propagatam*. The last clause is truncated: the canon reads *nulla hominum malitia, etiamsi nota et excludi non possit, sed pro temporum ratione toleranda iudicetur, extinguere* [valeat], so *excludi non possit* describes the malice, and we supply the canon's verb, "extinguish" (our supply). The print, as Wyclif quotes it, can also be construed without it: 'that which, established in the root of the apostolic see through the successions of bishops, cannot be shut out by the malice of any men, even if [that malice] is notorious', taking *quam … excludi non possit* loosely, with *malicia* as agent.

[^17]: **C/R.** *Iste papa*: Pelagius. *Divisi … caritatem*: Wyclif's summary of the canon, not a quotation. *Collegium Romanum*: the college of cardinals, written as the Schism of 1378 opened. *Dicit*: the canon; the canon has *Iure ergo execratus tantum, non consecratus poterit dici* (Friedberg I, 980; LEDGER E09a-8). Side-note 186.15.

[^18]: **S.** "Synagogue of Satan": Revelation 2:9. *Non est facile*: Decretum D. 40 c. 2 (our identification, from memory; Loserth gives no reference; LEDGER E09a-9). *In moribus*: in manners, in way of life.

[^19]: **C.** This closes the answer to the second objection (184.1). *De quanto*: in so far as. *Fide scripture* has no *ex* and is not the GLOSSARY formula. *Propter aliquod bonum mundi* goes with *amittendo*.
