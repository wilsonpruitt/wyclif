# *De ecclesia*, chapter 12, part 1: the charge of destroying the church's privileges; God cannot take away without recompense

## ¶1 · 250.18–252.25

> [p. 250] Ex istis colligi potest solucio tam vulgarium obiectuum quam scolasticorum quos quidam seminant, quando deficiunt eis efficacia argumenta. Sic enim declinant quidam ad commenta mendacii, dicentes quod nos nitimur destruere privilegia sancte matris ecclesie, unde et commovent milites et alios quos credunt ex sperato lucro defensioni privilegii predicti intendere quomodo defenderent ipsum, destruendo eius adversarios, sub obtentu eterni premii ex operibus pietatis. [p. 251] Sed tales attenderent ad scolam et fructum mendacii, Nam XXIIª q. ultima, ex auctoritate beati Ambrosii sic habetur; Cavete fratres mendacium, quia omnes qui ipsum amant filii diaboli sunt, Nec solum in falsis verbis sed eciam in simulatis operibus mendacium est. Mendacium quippe est christianum se dicere et opera Christi non facere. Mendacium est episcopum, sacerdotem vel clericum se profiteri et contraria huic ordini operari. Et tales leges ecclesie predicari debent tam clericis maioribus quam minoribus, cum sint precipua lex eorum, nec non et laicis, cum pater mendacii cum suis sophistis infecit mendaciis magnam partem vocate ecclesie. Nam pretendentes se esse sacerdotes vel clericos et non facientes quod incumbit illi officio, falso nomine, mendacio et ypocrisi decipiunt plebem Dei. Debent enim exproprietarie vivere tamquam humiles et utiles procuratores pauperum, quorum bona custodiunt. Unde beatus Ysidorus, III° libro, De Summo Bono c. XLII°: Agnoscat, inquit, episcopus servum se esse plebis, non dominum. Verum hoc caritas, non condicio exigit. Et XVIª, q. I, sub auctoritate Jeronymi Damaso pape scribitur quod quicquid habent clerici pauperum est. Pretendere ergo dominacionem secularem eciam plus quam laici super tam alienis bonis pauperum est horrendum mendacium. Non ergo debet fidelis propter talia mendacia non predicare populo periculum ex declinacione [p. 252] a lege domini, quia ut dicitur XIª, q. IIIª, inter verba: Sufficit christiano invocare in testimonium operis purgatam conscienciam atque Deum. Habemus, inquit Gregorius, Paulum dicentem: Gloria nostra hec est, testimonium consciencie nostre. Job quoque dicit: Ecce in celo testis meus. Si, inquit, est nobis testis in celo, testis in corde, dimittamus stultos foris loqui quod volunt. Quid enim aliud detrahentes faciunt nisi quod in pulverem sufflant et in oculos suos terram excitant, ut unde plus detraccionis perflant, inde magis, nichil veritatis videant. Si, inquam, publicarem istam sentenciam quam predico principaliter propter lucrum seculi vel laudem hominum, ego essem me ipso stulcior, lavans laterem, quia scio quod fama vel favor populi est valde flexibilis et vertibilis propter timorem, favorem vel odium instar venti. Sed cum veritas domini manet semper, non movet dicta infidelis commocio fidelem servum Christi, ut omnino taceat, quia Act. XVII° et XIX° legitur quomodo synagoga Judeorum et principes sacerdotum concitaverunt vulgum, Demetrium et artifices facientes edes argenteas Diane, ex quibus quesierunt questum non modicum, commovere populum clamare constanter: Magna Diana Ephesiorum, sed post conversi cognoverunt quod doctrina apostoli fuit eis utilior.

From these things can be gathered the solution both of the popular objections and of the scholastic ones which certain people sow when effective arguments fail them. For thus certain people decline to the fictions of falsehood, saying that we are striving to destroy the [privileges]{g:privilegium} of holy mother [church]{g:ecclesia}; and hence they also stir up [knights]{g:milites} and others whom they believe, from hope of gain, to be intent on the defence of the said privilege, [as to] how they should defend it, by destroying its adversaries, under pretext of an eternal reward for works of piety.[^1] [251] But such people should attend to the school and the fruit of falsehood. For in C. 22 q. 5 [the last question], on the authority of blessed Ambrose, it is had thus: "Beware of falsehood, brethren, for all who love it are sons of the devil. Nor is there falsehood only in false words, but also in feigned works. For it is a falsehood to call oneself a Christian and not to do the works of Christ. It is a falsehood to profess oneself a bishop, priest or cleric and to work things contrary to this order."[^2] And such laws of the church ought to be preached both to the greater clerics and to the lesser, since they are their chief law, and also to the laity, since the father of lies with his sophists has infected a great part of the [so-called]{g:vocatus} church with falsehoods. For those who pretend to be priests or clerics and do not do what belongs to that office deceive the people of God by a false name, by falsehood and by hypocrisy. For they ought to live [without property]{g:exproprietarie}, as humble and useful [proctors]{g:procurator} of the poor, whose goods they keep. Hence blessed Isidore, in book 3 of *De summo bono*, chapter 42: "Let the bishop recognize," he says, "that he is the servant of the people, not the lord. But [charity]{g:caritas}, not status, requires this."[^3] And in C. 16 q. 1, under the authority of Jerome, it is written to Pope Damasus that whatever clerics have belongs to the poor. To claim, then, secular [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio}, even more than the laity, over goods so much another's, the goods of the poor, is a horrible falsehood.[^4] Therefore the faithful man ought not, because of such falsehoods, to fail to preach to the people the danger from turning aside [252] from the law of the Lord; because, as is said in C. 11 q. 3 [c. 55], *Inter verba*: "It is enough for a Christian to call a cleansed conscience and God to witness of his work. 'We have,' says Gregory, 'Paul saying: For our glory is this, the testimony of our conscience. Job also says: Behold my witness is in heaven. If,' he says, 'we have a witness in heaven, a witness in the heart, let us leave the foolish to say outside what they will. For what else do detractors do but blow into the dust and raise earth into their own eyes, so that the more they blow out detraction, the more they see nothing of the truth?'"[^5] If, I say, I published this [teaching]{g:sentencia} which I preach chiefly for the world's gain or the praise of men, I should be more foolish than myself, washing a brick; for I know that the fame or favour of the people is very pliable and changeable, through fear, favour or hatred, like the wind.[^6] But since the truth of the Lord remains for ever, the commotion of the unbeliever does not move the faithful servant of Christ to be wholly silent about what has been said; for in Acts 17 and 19 it is read how the synagogue of the Jews and the chief priests stirred up the crowd, [and] Demetrius and the craftsmen who made silver temples for Diana, from which they sought no small gain, stirred up the people to cry out steadily, "Great is Diana of the Ephesians!"; but afterwards, converted, they recognized that the apostle's [doctrine]{g:doctrina} was more useful to them.[^7]

## ¶2 · 252.26–253.7

> [p. 252] Detestamur autem occisionem hominum nedum in Westmonasterio sed altrinsecus inter regna, sed distinguimus inter extraccionem pro tutela regni, et castigacione hominum salubri hominum legiorum de quo facta fuit nobis questio, et inter occisionem que facta fuit preter preceptum aut voluntatem regis vel sui consilii. Unde hominibus armorum plus optamus prosperitatem [p. 253] et requiem quam adversarii quorum falsa exheredacione et ceca excitacione tot prelia oriuntur. Unde testis sit mihi Deus: Ego vellem si Deo placeret quod nedum illa ecclesia sed quelibet ecclesia Anglicana haberet per precinctum viginti milliarium plenam libertatem pro omnibus fugitivis, unde plenam libertatem sancte matris ecclesie sicio modo meo.

But we detest the killing of men, not only at Westminster but elsewhere, between kingdoms; yet we distinguish between dragging out for the safeguard of the kingdom and for the wholesome chastisement of men, liege men, about which a question was put to us, and the killing which was done beyond the command or will of the king or of his council.[^8] Hence we wish men-at-arms prosperity [253] and rest more than [do] their adversaries, from whose false disinheriting and blind stirring-up so many battles arise. Hence God be my witness: I would wish, if it pleased God, that not only that church but every church in England had full liberty for all fugitives through a precinct of twenty miles; hence I thirst, in my own way, for the full liberty of holy mother church.[^9]

## ¶3 · 253.8–254.23

> [p. 253] Secundo principaliter arguitur per hoc quod dicta de potestate privilegiandi nedum sonant in Dei blasphemiam sed derogant potestati pape et regalie regum seculi. Dictum est enim quod Deus non potest auferre quicquam ab homine stante iure suo, nisi satisfaciat aliunde; quod repugnat potencie adnichilandi, et multo magis dominus papa non potest licite auferre beneficium a digno nisi deliquerit. Nec rex habet super omnibus bonis sui regni plenum dominium; que videntur sonare blasphemiam et sacrilegium. Hic negatur conclusio, cum Deus absolute necessario habet summam potestatem privilegiandi, et illa est infinitum maior quam aliqua potestas creata. Quantum ad primam instanciam, concedi debet conclusio: nam si Deus aufert quidquam ab homine stante iure suo sine hoc quod recompenset aliunde, tunc iniuriatur illi homini, quod non potest. Nam iustum est quod haberet ablatum, cum stat ius primum, ut ponitur, sed nemo est causa quare ius illud tollitur nisi Deus, ut ponitur; ergo solus Deus est causa illius iniurie. Et per consequens, cum iusticia sit constans et perpetua voluntas divina, tribuens unicuique quod suum est, patet cum veris sequi quod Deus est contrarius sibi ipsi, quia simul vult quod habent datum bonum, cum sit iustum, et vult quod nunc non habeat, cum aufert. Loquimur enim de habere opposito ablacioni [p. 254] qua Deus aufert, et de iure ad sic habendum pro eadem mensura. Similiter vel est ablatarius contentatus de ablacione Dei vel remurmurat vel est neuter. Sed si contentatus, meretur a Deo meritum vel augmentum meriti, quod est valencius quam foret abicio oblati, et per consequens, Deus per commutacionem vel recompensacionem super remunerat. Si ablatarius remurmurat, perit propter peccatum suum ius habendi, et sumus extra casum conclusionis, sicut Egipcii forisfacientes in capitalem dominum exciderunt a iure bonorum a quibus ex mandato domini habentis ipsa in fisco fuerant spoliati. Cui, inquam, debuit iustus iudex dare bona sic confiscata nisi servis suis qui prius ipsa meruerant? Si autem ablatarius sit neuter quoad actum explicitum, equanimiter tollerat ipsum eventum, et quicquid Deus decreverit faciendum, et per consequens, accrescit in merito; talem enim actum vel habitum confusum oportet inesse omni iusto; et patet conclusio. Unde ista fides de absolute necessaria iusticia et misericordia Dei facit christianos gaudenter pati et gratulanter acceptare quicquid prosperitatis vel pene eis acciderit, quia sciunt demonstrative quod Deus non potest eis humiliter pacientibus deficere, quin loco ablati tribuat pociora.

Second, [it is argued principally, by appeal to]{g:arguitur per} this: that what was said about the power of privileging not only sounds to the blasphemy of God but derogates from the power of the [pope]{g:papa} and the [regality]{g:regalia} of the kings of the world. For it has been said that God cannot take anything away from a man while his right stands, unless he makes satisfaction from elsewhere, which conflicts with the power of annihilating; and much more, that the lord pope cannot lawfully take a benefice away from a worthy man unless he has offended; nor does the king have full [lordship]{g:dominium} over all the goods of his kingdom: things which seem to sound of blasphemy and sacrilege.[^10] To this the [conclusion]{g:conclusio} is denied, since God, absolutely necessarily, has the highest power of privileging, and it is infinitely greater than any created power. As to the first [objection]{g:instancia}, the conclusion ought to be granted: for if God takes anything away from a man while his right stands, without recompensing from elsewhere, then he does injury to that man, which he cannot do. For it is just that he should have what was taken away, since the first right stands, as is supposed; but no one is the cause why that right is taken away except God, as is supposed; therefore God alone is the cause of that injury.[^11] And consequently, since justice is the constant and perpetual divine will that gives to each what is his, it is clear that it [follows]{g:sequitur} together with truths that God is contrary to himself, because at the same time he wills that they have the good given, since it is just, and wills that he now not have it, since he takes it away. For we are speaking of having as opposed to the taking away [254] by which God takes away, and of the right to have so, for the same measure [of time].[^12] Likewise: either the one deprived is content with God's taking away, or he murmurs against it, or he is neither. Now if content, he merits from God merit or an increase of merit, which is of more worth than the loss of what was taken away would be; and consequently God, by [exchange]{g:commutacio} or recompense, over-rewards. If the one deprived murmurs, his right of having perishes through his sin, and we are outside the case of the conclusion: as the Egyptians, [committing forfeiture]{g:forisfacere} against the [chief lord]{g:capitalis dominus}, fell from the right to the goods of which, by command of the Lord who had them in his treasury, they had been despoiled. To whom, I say, ought the just judge to have given the goods thus confiscated but to his servants, who had earlier earned them?[^13] But if the one deprived is neither, as regards an explicit act, he bears the event itself with equanimity, and whatever God has decreed is to be done; and consequently he grows in merit; for such an act or [indistinct]{g:confusus} habit must be present in every just man; and the conclusion is clear. Hence this faith concerning the absolutely necessary justice and mercy of God makes Christians suffer joyfully and accept thankfully whatever prosperity or pain befalls them, because they know demonstratively that God cannot fail them when they suffer humbly, without giving things of greater worth in place of what was taken away.[^14]

## Notes

[^1]: **C/T.** A in the margin *Prima obieccio* (250.18); side-note 250.18. *Commenta mendacii*: fictions of falsehood; *mendacium* is "falsehood" throughout, keeping Ambrose's double sense (lie in word, lie in deed). *Quos credunt … intendere*: accusative and infinitive after *credunt*; *quomodo defenderent* is loosely attached, "[thinking] how they might defend it". For *defensioni … intendere* A reads *defensioni … impedire*, A₁ *defensionem … impedire* (250.24); the print is translated. *Sub obtentu*: on the pretext.

[^2]: **S/T.** C. 22 q. 5 c. 20, attributed to Ambrose (Loserth, 251.2; *q. ultima* is q. 5; our modern form; LEDGER E12a-1). A in the margin *Contra mendacium* (251.7); B *Lex pugnans contra mendaces opere vel sermone. Mendacium duplex* (251.9); side-note 251.1. A has, over the alinea, *Malus episcopus, sacerdos vel clericus non est talis* (251.1). *Huic ordini*: the clerical order.

[^3]: **S/T.** A lacks *leges* (251.9). Side-note 251.14. Isidore, *Sententiae* (*De summo bono*) III.42, PL 83:711C: *Agnoscat episcopus servum se esse plebis, non dominum*, as Wyclif has it (Loserth, 251.18, reports *se esse conservum*; LEDGER E12a-2). A in the margin *Ysidorus* (251.18). *Condicio*: rank, status.

[^4]: **S.** C. 16 q. 1 c. 68 (Loserth, 251.23; LEDGER E12a-3); A in the margin *Jeronymus* (251.21); side-note 251.21. *Super tam alienis bonis pauperum*: goods so wholly another's, namely the poor's.

[^5]: **S/C/T.** C. 11 q. 3 c. 55, whose incipit is *Inter verba laudantium sive vituperantium* (Gregory, *Registrum* XI.2; Friedberg I, 657–58; Loserth, 252.1, 252.3; LEDGER E12a-4, E12a-5). The nested quotation follows the canon: the canon quotes Gregory, who quotes 2 Corinthians 1:12 and Job 16:20 (Wyclif omits *enim*; DR). A and A₁ omit *et in … perflant* (252.9–11). *Inde magis, nichil veritatis videant*: "the more … the more they see nothing", i.e. the less they see. Side-note 252.1.

[^6]: **C/T.** *Me ipso stulcior*: more foolish than myself, i.e. than I am. *Lavans laterem*: "washing a brick", the proverb for wasted labour (cf. Terence, *Phormio* 186; to be checked, LEDGER E12a-6). A reads *laudans laterem* (252.14).

[^7]: **S/C.** *Veritas domini manet*: cf. Ps. 116:2 (Vulgate). *Dicta*: taken as the object of *taceat*, "be silent about what has been said". Acts 17:5 (the Jews stir up the city) and 19:24–28 (Demetrius, "silver temples for Diana", "Great is Diana of the Ephesians!", DR). *Principes sacerdotum*: Acts 17 names no chief priests; Wyclif's addition. *Demetrium et artifices … commovere*: accusative and infinitive after *legitur*, joined loosely to the first clause. *Post conversi*: the conversion is Wyclif's inference (cf. Acts 19:18–20).

[^8]: **R/C.** Side-note 252.26. The killing at Westminster: the violation of the abbey's sanctuary in 1378, which occasioned this chapter's question (context to be checked, LEDGER E12a-7). *Altrinsecus inter regna*: elsewhere, in war between realms. *Et castigacione … legiorum*: the ablative *castigacione* is construed as parallel to *extraccionem* ("and [for] the wholesome chastisement"); *hominum legiorum*: liege men. *De quo*: the question put to Wyclif.

[^9]: **C/T.** *Hominibus armorum*: men-at-arms. *Falsa exheredacione*: false disinheriting (of the rightful holders). *Milliarium*: the manuscripts' form; Loserth notes *milliariorum* would be more correct (253.5). *Sicio*: *sitio*, "I thirst for".

[^10]: **C.** A in the margin *Contra. Secunda obieccio* (253.8); side-note 253.8. *Dictum est*: in the earlier treatment of the power of privileging (cap. 8–9). *Quod repugnat potencie adnichilandi*: the objector's point: God can annihilate, so he can take away. *Et multo magis …*: still governed by *dictum est*.

[^11]: **T/C.** B reads *Hic negetur*; A in the margin *Responsio* (253.18); side-note 253.18. A in the margin *Conclusio* (253.20). *Hic negatur conclusio*: the objection's conclusion, that the teaching is blasphemous; *concedi debet conclusio*: Wyclif's own thesis, that God cannot take away while the right stands without recompense. *Ut ponitur*: as is supposed in the case.

[^12]: **S/C/T.** *Iusticia … tribuens unicuique quod suum est*: the definition of justice in Justinian (*Inst.* 1.1 pr.), with *divina* added (to be checked, LEDGER E12a-8). A in the margin *Differencie iusticie* (253.27). A and A₁ lack *enim* (253.33). *Cum veris*: with truths (admitted premisses). *Quod habent … quod … non habeat*: the print shifts from plural to singular; both refer to the one deprived. *Pro eadem mensura*: for the same measure (instant).

[^13]: **C/S/T.** B reads *Vel per* (254.6). *Abicio oblati*: so the print; the sense requires "the loss (*ablacio*) of what was taken away (*ablati*)", which is translated. *Super remunerat*: rewards beyond. The Egyptians: Exodus 12:35–36 ("and they stripped the Egyptians", DR); *forisfacientes in capitalem dominum*: by their offence against God as chief lord they forfeited their right. A and A₁ read *confiscancia* (254.13).

[^14]: **C/T.** *Equanimiter*: the manuscripts' corrupt form is corrected by Loserth (254.15). *Quoad actum explicitum*: as regards an explicit act of the will; the *habitus confusus* is the indistinct standing disposition to accept God's will. A in the margin *Conclusio* (254.19). *Tribuat*: Loserth's correction of the manuscripts' *tribuet* (254.23), translated.
