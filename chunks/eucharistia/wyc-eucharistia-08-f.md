# *De eucharistia*, chapter 8, part 6: the continuity of time, and Thomas's two kinds of time

## ¶1 · 251.22–252.6

> [p. 251] Item, si tempus sit discontinuum, quia est per se numerus et sibi accidit continuacio ex motu, sequitur quod non potest esse tempus nisi discretum; nam non potest esse tempus nisi sit numerus; nec potest esse numerus nisi sit discretum: ergo non potest esse tempus nisi ipsum sit discretum; aliter enim nimis nude arguitur discontinuacio temporis ex hoc quod ipsum est numerus et inponitur magna stulticia Aristoteli dicenti quod numerus est genus temporis; et tamen non potest esse tempus nisi ipsum sit continuum. Cum enim pro tempore casus angeli fuit in [p. 252] mundo motus continuus, videtur quod pari evidencia qua tempus fuit tunc discretum per duo instancia immediata fuit et tunc simul continuum racione motus tunc continui. In mathematicis quidem, cuiusmodi sunt puncta et instancia, non differunt continuacio et contiguacio.

Again, if time be discontinuous because it is [of itself]{g:per-se} number and [continuation]{g:continuacio} befalls it from motion,[^1] it follows that there cannot be time unless [it is] discrete; for there cannot be time unless it is number; nor can there be number unless it is discrete: therefore there cannot be time unless it is discrete. For otherwise the discontinuation of time is argued [exceedingly]{g:nimis} baldly from the fact that it is number, and great folly is imputed to Aristotle, who says "number is the genus of time";[^2] and yet there cannot be time unless it is continuous. For since at the time of the angel's fall there was in the [252] world continuous motion, it seems that by the same [evidence]{g:evidencia} by which time was then discrete through two [immediate]{g:immediatus} instants, it was then also at the same time continuous by reason of the then continuous motion. In mathematical things, indeed, of which kind are points and instants, continuation and [contiguity]{g:contiguacio} do not differ.

## ¶2 · 252.7–253.16

> [p. 252] Item, probatur generaliter quod in eodem tempore sunt instancia inmediata ex principio huius vie, capiendo ut concessum quod eidem tempori accessit contingenter continuitas ex motu continuo, ut cum asserit tempus potest per se consequi affeccionem angeli; cum ergo angelus continue variat affeccionem ad instans et alia subito generata (ut probabile creditur) vel saltem est possibile, videtur quod pari evidencia quodlibet tempus sit discretum; nam tempus naturaliter prius est quam est motus continuus iuxta hanc viam, quia posset esse in motu affeccionis angelice non existente questionis continuo; cum ergo non prius naturaliter sit quam sit discretum quod est eius essencialis differencia, videtur quod continuacio motus qui hinc tam contingenter accidit, non tollit discrecionem temporis nec addit continuacionem que variet eius naturam; nam signato uno tempore cuius una medietas sit continua et alia discreta omnia argumenta que procederent contra immediacionem instancium procederent eciam contra illud; et possibilitatem huius probant adversarii, ponendo quod Deus continue per medietatem hore creet et annichilet sine interpolacione media angelum et per secundam medietatem hore cesset ab omni tali furia; tunc patet quod nulla est evidencia fienda contra composicionem [p. 253] continui ex non quantis quin pariter procederet contra composicionem talis temporis. Ideo radix huius sentencie stat in composicione permanentis continui ex non quantis. Ad quod arguo ex principiis adversarii, ponendo quod Deus annichilet omne continuum huius mundi sensibilis, servando omnem punctum eius inmotum et creet in omni situ indivisibili unum angelum, ut multiplicet eundem angelum utrobique, et preter hec servet A punctale quod moveatur localiter continue de punctali in punctale, et patet quod foret tunc tempus continuum sicut spacium permanens continuum ex immediacione indivisibilium, et tamen tempus componeretur ex instantibus; omnis enim situs qui ante fuit in mundo foret summe plenus punctalibus, cum non sit dare vacuum, ideo foret situs corporeus eque continuus, ut fuit in principio.

Again, it is proved generally that in the same time there are immediate instants, from the principle of this way, taking as granted that continuity came to that same time contingently from continuous motion, as when it asserts [that] time can of itself follow upon the affection of an angel. Since, then, an angel continually varies [his] affection at an instant, and other [affections are] suddenly generated (as is believed with probability), or at least this is possible,[^3] it seems that by the same evidence any time whatever is discrete. For time is naturally prior to continuous motion, according to this way, because it could be in the motion of angelic affection when the continuous [motion] in question does not exist;[^4] since, then, it is not naturally [time] before it is discrete, which is its essential [difference]{g:differencia}, it seems that the continuation of motion, which hence befalls it so contingently, does not take away the discreteness of time, nor add a continuation that would vary its nature. For if one time is designated, of which one half is continuous and the other discrete, all the arguments that would proceed against the [immediacy]{g:immediacio} of instants would proceed also against that; and the adversaries prove the possibility of this by positing that God, continually through half an hour, creates and annihilates an angel without intervening [interruption]{g:interpolacio}, and through the second half of the hour ceases from all such frenzy. Then it is clear that there is no evidence to be produced against the composition [253] of a continuum out of non-[quantified]{g:quantus} things that would not proceed equally against the composition of such a time. Therefore the root of this [teaching]{g:sentencia}[^5] stands in the composition of a [permanent]{g:permanens} continuum out of non-quantified things. To which I argue from the adversary's principles, positing that God annihilates every continuum of this [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} world, keeping every point of it unmoved, and creates in every indivisible site one angel, so as to multiply the same angel [on both sides]{g:utrobique},[^6] and besides these keeps a [punctual]{g:punctale} A,[^7] which is moved [locally]{g:localiter} and continuously from punctual to punctual; and it is clear that there would then be continuous time, as there is permanent continuous space from the immediacy of indivisibles, and yet time would be composed of instants. For every site that was before in the world would be supremely full of punctuals, since [one may not posit]{g:est-dare} a void; so the bodily site would be equally continuous, as it was in the beginning.

## ¶3 · 253.17–253.22

> [p. 253] Similiter, si in silva, in stellis celi et corporibus similibus distanter positis causatur figura, ut dicunt naturales, perspectivi et geometre, multo magis in indivisibilibus inmediate positis servato undique pleno; nam concessa continuitate loci concedi oportet continuitatem locati.

Likewise, if in a wood, in the stars of heaven, and in like bodies placed at a distance, a figure is caused, as the natural philosophers, the [perspectivists]{g:perspectivi} and the geometers say, much more [is it caused] in indivisibles placed [without intermediary]{g:immediate}, a plenum being kept on every side; for once continuity of the place is granted, continuity of the thing placed must be granted.

## ¶4 · 253.23–254.21

> [p. 253] Similiter, una legio angelorum quam fides vocat unum (ut patet Luc. VIIIº, 3o) posset circulariter circumduci, et sic motus localis et tempus inde consequens foret vere continuum; ymmo videtur quod illa legio que est unus angelus foret quodammodo circularis, quia circumduccio foret circularis que requirit continuitatem et continua circularitas illius motus requirit continuitatem subiecti, quod est legio; [p. 254] patet igitur quod nec sensus nec racio indicat nunc continuitatem in mundo quin per idem et tunc foret continuitas utrobique. Nec dubium quin moto tunc continuo a sperico vel punctali foret tunc inmediacio motuum et instancium, sicut foret inmediacio situum indivisibilium; ex quibus componeretur utrobique continuum. Ymmo corpus cubicum haberet correspondenter ad eius punctalia acervum punctalium suppositum et undique circumductum, et omniquaque foret tanta continuitas et sic verum balbutivit hec via vocis sue ignara, quia fuit sibi ipsi manifeste contraria. Nunc itaque tenet Aristotelem nunc negat, nunc tenet leges hominum et nunc negat; nunc tenet decreta ecclesie et nunc negat, nec fundat se in sic variando in fide scripture, ideo vana est testificacio huius vie. Quantum ad dicta Aristotelis katholica de genere accidentis, patet quod ipsa renuit sine causa. Sed alia dicta Aristotelis de composicione continui ex non quantis, ut sentenciant Plato, Augustinus et Lincolniensis, ipsa concedit explicite ad favorem Aristotelis sed negat ipsa implicite.

Likewise, one legion of angels, which faith calls one (as is clear, Luke 8:30),[^8] could be led round in a circle, and so the local motion and the time following from it would be [truly]{g:vere} continuous; indeed, it seems that that legion, which is one angel, would be in a manner circular, because the leading round would be circular, which requires continuity, and the continuous circularity of that motion requires continuity of the [subject]{g:subiectum}, which is the legion. [254] It is clear, therefore, that neither sense nor reason now indicates continuity in the world without there being, by the same token, continuity then also on both sides. Nor is there doubt that, a continuum being then moved by a spherical or punctual [thing], there would then be immediacy of motions and of instants, as there would be immediacy of indivisible sites, out of which a continuum would be composed on both sides. Indeed, a cubic body would have, corresponding to its punctuals, a heap of punctuals placed under it and led round it on every side, and everywhere there would be as much continuity. And so this way stammered the truth, ignorant of its own voice, because it was manifestly contrary to itself. So now it holds Aristotle, now denies him; now it holds the laws of men and now denies them; now it holds the [decrees]{g:decretum} of the [church]{g:ecclesia} and now denies them; nor in so varying does it ground itself in the faith of scripture; therefore the testimony of this way is vain. As to Aristotle's catholic sayings on the genus of [accident]{g:accidens}, it is clear that it rejects them without cause. But Aristotle's other sayings, on the composition of a continuum out of non-quantified things, as Plato, Augustine and [Lincoln]{g:lincolniensis} [pronounce]{g:sentenciare},[^9] it concedes explicitly in favour of Aristotle, but denies implicitly.

## ¶5 · 254.22–254.25

> [p. 254] Quantum ad leges hominum, patet quod nunc tantum favit illis quod dicit iudicem propter eas in casu debere contrariari divino iudicio quod ipse dyabolus non auderet concedere.

As to the laws of men, it is clear that it has now so far favoured them that it says a judge, on account of them, ought in a case to go against the divine judgement, which the devil himself would not dare to concede.[^10]

## ¶6 · 254.26–254.32

> [p. 254] Quantum ad leges ecclesie, patet quod contradicit decreto Nicholai IIⁱ de eukaristia. Sed de indulgenciis sine fundamento scripture vel causa concedit quod episcopus Avinonicus potest meritum supererogatum capere a beatis, ad votum distribuere discolis sine fine. Quis ergo crederet tali viro in isto triplici devio et similibus tanquam testi?

As to the [laws of the church]{g:leges-ecclesie}, it is clear that it contradicts the decree of Nicholas II on [the Eucharist]{g:eukaristia}.[^11] But on [indulgences]{g:indulgencie}, without foundation of scripture or cause, it concedes that the bishop of Avignon[^12] can take [supererogated]{g:supererogare} merit from the blessed [and] distribute it at will to the [froward]{g:discoli} without end. Who then would believe such a man as a witness, in this threefold straying and the like?

## ¶7 · 255.1–255.23

> [p. 255] Sed forte dicitur quod iste doctor ponit duplex tempus continuum et discretum; discretum componitur ex instantibus mensurantibus motus affeccionis evangelice. Et istam sentenciam tangit Thomas super II° Sentenciarum, distinccione secunda, questione XIIIᵃ: Dicendum, inquit, quod angelus potest considerari dupliciter: vel quantum ad suum esse quod est sine vicissitudine et sic mensuratur evo, vel quantum ad operacionem et sic quoad aliquam operacionem mensuratur eciam evo quoad operacionem qua intuetur res in verbo. In tali enim operacione non est vicissitudo aliqua sed secundum operacionem in qua est vicissitudo mensuratur tempore. Sed ipsum tempus est aliud a tempore quod mensurat motum primi mobilis, quia ista vicissitudo non ordinatur ad illam, nec illud tempus est continuum, sed est numerus discretus vicissitudinis non continue. Continuitas enim accidit tempori ex parte motus numerati. Et ita sicut ponit unum tempus mensurans totam universitatem sensibilem, sic ponit unum evum mensurans totam naturam angelicam secundum suum esse et intelligere permanens quod non est de genere successivi.

But perhaps it is said that this doctor posits a twofold time, continuous and discrete; the discrete is composed of instants measuring the motions of angelic affection.[^13] And Thomas touches on this teaching on the second [book] of the *Sentences*, distinction 2, question 13:[^14] "It must be said," he says, "that an angel can be considered in two ways:[^15] either as to his [being]{g:esse}, which is without [alternation]{g:vicissitudo}, and so he is measured by [aeviternity]{g:evum}; or as to operation, and so, as to some operation, he is measured also by aeviternity, as to the operation by which he beholds things in [the Word]{g:verbum}. For in such operation there is no alternation; but according to the operation in which there is alternation he is measured by time. But that time is other than the time which measures the motion of [the first movable]{g:primum-mobile}, because this alternation is not ordered to that, nor is that time continuous, but it is the discrete number of a non-continuous alternation. For continuity befalls time on the side of the motion numbered." And so, as he posits one time measuring [the whole]{g:universitas} perceptible universe, so he posits one aeviternity measuring the whole angelic [nature]{g:natura} according to its permanent being and understanding, which is not of the genus of the successive.

## Notes

[^1]: **G.** *Continuacio* is "continuation" (the row of bodies made continuous), kept apart from *continuitas* "continuity" and *contiguacio* "contiguity", which stand beside it here (251.24, 252.6). The conditional sets out Thomas's position of 250.32–251.12.
[^2]: **S.** Aristotle; Loserth's foot (251.30) reads in the OCR "Aristoteles, 3iob, 5 (ed. acad. Borus.)"; the Bekker reference is to be read on the plate and checked (*Physics* IV.11, 219b, on time as number, is our guess) (LEDGER K08f-1).
[^3]: **C.** *Continue variat affeccionem ad instans et alia subito generata*: construed as "varies his affection at an instant, and others [are] generated suddenly"; *alia* may instead be a second object of *variat* ("and other [things] generated suddenly"). The clause does not construe cleanly.
[^4]: **C.** *Non existente questionis continuo*: "the continuous [motion] of the question", i.e. the continuous motion under discussion; A reads *conclusionis*. Taken as an ablative absolute.
[^5]: **C.** *Sentencia* (CONTEXTUAL) is "teaching", the position this way holds.
[^6]: **C.** *Utrobique*: in both places, i.e. at every one of the indivisible sites, as in the multiplication of one body in several places (ch. 8).
[^7]: **G.** *Punctale*: a point-sized thing (Wyclif's noun from *punctus*), rendered "punctual" as a noun; it has no row.
[^8]: **S.** Luke 8:30: the man's devils answer to one name, "Legion. Because many devils were entered into him." Wyclif's *angelorum* is of the fallen angels. Not quoted.
[^9]: **C.** *Ut sentenciant Plato …* is taken with the composition of a continuum out of non-quantified things, the view Wyclif holds with Plato, Augustine and Grosseteste (*Lincolniensis*, Robert Grosseteste, bishop of Lincoln). The identifications are to be checked (LEDGER K08f-2).
[^10]: **S.** Thomas's teaching that a judge must judge by the evidence alleged, not by his private knowledge (*ST* II-II q. 67 a. 2), is our guess for the ground of this charge; the Latin names no place (LEDGER K08f-3).
[^11]: **S.** The decree of Nicholas II is *Ego Berengarius* (Gratian, *De cons.* D. 2 c. 42; the form is ours), sworn by Berengar at the Roman synod of 1059 (LEDGER K08f-4).
[^12]: **C.** *Episcopus Avinonicus*: the pope at Avignon. Wyclif's charge is Thomas's account of the treasury of merit (*In IV Sent.* d. 20; *Suppl.* q. 25 a. 1 is our guess) (LEDGER K08f-5). *Capere* is Loserth's text; A reads *sapere*, corrected *rapere*, margin *alias capere*; DE *sapere* (254.30).
[^13]: **T.** The print has *evangelice*, "evangelical", which does not construe; Loserth's foot (255.3–4) notes that C, in an erasure, reads "more correctly" *angelico*. "Angelic" is translated from C.
[^14]: **S.** Thomas, *In II Sent.*; the print reads d. 2 q. 13; Loserth's foot corrects to "Super II distinct. … quaestio I. art. [?]", his edition p. 23 (OCR rough). To be checked (LEDGER K08f-6).
[^15]: **T.** *Dupliciter* is Loserth's addition (255.7: "Codd.: dupliciter deest. Addidi."); the sense requires it.
