# *De potestate pape*, chapter 6, part 7: the pseudo-pope's sophistries: what a state is

## ¶1 · 125.32–127.31

> [p. 125] Multe sunt versucie velate sub istis XI, in quibus falluntur simplices versuciis Pseudochristi, et indubie quicumque fuerit talis Pseudo vel ei [p. 126] consenciens est secundum quod plus vel minus istis condicionibus participat Antichristus. Ideo docet beatus Evangelista epistola sua canonica; postquam Antichristum descripserit: Nolite, inquit, eum recipere in domum vestram, nec ave ei dixeritis; quod oportet intelligi nec in persona sua propria nec in membris, qui, inquit; dixerit illi ave, communicabit operibus eius malignis, et iste si est talis est heresiarcha solvens Christum infideliter in naturis persone, in Christi legibus et in membris, cuius scolam oportet cordatos theologos affeccione donorum suorum et timore suorum supplicacionum postpositis animose detegere. Et ipsum describunt omnes libri sapienciales vel prophetici alterius testamenti; et propter nostrorum vecordiam et membrorum suorum crescenciam in brevi tempore magis invaluit. Nec dubium quin sit infidelis Christo, qui aliquo quinque modorum consentit homini contra Christum. Sunt autem multa sophistica quibus homo iste illudit simplicibus christianis; dicitur enim primo quod status cleri sui est infinitum perfeccior quam status aliquis laycalis, ideo non pertinet ipsos de statu cleri cognoscere. Dicit secundo palpatus in metaphisica quod omnis status hominis est realiter ipse homo. Contra utrumque istorum invexi sepius: contra primum est decretum Crysostomi Imperfecto Omelia 26; ubi docet; quod homo debet iudicare de operibus manifestis, quia aliter precluderet lex Christi naturale iudicium et foret sibi ipsi contraria, [p. 127] cum ipsemet docet Matth. VII, 15 attendere a falsis prophetis ex fructibus cognoscendis, aliter enim necessario cecaret laycos ad elemosinas suas Antichristi discipulis improvide parciendum. Contra secundum patet quod repugnat priori, cum tunc omnis clericus foret infinitum perfeccior quam laycus; quod soli Christo potest competere; iterum omnis homo vel angelus maneret continue in eodem statu, sicut manet constanter in eadem humanitate, et sic nullus status posset maius aut minus suscipere. Et iterum periret status et christianorum distinccio, ymo propter casum Luciferi non foret status angelorum deterior, nisi forte dicatur quod status sit qualitas anime vel peccatum. Ideo certum est quod status iuxta methaphisicam scripture verissimam est denominacio, qua natura racionalis dicitur formaliter stare respectu finis ultimi, et sic in homine aliquis est status corporis, aliquis status anime et aliquis mixtum; aliquis eciam est status quoad mores et aliquis quoad ritus vel religionem, aliquis totalis et aliquis parcialis. Unde de statu corporali et statu mentali racionali vel integra individua potest intelligi illud Yob. XIII quod homo miserabilior lapsus nunquam in eodem statu permanet continue, nam secundum statum integrum variatur, ut patet de etate, de morum varietate et de circumstanciis statum integrantibus, que communiter variantur; manet tamen idem status communis et individuis successivis intercisus vel continuus.

Many are the wiles veiled under these eleven, in which the simple are deceived by the wiles of the [false christ]{g:pseudochristus}; and without doubt whoever is such a Pseudo, or [126] consents to him, is Antichrist according as he shares more or less in these conditions.[^1] Therefore the blessed Evangelist teaches in his canonical epistle, after he has described Antichrist: "Receive him not," he says, "into your house, nor say to him: God speed you" (which must be understood neither in his own person nor in his [members]{g:membrum}); "he who shall say to him: God speed you," he says, "shall communicate with his wicked works."[^2] And this man, if he is such, is a heresiarch, faithlessly dissolving Christ in the natures of his person, in Christ's laws and in his members; and stout-hearted theologians must courageously [expose]{g:detegere} his school, setting aside affection for his gifts and fear for their supplications.[^3] And all the sapiential or prophetic books of the other testament describe him; and through the [faint-heartedness]{g:vecordia} of our men and the growth of his members he has in a short time grown the stronger. Nor is there doubt that he is [faithless]{g:infidelis} to Christ who in any of the five ways consents to a man against Christ.[^4] And there are many sophistries by which this man mocks simple Christians. For it is said, first, that the state of his clergy is infinitely more perfect than any lay state, and therefore it does not pertain to [the laity] to take cognizance of the state of the clergy. He says, second, having groped about in [metaphysics]{g:metaphisica}, that every state of a man is really the man himself.[^5] Against both of these I have often inveighed. Against the first is the decree of Chrysostom, *Opus imperfectum*, homily 26, where he teaches "that a man ought to judge of manifest works, since otherwise the law of Christ would shut out natural judgment and would be contrary to itself",[^6] [127] since he himself teaches, Matthew 7:15, to "beware of false prophets", who are to be known "by their fruits"; for otherwise it would of necessity blind lay people into sharing out their alms improvidently to the disciples of Antichrist.[^7] Against the second, it is clear that it is [incompatible]{g:repugnare} with the former, since then every cleric would be infinitely more perfect than a layman, which can belong to Christ alone. Again, every man or angel would remain continually in the same state, as he remains constantly in the same humanity; and so no state could admit more or less. And again the distinction of states and of Christians would perish; indeed, through Lucifer's fall the state of the angels would not be worse, unless perhaps it be said that a state is a quality of the soul, or sin.[^8] Therefore it is certain that a state, according to the most true metaphysics of scripture, is a [naming]{g:denominacio} by which a rational nature is said formally to stand in [relation]{g:respectus} to its last end; and so in man there is some state of the body, some state of the soul, and some mixed; there is also some state as to manners and some as to [rites]{g:ritus} or religion, some total and some partial.[^9] Hence of the bodily state, and of the mental, rational or integral individual state, can be understood that [saying] of Job 13, that man, fallen [and] more wretched, "never continueth in the same state"; for he varies according to his integral state, as is clear of age, of the variety of manners and of the circumstances that make up the state, which commonly vary; yet the same common state remains, broken off or continuous in [successive]{g:successivum} individuals.[^10]

## ¶2 · 127.32–129.3

> [p. 127] Ex istis logici sollicitant se quod continuum componitur ex non quantis, cum status continuus sicut etas per indivisibile vel duo aut quotlibet [p. 128] indivisibilia excedit aliud, nam P. superstes, qui incepit cum Paulo nunc desinente est per instans antiquior quam fuit Paulus vel ipse; nec est possibile unum continuum per indivisibile excedere aliud nisi indivisibile faciens ad maioritatem eius sit pars talis maioris, ut est in numeris. Et sic potest argui de statu sanitatis vel infirmitatis, de quo quis meritorie continue elicit actum caritatis, gaudendo et laudando Deum de statu; et patet quod actus terminatur exclusive ad instans ut sanitas vel infirmitas, et (ut videtur) est continue novus actus, sicut continue novum obiectum, ad quod actus essencialiter terminatur. Et ita videtur quod captis duobus predestinatis, quorum primi meritum terminetur exclusive ad septimum gradum, et secundi totale meritum terminetur inclusive ad eundem gradum, status primum in patria correspondenter se habebunt; nec negabitur antecedens, cum stat de duobus predestinatis unum exclusive et alium inclusive incipere mereri ab eodem instanti. Postquam originaliter peccando continue antefuerant in mortali et continue equaliter merendo pro eodem instanti decedant. Constat quidem quod actuum merendi aliquis est successivus, cuius incepcio est per remocionem de presenti, et aliquis per posicionem de presenti. Sic enim Christus potuit incipere purgasse Baptistam ab originali pro primo instanti sui esse, et alium communem sibi et aliunde undique parem creasse in gracia et beatificasse eos correspondenter ad merita, et sic de mille casibus possibilibus et de facto in quibus implicatur unum quantum excedere aliud per indivisibile. Ex quibus oportet concedere aliquam esse maximam partem quantitativam, [p. 129] eius parem excesso, et adiectum cum facit quantitative totum maius, oportet esse partem quantitativam maiorati per ipsum.

From these things the logicians busy themselves [to show] that a continuum is composed of non-quantities, since a continuous state, like age, exceeds another by an indivisible, or by two or any number of [128] indivisibles: for P., the survivor, who began with Paul, who now ceases, is older by an instant than Paul was, or than he himself [was]; nor is it possible for one continuum to exceed another by an indivisible unless the indivisible that makes for its [greatness]{g:majoritas} is a part of the greater, as it is in numbers.[^11] And so it can be argued of a state of health or sickness about which someone meritoriously and continually elicits an act of [charity]{g:caritas}, rejoicing in the state and praising God for it: it is clear that the act is terminated exclusively at an instant, as the health or the sickness is, and (as it seems) there is continually a new act, as there is continually a new object, at which the act is essentially terminated.[^12] And so it seems that, taking two [predestinate]{g:predestinatus}, of whom the merit of the first is terminated exclusively at the seventh degree, and the whole merit of the second inclusively at the same degree, their states in the [homeland]{g:patria} will first stand correspondingly; nor will the [antecedent]{g:antecedens} be denied, since it is consistent that, of two predestinate, one should begin to merit exclusively and the other inclusively from the same instant, after they had previously been continually in mortal [sin] through original sinning, and, continually meriting equally, they die at the same instant.[^13] It is evident indeed that among acts of meriting some one is successive, whose beginning is by removal of the present, and some one by positing of the present.[^14] For so Christ could have begun to have cleansed the Baptist from original [sin] at the first instant of his [being]{g:esse}, and to have created another, common with him and otherwise equal in every respect, in grace, and to have beatified them correspondingly to their merits; and so of a thousand cases, possible and actual, in which it is implied that one quantity exceeds another by an indivisible.[^15] From which it must be granted that there is some greatest [quantitative part]{g:pars-quantitativa} [129] of it, equal to the [quantity] exceeded, and that what is added, since it makes the whole quantitatively greater, must be a quantitative part of what is made greater by it.[^16]

## ¶3 · 129.4–129.31

> [p. 129] Istam materiam diffuse prosecutus sum alibi cum aliis dubiis annexis, ideo transeo dictum repetens superius, quod status exproprietarie vivendi est quocumque statu civili perfeccior, sicut status virginitatis est quocumque statu coniugati perfeccior, natura angelica est naturaliter natura humana superior, vita contemplativa est vita activa perfeccior et sexus femineus est sexu masculino fragilior; et tamen constat quod multi coniugati sunt multis virginibus meliores, multi homines superiores dyabolis gracia non natura, multi activi sunt multis contemplativis sancciores, et multe femine virgines in iuvenili etate multis viris eciam decrepitis plus constantes, ut sepe meminit Augustinus in scriptis istius materie. Ideo necesse est volentem istam materiam intelligere considerare perfeccionem quam habet homo in quantum exproprietarie vivit, et perfeccionem quam habet in quantum civilis proprietarius, et illarum perfeccionum quas voco perfecciones simpliciter supra perfecciones individuas, primam inveniet plus perfectam. Ille autem perfecciones more universalium non possunt augmentari vel minui, sed perfecciones vel status individui secundum quod illis alia commoda sunt adiecta, et sic, ut sepe repecii, status exproprietarie vivendi propter excellenciam perfeccionis, congruit clero Christi.

This matter I have pursued at length elsewhere, with other doubts annexed to it; therefore I pass on, repeating what was said above: that the state of living [without property]{g:exproprietarie} is more perfect than any civil state, as the state of virginity is more perfect than any state of the married, angelic nature is by nature superior to human nature, the contemplative life is more perfect than the active life, and the female sex is frailer than the male sex.[^17] And yet it is evident that many married people are better than many virgins, many men superior to devils by grace, not by nature, many active people holier than many contemplatives, and many women, virgins of youthful age, more constant than many men, even decrepit ones, as Augustine often recalls in his writings on this matter.[^18] Therefore whoever wishes to understand this matter must consider the perfection that a man has inasmuch as he lives without property, and the perfection that he has inasmuch as he is a civil [proprietary]{g:proprietarius} [owner]; and of those perfections, which I call perfections [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}, over and above individual perfections, he will find the first more perfect. Now those perfections, after the manner of [universals]{g:universale}, cannot be increased or diminished; but individual perfections or states [can], according as other advantages are added to them.[^19] And so, as I have often repeated, the state of living without property, because of the excellence of its perfection, befits the [clergy]{g:clerus} of Christ.

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 126.21: The pope says that the clerical state is more perfect than the lay and that the laity have no right to judge the clergy. He says too that the man and his state are one.
- 126.28: A man may and must judge of deeds open and visible.
- 127.5: If the man and his state are one every cleric is infinitely more perfect than any layman. Moreover the state of every man and angel would be unchangeable.
- 129.4: The state of being without property is, as a State, better than that of a civil owner.

## Notes

[^1]: **T.** Apparatus 125.32: codd. *materie*; the print's *Multe* (Loserth's) is translated. With the manuscripts the sentence would open "There are wiles of the matter veiled …".
[^2]: **S.** 2 John 10–11, which Loserth cites (2 Joh. 10). Wyclif departs from the Vulgate (*nolite recipere eum in domum … qui enim dicit illi ave, communicat*): he adds *vestram* and puts *dixerit … communicabit* in the future; the English follows his Latin, in DR's words where they agree. The parenthesis is Wyclif's gloss inside the quotation. Apparatus 126.8: codd. *ille* for the print's *iste* (no change of sense).
[^3]: **C/S.** *Solvens Christum* echoes 1 John 4:3 (*qui solvit Iesum*). *Supplicaciones*: the petitions (rolls) for provision to benefices that the schools sent to the pope; *suorum* is read as the theologians' own, "fear for their supplications" (it may be the Pseudo's: fear of his response to them). *Cordatos*, "stout-hearted", is the opposite of *vecors*.
[^4]: **T/S.** B *infideles* (apparatus 126.18). The five ways of consenting are not listed here; Wyclif's list elsewhere is the canonists' verse *Consentit operans, defendens, consilium dans, / Ac auctorisans, non iuvans, nec reprehendens* (*De simonia* cap. 8, p. 98; *De officio regis* p. 68, where he counts six; *quinque modorum consensuum* also at *De officio regis* p. 117) (LEDGER P06g-1). *Infidelis* with a dative is "faithless" (GLOSSARY).
[^5]: **C.** *Ipsos* is the laity, from *laycalis*: "[the laity]" supplied. *Palpatus in metaphisica*: literally "having felt his way", a sneer at a dabbler. AB in marg. at 126.25: *Iudicare de prelatis*.
[^6]: **S.** Pseudo-Chrysostom, *Opus imperfectum in Matthaeum*, homily 26 as printed; Loserth gives "Opp. tom. VI, 743 (?)" and compares the genuine *In Matth. hom.* 23 (Opp. VII, 307) (editor, with his query, LEDGER P06g-2). The quotation bounds are Loserth's italics; whether the words are Chrysostom's own is to be checked with the source.
[^7]: **S/C.** Matt. 7:15–16; *ex fructibus cognoscendis* is Wyclif's compression of 7:16 (DR "By their fruits you shall know them"). *Ipsemet* is taken as Christ, the lawgiver; *cecaret* has the law (or Christ) as subject. *Parciendum* is read as *partiendum*.
[^8]: **T.** Apparatus 127.5: codd. *tercium* for *secundum*; 127.11: codd. *status christianorum* without *et* ("the distinction of the state of Christians"). The print is translated in both places.
[^9]: **G/C.** *Denominacio* "naming" (GLOSSARY): a state is not a thing in the man but a name taken from his standing toward his last end. AB in marg. at 127.15: *Status hominis quid est*.
[^10]: **S/C/T.** "Job 13" as printed; the words are Job 14:2 (DR "and never continueth in the same state"), the verse after 14:1 *homo natus de muliere*. *Homo miserabilior lapsus* is read "man, fallen and more wretched [than before]". *Integra individua* does not agree with *statu*; it is translated as "integral individual [state]" and flagged. *Intercisus* is Loserth's emendation of codd. *intricisus* (apparatus 127.30).
[^11]: **C.** The thesis that a continuum is composed of indivisibles is Wyclif's own (*De logica* III, tr. 3 cap. 9, ed. Dziewicki III, 30 ff.; LEDGER P06g-3); *sollicitant se quod* is read "busy themselves [to show] that". *P.* is a sample name. Apparatus 128.1: codd. *II* for *duo*; 128.6: one codex *m'us* for *numeris*. B in marg. at 128.7: *Casus notabilis*.
[^12]: **C.** *Terminatur exclusive ad instans*: the act has no last instant, as the state of health has none, so each instant brings a new object and a new act.
[^13]: **C/T.** *Status primum … se habebunt* does not construe smoothly: *primum* may be corrupt (for *eorum*?). Translated as printed, "first". The next printed sentence (*Postquam … decedant*) has no main verb and is read as continuing the case; the full stop before it is the print's.
[^14]: **C.** The two modes of beginning in the schools' *de incipit et desinit*: by "removal of the present" (it is not now, and immediately after this it will be) and by "positing of the present" (it is now, and immediately before this it was not).
[^15]: **C/S.** *Alium communem sibi* is read "another sharing [that first instant] with him". The Baptist's sanctification in the womb rests on Luke 1:15 ("he shall be filled with the Holy Ghost, even from his mother's womb"; our identification). *Beatificasse*: "beatified", from *beatificacio* (GLOSSARY).
[^16]: **C.** *Maximam partem quantitativam eius, parem excesso*: the greatest quantitative part of the greater, equal to the lesser that it exceeds; the added indivisible is then a quantitative part too.
[^17]: **S.** "Elsewhere": probably *De logica* III, tr. 3 cap. 9 (ed. Dziewicki III, 30 ff.), where whether the continuum is composed *ex non quantis* is one of three annexed *dubia* (LEDGER P06g-4). "Above": the eighth teaching's earlier conclusions in this chapter. AB in marg. at 129.4: *Status diversi secundum gradus*. The print's *alus* is *aliis*.
[^18]: **S/T.** Augustine, *De bono coniugali* and *De sancta virginitate* (e.g. *De bono coniug.* 23.28, PL 40, 392: *possunt conjugati esse continentibus meliores*; the young virgins' constancy is not traced; LEDGER P06g-5). B *et tantum* for *et tamen* (apparatus 129.12); the print is translated.
[^19]: **R.** B in marg. at 129.26: *Perfecciones in universali non augentur sed in singulari*, an early reader's summary. AB in marg. at 129.32 (the next paragraph): *Status exproprietarius approbatur a tota Trinitate*.
