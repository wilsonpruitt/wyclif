## Doubtful, left as they are

- **03-h, p. 82**: *aliter foret transinferioracio vel transsubieccio, **sed** iuxta viam predictam nec oportet panem perdere vel acquirere aliquam qualitatem*, rendered "but according to the way stated". This is the *novelli*'s objection to Hugh. Major: what is transubstantiated must be changed, and changed into a substance. *Sed*: on the stated way the bread gains or loses only a relation, and there is no motion to a relation. The unstated conclusion is that the bread is not transubstantiated. Read as the minor premiss of that implicit argument, it would be (a) "now". But no *ergo / ideo / igitur* follows, so the look-ahead rule does not decide it. The *sed* also sets "the way stated" against the requirement just laid down, so (b) "but" is defensible. Left.
- **04-a, p. 86**: ***Sed** magister optimus et Cesar semper Augustus … introduxit sollempniter hoc sacramentum; ex quo patet … quod hoc sacramentum sit efficacius*, rendered "But the best master". The other signs only signify and commemorate, *but* Christ solemnly brought in this one: that is (b). A conclusion follows (*ex quo patet*), so the clause could be read as the premiss that the conclusion is drawn from, which would make it (a) "now". *Ex quo patet* is not one of the card's look-ahead markers (*ergo / ideo / igitur*), and the contrast with *crux muta … et alia signa* is strong. Left.
- **04-a, p. 84**: *limites **autem** istius diffusionis expedit nobis viantibus ignorare*, rendered "But the limits". Read as (b): the philosophers dispute how far the species spreads, *but* its limits are not for wayfarers to know. It could also be read as (c) "And the limits", carrying on the account of the diffusion. Left.
