## The volume

*Tractatus de eucharistia* and *Tractatus de penitencia sive de confessione*, ed. Johann Loserth (Wyclif
Society, 1892), from the plates of archive.org `latinworks13wycl` (**leaf = page + 71**; leaf images in
`text/eucharistia/raw/leaves/`). Three texts:

Chunk ids: `prol-a … prol-c` the compendium, `01-a … 09-n` the nine chapters, `pen01 … pen06` *De
penitencia*, `quest` the *Questio*.

- **pp. 1–10, the compendium** (*Sentencia tractatus de Eukaristia in compendio*, under "Prologus"): a
  medieval summary of the nine chapters that came down with the work. Translate it like the text.
- **pp. 11–326, the *Tractatus de eucharistia* (maior)**, nine long chapters. Written in Wyclif's last
  years, before 1382–83 (Loserth, Introd. §4: it shares passages word for word with *Sermones* III, and
  it avoids the words *substancia* and *materialis* of the bread, which Wyclif says in *Sermo* III.54 he
  has stopped using). Wyclif's case against the "modern" doctrine: that the consecrated host is in its
  nature bread and wine and sacramentally, figuratively, the body and blood of Christ; that an accident
  without a subject is impossible; that the decree *Ego Berengarius* (*De cons.* D. 2 c. 42) is the
  faith of the ancient Roman church; against the friars, Thomas, Scotus and "the sect of the signs".
  A **treatise**: Wyclif speaks in the first person, states conclusions, raises objections and answers
  them. The tractatus minor (the *Confessio*) is NOT in this volume (Shirley, *Fasc. Ziz.*).
- **pp. 327–348, *De eucharistia et penitencia sive de confessione*** in six chapters (only ch. 1 is on
  the eucharist; the rest is penance and auricular confession), then the short ***Questio ad fratres de
  sacramento altaris*** (pp. 347–348, its first part only: Loserth notes that the rest of the
  manuscript's text is not on the sacrament, and is Hus's).

**Sigla. ⚠ They differ between the two works.** *De eucharistia* (Introd. §5): **A** Vienna 3927;
**B** Vienna 3932; **C** Vienna 1387 ("the best of the three" Vienna MSS, correcting A and B); **D** Prague
Univ. IV D 22 (1404–05, Andreas of Kouřim); **E** Prague Univ. XI E 3 (1460; Loserth: "the best and most
reliable text of all the five"). Loserth's lettering "does not in any way refer to their respective
value". The Vienna MSS are one group, the Prague MSS another. *De penitencia* (Introd. §6): **A** Vienna
3927; **B** Bautzen, Gersdorf Library M. St. 80.7; **C** Vienna 1337; **D** Prague Univ. III G 11; **E**
Vienna 3980; **F** Vienna 4527. Cite variants with the sigla of the work you are in. Marginal notes in the
manuscripts that Loserth prints ("F in marg.", "DB in marg.") are reception: an **R** note where they
show how a reader took the text.

**The page foot** (in your brief as "apparatus", by page) has two blocks, both keyed to the **line**:
the variants (`19. C: ecclesie in marg. alia manu.`) and Loserth's source references. The OCR of it is
rough: read it for sense, not letter by letter, and read the plate foot where a variant matters.

## The four error classes (Thakkar) — check every sentence

1. **Terms:** GLOSSARY.md governs, one term one rendering. Tag the **first occurrence in the chunk** of
   every §A–C term: `[accident]{g:accidens}`. The key must exist in GLOSSARY.md. A term that looks
   technical but has no row: render it sensibly, untagged, and propose it in your report.
   **CONTEXTUAL** rows hold two senses English splits: choose by context and write a **C** note at the
   first occurrence in the chunk and wherever it leans to the other sense (the dikaiosune rule,
   CONVENTIONS §6).
   Rows that already exist and bear on this volume: *accidens* "accident" · *accidens absolutum*
   "absolute accident" · *transsubstanciacio* "transubstantiation" · *conficere* "consecrate (the
   eucharist)" · *figura / figuratum* "figure / thing figured" · *res sacramenti* "reality of the
   sacrament" · *sacramentalia* "sacramentals" · *quantus* "quantified" · *per se / per accidens* "of
   itself / by accident" · *sensibile commune* "common sensible" · *ecclesia* church (**lower-case**) ·
   *caritas* charity · *sentencia* teaching (and *sentencia verborum* "sense", a separate row) · *ex fide* as faith teaches ·
   *opinio* opinion · *novelli* the newer [doctors] · *sancti* the saints · *concludere* infer ("conclude" only where a
   conclusion is literally drawn) · *deduccio* deduction · *maneries* kind · *principaliter* as a main head ·
   *sensibilis* perceptible · *declarare / ostendere* make clear / show · *ecclesia* is tagged at its first
   occurrence in every chunk.
   **This volume's vocabulary is now ruled (2026-11-06, `prol-a` review):** *sacramentum* (the eucharist) ·
   *hostia* host · *panis / vinum* bread / wine · *calix* chalice · *corpus Christi / Domini* kept apart ·
   *remanencia* the remaining · *accidens sine subiecto* · *substancia · quantitas · qualitas* · *esse /
   esse sacramentale* being / sacramental being · *esus / manducacio* eating · *confeccio* consecration ·
   *secta signorum* · *novella / antiqua ecclesia* · *predicacio ydemptica / tropica* identical / tropical
   predication. **The mode adverbs, one word each, never merged, each tagged at first occurrence:**
   *sacramentaliter* sacramentally · *realiter* really (never "actually") · *vere* truly (never "really") ·
   *spiritualiter* spiritually · *virtualiter* virtually · *figurative* figuratively · *tropice* tropically ·
   *ydemptice* identically · *invisibiliter* invisibly · *sensibiliter* perceptibly · *corporaliter* bodily ·
   *substancialiter* substantially · *dimensionaliter* dimensionally. A term still missing a row (*ydemptificacio*,
   *conversio*, *fractio*, *confessio auricularis*, *absolucio*, *claves* …): render it sensibly, untagged, and
   propose it. *Ego Berengarius* stays as the decree's title (Wyclif's spelling *Beringarius* in the Latin;
   "Berengar" for the man in the English). In ch. 1, *res sacramenti* "reality of the sacrament" is a ruled
   row: the compendium's *de rebus huius sacramenti* (1.3) is the three-membered distinction the chapter sets
   out, so keep its *res* consistent with that row.
2. **Connectives carry the argument.** ⛔ Never omit one, never turn one into filler.

   | Latin | English | Never |
   |---|---|---|
   | enim, nam | for | "now", "indeed" |
   | ergo, igitur, ideo | therefore, so, then | "now", "and", "indeed" |
   | sed / autem, marking the **minor premiss or next step** | now | (omitted) |
   | sed / autem, adversative | but | (omitted) |
   | autem, continuative | and | (omitted), and **not "now"** |
   | cum (concessive / causal) | although / since | "whenever" |
   | tamen | yet, nevertheless | "therefore" |
   | item | again, also | "now" |
   | vero (continuative) | and; but (adversative) | "now", "truly" |
   | verumtamen | nevertheless | "but truly" |
   | unde | hence, so | "whence" (archaic), "where" |
   | per idem | by the same token | "in the same way" |
   | ymmo | indeed, rather (correcting upward) | (omitted) |
   | patet; constat | it is clear; it is evident | |

   "Now" is reserved for the minor premiss, or the step that brings a new premiss to bear. An *autem*
   that only extends the same step is "and". ⚠ The commonest error in *De ecclesia* (close-out, ~50 of
   69 connective fixes): a *sed* that brings the minor premiss of a syllogism closed by *ergo / ideo /
   igitur* rendered "but". Before writing "but" for a *sed*, look ahead for the *ergo*: if one follows,
   it is "now".
3. **Ordinary words with a medieval sense:** GLOSSARY §D; look up any word whose classical and
   Wyclifite senses may differ, and propose additions.
4. **Syntax:** construe before rendering (main verb, subject, what every relative and genitive hangs
   on). Keep quantifier order where it is meaning. Keep Wyclif's long periods where English tolerates
   them. **A doubtful construal is never chosen silently:** translate the likelier reading and write a
   **C** note giving the alternative.
   ⚠ **Predication is the matter here, as in *De incarnacione*.** *Hoc est corpus meum*, *panis est
   corpus Christi*, *hoc sacramentum est panis*: Wyclif argues about what the pronoun demonstrates and
   in what mode the predicate holds. Keep subject and predicate in his order, keep *est* as "is" (never
   "exists", "becomes" or "represents" unless the Latin says so), and keep the mode adverb where he puts
   it (*est sacramentaliter corpus Christi* "is sacramentally the body of Christ"). ⛔ Never soften "is"
   into "signifies" or harden "figuratively" into "really": the difference is the treatise's matter.
   Demonstrative pronouns (*hoc*, *istud*) under discussion: render "this", and keep their gender
   visible in a C note where the argument turns on it (*hoc* neuter against *hic*, 348).
   **Propositions in indirect speech** whose sense is under discussion: render as the quoted proposition
   ("who say 'this is an accident without a subject'"), not "who say that …", which asserts it.

## Glossary (the 389 rows these chunks use; the full file is GLOSSARY.md)

# GLOSSARY — FROZEN 2026-09-28 (Wilson's pilot review)

*One Latin term, one English rendering, every time, across the whole corpus. A reader must be able
to recognise the same word coming back, because Wyclif's arguments turn on it. A rendering changes
only by a dated decision in CONVENTIONS' decision log, never silently in a chunk. Entries may be
*added* by recorded decision as new terms appear. **Tag keys:** a chunk tags a term as
`[English]{g:latin-form}` (CONVENTIONS §11), and the key must match a Latin form in one of the rows
below. These rows are also the source of the site's hover glossary. Where a term has two senses,
the entry says so and each chunk's note picks one. A term marked **CONTEXTUAL** holds together
senses English splits: its English is chosen by context, under the dikaiosune rule (CONVENTIONS §6).*

**Sources of the decisions.** "Pilot" = settled in `pilots/` this session. "T. p. N" = Mark
Thakkar, "Duces caecorum," *Vivarium* 58 (2020), page N (`reference/`), which documents the
errors of the two most recent translations. His corrections are adopted here unless an entry says
otherwise.

**Orthography.** Headwords are given in the Society editions' medieval spelling (*eciam*,
*racio*, *quiditas*, *ymmo*), because that is what the translator will meet on the page.

# *De eucharistia*, chapter 1, part 2: [TITLE]

## ¶1 · 15.27–16.29

> [p. 15] Hic dicitur quod primum non sequitur, sed destructo errore blasphemie servaretur et comprehenderetur sacerdotalis potestas in suis limitibus. Nichil enim horribilius quam quod quilibet sacerdos celebrans [p. 16] facit vel consecrat cotidie corpus Christi; nam Deus noster non est Deus recens nec corpus suum cum sit summe sacrum atque perpetuum est sic sacramentale vel noviter faciendum, sed nos sacerdotes facimus et benedicimus hostiam consecratam que non est corpus dominicum sed efficax eius signum. Ista autem benediccio est maior quam benediccio calicis vel alterius ornamenti strictissime episcopo reservata, cuius causam Deus novit. Satis ergo est ad honorem et potenciam sacerdotis quod ipse benedicit, consecrat et conficit sacramentum; quod est hostia consecrata et non corpus Christi sed eius signum vel tegumentum; unde quia sacerdos non habet potestatem faciendi hoc sacramentum nisi ministeriale Deo principaliter faciente, ideo dicitur conficere non corpus Christi sed nudum sacramentum, et sic propter sanctitatem vite et non propter illud adeo est laudandus. Et sic missa sacerdotis sancti est devocius audienda et missa peccatoris notorii est fugienda. Nec emimus suffragia sacerdotum pecunia sed sine convencione civili retribuimus eis vite necessaria, sicut Apostolus docet esse faciendum cum predicatoribus I Cor. IX. Nota tamen quod sicut laudative non effective benedicimus tam Deo quam domino, sic et benedicimus corpori Christi et sanguini, non faciendo ipsum esse beatum vel sanctum, sed laudando et promulgando sanctitatem et beatitudinem quam Deus in corpore suo instituit, et sic ymmolamus Christum et ipsum offerimus Deo patri.

[ENGLISH]

## ¶2 · 16.30–17.22

> [p. 16] Nota eciam ulterius ad accepcionem spiritualem corporis Christi quod non consistit in corporali accepcione, [p. 17] masticacione vel taccione hostie consecrate, sed in pastione anime ex fructuosa fide secundum quam nutritur spiritus noster in Domino. Et propter ignoranciam istius manducacionis corporis Christi et pocionis sui sanguinis multi discipuli abierunt retrorsum, ut patet Joh. VI°, 61: Durus, inquiunt, est iste sermo et quis potest eum audire? Nichil enim horribilius quam necessario manducare carnaliter carnem et bibere carnaliter sanguinem hominis tam tenere predilecti; ideo dicit Christus apostolis qui ultra indignos discipulos qui retrorsum abierant digni erant informari in sensu vivifico, quod sensus carnalis istius non proderit, sed spiritus est, hoc est spiritualis sensus, qui vivificat, ubi occasione male accepta sensus alius scandalizat. Oportet ergo fidelem credere quod illud quo anima pascitur obiective est spiritualis cibus anime; et sic oportet carnem Christi et sanguinem tam varie pro peccatoribus tribulatis accendere in nostris spiritibus amorem qui est cibus anime ut grate faciamus quodammodo recompensam. Et sic intelligit Veritas de manducacione et potacione spiritualibus quas oportet esse in quolibet salvandorum.

[ENGLISH]

## ¶3 · 17.23–17.30

> [p. 17] Unde Augustinus in Omelia: Hoc, inquit, est manducare illam escam et illum bibere potum, in Christo manere et illum manentem in se habere. Ac per hoc qui non manet in Christo et in quo non manet Christus, procul dubio non manducat spiritualiter eius carnem, licet carnaliter et visibiliter premat sacramenta corporis et sanguinis Christi, sed magis tante rei sacramentum ad iudicium sibi manducat et bibit.

[ENGLISH]

## ¶4 · 18.1–18.13

> [p. 18] Et sic ex verbis Christi et sui discipuli Augustini tria notabilia sunt notanda, primo quod caro Christi et sanguis solum spiritualiter solent comedi; nam Deus ordinavit eternaliter quod sanctus suus non videret corrupcionem in aliquo membro suo. Patet secundo quod nec bestia nec prescitus manducat sic corpus Christi, licet manducet corporaliter sacramentum. Et patet tercio mira subtilitas verborum doctoris qua dicit non quod indignus visibiliter premit dentibus corpus Christi sed quod visibiliter premit dentibus sacramentum corporis Christi et sanguinis. Illud enim sacramentum valde distinguitur a corpore Christi quod est res huius sacramenti.

[ENGLISH]

## ¶5 · 18.14–18.20

> [p. 18] Unde notanda est differencia quam ponit Augustinus inter esum carnalem et spiritualem. Nam in esu carnali comestum cedit in nutrimentum edentis, cum inbibitur eius membris. In comestione vero spirituali est econtra, cum comedens corpus Christi spiritualiter exhinc incorporatur membris ecclesie et sic Christo; comestus autem spiritualiter superat comedentem.

[ENGLISH]

## ¶6 · 18.21–18.29

> [p. 18] Nec valent argucie et ficticie sompniate contra istam sentenciam. Arguunt enim quidam quod, si homo sentit hominem, dum sentit eius indumenta ex motu vite latentis intrinsecus, quod per idem sentit corpus Christi, dum sentit sacramentum ex eius mocione. Sed ista argucia procedens per locum a simili argueret quod homo videt Deum et animam et sic de qualibet quinque sensacionum, quando sentit subiectum ex eorum mocione vitali intrinseca.

[ENGLISH]

## ¶7 · 18.30–19.8

> [p. 18] Conceditur ergo quod contingit sentire subiectum per accidens modo quo dicitur, sed tunc oportet sensum communem vel aliquem sensum intrinsecum [p. 19] comprehendere subiectum ex mocione per se sensibilis adiacentis. Sed modo nullus sensus hominis sed pure intellectus per fidem percipit corpus Christi; quod patet ex hoc quod commixta hostia consecrata cum aliis homo non plus discernit eam ab aliis subducta noticia fidei quam facit bestia. Sicut ergo bestia non sufficit apprehendere hanc rem sacramenti sub tali habitudine, ita nec sensus hominis.

[ENGLISH]

## ¶8 · 19.9–19.18

> [p. 19] Secundo sompniatur quod multi erant sancti qui tenuerunt quod sencierunt visu, auditu, olfactu, gustu et tactu corpus Christi in hostia consecrata. Illis ergo est credendum. Sicut, inquiunt, videmus in sacramento Eukaristie regem regum, sic audimus eum in fractura hostie, olfacimus eum in redolencia forme panis vel vini, gustamus eum quando inglutimus venerabile sacramentum et tangimus eum quando palpando hostiam trahimus vel tractamus digitis familiariter sacramentum.

[ENGLISH]

# *De eucharistia*, chapter 1, part 3: [TITLE]

## ¶1 · 19.19–20.17

> [p. 19] Quantum ad istud patet quod fingitur assumptum, sicut nunc finguntur miracula et nunc ex veris miraculis propter ignoranciam commune falsum creditur, ut audivi quendam fingere quomodo hostia de altari paulatim descendit ad ventrem ecclesie et ingressum est cor cuiusdam infirmi qui devote et publice professus est sic inquiens: Tu Deus nosti quod reverenter subducta infirmitate te sumerem, sed non infirmitas mentis, ymmo infirmitas corporis me retardat. Hostia vero fisso pectore cum corde infirmi surrepsit in cordis ventriculum, et sic infirmus constitutus est [p. 20] subito totus sanus. Et postquam narrator iste ex narracione et populi devocione fuit ad partem a quodam familiari socio commendatus, confessus est mendacium hoc turpe: Os finxit, inquit, hoc pulchrum mendacium. Sed esto quod fiant miracula circa hostiam consecratam (ut narrat beatus Gregorius), adhuc foret nimis extraneum noscenti consequencias ex istis inferre quod corpus Domini sit illa hostia consecrata Correspondenter dico quod, si sompniator iste noverit fidem infirmi credentis quod sentit modo dicto corpus dominicum quod non potest ab experimento vel scriptura convincere, adhuc dico quod iste credens non tunc vel propterea fuit sanctus sed, sicut sunt nimis multi, fuit ex cultu signorum Antichristi ydolatra manifestus. Revera sic posset ex signis seduci populus quod coleret talpas et vespertiliones et creaturas magis abhominabiles tanquam Deum.

[ENGLISH]

## ¶2 · 20.18–20.28

> [p. 20] Quantum ad quinque sensaciones predictas patet quod moderniores doctores negant hoc de visu, quia (ut inquiunt) nichil videtur ab oculo nisi secundum formam trianguli cuius conus est in oculo et basis in ipso visibili; sed corpus Domini cum sit multiplicatum ad omnem punctum hostie, non terminat talem basem, ideo secundum vera perspectivorum principia non sic videtur oculo corporali, in tantum quod sancti tenent nec oculum corporis glorificati nec eciam oculum Christi posse carnaliter videre corpus Christi vel sanguinem in hostia consecrata.

[ENGLISH]

## ¶3 · 21.1–21.11

> [p. 21] Item, quererem ab istis fictoribus, si in sacramento vident sensibiliter corpus Christi, utrum inibi stat vel sedet; sic enim Stephanus vidit Jesum stantem a dextris virtutis Dei (ut patet Act. VIIº, 55), nec sciunt hoc dicere ut nec primum; ideo sicut in examinacione testium sunt de mendacio convincendi. Sessio enim et alie circumstancie visibiles eque viderentur ut Christus in hostia consecrata ab oculo corporali. Sicut igitur deficiunt in circumstancia satis sensibili, sic in sensacione corporis Christi quam fingunt.

[ENGLISH]

## ¶4 · 21.12–21.29

> [p. 21] Item, si tam clare intuentur corpus Christi in sacramento altaris (ut inquiunt), queratur cuius coloris aut quantitatis et figure est corpus Christi ibidem; et oportet eos dicere quod in hostia una sit albius, in alia autem fuscius, in una maius, in alia minus, secundum proprietates accidentales hostie consecrate; et sic Deus noster foret in una hostia plene rotundus et integer, et in alia hostia oblongus et fractus, et cum corpus non videtur sic accidentatum nisi per accidens quod in eo fuerit subiective, oportet dicere quod corpus Christi subiacet cuilibet huiusmodi accidentalitati, et sic Deus noster foret cotidie quantumcunque varie contrarie et inhonorifice transmutatus, quia sacerdos alteraret eum a colore celesti, a figura humana et ceteris accidentibus que decent Christum in gloria ad turpia accidencia absoluta. Sed quid magis paganicum quam quod nos possumus figurare ad libitum Deum nostrum?

[ENGLISH]

## ¶5 · 21.30–22.20

> [p. 21] Et conformiter obicitur de auditu: Si enim audimus Dominum in fractura hostie, cum ipsa non sit nisi fraccio corporis quod auditur, oportet concedere quod [p. 22] Christus frangitur; quod est horribilius quam opus militum, quia ut dicitur Joh. XIXº, 33: Ad Jesum autem cum venissent et viderent eum iam mortuum, non fregerunt eius crura, quia Exod. XIIº, 46 de agno pascali figurante Christum precipitur: Os non comminuetis ex eo; multo magis ergo de corpore Christi vivo et glorificato christiani non frangerent eius membra. Sicut ergo facta collisione membrorum vel lapidum exhinc non auditur Deus vel anima, sic senciendum est de corpore Christi fracta hostia. Aliter enim sonus articulatus per organa dignissima foret nimis raucus et illiteratus. Oportet ergo dare duriciem in hostia cum aliis accidentibus non subiectatis in corpore Christi, ex quibus cum aere reverberante efficitur talis sonus; et sic senciendum est de tribus aliis sensibus, scilicet olfactu, gustu et tactu. Nichil enim paganius quam quod putrefacta hostia olet Deus, acefacto sacramento calicis acesceret Deus, et sic Deus noster in ecclesiis ruralibus fetet et acet, sed in civitatibus est sapidus atque recens.

[ENGLISH]

# *De eucharistia*, chapter 1, part 4: [TITLE]

## ¶1 · 22.21–24.9

> [p. 22] Nec docetur racio quare corpus Christi in mutando visum videtur eadem visione qua hostia, quin per idem in mutando alios sensus sentitur conformiter sicut hostia. Et revera tam fidelis quam infidelis horreret de concessione conclusionum quas oportet sic errantes concedere in ista materia; ideo beati qui non viderunt et crediderunt eorum opposita. Quid enim horribilius quam quod sacerdos post celebracionem portat corporaliter Deum in extremitatibus digitorum et quidquid tetigerit cum illis digitis facit [p. 23] corpus dominicum illud tangere? Non enim tollit locio quin sacramentum imbibitum remaneat secundum partes subtiles in corpore continente. Cum ergo remanent odor et sapor, patet naturalibus quod oportet subiectum corporeum remanere. Et patet ista sentencia secundum leges ecclesie; nam in De Consecracione, distinccione II, sub verbis Augustini sic scribitur: Nos autem sub specie panis et vini quam videmus res invisibiles, id est, carnem Christi et sanguinem honoramus. Ex quibus patenter patet quod illa species sensibilis quam sacramentum dicimus non est corpus Christi vel sanguis. Et idem patet eadem distinccione sub auctoritate Ambrosii Forte dicis: Similitudinem, inquit, non video carnis, non video sanguinis veritatem. Verumptamen, si viderem sanguinis Christi essenciam, viderem per idem omnem quantitatem vel qualitatem sensibilem, que informat corpus Christi in toto; quod est tercio expresse contra eundem Augustinum eadem distinccione Prima quidem et sepe inferius recitatum, quomodo sanctus doctor exponit dictum Christi Joh. VIº, 67, propter quod multi discipuli abierunt retrorsum; quod debet intelligi spiritualiter, non quod morsibus corporalibus comesturi erant corpus quod oportet esse sursum in eodem loco et figura inmobile, donec seculum finiatur, sed spiritualiter per idem non videret corporaliter corpus Christi. Unde sequitur huius sancti sentencia sub hiis verbis, De Consecracione, distinccione II: Non hoc corpus quod videtis manducaturi [p. 24] estis et bibituri illum sanguinem quem effusuri sunt illi qui me crucifigent, et post detegens equivocacionem sic loquitur: ipsum quidem et non ipsum, ipsum invisibiliter, non ipsum visibiliter; necesse est, inquit, celebrari illud visibiliter, necesse est tamen invisibiliter intelligi. Multa sunt similia dicta sanctorum et legum ecclesie que oportet fidelem credere et per consequens in eis propter carnales hereticos informari.

[ENGLISH]

## ¶2 · 24.10–24.18

> [p. 24] Sed contra ista arguitur per easdem leges ecclesie; nam in De Consecracione, distinccione II, dicit Pius papa: Si per negligenciam aliquid de sanguine stillaverit super terram, lingua lambetur; si super tabulam, tabula radetur, sicut terra vel aliud ipsum recipiens, et igne consumetur et cinis iuxta altare condatur. Et multe sunt leges talis sentencie que omnes sonant quod illud sensibile liquidum et fluxibile contentum in calice est realiter sanguis Christi.

[ENGLISH]

## ¶3 · 24.19–24.29

> [p. 24] Hic dicitur quod multa dicta decretorum oportet sane intelligere ad sensum quem debent facere; unde figurative vel mistice sepe in locucionibus istius materie figuratum intelligitur per figuram, ut sicut grammaticus dicit quod dominus est nomen, et loycus quod hoc signum homo est species, sic dicit theologus quod panis est corpus Christi et vinum eius sanguis, quod oportet figurative intelligi signo supposito pro signato. Si autem doctores signorum intelligant predicacionem secundum essenciam vel non figurativam sed formalem, est error in ianuis.

[ENGLISH]

## ¶4 · 24.30–25.18

> [p. 24] Et in ista equivocacione laborant multi. Unde absit katholicum credere quod propter effusionem huiusmodi [p. 25] sacramenti in calicem exhinc sanguis Domini effundatur. Tunc enim non preciperet ecclesia sanguinem illum comburere et cineres combusti Domini in ecclesia in detestacionem tam horrendi facinoris tamquam reliquias remanere; sed loquitur de nudo sacramento quod figurative nominat rem signatam modo quo posset vocare quamlibet creaturam Deum suum, cum sit signum efficax Dei sui. Tales enim locuciones tropicas non debemus nos admittere generaliter, quia sic diceremus quod Christus cotidie effundit per multos cados plus de sanguine quam umquam habuit hic in terris, et totidem racione multitudinis sui sanguinis foret complexione intemperatus, cum acquireret cotidie humores grossos inglorificatos cum aliis inconvenientibus reducendis; et sic glosari debet cantus ecclesie, dum dicit Quod fit vinum sanguis Christi, Etsi sensus deficit.

[ENGLISH]
