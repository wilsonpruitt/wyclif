# *De potestate pape*, chapter 9, part 7: human election, the Roman church, and the conditional law

## ¶1 · 214.3–215.17

> [p. 214] Et quantum ad implicatum de eleccione humana patet ex sepe dictis, quod confirmatur ex lege triplici; primo docetur LXXIX distinccione: Si quis pecunia vel gracia humana vel tumultu populari sine canonica eleccione fuerit intronizatus, non apostolicus sed apostaticus habeatur. Cum igitur sit maior error in eleccione activa, quando eligentes coacti sunt a dyabolo eligere personam quam Deus reprobat, quod patule patet ex opere et omissione papalis officii, multo magis vel a pari sequitur, quod electus talis non apostolicus sed apostaticus est habendus. Non enim licet humanitus reprobare, ut ex fide supponitur, nisi ut Deus ex lege sua docuerit reprobandum; nec valet vel deficit humana eleccio nisi de quanto eleccioni divine consentit vel discrepat. Ex quo patet, cum stat electores cogi humanitus ad eligendum aput Deum ydoneum, quod dicta lex debet intelligi, quando militaris vel popularis tumultus cogit ad eligendum inhabilem, quare igitur non, si dyabolus vel peccatum collegii electorum et observatis humanis ritibus cogit ad eligendum tam inhabilem, eque non apostolicus sed apostaticus habendus est? Nec est probacio legis humane tam valida, sicut sensibilis probacio docta ex vita prepositi: nam maior capta ex lege Domini et minor capta ad sensum ex vita prepositi, concludunt cercius, quam evidencie palliate ex humanis legibus. Secundo docetur eadem sentencia ex lege Jeronymi distinccione [p. 215] XL sic loquentis: Non est facile stare in loco Petri et Pauli et tenere cathedram, quia non sanctorum filii sunt, qui tenent loca sanctorum, sed qui exercent opera eorum. Ex quo patet quod nemo sit papa, nisi sit Christi et Petri filius, imitando eos in moribus, sed sicut non subiacet potestati eleccionis humane hoc facere, sic nec subiacet potestati humane papam constituere. Si autem vocetur papa quicumque quem occidentalis ecclesia acceptat ad capitaliter decidendum causas ecclesie, ad precipiendum fidelibus infundabiliter et blasfeme pro congregandis pecuniis et mundanis honoribus, abusio est termini infundabilis, iuxta quam oportet in casu concedere, quod maximus hereticus et antichristus sit papa, sed et rudissimus laycus vel femella; et periret fides vel religio capiti ecclesie adhibenda.

And as to what is implied about human election, it is clear from what has often been said; and this is confirmed from a threefold law. First, it is taught in distinction 79: "If anyone has been enthroned by money or human favour or popular tumult without canonical election, let him be held not apostolic but apostatic."[^1] Since, therefore, there is a greater error in active election when the electors are compelled by the devil to elect a person whom God reproves (which is openly clear from the doing and the omission of the papal office), it follows much more, or equally, that such a man elected is to be held not apostolic but apostatic. For it is not lawful to reprove humanly, as is supposed [as faith teaches]{g:ex fide}, except as God has taught by his law that one is to be reproved; nor does human election avail or fail except in so far as it agrees with or departs from divine election. From this it is clear, since it is possible for electors to be compelled humanly to elect one who is fit in God's sight, that the said law must be understood [of the case] when military or popular tumult compels the election of an [unqualified]{g:inhabilis} man. Why then, if the devil or the sin of the college of electors, even with the human rites observed, compels the election of a man so unqualified, is he not equally to be held not apostolic but apostatic?[^2] Nor is the proof from human law so strong as the [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} proof taught from the life of the [superior]{g:prepositi}: for a major taken from the law of the Lord and a minor taken by sense from the life of the superior [infer]{g:concludere} more certainly than [evidences]{g:evidencia} [cloaked]{g:palliare} from human laws. Secondly, the same [teaching]{g:sentencia} is taught from the law of Jerome in distinction [215] 40, speaking thus: "It is not easy to stand in the place of Peter and Paul and to hold the chair, for it is not the sons of saints who hold the places of saints, but those who do their works."[^3] From this it is clear that no one is [pope]{g:papa} unless he is a son of Christ and of Peter, imitating them in conduct; but just as it does not lie in the power of human election to bring this about, so neither does it lie in human [power]{g:potestas} to constitute a pope. But if whoever the western [church]{g:ecclesia} accepts to decide the church's cases [as head]{g:capitaliter}, to command the faithful [without foundation]{g:infundabiliter} and blasphemously for the gathering of money and worldly honours, is called pope, it is an abuse of the term, without foundation;[^4] by which one must grant, in the case, that the greatest heretic and antichrist is pope, and even the rudest layman or a little woman; and the faith or religion to be given to the [head]{g:caput} of the church would perish.

## ¶2 · 215.19–217.13

> [p. 215] Unde notaremus cronicas predictas, quomodo inolevit papalis dignitas. Et narrat dominus Ardmacanus libro VII cap. XXIV de questionibus Armenorum, quomodo imperator Constantinus circa annum domini trecentesimum primum hoc censuit et precepit, quod suus episcopus ab omnibus papa vocaretur. Et Foca imperator circa annum domini sexcentesimum hoc idem ex cleri instancia confirmavit, ut legitur in suis annalibus, quomodo igitur saltaret in summum sacramentum ecclesie, quod imperator terrenus tam irreligiose instituit, ymo ut inquit: Si imperator Thartarie ecclesiam de Cambalek aut de Cathay conversus ad [p. 216] christianismum caput omnium aliarum ecclesiarum constitueret, cederet ceteris paribus capitalitas Romane ecclesie. Horribile itaque atque necessario defectibile fundamentum ecclesie a seculari principe ita inductum. Numquid credimus quod humana eleccio plus iuris per se tribuit quam Christi eleccio? Christi autem eleccio non est per se sufficiens ad constituendum quamcumque partem ecclesie, cum requiritur eterna predestinacio cum gracia consumante. Et hinc credo quod ille qui nichil facit ex indifferencia reliquit nobis exemplum ad consenciendum isti sentencie per hoc quod ipsemet elegit dyabolum. Joh. VI, 71 dicitur: Nonne duodecim vos elegi et unus ex vobis dyabolus est? Tercio docetur eadem sentencia ex decreto beati Jeronymi posito XXIV questio I: Quoniam vetus oriens, ubi dicens mistice multa commendabilia de Romana ecclesia subiungit condicionem, quam oportet de ipsa subintelligi: Fatiscat, inquit, invidia Romani culminis, recedat ambicio; cum successore piscatoris et discipulo Christi loquor: Et concludit in fine. Quicumque tecum non colligit, spergit, hoc est qui Christi est, Antichristi est. In qua lege sunt tria notanda, primo quod Romana ecclesia est ubicumque habitaverit universitas fidelium viatorum, sic quod si iam non esset alius Romanus christianus, adhuc fideles conversi a quocumque apostolo, quia sunt discipuli Petri et Pauli, qui fuerunt Romani, possunt dici Romana ecclesia; tanta namque fuit Christi et omnium apostolorum concors [p. 217] amicicia, quod christiano sequente unum eorum in moribus, sequitur eorum quemlibet cuiuslibet eorum in gradu suo discipulus. Satis est, cum et omnino oportet quod christianus sit Christi discipulus, et sic nitetur explanacio beati Jeronymi in fine addita, quando dicitur: Quicumque non colligit cum Romano pontifice spargit, hoc est, inquit, qui Christi non est, Antichristi est, non tamen quod Romana ecclesia sepe sumitur pro qualicumque papa et suis cardinalibus, ubicumque habitaverint, quomodolibet conversando, sed ista significacio seculariter et infundabiliter est extorta, et ideo nimirum intricat ecclesiam.

Hence we should note the chronicles mentioned before, how the papal dignity grew up. And [the lord of Armagh]{g:dominus Ardmacanus} relates in book 7, chapter 24 of the *Questions of the Armenians* how the emperor Constantine, about the year of the Lord 301, decreed and commanded this: that his bishop should be called pope by all.[^5] And the emperor Phocas, about the year of the Lord 600, confirmed this same thing at the [urging]{g:instancia} of the clergy, as is read in his annals.[^6] How then would what an earthly emperor so irreligiously instituted leap up into the highest sacrament of the church?[^7] Indeed, as he says: "If the emperor of Tartary, converted to [216] Christianity, were to constitute the church of Cambalek or of Cathay head of all the other churches, the capital standing of the Roman church would give way, other things being equal."[^8] Horrible, then, and of necessity bound to fail, is a foundation of the church brought in in this way by a secular prince. Do we believe that human election of itself confers more [right]{g:ius} than Christ's election? Now Christ's election is not of itself sufficient to constitute any part of the church whatever, since eternal predestination is required, with [consummating grace]{g:gracia consumans}. And hence I believe that he who does nothing at random left us an example for assenting to this teaching, in that he himself chose a devil.[^9] In John 6:71 it is said: "Have not I chosen you twelve? And one of you is a devil." Thirdly, the same teaching is taught from the decree of blessed Jerome placed in Causa 24, question 1, *Quoniam vetus oriens*, where, saying mystically many commendable things of the Roman church, he adds a condition which must be [understood as implied]{g:subintelligere} of it: "Let envy of Roman eminence give way," he says, "let ambition withdraw; I speak with the successor of the fisherman and the disciple of Christ." And he concludes at the end: "Whoever gathereth not with thee, scattereth; that is, he who is [not] Christ's is Antichrist's."[^10] In this law three things are to be noted. First, that the Roman church is wherever [the whole body of the faithful]{g:universitas fidelium} who are [wayfarers]{g:viator} dwells; so that if there were now no other Roman Christian, the faithful converted by any apostle whatever, since they are disciples of Peter and Paul, who were Romans, could still be called the Roman church. For so great was the concordant [217] friendship of Christ and of all the apostles that, when a Christian follows one of them in conduct, he follows every one of them, a disciple of each of them in his degree.[^11] It is enough, since it is altogether necessary too that a Christian be Christ's disciple; and on this will rest the explanation of blessed Jerome added at the end, where it is said: "Whoever gathereth not with the [Roman pontiff]{g:Romanus pontifex}, scattereth; that is," he says, "he who is not Christ's is Antichrist's." Yet not as "Roman church" is often [taken for]{g:sumitur pro} any pope whatever and his cardinals, wherever they dwell and however they conduct themselves; but that signification has been extorted in a secular way and without foundation, and therefore no wonder it entangles the church.

## ¶3 · 217.14–218.6

> [p. 217] Secundo notandum ex dicto beati Jeronymi quod quicumque diviserit contra Christum est Antichristus, cuiusmodi sunt superbi et specialiter sacerdotes cesarei legi Christi contrarii. Nam secundum beatum Gregorium nullus in ecclesia magis nocet, ut allegatum est ex secundo cap. primi De Cura Pastorali, et per consequens nullus est nequior Antichristus. Et isto modo loquitur decretum de Antichristo sub autoritate Gregorii, XVI questio VII: Pervenit ad nos, ubi dicitur, quod quidam episcopi consumunt bona pauperum tribus modis: primo in militibus et aliis laycis, qui ipsos fovent et protegunt in gloria seculari, secundo in monstruosa familia et tercio quod est gravius in consanguineis. Et ex diffinicione sancti talis episcopus inter maximos here: oticos et antichristos non minimus habeatur, et forte si omnes episcopi hodie in Anglia cum religiosis et secularibus eis consentaneis essent signati in fronte signo bestie, pateret quod longe [p. 218] plures quam sunt catholici ex parte Christi forent infideles, quia heretici et realiter antichristi. Sic autem loquuntur tot sancti doctores de antichristis et hereticis cum lege ecclesie et scriptura. Alias autem significaciones somniant ut heretici et antichristi absconsius delitescant.

Secondly, it is to be noted from the saying of blessed Jerome that whoever divides against Christ is Antichrist; of which kind are the proud, and especially [caesarean priests]{g:sacerdos cesarius} contrary to the law of Christ. For according to blessed Gregory no one in the church does more harm, as was [alleged]{g:allegare} from the second chapter of the first [part] of the *Pastoral Care*; and consequently no one is a more wicked Antichrist.[^12] And in this way the decree speaks of Antichrist under the authority of Gregory, Causa 16, question 7, *Pervenit ad nos*, where it is said that "certain bishops consume the goods of the poor in three ways: first on [knights]{g:milites} and other laymen who cherish and protect them in worldly glory, second on a monstrous [household]{g:familia}, and third, which is graver, on kinsmen."[^13] And by the saint's [definition]{g:diffinicio} such a bishop is to be held not the least among the greatest heretics and antichrists. And perhaps, if all the bishops in England today, with [the religious]{g:religiosi} and [seculars]{g:secularis} who consent with them, were marked on the forehead with the mark of the beast, it would be clear that far [218] more than there are catholics on Christ's side would be [unbelievers]{g:infidelis}, because heretics and really antichrists.[^14] And so do so many holy doctors speak of antichrists and heretics, with the law of the church and scripture. But they dream up other significations, so that heretics and antichrists may lie hidden more secretly.[^15]

## ¶4 · 218.7–218.24

> [p. 218] Tercio notandum quod omnes leges que loquntur de papatu et obediencia fienda Romane ecclesie subintelligende sunt cum condicione, quod illa ecclesia correspondenter ad nomen excellencie in ministerio faciat recompensam. Aliter enim beatus Petrus non foret capitaneus inter apostolos, ut patet ex dictis; et aliter beatus Jeronymus distinccione XL allegata cum archidiacono contradiceret sibi ipsi, cum aliter racione loci et nudi nominis consenciendum foret in casu pessimo antichristo. Sicut enim Deus non potest facere hominem album sine albedine, sic non potest hominem facere papam vel membrum ecclesie sine inducta debita qualitate, ad quod locus vel eleccio humana est impertinens, nisi de quanto consentanea est dominio ipsum vere notificans populo, quod si false promulgat, est error in ianuis.

Thirdly, it is to be noted that all the laws which speak of the papacy and of the obedience to be rendered to the Roman church are to be understood as implied with a condition: that that church make recompense in ministry corresponding to its name of excellence.[^16] For otherwise blessed Peter would not be [captain]{g:capitaneus} among the apostles, as is clear from what has been said; and otherwise blessed Jerome, in distinction 40 as alleged, together with the Archdeacon, would contradict himself, since otherwise, by reason of place and bare name, one would have to consent, in the worst case, to Antichrist.[^17] For just as God cannot make a man white without whiteness, so he cannot make a man pope or a [member]{g:membrum} of the church without the due quality being brought in; to which place or human election is [irrelevant]{g:impertinens}, except in so far as it agrees with the lordship [of God], truly making him known to the people; and if it proclaims falsely, there is error at the doors.[^18]

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** Gratian, *Decretum* D. 79 c. 9 (*Si quis pecunia*), as Loserth gives it; the form "D. 79 c. 9" is ours (LEDGER P09g-1). The pun *apostolicus / apostaticus* is the canon's; "apostatic" keeps it. Both manuscripts note in the margin *Papa apostaticus*.
[^2]: **C.** *Et observatis humanis ritibus*: "even with the human rites observed", the *et* taken as "even". The argument: the canon names outward force; inward force (the devil, the electors' sin) disqualifies as much, though the forms are kept.
[^3]: **T/S.** *Decretum* D. 40 c. 2 (*Non est facile*), as Loserth gives it (LEDGER P09g-2). Both manuscripts read *Petri et Petri*; Loserth prints *Petri et Pauli*, the canon's reading, and we translate the print.
[^4]: **C.** *Abusio est termini infundabilis*: *infundabilis* may be nominative with *abusio* ("an abuse … without foundation", taken here) or genitive with *termini* ("an abuse of a term that has no foundation"). The sense is nearly the same.
[^5]: **S.** FitzRalph, *Summa in quaestionibus Armenorum* 7.24, cited by Wyclif; Loserth gives no check (LEDGER P09g-3, to be checked). The date "301" is Wyclif's (or FitzRalph's).
[^6]: **S.** Loserth compares Higden, *Polychronicon* 5.416: Boniface "obtained from the emperor Phocas that the church of blessed Peter at Rome should be head of all the churches in the world" (LEDGER P09g-4). **C.** *In suis annalibus*: "his annals", i.e. the annals of Phocas's reign; the possessive may also refer loosely to the chronicles named above. **G.** *Ex cleri instancia*: "at the urging of the clergy" (*instancia* in its second sense).
[^7]: **C.** The subject of *saltaret* is the clause *quod imperator terrenus … instituit*. *Summum sacramentum ecclesie* is the papacy treated as a sacrament of the church; the phrase is Wyclif's irony.
[^8]: **S.** Loserth's italics make this sentence a quotation; *ut inquit* most naturally refers back to FitzRalph (LEDGER P09g-5, to be checked). Cambalek (Khanbaliq, Peking) and Cathay are the Mongol capital and China. *Capitalitas*, the abstract of *capitalis*, is rendered "capital standing" to keep it apart from *capitas* "headship".
[^9]: **C.** *Ex indifferencia*: "at random", without discrimination; that Christ does nothing at random makes his choice of Judas an intended lesson.
[^10]: **T/S.** Gratian, *Decretum* C. 24 q. 1 c. 25 (*Quoniam vetus oriens*), from Jerome, *Ep.* 15.1–2 to Damasus (LEDGER P09g-6, to be checked). The print as established reads *qui Christi est, Antichristi est*; Loserth's italic line at 216.24 has *qui Christi non est*, and Wyclif repeats it with *non* at 217.4. We supply "[not]". At 216.20 B reads *cesset* for *fatiscat*, and A has *cesset* written above. The words echo Matthew 12:30 ("he that gathereth not with me, scattereth"), turned to Damasus.
[^11]: **C.** *Sequitur eorum quemlibet* is the deponent "he follows every one of them", not the impersonal "it follows"; *cuiuslibet eorum … discipulus* stands in apposition to the subject.
[^12]: **S.** Gregory, *Regula pastoralis* 1.2: "no one does more harm in the church than one who, acting perversely, has the name or order of holiness" (LEDGER P09g-7; Loserth refers to *De cura pastorali* 1, to be checked). *Ut allegatum est*: Wyclif cited it earlier in this work.
[^13]: **S.** *Decretum* C. 16 q. 7 c. 3 (*Pervenit ad nos*), which Loserth calls "loosely quoted" (LEDGER P09g-8).
[^14]: **S.** The mark of the beast on the forehead: Apocalypse 13:16 and 14:9.
[^15]: **C.** *Somniant* has no expressed subject: it is the defenders of the papal sense of "Roman church", against whom *sic autem loquuntur … sancti doctores* is set.
[^16]: **T.** At this line the apparatus reports *sub condicione* for the print's *cum condicione*; the sense is the same.
[^17]: **R.** "The Archdeacon" is Guido de Baysio, the commentator on the *Decretum* (*Rosarium*), whose gloss on D. 40 Wyclif adds to Jerome (LEDGER P09g-9, to be checked).
[^18]: **T/C.** *Consentanea est dominio*: the print has *dominio*, "lordship"; *Domino* "the Lord" would construe more easily. We translate the print and supply "[of God]". *Error in ianuis* echoes "at the doors" (Matthew 24:33).
