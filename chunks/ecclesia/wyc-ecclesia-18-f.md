# *De ecclesia*, chapter 18, part 6: a multitude has parts; the church from the beginning of the world

## ¶1 · 435.26–436.18

> [p. 435] nec ipsa nec aliqua pars eius haberet proprie possessiones, cum non sit proprie unum totum vel aliquid, nec quisquam foret in ecclesia vel de ecclesia. Non enim in ecclesia, quia sic foret pars datorum fidelium, et sic omnes componerent aliquod unum corpus; nam si sufficit ad esse hominis vel alterius rei esse de aliquibus quod sit de numero illorum, tunc quidlibet foret de mundo et omnis prescitus vel predestinatus foret tam de prescitis quam predestinatis et quelibet [p. 436] multitudo de qualibet, et sic (ut sophiste arguunt) aliquis numerus non haberet partem vel partes ut numerus indivisibilium; aliquis autem est numerus qui habet partes et non partem, ut numerus rerum divisibilium non communicancium, aliquis vero est numerus qui habet partes et partem, ut Petrus et caput suum. Deliramenta sunt ista sophismata et modo loquendi philosophorum contraria de populo, exercitu et aliis artificialibus, sed specialiter sunt contraria scripture et sanctis doctoribus. Oportet ergo omnem talem multitudinem habere partes (sicut patet ad sensum). Nam videndo silvam, populum vel arenam videtur aliquid distincte secundum pyramidem radialem cuius basis terminatur ad multitudinem, et constat quod in casu non sic videtur una simplex pars talis multitudinis, sicut nec videtur punctus vel linea. Ideo relinquitur quod talis multitudo divisibiliter videatur, sicut habet operaciones proprias et est obiectum multorum actuum.

neither it nor any part of it would properly have possessions, since it is not properly one whole or something; nor would anyone be in the [church]{g:ecclesia} or of the church. For [he would] not [be] "in the church", since so he would be a part of the given faithful, and so all would compose some one body;[^1] for if, for a man or any other thing to be of certain [things], it suffices that he be of their number, then anything whatever would be of the world, and every [foreknown]{g:prescitus} or [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} [man] would be as much of the foreknown as of the predestinate, and any [436] multitude of any other; and so (as the sophists argue) some number would not have a part or parts, as a number of indivisibles; and some number has parts and not a part, as a number of divisible things that do not overlap; and some number has parts and a part, as Peter and his head.[^2] These [sophisms]{g:sophisma} are ravings, and contrary to the philosophers' manner of speaking of a people, an army and other artificial things; but above all they are contrary to scripture and the holy doctors. Every such multitude must therefore have parts (as is clear to sense). For in seeing a wood, a people or sand, something is seen distinctly according to the radiant pyramid whose base is bounded at the multitude; and it is evident that in that case one simple part of such a multitude is not so seen, just as neither a point nor a line is seen. Therefore it remains that such a multitude is seen divisibly, as it has operations of its own and is the object of many acts.[^3]

## ¶2 · 436.19–438.18

> [p. 436] Et confirmacio huius est quod continuacio non est nisi accidens, non faciens substanciam esse aliquid, et per idem sequitur quod vocata religio vel ritus humani non faciunt personas esse substanciam postquam erant non substancia, nec capit sensus dispositi quod conventus tam sensibilis, tam edax et tam multipliciter operativus sit indivisibiliter multiplicatus, ut quiditas hominis, nec negabitur quin talis conventus sit ecclesia particularis; et si sit nichil, tunc mutari potest per hominem ab aliquo in non aliquod et econtra. Et est obiectum sensus subiectumque accidentis, et tamen non est res obiecta vel subiecta, quod est contra grammaticam. Foret eciam omnino sine peccato vel gracia, quia nullum tale vel accidens sibi inesset et a pari nulla pars vel denominacio sibi inesset, et tunc ecclesia ex tribus hominibus et tribus angelis in nulla proporcione se [p. 437] haberet ad aliquam partem sui, quia sic una medietas foret indivisibilis et alia medietas quantitativa foret equalis suo toti, si debeat dici totum; et sic illud totum haberet quatuor medietates inequales, ut ecclesia ex quatuor hominibus inequalibus haberet duos minores homines et quatuor medietates quantitativas illorum quatuor hominum pro suis medietatibus, et sic in quacunque proporcione volueris una medietas est maior reliqua. Et mirum videtur quod pars, medietas, mundus et maior pars rerum artificialium sit nichil, nam scriptura ita Johannis V° dicit spiritum, aquam et sanguinem esse unum, et communiter patet idem de aliis aggregatis que dicuntur unum. Nam predicato per se inferiori predicatur per se superius, ut si ecclesia sit persona, tunc est racionalis nature individua substancia. Redeundo ergo ad propositum notandum est fideli quod diligenter caveat ab heresi ista recenti qua dicitur quod ante mortem Christi non fuit sancta mater ecclesia nec quod ex militante et triumphante est una totalis ecclesia constituta sed quod presciti et membra dyaboli sunt partes sancte matris ecclesie, domus Dei et speciale habitaculum trinitatis, quia (ut sepe assumpsi) Augustinus in Encheridion LVI° exponens fidem ecclesie super ista particula Sanctam ecclesiam catholicam ita scribit: Rectus itaque confessionis ordo poscebat ut trinitati subiungeretur ecclesia tamquam habitatori domus sua et Deo templum suum et conditori civitas sua; que tota hic accipienda est non solum ex parte que peregrinatur in terris a solis ortu usque ad occasum laudans nomen domini et post captivitatem vetustatis cantans canticum [p. 438] novum, verum eciam ex illa que in celis semper ex quo condita est cohesit domino nec ullum malum sui casus est experta; hec ecclesia in sanctis angelis beata persistit et sue parti peregrinanti sicut oportet opitulatur, quia utraque una erit eternitatis consorcio et nunc una est vinculo caritatis. Iste sanctus episcopus condidit ecclesie libellum quem vocavit Manuale ad finem quod fideles recte intelligant fidem suam, ubi quoad primum articulum notat quomodo immediate post eternitatem trinitatis increate, hoc est, in mundi principio secuta est sancta mater ecclesia in qua sit trinitatis habitacio et expresse duas partes illius totalis ecclesie scilicet celestem et terrenam commemorat. Unde, ut spes nostra in ista fide catholica confirmetur, ordinatur in ympno ecclesie quod postquam nulla nostra in Christo accepit cesivam in celo, et angelus conservus hominum prohibuit se adorari a Johanne, Apocalypsis penultimo, ut sic caveant fideles peregrinantes a domino:

And the confirmation of this is that continuity is nothing but an accident, not making a substance be something; and by the same token it follows that a [so-called]{g:vocatus} religion or human [rites]{g:ritus} do not make persons be a substance after they were not a substance; nor does a well-disposed sense grasp that a convent so [perceptible]{g:sensibilis}, so given to eating and so manifoldly active is indivisibly multiplied, like the [quiddity]{g:quiditas} of man; nor will it be denied that such a convent is a particular church; and if it is nothing, then it can be changed by a man from something into not-something, and conversely.[^4] And it is the object of sense and the subject of an accident, and yet it is not a thing objected or subjected, which is against grammar. It would also be altogether without sin or grace, because no such thing or accident would be in it, and by parity no part or [naming]{g:denominacio} would be in it;[^5] and then a church of three men and three angels would stand in no proportion [437] to any part of itself, because so one half would be indivisible, and the other, quantitative half would be equal to its whole, if it ought to be called a whole; and so that whole would have four unequal halves, as a church of four unequal men would have the two lesser men and the four quantitative halves of those four men for its halves; and so, in whatever proportion you please, one half is greater than the other. And it seems a wonder that a part, a half, the world and the greater part of artificial things should be nothing; for scripture says thus, in 1 John 5, that the spirit, the water and the blood are one; and commonly the same is clear of other aggregates which are called one. For when the lower is predicated per se, the higher is predicated per se: as, if the church is a person, then it is an individual substance of a rational nature.[^6] Returning therefore to the matter in hand, the faithful [man] must note that he should diligently beware of that recent heresy by which it is said that before the death of Christ there was no holy [mother]{g:mater} church, nor that one total church is constituted of the militant and the triumphant, but that the foreknown and [members]{g:membrum} of the devil are parts of holy mother church, the house of God and special dwelling of the Trinity;[^7] for (as I have often assumed) Augustine in the Enchiridion 56, expounding the faith of the church on this clause, "the holy catholic church", writes thus: "The right order of the confession therefore required that the church should be joined to the Trinity, as his house to the dweller, and his temple to God, and his city to the founder; which is here to be taken whole, not only in the part which [is a pilgrim]{g:peregrinari} on earth, from the rising of the sun to its going down praising the name of the Lord, and after the captivity of oldness singing a new [438] song, but also in that which in heaven has always, since it was founded, cleaved to the Lord, and has experienced no evil of its own fall. This church in the holy angels persists blessed, and helps its pilgrim part as it ought; for both will be one in the fellowship of eternity, and now are one in the bond of [charity]{g:caritas}."[^8] This holy bishop composed for the church a little book which he called the Manual, to the end that the faithful should rightly understand their faith; where, as regards the first [article]{g:articulus}, he notes how, immediately after the eternity of the uncreated Trinity, that is, in the beginning of the world, holy mother church followed, in which the Trinity should have its dwelling; and he expressly mentions two parts of that total church, namely the heavenly and the earthly.[^9] Whence, so that our hope may be confirmed in this catholic faith, it is set down in a hymn of the church (after our nature in Christ received [a seat] in heaven, and the angel, the fellow servant of men, forbade John to adore him, in the penultimate [chapter] of the Apocalypse), so that the faithful who are absent from the Lord may in this way beware:[^10]

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** The sentence runs on from 435.25 (the end of part 5): "it" is the church, which on the opponent's view is not properly one whole. *Non enim in ecclesia*: Wyclif takes up the first of the two phrases, "in the church", and argues that it would make each believer part of a body. *Datorum fidelium*: "of the given faithful", that is, of certain designated faithful. **T.** At 435.29 AA₁ read *doctorum fidelium*, "of the faithful doctors"; the print is translated.

[^2]: **C.** *Esse de aliquibus*: "to be of" a group, membership taken as mere inclusion in a number, which Wyclif reduces to absurdity. The sophists' three cases: a number of indivisibles has neither part nor parts; a number of divisible things that do not overlap (*non communicancium*) has parts but no one part; Peter and his head have parts and a part, since the head is a part of Peter.

[^3]: **C.** *Pyramidem radialem*: the perspectivists' visual pyramid, with its apex in the eye and its base on the thing seen; since a point or a line is not seen, what is seen of a wood or a crowd is the multitude itself, seen part by part. *Sicut habet operaciones proprias*: taken as "as [also] it has", a further ground. **T.** At 436.16 B reads *nec linea*.

[^4]: **C.** *Continuacio*: the continuity of a body's parts. If continuity, an accident, does not make a substance be something, neither does discontinuity make a group nothing. *Vocata religio*: a religious order, "so-called" in Wyclif's sense. *Edax*: given to eating. *Ut quiditas hominis*: a universal, which is wholly in each of its many instances. **R.** A in the margin at 436.19: *Nota*; B in the margin: *Nota quod continuacio non facit aliquod corpus esse unum*. **T.** At 436.24 B reads *quam edax*; AA₁ omit *sit … conventus*.

[^5]: **C.** *Res obiecta vel subiecta*: Wyclif plays on the participles of *obicere* and *subicere*; what is an object and a subject must be a thing "objected" and "subjected". *A pari*: "by parity"; the clause stands without a connective after *inesset*. *Peccatum vel gracia* are accidents of the soul; a nothing can bear no accident.

[^6]: **S.** 1 John 5:8 (Wyclif "Johannis V°"); DR "and these three are one". The definition of a person paraphrases Boethius, *Contra Eutychen* 3: *naturae rationabilis individua subsistentia* (PL 64; Wyclif *substancia*; LEDGER E18f-1). **C.** *Predicato per se inferiori*: the ablative absolute, "the lower being predicated per se". If the church were a person, it would be a rational substance. **T.** At 437.13–14 B reads *predicatur* for *predicato*.

[^7]: **C.** The three tenets of the "recent heresy" are reported as the opponent's; Wyclif names no one. **R.** A in the margin at 437.17: *Nota bene*; at 437.19, below the folio mark: *Longe ante Christi mortem fuit sancta ecclesia de qua non sunt presciti*.

[^8]: **S.** Augustine, *Enchiridion* 15.56 (Loserth: *Opp.* VI, p. 217; LEDGER E18f-2, editor). Augustine echoes Psalm 112:3 ("from the rising of the sun unto the going down of the same") and the "new song" of the psalms. **T.** Loserth corrects the manuscripts' chapter LXIV to LVI (437.24); their *Rectus itaque et conformis ordo* to *Rectus itaque … ordo* (437.26); their *creatori* to *conditori* (437.29); and their *perstitit* to *persistit* (438.4). Each correction is from Augustine's text and is translated. **R.** A in the margin at 437.24: *Augustinus*.

[^9]: **C.** *Manuale*: the *Enchiridion* ("handbook"). *Quoad primum articulum*: the article of the creed on the church, which Augustine treats after the Trinity; taken as "first" in that order.

[^10]: **T.** At 438.15 the print has *nulla nostra*; Loserth's apparatus reads *recte: natura*, and "our nature" is translated. At 438.16 *cesivam* does not construe; "[a seat]" is our conjecture from the sense (the ascension), not a reading. **C.** The sentence is loose: *ordinatur … quod* takes the hymn as its object, and the clauses on the ascension and the angel are taken as parenthetic. *Caveant*, "may beware", is printed; the sense before a hymn would suit a verb of singing, but the print is translated. **S.** Apocalypse 22:9 (also 19:10): DR "I am thy fellow servant … Adore God". *Peregrinantes a domino*: 2 Corinthians 5:6, DR "we are absent from the Lord".

[^11]: **S.** A stanza of the Easter hymn *Chorus novae Ierusalem*; Loserth (citing Matthew) places it in the Sarum Breviary at Vespers on the Sunday after Easter (LEDGER E18f-3, editor). The hymn is printed among Fulbert of Chartres's hymns (PL 141), where this is stanza 4 of 5, word for word (LEDGER E18f-4); the attribution is the PL's.

[^12]: **C.** *Solum* and *polus*: the ground and the sky, the hymn's "earth and heaven"; Wyclif's gloss takes them as the church militant and the church triumphant. *Dignus amplitudine*: glossed as worthy to have "a city so ample".
