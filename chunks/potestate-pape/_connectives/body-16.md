### 12-s ¶1 · 385.32–386.26  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 385] Ulterius quantum ad decisiones heresum, pro quibus dicitur esse necessarium esse papam, patet quod a tempore dotacionis cesaree alii fuerant [p. 386] apciores ad discernendum veritatem catholicam quam papa cesareus. Et si dicitur quod auctoritate sui debent vocati decidere, cum solus papa potest synodum congregare, picta et ficta tradicio balbutitur; nam auctoritate illius debet christianus ad edificacionem ecclesie proferre sentenciam a quo capit scienciam; quod est pape impertinens, cum singulariter fit a Deo; debet igitur fieri synodus ex mocione divina consensu unanimi. Ad quem consensum debet papa precipue laborare et in defectu sui alius fidelis ecclesie, ut cesar vel alius magis ydoneus. Unde credo quod facta synodo ad discernendum quid est naturaliter hostia consecrata Robertus et multi prelati cesarei proferrent satis nudam sentenciam nec excederent in diccione procuratoria cesarem vel simplicem ydiotam. Alii **autem** dicunt quod est quantitas, alii vero quod est qualitas, et tercii quod est panis et sic de aliis variis sentenciis, quas non probant. Ideo sanum videtur fideles se tenere in limitibus quos lex Christi edocet et in aliis curiositatibus non musitare vel plus sapere quam oportet; sufficit enim fides scripture certificare quoad omnia agibilia vel credibilia, que necesse est viantes cognoscere. De toto isto patet diffuse alibi et cetera.

ENGLISH: Further, as to the deciding of heresies, for which it is said to be necessary that there be a [pope]{g:papa}, it is clear that from the time of the caesarean [endowment]{g:dotacio} others have been [386] more apt to discern catholic truth than the [caesarean pope]{g:papa-cesareus}.[^1] And if it is said that those called [to a synod] ought to decide by his [authority]{g:auctoritas}, since the pope alone can gather a synod, a painted and feigned tradition is being stammered out; for a Christian ought to put forth a [teaching]{g:sentencia} for the edifying of the [church]{g:ecclesia} by the authority of him from whom he takes his knowledge; and this is [irrelevant]{g:impertinens} to the pope, since it comes uniquely from God. A synod therefore ought to be held by divine motion, with unanimous consent. For that consent the pope ought chiefly to labour, and in his default another of the church's faithful, such as the emperor or another more fit. Hence I believe that, were a synod held to discern what the consecrated host is in its nature, Robert and many [caesarean prelates]{g:prelatus-cesarius} would put forth a teaching bare enough, and would not surpass the emperor or a plain [unlettered man]{g:ydiota} in proctorial pronouncement.[^2] And some say that it is quantity, and others that it is quality, and a third party that it is bread, and so of other various teachings, which they do not prove. Therefore it seems sound for the faithful to keep themselves within the [limits]{g:limites} that the [law of Christ]{g:lex-christi} teaches, and not to mutter about other curiosities, or "to be more wise than it behoveth"; for the faith of scripture suffices to make certain all things to be done or believed that [those on the road]{g:viantes} must know. Of all this it is clear at length elsewhere, etc.[^3]

### 12-s ¶2 · 386.27–386.35  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 386] Possunt **autem** abusiones Romani pontificis reduci ad numerum duodecim, ut alie abusiones communiter reducuntur. Prima quod institutis ad hoc cardinalibus dimissis ministeriis que Christus instituit, eligant unum caput, quem ex institucione cesarea papam vocant. Hoc nedum caret auctoritate scripture, **sed** videtur societati apostolorum et eleccioni Mathie singulariter facte per Dominum repugnare.

ENGLISH: And the abuses of the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex} can be reduced to the number twelve, as other abuses are commonly reduced.[^4] The first: that cardinals, instituted for this, setting aside the ministries that Christ instituted, elect one [head]{g:caput}, whom by caesarean institution they call pope. This not only lacks the authority of scripture, but seems [to be incompatible]{g:repugnare} with the fellowship of the apostles and with the election of Matthias, made uniquely by the Lord.[^5]

### 12-s ¶3 · 387.1–387.21  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 387] Secunda quod mundanos honores contra Christum et apostolos tantum affectant. Debet enim recolere, quia ex Matth. XX debet esse omnibus fratribus suis servus, unde Gregorius II. Pastoral. VI: Petrus, inquit, autore Deo sancte ecclesie principatum tenens a bene agente Cornelio, et sese ei humiliter prosternente, inmoderacius venerari recusavit seque illi similem recognovit dicens: Surge, ne feceris, et ego ipse homo sum, sicut et tu. Patet historia Act. X. 26. Et sequitur: Paulus bene agentibus fratribus prelatum se esse nesciebat, cum diceret: Non quia dominamur fidei vestre **sed** adiutores sumus gaudii vestri. Atque illico adiunxit: In fide enim statis, ac si id quod pretulerat, aperiret dicens: Ideo non dominamur fidei vestre, quia fide statis; equales enim vobis sumus, in quo vos stare cognoscimus; quasi prelatum se esse fratribus nesciebat, cum diceret: Facti sumus sicut parvuli in medio vestrum, prima Tess. II, 7. Et rursum, nos **autem** servos vestros per Christum. Patet . . . .

ENGLISH: The second: that they [desire]{g:affectare} worldly honours so much, against Christ and the apostles. For he ought to recall that by Matthew 20 he ought to be the servant of all his brethren; hence Gregory, *Pastoral [Rule]* II.6:[^6] "Peter," he says, "holding by God's authority the chief rule of the holy church, refused to be venerated beyond measure by Cornelius, who was doing well and humbly prostrating himself before him, and acknowledged himself like him, saying: Arise, do not do it; I myself also am a man, as thou art." The story is plain in Acts 10:26. And it follows: "Paul did not know himself to be [prelate]{g:prelatus} over brethren who did well, when he said: Not because we exercise dominion over your faith: but we are helpers of your joy. And at once he added: For in faith you stand; as if he were opening what he had put forward, saying: Therefore we exercise no dominion over your faith, because you stand by faith; for we are equal to you in that in which we know you to stand. As if he did not know himself to be prelate over the brethren, when he said: We became as little ones in the midst of you," 1 Thessalonians 2:7. "And again: and ourselves your servants through Christ." It is clear . . . .[^7]

### 12-s ¶4 · 387.22–388.2  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 387] Tercia est quod papa innitatur seculari dominio. Unde Gregorius ubi supra: Ne presidentis animus ad elacionem potestatis sue delectacione rapiatur. Recte Ecclesiastici XXXII. 1 dicitur: Ducem te posuerunt, noli extolli **sed** esto in illis quasi unus ex illis. Hinc eciam Petrus ait: Non dominantes in clero **sed** forma facti gregis. Hinc eciam Veritas per semetipsam dicit: Scitis quia principes gencium dominantur eorum, non sic erit inter vos. [p. 388] Ideo secundum Bernhardum: Nihil intoxicat ani- mum pape plus quam dominandi libido.

ENGLISH: The third is that the pope relies on secular [lordship]{g:dominium}. Hence Gregory in the same place: "Lest the mind of him who presides be carried off into pride by delight in his own [power]{g:potestas}, it is rightly said in Ecclesiasticus 32:1: They have made thee leader: be not lifted up, but be among them as one of them. Hence also Peter says: Not lording it over the clergy, but being made a pattern of the flock. Hence also the Truth in his own person says: You know that the princes of the Gentiles lord it over them; it shall not be so among you."[^8] [388] Therefore, according to Bernard: "Nothing poisons the mind of the pope more than the lust of lording it."[^9]

### 12-s ¶5 · 388.3–388.12  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 388] Quarta quod papa sit dux loquens grandia, magnificando suam potenciam et faciens modicum in effectu. Ex hinc itaque docet beatus Gregorius, ut patet XCIX distinccione: Quod papa non debet vocari universalis episcopus. Hec enim est condicio Antichristi, cum Christus cepit facere et docere, celans suam potenciam, nisi de quanto est necessarium ipsam detegere et credere ad salutem. Mendax **autem** potestatis ostensio inducit perfidiam Antichristi.

ENGLISH: The fourth: that the pope is a leader speaking great things, magnifying his [power]{g:potencia} and doing little in effect. Hence, accordingly, blessed Gregory teaches, as is clear in Distinction 99: "That the pope ought not to be called universal [bishop]{g:episcopus}." For this is the condition of Antichrist, since Christ began to do and to teach, hiding his power, except so far as it is necessary to [expose]{g:detegere} it and [for it] to be believed unto salvation. But a lying display of power brings in the perfidy of Antichrist.[^10]

### 12-s ¶6 · 388.13–388.30  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 388] Quinta quod papa, dimissa arte paciendi iniurias et facta evangelica inducendi timorem filialem subiectis, innitatur crudeliter pugnis corporalibus et vindictis non solum in causa Christi ad vindicandum suas iniurias, **sed** precipue in causa propria ad magnificandum suas potencias, et ad hoc allegat Gregorius II Pastor ubi supra, illud Matth. XXIV, 48: Quod si dixerit malus ille servus in corde suo: Moram facit Dominus meus venire, et ceperit percutere conservos suos, Dominus suus inopinate veniet et ponet partem eius cum ypocritis. Inter ypocritas, inquit, iure deputatur qui ex simulacione discipline ministerium regiminis vertit in usum dominacionis. Deficiente enim spirituali armatura et specialiter gladio spiritus, necesse est quod papa cesareus innitatur refrigescentibus armis timorem servilem inducentibus prepostere contra Christum.

ENGLISH: The fifth: that the pope, setting aside the art of suffering injuries and the gospel deeds of inducing filial fear in subjects, relies cruelly on bodily fights and vengeances, not only in Christ's cause, to avenge his injuries, but chiefly in his own cause, to magnify his powers; and on this Gregory, *Pastoral* II, in the same place, [alleges]{g:allegare} that of Matthew 24:48: "But if that evil servant shall say in his heart: My lord is long a coming: and shall begin to strike his fellow servants, his lord shall come unexpectedly and shall appoint his portion with the hypocrites. Among the hypocrites," he says, "is he rightly reckoned who under pretence of discipline turns the ministry of rule to the uses of the [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio}."[^11] For when the spiritual armour fails, and especially the sword of the spirit, it is necessary that the caesarean pope rely on arms that grow cold, inducing servile fear, preposterously, against Christ.[^12]

### 12-s ¶7 · 388.31–389.27  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 388] Sexta quod papa appetat dominari nedum pecunia et prediis **sed** suis fratribus, non secundum racionem, qua similantur bestiis ad brutalitatem [p. 389] in honorem Domini expurgandam, **sed**, dimissa illa sollicitudine vicaria pro Christo, nitatur modo Luciferi secundum omnem racionem que sapit excellenciam suam fratribus suis et specialiter in testinacionem veritatis precellentibus dominari. Istam materiam declarat Gregorius II Pastor. VI notans primo quod culpa ex insufficiencia proprii regiminis induxit huiusmodi prelaciam, secundo quod prelatus debet esse humilis socius bonis subditis, et tercio quod regimen suum debet exercere cum debita severitate in feras egentes regimine, ut puta contra eos in quibus Ihesus contempnitur. Et adducit ad hoc illud Genesis XLVI, 34: Pastores ovium sumus. Secundo Genes. IX, 1 dicit Deus terror vester ac tremor sit super cuncta animalia terre; in quo esse super homines prohibetur. In hoc enim, quod metum sibi a perverse viventibus exigunt, quasi non hominibus **sed** animalibus dominantur. Ex istis elicit sanctus tercio multas demonstrabiles veritates, prima quod contra naturam est et superbia Luciferi velle ab equalibus in natura et moribus dominari, secunda quod curatus ut prosit debet appetere illud officium, non ut presit, et tercia quod nemo assumeret pastoris officium nisi evidenter haberet artem spiritualis medici ad prodessendum anime subditorum.

ENGLISH: The sixth: that the pope seeks [to lord it]{g:dominari} not only over money and estates but over his brethren; not according to the respect in which they are likened to beasts, [389] for purging brutishness to the honour of the Lord, but, setting aside that vicarial solicitude for Christ, he strives after the manner of Lucifer, according to every respect that savours of his own excellence, to lord it over his brethren, and especially over those who excel in [witness] to the truth.[^13] This matter Gregory sets out, *Pastoral* II.6, noting first that it was fault, from the insufficiency of one's own rule, that brought in prelacy of this kind; second, that a prelate ought to be a humble fellow to good subjects; and third, that he ought to exercise his rule with due severity upon the wild beasts that need rule, as, say, against those in whom Jesus is despised. And he brings to this that of Genesis 46:34: "We are shepherds of sheep." Second, in Genesis 9:1 God says: "Let the fear and dread of you be upon all the beasts of the earth"; in which to be over men is forbidden. "For in this, that they require fear of themselves from those who live perversely, they lord it, as it were, not over men but over animals."[^14] From these the saint draws, third, many demonstrable truths: first, that it is against nature, and the pride of Lucifer, to wish to lord it over one's equals in nature and morals; second, that a [curate]{g:curatus} ought to desire that office in order to profit, not in order to preside; and third, that no one should take up the office of shepherd unless he evidently had the art of the spiritual physician for profiting the souls of his subjects.[^15]

### 12-t ¶1 · 389.28–391.10  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 389] Septima quod papa negligat disponere se in mentis mundicia, ut pro subiectis debite interpellet. Hoc enim est inseparabiliter papale officium, cum talis debet tamquam intercessor pacificans inter Deum et populum mediare. Inficitur **autem** oratoris officium per superbiam, ut patet in sex abusionibus predictis et per avariciam [p. 390] que est alia radix malicie. Sicut enim fistula per quam transiret liquor inficitur per metalli rubiginem inherentem, sic inficitur rectoris fistula ad orandum, quando temporalibus nimis afficitur, cum ad hoc consequitur servitus ydolorum; et sicut liquor non descendit per fistulam ad subiectum, nisi proporcionaliter ad scaturiginem sit depressa, sic sacerdos orando non mediat inter Deum et populum nisi deponendo elacionem de se ipso humiliter senciat, subiciendo se debite quoad Deum. Ideo caveret papa, quomodo de necessitate salutis tenetur orare pro populo, et precipue emundare spirituale organum ab istis duobus criminibus; aliter enim nedum omittendo peccaret, **sed** mediando inficeret gregem suum, dicente Gregorio primo Pastoralis X: Cuncti, inquit, liquido novimus, quia cum is qui displicet ad intercedendum mittitur, irati animus ad deteriora provocatur. Qui igitur adhuc desideriis terrenis astringitur, caveat ne districti iram iudicis gravius accendens, dum loco delectatur glorie fiat subditis autor ruine. Octava abusio est quod papa oneret se infundabiliter onere alieno. Hic **autem** exprimit Gregorius primo Pastoralis sub hiis verbis: Hinc precipites colligant cum quanta culpa ex proprio appetitu ceteris preferri non metuunt, si sancti viri plebium ducatum suscipere Deo eciam iubente timuerunt. Moyses suadente Domino trepidat, infirmus quisque, ut honoris onus suscipiat, anhelat; et qui ad casum valde urgetur ex propriis, humerum libenter opprimendus submittit ponderibus alienis; que egit ferre non valet, eciam auget, quod [p. 391] portet. Ex istis videtur quod prelacia ecclesiastica sine peccato saltem veniali non poterit acceptari. Omnes enim quotquot venerunt sunt fures et latrones, qui accipiunt illud propter honorem vel commodum temporale, quod plus prelaciam quesitam sollicitudo et actus eorum diiudicant. Si igitur sine tali obligacione superaddita possumus plus vel tantum prodesse populo, ad quid querimus vincula temporalium et professione superaddita colligari?

ENGLISH: The seventh: that the [pope]{g:papa} neglects to dispose himself in purity of mind so as to intercede duly for his subjects. For this is inseparably the papal office, since such a one ought, as a peacemaking intercessor, to mediate between God and the people. And the office of the [suppliant]{g:orator} is infected by pride, as is clear in the six abuses aforesaid, and by avarice, [390] which is the other root of malice.[^1] For as a pipe through which liquor would pass is infected by the rust of the metal clinging to it, so the ruler's pipe for praying is infected when he is too much affected by temporal things, since upon this follows the servitude of idols; and as the liquor does not descend through the pipe to what lies beneath unless it is lowered in proportion to the spring, so the priest in praying does not mediate between God and the people unless, putting down elation, he thinks humbly of himself, subjecting himself duly with respect to God. Therefore let the pope take heed how he is bound, of necessity of salvation, to pray for the people, and above all to cleanse the spiritual organ from these two crimes; for otherwise he would not only sin by omitting, but by mediating would infect his flock, as Gregory says, *Pastoral* I.10: "We all know clearly," he says, "that when one who displeases is sent to intercede, the mind of the angered one is provoked to worse. Let him therefore who is still bound by earthly desires beware lest, kindling the more heavily the wrath of the strict judge, while he delights in the place of glory he become to his subjects the author of ruin."[^2] The eighth abuse is that the pope burdens himself [without foundation]{g:infundabiliter} with another's burden. And this Gregory expresses, *Pastoral* I, in these words: "Hence let the headlong gather with how great a fault they do not fear to be preferred to others from their own appetite, if holy men feared to undertake the leadership of peoples even at God's bidding. Moses trembles when the Lord persuades him; every weak man pants to undertake the burden of honour; and he who is sorely pressed toward a fall by his own [weights] willingly puts his shoulder, to be crushed, under the weights of others; what he has done he cannot bear, and he even increases what [391] he must carry."[^3] From this it seems that ecclesiastical prelacy cannot be accepted without at least venial sin. For "all, as many as have come, are thieves and robbers" who accept it for honour or temporal advantage, which their care and their acts, more than the prelacy sought, make plain.[^4] If therefore, without such an obligation added on, we can profit the people more or as much, to what end do we seek the chains of temporal things, and to be bound by a profession added on?

### 12-t ¶2 · 391.11–391.27  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 391] Et sub ista octava stulticia sunt mille alie germinantes, ut nona abusio est, quod papa presumit per totum christianismum statuere duces spirituales et medicos, pro quorum ducatu oportet ipsum summo iudici respondere. Sic, inquam, non fecerunt Petrus, Gregorius et ceteri sancti ecclesie primitive, **sed** permiserunt aliorum apostolorum instituciones et expertorum elecciones procedere. Nam hoc debet populus plus curare, dicente Gregorio primo Pastoralis IX capitulo: Cum curam populi electus presul suscipit, quasi ad egrum medicus accedit. Unde creditur quod provisiones, reservaciones et privilegiaciones tam amplas induxit non amor salutis subditi vel excellens specularis prudencia **sed** mater cupiditas; cum ubi lucrum tollitur cessant constituciones huiusmodi, eciam licet magis prodessent ecclesie.

ENGLISH: And under this eighth folly a thousand others are sprouting, as the ninth abuse is that the pope presumes throughout all Christendom to appoint spiritual leaders and physicians, for whose leadership he must answer to the highest judge. Not so, I say, did Peter, Gregory and the other saints of the primitive [church]{g:ecclesia} do; but they allowed the institutions of the other apostles and the elections of experienced men to proceed. For the people ought to care more for this, as Gregory says, *Pastoral* I, chapter 9: "When the bishop chosen undertakes the care of the people, he comes as a physician to the sick." Hence it is believed that not love of the subject's salvation, nor the excellent prudence of a watchman, but mother cupidity brought in [provisions]{g:provisio}, reservations and [privilegings]{g:privilegiacio} so ample; since where gain is taken away constitutions of this kind cease, even though they would profit the church more.[^5]

### 12-t ¶3 · 391.28–392.7  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 391] Decima abusio est, quod papa presumit ultra Christum vel sanctos apostolos concedere sic cece indulgencias et privilegia, cum ignorat communiter si Deus hec approbat, vel ad utriusque partis deterius reprehendit. Nam vel est indulgencia talis sophistica vel personalis et empta vel dignis quibus proficeret nimis parca. Si igitur [p. 392] servus timeret sibi expendere corporalem thesaurum domini dominorum, et specialiter presumendo blasfeme potestatem et modum proprium regi regum, cum enim talis non humiliter intrat per ostium, **sed** ascendit superbe dirumpens tectum ecclesie caritatem, et sophisticat thesaurum Domini, restat ut in finali iudicio condempnetur.

ENGLISH: The tenth abuse is that the pope presumes, beyond Christ or the holy apostles, to grant [indulgences]{g:indulgencie} and [privileges]{g:privilegium} so blindly, since he commonly does not know whether God approves them, or reproves them, to the worse of both parties. For such an indulgence is either sophistical, or personal and bought, or too sparing to the worthy whom it would profit. If therefore [392] a servant should fear to spend for himself the bodily treasure of the lord of lords, and especially by blasphemously presuming the [power]{g:potestas} and manner proper to the king of kings (for such a one does not enter humbly by the door, but climbs up proudly, breaking through the roof of the church, [charity]{g:caritas}, and [counterfeits]{g:sophisticare} the Lord's treasure), it remains that he be condemned in the final judgment.[^6]

### 12-u ¶1 · 393.31–395.7  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 393] A subiectis vero inferiora gerenda sunt, arccioribus celestia cogitanda ut scilicet oculum, qui pro videndi gressibus preeminet, cura pulveris non obscuret. [p. 394] Caput namque subiectorum et oculi sunt cuncti qui presunt. Et hinc creditur quod dotati sunt exinde more peccancium ad contradictoria obligati. Tenentur enim racione superaccepti doni a mundi principibus amplius servire illis quam prius, et debent minus deservire eis, cum non illuminantur ex accepcione temporalium **sed** cecantur et per ante tenebantur servire illis secundum totum ingenium vel donum, quod a Deo habebant, quia in toto illo eis proficere quoad Deum, et aliud mundanum ac laycale servicium est abhominabile dominis et sui meriti tardativum. Cum igitur tenentur habere aliquam providenciam et gubernacionem circa bona collata et ingeniacio sua sit tantum finita, videtur quod oportet ipsam quoad servicium meritorium post dotacionem diminui, cum exinde fracta sit et debilitata, quia circa contraria est dispersa. Et hoc creditur movisse apostolos constituisse dyaconos ad ministrandum mensis, apostolis ipsis intendentibus verbo Dei, ut legitur Act. VI. Et revera illud mensarum ministerium videtur habere maiorem racionem meriti quam occupacio qua dotati sunt circa temporalia preter necessaria occupati. Et ex istis videtur quod sicut dotati ad onus dampnosum spiritus receperant coniugium cum temporalibus monstruosum, sic dotantes ex ceca pietate dampnificarunt ambas partes altrinsecus directe ad contrarium meriti, quod stulte intenderant. Unde creditur beatum Gregorium non obstante reliccione tocius patrimonii, quod reliquerat, habens omnia in communi, nec obstante humilitate tota ac parcitate post pinguem dotacionem ecclesie, nec obstante tota suspensione [p. 395] sollicitudinis circa temporalia et inveccione contra philargiam, propter admissionem temporalium venialiter peccavisse et hinc ad purgacionem sui et augmentum meriti Deum ipsum in febribus et dolore capitis per longa tempora punivisse. Alii **autem** fingunt quod hoc accidit, quia oravit temere pro Trayano.

ENGLISH: "And the lower things are to be carried out by the subjects, the heavenly things to be thought on by the stricter, so that, namely, the care of the dust may not darken the eye, which stands out above for seeing the steps. [394] For all who preside are the [head]{g:caput} and the eyes of the subjects."[^1] And hence it is believed that the endowed are thereby, after the manner of sinners, bound to contradictories. For by reason of the gift received over and above from the princes of the world they are bound to serve them more than before; and they ought to serve them less, since they are not enlightened by the receiving of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} but blinded; and beforehand they were bound to serve them according to the whole wit or gift that they had from God, because [they were bound] in all of that to profit them with respect to God; and other, worldly and lay, [service]{g:servicium} is abominable to the lords and a hindrance to their merit.[^2] Since therefore they are bound to have some providence and government over the goods conferred, and their wit is only finite, it seems that it must be diminished as to meritorious service after the [endowment]{g:dotacio}, since thereby it is broken and weakened, because it is dispersed about contraries. And this is believed to have moved the apostles to appoint deacons to serve tables, the apostles themselves attending to the word of God, as is read in Acts 6.[^3] And in truth that ministry of tables seems to have a greater ground of merit than the occupation in which the endowed are occupied about temporal goods beyond what is necessary. And from this it seems that, as the endowed, to the damaging burden of the spirit, received a monstrous marriage with temporal goods, so the endowers, from blind [piety]{g:pietas}, damaged both parties on either side, directly contrary to the merit that they had foolishly intended. Hence it is believed that blessed Gregory, notwithstanding the relinquishing of his whole patrimony, which he had relinquished, having all things in common, nor notwithstanding all his humility and frugality after the fat endowment of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, nor notwithstanding all his suspension [395] of solicitude about temporal goods and his invective against [love of money]{g:philargiria}, sinned venially by the admission of temporal goods; and hence that God himself punished him with fevers and pain of the head for long periods, for his purging and the increase of his merit. Others, however, feign that this befell because he prayed rashly for Trajan.[^4]

### 12-u ¶2 · 395.8–395.21  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 395] **Sed** mirantur nonnulli, quare Romana ecclesia non canonizat Constantinum magnum, qui dotavit ecclesiam nec tantum sollempnizat eum, ut orientalis ecclesia, ut notat Cestrensis libro IV, cap. CXII quomodo Greci sollempnizant eius festum XI die. Maii. **Sed** audivi ad hoc poni causam triplicem, prima fundatur super mendacem blasfemiam, quod debuit dedisse totum imperium suum clero, et quia eius medietatem iniuste detinuit ac in sacrilegio illo sine satisfaccione obiit, tamquam hereticus est dampnandus et per consequens a sanctorum cathalogo semovendus, et sic forent dampnandi omnes morientes reges vel domini temporales.

ENGLISH: But some wonder why the Roman church does not [canonize]{g:canonizare} Constantine the Great, who endowed the church, nor solemnizes him so much as the eastern church does; as [the Chester chronicler]{g:cestrensis} notes, book IV, chapter 112, how the Greeks solemnize his feast on the 11th day of May.[^5] But I have heard a threefold cause put for this. The first is founded on a lying blasphemy: that he ought to have given his whole empire to the [clergy]{g:clerus}, and because he unjustly kept back half of it and died in that sacrilege without [satisfaction]{g:satisfaccio}, he is to be condemned as a heretic, and consequently removed from the catalogue of the saints; and so all kings or [temporal lords]{g:temporales-domini} who die [so] would have to be condemned.[^6]

### 12-u ¶4 · 395.29–397.16  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 395] Tercia causa realis ponitur quod hoc ideo non evenit, quod tantum onerando ecclesiam cum temporalibus stulte, tantum dampnificaverat se et clerum, quod dispendium quia subtraxit ab [p. 396] orientali ecclesia, propterea ipsum laudat. Ipsi enim ut fratres de statu exproprietario cum iubilo Deum laudant. Nec est color, si talis dominacio vel mundi gloria videatur preciosa febricitanti in animo cuius affeccio discrasiata non sapit celestia, propterea sic est de illo et eius opposito senciendum, quia oportet fidelem credere in omnibus sano palato et iudicio Ihesu nostri. Nec cecemur in isto, quod dominari sic seculariter sit licitum ac meritorium, ideo congruit clero nostro, quia sic purgare latrinas foret eis conveniens. **Sed** (si non fallor) facere illud officium foret clero minus inconsonum, quam sic dominari terrestribus, que Apostolus vocat stercora, cum nec foret Deo tantum displicens nec adeo ledens mentem. Ista pauca dixerim, alia recitative, alia reputative, assertive ad honorem matris ecclesie; unde super omnibus dictis meis in ista materia testem consciencie mee invoco Deum meum, quod intendo primo honorem et utilitatem sancte matris ecclesie: ad hoc enim obligatur strictissime ex mandato Domini quilibet christianus, cum quoddammodo infinite plus debemus matrem illam diligere quam parentes carnales vel temporalia aut nosmet ipsos. Intendo secundo quod honor et profectus predicte femine stat principaliter in similitudine conversandi et sequendi sponsum ecclesie, excuciendo omnes tradiciones humanas extraneas que ab expedicione illius itineris retardarent. Et cum ista ambo sint articuli fidei christiane, que cuncti fideles ultra opinionem magnificare tenentur ac cum in mediis ad hunc finem contingit [p. 397] multipliciter oberrare, intendo tercio et protestor quod docto per papam, per quodcumque membrum ecclesie vel aliquam creaturam quod erro in mediis, volo humillime ac paratissime revocare; unde ut procedam securius innitor fundamentaliter summo lapidi, qui est via, veritas et vita, domino Ihesu Christo, capiens testimonium a sanctis doctoribus, de quanto consonant legi sue. Alia vero ab istis tamquam tardativa vel contraria parvipendo; unde credo ut fidem ecclesie, quod quicumque non observat statum et legem quam Christus instituit, quam eciam in se et suis apostolis vita et verbo docuit et servavit, non sequitur Christum per artam viam, que ducit ad celum, **sed** incedit per latam viam, que ducit membrum dyaboli ad infernum.

ENGLISH: The third, the real cause, is put thus: that this did not come about because, by so foolishly burdening the church with temporal goods, he had so damaged himself and the clergy; which loss, because he withdrew it from the [396] eastern church, [that church] therefore praises him. For they, like brethren of the [propertyless]{g:exproprietarii} state, praise God with jubilation.[^8] Nor is it a [colour]{g:color}, if such [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio} or the world's glory seems precious to one fevered in mind, whose [distempered]{g:discrasia} affection has no taste for heavenly things, [for holding] that one must therefore so judge of it and of its opposite; for the faithful must believe in all things by the sound palate and judgement of our Jesus.[^9] Nor let us be blinded in this, [arguing] that to [lord it]{g:dominari} thus in secular fashion is lawful and meritorious, [and] therefore befits our clergy; for so cleaning latrines would be fitting for them. But (if I am not mistaken) to do that office would be less unbecoming to the clergy than so to lord it over earthly things, which the Apostle calls dung, since it would be neither so displeasing to God nor so hurtful to the mind.[^10] These few things I have said, some by way of recital, some [by estimation]{g:reputative}, [some] [assertively]{g:assertive}, to the honour of mother church; hence over all my sayings in this matter I call upon my God as witness of my conscience that I intend first the honour and utility of holy mother church: for to this every Christian is most strictly bound by the Lord's command, since in a certain manner we ought to love that mother infinitely more than carnal parents or temporal goods or our very selves.[^11] I [maintain]{g:intendere} secondly that the honour and advancement of the said lady stands principally in likeness of living and of following the bridegroom of the church, shaking off all extraneous human traditions that would hold [her] back from making that journey. And since both of these are [articles]{g:articulus-fidei} of the Christian faith, which all the faithful are bound to magnify beyond [opinion]{g:opinio}, and since in the means to this end it happens [397] in many ways to go astray, I maintain thirdly, and [declare solemnly]{g:protestari}, that if it is shown by the pope, by any [member]{g:membrum} of the church, or by any creature, that I err in the means, I am willing most humbly and most readily to revoke it.[^12] Hence, to proceed more securely, I rely fundamentally on the chief stone, who is the way, the truth and the life, the Lord Jesus Christ, taking testimony from the holy doctors so far as they agree with his law. And other things besides these I make little of, as hindering or contrary; hence I believe, as the faith of the church, that whoever does not observe the state and law that Christ instituted, which he also taught and kept in himself and his apostles by life and word, does not follow Christ by the strait way that leads to heaven, but walks by the broad way that leads the member of the devil to hell.[^13]

### comp-a ¶3 · 399.1–399.18  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 399] Capitulum secundum obicit tripliciter et dissolvit, primo suadet tripliciter, quod nemo habet potestatem ad faciendum aliud nisi quod Deus intendit quod faciat, quod concedit declarans sensum, et quomodo potestas talis perditur ad non gradum. Secundo obiciens de potestate faciendi miracula ostendit quid est miraculum, et non potest creature ut talis competere, docet quomodo est de miraculis senciendum, secundo principaliter docet quomodo senciendum est de potestate spiritus sancti et gracie, et tercio diffuse declarat quomodo sacerdos non tollit peccata, **sed** dum fuerit conformis divino iudicio quodammodo remittit et absolvit hominem a peccato. Quarto declarat potestatem excomunicandi et puniendi subiectos, et quinto tractat de modo committendi potestates, de origine et perdicione earum et que sit alia maior.

ENGLISH: The second chapter raises objections in three ways and resolves them. First, it makes the case in three ways that no one has power to do anything other than what God intends that he do; which it grants, declaring the sense, and [it declares] how such power is lost to the point of no degree.[^7] Second, objecting about the power of working miracles, it shows what a miracle is, and [that it] cannot belong to a creature as such, [and] teaches how one should think about miracles. Second, as a main head, it teaches how one should think about the power of the Holy Spirit and of grace; and third it declares at length how a [priest]{g:sacerdos} does not take away sins, but, while he is conformed to the divine judgement, in a certain manner [remits]{g:remittere} and absolves a man from sin. Fourth, it declares the power of excommunicating and punishing subjects; and fifth it treats of the manner of committing powers, of their origin and their loss, and which [power] is greater than another.

### comp-a ¶5 · 399.31–401.27  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 399] Capitulum quartum declarat primo quod Petrus non solum accepit illum primatum pro se ipso, **sed** pro eius vicariis succedentibus in ecclesia militante. Secundo declarat quod, sicut Petrus exhinc fuit per se Christi vicarius, quod suum [p. 400] magistrum quoad actus et mores secutus fuit conformius, sic oportet P. vicarium similius sequi ipsum. Tercio docet, quod humana eleccio non valet, nisi de quanto promulgaverit conformiter ad divinam. Quarto quod precise de tanto debet subditus sequi suum privatum prepositum, de quanto ipse sequitur Ihesum nostrum. Quinto sive electores bene vel male elegerint operibus meritoriis est credendum, et quicumque modus noster eligendi prepositum fuerit eleccioni Dei conformior est eo melior, et obicit quadrupliciter et dissolvit, declarans quod de quatuor modis eligendi prepositum eleccio per sortem foret modo securior. Sexto notat, quod non docetur ex scriptura, quomodo preeminencia Petri super alios apostolos stat in universali iurisdicione super totam militantem ecclesiam, aut quod particularis potestas iurisdicionis debet esse in alios apostolos per illam potestatem ad locum et populum limitata. Capitulum quintum declarat quintupliciter sextam sentenciam, quod Romanus pontifex debet racione officii vicarii sequi Christum in altissima paupertate, et post obicit tripliciter et dissolvit. Primo per hoc quod papa non posset perficere suum officium nec honorifice tenere statum papalem sine tali dominio, et declarat oppositum per Bernhardum. Secundo obicit per hoc, quod multi sancti episcopi approbarunt dotacionem huiusmodi et dissolvit, declarans quantum malum contingit ex isto porismate, cum sequimur mixtim bona secundum racionem qua sunt mala. Tercio obicit per hoc, quod est distinccio inter potestatem iurisdicionis et ordinis, eo quod solus papa habet potestatem super universalem ecclesiam; non enim est aliud proprium pape officium, et dissolvit, concedens distinccionem et negans [p. 401] illam universalem potestatem et obiciendo tripliciter ex domino Ardmacano dissolvit, declarans quod Paulus non habuit a Petro **sed** a Christo licenciam predicandi, et concludit quod, sicut apostoli erant socii et amici, sic debet esse hodie, cum ditacio non dissolveret amiciciam, eo quod Christus non inveniret amicos inter nostros episcopos conversando et declarat in tribus conclusionibus pastoris officium. Capitulum sextum connectit cum declaracione septimam sentenciam et octavam, quarum prior dicit sic: Quod sicut nullus apostolorum habuit tantam potestatem quantam habuit Christus humanitus, sic nec omnis papa habuit tantam potestatem quantam habuit beatus Petrus vel alius Christi apostolus, et declarando utrumque membrum tripliciter obicit et dissolvit. Octava sentencia dicit quod Romano pontifice vel quocumque alio fingente se gerere primatum vicarie potestatis Christi in terris ab eius semita declinante secundum sensum scripture nullus plenius vel periculosius gerit nomen et opus Antichristi; quod probat ex condicionibus undecim, annectens materiam de quiditate et divisione status, quomodo componitur ex non quantis et, declarans quomodo status potest alterari, tangit quomodo subditus potest prelatum corripere et dissolvit.

ENGLISH: The fourth chapter declares, first, that Peter received that primacy not only for himself but for his vicars succeeding [him] in the church militant. Second, it declares that, as Peter was of himself the [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius-christi} on this ground, that he followed his [400] master more conformably in acts and conduct, so P[eter]'s vicar must follow him more closely.[^10] Third, it teaches that human election is of no avail except in so far as it has proclaimed in conformity with the divine [election]. Fourth, that a subject ought to follow his private [superior]{g:prepositi} precisely so far as he follows our Jesus.[^11] Fifth, that, whether the electors have chosen well or ill, credence is to be given to meritorious works; and whichever of our ways of electing a superior is more conformed to God's election is by that the better; and it raises objections in four ways and resolves them, declaring that of the four ways of electing a superior, election by lot would now be the safer.[^12] Sixth, it notes that it is not taught from scripture how Peter's [preeminence]{g:preeminencia} over the other apostles consists in a universal jurisdiction over the whole church militant, or that a particular [power of jurisdiction]{g:potestas-iurisdiccionis} ought to be in the other apostles, [assigned]{g:limitare} by that power to a place and a people. The fifth chapter declares in five ways the sixth teaching, that the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex} ought, by reason of the office of vicar, to follow Christ in the highest poverty; and afterwards it raises objections in three ways and resolves them.[^13] First, [it objects] by appeal to this, that the [pope]{g:papa} could not fulfil his office nor honourably maintain the papal state without such [lordship]{g:dominium}; and it declares the opposite by Bernard.[^14] Second, it objects by appeal to this, that many holy [bishops]{g:episcopus} approved an [endowment]{g:dotacio} of this kind; and it resolves it, declaring how much evil comes from this [porism]{g:porisma}, when we follow mixed goods in the respect in which they are evil.[^15] Third, it objects by appeal to this, that there is a distinction between the power of jurisdiction and [the power] of order, in that the pope alone has power over the universal church; for there is no other office proper to the pope; and it resolves it, granting the distinction and denying [401] that universal power; and, objecting in three ways out of [the lord of Armagh]{g:dominus-ardmacanus}, it resolves [the objections], declaring that Paul had his [licence]{g:licencia} to preach not from Peter but from Christ;[^16] and it [infers]{g:concludere} that, as the apostles were companions and friends, so it ought to be today, since enrichment ought not to dissolve friendship, in that Christ, keeping company among our bishops, would [otherwise] not find friends; and it declares in three [conclusions]{g:conclusio} the office of a shepherd.[^17] The sixth chapter joins together, with a declaration, the seventh teaching and the eighth, of which the former says thus: that, as none of the apostles had as much power as Christ had in his humanity, so neither has every pope had as much power as blessed Peter or another apostle of Christ had; and, declaring each member [of the comparison], it raises objections in three ways and resolves them.[^18] The eighth teaching says that, when the Roman pontiff, or anyone else who feigns that he bears the primacy of Christ's [vicarial power]{g:potestas-vicaria} on earth, turns aside from his path, according to the sense of scripture no one bears more fully or more dangerously the name and work of Antichrist; which it proves from eleven conditions, annexing the matter of the [quiddity]{g:quiditas} and division of [state]{g:status}, how it is composed of things without quantity; and, declaring how a state can be altered, it touches on how a subject can [reprove]{g:corripere} a [prelate]{g:prelatus}, and resolves [the objection].[^19]

### comp-b ¶1 · 402.34–403.23  (1 sed/autem)

Sexto tollitur tripliciter tercia ficticia, qua fingitur quod eleccione humana oportet Romanum pontificem esse papam. Septimo narrat ex cronicis quomodo regula et preeminencia pape originabatur a cesare et irreligiose ac fraudulenter continuabatur a brachio seculari. Octavo narrat tres condiciones Antichristi ex testimonio Augustini. Nono narrat quomodo tres pape occisi sunt a beatis et tercius papatu deposito gloriose occiditur a tyranno. Seventh, it recounts from the chronicles how the rule and [preeminence]{g:preeminencia} of the pope took its origin from Caesar, and was continued irreligiously and fraudulently by [the secular arm]{g:brachium-seculare}. Eighth, it recounts three conditions of Antichrist from the testimony of Augustine.[^3] Ninth, it recounts how three popes were slain by the blessed, and the third, having laid down the [papacy]{g:papatus}, is gloriously slain by a tyrant.[^4] Tenth, recounting the perils of the Roman pontiff, it expounds how we ought to "redeem the time".[^5]
