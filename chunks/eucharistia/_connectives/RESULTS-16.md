# Connective check: *sed* / *autem*, De eucharistia, wave 16 (chunks 06-f, 06-g, 06-h)

Classes: (a) minor premiss / next step, "now" · (b) adversative, "but" · (c) continuative *autem*, "and";
*non … sed* (incl. *nec / nedum / nullibi / ne … sed*), "but".

## Changes

| chunk | page | Latin phrase | was | now | reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 06-f | 180 | *Fragilitas itaque indisponit hominem sed non per se **sed** inordinata affeccio* | not of itself; rather inordinate affection does | not of itself, but inordinate affection does | *non … sed*: "but", not "rather" (the card keeps "rather" for *ymmo / pocius*). The first *sed* ("but not of itself") stays as it is |
| 06-h | 188 | *ne credat illud quod plane sentit **sed** cogatur credere false fictum incredibile* | lest it believe what it plainly [perceives]{g:sentire}, and so that it be forced to believe | so that it may not believe what it plainly [perceives]{g:sentire}, but be forced to believe | *ne … sed*, a negated purpose followed by its positive: "not X but Y". The English turned the *sed* into "and so that", which gives two separate purposes and loses the opposition. Recast so that "but" can carry the *sed* |
| 06-h | 189 | *ad racionem illam debemus parum attendere **sed** in racionem sui signati totam cogitacionem nostram suspendere* | we ought to attend little, and rather hang our whole thought | we ought to attend little, but hang our whole thought | *parum … sed* is a quasi-negated *non … sed*: "but". "And rather" adds the unwanted "rather" and turns the *sed* into "and" |

## Doubtful, left as they are

- **06-f, p. 180**: *Ad quantum **autem** Deus iuvat efficaciter cultorem … non est nostrum discutere*, rendered "But how far God". Read as (b): it turns from what the worshipper should attend to, to the limit of what can be said about God's help. "And" (c) is defensible. Left.
- **06-f, p. 181**: *Dicitur **autem** nostros mundo divites se ipsos et plebem prodere*, rendered "And it is said". Read as (c). It could be (a), since it applies the "traitors of the soul" premiss to the rich who ordain unfit curates, but no *ergo / ideo / igitur* closes it (a rhetorical question follows). Left.
- **06-h, p. 189**: *Cum **autem** omne per accidens sit reducibile ad aliquod per se, patet quod …*, rendered "Now since". Read as (a): a new premiss brought to bear, with *patet* drawing the consequence. "And since" (c) is defensible. Left.
- **06-h, p. 187** (Rabanus): *vinum **autem** quia sanguinem operatur in carne*, rendered "and wine". Judged in Rabanus's sentence, it is the second member of a bread/wine pair: (c). A light "but" would also be possible. Left.
