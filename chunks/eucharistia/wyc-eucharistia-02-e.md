# *De eucharistia*, chapter 2, part 5: the flood, the authority of doctors, and the definition of transubstantiation

## ¶1 · 44.1–45.29

> [p. 44] Secundo premittit eternam displicenciam, dum dicitur: Factus dolore cordis intrinsecus; non enim credendum est quod Deus habet penam secundum naturam divinam, etsi sibi mala displiceant; nec est credendum quod noviter habuit vel eternaliter elicuit displicenciam, cum fuit intrinsecus in corde; per hoc autem notatur quod finaliter specificat modum delendi occasionem ab inusu hominis ad terrestria, cum finaliter dicit: Delebo, inquit, hominem quem creavi a facie terre etc. Consumpsit enim mixta ornancia terram et aerem ac indisposuit raciones seminales, sicut pater misericors precavens petulanciam filii aufert ab eo occasionem peccandi; non enim punit Deus predestinatum ut prescitum, nisi misericorditer ad puniti demeritum; unde punicio aque preternaturaliter inundantis correspondet eorum inordinate luxurie. Corrupte vero exalaciones causantes salsedinem et indisponentes terram et aerem ut influencia celestis sit proporcionalis cremento et aure temporanee, sicut ante correspondet eorum affeccioni inordinate circa terrena violenter rapiendo. Unde pro istis duobus peccatis subditur: Corrupta est autem terra coram Deo quoad primum peccatum carnale, et repleta est iniquitate quoad secundum peccatum avaricie. Si autem placet intelligere per punicionem iustam execucionem vindicte que est finis penitudinis in potente, excludendo mocionem, passionem, inprovisionem etc., que dicunt inperfeccionem in penitente, non contradico sentencie. Sic enim intelligitur illud Psalmi VI, 2 et Psalmi XXXI: Domine ne in furore tuo arguas me, [p. 45] neque in ira tua corripias me. Furor enim sonat in vehemenciam et acceleracionem vindicte pro peccato irremissibili continuando usque ad finem vite pro quo Deus arguit insolubiliter concludens, ut patet Matth. XXVᵒ, 31: Cum venerit filius hominis. Talium enim terrenorum inventa est iniquitas ad odium perpetuum; secundo quia horrendum est incidere in manus Dei viventis ad Hebr. Xᵒ, 31; ideo petit quod non corripiatur propter lignum, fenum et stipulam in purgatorio expurgandum. Quamvis enim ira talis sit micior iuxta illud Abakuk IIIᵒ, 2: Cum iratus fueris, misericordie recordaberis, tamen utrumque est timendum, et pro supplecione tocius penitencie in via humiliter est petendum. Absit autem quod in talibus locucionibus credamus passionem vel perturbacionem in Deo accidere, cum Sap. XIIᵒ, 18 scribitur: Tu autem Domine virtutum cum tranquillitate iudicas. Et conformiter iudicandum est de austeritate et usura Dei in parabola Salvatoris Luc. XIXᵒ. Et eodem modo intelligendum est de Dei serenitate et quibuscunque aliis sibi attributis, que omnia ex equo concurrunt; ad Rom. IIᵒ scribit Apostolus: Vide ergo bonitatem et serenitatem Dei; et illud Apok. XVI, 15: Ecce venio sicut fur; beatus qui vigilat et custodit vestimenta sua. Et sic intelligendum est illud Psalmi LXXVIIᵒ, 65: Et excitatus est tanquam dormiens Dominus et tanquam potens crapulatus a vino cum quotlibet similibus, quorum particulares exposiciones continent indubie profunda misteria.

Secondly, he sets first the eternal displeasure, when it is said: "Touched inwardly with sorrow of heart";[^1] for it is not to be believed that God has pain according to the divine [nature]{g:natura}, even though evils displease him; nor is it to be believed that he had the displeasure newly, or [elicited]{g:elicere} it eternally, since it was inward in the heart.[^2] And by this it is marked that he finally specifies the manner of destroying the occasion [arising] from man's use of earthly things,[^3] when he says finally: "I will destroy", he says, "man, whom I have created, from the face of the earth" etc.[^4] For he consumed the [mixed bodies]{g:mixtum} adorning the earth and the air, and [made unfit]{g:indisponere} the [seminal reasons]{g:raciones-seminales}, as a merciful father, guarding against the wantonness of his son, takes away from him the occasion of sinning; for God does not punish [the predestinate]{g:predestinatus} as [the foreknown]{g:prescitus}, except mercifully, in proportion to the demerit of the one punished;[^5] hence the punishment of water flooding beyond nature corresponds to their inordinate lust. And the corrupt exhalations, causing saltness and making the earth and air unfit, so that the celestial [influence]{g:influencia} should be proportioned to growth and to a seasonable air, correspond, as before, to their inordinate affection towards earthly things, in seizing [them] violently.[^6] Hence for these two sins it is added: "And the earth was corrupted before God", as to the first sin, the carnal, "and was filled with iniquity", as to the second, the sin of avarice.[^7] But if it pleases [anyone] to understand by punishment the just execution of vengeance, which is the end of repentance in one who is powerful, excluding motion, passion, want of foresight and the like, which [signify]{g:dicere} imperfection in one who repents, I do not contradict the [teaching]{g:sentencia}.[^8] For so is that of Psalm 6:2 and of Psalm 31 understood: "Rebuke me not, O Lord, in thy indignation; [45] nor chastise me in thy wrath."[^9] For indignation sounds the vehemence and speeding of vengeance for an unremittable sin continued until the end of life, for which God rebukes, concluding [inextricably]{g:insolubiliter},[^10] as is clear from Matt. 25:31: "When the Son of man shall come". For the iniquity of such earthly men is found unto perpetual hatred; secondly, because "it is a fearful thing to fall into the hands of the living God", Heb. 10:31; therefore he asks that he be not chastised for wood, hay and stubble, to be purged in purgatory.[^11] For although such wrath is milder, according to that of Habakkuk 3:2, "When thou art angry, thou wilt remember mercy", yet both are to be feared, and for the supplying of the whole of penance one must humbly pray in [the way]{g:via}. But far be it that in such locutions we should believe passion or disturbance to befall God, since it is written in Wisd. 12:18: "But thou, Lord of hosts, judgest with tranquillity."[^12] And in conformity one must judge of the austerity and usury of God in the Saviour's parable, Luke 19. And in the same way one must understand the serenity of God and whatever other things are attributed to him, all of which concur equally; the Apostle writes in Rom. 2: "See then the goodness and the serenity of God";[^13] and that of Apoc. 16:15: "Behold, I come as a thief. Blessed is he that watcheth and keepeth his garments." And so must that of Psalm 77:65 be understood: "And the Lord was awaked as one out of sleep, and like a mighty man that hath been surfeited with wine", with any number of like [texts], whose particular expositions without doubt contain deep [mysteries]{g:misterium}.[^14]

## ¶2 · 45.30–46.12

> [p. 45] Ex istis patet quod non oportet si Deus intelligit per dolorem cordis displicenciam Dei Patris, quod nos in toto debemus loqui conformiter. Sicut enim [p. 46] dicit Augustinus quod Deus est iustus iusticia et non qualitate, sic concedendum est quod Deus est tactus dolore cordis sine passione vel penitencia dolorosa. Et patet in quibus debemus loqui conformiter ad scripturam et in quibus debemus loqui expositorie suspensis parabolis admirando eius subtilem sentenciam, ut quidam intelligunt per hoc quod Deus est tactus dolore cordis intrinsecus, quomodo eternaliter displicencia Dei Patris fecit quod misit unigenitum suum incarnandum; unio enim ypostatica fuit taccio infirmitatis filii, quia ex hoc filius qui est natura patris intrinsecus fuit nostris doloribus oneratus.

From these things it is clear that it is not necessary, if God understands by sorrow of heart the displeasure of God the Father, that we must in everything speak in conformity.[^15] For as [46] Augustine says that God is just by justice and not by [quality]{g:qualitas}, so it is to be granted that God is touched with sorrow of heart without passion or sorrowful repentance.[^16] And it is clear in what things we ought to speak in conformity with scripture, and in what we ought to speak [by way of exposition]{g:expositorie}, the parables held in suspense, wondering at its subtle [sense]{g:sentencia-verborum}; as some understand by this, that God is touched inwardly with sorrow of heart, how eternally the displeasure of God the Father brought it about that he sent his only-begotten to be incarnate; for the [hypostatic]{g:ypostaticus} [union]{g:unio} was the touching of the Son's [weakness]{g:infirmitas}, since by this the Son, who is the nature of the Father, was inwardly burdened with our sorrows.[^17]

## ¶3 · 46.13–47.9

> [p. 46] Quantum ad secundum allegatum de autenticacione doctoris, patet quod quilibet doctor est concedendus, sed nullius posterioris doctoris sentencia nisi de quanto est fundabilis ex scriptura. Credo autem quod sentencia beati Bernhardi fuit katholica sed logica eius est hodie postponenda. Sic enim facimus cum loyca ecclesie de Eukaristia, sic eciam sepe prohibet Augustinus quod defendantur vel teneantur dicta sua nisi de quanto consonant cum scriptura. Intellexit autem Bernhardus per manus, oculos et aures personaliter verbum Dei et sic posuit figurative illa organa crucifigi; hoc est signum signatum per illa organa vel secundum alium sensum nobis absconditum. Sed nos non debemus predicare taliter et specialiter in vulgari sine explanacione sensus katholici, cum sentencia heretica sic de facile surriperet in auditorio ex negligencia predicantis; [p. 47] debemus enim loqui palam populo sentencias scripture necessarias ad salutem. Nec sufficit quod nos habeamus sensum katholicum, quia sic possemus seminare quotcunque hereses adversando toti scripture, dum tamen debemus niti apciori modo quo sufficimus imprimere in auditorio katholicas et edificatorias veritates, quia aliter non sumus adiutores Dei sed dyaboli, nostrum honorem vel seculare commodum proditorie procurantes.

As to the second [allegation]{g:allegare}, concerning the [authentication]{g:autenticacio} of a doctor, it is clear that any doctor is to be granted, but the teaching of no later doctor except insofar as it can be founded on scripture.[^18] And I believe that the teaching of blessed Bernard was catholic, but his [logic]{g:loyca} is today to be [set aside]{g:postponere}. For so we do with the logic of the [church]{g:ecclesia} concerning the Eucharist; so also Augustine often forbids that his sayings be defended or held except insofar as they accord with scripture.[^19] And Bernard understood by hands, eyes and ears, personally, [the Word]{g:verbum} of God, and so posited [figuratively]{g:figurative} that those organs were crucified; that is, the sign signified by those organs, or according to another sense hidden from us.[^20] But we ought not to preach thus, and especially in the vernacular, without explanation of the catholic sense, since a heretical teaching would thus easily creep in among the audience through the negligence of the preacher; [47] for we ought to speak openly to the people the teachings of scripture necessary to salvation. Nor is it enough that we have the catholic sense, since thus we could sow any number of heresies, opposing the whole of scripture, while yet we ought to strive, in the aptest manner we are able, to imprint on the audience catholic and edifying truths; since otherwise we are not helpers of God but of the devil, traitorously procuring our own honour or secular advantage.

## ¶4 · 47.10–47.27

> [p. 47] Ulterius credo quod utraque ecclesia tempore Beringarii et tempore novellorum doctorum fuit katholica, cum equivocarunt in terminis; nec credo quod scola illa de Eukaristia tantum intricavit ecclesiam ut moderna; hoc tamen videtur omnino quod caveamus denotando quamlibet partem sacramenti sensibilis esse corpus Christi vel sanguinem, quia vulgus et litterati viri sunt proni ad intelligendum panem post consecracionem esse ydemptice corpus Christi, ideo propter sensibilem erroris percepcionem tam in clero quam in layco debent defectus corrigi in loquendo, recolendo quomodo circa Eukaristiam fuit prima heresis in Christi discipulis abeuntibus retrorsum propter duriciam sermonis, ut patet Joh. VIᵒ, 61, 67, et meminit decretum in De Consecracione, distinccione 2ᵃ, Prima quidem heresis ex testimonio Augustini. Et hec est racio quare oportet sacerdotes qui panes offerunt omnino instrui in ista materia.

Further, I believe that each church, in the time of Berengar and in the time of [the newer]{g:novelli} doctors, was catholic, since they equivocated in terms;[^21] nor do I believe that that school concerning the Eucharist entangled the church as much as the modern one does. Yet this much seems altogether [required]: that we beware in denoting any part of the [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} to be [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} or [his] blood, since the common people and lettered men are prone to understand the [bread]{g:panis} after [consecration]{g:consecracio} to be [identically]{g:ydemptice} the body of Christ; therefore, on account of the perceptible perception of error, in the clergy as much as in the laity, the defects in speaking ought to be corrected,[^22] recalling how the first heresy was concerning the Eucharist, in Christ's disciples who went back on account of the hardness of the saying, as is clear from John 6:61, 67; and the [decree]{g:decretum} in *De consecratione*, distinction 2, *Prima quidem heresis*, makes mention of it from the testimony of Augustine.[^23] And this is the reason why it behoves the [priests]{g:sacerdos} who offer the breads to be thoroughly instructed in this matter.

## ¶5 · 47.28–48.7

> [p. 47] Sed tercio principaliter replicatur per hoc quod novella ecclesia ponit transsubstanciacionem panis et vini in corpus Christi et sanguinem; hoc autem non posuit ecclesia primitiva, ergo in sentencia contradicunt. Assumptum patet Iᵒ Decretalium De summa [p. 48] Trinitate et Fide katholica, Una vero: Corpus Christi, inquit, et sanguis sub speciebus panis et vini in sacramento altaris veraciter continentur transsubstanciatis pane in corpus et vino in sanguinem potestate divina. Si autem panis et vinum manent integra et· non alterata post consecracionem, quomodo sunt transsubstanciata per verba sacerdotis?

But thirdly, [as a main head]{g:principaliter}, [it is rejoined]{g:replicatur} on the ground that [the newer church]{g:novella-ecclesia} posits the [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio} of the bread and [wine]{g:vinum} into the body and blood of Christ; now the primitive church did not posit this; therefore they contradict each other in teaching. [The assumption]{g:assumptum} is clear from [Decretals]{g:decretalis} I, *De summa* [48] *Trinitate et fide catholica*, *Una vero*: "The body of Christ", it says, "and the blood are [verily]{g:veraciter} contained in the [sacrament of the altar]{g:sacramentum-altaris} under the [species]{g:species} of bread and wine, the bread being transubstantiated into the body and the wine into the blood by divine [power]{g:potestas}."[^24] But if the bread and wine remain whole and unaltered after the consecration, how are they transubstantiated by the words of the priest?

## ¶6 · 48.8–48.22

> [p. 48] In ista materia nostri katholici, eciam decretiste, didicerunt sic describere terminum supradictum: Transsubstanciacio est transitus unius substancie secundum se totam in aliam tota accidencium multitudine remanente, ut nec materia nec forma substancialis que fuerunt in pane et vino remanent post consecracionem, sed omnis substancia materialis vel formalis que fuit in eis corrumpitur, et succedit per conversionem sub eisdem accidentibus corpus Christi, et sic non est alicuius substancie annichilacio, tum quia conversio tocius substancie in melius, tum eciam quia accidencia remanent que prius erant in pane et vino, quia ipsis remanentibus committeretur ydolatria adorando panem et vinum ut corpus dominicum et sic Deum.

In this matter our catholics, the [decretists]{g:decretiste} too, have learned to [define]{g:describere} the aforesaid term thus: transubstantiation is the passing of one [substance]{g:substancia}, according to its whole self, into another, the whole multitude of [accidents]{g:accidens} remaining; so that neither the matter nor the [substantial form]{g:forma-substancialis} which were in the bread and wine remain after consecration, but every substance, material or formal, that was in them is corrupted, and the body of Christ succeeds by [conversion]{g:conversio} under the same accidents; and so there is no annihilation of any substance, both because [it is] a conversion of the whole substance into something better, and also because the accidents remain which were before in the bread and wine; since, if they [the bread and wine] remained, idolatry would be committed in adoring the bread and wine as [the Lord's body]{g:corpus-dominicum}, and so as God.[^25]
