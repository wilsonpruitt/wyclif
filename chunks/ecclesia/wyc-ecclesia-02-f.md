# *De ecclesia*, chapter 2, part 6: explicit and implicit faith; what is owed to prelates and to the canonized

## ¶1 · 42.10–43.23

> [p. 42] Sed constat ex dictis quod neutrum sequitur, sed eius oppositum; pro quo notandum quod fides nunc sumitur pro actu credendi quo creditur, nunc pro habitu credendi per quem creditur, et nunc pro veritate que creditur, ut docet Augustinus XIII°, De Trinitate capitulo II° et III°. Secundo notandum quod alia est fides que est credulitas fidelis explicita et alia fides implicita, ut catholicus habens habitum fidei infusum vel acquisitum explicite credit ecclesiam catholicam in communi, et in illa fide communi credit implicite vel confuse quodcunque singulare contentum sub sancta matre ecclesia, sicut logici vere dicunt quod sciendo ens esse in suo analogo sciunt omnia et singula in communi. Sic (ut sepe dixi) si quicunque christianus habuerit fidem caritate formatam in quantumcunque communi, sufficit cum virtute perseverancie ad salutem. Deus enim qui dedit primam fidem dabit clariorem, nisi fidelis ponat obicem. Non enim exigit Deus, ut omnes filii sui pro omni viacione sua cogitent continue in actu particulari de qualibet fidei particula, sed [p. 43] satis est quod postposita desidia habeant fidem in habitu formatam. Ex istis patet tercio quod quilibet laicus fidelis tenetur credere quod habet Christum sacerdotem suum, rectorem, episcopum atque papam, et sic de aliis sanctis Christi vicariis. Sed accedendo ad difficultatem dicitur: cum actus sunt suppositorum singularium et nostri fideles laici communicant cum suis presbyteris et curatis, necesse est quod habeant fidem de illis: ideo laici tenentur de aliquibus prepositis suis credere saltem implicite quod sunt partes ecclesie et capita sua, et de aliis non tenentur, et utrumque membrum potest subdividi, vel quod tenentur credere sic de illis secundum presentem iusticiam vel simpliciter secundum predestinacionem. Tunc dicitur quod laici non tenentur credere aliter de suo preposito nisi verum. Patet sic: Nemo tenetur quidquam credere, nisi quod Deus movet eum credere; sed Deus non movet hominem ad credendum falsum: ideo non debet credere falsum; licet enim ex fide falsa quandoque bonum proveniat et Deus moveat ad substanciam actus, tamen Deus non sic movet hominem, ut fallatur (ut patet libro sexto, tractatu de mendacio, capitulo —).

But it is evident from what has been said that neither follows, but its opposite.[^1] For which it must
be noted that faith is taken now for the act of believing by which one believes, now for the habit of
believing through which one believes, and now for the truth which is believed, as Augustine teaches, *De
Trinitate* 13, chapters 2 and 3.[^2] Second, it must be noted that one faith is the faithful man's
explicit [credence]{g:credulitas}, and another is [implicit faith]{g:fides-implicita}: as a catholic
having the habit of faith, infused or acquired, believes explicitly the catholic church in general, and
in that general faith believes implicitly or indistinctly every singular thing contained under holy
mother church; just as logicians truly say that, in knowing that being is in its analogue, they know all
things and each one in general.[^3] So (as I have often said), if any Christian has faith [formed]{g:fides-formata} by [charity]{g:caritas}, however general, it suffices, with the virtue of perseverance, for
salvation. For God, who gave the first faith, will give a clearer one, unless the faithful man puts an
obstacle in the way. For God does not require that all his sons, through the whole of their [wayfaring]{g:viacio},
should think continually in a particular act of each particle of the faith; but [43] it is enough that,
sloth set aside, they have faith formed in habit.[^4] From these things it is clear, third, that every
faithful layman is bound to believe that he has Christ as his priest, rector, bishop and
[pope]{g:papa}, and so of the other holy [vicars of Christ]{g:vicarius-christi}. But, coming to the difficulty, [the answer
is]{g:dicitur}: since acts belong to singular supposits, and our faithful lay people communicate with
their [presbyters]{g:presbiter} and [curates]{g:curatus}, it is necessary that they have faith about
them; therefore lay people are bound, of some of their [superiors]{g:prepositi}, to believe at least
implicitly that they are parts of the [church]{g:ecclesia} and their heads, and of others they are not
bound; and each member [of this division] can be subdivided: either they are bound to believe so of
them according to present justice, or [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} according to
predestination.[^5] Then the answer is that lay people are not bound to believe otherwise of their
superior than the truth. It is clear thus: no one is bound to believe anything except what God moves
him to believe; now God does not move a man to believe what is false; therefore he ought not to believe
what is false. For although good sometimes comes from false faith, and God moves to the substance of the
act, yet God does not so move a man that he is deceived (as is clear in the sixth book, the treatise on
lying, chapter[^6]

## ¶2 · 43.24–44.7

> [p. 43] Ex istis patet secundo quod pastor tenetur monere subditos suos per instrucciones operum virtuosorum, ut fidem sibi adhibeant quod sit talis. Patet ex hoc quod aliter acceptando tale officium peccaret mortaliter, ymmo inducendo hostes in ecclesiam domum Dei foret proditor Dei sui. Quando ergo subditus non cognoscit talia fructuosa opera sui prepositi, non tenetur credere quod sit talis. Si autem cognoscit implicite, tenetur proporcionaliter credere, et quando [p. 44] cognoscit explicite, tunc tenetur credere quod sit talis secundum presentem iusticiam et cum formidine ac condicionaliter quod sit talis simpliciter (ut dictum est superius de papa nostro). Non enim oneravit Deus sic suam ecclesiam, ut necessitata sit falsum credere de suis prepositis cum hoc quod non dent materiam sic credendi.

From these things it is clear, second, that a pastor is bound to admonish his subjects by the
instruction of virtuous works, so that they may give him credence that he is such [a part of the
church].[^7] It is clear from this: that otherwise, in accepting such an office, he would sin mortally;
indeed, by bringing enemies into the church, the house of God, he would be a traitor to his God. When,
therefore, a subject does not know such fruitful works of his superior, he is not bound to believe that
he is such. But if he knows them implicitly, he is bound to believe proportionately; and when [44] he
knows them explicitly, then he is bound to believe that he is such according to present justice, and,
with [misgiving]{g:formido} and conditionally, that he is such without qualification (as was said above
of our pope). For God has not so burdened his church that it is necessitated to believe what is false of
its superiors when they give no matter for so believing.[^8]

## ¶3 · 44.8–46.22

> [p. 44] Ad aliam difficultatem de canonizacione sanctorum patet quod correspondenter quodammodo est dicendum: Quamvis enim sit vehemencior evidencia ex testimonio Romane ecclesie quod persona quam canonizat sit membrum ecclesie triumphantis, et amplior evidencia, si pro sancto Deus operetur miracula, tamen utrobique in tali materia potest esse illusio. Nam sicut ex confessione legum ecclesie ipsa militans decipit et decipitur (ut patet V° decretalium, de sentencia excommunicacionis A nobis) in tantum quod quandoque multos excommunicat qui sunt soluti et valde acceptabiles quoad Deum: sic contingit quod aliquos absolvat et ascribat cathalogo sanctorum cum hoc quod apud Deum sint iustissime condempnati. Ideo absit christianum credere quod de necessitate salutis oportet omnem fidelem credere explicite de isto et quocunque quem ecclesia nostra canonizat, ut eo ipso sit beatus, quia tunc haberemus simbolum nimis longum. Nec debemus explicite credere de quoquam tali quod non sit sanctus, sed excuciendo consideracionem de talibus, adherendo parti nobis probabiliori, credendo cum condicione vel pie cum formidine dubitando; de aliquibus autem sanctis credimus absque formidine eciam explicite quod sunt sancti, ut de apostolis, martiribus et sanctis primitive ecclesie; de aliis autem [p. 45] modernioribus qui canonizantur racione parentele, questus vel muneris non oportet nos apponere tantam fidem; nec quemquam canonizatum intendo asserere vel defendere non esse partem ecclesie triumphantis, sed cum quadam formidine dubito, si hoc credo, cum scio quod hoc est possibile; et hoc existente vero credo illud implicite quod ignoro, nec volo contendere ad probandum proterve de quolibet citra Christum quod sit beatus, quamvis de multis hoc credam absque formidine. Nec miracula iam currencia dant omnibus veram fidem, cum potest committi potestas diabolo in penam peccati generacionis adultere signa querentis et fidem necessariam insensibilium dimittentis, ut portentis suis antichristiane inducant plurimos in errorem (de quorum indiciis dictum est libro sexto, sexto capitulo). Solet autem vocari miraculum nunc materialiter quodcunque opus a Deo miraculose factum, et sic sollicitantur multi ad deducendum conclusiones male sonantes, cum quelibet creatura videtur sic esse miraculum quoad Deum. Sed formaliter sumitur miraculum pro operacione Deo propria mirabili creature, et sic beati non faciunt miracula, licet sint quandoque occasio, ut Deus ad instanciam precum eorum seu meriti faciat miraculum. Et tunc dicuntur hii facere tale miraculum subauctentice vel ministratorie sicut solent. Et illa discrecio miraculi est eis abscondita nisi quibus Deus revelare voluerit. Et utinam non multiplicarentur tot ceremonie et signa in nostra ecclesia, cum facta sanctorum et omnes ritus alii non sunt laudabiles, nisi de quanto elevant animum ad [p. 46] diligendum dominum Jesum Christum. Et videtur multis quod fides confusa de oracione sanctorum unicius, et per consequens, intencius, securius et melius colligeret in christicolis fidem Christi quam tanta explicita multiplicitas sanctorum in dies introducta. Sic enim confuse cognoscimus de quolibet beato vel dampnato, prescito vel predestinato quod ipse est huiusmodi, cum qua confusa noticia stat dubietas particularis, et ignorancia cum distincta noticia de communi: ex tali itaque canonizacione multiplici ultra ritum veteris testamenti et nostre primitive ecclesie secundum partem prodest et secundum partem obest; et verisimile est multis quod plus nocet, cum multitudo ritus tollens libertatem ecclesie est causa multorum errorum. Et omnis talis sanctus oraret pro nobis efficacius, supposito quod dimisso cultu suo diligeremus amplius Jesum nostrum. Multi enim sunt sancti nobis incogniti canonizatis beaciores qui plus prosunt nobis orando quam dicti sancti quorum festa devocione particulari percolimus, cum secundum dignitatem elemosinarii prosit oracio sanctorum qui plus diligunt eos qui magis diligunt Deum suum.

To the other difficulty, about the [canonization]{g:canonizare} of saints, it is clear that the answer
must in a way correspond. For although there is stronger [evidence]{g:evidencia}, from the testimony of
the Roman church, that the person whom it canonizes is a [member]{g:membrum} of the [church triumphant]{g:ecclesia-triumphans}, and fuller evidence if God works miracles for the saint, yet in both cases, in such a
matter, there can be illusion. For just as, by the confession of the church's laws, the [church]
[militant]{g:ecclesia-militans} itself deceives and is deceived (as is clear in the fifth book of the Decretals, on the
sentence of excommunication, *A nobis*), so far that it sometimes excommunicates many who are loosed
and very acceptable as regards God: so it happens that it absolves some and enrols them in the
catalogue of the saints, when before God they are most justly condemned.[^9] Therefore God forbid that
a Christian should believe that it is of necessity for salvation that every faithful man believe
explicitly of this man, and of whomever our church canonizes, that by that very fact he is blessed;
for then we should have a [creed]{g:symbolum} far too long. Nor ought we to believe explicitly of any such man that
he is not a saint; but [we ought to proceed] by sifting our consideration of such men, adhering to the
side more probable to us, believing with a condition, or piously doubting with misgiving. Of some
saints, though, we believe without misgiving, even explicitly, that they are saints, as of the
apostles, the martyrs and the saints of the primitive church; but of others, [45] more modern ones, who
are canonized by reason of kinship, gain or gift, we need not lay down so much faith.[^10] Nor do I mean
to assert or defend of anyone canonized that he is not part of the church triumphant; but I doubt with a
certain misgiving, if I believe this, since I know that it is possible; and, if this is true, I believe
implicitly what I am ignorant of; nor do I wish to contend wantonly to prove of anyone short of Christ that
he is blessed, although of many I believe this without misgiving.[^11] Nor do miracles now current give
true faith to all, since power can be committed to the devil, in punishment of the sin of an adulterous
generation that seeks signs and lets go the necessary faith in [imperceptible]{g:insensibilis} things,
so that by their portents they may in antichristian fashion lead very many into error (of whose marks it
was said in the sixth book, sixth chapter).[^12] And a miracle is commonly called, now, materially, any
work done by God miraculously; and so many are drawn to deduce ill-sounding conclusions, since every
creature seems in this way to be a miracle as regards God. But formally a miracle is taken for an
operation proper to God, wonderful to the creature; and so the blessed do not work miracles, although
they are sometimes the occasion, so that God at the instance of their prayers or merit works a miracle.
And then they are said to work such a miracle sub-authentically, or ministerially, as they commonly
are.[^13] And that discernment of a miracle is hidden from them, except those to whom God has willed to
reveal it. And would that so many ceremonies and signs were not multiplied in our church, since the
deeds of the saints and all other rites are not praiseworthy except in so far as they raise the mind to
[46] love the lord Jesus Christ. And it seems to many that an indistinct faith about the prayer of the
saints would gather the faith of Christ in [worshippers of Christ]{g:christicole} more singly, and
consequently more intently, more securely and better, than so great an explicit multiplicity of saints
brought in from day to day.[^14] For in this way we know indistinctly of anyone blessed or damned,
[foreknown]{g:prescitus} or [predestinate]{g:predestinatus}, that he is such; with which indistinct
knowledge a particular doubt is compatible, and ignorance [is compatible] with distinct knowledge of the
general. So from such manifold canonization, beyond the rite of the old testament and of our primitive
church, there is in part profit and in part harm; and it is likely to many that it harms more, since the
multitude of rites, taking away the freedom of the church, is the cause of many errors. And every such
saint would pray for us more effectually, supposing that, his cult set aside, we loved our Jesus more.
For there are many saints unknown to us, more blessed than the canonized, who profit us more by praying
than the said saints whose feasts we keep with particular devotion; since the prayer of the saints, who
love more those who love their God more, profits according to the worthiness of the [almsman]{g:elemosinarius}.[^15]

## ¶5 · 46.24–47.5

> [p. 46] exigunt ut credatur suis approbacionibus et reprobacionibus in cunctis suis iudiciis, quam exigunt ut credatur fides sacre scripture que est fides catholica, et plus persequuntur pro complecione suarum tradicionum, puniendo contravenientes asperius, quam faciunt contra blasphemantes in precipuam fidem Christi, sic quod de eis dici potest illud Psalmi XLI, 10: Qui edebat panes meos magnificavit super me supplantacionem. Voluptuosus quippe presidens sub pallio pacis ecclesie querit bona pauperum que sunt autonomatice [p. 47] panes Christi, et ille magnificat phariseice tradiciones proprias quibus tyrannice spoliat populum cui dicit verbo et opere quod equivalent vel superant legem Dei. Hoc autem est in effectu supplantacio legis Christi etc.

more require that credence be given to their approvals and reprobations in all their judgments than
they require that credence be given to the faith of holy scripture, which is the catholic faith;[^16]
and they persecute more for the fulfilment of their traditions, punishing those who contravene them
more harshly, than they do against those who blaspheme against the chief faith of Christ; so that of
them can be said that word of Psalm 40:10: "Who ate my bread, hath greatly supplanted me."[^17] For the
pleasure-loving president, under the cloak of the church's peace, seeks the goods of the poor, which
are [by antonomasia]{g:autonomatice} [47] the bread of Christ; and he magnifies, like a Pharisee, his
own traditions, by which he tyrannically despoils the people, to whom he says by word and deed that they
are equal to or surpass the law of God. And this is in effect the supplanting of the law of Christ, etc.[^18]

## Notes

[^1]: **C/R.** The answer to the second objection (42.1–9): neither the loss of honour to prelates nor
    the collapse of canonization follows. *Neutrum sequitur*: "follows" of an inference. AB margin:
    *Responsio*; A: *Fides*.

[^2]: **S.** Augustine, *De Trinitate* 13.2.5–3.6, on *fides quae creditur* and *fides qua
    creditur*; PL 42:1016–1017; Loserth: *Opp.* VIII, p. 929 (OCR doubtful) (LEDGER E02f-1). B *habitum* (42.13); B's
    margin: *Fides sumitur tripliciter secundum Augustinum*. Wyclif's second sense (the habit) is not
    Augustine's.

[^3]: **C/R.** *Confuse*: "indistinctly", the scholastic *cognitio confusa* (knowledge of the general
    without the particulars), not "confusedly"; so *fides confusa* (46.2) and *confusa noticia* (46.6).
    *Sciendo ens esse in suo analogo*: knowing *ens*, the analogous term, one knows in general whatever
    falls under it. B lacks *Secundo* (42.16); B *singulare* (42.21). A's margins: *Fidei distinccio*;
    *Fides de ecclesia catholica cum caritate sufficit ad salutem*. Hus adds *hominis* after *fidelis*
    (42.17, Loserth).

[^4]: **C.** *Viacio*: the state of the wayfarer, "wayfaring" (cf. *viator*). *Ponat obicem*: the
    school phrase for the obstacle that blocks grace. *In habitu*: as a habit, against *in actu
    particulari*.

[^5]: **C/R.** *Actus sunt suppositorum singularium*: the scholastic maxim *actiones sunt
    suppositorum*: acts belong to individual subjects, so the layman's dealings with his priest require a
    belief about that individual. *Membrum*: a member of the division, not of the church. *Secundum
    presentem iusticiam … simpliciter secundum predestinacionem*: the two senses of being of the church
    (cf. the *presciti* in present grace). AB margin: *Conclusio*; A: *De prepositis quomodo tenentur
    subditi credere*; B: *Laici tenentur credere de aliquibus suorum prepositorum quod sunt partes
    ecclesie*.

[^6]: **S/T.** The sentence breaks off at *capitulo* (43.23), the chapter number wanting, and the
    parenthesis is never closed. *Libro sexto*: in Wyclif's *Summa theologie* the sixth book is *De
    veritate sacre scripture*, as Loserth supposes: its chapters 16–18 are the treatise on lying (c. 18 opens *faciendo finem in materia de mendacio*), which Wyclif also cites as *tractatus De Mendacio* at 159 (08-a); B reads *quinto*, and has *tractatu de mendacio* in
    the margin, not in the text (LEDGER E02f-2).

[^7]: **C.** *Quod sit talis*: that he is a part of the church (Loserth's side-note: "a part of the
    Church"); *talis* refers back to *partes ecclesie* (43.8). *Monere … per instrucciones operum
    virtuosorum*: he teaches by works, not words.

[^8]: **T/C.** *Cum formidine*: the misgiving that qualifies assent (GLOSSARY §C). *Ut dictum est
    superius de papa nostro*: 37.26–38.12, on Urban VI. *Oneravit*: B *ornavit* (44.4), which does not suit
    *ut necessitata sit*. *Cum hoc quod*: "when", "given that".

[^9]: **S/R.** *Decretals* of Gregory IX, *X* 5.39.28 (*A nobis est saepe quaesitum*; Friedberg II, 899–900), form ours
    (LEDGER E02f-3): *iudicium ecclesiae nonnunquam opinionem sequitur, quam et fallere saepe contingit et
    falli*. Loserth's 5.39.21 is another *A nobis*, on general excommunication. A lacks
    *patet* (44.16); B's margin: *V. decretalium*. A's margin: *de canonisacione sanctorum ab ecclesia
    Romana quomodo est credendum*.

[^10]: **C.** *Excuciendo consideracionem*: *excutere*, "to sift, examine closely". *Apponere tantam
    fidem*: "lay down", "apply" so much faith. *Racione parentele, questus vel muneris*: kinship, gain or
    gift, the motives of modern canonizations. A's margin: *De sanctis quomodo est credendum*; B: *Quomodo
    sciendum est de canonisacione sanctorum modernorum*.

[^11]: **C.** *Intendo* of purpose, "I mean". *Dubito, si hoc credo*: we take *si* concessively: even
    if I believe him blessed, I hold it with misgiving, because the contrary is possible; it might also
    be read "I doubt whether I believe this". *Citra Christum*: "short of Christ", anyone but Christ
    himself. B *formidine subito* (45.5).

[^12]: **S/T/C.** *Generacionis adultere signa querentis*: Matt. 12:39, DR "An evil and adulterous
    generation seeketh a sign". *Fidem … insensibilium*: faith in things not perceived (cf. Heb. 11:1).
    AB *generalis*, A₁ *genera'* (45.12). *Antichristiane*: Loserth's text; the MSS have *antichristine*
    (45.14); the subject of *inducant* is unexpressed (the devil's agents). *Libro sexto, sexto
    capitulo*: B *quinto capitulo* (45.16); the place in *De veritate* is unidentified (LEDGER E02f-4).

[^13]: **C.** *Subauctentice vel ministratorie*: as secondary, not principal, authors, or as
    ministers; God alone is the author of the miracle. B's margins: *Miraculum dupliciter sumitur*;
    *Miraculum fit a sanctis subautentice*. *Eis*: we take it of men generally; it might be the saints.

[^14]: **C.** *Unicius*: comparative of *unice*,
    "more singly", with one object. B's margins: *De quanto sunt ritus laudabiles* (45.31); *De oracione
    sanctorum* (46.2).

[^15]: **C.** *Secundum partem prodest et secundum partem obest*: impersonal. *Elemosinarii*: the
    recipient of alms, here the one for whom the saints pray (their prayer is alms); GLOSSARY
    *elemosinarius*, "almsman". *Qui plus diligunt eos qui magis diligunt Deum suum*: the
    saints love those more who love God more, so the worthier recipient profits more.

[^16]: **T.** The sentence runs on from ¶4 (46.23). *Plus* (46.24) stands at the end of ¶4 in our
    Latin because the ¶ break is false; restored from the plate. B's margin: *Prelati exigunt
    quod suis sentenciis adhibeatur fides quarum transgressores vehementer puniunt*. *Superiores nostri*:
    not the glossary's *prepositi*.

[^17]: **S/C.** Ps. 40:10 (Wyclif's XLI, 10), DR "who ate my bread, hath greatly supplanted me"; the
    Latin is the Vulgate's, abridged. Wyclif's exposition takes *magnificavit … supplantacionem*
    literally, "has magnified supplanting", hence *magnificat* and *supplantacio* below. B *punientes*
    (46.28); B's margin quotes the verse.

[^18]: **T/R.** *Autonomatice*: Loserth's text, the MSS *autonomastice* (47.1). *Equivalent*: the
    subject is the traditions. *Legis Christi etc.* (47.5): the print's last words, lost from our transcription at the chapter end and restored from the plate; A adds the written words
    *Kenmerton psano* (47.5); Loserth identifies Kenmerton (Kemerton) near Tewkesbury, a district that
    sent supporters to Oldcastle (Walsingham II, 307). On chapter 2, cf. Hus, *De eccl.* fol. 199a ff.,
    204b, 209b, 212a (Loserth; LEDGER E02f-5).
