# *De potestate pape*, chapter 6, part 2: the apostles' powers and the authority of scripture

## ¶1 · 106.29–107.16

> [p. 106] Item, si nostri episcopi habent tantam potestatem faciendi miracula eciam primitiva ut olim, cum sepe foret necessarium uti huiusmodi potestate, culpandi sunt ex ocio potestatis et discredendi tamquam fictores pompatici, cum nesciunt [p. 107] racione vel facto ostendere huiusmodi potestatem. Apostoli enim sicut habuerunt potestatem vexatos a sathana liberandi, sic eciam habuerunt potestatem tradendi Sathane peccatores inficientes ecclesiam. Nam Mar. ultimo dicit Christus: Signa autem eos qui crediderint, hec sequentur: In nomine meo demonia eiicient, linguis loquentur novis, serpentes tollent, et si mortiferum quid biberint non eis nocebit. Et patet in cronicis quod apostoli habuerunt huiusmodi potestates, ut patet de Paulo Act. ultimo; et quod apostoli habuerunt potestatem tradendi perversos sathane, patet Act. V de Anania et Saphira, et prima Cor. V de fornicario, qui cognovit uxorem patris et prima Thim. primo de Ymeneo et Alexandro, quos Apostolus tradidit sathane propter blasphemiam.

Again, if our [bishops]{g:episcopus} have so great a [power]{g:potestas} of working miracles, even the miracles of the beginning, as of old, then, since it would often be necessary to use such power, they are to be blamed for the idleness of their power and disbelieved as pompous pretenders, since they cannot [107] show such power by reason or by deed. For the apostles, just as they had power to free those vexed by Satan, so also had power to deliver to Satan sinners who infected the [church]{g:ecclesia}. For in the last [chapter] of Mark Christ says: "And these signs shall follow them that believe: In my name they shall cast out devils. They shall speak with new tongues. They shall take up serpents: and if they shall drink any deadly thing, it shall not hurt them."[^1] And it is clear in the chronicles that the apostles had such powers, as is clear of Paul in the last [chapter] of Acts;[^2] and that the apostles had the power to deliver the perverse to Satan is clear in Acts 5 of Ananias and Sapphira, and in 1 Corinthians 5 of the fornicator who knew his father's wife, and in 1 Timothy 1 of Hymenaeus and Alexander, whom the Apostle delivered to Satan on account of blasphemy.[^3]

## ¶2 · 107.18–108.24

> [p. 107] Item, secundum Apostolum prima Cor. XII, 4 divisiones graciarum sunt, quas Dominus dividit singulis prout vult; et ille gracie vel sunt potestates vel ad potestates consequentes, igitur sicut non omnes parificantur in graciis, sic nec in potestate. Nec valet dicere quod nostri prelati in potestate precipua sunt pares apostolis, quia summa potestas concessa christiano stat in hoc, quod sit Dei filius adoptivus, cum aliis inde sequentibus, ut puta quod constituatur super omnia bona Dei. Sed ex dubio cuiquam prelato nostro simplex ydiota sibi subiectus in hoc ipsum exuperat, igitur non in hoc debet pompare de preeminencia potestatis, ymo si parificatur apostolis in potestate ministrandi sacramenta et aliunde apostoli ipsum excesserant, videtur quod [p. 108] tota aggregata potestas in apostolis fuit maior, ymo cum apostoli mediate eadem potestate suffecerunt instituere sacramenta et tam miraculose ac tam efficaciter ministrare eadem sacramenta, videtur quod apostoli habuerunt ampliorem et efficaciorem potestatem ad dispensandum ecclesie sacramenta. Nec ostendunt in signis efficacibus, quod habeant vel absconditam potestatem; cum igitur posset esse error in institucione et error in potestatis abusione, racione cuius Deus non conferat sed auferat huiusmodi potestatem, videtur signum luciferinum gloriari sine efficacia operis de huiusmodi potestate. Opus inquam meritorium de celo ecclesie instanter tractum in preteritum gigneret consolacionem et gaudium potentatus et daret fidem subditis quod Deus sibi communicat potestatem. Status autem et potestas magna viancium incuteret eis ex altitudine casus formidinem. Et patet ex secundo capitulo per multa media quomodo in qualibet potestatum novem patres in primitiva ecclesia excesserunt. Aliter enim prelati nostri nimis illuderent subtrahendo usum huiusmodi potestatum.

Again, according to the Apostle, 1 Corinthians 12:4, "there are diversities of graces", which the Lord divides "to every one according as he will";[^4] and those graces either are powers or are consequent upon powers; therefore, just as not all are made equal in graces, so neither in power. Nor does it avail to say that our [prelates]{g:prelatus} are equal to the apostles in the principal power, because the highest power granted to a Christian consists in this, that he be an adopted son of God, with the other things that follow from it, for instance that he be set "over all the goods" of God.[^5] Now, for all anyone knows, a simple [unlettered man]{g:ydiota} subject to any prelate of ours surpasses him in this very thing;[^6] therefore he ought not to vaunt himself on preeminence of power in this; indeed, if he is made equal to the apostles in the power of ministering the sacraments, and the apostles had exceeded him in other respects, it seems that [108] the whole aggregated power in the apostles was greater; indeed, since by means of the same power the apostles sufficed to institute sacraments and to minister those same sacraments so miraculously and so effectively, it seems that the apostles had an ampler and more effective power for dispensing the sacraments to the church. Nor do they show by effective signs that they have even a hidden power; since, therefore, there could be error in the institution and error in the abuse of the power, by reason of which God would not confer but take away such power, it seems a Luciferian sign to glory in such power without the effect of the work. A meritorious work, I say, drawn down from heaven for the church at urgent asking, as in time past, would beget consolation and joy for the [potentate]{g:potentatus}, and would give faith to his subjects that God communicates power to him.[^7] But the great state and power of wayfarers would strike [fear]{g:formido} into them from the height of the fall.[^8] And it is clear from the second chapter, by many middle terms, how in each of the nine powers the fathers in the primitive church excelled.[^9] For otherwise our prelates would mock [us] too much by withholding the use of such powers.

## ¶3 · 108.25–110.19

> [p. 108] Quantum ad auctorizacionem scripture sacre patet ex modo loquendi beati Augustini IV De Doctrina Christiana VI cap.; quod apostoli et evangeliste qui scripserunt libros canonicos erant eorum autores, licet solus Deus fuerit autor summus. Aliter enim nulla creatura haberet auctoritatem ad aliquid faciendum. Quod autem sacerdotes nostri temporis non habent tantam auctoritatem vel potestatem canonizandi libros sicut sunt libri de biblia, videtur ex universali sermone dicto [p. 109] de veritate et auctoritate scripture. Et patet ex hoc; quod idem esset parificare eorum dicta auctoritati scripture, et asserere quod Spiritus Sanctus et Jhesus noster hec in ipsis loquitur. Sed vite eorum elongacio, sentencie contrariacio et propria confessio contradicunt; vivunt enim voluptuose et sermo eorum sapit avariciam. Sed Sapiencie I, 5 dicitur, quod Spiritus Sanctus discipline effugiet fictum, ideo perit illis evidencia auctoritatis, quod Spiritus Sanctus dicit in illis suas sentencias et cum in Christo non fuerunt Est et Non, ut patet II Cor. I, 17, 18; ipsi autem sepe contrariantur evangelio ac eciam sibi ipsis; ideo perit illis quantum ad illa in quibus extraneant, Christi auctoritas: sepe enim contradicit posterior precedenti. Et quantum ad conformacionem propriam, sunt multa dicta sanctorum paparum eminenciam scripture concorditer confitencium, sicut faciunt sancti doctores, in quibus fundant suam sentenciam, ymo in V Decretalium de sentencia excommunicacionis cap A nobis confitetur papa de sua ecclesia, quod sepe fallit et fallitur, eciam de excommunicacione et absolucione in quibus magis attenderet. Ideo non possent patencius accusare se ipsos de supina blasfemia quam parificare dicta sua extranea evangelio, specialiter cum deficiunt in potestate minori, ut puta interpretacione sermonum facta in loquendo omnia linguarum genera, ut fecerunt apostoli et sui conversi, ymo in exercendo gracias sanitatum. Et ista sunt modicum in comparacione ad [p. 110] superiora carismata. Ostendant igitur istas potestates, conversiones infidelium vel sanaciones infirmorum per suas epistolas, ut factum est cum scripturis apostolorum; et est evidencia quod Christus in eis loquitur, ymo ut dictum est superius, generaliter potestas subtrahitur pro mortali. Sed multi eorum ex ingratitudine incidunt in mortale, ideo necesse est quod potestas si quam in principio habuerunt ex ingratitudine sit subtracta. Item, si sint paris auctoritatis cum nostris auctoribus, cum continue multiplicant leges suas addiscere sicut legem fidei scripturarum, quod est contra leges multas ecclesie, que preferunt theologiam et privilegiant eius discipulos, similiter tunc destruerentur iura canonica, quia tota illa facultas foret pura theologia, quia sermo quem Deus in eis loquitur eque evidenter sicut locutus est olim in scribis fidei scripturarum.

As to the [authorizing]{g:autorizare} of holy scripture, it is clear from the manner of speaking of blessed Augustine, *On Christian Doctrine* book 4, chapter 6, that the apostles and evangelists who wrote the canonical books were their authors, although God alone was the highest author.[^10] For otherwise no creature would have [authority]{g:auctoritas} to do anything. And that the priests of our time do not have so great authority or power of [canonizing]{g:canonizare} books such as are the books of the Bible appears from the general discourse given [109] *On the Truth and Authority of Scripture*.[^11] And it is clear from this: that to make their sayings equal to the authority of scripture would be the same as to assert that the Holy Spirit and our Jesus speaks these things in them. But the distance of their life [from Christ's], the contrariety of their [teaching]{g:sentencia} and their own confession contradict [this]; for they live voluptuously and their speech savours of avarice. Now in Wisdom 1:5 it is said that "the Holy Spirit of discipline will flee from the deceitful"; therefore the [evidence]{g:evidencia} of authority perishes for them, [the evidence] that the Holy Spirit speaks his teachings in them. And since in Christ there was not "It is and It is not", as is clear in 2 Corinthians 1:17–18,[^12] now they often contradict the gospel and even themselves; therefore Christ's authority perishes for them as to those things in which they stray from it: for often the later contradicts the earlier. And as to their own conformity, there are many sayings of holy popes who with one accord confess the eminence of scripture, as the holy doctors do, on whom they ground their teaching;[^13] indeed, in book 5 of the Decretals, title *On the Sentence of Excommunication*, chapter *A nobis*, the [pope]{g:papa} confesses of his church that it often deceives and is deceived, even concerning excommunication and absolution, in which it should take the more heed.[^14] Therefore they could not more openly accuse themselves of supine blasphemy than by making their sayings, foreign as they are, equal to the gospel,[^15] especially since they fall short in a lesser power, for instance "interpretation of speeches" made in speaking all "kinds of tongues", as the apostles and their converts did, indeed in exercising "the graces of healing".[^16] And these are slight in comparison with [110] the higher charisms. Let them therefore show these powers, conversions of [unbelievers]{g:infidelis} or healings of the sick, through their epistles, as was done with the writings of the apostles; and that is evidence that Christ speaks in them; indeed, as was said above, power is in general withdrawn for mortal [sin]. Now many of them fall into mortal [sin] through [ingratitude]{g:ingratitudo}; therefore it is necessary that the power, if they had any in the beginning, has been withdrawn for ingratitude. Again, if they are of equal authority with our authors, then, since they continually multiply their laws, [we should have] to learn [them] like the law of the faith of the scriptures, which is against many laws of the church, which prefer theology and [privilege]{g:privilegiare} its students;[^17] likewise the canon [laws]{g:ius} would then be destroyed, because that whole faculty would be pure theology, because [it would be] the word that God speaks in them as evidently as he spoke of old in the scribes of the faith of the scriptures.

## Notes

[^1]: **S/T.** Mark 16:17–18, Douay–Rheims. The print has the Vulgate's futures *sequentur*, *loquentur*; the manuscripts read the presents *sequuntur*, *loquuntur* (apparatus 107.6–7), which do not change the sense. The print's *Unguis* is the Vulgate's *linguis*, "tongues".
[^2]: **S.** Acts 28:3–6, the viper at Malta, which did Paul no harm (the "serpents" of Mark 16:18).
[^3]: **S.** Acts 5:1–11; 1 Cor. 5:1–5; 1 Tim. 1:20 ("whom I have delivered up to Satan, that they may learn not to blaspheme").
[^4]: **S.** 1 Cor. 12:4, Douay–Rheims; the relative clause is 12:11, where the Vulgate's subject is the Spirit (*dividens singulis prout vult*). Wyclif makes *Dominus*, "the Lord", the subject.
[^5]: **S.** Matt. 24:47, "he shall place him over all his goods", turned from the master's goods to God's.
[^6]: **C.** *Ex dubio*, literally "out of doubt": taken here as "it is possible that" (no one knows who is predestinate). If it stands for *sine dubio*, "without doubt", the sentence asserts that some unlettered subject does surpass his prelate.
[^7]: **C.** *De celo ecclesie instanter tractum in preteritum* is hard to construe: literally "urgently drawn from heaven of the church into the past". Taken as a miracle won from heaven by the church's urgent prayer, as in the primitive church. *Potentatus* is the person who holds power, here the prelate (*sibi*).
[^8]: **G.** *Formido* here is plainly the emotion, so "fear", not the scholastic "misgiving". *Viancium*, "wayfarers", is Wyclif's word for those still on the way.
[^9]: **S.** The nine powers are set out in ch. 2 of this treatise; *media*, the middle terms of the arguments there.
[^10]: **S.** Augustine, *De doctrina christiana* 4.6.9 (Loserth: *Opp.* tom. III, 1, 67), where the scriptural writers are called "our authors" (LEDGER P06b-1).
[^11]: **S.** Wyclif's *De veritate sacre scripture* (Loserth's identification: Buddensieg's edition, 1904) (LEDGER P06b-2).
[^12]: **S.** 2 Cor. 1:17–19, Douay–Rheims; the phrase is 1:17 and 1:19 (Loserth cites 17, 18).
[^13]: **C.** *Conformacionem propriam* answers the third item of the list above, *propria confessio*, "their own confession": the popes' own words confirm scripture's preeminence. The word may stand for *confirmacionem* or *confessionem*; we translate the print.
[^14]: **S/C.** *Liber extra* 5.39.28 (*A nobis*, Innocent III), where the church's judgement "sometimes follows opinion, which it often happens both deceives and is deceived" (modern form ours; LEDGER P06b-3). *Sentencia* here is the judicial sentence of excommunication, so "sentence".
[^15]: **C.** *Extranea* agrees with *dicta*: "their sayings, foreign [to it]". The dative *evangelio* could also hang on *extranea* alone ("sayings foreign to the gospel"), with the object of *parificare* understood as scripture; the sense is the same.
[^16]: **S.** 1 Cor. 12:10 and 12:28 (Douay–Rheims "interpretation of speeches", "kinds of tongues", "the graces of healings"); the "higher charisms" that follow recall 1 Cor. 12:31.
[^17]: **T/C.** *Multiplicant leges suas addiscere*: so all the manuscripts (Loserth, apparatus 110.12–13). The infinitive has no governing verb; we supply "[we should have] to learn". At 110.14 the manuscripts read *prefuerunt*; Loserth prints *preferunt*, which we translate.
