# *De eucharistia*, chapter 5, part 5: Anselm and Augustine against an accident without a subject

## ¶1 · 131.14–133.11

> [p. 131] Unde, ut loquuntur Ambrosius, Hugo et alii veritatis discipuli quod eadem essencia que prius fuit non sacramentum post fit sacramentum virtute verborum Christi, et sic quoad figuracionem convertitur in corpus Christi, ut loquitur Anshelmus, et sic substancia panis et vini non remanet in actu consideracionis ut prius, sicut philosophi loquuntur, ponentes sensibile et tempus poni in actu per sensacionem et intelleccionem. Non enim intellexit quod sit accidens sine subiecto, cum in De Veritate capitulo ultimo ita scribit: Existente namque corpore colorem eius esse necesse est, et pereunte corpore colorem eius manere inpossibile est. Quamvis enim illud dicat in persona discipuli, tamen consequenter illud approbat ponendo diversitatem inter colorem et eius subiectum ac rectitudinem et eius signum. Rectitudo enim non dependet ab illo signo, sicut color dependet [p. 132] ab subiecto. Quando enim Augustinus et alii sancti in suis dyalogis loquuntur ut discipuli et pretereunt ac non reprobant, indubie illud quod ponunt tanquam discipuli ut magistri approbant. Unde in confirmacionem istius sentencie allegavi Augustinum superius quod accidens non potest esse sine subiecto tam in dyalogo suo quam alibi. Docet enim racio naturalis quod non potest esse accidens sine subiecto, quia non potest esse subiectum sine suo accidente, ergo multo magis econtra; nam secundum omnes qui intelligunt se ipsos maius vel tantum foret vinculum ac dependencia accidentis ad subiectum quam econtra. Et argumentum patet ex hoc quod si aliqua creatura est, tunc est dependencia eius ad Deum; quod est relacio inseparabilis; et sic non potest esse substancia materialis sine quantitate continua et discreta, sine relacione suarum parcium ad omnem eius punctum, et sic de infinitis aliis accidentibus. Numquid credimus quod analogum accidentis sit prius naturaliter quam genus substancie quod est basis cuilibet accidenti, sic quod accidens potest esse per totum mundum sine substancia, non econtra? unde genus substancie dependeret a genere accidentis, non econtra; ymmo species substancie materialis dependeret a corpore de genere quantitatis, non econtra, cum conveniunt invicem, ut quantitas potest quantumlibet rarefieri et condensari, ut coguntur dicere in eukaristia. Et sic substancia potest mutari in quantitatem sine acquisicione vel deperdicione quantitatis, cum quantitas ita potest; et sic punctus posset in infinitum maiorari, cum sicut pedalitas per accidens est pedalitas, sic quantitas per accidens est quantitas. [p. 133] Sed longe aliter sentit Augustinus de quantitate, ut patet Vᵗᵒ de Trinitate IXᵒ et XIIIᵒ, sic loquitur: Sicut ergo non dicimus tres essencias, ita non dicimus tres magnitudines. In rebus, inquit, que participacione magnitudinis magne sunt, quibus aliud est esse, aliud magnum esse, sicut magna domus et magnus mons et magnus animus, in hiis ergo rebus aliud est magnitudo, aliud quod ab ea magnitudine magnum est, et prorsus non hoc est magnitudo quod magna domus, sed vera magnitudo in Deo est ipse Deus et non sibi accidens.

Hence,[^1] as Ambrose, Hugh and other disciples of the truth say, that the same [essence]{g:essencia} which before was not a [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} afterwards becomes a sacrament by virtue of the words of Christ, and so, as to [figuration]{g:figuracio}, is converted into [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}, as Anselm says;[^2] and so the [substance]{g:substancia} of the [bread]{g:panis} and [wine]{g:vinum} does not remain in act of consideration as before, as the philosophers say, who posit that the perceptible and time are put in act by [sensation]{g:sensacio} and intellection.[^3] For he did not understand[^4] that there is an [accident without a subject]{g:accidens-sine-subiecto}, since in *De veritate*, in the last chapter, he writes thus: "For when the body exists its colour must exist, and when the body perishes it is impossible that its colour remain."[^5] For although he says it in the person of the disciple, yet he afterwards approves it, by positing a difference between colour and its [subject]{g:subiectum}, and rectitude and its sign. For rectitude does not depend on that sign as colour depends [132] on the subject. For when Augustine and the other [saints]{g:sancti} speak in their dialogues as disciples, and pass on and do not reject [what is said], beyond doubt what they posit as disciples they approve as masters. Hence in confirmation of this [teaching]{g:sentencia}[^6] I [alleged]{g:allegare} Augustine above, that an [accident]{g:accidens} cannot be without a subject, both in his dialogue and elsewhere. For natural reason teaches that there cannot be an accident without a subject, because there cannot be a subject without its accident; therefore much more the converse; for according to all who understand themselves, the bond and dependence of accident on subject would be greater than, or as great as, the converse. And the argument is clear from this, that if any [creature]{g:creatura} is, then there is its dependence on God, which is an inseparable relation; and so a material substance cannot be without continuous and discrete [quantity]{g:quantitas}, without the relation of its parts to its every point, and so of infinitely many other accidents. Are we to believe that the [analogous term]{g:analogum} accident[^7] is naturally prior to the genus of substance, which is the base for every accident, so that accident can be throughout the whole world without substance, and not the converse? so that the genus of substance would depend on the genus of accident, not the converse; indeed, a species of material substance would depend on a body of the genus of quantity, not the converse, since they [agree]{g:convenire} with each other, as quantity can be rarefied and condensed to any degree, as they are forced to say in the eucharist. And so substance can be changed as to quantity[^8] without the acquiring or losing of quantity, since quantity can be so; and so a point could be enlarged to infinity, since just as [foot-length]{g:pedalitas} is foot-length [by accident]{g:per-accidens}, so quantity is quantity by accident. [133] But Augustine [holds]{g:sentire} far otherwise of quantity, as is clear in *De Trinitate* V, [chapters] 9 and 13;[^9] he speaks thus: "Just as therefore we do not say three essences, so we do not say three magnitudes." "In things," he says, "which are great by participation in magnitude, for which it is one thing to be and another to be great, as a great house and a great mountain and a great mind, in these things therefore magnitude is one thing, and that which is great from that magnitude another; and magnitude is not at all the same thing as a great house; but true magnitude in God is God himself, and not an accident to him."

## ¶2 · 133.12–135.2

> [p. 133] Ex istis patet loyca huius sancti qua utitur probando distinccionem accidencium. Et patet secundo, quod non vocat accidencia res que poterunt per se esse (ut dictum est capitulo IIIᵒ). Et patet tercio error eorum qui ponunt quantitatem primi generis creature non distingui a sua substancia. Error enim in materia de eukaristia precipitat eos profundius ad implicandum quamlibet substanciam esse Deum, quia indubie, si non sit distinccio inter magnitudinem materialis substancie et suam essenciam, tunc (ut Augustinus arguit) ipsa est Deus; et credo quod error quo colitur accidens tanquam Deus induxit in errores huiusmodi cecans multos in loyca et methaphisica; et hii sunt qui dicunt sacramentum altaris esse quantitatem que est tempus maius aut minus, ut unam hostiam dicunt esse momentum et aliam diem dominicum, sed terciam dicunt septimanam vel mensem, hii magnificant tantum suam scienciam quod dicunt se scire modo distincte qua hora erit finale iudicium; oportet enim ipsos dicere quod hoc sacramentum [p. 134] sit quantitas successiva. Statuunt enim sibi tanquam principium hoc porisma dyaboli: Si aliquod ens creatum sit distinctum a substancia, tunc ipsum poterit per se esse, et ex isto profundant se in erroribus infinitis, dicentes quod accidens non potest esse nisi accidens, sicut substancia non potest esse nisi substancia, quia aliter quidditas nature sibi accideret, verumptamen accidens intelligi potest dupliciter, scilicet neutraliter vel adiective; neutraliter intelligendo sacramentum altaris esse accidens sed non secundo modo, cum nulli accidit; nec Deus supplet vicem cause materialis in isto, licet omnis creatura accidentaliter inest Deo, sed remanet in accidente per se posito quedam naturalitas ad formaliter inherendum; et sic quedam accidentalitas qualis non potest inesse substancie. Sed consequencius dicerent ponendo inesse cuiuslibet forme substanciali quandam naturalitatem remotam obediencialem, sicut cuilibet forme accidentali inest quedam remota naturalitas obediencialis, ut inhereat materiali substancie; nam ille qui potest sic mutare naturas ut forma accidentalis per se existat et agat, nedum producendo formas accidentales sed formas substanciales quod videtur naturali esse proprium substancie: ille, inquam, potest facere formas substanciales materiis inherere, potissime cum potest actuaciones formarum remanencium omnino suspendere; quod est plus quam appetitus et actuaciones remittere vel mutare; ideo homo istius scole timeret sibi ne blaspheme neget potenciam infinitam absolute necessariam Dei sui; unde ego utor hac arte, non admitto tales casus insolitos timendo blasphemias, nisi probetur mihi hoc esse de Dei potencia; et hoc facto paratus sum ad concedendum probatum [p. 135] et infinitum excellenciora quam ipsi ponunt Deo et sacramento suo competere.

From these [words] the [logic]{g:loyca} of this saint is clear, which he uses in proving the distinction of the accidents. And it is clear, secondly, that he does not call accidents things that could be [of themselves]{g:per-se}[^10] (as was said in chapter 3). And, thirdly, the error is clear of those who posit that the quantity of a creature of the first genus is not distinguished from its substance.[^11] For the error in the matter of the eucharist casts them down deeper, to implying that every substance is God, because beyond doubt, if there is no distinction between the magnitude of a material substance and its essence, then (as Augustine argues) it is God; and I believe that the error by which an accident is worshipped as God has led into errors of this kind,[^12] blinding many in logic and metaphysics; and these are the ones who say "the sacrament of the altar is a quantity which is a greater or lesser time", as they say one [host]{g:hostia} is a moment and another the Lord's day, but a third they say is a week or a month; these so magnify their knowledge that they say they know now distinctly at what hour the final judgement will be; for they must say that this sacrament [134] is a [successive quantity]{g:quantitas-successiva}. For they set up for themselves as a principle this [porism]{g:porisma} of the devil: "If any created [being]{g:ens} is distinct from substance, then it can be of itself"; and from this they sink themselves in infinite errors, saying that an accident cannot be anything but an accident, just as a substance cannot be anything but a substance, because otherwise the quiddity of its nature would befall it;[^13] nevertheless "accident" can be understood in two ways, namely [as a neuter]{g:neutraliter} [noun] or as an adjective: understanding it as a neuter, the sacrament of the altar is an accident, but not in the second way, since it befalls nothing; nor does God [supply the place]{g:vicem-supplere} of the material cause in this, although every creature is accidentally in God, but there remains in the accident posited of itself a certain naturalness toward formally inhering; and so a certain [accidentality]{g:accidentalitas} such as cannot be in a substance. But they would speak more consistently by positing that there is in every[^14] [substantial form]{g:forma-substancialis} a certain remote [obediential naturalness]{g:naturalitas-obediencialis}, just as in every accidental form there is a certain remote obediential naturalness, that it should inhere in a material substance; for he who can so change natures that an accidental form exists and acts of itself, [not only]{g:nedum} producing accidental forms but substantial forms, which seems to be proper to natural substance: he, I say, can make substantial forms inhere in matters, most of all since he can wholly suspend the actuations of the forms that remain; which is more than to remit or change appetites and actuations. Therefore a man of this school would fear for himself lest he blasphemously deny the infinite, absolutely necessary [power]{g:potencia}[^15] of his God. Hence I use this art: I do not admit such unusual cases, fearing blasphemies, unless it is proved to me that this is within God's power; and that done, I am ready to grant what is proved, [135] and things infinitely more excellent than they posit to belong to God and to his sacrament.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** The period opens with *ut loquuntur … quod* ("as they say … that"), a construction that never closes: the *quod*-clause gives what Ambrose, Hugh and the others say, and the two *et sic* clauses draw from it. Translated as it stands.
[^2]: **S.** Ambrose, Hugh (of St Victor) and Anselm are named without a work. Ambrose's words on the bread made the body by Christ's word are perhaps *De sacramentis* IV.4 and *De mysteriis* 9; to be checked (LEDGER K05e-1). Hugh's place and Anselm's *quoad figuracionem convertitur*, to be checked (LEDGER K05e-2, K05e-3).
[^3]: **S.** "The philosophers": Aristotle, for whom the perceptible is in act in sensation (*De anima* III.2) and time is not without the soul that numbers it (*Phys.* IV.14); to be checked (LEDGER K05e-4). ABCD read *sensibilem*.
[^4]: **C.** *Intellexit*: the subject is Anselm, just named; "understand" in the sense "take it that".
[^5]: **S/T.** Anselm, *De veritate*, the last chapter (c. 13), in the disciple's mouth; Loserth: *Dial. De veritate*, p. 114 (ed. Gerberon) (LEDGER K05e-5). C has *perempto* in the text, *pereunte* in the margin; DE omit *esse*; C reads *a signo* at 131.30.
[^6]: **C/S.** *Sentencia*, the CONTEXTUAL row, here "teaching": the position that an accident cannot be without a subject. The earlier place (*superius*) and the dialogue meant (perhaps the *Soliloquia*) are to be checked (LEDGER K05e-6).
[^7]: **G/C.** *Analogum accidentis*: accident taken as an analogous term, said of the categories other than substance by analogy and not as one genus; set against *genus substancie*.
[^8]: **C.** *Mutari in quantitatem*, literally "be changed into quantity"; the run of the argument (rarefaction and condensation without gain or loss of quantity) asks for "as to quantity". *Cum conveniunt invicem* is taken of substance and quantity agreeing, so that what is said of the one is said of the other. E reads *perdicione*; DE *posset*.
[^9]: **S.** Loserth: *De Trin.* V.8 and V.10 (Maurist ed. VIII, 838); the chapter numbers 9 and 13 are Wyclif's. Loserth notes that Augustine's text at *sed vera magnitudo* runs otherwise: *Sed illa est vera magnitudo qua non solum magna est domus quae magna est et qua magnus est mons quisquis magnus est* ("but that is true magnitude by which not only is a house great that is great, and by which a mountain is great, whatever mountain is great"). Wyclif's form is translated (LEDGER K05e-7).
[^10]: **C.** *Per se*, the CONTEXTUAL row: "of themselves", existing on their own without a subject. The reference to chapter 3 is to be checked (LEDGER K05e-8). E omits *res*.
[^11]: **S/C.** *Quantitatem primi generis creature*: the quantity of a creature in the first category, substance. Wyclif names no one; the position that quantity is not distinct from substance is Ockham's (*De corpore Christi*, *De sacramento altaris*), to be checked (LEDGER K05e-9).
[^12]: **T.** *Huiusmodi* is Loserth's; the manuscripts read *huius*, without *modi* ("into the errors of this [one]"). C reads *cecantes*.
[^13]: **C.** *Accideret*: "befall", the verb of *accidens*: if an accident could be other than an accident, its own nature would be accidental to it. The adversaries' position runs from *Statuunt* to *inesse substancie*, the distinction *neutraliter vel adiective* included; Wyclif's answer begins at *Sed consequencius*.
[^14]: **T.** Loserth prints *cuiuslibet forme substanciali*, which does not construe; C reads *cuilibet*, which is translated (it matches *cuilibet forme accidentali* after it). D reads *conveniencius* for *consequencius*.
[^15]: **C.** *Potencia*, the CONTEXTUAL row: God's power, so "power".
