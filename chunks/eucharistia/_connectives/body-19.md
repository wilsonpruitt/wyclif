# sed/autem extract, wave 19: chunks 07-g 07-h 07-i

### 07-g ¶1 · 213.21–214.3  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 213] **Sed** ulterius notandum quod Augustinus dicit Deum apparere oculis corporalibus in dicto casu et in ista carne; **sed** loquitur de apparicione remota et abstracta. Nam videndo corpus assumptum ab angelo ad dandum officium dicit scriptura sepe quod homo videt illum angelum, ubi oportet quod visus sumatur aggregative pro visione sensus et visione intellectus terminatis ad visibile aggregatum ex assumpto corpore et ipso angelo. Et sepe dicit scriptura quod homo taliter videt Deum; unde unus in casu videt angelum, et bestia vel alius homo videns eandem corporalem ymaginacionem non videt angelum, quia deficit sibi visio [p. 214] intellectus appropriata spiritui elaborata a tali noticia sensitivo. **Sed** secus est de visione hominis, cum ipse sit realiter ipsum corpus.

ENGLISH: But further it is to be noted that Augustine says that God appears to bodily eyes in the said case and in this flesh;[^1] but he speaks of a remote and abstract appearing. For when the body assumed by an angel to discharge an office is seen, scripture often says that a man sees that angel, where "seeing" must be taken [aggregatively]{g:aggregative} for the vision of sense and the vision of the intellect, terminated at the visible thing aggregated from the assumed body and the angel himself. And scripture often says that a man sees God in this way; hence one man in the case sees the angel, and a beast or another man seeing the same bodily image does not see the angel, because he lacks the vision [214] of the intellect appropriated to a spirit, worked out from such sensitive knowledge.[^2] But it is otherwise with the seeing of a man, since he is [really]{g:realiter} the body itself.[^3]

### 07-g ¶2 · 214.4–215.4  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 214] **Sed** quarto obicitur sic: Homo videt angelum, sicut oportet concedere ex fide scripture, et ita abscondita est natura angeli in illo corpore assumpto, sicut corpus Christi in hostia consecrata, ergo per idem homo videt in hostia sensibiliter corpus Christi. Nec valet dicere quod homo videt corpus oculo corporis et angelum solum oculo mentis, quia tunc falsa foret scriptura Genes. XVIIIᵒ, 2 ubi dicitur: Cum Abraham elevasset oculos, apparuerunt ei tres viri stantes iuxta eum; quos cum vidisset, cucurrit in occursum eorum; ubi notum est quod loquitur de visione oculi corporalis. Unde Num. XXIIᵒ, 24 dicitur quod asina Balaam vidit angelum in angustiis duarum maceriarum, ubi patet quod intelligitur solum de visione sensitiva, cum asinus non habuit intellectum. Et idem patet expresse Judic. XIIIᵒ, 3 de parentibus Sampsonis quod viderunt angelum oculo corporali. Juxta quem modum loquendi loquitur evangelium. Nam Math. XXVIIIᵒ, 3 dicitur de angelo: Erat **autem** aspectus eius sicut fulgur, et vestimenta eius sicut nix. Et Marc. XVIᵒ, 5 de eodem angelo dicitur quod mulieres introeuntes in monumentum viderunt revolutum lapidem et iuvenem sedentem in dextris coopertum stola candida. Hic et in quotlibet locis aliis patet quod loquitur de visione corporea. Cum ergo non sit phas fideli negare istam scripturam nec argumentum captum ab ea per locum a completa similitudine, videtur quod si homo potest videre angelum qui est omnino invisibilis in sua natura per visionem corporis contingentissime sibi coniuncti; multo magis [p. 215] homo videret in sacramento corpus Christi, cum sit eque presens in hostia, cui permanencius copulatur quam angelus corpori sibi magis alieno et habet in se racionem sensibilis.

ENGLISH: But fourthly it is objected thus: a man sees an angel, as must be granted [from the faith of scripture]{g:ex-fide}, and the [nature]{g:natura} of the angel is hidden in that assumed body just as the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} is in the consecrated [host]{g:hostia}; therefore by the same token a man sees [perceptibly]{g:sensibiliter} in the host the body of Christ. Nor does it avail to say that a man sees the body with the eye of the body and the angel only with the eye of the mind, for then scripture would be false, Gen. 18:2, where it is said: "When Abraham had lifted up his eyes, there appeared to him three men standing near him: and when he saw them, he ran to meet them";[^4] where it is known that it speaks of the vision of the bodily eye. Hence in Num. 22:24 it is said that the ass of Balaam "saw the angel" "in a narrow place between two walls",[^5] where it is clear that this is understood only of sensitive vision, since the ass did not have intellect. And the same is expressly clear in Judg. 13:3 of the parents of Samson, that they saw the angel with the bodily eye. In keeping with this manner of speaking the gospel speaks. For in Matt. 28:3 it is said of the angel: "And his countenance was as lightning and his raiment as snow." And in Mark 16:5 it is said of the same angel that the women "entering into the sepulchre saw the stone rolled back and a young man sitting on the right side, clothed with a white robe."[^6] Here and in any number of other places it is clear that it speaks of bodily vision. Since therefore it is not lawful for a faithful man to deny this scripture, nor the argument taken from it by [the topic from complete likeness]{g:locus-a-completa-similitudine},[^7] it seems that if a man can see an angel, who is wholly invisible in his nature, through the vision of a body most contingently joined to him, much more [215] would a man see in the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} the body of Christ, since it is equally present in the host, to which it is more permanently coupled than the angel is to a body more alien to him, and it has in itself the character of a [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} thing.[^8]

### 07-g ¶3 · 215.5–215.18  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 215] Hic dicitur quod sancti patres negantes visionem corporalem corporis Christi in hostia satis preceperunt hanc evidenciam et quotquot alias quas scimus facere. Conceditur ergo scriptura tanquam fides, **sed** ad sensum katholicum, scilicet quod homo videt oculo corporali corpus assumptum ab angelo et oculo mentis videt naturam angeli; et illa visio aggregata terminatur ad duplex visibile aggregatum, scilicet corpus sentitum et angelum. Unde ad docendum nos istam secundam partem visionis dicitur Genes. XVIIIᵒ, 3 sic singulariter: Domine si inveni graciam in oculis tuis, ne transeas servum tuum. Et multa sunt similia singulariter dicta pro visione mentali que principaliter terminabatur ad Deum.

ENGLISH: To this the answer is that the holy fathers, denying [bodily vision]{g:visio-corporalis} of the body of Christ in the host, anticipated well enough this [evidence]{g:evidencia} and as many others as we know how to make.[^9] Scripture is therefore granted as faith, but in the catholic [meaning]{g:sensus}, namely that a man sees with the bodily eye the body assumed by the angel and with the eye of the mind sees the nature of the angel; and that aggregated vision terminates at a twofold aggregated visible, namely the body [perceived]{g:sentire} and the angel. Hence, to teach us this second part of the vision, it is said in Gen. 18:3, thus [in the singular]{g:singulariter}:[^10] "Lord, if I have found favour in thy sight, pass not away from thy servant." And there are many similar things said in the singular for the mental vision, which terminated [principally]{g:principaliter} at God.

### 07-g ¶4 · 215.19–215.24  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 215] Et quantum ad illud Num. XXIIᵒ, patet quod loquitur de nuda visione sensitiva que terminabatur ad corpus quod secundum tropum sepe dictum vocatur angelus; non enim fuit asina elevata ad cognoscendum Deum **sed** docuit hominem quantum sibi pertinuit.

ENGLISH: And as for that text of Num. 22, it is clear that it speaks of the bare sensitive vision, which terminated at the body that, by the [trope]{g:tropus} often spoken of, is called the angel; for the ass was not raised up to know God, but taught the man as far as it belonged to her.

### 07-g ¶5 · 215.25–216.7  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 215] Triplex **autem** ponitur racio diversitatis quare conceditur quod homo sic videt angelum et non sic corpus Christi in hostia. Primo quia figura, motus et vox viva que apparent in corpore assumpto per angelum significant expressius presenciam racionalis nature quam significat hostia vel suum accidens corpus Christi, ideo solo intellectu et fide illud concipitur. Secundam causam videtur beatum Gregorium [p. 216] notare quod angelus cum sit racionale animal assumpsit corpus in unitatem persone, et sic videndo idem corpus videt homo suppositum quod est angelus, quia illud corpus foret persona angeli. Et tercio quia natura angeli est in corpore illo diffinitive, **sed** corpus Christi licet sit ibi vere et realiter, tamen est ibi non verius quam sacramentaliter ut in signo.

ENGLISH: And a threefold reason of the difference is given why it is granted that a man sees an angel in this way and does not in this way see the body of Christ in the host. First, because the figure, motion and living voice that appear in the body assumed by the angel signify the presence of a rational nature more expressly than the host or its [accident]{g:accidens} signifies the body of Christ;[^11] therefore that [body] is conceived by intellect and faith alone. The second cause blessed Gregory seems [216] to note:[^12] that the angel, since he is a rational animal, assumed a body into unity of [person]{g:persona}, and so in seeing that same body a man sees the [supposit]{g:suppositum} which is the angel, because that body would be the person of the angel.[^13] And third, because the nature of the angel is in that body [definitively]{g:diffinitive}, but the body of Christ, although it is there [truly]{g:vere} and really, yet is there no more truly than [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter}, as in a [sign]{g:signum}.

### 07-g ¶6 · 216.8–216.11  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 216] **Sed** istas difficultates hic non discucio; **sed** faciat adversarius in toto plenam similitudinem utrobique et concedam sibi quod ipse videt corpus Christi in hostia oculo corporali.

ENGLISH: But I do not discuss these difficulties here; but let the adversary make out a full likeness [on both sides]{g:utrobique} in every respect, and I will grant him that he sees the body of Christ in the host with the bodily eye.

### 07-g ¶8 · 216.21–217.3  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 216] Hic dicitur primo quod multe rime reperte sunt ad colendum false hoc sacramentum, ut patet de transsubstanciacione, de conversione, de ydemptificacione et de inpanacione cum eis similibus, in quibus omnibus oportet fidelem cavere de heresi, quia sicut prima heresis discipulorum orta est circa hoc sacramentum, sic et heresis multiplicior exinde est orta. Ideo oportet attendere ad sensum scripture katholicum et ad prudencias sanctorum doctorum in materia ista loquencium; hoc **autem** servabitur pro theoreumate quod scriptura debet undique concedi ut fides katholica [p. 217] et specialiter verba Christi quibus sacramentum conficitur: Hoc est corpus meum; cuius sensum sepe exposui.

ENGLISH: To this the answer is, first, that many [chinks]{g:rima} have been found for worshipping this sacrament falsely,[^16] as is clear of [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio}, of conversion, of identification and of [impanation]{g:inpanacio}, with the things like them, in all of which the faithful man must beware of heresy; for just as the first heresy of the disciples arose about this sacrament, so also a more manifold heresy has arisen from it.[^17] Therefore one must attend to the catholic meaning of scripture and to the wise sayings of the holy doctors who speak on this matter; and this will be kept as a theorem, that scripture must be granted everywhere as the catholic faith, [217] and especially the words of Christ by which the sacrament is [consecrated]{g:conficere}: "This is my body" (Matt. 26:26); whose meaning I have often expounded.

### 07-h ¶1 · 217.23–218.14  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 217] **Sed** supponamus totam communitatem christianorum irregulariter cognoscere hunc sensum, non fingendo mendacium ex parte accidencium per se existencium et admittere talem modum loquendi: Hoc signum est corpus Christi ad sensum katholicum, ita quod esse significet sic sacramentaliter figurare, adhuc non sequitur quod panis ydemptificetur corpori Christi, ut patet attendenti ad sensus. Non enim [p. 218] sequitur, si panis sic figurabit corpus Christi quod tunc fiet idem numeraliter eidem corpori; nec subest tanta auctoritas ad concedendum locucionem unam ut aliam. Nunquam enim crediderunt patres vere ecclesie quod panis ille sit idem numeraliter cum corpore Christi, sicut nec credunt hodie sacerdotes qui conficiunt; tunc enim tangerent infirmi illum devocius quam mulier que tetigit fimbriam vestimenti Christi Luc. VIIIᵒ, 44; et infinitum honorabilius tractarent corpus dominicum vivum et gloriosum quam de facto tractant panem illum. Et patet quod nec fides scripture nec sancti doctores nec leges ecclesie dictant quod omnis talis panis consecratus fiet ydemptice corpus Christi.

ENGLISH: But let us suppose that the whole community of Christians [irregularly]{g:irregulariter} knows this [meaning]{g:sensus},[^1] not feigning a lie on the part of [accidents]{g:accidens} existing [of themselves]{g:per-se}, and admits such a manner of speaking: "This sign is the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}" in the catholic meaning, so that "is" signifies "thus [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} to [figure]{g:figurare}"; still it does not [follow]{g:sequitur} that the [bread]{g:panis} is [identified]{g:ydemptificare} with the body of Christ, as is clear to one who attends to the meanings. For it does not [218] follow, if the bread will thus figure the body of Christ, that then it will become numerically the same with that same body; nor is there so great an authority underlying the granting of the one locution as of the other.[^2] For the fathers of the true [church]{g:ecclesia} never believed that that bread is numerically the same with the body of Christ, just as the priests who [consecrate]{g:conficere} do not believe it today; for then the sick would touch it more devoutly than the woman who "touched the hem of" Christ's "garment", Luke 8:44,[^3] and they would handle [the Lord's body]{g:corpus-dominicum}, living and glorious, infinitely more honourably than in fact they handle that bread. And it is clear that neither the faith of scripture nor the holy doctors nor the [laws of the church]{g:leges-ecclesie} dictate that every such consecrated bread will become [identically]{g:ydemptice} the body of Christ.

### 07-h ¶2 · 218.15–218.28  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 218] Et si replicatur per locum a simili, si panis quem Christus accepit in manibus fiebat et fit corpus Christi per consecracionem, per idem panis quem sacerdos hodie rite consecrat, fieret et foret sic corpus Christi: patet quod doctores moderni negarent assumptum ut summe hereticum; ille **autem** qui fideliter concederet consequens quod panis ille est corpus Christi ad sensum expositum, negaret quod panis ille fiet vel erit idem numeraliter cum corpore Christi, quia illud esse quod est sic figurare non est sic ydemptificari; et talis fidelis crebro concederet sine repugnancia quod ille panis est et non est corpus Christi, ut equivocacio in qua non est contradiccio melius detegatur.

ENGLISH: And if [it is rejoined]{g:replicatur} by [the topic from likeness]{g:a-simili} — if the bread which Christ took in his hands became and becomes the body of Christ by [consecration]{g:consecracio}, by the same token the bread which the priest today duly consecrates would become and would be thus the body of Christ — it is clear that the [modern doctors]{g:moderni-doctores} would deny [the assumption]{g:assumptum} as supremely heretical;[^4] but he who faithfully granted the [consequent]{g:consequens}, that that bread is the body of Christ in the meaning expounded, would deny that that bread will become or will be numerically the same with the body of Christ, because that [being]{g:esse} which is thus to figure is not thus to be identified; and such a faithful man would often grant without [incompatibility]{g:repugnancia} that that bread is and is not the body of Christ, so that the [equivocation]{g:equivocacio}, in which there is no contradiction, may be better [exposed]{g:detegere}.

### 07-h ¶3 · 218.29–218.31  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 218] **Sed** difficultas est de virtute qua alteratur panis vel ut alii locuntur, convertitur, transsubstanciatur vel consecratur ut sit sacramentum.

ENGLISH: But the difficulty is about the [power]{g:virtus} by which the bread is altered or, as others speak, is converted, transubstantiated or consecrated, so as to be a [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}.[^5]

### 07-h ¶4 · 218.32–219.26  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 218] Hic dicitur primo quod multitudo talium terminorum introducta sine auctoritate scripture multum alteravit et perturbavit ecclesiam; secundo dicitur quod illa [p. 219] virtus quam habet panis est transitoria et nobis insensibilis, nec scimus pro quo instanti advenit vel recedit, cum tamquam species lucis fundatur in corpore Christi; ideo dicunt quidam philosophi quod panis non exinde alteratur, alii **autem** dicunt quod alteratur spiritualiter, extense loquendo de alteracione, et terminatur ad conversionem panis in corpus Christi, ut loquitur Ambrosius. Et per illam conversionem diversi diversimode intelligunt, illi minus bene quod panis secundum se totum destruitur, et illi quod panis fit corpus Christi ad sensum expositum; et sic potest intelligi Thomas quod corpus Christi est ibi presens per conversionem panis in ipsum; sic Christus est presens humanitus fidelibus congregatis in nomine suo per fidem et dileccionem conversis ad ipsum. Nec est alia sanctitas in sacramento, ut patet ex dictis; et illa sanctitas videtur esse subiective in corpore Christi et contentive vel diffinitive in sacramento non corporaliter **sed** causaliter, sicut sacramentum habet aliam virtutem ad movendum animam fidelem ad devote memorandum Christum, que virtus est subiective in anima et obiective in sacramento; nunquam tamen est nota fidelitas quod virtus talis sit res potens per se existere vel quod illa mutacio vel conversio terminatur ad ydemptitatem numeralem sacramenti et corporis Christi.

ENGLISH: To this the answer is, first, that a multitude of such terms, introduced without the [authority]{g:auctoritas} of scripture, has much altered and disturbed the church; secondly it is said that that [219] power which the bread has is transitory and [imperceptible]{g:insensibilis} to us, nor do we know at what instant it comes or departs, since, like a species of light, it is founded in the body of Christ;[^6] therefore some philosophers say that the bread is not altered by it, but others say that it is altered [spiritually]{g:spiritualiter}, [speaking in an extended sense]{g:extense-loquendo} of alteration, and that it terminates at the [conversion]{g:conversio} of the bread into the body of Christ, as Ambrose speaks.[^7] And by that conversion different men understand different things: those less well, that the bread according to its whole self is destroyed; and these, that the bread becomes the body of Christ in the meaning expounded; and so Thomas can be understood, that the body of Christ is present there by the conversion of the bread into it;[^8] so Christ is present [humanly]{g:humanitus} to the faithful gathered in his name, converted to him by faith and [love]{g:dileccio}.[^9] Nor is there other holiness in the sacrament, as is clear from what has been said; and that holiness seems to be subjectively in the body of Christ and [by way of containing]{g:contentive}, or definitively, in the sacrament, not [bodily]{g:corporaliter} but causally,[^10] just as the sacrament has another power, to move the faithful soul to remember Christ devoutly, which power is subjectively in the soul and objectively in the sacrament; yet it is never a known [point of] fidelity that such a power is a thing able to exist of itself, or that that change or conversion terminates at the [numerical identity]{g:ydemptitas-numeralis} of the sacrament and the body of Christ.[^11]

### 07-h ¶5 · 219.27–220.7  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 219] **Sed** pro istis mutacionibus notandum quod possunt tam bene quam male intelligi. Bene, ut transsubstanciacio sit conversio, quando una substancia transit in aliam vel fit alia. Contentarentur **autem** superiores ecclesie de sensu transsubstanciacionis manente transsubstanciati essencia, cum nullus alius sit fundandus. Ponunt enim papam in sua intronizacione creari [p. 220] mutato nomine et creare hos quos promovet; illud **autem** foret plus quam transsubstanciare, quod fit quando Deus mutat creaturam substancialem in melius. Unde Ambrosius IIᵒ libro De Penitencia: Interiit, inquit, caro, cum substancia eius transit in Christum. Ideo sepe dicunt doctores quod conversus sit ipse Christus.

ENGLISH: But as for these changes it is to be noted that they can be understood well as well as badly. Well, so that [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio} is a conversion, when one [substance]{g:substancia} passes into another or becomes another. And the superiors of the church would be content with the meaning of transubstantiation in which the [essence]{g:essencia} of the transubstantiated thing remains, since no other is to be founded. For they hold that the [pope]{g:papa} at his enthronement is created [220] with his name changed, and creates those whom he promotes;[^12] and that would be more than to transubstantiate, which happens when God changes a substantial [creature]{g:creatura} for the better. Hence Ambrose in the second book *On Penance*: "The flesh," he says, "perished, when its substance passes into Christ."[^13] Therefore the doctors often say that the converted man is Christ himself.[^14]

### 07-h ¶8 · 220.29–221.9  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 220] Et quantum ad decretalem que videtur dicere quod accidens est sine subiecto, dicitur quod hoc est inpossibile [p. 221] de virtute sermonis, ut patet ex determinacione Augustini; **sed** potest sic sane intelligi quod accidens sensibile in eukaristia est sine corpore sibi subiecto existente in actu consideracionis fidelis ad quod magis debet attendere. Nam (ut dictum est) cogitacio nostra principaliter debet esse de corpore Christi in celo et cogitacio de natura eukaristie est propterea suspendenda. Et ad istum sensum videntur sancti dicere quod in hostia solum remanent qualitates.

ENGLISH: And as for the [decretal]{g:decretalis} which seems to say "an accident is without a subject",[^20] the answer is that this is impossible [221] [in the proper sense of the words]{g:de-virtute-sermonis}, as is clear from the determination of Augustine;[^21] but it can soundly be understood thus: that the [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} accident in [the Eucharist]{g:eukaristia} is without a body subject to it existing in the act of the faithful man's consideration, to which he ought rather to attend.[^22] For (as has been said) our thought ought [principally]{g:principaliter} to be of the body of Christ in heaven, and thought of the nature of the Eucharist is for that reason to be [suspended]{g:suspendere}. And in this meaning the saints seem to say that in the [host]{g:hostia} only [qualities]{g:qualitas} remain.

### 07-h ¶9 · 221.11–221.18  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 221] **Sed** quarto principaliter circumpalpitat infidelis per aliam rimam vacuam, dum arguitur pro ydemptificacione per hoc quod magis consonus et devotus modus in eukaristia est ponendus; **sed** huiusmodi est inpanacio, ut dicit sic opinans quod sicut verbum assumpsit humanitatem faciendo ex deitate et humanitate idem suppositum, ita videtur in materia de eukaristia.

ENGLISH: But fourth, as a main head, the [unbeliever]{g:infidelis} gropes about through another empty chink,[^23] when [it is argued]{g:arguitur-per} for [identification]{g:ydemptificacio} by appeal to this, that the more consonant and devout mode is to be posited in the Eucharist; now such is [impanation]{g:inpanacio}, as one who so opines says: that just as [the Word]{g:verbum} [assumed]{g:assumere} [the humanity]{g:humanitas}, making of [the Godhead]{g:deitas} and the humanity the same [supposit]{g:suppositum}, so it seems in the matter of the Eucharist.

### 07-i ¶1 · 221.31–222.17  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 221] Hic tangitur deliramentum tercium quo ceci rimantur ficticias ad palliandum sua mendacia. Nam (ut [p. 222] supra) primum est transsubstanciacio in qua ponunt quod panis et vinum omnino destruitur. Secundum est ydemptificacio in qua ponunt unum suppositum ydemptice fieri aliud. Et tercium est inpanacio in qua ponunt non suppositum fieri suppositum, **sed** naturam assumi ypostatice a supposito, sic quod in prima ficticia ponunt de transsubstanciato nec remanere materiam nec formam **sed** totum desinere. In secunda vero ponunt tolerabilius quod panis et vinum remanent in suis suppositis; que supposita sunt panis et vinum et per viam ydemptificacionis fiunt corpus Christi et sanguis. Tercia via dicit coloracius quod non remanent supposita panis et vini, **sed** nature panis et vini servantur per ypostaticam unionem; et sic intelligunt quidam sentenciam triplicem quam recitat Magister IVᵒ Sentenciarum, distinccione XIᵒ, capitulo primo.

ENGLISH: Here the third [raving]{g:deliramenta} is touched on, by which the blind pry out [fictions]{g:ficticia} to [cloak]{g:palliare} their lies.[^1] For (as [222] above) the first is [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio}, in which they posit that the [bread]{g:panis} and [wine]{g:vinum} is wholly destroyed. The second is [identification]{g:ydemptificacio}, in which they posit that one [supposit]{g:suppositum} [identically]{g:ydemptice} becomes another. And the third is [impanation]{g:inpanacio}, in which they posit not that a supposit becomes a supposit, but that a [nature]{g:natura} is assumed [hypostatically]{g:ypostatice} by a supposit; so that in the first fiction they posit that of the transubstantiated thing neither matter nor form remains, but the whole ceases. And in the second they posit more tolerably that the bread and wine remain in their supposits, which supposits are bread and wine, and by way of identification become the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} and his blood. The third way says [more colourably]{g:coloracior} that the supposits of bread and wine do not remain, but the natures of bread and wine are kept through [hypostatic]{g:ypostaticus} [union]{g:unio}; and so some understand the threefold [teaching]{g:sentencia} which [the Master]{g:magister} [recites]{g:recitare} in the fourth [book] of the *Sentences*, distinction 11, chapter 1.[^2]

### 07-i ¶2 · 222.18–223.14  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 222] **Sed** arguendo contra istam sentenciam notandum est primo quod (ut referunt cronicantes) erat quidam frater predicator Parisius qui propter magnificacionem istius sentencie fuit ibi excommunicatus et appellando ad curiam Avinonicam fuit tempore Clementis quinti in ista sentencia condempnatus, sicut tempore Urbani quinti fuit alius predicator et inquisitor pravitatis heretice dampnatus, quia voluit defendere quod homo videt corpus Christi in hostia oculo corporali. Et racionabile est quod seminans in materia fidei ultra quam scit defendere vel fundare sit tanquam ecclesie inimicus defamandus, cum Spiritus Sanctus dedit sponse [p. 223] sue quid et quantum debet credere. Ideo odibiles sunt qui ad onus et illusionem ecclesie fingunt mendacia. Et utinam ecclesia Avinonica ponderaret istam sentenciam. Licet enim usque ad tempus Clementis Vᵗⁱ stetit episcopus Romanus (ut debuit) intra Romam vel suam diocesim, ut cronicat Cistrensis libro ultimo cap. XXXIV, et diu ante cepit generacio ista declinare ab antiqua fide de quidditate hostie, tamen tota illa ecclesia propter diuturnitatem more in irreligiosa lascivia potest dici Avinonica, sicut fingit se Romanam ecclesiam; si enim vendicat universalem iurisdiccionem, ut Petrus, debet secundum regulas Christi illuc accedere quo in plus populosa patria novit gentilitatem barbaram plus fervere.

ENGLISH: But in arguing against this teaching it is to be noted first that (as the chroniclers report) there was a certain [friar]{g:frater} preacher at Paris who, for magnifying this teaching, was excommunicated there, and, appealing to [the Avignon curia]{g:curia-avinonica}, was condemned in this teaching in the time of Clement V;[^3] just as in the time of Urban V another preacher and inquisitor of [heretical depravity]{g:heretica-pravitas} was condemned, because he wished to defend that a man sees the body of Christ in the [host]{g:hostia} with the bodily eye.[^4] And it is reasonable that one who sows in the matter of faith beyond what he knows how to defend or found should be defamed as an enemy of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, since the Holy Spirit gave his [bride]{g:sponsa} [223] what and how much she ought to believe. Therefore those are hateful who, to the burden and [illusion]{g:illusio} of the church, [feign]{g:fingere} lies. And would that the Avignon church would weigh this teaching! For although until the time of Clement V the [Roman bishop]{g:romanus-episcopus} stood (as he ought) within Rome or his diocese, as the Cestrensis chronicles in his last book, chapter 34,[^5] and long before, this generation began to decline from the ancient faith about the [quiddity]{g:quiditas} of the host, yet that whole church, because of the long duration of its stay in irreligious wantonness, can be called Avignonese, as it feigns itself the Roman church; for if it claims universal [jurisdiction]{g:iurisdiccio}, as Peter [did], it ought according to the rules of Christ to go thither where, in a more populous country, it knows barbarous [paganism]{g:gentilitas} rages most.[^6]

### 07-i ¶5 · 224.7–225.6  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 224] Secundo notandum quod inter omnia decreta sanctorum istud decretum cum glossa sua magis facit pro nostra sentencia. Augustinus enim distinguens inter sacrificium et sacramentum dicit quod sacrificium conficitur ex sacramento et re sacramenti que est corpus Christi et sanguis, ut est in persona Christi, non quod deitas et humanitas sint partes Christi **sed** eius quidditates, quarum utraque est totus Christus. Sic sacrificium dicit aggregative et non per viam composicionis ex partibus sacramentum sensibile et corpus Christi cum sanguine. Ulterius Augustinus approbans locucionem tropicam ita scribit: Caro Christi et quam sub forma panis opertam in sacramento accipimus, et sanguis eius quem sub vini specie ac sapore potamus, sic quod caro est corporis et sanguis est sanguinis sacramentum. Hic patet quod iste sanctus loquitur tropice ut scriptura et docet quod corpus Christi ibi absconditur sic quod non ibi videtur oculo corporali. Et tercio explanat et applicat hunc sensum expressius: Panis, inquit, celestis qui vere est caro Christi suo modo vocatur corpus Christi, cum revera sit sacramentum corporis Christi, quod exponit anterius per hoc quod istis signatur vera caro Christi et sanguis. Unde glossa dicit quod hoc celeste [p. 225] sacramentum, quando sic representat Christi carnem, dicitur corpus Christi **sed** inproprie, scilicet signandi misterio. Sic, inquit Augustinus, ipsa carnis inmolacio que fit manibus sacerdotis vocatur Christi passio, mors et crucifixio, non rei veritate **sed** signandi misterio, sicut sacramentum fidei baptismi est fides.

ENGLISH: Second, it is to be noted that among all the [decrees]{g:decretum} of the saints this decree with its gloss makes most for our teaching.[^12] For Augustine, distinguishing between sacrifice and [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}, says that the sacrifice "is made up of the sacrament and the [reality of the sacrament]{g:res-sacramenti}, which is the body and blood of Christ",[^13] as it is in the [person]{g:persona} of Christ: not that [the Godhead]{g:deitas} and [the humanity]{g:humanitas} are parts of Christ, but his quiddities, each of which is the whole Christ. So the sacrifice [signifies]{g:dicere} [aggregatively]{g:aggregative}, and not by way of composition from parts, the [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} sacrament and the body of Christ with the blood. Further, Augustine, approving [tropical speech]{g:locucio-tropica}, writes thus: "The flesh of Christ, which we receive covered under the form of bread in the sacrament, and his blood, which we drink under the [species]{g:species} and savour of wine, so that the flesh is the sacrament of the body and the blood is the sacrament of the blood."[^14] Here it is clear that this saint speaks [tropically]{g:tropice}, as scripture does, and teaches that the body of Christ is hidden there, so that it is not seen there with the bodily eye. And third, he explains and applies this [meaning]{g:sensus} more expressly: "The heavenly bread," he says, "which is [truly]{g:vere} the flesh of Christ, is in its manner called the body of Christ, although [in truth]{g:revera} it is the sacrament of the body of Christ";[^15] which he expounds earlier by this, that by these the true flesh and blood of Christ are signified. Hence the gloss says that this heavenly [225] sacrament, when it thus represents the flesh of Christ, is called the body of Christ, but improperly, namely by a [mystery]{g:misterium} of signifying.[^16] "So," says Augustine, "the very immolation of the flesh, which is done by the hands of the priest, is called the passion, death and crucifixion of Christ, not in the truth of the thing but by a mystery of signifying"; just as the sacrament of faith, of baptism, is faith.[^17]

### 07-i ¶6 · 225.7–225.29  (7 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 225] In isto et aliis sibi similibus vellem emulos notare distinccionem predicacionis duplicis et sensum proposicionis sacramentalis, et tunc non mirarentur de sanctorum doctorum decretis cum ecclesie legibus nec de nobis ipsos tunc exponentibus. Quis (rogo) dubitat quin glossa ista sit duplex ex mendacio glossatorum sumpta que datur decreto Nicholai IIⁱ Ego Beringarius, cum textus sic loquitur: Profiteor panem et vinum que in altari ponuntur post consecracionem non solum sacramentum **sed** verum corpus Christi et sanguinem domini nostri Jesu Christi esse. Super quo textu dat glossa pro regula ut omnia referas ad species ipsas, ita quod iste sit sensus: profiteor non panem et vinum **sed** species panis et vini per se positas non solum esse sacramentum nec corpus Christi, **sed** sub illis contineri corpus Christi. Et sic confessio Beringarii est inpossibilis et heretica de virtute sermonis, **sed** debet glossari per suum contradictorium, cum hereticum sit quod panis et vinum remaneant post consecracionem sacramentum, **sed** sunt res aliene nature, non panis et vinum **sed** accidencia que non possunt esse corpus Christi, **sed** in illis est corpus Christi.

ENGLISH: In this and in others like it I would wish the rivals to note the distinction of the twofold [predication]{g:predicacio} and the meaning of the sacramental [proposition]{g:proposicio},[^18] and then they would not wonder at the decrees of the holy doctors together with the [laws of the church]{g:leges-ecclesie}, nor at us when we expound them. Who (I ask) doubts but that this gloss is double, taken from the lie of the glossators, which is given to the decree of Nicholas II, *Ego Berengarius*,[^19] since the text speaks thus: "I profess that the bread and wine which are placed on the altar are after consecration not only a sacrament but the true body of Christ and the blood of our Lord Jesus Christ." On which text the gloss gives as a rule that you refer everything to the species themselves, so that this is the meaning: "I profess, not the bread and wine, but the species of bread and wine set [by themselves]{g:per-se}, to be not only a sacrament nor the body of Christ, but that under them the body of Christ is contained."[^20] And so the confession of Berengar is impossible and heretical [in the proper sense of the words]{g:de-virtute-sermonis}, but must be glossed by its contradictory, since it is heretical that bread and wine remain a sacrament after consecration; but they are things of an alien nature, not bread and wine but accidents, which cannot be the body of Christ, but in them is the body of Christ.[^21]
