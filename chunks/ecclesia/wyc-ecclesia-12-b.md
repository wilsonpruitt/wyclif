# *De ecclesia*, chapter 12, part 2: every grant implies a condition of worthiness; third objection: privileges to be read mercifully

## ¶1 · 254.24–258.7

> [p. 254] Unde vani sunt casus quibus ponitur Deum auferre a justo graciam vel adnichilare hominem sine suo demerito; hoc enim derogaret omnipotencie et supremo dominio Dei nostri. Ideo cum notum sit quod Deus, si sic faceret juste faceret, non restat nisi expectare in fide predicta, quousque probatum fuerit, si poterit, quod Deus possit sic facere; nichil itaque repugnat potencie adnichilandi cum illa non potest esse, et ista exemplaris iusticia in Deo debet esse exemplar cuilibet eius vicario, tam pape quam regi, ut non faciat [p. 255] iniuriam alicui. Unde blasphemant quidam extollentes papam sophistice super omne quod dicitur Deus, qui dicunt quod licet sibi auferre beneficium a subdito non demerente nec facta debita recompensacione aliunde; hoc enim non est Deo licitum et si Antichristus illud presumpserit, tunc Christus faceret humiliter pacienti iniuriam recompensam. Et eodem modo dicitur de dominis temporalibus; non enim licet eis spoliare sanctam matrem ecclesiam, nisi prius deliquerit abutendo elemosinis datis pauperibus, et multo magis non licet regi concedere membris diaboli licenciam iniuste oblata a sancta matre ecclesia perpetuo detinendi, quia hoc foret maior iniuria. Unde notare potest indifferens quomodo sum specialior procurator pro conservandis privilegiis et bonis sancte ecclesie, quam illi qui dicunt quod licet dominis temporalibus concedere latronibus ut sine restitucione retineant quecunque bona sancte ecclesie. Et si obicitur quod volenti non fit iniuria, sed totum regnum est virtualiter congregatum in parliamento, ergo consenciente parliamento ad aliquid non fit regno iniuria, licet scelerati habuerint in ecclesia privilegium supradictum, patet quod consequencia non procedit. Nam stat quod signando volenti fiat manifesta iniuria, ut posito quod quis velit peccare et habeat excitatorem et fautorem criminis, hinc primo peccatori volenti fit iniuria a proximo culpabiliter excitante. Ymmo quandocunque prepositus omittit debitam correpcionem propter falsam pietatem volentis sordescere, iniuriatur eidem, ut Hely dampnabiliter iniuriatus est filiis suis Ophni et Finees, quam iniuriam ipsi dampnabiliter voluerunt. Et multo magis fovens hominem in peccato propter pecuniam, iniuriatur [p. 256] eidem, et ita generaliter quandocunque quis consentit culpabiliter temptatori; nec est hic sophisma: Petrus vult id quod de facto est peccatum vel formaliter antecedens ad illud, sed non vult peccatum, sicut sophiste dicunt quod formaliter vult percutere Petrum scit quod ille est sacerdos, et tamen non vult percutere sacerdotem. Nam sophisma istud est impossibile (sicut alias declaravi); tunc enim non foret volucio vel concessio implicita, quia si homo vult particulare per accidens implicitum in universali per se primo volito, multo magis vult implicite formaliter consequens ad per se volitum, ut si rex vult quod Petrus clericus habeat dictum privilegium, quia vult quod omnis clericus regni sui habeat dictum privilegium, et Petrus est aliquis talis clericus, multo magis vult omne formaliter sequens ad illud. Unde necessitas cogit eciam avaros concedere hanc logicam, cum aliter non participarent privilegiis vel elemosinis principum, nisi in concessione vel volucione generali, notata in cartis illis, implicite sit concessa participacio elemosine generalis. Et isto modo concedunt catholici quod in Petro procuratore ecclesie concessa est a Christo generaliter in quodam involucro potestas ordinis cuilibet sacerdoti sequenti. Cum ergo peccatum patenter sequitur ex actibus volitis cum suis circumstanciis, videtur quod volens illud antecedens vult saltem implicite peccatum consequens. Sic enim dicit Augustinus quod omne peccatum est voluntarium, et fides scripture sepe dicit quod homo vult et diligit multa mala, et tamen notum est quod non vult ipsa explicite secundum racionem qua mala. Et hec racio quare necesse est dominos temporales in quacunque concessione generalis privilegii vel elemosine perpetue subintelligere [p. 257] condicionem dignitatis persone capacis (ut dictum est XX° cap. libri proximi et sepe alias). Unde (ut dixi hic) Deus non potest subintelleccionem talis condicionis pretermittere, et curia Romana declarat in omni concessione sua de privilegio condicionem debere subintelligi (ut in proximo capitulo allegatur decretum pape XXVª q. I, Ideo permittente). Absit itaque ut imponamus dominis temporalibus tantam blasphemiam quod ipsi non sint Deo subiecti subintelligentes condicionem, si Deo placuerit, cum Jacobi IV° docetur tota christiana religio de cunctis operibus futuris exprimere vel saltem subintelligere si Deus voluerit; aliter enim posset discolus vendicare elemosinas principum stante maxima discolia; et sic inficerentur elemosine (ut dictum est XXIV cap. libri proximi). Unde Archidiaconus dicit quod tales discoli non sunt illi quibus elemosine sunt concesse. Attenderemus ergo ad dictum Dei, 1° Regum II°, 30, concedentis Hely et suo generi sacerdocium perpetuum: Loquens, inquit, locutus sum, ut domus tua et domus patris tui ministraret in conspectu meo usque in sempiternum. Nunc autem dicit Dominus: Absit hoc a me, sed quicunque honorificaverit me, glorificabo eum, qui autem contempnunt me, erunt ignobiles; non potest blaspheme intelligi quod Deus sit contrarius sibi ipsi, cum secundum Apostolum se ipsum negare non potest, sed quia subintellexit condicionem de Hely et suo genere quantum ad sacerdocium perpetuum, quam condicionem ipsi peccando diruperant, necesse fuit ex iusto iudicio veritatis, ut transferretur ab illo ad alium per donum Dei. Aliter enim Deus foret auctor iniurie. Ideo sepe dedi clericis nostris [p. 258] consilium quod honorarent scripturam sacram, tam verbo quam opere, quia sine conservacione huius carte impossibile est quod maneat dignitas ad privilegium vel aliquod bonum gratuitum capiendum. Alie autem carte prosunt quandoque per accidens, sed nunquam nisi in virtute testamenti domini principaliter observati.

Hence the cases are vain in which God is supposed to take grace away from a just man, or to annihilate a man, without his demerit; for this would derogate from the omnipotence and the supreme [lordship]{g:dominium} of our God. Therefore, since it is known that God, if he did so, would do it justly, nothing remains but to wait in the aforesaid faith until it has been proved, if it can be, that God can do so; and so nothing conflicts with the power of annihilating, since that [unjust taking away] cannot be; and this exemplary justice in God ought to be an exemplar to every vicar of his, pope as well as king, that he do no [255] injury to anyone.[^1] Hence certain people blaspheme, sophistically exalting the [pope]{g:papa} above all that is called God, who say that it is lawful for him to take a benefice away from a subject who has not deserved it, without due recompense being made from elsewhere; for this is not lawful for God; and if Antichrist should presume it, then Christ would make recompense for the injury to the one who suffers it humbly.[^2] And the same is said of [temporal lords]{g:temporales domini}; for it is not lawful for them to despoil holy mother [church]{g:ecclesia} unless she has first offended by abusing the [alms]{g:elemosina} given to the poor; and much more is it not lawful for a king to grant to [members]{g:membrum} of the devil licence to withhold unjustly, in perpetuity, from holy mother church what has been offered [to her], since this would be a greater injury.[^3] Hence an impartial man can note how I am a more special [proctor]{g:procurator} for preserving the [privileges]{g:privilegium} and goods of holy church than those who say that it is lawful for temporal lords to grant to robbers that they keep, without restitution, any goods whatever of holy church. And if [it is objected]{g:obicitur} that no injury is done to one who is willing; now the whole [kingdom]{g:regnum} is virtually assembled in parliament; therefore, when parliament consents to anything, no injury is done to the kingdom, even though criminals have had the aforesaid privilege in the church: it is clear that the [inference]{g:consequencia} does not hold.[^4] For it can be that, to point out [a case], a manifest injury is done to one who is willing: as, supposing that someone wills to sin and has an inciter and abettor of the crime, hence, first, injury is done to the willing sinner by his neighbour who culpably incites him. Indeed, whenever a [superior]{g:prepositi} omits due correction out of false piety toward one who wills to grow filthy, he does him injury, as Eli damnably did injury to his sons Hophni and Phinehas, an injury which they themselves damnably willed.[^5] And much more does one who fosters a man in sin for money do injury [256] to him; and so, generally, whenever someone culpably consents to a tempter. Nor is there place here for the [sophism]{g:sophisma}: "Peter wills that which in fact is a sin, or formally antecedent to it, but does not will the sin"; as the sophists say that [someone] formally wills to strike Peter, knows that he is a priest, and yet does not will to strike a priest.[^6] For that sophism is impossible (as I have shown elsewhere); for then there would be no implicit willing or [granting]{g:concessio}; because if a man wills a particular that is implied accidentally in a universal willed per se and primarily, much more does he implicitly will what follows formally, the [consequent]{g:consequens} of what is willed per se: as, if the king wills that Peter the [cleric]{g:clericus} have the said privilege because he wills that every cleric of his kingdom have the said privilege, and Peter is some such cleric, much more does he will everything that follows formally from that.[^7] Hence necessity forces even the avaricious to grant this logic, since otherwise they would not share in the privileges or alms of princes, unless in the general grant or willing recorded in those charters a share in the general alms were implicitly granted. And in this way catholics grant that in Peter, the proctor of the church, the power of order was granted by Christ generally, in a kind of wrapping, to every priest who follows.[^8] Since, then, sin plainly [follows]{g:sequitur} from acts that are willed, with their circumstances, it seems that one who wills that antecedent wills, at least implicitly, the consequent sin. For so Augustine says that every sin is voluntary; and the faith of scripture often says that man wills and loves many evils; and yet it is known that he does not will them explicitly under the aspect by which they are evils.[^9] And this is the reason why temporal lords, in any grant whatever of a general privilege or of perpetual alms, must [understand as implied]{g:subintelligere} [257] the condition of the worthiness of a capable person (as was said in chapter 20 of the preceding book, and often elsewhere).[^10] Hence (as I have said here) God cannot omit the implied understanding of such a condition; and the [Roman curia]{g:curia romana} declares that in every grant of its concerning privilege the condition must be understood as implied (as the pope's decree in C. 25 q. 1 [c. 16], *Ideo permittente*, is [alleged]{g:allegare} in the preceding chapter).[^11] Far be it, therefore, that we should impute to temporal lords so great a blasphemy as that they are not subject to God, understanding as implied the condition: if it please God; since in James 4:15 the whole Christian religion is taught, concerning all future works, to express, or at least to understand as implied: if God will. For otherwise a [froward]{g:discoli} man could claim the alms of princes while the greatest frowardness persisted; and so the alms would be infected (as was said in chapter 24 of the preceding book). Hence the Archdeacon says that "such froward men are not those to whom the alms have been granted".[^12] We should attend, then, to the word of God in 1 Samuel 2:30, when he granted Eli and his line a perpetual priesthood: "I said indeed," he says, "that thy house, and the house of thy father, should minister in my sight, for ever. But now saith the Lord: Far be this from me: but whosoever shall honour me, him will I glorify: but they that despise me, shall be despised." It cannot be understood, except blasphemously, that God is contrary to himself, since according to the Apostle "he cannot deny himself" (2 Timothy 2:13); but because he understood as implied a condition concerning Eli and his line as to the perpetual priesthood, a condition which they had broken by sinning, it was necessary, by the just judgment of truth, that it be transferred from him to another by the gift of God. For otherwise God would be the author of injury.[^13] Therefore I have often given our clerics [258] the counsel that they should honour holy scripture, in word as well as in deed; because without the keeping of this charter it is impossible that worthiness should remain for receiving a privilege or any gratuitous good. But other charters are sometimes of use accidentally; but never except in virtue of the testament of the Lord, kept as the principal [charter].[^14]

## ¶2 · 258.8–258.22

> [p. 258] Tercio principaliter obicitur per hoc quod privilegium regum est pie et misericorditer interpretandum, ymmo necessitante casu ad aliquod inconveniens fugiendum est maius inconveniens et inconveniens minus tollerandum. Non ergo sequitur: dato privilegio sequitur inconveniens, ergo ipsum est impium vel tollendum, cum tunc evangelium, passio Christi et omne bonum foret tollendum, cum occasione male accepta malum ex illo provenit. Et istum obiectum clamant tam scolastici quam vulgares in tantum quod unus iuridicus dixit michi nullam esse legem universalem humanam, quin sepe in casu necesse sit ex illa sequi iniusticiam iudicando. Ideo attendendo ad maius bonum fructus quam malum defectus, debet malum nedum permitti sed fieri propter bonum.

The third main objection is [by appeal to]{g:obicitur-per} this: that the privilege of kings is to be interpreted piously and mercifully; indeed, when a case forces [one] into some [ill consequence]{g:inconveniens}, the greater ill consequence is to be fled and the lesser ill consequence tolerated.[^15] It does not follow, therefore: "Given the privilege, an ill consequence follows; therefore it is impious or to be done away with"; since then the gospel, the passion of Christ and every good would have to be done away with, since, by occasion wrongly taken, evil comes from it. And both the schoolmen and the [common people]{g:vulgares} cry up this [objection]{g:objectum}, so much so that a certain jurist told me that there is no universal human law from which it is not often necessary, in a [particular] case, that injustice follow in judging. Therefore, attending to the greater good of the fruit rather than to the evil of the defect, evil ought not only to be permitted but to be done for the sake of good.[^16]

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** Side-note 254.24; A in the margin, above fol. 164 without reference mark, *Deus non potest iniuste auferre nec potest annichilare* (254.24). *Ponitur*: is supposed (in the case put). *In fide predicta*: the faith stated at the end of 12-a (254.19–23). *Cum illa non potest esse*: *illa* is taken as the unjust taking away or annihilation of the cases just named; it could also be read of a power so used, "since such [a power] cannot be". *Exemplaris … exemplar*: Wyclif's play is kept.

[^2]: **S/C.** An allusion, roman in the print: 2 Thessalonians 2:4, "above all that is called God" (DR; Wyclif *super*, Vulgate *supra*), turned on the pope's flatterers. Side-note 255.1. *Non demerente*: who has not deserved [the loss]. *Faceret … iniuriam recompensam*: literally "would make the injury recompensed".

[^3]: **C/T.** A and A₁ read *dicetur*, "will be said" (255.8); side-note 255.8. *Deliquerit*: the subject is holy mother church. *Licenciam iniuste oblata … perpetuo detinendi*: construed with *detinere a*, "to withhold from"; *oblata* taken as the offerings made to the church. A and A₁ *membro* for *membris* (255.11); A *detenendi* (255.12).

[^4]: **S/C.** Side-notes 255.13, 255.19; A in the margin, without reference mark, *Obieccio. Materia super voluntate peccati* (255.19). *Specialior procurator*: Wyclif turns back the charge of 12-a (250.20) that he would destroy the church's privileges. *Volenti non fit iniuria*: the legal maxim (cf. Digest 47.10.1.5; *Liber Sextus* 5.12 reg. 27, *Scienti et consentienti non fit iniuria*, Friedberg II; LEDGER E12b-1). *Sed totum regnum*: the minor premiss, hence "now". *Virtualiter*: by representation.

[^5]: **C/S.** *Signando*: taken as "to point out [a case]", introducing *ut posito*; the gerund has no expressed object. A in the margin *Conclusio* (255.23); B in the margin *Volenti fit iniuria* (255.27). *Primo*: the first of the cases the side-notes letter a–c (255.19, 255.27, 255.31). Eli's failure to correct his sons: 1 Samuel 2:22–25 and 3:13 ("because he knew that his sons did wickedly, and did not chastise them", DR). *Volentis sordescere*: one who wills to grow filthy.

[^6]: **C/T.** A in the margin *Sophisma* (256.2); side-note 256.1. *Quod formaliter vult percutere Petrum scit quod ille est sacerdos*: the print has no subject and no connective before *scit*; construed as "[someone] … wills …, knows …". B reads *cum non vult* at 256.6.

[^7]: **C.** B in the margin *Volucio duplex implicita scilicet et explicita* (256.10). *Particulare per accidens implicitum in universali per se primo volito*: a particular implied accidentally in a universal that is willed per se and primarily. *Formaliter consequens ad per se volitum*: what follows formally from what is willed per se. The king's grant to "every cleric" stands for the sanctuary charter's grant to every fugitive.

[^8]: **C.** *In quodam involucro*: "in a kind of wrapping", i.e. contained implicitly in the grant to Peter. *Potestas ordinis*: the power of order, the sacramental power. *Sequenti*: taken as "who follows [him]" in succession.

[^9]: **S.** A in the margin *Augustinus* (256.27); side-note 256.27. Augustine on sin as voluntary: *De vera religione* 14.27 (PL 34), *usque adeo peccatum voluntarium est malum, ut nullo modo sit peccatum, si non sit voluntarium*, which Wyclif paraphrases (LEDGER E12b-2). *Secundum racionem qua mala*: under the aspect by which they are evils.

[^10]: **C/R.** A in the margin *Conclusio* (256.31); side-note 257.3. *Subintelligere*: to understand [a condition] as implied, though not written. *Dignitatis persone capacis*: the worthiness of a person capable [of the grant]. *Libri proximi*: the book of Wyclif's *Summa* preceding *De ecclesia*; which work, and its chapters 20 and 24, to be checked (LEDGER E12b-3).

[^11]: **S.** C. 25 q. 1 c. 16, *Ideo permittente* (Loserth, 257.7: *Causa XXV, q. I, cap. XVI*; the modern form is ours; LEDGER E12b-4). *In proximo capitulo*: chapter 11. *Ut dixi hic*: earlier in this chapter (12-a, 253.20–254.23).

[^12]: **S/R.** James 4:15 has *Si Dominus voluerit* ("If the Lord will", DR); Wyclif's *si Deus voluerit* is translated. *Discolia*: frowardness. A in the margin *Archidiaconus* (257.16). The Archdeacon is the canonists' name for Guido de Baysio (*Rosarium* on the Decretum); so Loserth's index; the passage is not checked (LEDGER E12b-5).

[^13]: **S/T.** A in the margin *Deus donat sub condicione* (257.20); side-note 257.18. 1 Samuel 2:30 (DR; Loserth prints *1° Regum II°, 30*), except that Wyclif reads *honorificaverit* ("shall honour") for the Vulgate's *glorificaverit* ("shall glorify"), and adds *inquit*. "He cannot deny himself": 2 Timothy 2:13 (DR; Loserth, 257.26). *Blaspheme*: "except blasphemously". *Transferretur*: Loserth's correction of the manuscripts' *transferreretur* (257.30), translated.

[^14]: **C.** A and B in the margin *Consilium bonum* (258.1); side-note 258.1. *Sine conservacione huius carte impossibile est quod maneat dignitas*: so the print (plate); our Latin had lost *carte impossibile*. *Huius carte*: holy scripture, the charter. *Testamenti domini principaliter observati*: the Lord's testament (scripture), kept as the principal charter, in whose virtue other charters hold.

[^15]: **G/C.** A in the margin *Contra. Tercia obieccio* (258.8); side-note 258.8. *Inconveniens* here in its practical sense, an ill consequence in affairs (CONTEXTUAL, GLOSSARY §C), and so through the next sentence. *Pie*: "piously"; *pietas* is "piety" throughout this chapter. *Necessitante casu ad aliquod inconveniens*: when a case forces [one] into some ill consequence.

[^16]: **T/C.** B lacks *Non ergo … tollendum cum* (258.12) in its text and gives it in the lower margin. Side-note 258.14. *Occasione male accepta*: occasion wrongly taken, the distinction Wyclif answers with at 263.33 ff. (12-d). *Scolastici … vulgares*: as at the chapter's opening (250.18), "schoolmen" and "common people". *Quin … necesse sit*: every such law, in some case, necessarily yields injustice. The last sentence is the objector's conclusion, answered with Romans 3:8 at 263.27 (12-d).
