# *De ecclesia*, chapter 14, part 4: objections: the patrimony of the crucified and the two swords

## ¶1 · 310.30–312.13

> [p. 310] Sed contra dicta arguitur per hoc quod omnia bona mundi sunt patrimonium crucifixi, sed totum suum patrimonium concessit Petro et eius vicariis: ergo illorum sunt omnia bona mundi, licet ergo eis super suo patrimonio dominari. [p. 311] Hic concedenda est conclusio (ut alias diffuse exposui), nam iusti sunt omnia iure poli (ut docet Apostolus, Romanorum VIII°, et Augustinus, epistola XXXVIIª, ad Macedonium). Unde clerici debent sequi patrem suum abiciendo dominacionem civilem, dominando pure evangelice ex titulo caritatis. Hoc enim habent exemplatum a Christo et aliud usurpant a cesare; quod si plus appreciantur imitari cesarem quam Christum, quis dubitat quin ex degeneracione perdunt utrumque? Potest ergo clerus habere quotlibet bona ex titulo elemosine ad supplendum suum officium et distribuendum pauperibus, et in hoc imitarentur Christum et eius apostolos, non autem in dominando civiliter, cum hoc foret oppositum in adiecto, et secundum istam racionem foret eis patrimonium crucifixi. Unde consideraremus ne blaspheme nominemus occupacionem bonorum Christi abutendo eis heretice (ut dictum est in fine proximi capituli) vel ne nominemus divicias secundum illam racionem patrimonium crucifixi. Si, inquam, sit patrimonium crucifixi, faciamus ut utendo eis ad similitudinem crucifixi imperciamur ea fideliter membris pauperibus crucifixi, ut demum participemus merito crucifixi, unde abusus noster foret maxime revocabilis considerando attente de nomine patrimonii crucifixi. Contra ista arguo, primo quod omnia bona ecclesie, sive in laicis sive in clero, sunt de patrimonio crucifixi, ipse enim est heres habens in capite omnia bona mundi, et nos titulo adopcionis filialis participamus [p. 312] eorum. Nam Psalmo VIII°, 8, de illo dicitur: Omnia subiecisti sub pedibus eius, et Luce X°, 22, dicit iste primogenitus ex multis fratribus: Omnia mihi tradita sunt a patre meo, et Psalmo II° alloquitur pater eum: Postula a me et dabo tibi gentes hereditatem tuam, et possessionem tuam terminos terre. Ex istis sic arguo: Nemo vere possidet quicquam in terra nisi vel Christus vel eius filius, sed quicquid talis possederit, est patrimonium crucifixi, ergo nemo de iure quidquam possidet nisi titulo patrimonii crucifixi, et per consequens superiores filii, cuiusmodi sunt clerici et specialiter eius vicarii, prius et excellencius debent possidere de eius patrimonio quam alii seculares.

But against what has been said [it is argued by appeal to]{g:arguitur per} this, that all the goods of the world are the patrimony of the crucified; now he granted his whole patrimony to Peter and his vicars; therefore all the goods of the world are theirs; therefore it is lawful for them to [exercise lordship]{g:dominari} over their patrimony.[^1] [311] To this the [conclusion]{g:conclusio} is to be granted (as I have set out at length elsewhere), for to the just all things belong by [the law of heaven]{g:ius poli} (as the Apostle teaches, Romans 8, and Augustine, Epistle 37 to Macedonius).[^2] Hence clerics ought to follow their father by casting off civil [lordship]{g:dominacio}, exercising lordship purely in the manner of the gospel by [title]{g:titulus} of [charity]{g:caritas}. For this they have as exemplified by Christ, and the other they usurp from Caesar; and if they value imitating Caesar more than Christ, who doubts that by degeneracy they lose both? The [clergy]{g:clerus} therefore can have any amount of goods by title of [alms]{g:elemosina}, to supply their office and to distribute to the poor, and in this they would imitate Christ and his apostles; but not in exercising civil lordship, since this would be a contradiction in the adjunct;[^3] and on this ground it would be for them the patrimony of the crucified.[^4] Hence we should take care lest we blasphemously give that name to the occupation of Christ's goods while abusing them heretically (as was said at the end of the preceding chapter),[^5] or lest we call riches on that ground the patrimony of the crucified. If, I say, it is the patrimony of the crucified, let us so act that, using these things after the likeness of the crucified, we faithfully impart them to the poor [members]{g:membrum} of the crucified, so that at last we may share in the merit of the crucified; hence our abuse would be most readily recalled by considering attentively the name "patrimony of the crucified". Against this I argue, first, that all the goods of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, whether among the laity or among the clergy, are of the patrimony of the crucified; for he is the heir holding in chief all the goods of the world,[^6] and we by title of filial adoption share [312] in them. For in Psalm 8:8 it is said of him: "Thou hast subjected all things under his feet"; and in Luke 10:22 this "Firstborn amongst many brethren" says: "All things are delivered to me by my Father"; and in Psalm 2 the Father addresses him: "Ask of me, and I will give thee the Gentiles for thy inheritance, and the utmost parts of the earth for thy possession."[^7] From these I argue thus: no one truly possesses anything on earth except either Christ or his son; now whatever such a one has possessed is the patrimony of the crucified; therefore no one by right possesses anything except by title of the patrimony of the crucified;[^8] and consequently the superior sons, of which kind are clerics and especially his vicars, ought to possess of his patrimony earlier and more excellently than other [seculars]{g:secularis}.

## ¶2 · 312.14–312.33

> [p. 312] Hic dicitur quod utraque conclusio est concedenda. Nam iure altissimo habet ipse tam secundum deitatem quam secundum humanitatem omnia bona mundi, et secundum derivacionem ab ipso fonte iusticie, secundum gradus dignitatum participant alii bonis suis, in tantum quod si ipse non fuisset incarnatus, non fuisset in toto genere humano lapso simpliciter iusta possessio, sic quod omnes patres veteris testamenti quibus Deus disposuit regnum, sive gentiles ut Job, Nabuchodonosor et similes, sive Hebrei tempore utriusque legis, omnes, inquam, receperunt possessiones iure Christi; qui passione sua rectificavit titulos omnium membrorum suorum, ymmo quodammodo profuit toti mundo, cum omnes dampnati, omnia inanimata, et breviter tota universitas creata recipit quandam perfeccionem secundam ex vita Christi, cum totus mundus secundum quamlibet partem sui racione meriti Christi perfeccius servit Deo, cum aliter mundus foret colobon, et partes inordinate forent perpetuo sibi ipsis contrarie.

To this [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that each conclusion is to be granted. For by the highest [right]{g:ius} he has, according to his [Godhead]{g:deitas} as well as according to his humanity, all the goods of the world; and by derivation from him as the very fount of justice, others share in his goods according to the grades of their dignities; so much so that, if he had not become incarnate, there would not have been in the whole fallen human race any just possession [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}; so that all the fathers of the old testament to whom God appointed a [kingdom]{g:regnum}, whether gentiles like Job, Nebuchadnezzar and the like, or Hebrews in the time of either law, all, I say, received their possessions by Christ's right.[^9] For by his passion he set right the titles of all his members; indeed in a way he profited the whole world, since all the damned, all inanimate things, and in short the whole created universe receive a certain second perfection from the life of Christ, since the whole world in every part of it serves God more perfectly by reason of Christ's merit; for otherwise the world would be maimed, and its parts, disordered, would be perpetually at odds with themselves.[^10]

## ¶3 · 313.1–314.9

> [p. 313] Ideo creditur probabiliter quod Christus necessitatus fuit lapsu supposito ad suam fabricam reparandam. Ideo dicit Luce ultimo quod oportuit Christum pati. Omnes ergo domini seculares et breviter omnia membra ecclesie titulo illius domini utuntur patrimonio crucifixi, et ideo oportet quod omnes eius filii, tam clerici quam laici, caveant de abusu. Et ita concedendum est quod clerici et specialiter Christi vicarii habeant prius et perfeccius patrimonium crucifixi; sed tunc oportet quod imitentur modum suum habendi et utendi quantum sufficiunt. Ipse enim habuit hec in via puro titulo gracie et humane elemosine et precise sub illa mensura sub qua fuit exproprietarie necessarium ad edificacionem ecclesie et medium ad supportandum ministerium spirituale, et idem genus modi habendi reliquit appropriate clericis sortis sue, ut patet XII, q. Iª, Clericus. Iste autem est modus habendi perfeccior, quia a modo mundano distraccior et modo habendi beatorum similior. Quando enim triumphantes vitam eternam possidebunt, Deus eos constituit super omnia bona sua, et tunc non habebunt usum vescendi vel corporalem imperfectum ut modo, sed usum spiritualem per intencionem in verbo, et de quanto viator ceteris paribus plus ad talem usum accesserit, de tanto perfeccius procedit; quod faciunt hii qui plus laborant ad edificacionem ecclesie et stricte, humiliter et immundialiter accipiunt graciam necessitatis nature, quantum requiritur pro hoc fine. Sed secundo obicitur per hoc quod Christus fuit simul rex et sacerdos humanitus, sed omne privilegium quod habuit humanitus concessit Petro et eius vicariis, ergo ipsi debent esse simul reges et sacerdotes, [p. 314] habentes utrumque gladium. Sic enim legitur apostolos habuisse tempore capcionis Christi duos gladios, et Petro precepit Christus quod poneret gladium suum in vaginam. Ex quo verbo innuitur quod ecclesia Christi et navicula Petri debent habere utrumque gladium, ut notat beatus Bernardus et quotlibet iura canonica cum concordanciis doctorum. Et hoc videtur beatum Petrum innuere Ia Petri II°, 9, quando dicit: Vos estis genus electum, regale sacerdocium etc.

Therefore it is believed with probability that Christ was necessitated, the fall being supposed, to repair his workmanship. Therefore he says in the last [chapter] of Luke that "it behoved Christ to suffer".[^11] All secular lords, therefore, and in short all the members of the church use the patrimony of the crucified by title of that lord; and therefore all his sons, clerics as well as laymen, must beware of abuse. And so it must be granted that clerics, and especially Christ's vicars, have the patrimony of the crucified earlier and more perfectly; but then they must imitate his manner of having and using it, as far as they are able. For he had these things on [the way]{g:via} by pure title of grace and of human alms, and precisely under that measure under which it was necessary, without property, for the building up of the church, and a means of supporting the spiritual ministry; and the same kind of manner of having he left as their own to the clerics of his lot, as is clear in [Decretum C.] 12 q. 1 [c.] *Clericus*.[^12] And this is the more perfect manner of having, because it is further withdrawn from the worldly manner and more like the manner of having of the blessed. For when the triumphant shall possess eternal life, God has set them over all his goods,[^13] and then they will not have the use of eating, or an imperfect bodily use as now, but a spiritual use through attention in the Word;[^14] and the more a [wayfarer]{g:viator}, other things being equal, has approached such use, the more perfectly he proceeds; which those do who labour more for the building up of the church, and strictly, humbly and unworldly receive the favour that nature's necessity needs, as much as is required for this end. But secondly [it is objected, by appeal to]{g:obicitur per} this, that Christ was at once king and priest humanly; now every privilege that he had humanly he granted to Peter and his vicars; therefore they ought to be at once kings and priests, [314] having both swords. For so we read that the apostles had two swords at the time of Christ's arrest, and Christ commanded Peter to put his sword into its sheath.[^15] From which word it is hinted that the church of Christ and the bark of Peter ought to have both swords, as blessed Bernard notes, and any number of canon laws with the concordances of the doctors.[^16] And blessed Peter seems to hint this in 1 Peter 2:9, when he says: "You are a chosen generation, a kingly priesthood," etc.[^17]

## ¶4 · 314.10–314.21

> [p. 314] Hic respondeo concedendo maiorem, sed nego minorem tamquam summe hereticam. Christus enim habuit humanitus ius primogeniture nomine redemptoris humani generis et nomine capitis universalis ecclesie cum multis aliis singularibus privilegiis, que nec quanta potuit facere inesse Petro vel alicui vicario Christi formaliter, sicut Augustinus recitat super Joh., Omelia VIª. Verumtamen Augustinus recitat super Joh., Omelia Vª, Christus potuit communicasse Petro, Johanni vel alteri potestatem, ut christiani baptizarentur in nomine eorum, si sic voluisset, sed nec voluit nec docuit, quod implicat quod non sic potuit voluisse.

To this I reply by granting the major, but I deny the minor as supremely heretical. For Christ had humanly the right of primogeniture, in the name of redeemer of the human race and in the name of [head]{g:caput} of the universal church, with many other singular [privileges]{g:privilegium}, which, great as they were, he could not make to belong formally to Peter or to any [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius Christi},[^18] as Augustine relates on John, Homily 6. Yet, as Augustine relates on John, Homily 5, Christ could have communicated to Peter, John or another the power that Christians should be baptized in their name, if he had so willed; but he neither willed it nor taught it, which implies that he could not have so willed.[^19]

## ¶5 · 314.22–314.27

> [p. 314] Sed ulterius concedo (ut sepe alias) quod nedum ecclesia sed clerus Christi habet utrumque gladium. Ecclesia quidem cum habeat duas partes, scilicet clerum et laycos, quarum prior pars habet gladium spiritualem in se formaliter et pars laycalis secundum principes seculi habet gladium corporalem in se formaliter.

But further I grant (as often elsewhere) that not only the church but the clergy of Christ has both swords. The church indeed [has both], since it has two parts, namely clergy and laity, of which the former part has the spiritual sword formally in itself, and the lay part, in the princes of the world, has the bodily sword formally in itself.[^20]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 310.30: Objection: All things belong to Christ and he has given all to his vicars.
- 311.1: Answer: It is true that the righteous have all things, but to use them in Christ's service.
- 311.26: Objection: 1. All things are Christ's,
- 312.7: therefore his chief followers have a special right to possession.
- 312.14: Answer: This also is true;
- 313.4: but that special possession binds them all the more to use and not to abuse.
- 313.30: Objection: 2. Christ was both king and priest, so the Popes should be the same.
- 314.10: Answer: Christ had rights which he did not give to Peter.

## Notes

[^1]: **T.** A and A₁ read *obicitur* for *arguitur* (310.30); the sense is the same. A's margin: *Contra. Obieccio.*
[^2]: **S.** Augustine, Ep. 153 to Macedonius (the editor, 311.4 foot: "Ep. CLIII in the modern editions"), which quotes Prov. 17:6 (LXX), "to the faithful man belongs the whole world of riches" (LEDGER E14d-1). Wyclif's Romans 8 is most likely 8:32, *cum illo omnia nobis donavit*, which he quotes at 306.10 (LEDGER E14d-2).
[^3]: **C.** *Oppositum in adiecto*: a contradiction between a term and what is added to it ("civil lordship" joined to "imitating Christ").
[^4]: **C.** *Secundum istam racionem*: "on this ground", i.e. goods held by title of alms for office and the poor are rightly called the patrimony of the crucified.
[^5]: **S.** The editor refers to p. 297 (311.18 foot), the end of chapter 13 (LEDGER E14d-3). *Nominemus occupacionem*: lest we give the name "patrimony of the crucified" to mere occupation of Christ's goods.
[^6]: **C.** *In capite*: feudal "in chief", the tenure of a tenant holding directly of the lord (cf. *capitalis dominus*, GLOSSARY §B). B reads *finalis* for *filialis* (311.29).
[^7]: **S.** Ps. 8:8; Luke 10:22; Rom. 8:29 ("Firstborn amongst many brethren"); Ps. 2:8 (all Vulgate; DR wording).
[^8]: **T.** A and A₁ omit *ergo … crucifixi* (312.9), an eye-skip between two *crucifixi*; the print's fuller text is translated.
[^9]: **T/C.** *Inquam* is Loserth's emendation; the manuscripts read *omnes in quantum* (312.24 foot). *Utriusque legis*: probably the law of nature and the law of Moses.
[^10]: **T.** *Colobon* (312.32) is the manuscripts' form; Loserth notes that *colobos*, "truncated", is right. Rendered "maimed". A reads *et vita* for *ex vita* (312.29).
[^11]: **S.** Luke 24:46 (DR "it behoved Christ to suffer"; cf. 24:26).
[^12]: **S.** Decretum, C. 12 q. 1 c. 5 *Clericus* (the editor, 313.17 foot: "Decret. II pars, causa XII, q. I, c. V"; the modern form is ours) (LEDGER E14d-4). *Sortis sue*: "of his lot", the play on *clerus*, Greek *kleros*. *Appropriate* is adverbial: as proper to them.
[^13]: **T/S.** The print reads *constituit*, "has set"; A and A₁ read *constituet*, "will set" (313.21), the words of Matt. 24:47 (DR "he shall place him over all his goods"). The print is translated; the future gives the more natural sense.
[^14]: **C.** *Per intencionem in verbo*: the beatific use of goods by attending to them in the divine Word, not by consuming them.
[^15]: **S.** Luke 22:38 (the two swords); John 18:11 (DR "Put up thy sword into the scabbard").
[^16]: **S.** Bernard, *De consideratione* IV.3.7, on the two swords; the canon law is presumably Boniface VIII's *Unam sanctam* (*Extrav. comm.* 1.8.1), which takes up the figure. Neither is referenced by the editor here. To be checked (LEDGER E14d-5, E14d-6).
[^17]: **S.** 1 Pet. 2:9, without the Vulgate's *autem* (DR "But you are a chosen generation, a kingly priesthood").
[^18]: **C.** *Que nec quanta potuit facere inesse*: the print, which does not construe cleanly. Rendered as if *quanta* agreed with *que* ("great as they were"); *quantum*, "however much he could", would give "which, however great his power, he could not make …". B omits *ius* (314.12).
[^19]: **S/C.** Augustine, *In Iohannis evangelium tractatus* 5.7, 9, 11 (PL 35, 1417–19; the editor, 314.18 foot: "Aug. In Joh. Ev. cap. I, tract. V, Opp. tom. III, pars II, p. 323") (LEDGER E14d-7); the reference to *Omelia VIª* is Wyclif's and is not identified (LEDGER E14d-8). *Verumtamen Augustinus recitat … Omelia Vª* repeats the formula of the clause before (both are printed, 314.16–18). *Potuit voluisse*: "could not have willed it", given his will and teaching.
[^20]: **T/C.** The sentence has no main verb: *Ecclesia quidem cum habeat …* is followed only by the relative clauses. "[Has both]" is supplied from the sentence before (our supplement). *Secundum principes seculi*: the lay part bears the sword in, or by way of, its princes.
