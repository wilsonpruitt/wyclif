# *De potestate pape*, chapter 9, part 5: holiness of life: Jude's six marks of the proud

## ¶1 · 207.23–209.16

> [p. 207] Quantum ad tercium scilicet vite sanctitatem, patet quod necesse est gloriantem sic in laudibus hominum considerare se sollicite in speculo scripturarum. Consideret igitur papa et impleat ea que exposita sunt de Petro, et tunc non delectabitur in laude hominum nec in mundana gloria, sed in spe premii absconditi in Christo, et sic non video, quin affectans humanas laudes et gloriam propter vite sanctitatem sit detestandus ypocrita et mendaciter contrarius sibi ipsi, nam [p. 208] si talis prius est sanctus, luciferina affeccio laudis hominum extinguit sanctitatem quam reperit. Si sit semper non sanctus, inducit in se et populum blasfemum mendacium. Unde papa Petrus II Petri II, 1 prophetat de talibus: Erunt, inquit, pseudoprophete in populo, sicut in vobis erunt magistri mendaces, et post diffuse describit eos dicens, quod sunt velut irracionabilia pecora, in hiis que ignorant blasfemantes. Tales forent illi, qui dicerent, quod habent ex eleccione hominum eciam notorie perversorum potestatem absolvendi a pena et a culpa et concedendi ex supererogatis meritis sanctorum, distributis ad libitum indulgencias sine fine. Et tales communiter ignorant voces proprias: et illos beatus Judas describit per hoc, quod ma iestatem blasfemant, et ubi sanctus Michael disputans cum dyabolo pro Moysi corpore, non est ausus in ipsum inferre iudicium, hii tamquam muta animalia offendunt in hiis, que norunt noticia sensibili, cum audent ultra archangelum maledicere quibuscumque qui voluntati sue restiterint, licet ignorent de eis divinum iudicium. Et super hec blasfemant gravius dicentes, quod oportet Deum virtute eleccionis eis assistere et quecumque promiserint ad implere. Unde sanctus Apostolus describit eos nota sextuplici superborum: Hii, inquit, sunt in epulis suis macule, convivantes, sine timore se ipsos pascentes, nubes sine aqua, que a ventis circumferuntur, arbores autumpnales infructuose, bis mortue, eradicate fluctus feri maris, despumantes suas confusiones, sidera errancia, quibus procella tenebrarum conservata est in eternum. Et ista sex, cum sint scriptura sacra, decet [p. 209] exponere. Quantum ad duo prima, patet quod spectant ad peccatum carnis, cum quantum ad modum convivandi sunt macule in abstracto, convivant enim mundi dominos et carnales amicos contra doctrinam Christi Luce XIV, 12— 14: Cum facis prandium aut cenam, noli vocare amicos tuos neque cognatos neque vicinos neque divites, ne forte et ipsi reinvitent te et fiat tibi retribucio. Sed cum facis convivium, voca pauperes, debiles, claudos, cecos, et beatus eris. Istud autem servaretur in nobis potissime, qui vivimus de elemosinis laycorum et bonis pauperum. Quamvis autem licet convivare, tam spiritualiter infirmos quam sanos, ut spiritualiter edificentur et reedificent, hoc tamen non debet fieri racione commodi temporalis.

As to the third, namely holiness of life, it is clear that one who glories thus in the praises of men must consider himself carefully in the mirror of the scriptures. Let the [pope]{g:papa} therefore consider and fulfil the things that have been set out concerning Peter, and then he will not delight in the praise of men nor in worldly glory, but in the hope of the reward hidden in Christ;[^1] and so I do not see how one who [desires]{g:affectare} human praises and glory on account of holiness of life is not a detestable hypocrite, lyingly contrary to himself. For [208] if such a man is holy beforehand, the luciferine affection for the praise of men extinguishes the holiness it finds; if he is always unholy, he brings a blasphemous lie upon himself and the people. Hence Pope Peter, 2 Peter 2:1, prophesies of such: "There shall be," he says, "false prophets among the people, even as there shall be among you lying teachers";[^2] and afterwards he describes them at length, saying that they are "as irrational beasts", "blaspheming those things which they know not". Such would be those who should say that they have, from the election of men, even of notoriously perverse men, the [power]{g:potestas} of absolving from punishment and from guilt, and of granting [indulgences]{g:indulgencie} without end out of the [supererogated]{g:supererogare} merits of the saints, distributed at their pleasure.[^3] And such men are commonly [ignorant of]{g:ignorare} their own words; and blessed Jude describes them by this, that they "blaspheme majesty"; and whereas Saint Michael, "disputing with the devil" about "the body of Moses, durst not bring against him judgment", these men, "like dumb beasts, offend in the things which they know with a [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} knowledge", since they dare, beyond the archangel, to curse whoever has resisted their will, although they are ignorant of God's judgement concerning them.[^4] And beyond these things they blaspheme more gravely, saying that God must, by virtue of the election, stand by them and fulfil whatever they have promised. Hence the holy apostle describes them by a sixfold mark of the proud: "These," he says, "are spots in their banquets, feasting together without fear, feeding themselves: clouds without water, which are carried about by winds: trees of the autumn, unfruitful, twice dead, plucked up by the roots: raging waves of the sea, foaming out their own confusion: wandering stars, to whom the storm of darkness is reserved for ever."[^5] And these six, since they are holy scripture, it is fitting [209] to expound. As to the first two, it is clear that they pertain to the sin of the flesh, since as to the manner of feasting they are spots in the abstract; for they feast the lords of the world and carnal friends, against the [doctrine]{g:doctrina} of Christ, Luke 14:12–14: "When thou makest a dinner or a supper, call not thy friends nor thy kinsmen nor thy neighbours nor the rich, lest perhaps they also invite thee again, and a recompense be made to thee. But when thou makest a feast, call the poor, the maimed, the lame and the blind, and thou shalt be blessed."[^6] And this should be kept most of all among us, who live on the [alms]{g:elemosina} of the laity and the goods of the poor. And although it is lawful to feast both the spiritually sick and the healthy, so that they may be spiritually edified and edify in turn, yet this must not be done for the sake of temporal advantage.

## ¶2 · 209.17–209.22

> [p. 209] Secunda gentilitas est sessio, quam Christus docuit in facto aufugere, quando in eadem tabula atque perapside discipulos suos pavit, ut patet Matth. XXVI, quod non observant hii, qui sollempnizant convivia de bonis pauperum sedentes singulariter tamquam reges in solio.

The second [paganism]{g:gentilitas} is the seating, which Christ taught by deed to shun, when he fed his disciples at the same table and the same dish, as is clear from Matthew 26; which those do not observe who solemnize feasts from the goods of the poor, sitting apart like kings on a throne.[^7]

## ¶3 · 209.23–209.30

> [p. 209] Et quoad tercium scilicet modum prandii: Si in nostris episcopis sit gentilitas ritus ex superbia episcopi introducta, patet quod omnia eorum convivia sunt macule in abstracto, quod si secundo timore Dei postposito nimis sumptuose paverint se ipsos non pauperes, et hoc gracia voluptatis sue ac glorie secularis, quis dubitat, quin de illis prophetaverat sanctus Judas?

And as to the third, namely the manner of the meal: if there is in our [bishops]{g:episcopus} a paganism of [rite]{g:ritus} brought in from the pride of a bishop, it is clear that all their feasts are spots in the abstract; and if, secondly, setting aside the fear of God, they have fed themselves, not the poor, too sumptuously, and this for the sake of their own pleasure and secular glory, who doubts that Saint Jude prophesied of them?[^8]

## ¶4 · 209.31–210.14

> [p. 209] Quantum ad secundum iugum, quod concernit peccatum mundi, dicitur primo, quod sunt nubes sine aqua, que a ventis circumferuntur, nubes, que exalantur de terra, ut sint medium inter laycos et celum, scilicet ecclesiam triumphantem, quod si sint sine pluvia predicacionis, ad quemlibet impulsum venti fortune pro congregandis et [p. 210] conservandis temporalibus circumducti, signum est quod eclipsant radiacionem solis iusticie et signant pestilencias spirituales. Et quantum ad secundum, patet quod prelati racione ordinacionis divine, qua debent spiritualiter fructificare subiectis, debent esse arbores autumpnales. Quod si habent istam triplicitatem dyaboli, primo quod sint infructuosi quoad opus ewangeliste, secundo quod sint bis mortui secundum spiritualem racionem proprie persone et prelati, et tercio quod sint eradicati sine spe insercionis in vite vera fructificando secundum legem Christi, tunc sunt illi de quibus Apostolus prophetavit.

As to the second [yoke]{g:iugum}, which concerns the sin of the world, it is said first that they are "clouds without water, which are carried about by winds": clouds which are exhaled from the earth so as to be a mean between the laity and heaven, that is, the [church]{g:ecclesia} triumphant; and if they are without the rain of preaching, carried about at every push of the wind of fortune for gathering and [210] keeping temporal goods, it is a sign that they eclipse the radiance of the sun of justice and signify spiritual pestilences.[^9] And as to the second, it is clear that [prelates]{g:prelatus}, by reason of the divine [ordinance]{g:ordinacio} by which they ought to bear fruit spiritually for their subjects, ought to be "trees of the autumn". And if they have this threefold [mark] of the devil, first, that they are "unfruitful" as to the work of an evangelist, secondly, that they are "twice dead" in the spiritual respect of their own person and of a prelate, and thirdly, that they are "plucked up by the roots" without hope of grafting into the true vine, bearing fruit according to the law of Christ, then they are those of whom the Apostle prophesied.[^10]

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** "The things set out concerning Peter": the exposition of Peter earlier in the work. "The reward hidden in Christ" recalls Col. 3:3 ("your life is hid with Christ in God").
[^2]: **S/C.** 2 Pet. 2:1, with Wyclif's *Erunt* ("there shall be") for the Vulgate's *Fuerunt* (DR "there were"); then 2 Pet. 2:12 in DR. *papa Petrus*: Wyclif gives Peter the title, as he gives it to Paul at 211.15.
[^3]: **C/R.** *a pena et a culpa*: the formula of plenary indulgence, "from punishment and from guilt". *distributis ad libitum* agrees with *meritis*: the merits are distributed at pleasure. The manuscripts in the margin (208.4): *Prelati mali quomodo ab apostolis sunt descripti*.
[^4]: **S/T.** Jude 8–10 (Loserth), paraphrased. *noticia sensibili* stands where the Vulgate has *naturaliter* (DR "naturally know"): knowledge through the senses. B reads *ut ubi* for *et ubi* (208.16). The manuscripts read *restituerint* (208.21); Loserth's *restiterint*, "resisted", is translated.
[^5]: **S.** Jude 12–13 (Loserth), in DR; "the holy apostle" is Jude. Wyclif's *conservata* for the Vulgate's *servata* does not change the sense. The six phrases fall into three "yokes" (pairs): of the flesh, of the world, of the devil.
[^6]: **S/C.** Luke 14:12–14, shortened: Wyclif omits "thy brethren", makes the rich a class of their own (*neque divites* for *vicinos divites*, DR "neighbours who are rich"), and stops at "thou shalt be blessed". *macule in abstracto*: "spots in the abstract", stains themselves, not merely stained men.
[^7]: **S/C.** Matt. 26:23 ("he that dippeth his hand with me in the dish"; Vulgate *paropsis*, Wyclif *perapsis*). The three heads under "feasting together" are the manner of feasting, the seating and the meal.
[^8]: **C.** *secundo*: the second of Jude's phrases, "feeding themselves" "without fear". *gracia voluptatis*: "for the sake of" their pleasure.
[^9]: **G/S.** *iugum*, "yoke", is a pair of Jude's six phrases. "The sun of justice": Mal. 4:2.
[^10]: **S/C.** "The work of an evangelist": 2 Tim. 4:5; "the true vine": John 15:1. "Twice dead": once as a person, once as a prelate. "The Apostle" is Jude.
