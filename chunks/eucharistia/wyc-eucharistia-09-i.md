# *De eucharistia*, chapter 9, part 9: the sophism resolved, and the manifold being of one thing

## ¶1 · 304.15–305.9

> [p. 304] Hic dicitur ut supra quod illud (false vocatur sylogismus expositorius) propter equivocacionem manifeste deficit, sicut patet exprimendo sensum minoris: Hic panis ibidem extenditur et ille figurat sacramentaliter corpus Christi, ergo corpus Christi ibidem extenditur; nam in consequente intelligitur corpus Christi personaliter pro illo supposito quod creabatur ex virgine, paciebatur et sedet in celo in pocioribus donis Dei; et ita equivocant sancti in secunda argucia, concedentes quod corpus Christi est in altari corpus Christi, sicut beatus Jeronimus dicit quod corpus Christi intelligi potest dupliciter; et ita panem illum post conversionem concedunt secundum argumentum expositorium esse delatum in celum ante oculos Dei et angelorum. Sed in omnibus istis est solamen fidelibus quod cognoscunt sensum katholicum et possunt licite ac meritorie [p. 305] sine contradiccionis repugnancia nunc loqui uno modo nunc alio secundum capacitatem auditorii, cui loquuntur, ut aliter loquendum est sophistis et aliter piis simplicibus. Ego autem elegi laicis loqui planius quod illud sacramentum figurat corpus Christi et conficitur, colitur et manducatur intencione memorandi et imitandi Christum; in quo quia contingit tanta duplicitas ac sophisticacio, permittit Deus in ista materia sophistas infructuose contendere.

Here [the answer]{g:dicitur} is, as above, that that [argument] (falsely called an [expository syllogism]{g:sillogismus-expositorius}) manifestly fails through [equivocation]{g:equivocacio}, as is clear by expressing the [meaning]{g:sensus} of the minor: "This [bread]{g:panis} is extended there, and it [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} [figures]{g:figurare} the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}; therefore the body of Christ is extended there." For in the [consequent]{g:consequens} "the body of Christ" is understood personally, for that [supposit]{g:suppositum} which was created from the virgin, suffered, and sits in heaven in the greater gifts of God.[^1] And so [the saints]{g:sancti} equivocate in the second [quibble]{g:argucia}, granting "the body of Christ is on the altar the body of Christ", as blessed Jerome says that the body of Christ can be understood in two ways;[^2] and so they grant, according to an expository argument, that that bread after the [conversion]{g:conversio} is carried into heaven before the eyes of God and of the angels.[^3] But in all these there is comfort for the faithful, that they know the catholic meaning and can lawfully and meritoriously, [305] without the [incompatibility]{g:repugnancia} of contradiction, speak now in one way, now in another, according to the capacity of the audience to which they speak, as one must speak otherwise to sophists and otherwise to the pious simple. But I have chosen to speak more plainly to lay people: that that [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} figures the body of Christ, and is [consecrated]{g:conficere}, worshipped and eaten with the intention of remembering and imitating Christ. And because in this so much doubleness and [sophistry]{g:sophisticacio} happens, God permits sophists to contend fruitlessly in this matter.

## ¶2 · 305.10–307.17

> [p. 305] Tercio autem replicatur per hoc quod non efficaciter docetur corpus Christi esse in altari sacramentaliter; et super isto dependet tota contencio. Hic non oportet suadere istam conclusionem fidelibus, cum christiani pocius excedant in ista quam distrahunt veritatem. Sed alloquendo infideles hortandi sunt layci, ut in ista parte credant fidei scripture superius allegate. Infideles autem philosophi instruendi sunt quomodo eadem res habet esse multiplex. Deus enim qui est basis tocius universitatis create est ubique per se primo essencialiter, virtualiter et potencialiter; et quia in eo idem est essencia, virtus et potencia (ut declarat Augustinus in De Cognicione Vere Vite), ideo ipse est ubique indivisibilis secundum essenciam, potenciam et virtutem. In creaturis triplex esse precedit suam existenciam; primum est esse intelligibile eternum quod secundum raciones seminales habet esse in Deo; secundum est esse potenciale in causis secundis secundum raciones seminales, et tercium est esse propinquum quod creatura habet in suo efficiente secundum applicacionem cause superioris, ut omnis [p. 306] homo secundus est in suo parente; et in istis sunt multi gradus. Secundum vero genus essendi subsequitur existenciam creature et specialiter hominis qui cum fit ad ymaginem Dei, habet esse existencie vel esse actuale; secundo habet esse presenciale et tercio habet esse virtuale, ut exemplificat Magister in principio Historiarum de rege. Unde Augustinus ubi supra: Deus, inquit, iusticia est et ubicunque iusticia exercetur, Deus ibi creditur. Sic denique adest imperator latroni dum eum dampnat, sicut militi dum eum remunerat. Et nemo enim opinetur sordes abhominari sed solas peccatorum sordes eum noverit execrari: nec eum posse ulla re maculari, sicut nec radium solis ulla inmundicia sordidari. Nec umquam creditur istum sanctum vel aliquem ex hoc katholicum corpus Christi habuisse in eukaristia existenciam singularem in sua essencia, dicente Augustino ubi supra: Ultimus, inquit, in regno celorum ut sol fulgebit, qui tunc sepcies clarior quam nunc erit. Et in hac claritate tantum quisque ab alio pro meritis differt quantum stella a stella in claritate differt; nos autem omnes tantum corpus Christi in claritate excedit quantum creator creaturas transcendit; igitur talis visio talis glorie regnum celorum dicitur, quia soli celi, id est iusti, hoc fruuntur. Ideo humanitatem Christi solo oculo mentis non corporis nunc, videmus sicut et beatus Stephanus fide vidit, dicente Augustino [p. 307] ubi supra: Stephanus, inquit, non corporeis oculis Deum in celo ultra firmamentum vidit, cum nullius hominis visus ullomodo usque ad celum pertingere possit. Sed et concio illa in qua Stephanus stans Christum conspexit in domo aliqua fuit cuius tecti culmen ipsius corporeus oculus minime penetrare potuit. Constat igitur, inquit, quod nec oculo corporis sed intuitu interioris hominis Christum non in corporeo sed intellectuali celo viderit. Quod si sophista recalcitrat quod homo sit in loco a quo distat, studeat philosophos dicentes res esse in anima, videat theologos dicentes homines ante suam existenciam esse in suis parentibus, in quibus ex scripture testimonio fuerant decimati, et (ut loyci locuntur) homo post sui corrupcionem generat vel occidit et sic post mortem habet esse in suo effectu et evidencius corpus Christi in hostia.

And thirdly [it is rejoined]{g:replicatur} by appeal to this: that it is not effectively taught that the body of Christ is on the altar sacramentally; and on this the whole dispute depends. Here there is no need to [make the case]{g:suadere} for this [conclusion]{g:conclusio} to the faithful, since christians rather exceed in this than detract from the truth. But in addressing [unbelievers]{g:infidelis}, lay people are to be exhorted to believe, in this part, the faith of scripture [alleged]{g:allegare} above. And unbelieving philosophers are to be instructed how the same thing has manifold [being]{g:esse}. For God, who is the basis of the whole [created totality]{g:universitas-creata}, is everywhere [of himself]{g:per-se} and first, [essentially]{g:essencialiter}, [virtually]{g:virtualiter} and potentially; and because in him [essence]{g:essencia}, power and [potency]{g:potencia} are the same (as Augustine [makes clear]{g:declarare} in *On the Knowledge of the True Life*), therefore he is everywhere indivisible as to essence, potency and power.[^4] In [creatures]{g:creatura} a threefold being precedes their existence: the first is eternal [intelligible being]{g:esse-intelligibile}, which according to [exemplary reasons]{g:raciones-exemplares} has being in God;[^5] the second is potential being in secondary causes according to seminal reasons; and the third is proximate being, which the creature has in its efficient [cause] according to the application of the higher cause, as every [306] second man is in his parent;[^6] and in these there are many degrees. And the second kind of being follows upon the existence of the creature, and especially of man, who, since he is made to the image of God, has the being of existence, or actual being; secondly he has presential being, and thirdly he has virtual being, as the Master exemplifies at the beginning of the *Histories* with a king.[^7] Hence Augustine, [in the place cited above]{g:ubi-supra}: "God," he says, "is justice, and wherever justice is exercised, God is believed to be there. Thus, in the end, the emperor is present to the robber while he condemns him, as to the soldier while he rewards him. And let no one think that he abhors filth, but let him know that he execrates only the filth of sins; nor can he be stained by anything, as neither is the ray of the sun soiled by any uncleanness."[^8] Nor is it ever believed that this saint, or anyone catholic, held on this ground that the body of Christ had in [the Eucharist]{g:eukaristia} a singular existence in its essence,[^9] as Augustine says in the place cited above: "The last," he says, "in the [kingdom]{g:regnum} of heaven shall shine as the sun, which will then be seven times brighter than now. And in this [clarity]{g:claritas} each differs from another according to merits as much as star differs from star in clarity; but the body of Christ exceeds all of us in clarity as much as the creator transcends creatures; therefore such a vision of such glory is called the kingdom of heaven, because the heavens alone, that is, the just, enjoy this."[^10] Therefore we now see the [humanity]{g:humanitas} of Christ with the eye of the mind alone, not of the body, as blessed Stephen also saw by faith, as Augustine says [307] in the place cited above: "Stephen," he says, "did not see God in heaven beyond the firmament with bodily eyes, since no man's sight can in any way reach as far as heaven. And besides, that assembly in which Stephen, standing, beheld Christ was in some house, the peak of whose roof his bodily eye could by no means penetrate. It is evident, therefore," he says, "that not with the eye of the body but with the gaze of the inner man he saw Christ, not in a bodily but in an intellectual heaven."[^11] And if the sophist kicks against [it], that a man should be in a place from which he is distant, let him study the philosophers who say that things are in the soul; let him look at the theologians who say that men before their existence are in their parents, in whom, by the testimony of scripture, they had been tithed; and (as the logicians speak) a man after his corruption begets or kills, and so after death has being in his effect; and more evidently the body of Christ in the [host]{g:hostia}.[^12]

## ¶3 · 307.18–307.23

> [p. 307] Sed sexto principaliter arguitur de mocione et manducacione corporis Christi in hostia et tenetur communiter quod corpus Christi movetur cum hostia et sacramentaliter manducatur et sic in casu exscreatur, evomitur, et a sorice manducatur non solum in suo signo sed in sua essencia.

But sixthly, [as a main head]{g:principaliter}, it is argued about the [motion]{g:mocio} and [eating]{g:manducacio} of the body of Christ in the host; and it is commonly held that the body of Christ is moved with the host and is sacramentally eaten, and so, in a given case, is spat out, vomited up, and eaten by a mouse, not only in its [sign]{g:signum} but in its essence.[^13]

## Notes

[^1]: **C/S.** "That" argument is the sophist's second rejoinder (304.8–10); *ut supra* "as above". "Understood personally" is personal supposition: the term stands for the person born of the virgin. *In pocioribus donis Dei*, "in the greater gifts of God", glosses "at the right hand of God" after the school gloss on the session at the right hand, *id est in potioribus bonis Dei* (Lombard, *Collectanea* on Rom. 8:34, PL 191, 1452C; on Heb. 10:12, PL 192, 481D); Augustine’s own exposition of the creed has *in summa beatitudine* (*De fide et symbolo* 7.14, PL 40, 188) (LEDGER K09i-1). ABCE lack *paciebatur*. A reads *vocatum*, corrected to *vocatur*; C *vocatum*.

[^2]: **S/C.** Jerome on the twofold flesh and blood of Christ (the spiritual and the crucified), *In Eph.* I on 1:7 (PL 26, 451A: *Dupliciter vero sanguis Christi, et caro intelligitur*), taken into Gratian as *De cons.* D. 2 c. 49 (LEDGER K09i-2). The granted proposition keeps Wyclif's order: the first "body of Christ" is the bread on the altar, the second the person.

[^3]: **C/S.** *Post conversionem* is taken of the eucharistic conversion of the bread; ABC read *per conversionem*. It could instead be the logical conversion of the proposition "this bread is the body of Christ". The carrying to heaven "before the eyes of God" recalls the canon of the Mass, *Supplices te rogamus* (*iube haec perferri per manus angeli tui in sublime altare tuum, in conspectu divinae maiestatis tuae*; PL 78, 27D; LEDGER K09i-3). AB read *omne delatum*.

[^4]: **S/C.** Ps.-Augustine, *De cognitione verae vitae* cc. 28–29 (PL 40, 1021–1022: *Dei potentia nihil est aliud quam ejus essentia … ubique essentialiter est*; Loserth at 305.22: *S. Augustini Opp.* VI, Appendix, p. 171; LEDGER K09i-4). *Virtus* is "power", *potencia* "potency" here, to keep the three terms apart; the order of the second list (essence, potency, power) is Wyclif's.

[^5]: **T.** The print reads *raciones seminales* both for being in God and for being in secondary causes. ABCE read *exemplares habent in Deo* for the first, which gives the sense of the two distinct modes, and is translated ("exemplary reasons"); E also has *secundum quod*.

[^6]: **C.** *Omnis homo secundus*, "every second man", is taken as every man after the first, who is in his parent before he exists; B lacks *homo*.

[^7]: **G/S.** *Magister* here is not the Lombard but the Master of the *Histories*, Peter Comestor, *Historia scholastica*, the opening of the work (Loserth at 306.6 gives Genesis c. 1, *De creatione empirei caeli*; LEDGER K09i-5). The example of the king is its first sentence, before c. 1 (PL 198, 1053): *Imperatoriae majestatis est, in palatio tres habere mansiones*, the world, Scripture and the just soul being God’s three.

[^8]: **S/T.** *De cognitione verae vitae* c. 24 (PL 40, 1020; Loserth at 306.8: p. 176; LEDGER K09i-6). The codices lack *opinetur* and read *nemo enim sordes*; *eum* (306.12) is Loserth's correction (D *solas enim*; ABCE *solas eum*). ABCE read *sic dicimus*, D *Sic Deus*, for *Sic denique*. The English follows the print.

[^9]: **C.** The accusative-infinitive is construed with "this saint, or anyone catholic" as subject of *habuisse*, "held … had"; *ex hoc*, "on this ground", is taken with the verb.

[^10]: **S/T.** *De cognitione verae vitae* c. 45 (Loserth at 306.18: p. 182; c. 46 in PL 40, 1030; LEDGER K09i-7). It echoes Matt. 13:43 (DR "Then shall the just shine as the sun") and 1 Cor. 15:41 (DR "For star differeth from star in glory"); "clarity" keeps the glossary's word for *claritas*. *Ultimus* and *nos* are Loserth's corrections (codd. *Ulterius*; ABCE *homines autem*, D *Hos autem*). ABC read *celici iusti*.

[^11]: **S/T.** *De cognitione verae vitae* c. 42 (PL 40, 1028; Loserth at 306.27; LEDGER K09i-8), on Acts 7:55 (DR "saw the glory of God and Jesus standing on the right hand of God"). *Oculo* (307.7) is Loserth's correction of the codices' *oculus*; the codices read *nullo modo* for *ullomodo*.

[^12]: **S.** Levi tithed in Abraham: Heb. 7:9–10 (DR "even Levi who received tithes paid tithes in Abraham: For he was yet in the loins of his father"). "A man after his corruption begets or kills" is the logicians' stock case of an effect that comes after its cause has ceased.

[^13]: **S.** The mouse that eats the host is the schools' question from the *Sentences* (the Lombard, *Sent.* IV d. 13 c. 1, PL 192, 868: *Quid ergo sumit mus, vel quid manducat? Deus novit hoc*; the Lombard denies that beasts take the body, while the common view Wyclif reports is the later school’s, e.g. Thomas, *ST* III q. 80 a. 3 ad 3; LEDGER K09i-9). B lacks *et … manducatur*; A and C read *eciam a*.
