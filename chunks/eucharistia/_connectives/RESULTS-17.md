## Changes

| chunk | page | Latin phrase | was | now | reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 07-b | 194 | *quidquid erit Petrus illud nunc est, **sed** asinus erit Petrus, ergo asinus ipse nunc est* | but an ass will be Peter | now an ass will be Peter | The minor premiss of the quoted syllogism, closed by *ergo*: "now". | 07-b | 194 | *quidquid tempore nativitatis tue fuisti illud nunc es, **sed** infidelis ydiota tunc fuisti, ergo …* | but you were then | now you were then | The same structure: the minor premiss of the parallel syllogism, closed by *ergo*: "now" |

## Doubtful, left as they are

- **07-b, p. 195**: *Sed contra pro toto tempore ambulandi … fit non homo sed lapis …; non ergo scitur quod homo ambulat*, rendered "Now, on the contrary". Read as (a): it is the premiss that the *non ergo* closes. "But on the contrary" (b) is the more usual sense of *sed contra* and is defensible. Left.
- **07-b, p. 195**: *Sed totum hoc ignoras et dubitas, cum ydemptificacio sit habitudo reciproca …*, rendered "But all this you are ignorant of". Read as (b). It could be (a), as the premiss behind *Nichil itaque stulcius*, but that *itaque* sums up the whole case against the teaching, not this step alone. Left.
- **07-c, p. 199**: *Sacramentum **autem** altaris potest vocari …*, rendered "And the sacrament of the altar". Read as (c): it resumes the positive account of what can be called the sacrament. A turn from the rejected view, "but" (b), is defensible. Left.
