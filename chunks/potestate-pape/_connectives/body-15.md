### 12-m ¶1 · 360.19–361.11  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 360] Non igitur sequitur: Multum malum exinde contingeret, igitur non hoc, **sed** eius contrarium est tenendum. Sic enim destrueretur maior pars legum et sermonum Christi et facienda forent mala ut bona eveniant. Propter talia igitur inconveniencia expedit, quod fidelis teneat se in limitibus legis Christi, videlicet non exigendo plus vel attencius de temporalibus quam hec docet. Quantum vero ad gravandos videtur quod a summo pontifice usque ad minimum sacerdotem omnes peccant in recipiendo bona pauperum et ecclesiasticas dignitates, nam quilibet papa in quantum talis tenetur esse sanctissimus viatorum, et sub illo colore accipit illud nomen; quod videtur [p. 361] esse nimis magna presumpcio. Nam iuxta decretum beati Jeronymi recitatum X capitulo et testimonium Bernhardi et Lyncolniensis papa debet esse qualis ibi reputative describitur; **sed** tunc foret sanctissimus viatorum, igitur ad hoc tenetur; non enim est evidens quod tam sancti et scioli doctores oleo adulacionis linirent pontificem menciendo de illo, pronunciantes de ipso laudes que nec insunt nec inesse debeant. Ideo planum est ex eorum sentencia quod papa debet esse sanctissimus et per idem quilibet papa.

ENGLISH: It does not [follow]{g:sequitur}, therefore: "Much evil would come of this; therefore not this, but its contrary, is to be held." For so the greater part of the laws and sermons of Christ would be destroyed, and evils would have to be done that good may come.[^1] Because of such [absurdities]{g:inconveniencia},[^2] therefore, it is expedient that the faithful man keep himself within the [limits]{g:limites} of the [law of Christ]{g:lex-christi}, namely by not demanding more, or more eagerly, of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} than it teaches. And as to those who are to be charged,[^3] it seems that from the highest pontiff down to the least [priest]{g:sacerdos} all sin in receiving the goods of the poor and ecclesiastical dignities; for every [pope]{g:papa}, as such, is bound to be the holiest of [wayfarers]{g:viator}, and under that [colour]{g:color}[^4] he takes that name; which seems [361] to be too great a presumption. For according to the decree of blessed Jerome recited in chapter 10, and the testimony of Bernard and of Lincoln, the pope ought to be such as he is there described [by estimation]{g:reputative};[^5] but then he would be the holiest of wayfarers; therefore he is bound to this. For it is not evident that doctors so holy and so [knowing]{g:sciolus} would anoint the pontiff with the oil of flattery by lying about him, pronouncing of him praises which neither are in him nor ought to be. So it is plain from their [teaching]{g:sentencia} that the pope ought to be the holiest, and by the same token every pope.

### 12-m ¶2 · 361.12–361.23  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 361] Item, beatus Gregorius libro II De Cura Pastorali cap. primo dicit quod pastor ecclesie debet se habere ad subditos sicut pastor bestiarum se habet ad bestias quas custodit. **Sed** constat quod nulla earum equiparatur pastori in prudencia vel virtute, igitur pastor ecclesie debet in virtute omnes subditos suos excellere, quia alia excellencia non est pertinens. Cum igitur papa sit pastor tocius residue universitatis fidelium, videtur quod debet supponi ipsum in prudencia et virtute cunctos illos excellere. Aliter enim eque maneret et pasceret eum subditus ut econtra.

ENGLISH: Again, blessed Gregory, in book 2 of the *Pastoral Care*, chapter 1, says that the shepherd of the [church]{g:ecclesia} ought to bear himself toward his subjects as the shepherd of beasts bears himself toward the beasts he keeps.[^6] Now it is evident that none of them is equal to the shepherd in prudence or [virtue]{g:virtus}; therefore the shepherd of the church ought to excel all his subjects in virtue, since no other excellence is relevant. Since therefore the pope is shepherd of the [whole]{g:universitas-fidelium} remaining body of the faithful, it seems that he ought to be supposed to excel all of them in prudence and virtue. For otherwise the subject would equally abide [with] him and feed him, as the other way round.[^7]

### 12-m ¶4 · 362.7–363.13  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 362] Item, papa (ut ipse et sui fautores asserunt) gerit singulariter vicariam vicem Christi m terris, sic quod ipse infinitum excedit alios quoscumque episcopos, sicut Christus infinitum excessit apostolos, ut patet de indulgenciis et equiparanciis quotquot aliis episcopis et sibi in excellencia; **sed** totum illud foret mendacium, nisi eos proporcionaliter excederet in virtute, igitur hoc debet supponi. Non enim debet supponi quod papa propter fastum et questum seculi mendaciter debeat ad talia aspirare. Ideo dicit Gregorius ubi supra: Oportet namque ut pastor metiri sollicite studeat, quanta tenende rectitudinis necessitate constringitur, sub cuius estimacione populus grex vocatur; igitur necesse est ut sit cogitacione mundus, accione precipuus, pre cunctis contemplacione suspensus. Item, ad esse veri pape requiritur originalis Dei eleccio, **sed** Deus non eligit ad papale officium nisi dignum et virtute cunctos ceteros preeminentem, igitur aliter non est papa. Maior ex fide sepe dicta supponitur; oportet enim Deum concurrere cum quolibet agente creato et omne opus creature legitimum primitus approbare; cum igitur secundum humanitatem non eligit regulariter papas, patet quod oportet eum secundum divinitatem eleccione eterna eligere [p. 363] quemlibet verum papam. Minor **autem** patet ex hoc, quod pastor (in quantum huiusmodi) debet esse medium inter Deum et populum et per consequens in virtute Deo propinquior, ut diffuse declarat beatus Gregorius ubi supra. Et patet ex hoc quod distribuere beneficia, decidere causas civiles et cetera, que non sunt preeminencie virtutum officia, sunt abiectum officium laycale, ideo iuxta beatum Gregorium ad papam non pertinent. Deus igitur eligit eum in papam qui ad iuvandum populum preeminet potestate, et aliter in isto deficiens est Antichristus et pseudopapa, ex ficcione mendacii et fraudacione officii.

ENGLISH: Again, the pope (as he and his supporters assert) singularly bears the vicarial office of Christ on earth, so that he infinitely exceeds any other [bishops]{g:episcopus} whatever, as Christ infinitely exceeded the apostles, as is clear from the [indulgences]{g:indulgencie} and the comparisons of any number of other bishops with himself in excellence.[^10] Now all that would be a lie unless he exceeded them proportionally in virtue; therefore this ought to be supposed. For it ought not to be supposed that the pope, for the pride and gain of the world, should lyingly aspire to such things. So Gregory says in the place cited: "For the shepherd must study carefully to measure by what necessity of holding to uprightness he is bound, under whose estimation the people is called a flock; therefore it is necessary that he be pure in thought, foremost in action, lifted up in contemplation above all."[^11] Again, to the being of a true pope there is required God's original election; now God does not choose for the papal office any but one who is worthy and [preeminent]{g:preeminencia} in virtue over all the rest; therefore otherwise he is not pope. The major, often stated, is supposed [as faith teaches]{g:ex-fide};[^12] for God must concur with every created agent and first approve every lawful work of a creature; since therefore according to his humanity he does not [as a rule]{g:regulariter} elect popes, it is clear that he must, according to his [divinity]{g:divinitas}, choose by eternal election [363] every true pope. And the minor is clear from this, that the shepherd (as such) must be a mean between God and the people, and consequently nearer to God in virtue, as blessed Gregory declares at length in the place cited. And it is clear from this that distributing benefices, deciding civil causes and the rest, which are not offices of preeminence in the virtues, are an abject lay office; so according to blessed Gregory they do not pertain to the pope. God therefore chooses as pope him who is preeminent in [power]{g:potestas} for helping the people, and one who otherwise falls short in this is Antichrist and a [pseudo-pope]{g:pseudopapa}, by the [fiction]{g:ficcio} of a lie and the defrauding of the office.

### 12-m ¶5 · 363.14–364.33  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 363] Ex istis videtur quod nemo sumeret sibi honorem papalem, ut dicit Apostolus, nisi a Deo revelacione abscondita sit vocatus; nam debet cognoscere quod nisi virtute preemineat est contrarius veritati. **Sed** quis scit hoc? Cum igitur gracia fastus vel questus accipiens dignitatem huiusmodi se ipsum excommunicat in principio, patet quam magnum periculum est in presumpcione talis officii. Ideo dicit Gregorius primo Pastoralium IX: Cum curam populi electus presul suscipit, quasi ad egrum medicus accedit. Si igitur adhuc in eius corpore passiones vivunt qua presumpcione percusso mederi properat, qui in facie vulnus portat. Et infra XI: Sollerter se quisque meciatur, ne locum regiminis assumere audeat, si adhuc in se vicium dampnabiliter regnat. Et hinc superna voce Levit. XXI. 17 ad Moysen dicitur, homo de semine tuo per familias qui habuerit maculam non offerat panes Deo suo nec accedat ad ministerium eius. Et forte si prelati forent a Deo examinati radicitus, cognosceretur quod ex [p. 364] perversa affeccione pocius preessendi quam promerendi suscipiunt illud onus. Quod evidenter convincitur ex hoc quod amplectentes temporalia dimittunt officium promerendi, quod oportet laycos si volunt memores esse salutis proprie iudicare. Et sic videtur melius fore ecclesie quod non foret prelatus cesareus. Sic enim Petrus et ceteri apostoli instar Christi preeminenciam illam aufugerant et ministerialiter propter meritum apud Deum absconditum subesse elegerant. Unde Gregorius primo Pastoralium III: Hec breviter diximus, ut quantum sit pondus regiminis monstraremus, ne temerare sacra regimina quisquis impar his est audeat et per concupiscenciam culminis ducatum suscipiat perdicionis. Hinc etenim Jacobus pie prohibet dicens: Nolite plures magistri fieri, fratres mei. Hinc mediator Dei et hominum percipere regnum vitavit in terris, qui supernorum spirituum scienciam sensumque transcendens ante secula regnat in celis, Scriptum quippe est, Ihesus cum cognovisset, quia venturi essent ut raperent et facerent eum regem, fugit in montem. Quis enim principari hominibus tam sine culpa potuisset quam is, qui inculpate regeret illos quos creavit? **Sed** quia idcirco in carne venerat, ut non solum nos per passionem redimeret, verum eciam per conversacionem doceret, exemplum se sequentibus prebens rex fieri noluit, ad crucis vero patibulum sponte convenit, oblatam gloriam culminis fugit, penam probrose mortis expeciit, ut membra eius discerent favores mundi fugere, terrores hominum non timere, pro veritate adversa diligere timendo prospera declinare.

ENGLISH: From these things it seems that no one should take to himself the papal honour, as the Apostle says, unless he has been called by God by a hidden revelation;[^13] for he must know that unless he is preeminent in virtue he is contrary to the truth. But who knows this? Since therefore one who accepts such a dignity for the sake of pride or gain excommunicates himself at the outset, it is clear how great a danger lies in presuming to such an office. So Gregory says in *Pastoral [Care]* 1.9: "When a prelate who has been elected takes up the care of the people, he comes like a physician to the sick. If therefore passions still live in his body, with what presumption does he hasten to heal the stricken, who carries a wound on his own face?" And below, 11: "Let each one skilfully measure himself, lest he dare to assume the place of rule while vice still reigns damnably in him. And hence by the voice from above (Leviticus 21:17) it is said to Moses: 'Whosoever of thy seed throughout their families, hath a blemish, he shall not offer bread to his God, neither shall he approach to minister to him.'"[^14] And perhaps, if [prelates]{g:prelatus} were examined by God to the root, it would be known that they take up that burden out of [364] a perverse affection for presiding rather than for meriting.[^15] Which is evidently proved from this, that those who embrace temporal goods abandon the office of meriting; which laymen must judge, if they wish to be mindful of their own salvation. And so it seems that it would be better for the church that there were no caesarean prelate. For so Peter and the other apostles, after the pattern of Christ, had fled that preeminence, and had chosen to be subject in ministry for the sake of merit hidden with God. Hence Gregory, *Pastoral [Care]* 1.3: "These things we have said briefly, to show how great is the weight of rule, lest whoever is unequal to it dare to profane the sacred offices of rule, and through desire of eminence take up the leadership of perdition.[^16] For hence James piously forbids, saying: 'Be ye not many masters, my brethren.' Hence the mediator of God and men, who, transcending the knowledge and understanding of the spirits above, reigns in heaven before the ages, avoided receiving a kingdom on earth. For it is written: 'Jesus, when he knew that they would come to take him by force and make him king, fled into the mountain.'[^17] For who could have ruled over men so without fault as he who would rule blamelessly those whom he had created? But since he had come in the flesh for this purpose, not only to redeem us by his passion but also to teach us by his [manner of life]{g:conversacio}, offering an example to those who follow him he would not be made king, but went of his own accord to the gibbet of the cross; he fled the glory of eminence offered him, he sought the penalty of a shameful death, that his [members]{g:membrum} might learn to flee the favours of the world, not to fear the terrors of men, to love adversity for the truth's sake, and in fear to turn away from prosperity."

### 12-n ¶1 · 365.1–367.3  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 365] Hic possumus palpare si plus diligimus Christum sequendo eius vestigia, quam sacerdotes cesareos propter mundum. Et hec racio (ut credo) movit Augustinum epistola ad Cirillum, quando dixit, postquam allocutus est beatum Jeronymum iam mortuum: Testor Deum, quod a tempore illo pocius desideravi subesse quam preesse. Nam iuxta legem et diffinicionem Christi Matth. XX: Maior, superior vel prelatus ecclesie, debet subesse ministerio suis subditis, que servitus dum defuerit prelato cesareo non est papa, non enim habet cesar vel Deus potestatem sic mutandi officia prelatorum ecclesie, stante vero nomine eorum quod Christus instituit; Deus enim non potest in contradiccionem, cum tunc foret contrarius sibi ipsi. Ideo licet cesar ordinet, quod regulariter episcopus suus presit aliis episcopis, recipiendo a toto populo honores mundanos, et totus populus subsit generaliter suo decreto, non propterea sequitur quod sit papa; hoc enim potest competere layco ut cesari vel alii abiecto membro dyaboli. Racio igitur papatus consistit in humiliori officio ministrandi, sicut stetit in beato Petro et ceteris, quod solius Dei est creature concedere. Et patet, quod magis periculosa mutacio regis superbie stat in hoc quod excecat homines, mutando sacerdotes ad contrarium officii quod Christus eis instituit. Equivocato enim officio necesse est ut producant ecclesiam et fructus equivocatos. Et patet generaliter de omni curato sive presbitero quod a signo valde probabili indigne appetit pastoris officium, quando tam [p. 366] remote, tam sumptuose et periculose laborat ad primatum ecclesiasticum obtinendum, quia si pure vel principaliter intendit edificacionem ecclesie, plana est via, brevior, securior et Deo placencior, laborando indiffinite sicut fecerunt apostoli. Quod si primatum pure aut primo intendit, inhabilitat se ad omnem statum ecclesie, cum preponit regem superbie regi vero; et sic videtur quod ecclesia regulanda ut olim eligeret sibi presbiterum vel quod apostoli infidelis gentis constituant sibi presbiteros, quos percepto veneno dyaboli debent abicere. Et patet quam extraneum est a Romano pontifice symoniace principatus ecclesiis assignare; hoc enim foret minus dissonum in milite vel alio populari; ex quibus convincitur quod papa tam perplexe se onerans in tam alieno officio atque ambiguo, propter reputatum commodum temporale nimis gravat suum officium, cum fatue reddit se participem criminis hominum, quos sic prefert. Sic eciam dicitur ampliare potestatem eorum quam Deus minuit vel non causat, et restringere potestatem eorum, quam Deus amplificat. Quomodo igitur respondebit Deo stultus tam precipitanter et blasfeme sibi contrarius; non enim est fundabile quod habet potestatem in episcopis, ipsis sciencioribus et copiosius claves ecclesie habentibus potestatem ordinis sic restringere vel laxare. Nam de potestate propria hoc non potest, quos igitur funes habet circa potestatem alienam maiorem sua potestate, quam Deus dat appropiate ad prodessendum ecclesie? Non igitur valet fingere blasfemiam ad magnificandum suam potenciam, **sed** necesse habet stabilire ecclesiam in statu et religione primeva, quam Christus instituit, [p. 367] non excedendo limites in sacramentis et sacramentalibus evangelicis, quia tunc est indubie Antichristus.

ENGLISH: Here we can feel out whether we love Christ more, by following his footsteps, than [we love] [caesarean priests]{g:sacerdos-cesarius} for the world's sake. And this reason (as I believe) moved Augustine in the letter to Cyril, when, after he had addressed blessed Jerome, now dead, he said: "I call God to witness that from that time I have desired rather to be under than to be over."[^1] For according to the law and definition of Christ, Matthew 20, the greater, the superior or the [prelate]{g:prelatus} of the [church]{g:ecclesia} must be under his subjects in ministry;[^2] and while this [servitude]{g:servitus} is wanting to a caesarean prelate he is not [pope]{g:papa}. For neither Caesar nor God has [power]{g:potestas} so to change the offices of the prelates of the church while yet their name, which Christ instituted, stands;[^3] for God cannot [do] what is a contradiction, since then he would be contrary to himself. So although Caesar ordain that his bishop [as a rule]{g:regulariter} preside over other bishops, receiving worldly honours from the whole people, and that the whole people be generally subject to his decree, it does not on that account [follow]{g:sequitur} that he is pope; for this can belong to a layman, as to Caesar or to another abject [member]{g:membrum} of the devil. The nature of the papacy therefore consists in the humbler office of ministering, as it stood in blessed Peter and the others; and this it is God's alone to grant to a creature. And it is clear that the more dangerous change wrought by the king of pride stands in this, that he blinds men, changing priests to the contrary of the office which Christ instituted for them. For when the office is made [equivocal]{g:equivoce}, they must produce an equivocal church and equivocal fruits. And it is clear generally of every [curate]{g:curatus} or [presbyter]{g:presbiter} that, by a very probable sign, he seeks the shepherd's office unworthily when he labours so [366] far off, at such cost and with such danger, to obtain ecclesiastical [primacy]{g:primatus}; for if he purely or principally intends the building up of the church, the way is plain, shorter, safer and more pleasing to God: labouring without fixed bounds, as the apostles did.[^4] But if he intends primacy purely or first, he disqualifies himself for every [state]{g:status} of the church, since he puts the king of pride before the true king; and so it seems that the church, to be ruled as of old, should choose a presbyter for itself, or that the apostles of an [unbelieving]{g:infidelis} people should appoint presbyters for them, whom, once the poison of the devil is perceived, they should cast off. And it is clear how foreign it is to the Roman pontiff to assign principalities to churches simoniacally;[^5] for this would be less discordant in a knight or another man of the people; from which it is proved that the pope, burdening himself so [perplexedly]{g:perplexus} in an office so alien and so doubtful, for the sake of a reputed temporal advantage, burdens his own office too heavily, since he foolishly makes himself a partaker in the crime of the men whom he so prefers. So too he is said to enlarge the power of those men which God diminishes or does not cause, and to restrict the power of those men which God amplifies. How then will the fool answer God, so headlong and so blasphemously contrary to himself? For there is no ground for holding that he has power over bishops, who are themselves more knowing and more abundantly have the keys of the church, so to restrict or [loose]{g:laxare} their [power of order]{g:potestas-ordinis}. For he cannot do this by his own power; what cords then has he about another's power, greater than his own power, which God gives fittingly for profiting the church? He may not therefore feign blasphemy to magnify his own [power]{g:potencia}, but he must establish the church in the primitive state and religion which Christ instituted, [367] not exceeding the [limits]{g:limites} in the gospel sacraments and [sacramentals]{g:sacramentalia}; for then he is undoubtedly Antichrist.

### 12-n ¶2 · 367.4–367.13  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 367] **Sed** obicitur per hoc quod, si papa debet esse sanctissimus viatorum et aliter non est papa, sequitur quod omne peccatum mortale vel veniale debet degradare papam, quod est contra legem distinccione XL: Si papa, in qua dicitur quod solum debet deponi pro heresi. Et iterum videtur idem de regibus, episcopis et quibuscumque ministris ecclesie, ymo summus sacerdos in lege veteri non fuit regulariter sanctissimus, cum sepe fuit pollutus.

ENGLISH: But [it is objected, by appeal to]{g:obicitur-per} this, that if the pope must be the holiest of [wayfarers]{g:viator} and otherwise is not pope, it follows that every sin, mortal or venial, must degrade the pope; which is against the law in distinction 40, *Si papa*, where it is said that he is to be deposed only for heresy.[^6] And again the same seems [to hold] of kings, [bishops]{g:episcopus} and any ministers of the church whatever; indeed the [high priest]{g:summus-sacerdos} in the old law was not as a rule the holiest, since he was often polluted.

### 12-n ¶3 · 367.14–368.10  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 367] Hic dicitur quod maior pars huius difficultatis stat in signacione termini magistralis, ut aliqui intelligunt papam, ut dicit autonomastice super quoscumque alios mirabilem sanctitatem, sicut Christus se habuit ad suos apostolos. Et sic intelligunt homines de Romano pontifice in hiis que lucrum et honorem mundanam sapiunt, **sed** non in vita secundum forum consciencie quoad Deum. Et isto modo loquuntur tres sancti doctores predicti. Alii **autem** intelligunt papam, ut dicit episcopum supereminentem in aliqua sanctitate, ut Ambrosius, Augustinus et ceteri vocati sunt pape. **Sed** postquam declinavit clerus ad civilitatem, vocati sunt quicumque pape, qui ad decidendum principaliter causas ecclesie seculares sunt humanitus instituti. Et super ista equivocacione currunt raciones, nam Christus fuit papa, quem degradaret quodcumque peccatum, ideo stultum foret christianum ea intencione assumere [p. 368] nomen pape. Ideo caverunt de hoc Petrus, Gregorius et alii sancti, ignorantes si sunt membra dyaboli. In medio vero acceperant sibi sancti episcopi nomen pape, et in tercio propter blasfemum fastum et questum assumpserunt sibi confuse illud nomen prelati cesarei ultra auctoritatem novi vel veteris testamenti. Unde secus est de nomine pape et aliis nominibus primo officiorum, cum papa dicit principaliter preeminenciam sanctitatis. Et patet ex equivocacione solucio.

ENGLISH: [The answer]{g:dicitur} here is that the greater part of this difficulty stands in the [signification]{g:signacio} of a [masters' term]{g:termini-magistrales}:[^7] for some understand "pope" as it signifies, [by antonomasia]{g:autonomatice}, a marvellous holiness above all others whatever, as Christ stood toward his apostles.[^8] And so men understand [it] of the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex} in those things that savour of worldly gain and honour, but not in life according to the court of conscience before God. And in this way the three holy doctors aforesaid speak.[^9] But others understand "pope" as it signifies a bishop supereminent in some holiness, as Ambrose, Augustine and the rest were called popes. But after the clergy declined to [civil standing]{g:civilitas}, any were called popes who are humanly instituted principally for deciding the secular causes of the church. And the arguments run upon this [equivocation]{g:equivocacio}; for Christ was pope, whom any sin whatever would degrade; so it would be foolish for a Christian to take [368] the name of pope with that intention. So Peter, Gregory and other saints were wary of this, not knowing whether they are members of the devil. But in the middle [sense] holy bishops had taken the name of pope to themselves, and in the third, out of blasphemous pride and gain, they assumed to themselves [indistinctly]{g:confuse} that name of a caesarean prelate, beyond the [authority]{g:auctoritas} of the new or the old testament. Hence it is otherwise with the name of pope than with other names that are primarily names of offices, since "pope" signifies principally a [preeminence]{g:preeminencia} of holiness.[^10] And the solution is clear from the equivocation.

### 12-n ¶5 · 368.15–368.34  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 368] Hic dicitur quod logica secundum sensum compositum et divisum in ista materia est cavenda. Non enim est aliqua sanctitas qua prepolleret peccator, quin debet esse sanctior, quia aliter non peccaret. Verumtamen nec in statu vie nec statu lapsus debet homo composite esse sanctissimus viatorum, quia subducto omni peccato foret gradus excessuum sanctitatum. **Sed** post lapsum non est alius sanctus vivens vitam communem quin debet esse sancior. **Sed** ut credo quilibet predestinatus debet finire in gradu sanctitatis non summo **sed** sibi debito. Unde luciferina presumpcio videtur quod christianus accipiat sibi nomen talis preeminencie sanctitatis. Ideo sancti concorditer illud respuerant. Quid igitur moveret Romanum episcopum ultra obligacionem apud Deum absconditam ex professione humana spontanea sibi assumere tale nomen; professio namque et officium mutantur ab officio et more apostolico ad legem et officium Antichristi.

ENGLISH: The answer here is that in this matter one must beware of the logic of the [composite and divided sense]{g:sensus-compositus}. For there is no holiness in which a sinner may excel but that he ought to be holier, since otherwise he would not sin. Nevertheless, neither in the state of the way nor in the state of the fall ought a man, in the composite [sense], to be the holiest of wayfarers, since, with all sin taken away, there would [still] be a gradation of holinesses exceeding one another.[^11] But after the fall there is no other saint living the common life but ought to be holier. But, as I believe, every [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} [man] must end in a degree of holiness, not the highest, but the one due to him. Hence it seems a Luciferian presumption that a Christian take to himself the name of such a preeminence of holiness. So the saints with one accord rejected it. What then should move the [Roman bishop]{g:romanus-episcopus}, beyond an obligation hidden with God, to assume such a name to himself by a spontaneous human profession? ### 12-r ¶2 · 383.3–384.14  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 383] Unde logici multiplicant in ista materia argumenta, ut posito quod papa vel alius prelatus iniungat cuicumque clerico sibi subiecto virtute sacre obediencie et sub obtentu absolucionis a pena et culpa vel alterius suffragii spiritualis quod obviet primo plene pape obedienti et iniungat cuicumque layco sub forma consimili, quod obviet primo pape inobedienti, et fiat ista iniunccio sub gravissimo anathemate, et posito cum hoc quod omnis clericus vel laycus pape isti subiectus fuit primo plene sibi obediens, et quod omnis clericus obviet primo singulariter layco et econtra, et videtur sequi contradiccio. Quia da quod non, de Petro clerico et de Paulo layco sibi primo obviantibus, et quero utrum Petrus pro instanti obvie sit pape obediens; si sic, tunc in casu oportet dicere quod Paulus pro illo instanti sit pape inobediens, quia cum primo obviat Petro, qui est plene pape obediens, et iniunctum est sibi quod primo obviet pape inobedienti, sequitur quod Paulus incurrit notam inobediencie et sic Petrus, si in casu isto Petrus sit pro instanti obvie pape inobediens, tunc oportet concedere Paulum esse obedientem et consequenter Petrum, quia ante obviam fuit uterque obediens, et Petrus ex obvia non fit inobediens, **sed** confirmatur eius obediencia iuxta dicta. Nec valet negare casum propter tria: primo quia solum neutrum vel possibile est preceptum, secundo [p. 384] quia prelatus potest precipere simpliciter impossibile et omnino irracionale, igitur per idem vel a maiori potest istud precipere, ideo non restat responsio nisi veritas quod propter suum precipere nec magis nec minus incurrit clerus vel laycus penam vel premium, quia oportet preceptum cui obedietur esse prius racionabile apud Deum: et tunc fieret, licet nemo sub obediencia humana illud preciperet, cum aliter foret homo inobediens racioni. Et patet quod sicut in isto casu non restat expectare plenam remissionem vel anathema, sic generaliter in papali iudicio, nisi de quanto quis meruerit apud Deum; oportet enim quod obiectus sophisticus sit solutus.

ENGLISH: Hence logicians multiply arguments in this matter.[^10] For example, suppose that the pope or another prelate enjoins on every cleric subject to him, by virtue of holy obedience and on the inducement of absolution from penalty and fault or of another spiritual [suffrage]{g:suffragia}, that he first meet one fully obedient to the pope; and that he enjoin on every layman in like form that he first meet one disobedient to the pope; and let this injunction be made under the gravest anathema; and suppose with this that every cleric or layman subject to this pope was at first fully obedient to him, and that every cleric first meets a single layman, and conversely: then a contradiction seems to follow. For grant that it does not, and take Peter, a cleric, and Paul, a layman, first meeting each other; and I ask whether Peter at the instant of the meeting is obedient to the pope. If so, then in the case one must say that Paul at that instant is disobedient to the pope; for since he first meets Peter, who is fully obedient to the pope, and it was enjoined on him that he first meet one disobedient to the pope, [it follows]{g:sequitur} that Paul incurs the mark of disobedience, and so [does] Peter.[^11] If in this case Peter is at the instant of the meeting disobedient to the pope, then one must grant that Paul is obedient, and consequently Peter; for before the meeting each was obedient, and Peter does not become disobedient by the meeting, but his obedience is confirmed, according to what has been said. Nor is it any use to deny the case, for three reasons:[^12] first, because only a neutral or possible thing is commanded; second, [384] because a prelate can command what is impossible [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} and altogether irrational; therefore by the same token, or a fortiori, he can command this. Therefore no reply remains but the truth: that on account of his commanding, a cleric or a layman incurs neither more nor less penalty or reward; for the command which is obeyed must first be reasonable before God, and then it would be done even though no one commanded it under human obedience, since otherwise a man would be disobedient to reason. And it is clear that, as in this case there is no room to expect full [remission]{g:remissio} or anathema, so generally in papal judgement, except in so far as one has merited before God; for the sophistical [objection]{g:objectum} must be solved.

### 12-r ¶4 · 384.28–385.31  (5 sed/autem)

ENGLISH: Further, as to dispensation in affinity or consanguinity, it is clear that [for the most part]{g:ut-plurimum} a delusion of [sophists]{g:sophista} is fabricated. For if the contract is rational and agreeable to God's law, it is valid notwithstanding [385] papal prohibition; nor is it lawful for the pope to dissolve it, for it is said in Matthew 19:6: "Whom God hath joined together, let no man put asunder."[^15] But if a marriage of this kind were destructive of God's law, papal dispensation would lead both parties astray. It seems to me therefore that the marriage of a parent to his nearest offspring would be contrary to the law of nature and so of God; for honour and subjection are owed to parents, but to spouses companionable friendship. And brothers and sisters had to contract [marriage] at the beginning of the world, as is clear of the patriarchs, who were [limited]{g:limitare} to marrying within their own kindred;[^16] but in the time of the [law of grace]{g:lex-gracie}, the whole race of the gentiles having been converted to the Lord, it was reasonably ordained that marriage be contracted outside the kindred, so that the [league]{g:liga} of the covenant of kinship might have another league, of affinity, from carnal espousal, and so there might be, more strongly and more widely, a threefold bond for binding together the covenant of the human race.[^17] And so, the league of spiritual brotherhood in the Lord being let go, the traditions of men about the degree of lawful contract are multiplied and at other times revoked; and dispensation for money and on account of kinship with power comes in.[^18] Yet it is certain that papal dispensation is of no avail unless a reason from scripture underlies it; but papal [definition]{g:diffinicio} avails just so far as he prudently makes the reason clear, and no further. A thousand conclusions follow from these, all of which must be granted; hence folly makes men value the traditions of men about the marriage of kin more than the commandments of the decalogue.
