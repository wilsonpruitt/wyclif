### 08-e ¶1 · 138.23–140.21  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 138] Et si dicatur quod Deus, non potest per se, nec cum tam disparibus secundis agentibus, tales effectus producere; tum,¹⁰ quia nec est¹¹ auctenticum nec expertum; **sed** raciones seminales forent superflue limitate¹²: quoad illud videtur michi quod talis contencio de Dei persona sit satis superflua, ubi non evidenter scimus racionem pro vel contra producere: unde leve verbum multorum est et satis inevidens—‘ego non sum expertus hoc, vel ego non inveni¹³ hoc scriptum in historiis michi auctenticis: ergo non est verum’.—Et ex [p. 139] alio latere sollicitando fingere quod agens secundum potest naturaliter in talia¹ inexperta excedit limites philosophi naturalis. **Sed** inter alia opera, que de Deo credimus, partus virginis tenet locum satis probabilem in lumine² naturali; cum in multis aliis speciebus animalium narrantur femelle sine commixtione³ seminis vel tactu maris concipere. **Sed** admiracio ineffabiliter nobis mirabilis est quod post limitacionem⁴ subitam corporis capti⁵ de pura virgine copulatus sit Deus⁶ pro instanti generacionis⁶ nature complete, et hoc ypostatica unione. Ideo⁷ hoc oportet nos ex fide supponere credendo quod nullum aliud tale novum potest fieri, licet hoc fuerit in natura creata analogice exemplatum de ypostatica unione⁷ mentis cum corpore. Et hinc tam sepe exemplificat Augustinus cum simbolo Athanasii quod ‘sicut anima racionalis et caro unus est homo, ita⁸ Deus et homo unus est⁸ Christus.’ Decuit enim auctorem nature specialiter post peccatum hominis remissibile per talem assumpcionem viri⁹ ex femina speciem hominis innovare: ut, sicut primo formatus est mas ex non-homine, secundo¹⁰ femina ex homine¹⁰ **sed** ex mare solummodo, tercio **autem** ex his sexubus commixtis sunt alii communiter procreati; sic quarto pro complemento iusticie¹¹ per se stantis in quatuor¹¹ compleat Deus virum celestem pure ex femina; cuius necessitas patet alibi. Nec sequitur ex illis superfluitas limitacionis racionis seminalis diversis mixtorum generibus, ut experiencia philosophorum testatur, quod multa genera animalium, in quibus est distinccio sexuum, tam ex putrefaccione quam ex semine procreantur. Et tamen, cum hoc dicunt philosophi quod nec sexuum distinccio, nec racionis seminalis limitacio est aliquo modo superflua; sic¹² latet in visceribus nature, quid Deus disposuit secundum potenciam obediencialem creature sue per se facere, et quid agens [p. 140] creatum a seculis multis absconditum sufficit preter communem cursum nature producere. Et circa¹ possibilitatem talium sollicitantur multi superflue: ut illi, quod demones² possunt in aspectu placido celi, mediantibus virtutibus mixtorum sicut herbarum et lapidum, mirabilia vulgo inopinabilia per se efficere: illi quod celum secundum diversas figuras et aspectus astrorum per se³ potest in talia: et hi quod homines possunt cum iuvamine demonum et constellacione celestium mirabilia inopinata producere. Et in speculacione talium theologizant speculativi inaniter. In practica vero mathematici consumunt tempus figuras celi superflue prestolantes, et demones periculosius consulentes. Sufficit ergo pio theologo quod sciat in genere talia mirabilia ab agente secundo posse fieri: 2° quod sciat istis agentibus secundis a Deo, a quo est omnis potencia, terminos secundum maximum et minimum limitari; nec⁴ creaturam aliquam posse in aliquid, nisi secundum eius ordinacionem et benevolenciam limitantem: et 3°. quod Deus ex condicione omnipotencie⁵ facit eo ipso quodlibet dandum opus quo vult ipsum a quoquam fieri, et istam condicionem potencie non potest communicari alteri.

ENGLISH: And if it is said that God cannot, [of himself]{g:per-se} or with such disparate secondary agents, produce such effects after all,[^1] because it is neither authentic nor experienced, but [otherwise] the [seminal reasons]{g:raciones-seminales} would be limited superfluously:[^2] as to that, it seems to me that such contention about the [person]{g:persona} of God is quite superfluous, where we do not evidently know how to produce a reason for or against. Hence it is a light word of many, and quite without evidence: "I have not experienced this, or I have not found this written in histories authentic to me: therefore it is not true." And on the [139] other side, to be anxious to feign that a secondary agent can naturally [act] on such untried things exceeds the limits of the natural philosopher. But among the other works which we believe of God, the virgin's childbearing holds a place quite probable in the natural light, since in many other species of animals females are related to conceive without mixture of seed or touch of the male.[^3] Now the marvel, ineffably wonderful to us, is that after the sudden limiting of the body taken from the pure virgin, God was joined to the complete [nature]{g:natura} at the instant of generation,[^4] and this by hypostatic [union]{g:unio}. Therefore we must suppose this from faith, believing that no other such new thing can be made, although it has been analogically exemplified in created nature by the hypostatic union of mind with body.[^5] And hence Augustine, with the [creed]{g:symbolum} of Athanasius, so often gives the example that "as the reasonable soul and flesh is one man, so God and man is one Christ."[^6] For it was fitting that the author of nature, especially after man's sin, which could be remitted, should renew the species of man through such an [assumption]{g:assumpcio} of a [man]{g:vir} (*vir*) from a woman: so that, as first the male was formed from no man, second the female from man, but from the male only, and third the others have commonly been procreated from these sexes mingled; so fourth, for the completion of the justice that stands of itself in four, God should complete the heavenly man (*vir*) purely from a woman; the necessity of which is clear elsewhere.[^7] Nor does it [follow]{g:sequitur} from these that the limiting of the seminal reason to the diverse kinds of mixed bodies is superfluous, as the experience of the philosophers testifies that many kinds of animals in which there is distinction of the sexes are procreated both from putrefaction and from seed.[^8] And yet, although the philosophers say this, that neither the distinction of the sexes nor the limiting of the seminal reason is in any way superfluous, so it lies hidden in the bowels of nature what God has disposed to do of himself according to the obediential [potency]{g:potencia} of his [creature]{g:creatura},[^9] and what, hidden for many ages, a created [140] agent is sufficient to produce beyond the common course of nature.[^10] And about the possibility of such things many are superfluously anxious: as those [who hold] that demons can, under a favourable aspect of the heaven, by means of the [powers]{g:virtus} of mixed bodies such as herbs and stones, of themselves effect marvels unimaginable to the common people; those, that the heaven, according to the diverse figures and aspects of the stars, can of itself [act] on such things; and these, that men can, with the help of demons and the constellation of the heavenly bodies, produce unexpected marvels. And in speculation on such things the speculative theologize vainly. But in practice the [mathematicians]{g:mathematici} consume their time, superfluously waiting on the figures of the heaven, and more perilously consulting demons.[^11] It suffices, therefore, for the devout theologian that he know in general that such marvels can be done by a secondary agent; second, that he know that for these secondary agents terms are limited by God, from whom is all power,[^12] as to maximum and minimum; and that no creature can [act] on anything except according to his [ordinance]{g:ordinacio} and limiting benevolence; and third, that God, by the condition of omnipotence, does every work that is to be given by the very fact that he wills it to be done by anyone, and this condition of power cannot be communicated to another.[^13]

### 08-e ¶2 · 140.22–141.15  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 140] Quantum ad testimonium Commentatoris patet quod dictum Averrois⁶ non debet esse alicui philosopho, et minus theologo, testis auctenticans, cum tam in philosophia quam fide presumptive⁷ vel invide sepius⁷ deviavit. Fautor **autem** Commentatoris, cui placeret⁸ dictum suum palliare, posset fingere ipsum de Avicenna concipere; quod posset esse homo, cuius proxima materia subiecta anime foret terra, et per consequens corpus alterius speciei quam mixtum organicum⁹ a sibi simili in specie procreatum; et cum forme dantur secundum disposicionem materie, si haberet¹⁰ animam, ipsa foret ab humanitate nostra disparis racionis. Nam in tam [p. 141] vilem materiam non inducit natura formam¹ simee vel pigmee:¹ vel 2°. quod natura plus ingenians² circa produccionem hominis quam animalis alterius imperfecti, quod corpus sicut et anima³ non posset produci naturaliter nisi a racionali supposito producatur. Ideo non sequitur—animal imperfectum potest ex putrefaccione produci: ergo homo— qui est finis uniuscuiusque alterius animalis, quia effectum preciosiorem virtus regitiva universitatis plus curat. Unde figuracionem embrionis oportet fieri a virtute informativa ab anima hominis per species triplices dirivata, per fomentum caloris consimilis in matrice, et per nutricionem concepti de sanguine menstruo parientis. Quod si alius sit modus producendi quoad corpus hoc est auctori nature specialiter reservatum. **Sed** totum illud vix moveret protervum ad credendum possibilitatem negative que petitur.

ENGLISH: As to the testimony of the Commentator, it is clear that the saying of Averroes ought not to be an authenticating witness for any philosopher, and less for a theologian, since both in philosophy and in faith he has quite often strayed, presumptuously or enviously.[^14] And a supporter of the Commentator whom it pleased to [cloak]{g:palliare} his saying could feign that he was speaking of Avicenna['s view]: that there could be a man whose [proximate matter]{g:materia-proxima}, subject to the soul, would be earth, and consequently a body of another species than the organic mixed body procreated from one like itself in species; and since forms are given according to the disposition of matter, if it had a soul, that soul would be of a kind disparate from our [humanity]{g:humanitas}. For into such [141] vile matter nature does not induce the form of an ape or a pygmy: or second, [that he meant] that nature, taking more pains over the production of man than of another, imperfect animal, [brings it about] that the body, as well as the soul, could not be produced naturally unless it is produced by a rational [supposit]{g:suppositum}.[^15] Therefore it does not follow: "an imperfect animal can be produced from putrefaction: therefore a man [can]", [man] who is the end of every other animal; since the ruling power of the universe takes more care for the more precious effect. Hence the shaping of the embryo must be done by an informing power derived from the soul of the man through threefold species, by the fostering of like heat in the womb, and by the nourishment of what is conceived from the menstrual blood of the mother.[^16] And if there is another mode of producing as to the body, this is specially reserved to the author of nature. But all that would hardly move an [obstinate]{g:protervus} man to believe the possibility that is sought negatively.[^17]

### 08-e ¶3 · 141.16–142.9  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 141] Tercii **autem** dicunt quod nemo post primum parentem potest produci nisi materialiter⁴ producatur ab homine propter amorem humani generis nutriendum. Nam racione stipitis et cognacionis oportet nutriri amorem inter homines ultra bruta, quia aliter fuisset facile post peccatum Adam totam suam generacionem sicut genus Caim⁵ exstinguere et aliud genus hominum ex disparato principio⁶ procreare⁶ sine hoc quod Christus paciatur pro salvanda specie. Et tunc esset homo species imperfecta, sine qua mundus posset existere; cum, nullo homine existente, celum posset ut vermes hominem⁷ de novo producere; †et multo magis elementum⁸ quod est⁹ gracia hominis tamquam finis non esset de substancia universali;¹⁰ et, sicut¹¹ situs spere¹² corruptibilium posset vacuus a corpore.†¹³ **Sed** cum iste tractatus multum¹⁴ distat a puncto proposito, redeundo dicitur quod philosophicum et facile est catholicum sustinere quod primus homo fiebat de terra, secundus sexus [p. 142] ab illo, et tercius ab ambobus; et sic gradatim de genere consequente quousque novus homo atque novissimus eiusdem speciei cum aliis fiebat ex femina; et quinto¹ omnis homo secundum animam fit ab illo circulariter a quo ultimate reficitur: quinarius **autem** et senarius sunt numeri circulares. Non enim credo quod species humana peribit, vel quod possit sub tanto auctore eciam quoad perfeccionem illam² deficere.

ENGLISH: And the third say that no one after the first parent can be produced unless he is materially produced by a man, for the sake of nourishing the love of the human race. For by reason of stock and kinship love must be nourished among men beyond [what there is among] the brutes; since otherwise it would have been easy after Adam's sin to extinguish his whole generation, like the race of Cain, and to procreate another race of men from a disparate principle, without Christ's suffering for the saving of the species. And then man would be an imperfect species, without which the world could exist; since, with no man existing, the heaven could produce a man anew, as [it does] worms; †and much more, the element which is for the sake of man as its end would not be of the universal substance; and, as the place of the sphere of corruptible things could [be] empty of body.†[^18] But since this treatise stands far from the point proposed, returning: [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that it is philosophical, and easy for a catholic, to hold that the first man was made from earth, the second sex [142] from him, and the third from both; and so by degrees, of the kind that follows, until the new and newest man, of the same species with the others, was made from a woman; and fifth, every man as to the soul is made by him circularly, by whom he is ultimately remade: and five and six are circular numbers.[^19] For I do not believe that the human species will perish, or that it could, under so great an author, fail even as to that perfection.[^20]

### 09-a ¶7 · 143.9–143.23  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 143] 2°. oportet cavere ne credatur conversio⁶ vel confusio naturarum; cum manet plene natura divina⁷ cum duabus naturis incommunicantibus **sed** creatis constituentibus humanitatem perfectam, omnes⁸ et singule eadem persona in numero. Si **autem** esset conversio tunc natura conversa pro mensura conversionis⁹ non maneret; ut lignum conversum in ignem pro mensura conversionis desinit, nova forma inducta et veteri desinente; quod non potest esse de tribus formis incommunicantibus¹⁰ supra dictis. Si **autem** esset confusio naturarum, tunc forent reciproce quelibet eadem cuilibet, vel omnis vel aliqua incompleta, sicut est de elementis et¹¹ mixtis integraliter componentibus ipsum mixtum.¹² Modo **autem** manent tres incommunicantes nature, quelibet plene incommunicans cuilibet; **sed** utraque creatura per unionem personalem idem ypostatice Verbo Dei.

ENGLISH: Second, one must beware lest a conversion or [confusion of the natures]{g:confusio-naturarum} be believed;[^3] since the divine nature remains fully, with two natures [that do not communicate]{g:incommunicantes} but are [created]{g:creatura}, constituting the perfect humanity, all and each the same person in number. Now if there were conversion, then the converted nature would not remain, to the measure of the conversion; as wood converted into fire ceases to the measure of the conversion, a new form being induced and the old ceasing; which cannot be the case with the three forms, that do not communicate, spoken of above.[^4] And if there were confusion of the natures, then any would be reciprocally the same as any, or all or some incomplete, as is the case with elements and mixed bodies integrally composing the mixed body itself.[^5] But as it is, three natures remain that do not communicate, each fully not communicating with each; but each of the two creatures is, through the personal union, the same [hypostatically]{g:ypostatice} with the Word of God.

### 09-a ¶8 · 143.24–144.24  (9 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 143] 3°. oportet cavere ne¹³ credatur ex humanitate et deitate fieri unum aggregative, ut est cumulus lapidum, vel accidens et subiectum. Nam unio ypostatica, que est ydemptitas¹⁴ personalis facit quod quelibet istarum trium incommunicancium naturarum est plene eadem communis persona; licet inter se naturaliter distinguantur per totum ex multis sanctorum testimoniis supradictis, ut Augustini, Gregorii, et Anselmi. Unde istam sentenciam colligit¹⁵ venerabilis Anselmus in De Incarnacione Verbi ca°. 7. sub his verbis: [p. 144] ‘Sicut, inquit, in Deo una natura est plures persone, et plures persone sunt una natura; sic in Christo una persona est plures nature, et plures nature sunt una persona. “Quemadmodum enim¹ Pater est Deus, Filius Deus et Spiritus sanctus Deus; et² non tamen tres dii, **sed** unus Deus;”² ita in Christo Deus est persona, et homo est persona, non tamen due persone **sed** una persona.’ Intelligit **autem** iste doctor per Deum et hominem deitatem et humanitatem, quia aliter esset similitudo impertinens, et verba sequencia nimis falsa. ‘Non, inquit, alius Deus alius homo in Christo, quamvis aliud sit Deus, aliud homo, **sed** idem ipse Deus qui et homo.’ Quando ergo dicit cum beato Augustino, Ieronimo, Gregorio et aliis quod aliud Deus in Christo et aliud homo, intelligit quod alia natura est deitas et alia humanitas, **sed** utraque non alia **sed** eadem persona; quia homo, ut dicit, solum naturam significat, et illum assumptum hominem dicit postea esse Iesum, et eandem personam cum Verbo. Unde non dubium quin per assumptum hominem intelligit naturam humanam assumptam. ‘Verbum, inquit, naturam aliam assumpsit, non aliam personam.’ Et ita generaliter intendunt sancti quando dicunt ‘aliud Deus, aliud homo; **sed** non alius,’ quod alia natura sit deitas et alia natura³ humanitas, non **autem** persona alia **sed** eadem.

ENGLISH: Third, one must beware lest it be believed that from the humanity and the [Godhead]{g:deitas} there comes to be one thing [aggregatively]{g:aggregative}, as a heap of stones is, or an [accident]{g:accidens} and its [subject]{g:subiectum}. For the hypostatic union, which is personal identity, makes each of these three natures that do not communicate to be fully the same common person; although among themselves they are naturally distinguished throughout, [as appears] from the many testimonies of [the saints]{g:sancti} cited above, as of Augustine, Gregory and Anselm. Hence the venerable Anselm gathers this teaching in *On the Incarnation of the Word*, chapter 7, in these words: [144] "As," he says, "in God one nature is several persons, and several persons are one nature, so in Christ one person is several natures, and several natures are one person. 'For as the Father is God, the Son God and the Holy Spirit God, and yet not three gods but one God,' so in Christ God is a person and man is a person, yet not two persons but one person."[^6] And this doctor understands by "God" and "man" the Godhead and the humanity, since otherwise the likeness would be [irrelevant]{g:impertinens}, and the words that follow [exceedingly]{g:nimis} false. "There is not," he says, "another one who is God and another one who is man in Christ, although God is one thing and man another; but the very same is God who is also man."[^7] When therefore he says, with the blessed Augustine, Jerome, Gregory and others, that God in Christ is one thing and man another, he understands that the Godhead is one nature and the humanity another, but each is not another but the same person;[^8] since "man", as he says, signifies only the nature, and he says afterwards that that [assumed man]{g:homo-assumptus} is Jesus, and the same person with the Word. Hence there is no doubt that by "the assumed man" he understands the assumed human nature. "The Word," he says, "assumed another nature, not another person." And so the saints generally [maintain]{g:intendere}, when they say "God is one thing, man another, but not another one", that the Godhead is one nature and the humanity another nature, but not another person, rather the same.

### 09-a ¶11 · 145.16–146.16  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 145] 3ª radix est abieccio predicacionis secundum essenciam, ut⁵ recte admittentes predicacionem secundum essenciam concedunt cum Anselmo De Incarnacione 7 quod quelibet universalis forma est idem cum omnibus et singulis eius suppositis, et correspondenter de aliis rebus communibus: concedunt eciam quod tam in creatore quam eciam⁶ in qualibet creatura omne esse est essencia et e contra, licet in omnibus istis sit formalis differencia vel distinccio racionis. Cum ergo moderniores variant in istis tribus radicibus, non mirum si variant in germine consequente. Ponunt enim quod non est possibile⁷ plures naturas vel res alias⁸ singulas esse unam. Ponunt 2° quod omnis forma substancialis compositi est distincta essencialiter a subiecto, in tantum quod aliqui dicunt omnem formam materialem substancie posse a natura separari. Et 3° dicunt⁹ quod in omni creatura est dare essenciam, que non est esse, **sed** eius causa, sicut lux luminis vel¹⁰ lucere. Ex istis dicunt consequenter [p. 146] quod non est possibile multas naturas esse personam Verbi. Dicunt eciam quod in quolibet homine esse suum et essencia et per consequens ipse homo ac sua humanitas distinguantur, sic quod omnis homo vel non est humanitas, vel humanitas solum est homo per accidens: unde Christus ut dicunt non suscepit hominem, **sed** humanitatem, que non potest esse idem ypostatice Verbo Dei. In istis ergo tribus radicibus et aliis ex eis consequentibus stat modernorum variacio ab antiquis. Magnam¹ quidem vim habent principia, cum eorum variacio dilatat distinccionem multiplicem posterius dicendorum. Oportet igitur supponere res communes: quod idem est forma substancialis cuiuslibet et formatum; ac demum, admittendo² predicacionem secundum essenciam, quod sicut in omni creato vel increato omne esse est essencia et e contra; sic omnis humanitas vel³ esse hominis est homo sive assumpta sive suppositacione propria terminata.

ENGLISH: The third root is the rejection of [predication by essence]{g:predicacio-secundum-essenciam}; as those who rightly admit predication by essence grant, with Anselm, *On the Incarnation* 7, that every universal form is the same with all and each of its supposits, and correspondingly of other common things:[^12] they grant also that both in the creator and in every creature all being is essence and conversely, although in all these there is a formal [difference]{g:differencia} or [distinction of reason]{g:distinccio-racionis}. Since therefore the more modern [doctors] vary in these three roots, it is no wonder if they vary in the shoot that follows. For they posit that it is not possible for several natures or other singular things to be one. They posit, second, that every substantial form of a composite is essentially distinct from the subject, so far that some say that every material form of a substance can be separated by nature. And third they say that in every creature [one may posit]{g:est-dare} an essence which is not being but its cause, as light [is the cause] of radiance, or of shining.[^13] From these they say consequently [146] that it is not possible for many natures to be the person of the Word. They say also that in every man his being and essence, and consequently the man himself and his humanity, are distinguished, so that every man either is not humanity, or humanity is man only [by accident]{g:per-accidens}: hence Christ, as they say, did not take up a man but a humanity, which cannot be the same hypostatically with the Word of God. In these three roots, therefore, and in others following from them, stands the variation of the moderns from the ancients. Great indeed is the force that principles have, since their variation widens the manifold distinction of what is to be said afterwards. We must therefore suppose common things; that the substantial form of anything and what is formed are the same; and finally, admitting predication by essence, that as in everything created or uncreated all being is essence and conversely, so every humanity, or being of a man, is man, whether assumed or terminated by its own [suppositation]{g:suppositacio}.[^14]

### 09-b ¶1 · 146.25–147.18  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 146] Hic dicitur quod consequencia non valet, cum idem sit ac si sic argueretur—Verbum fecit se esse hominem, et esse hominem est esse personam: ergo Verbum fecit se esse personam—ubi constat quod antecedente existente necessario consequens est impossibile; cum tantum⁴ una persona, que absolute necessario est Verbum, poterit esse Verbum, et per consequens cum Verbum non potest se facere illam personam, patet quod Verbum non potest facere se personam. **Sed** pro ulteriori declaracione notandum quod assumere personale, de quo scriptura cum sanctis doctoribus in proposito loquitur, [p. 147] est duas naturas personaliter unire, id est,¹ duas naturas facere esse unum suppositum vel² personam, et ideo vocatur² unio personalis. Et potest secundum³ tres gradus active intelligi. Primo pro active principiare unionem huiusmodi: et sic tota Trinitas assumpsit naturam humanam de quanto univit⁴ eam ypostatice Verbo Dei. 2° striccius pro activa⁵ causancia principali unientis, ut ipsum fiat eadem persona cum natura unita; et sic natura divina univit vel assumpsit in supposito Verbi⁶ naturam hominis; quia fecit naturam hominis esse eandem personam non eandem naturam cum persona divina. 3° strictissime dicitur assumere personale pro causancia persone quia fecit⁷ naturam creatam⁸ esse idem personaliter sibi ipsi. Et illo modo solum Verbum assumpsit hominem. Ex istis patet, cum⁹ nulla assumpcio ypostatica sit eterna, quod oportet assumptum esse solummodo creaturam, quia nec alia persona, nec alia natura est Christi humanitas; licet sit creator, quia persona Verbi, que secundum deitatem non secundum ipsam creat omnia.

ENGLISH: Here [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that the [inference]{g:consequencia} is not valid, since it is the same as if it were argued thus: "The [Word]{g:verbum} made himself to be a man, and to be a man is to be a [person]{g:persona}: therefore the Word made himself to be a person"; where it is evident that, the [antecedent]{g:antecedens} being necessarily [true], the [consequent]{g:consequens} is impossible;[^1] since only one person, which absolutely of necessity is the Word, could be the Word; and consequently, since the Word cannot make himself that person, it is clear that the Word cannot make himself a person. But for further clarification it is to be noted that personal [assuming]{g:assumere}, of which scripture with the holy doctors speaks in the matter in hand, [147] is to unite two [natures]{g:natura} personally, that is, to make two natures to be one [supposit]{g:suppositum} or person; and therefore it is called personal [union]{g:unio}. And it can be understood actively in three degrees. First, for actively being [the principle of]{g:principiare} such a union: and so the whole Trinity assumed the human nature, inasmuch as it united it hypostatically to the Word of God.[^2] Second, more strictly, for the active principal causing of the one uniting, so that it becomes the same person with the nature united; and so the divine nature united or assumed in the supposit of the Word the nature of man; since it made the nature of man to be the same person, not the same nature, with the divine person. Third, most strictly, [personal assuming]{g:assumere-personale} is said for the causing [proper] to the person, since it made the created nature to be the same personally with itself. And in that way the Word alone assumed man.[^3] From these it is clear, since no hypostatic [assumption]{g:assumpcio} is eternal, that what is assumed must be only a [creature]{g:creatura}, since Christ's [humanity]{g:humanitas} is neither another person nor another nature; although it is creator, since [it is] the person of the Word, which as to the [Godhead]{g:deitas}, not as to it, creates all things.[^4]

### 09-b ¶2 · 147.19–148.2  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 147] 2° patet, quod non potest esse personalis assumpcio nisi persona naturam assumpserit non personam.¹⁰ Oportet enim personam assumere, quia aliter non esset unio personalis, nisi persona ydemptificaret sibi naturam. Et quod oportet personam assumentem naturam assumere non personam, patet ex hoc quod personarum personalis¹¹ ydemptificacio vel naturarum essencialis ydemptificacio est impossibilis, ut nullum unum suppositum potest esse aliud, nec una natura fieri potest alia. **Sed** sicut persone maxime entitatis uniuntur in suprema essencia vel natura, sic nature maxime distantes ydemptificantur in eodem supposito vel persona. Et patet quod natura divina non potest primo sibi assumere naturam aliam vel personam. Patet ex hoc quod non potest esse alia natura vel persona quam est ab intrinseco necessario absolute, et nullius talis potest esse assumpcio. Oportet [p. 148] ergo naturam divinam unire¹ Verbo non nature primo quam sumpsit.²

ENGLISH: Second, it is clear that there cannot be a personal assumption unless a person has assumed a nature, not a person. For a person must assume, since otherwise there would not be a personal union, unless a person identified a nature with itself. And that the person assuming must assume a nature, not a person, is clear from this, that the personal identification of persons, or the essential identification of natures, is impossible, as no one supposit can be another, nor can one nature become another. But as the persons of the greatest entity are united in the supreme [essence]{g:essencia} or nature, so the most distant natures are identified in the same supposit or person. And it is clear that the divine nature cannot primarily assume to itself another nature or person. It is clear from this, that it cannot be another nature or person than it is, from within, necessarily and absolutely; and of no such thing can there be assumption. [148] Therefore the divine nature must unite [the nature] which it took to the Word, not primarily to a nature.[^5]

### 09-b ¶3 · 148.3–149.9  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 148] 3° patet quod assumpcio ypostatica, unio, vel incarnacio, aut quomodocunque³ aliter nominetur,³ nec est generacio, nec conversio, nec alteracio, **sed** ydemptificacio; et si est mutacio, tunc est potissima mutacio dextere excelsi. Non enim est generacio, licet⁴ generacio⁴ ad ipsam consequatur:⁵ sicut motus localis ad motum augmentacionis, cum omnis generacio temporalis sit a non-esse ad esse produccio: assumpcio⁶ autem⁶ videtur esse subsistencie⁷ summe minoracio, cum Verbum ex incarnacione minoratum est paulo minus ab angelis, ut exponit Apostolus ad Hebre. 2° illud Psalmi 8ⁱ; ‘minuisti eum paulo minus ab angelis.’ Nec est conversio, cum subsistencia Verbi manet summa secundum omnes eius proprietates non composita⁸ cum aliquo. Nec est alteracio, tum quia humanitas assumpta non est inherens accidens; tum eciam quia persona secundum naturam precedentem humanitatem assumptam est omnino immobilis eo quod illi nature non potest accidens, ad quod est motus, formaliter inherere. Ex istis colligitur⁹ racio quare sequitur quod Verbum assumendo humanitatem assumpsit essenciam, substanciam, aliquid, et naturam; et non assumendo illam humanitatem assumpsit individuum, suppositum, vel personam. Nam¹⁰ illa¹⁰ humanitas est per se inferius ad aliquid, essenciam, vel naturam; cum sit per se quiditas singularis in specie humana; persona autem¹¹ cum sit extra racionem generis creati est solum accidentaliter superius ad substanciam, aliquid et¹² quodcunque genus creatum. Cum ergo persona Verbi sit equivoce Verbum respectu humanitatis, patet quod non sequitur, si Verbum illam humanitatem assumpsit, que non primo per [p. 149] se **sed** a Deo equivoce est persona, quod exinde assumpsit personam: ut in exemplo non sequitur—Petrus fecit se septipedalem, album, patrem, et sic de ceteris generibus accidencium: ergo Petrus fecit se esse aliquid—; quia est aliquid extra racionem quanti, vel qualis etc. Correspondenter Verbum esse personam est tanquam longe prius extra racionem humanitatis, **sed** esse aliquid est racio per se humanitatis; cum Christus, in quantum homo, est aliquid eciam quod prius non fuerat: ideo assumendo hominem assumpsit aliquid non personam.

ENGLISH: Third, it is clear that hypostatic assumption, union or incarnation, or however else it may be named, is neither generation, nor conversion, nor alteration, but identification; and if it is a change, then it is the mightiest "change of the right hand of the most High".[^6] For it is not generation, although generation follows upon it, as local motion [follows] upon the motion of augmentation; since every temporal generation is a production from not-being to being: but assumption seems to be a [lowering]{g:minoracio} of the highest [subsistence]{g:subsistencia}, since the Word through the incarnation was made a little lower than the angels, as the Apostle, Hebrews 2, expounds that [verse] of Psalm 8, "Thou hast made him a little less than the angels."[^7] Nor is it conversion, since the subsistence of the Word remains highest according to all its properties, not composed with anything.[^8] Nor is it alteration, both because the assumed humanity is not an inhering [accident]{g:accidens}, and also because the person, according to the nature preceding the assumed humanity, is altogether [immovable]{g:immobilis}, since no accident, to which motion tends, can inhere formally in that nature. From these the reason is gathered why it [follows]{g:sequitur} that the Word, by assuming the humanity, assumed an essence, a substance, something and a nature; and [why it does] not [follow that], by assuming that humanity, he assumed an [individual]{g:individuum}, a supposit or a person. For that humanity is [of itself]{g:per-se} an inferior to "something", "essence" or "nature", since it is of itself a singular [quiddity]{g:quiditas} in the human species; but "person", since it is outside the account of the created genus, is only accidentally a superior to "substance", "something" and any created genus whatever. Since therefore the person of the Word is [equivocally]{g:equivoce} the Word with respect to the humanity,[^9] it is clear that it does not follow, if the Word assumed that humanity, which is a person not first of [149] itself but from God, equivocally, that he thereby assumed a person: as in the example it does not follow: "Peter made himself seven feet tall, white, a father, and so of the other genera of accidents: therefore Peter made himself to be something"; since he is something outside the account of the [quantified]{g:quantus} or the qualified, etc. Correspondingly, the Word's being a person is, as something long prior, outside the account of the humanity; now being something is of itself the account of the humanity; since Christ, [insofar as]{g:in-quantum} he is man, is something also which he had not been before: therefore by assuming man he assumed something, not a person.

### 09-b ¶4 · 149.10–149.28  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 149] Et si arguatur ex illo sequi quod Christus assumpsit personam hominis, sicut fecit se esse personam hominis: dicitur quod non hoc **sed** oppositum sequitur ex predictis; cum assumpcio non sit nisi nature per se possibilis, et, nulla¹ inherente accidente,¹ mutacio est assumpcio. Non enim quelibet faccio, **sed** solum faccio, qua natura racionalis fit Deus, est assumpcio vel ypostatica unio; et per conquens solum ydemptificacio est assumpcio.² Et patet quod sicut Christus fecit se esse hominem, animal, corpus, substanciam, et aliquid; sic assumpsit hec omnia in illa substancia singulari. Unde idem aliquid, quod fuit eternaliter est equivoce ad³ illud aliquid quod³ assumpsit. Ideo sicut albefactum sit⁴ ut sic⁴ coloratum et quale, licet perante fuerat coloratum, quia aliter non esset alteracio possibilis: sic in proposito per incarnacionem Christus fit aliquid licet prius fuit aliquid. Sicut enim sufficit ad alteratum, quod ipsum sit alterum secundum gradum quam prius; sic sufficit ad alietatem Christi quod aliquid aliud sit Christus quam prius fuerat.

ENGLISH: And if it is argued that from that it follows that Christ assumed the person of a man, as he made himself to be the person of a man: the answer is that not this but the opposite follows from what has been said; since assumption is only of a nature possible of itself, and, with no accident inhering, the change is assumption.[^10] For not every [making]{g:faccio} whatever, but only the making by which a rational nature becomes God, is assumption or hypostatic union; and consequently only identification is assumption. And it is clear that, as Christ made himself to be man, animal, body, substance and something, so he assumed all these in that singular substance. Hence the same something which was eternally is equivocally [something] with respect to that something which he assumed. Therefore, as what is whitened becomes, as such, coloured and qualified, although it had been coloured before, since otherwise alteration would not be possible:[^11] so in the matter in hand, through the incarnation Christ becomes something, although before he was something. For as it suffices for what is altered that it be other in degree than before, so it suffices for Christ's [otherness]{g:alietas} that something other is Christ than he had been before.

### 09-b ¶5 · 149.29–150.16  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 149] Et patet quod non est assumpcio nisi substancie; et per consequens nullius forme inherentis est assumpcio: ut Christus non assumpsit ypostatice se pati, mori, vel ascendere, quamvis se fecit huiusmodi; quia tunc ydemptificasset passionem, mortem, et ascensionem ypostasi Verbi, sicut fecit [p. 150] humanitatem; quod est impossibile. Et sic quelibet nature racionalis accidentalis transmutacio esset assumpcio; quod repugnat supradictis. Persona ergo Verbi accidentaliter est persona hominis. Ergo sicut non assumpsit dominium¹ in mundi principio, nec filium hominis pro instanti incarnacionis; sic nec personam hominis, cum non assumpsit personam. **Sed** assumendo ‘formam servi’ fecit se esse personam hominis; licet nullam personam se fecerit, **sed** illam naturam, quam assumpsit, se fecerit:² cum sophisma illud non habet hic locum—Christus fecit se esse hominem, **sed** nullum hominem—; cum illam³ humanitatem, et per consequens hoc aliquid se fecit, quod prius non fuerat. Unde si correspondenter esset persona hominis, que tunc et non prius⁴ fuit Verbum Dei; tunc concedendum esset quod personam hominis assumpsit: **sed** assumptum est impossibile.

ENGLISH: And it is clear that there is no assumption except of substance; and consequently there is no assumption of any inhering form: as Christ did not assume [hypostatically]{g:ypostatice} his suffering, dying or ascending, although he made himself such; since then he would have identified the passion, death and ascension with the [hypostasis]{g:ypostasis} of the Word, as he did [150] the humanity; which is impossible. And so every accidental transmutation of a rational nature would be assumption; which [is incompatible]{g:repugnare} with what was said above. The person of the Word, therefore, is accidentally the person of a man. Therefore, as he did not assume [lordship]{g:dominium} in the beginning of the world, nor [the] son of man at the instant of the incarnation, so neither [the] person of a man, since he did not assume a person.[^12] But by assuming "the form of a servant" he made himself to be the person of a man;[^13] although he made himself no person, but made himself that nature which he assumed: since that [sophism]{g:sophisma} has no place here: "Christ made himself to be man, but no man"; since he made himself that humanity, and consequently this something, which had not been before. Hence, if correspondingly there were a person of a man which then and not before was the Word of God, then it would have to be granted that he assumed the person of a man: but [the assumption]{g:assumptum} is impossible.[^14]

### 09-c ¶1 · 150.25–151.16  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 150] Hic dicitur sicut⁷ in materia De Trinitate, quod multiplex est distinccio, ut alia essencialis, alia personalis, et alia racionis. Unde distinccio racionis non interimit mutuam predicacionem distinctorum; ut natura specifica hominis, licet a Petro racione distinguatur,⁸ cum ipsa sit communicabilis omni homini, Petrus **autem** non; et⁹ tamen Petrus est illa natura specifica, et conversim, **sed** non ex equo. Et natura vel essencia divina racione distinguitur a persona, cum ipsa communicatur tribus personis producentibus vel [p. 151] productis; nulla **autem** persona sic communicari poterit,¹ sicut nec natura divina adintra producere poterit¹ vel produci. Et tamen non obstante distinccione huius racionis omnis persona divina est natura divina et e contra. Correspondenter cum persona Verbi communicatur² humanitati et divinitati, patet quod est distinccio racionis inter personam illam et humanitatem; cum illa persona sit deitas, illa persona est eterna absolute necessaria equalis simpliciter Deo Patri. Illa quidem³ humanitas non potest esse deitas; **sed** est necessario temporalis, contingens, et minor simpliciter Deo Patre, licet sit personaliter illud, quod est deitas, absolute necessarium et equale simpliciter Deo Patri. Et patet illa distinccio non tollit predicacionem mutuam forme hominis et persone. Et patet quod assumptum in argumento est verum, **sed** negatur minor, cum nemo recte dicit oppositum.

ENGLISH: Here [the answer is]{g:dicitur}, as in the matter of the Trinity,[^1] that distinction is manifold: one essential, another personal, and another of reason. Hence a [distinction of reason]{g:distinccio-racionis} does not destroy the mutual [predication]{g:predicacio} of the things distinguished; as the [nature of the species]{g:natura-specifica} of man, although it is distinguished from Peter by reason, since it is communicable to every man, while Peter is not, yet Peter is that nature of the species, and conversely, but not on equal terms.[^2] And the divine [nature]{g:natura} or essence is distinguished by reason from a [person]{g:persona}, since it is communicated to the three persons, producing or [151] produced; but no person could be so communicated, just as the divine nature [within]{g:adintra} could not produce or be produced.[^3] And yet, notwithstanding the distinction of this reason, every divine person is the divine nature, and the converse. Correspondingly, since the person of the [Word]{g:verbum} is communicated to [the humanity]{g:humanitas} and to the [divinity]{g:divinitas}, it is clear that there is a distinction of reason between that person and the humanity; since that person is [the Godhead]{g:deitas}, that person is eternal, absolutely necessary, equal [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} to God the Father. That humanity indeed cannot be the Godhead; but it is necessarily temporal, contingent, and less without qualification than God the Father, although it is personally that which is the Godhead, absolutely necessary and equal without qualification to God the Father. And it is clear [that] that distinction does not take away the mutual predication of the form of man and the person. And it is clear that [the assumption]{g:assumptum} in the argument is true, but the minor is denied, since no one rightly says the opposite.[^4]

### 09-c ¶2 · 151.17–152.23  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 151] Unde si moderniores admisissent predicacionem secundum essenciam vel personam, licet non formalem, concedendo tamen cum Anselmo in De Incarnacione 7°. quod natura specifica est singulus⁴ hominum, sicut natura divina est singulum suorum suppositorum; et correspondenter quod persona cuiuscumque hominis est tam corpus quam anima, licet ille nature essencialiter distinguantur; et quod inter quamlibet personam hominis et illas naturas sit cum predicacione mutua distinccio racionis: tunc, inquam, saperet illis quod natura divina et natura humana sunt utraque persona Verbi, licet ille nature essencialiter distinguantur, et inter Verbum et utramque naturam sit distinccio racionis; et tunc saperet illis exemplum illud⁵ philosophicum fidei in simbolo Athanasii quod⁶ Augustinus tam crebro recitat; ‘sicut anima racionalis et caro unus est homo, ita Deus et homo unus est Christus.’ Diversio autem⁷ ab istis principiis facit nimirum variacionem maximam consequenter. [p. 152] Ulterius concedendum est de virtute sermonis¹ quod Verbum hominem assumpsit, sicut persepe dicunt Augustinus, Ieronimus, et Anselmus, et antiqui qui recte sapuerunt in logica. Cum enim homo sit concretum humanitatis, sicut Deus deitatis, patet quod potest indifferenter simpliciter supponere pro natura, vel personaliter pro persona; ut sicut Deus communicatur omnibus tribus personis divinis, quia deitas est sic communis, et tamen idem Deus incommunicatus, cum sit persona gignens; sic homo communicatur omni persone hominis,² cum sit forma specifica; et tamen homo non potest sic communicari, cum sit persona hominis; et correspondenter de aliis secundum supposicionem simplicem et personalem equivoce variatis. Unde quandocunque dicitur quod hominem Verbum assumpsit, intelligendum est simpliciter pro natura; ut humanitatem nedum specificam **sed** singularem assumpsit, quam Anselmus nominat Iesum Christum. Imo si diligenter attendimus quomodo homo quale-quid vel substancialem qualitatem primo significat, ut dicunt Aristoteles et Anselmus, prius significaret talis proposicio—hominem Verbum assumpsit—quod naturam humanam assumpserat⁴ quam quod personam hominis assumpserat.⁴ Ideo est talis proposicio ad sensum primarium concedenda et sensus alius est negandus.

ENGLISH: Hence, if [the more modern]{g:moderniores} had admitted [predication by essence]{g:predicacio-secundum-essenciam} or by person, though not formal [predication], granting nevertheless with Anselm, in *De incarnatione* chapter 7, that the nature of the species is each one of men, as the divine nature is each one of its [supposits]{g:suppositum};[^5] and correspondingly that the person of any man is both the body and the soul, although those natures are essentially distinguished; and that between any person of a man and those natures there is, with mutual predication, a distinction of reason: then, I say, it would savour to them that the divine nature and the human nature are each the person of the Word, although those natures are essentially distinguished, and between the Word and each nature there is a distinction of reason; and then that philosophical example of the faith in the creed of Athanasius, which Augustine so often recites, would savour to them: "as the rational soul and flesh is one man, so God and man is one Christ."[^6] Therefore the turning aside from these principles makes, no wonder, the greatest variation in what follows.[^7] [152] Further, it is to be granted, [in the proper sense of the words]{g:de-virtute-sermonis}, that the Word assumed man, as Augustine, Jerome and Anselm very often say, and the ancients who were rightly wise in logic.[^8] For since "man" is the [concrete]{g:concretum} [term] of the humanity, as "God" of the Godhead, it is clear that it can stand indifferently, with simple supposition, for the nature, or with personal supposition for the person; so that, as God is communicated to all three divine persons, because the Godhead is so common, and yet the same God is uncommunicated, since he is the begetting person; so man is communicated to every person of a man, since it is the form of the species; and yet man cannot be so communicated, since it is the person of a man; and correspondingly of other [terms] varied [equivocally]{g:equivoce} according to simple and personal supposition. Hence whenever it is said that the Word assumed man, it is to be understood with simple supposition, for the nature; so that he [assumed]{g:assumere} the humanity [not only]{g:nedum} of the species but singular, which Anselm names Jesus Christ.[^9] Indeed, if we attend diligently to how "man" primarily signifies a what-of-a-kind or substantial quality, as Aristotle and Anselm say, such a [proposition]{g:proposicio} (the Word assumed man) would signify that he had assumed the human nature before [it would signify] that he had assumed the person of a man.[^10] Therefore such a proposition is to be granted in its primary sense, and the other sense is to be denied.

### 09-c ¶3 · 152.24–153.9  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 152] Et si queritur⁵ diversitas inter illos terminos humanitas et homo quoad modum significandi cum, licet de se mutuo predicentur, refert tamen multibi⁶ ponere unum vel relicum: dicitur quod more aliorum terminorum quorum unus est concretus aliusque abstractus, concretum supponit indifferenter personaliter pro persona vel simpliciter pro natura; et hinc recipit formalem predicacionem cuiuscumque generis accidentis: abstractum **autem** abstractive tentum non suscipit formaliter nisi predicacionem per se et predicacionem secundum habitudinem abiciendo omnem formalem predicacionem [p. 153] suppositalem; ut humanitas non est qualis aut quanta accidenter, nec agit, nec patitur, sedet vel ditatur,¹ **sed** predicaciones respectivas recipit modo quo dictum est de universalibus; ut, si humanitas esset alba, cum humanitas sit esse hominem, tunc esse hominem foret formaliter esse album; et per consequens, ut arguit Aristoteles, homo tunc esset albedo. Et patet quod Verbum solum humanitatem vel naturam humanum assumpsit, non personam hominis; licet secundum alium sensum omnis humanitas sit persona.

ENGLISH: And if the difference is asked between those terms "humanity" and "man" as to the mode of signifying, since, although they are predicated of each other, yet it matters in many places to put the one or the other: the answer is that, in the manner of other terms of which one is concrete and the other abstract, the concrete stands indifferently, with personal supposition for the person or with simple supposition for the nature; and hence it receives formal predication of any kind of accident: but the abstract, taken [abstractively]{g:abstractive}, does not formally take up any predication but predication [of itself]{g:per-se} and predication by [respect]{g:habitudo},[^11] rejecting all formal [153] suppositional predication; so that the humanity is not qualified or [quantified]{g:quantus} by accident, nor acts, nor is acted on, sits or is enriched,[^12] but it receives [relational]{g:respectivus} predications in the way that was said of [universals]{g:universale}; so that, if the humanity were white, since the humanity is to be a man, then to be a man would be formally to be white; and consequently, as Aristotle argues, man would then be whiteness.[^13] And it is clear that the Word assumed only the humanity or human nature, not the person of a man; although in another sense every humanity is a person.

### 09-c ¶4 · 153.10–154.11  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 153] Verumptamen notandum est quod in materia de incarnacione doctores sancti utuntur isto termino ‘humanitas’ tanquam medio inter abstractum et concretum: ut Augustinus Ome. 19. super Iohannem dicit, quod ‘Maria humanitatem genuit que eciam in cruce passa est’; et Enchiridion 29°; ‘illam, inquit, creaturam quam virgo concepit et peperit, quamvis ad solam personam Filii pertinentem, tota² Trinitas fecit.’ Inseparabilia enim³ sunt opera Trinitatis, et indubie per ‘illam creaturam’ intelligit substanciam humanam, que est humanitas, cum paulo ante dicit utramque substanciam, divinam scilicet⁴ et humanam, esse Filium unicum Dei Patris. ENGLISH: Nevertheless it is to be noted that in the matter of the incarnation the holy doctors use this term "humanity" as a mean between abstract and concrete: as Augustine says in homily 19 on John, that "Mary bore the humanity, which also suffered on the cross"; and *Enchiridion* 29: "that [creature]{g:creatura}," he says, "which the virgin conceived and bore, although belonging to the person of the Son alone, the whole Trinity made." For the works of the Trinity are inseparable, and undoubtedly by "that creature" he understands the human substance, which is the humanity, since a little before he says that each substance, namely the divine and the human, is the only Son of God the Father.[^14] And so of many like [sayings], all of which, with those like them, are to be understood [to mean] that predicates of this kind are in the person of the Word of God [insofar as]{g:secundum-quod} [he is] man; that is, the humanity is the means or cause according to which predicates of this kind are in [him]; so that he would not have whence to be tempted, to be begotten, or meritoriously to suffer, and so of the other [communications of idioms]{g:communicacio-ydiomatum}, except by assuming the nature of man. Nor is this uneven [transumption]{g:transsumpcio} in that matter a wonder, since only the Word of God is man by accident, being person before man;[^15] since only that personality draws to itself a singular nature into its [hypostasis]{g:ypostasis}; but the other persons of like nature are [persons] with other singular forms. [154] Therefore thus, when I say this, "he suffered on the cross", many of the faithful are indifferent to understanding that passion [as to the Godhead]{g:secundum-deitatem} or as to the humanity; but many are more inclined to understand it as to that nature according to which he has [subsistence]{g:subsistencia} and person.[^16] Therefore [the saints]{g:sancti} reasonably ordained, especially in that matter, for the destruction of ambiguity of this kind and the making certain of the pertinent [teaching]{g:sentencia}, that the abstract should limit to the sense expounded. Whenever, therefore, it is said that the humanity of Christ took up predications of this kind, it is to be understood that Christ as to it so takes them up.[^17]

### 09-c ¶5 · 154.12–154.21  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 154] 3° principaliter arguitur deducendo⁶ multiplicia inconveniencia⁶ que secuntur. Nam primo videtur quod nedum idem est assumens et eciam assumptum, **sed** quod assumens est assumptum, creans est creatum, causa suum causatum; ymo si Dominus Iesus Christus sit assumptus, tunc et Deus assumitur, sicut humanitas⁷ humiliatur:⁷ et per consequens secundum duplicem naturam fuit duplex Christi assumpcio: ymo si nature tam distantes ydemptificari potuerunt, multo evidencius ypostases, que in natura magis conveniunt, tanta ydemptitate uniri potuerunt.⁸

ENGLISH: Third, [as a main head]{g:principaliter}, it is argued by deducing many [absurdities]{g:inconveniens} that follow.[^18] For first it seems that not only is the same thing assuming and also assumed, but that the assuming is the assumed, the creating is the [created]{g:creatus}, the cause its caused; indeed, if the Lord Jesus Christ is assumed, then God too is assumed, as the humanity is [humbled]{g:humiliacio}:[^19] and consequently, according to the twofold nature, there was a twofold [assumption]{g:assumpcio} of Christ: indeed, if natures so distant could be [identified]{g:ydemptificare}, much more evidently could hypostases, which agree more in nature, be united by so great an identity.

### 09-d ¶1 · 154.22–156.16  (10 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 154] Ad illud conceditur quod sicut natura divina est generans et generatum, et natura specifica humana cum sit homo pater et eius filius, et per consequens efficiens⁹ et effectum; sic Verbum commune¹⁰ ad hec tria deitas, corpus, et anima est simul assumens et assumptum; et racione communicacionis personalis, que distinguitur a duabus prioribus, in quibus est communicacio naturarum, suppositis¹¹ istis, conceditur quod assumens et assumptum, quamvis in Trinitate non generans, **sed** illud quod est gignens est genitum; et sic non communis humanitas, **sed** persona, gignit et gignitur. Ideo aliter [p. 155] loquendum est in communicacione personarum quam communicacione¹ naturarum eidem supposito. Verumptamen licet Verbum sit assumens et assumptum, tamen non assumitur, **sed** est natura que assumitur. Et conformiter conceditur quod causans est suum causatum, et² creans est suum creatum.² Nec ex illo sequitur quod idem secundum³ idem³ causat se, cum Verbum solum secundum naturam divinam causat se secundum naturam humanam. Unde non sequitur, quod Deus assumitur, quia predicatum assumpcionis limitat ad naturam creatam excludendo actum persone vel nature divine assumpto competere: et aliter non esset concedendum quod Deus assumitur, nisi quia vel⁴ natura vel persona divina assumitur, quorum⁵ utrumque cum sit impossibile docet negandum esse quod Deus assumitur.⁵ Et patet quod non est color concedendi quod natura divina creatur, assumitur, patitur, moritur etc. licet Christus, qui est natura divina, predicaciones tales recipiat; cum non secundum deitatem **sed** secundum humanitatem illas suscipiat. Et in isto sophismate⁶ cecatus est ille Clericus in fide devius,⁷ contra quem Anselmus fecit librum De Incarnacione; credidit enim si natura divina sit homo, tunc et omne, quod est natura divina, est homo, et⁸ per consequens confuse quelibet persona divina est quelibet, vel tres persone⁸ divine sunt tres homines; ignoravit enim naturam rerum communium. Licet **autem** negetur quod Deus assumitur, conceditur tamen quod Deus subicitur, humiliatur, et sic de similibus predicatis formalibus; generari quidem vel nasci non solum competit supposito **sed** nature. Ideo conceditur quod Christus, sicut est duplex essencia vel natura, sic habet duplex esse et duplicem nativitatem: et⁹ iuxta illos qui restringunt racionem filii ad suppositum vel personam, tunc tantum una est filiacio in Christo, sicut tantum inest sibi una personalitas, ita quod filiacio [p. 156] humana accidit¹ filiacioni divine, sicut singularitas hominis accidit personalitati² divine. Et sic filiacio humana in Christo simpliciter³ non est filiacio; cum, multiplicata filiacione que est racio filii, multiplicarentur et filii; **sed** est genitura temporalis. Aliis **autem** plus⁴ placet, cum quibus et michi, quod est dare in Christo⁵ duplicem filiacionem, sicut et duplicem nativitatem, non **autem** duplicem personalitatem, cuius racio est nasci, sicut et mori competit persone Christi⁶ racione humanitatis et non per se⁶ primo illi nature. Ergo⁷ per idem vel evidencius filiacio competit supposito primo racione nature, et non primo illi nature.⁷ Et hoc sentenciant moderni, qui dicunt, quod natura assumpta non est filius, **sed** per eius dimissionem⁸ Maria potuit⁹ duos filios habuisse sine actu gignicionis duplici, vel sine hoc quod illa natura fuit filius Marie pro instanti aliquo vite sue, cum senex inciperet esse filius hominis, quando inciperet dimitti.

ENGLISH: To that it is granted that, as the divine [nature]{g:natura} is generating and generated, and the human [nature of the species]{g:natura-specifica}, since it is a man [who is] father and his son, is consequently efficient and effect; so the [Word]{g:verbum}, common to these three, [the Godhead]{g:deitas}, body and soul, is at once assuming and assumed; and by reason of the personal communication, which is distinguished from the two prior ones, in which there is communication of natures to [supposits]{g:suppositum},[^1] it is granted that the assuming [is] the assumed, although in the Trinity [it is] not [granted that] the generating [is the generated], but that that which is begetting is begotten; and so not the common [humanity]{g:humanitas} but the [person]{g:persona} begets and is begotten.[^2] Therefore one must [155] speak otherwise in the communication of persons than in the communication of natures to the same supposit. Nevertheless, although the Word is the assuming and the assumed, yet he is not assumed, but it is a nature that is assumed.[^3] And conformably it is granted that the causing is its caused, and the creating is its [created]{g:creatus}. Nor does it follow from that that the same thing causes itself according to the same, since the Word causes himself as to the human nature only as to the divine nature. Hence it does not follow that God is assumed, because the predicate of [assumption]{g:assumpcio} limits to the created nature, excluding that an act of the divine person or nature belongs to what is assumed: and otherwise "God is assumed" would not be to be granted except because either the divine nature or the divine person is assumed; and since each of these is impossible, it teaches that "God is assumed" must be denied.[^4] And it is clear that there is no [colour]{g:color} for granting that the divine nature is created, is assumed, suffers, dies, etc., although Christ, who is the divine nature, receives such predications; since he takes them up not [as to the Godhead]{g:secundum-deitatem} but as to the humanity.[^5] And in this [sophism]{g:sophisma} that cleric astray in the faith was blinded, against whom Anselm made the book *De incarnatione*; for he believed that if the divine nature is man, then everything too that is the divine nature is man, and consequently, [indistinctly]{g:confuse}, any divine person is any [other], or the three divine persons are three men; for he was ignorant of the nature of [common things]{g:res-communes}.[^6] And although it is denied that God is assumed, it is nevertheless granted that God is subjected, is [humbled]{g:humiliacio}, and so of like formal predicates; to be generated, indeed, or to be born belongs not only to the supposit but to the nature. Therefore it is granted that Christ, as he is a twofold essence or nature, so has a twofold [being]{g:esse} and a twofold [nativity]{g:nativitas}: and according to those who restrict the account of a son to the supposit or person, there is then only one [sonship]{g:filiacio} in Christ, as only one [personality]{g:personalitas} is in him, so that the human [156] sonship befalls the divine sonship, as the singularity of a man befalls the divine personality. And so the human sonship in Christ is not, [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}, a sonship; since, if sonship, which is the account of a son, were multiplied, sons too would be multiplied; but it is a temporal begetting. But it pleases others more, and with them me too, that [one may posit]{g:est-dare} in Christ a twofold sonship, as also a twofold nativity, but not a twofold personality; the account of which [sonship] is to be born, as also to die belongs to the person of Christ by reason of the humanity, and not [of itself]{g:per-se} first to that nature.[^7] Therefore, by the same token or more evidently, sonship belongs to the supposit first by reason of the nature, and not first to that nature.[^8] And this [the moderns]{g:moderni} [pronounce]{g:sentenciare}, who say that the assumed nature is not a son, but that by [its laying-aside]{g:dimissio} Mary could have had two sons without a twofold act of begetting, or without that nature's having been the son of Mary for any instant of its life, since an old man would begin to be the son of a man when it began to be laid aside.[^9]

### 09-d ¶2 · 156.17–157.11  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 156] Licet **autem** talia ludicria sint impossibilia, tamen videtur probabile quod Christus racione humanitatis habeat filiacionem humanam, sicut racione eiusdem nature habet nativitatem¹⁰ temporalem; potissime cum filiacio sit realis relacio persone¹¹ in genere sicut alia accidencia Christo humanitus dependent a parente temporali: non sic **autem** personalitas absoluta, cum persona dicitur ethimologice per se una: illa ergo non dependet relative ab extremo, nec ab actu temporali, ut nativitas, concepcio, sive gignicio. Unde vel oportet dici¹² quod filiacio eterna accidentaliter sit filiacio hominis, vel quod filiacio hominis non sit filiacio. Secundum non est dandum cum iste terminus hominis vel humanus non distrahit. Primum **autem** ex hoc videtur deficere quod filiacio eterna non potest fieri filiacio temporalis. Christus ergo habet in se tot filiaciones quot parentes, sicut Petrus in se tot proporciones duplas et similitudines [p. 157] quot rebus ipse¹ duplus vel similis est. Et sic¹ cum Ioseph fuit pater eius, ut patet² testimonio beate virginis Luce 2°; ‘ecce pater tuus et ego dolentes querebamus te;’ patet quod Christus habuit ad istum parentem vel nutricium filiacionem legitimam putativam; non **autem** fuit filius adoptivus, cum non potuit esse filius perdicionis, et illa potencia vel imperfeccio presupponitur ad filium adoptivum. Verumptamen ‘predestinatus est Filius Dei,’ ut dicit Apostolus ad Rom. 1°. illa³ quidem creatura ordinata est eternaliter esse Dei Filius naturalis ut³ sepe dicit Augustinus, et recitat eum Magister 3°⁴ Sentenciarum Dist. 6ᵃ.

ENGLISH: But although such playthings are impossible, yet it seems probable that Christ by reason of the humanity has a human sonship, as by reason of the same nature he has a temporal nativity; most of all since sonship is a real relation of the person in its category, as the other accidents depend in Christ [humanly]{g:humanitus} on a temporal parent: but not so the absolute personality, since a person is called, by etymology, "one by itself": that, therefore, does not depend relatively on a term, nor on a temporal act, such as nativity, conception or begetting.[^10] Hence it must be said either that the eternal sonship is accidentally the sonship of a man, or that the sonship of a man is not a sonship. The second is not to be posited, since this term "of a man" or "human" is not [a diminishing qualifier]{g:distrahens}. And the first seems to fail from this, that the eternal sonship cannot become a temporal sonship. Christ, therefore, has in himself as many sonships as parents, as Peter has in himself as many double proportions and likenesses [157] as there are things to which he is double or like. And so, since Joseph was his father, as is clear from the testimony of the blessed virgin, Luke 2[:48], "behold thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing", it is clear that Christ had toward this parent or foster-father a lawful reputed sonship; but he was not an adoptive son, since he could not be a son of perdition, and that [potency]{g:potencia} or imperfection is presupposed to an adoptive son.[^11] Nevertheless, "he was predestinated the Son of God", as the Apostle says, Romans 1[:4]: that [creature]{g:creatura}, indeed, was ordained eternally to be the natural Son of God, as Augustine often says, and [the Master]{g:magister} recites him in book 3 of the *Sentences*, distinction 6.[^12]

### 09-d ¶5 · 158.10–158.23  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 158] Quoad ultimum de ydemptitate constat michi quod numerose evidencie volant hodie⁵ ad probandum per locum a simili quod quidlibet⁶ potest ydemptificari cuilibet, si natura⁷ prope nichil potest fieri⁷ Deus: exinde enim concluditur quod Deus quamlibet naturam creatam potest assumere, et sic de multis inaniter geminatis. Utrum **autem** ydemptitas personalis naturarum disparium in Christo sit maior quam ydemptitas personarum⁸ in eadem natura simplici, est difficultas. Certum tamen est quod iste ydemptitates sunt disparium racionum et ydemptitas Trinitatis in prima essencia, cum presupponitur ad omnem aliam, est maior quam reliqua, licet non in proporcione racionali.⁹ Quoad instancias factas per locum a simili, patet ex dictis quod similitudo non est probabilis.

ENGLISH: As to the last, about identity, it is evident to me that numerous [evidences]{g:evidencia} fly about today to prove, by the topic from likeness, that anything whatever can be [identified]{g:ydemptificare} with anything whatever, if a nature next to nothing can become God: for thence it is [inferred]{g:concludere} that God can assume any created nature whatever, and so of many things idly doubled.[^18] But whether the personal identity of unlike natures in Christ is greater than the identity of persons in the same simple nature is a difficulty. Yet it is certain that these identities are of unlike kinds, and the identity of the Trinity in the first essence, since it is presupposed to every other, is greater than the other, though not in a natural proportion.[^19] As to the [objections]{g:instancia} made by the topic from likeness, it is clear from what has been said that the likeness is not probable.

### 09-e ¶1 · 158.24–160.9  (6 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 158] **Sed** dimissis arguciis queritur communiter racio diversitatis, quam est difficile placide assignare, cum sit de fundamento nature; ut quare Deus agens libere contradictorie adextra potuit illud tempus eternum, et non aliud nisi eius partem producere et sic de similibus. Prima ergo pars questionum huiusmodi vel non habet causam,¹¹ vel consequitur essencialiter ad Deum, sicut passio ad subiectum. Causa **autem** negative, que est secunda pars questionis, absolute necessaria est per se divina essencia. Cum enim¹² [p. 159] non potest fieri quod due persone fiant eadem persona in numero, aut¹ due nature eadem natura in numero,¹ vel quod Verbum aliam naturam assumpserat;² patet quod solum Deus est causa huius. Quod si dicatur adversarium eque faciliter sustinere oppositum sicut illud a nobis fingitur, dicitur quod tales contenciones sophistice secundum Apostolum sunt nimis inutiles, et ideo debet theologus ipsas abicere³ tenendo illud quod fides Christiana, scriptura sacra, et sanctorum doctorum consors sentencia ab antiquo nos edocet. Et in omni secta sunt multa credita⁴ que nec sufficiunt nec expedit protervo deducere. In⁵ hoc tamen⁵ excedit secta Christiana quascunque alias, quod maxime archana sue fidei copiose probari possunt, miracula explanari in naturali lumine evidenciis et exemplis philosophicis, necnon omnes impugnatorum argucie evidenter tolli eciam ex naturalibus et propriis eorum principiis. Ut triplex exemplum⁶ ponitur quod fidelis poterit manuduci ad credendum incarnacionis misterium. Primum quomodo duo accidencia diversorum generum, quorum nullum secundum genus potest esse reliquum, sunt idem subiecto ut quantitas et qualitas; non quod sint⁷ nature, res, sive substancie⁷ que possunt a subiecto et⁸ a se invicem separari, **sed** quod eidem subiecto inest esse quantum et quale; sicut eidem supposito Verbi inest esse tam Deum quam hominem. 2ᵐ exemplum propius est, quod eidem⁹ essencie corporee inest posse igniri, quod est materia prima, et accidentaliter esse ignem quod est forma; et sic materia et forma tam dispares sunt eadem essencia, que¹⁰ adventu forme sit aliud quam prefuit; sicut humanitas et deitas sunt eadem persona,¹⁰ que est aliud quam prefuit. 3ᵐ exemplum propinquissimum creatorum est de unione ypostatica corporis humani ad animam. Nam eadem persona est tam corpus [p. 160] quam anima, aliud propter corpus et aliud propter spiritum, **sed** non alius. Proporcionaliter est de Christo, qui est duarum naturarum utraque. Nam sicut eadem persona hominis nunc est tantum spiritus, nunc cum hoc aliud quia corpus; sic et Christus necessario est deitas **sed** contingenter humanitas. Raciones **autem** alias diversitatis et alias conveniencias scit peritus satis elicere et raciones oppositas plane dissolvere.

ENGLISH: But, [quibbles]{g:argucie} laid aside, the reason of the difference is commonly asked, which it is difficult to assign calmly, since it belongs to the foundation of nature: as, why God, acting [freely, as between contradictories]{g:libere-contradictorie}, [outward]{g:adextra}, could produce that eternal time, and no other but its part, and so of like things.[^1] The first part of questions of this kind, therefore, either has no cause, or follows essentially upon God, as an [attribute]{g:passio} upon its [subject]{g:subiectum}. And the cause of the negative, which is the second part of the question, is, absolutely necessary, the divine [essence]{g:essencia} of itself.[^2] For since [159] it cannot be that two [persons]{g:persona} become the same person in number, or two [natures]{g:natura} the same nature in number, or that [the Word]{g:verbum} had assumed another nature, it is clear that God alone is the cause of this. And if it is said that an adversary sustains the opposite as easily as that [position] is devised by us, the answer is that such [sophistical]{g:sophistice} contentions, according to the Apostle, are [exceedingly]{g:nimis} unprofitable,[^3] and therefore the theologian ought to reject them, holding that which the Christian faith, holy scripture and the concordant [teaching]{g:sentencia} of the holy doctors teach us from of old. And in every [sect]{g:secta} there are many things believed which neither suffice, nor is it expedient, to be deduced for an [obstinate]{g:protervus} man.[^4] Yet in this the Christian sect exceeds all others whatever, that the secrets of its faith can most of all be copiously proved, its miracles explained in natural light by evidences and philosophical examples, and also all the quibbles of its assailants evidently removed, even from their own natural and proper principles. So a threefold example is set down by which a faithful man could be led by the hand to believing the [mystery]{g:misterium} of the incarnation.[^5] The first: how two [accidents]{g:accidens} of different genera, of which neither can, as to its genus, be the other, are the same in subject, as quantity and quality; not that they are natures, things or substances that can be separated from the subject and from each other, but that to be [quantified]{g:quantus} and qualified is in the same subject; as to be both God and man is in the same [supposit]{g:suppositum} of the Word.[^6] The second example is nearer: that in the same bodily essence there is being able to be set on fire, which is [prime matter]{g:materia-prima}, and accidentally being fire, which is form; and so matter and form, so unlike, are the same essence, which at the coming of the form is other than it was before; as [the humanity]{g:humanitas} and [the Godhead]{g:deitas} are the same person, which is other than it was before.[^7] The third example, the nearest of created things, is of the hypostatic [union]{g:unio} of the human body to the soul. For the same person is both body [160] and soul, one thing by reason of the body and [another thing]{g:aliud} by reason of the spirit, but not another one.[^8] Proportionally it is with Christ, who is each of two natures. For as the same person of a man is now only spirit, now with this something else, because body, so Christ too is necessarily the Godhead but contingently the humanity. And other reasons of difference and other [agreements]{g:conveniencia} the skilled man knows well enough how to draw out, and to dissolve plainly the opposite arguments.
