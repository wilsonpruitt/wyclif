### 08-c ¶2 · 174.12–176.4  (6 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 174] Item, non subiacet humane potestati **sed** solum divine constituere quemquam summum pontificem, igitur non debet supponi in Petro tanta presumpcio, ut eligat hominem de quo non fuit sibi ad hoc facta revelacio in summum pontificem et immediatum Christi vicarium super terram, specialiter cum super ista fatuitate, non relinquitur nobis scriptum autenticum, **sed** in eleccione Mathie reliquit hoc divine iudicio, ut patet Actuum primo; quanto plus in eleccione maioris. Quis, inquam, crederet quod Petrus diffiniret beatum Clementem sequi Christum similius quam Evangelistam, qui diu vixit post Petrum, vel beatum Evangelistam debere Clementi ut maiori? vel capiti necessario regulandi obedienciam et reverenciam, qualem pape iam exigunt a subiectis. vel quod Christus aut alius apostolus talem maioritatem instituit, vel policiam in sua ecclesia approbavit? Voluit enim omnes apostolos esse socios sibi invicem obedientes et servientes sine dominio vel magisterio alicuius capitanei preter Christum. Et ex istis infirmatur tercia ficticia quod per viam eleccionis et institucionis humane ponitur [p. 175] quis Romanus et summus pontifex. Et quamvis ista sentencia sit maxime propinqua facto, tamen nimis elongatur a religione fideli et stabili fundamento. Cum enim aliquem stat esse Romanum pontificem et non papam et econtra, ut patet in Christo et Petro, patet quod oportet istas duas elecciones esse racionis disparis et nature ordine separatas. Stat enim aliquem esse Romanum pontificem et pessimum Antichristum, sicut econtra stat aliquem esse summum pontificem, cum hoc quod sit loco illi indifferens, ut patet ex dictis. Unde dominus Ardmacanus libro VII, cap. XVIII De Questionibus Armenorum dicit quod sunt duo pontificatus naturaliter differentes, et oportet quod sint eciam apud Deum due elecciones. **Sed** videtur michi quod eleccio qua quis eligeretur in papam vel summum pontificem non subiacet potestati vel eleccioni humane. Nam idem est hominem eligere in papam vel summum pontificem et eligere ipsum in apostolicum maxime propinque per graciam sequentem Christi vestigia; **sed** hoc non subiacet ordinacioni vel eleccioni humane, igitur nec aliud sive idem; sic enim eligeret quis hominem in predestinatum vel quocumque aliter graciosum. Et confirmatur ex factis et verbis apostolorum Act. I in eleccione Mathie apostoli, quod est minus quam eligere summum pontificem, ubi ipsi non eligunt **sed** rogant Deum; ut ostendat ipse quem elegerit. Sicut igitur electores non eligunt summum pontificem in eligendo alterum Scarioth vel Antichristum; sic casualiter eligendo eum quem Deus elegerit non [p. 176] eligunt talem in summum pontificem, **sed** in fungentem tali officio, quod non dicit excellenciam gracie vel alia carismata, que sunt propria dona Dei.

### 08-d ¶1 · 176.5–178.16  (6 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 176] Unde post dotacionem sunt elecciones hominum introducte et cadunt super officio, dignitate vel dominacione humanitus instituta et non super carismata Deo propria. Ut communitas eligit hominem in pontificem cesareum vel nuncupative dictum, et sic cadit eleccio humana super episcopum, ut dicit communiter episcopum secundum reputacionem vel approbacionem humanam, quod fit sepius cum errore, vel secundum autorizacionem divinam, quod semper sit iuste, unde quando contingit quod eleccio humana concordat cum eleccione divina, tunc homo eligit verum episcopum et summum pontificem, sicut sepe contigit de Romanis pontificibus veris papis. Et quando discordat ab eleccione divina, tunc eligit false reputatum summum pontificem et non summum pontificem, licet errando illud crediderit. Unde cum iste terminus papa et summum dicunt summitatem in moribus, quam mirabilitatem solius Dei est facere, videtur quod solus Deus eligit hominem in papam vel summum pontificem, quia non videtur fundabile quod isti termini extendantur ad false nuncupatum huius, quia sic dyabolus foret summus pontifex. Episcopus **autem** secundum apostolum est nomen officii et sic aliud est eligere papam et aliud eligere hominem in papam, quia secunda proposicio implicat quod cadat eleccio in papatum, **sed** prima proposicio verificaretur de eleccione persone ad quodcumque officium vel preposituram, ut populus eligit [p. 177] papam, quando instituit vel acceptat personam ad officium vel dignitatem humanam, quam personam Deus eligit ad preeminenciam sanctitatis, unde, cum scriptura Deutro. XXVI, 17 signanter dicit: Dominum elegisti hodie, patet quod quedam eleccio est institucio vel quedam faccio, et quedam eleccio est acceptacio; et per consequens oportet preter eleccionem instituencium esse eleccionem equivocam, que sit approbacio subditorum. Sic enim populus eligit dominum, quando effectualiter acceptat, quod ille sit dominus suus et spondet fideliter se esse sibi ut domino serviturum. Unde quando successione hereditaria vadit dominium, adhuc est alia eleccio subditorum; ad quod oportet movere vitam et mores in spirituali preposito, et aliter est intrusoris monstruosa extorsio. Et patet quod debet esse pape duplex eleccio, una humana in Romanum episcopum et alia pure divina in papam vel summum pontificem; qua deficiente Romanus pontifex non est papa **sed** unus alius mundo absconditus. **Sed** cum tres ficciones non sufficiunt, remanet dubium, quomodo dicta duo nomina sunt connexa. In qua materia credendum est cronicis. Narrat enim Cestrensis libro IV, cap. XXXVI, quomodo excellencia Romani imperii adinvenit papatum sui pontificis super alios: Nycena, inquit, synodus hoc contulit privilegium Romano pontifici, ut sicut Augustus pre ceteris regibus, ita Romanus pre ceteris pontifex haberetur episcopis et papa velut principalis pater vocaretur. Origo **autem** istius nominis et excellencie fuit in dotacione ecclesie, [p. 178] ut specificat Decretum XLVI distinccione Constantinus. Ideo superfluum et labor inutilis foret fingere mendacia, quod sic oportuit esse ex racionibus ad hoc urgentibus vel scriptura. Unde quia ista videtur levis fundacio et ecclesia Romana tunc sibi cadere a papatu, narrat idem Cestrensis libro V cap. X, quomodo Bonifacius IV impetravit a Foca cesare, ut ecclesia beati Petri Rome Caput esset ecclesiarum, quia caput ecclesie Constantinopolitane se scripsit papam. Quotlibet tales fideles cronice et decreta testantur primatum Romani pontificis fuisse humanitus adinventum. Nam si papa fieret vagus ut olim et nulli loco affixus **sed** in mari mortuo vel deserto, et sic post eum papa secundus, et sic ad finem viacionis ecclesie, patet quod non obstante isto vere foret papa.

ENGLISH: Hence after the [endowment]{g:dotacio} the elections of men were brought in, and they fall upon an office, dignity or [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio} humanly instituted, and not upon the charismata proper to God. So a community elects a man as caesarean pontiff, or pontiff so called in name, and in this way human election falls upon a bishop, as one commonly speaks of a bishop either according to human reputation or approval, which is often done with error, or according to divine [authorization]{g:autorizare}, which is always done justly;[^1] hence when it happens that human election agrees with divine election, then a man elects a true bishop and highest pontiff, as has often happened with Roman pontiffs who were true popes. And when it is at odds with divine election, then it elects one falsely reputed highest pontiff and not a highest pontiff, though in erring it believed him to be so. Hence, since the terms "pope" and "highest" signify highness in morals, a marvel which God alone can work, it seems that God alone elects a man pope or highest pontiff, since it does not seem to be groundable that these terms be extended to one falsely so named; for in that way the devil would be highest pontiff.[^2] Now "bishop", according to the apostle, is the name of an office;[^3] and so it is one thing to elect a pope and another to elect a man pope, since the second proposition implies that the election falls upon the papacy, but the first proposition would be verified of the election of a person to any office or [office of superior]{g:prepositura} whatever, as the people elect [177] a pope when they institute or accept a person for an office or human dignity, which person God elects to [preeminence]{g:preeminencia} of holiness.[^4] Hence, since scripture, Deut. 26:17, says pointedly, "Thou hast chosen the Lord this day", it is clear that one kind of election is an institution or a kind of making, and another kind of election is an acceptance; and consequently, besides the election of those who institute, there must be an equivocal election, which is the approval of the subjects. For in this way a people elects a lord, when it effectually accepts that he be its lord and pledges faithfully to serve him as its lord. Hence when [lordship]{g:dominium} passes by hereditary succession, there is still another election, that of the subjects; and to this the life and morals in a spiritual [superior]{g:prepositi} must move [them], and otherwise it is the monstrous extortion of an intruder.[^5] And it is clear that there must be a twofold election of the pope, one human, to Roman bishop, and the other purely divine, to pope or highest pontiff; and if this is lacking, the Roman pontiff is not pope, but some other man, hidden from the world, is.[^6] But since three [fictions]{g:ficcio} do not suffice, a doubt remains how the two said names are connected. In this matter the chronicles are to be believed. For the Chester [chronicler] relates in book 4, chapter 36, how the excellence of the Roman empire devised the papacy of its pontiff over others: "The synod of Nicaea," he says, "conferred this [privilege]{g:privilegium} on the Roman pontiff, that as Augustus [was held] before the other kings, so the Roman pontiff should be held before the other bishops, and be called pope, as being the principal father."[^7] And the origin of this name and excellence was in the endowment of the church, [178] as the Decretum specifies, distinction 96, *Constantinus*.[^8] Therefore it would be superfluous and useless labour to feign lies, that it had to be so from reasons urging it or from scripture. Hence, since this seemed a light foundation, and the Roman church then [seemed] to be falling from the papacy, the same Chester [chronicler] relates in book 5, chapter 10, how "Boniface IV [obtained]{g:impetrare} from Caesar Phocas that the church of blessed Peter at Rome should be Head of the churches, since the head of the church of Constantinople wrote himself pope."[^9] Any number of such faithful chronicles and decrees testify that the [primacy]{g:primatus} of the Roman pontiff was humanly devised. For if the pope became a wanderer, as of old, and fixed to no place, but on the Dead Sea or in the desert, and so the second pope after him, and so to the end of the church's [wayfaring]{g:viacio}, it is clear that notwithstanding this he would truly be pope.

### 08-d ¶2 · 178.17–179.7  (1 sed/autem)

Et foret inanis ficticia quod, ubicumque fuerit talis electus humanis ritibus, consequitur quod sit Roma; non enim est racio, quare pocius fieret ibi Roma quam Anthiochia, Jerusalem vel Constantinopolis. Tales enim locorum illorum metropolitani dixerunt se esse papas propter disparem racionem: Anthiochia, quia ibi vocati sunt primo discipuli christiani, ut patet Act. XI, et beatus Petrus ibi resedit episcopus multis annis, et tercio quia prius recepit fidem Christi quam Roma. Episcopus **autem** Jerosolimitanus [p. 179] vendicat papatum propter Christum, propter primitatem fidei, propter mortem Jacobi, qui ibi primo secutus est mortem Domini. Constantinopolis vero propter nomen cesaris, qui dotando tantum decoravit ecclesiam. Et Roma, quia ibi sancti apostoli sunt occisi et realiter, quia impetrarunt a cesare hoc privilegium.

ENGLISH: Therefore there must be some ground of [being] pope prior to any designation of this place whatever. Therefore the lord of Armagh says truly, in book 7, chapter 21 *Of the Questions of the Armenians*: "That Peter was Christ's vicar for this reason, that he was more like him in [virtue]{g:virtus}."[^10] And it would be an empty [fiction]{g:ficticia} that wherever such a man has been elected by human rites, it follows that it is Rome; for there is no reason why Rome should come to be there rather than Antioch, Jerusalem or Constantinople. For the metropolitans of those places said that they were popes, each for a different reason:[^11] Antioch, because there the disciples were first called Christians, as is clear from Acts 11, and blessed Peter sat there as bishop for many years, and third because it received the faith of Christ before Rome. And the bishop of Jerusalem [179] claims the papacy on account of Christ, on account of the [firstness]{g:primitas} of the faith, on account of the death of James, who there first followed the death of the Lord.[^12] And Constantinople on account of the name of Caesar, who by endowing so greatly adorned the [church]{g:ecclesia}. And Rome, because the holy apostles were slain there, and, in reality, because they obtained this privilege from Caesar.

### 08-d ¶3 · 179.8–180.20  (6 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 179] Et revera omnes et singulas istarum causarum propter impertinenciam ad papatum est turpe fingere, **sed** turpissimum quod papa aliquis impetrat privilegium illud concedere vel donare, quam potest concedere, quod datus episcopus vivat Christo similius. **Sed** sicut sophista vel maniacus concedit quod Deus regnat in angelis et tamen non sequitur propter suum concedere sive imperium plus vel minus, ita est de concessione privilegii pape vel cesaris, ut quis sequatur Christum similius in virtute. **Sed** cum locus sit huic dignitati impertinens, oportet rogare trinitatem obnixius, implorare suffragium ecclesie utriusque humilius et attendere devote bonis operibus, ut Deus eligat quem voluerit, et ostendat oraculis vel sanctis operibus, que precedant eleccionem humanam, quia eleccio humana est istis impertinens. Concedo igitur cum domino Ardmacano historiam de beato Petro quomodo timore cesaris proposuit sub colore Christi consilii a Roma aufugere, et quomodo obvianti Christo in porta civitatis et querenti: Domine, quo vadis, responsum est ab eo venio Romam iterum crucifigi. Per quod Petrus expectans concepit, quod dominus crucifigendus fuit ibi in ipso, quia iuxta rectam logicam Christi Matth. XXV [p. 180] Christo faciunt vel non faciunt homines quicquid fecerint vel non fecerint membris suis, et sic nedum fuit ex divina ordinacione, quod Petrus et Paulus inibi morerentur, **sed** omne quod fit in mundo fit ex ordinacione divina accessoria vel directa. Accessoria in viciosis operibus, de quibus bonum ordinat, et directa in virtuosis operibus vel operibus naturalibus. Causam **autem** quare Christus ordinavit gloriosos apostolos ibi pati martyrium est exemplar et speculum aliis martyribus, quod ordinavit postmodum ibi pati, cum aliis bonis exemplum sequentibus. **Sed** credo quod nullus fidelis tantum insaniet, quod fingat dominum ordinasse ibi illorum martyrium, ut doceat fore papam quemcumque Romanum episcopum successurum, quia (ut dictum est) nomen tale inolevit ex institucione humana et sepe opinioni hominum in istis falsitas est substrata; locus enim ille est impertinens, ut eius episcopus sit precipuus sequax Christi.

ENGLISH: And in truth it is shameful to feign each and every one of these causes, for their irrelevance to the papacy; but most shameful [to feign] that some pope obtains [the power] to grant or bestow that privilege, which he can grant [only as he could grant] that a given bishop should live more like Christ.[^13] But as a [sophist]{g:sophista} or a madman grants that God reigns in the angels, and yet nothing more or less follows on account of his granting or command, so it is with the [grant]{g:concessio} of a privilege by pope or Caesar that someone should follow Christ more closely in virtue.[^14] But since the place is irrelevant to this dignity, one must pray the Trinity more earnestly, implore more humbly the [suffrage]{g:suffragia} of the church of both [states], and attend devoutly to good works, that God may choose whom he will and show [him] by oracles or holy works which precede the human election, since human election is irrelevant to these.[^15] I grant, therefore, with the lord of Armagh, the story of blessed Peter: how for fear of Caesar he proposed, under [colour]{g:color} of Christ's counsel, to flee from Rome, and how, when he met Christ at the gate of the city and asked, "Lord, whither goest thou?", the answer was given by him: "I come to Rome to be crucified again."[^16] By which Peter, considering, conceived that the Lord was to be crucified there in himself; for according to Christ's right logic, Matt. 25, [180] men do or do not do to Christ whatever they have done or not done to his [members]{g:membrum}.[^17] And so not only was it from divine [ordinance]{g:ordinacio} that Peter and Paul died there, but everything that is done in the world is done by divine ordinance, accessory or direct: accessory in vicious works, from which he ordains good, and direct in virtuous works or natural works.[^18] And the cause why Christ ordained the glorious apostles to suffer martyrdom there is [that it is] an exemplar and mirror to other martyrs, whom he ordained afterwards to suffer there, with other good men following the example.[^19] But I believe that no believer will be so mad as to feign that the Lord ordained their martyrdom there in order to teach that whatever Roman bishop should succeed would be pope; for (as has been said) such a name grew up from human institution, and falsehood often underlies men's [opinion]{g:opinio} in these matters; for that place is irrelevant to its bishop's being a principal follower of Christ.[^20]

### 08-d ¶4 · 180.21–180.32  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 180] Unde videtur non esse de substancia fidei, quod Clemens, Linus, Cletus vel alius post Petrum Romanus episcopus fuit papa, intelligendo papam, ut dicit primitatem alienum excludentem. Intelligendo **autem** papam communiter, modo quo Ambrosius, Augustinus et ceteri sancti episcopi erant pape, manifestum est quod Paulus, Clemens et ceteri sancti martyres et confessores Romani episcopi erant pape simul cum aliis, quod si aliquis eorum in regimine ovium Christi omnes conversantes secum excesserat, tunc fuit iste papa ad sensum superius limitatum.

ENGLISH: Hence it seems not to be of the substance of faith that Clement, Linus, Cletus or another Roman bishop after Peter was pope, understanding "pope" as it signifies a firstness that excludes another. But understanding "pope" commonly, in the way in which Ambrose, Augustine and the other holy bishops were popes, it is manifest that Paul, Clement and the other holy martyrs and confessors who were Roman bishops were popes together with others;[^21] and if any of them had excelled all who lived with him in the rule of Christ's sheep, then he was pope in the sense limited above.[^22]

### 08-e ¶1 · 180.33–181.15  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 180] Abusio **autem** termini infundabilis videtur michi vocare quemcumque pseudopapam nudo nomine [p. 181] papam vel caput ecclesie. Unde gentilitas et primatus affeccio docuerunt quod quicumque Romanus pontifex censeatur papa et gerat primatum huiusmodi super singulos alios sacerdotes; unde patet quod Deus non ordinasset apostolorum martyrium Rome propter hunc finem, nisi verus papatus regulariter sequeretur. **Sed** frequenter post dotacionem ecclesie separatus est papatus a Romano pontifice, igitur Deus non intenderat per illud martyrium illum finem; tunc enim Deus frustratus a suo proposito improvide ordinasset et negligenter omisisset in suo testamento tam necessarium primatum ad regimen sue ecclesie. Ideo certum est quod invencio humana fabricavit institucionem istam de papa.

ENGLISH: And it seems to me an ungroundable abuse of the term to call any pseudo-pope, by the bare name, [181] [pope]{g:papa} or [head]{g:caput} of the [church]{g:ecclesia}.[^1] Hence it was [paganism]{g:gentilitas} and the affection for [primacy]{g:primatus} that taught that whoever is [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex} is reckoned pope and holds a primacy of this kind over every other [priest]{g:sacerdos};[^2] hence it is clear that God would not have ordained the martyrdom of the apostles at Rome for this end unless true papacy followed [as a rule]{g:regulariter}. Now often, after the [endowment]{g:dotacio} of the church, the papacy has been separated from the Roman pontiff; therefore God had not intended that end by that martyrdom; for then God, frustrated of his purpose, would have ordained improvidently, and negligently omitted from his testament a primacy so necessary to the rule of his church. Therefore it is certain that human invention fabricated this institution of the pope.

### 08-e ¶2 · 181.16–181.32  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 181] In cronicis **autem** Cestrensis narravi alias quomodo multi Romani pontifices de papatu defecerant, et repeto illa que scripta sunt in cronica aurea Radulphi de Dyceto decani sancti Pauli, cui Anglici darent fidem. Narrat enim libro I cap. II, quomodo anno domini CCCCXX presidebat in Roma Boni facius papa et contra hunc ordinato Eulalio, ac pro hoc dissidente ecclesia ambo iussu Honorii Augusti urbe egrediuntur, et sic reprobato Eulalio iussu Augusti Bonifacius, quia prior ordinatus fuerat, sedi apostolice restituitur. Nondum enim tunc tantum sopitum fuit dominium Romani imperii nec ad tantum invaluit usurpacio Romani pontificis quod fuit reputatum hereticum, quod domini temporales possunt auferre temporalia, ymo dignitates atque officia ab ecclesia delinquente.

ENGLISH: And in the chronicles of the Chester [chronicler] I have related elsewhere how many Roman pontiffs had fallen away from the papacy, and I repeat what is written in the golden chronicle of Ralph de Diceto, dean of St Paul's, to whom Englishmen would give credence.[^3] For he relates in book 1, chapter 2, how "in the year of the Lord 420 Pope Boniface presided in Rome, and when Eulalius had been ordained against him, and the church was divided over this, both left the city by order of Honorius Augustus; and so, Eulalius being rejected, Boniface, by order of Augustus, because he had been ordained first, is restored to the apostolic see."[^4] For not yet then had the [lordship]{g:dominium} of the Roman empire been so lulled to sleep, nor had the usurpation of the Roman pontiff grown so strong, that it was reputed heretical that [temporal lords]{g:temporales-domini} can take away [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, indeed dignities and offices, from a church that is delinquent.[^5]

### 08-e ¶7 · 182.12–182.30  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 182] Sexto, anno domini DCCCCLXVIII collecta a tota Ytalia episcoporum synodo Johannes papa de nephariis causis infamatur, qui dum se excusaturum venire cunctatur, Leo adhuc laycus eleccione omnium et consensu imperatoris papa substituitur. Sic Leo fecit ordinaciones et alia, que erant apostolica. Nec longum Romani alterata fide apud imperatorem papam recipiunt Johannem. Ille **autem** synodo collecta Leonem deposuit et eius gesta cassavit. Statutumque est synodum a Leone non nominandam synodum **sed** prostibulum favens adulteris. Quicumque igitur eo ordinante erant dampnati iussi suam ipsorum proscripsionem presentare in cartha hec continente: Pater meus nichil sibi habuit, nichil michi dedit, et sic depositi manserunt in illis gradibus, quos habuerant nondum a Leone ordinati. Iste Johannes papa cum uxore cuiusdam oblectans a dyabolo in tempore coitus percutitur et sine viatico dominico obiit.

ENGLISH: "Sixth, in the year of the Lord 968, a synod of bishops having been gathered from all Italy, Pope John is defamed for nefarious causes; and while he delays to come to excuse himself, Leo, still a layman, is put in his place as pope by the election of all and the consent of the emperor. So Leo made [ordinations]{g:ordinacio} and other acts which were apostolic. Not long after, the Romans, having broken faith with the emperor, receive John as pope. And he, gathering a synod, deposed Leo and annulled his acts. And it was decreed that the synod [held] by Leo should be named not a synod but a brothel favouring adulterers. All, therefore, who stood condemned [as ordained] by him were ordered to present their own proscription in a charter containing this: 'My father had nothing for himself, gave nothing to me'; and so, deposed, they remained in those grades which they had held when not yet ordained by Leo. This Pope John, dallying with a certain man's wife, is struck by the devil in the act of intercourse, and died without the Lord's viaticum."[^8]

### 08-e ¶10 · 183.14–184.4  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 183] Nono, anno domini MXLVI Rome uno contra duos et duobus contra unum de papatu altercantibus rex Henricus contra eos Romam vadit et eis canonica et imperiali censura depositis, Clemens presidet, et ab eo rex Henricus in imperatorem benedicitur, iurantibus Romanis se sine eius consensu nunquam papam electuros. Sicut, inquit, presbiteri sciunt se esse ex ecclesie consuetudine ei, qui sibi prepositus fuerit, esse subiectos, ita episcopi noverint se magis consuetudine quam [p. 202] dispensacionis dominice veritate presbiteris esse maiores et in communi debere ecclesiam regere.

ENGLISH: Again, in 1 Timothy 3:2 the Apostle writes to him what conditions a [bishop]{g:episcopus} must have: "It behoveth," he says, "a bishop to be blameless, the husband of one wife." And immediately he adds: "And deacons chaste, not double tongued, not given to [201] much wine, not greedy of filthy lucre."[^1] If, I say, there were a middle order between the episcopal order and the order of the diaconate, this great doctor of the gentiles, who learnt his gospel completely and immediately from the Lord Jesus Christ, as is clear from Galatians 1, would not have passed over the distinction and the rules of that order; indeed, nowhere in any epistle does he set down a distinction of those orders, but he comprehends [presbyters]{g:presbiter}, [priests]{g:sacerdos} and bishops under the name of apostle, the grade of the [church]{g:ecclesia} which he names first among nine, as the most worthy, as is clear from 1 Corinthians 12. Just as," he says, "presbyters know that by the [custom]{g:consuetudo} of the church they are subject to him who has been set over them, so let bishops know that they are greater than presbyters more by custom than by the truth of the Lord's [202] arrangement, and that they ought to rule the church in common."[^6]

### 09-c ¶3 · 202.9–202.31  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 202] Assumptum patet de pastu populi verbo Dei, ad quam ydoneitatem non attenditur, **sed** omnino postponitur in eleccione huiusmodi. Et constat quod per se ab illo pastu pastor ecclesie trahit nomen, non **autem** a pastu corporali, cum hoc sit officium laycale, ut pulchre declarat Augustinus in De Verbis Domini Sermone XLIX, exponens textum Joh. X de pastore mercenario et lupo. Mercenarius, inquit, videns aliquem loqui peccata aut sentire secundum perniciem anime sue vel aliquid facere sceleratum, et tamen quia videtur habere alicuius momenti personam in ecclesia, unde si sperat commodum, obmutescit. Videt, inquit, hominem perire in peccato, videt illum sequi lupum id est dyabolum, videt illius guttur morsu trahi ad supplicium, et tamen non illi dicit: Peccas, uec illum obiurgat, ne perdat commoda sua; talis mentaliter videt lupum venientem et fugit. **Sed** bonus pastor usque ad mortem facit contrarium. In quo secundum Augustinum est integra racio pastoralis officii. Securos, inquit, nos fecit Spiritus Sanctus per Ezechielem XXXIII. 7, quia limitavit nobis speculatoris officium.

ENGLISH: The [assumption]{g:assumptum} is clear from the feeding of the people with the word of God, a fitness to which no attention is paid, but which is wholly set aside in an election of this kind. And it is evident that the pastor of the church takes his name essentially from that feeding, and not from bodily feeding, since this is a lay office, as Augustine finely declares in *On the Words of the Lord*, sermon 49, expounding the text of John 10 on the hireling shepherd and the wolf.[^8] "The hireling," he says, "seeing someone speak sins, or think to the ruin of his soul, or do something wicked, and yet, because he seems to have a person of some weight in the church, whence, if he hopes for advantage, keeps silent.[^9] He sees," he says, "a man perishing in sin, sees him following the wolf, that is the devil, sees his throat dragged by the bite to punishment, and yet does not say to him, 'You sin', nor rebukes him, lest he lose his advantages: such a one sees the wolf coming in his mind, and flees." But the good shepherd does the contrary even to death. In this, according to Augustine, lies the whole account of the pastoral office. "The Holy Ghost," he says, "has made us secure through Ezekiel 33:7, for he has [assigned]{g:limitare} to us the office of [watchman]{g:speculator}."[^10]

### 09-c ¶4 · 202.32–204.29  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 202] Et mundum in testem invoco, quod ista papalis eleccio et sua beneficiorum distribucio seminat [p. 203] in ecclesia mercenarios nimis late, ex parte vero paparum dat occasionem nimis extollendi vicariam potestatem, nimis appreciandi seculi dignitatem et nimis affectandi fantasticam sanctitatem. Illi **autem** doctores, qui expectant remuneracionem temporalem a papa, dicunt quod est incomprehensibilis potestatis, quia potest omnia que Christus potuit humanitus et multa ultra, que concernunt regimen ecclesie activorum. Nec est aliqua opinio Antichristi magis heretica vel blasfema, quia certum est ex fide quod non habet aliquam potestatem nisi quam Deus dat sibi ad edificandum ecclesiam conformiter legi sue. Quod si habet tantam potestatem, adhuc reddit nimis se dampnabilem, quod non exponit scintillam eius ad regimen presentis ecclesie adeo vacillantis, ymo ut declarat Lumbardus IV sentenciarum distinccione XVIII cap. II et infra ex testimoniis sanctorum Augustini, Ambrosii. Jeronymi, divisus ab ecclesia, ut prescitus non habet potestatem huiusmodi; et quantumcumque verus sacerdos christianus non habet potestatem absolvendi, nisi ad promulgandum divinam sentenciam vel arbitrandum penitenciam aut acceptandum vel refutandum personam secundum quod digna fuerit aut indigna; quod si quis abutitur clavibus discordans ab ecclesia triumphante ostendit se expertem a clavibus sancte ecclesie; et creditur quod supercilium phariseicum ad tantum vibravit claves contra semitas immobiles ecclesie triumphantis, quod perit evidencia et surrepit diffidencia in opere clavium pretendencium se superiorem primatum gerere in ecclesia militante. **Sed** benedictus Yerarcha tocius ecclesie, quod [p. 204] satis est fidei in ipsum credere. Unde Jeronymus exponens illud Matth. XVI. 29: Tibi dabo claves regni celorum, hunc inquit, locum aliqui non intelligentes, sumunt aliquid de supercilio phariseorum, ut dampnare innoxios vel solvere se putent noxios, cum apud Deum non sentencia sacerdotum **sed** vita reorum queratur. **Sed** sicut Levit. XIV ostendere se sacerdotibus iubentur leprosi, quos illi mundos vel leprosos non faciunt, **sed** discernunt, ita et hic. Unde non video de quo prelatus sic posset pompare inanius quam de huiusmodi potestate, quia ignorat si habeat aliquam huiusmodi potestatem. Ignorat si exercens potestatem reddit se dampnabilem ex errore mendaci ab ecclesia triumphante. Ignorat tercio, si simplex subiectus suus habet eo quantumlibet amplius potestatis. Ideo indubie sacerdotalis potestatis pompacio fetet coram Deo et homine. Coram Deo, quia illi quorum potestas a Deo suspenditur per peccatum vel ex inhabilitate nunquam acceperant potestatem, inmiscent in Christum pompose blasfemias, dicentes se posse ultra hoc quod poterunt apostoli vel eciam Ihesus noster. Addunt eciam in palliacionem sue versucie horrendam heresim, quod Deus pepigit cum eis, quod ipsis assisteret quicquid pretenderent se solvere vel ligare, eo quod quicquid apostoli rite secundum claves ecclesie sic solverint vel ligaverint, Dei assistencia confirmabat.

ENGLISH: And I call the world to witness that this papal election, and its distribution of benefices, sows [203] hirelings in the church far too widely, and on the part of the popes gives occasion for exalting the vicarial [power]{g:potestas} too much, for prizing the dignity of the world too much, and for [desiring]{g:affectare} an imaginary sanctity too much.[^11] And those doctors who expect temporal reward from the pope say that he is of incomprehensible power, since he can do all that Christ could do as man, and much beyond, which concerns the rule of the church of the active.[^12] Nor is there any [opinion]{g:opinio} of Antichrist more heretical or blasphemous, for it is certain, [as faith teaches]{g:ex-fide}, that he has no power except what God gives him for building up the church in conformity with his law. And if he has so much power, he still renders himself exceedingly damnable, in that he does not put forth a spark of it for the rule of the present church, wavering as it is; indeed, as Lombard declares in [book] 4 of the *Sentences*, distinction 18, chapter 2 and following, from the testimonies of the saints Augustine, Ambrose and Jerome, one divided from the church, as one [foreknown]{g:prescitus}, does not have power of this kind;[^13] and however true a Christian priest [he be], he does not have the power of absolving except to promulgate the divine [sentence]{g:sentencia}, or to assign penance by his judgement, or to accept or refuse a person according as he shall be worthy or unworthy; and if anyone abuses the keys, in discord with the [church triumphant]{g:ecclesia-triumphans}, he shows himself to have no part in the keys of holy church;[^14] and it is believed that pharisaic haughtiness has brandished the keys so far against the immovable paths of the church triumphant that [evidence]{g:evidencia} perishes and distrust creeps in, in the work of the keys of those who claim to hold a superior [primacy]{g:primatus} in the church militant.[^15] But blessed be the Hierarch of the whole church, that [204] it is enough for faith to believe in him. Hence Jerome, expounding that of Matthew 16:19, "I will give to thee the keys of the kingdom of heaven", says: "This place some, not understanding, take up something of the haughtiness of the Pharisees, so as to think they condemn the innocent or loose the guilty, since with God it is not the sentence of the priests but the life of the accused that is sought. But as in Leviticus 14 the lepers are commanded to show themselves to the priests, whom these do not make clean or leprous but discern, so here too."[^16] Hence I do not see of what a [prelate]{g:prelatus} could boast more emptily than of power of this kind, since he [is ignorant]{g:ignorare} whether he has any power of this kind. He is ignorant whether, in exercising the power, he renders himself damnable by a lying error, [cut off] from the church triumphant. He is ignorant, thirdly, whether his simple subject has however much more power than he.[^17] Therefore without doubt the boasting of priestly power stinks before God and man. Before God, because those whose power is suspended by God through sin, or [who] through lack of [qualification]{g:habilitas} never received the power, pompously mix blasphemies into Christ, saying that they can [do] beyond what the apostles could, or even our Jesus.[^18] They add also, for the [cloaking]{g:palliacio} of their craftiness, a horrible heresy: that God made a pact with them that he would stand by them in whatever they pretended to loose or bind, because whatever the apostles rightly, according to the keys of the church, so loosed or bound, God's assistance confirmed.[^19]

### 09-d ¶1 · 204.30–205.26  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 204] **Sed** ceca argucia, unde cap. Tales blasfemias et mille permittit Deus in peccatum ecclesie, confitens vero erit optimus iudex peccati proprii, considerans si perfecte dolet de commisso in Deum, si humiliter proponit et non vult sic peccare amplius. Sacerdos **autem** qui apponit materiam in confitentem ignorat hoc; verumtamen multum valet apud utramque ecclesiam, quod quis humiliter confiteatur presbitero, quod ipse secundum clavem sciencie aptet sanificam medicinam, et quod oret ac supportet cum ceteris membris Christi pro scelere confitentis. ENGLISH: But [this is] a blind [quibble]{g:argucia}; hence in chapter 9 the Master, on the authority of blessed Augustine, speaks thus:[^1] "'Whatsoever thou shalt bind upon earth,' etc., that is, I God [205] and all the orders of the heavenly [soldiery]{g:milicia} and all the saints in my glory praise with you and confirm those whom you bind and loose. He did not say, those whom you think you bind and loose, but those upon whom you exercise the work of justice or of mercy. Otherwise I do not acknowledge your works upon sinners." Who then would not shudder [at the claim] that a [priest]{g:sacerdos} compels God and the whole [church]{g:ecclesia} triumphant to consent culpably to his folly, or to set down whatever he has done in this to ratified prudence, or that the people must believe without judgement that whatever the priest has done in this matter has been confirmed by God? Such blasphemies, and a thousand [more], God permits for the sin of the church;[^2] but the one confessing will be the best judge of his own sin, considering whether he grieves perfectly for what he committed against God, whether he humbly purposes and wills not to sin so any more. But the priest who applies the matter to the one confessing is ignorant of this;[^3] nevertheless it avails much with both churches that one humbly confess to a [presbyter]{g:presbiter}, that he according to the key of knowledge fit a healing medicine, and that he pray and bear [him] up with the other [members]{g:membrum} of Christ for the crime of the one confessing. For thus, according to Augustine on John, Sermon 121, the church of the saints forgives sins by reason of merit.[^4]

### 09-d ¶2 · 205.27–207.22  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 205] Quantum ad secundum pomposum, quod est ordinis vel status dignitas, consideraret sic pompans dignitatem naturalem et statum dyaboli, et in quantam indignitatem incidit per peccatum, ut pulchre declarat Augustinus in De Verbis Domini Sermone LIII: Melior est, inquit, virginalis [p. 206] sanctitas quam pudicicia coniugata, **sed** attende superbiam et humilitatem, iungeque virginitati tue humilitatem et eris matre tua melior, quod si tu virgo admittas superbiam et mater tua servet humilitatem, ipsa est longe melior te superba. Nam sicut bonum bono adiunctum facit integrum magis bonum, sic hoc malum facit totum malum sive bono adiunctum fuerit sive malo; privat enim te racione membri humilis capitis, quod est Christus, quod si est, ut patet in lucifero, qui est melior te natura, ubi est potestas vel dignitas, de qua pompas? Nichil, inquam, foret clero utilius et specialiter Christi vicario quam sentire humiliter de seipso, quod Augustinus declarat subtiliter et devote, dicens quod genus humanum post lapsum omnino indiguit celesti medico, quod quia non suffecit ad ipsum ascendere, Deus ineffabiliter descendit ad ipsum, docens vita, opere et sermone, quomodo oportet genus humanum si sanabitur humilitate contraria vie superbie qua adeo recesserat sanum redire ad dominum. Et hec racio, quare Christus servavit in vita et doctrina summam pauperiem et humilitatem; Antichristus **autem** querit mundanam gloriam Christo contrarie, ut dicitur Joh. V; et ideo licet papa sic fecerit (ut dicit Augustinus) non est de Christi ecclesia, quia si sit verus papa est homo humillimus, mundanos honores et lucrum seculi parvipendens. Et sic Christus est humanitus via, per quam oportet omnem salvandum incedere, que via cum sit arta impossibile est tumentem superbia per illam semitam penetrare. **Sed** de quanto collisus stante illo tumore plus illi vie facietenus innititur, de tanto comportando prohibens [p. 207] plus offendit. Nec est talis tumorositas vera magnitudo, **sed** innaturalis mundana aquositas, que repugnat omnino Christi vicario, quia aliter est mendacissimus Antichristus. Et sicut dicitur Johannis XIV. 6: Viator habet, qua vult ire, cum Christus est via, quo vult ire, Christus est veritas et ubi vult manere, Christus est cursiv. Quamvis **autem** in via non sunt insidie, cum sit Christus, tamen iuxta illam sunt multa pericula, iuxta illud Psalmi CXXXIX, 6: Iuxta iter scandalum posuerunt michi. Vis secure incedere, tene te in via regia et omnino desere mundum ac eius gloriam, quia aliter es indubie Antichristus et non de Christi ovibus. Christus enim non potest superbum talem ad se trahere, cum oportet exaltacionem ad Deum per humilitatem procedere. Ideo quomodocumque pompaveris, exaltando te ipsum et querendo mundi gloriam, ille qui sentenciavit, quod qui se exaltat humiliabitur, et qui se humiliat exaltabitur, non potest mutare suam sentenciam, quin talis sit magis alienus a Christo et per consequens Antichristus.

ENGLISH: As to the second object of [boasting]{g:pompare}, which is the dignity of order or state, one who boasts so should consider the natural dignity and state of the devil, and into how great an indignity he fell through sin, as Augustine finely declares in *On the Words of the Lord*, Sermon 53: "Virginal holiness," he says, "is better [206] than wedded chastity; but attend to pride and humility, and join humility to your virginity, and you will be better than your mother; but if you, a virgin, admit pride, and your mother keeps humility, she is far better than you in your pride."[^5] For as a good joined to a good makes the whole more good, so this evil makes the whole evil, whether it has been joined to good or to evil; for it deprives you of the standing of a member of the humble head, which is Christ. And if this is so, as is clear in Lucifer, who is better than you by nature, where is the [power]{g:potestas} or dignity of which you boast?[^6] Nothing, I say, would be more useful to the clergy, and especially to the [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius-christi}, than to think humbly of himself; which Augustine declares subtly and devoutly, saying that the human race after the fall wholly needed a heavenly physician; and because it was not able to ascend to him, God ineffably descended to it, teaching by life, work and word how the human race, if it is to be healed, must return whole to the Lord by a humility contrary to the way of pride by which it had gone so far away.[^7] And this is the reason why Christ kept, in life and teaching, the highest poverty and humility; but Antichrist seeks worldly glory contrary to Christ, as is said in John 5; and therefore, although a pope do so (as Augustine says), he is not of Christ's church; for if he is a true pope he is a most humble man, setting little store by worldly honours and the gain of the world.[^8] And so Christ in his humanity is the way by which everyone who is to be saved must walk; and since that way is narrow, it is impossible for one swollen with pride to pass through by that path. But the more one who is bruised presses outwardly upon that way while that swelling remains, the more, obstructing [it] by what he carries, does he [207] stumble.[^9] Nor is such swollenness true greatness, but an unnatural worldly wateriness, which is wholly [incompatible]{g:repugnare} with the vicar of Christ, since otherwise he is a most lying Antichrist. And as is said, John 14:6: the [wayfarer]{g:viator} has that by which he wishes to go, since Christ is "the way"; whither he wishes to go, Christ is "the truth"; and where he wishes to abide, Christ is the life.[^10] And although there are no snares in the way, since it is Christ, yet beside it there are many dangers, according to that of Psalm 139:6: "They have laid for me a stumblingblock by the wayside." Do you wish to walk safely? Keep yourself on the king's highway and wholly forsake the world and its glory, for otherwise you are without doubt Antichrist and not of Christ's sheep. For Christ cannot draw such a proud man to himself, since exaltation to God must proceed through humility. Therefore, howsoever you boast, exalting yourself and seeking the glory of the world, he who [pronounced]{g:sentenciare} that "he that exalteth himself shall be humbled: and he that humbleth himself shall be exalted" cannot change his [sentence]{g:sentencia}, so that such a man should not be the more estranged from Christ, and consequently Antichrist.[^11]

### 09-e ¶1 · 207.23–209.16  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 207] Quantum ad tercium scilicet vite sanctitatem, patet quod necesse est gloriantem sic in laudibus hominum considerare se sollicite in speculo scripturarum. Consideret igitur papa et impleat ea que exposita sunt de Petro, et tunc non delectabitur in laude hominum nec in mundana gloria, **sed** in spe premii absconditi in Christo, et sic non video, quin affectans humanas laudes et gloriam propter vite sanctitatem sit detestandus ypocrita et mendaciter contrarius sibi ipsi, nam [p. 208] si talis prius est sanctus, luciferina affeccio laudis hominum extinguit sanctitatem quam reperit. Et ista sex, cum sint scriptura sacra, decet [p. 209] exponere. Quantum ad duo prima, patet quod spectant ad peccatum carnis, cum quantum ad modum convivandi sunt macule in abstracto, convivant enim mundi dominos et carnales amicos contra doctrinam Christi Luce XIV, 12— 14: Cum facis prandium aut cenam, noli vocare amicos tuos neque cognatos neque vicinos neque divites, ne forte et ipsi reinvitent te et fiat tibi retribucio. **Sed** cum facis convivium, voca pauperes, debiles, claudos, cecos, et beatus eris. Istud **autem** servaretur in nobis potissime, qui vivimus de elemosinis laycorum et bonis pauperum. Quamvis **autem** licet convivare, tam spiritualiter infirmos quam sanos, ut spiritualiter edificentur et reedificent, hoc tamen non debet fieri racione commodi temporalis.

ENGLISH: As to the third, namely holiness of life, it is clear that one who glories thus in the praises of men must consider himself carefully in the mirror of the scriptures. Let the [pope]{g:papa} therefore consider and fulfil the things that have been set out concerning Peter, and then he will not delight in the praise of men nor in worldly glory, but in the hope of the reward hidden in Christ;[^1] and so I do not see how one who [desires]{g:affectare} human praises and glory on account of holiness of life is not a detestable hypocrite, lyingly contrary to himself. For [208] if such a man is holy beforehand, the luciferine affection for the praise of men extinguishes the holiness it finds; if he is always unholy, he brings a blasphemous lie upon himself and the people. Hence Pope Peter, 2 Peter 2:1, prophesies of such: "There shall be," he says, "false prophets among the people, even as there shall be among you lying teachers";[^2] and afterwards he describes them at length, saying that they are "as irrational beasts", "blaspheming those things which they know not". Such would be those who should say that they have, from the election of men, even of notoriously perverse men, the [power]{g:potestas} of absolving from punishment and from guilt, and of granting [indulgences]{g:indulgencie} without end out of the [supererogated]{g:supererogare} merits of the saints, distributed at their pleasure.[^3] And such men are commonly [ignorant of]{g:ignorare} their own words; and blessed Jude describes them by this, that they "blaspheme majesty"; and whereas Saint Michael, "disputing with the devil" about "the body of Moses, durst not bring against him judgment", these men, "like dumb beasts, offend in the things which they know with a [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} knowledge", since they dare, beyond the archangel, to curse whoever has resisted their will, although they are ignorant of God's judgement concerning them.[^4] And beyond these things they blaspheme more gravely, saying that God must, by virtue of the election, stand by them and fulfil whatever they have promised. Hence the holy apostle describes them by a sixfold mark of the proud: "These," he says, "are spots in their banquets, feasting together without fear, feeding themselves: clouds without water, which are carried about by winds: trees of the autumn, unfruitful, twice dead, plucked up by the roots: raging waves of the sea, foaming out their own confusion: wandering stars, to whom the storm of darkness is reserved for ever."[^5] And these six, since they are holy scripture, it is fitting [209] to expound. As to the first two, it is clear that they pertain to the sin of the flesh, since as to the manner of feasting they are spots in the abstract; for they feast the lords of the world and carnal friends, against the [doctrine]{g:doctrina} of Christ, Luke 14:12–14: "When thou makest a dinner or a supper, call not thy friends nor thy kinsmen nor thy neighbours nor the rich, lest perhaps they also invite thee again, and a recompense be made to thee. But when thou makest a feast, call the poor, the maimed, the lame and the blind, and thou shalt be blessed."[^6] And this should be kept most of all among us, who live on the [alms]{g:elemosina} of the laity and the goods of the poor. And although it is lawful to feast both the spiritually sick and the healthy, so that they may be spiritually edified and edify in turn, yet this must not be done for the sake of temporal advantage.

### 09-f ¶1 · 210.15–212.14  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 210] Quantum ad tercium iugum, quod concernit peccatum dyaboli, dicitur primo quod sunt fluctus feri maris despumantes suas confusiones; quod fit, quando Antichristi sunt instrumenta impii et crudelis dyaboli, cui proprietates maris inseparabiliter sunt coniuncte; tales enim involvunt simplices in caribdim, quando spumant mendacia de sua fantastica potestate; hoc enim erit eis blasfema confusio in die iudicii. Sexto et ultimo dicitur quod tales pseudo sunt sydera volancia; tradunt enim naturales quod ex materia terrestri viscosa ad supremum intersticium aeris exalata fit materia secundum formam et disposicionem variam sublimita; et dicitur, postquam fuerit ignita et mota, nunc stella cadens, nunc draco ardens, et nunc stella cometa. Ita mortaliter ex statu laycali lutoso sunt aliqui ad supremum statum militantis ecclesie exaltati, quos cum malignus tetigerit, sunt succensi superbia, cupiditate et omni genere peccatorum, **sed** cum summe in Deum, qui est ignis, recalcitrant, decidunt secundum [p. 211] raciones Deo absconditas, nunc uno modo, nunc alio, in formam polipi vel abiecte materie, quibus procella tenebrarum perpetuo parata est post diem iudicii. Prepositi **autem** et specialiter summus pontifex timerent sibi de sensu hoc mistico et specialiter de superbia vel laude humana, de extollencia sui status; ipsa enim est spuma fantasticum Antichristi, cum ad nichil valet nisi ad frustrandum mercedem Domini dicentis Matth. VI, 2: Amen, dico vobis receperunt mercedem suam, et Joh. V, 44 quomodo potestis credere, qui gloriam ab invicem recipitis, et gloriam que a solo Deo est non queritis? Debemus enim, laudes humanas et occasiones earum aufugere, dicente Paulo papa, prima Tessa. II, 5, 6. 9 Neque, inquit, aliquando fuimus in sermone adulacionis sicut scitis, neque in occasione avaricie, Deus testis est, neque querentes ab hominibus gloriam, operantes continue, ne aliquem vestrum gravaremus, predicavimus in vobis evangelium Dei. Et antequam papa fuerit conversacione contrarius vite Pauli, dimitteret omnem eleccionem, omnem gloriacionem et omnem occupacionem, que hodie consequitur papas nostros. Unde beatus Crysostomus Omelia XXVIII Operis Imperfecti super Matth. XX, 27: Quicumque voluerit inter vos primus esse erit vester servus. Secularem, inquam, primatum desiderare et si racio non est, tamen causa est, quia etsi iustum non est, tamen utile est, primatum **autem** ecclesiasticum concupiscere neque racio est neque causa, quia neque iustum est neque utile. Quis enim sapiens ultro se subiceret servituti laboris, et quod [p. 212] maius est periculo tali, ut det racionem pro omni ecclesia apud iustum iudicem, nisi forte qui non credit iudicium Dei, nec timet uti abutens primatu suo ecclesiastico seculariter convertat eum in secularem. Ex istis videtur primo, quod non licet papatum vel aliquam dignitatem ecclesiasticam eleccione humana appetere, quia ut sic primatus stulte se supponit periculo, stulte quia non humana eleccio facit ius presidendi, **sed** promulgat in casu, quo in electo presint virtutum insignia, et insuper constituit se obligaciorem ad populum, nec accrescit exinde profectus, **sed** periculum, et ideo sancti apostoli non susceperunt primatum talem ab homine.

ENGLISH: As to the third [yoke]{g:iugum}, which concerns the sin of the devil, it is said first that they are "raging waves of the sea, foaming out their own confusion"; which comes about when Antichrists are the instruments of the impious and cruel devil, to whom the properties of the sea are inseparably joined; for such men wrap the simple in Charybdis when they foam out lies about their [imaginary]{g:fantasticus} [power]{g:potestas}; for this will be a blasphemous confusion to them on the day of judgement. Sixthly and lastly, therefore, it is said that such false men are "flying stars";[^1] for the natural philosophers teach that from earthy, viscous matter exhaled to the highest interval of the air there comes to be matter sublimed according to varied form and disposition; and it is called, after it has been kindled and moved, at one time a falling star, at another a burning dragon, and at another a comet.[^2] So, to their death, some are exalted from the muddy lay state to the highest state of the [church]{g:ecclesia} militant; and when the evil one has touched them they are kindled with pride, covetousness and every kind of sin; but since they kick back to the utmost against God, who is fire, they fall down, for [211] reasons hidden with God, in one way at one time and in another at another, into the form of a polyp or of cast-off matter, "to whom the storm of darkness" is perpetually prepared after the day of judgement.[^3] And [superiors]{g:prepositi}, and especially the highest pontiff, should fear for themselves from this mystical sense, and especially from pride or human praise, from the exaltation of their state; for it is the imaginary foam of Antichrist,[^4] since it avails for nothing except to frustrate the reward, as the Lord says, Matthew 6:2: "Amen I say to you, they have received their reward", and John 5:44: "How can you believe, who receive glory one from another: and the glory which is from God alone, you do not seek?" For we ought to shun human praises and the occasions of them, as Paul the [pope]{g:papa} says, 1 Thessalonians 2:5, 6, 9: "Neither," he says, "were we at any time in the speech of flattery, as you know, nor in an occasion of covetousness, God is witness, nor seeking glory of men; working continually, lest we should be chargeable to any of you, we preached among you the gospel of God."[^5] And before a pope should be contrary in [manner of life]{g:conversacio} to the life of Paul, he should give up every election, every glorying and every occupation that today attends our popes. Hence blessed Chrysostom, Homily 28 of the *Unfinished Work* on Matthew 20:27: "Whosoever will be first among you shall be your servant. To desire secular [primacy]{g:primatus}," I say, "even if there is no reason, yet there is a cause, because even if it is not just, yet it is useful; but to covet ecclesiastical primacy there is neither reason nor cause, because it is neither just nor useful. For what wise man would of his own accord subject himself to a servitude of labour, and, what [212] is greater, to such a danger, that he must render account for the whole church before a just judge, unless perhaps one who does not believe the judgement of God, nor fears, so that, abusing his ecclesiastical primacy in a secular way, he turns it into a secular one?"[^6] From these things it appears, first, that it is not lawful to seek the papacy or any ecclesiastical dignity by human election, because as such [one seeking] primacy foolishly puts himself in danger: foolishly, because human election does not make the [right]{g:ius} of presiding, but promulgates it in the case where the marks of the virtues are present in the one elected; and moreover he makes himself more bound to the people, nor does profit accrue from it, but danger; and therefore the holy apostles did not accept such primacy from man.[^7]

### 09-f ¶2 · 212.15–213.13  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 212] Videtur secundo quod, sive Robertus sive Urbanus, sive quicumque alius, appetit sic preesse ecclesie Anglie, signum probabile est quod sit fugibilis Antichristus; unde beatus Crysostomus ubi supra: Quicumque desideraverit primatum in terra, inveniet confusionem in celo, ut iam inter servos Christi non sit de primatu certamen, non festinet unusquisque, quomodo aliis maior appareat, **sed** quomodo omnibus inferior videatur, quoniam non qui maior fuerit in honore ille est iustior, **sed** qui inferior fuerit hic est maior. Ideo primatus in populo non est ab aliquo appetendus. Quomodo igitur non habebit ecclesia nostra talem suspectum, qui et nescit fidem scripture et se ipsum vel familiam suam privatam regere, et tamen presumit se digniorem sciencia et virtute, ut regat tantam ecclesiam? Utinam ecclesia vellet dicere talibus sentenciam Christi et beati Crysostomi Ad talem primatum pertinet respondere Deo pro animabus sibi subditis in die iudicii et servire illis evangelice laboriosius atque humilius, [p. 213] contempto omni seculari commodo vel honore. Quod si Robertus vel alius nobis facere voluerit, non contradicimus, Deus sibi retribuat, unde beatus Crysostomus ubi supra: Principes ecclesie fiunt, ut serviant minoribus suis et ministrent eis quecumque acceperunt a Christo, ut suas utilitates negligant et illorum procurent, ut si opus fuerit nec mori recusent pro salute eorum, sicut fecit Apostolus. Cum igitur hec sit religio paparum, patet quam culpabiles a Deo sunt principes seculi, qui consensu stulto ipsam dissolverint, specialiter promittendo thesaurum regni et legiorum suorum ad ipsos confluere.

ENGLISH: It appears, secondly, that whether Robert or Urban or anyone else so seeks to preside over the church of England, it is a probable sign that he is an Antichrist to be fled;[^8] hence blessed Chrysostom in the place cited: "Whoever desires primacy on earth will find confusion in heaven, so that now among the servants of Christ there may be no contest about primacy; let not each one hasten how he may appear greater than others, but how he may seem lower than all; since it is not he who is greater in honour that is more just, but he who is lower is the greater." Therefore primacy among the people is not to be sought by anyone. How then shall our church not hold such a man suspect, who knows neither the faith of scripture nor how to rule himself or his own private [household]{g:familia}, and yet presumes himself worthier in knowledge and virtue, so as to rule so great a church? Would that the church would tell such men the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of Christ and of blessed Chrysostom: to such a primacy it belongs to answer to God for the souls subject to it on the day of judgement, and to serve them in the manner of the gospel more laboriously and more humbly, [213] scorning every secular advantage or honour.[^9] And if Robert or another will do this for us, we do not gainsay it; may God reward him. Hence blessed Chrysostom in the place cited: "The princes of the church are made so that they may serve their inferiors and minister to them whatever they have received from Christ, so that they may neglect their own interests and see to theirs, so that if need be they may not refuse even to die for their salvation, as the Apostle did." Since, then, this is the religion of popes, it is clear how culpable before God are the princes of the world who by foolish consent have dissolved it, especially by promising that the treasure of the realm and of their lieges should flow to them.[^10]

### 09-f ¶3 · 213.14–213.28  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 213] Tercio patet ex dictis quomodo sancti susceperunt primatus huiusmodi, scientes quod humana eleccio vel est primatui suo impertinens vel nociva. Est enim in primatu tali servitus laboriosa et mundo abiecta ac infructuosa, **sed** meritoria quoad Deum, et illam voluerunt sancti doctores pro fructu premii promerendo. Est secundo in primatu extollencia mundana periculosa et insufficiencia vel negligencia perficiendi officium ruinosa, que paulative augebatur racione dotacionis ecclesie, et illam noluerunt sancti pastores ecclesie; et hinc post dotacionem inolevit religio prelatorum, quod verbo et mente dicerent in talibus eleccionibus nolens volo, quia nolens hoc secundum nefarium volo primum.

ENGLISH: Thirdly, it is clear from what has been said how the saints accepted primacies of this kind, knowing that human election is either [irrelevant]{g:impertinens} to their primacy or harmful. For there is in such a primacy a laborious servitude, cast off by the world and unfruitful [in it], but meritorious with respect to God; and this the holy doctors willed, for the fruit of the reward to be merited. There is, secondly, in primacy a dangerous worldly exaltation and a ruinous insufficiency or negligence in performing the office, which increased little by little by reason of the [endowment]{g:dotacio} of the church; and this the holy pastors of the church did not will. And hence, after the endowment, the religion of [prelates]{g:prelatus} grew up, that in such elections they should say in word and mind, "Unwilling, I will": because, not willing this second, wicked thing, I will the first.[^11]

### 09-f ¶4 · 213.29–214.2  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 213] Et patet in parte quomodo solvenda sunt dicta Crysostomi et obiectus communes facti in ista materia. Modo **autem** cautela dyaboli et incuria secularium ad perturbacionem magnam ecclesie, dicitur in facto contrarium, scilicet quod nolens ministerium evangelicum vel mercedem volo primatum et commodum temporale, et necesse [p. 214] est istam perversionem currere quousque hec duo separata fuerint sicut in ecclesia primitiva.

ENGLISH: And it is clear in part how the sayings of Chrysostom and the common [objections]{g:objectum} made in this matter are to be resolved. But now, by the [wile]{g:cautela} of the devil and the carelessness of [seculars]{g:secularis}, to the great disturbance of the church, the contrary is said in deed, namely: "Not willing the gospel ministry or the reward, I will the primacy and temporal advantage"; and [214] this perversion must run on until these two have been separated, as in the primitive church.[^12]
