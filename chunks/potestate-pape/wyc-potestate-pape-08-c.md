# *De potestate pape*, chapter 8, part 3: no man can elect the highest pontiff

## ¶1 · 173.33–174.11

> [p. 173] Item, quicumque superstes dat alteri potestatem solum dat sibi potestatem vicariam et non parem, [p. 174] igitur ex hoc quod Petrus instituit Clementem Rome pontificem, non sequitur successorem suum fuisse summum pontificem sicut Petrus. Nec valet ficticia, qua dicitur quod Petrus Rome obiit, ideo successor suus ibidem debuit esse papa. Nam Christus papa summus obiit Jerusolimis et tamen Jacobus Alphei tenens ibidem episcopatum a Christo datum et ibi obiens non propterea fuit papa. Que igitur liga Petri ad Clementem pocius quam Christus ad Jacobum, ut episcopus succedens loco quo obiit foret papa?

Again, whoever, while he survives, gives [power]{g:potestas} to another gives him only a vicarious power and not an equal one;[^1] [174] therefore from the fact that Peter instituted Clement pontiff of Rome it does not follow that his successor was [highest pontiff]{g:summi-pontifices} as Peter was. Nor does the [fiction]{g:ficticia} hold by which it is said that Peter died at Rome, and therefore his successor there had to be [pope]{g:papa}. For Christ, the highest pope, died at Jerusalem, and yet James of Alphaeus, holding there the bishopric given by Christ and dying there, was not on that account pope.[^2] What [league]{g:liga}, then, [was there] of Peter to Clement rather than of Christ to James, so that the bishop succeeding in the place where he died should be pope?[^3]

## ¶2 · 174.12–176.4

> [p. 174] Item, non subiacet humane potestati sed solum divine constituere quemquam summum pontificem, igitur non debet supponi in Petro tanta presumpcio, ut eligat hominem de quo non fuit sibi ad hoc facta revelacio in summum pontificem et immediatum Christi vicarium super terram, specialiter cum super ista fatuitate, non relinquitur nobis scriptum autenticum, sed in eleccione Mathie reliquit hoc divine iudicio, ut patet Actuum primo; quanto plus in eleccione maioris. Quis, inquam, crederet quod Petrus diffiniret beatum Clementem sequi Christum similius quam Evangelistam, qui diu vixit post Petrum, vel beatum Evangelistam debere Clementi ut maiori? vel capiti necessario regulandi obedienciam et reverenciam, qualem pape iam exigunt a subiectis. vel quod Christus aut alius apostolus talem maioritatem instituit, vel policiam in sua ecclesia approbavit? Voluit enim omnes apostolos esse socios sibi invicem obedientes et servientes sine dominio vel magisterio alicuius capitanei preter Christum. Et ex istis infirmatur tercia ficticia quod per viam eleccionis et institucionis humane ponitur [p. 175] quis Romanus et summus pontifex. Et quamvis ista sentencia sit maxime propinqua facto, tamen nimis elongatur a religione fideli et stabili fundamento. Cum enim aliquem stat esse Romanum pontificem et non papam et econtra, ut patet in Christo et Petro, patet quod oportet istas duas elecciones esse racionis disparis et nature ordine separatas. Stat enim aliquem esse Romanum pontificem et pessimum Antichristum, sicut econtra stat aliquem esse summum pontificem, cum hoc quod sit loco illi indifferens, ut patet ex dictis. Unde dominus Ardmacanus libro VII, cap. XVIII De Questionibus Armenorum dicit quod sunt duo pontificatus naturaliter differentes, et oportet quod sint eciam apud Deum due elecciones. Sed videtur michi quod eleccio qua quis eligeretur in papam vel summum pontificem non subiacet potestati vel eleccioni humane. Nam idem est hominem eligere in papam vel summum pontificem et eligere ipsum in apostolicum maxime propinque per graciam sequentem Christi vestigia; sed hoc non subiacet ordinacioni vel eleccioni humane, igitur nec aliud sive idem; sic enim eligeret quis hominem in predestinatum vel quocumque aliter graciosum. Et confirmatur ex factis et verbis apostolorum Act. I in eleccione Mathie apostoli, quod est minus quam eligere summum pontificem, ubi ipsi non eligunt sed rogant Deum; ut ostendat ipse quem elegerit. Sicut igitur electores non eligunt summum pontificem in eligendo alterum Scarioth vel Antichristum; sic casualiter eligendo eum quem Deus elegerit non [p. 176] eligunt talem in summum pontificem, sed in fungentem tali officio, quod non dicit excellenciam gracie vel alia carismata, que sunt propria dona Dei.

Again, to appoint anyone highest pontiff is subject not to human power but only to divine; therefore so great a presumption ought not to be supposed in Peter as that he should elect as highest pontiff and immediate [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius-christi} on earth a man about whom no revelation had been made to him for this, especially since on this folly no [authentic]{g:autenticus} writing is left to us; but in the election of Matthias he left this to the judgement of God, as is clear from Acts 1;[^4] how much more in the election of a greater one. Who, I say, would believe that Peter would define blessed Clement to follow Christ in closer likeness than the Evangelist, who lived long after Peter, or that the blessed Evangelist owed to Clement, as to a greater, or as to a head necessary for ruling, obedience and reverence such as popes now exact from their subjects?[^5] or that Christ or another apostle instituted such [greatness]{g:majoritas}, or approved such a polity in his [church]{g:ecclesia}? For he willed all the apostles to be companions, obeying and serving one another, without the [lordship]{g:dominium} or [rule]{g:magisterium} of any [captain]{g:capitaneus} besides Christ. And from these things the third fiction is weakened, that by way of human election and institution someone is set up [175] as Roman and highest pontiff. And although this [teaching]{g:sentencia} is the closest to the fact, yet it is [exceedingly]{g:nimis} far removed from faithful religion and a stable foundation.[^6] For since it is possible for someone to be [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex} and not pope, and conversely, as is clear in Christ and Peter, it is clear that these two elections must be of disparate character and separated in the order of nature. For it is possible for someone to be Roman pontiff and the worst Antichrist, just as, conversely, it is possible for someone to be highest pontiff while being indifferent to that place, as is clear from what has been said. Hence the [lord of Armagh]{g:dominus-ardmacanus}, in book 7, chapter 18 of the *Questions of the Armenians*, says that there are two pontificates naturally differing, and that there must also be two elections before God.[^7] But it seems to me that the election by which someone would be elected pope or highest pontiff is not subject to the power or election of men. For it is the same to elect a man pope or highest pontiff and to elect him an [apostolic man]{g:apostolicus} following most closely, through grace, the footsteps of Christ; now this is not subject to human [ordinance]{g:ordinacio} or election; therefore neither is the other, or rather the same thing; for so one would elect a man to be [predestinate]{g:predestinatus}, or gracious in any other way.[^8] And it is confirmed from the deeds and words of the apostles, Acts 1, in the election of the apostle Matthias, which is less than electing a highest pontiff, where they do not elect but ask God that he himself may show whom he has chosen. As, then, the electors do not elect a highest pontiff in electing another Iscariot or Antichrist, so, electing by chance the one whom God has chosen, they do not [176] elect such a one as highest pontiff, but as one discharging such an office, which does not signify excellence of grace or other charisms, which are the proper gifts of God.[^9]

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Superstes*, "while he survives": the giver lives on and keeps his own power, so what he grants is delegated, not equal. The argument answers the second fiction, Peter's institution of Clement (173.21 above).
[^2]: **S.** James son of Alphaeus taken as bishop of Jerusalem, that is, identified with "James the brother of the Lord" (Gal. 1:19), the first bishop there in the tradition reported by Jerome, *De viris illustribus* 2 (LEDGER P08c-2). **T.** The apparatus at 174.6–7 is damaged in our copy ("A -. et tantum"); it seems to report A's *et tantum* for *et tamen*. The print is translated.
[^3]: **C.** The print has *Christus ad Jacobum*, nominative, where the parallel with *Petri ad Clementem* wants *Christi*; translated as that genitive, "of Christ to James".
[^4]: **S.** Acts 1:23–26: two are put forward, the apostles pray, and the lot falls on Matthias.
[^5]: **C.** "The Evangelist" is John (side-note 174.24), who outlived Peter. The print's question marks after *maiori* and *subiectis* break one sentence: every clause hangs on *crederet quod*, and *capiti necessario regulandi* is a second dative beside *ut maiori*. **R.** A in the margin (174.22): *Nota tempore apostolorum et evangelistarum Clemens a Petro eligitur, ergo non secundum sanctitatem*, "Note: in the time of the apostles and evangelists Clement is chosen by Peter, therefore not according to holiness."
[^6]: **C.** "The third fiction" is the last of the three named at 168.27 (*triplex ficticia*). *Sentencia* is "teaching", the position held; *propinqua facto*, "closest to the fact", means to how popes are in fact made (side-note 175.1, "true to fact"). **R.** AB in the margin (175.4): *Romanum pontificem stat esse et non esse papam*, "It is possible to be Roman pontiff and not to be pope."
[^7]: **S.** FitzRalph, *Summa in quaestionibus Armenorum* 7.18 (the codices *cap. XIX*; Loserth: "loosely quoted"). Richard writes that the same man *legittime potest eligi duplici electione in romanum et in summum: ex quo sunt due dignitates distincte quamvis annexe* (Paris 1512, fol. 50r col. b), and holds the two annexed. "Naturally differing" is closer to the interlocutor John at the end of 7.17 (fol. 50r col. a), for whom the two pontificates *videntur penitus dispares* (LEDGER P08c-1).
[^8]: **C.** *Sequentem* agrees with *apostolicum* and governs *vestigia*, with *per graciam* between; read as *per graciam sequentem*, "through subsequent grace", it would leave *vestigia* without a verb. *Sive idem*, "or rather the same thing": the two elections are one. **R.** AB in the margin (175.12): *Eleccio pontificis summi non subiacet eleccioni humane*, "The election of the highest pontiff is not subject to human election."
[^9]: **S.** Acts 1:24, in Wyclif's own words; the Vulgate has *ostende quem elegeris ex his duobus unum*, "shew whether of these two thou hast chosen". *Scarioth* is Iscariot: "another Judas". *Casualiter*, "by chance", as by the lot. **T.** *Dicit* (176.2): Loserth, "Rectius: dat", "better *dat*", "gives". The print construes and is translated. **R.** AB in the margin (175.29): *Eleccio pontificis secundum reputacionem hominum*, "The election of the pontiff according to the reputation of men."
