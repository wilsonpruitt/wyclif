# sed/autem extract 06: chunks 15-h–17-f

### 15-h ¶1 · 356.17–358.18  (6 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 356] Innuunt quidam quod habent potestatem ad hoc, quam potestatem vocant dominium, et cum illa immediate sit data a Christo qui dat singulis suis sacerdotibus affluenter, illa potestas est prior, principalior et extencior quam potestas iure hereditario carnali, conquestu vel titulo mundano seculari domino acquisita. Et ad hoc vadunt quotquot conclusiones quas false contra scripturam discipuli Antichristi innuunt ad perturbacionem regnorum et diminucionem religionis christiane, in tantum quod illi Antichristi discipuli sunt capitales inimici illis quibus videntur carnaliter affici, quia dyabolus et omnia membra sua, sicut in imitando sibi ipsis continue sese dampnificant, sic obsunt vere paci aliorum quibus videntur proficere. Allegant **autem** illud Apostoli Roman. VIII°, 32: Proprio filio suo non pepercit, **sed** pro nobis omnibus tradidit illum. Quomodo non cum ipso omnia nobis donavit? Et Iª Corinth. III°, 22 [p. 357] Omnia vestra sunt, et Marci X°, 29, dicit Christus quod quicunque reliquerit mundum et carnem propter nomen suum, accipiet cencies tantum. Et in hoc mundo sacerdos (inquiunt) et specialiter religiosus habet ius quod est dominium ad toti mundo seculariter dominandum, et sic Christus docuit eos mundum relinquere, ut secularius conversentur. **Sed** quesito si layci relinquentes cuncta pro Christo erunt eis participes, si unus excludens alium facit sibi iniuriam, cum aliis que concernunt civile dominium, paciuntur angustias. Ideo defendunt unum capitaneum quem papam nominant sub cuius umbra infideliter sunt protecti. Et si obicitur quod curia Romana extinguit multos errores, dicitur quod de tanto est laudabilis et suis satrapis est parendum. Si **autem** vulpes parvule que demoliuntur particulares vineas habent inibi foveas in quibus convoluti criminibus callidius et constancius defendantur, quis dubitat quin nidus ille foret fons veneni ex cuius rivulis scateat toxica in fidelem ecclesiam militantem? Scutum **autem** et velamentum huius toxice videntur esse congregacio ista perversa et triplex velum quo mentes fidelium sunt cecate. Nam appellante pecunioso ad illam curiam, quid per mendacia, quid per mala exempla sceleris ibi capta et quid per tradiciones callidas tam de nostris quam de aliis inibi adinventas inductum est scutum infidelitatis ad membrum dyaboli contra regna et sanctam ecclesiam defendendam? Velum **autem** trimembre statuit inprimis principium quod non licet christiano dictum vel factum eorum discernere, cum sint dii subterranei quibus donata est potestas clavium par cum Christo, qui possunt scripturam sacram statuere, [p. 358] corrigere et mutare, et cum solum convincerentur ex scriptura sacra et factis propriis, patet quod non restat in eos medium accusandi. Secunda pars veli consequenter adicit quod subdi illis tamquam capiti sancte matris ecclesie quomodocunque vixerint est cuilibet christiano de necessitate salutis, et per consequens implere quidquid preceperint, cum non stat (ut inquiunt) quod eorum aliquis sit dampnandus. Tercia pars veli dyaboli est ficta plenitudo potestatis qua exterrent mundum cum pretensa legacione et excommunicacione sic quod, si quidquam decreverint in altera ecclesia faciendum, eo ipso oportet ut fiat; et quia reservarunt sibi cum istis armis potestatem conferendi beneficia ecclesie et superiores ac graviores persone ecclesie extere appetunt symoniace fastum et commodum temporale, ideo propter multitudinem, propter famam et propter terrorem istorum satellitum exterriti sunt pauci simplices dicere veritatem.

ENGLISH: Some hint that they have power for this, which power they call [lordship]{g:dominium}, and since it is given immediately by Christ, who gives to each of his priests abundantly, that power is prior, more principal and more extensive than the power acquired by a secular lord by carnal hereditary right, by conquest or by worldly [title]{g:titulus}. And to this tend all the conclusions which the disciples of Antichrist, falsely, against scripture, hint at, to the disturbance of kingdoms and the diminution of the Christian religion, insomuch that those disciples of Antichrist are the chief enemies of those to whom they seem carnally attached, because the devil and all his [members]{g:membrum}, just as in imitating [him] they continually harm themselves, so they hurt the true peace of others whom they seem to profit.[^1] Now they [allege]{g:allegare} that [saying] of the Apostle, Romans 8:32: "His own Son he spared not, but delivered him up for us all. How hath he not with him given us all things?"[^2] And 1 Corinthians 3:22: [357] "All things are yours"; and in Mark 10:29 Christ says that whoever has left the world and the flesh for his name will receive a hundred times as much.[^3] And in this world, they say, the priest, and especially the [religious]{g:religiosi}, has the [right]{g:ius} which is lordship, to exercise lordship over the whole world in secular fashion; and so Christ taught them to leave the world, that they might live more secularly. But when it is asked whether lay people who leave all things for Christ will be partakers with them, whether one who excludes another does him injury, with other things that concern civil lordship, they are in straits. Therefore they defend one [captain]{g:capitaneus} whom they name pope, under whose shadow they are faithlessly protected. And if [it is objected]{g:obicitur} that the [Roman curia]{g:curia romana} extinguishes many errors, the answer is that in so far it is praiseworthy, and [in so far] its [satraps]{g:satrapa} are to be obeyed. But if the little foxes that destroy particular vineyards have there holes in which, wrapped in crimes, they are defended more cunningly and more steadily, who doubts that that nest would be a fountain of poison from whose streams venom gushes into the faithful [church militant]{g:ecclesia militans}?[^4] Now the shield and covering of this venom seem to be that perverse congregation and the triple veil by which the minds of the faithful are blinded. For when a moneyed man appeals to that curia, what with lies, what with the evil examples of crime taken up there, and what with the cunning traditions devised there both by our people and by others, a shield of [unbelief]{g:infidelitas} has been brought in to defend the member of the devil against kingdoms and holy church.[^5] Now the threefold veil establishes first the principle that it is not lawful for a Christian to judge their word or deed, since they are gods beneath the earth, to whom is given the power of the keys equal with Christ, who can establish holy scripture, [358] correct and change it;[^6] and since they could be convicted only from holy scripture and their own deeds, it is clear that no means of accusing them remains. The second part of the veil consequently adds that to be subject to them as the head of holy mother church, however they have lived, is for every Christian of necessity of salvation,[^7] and consequently to fulfil whatever they have commanded, since it is not possible (as they say) that any of them should be damned. The third part of the devil's veil is the feigned [plenitude of power]{g:plenitudo potestatis}, by which they terrify the world with pretended legation and excommunication, so that, if they have decreed anything to be done in another church, by that very fact it must be done; and because with these arms they have reserved to themselves the power of conferring the benefices of the church, and the higher and weightier persons of the church abroad simoniacally covet pomp and temporal advantage, therefore, because of the multitude, because of the reputation and because of the terror of these satellites, the few simple men are frightened from speaking the truth.[^8]

### 15-h ¶2 · 358.19–359.4  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 358] **Sed** benedictus sponsus ecclesie qui occidit Gregorium XI. et dispersit suos complices quorum scelera per Urbanum VI. ecclesie sunt detecta, et qualitas veneni ex divisione eorum est quodammodo mitigata. Et utinam placeret sponso ecclesie perficere quod quidam prenosticant et alii imprecantur, scilicet quod numerus undenarius infamis et sterilis, prevaricans in totum decalogum, imponat finem numero pseudoprepositorum dicencium: Hereditate, civilitate et gladio possideamus Dei sanctuarium et Christi patrimonium. Ipse, inquiunt, crucifixus est ligno et apostolus mundo, ut nos occidamus gladio et nobis rebelles crucifigamus in patibulo, quia quam necessaria, inquiunt, fuit in primitiva ecclesia potestas faciendi miracula, tam necessaria est hodie potestas corporalis acquirendi et defendendi temporalia. Scimus tamen quod una potestas infinitum excedit aliam, et quod vita exproprietaria est vita seculari perfeccior et quod tam necessaria erant temporalia clero in primitiva [p. 359] ecclesia sicut modo et quod necessarior foret modo eorum subtraccio et suspensio rapine bonorum cum gaudio, quam tunc fuit, nec est illa ficta defensio fundabilis ex scriptura sacra.

ENGLISH: But blessed be the [bridegroom]{g:sponsus} of the church, who killed Gregory XI and scattered his accomplices, whose crimes have been laid bare to the church by Urban VI, and the quality of the poison has been somewhat mitigated by their division.[^9] And would that it pleased the bridegroom of the church to accomplish what some foretell and others pray for: namely that the number eleven, infamous and sterile, transgressing the whole decalogue,[^10] should put an end to the number of false superiors who say: "Let us possess by inheritance, [civil standing]{g:civilitas} and sword the sanctuary of God and the patrimony of Christ."[^11] He, they say, was crucified on the wood, and the apostle to the world,[^12] that we might kill with the sword and crucify on the gibbet those who rebel against us; because, they say, as necessary as was the power of working miracles in the primitive church, so necessary today is the bodily power of acquiring and defending [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}. Yet we know that the one power infinitely exceeds the other, and that the [propertyless]{g:exproprietarii} life is more perfect than the secular life, and that temporal goods were as necessary to the clergy in the primitive [359] church as now, and that their withdrawal, and the [suspension]{g:suspencio} of the plunder of goods with joy, would be more necessary now than it was then;[^13] nor is that feigned defence founded on holy scripture.[^14]

### 16-a ¶2 · 359.14–360.2  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 359] Prima itaque racio sic formatur: quam licite recipiunt sancti elemosinas pro relevandis indigenciis presentis vite, tam licite ipsas elemosinas servant idem sancti distribuendas indigentibus in perpetuum. Non enim videtur racio diversitatis quin, si licet recipere elemosinas propter indigenciam propriam relevandam, a pari erit licitum ipsas elemosinas sic collatas propter indigenciam futuram perpetuo conservare; ergo si licet indigenti recipere elemosinas propter se relevandum et hoc absque peccato, a pari licet conservare elemosinas sic receptas in perpetuum ad usus pauperum absque peccato. **Sed** beatus Silvester in faciendo sibi licitum simpliciter nullo modo verisimiliter peccavit; ergo beatus Silvester in retinendo dotacionem [p. 360] perpetuam ecclesie nullo modo verisimiliter peccavit.

ENGLISH: The first reason, then, is framed thus: as lawfully as the saints receive [alms]{g:elemosina} for relieving the needs of the present life, so lawfully do the same saints keep those alms to be distributed to the needy in perpetuity. For there seems to be no reason for a difference why, if it is lawful to receive alms in order to relieve one's own need, it will not by parity be lawful to keep in perpetuity the alms so conferred on account of future need; therefore if it is lawful for a needy man to receive alms in order to relieve himself, and this without sin, by parity it is lawful to keep alms so received in perpetuity for the uses of the poor without sin.[^2] Now blessed Silvester, in doing what was lawful to him [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}, in all likelihood in no way sinned; therefore blessed Silvester, in retaining the [360] perpetual endowment of the church, in all likelihood in no way sinned.[^3]

### 16-a ¶3 · 360.3–362.27  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 360] Quantum ad istud argumentum, non oportet respondere ad formam, nec vidi probacionem assumpti, cum Christus recepit elemosinas temporales, sicut et apostoli ab ipso receperant pro temporali indigencia pauperum relevanda (ut patet Joh. VI°), et tamen non legitur apostolos recepisse elemosinas pro pauperibus perpetue conservandas. Et quantum ad illud brogardicum, ergo per idem vel questionem vagam que est ultimum refugium simplicis arguentis qua queritur que sit racio diversitatis, retorqueo utrumque in arguentem. Si Christus docuit nos in suis apostolis non solicitari in crastinum, quia sufficit diei malicia sua, Matthei VI°, 34, et non videtur racio, quare non preciperet eis providere de elemosinacione perpetua, per idem preciperet eis providere de elemosinacione perpetua; vel racio diversitatis confirmatur ex hoc quod tempore apostolorum fuerunt homines devociores et ad elemosinas prompciores et post avariores, ergo fuisset congruum quod ipsi obligassent suos heredes ad elemosinas perpetuas. Si dicatur quod voluntas Christi est racio diversitatis sufficiens, revera sic doctrina Christi exemplata future ecclesie est racio diversitatis, et forma sufficiens ad movendum nos clericos ritum gentilem deserere et omnem solicitudinem nostram inpresenciarum proicientes in ipso confidere. Non solicitemur ergo quomodo providebitur nunc ad mille annos Christi pauperibus de pabulo corporali, **sed** permittentes mortuos sepelire mortuos suos sequamur Christum attencius et sic christianis futuris ad mille annos magis proficimus, et ita (ut dixi superius) perpetua elemosinacio est dotanti inductivum blaspheme superbie et elemosinato fomentum mundane confidencie et vigilanter requirendi divinum [p. 361] auxilium aversive desidie, et utrumque istorum per elemosinacionem temporalem excluditur. Et istam racionem assignant doctores, quare Deus non particulariter premunivit primos parentes, ut caveant de calliditate serpentis promissa eius fallacia detestando. Nam dixit illis illabendo generale principium, cui innitentes, prudenter confidendo in domino et consulendo ipsum in ambiguis, amplius meruissent, et sic negata penultima consequencia negatur assumptum sequens, scilicet quod beatus Silvester in faciendo sibi licitum simpliciter nullo modo verisimiliter peccavit. Nam omnis conversacio secularis est inseparabiliter cum peccato, sicut dicit fides scripture et Augustinus persepe commemorat; et cum multe conversaciones seculares sint licite ac meritorie, patet quod beatus Silvester in faciendo sibi licitum sepe peccavit, sicut de se confitetur beatus Evangelista qui fuit Silvestro sanccior. Unde notet homo formam dotacionis, XCVIª dist. Constantinus, quomodo imperator tribuit Silvestro potestatem, vigorem et honorificenciam imperialem, decernendo quod habeat primatum super quatuor sedes et princeps sacerdotibus tocius mundi existat. Consideret secundo quomodo tradidit beato Silvestro et successoribus suis Lateranense palacium, diadema et frigium, superhumerale, clamidem purpuream et tunicam coccineam cum aliis insigniis et potestatibus imperialibus, et breviter cum omnibus dignitatibus quas papa vendicat non fundabilibus ex scriptura, ut puta, quod equitet super equum candidum cuius frenum cesar tenuit et alias reverencias quas mundo cecati in ipsis preponderant sibi exhibuit. Nam illud [p. 362] affeccione ceca quis tribuit quod in se reputat preciosum. Consideret ergo evangelicus attente ordinem predicte carte, et miror si excuset beatum Silvestrum ab omni elacione, ab omissione contemptus mundi et defectu informacionis imperatoris et populi. Unde exhinc irreligiose accepta fuit occasio faciendo scisma in ecclesia instinctu dyaboli, ut notat decretalis, XCVª dist., Olim, ex dicto Jeronimi. Et hec creditur racio, quare ecclesia orientalis remanens in sua pauperie detestata est monstruosam superbiam occidentalis ecclesie. Unde legitur quod ecclesia Grecorum misit domino Johanni pape XXII magnam epistolam ad exhortacionem suam qua suadebat eos sue coactive iurisdiccioni subici plenarie responsivam et in fine inseruisse talem clausulam: Potenciam tuam summam super tuos subditos firmiter credimus, superbiam tuam summam tollerare non possumus, avariciam tuam summam saciare non intendimus, dominus tecum, quia dominus nobiscum est. Consideret ergo procurator Romane ecclesie cum approbabili: beatus Augustinus fuit sciencior, religiosior et sanccior beato Silvestro; ipse **autem** asserit irrevocabiliter cum aliis sanctis eum sequentibus: si quid utile est, in scriptura sacra invenitur, et moneat ut fautores illius dotacionis dicentes quod fuit omnino sine peccato ipsam fundent clarius ex scriptura, quod nulli usque hodie potuerunt.

ENGLISH: As to this argument, there is no need to reply to its form; nor have I seen a proof of the [assumption]{g:assumptum}, since Christ received temporal alms, as the apostles too had received them from him, for relieving the temporal need of the poor (as is clear from John 6), and yet we do not read that the apostles received alms to be kept in perpetuity for the poor.[^4] And as to that brocard, "therefore, by the same token", or the vague question which is the last refuge of a simple arguer, in which it is asked what the reason for the difference is, I turn both back on the arguer.[^5] If Christ taught us, in his apostles, not to be solicitous for the morrow, because sufficient for the day is its own evil (Matthew 6:34), and there seems to be no reason why he should not command them to provide for perpetual [almsgiving]{g:elemosinacio}, then by the same token he would command them to provide for perpetual almsgiving; or else the reason for the difference is confirmed from this, that in the time of the apostles men were more devout and readier to give alms, and afterwards more avaricious: therefore it would have been fitting that they should have bound their heirs to perpetual alms.[^6] If it is said that the will of Christ is a sufficient reason for the difference, truly in this way the [doctrine]{g:doctrina} of Christ, set as an example to the future church, is the reason for the difference, and a sufficient form to move us clerics to desert the heathen rite and, casting all our care at present upon him, to trust in him.[^7] Let us not, therefore, be solicitous how provision will be made now, a thousand years ahead, for the poor of Christ in bodily food; but, letting "the dead bury their dead", let us follow Christ more attentively, and so we profit the Christians of a thousand years to come the more. And so (as I said above) perpetual almsgiving is, to the endower, a thing that induces blasphemous pride, and to the [almsman]{g:elemosinatus} a kindling of worldly confidence and of a sloth averse to vigilantly seeking divine [361] help; and each of these is excluded by almsgiving from time to time.[^8] And the doctors assign this reason why God did not forewarn our first parents in particular to beware of the craftiness of the serpent by detesting his promised [deceit]{g:fallacia}: for he spoke to them by [flowing into]{g:illabi} them a general principle, leaning on which, prudently trusting in the Lord and consulting him in doubtful things, they would have merited more.[^9] And so, the penultimate [inference]{g:consequencia} being denied, the assumption that follows is denied, namely that blessed Silvester, in doing what was lawful to him without qualification, in all likelihood in no way sinned. For every secular [manner of life]{g:conversacio} is inseparably joined with sin, as the faith of scripture says and Augustine very often recalls; and since many secular manners of life are lawful and meritorious, it is clear that blessed Silvester, in doing what was lawful to him, often sinned, as the blessed Evangelist, who was holier than Silvester, confesses of himself.[^10] Hence let a man note the form of the endowment, Dist. 96, *Constantinus*: how the emperor granted to Silvester imperial power, vigour and honour, decreeing that he should have primacy over the four sees and should be prince over the priests of the whole world.[^11] Let him consider, second, how he handed over to blessed Silvester and his successors the Lateran palace, the diadem and the phrygium, the superhumeral, the purple chlamys and the scarlet tunic, with the other imperial insignia and powers; in short, with all the dignities which the [pope]{g:papa} claims that cannot be grounded in scripture: for instance, that he should ride on a white horse whose bridle Caesar held; and he showed him other marks of reverence on which those blinded by the world set great weight. For one grants with [362] blind affection what one reckons precious in itself. Let the evangelical man, therefore, consider attentively the order of the aforesaid charter, and I wonder whether it excuses blessed Silvester from all elation, from the omission of contempt of the world, and from a failure to instruct the emperor and the people.[^12] Hence, from this, occasion was irreligiously taken for making schism in the church at the devil's instigation, as the decretal notes, Dist. 95, *Olim*, from a saying of Jerome.[^13] And this is believed to be the reason why the eastern church, remaining in its poverty, has detested the monstrous pride of the western church. Hence we read that the church of the Greeks sent to the lord Pope John XXII a great letter in reply to his exhortation, by which he urged them to submit fully to his [coactive]{g:coactivus} jurisdiction, and that it inserted at the end such a clause as this: "Your supreme power over your subjects we firmly believe; your supreme pride we cannot bear; your supreme avarice we do not intend to satisfy. The Lord be with you, for the Lord is with us."[^14] Let the [proctor]{g:procurator} of the Roman church, therefore, consider [this] with an approvable [argument]: blessed Augustine was more learned, more religious and holier than blessed Silvester; now he asserts irrevocably, with other saints following him, "if anything is useful, it is found in holy scripture"; and let him urge that the abettors of that endowment, who say that it was altogether without sin, ground it more clearly from scripture, which no one has been able to do to this day.[^15]

### 16-a ¶4 · 362.28–363.12  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 362] Et si obicitur quod eque evidenter credendum est errori cuiuscunque hominis in scriptis sicut in factis, et sic periret testificacio ex scripturis. **Sed** quod falsum assumitur (ut beatus Augustinus declarat epistola VIIIª ad Jeronynum), cum ex veritate scripture [p. 363] credimus quod propheta David peccavit cum uxore Urie et quod Petrus peccavit tempore passionis et post ascensionem, tamen scripta eorum credimus esse summe autentica, et sic maiori probabilitate credimus scriptis famosorum doctorum quam nudis factis, tum quia communiter ex maiori deliberacione, ex minori temptacione et pro ulteriori explanacione scribunt personis gravibus nec posterius revocant. **Sed** alii multi sancti post et ante in sentenciam illam concordant, **sed** (ut reor) nec ex scriptura nec racione fundabitur quod beatus Silvester non peccavit recipiendo dotacionem cesaream.

ENGLISH: And if [it is objected]{g:obicitur} that a man's error, whoever he is, is to be believed as evidently in his writings as in his deeds, and so testimony from writings would perish: [the answer is] that what is assumed is false (as blessed Augustine declares in the eighth letter to Jerome), since, although from the truth of scripture [363] we believe that the prophet David sinned with the wife of Urias, and that Peter sinned at the time of the passion and after the ascension, yet we believe their writings to be supremely [authentic]{g:autenticus}; and so we believe the writings of famous doctors with greater probability than their bare deeds, both because they commonly write from greater deliberation, under less temptation and for further explanation, to weighty persons, and do not afterwards retract.[^16] Now many other saints, after and before, agree in that [teaching]{g:sentencia}; but (as I think) it will be grounded neither from scripture nor from reason that blessed Silvester did not sin in receiving the caesarean endowment.[^17]

### 16-b ¶1 · 363.13–364.6  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 363] Secundo sic arguitur: Quidquid fuerit meritorium beato Silvestro, nullo modo fuit sibi demeritorium, cum iste denominaciones ex opposito distinguuntur, **sed** fuit meritorium beato Silvestro recipere temporalia distribuenda pauperibus in perpetuum, hoc idem nullo modo fuit sibi demeritorium. Hic dicitur quod theologus logicus ponit calumpniam in maiori, cum idem opus successive est prius meritorium, et post demeritorium vel econtra; et sic conversacio beati Silvestri que ante baptizacionem vel conversionem fuit sibi demeritoria postea fuit sibi meritoria. Sic enim dicimus motum difformem quoad tempus vel subiectum, licet maior pars eius quantitativa fuerit uniformis. Minor **autem** non probatur, **sed** sub verbis illis capit calumpniam, quia nimis onerosum foret tali sancto recipere tale perpetuum officium, sive in se sive in suis ministris; nec probatur minor ista per hoc quod relevare pauperes indigentes est simpliciter meritorium cuicunque, tum quia multi sunt quibus hoc non convenit, tum secundo quia multis infidelibus et ypocritis in hoc demerentibus hoc convenit, tum tercio quia hoc dato non exhinc sequitur quod meritorium fuerat beato Silvestro recipere elemosinas [p. 364] distribuendas pauperibus in perpetuum. **Sed** finaliter, dato quod hoc fuit meritorium, sic quod nullo modo demeritorium beato Silvestro, non exhinc sequitur quod beatus Silvester nullo modo peccavit sic faciendo. Homo enim peccat venialiter merendo continue et non demerendo **sed** remissius promerendo.

ENGLISH: Second, it is argued thus: whatever was meritorious for blessed Silvester was in no way demeritorious for him, since these denominations are distinguished as opposites; now it was meritorious for blessed Silvester to receive [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} to be distributed to the poor in perpetuity; [therefore] this same thing was in no way demeritorious for him.[^1] To this [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that the theologian who is a logician lays a challenge against the major, since the same work is successively first meritorious and afterwards demeritorious, or conversely; and so the [manner of life]{g:conversacio} of blessed Silvester, which before his baptism or conversion was demeritorious for him, was afterwards meritorious for him.[^2] For so we call a motion difform with respect to time or subject, although the greater quantitative part of it was uniform.[^3] Now the minor is not proved, but under those words it is open to challenge, because it would be too burdensome for such a saint to receive such a perpetual office, whether in himself or in his ministers; nor is this minor proved by the fact that to relieve the needy poor is meritorious [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} for anyone, both because there are many to whom this is not fitting, and secondly because it is fitting for many [unbelievers]{g:infidelis} and hypocrites who demerit in it, and thirdly because, granting this, [it does not follow]{g:sequitur} from it that it had been meritorious for blessed Silvester to receive [alms]{g:elemosina} [364] to be distributed to the poor in perpetuity. But finally, granted that this was meritorious, so that it was in no way demeritorious for blessed Silvester, it does not follow from this that blessed Silvester in no way sinned in so doing. For a man sins venially while meriting continually, not by demeriting but by meriting more remissly.[^4]

### 16-b ¶2 · 364.7–365.6  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 364] Tercio sic arguitur: Quicquid potuit Silvester papa licite et absque peccato in usum suum recipere, potuit sic recipere in usus aliorum; sic enim absque peccato recipiunt domini temporales predia, redditus et huiusmodi, tam sibi quam aliis, **sed** licite et absque peccato eciam veniali recepit Silvester temporalia privilegia pro se ipso; ergo sic fecit vel potuit pro aliis. Hic respondeo concedendo asumptum et conclusionem illatam, **sed** non credo aliquos sanctos asserere dominos temporales absque peccato recipere redditus temporales, cum vivens activam vitam peccat continue mortaliter vel venialiter; et sic non credo quod absque peccato veniali recepit Silvester temporalia privilegia pro se ipso. ENGLISH: First, by this: that the [church]{g:ecclesia} of the [clergy]{g:clerus} lived thus [without property]{g:exproprietarie} by the [ordinance]{g:ordinacio} of Christ and the apostles up to the caesarean [endowment]{g:dotacio}; and even to this day that life remains in the more religious clerics, and I declared above that this [manner of life]{g:conversacio} is more [authentic]{g:autenticus}, more meritorious and more secure. ### 16-e ¶4 · 376.1–376.25  (1 sed/autem)

Quid est opus custodire quod nihil adiuvat? An ignoramus quantum auri atque argenti de templo domini Assirii sustulerunt? ENGLISH: The knight ought therefore to go to the lord of the land and tell him the conditions of such a college; and the whole kingdom would be bound to expel such a poisonous college and, with the goods which such a legion would occupy, if they are alms, to relieve the knight.[^12] And it is clear that this could be done lawfully, from the saying of blessed Ambrose placed in the decrees, [Causa] 12, q. 2, *Aurum*: "The church has gold," he says, "not to keep but to lay out, and to bring help in necessities. What need is there to guard what is of no help? Are we ignorant how much gold and silver the Assyrians carried off from the temple of the Lord? Does not a priest do better to melt it down for the sustenance of the poor, if other supplies are lacking, than that a sacrilegious enemy should defile and carry it off? Will not the Lord say: Why did you suffer so many needy to die of hunger? And certainly you had gold, with which you might have ministered sustenance. Why were so many captives led away into trade and, not redeemed, slain by the enemy? It had been better that you kept the vessels of the living than those of metals. To these things no answer could be brought. For what would you say: I feared lest the temple of God should lack its adornment? The sacraments," he says, "do not seek gold, nor do things please by gold which are not bought with gold. The adornment of sacred things is the redemption of captives; and truly those are precious vessels which redeem souls from death. ### 16-f ¶4 · 378.16–380.5  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 378] Hic dico, primo quod conclusio deducta est concedenda de communitate civili que est capax civilis dominii, ut patet de multis communitatibus civium que licite civiliter dominantur. **Sed** procul hoc ad inferendum quod clerico (in quantum huiusmodi) licet dominari civiliter, et multo periculosius foret communitatem cleri dominari civiliter, cum error ille vix foret extinguibilis, **sed** tamquam cancer serperet ampliandoque procederet, quousque magnam partem regnorum inficeret. III° et VII°. Layci, inquit, quia terrena et terrene vite necessaria tractant, pars corporis Christi sinistra sunt. Clerici vero, [p. 379] quoniam ea que ad spiritualem vitam pertinent dispensant quasi dextera pars sunt corporis Christi. Et post interpretando utrumque terminum sic infert: Clericus nisi Deum et ea que ad Deum spectant habere non debet, cui statutum est decimis et oblacionibus que Deo offeruntur sustentari. Laicis vero christianis fidelibus terrena possidere conceditur, **sed** clericis spiritualia committuntur, sicut fuit in veteri testamento. Et cap. VII° declarat quomodo quedam ecclesie Christi devocione fidelium conceduntur salvo iure terrene potestatis, ne fiat confusio, cum nihil inordinatum approbare potest Deus. And afterwards, interpreting each term, he infers thus: "A cleric ought to have nothing but God and the things that belong to God, he for whom it is appointed to be sustained by the [tithes]{g:decime} and offerings which are offered to God. And in chapter 7 he declares how certain things are granted to the churches of Christ by the devotion of the faithful, saving the right of the earthly power, lest there be confusion, since God can approve nothing disordered. Hence earthly princes sometimes grant the church the bare use, and sometimes the use and the power of exercising justice, which a cleric cannot exercise through ecclesiastical persons or clerical individuals. Concedit itaque quod Christus Deus noster est christianus, nec sequitur, inquit, si a Christo dicitur quilibet christianus, quod Christus ille non sit christianus: sic a creatore dicitur quelibet creatura, Christus est creator et sic ab eo dicitur quelibet creatura et tamen ipsemet est creatura, ut ego assero.

ENGLISH: The second reply, to the second little argument, denies [the inference]{g:consequencia}; but it seems [to hold]: if there is no church except a multitude of Christians, now Christ is not a Christian, then Christ is not a part or member of the church. He therefore grants that Christ our God is a Christian; nor does it follow, he says, if every Christian is named from Christ, that that Christ is not a Christian: so every creature is named from the creator; Christ is the creator, and so every creature is named from him, and yet he himself is a creature, as I assert.[^15]

### 17-d ¶1 · 398.27–400.14  (8 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 398] Hic primo notandum quod, si locus a simili (quantumcunque fuerit similitudo insufficiens) valeat ad probandum intentum, periret omnis consequencia et foret omne signabile concedendum, eo quod omnes creature sunt similiores ad invicem, quam natura divina est similis nature create. Ideo necesse est [p. 399] arguentem vel respondentem per locum a simili docere sufficienciam similitudinis quantum attinet ad propositum. Secundo notandum quod Christus est simul creator et creatura, nec posset esse ista nisi esset simul duarum naturarum utraque, sic quod secundum naturam divinam est creator et secundum humanitatem est creatura; correspondenter ad propositum, si ipse simul esset Christus et christianus, tunc essent due nature, secundum quarum unam esset Cristus et secundum aliam christianus, **sed** nec secundum deitatem est Christus pars ecclesie; cum secundum illam non potest parcialiter quicquam componere, nec secundum humanitatem est ipse christianus **sed** caput omnium christianorum dicente Augustino super Johannem, Sermone CXXIII°, quod super petram que erat Christus fundata est ecclesia; a qua petra Petrus figurans ecclesiam nomen accepit: Non enim a Petro petra **sed** Petrus a petra, sicut non Christus a christiano **sed** christianus a Christo vocatur. **Sed** modo secundum istam ficticiam a christiano et Petro eque dicitur Christus, sicut econtra, cum verbum Dei sit christianus, ut false fingitur. Ulterius declaratur tam per grammaticos quam doctores ex testimonio scripture quod multi erant Christi in veteri testamento, quia reges, sacerdotes et prophete qui crismate erant uncti. Illud **autem** gratanter accepto cum modificacione tamen quod intelligatur de unccione spirituali qua anima ungitur gracia predestinacionis; solum enim membrum Christi unitum gracia predestinacionis et omne huiusmodi est proprie christianus. Non enim unccio corporalis [p. 400] facit per se christianum, eo quod tunc utraque barba Aaron scilicet superior et inferior foret cum suis partibus christianus. Nec sequitur quod Jesus Christus sit christianus, licet Deus unxit illum oleo leticie pre consortibus suis (ut dicitur Psalmo XLIV°, 8), quia non est membrum Christi, **sed** caput tocius ecclesie. Unde vere dicitur quod Christus est nomen appellativum, autonomatice conveniens Jesu nostro; ideo propter illam spiritualem unccionem derivatam a Jesu nostro dicimur Christiani et non Jesuani. Omnes enim convenimus cum eo in unccione secundum graciam predestinacionis a qua veste dicimur christiani, non **autem** sumus sic Christo conformiter salvatores, ideo non dicimur Jesuani.

ENGLISH: Here it must first be noted that, if [the topic from likeness]{g:locus a simili} (however insufficient the likeness may be) availed to prove what is intended, every [inference]{g:consequencia} would perish and everything signifiable would have to be granted, since all creatures are more like one another than the divine nature is like created nature. Therefore it is necessary [399] for one arguing or answering by the topic from likeness to show the sufficiency of the likeness as far as concerns the matter in hand.[^1] Second, it must be noted that Christ is at once creator and creature, nor could he be these unless he were at once of both natures, so that according to the divine nature he is creator and according to the humanity he is creature. Correspondingly to the matter in hand: if he were at once Christ and Christian, then there would be two natures, according to one of which he would be Christ and according to the other Christian; but neither according to [the Godhead]{g:deitas} is Christ part of [the church]{g:ecclesia}, since according to it he cannot compose anything as a part; nor according to the humanity is he a Christian, but [head]{g:caput} of all Christians,[^2] as Augustine says on John, Sermon 123, that upon the rock which was Christ the church is founded, from which rock Peter, figuring the church, took his name: "For not from Peter the rock, but Peter from the rock; as not Christ from the Christian, but the Christian is called from Christ."[^3] But now, according to this [fiction]{g:ficticia}, Christ is named from the Christian and from Peter equally as conversely, since the Word of God is a Christian, as is falsely feigned. Further, it is declared as well by grammarians as by doctors, from the testimony of scripture, that there were many christs in the old testament, namely kings, [priests]{g:sacerdos} and prophets, who were anointed with chrism. And that I gladly accept, with this qualification however: that it be understood of the spiritual anointing by which the soul is anointed with the grace of predestination; for only a [member]{g:membrum} of Christ united by the grace of predestination, and every such, is properly a Christian.[^4] For bodily anointing [400] does not of itself make a Christian, since then both beards of Aaron, namely the upper and the lower, would with their parts be a Christian. Nor [does it follow]{g:sequitur} that Jesus Christ is a Christian, although God anointed him "with the oil of gladness above his fellows" (as is said in Psalm 44:8), because he is not a member of Christ but head of the whole church.[^5] Hence it is truly said that "Christ" is an appellative name, belonging [by antonomasia]{g:autonomatice} to our Jesus; therefore on account of that spiritual anointing derived from our Jesus we are called Christians and not Jesuans. For we all agree with him in the anointing according to the grace of predestination, from which garment we are called Christians; but we are not in that way saviours in conformity with Christ, therefore we are not called Jesuans.[^6]

### 17-d ¶3 · 400.24–402.4  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 400] Ad primum patet quod non plus sequitur quam si lapis dicitur quasi ledens pedem, et gladius ledit pedem, ergo gladius ille est lapis: Non enim omnis interpretacio est diffinicio. Et per hoc tollitur secunda argucia; nam tota trinitas et angeli, ymmo quelibet substancia creata observat legem Christi, et tamen non pocius quam apostema unctum crismate prescitus dicitur christianus. Quoad tercium dicitur quod, admisso [p. 401] isto triplici assumpto, quod tamen non probatur, non exinde sequitur per locum a simili quod Jesus principium secte nostre sit christianus, quia in dictis generibus fuit unum principium ante predictos, **sed** non potest esse homo prior dignitate prius principians genus nostrum, quam fuit dominus Jesus Christus; nam fuit ante Jacob unus vir clarius videns Deum, fuit ante Romulum unus homo principium generis Romanorum, et fuit ante Arrium unus heresiarcha qui pertinaciter asseruit Jesum nostrum fuisse solummodo creaturam, ut patet de dyabolo et principibus sacerdotum. **Sed** fundamentum aliud ecclesie vel generis christiani nemo potest ponere preter id quod positum est Christus Jesus. Ideo secundum Augustinum super Johannem, Omelia ultima, non a christiano dicitur Christus **sed** econtra; aliquid enim oportet nomen christiani addere super Christum, sicut oportet residuum domus presupponere fundamentum. Unde (si non fallor) non habetur testimonium a sanctis doctoribus quod Jesus Christus fuerat christianus. Non enim sequitur hoc ex sensu Crisostomi, Omelia LIIª Operis Imperfecti, dicentis quod ex hoc dicitur aliquis christianus, quod servat iusticiam, nam intencio sua est dicere quod solum membrum Christi, sive militans sive triumphans, est proprie christianus; aliter enim diceret quod trinitas et omne membrum dyaboli, ymmo quelibet creatura foret christianus, cum omne tale modo suo servat iusticiam. Nam de qualibet creatura iustum est quod se habeat ut Deus ipsam decreverit se habere. Nec sequitur: Porphirius dicit quod genus quandoque sumitur pro generacionis [p. 402] principio et quandoque pro ipsa generacione, ergo Christus Deus noster est christianus. **Sed** colorate infertur quod Jesus Christus noster sit genus christianorum, quia illius generacionis principium.

ENGLISH: To the first it is clear that it follows no more than if "stone" (*lapis*) is said as if "hurting the foot" (*ledens pedem*), and a sword hurts the foot, therefore that sword is a stone: for not every interpretation is a definition.[^8] And by this the second [quibble]{g:argucia} is removed; for the whole Trinity and the angels, indeed every created substance, observes the law of Christ, and yet is no more called a Christian than one [foreknown]{g:prescitus}, an [abscess]{g:apostema} anointed with chrism, is called a Christian.[^9] As to the third, [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that, granting [401] this threefold [assumption]{g:assumptum}, which however is not proved, it does not follow from it by the topic from likeness that Jesus, the beginning of our sect, is a Christian; because in the said races there was one beginning before those named, but there cannot be a man prior in dignity, beginning our race earlier, than was the Lord Jesus Christ. For there was before Jacob one man seeing God more clearly; there was before Romulus one man who was the beginning of the race of the Romans; and there was before Arius one heresiarch who pertinaciously asserted that our Jesus was only a creature, as is clear of the devil and of the chief priests.[^10] But other foundation of the church or of the Christian race no man can lay, but that which is laid: Christ Jesus. Therefore, according to Augustine on John, the last homily, Christ is not named from the Christian but conversely; for the name of Christian must add something over Christ, as the rest of a house must presuppose the foundation.[^11] Hence (if I am not mistaken) no testimony is had from the holy doctors that Jesus Christ was a Christian. For this does not follow from the sense of Chrysostom, Homily 52 of the *Unfinished Work*, saying that someone is called a Christian from this, that he keeps justice; for his intention is to say that only a member of Christ, whether militant or triumphant, is properly a Christian; for otherwise he would say that the Trinity and every member of the devil, indeed every creature, would be a Christian, since every such thing keeps justice in its own way. For of every creature it is just that it should be as God has decreed it to be.[^12] Nor does it follow: Porphyry says that genus is sometimes taken for the beginning [402] of generation and sometimes for the generation itself, therefore Christ our God is a Christian. But it is inferred [with colour]{g:colorare} that our Jesus Christ is the genus of Christians, because [he is] the beginning of that generation.[^13]

### 17-d ¶4 · 402.5–402.24  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 402] **Sed** finaliter colligitur ex dictis quomodo ista sentencia obviat sibi ipsi. Nam respondendo ad primum articulum dicitur conclusionaliter quod vel ante incarnacionem erat ecclesia catholica, et adiungitur una alia conclusio gloriosa, scilicet quod ista ecclesia que incepit in morte Christi per effluxum sanguinis et aque a corpore eius diu fuit ante incarnacionem Christi, **sed** nunquam ante dictam incarnacionem fuit ipsa ecclesia, **sed** synagoga. Mirantur logici quo sensu diceretur ista conclusio tamquam logica, cum ista ecclesia cepit esse, antequam fuit synagoga, et per consequens, antequam fuit ecclesia. Ista ergo ecclesia non cepit hora mortis Christi, cum ante incarnacionem fuit synagoga; nec potest hoc pertinenter verificari de aggregato ex talibus personis et forma a qua denominatur ecclesia, quia illud nec fuit ante incarnacionem nec fuit synagoga nec incepit per dictum effluxum, cum pro tunc secundum istam opinionem non fuit Christus, eo quod ex fide scripture Johannis XX° Christus tunc fuit mortuus.

ENGLISH: But finally it is gathered from what has been said how this [teaching]{g:sentencia} goes against itself. For in replying to the first article it is said by way of [conclusion]{g:conclusio}: "or else before the incarnation there was a catholic church"; and another glorious conclusion is joined to it, namely that this church which began in the death of Christ, through the outflow of blood and water from his body, existed long before the incarnation of Christ, but never before the said incarnation was it the church, but the synagogue.[^14] Logicians wonder in what sense this conclusion would be called logical, since this church began to be before it was the synagogue and, consequently, before it was the church. This church therefore did not begin at the hour of Christ's death, since before the incarnation it was the synagogue; nor can this be verified pertinently of the aggregate of such persons and the form from which it is named church, because that neither existed before the incarnation, nor was the synagogue, nor began through the said outflow, since at that time, according to this [opinion]{g:opinio}, Christ was not, because, [from the faith of scripture]{g:ex fide}, John 20, Christ was then dead.[^15]

### 17-e ¶1 · 402.25–404.26  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 402] Item, iuxta hec dicta sacerdotes, prophete et reges legis veteris erant Christi ut Israel, Aaron, David, et per consequens, Israelite, Aaronite et domus David fuerant christiani, quia discipuli Christi servantes iusticiam, et ita secundum omnem descripcionem quam doctor dat de christiano, sequitur quod ante incarnacionem fuerant christiani. Et in confirmacionem illius sic scribit mihi: Qui, inquit, observant legem Christi omnes dicuntur christiani, prout notat beatus Augustinus, Epistola XLIIª, [p. 403] allegans Apostolum Romanorum XIª dicentem quod non omnes qui ex Israel sunt sunt Israelite; pocius, inquit Augustinus, christiani sunt non qui secundum Ismael filii sunt carnis, **sed** secundum Isaac filii sunt promissionis ad spirituale ministerium pertinentes. Ex isto processu Augustini bene notato sequitur quod patres veteris testamenti fuerant christiani et membra ecclesie; quod negatur. Omnes, inquit, viventes secundum legem Christi sunt filii Abrahe et veri christiani. Ex qua conclusione patet quod per tempus veteris testamenti fuerant christiani, vel secundo quod modo nulli sunt filii Abrahe, vel quod in sancto triduo beata virgo non fuerat christiana, quia tunc non fuit ita quod ipsa observat legem Christi, cum tunc fuit ita quod non est Christus. Unde finaliter videtur inconsequenter dictum quod fuit ecclesia catholica cuius beata virgo fuit pars pro triduo mortis Christi, primo quia, posito quod loquamur pro illo triduo, ex tibi dubio sola virgo est persona fidelis superstes, et cum ipsa non sit christiana ex tibi dubio, non est multitudo omnium christianorum. Similiter, per totam vitam Christi corpoream ante mortem non fiebat ecclesia (ut dicitur), quia solum pro tempore fluxus sanguinis et aque de suo latere, quod fuit post mortem Christi. Similiter, posito quod loquamur pro dicto triduo et arguamus a diffinicione ad diffinitum: Omnis ecclesia catholica est universalis multitudo omnium christianorum habencium ex integro rectam et eandem fidem, per Christum et suos apostolos promulgatam et postea a sanctis patribus in conciliis generalibus declaratam, **sed** nunc est ecclesia catholica cuius mater domini est pars, ergo nunc est universalis multitudo omnium christianorum, habencium ex integro rectam et eandem fidem per Christum et suos apostolos [p. 404] promulgatam et postea a sanctis patribus in conciliis generalibus declaratam; consequens falsum pro dicto triduo, ergo antecedens. **Sed** minor fuit tunc vera ut datur; ergo cum tunc fuit dicta consequencia, ut suppono, relinquitur quod tunc fuit maior falsa, que est descripcio universalis ecclesie, ut fingitur perpetuo permansura. Quod **autem** consequens fuit tunc falsum, patet quia dato per impossibile quod tunc fuit multitudo omnium christianorum habencium ex integro rectam et eandem fidem per Christum et suos apostolos promulgatam, adhuc non fuit tunc verum quod illa fides sit postea a sanctis patribus in conciliis generalibus declarata. Sequitur enim illa fides a sanctis patribus in conciliis generalibus declarata; ergo nec post nec ante nec unquam est a sanctis patribus in conciliis generalibus declarata; si (inquam) sufficeret quod fides illa erit a sanctis patribus in conciliis generalibus declarata tunc sequitur cum veris quod diu ante mortem Christi fuit ecclesia catholica; quod negatur. **Sed** male, cum tunc fuit christiana religio, eo quod tunc fuit abbas secundum regulam apostolicam, regens suum capitulum de electis apostolis. Ipsi enim observarunt tunc melius quam nos facimus religionem christianam, et ex nomine talis religionis dicto primo Retractacionum, capitulo XXII°, infertur vere res et nomen ecclesie.

ENGLISH: Again, according to these sayings the [priests]{g:sacerdos}, prophets and kings of the old law were christs, as Israel, Aaron, David; and consequently the Israelites, the Aaronites and the house of David had been Christians, because [they were] disciples of Christ keeping justice; and so, according to every [definition]{g:descripcio} which the doctor gives of a Christian, [it follows]{g:sequitur} that before the incarnation there had been Christians.[^1] And in confirmation of this he writes to me thus: "All who keep the law of Christ," he says, "are called Christians, as blessed Augustine notes, Letter 42, [403] [alleging]{g:allegare} the Apostle, Romans 11, who says that not all who are of Israel are Israelites. Rather," says Augustine, "those are Christians who are sons of the flesh not according to Ishmael, but sons of the promise according to Isaac, belonging to the spiritual ministry."[^2] From this [argument]{g:processus} of Augustine, well noted, it follows that the fathers of the old testament had been Christians and [members]{g:membrum} of [the church]{g:ecclesia}; which is denied. "All," he says, "who live according to the law of Christ are sons of Abraham and true Christians." From which [conclusion]{g:conclusio} it is clear that through the time of the old testament there had been Christians; or, second, that now none are sons of Abraham; or that in the holy three days the blessed Virgin had not been a Christian, because it was not then so that she keeps the law of Christ, since it was then so that Christ is not.[^3] Hence, finally, it seems inconsistently said that there was a catholic church of which the blessed Virgin was part for the three days of Christ's death. First, because, supposing that we speak for that triduum, on your own doubt the Virgin alone is the faithful person surviving; and since on your own doubt she is not a Christian, there is no multitude of all Christians.[^4] Likewise, through the whole bodily life of Christ before his death the church was not coming to be (as is said), because [it came to be] only for the time of the flowing of blood and water from his side, which was after the death of Christ. Likewise, supposing that we speak for the said triduum, and argue from the definition to the thing defined: Every catholic church is the universal multitude of all Christians having wholly the right and same faith, promulgated by Christ and his apostles and afterwards declared by the holy fathers in general councils; now there is at this time a catholic church of which the mother of the Lord is part; therefore there is at this time the universal multitude of all Christians having wholly the right and same faith, promulgated by Christ and his apostles [404] and afterwards declared by the holy fathers in general councils. The [consequent]{g:consequens} is false for the said triduum; therefore [so is] the [antecedent]{g:antecedens}.[^5] Now the minor was then true, as is granted; therefore, since the said [inference]{g:consequencia} then held, as I suppose, it remains that the major was then false, which is the definition of the universal church, feigned to be going to remain for ever. And that the consequent was then false is clear, because, granted *per impossibile* that there was then a multitude of all Christians having wholly the right and same faith promulgated by Christ and his apostles, still it was not then true that that faith is afterwards declared by the holy fathers in general councils. For it follows: that faith [is not] declared by the holy fathers in general councils; therefore neither after nor before nor ever is it declared by the holy fathers in general councils.[^6] If (I say) it sufficed that that faith will be declared by the holy fathers in general councils, then it follows, with true [premisses], that long before the death of Christ there was a catholic church; which is denied. But wrongly, since there was then the Christian religion, in that there was then an abbot according to the apostolic rule, ruling his chapter of chosen apostles. For they then kept the Christian religion better than we do; and from the name of such a religion, spoken of in the first book of the *Retractations*, chapter 22, the thing and the name of the church are truly inferred.[^7]

### 17-e ¶2 · 404.27–406.11  (10 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 404] In tercio argumento fert doctor graviter quod dixi descripcionis sue particulas congregatas inculte. Fateor, inquit, quod inculte, **sed** malo inculto sermone et absque dictamine scribere veritates quam sub fuco verborum politorum tradere falsitates. **Sed** constat quod descripcio data est rethorica et diffusa, [p. 405] nec solum falsitatem implicans **sed** errorem contrarium fidei christiane; et revera descripcionem sic diffusam et falsam non excusat color rethoricus quin sit a catholicis condempnata; descripcio **autem** ecclesie catholice est condicionis opposite, dum recte breviter et plane dicitur quod ecclesia catholica est universitas predestinatorum. **Sed** quoad materiam argumenti dicitur quod ubique assumit falsum incomprobatum, cum aliquam unam fidem qua creditur habent omnes christiani. Unam, inquit, tam genere quam specie **sed** non numero (ut allegatur de Augustino XIII° De Trinitate VI° et VII°), et eadem est fides que creditur aput omnes. Et placet de ista responsione: exhinc enim feci argumentum ad hominem, quia credidi doctorem posuisse ut olim quod non fuisset species vel aliquod universale nisi terminus vel conceptus; non quia credidi hoc esse verum, cum scio ex fide scripture quod una est fides que creditur, et fides qua creditur in quolibet christiano; aliter enim impertinenter diceret Apostolus Ephes. IV°, 5. Unus dominus, una fides. Et illud expresse dicit Augustinus XIII° De Trinitate, II° cap° X. Fides (inquam), scilicet que creditur, communis est sicut dici potest omnibus hominibus facies humana communis; nam hoc ita dicitur, ut tamen singuli suas habeant. Ex una sane doctrina impressam fidem credencium cordibus singulorum qui hoc idem credunt, verissime dicimus; **sed** aliud sunt ea que creduntur, aliud fides qua creduntur. Illa quippe in rebus sunt que vel esse vel fuisse vel futura esse dicuntur, hec **autem** in animo credentis est, ei tantum conspicua cuius est, quamvis sit in [p. 406] aliis non ipsa **sed** similis. Non enim una est numero **sed** genere propter similitudinem, tamen et nullam diversitatem magis unam dicimus esse quam multas. Nam duos homines simillimos cum videmus, unam faciem habere dicimus, facilius itaque dicitur multas animas fuisse singulas utique singulorum, de quibus legitur in actibus Apostolorum quod eis fuerit anima una, quoniam, ubi dixit Apostolus: Una fides, tot eas quisquam audet dicere quot fideles. Et tamen qui dixit: O mulier magna est fides tua, et alteri: Modice fidei quare dubitasti? suam cuique esse signat.

ENGLISH: In the third argument the doctor takes it hard that I said the parts of his definition were gathered together uncouthly.[^8] "I confess," he says, "that [they were gathered] uncouthly; but I prefer to write truths in uncouth speech and without the art of composition, than to hand down falsehoods under the paint of polished words." But it is evident that the definition given is rhetorical and diffuse, [405] implying not only falsehood but an error contrary to the Christian faith; and in truth rhetorical [colour]{g:color} does not excuse a definition so diffuse and false from being condemned by catholics.[^9] But the definition of the catholic church is of the opposite condition, when it is said rightly, briefly and plainly that the catholic church is the whole body of the [predestinate]{g:predestinatus}. But as to the matter of the argument, it is said that everywhere it assumes a falsehood unproved, since [it takes it that] all Christians have some one faith by which it is believed. "One," he says, "in genus as well as in species, but not in number (as is alleged from Augustine, *On the Trinity* 13, [chapters] 6 and 7); and the faith which is believed is the same among all."[^10] And I am pleased with this reply; for from this I made an argument to the man, because I believed that the doctor had held, as once he did, that there was no species or any [universal]{g:universale} except a term or a concept; not because I believed this to be true, since I know [from the faith of scripture]{g:ex fide} that one is the faith which is believed, and [one] the faith by which it is believed in every Christian; for otherwise the Apostle would say irrelevantly, Ephesians 4:5: "One Lord, one faith."[^11] And Augustine says this expressly, *On the Trinity* 13, chapter 2: "Faith (I say), namely that which is believed, is common, as the human face can be said to be common to all men; for this is so said that yet each has his own. We say most truly that from one [doctrine]{g:doctrina} faith is impressed on the hearts of each of the believers who believe this same thing; but the things which are believed are one thing, the faith by which they are believed another. For those are in things which are said either to be or to have been or to be going to be; but this is in the mind of the believer, visible only to him whose it is, although it is in [406] others, not itself but like it.[^12] For it is one not in number but in genus; yet because of the likeness and the absence of any difference we say that it is one rather than many.[^13] For when we see two men very alike, we say that they have one face. So it is more easily said that there were many souls, each surely the soul of each, of whom we read in the Acts of the Apostles that they had one soul, than that anyone dare say, where the Apostle said 'one faith', that there are as many [faiths] as faithful.[^14] And yet he who said, 'O woman, great is thy faith', and to another, 'O thou of little faith, why didst thou doubt?', signifies that each has his own."[^15]

### 17-e ¶3 · 406.12–406.33  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 406] Ex istis notare potest logicus quod Augustinus loquitur de fide que est multitudo habituum credendi ab uno principio infusorum et nec de genere nec de specie logico, quia illud universale non recipit predicaciones huiusmodi peregrinas; nam ad species tales accidencium non est motus, **sed** ad partes quantitativas generis quod est multitudo dissimilium qualitatum **sed** nulla earum. Patet secundo quod fides que creditur est veritas de presenti, de preterito vel futuro et non subiective in credente, et per consequens, omnia que fuerunt vel erunt sunt pro suo tempore; aliter enim non diceret Augustinus quod ea que creduntur sunt in rebus sicut articuli preteriti et futuri. Et gaudeo tercio quod magistra et mater nostra scriptura habet testes conformes sanctis doctoribus, eius eminenciam attestantes, sic tamen quod dicant consequenter illam unam fidem habere unum subiectum quod est corpus ecclesie, et tunc oportet dicere quod illud corpus dispergitur inter patres veteris testamenti tam Hebreos quam gentiles et non artare ipsum ad tempus post mortem Christi et gentem vulgari nomine christianam.

ENGLISH: From these things the logician can note that Augustine speaks of faith which is a multitude of habits of believing infused from one principle, and not of a logical genus or species, because that universal does not receive foreign predications of this kind; for there is no motion to such species of accidents, but to the quantitative parts of the genus, which is a multitude of unlike qualities but none of them.[^16] It is clear, second, that the faith which is believed is truth about the present, about the past or the future, and is not in the believer as in its [subject]{g:subiectum}; and consequently all things that were or will be are, for their time; for otherwise Augustine would not say that the things which are believed are in things, as the articles past and future.[^17] And I rejoice, third, that our mistress and [mother]{g:mater nostra}, scripture, has witnesses agreeing with the holy doctors, attesting her eminence; yet so that they should say consistently that that one faith has one subject, which is the body of the church; and then one must say that that body is dispersed among the fathers of the old testament, Hebrews as well as gentiles, and not confine it to the time after the death of Christ and to the people called Christian by the common name.[^18]

### 17-f ¶1 · 407.1–408.25  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 407] Ulterius asseritur falsum esse quod defective et corrupte habet multitudo christianorum fidem Christi, eo quod multitudo christianorum habet in fide quidquid est ei necessarium ad salutem credere. **Sed** constat quod imprudenter ex causa particulari falsum inducitur, nam iuxta sic asserentem prescitus dum est in mortali habet habitum fidei sicut et quilibet falsus christianus. Et credo quod nullus dubitat quin defective et corrupte quilibet talis habet fidem Christi defective, quia peccabiliter, sicut et habuerunt apostoli qui rogaverunt ut Christus adaugeat eis fidem Luce XVII°, 5. Si enim Petro non deficeret gradus fidei, Christus exprobrando non ei diceret Matthei XIV°, 31: Modice fidei quare dubitasti? Et corrupte habent fidem qui irretiti peccatis mortalibus corrumpunt bonos mores et ex informitate fidei finaliter corrumpuntur, nam secundum Apostolum Iª. Cor. V°, 6, modicum fermentum totam massam corrumpit. Ideo oportet doctorem consequenter dicere quod aliqua multitudo christianorum habet fidem valde defective et corrupte sicut homines opinionis sue. Nec moveat evidencia doctoris: si eandem fidem habent boni christiani et mali singuli, sequitur quod mali christiani eque perfectam et integram fidem habeant ut boni. Nam pertinens est hic loqui limitate de fide qua creditur, quia de fide que creditur eque perfectam fidem habent demones et creature singule ut beate. Ideo Augustinus loquitur de genere fidei que est tota multitudo habituum fidei in corpore ecclesie. Illud **autem** genus fidei habet unam partem quantitativam, perfectam in uno et in alio imperfectam, sicut genus motus localis habet in celo [p. 408] unam partem quantitativam uniformem quoad subiectum et tempus, **sed** aliam partem quantitativam utrimque difformem. In patria **autem** sunt singuli fidei uniformis, cum ibi evacuabitur imperfectum. Si enim eundem habitum fidei in numero omnino simpliciter habet quilibet christianus, sequitur quod ille habitus eque perfecte denominaret subiectum suum credere ut alius quicunque. Omnis enim qualitas denominat subiectum suum primum secundum sui ultimum, quia variacio disposicionis variaret gradum habitus et sic ipsum habitum. Et ista est sentencia Augustini, non quod idem in numero scilicet actus vel habitus fidei sit formaliter in diversis personis, quia tunc quelibet illarum personarum foret fides quelibet. Fides enim non est nisi personam credere vel disponi ad credendum. **Sed** fides que creditur est veritas a credentibus aliena. Et sic quamvis infidelis eciam dyabolus credat veritatem fidei catholice, verumptamen secundum partem est infidelis et racione mortalis habet fidem corruptam et informem. Nec refert quoad illam informitatem, sive fides acquisita fuerit sive infusa, ut loquitur decretalis in Clementinis De Summa Trinitate et Fide Catholica, capitulo Firmiter, dicens quod regulariter fides infunditur in baptismo; quod ego credo de omni predestinato rite baptizato.

ENGLISH: Further, it is asserted to be false that the multitude of Christians holds the faith of Christ defectively and corruptly, in that the multitude of Christians has in faith whatever it is necessary for it to believe for salvation.[^1] But it is evident that the falsehood is imprudently induced from a particular case; for according to the one who so asserts, a [foreknown]{g:prescitus} man, while he is in mortal [sin], has the habit of faith, as does any false Christian. And I believe that no one doubts that every such man has the faith of Christ defectively and corruptly. Defectively, because in a way liable to sin, as the apostles too had it, who asked that Christ increase faith in them, Luke 17:5. For if Peter lacked no degree of faith, Christ would not say to him in reproach, Matthew 14:31: "O thou of little faith, why didst thou doubt?"[^2] And they have faith corruptly who, ensnared in mortal sins, corrupt good morals, and from the unformedness of faith are finally corrupted; for according to the Apostle, 1 Corinthians 5:6, "a little leaven corrupteth the whole lump."[^3] Therefore the doctor must consistently say that some multitude of Christians has faith very defectively and corruptly, as [do] the men of his opinion. Nor let the doctor's [evidence]{g:evidencia} move [anyone]: if good Christians and bad each have the same faith, [it follows]{g:sequitur} that bad Christians have as perfect and entire a faith as the good.[^4] For it is pertinent here to speak, with limitation, of the faith by which it is believed; because, as to the faith which is believed, demons and every single creature have as perfect a faith as the blessed. Therefore Augustine speaks of the genus of faith, which is the whole multitude of habits of faith in the body of [the church]{g:ecclesia}. Now that genus of faith has one quantitative part perfect in one and in another imperfect, as the genus of local motion has in the heaven [408] one quantitative part uniform as to [subject]{g:subiectum} and time, but another quantitative part difform in both.[^5] But in the [homeland]{g:patria} each is of uniform faith, since there the imperfect shall be done away.[^6] For if every Christian has altogether, [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}, the same habit of faith in number, it follows that that habit would [name]{g:denominare} its subject as believing equally perfectly as any other. For every quality names its first subject according to its utmost [degree], because a variation of the disposition would vary the degree of the habit, and so the habit itself.[^7] And this is the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of Augustine: not that the same in number, namely an act or habit of faith, is formally in diverse persons, because then each of those persons would be each faith. For faith is nothing but a person's believing, or being disposed to believe. But the faith which is believed is a truth other than the believers.[^8] And so, although an [unbeliever]{g:infidelis}, even the devil, believes the truth of the catholic faith, nevertheless he is in part an unbeliever, and by reason of mortal [sin] has a faith corrupt and [unformed]{g:fides informis}. Nor does it matter, as to that unformedness, whether the faith was acquired or infused, as the decretal in the Clementines, *De summa Trinitate et fide catholica*, chapter *Firmiter*, speaks, saying that as a rule faith is infused in baptism; which I believe of every predestinate person duly baptized.[^9]

And that church is not the body of Christ, nor the holy catholic church, nor a part of it.[^11] In the second way the church is [taken for]{g:sumitur pro} the [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} and the foreknown mixedly, while they are in grace according to present justice; and this church shares in part but not in whole with the holy church of God. ### 17-f ¶4 · 410.3–410.29  (6 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 410] Istis premissis dicitur quod omnis prescitus nunquam est de ecclesia tercio modo dicta; est **autem** de ecclesia primo et secundo modo dicta, quando splendet in eo lumen gracie et pro quocunque mortali veniente est extra triplicem ecclesiam, licet habuerit tantam fidem infusam, ut montes transferat; nam fides omnino informis, sive infusa sive adquisita, non sufficit constituere personam, ut sit de ecclesia, quia cum illa fide stat hominem dampnari perpetuo; fides **autem** cum gracia predestinacionis constituit hominem membrum ecclesie sive sit in gracia secundum presentem iusticiam, sive ad tempus iniustus fuerit vel eciam infidelis. Generacio enim qua talis fit filius salvat eum, ne possit cum datis paribus peccare peccato finalis impenitencie ut prescitus, et sic nemo potest desinere esse membrum sancte ecclesie tercio modo dicte quia tunc desineret esse predestinatus; incipit **autem** esse membrum illius ecclesie secundum disparem racionem, nunc quando incipit esse et nunc quando incipit esse iustus (ut notat Augustinus super Johannis Omelia XLIXª). De prima **autem** ecclesia nunc cadunt et nunc incorporantur presciti, secundum quod accenditur vel extinguitur in eis scintilla gracie; de secunda vero ecclesia cadunt predestinati casu secundum quid, **sed** presciti quandoque cadunt simpliciter, illa **autem** ecclesia desinet esse ad diem iudicii, cum tunc desinet esse commixtio grani cum palea.

ENGLISH: These things premised, the answer is that every foreknown man is never of the church said in the third way; but he is of the church said in the first and second way when the light of grace shines in him; and for whatever mortal [sin] comes upon him he is outside the threefold church, although he had so much infused faith that he could remove mountains.[^16] For faith altogether unformed, whether infused or acquired, does not suffice to constitute a person so that he be of the church, because with that faith it stands that a man be damned perpetually; but faith with the grace of predestination constitutes a man a [member]{g:membrum} of the church, whether he be in grace according to present justice, or be for a time unjust, or even an unbeliever.[^17] For the generation by which such a one becomes a son saves him, so that he cannot, all else being equal, sin with the sin of final impenitence as the foreknown man does; and so no one can cease to be a member of holy church said in the third way, because then he would cease to be predestinate. But he begins to be a member of that church on a different ground, at one time when he begins to be and at another when he begins to be just (as Augustine notes on John, Homily 49).[^18] And from the first church the foreknown now fall away and now are incorporated, according as the spark of grace is kindled or quenched in them; but from the second church the predestinate fall with a fall in a certain respect, whereas the foreknown sometimes fall without qualification; and that church will cease to be at the day of judgement, since then the commingling of grain with chaff will cease to be.[^19]
