## Doubtful, left as they are

- **10-k ¶5, p. 260**: *sed et illum articulum sollempnizatum … potest nedum imperator facere*, rendered "And indeed". Intensive *sed et* ("nay, even"), escalating from the fly to "this solemnized article"; neither adversative nor a minor premiss. "And indeed" carries it (cf. *sed precipue*, De ecclesia 01-d). Note the inconsistency with 11-b below.
- **11-b ¶1, p. 269**: *Sed et Bernhardus libro II de papa sic loquitur*, rendered "But Bernard too". Additive *sed et* (Jerome, Lincoln, and Bernard as well), not a contrast; "And Bernard too" would be truer, but "but … too" is a common idiom for *sed et*. Coordinator may wish to align it with 10-k ¶5.
- **10-k ¶3, p. 259**: *Sed ille imperator non habet plus potestatem condendi novos articulos fidei*, rendered "Now". Minor premiss of an unstated conclusion (the article was made by Caesar; the emperor cannot make articles; [so it is none]); no *ergo* follows, but (a) is the right reading.
- **10-l ¶3, p. 263**: *Quoddam autem imperium est per se*, rendered "But another". Either (b), the second member of *quoddam … quoddam autem* set against the first, or (c). "But" stands.
- **10-l ¶6, p. 264**: *Sed tercio arguitur*, rendered "But, thirdly". The opponents' third argument after Wyclif's rebuttal of the second: either (b), turning back to the opponents, or (a), the next item. "But" defensible.
- **11-b ¶1, p. 271**: *Illud autem, quod Christus docuit latenter*, rendered "And". Either (c), carrying on from *quia Christus docuit illud abscondite*, or (b), Christ set against Antichrist (the contrast is carried inside the sentence). "And" stands.
- **11-c ¶2, p. 273**: *Sed ulterius pro materia argumenti miror*, rendered "But further". A next step rather than a contrast; "Now further" would also do. Left.
- **10-l ¶6, p. 264**: *Nunc autem divina* is the canon's incipit (D. 21), kept in Latin italic as a title; correctly untranslated.
