# *Dialogus*, chapter 1: the three parts of the church militant

## Headnote

**Occasion.** Written "some time in 1379" according to its editor, since it treats the double
papal election as a fresh scandal (Pollard, pp. xvi–xvii). It is a dialogue between Truth and
Falsehood on the endowment of the church. Pollard's outline: chapters 1–10 make the case from
scripture; 11–23 answer Falsehood's appeals to church authority; 24–33 meet the practical
objections to disendowment; 34–36 conclude (pp. vi–vii). Truth speaks as God in the opening
chapters, and "after Chapter 3 Veritas is Wyclif, and Wyclif only" (p. vi).

**The text.** Pollard prints the only English manuscript, Ashburnham XXVII C, and collates three
Vienna manuscripts (ÖNB 1387, 3930, 4505; his sigla B, B¹, C `[verify which is which]`). Ten
copies survive in all, nine of them made by Bohemian scribes. Our Latin is the Fordham
transcription, collated word by word against the plates. In the printed scripture references the
chapter numbers are Wyclif's; the verse numbers are Pollard's.

**The move.** Before any argument, Truth lays down the plan of the church. It has three parts,
clergy, temporal lords and common people, each with its office, and charity binds them. The clergy
are to live on alms and tithes, as Christ did; the lords hold what they have of Christ by service.
Every later chapter measures the endowed church of 1379 against this plan.

**Where it is answered.** It has more surviving manuscripts than any other work of Wyclif's. It was
banned from public lectures at Prague in 1408, and it stands first in the list of his writings
condemned there in 1410. Loserth used it in proving Hus's debt to Wyclif (Pollard, p. v n. 1). No
English translation has been traced.

## Prologue · 1.1–1.12

> [p. 1] Cum idemptitas sit mater fastidie et pulcra alternacio delectat animum, ac lingua latina
> plus regulariter dilatatur atque extensius, visum est quibusdam quod sentencia catholica collecta
> fidelibus in vulgari reseretur communius in latino. Veritas enim utilis de quanto diffusius
> dilatatur de tanto mentes fidelium plus illustrat. Et quia multi delectantur in loquela dialogi,
> moventur a deo duas personas adducere, scil. veritatem atque mendacium, que per modum
> disputacionis loquuntur in ista materia alternatim. Et quia veritas est Christus, ut patet jo 14°,
> et diabolus ipsum mendacium, ut patet jo 8°, racionabile videtur veritatem proponere et sermonem
> istum, mediante Christi gracia stabilire.

[1] Since sameness is the mother of tedium, and graceful alternation delights the mind,[^1] and the
Latin tongue spreads more regularly and more widely, it has seemed good to some that the catholic
teaching gathered for the faithful in the vernacular should be opened up more commonly in
Latin.[^2] For useful truth enlightens the minds of the faithful the more, the more widely it is
spread. And since many delight in the speech of dialogue, they are moved by God to bring in two
persons, namely Truth and Falsehood, who speak in turn on this matter by way of disputation. And
since Truth is Christ, as is clear from John 14, and the devil is Falsehood itself, as is clear
from John 8,[^3] it seems reasonable that Truth should propound this discourse and, by the
mediation of Christ's grace, establish it.

## Truth · 1.13–2.11

> VERITAS. Cum ego sum deus conditor cujuslibet creature et judicabo quoscunque homines vel ad
> beatitudinem vel ad eternum supplicium in finali judicio, racio exigit quod cuncti homines per
> meam legem, tam in suis conscienciis quam in operibus suis cunctis extrinsecis regulentur. Hec
> enim est racio quare Christus fuerat incarnatus et 30 annis et amplius cum suis fratribus
> conversatus, ut sermonibus suis [p. 2] mansuetis veritas catholica plus lucescat. Finis autem tam
> digni laboris et tam assidui est ut homines qui sunt Christi ecclesia sint beati. Dividebam autem
> meam militantem ecclesiam in tres partes quarum prima foret clerus meus, qui vocantur sacerdotes
> Christi; qui me instar apostolorum propinquius in moribus sequerentur. Et sic ad animandum eos in
> humilitate, viverent de elemosinis instar mei. Per hoc enim de me magis recolerent; cum sim deus
> qui vendico partem decimam tanquam meam. Qui enim vivit de parte domini recoleret recencius
> dominum partem istam tam libere largientem.

TRUTH. Since I am God, the maker of every creature, and will judge all men at the final judgement,
whether to blessedness or to eternal punishment,[^4] reason requires that all men be ruled by my
law, both in their consciences and in all their outward works. For this is the reason why Christ
had become incarnate and had lived for thirty years and more with his brethren: that by his gentle
[2] sermons catholic truth might shine the more. And the end of so worthy and so constant a labour
is that the men who are Christ's church may be blessed. Now I divided my church militant into three
parts, of which the first should be my clergy, who are called priests of Christ, and who should
follow me more closely in their manner of life, after the pattern of the apostles.[^5] And so, to
hearten them in humility, they should live on alms, as I did. For by this they would be more mindful
of me, since I am God, who claim the tenth part as my own. For he who lives on the Lord's portion
would remember the more freshly the Lord who bestows that portion so freely.[^6]

## Truth (cont.) · 2.12–3.14

> Secunda pars mee militantis ecclesie forent domini temporales, qui debent esse vicarii deitatis.
> Ideo sepe meminit augustinus quomodo rex est vicarius deitatis. Sacerdos autem qui secundum
> humilitatem et pauperiem debet procedere est vicarius humanitatis domini jhu Christi. Et sic
> secundum potentiam regulatam racione debent hii domini virtuose defendere legem dei. Nam titulo
> istius servicii tenent de Christo tanquam capitali domino quicquid habent. Tercia autem pars
> militantis ecclesie sunt vulgares qui in operarios, mercantes et iconomos multipliciter sunt
> divisi. Ista autem tercia pars et infima ut fundamentum sustinet duas partes alias in
> corporalibus necessariis vite. Sed caritas que est [p. 3] tercia persona in divinis debet
> connectere has tres partes. Omnes enim iste tres debent sibi ipsis prodesse reciproce et juvare.
> Clerus autem debet esse vita aliis duabus partibus inferioribus, et vivere ipsis spiritualius ac
> quo ad mores mundius; et sic tam vita quam verbis docere eos viam ad beatitudinem promerendum.
> Media autem pars que est domini temporales debet quadam austeritate modesta juvare partes
> alterutras extremales, et specialiter secundum legem dei in his que promovent ad beatitudinem
> [conse]quendam. Tercia vero pars vulgarium debet ex animo secundum regulam legis dei servire
> partibus duabus superioribus; cum omnes iste tres partes debent esse unum corpus ecclesie,
> currens hilariter in amore ad beatitudinem patrie consequendam.

The second part of my church militant should be the temporal lords, who ought to be vicars of the
Godhead. Therefore Augustine often recalls how the king is the vicar of the Godhead.[^7] But the
priest, who ought to proceed according to humility and poverty, is the vicar of the humanity of the
Lord Jesus Christ. And so these lords, according to power regulated by reason, ought virtuously to
defend God's law. For by title of this service they hold of Christ, as chief lord, whatever they
have.[^8] Now the third part of the church militant is the common people, who are divided in many
ways into workers, merchants and householders.[^9] And this third and lowest part, as a
foundation, sustains the two other parts in the bodily necessities of life. But charity, which is
[3] the third person in the Godhead, ought to bind these three parts together.[^10] For all these
three ought to benefit and help one another reciprocally. Now the clergy ought to be a life to the
other two, lower parts, and to live more spiritually than they and, as regards conduct, more
cleanly;[^11] and so, as much by life as by words, to teach them the way to merit blessedness. And
the middle part, which is the temporal lords, ought with a certain measured severity to help both
the outer parts, and especially, according to God's law, in the things that advance toward
attaining blessedness.[^12] And the third part, of the common people, ought from the heart,
according to the rule of God's law, to serve the two higher parts; since all these three parts
ought to be one body of the church, running joyfully in love toward attaining the blessedness of
the homeland.[^13]

## Pollard's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 1.2: The work is to be in Latin as more widely known.
- 1.7: And in the form of a dialogue between Truth and Falsehood.
- 1.10: Truth standing for Christ, Falsehood for the Devil.
- 1.16: Men must rule their lives by Truth.
- 1.18: For this cause came Christ.
- 2.3: The church militant is divided into three parts.
- 2.5: (i.) The Clergy, who especially must follow Christ; and must live by alms, thanking God for
  the tithes He gives them.
- 2.12: (ii.) The Lords Temporal, who should represent the Divine power of Christ as the clergy his
  human humility.
- 2.16: These must defend God's law, and hold their goods from Christ by this service.
- 2.19: (iii.) The People, who must support the other two orders.
- 3.1: These three parts must be knit by love.
- 3.3: The clergy must set a good example.
- 3.6: The temporal lords must promote Christian welfare.
- 3.9: The people must serve clergy and lords, as members all of one body.

## Notes

[^1]: **T.** *Fastidie* = *fastidii*, as printed. The indicative *delectat* after *cum … sit* is also
    as printed; Pollard records no variant. The opening has the form of a proverb.

[^2]: **C/R.** *Collecta fidelibus*: most naturally a dative, "gathered *for* the faithful". But
    medieval Latin allows an ablative of agent without *a*: "gathered *by* faithful men". On the
    second reading the vernacular teaching was compiled by Wyclif's followers. The Latin cannot
    decide it, and the English keeps the likelier dative. Either way, the sentence presents the
    *Dialogus* as a Latin opening-up of teaching already current in English. Pollard observes that
    "nearly the whole of the work can be paralleled from the English writings", and explains the
    nine Bohemian copies by its popular character (p. v).

[^3]: **S/R.** John 14:6, "I am the way, and the truth, and the life"; John 8:44, the devil "is a
    liar, and the father thereof". Pollard notes that Truth speaks as God in chapters 1–3 but
    mentions Christ in the third person alongside (as at 1.16–19). After chapter 3 Truth is simply
    Wyclif (p. vi).

[^4]: **T.** *Cum ego sum*: so Pollard's text (the Ashburnham MS). B and C read *sim*, and Pollard's
    own introduction quotes the line with *sim* (p. vi). The mood does not change the English.

[^5]: **R/G.** The three parts are the medieval three orders (those who pray, those who fight, those
    who work), recast as parts of the church militant. *Instar apostolorum … instar mei*: the
    apostolic and dominical pattern of poverty. The treatise's case against endowment starts here.

[^6]: **R.** The priests live on the Lord's portion, as the Levites did. Pollard notes that the
    Old Testament proof in the next chapters rests mainly on Num. 18:20 and Deut. 18:1, the
    Levites' having no inheritance but the Lord (p. vii). *Recencius*: "more freshly".

[^7]: **S/T.** "Augustine" is not identified by Pollard. The same attribution comes in *De officio
    regis* (13.8–9: *dicit Augustinus quod rex habet ymaginem dei sed episcopus ymaginem Cristi*),
    unidentified there too. Identification pending (LEDGER V-13). The apparatus to 2.13 shows the
    sentence omitted in some MSS (printed "om. B, B, C¹", apparently a misprint). B reads
    *divinitatis* for the first *deitatis*, and has in its margin *Sacerdos vicarius Christi, Dominus
    vicarius divinitatis*. See GLOSSARY §B, *vicarius deitatis*.

[^8]: **G.** Tenure language, kept literal: the lords "hold of Christ as chief lord", like a
    tenant-in-chief of the crown, and *titulo istius servicii*, "by title of this service". The
    doctrine that all just lordship is held of God, and by service, is the doctrine of *De civili
    dominio*.

[^9]: **G.** *Iconomos* = *oeconomos*. We render "householders": those who manage a household or
    holding. It may mean stewards, or husbandmen. Provisional; see GLOSSARY §B.

[^10]: **S.** Charity as the Holy Spirit, "the third person in the Godhead", is Peter Lombard's
    thesis in *Sentences* I d.17. The house has its pilot at sententiae.wrootpress.com. *In divinis*:
    "in the divine [persons]", i.e. in God.

[^11]: **C.** *Esse vita aliis*: "be a life to the others". Pollard's side-note takes it as example
    ("must set a good example"). *Vita* may be an error for *via* ("a way"), but no MS reads so,
    and it stands. *Vivere ipsis spiritualius*: we take *ipsis* as an ablative of comparison, "more
    spiritually than they", rather than a dative ("live for them"). The comparative settles it.

[^12]: **T.** *[conse]quendam*: Pollard's brackets supply the syllable from B, B¹, C (apparatus to
    3.9), so his base MS evidently reads *quendam*. At 3.6 *promerendum* is printed so; B has been
    corrected by another hand to *promerendam*. The English is unaffected in both places.

[^13]: **G.** *Patria*, "the homeland": heaven, as against the *via* of this life (GLOSSARY §A).
