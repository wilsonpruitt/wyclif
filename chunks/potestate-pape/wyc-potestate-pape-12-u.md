# *De potestate pape*, chapter 12, part 21: the endowed bound to contradictories; why Constantine is not canonized; the protestation

## ¶1 · 393.31–395.7

> [p. 393] A subiectis vero inferiora gerenda sunt, arccioribus celestia cogitanda ut scilicet oculum, qui pro videndi gressibus preeminet, cura pulveris non obscuret. [p. 394] Caput namque subiectorum et oculi sunt cuncti qui presunt. Et hinc creditur quod dotati sunt exinde more peccancium ad contradictoria obligati. Tenentur enim racione superaccepti doni a mundi principibus amplius servire illis quam prius, et debent minus deservire eis, cum non illuminantur ex accepcione temporalium sed cecantur et per ante tenebantur servire illis secundum totum ingenium vel donum, quod a Deo habebant, quia in toto illo eis proficere quoad Deum, et aliud mundanum ac laycale servicium est abhominabile dominis et sui meriti tardativum. Cum igitur tenentur habere aliquam providenciam et gubernacionem circa bona collata et ingeniacio sua sit tantum finita, videtur quod oportet ipsam quoad servicium meritorium post dotacionem diminui, cum exinde fracta sit et debilitata, quia circa contraria est dispersa. Et hoc creditur movisse apostolos constituisse dyaconos ad ministrandum mensis, apostolis ipsis intendentibus verbo Dei, ut legitur Act. VI. Et revera illud mensarum ministerium videtur habere maiorem racionem meriti quam occupacio qua dotati sunt circa temporalia preter necessaria occupati. Et ex istis videtur quod sicut dotati ad onus dampnosum spiritus receperant coniugium cum temporalibus monstruosum, sic dotantes ex ceca pietate dampnificarunt ambas partes altrinsecus directe ad contrarium meriti, quod stulte intenderant. Unde creditur beatum Gregorium non obstante reliccione tocius patrimonii, quod reliquerat, habens omnia in communi, nec obstante humilitate tota ac parcitate post pinguem dotacionem ecclesie, nec obstante tota suspensione [p. 395] sollicitudinis circa temporalia et inveccione contra philargiam, propter admissionem temporalium venialiter peccavisse et hinc ad purgacionem sui et augmentum meriti Deum ipsum in febribus et dolore capitis per longa tempora punivisse. Alii autem fingunt quod hoc accidit, quia oravit temere pro Trayano.

"And the lower things are to be carried out by the subjects, the heavenly things to be thought on by the stricter, so that, namely, the care of the dust may not darken the eye, which stands out above for seeing the steps. [394] For all who preside are the [head]{g:caput} and the eyes of the subjects."[^1] And hence it is believed that the endowed are thereby, after the manner of sinners, bound to contradictories. For by reason of the gift received over and above from the princes of the world they are bound to serve them more than before; and they ought to serve them less, since they are not enlightened by the receiving of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} but blinded; and beforehand they were bound to serve them according to the whole wit or gift that they had from God, because [they were bound] in all of that to profit them with respect to God; and other, worldly and lay, [service]{g:servicium} is abominable to the lords and a hindrance to their merit.[^2] Since therefore they are bound to have some providence and government over the goods conferred, and their wit is only finite, it seems that it must be diminished as to meritorious service after the [endowment]{g:dotacio}, since thereby it is broken and weakened, because it is dispersed about contraries. And this is believed to have moved the apostles to appoint deacons to serve tables, the apostles themselves attending to the word of God, as is read in Acts 6.[^3] And in truth that ministry of tables seems to have a greater ground of merit than the occupation in which the endowed are occupied about temporal goods beyond what is necessary. And from this it seems that, as the endowed, to the damaging burden of the spirit, received a monstrous marriage with temporal goods, so the endowers, from blind [piety]{g:pietas}, damaged both parties on either side, directly contrary to the merit that they had foolishly intended. Hence it is believed that blessed Gregory, notwithstanding the relinquishing of his whole patrimony, which he had relinquished, having all things in common, nor notwithstanding all his humility and frugality after the fat endowment of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, nor notwithstanding all his suspension [395] of solicitude about temporal goods and his invective against [love of money]{g:philargiria}, sinned venially by the admission of temporal goods; and hence that God himself punished him with fevers and pain of the head for long periods, for his purging and the increase of his merit. But others feign that this befell because he prayed rashly for Trajan.[^4]

## ¶2 · 395.8–395.21

> [p. 395] Sed mirantur nonnulli, quare Romana ecclesia non canonizat Constantinum magnum, qui dotavit ecclesiam nec tantum sollempnizat eum, ut orientalis ecclesia, ut notat Cestrensis libro IV, cap. CXII quomodo Greci sollempnizant eius festum XI die. Maii. Sed audivi ad hoc poni causam triplicem, prima fundatur super mendacem blasfemiam, quod debuit dedisse totum imperium suum clero, et quia eius medietatem iniuste detinuit ac in sacrilegio illo sine satisfaccione obiit, tamquam hereticus est dampnandus et per consequens a sanctorum cathalogo semovendus, et sic forent dampnandi omnes morientes reges vel domini temporales.

But some wonder why the Roman church does not [canonize]{g:canonizare} Constantine the Great, who endowed the church, nor solemnizes him so much as the eastern church does; as [the Chester chronicler]{g:cestrensis} notes, book IV, chapter 112, how the Greeks solemnize his feast on the 11th day of May.[^5] But I have heard a threefold cause put for this. The first is founded on a lying blasphemy: that he ought to have given his whole empire to the [clergy]{g:clerus}, and because he unjustly kept back half of it and died in that sacrilege without [satisfaction]{g:satisfaccio}, he is to be condemned as a heretic, and consequently removed from the catalogue of the saints; and so all kings or [temporal lords]{g:temporales-domini} who die [so] would have to be condemned.[^6]

## ¶3 · 395.22–395.28

> [p. 395] Secunda causa videtur coloracior quod canonizacio videtur fuisse alieno Cesari nimium sumptuosa, ideo ut papa habeat cesares magis subiectos suo imperio noluit tantum colere servum suum, fecit tamen magis mirabilia in vita sua, quam illi reges fecerant in morte quos ex nuda relacione vel gracia pecunie canonizat.

The second cause seems [more colourable]{g:coloracior}: that canonization seems to have been too costly to a Caesar not [the pope's] own; therefore, that the [pope]{g:papa} may have the Caesars more subject to his [command]{g:imperium}, he would not honour his servant so much. Yet he did more wonders in his life than those kings had done in death whom [the pope] canonizes on bare report or for the sake of money.[^7]

## ¶4 · 395.29–397.16

> [p. 395] Tercia causa realis ponitur quod hoc ideo non evenit, quod tantum onerando ecclesiam cum temporalibus stulte, tantum dampnificaverat se et clerum, quod dispendium quia subtraxit ab [p. 396] orientali ecclesia, propterea ipsum laudat. Ipsi enim ut fratres de statu exproprietario cum iubilo Deum laudant. Nec est color, si talis dominacio vel mundi gloria videatur preciosa febricitanti in animo cuius affeccio discrasiata non sapit celestia, propterea sic est de illo et eius opposito senciendum, quia oportet fidelem credere in omnibus sano palato et iudicio Ihesu nostri. Nec cecemur in isto, quod dominari sic seculariter sit licitum ac meritorium, ideo congruit clero nostro, quia sic purgare latrinas foret eis conveniens. Sed (si non fallor) facere illud officium foret clero minus inconsonum, quam sic dominari terrestribus, que Apostolus vocat stercora, cum nec foret Deo tantum displicens nec adeo ledens mentem. Ista pauca dixerim, alia recitative, alia reputative, assertive ad honorem matris ecclesie; unde super omnibus dictis meis in ista materia testem consciencie mee invoco Deum meum, quod intendo primo honorem et utilitatem sancte matris ecclesie: ad hoc enim obligatur strictissime ex mandato Domini quilibet christianus, cum quoddammodo infinite plus debemus matrem illam diligere quam parentes carnales vel temporalia aut nosmet ipsos. Intendo secundo quod honor et profectus predicte femine stat principaliter in similitudine conversandi et sequendi sponsum ecclesie, excuciendo omnes tradiciones humanas extraneas que ab expedicione illius itineris retardarent. Et cum ista ambo sint articuli fidei christiane, que cuncti fideles ultra opinionem magnificare tenentur ac cum in mediis ad hunc finem contingit [p. 397] multipliciter oberrare, intendo tercio et protestor quod docto per papam, per quodcumque membrum ecclesie vel aliquam creaturam quod erro in mediis, volo humillime ac paratissime revocare; unde ut procedam securius innitor fundamentaliter summo lapidi, qui est via, veritas et vita, domino Ihesu Christo, capiens testimonium a sanctis doctoribus, de quanto consonant legi sue. Alia vero ab istis tamquam tardativa vel contraria parvipendo; unde credo ut fidem ecclesie, quod quicumque non observat statum et legem quam Christus instituit, quam eciam in se et suis apostolis vita et verbo docuit et servavit, non sequitur Christum per artam viam, que ducit ad celum, sed incedit per latam viam, que ducit membrum dyaboli ad infernum.

The third, the real cause, is put thus: that this did not come about because, by so foolishly burdening the church with temporal goods, he had so damaged himself and the clergy; which loss, because he withdrew it from the [396] eastern church, [that church] therefore praises him. For they, like brethren of the [propertyless]{g:exproprietarii} state, praise God with jubilation.[^8] Nor is it a [colour]{g:color}, if such [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio} or the world's glory seems precious to one fevered in mind, whose [distempered]{g:discrasia} affection has no taste for heavenly things, [for holding] that one must therefore so judge of it and of its opposite; for the faithful must believe in all things by the sound palate and judgement of our Jesus.[^9] Nor let us be blinded in this, [arguing] that to [lord it]{g:dominari} thus in secular fashion is lawful and meritorious, [and] therefore befits our clergy; for so cleaning latrines would be fitting for them. But (if I am not mistaken) to do that office would be less unbecoming to the clergy than so to lord it over earthly things, which the Apostle calls dung, since it would be neither so displeasing to God nor so hurtful to the mind.[^10] These few things I have said, some by way of recital, some [by estimation]{g:reputative}, [some] [assertively]{g:assertive}, to the honour of mother church; hence over all my sayings in this matter I call upon my God as witness of my conscience that I intend first the honour and utility of holy mother church: for to this every Christian is most strictly bound by the Lord's command, since in a certain manner we ought to love that mother infinitely more than carnal parents or temporal goods or our very selves.[^11] I [maintain]{g:intendere} secondly that the honour and advancement of the said lady stands principally in likeness of living and of following the bridegroom of the church, shaking off all extraneous human traditions that would hold [her] back from making that journey. And since both of these are [articles]{g:articulus-fidei} of the Christian faith, which all the faithful are bound to magnify beyond [opinion]{g:opinio}, and since in the means to this end it happens [397] in many ways to go astray, I maintain thirdly, and [declare solemnly]{g:protestari}, that if it is shown by the pope, by any [member]{g:membrum} of the church, or by any creature, that I err in the means, I am willing most humbly and most readily to revoke it.[^12] Hence, to proceed more securely, I rely fundamentally on the chief stone, who is the way, the truth and the life, the Lord Jesus Christ, taking testimony from the holy doctors so far as they agree with his law. And other things besides these I make little of, as hindering or contrary; hence I believe, as the faith of the church, that whoever does not observe the state and law that Christ instituted, which he also taught and kept in himself and his apostles by life and word, does not follow Christ by the strait way that leads to heaven, but walks by the broad way that leads the member of the devil to hell.[^13]

## Notes

[^1]: **T/S.** The quotation of Gregory, *Regula pastoralis* II.7, runs on from the last part to *qui presunt* (Loserth's italics). *Arccioribus*, "the stricter", is the print and fits poorly; Gregory's text reads *a rectoribus summa cogitanda*, "higher things are to be thought on by the rulers" (PL 77, 39D), of which it is a corruption; for *summa* Wyclif has *celestia* (LEDGER P12u-1). We translate the print.
[^2]: **C.** *Ad contradictoria obligati*: bound to two incompatible things, to serve their benefactors more (for the gift) and less (since the gift blinds them). *Per ante*: "beforehand". The verb of *in toto illo eis proficere* is supplied from *tenebantur*. *Abhominabile dominis*: "to the lords", which we take as the endowed clergy themselves, now lords; *Domino*, "to the Lord", would read more easily, but print and apparatus agree on *dominis*.
[^3]: **S.** Acts 6:2–4 (DR "It is not reason that we should leave the word of God and serve tables").
[^4]: **T/R.** The manuscripts read *patrimonium*; Loserth prints *patrimonii* (we translate the print). The manuscripts' margin: *Dotacionem sanctus Gregorius admittens non excusatur quin peccat venialiter*. *Philargiam* is the print's form. On Gregory's prayer for Trajan Loserth refers to Wyclif's *De ecclesia*, pp. 531–534 (LEDGER P12u-2).
[^5]: **S.** Ranulf Higden, *Polychronicon* IV.26 (Rolls Series V, 148–150; Loserth: "Cf. Ranulphi Polychr. V, 148/9"); the print's chapter *CXII* is wrong (LEDGER P12u-3). Higden ends the chapter *et Graeci festum faciunt de illo undecimo die Maii*, so Wyclif's *XI* is his source's; the Greek church in fact keeps Constantine and Helen on 21 May (LEDGER P12u-4). The manuscripts' margin: *Constantinum magnum dotatorem quare non canonizat ecclesia*. A reads *sacrilepo* (395.17).
[^6]: **C.** *Debuit dedisse*: "ought to have given"; medieval usage also allows "is supposed to have given", which would make the blasphemy the claim of the gift itself. Either way the half "kept back" is the half of the empire that the Donation did not give. *Morientes*: we supply "[so]", that is, without satisfaction.
[^7]: **C.** *Alieno Cesari nimium sumptuosa*: we take it that honouring an emperor who is not the pope's would cost the pope too much in standing; the sense is doubtful. *Servum suum*: Constantine, counted the pope's servant. *Gracia pecunie*: "for the sake of money" (as GLOSSARY *gracia concomitancie*).
[^8]: **C.** We take the subject of *laudat* to be the eastern church: Constantine took the ruinous burden of endowment away from it (*subtraxit ab orientali ecclesia*), and for that it praises him. Loserth's side-note reads it otherwise, that he took the endowments from the eastern church. *Fratres de statu exproprietario* may glance at the friars, who profess to own nothing.
[^9]: **C.** *Color*: a plausible ground, here for concluding from a fevered taste to the worth of the thing. *Discrasiata*: "distempered", the physicians' word.
[^10]: **S.** Phil. 3:8 (DR "count them but as dung").
[^11]: **T/S.** *Alia recitative, alia reputative, assertive*: a third *alia* seems lost before *assertive*, giving three modes (reported, estimated, asserted); we supply "[some]". Without it, *reputative, assertive* would be "by estimation, [yet] assertively". *Testem … invoco Deum*: 2 Cor. 1:23 (DR "I call God to witness upon my soul"). *Intendo* is purpose here ("intend"); at its second and third use it states a thesis ("maintain").
[^12]: **R/T.** The protestation, Wyclif's offer to retract if shown to err. The passage is in his *Protestationes* (Loserth: ed. C. Höfler, *Anna von Luxemburg*, Denkschriften der Wiener Akademie 20, 235–236 (Loserth: 147, presumably the offprint's page), from Prague Univ. XI E 3 = C; LEDGER P12u-5). A in marg.: *Nota. Protestacio*. C reads *quia intendo primum* (396.19–20), *infinito* (23), *carnalia* (24); the manuscripts read *ac tamen* for *ac cum* (396.32). *In mediis*: the means to the end, not the middle terms of an argument.
[^13]: **S/T.** *Summo lapidi*: 1 Pet. 2:6 (DR "a chief corner stone"); John 14:6; Matt. 7:13–14 (DR "strait is the way", "broad is the way"). C reads *retardancia* (397.9), *in suis* (12), *vite et racio* (13).
[^14]: **R.** B reads *Wyk*. A (fol. 262, col. 2) adds a scribe's couplet, *Scriptoris cuncti memores sint ista legentes / atque precentur ei requiem iubilei* ("Let all who read these things be mindful of the scribe, and pray for him the rest of jubilee"), and a line in Czech, which Loserth renders *isto namque dignissimus est*, "for he is most worthy of it".
