### 12-b ¶1 · 205.25–206.3  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 205] **Sed** contra illud tripliciter arguitur.⁷ Primo posito quod duo corpora coextendantur et utrumque simul pro eodem loco et tempore gradiatur, videtur quod sunt duo motus progressivi,⁸ sicut due quantitates et cetera accidencia coextensa. Oppositum tamen videtur, cum motus individuatur sufficienter a spacio, tempore, et subiecto; **sed** idem est homo in numero, idem tempus et spacium; ergo cum nulla pars quantitativa hominis graditur, eadem est mocio progressiva. Et per idem posito quod secundum unam humanitatem [p. 206] penetraret progrediendo multas continuas humanitates¹ quiescentes continue,² idem Christus simul et semel secundum idem quiesceret,² et motu contrario moveretur.

ENGLISH: But against that, in three ways.[^1] First, supposing that two bodies are coextended and each walks at once for the same place and time, it seems that there are two progressive motions, just as [there are] two quantities and the other [accidents]{g:accidens} coextended. Yet the opposite seems [true], since a motion is individuated sufficiently by space, time and [subject]{g:subiectum}; now the man is the same in number, the time and the space the same; therefore, since no [quantitative part]{g:pars-quantitativa} of the man walks, the [progressive motion]{g:mocio-progressiva} is the same.[^2] And by the same token, supposing that as to one [humanity]{g:humanitas} [206] he were to penetrate, in going forward, many continuous humanities that are continually at rest, the same Christ would at one and the same time be at rest in the same respect, and be moved with a contrary motion.[^3]

### 12-b ¶4 · 208.6–208.17  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 208] Item hoc corpus et hec anima cum ista unione ypostatica et aliis que secuntur sunt sufficiencia principia ad faciendum Christum esse hominem; cum illis sive² aliis quibuscunque² positis, hoc sequatur. **Sed** multiplicatis completis principiis oportet multiplicari effectum formaliter consequentem. Ergo si sint multe humanitates assumpte, sunt multi homines; ut si Christus iam ad humanitatem habitam assumeret aliam humanitatem incommunicantem, illa assumpcio cum sit sufficiens principium, sufficit facere Christum iterum esse hominem; et sic quociens illa principia fuerint geminata.³ Ex quo sequitur quod si Christus multas humanitates assumeret, foret multi homines consequenter.

ENGLISH: Again, this body and this soul, with this hypostatic [union]{g:unio} and the other things that follow, are sufficient principles for making Christ to be a man; with these, or with any others whatever, posited, this would follow. Now when complete principles are multiplied, the effect that follows formally must be multiplied. Therefore, if there are many humanities assumed, there are many men; as, if Christ now, besides the humanity he has, assumed another humanity that does not communicate [with it], that assumption, since it is a sufficient principle, suffices to make Christ to be a man again; and so as often as those principles have been generated.[^14] From which it follows that, if Christ assumed many humanities, he would consequently be many men.

### 12-b ¶6 · 208.31–209.9  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 208] Similiter impossibile est formas completas eciam accidentales multiplicari simul et semel, nisi eo ipso multiplicentur supposita. **Sed** omnis humanitas est forma, cum sit illud [p. 209] quo substancia formaliter est homo; ergo multiplicatis humanitatibus multiplicabuntur et homines. Assumptum declaratum est de formis, accidentibus, et earum substanciis proximis, ut si sint multe ambulaciones simul et semel oportet multa supposita ambulare; et ita de aliis; ergo a maiori vel pari, cum humanitas sit suppositum esse hominem non individuata ex successione temporis vel adquisicione adventicii accidentis, quod positis multis humanitatibus multi homines consecuntur.

ENGLISH: Likewise, it is impossible for complete forms, even accidental ones, to be multiplied at one and the same time, unless by that very fact the supposits are multiplied. Now every humanity is a form, since it is that [209] by which a substance is formally a man; therefore, when humanities are multiplied, men too will be multiplied. [The assumption]{g:assumptum} has been [made clear]{g:declarare} of forms, accidents, and their proximate substances: as, if there are many walkings at one and the same time, many supposits must walk; and so of the others; therefore, from the greater or from the equal, since the humanity is a supposit's being a man, not individuated by succession of time or by the acquiring of an adventitious accident, [it follows] that, when many humanities are posited, many men follow.[^18]

### 12-b ¶7 · 209.10–209.26  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 209] 3°· idem patet, posito quod Verbum assumat multas embriones et post completas humanitates¹ animabus congenitis, patet quod anime advenientes, cum sint forme substanciales, faciunt Verbum esse aliud; et per consequens non relinquitur quid² aliud nisi illum hominem. Prius enim fuit completus Iesus et per consequens per posteriores animas non fit Iesus. Et si dicatur quod anime advenientes non faciunt Christum esse aliud **sed** alterius³ modi,³ tunc sunt forme accidentales advenientes enti in actu et non eiusdem racionis cum ceteris animabus, que in quantum⁴ huius modi, faciunt corpora animata. Patet igitur, si experiencia et principia philosophica salvari debeant, quod Verbo assumente multas humanitates est multi homines. Nam numerus hominum, qui est sensibile commune ex 2ᵈᵒ De Anima, ita patescit sensui secundum quantitates continuas dislocatas et secundum quotlibet differencias accidentales illarum naturarum, sicut innotescit aliquis sensibilis numerus alicui.

ENGLISH: Third, the same is clear, supposing that the Word assume many embryos, and, after the humanities have been completed with souls generated along with them, it is clear that the souls that come to them, since they are [substantial forms]{g:forma-substancialis}, make the Word to be [another thing]{g:aliud}; and consequently nothing else is left but that man.[^19] For before, Jesus was complete, and consequently he is not made Jesus through the later souls. And if it be said that the souls that come do not make Christ to be another thing but [to be] of another mode, then they are accidental forms coming to a [being]{g:ens} in act, and not [of the same kind]{g:eiusdem-racionis} as the other souls, which, [insofar as]{g:in-quantum} [they are] of this kind, make bodies [animate]{g:animare}.[^20] It is clear, therefore, if experience and the principles of philosophy are to be saved, that, the Word assuming many humanities, he is many men. For the number of men, which is a [common sensible]{g:sensibile-commune}, from the second [book] *On the Soul*, is as plain to sense, according to continuous quantities set in different places and according to any number of accidental [differences]{g:differencia} of those natures, as any perceptible number becomes known to anyone.[^21]

### 12-c ¶1 · 209.27–211.11  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 209] Item posito quod quodlibet suppositum generis animalis servatis⁵ omnibus positivis sit Verbo ypostatice copulatum; patet quod non exinde periret genus, species, vel differencia animalis; et per consequens non omne animal foret Christus; ex quo per locum a sufficienti similitudine sequitur quod Verbum assumendo multas humanitates non foret solummodo unus homo. Assumptum patet eo quod Verbum non⁶ necessitatur ex unione ypostatica corrumpere aliquod positivum; [p. 210] et, cum genus, species, et differencia sint plurimum positiva, patet quod non ex assumpcione oportet illas exstinguere; et cum non sit genus in actu nisi habeat species et illa supposita, que specifice¹ distinguantur, patet ex casu possibili illis quos alloquor² de facto posito; consequens est quod sint multa individua³ distincta specifice; non ergo omne individuum³ de genere vivencium foret Christus; maiorem enim distinccionem oportet esse individuis quam in formis communibus, cum omnia inferiora in suis communioribus uniuntur. Nec potest dici quod manet genus animalis cum suis divisivis differenciis sicut prius, cum indifferenter omnis homo sit asinus, leo, capra, et sic de aliis, si Christus non sit multa animalia racione multarum animalitatum ypostatice coniunctarum. Ubi ergo foret genus distinctum a specie vel individuo secundum communioritatem, si idem individuum cuiuslibet generis animalis sit commune cuilibet animali? Ubi eciam tunc⁴ foret differencia inter racionale et irracionale, rudibile et irrudibile⁵ et ita de ceteris,⁶ si omne animal racionale sit animal irracionale, rudibile sit irrudibile, et ita de⁶ omnibus aliis⁷ differenciis assignandis? Omnes ergo termini per se in predicamento substancie nec predicarentur in quid, nec de rebus distinctis specifice cum omne animal sit omne animal, nec forma aliqua est sufficiens medium distinguendi unum suppositum vel naturaliter⁸ ab altero eo quod species permixte de se mutuo predicantur; et cum⁹ tam species differant⁹ quam individua speciei, videtur quod ydemptificatis speciebus individua ydemptificari poterunt. Nec valet dicere quod ex assumpcione huiusmodi species corrumpuntur, quia omnis species, que prius fuit, habet omnem singularem essenciam, quam prius habuit; et Verbum nedum est suppositum hominis **sed** bovis, leonis, et cuiuslibet animalis. Cum ergo sufficit ad existenciam [p. 211] speciei quod unum suppositum illius nature¹ existat,² sequitur quod manet omnis species, que perante. Non enim est hic fallacia accidentis—quicquid fuerit in principio huius³ hore est modo; species leonis est⁴ aliquid quod fuit in principio⁵ illius hore; ergo omnis talis species est modo—. Et cum⁶ nulla possunt nunc plus differre et nunc minus, manencia omnino immota; sequitur quod, sicut nature per ante differebant specifice, sic et modo; et per consequens modo est species. Confirmatur ex hoc quod omnis species est natura,⁷ omnis⁷ natura animalis, que prius fuit, modo est; ergo⁸ omnis species, que prius fuit, modo est.⁸

ENGLISH: Again, supposing that every [supposit]{g:suppositum} of the genus animal, with all [its] positive [features] preserved, is joined [hypostatically]{g:ypostatice} to the [Word]{g:verbum}: it is clear that the genus, species or [difference]{g:differencia} of animal would not perish from that; and consequently not every animal would be Christ; from which, by [the topic from sufficient likeness]{g:locus-a-sufficienti-similitudine}, it follows that the Word, by assuming many [humanities]{g:humanitas}, would not be only one man.[^1] [The assumption]{g:assumptum} is clear in that the Word is not necessitated by the hypostatic [union]{g:unio} to corrupt anything positive; [210] and, since genus, species and difference are very much positive, it is clear that there is no need to extinguish them by the [assumption]{g:assumpcio}; and since there is no genus in act unless it has species and those supposits which are distinguished in species, it is clear from the case, possible for those whom I address, posited as fact: the [consequent]{g:consequens} is that there are many [individuals]{g:individuum} distinct in species; not every individual of the genus of living things, therefore, would be Christ; for there must be greater distinction in individuals than in common forms, since all lower things are united in their more common [ones].[^2] Nor can it be said that the genus animal remains with its dividing differences as before, since, indifferently, every man would be an ass, a lion, a goat, and so of the others, if Christ is not many animals by reason of many animalities hypostatically conjoined. Where, then, would the genus be, distinct from the species or the individual by [greater commonness]{g:communioritas}, if the same individual of every genus of animal is common to every animal? Where, too, would the difference then be between rational and irrational, able to bray and unable to bray, and so of the rest, if every rational animal is an irrational animal, the able-to-bray is the unable-to-bray, and so of all the other differences to be assigned?[^3] All the terms, therefore, that are [of themselves]{g:per-se} in the [category]{g:predicamentum} of substance would be predicated neither [*in quid*]{g:in-quid} nor of things distinct in species, since every animal would be every animal; nor is any form a sufficient [middle term]{g:medium} for distinguishing one supposit even naturally from another, in that the species are predicated of one another in a mixed way; and since species differ just as the individuals of a species do, it seems that, the species being [identified]{g:ydemptificare}, the individuals could be identified.[^4] Nor does it avail to say that by such an assumption the species are corrupted, because every species that was before has every singular [essence]{g:essencia} that it had before; and the Word is [not only]{g:nedum} the supposit of man but of ox, of lion, and of every animal. Since, therefore, it suffices for the existence [211] of a species that one supposit of that [nature]{g:natura} exist, it follows that every species remains that [was] before. For there is no [fallacy]{g:fallacia} of [accident]{g:accidens} here: "whatever was at the beginning of this hour is now; the species of lion is something that was at the beginning of that hour; therefore every such species is now."[^5] And since no things can differ now more and now less while remaining wholly unmoved, it follows that, as the natures differed in species before, so also now; and consequently species is now. This is confirmed from this, that every species is a nature; every nature of animal that was before is now; therefore every species that was before is now.[^6]

### 12-c ¶3 · 212.4–213.14  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 212] 2°. videtur sequi quod Christus sit quotlibet partes sue quantitative. Nam ante continuacionem Christus fuit compositum ex qualibet istarum animarum et suo corpore; ex quo sequitur quod Christus sit multe partes quantitative. Nec valet dicere quod natura humana, et non homo, sit pars hominis; quia partes quantitative corporis² sunt substancie per se in genere, quod negatur de humanitate; ergo sicut monstrum quod est homo geminus et a dyafragmate versus superius habet hominem eius partem, sic et Christus in casu posito. Non enim desinit esse completus homo secundum illam humanitatem propter continuacionem; sicut nec inciperet esse homo secundum istam humanitatem propter discontinuacionem. Concesso **autem** consequente videtur primo, si Christus sit quotlibet partes sue, quod multa quorum quodlibet³ est Christus; et cum Christus non sit aliud quam homo, sequitur quod multi homines sunt Christus et non propter continuacionem; ergo ante continuacionem fuit Christus multi homines; et non, si non ex assumpcione multarum humanitatum; ergo posito quod Christus multas humanitates assumat, consequitur ipsum esse multos homines, quod est intentum. Nec valet dicere quod Christus non habet partes huiusmodi, cum ex illis denominatur magnus, agens, beatus, et sic de formis ceteris que partibus suis insunt. Et si dicatur quod omnes sunt Christus et non pars eius, **sed** idem cum toto, tunc restat dicere secundum quam humanitatem Christus a Deo maioratur; cum non sit possibile humanitatem aggregari ex multis humanitatibus, nisi illa a qualibet illarum differat. Et patet quod secundum nullam illarum humatatum parcialium maioratur; ergo secundum illam totalem; et per consequens est nova humanitas sine correspondenti [p. 213] anima generata; quod est impossibile; cum nec corpus organicum correspondens tali humanitati fingi poterit, nec spiritus¹ implens materiam; nec corpus est maius propter iuxtaposicionem corporis quod non incidit in partem sui. Patet itaque logicis in casu posito quod ad omnem punctum Christi est non gradus augmentacionis, cum non sit motus nisi subiectum aliquod primo moveatur. Et hoc replicatur consequenter contra hoc Euclidis principium, ‘omne totum est maius sua parte quantitativa.’ Et posito² quod Christus assumat³ omnem naturam animalis; tunc post continuacionem omnium illorum factus monstrum foret alicubi non monstrum, et sic foret multe res, et per consequens multe substancie; quia aliter non foret pars sui, nisi pars et totum ponant in numerum.

ENGLISH: Second, it seems to follow that Christ is any number of his [quantitative parts]{g:pars-quantitativa}. For before the continuation Christ was a composite of each of these souls and its body; from which it follows that Christ is many quantitative parts. Nor does it avail to say that the human nature, and not the man, is the part of a man; because the quantitative parts of a body are substances of themselves in a genus, which is denied of the humanity; therefore, as the monster that is a twin man, and from the diaphragm upward has a man [as] its part, so too Christ in the case supposed.[^10] For he does not cease to be a complete man as to that humanity on account of the continuation; just as neither would he begin to be a man as to this humanity on account of the discontinuation. And, the consequent being granted, it seems, first, if Christ is any number of his parts, that [he is] many [things] of which each is Christ; and since Christ is nothing other than a man, it follows that many men are Christ, and not on account of the continuation; therefore before the continuation Christ was many men; and not [so], if not from the assumption of many humanities; therefore, supposing that Christ assume many humanities, it follows that he is many men, which is the point intended.[^11] Nor does it avail to say that Christ does not have such parts, since from them he is [named]{g:denominare} great, acting, blessed, and so of the other forms which are in his parts. And if it be said that all are Christ and not a part of him, but the same with the whole, then it remains to say as to which humanity Christ is made greater by God; since it is not possible for a humanity to be aggregated from many humanities unless it differs from each of them. And it is clear that he is made greater as to none of those partial humanities; therefore as to that total [one]; and consequently there is a new humanity generated without a corresponding [213] soul; which is impossible; since neither could an organic body corresponding to such a humanity be feigned, nor a spirit filling the matter; nor is a body greater on account of the juxtaposition of a body that does not fall into its part.[^12] It is clear, therefore, to logicians, in the case supposed, that at every point of Christ there is no degree of [augmentation]{g:augmentacio}, since there is no motion unless some subject is first moved. And this is [rejoined]{g:replicatur} consequently against this principle of Euclid's: "every whole is greater than its quantitative part."[^13] And supposing that Christ assume every nature of animal: then, after the continuation of all of them, having been made a monster, he would somewhere not be a monster, and so would be many things, and consequently many substances; because otherwise he would not be a part of himself, unless part and whole make up a number.[^14]

### 12-d ¶1 · 213.29–216.7  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 213] Item ex evidencia sancti Thome videtur quod Christus assumendo multas humanitates sit multi homines. Nam omne nomen appellativum, cuius principalia significata⁸ multiplicantur, predicatur⁹ vere⁹ pluraliter de eisdem. **Sed** hoc nomen homo primo et principaliter significat qualitatem [p. 214] substancialem ut humanitatem: ergo multiplicatis humanitatibus in Christo, et¹ homo vere predicatur pluraliter de eodem.¹ Maior ex hoc est evidens quod multiplicatis significatis multiplicanda sunt nomina significatorum; ut si tres persone divine haberent multas divinitates, tunc essent multi dii;² et sic de similibus quibuscunque. Minor **autem** patet ex hoc quod substancia secunda secundum Aristotelem in Predicamentis capitulo De Substancia significat quale-quid hoc est, substancialem³ qualitatem.³ Ut patet in methaphisica; homo enim principaliter significat humanitatem, album albedinem. Concesso ergo consequente et posito quod Verbum assumat omnes humanitates; consequens est quod sint multi homines ex assumpto; et cum Verbum sit omnis homo, sequitur quod Verbum sit multi homines. Negato vero assumpto de talibus concretis, **sed** concesso quod principaliter significant talia supposita, oportet dicere consequenter quod iste terminus homo⁴ omne quod est de sua primaria imposicione significat; et per idem quodlibet nomen appellativum vel concretum quodcunque significat.⁵ Nam omnem personam divinam homo de imposicione sua primaria significat, et cum hoc singulas creaturas; ergo conclusio. Maior patet ex hoc quod omne suppositum quod potest esse homo, signum significat; omnis persona divina potest esse homo; ergo etc. Et per idem bovem, asinum, leonem, et quamlibet speciem creature; cum omnis natura singularis huiusmodi potest a Verbo assumi stante unione personali ad hominem; quo posito de facto omnem talem naturam significaret, quia omnem hominem significaret;⁶ omnis talis species foret homo;⁶ ergo omnem talem speciem significaret; et cum nullum nomen significat universaliter formas specificas nisi eo ipso significaverit omnes formas individuas sub eis contentas, sequitur conclusio. Certum est tamen quod non fuit de intencione imponencium terminos ad significandum, [p. 215] quod iste terminus homo significaret deitatem; et ita de quolibet designando, quia tunc essent omnes tales termini analogi¹ et equivoci. Perirent eciam superioritas et inferioritas terminorum, et multi sillogismi in terminis substancialibus per fallaciam accidentis; ut sic arguendo— quicquid nascetur ex homine non eternaliter existebat; iste² homo nascetur ex homine; ergo non eternaliter existebat²; et sic de similibus. Ymo non est verisimile terminum significare philosopho illud quod nescit sibi significare, ymo quod credit non posse esse hominem. **Sed** constat quod gentiles philosophi assererent³ Deum non directe significari per terminos tales specificos, quia tunc concederent Deum posse⁴ esse hominem; ergo⁵ non significaverat illis Deum; et per idem nec nobis; cum univoce nobis significaret sicut illis nisi⁶ per accidens, quia contingit humanitatem esse Deum. Aliter enim cognoscerent et crederent omnes philosophi in confuso Deum fore hominem in hoc universali—omnis homo erit homo—et nunquam negarent differencias divisivas generi de se invicem predicari, ut sic—omnis homo est racionalis; asinus est homo; ergo est racionalis—. Sic enim concedunt theologi quod homo creavit mundum, quia persona Verbi, que est homo, creavit mundum. Pono itaque quod in mente philosophi ignorantis incarnacionem sit ista intencio specifica ‘homo’ naturaliter significans hominem et solum hominem. Et pono quod stante illa unione Deus assumat quamcunque naturam specificam animalis; et patet ex dictis et dicendis quod homo foret in qualibet specie animalis; et sic cum veris⁷ sequitur quod ista intencio homo continue naturaliter significans significaret asinum, leonem etc, sicut modo significat; et ita, ut arguitur in materia de ydemptificacione, quilibet terminus eque indifferenter significaret quidlibet. Quo dato non foret negandum quod homo est asinus; et sic quandocumque [p. 216] predicatur positivum de aliquo positivo, quod idem est directe significatum per predicatum et subiectum, quod sufficit ad verificandum enunciaciones huiusmodi cum paribus que ponuntur. Nec valet dicere quod nullus talis terminus naturam significat; cum sic iste terminus deus de sua intencione primaria hominem et omne assertibile significat.

ENGLISH: Again, from the [evidence]{g:evidencia} of St Thomas it seems that Christ, by [assuming]{g:assumere} many [humanities]{g:humanitas}, is many men. For every [appellative name]{g:nomen-appellativum} whose principal [significates]{g:significatum} are multiplied is truly predicated in the plural of them. Now this name "man" first and principally signifies a substantial quality, [214] as humanity: therefore, when the humanities are multiplied in Christ, "man" is truly [said] in the plural of him.[^1] The major is evident from this, that when the significates are multiplied, the names of the significates are to be multiplied; as, if the three divine [persons]{g:persona} had many divinities, they would then be many gods; and so of any like [cases]. And the minor is clear from this, that [second substance]{g:substancia-secunda}, according to Aristotle in the *Categories*, the chapter *On Substance*, signifies a "what-of-a-sort" (*quale quid*), that is, a substantial quality; as is clear in the *Metaphysics*; for "man" principally signifies humanity, [as] "white" whiteness.[^2] The [consequent]{g:consequens} being granted, then, and supposing that the [Word]{g:verbum} assume all humanities, the consequent is that there are many men from what is assumed; and since the Word is every man, it follows that the Word is many men.[^3] But if [the assumption]{g:assumptum} is denied of such [concrete]{g:concretum} [terms], and it is granted that they principally signify such [supposits]{g:suppositum}, one must say consequently that this term "man" signifies, by its [primary imposition]{g:imposicio-primaria}, everything that is; and by the same token that any appellative or concrete name whatever [so] signifies. For "man", by its primary imposition, signifies every divine person, and with this the single [creatures]{g:creatura}; therefore the conclusion. The major is clear from this, that the [sign]{g:signum} signifies every supposit that can be a man; every divine person can be a man; therefore etc.[^4] And by the same token [it signifies] ox, ass, lion and every species of creature; since every such singular [nature]{g:natura} can be assumed by the Word while the personal [union]{g:unio} to the man stands; and, this being posited in fact, it would signify every such nature, because it would signify every man; every such species would be a man; therefore it would signify every such species; and since no name signifies the forms of species universally unless by that very fact it has signified all the individual forms contained under them, the conclusion follows. Yet it is certain that it was not of the intention of those who imposed terms to signify [215] that this term "man" should signify the [Godhead]{g:deitas}; and so of any [term] designating, because then all such terms would be analogous and equivocal.[^5] There would perish too the superiority and inferiority of terms, and many syllogisms in substantial terms [would fail] by the [fallacy]{g:fallacia} of [accident]{g:accidens}, as by arguing thus: "whatever will be born of man did not exist eternally; this man will be born of man; therefore he did not exist eternally"; and so of like [ones].[^6] Indeed, it is not likely that a term signifies to a philosopher what he does not know it signifies to him, indeed what he believes cannot be a man. Now it is evident that the pagan philosophers would assert that God is not directly signified by such terms of species, because then they would grant "God can be a man"; therefore it had not signified God to them; and by the same token not to us; since it would signify to us univocally as to them, except by accident, because it happens that the humanity is God.[^7] For otherwise all the philosophers would know and believe [indistinctly]{g:confuse} "God will be a man" in this universal, "every man will be a man", and would never deny that the differences dividing a genus are predicated of one another, thus: "every man is rational; an ass is a man; therefore it is rational." For so the theologians grant "man created the world", because the person of the Word, which is man, created the world.[^8] I posit, then, that in the mind of a philosopher ignorant of the incarnation there is this intention of the species, "man", naturally signifying man and only man. And I posit that, while that union stands, God assume any [nature of the species]{g:natura-specifica} of animal whatever; and it is clear from what has been said and is to be said that man would be in every species of animal; and so, with true [premisses], it follows that this intention "man", continuing to signify naturally, would signify ass, lion, etc., as it signifies now; and so, as is argued in the matter of [identification]{g:ydemptificacio}, any term would signify anything equally indifferently.[^9] Which being given, it would not be to be denied that a man is an ass; and so, whenever a positive is predicated of some positive, [it would not be to be denied] [216] that the same thing is directly signified by predicate and [subject]{g:subiectum}, which suffices for making such statements true, with the like [conditions] that are posited. Nor does it avail to say that no such term signifies a nature; since in that way this term "god", by its primary intention, signifies man and every [assertible]{g:assertibile}.[^10]

Ymo videtur quod quilibet illorum trium hominum habeat peccatum maximum naturale, cum quilibet caret proprio capite, et sic de ceteris organis quibuscunque; et per idem sunt unum¹ corpus tantum. Licet **autem** tres persone assumendo tres homines forent indubie tres create substancie; tamen inconveniens est quod increata substancia sic plurificetur² in multas substancias, cum divina substancia non in plura dispergitur, **sed** in ipsam omnis pluralitas creature unite³ colligitur; quamvis enim Verbum sit due substancie, divina scilicet et humana, divina tamen substancia est tantum una substancia, cum non sit substancia creata: accipiendo enim huiusmodi substanciam pro natura et substanciam personaliter, sic non ponitur⁴ in numerum cum divina,⁵ cum sit ipsa. Unde videtur quod subiectum abstractum contrahat predicatum ad supponendum simpliciter pro natura, quando sic dicitur—substancia divina est substancia creata—et per consequens proposicio est neganda. **Sed** cum subiecto personali conceditur quod Verbum est substancia creata, quia homo et humanitas, quod non est deitas. Cuius racionis diversitas est variacio a supposicione simplici in personalem secundum limitacionem personalis termini vel abstracti. Et tunc non oportet concedere quod substancia increata sit creata substancia; licet Verbum, quod est increata substancia, sit creata substancia. Trinitas ergo foret in casu illo tres create substancie; et per consequens necessaria⁶ esset ad⁷ finem attingendum⁷ tanta in Deo pluralitas naturarum; quod est impossibile; cum ad redempcionem humani generis, ad beatitudinem hominis secundum [p. 225] corpus et animam, et quemlibet alium effectum eque sufficit Christi incarnacio, sicut incarnacio horum trium. Sicut ergo non sunt ponendi plures dii propter impossibilitatem superflui, sic nec plurium personarum incarnacio propter causam consimilem.

ENGLISH: Hence I wonder how [the moderns]{g:moderni}, denying "Christ is a [creature]{g:creatura}", and with this admitting "three divine [persons]{g:persona} [assume]{g:assumere} the same [humanity]{g:humanitas}, or three wholly separate ones", say "the divine substance is three men", and consequently three substances; for not three uncreated substances, since then they would be so eternally. But if it is three created substances, then three creatures would be the divine substance by [conversion]{g:conversio};[^1] and so [it would be] something it had not been before. For if these three substances differ personally, then the three divine persons are three substances, which, it is known, must be distinguished from the common divine substance. And if it is said that, after they were eternally persons and not substances, they were made substances, this is [said] [equivocally]{g:equivoce} with respect to eternal substance or person; because otherwise the uncreated Trinity would be a single substance and not several. And much more evidently, if only one humanity is assumed by the whole Trinity, there would not be three [224] material substances, for example three animals or three bodies; and so of the other human [attributes]{g:passio} with the other separable [accidents]{g:accidens}; because the adequate body, composed of this body and this soul, is every animal that is a man in a given place, since the principles of the singulars are the same, as was deduced above. Indeed it seems that each of those three men would have the greatest natural defect, since each lacks a head of his own, and so of any other organs whatever;[^2] and by the same token they are one body only. And although three persons, by assuming three men, would undoubtedly be three created substances, yet it is [an absurdity]{g:inconveniens}[^3] that the uncreated substance should be thus multiplied into many substances, since the divine substance is not dispersed into many things, but into it all the plurality of the united creature is gathered; for although the [Word]{g:verbum} is two substances, namely divine and human, yet the divine substance is only one substance, since it is not a created substance: for, taking substance of this kind for the [nature]{g:natura}, and substance personally, it is thus not put into number with the divine, since it is it.[^4] Hence it seems that an [abstract]{g:abstractum} subject restricts the predicate to stand with [simple supposition]{g:supposicio-simplex} for the nature,[^5] when it is said thus: "the divine substance is a created substance"; and consequently the [proposition]{g:proposicio} is to be denied. But with a personal subject it is granted that "the Word is a created substance", because [he is] a man and a humanity, which is not the [Godhead]{g:deitas}. The diversity of this reasoning is a variation from simple supposition to personal, according to the limitation of a personal or an abstract term.[^6] And then it is not necessary to grant "the uncreated substance is a created substance", although the Word, which is uncreated substance, is created substance. The Trinity, then, would in that case be three created substances; and consequently so great a plurality of natures in God would be necessary for attaining the end;[^7] which is impossible, since for the redemption of the human race, for the blessedness of man as to [225] body and soul, and for any other effect whatever, the incarnation of Christ suffices equally, as would the incarnation of these three. Just as, then, several gods are not to be posited because of the impossibility of the superfluous, so neither is the incarnation of several persons, for a like cause.

### 13-c ¶2 · 225.6–225.20  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 225] Ponunt **autem** doctores congruenciam¹ in Christo quare incarnatus est et nec Pater nec Spiritus sanctus. Primo quia² Verbum,² quod est naturalis ymago Patris; per quod factus est homo ad Dei ymaginem, per eandem ymaginem debuit reformari. Unde ex proprietate ymaginis fuit consonancius quod ymago ymaginem assumeret, quam potencia vel benevolencia alterius persone. Forme enim est reformare deformitatem; sapiencie vincere peccati maliciam remissibilem ex ignorancia contractam; et ymaginis exemplaris fabricam turpatam ad dictam ymaginem prius factam [revocare].³ Verumptamen sicut vir non peccavit nisi excitante muliere et dyabolo, et sic iuvamine alterius⁴ debuit relevari:⁵ sic nec persona Verbi fecit redempcionis misterium sine coefficiencia Trinitatis, aliter tamen Verbum quam duo alii.

ENGLISH: And the doctors posit a [fittingness]{g:congruencia} in Christ, why he was incarnate and neither the Father nor the Holy Spirit.[^8] First, because the Word is the natural image of the Father; through him man was made to the image of God, [and] through the same image he had to be reformed.[^9] Hence from the property of an image it was more consonant that the image should assume the image than the [power]{g:potencia} or benevolence of another person. For it belongs to form to reform deformity; to wisdom to overcome the malice of sin, remissible [as] contracted from ignorance; and to the exemplar image to [restore] the defaced workmanship to the said image after which it was first made.[^10] Nevertheless, just as the [man]{g:vir} did not sin except at the instigation of the woman and the devil, and so had to be relieved by the help of another, other than the devil,[^11] so neither did the person of the Word perform the [mystery]{g:misterium} of redemption without the co-working of the Trinity, though the Word [did so] otherwise than the two others.

### 13-c ¶4 · 226.1–226.25  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 226] 3° concludendo¹ breviter, non est aliqua Verbi proprietas quin limitat Verbo² congruere² incarnacionis misterium ultra quam aliis. Mediam quidem³ personam oportet esse mediatorem Dei et hominum; Verbum eternaliter de corde ingenito eructatum decet verbis predicacionis ostendere Patrem suum Ioh. 17°. ‘Pater, manifestavi nomen tuum;’ ‘Dei virtus atque Dei sapiencia,’ prima ad Cor. 1°, debuit vicium ex ignorancia superare;⁴ ut homo, qui⁴ in sapiencia conditus est per insipienciam perditus appetendo equalitatem increate sapiencie, per illam sapienciam restauretur, et dyabolus usurpando acceptam virtutem et potenciam⁵ per dantem⁶ virtutem et⁵ bracchium deprimeretur, Luc 1°. ‘fecit potenciam in bracchio suo.’ Et sicut secundum Augustinum est equalitas immediate consequens ad suum principium, sic ab eo specialiter omnis morbi inequalitas ad temperanciam reducatur: ut inequalitas sanitatis in qua dyabolus cecidit, appetendo equalitatem divine auctoritatis sive potencie, secundum iudicium datum Filio per impotenciam puniatur, qua non possit a peccato in Spiritum⁷ sanctum resurgere; inequalitas **autem**, in qua homo ignoranter peccando in sapienciam, stulte appetendo eius equalitatem, ceciderat, est⁸ precise equalitatis terminus,⁹ cum sit sapiencia, verbum, virtus, pulcritudo, et species reducenda; et ita de ceteris sacramentis que scriptura de Christo commemorat.

ENGLISH: Thirdly, concluding briefly, there is no property of the Word that does not [assign]{g:limitare} to the Word that the mystery of the incarnation befits [him] beyond the others. The middle person, indeed, must be the mediator of God and men;[^14] it befits the Word, eternally uttered from the unbegotten heart, to show his Father by words of preaching, John 17[:6]: "Father, I have manifested thy name";[^15] "the power of God and the wisdom of God", 1 Corinthians 1[:24], had to overcome the vice [that comes] from ignorance; so that man, who was established in wisdom [and] was lost through folly by desiring equality with the uncreated wisdom, might be restored through that wisdom; and that the devil, by usurping the power and might he had received, might be pressed down through the power and arm of the one who gave [them], Luke 1[:51]: "He hath shewed might in his arm."[^16] And as, according to Augustine, equality follows [without intermediary]{g:immediate} upon its principle,[^17] so by him in particular every inequality of disease should be brought back to [temperance]{g:temperancia}: so that the inequality of health in which the devil fell, desiring equality of divine authority or power, should be punished, according to the judgement given to the Son, by an impotence through which he cannot rise again from the sin against the Holy Spirit;[^18] and the inequality in which man, sinning ignorantly against wisdom by foolishly desiring its equality, had fallen, is to be brought back through precisely the term of equality, since he is wisdom, word, power, beauty, and form;[^19] and so of the other [sacraments]{g:sacramentum}[^20] which scripture records of Christ.

### 13-c ¶6 · 226.31–227.26  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 226] Quod si obicitur omnes tres sectas concorditer admittere quod quelibet persona¹¹ divina eandem naturam assumat [p. 227] vel quotlibet¹ separatim; dicitur tripliciter concordando istam radicem. Primo quod in ista materia opinative locuti sunt nichil temere asserendo, ut patet ex scriptis eorum. 2ᵈᵒ quod ipsi intelligunt ipsam possibilitatem condicionaliter, si Deus voluerit; ad modum loquendi Anselmi. Vel 3° si replicando inutiliter de sensibus eorum argueretur, quod ipsi sic simpliciter sencierunt, potest dici quod non ad laudem eorum opinio eorum forsitan fuit talis. Et taliter glosant ipsi superiores doctores, quorum sensus pocius debet credi. **Sed** non valet—si isti sic senciunt opinando; ergo verum—cum per idem contradiccio sequeretur; ipsi enim discrepant a seipsis. **Sed** et sanctus Thomas super Dist. 6. 3ⁱⁱ Sentenciarum Questione....,² dicit quod sentencia concedens humanitatem assumptam esse Christum non est heretica, licet sit impossibilis in precipua materia fidei. De quo miror, cum tot sancti doctores qui in aliis materiis ambigue sunt locuti, illam sentenciam in materia fidei affirmaverant tam constanter. Cum ergo pium et salubre foret de fide Christiana sentenciam impossibilem tocius fidei corruptivam investigando excludere, videtur quod ecclesia sollicite discuteret quid in ista materia foret catholice sustinendum; quia certum est quod una pars est formaliter impossibilis; ex qua sequitur oppositum tocius fidei, ymo ut noverunt logici, quidlibet inferendum; et talis latens error in principio per vias inopinatas infirmat partes fidei consequenter.

ENGLISH: But if it is objected that all three [sects]{g:secta}[^22] with one accord admit "each divine person assumes the same nature, [227] or any number separately", the answer is, bringing this root into agreement in three ways: first, that in this matter they spoke by way of opinion, asserting nothing rashly, as is clear from their writings. Secondly, that they understand that possibility conditionally, "if God should will", after Anselm's manner of speaking.[^23] Or thirdly, if, by rejoining uselessly about their meanings, it were argued that they thought so [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}, it can be said that perhaps their opinion was such, not to their praise. And in this way the superior doctors themselves [gloss]{g:glosare} [it], whose sense ought rather to be believed. But it does not hold: "if these think so by way of opinion, therefore it is true", since by the same token a contradiction would follow; for they disagree with themselves. Moreover, Saint Thomas too, on distinction 6 of the third [book] of the *Sentences*, question ...,[^24] says that the [teaching]{g:sentencia} granting "the assumed humanity is Christ" is not heretical, although it is impossible in a principal matter of faith. At which I wonder, since so many holy doctors, who in other matters have spoken ambiguously, affirmed that teaching in a matter of faith so constantly. Since, then, it would be pious and salutary to exclude from the Christian faith, by investigation, an impossible teaching, corruptive of the whole faith, it seems that the [church]{g:ecclesia} should carefully discuss what in this matter should be held as catholic; because it is certain that one side is formally impossible; from which follows the opposite of the whole faith, indeed, as the logicians know, anything whatever is to be inferred; and such a hidden error in the principle, by unexpected ways, consequently weakens parts of the faith.

### 13-d ¶2 · 228.4–228.21  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 228] Hic dicitur ut supra quod variacio in methaphisica et honoris extraneandi ambicio est in causa. In cuius signum vix duos invenies quin discordent. Alii **autem** superiores doctores ut Augustinus, Ieronimus, Gregorius, et Anselmus cum suis sequacibus dicunt concorditer quod Christus post incarnacionem est aliquid, quod prius non fuerat, ut patet superius; et illi concordant cum racione, et verbaliter ac sentencialiter cum scriptura. Verumptamen vanum est excludere illos simpliciter a peccato; cum laborando circa¹ veram sentenciam secundum studium bonum² de genere contingit catholicum presumptiva superbia vel³ inani gloria peccare facillime; quanto magis defendendo impossibile, licet intencio secundum aliquid sit recta et vera sentencia: quodlibet⁴ enim impossibile disperiret,⁵ si per se sine mixtura veri defensum fuerit!

ENGLISH: Here the answer is, as above, that variation in metaphysics and the ambition for the honour of [estrangement]{g:extraneare} is the cause. In token of which you will scarcely find two who do not disagree. But the other, superior doctors, such as Augustine, Jerome, Gregory, and Anselm with their followers, say with one accord that Christ after the incarnation is something which he had not been before, as is clear above; and they agree with reason, and in word and in [teaching]{g:sentencia} with scripture.[^2] Nor does [it follow]{g:sequitur} from that that the later [doctors] were heretics, since a catholic can defend a heretical teaching without pertinacity, as I believe has happened in the case at hand. Nevertheless it is vain to exclude them [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} from sin; since in labouring about a true teaching, with a study [good in its kind]{g:bonum-de-genere},[^3] it happens most easily that a catholic sins by presumptuous pride or vain glory; how much more by defending the impossible, although the intention in some respect be right and the teaching true: for every impossible [position], indeed, would perish, if it were defended by itself without admixture of truth![^4]

### 13-d ¶4 · 228.27–229.8  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 228] Hic videtur michi quod argumentum peccat tam in materia quam in forma. In materia, quia solum veritas Deo subicitur; nulla impossibilitas est veritas; ergo assumptum peccat. Quod si intelligamus per impossibilitatem signi impossibilis falsitatem vel veritatem negativam, que est absoluta necessitas, nichil ad propositum; **sed** ad oppositum adducitur. Nam non prodest quod hoc signum sit impossibile—Pater [p. 229] potest incarnari—vel—quod necessario non potest esse quod Pater potest incarnari—ad concludendum propositum. Nec oportet instare pro improbacione argumenti, cum reductum ad formam patenter peteret; sicut facit secundum argumentum, quo capit humanam naturam infinitum distare a qualibet persona divina, et omnium¹ eque distancium est eque possibilis coniunccio. Nam maior est impossibilis, et sensus minoris petitur.

ENGLISH: Here it seems to me that the argument errs both in matter and in form. In matter, because only truth is subject to God; no impossibility is a truth; therefore [the assumption]{g:assumptum}[^7] errs. And if by "impossibility" we understand the falsity of an impossible [sign]{g:signum}, or a negative truth, which is absolute necessity, it is nothing to the purpose; rather, it is brought forward for the opposite. For it does not help, for [concluding]{g:concludere} the purpose, that this sign is impossible, "the Father [229] can be incarnated", or "necessarily it cannot be that the Father can be incarnated". Nor is there need to object in disproof of the argument, since, reduced to form, it would plainly [beg]{g:petitur} [the question]; as does the second argument, in which he takes "human nature is infinitely distant from each divine person, and of all things equally distant the conjunction is equally possible". For the major is impossible, and the sense of the minor is begged.[^8]

### 13-d ¶7 · 229.15–229.30  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 229] Et conformiter dicitur ad argumenta quibus videtur probare quod eadem natura potest assumi a qualibet personarum. Primo ex hoc quod omnis humanitas est indifferenter ad ymaginem Trinitatis, et³ illa est racio assumendi; ymo cum Verbum humanitatem assumpserit,⁴ est ipsa similior et habilior⁵ ut ab utraque reliqua assumatur. Constat quidem quod faccio ad ymaginem Dei non est communis racio ut quelibet humanitas⁶ a qualibet⁶ persona assumi poterit, quia sic omnis homo posset esse Trinitas increata; **sed** Christo inest racio singularis, unde ille singulariter a Verbo assumi poterit; et petitur utrobique probandum. Unde multum distat unio potenciarum anime eidem organo et⁷ unio humanitatis ad Filium; ita quod non sequitur per locum a simili—si multe potencie anime possunt simul uniri eidem organo,⁷ ergo per idem multe persone divine possunt uniri⁸ ypostatice eidem corpori—.

ENGLISH: And conformably the answer is given to the arguments by which he seems to prove that the same [nature]{g:natura} can be assumed by any of the persons. First, from this: that every [humanity]{g:humanitas} is indifferently to the image of the Trinity, and that is the reason for assuming; indeed, since the [Word]{g:verbum} has assumed the humanity, it is the more like and the more [qualified]{g:habilis}[^11] to be assumed by either of the others. It is evident, indeed, that the [making]{g:faccio} to the image of God is not a common reason why any humanity whatever could be assumed by any person whatever, because then every man could be the uncreated Trinity; but in Christ there is a singular reason, whence he could singularly be assumed by the Word; and in both places what is to be proved is begged. Hence the [union]{g:unio} of the powers of the soul to the same organ is far different from the union of the humanity to the Son; so that it does not follow by the topic [from likeness]{g:a-simili}: "if many powers of the soul can be united at once to the same organ, therefore by the same token many divine persons can be united [hypostatically]{g:ypostatice} to the same body".[^12]

### 13-d ¶8 · 229.31–230.18  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 229] Quod si queratur⁹ racio diversitatis, satis est quod hoc facto tres persone possent¹⁰ esse unus homo et quelibet [p. 230] persona divina multi homines; sicut consequenter annuit sanctus Thomas, et¹ hoc posito non sic esset, ut patet ex dictis. Quantum ad argumenta, que facit Doctor subtilis in principio 3ⁱⁱ pro hac parte, respondet ipsemet satis clare. Quod si addatur— Verbum divinum liberius copulatum Christo quam aliquam² carnem carni ypostatice copulatam; **sed** anima potest multis carnibus copulari; ergo multo magis Verbum alii humanitati—; dicitur quod consequencia non valet per locum a simili. Nam Verbum liberius produxit hoc tempus perpetuum quam Petrus hunc filium; et tamen Verbum non potuit produxisse aliud tempus, quod nec sit illud nec eius pars, licet Petrus potuit alium filium produxisse, ymo filium qui non fuisset pertinens huic nato. Conceditur tamen quod quamcunque carnem spiritus assumpserit vel sibi personaliter uniret,³ foret solummodo idem homo. Et sic licet homo posset esse aliud quam est modo, quia alia natura corporea, non tamen aliud suppositum vel persona, ut patet in materia De Anima.

ENGLISH: And if the reason for the difference is asked, it is enough that, this done, three persons could be one man and each [230] divine person many men, as Saint Thomas consequently grants; and, this posited, it would not be so, as is clear from what has been said.[^13] As for the arguments which the [Subtle Doctor]{g:doctor-subtilis} makes for this side at the beginning of the third [book], he himself answers them clearly enough.[^14] And if it is added: "the divine Word [is] more freely joined to Christ than another flesh hypostatically joined to flesh; but a soul can be joined to many fleshes; therefore much more the Word to another humanity", the answer is that the [inference]{g:consequencia} does not hold by the topic from likeness.[^15] For the Word more freely produced this perpetual time than Peter this son; and yet the Word could not have produced another time, which would be neither this one nor a part of it, although Peter could have produced another son, indeed a son who would not have been related to this one born. It is granted, however, that whatever flesh the spirit had assumed or personally united to itself, it would be only the same man. And so, although a man could be another thing than he is now, because another bodily nature, yet not another [supposit]{g:suppositum} or person, as is clear in the matter of the *De anima*.[^16]

### 13-d ¶9 · 230.19–231.22  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 230] Ex istis colligitur quomodo aliqua insint Verbo pure in quantum Deus; aliqua⁴ in quantum homo; et⁴ aliqua mixtim. In quantum Deus, creavit mundum; in quantum homo, passus est mortem; et in quantum Deus et homo, redemit hominem. Nec debet signum reduplicatum intelligi utrobique, ut dicat⁵ consequenciam formalem logicam et causalem; sicut hic Christus in quantum homo est creatura; **sed** et⁶ quomodolibet dicit causam, ut licet potest esse Deus et non produxisse mundum, tamen deitas fuit⁷ causa quare produxit mundum et non sua humanitas. Et licet deitas fuit⁸ causa quare paciebatur, non tamen secundum illam; quia tunc fuisset causa formalis vel⁹ subiectum passionis; conceditur tamen quod humanitas fuit causa⁹ formalis Christi et causa subiectiva mortis: et sic in quantum homo passus est mortem, quia secundum humanitatem. Et [p. 231] intelligendum est in quantum, ut dicat¹ generaliter causam² quamlibet parcialem vel totalem, completam vel incompletam. Ego non video quin sane concedi poterit quod, in quantum Deus, paciebatur; cum deitas fuit per se causa illius passionis, **sed** non secundum deitatem, cum tunc deitas principaliter pateretur. Redempcio **autem** humani generis pro offensa dicit infinitatem precii proporcionaliter ad delictum; et sic oportet redimentem esse precium infinitum; et per consequens Deum; et preter hoc oportet quod distinguatur essencialiter a solvente, cum non sit possibile quidquam³ mercari primo et immediate secundum seipsum; et per consequens oportet precium, quod secundum deitatem est infinitum, esse naturam alienam⁴ creatam. Cum **autem** nulla alia potuit pertinere nisi illa que peccaverat, que compendiosissime sit⁵ et satisfaciens et precium, mercans et mercatum, reconcilians et reconciliatum; relinquitur quod oportet ipsam esse humanitatem ‘mediatorem⁶ Dei et hominum, hominem Christum Iesum.’ Et sic quidquid infuit sibi formaliter,⁷ infuit sibi⁷ in quantum Deus; licet non omne, quod sibi infuit, inerat secundum quod Deus, nec in quantum homo. Deitas enim fuit per se causa cuiuscunque causati, quod sic infuit Iesu nostro.

ENGLISH: From these things it is gathered how some things belong to the Word purely [insofar as]{g:in-quantum} God; some insofar as man; and some [in a mixed way]{g:mixtim}. Insofar as God, he created the world; insofar as man, he suffered death; and insofar as God and man, he redeemed man. Nor ought the [reduplicated sign]{g:signum-reduplicatum} to be understood in both places as [signifying]{g:dicere} a formal, logical and causal inference, as here: "Christ insofar as man is a [creature]{g:creatura}"; but also as it signifies a cause in any way whatever; as, although he can be God and not have produced the world, yet the [Godhead]{g:deitas} was the cause why he produced the world, and not his humanity. And although the Godhead was the cause why he suffered, yet not as to it; because then it would have been the formal cause or the subject of the passion; it is granted, however, that the humanity was the formal cause of Christ and the subjective cause of death: and so insofar as man he suffered death, because [he suffered it] as to the humanity. And [231] "insofar as" is to be understood as it signifies generally any cause, partial or total, complete or incomplete.[^17] I do not see but that it could soundly be granted that, insofar as God, he suffered; since the Godhead was [of itself]{g:per-se} the cause of that passion, but not [as to the Godhead]{g:secundum-deitatem}, since then the Godhead would principally suffer. And the redemption of the human race for the offence signifies an infinity of price in proportion to the fault; and so the redeemer must be an infinite price, and consequently God; and besides this he must be distinguished essentially from the one paying, since it is not possible for anything to buy, first and [without intermediary]{g:immediate}, according to itself; and consequently the price, which as to the Godhead is infinite, must be a foreign created nature.[^18] And since no other could pertain except that which had sinned, which most compendiously would be both the satisfying and the price, the buyer and the bought, the reconciler and the reconciled, it remains that it must be the humanity, "the mediator of God and men, the man Christ Jesus".[^19] And so whatever was in him formally was in him insofar as (*in quantum*) God; although not all that was in him was in him [insofar as]{g:secundum-quod} (*secundum quod*) God, nor insofar as man.[^20] For the Godhead was of itself the cause of anything caused that was thus in our Jesus.
