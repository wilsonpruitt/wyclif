## Doubtful, left as they are

- **01-b, p. 4**: *Habet autem iste cibus … virtutem assimilandi sibi manducantem*, rendered "Now". Either (a), the premiss that eating assimilates the eater to the four digestions (so eaters are predestinate), or (c), a remark extending the John 6 step. "Now" is defensible.
- **01-d, p. 12**: *nullus christianus, sed precipue Christi vicarius non est membrum*, rendered "no Christian, and especially no vicar of Christ". Intensive *sed precipue* ("and above all") fits none of the classes cleanly; "but especially" would read as a contrast. The English "and especially" carries it.
- **01-h, p. 22**: *Ecclesia autem universalis … habet duo capita*, rendered "But". Either (b), set against the three heads of a particular church, or (c), the next item of the reply ("And the universal church").
- **02-c, p. 36**: *Loquitur autem scriptura de nomine reali*, rendered "Now". Either (a), the premiss that closes the Acts 4 argument (the pope's name is another real name), or (c), a gloss added to it.
- **03-a, p. 50** (changed above): *Cum autem …* could also be (a), "Now since", the second half of the premiss behind *quis dubitat*; "But" chosen as the plainer reading.
