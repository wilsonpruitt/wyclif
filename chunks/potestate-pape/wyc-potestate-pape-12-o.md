# *De potestate pape*, chapter 12, part 15: the Franciscan pope, insignia, and the propertyless state

## ¶1 · 369.1–370.32

> [p. 369] Sed tercio obicitur per hoc, quod Nicolaus de ordine fratrum Minorum dicitur de comuni consensu cardinalium et cleri ordinasse, quod non erit amplius papa nisi de suo ordine; quod videtur racionabile, cum ille sit status perfectissimus viatoris, ut patet in Sexto: Exiit qui seminat. Cum igitur illa ordinacio non potest dissolvi nisi per fratrem minorem papam, quod nunquam contigit, videtur quod abhinc non fuit aliquis verus papa. Pro isto supponuntur leges ecclesie, primo quod hec ordinacio fuit valida, secundo quod illa non potest tolli nisi per papam et clerum, et tercio quod nunquam in aliqua synodo tollebatur; tunc videtur quod repugnat ipsam tolli nisi per papam fratrem minorem, quia esto quod cras tolleretur per papam secularem. Sed contra repugnat stante lege ista quod papa huiusmodi sit electus, igitur repugnat legibus predictis quod ordinacio illa taliter dissolvatur. Hic patet quod ecclesia humanis tradicionibus insolubiliter cathenatur; et utinam non forent leges alique papales minus racionales quam est ista. Stat igitur solucio in duobus, primo quod decretalis talis non valuit, secundo quod ordines et alia sacramenta possunt ministrari licite, nullo papa cesareo existente, nec oportet plus dilatare istam materiam. Quantum ad tercium punctum de necessitate privilegiorum et regiminis capitalis in fide catholica, patet quod Christus cum lege sua per se sufficit. Signa autem ut cruces, baculum et similia, que Cesar instituit, ut [p. 370] patet XCVI distinccione Constantinus, non vocamus privilegia, sed deliramenta, et, ut Crysostomus loquitur super epistolam ad Thym racionabiliter deridenda. Si enim videres senem puerilibus ornamentis indutum non gracia ludendi, sed maniace necessaria regimini sui corporis repudiantem, nonne deridendo diceres huiusmodi insanire? Multo magis presbiter vel prelatus, qui preponderat signa muta et putrida ante virtutes signatas est ut maniacus deridendus, et per tutorem tamquam decrepitus insaniens regulandus. Ideo necesse est reges et principes honorem melancolicum, ut puta copiam temporalium suorum subtrahere, quia ille facit omnes tales prelatos, ultra hoc quod sapuerunt apostoli vel primitiva ecclesia insanire. Contentemur igitur de paucis ritibus quos ex doctrina Christi servarunt apostoli, et omnino honeste superadditos vere et modeste non preponderanter vel ypocritice observemus. Omnia enim talia non expediunt vel sunt licita, nisi de quanto iuvant ad observanciam mandatorum. Unde videtur adulterina degeneracio, quod mandata Dei quasi neutra respicimus, et cum tradicionibus hominum sumus insolubiliter desponsati. Domini igitur, quorum bonis abutimur, debent sua subtrahere, ne ex alieni peccati participio maculentur; nec obest huic sed prodest quod principibus limitatum est a Deo officium ad tuendam ecclesiam, ut patet in decreto beati Ysidori in de summo bono libro III. Et hic notant multi quod dicit ad tuendum, non spoliandum ecclesiam.

But thirdly [it is objected, by appeal to]{g:obicitur-per} this, that Nicholas, of the order of Friars Minor, is said to have [ordained]{g:ordinacio}, with the common consent of the cardinals and the clergy, that there shall be no pope any more except from his order;[^1] which seems reasonable, since that is the most perfect [state]{g:status} of a [wayfarer]{g:viator}, as is clear in the Sext, *Exiit qui seminat*.[^2] Since therefore that ordinance cannot be dissolved except by a Friar Minor pope, which never happened, it seems that from then on there has not been any true pope. For this the laws of the [church]{g:ecclesia} are supposed: first, that this ordinance was valid; second, that it cannot be removed except by the pope and the clergy; and third, that it was never removed in any synod. Then it seems that it [is incompatible]{g:repugnare} that it be removed except by a Friar Minor pope; for suppose that tomorrow it were removed by a secular pope. But against this: it is incompatible, while that law stands, that such a pope be elected; therefore it is incompatible with the aforesaid laws that that ordinance be dissolved in such a way. Here it is clear that the church is insolubly chained by human traditions; and would that there were no papal laws less reasonable than this one.[^3] The solution therefore stands in two things: first, that such a decretal was not valid; second, that orders and the other sacraments can be ministered lawfully with no [caesarean pope]{g:papa-cesareus} existing; nor is there need to enlarge further on this matter. As to the third point, on the necessity of [privileges]{g:privilegium} and of [capital]{g:capitalis} rule in the catholic faith, it is clear that Christ with his law suffices of himself. And signs such as crosses, the staff and the like, which Caesar instituted, as [370] is clear in distinction 96, *Constantinus*, we call not privileges but [ravings]{g:deliramenta}, and, as Chrysostom speaks on the epistle to Timothy, reasonably to be derided.[^4] For if you saw an old man dressed in childish ornaments, not for the sake of play, but maniacally repudiating what is necessary to the rule of his body, would you not say in derision that such a one is mad? Much more is a presbyter or [prelate]{g:prelatus} who weighs mute and rotten signs above the virtues they signify to be derided as a maniac, and to be ruled by a guardian as one decrepit and insane. So it is necessary that kings and princes withdraw the melancholic honour, for example the [plenty]{g:copia} of their temporal goods; for that honour makes all such prelates mad beyond what the apostles or the primitive church were wise in.[^5] Let us therefore be content with the few rites which the apostles kept from the [doctrine]{g:doctrina} of Christ, and let us observe those decently added on wholly, truly and modestly, not preponderantly or hypocritically. For all such things are not expedient or lawful except in so far as they help toward the keeping of the commandments. Hence it seems an adulterous degeneration that we regard the commandments of God as if they were [neutral things]{g:neutra}, and are insolubly espoused to the traditions of men. The [lords]{g:dominus} therefore whose goods we abuse ought to withdraw what is theirs, lest they be stained by [participation]{g:participium} in another's sin; nor does it hinder this, but helps it, that the office of protecting the church is [assigned]{g:limitare} to princes by God, as is clear in the decree of blessed Isidore, in *On the Highest Good*, book 3.[^6] And here many note that he says "to protect", not "to despoil", the church.

## ¶2 · 371.1–371.33

> [p. 371] Sed constat sciolos quod non est color: Si beatus iste dicit quod principes debent tueri, et non dicit quod debent spoliare ecclesiam, igitur non licet eis ex caritate medicinaliter auferre temporalia ab ecclesia delinquente. Sicut enim magna est distancia inter celum et terram, sic multum distant predanter spoliare et medicinaliter sic auferre. Ideo notaret sic arguens, quod sanctus doctor dicit: Principes debere tueri ecclesiam, non dicit dotare temporaliter vel ditare. Unde maior elemosina foret principibus tueri ecclesiam in limitibus religionis quam Christus instituit quam dare sibi quantamlibet elemosinam corporalem; et hoc possunt, cum possunt culpabiliter hos privilegii limites per accidens destruere, et per consequens possunt meritorie eosdem privilegii limites stabilire. Qui enim habet secundum leges dyaboli facultatem peccandi, habet pocius facultatem secundum leges Dei sibi et ecclesie promerendi. Ideo sicut seculares possunt obesse ecclesie per stultam et secularem dominorum dotacionem, sic possunt facilius atque licencius prodesse ecclesie per prudentem eorum subtraccionem. Pro declaracione autem conclusionis predicte capio quod status exproprietarie vivendi, in quo vixit clerus ante dotacionem, sit melior clero atque perfeccior quam quotquot seculares mundi divicie; quod probo per hoc quod proporcionaliter ut aliquid adiacet viatori plus ipsum habilitans ad beatitudinem acquirendam est ipsum melius atque utilius; sed status ille ad finem illum magis habilitat, igitur est habenti plus utilis.

But it is evident to the [knowing]{g:sciolus} that there is no [colour]{g:color} [in this]:[^7] "If this blessed man says that princes ought to protect, and does not say that they ought to despoil the church, therefore it is not lawful for them, out of [charity]{g:caritas}, to take temporal goods away medicinally from a delinquent church." For as there is a great distance between heaven and earth, so to despoil like a plunderer and to take away medicinally in this way are far apart. So one who argues thus should note that the holy doctor says: "Princes ought to protect the church"; he does not say "endow it temporally or [enrich]{g:ditare} it".[^8] Hence it would be greater [alms]{g:elemosina} for princes to protect the church within the [limits]{g:limites} of the religion which Christ instituted than to give it any bodily alms whatever; and this they can do, since they can culpably destroy these limits of privilege by accident, and consequently they can meritoriously establish the same limits of privilege. For he who has, according to the laws of the devil, the faculty of sinning, has rather the faculty, according to the laws of God, of meriting for himself and for the church. So, as [seculars]{g:secularis} can harm the church by the foolish and secular [endowment]{g:dotacio} of lords, so they can more easily and more freely profit the church by their prudent withdrawal of it.[^9] And for the clarifying of the aforesaid [conclusion]{g:conclusio} I take it that the state of living [without property]{g:exproprietarie}, in which the clergy lived before the endowment, is better for the clergy and more perfect than all the secular riches of the world; which I prove by this, that in proportion as something attaches to a wayfarer that [qualifies]{g:habilitare} him more for acquiring blessedness, it is better and more useful [to him]; now that state qualifies more for that end; therefore it is more useful to the one who has it.

## ¶3 · 371.34–372.5

> [p. 371] Minor probatur tripliciter: primo per hoc quod Ihesus noster aliter inconvenienter daret ecclesie [p. 372] illum statum, cum posset eque faciliter dotasse quantumcumque clerum suum in mundi diviciis; non enim est potentis, prudentis et benevolentis sic magis bonum postponere, quia tunc vel foret invidus vel ignarus.

The minor is proved in three ways: first by this, that otherwise our Jesus would unfittingly have given the church [372] that state, since he could as easily have endowed his clergy to any extent whatever in the riches of the world; for it is not [the part] of one powerful, prudent and benevolent so to put the greater good after, since then he would be either envious or ignorant.

## ¶4 · 372.6–372.16

> [p. 372] Similiter, quantumcumque divicie accumulentur, eciam cum securitate mundana perpetua non ponunt possessionatum nisi profundius in statu proprietario minus perfecto. Cum igitur status ille ideo dicitur principaliter minus perfectus, quia est plus a beatitudine tardativus, (ut sepe patet ex fide evangelii) sequitur quod status ille sit proprietate contraria clero utilior. Contrariorum enim oportet unum esse perfeccius tamquam habitus et reliquum imperfeccius tamquam privacio.

Likewise, however much riches are accumulated, even with perpetual worldly security, they only set the [possessioner]{g:possessionati} more deeply in the [proprietary]{g:proprietarius} state, which is less perfect. Since therefore that state is said to be less perfect principally because it holds a man back more from blessedness (as is often clear [from the faith]{g:ex-fide} of the gospel), it follows that that [other] state is more useful to the clergy than the property contrary to it.[^10] For of contraries one must be more perfect, as habit, and the other more imperfect, as privation.

## ¶5 · 372.17–373.8

> [p. 372] Similiter, omne opus misericordie vel elemosine spiritualis est pocius quam opus misericordie vel elemosine corporalis, sed dare tuicionem quoad statum et privilegia que Christus instituit est opus misericordie spirituale, non comparacione ad ditacionem mundanam, igitur est ipsa prestancius. Aliter enim Christus invide peiorasset apostolos, vocando eos a mundi diviciis ad purum statum exproprietarium, sicut et fratres contra caritatis regulas dampnificarent pueros et alios divites seculares, dum faciunt eos fratres, ymo procurarent et laudarent plus homines qui facerent eos proprietarios quam conditores suorum ordinum, vel patres qui fecerant eos fratres; quod credo eos dicere esse apostatarum sentenciam; vel aliter sequitur, quod donantes ordinibus illis loca et domicilia ad habitandum et contractas, peccarent graviter, et sic ipsi suum ordinem observando; debent enim iuxta falsigraphum vivendo [p. 373] in statu possessionato meritorius plus prodesse ecclesie. Ideo stulte et sine causa quam Deus approbat viverent sic parce atque penaliter, si sic deficiat perfeccio sui status. Ideo ipsi debent esse singulariter pugiles huius sentencie, nedum quia est fundamentum sui ordinis, sed quia leges ecclesie et fides scripture cum racionibus idem dicunt.

Likewise, every work of mercy or of spiritual alms is preferable to a work of mercy or of bodily alms;[^11] now to give protection as regards the state and the privileges which Christ instituted is a spiritual work of mercy, not to be compared with worldly [enrichment]{g:ditare}; therefore it is more excellent than that. For otherwise Christ would enviously have made the apostles worse off, calling them from the riches of the world to the pure [propertyless]{g:exproprietarii} state; just as the friars too, against the rules of charity, would do harm to boys and other rich seculars when they make them friars; indeed they would seek out and praise more the men who would make them proprietaries than the founders of their orders, or the fathers who had made them friars; which I believe they themselves call the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of apostates. Or else it follows that those who give those orders places and dwellings to live in, and districts, would sin gravely,[^12] and so would the friars themselves in keeping their order; for according to the [false writer]{g:falsigraphus} they ought, by living [373] in a [possessioned]{g:possessionatus} state, to profit the church more meritoriously. So they would live thus sparingly and [in hardship]{g:penaliter} foolishly, and without a cause that God approves, if the perfection of their state fails in this way. So they ought to be singularly the champions of this teaching, not only because it is the foundation of their order, but because the laws of the church and the faith of scripture, with reasons, say the same.

## ¶6 · 373.9–373.28

> [p. 373] Et per idem sequitur quod in dominis temporalibus et tota ecclesia foret status ille utilior, quia aliter religiosi stulte et irreligiose facerent multos fratres. Et patet ex eodem quod successio temporis non impedit, sed requirit quod debemus sic vivere, ymo temporis periculositas, temptacionis crudelitas et temporalium detestabilitas docent nos nunc pocius mundana relinquere; nam senescente mundo est homo pronior ad avariciam, et hinc dyabolus temptans in hoc acucius est caucius obviandus. Et patet, quod est contra racionem hec avaricia, quia res mundane sunt plus solito magis flexibiles, et peiores homines sunt plus debiles ad gaudendum in illis et vita breviores; cum igitur non faciant sed excitant ad sitibundius appetendum, patet ex doctrina in caritate sapiencie quod illis remotis sunt futura spiritualia appetenda, et illi qui ex obligacione vivunt vitam exproprietariam nimis degenerant, si isti sentencie tacite vel expresse contradicant.

And by the same token it follows that in [temporal lords]{g:temporales-domini} and in the whole church that state would be more useful; for otherwise the [religious]{g:religiosi} would foolishly and irreligiously make many friars. And it is clear from the same that the succession of time does not hinder, but requires, that we should live so; indeed the dangerousness of the time, the cruelty of temptation and the detestableness of temporal goods teach us now rather to relinquish worldly things; for as the world grows old man is more prone to avarice, and hence the devil, tempting more sharply in this, must be met more warily. And it is clear that this avarice is against reason, because worldly things are more changeable than usual, and worse men are weaker for enjoying them and shorter of life; since therefore they do not [satisfy] but excite to a thirstier desire,[^13] it is clear from the teaching in the charity of wisdom that, these being removed, the spiritual things to come are to be desired; and those who live the propertyless life by obligation degenerate too far if they contradict this teaching, tacitly or expressly.

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 369.1: Pope Nicholas is said to have ordained that the Pope should always be a Franciscan.
- 369.10: This ordinance can be repealed only by a franciscan Pope and has not been so repealed,
- 369.20: All such decrees are void.
- 370.2: It is madness to set symbols above the virtue symbolized.
- 370.16: Let us be content with the rites which the apostles learned from Christ.
- 370.25: Rulers should withdraw endowments abused by the clergy.
- 371.1: To do so is not Church but to protect it.
- 372.17: To maintain the ordinances of Christ is a work of charity far higher than worldly gifts.
- 372.27: Otherwise the state of possessioners is higher than that of Friars.
- 373.9: As the world grows older avarice increases and needs to be more resisted.

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** Loserth refers to Higden, *Polychronicon* 8, p. 268 (Rolls Series) for the story (LEDGER P12o-1). The Franciscan pope is Nicholas IV (1288–92), to be checked (LEDGER P12o-2).
[^2]: **S.** *Exiit qui seminat* (Nicholas III, 1279), *Sext* 5.12.3 (Loserth "Tit. XII, Cap. III"; the modern form is ours) (LEDGER P12o-3).
[^3]: **T.** *Racionales* is Loserth's text; the manuscripts read *irracionales* (369.22), "less irrational".
[^4]: **S.** *Decretum* D.96 c.14 *Constantinus* (Loserth "Cap. XIV"; the modern form is ours), the Donation of Constantine (LEDGER P12o-4). Chrysostom on 1 or 2 Timothy: Loserth gives "Chrysostomus l. c.", to be checked (LEDGER P12o-5).
[^5]: **C.** *Insanire ultra hoc quod sapuerunt*: the play is on *sapere* "be wise" against *insanire* "be mad". The *honor melancolicus* is the honour that breeds madness (melancholy, the humour of madness).
[^6]: **S.** *Decretum* C.23 q.5 c.20, from Isidore, *Sententiae* (*De summo bono*) 3, cap. 53 in Loserth's numbering (the modern form is ours) (LEDGER P12o-6).
[^7]: **C.** *Constat sciolos*: the accusative is irregular (one expects *sciolis*); we take it as "it is evident to the knowing". *Color*: a plausible ground; the argument quoted has none.
[^8]: **C.** Loserth italicises *Principes debere tueri ecclesiam, non dicit dotare temporaliter vel ditare* as one run; *non dicit* is Wyclif's, so we quote the two halves apart.
[^9]: **C.** *Licencius*: "more freely" (from *licenter*); "more lawfully" is possible if the word stands for *licitius*.
[^10]: **C.** *Status ille* changes referent: in the first clause the proprietary state, in the second the propertyless state set against *proprietas*. We add "[other]".
[^11]: **T.** *est pocius*: we take *pocius* as the adjective, "preferable" (*potior*); a comparative (*melius*, *prestancius*) may have dropped out of the text at 372.17.
[^12]: **G.** *Contractas*: Loserth, *contratas*, "country"; here "districts", the friars' begging limits. **T.** *Ad* (*ad habitandum*) is Loserth's supply; the manuscripts omit it (372.35).
[^13]: **T.** *Faciant* stands in both manuscripts (Loserth, *ita codd.*); *satiant*, "satisfy", seems meant, and we supply it in brackets.
