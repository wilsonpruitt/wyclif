# *De eucharistia*, chapter 6, part 1: the disposition of the worthy receiver: faith, hope, charity

## ¶1 · 158.12–158.31

> [p. 158] Sed dimissa ista contenciosa materia superest videre (ut tactum est superius) quomodo christianus debet disponi ad ministrandum et recipiendum hoc sacramentum. Iste enim foret fructus tractatus scolastici istius materie quoad dignitatem conficiencium et recipiencium hoc sacramentum; patet quod oportet digne sumentem ipsum habere actus et habitus virtuosos, cum oportet quod Dei virtus et Dei sapiencia sit munde secundum habitus et opera quoad humanitatem suam undique ministrata; oportet, inquam, digne sumentem hoc sacramentum habere tres virtutes theologicas et conversacionem virtuosam imitatoriam capitis sui Christi; quod quidam intelligunt ex verbis Apostoli prima I. Cor. Vᵒ, quando docet quod non debemus epulari in fermento veteri quoad maculam peccati sed in azimis sinceritatis quoad virtutes que sunt disposicio ad recipiendum lumen gracie et operis virtuosi et veritatis vite qua fidelis servus Christi ostenditur in opere. Et hec est plena disposicio ad digne accipiendum corpus dominicum.

But, this contentious matter set aside, it remains to see (as was touched on above) how a Christian ought to be disposed for ministering and receiving this [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}. For this would be the fruit of a scholastic treatise on this matter, as regards the worthiness of those who [consecrate]{g:conficere} and those who receive this sacrament: it is clear that one who takes it worthily must have virtuous acts and habits, since the power of God and the wisdom of God must be ministered cleanly on every side, in habits and works, as regards his [humanity]{g:humanitas};[^1] one who takes this sacrament worthily must, I say, have the three theological virtues and a virtuous [manner of life]{g:conversacio} imitative of his head, Christ. This some understand from the words of the Apostle in 1 Cor. 5, where he teaches that we ought not to feast with the old leaven, as to the stain of sin, but with the unleavened bread of sincerity, as to the virtues, which are the disposition for receiving the light of grace and of virtuous work, and of truth of life, by which the faithful servant of Christ is shown in work.[^2] And this is the full disposition for worthily receiving [the Lord's body]{g:corpus-dominicum}.[^3]

## ¶2 · 159.1–159.11

> [p. 159] Quoad virtutes theologicas oportet omnino habere fidem sanctam de humanitate et deitate Christi et de concernentibus hoc sacramentum, quia inpossibile est infidelem in istis placere Deo manducando vel bibendo hoc sacramentum in memoriam Christi, cum ipse sit veritas odiens duplicitatem falsitatis; ideo infidelis ut sic vertit dorsum mentis in Dominum et ostendit sibi arma peccati Deo summe odibilia, convertit se ad patrem mendacii proditorie et derisorie, ideo talis iudicium sibi manducat et bibit secundum Apostolum.

As to the theological virtues, one must altogether have a holy faith about the humanity and [Godhead]{g:deitas} of Christ and about the things that concern this sacrament, for it is impossible for one who is an [unbeliever]{g:infidelis} in these to please God by eating or drinking this sacrament in memory of Christ, since he is the truth, hating the doubleness of falsehood;[^4] therefore the unbeliever as such turns the back of his mind to the Lord and shows him the arms of sin, supremely hateful to God; he turns himself to the father of lies traitorously and mockingly; therefore such a one "eateth and drinketh judgment to himself", according to the Apostle.[^5]

## ¶3 · 159.12–160.14

> [p. 159] Habita autem dignitate ex fundamento fidei necesse est quod erigatur spes in Domino omnipotente, omnisciente et benivolente, qui tantam gratitudinem nobis sibi ingratis ostendit humanitus paciendo, moriendo, ascendendo et sacramentum illud in memoriam istorum et aliorum mirabilium tribuendo. Unde Augustinus Enchiridion LIIIᵒ: Quidquid autem gestum est in cruce Christi, in sepultura, in resurreccione tercia die, in ascensione in celum et in eo quod sedet ad dextram patris, ita gestum est ut hiis rebus non mistice tantum dictis sed eciam gestis configuraretur vita christiana que hic geritur. Nam propter eius crucem dictum est: Qui autem Christi sunt carnem suam crucifixerunt cum viciis et concupiscenciis. Propter sepulturam: Consepulti enim sumus Christo per baptismum in mortem. Propter resurreccionem: Ut quemadmodum Christus resurrexit a mortuis per gloriam patris, ita et nos in novitate vite ambulemus. Propter ascensionem in celum sedemque ad [p. 160] dextram Patris: Si consurrexistis cum Christo, que sursum sunt sapite, non que super terram: Mortui enim estis et vita vestra abscondita est cum Christo in Deo. Ecce quomodo iste sanctus connectit ex fide scripture istam figuram nostram quintuplicem et rem gestam ad nostram doctrinam in moribus. Et totum istud debemus secundum usum ecclesie in sacramento eukaristie specialiter memorari, cum Christus precipiat pro istis Luc. XXᵒ, 19: Hoc facite in meam commemoracionem; et addit ecclesia: Hec quocienscunque feceritis in mei memoriam facietis. Et patet quomodo dignitas manducacionis huius sacramenti requirit quod ipsum digne accipiens per spem ad Dominum erigatur.

And, worthiness had from the foundation of faith, it is necessary that hope be raised up in the Lord, almighty, all-knowing and benevolent, who showed such great [kindness]{g:gratitudo} to us who are [ungrateful]{g:ingratus} to him,[^6] [humanly]{g:humanitus} suffering, dying, ascending, and granting that sacrament in memory of these and other wonders. Hence Augustine, *Enchiridion* 53: "And whatever was done in the cross of Christ, in his burial, in his resurrection on the third day, in his ascension into heaven and in his sitting at the right hand of the Father, was so done that by these things, not only said [mystically]{g:mistice} but also done, the Christian life which is lived here might be configured. For on account of his cross it is said: 'And they that are Christ's have crucified their flesh, with the vices and concupiscences.' On account of his burial: 'For we are buried together with Christ by baptism into death.' On account of his resurrection: 'That, as Christ is risen from the dead by the glory of the Father, so we also may walk in newness of life.' On account of his ascension into heaven and his seat at the [160] right hand of the Father: 'If you be risen with Christ, mind the things that are above, not the things that are upon the earth. For you are dead: and your life is hid with Christ in God.'"[^7] See how this saint connects, [from the faith of scripture]{g:ex-fide}, this fivefold [figure]{g:figura} of ours and the thing done, for our [doctrine]{g:doctrina} in morals. And all this we ought, according to the use of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, to remember specially in the sacrament of the Eucharist, since Christ commands for these things, Luke 22:19: "Do this for a commemoration of me"; and the church adds: "As often as you do these things, you shall do them in memory of me."[^8] And it is clear how the worthiness of [eating]{g:manducacio} this sacrament requires that one who receives it worthily be raised up to the Lord through hope.

## ¶4 · 160.15–161.2

> [p. 160] Et quoad caritatem patet quod nullum sacramentum notat expressius caritatem et pacem habendam cum Deo et homine quam hoc signum. Exhinc enim Christus tradens apostolis hoc sacramentum finaliter commendat tam crebro et specialiter in fine (ut patet Joh. XXᵒ) pacem apostolis. Hinc eciam secundum usum ecclesie datur signum pacis, antequam sacerdos vel laycus manducat hanc hostiam. Debet enim habere pacem cum fratre et per consequens cum Deo qui vult digne recipere sacramentum, ymmo secundum maximum signum caritatis debet attendi per hoc sacramentum ad ponendum tam corpus quam animam pro amicis, ut supra dictum est per Augustinum. Ex hinc Deus posuit sacramentum huius rei in illis naturis que ad unum aliquid reducuntur. Oportet enim quod recipiens digne simpliciter istam hostiam sit originatus predestinacione tamquam spiritualiter generatus univoce a grano frumenti quod cadens in [p. 161] terram mortuum est afferens multum fructum (ut dicitur Joh. XIIº).

And as to [charity]{g:caritas}, it is clear that no sacrament marks more expressly the charity and peace to be had with God and man than this [sign]{g:signum}. For hence Christ, giving this sacrament to the apostles, finally commends peace to the apostles so often and specially at the end (as is clear from John 20).[^9] Hence also, according to the use of the church, the sign of peace is given before [priest]{g:sacerdos} or layman eats this [host]{g:hostia}. For he who wishes to receive the sacrament worthily ought to have peace with his brother and consequently with God; indeed, according to the greatest sign of charity, heed ought to be given through this sacrament to laying down both body and soul for one's friends, as was said above through Augustine.[^10] Hence God placed the sacrament of this reality in those [natures]{g:natura} which are brought together into some one thing.[^11] For one who receives this host worthily [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}[^12] must have his origin in predestination, as [spiritually]{g:spiritualiter} begotten [univocally]{g:univoce} from the grain of wheat which, falling into [161] the ground, died, bringing forth much fruit (as is said in John 12).[^13]

## ¶5 · 161.3–161.13

> [p. 161] Nec sufficit ista predestinacio nisi granum abiecto furfure molitum sit et tritum a Deo, que causaliter prevenit promerentem. Oportet enim predestinatum post primam graciam simul tempore presenciali labore mereri secundam graciam; et sic simul tempore post istam disposicionem oportet tercio quod sit gracia cooperans ad formam panis prandendi in patria, sic quod in isto tercio signo nature homo ex duplici gracia priori incipiat meritorie operari; que quidem gracia ex opere persone meritorie inpastans disponit ad graciam ampliorem.

Nor is this predestination enough unless the grain, its bran cast off, has been milled and ground by God, which causally goes before the one who merits.[^14] For [the predestinate]{g:predestinatus} [person] must, after the first grace, at the same time, by present labour, merit the second grace;[^15] and so, at the same time, after this disposition, there must thirdly be cooperating grace towards the form of the [bread]{g:panis} to be dined on in [the homeland]{g:patria}, so that in this third [instant of nature]{g:signum-nature} a man begins to work meritoriously from the twofold prior grace;[^16] which grace, [kneading]{g:inpastare} meritoriously from the work of the person, disposes to an ampler grace.[^17]

## Notes

[^1]: **S/C.** *Dei virtus et Dei sapiencia* is Christ, after 1 Cor. 1:24 (DR "Christ, the power of God and the wisdom of God"); *virtus* is therefore "power". He is "ministered" in the sacrament as regards his humanity. In C, *virtus et Dei* (158.19) is written above the line.
[^2]: **S/C.** 1 Cor. 5:8, DR "let us feast, not with the old leaven … but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth"; Wyclif glosses as he quotes, and *et veritatis vite* is taken as resuming the Vulgate's *et veritatis* after the gloss on *sinceritatis*. *Prima I. Cor.* doubles the "first" as printed. D reads *ad conversacionem* (158.23), AB *quidem* (158.24), D *recipiendum* (158.31).
[^3]: **G.** *Corpus dominicum*, the adjective, is rendered "the Lord's body", kept apart from *corpus Domini* "the body of the Lord".
[^4]: **S/T.** After Heb. 11:6, DR "without faith it is impossible to please God". D reads *fidem rectam* and *divinitate* (159.2), E *et bibendo* (159.5–6); for *duplicitatem falsitatis* D has *secunditatem falsitatis*, B *duplicem falsitatem* (159.6).
[^5]: **S.** *Patrem mendacii*: John 8:44 (DR "he is a liar, and the father thereof"). *Iudicium sibi manducat et bibit*: 1 Cor. 11:29, as Loserth notes (DR "eateth and drinketh judgment to himself").
[^6]: **G.** *Gratitudo* is the kindness shown, not thanks returned; *ingratis* here is of persons ungrateful to God, so "ungrateful" (the §D row), not "displeasing".
[^7]: **S/T.** Augustine, *Enchiridion* 53 (14.53), Loserth's reference *Opp.* VI p. 216 (LEDGER K06a-1). ABCE give the chapter as LII, D as LV; *LIII* is Loserth's correction (159.18). The scripture is Gal. 5:24; Rom. 6:4 (Vulgate *cum illo*, Wyclif *Christo*); Col. 3:1–3, from which the text omits "seek the things that are above, where Christ is sitting at the right hand of God". In C, *tercia die* (159.19–20) is in the margin in another hand; E reads *ascendit ad dextram*, A *alias a dextris* (159.20); AB *sedens que* (159.30).
[^8]: **S.** Wyclif's *Luc. XX* is Luke 22:19 (DR). The church's addition is the words of the canon of the mass after the consecration of the chalice, *Haec quotiescunque feceritis, in mei memoriam facietis* (Sarum Missal, ed. Dickinson, col. 617) (LEDGER K06a-2). AB lack *nostram* (160.5), C adds it in the margin; E reads *qualiter* and *manducandi* (160.12).
[^9]: **S.** *Joh. XX* (confirmed on the plate): Christ's "Peace be to you", John 20:19, 21, 26. *Exhinc* gives the reason: the sacrament signifies charity, and so Christ commended peace. In C, *in fine* is in the margin in another hand.
[^10]: **S/T.** The greatest sign of charity: John 15:13, DR "that a man lay down his life for his friends"; Wyclif says *corpus quam animam*. The manuscripts read *ut ut* (160.27); D lacks *hanc* (160.22). A has *Nota* in the margin at 160.29.
[^11]: **C/S.** *Sacramentum huius rei*: *res* is the reality of the sacrament (charity and unity), as the ruled row *res sacramenti*. The natures "brought into one" are the many grains in one bread and grapes in one wine, after Augustine, *Tract. in Ioh.* 26.17 (PL 35, 1614): *in eis rebus commendavit, quae ad unum aliquid rediguntur ex multis* (LEDGER K06a-3). ABD read *specialiter* (160.31).
[^12]: **C.** *Simpliciter* is taken with *digne* ("worthily without qualification"); it could go with *recipiens*.
[^13]: **S.** John 12:24–25 (DR "unless the grain of wheat falling into the ground die … if it die it bringeth forth much fruit"); Wyclif's *mortuum est afferens* is his own wording. Here begins the figure of the bread that Loserth's side-notes number: the grain, the grinding, the kneading, the baking.
[^14]: **C/T.** *Que* (feminine) is taken as *predestinacio*: predestination causally goes before (*prevenit*, cf. prevenient grace) the one who merits. D reads *casualiter* (161.4).
[^15]: **T/C.** The manuscripts read *prius primam* (161.6), where the print has *post primam*; ABCD read *principali* for *presenciali* (161.6). *Simul tempore* "at the same time": the order of the graces is of nature, not of time (*signo nature*, 161.9).
[^16]: **G/T.** *Signum nature*: "instant of nature", the scholastic moment prior by nature though not by time; not the §D row *signa* "stages" of an argument. ABC read *ex secunda* (161.10).
[^17]: **C/T.** *Meritorie* is taken as the adverb; it could be the genitive *persone meritorie* "of the meritorious person". *Inpastans*: kneading, the third stage of the bread (side-note 161.8). E reads *meritoria* (161.12), DE *disponat*.
