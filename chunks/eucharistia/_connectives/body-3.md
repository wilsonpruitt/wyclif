# sed/autem extract, wave 3: chunks 01-e 02-a 02-b

### 01-e ¶1 · 25.19–27.19  (8 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 25] Et per hoc potest glosari decretum sequens: Ego Beringarius, ubi iste diaconus anathematizavit heresim qua prius fuerat irretitus; et facit protestacionem sue fidei sub hiis verbis: Consencio **autem** sancte Romane ecclesie et apostolice sedi, et ore et corde profiteor de sacramentis dominice mense eandem fidem me tenere quam dominus et venerabilis papa Nicholaus et hec sancta sinodus auctoritate evangelica et apostolica tenendam tradidit michique firmavit, scilicet panem et vinum que in altari ponuntur post consecracionem non solum sacramentum **sed** eciam verum corpus et sanguinem domini nostri Jesu Christi [p. 26] esse sensualiter, non solum sacramentaliter **sed** in veritate manibus sacerdotum tractari et frangi et fidelium dentibus atteri, iurans per sanctam et omousion Trinitatem et per hec sacrosancta Christi evangelia. Eos qui contra hanc fidem venerint cum dogmatibus et consecutoribus suis eterno anathemate dignos esse pronuncio. Ista tam sollempnis confessio non potest intelligi quod panis et vinum sit formaliter vel essencialiter corpus Christi et sanguis, **sed** figurative vel tropice, modo quo loquitur Apostolus I. Cor. Xº, 4: Bibebant **autem** de spirituali consequente eos petra, petra **autem** erat Christus. Unde glosa ordinaria intelligit per panem et vinum speciem panis et vini, modo quo loquitur perante eadem distinccio sub auctoritate Gregorii: Species et similitudo earum rerum vocabula sunt que ante fuerant, scilicet panis et vini; unde in fine cuiusdam misse oratur et dicitur: Perficiant in nobis, quesumus, Domine tua sacramenta que continent, ut que nunc sub specie panis et vini gerimus rerum veritate capiamus. Quotlibet sunt dicta sanctorum atque ecclesie que docent quod illa sacramenta sensibilia non sunt corpus Christi et sanguis **sed** eorum figura; et forte in isto multi sunt christiani nomine in fidelitate paganis peiores. Nam minus malum foret quod homo illud quod primo videt mane per totum residuum diei honoret ut Deum quam quod regulariter illud accidens quod videt in missa inter manus sacerdotis in hostia consecrata sit [p. 27] realiter Deus suus; et per hec est medium ad intelligendum decreta similia, ut distinccione XCVª, Ecce ego dico sub auctoritate Jeronimi sic habetur: Quid maius est Christo aut quid poterit corpori et sanguini eius anteponi? Si presbiter Christum consecrat, cum in altari sacramenta benedicat, benedicere populo non debet qui Christum consecrare non metuit? Videtur **autem** sanctum intelligere figurative per Christum hostiam consecratam, ita quod eadem sit consecracio qua consecrat hostiam et qua consecrare dicitur corpus Christi; hostiam tamen facit esse sacram saltem occasionaliter, dum rite facit ministerium sacerdotis, et Deus appropriate perficit quod suum est supernaturaliter alterando; quod si equivocetur in consecracione, vocando eam ostensionem rei sacre, tunc sicut laycus potest benedicere Trinitati, sic eciam consecrare, sicut laycus facit quod corpus Christi sit sacrum in isto loco vel sub isto sacramento et tamen non propterea consecrat corpus Christi.

ENGLISH: And by this the following [decree]{g:decretum} can be [glossed]{g:glosare}: *Ego Berengarius*,[^1] where this deacon anathematized the heresy in which he had before been entangled; and he makes a [declaration]{g:protestacio} of his faith in these words: "And I consent to the holy Roman [church]{g:ecclesia} and the apostolic see, and with mouth and heart I profess that I hold, concerning the [sacraments]{g:sacramentum} of the Lord's table, the same faith which the lord and venerable pope Nicholas and this holy synod have delivered to be held by evangelical and apostolic authority and have confirmed to me, namely that the [bread]{g:panis} and wine which are placed on the altar are after the consecration not only a sacrament but also the true body and blood of our Lord Jesus Christ, [26] [and that] [sensually]{g:sensualiter},[^2] not only [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} but in truth, they are handled and broken by the hands of the priests and ground by the teeth of the faithful; swearing by the holy and homoousion Trinity and by these most holy gospels of Christ. Those who come against this faith, with their dogmas and their followers, I pronounce worthy of eternal anathema." So solemn a confession cannot be understood [to mean] that the bread and wine is formally or essentially the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} and [his] blood,[^3] but [figuratively]{g:figurative} or [tropically]{g:tropice}, in the way the Apostle speaks, 1 Cor. 10:4: "And they drank of the spiritual rock that followed them: and the rock was Christ." Hence the ordinary [gloss]{g:glosa} understands by "bread and wine" the [species]{g:species} of bread and wine,[^4] in the way the same distinction speaks earlier under the authority of Gregory: "The species and the likeness are the names of those things which were before", namely of bread and wine;[^5] hence at the end of a certain mass it is prayed and said: "May thy sacraments, we beseech thee, O Lord, perfect in us what they contain, that what we now perform under the species of bread and wine we may receive in the truth of the things."[^6] There are any number of sayings of [the saints]{g:sancti} and of the church that teach that those [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} sacraments are not the body of Christ and [his] blood but their [figure]{g:figura}; and perhaps in this many are Christians in name, worse than pagans in faithfulness.[^7] For it would be a lesser evil that a man should honour as God, through the whole rest of the day, that which he first sees in the morning, than that, [as a rule]{g:regulariter}, that [accident]{g:accidens} which he sees at mass between the hands of the priest in the [consecrated host]{g:hostia-consecrata} should be [27] [really]{g:realiter} his God;[^8] and by these things there is a means of understanding similar decrees, as in distinction 95, *Ecce ego dico*, it is held thus under the authority of Jerome: "What is greater than Christ, or what can be put before his body and blood? If a [presbyter]{g:presbiter} consecrates Christ when he blesses the sacraments on the altar, ought he who does not fear to consecrate Christ not to bless the people?"[^9] And the saint seems to understand figuratively by "Christ" the consecrated host, so that the consecration by which he consecrates the host and that by which he is said to consecrate the body of Christ is the same; yet he makes the host to be [sacred]{g:sacer} at least [by occasion]{g:occasionaliter}, while he duly performs the ministry of the priest, and God appropriately completes what is his, by altering [it] supernaturally; and if one equivocates on "consecration", calling it the showing of a sacred thing, then just as a layman can bless the Trinity, so [he can] also consecrate, as a layman brings it about that the body of Christ is sacred in this place or under this sacrament and yet does not on that account consecrate the body of Christ.[^10]

### 01-e ¶3 · 27.29–28.6  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 27] **Sed** arguitur quod sacerdos potest consecrare corpus Christi, quia potest benedicere deitati, potest [p. 28] ymmolare Filium Deo Patri et potest sanctificare Deum in corde suo (ut dicitur prima Petri IIIº, 15). Quodlibet **autem** istorum est plus quam consecrare corpus Christi; si ergo sacerdos habet potestatem in maius, habet potestatem et in minus, sicut dicit sanctus doctor linguarum peritissimus.

ENGLISH: But it is argued that a [priest]{g:sacerdos} can consecrate the body of Christ, because he can bless the [Godhead]{g:deitas}, he can [28] immolate the Son to God the Father, and he can sanctify God in his heart (as is said in 1 Peter 3:15).[^14] Now each of these is more than to consecrate the body of Christ; if therefore a priest has [power]{g:potestas} for the greater, he has power also for the lesser, as the holy doctor most skilled in tongues says.[^15]

### 01-e ¶4 · 28.7–28.17  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 28] Hic dicitur quod benedicere potest homo tripliciter: scilicet laudative, inprecative et effective. Laudative omnis creatura benedicit Dominum, ut patet dictum in cathalogo sanctorum. Inprecative unus orans pro alio misero, ut Christus Deus noster benedicat; effective... Talem **autem** equivocacionem non vidi in consecrare, cum secundum grammaticos sit dare Deo vel simul sacrare; nam in veteri testamento dederunt Deo bestias et oblaciones que dicebantur Domino postea consecrate; **sed** quomodo consecramus corpus Christi vel sanguinem, cum sacrare sit sacrum facere?

ENGLISH: To this [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that a man can bless in three ways: namely [by way of praise]{g:laudative}, [by way of imprecation]{g:inprecative},[^16] and [by way of effect]{g:effective}. By way of praise every [creature]{g:creatura} blesses the Lord, as is clear from what is said in the catalogue of the saints.[^17] By way of imprecation, one praying for another, a wretched [man], as "May Christ our God bless"; by way of effect …[^18] But such an [equivocation]{g:equivocacio} I have not seen in "consecrate", since according to the grammarians it is to give to God, or to [hallow]{g:sacrare} together;[^19] for in the old testament they gave to God beasts and oblations, which were afterwards said to be consecrated to the Lord; but how do we consecrate the body of Christ or [his] blood, since to hallow is to make sacred?

### 01-e ¶6 · 28.22–29.9  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 28] Quantum ad tercium, dicitur quod sanctificare est iterum equivocum ad facere sanctum et ad sancte colendum, ut Deus sanctificat se in homine, quando facit se sancte coli per eum; quod homo potest facere **sed** non corpus Domini consecrare. Nec movet sentencia beati Jeronimi ut sic equivocetur in consecrare, cum ipse videtur loqui de consecracione in signo que terminatur ad sacramentum et non ad [p. 29] Dominum, nisi dicatur quod sacerdos facit Dominum quia in signo. Equivocaciones **autem** que excludunt contradiccionem oportet loquentes in ista materia diligenter attendere, quia sicut conceditur quod sacerdos conficit corpus Christi, sic concederetur quod ista hostia sit corpus Christi quam loycam propter periculum est bonum suspendere et equivocacionem detegere. Et patet quod hoc sacramentum est forma panis et vini et non Christus vel pars eius.

ENGLISH: As to the third, the answer is that "sanctify" is again equivocal, to making holy and to worshipping in a holy way,[^21] as God sanctifies himself in a man when he makes himself to be worshipped in a holy way by him; which a man can do, but not consecrate the [body of the Lord]{g:corpus-domini}. Nor does the [teaching]{g:sentencia}[^22] of blessed Jerome move [us] to equivocate thus on "consecrate", since he seems to speak of consecration in the [sign]{g:signum}, which is terminated at the sacrament and not at [29] the Lord, unless it be said that the priest makes the Lord because [he makes him] in the sign.[^23] And those who speak in this matter must carefully attend to the equivocations that exclude contradiction, because just as it is granted that the priest confects the body of Christ, so it would be granted that this [host]{g:hostia} is the body of Christ; which logic, because of the danger, it is good to suspend, and to [expose]{g:detegere} the equivocation. And it is clear that this sacrament is the form of bread and wine, and not Christ or a part of him.[^24]

### 01-e ¶7 · 29.10–29.24  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 29] Et quamvis omnes christiani teneantur cognoscere istam sentenciam, specialiter tamen theologi et professores legum ecclesie; leges enim ipsorum quas oportet eos defendere sunt plene ista sentencia, ut specialiter patet IIIº Decretalium, De Celebracione Missarum, Cum Marthe. Leges, inquam, iste vel sunt abrogande tanquam heretice vel defendende et docende tanquam katholice, et eo diligencius quo supra alias leges sunt de pane celesti lucrando necessarie; ideo nota infidelitatis est dicere quod ille sunt tacende et alie leges licium declarande. Dicunt enim quod sacramentum tantum et non pars Christi est hostia consecrata **sed** Christus insensibiliter absconsus est sub illa.

ENGLISH: And although all Christians are bound to know this teaching, yet especially theologians and professors of [the laws of the church]{g:leges-ecclesie}; for their laws, which they must defend, are fully this teaching, as is especially clear in the third [book] of the Decretals, *De celebratione missarum*, *Cum Marthe*.[^25] These laws, I say, are either to be abrogated as heretical or to be defended and taught as catholic, and the more diligently as, above other laws, they are necessary for gaining the heavenly bread; therefore it is a mark of [unbelief]{g:infidelitas} to say that those are to be passed over in silence and the other laws, of lawsuits, to be [made clear]{g:declarare}. For they say that the consecrated host is [a sacrament only]{g:sacramentum-tantum}, and not a part of Christ, but Christ is imperceptibly hidden under it.[^26]

### 02-a ¶1 · 29.25–30.5  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 29] Premissa ista communi sentencia de isto venerabili sacramento restat scrutari quid ipsum sit realiter. In qua materia sunt tres vie: prima videtur esse sentencia sancti Thome cum suis sequacibus quod Eukaristia [p. 30] sit magnitudo de genere quantitatis, quia cum oportet ipsum esse accidens, quantitas **autem** est primum accidens post genus substancie, oportet illud sacramentum sensibile esse quantitatem continuam permanentem qua panis perante formaliter fuit quantus.

ENGLISH: This common [teaching]{g:sentencia}[^1] about this venerable [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} having been set out first, it remains to scrutinize what it is [really]{g:realiter}. In this matter there are three ways: the first seems to be the teaching of St Thomas with his followers, that the eucharist [30] is a magnitude of the genus of [quantity]{g:quantitas}, because, since it must be an [accident]{g:accidens}, and quantity is the first accident after the genus of [substance]{g:substancia}, that [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} sacrament must be the continuous permanent quantity by which the [bread]{g:panis} was before formally [quantified]{g:quantus}.[^2]

### 02-a ¶2 · 30.6–31.20  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 30] Secunda opinio videtur esse Scoti qui inprobat opinionem priorem per hoc quod hoc sacramentum potest rarefieri et condensari, quod non potest competere quantitati; ideo cum sit accidens consonum, videtur ipsum ponere qualitatem, cum oportet quod sit accidens absolutum. Unde aliqui istius secte dicunt quod est figura, aliqui quod est qualitas prima, et alii quod est qualitas secunda, ut color, sapor vel aliud huiusmodi sextuplex vel ex multis accidentibus aggregatum. Tercia opinio est Beringarii, et videtur esse decretum sancte sinodi. Constat quidem ex publica et sollempni confessione Beringarii posita in De Consecracione, distinccione 2ª, Ego Beringarius, quod ipse corrigendo errorem priorem confitetur panem et vinum esse hoc sensibile sacramentum. Nam (ut recitavi superius) ibi inscribitur sua confessio sub hiis verbis: Credo panem et vinum que in altari ponuntur post consecracionem non solum sacramentum **sed** eciam verum corpus et sanguinem domini nostri Jesu Christi esse sensualiter non solum sacramentum, **sed** in veritate manibus sacerdotum tractari et frangi et fidelium dentibus atteri. Ubi patet quod ille sensit quod idem panis et vinum quod ponebatur ante missam super altare remanet post consecracionem [p. 31] tam sacramentum quam corpus dominicum. Quod **autem** ista fuit tunc sentencia Romane ecclesie, patet ex tribus: primo quia in eodem decreto ex verbis notarii sic habetur: Hoc lecto et perlecto sponte subscripsi. Hanc confessionem sue fidei de corpore et sanguine domini nostri Jesu Christi a Beringario Rome coram centum 14ᶜⁱᵐ episcopis factam. Ex quo patet quod ecclesia tante sinodi non permitteret revocacionem prioris heresis et correpcionem suam sine vituperio sub hiis verbis nisi suam fidem tamquam katholicam approbaret. Secundo confirmatur ex eodem decreto quod ista fuit tunc Romane ecclesie sentencia. Nam ibidem sic scribitur: Misit papa Nicholaus per urbes Italie, Germanie Gallie et ad quecunque loca fama pravitatis eius pervenire ante potuit, ut ecclesie que prius doluerant de adverso atque diverso, post ea gauderent de reverso atque converso. Ex istis luculenter patet quod ecclesia tunc sensit sentenciam predictam Beringarii esse katholicam et sentenciam illi contrariam esse hereticam.

ENGLISH: The second [opinion]{g:opinio} seems to be that of Scotus, who disproves the prior opinion by this, that this sacrament can be rarefied and condensed, which cannot belong to quantity;[^3] therefore, since it is an accident in keeping [with this], he seems to posit it to be a [quality]{g:qualitas}, since it must be an [absolute accident]{g:accidens-absolutum}.[^4] Hence some of this sect say that it is figure, some that it is a first quality, and others that it is a second quality, such as colour, taste or something else of this kind, sixfold, or aggregated out of many accidents.[^5] The third opinion is Berengar's, and it seems to be the [decree]{g:decretum} of the holy synod. For it is evident from the public and solemn confession of Berengar, placed in *De consecratione*, distinction 2, *Ego Berengarius*, that he, correcting his earlier error, confesses the bread and wine to be this perceptible sacrament.[^6] For (as I [recited]{g:recitare} above) his confession is there written in these words: "I believe that the bread and wine which are placed on the altar are after the consecration not only a sacrament but also the true body and blood of our Lord Jesus Christ, [sensually]{g:sensualiter} not only a sacrament, but in truth handled and broken by the hands of the priests and ground by the teeth of the faithful."[^7] Where it is clear that he [held]{g:sentire} that the same bread and wine which was placed on the altar before the mass remains after the consecration [31] both a sacrament and the Lord's body.[^8] And that this was then the teaching of the Roman [church]{g:ecclesia} is clear from three [things]: first, because in the same decree, from the notary's words, it is held thus: "This having been read and read through, I have subscribed of my own accord. This confession of his faith concerning the body and blood of our Lord Jesus Christ, made by Berengar at Rome before one hundred and fourteen bishops."[^9] From which it is clear that the church of so great a synod would not have let the revocation of the earlier heresy and his [reproof]{g:correpcio} pass without censure in these words unless it approved his faith as catholic.[^10] Secondly, it is confirmed from the same decree that this was then the teaching of the Roman church. For it is written there thus: "Pope Nicholas sent [it] through the cities of Italy, Germany, Gaul, and to whatever places the fame of his depravity had been able to reach before, so that the churches which before had grieved over him turned against and turned aside might afterwards rejoice over him turned back and converted."[^11] From these it is luminously clear that the church then held the aforesaid teaching of Berengar to be catholic and the teaching contrary to it to be heretical.

### 02-a ¶3 · 31.21–32.4  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 31] Tercio confirmatur idem per hoc quod in prima revocacione sua protestatus fuerat sub hiis verbis: Consencio **autem** sancte Romane ecclesie et apostolice sedi, et ore ac corde profiteor de sacramentis dominice mense eandem fidem me tenere quam dominus et venerabilis papa Nicholaus et hec sancta sinodus auctoritate evangelica et apostolica tenendam tradidit michique confirmavit. Et post confitetur sentenciam supradictam. Ex quibus colligitur quod sentenciam [p. 32] illam receperat a papa et sinodo confitendam; et patet quod olim fuit sensus ecclesie, ut de articulo fidei quod panis et vinum remanent post consecracionem Eukaristie sicut ante.

ENGLISH: Thirdly, the same is confirmed by this, that in his first revocation he had [declared solemnly]{g:protestari} in these words: "And I consent to the holy Roman church and the apostolic see, and with mouth and heart I profess that I hold, concerning the sacraments of the Lord's table, the same faith which the lord and venerable [pope]{g:papa} Nicholas and this holy synod have delivered to be held by evangelical and apostolic [authority]{g:auctoritas} and have confirmed to me."[^12] And afterwards he confesses the teaching mentioned above. From which it is gathered that he had received that teaching [32] from the pope and the synod, to be confessed; and it is clear that it was once the mind of the church, as of an [article of faith]{g:articulus-fidei}, that the bread and wine remain after the consecration of the eucharist as before.

### 02-a ¶5 · 32.10–32.15  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 32] **Sed** constat quoad hoc tercium, quod equa racione verum foret quod panis et vinum remanent post consecracionem in Eukaristia, sicut umquam fuit verum vel credendum; ideo cum equivocacio in materia fidei non excusat, videtur quod altera ecclesia erravit.

ENGLISH: Now it is evident as to this third [way], that by equal reason it would be true that the bread and wine remain after the consecration in the eucharist, as ever it was true or to be believed; therefore, since [equivocation]{g:equivocacio} in a matter of faith does not excuse, it seems that one or the other church erred.

### 02-a ¶7 · 33.1–33.8  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 33] Quod **autem** plus consonat sensui et racioni patet inferius, et quod plus consonat sanctis doctoribus patet ex tribus: primo ex dicto beati Ambrosii, posito in De Consecracione, distinccione IIª Omnia quecunque: Licet figura panis et vini videatur, nichil tamen aliud quam caro Christi et sanguis post consecracionem credenda sunt. Ecce quod panis et vinum credenda sunt esse caro Christi et sanguis.

ENGLISH: And that it is more consonant with sense and reason is clear below; and that it is more consonant with the holy doctors is clear from three [sayings]: first from the saying of blessed Ambrose, placed in *De consecratione*, distinction 2, *Omnia quecunque*: "Although the [figure]{g:figura} of bread and wine is seen, yet nothing other than the flesh and blood of Christ are to be believed [to be there] after the consecration."[^16] See: the bread and wine are to be believed to be the flesh and blood of Christ.

### 02-a ¶8 · 33.9–33.19  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 33] Secundo confirmatur eadem sentencia ex dicto beati Augustini eadem distinccione, Qui manducant, ubi loquens de hoc sacramento sic dicit: Quod videtur, inquit, panis est et calix quod oculi renunciant; quod **autem** fides postulat instruenda, panis est corpus Christi et calix est sanguis. Ista ideo dicuntur sacramenta, quia in eis aliud videtur et aliud intelligitur. Quod videtur speciem habet corporalem, quod intelligitur fructum habet spiritualem. Ecce quod iste sanctus ponit panem et vinum esse sacramentum visibile.

ENGLISH: Secondly, the same teaching is confirmed from the saying of blessed Augustine in the same distinction, *Qui manducant*, where, speaking of this sacrament, he says thus: "What is seen," he says, "is bread and the [chalice]{g:calix}, which the eyes report; but what faith, which is to be instructed, requires: the bread is the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} and the chalice is the blood. These are therefore called sacraments, because in them one thing is seen and another understood. What is seen has a bodily [species]{g:species}; what is understood has spiritual fruit."[^17] See: this saint posits the bread and wine to be the visible sacrament.

### 02-a ¶9 · 33.20–33.26  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 33] Tercio confirmatur ex dicto Hilarii eadem distinccione, ubi sic scribitur: Corpus Christi quod sumitur de altari figura est, dum panis et vinum extra videntur, veritas **autem**, dum corpus Christi et sanguis in veritate interius creduntur. Quotlibet sunt dicta similia dictis istorum trium sanctorum que expresse dicunt sentenciam quam utrobique Beringarius confitetur.

ENGLISH: Thirdly, it is confirmed from the saying of Hilary in the same distinction, where it is written thus: "The body of Christ which is taken from the altar is a figure, while the bread and wine are seen outwardly; but the truth, while the body and blood of Christ are believed inwardly in truth."[^18] There are any number of sayings like the sayings of these three [saints]{g:sancti} which expressly say the teaching that Berengar confesses in both places.[^19]

### 02-b ¶1 · 33.28–34.11  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 33] Quantum ad testimonium scripture patet Math. XXVIº, Marc. XIVº, Luc. XXIIº et Iª Cor. XIº, et Act. IIº [p. 34] ac Joh. VIº, ubi fit mencio de hoc sacramento et datur forma conficiendi; utrobique narratur quod Jesus accepit panem et de illo dixit: Hoc est corpus meum. Nam Luc. XXIIº, 19 sic scribitur: Et accepto pane gracias egit et fregit et dedit eis dicens: Hoc est corpus meum; ubi patet non protervo quod illum panem quem accepit postea demonstravit. Et constat quod illo manente pane non est realiter corpus Christi **sed** signum efficax eius. Ex istis colligitur quod confessio Beringarii et antiquum decretum ecclesie sunt undique plus consona veritati.

ENGLISH: As to the testimony of scripture, it is clear in Matthew 26, Mark 14, Luke 22 and 1 Corinthians 11, and Acts 2[^1] [34] and John 6, where mention is made of this [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} and the form of [consecrating]{g:conficere} is given; in both places it is [related]{g:narrare} that Jesus took [bread]{g:panis} and said of it: "This is my body."[^2] For in Luke 22:19 it is written thus: "And taking bread, he gave thanks and brake and gave to them, saying: This is my body"; where it is clear to one not [obstinate]{g:protervus} that he afterwards demonstrated the bread which he took.[^3] And it is evident that, while that bread remains, it is not [really]{g:realiter} the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} but an efficacious [sign]{g:signum} of it. From these it is gathered that the confession of Berengar and the ancient [decree]{g:decretum} of the [church]{g:ecclesia} are on every side more consonant with the truth.[^4]

### 02-b ¶2 · 34.12–34.17  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 34] **Sed** obicitur per hoc quod secunda pars decreti repugnat priori et katholice veritati et per consequens tota confessio est infecta; ponit enim quod panis et vinum nedum sunt sacramentum post consecracionem, **sed** verum corpus Christi et sanguis quod frangitur et sentitur ut panis, quod est contra dicta.

ENGLISH: But [it is objected, by appeal to]{g:obicitur-per} this, that the second part of the decree [is incompatible with]{g:repugnare} the first and with catholic truth, and consequently the whole confession is infected; for it posits that the bread and wine are [not only]{g:nedum} a sacrament after the consecration, but the true body and blood of Christ, which is broken and [perceived]{g:sentire} as bread, which is against what has been said.[^5]

### 02-b ¶3 · 34.18–35.14  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 34] Hic dico (ut supra) quod oportet omnino equivocare in isto secundo dicto, cum nec substancia panis nec illud quod fuit eius forma accidentalis sit realiter corpus Christi, sicut oportet tam antiquos quam novellos concedere; ideo dicit glosa ordinaria quod oportet confessionem Beringarii sane intelligi, quia aliter foret novissimus error peior priore ab ecclesia dampnato tanquam heretico. Hodie namque approbaretur prior Beringarii opinio secundum tres partes quas ecclesia tunc dampnavit tanquam hereticas, scilicet quod illud album manet post consecracionem solummodo sacramentum, secundo quod illud non est corpus Christi, et tercio quod per sensacionem illius [p. 35] corpus Christi non frangitur vel sentitur. In istis tribus se correxit iuxta decretum ecclesie; et revera nunc defendere quod ecclesia illa tunc erravit in fide tanquam ignara loyce que per sapienciorem ecclesiam est correcta foret inponere toti determinacioni nostre quoad materiam fidei suspectam calumpniam; per idem est dicere quod scriptura cum sanctis doctoribus defecit in istis. **Sed** quis tunc crederet verbis nostris, cum possumus ad nutum fidem pro pecunia variare? Oportet ergo glosare dictum utriusque ecclesie secundum regulam fidei scripture, et hoc est plus consonum et honorabilius utrobique omnino ad intelligendum scripturam, sanctos doctores et confessionem Beringarii, que fuit decretum ecclesie.

ENGLISH: Here I say (as above) that one must altogether equivocate in this second saying, since neither the [substance]{g:substancia} of the bread nor that which was its accidental form is really the body of Christ, as both the ancients and the [newer]{g:novelli} [doctors] must grant; therefore the ordinary [gloss]{g:glosa} says that the confession of Berengar must be understood soundly, because otherwise the last error would be worse than the first, which was condemned by the church as heretical.[^6] For today Berengar's earlier [opinion]{g:opinio} would be approved in the three parts which the church then condemned as heretical: namely, that that white [thing] remains after the consecration only a sacrament; second, that it is not the body of Christ; and third, that by the [sensation]{g:sensacio} of it [35] the body of Christ is not broken or perceived. In these three he corrected himself according to the decree of the church; and in truth, to defend now that that church then erred in faith, as ignorant of logic, and has been corrected by a wiser church, would be to lay a suspect [challenge]{g:calumpnia} against our whole determination in the matter of faith;[^7] by the same token it is to say that scripture with the holy doctors failed in these things. Now who then would believe our words, since we can at a nod vary the faith for money?[^8] We must therefore [gloss]{g:glosare} the saying of each church according to the rule of the faith of scripture; and this is more consonant, and more honourable on both sides, altogether, for understanding scripture, the holy doctors and the confession of Berengar, which was the decree of the church.[^9]

### 02-b ¶4 · 35.15–36.23  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 35] In ista materia oportet (ut supra) signanter notare distinccionem inter predicacionem ydempticam et predicacionem tropicam. Ydemptica est (ut dixi), quando predicatum ydemptice asseritur de subiecto, ut Christus est homo. **Sed** predicacio tropica vel figurativa est, quando unum extremum notatur figurari per reliquum, ut prius exposui: Petra **autem** erat Christus. Unde triplex exemplum adduco de omnibus pro hoc sensu; primum est dictum Apostoli I. Cor. Xº, 1, 4: Nolo **autem** vos ignorare fratres, quoniam patres nostri omnes sub nube fuerunt, et omnes mare transierunt, et omnes eundem potum spiritualem biberunt. Bibebant **autem** de spirituali consequente eos petra: petra **autem** erat Christus. Et patet quoad intellectum istius scripture et sibi similium quod oportet notare sensum figurativum vel tropicum; ad quem sensum est hoc dictum eque verum sicut alia pars scripture; omnes namque patriarche fuerunt sub proteccione [p. 36] divina, secundum quam fuit ardor vindicte Domini effugatus; omnes transierunt mundi et omnium adversancium populorum periculum; et cum fides non variatur propter fluxum temporis, patet quod nutriebantur eadem fide qua christiani postmodum sunt nutriti. Et totum hoc figuratum est per ambulacionem quadraginta annorum ad terram promissionis, sic quod nichil contigit itinerantibus de Egypto quin fuit figura futura succedentibus in peregrinacione sua ad patriam. Sicut enim populus bibebat de aqua fluente de petra quam Moyses bis percussit (ut patet Exodi XVIIº, 6), sic populus christianus bibit spiritualiter subtilitatem fidei Jesu Christi postquam populus iudaicus Christum infamiter sic percussit; et sicut secutum fuit obiurgacionis obprobrium de percussione predicta, ita consequitur iudeos obprobrium, quod Christum sic in patibulo percusserunt. Petra, inquit, erat Christus, hoc est, illa petra Christum mistice figuravit. Notandum tamen quod tales locuciones possunt dupliciter sane intelligi, scilicet quod talis figura figuravit huius figuratum, vel dimittendo litteralem sensum figure intelligere equivoce figuratum, et utrobique erit sensus katholicus absque scrupulo falsitatis.

ENGLISH: In this matter one must (as above) expressly note the distinction between [identical predication]{g:predicacio-ydemptica} and tropical predication. Identical [predication] is (as I said) when the predicate is asserted [identically]{g:ydemptice} of the subject, as "Christ is man". But tropical or [figurative predication]{g:predicacio-figurativa} is when one extreme is marked as being [figured]{g:figurare} by the other, as I explained before: "And the rock was Christ."[^10] Hence I adduce a threefold example, out of all [of them], for this sense; the first is the saying of the Apostle, 1 Cor. 10:1, 4: "And I would not have you ignorant, brethren, that our fathers were all under the cloud: and all passed through the sea, and all drank the same spiritual drink. And they drank of the spiritual rock that followed them: and the rock was Christ."[^11] And it is clear, as to the understanding of this scripture and those like it, that one must note the [figurative]{g:figurative} or tropical sense; in which sense this saying is as true as another part of scripture; for all the patriarchs were under the [36] divine protection, by which the burning of the Lord's vengeance was put to flight; all passed through the danger of the world and of all hostile peoples; and since faith is not varied by the flux of time, it is clear that they were nourished by the same faith by which Christians were afterwards nourished.[^12] And all this is figured by the walking of forty years to the land of promise, so that nothing befell those journeying from Egypt but it was a figure to come for those who followed in their [pilgrimage]{g:peregrinacio} to the [homeland]{g:patria}. For just as the people drank of the water flowing from the rock which Moses struck twice (as is clear in Exodus 17:6), so the Christian people drinks [spiritually]{g:spiritualiter} the subtlety of the faith of Jesus Christ, after the Jewish people so infamously struck Christ; and just as the reproach of chiding followed from the aforesaid striking, so the reproach follows the Jews, that they so struck Christ on the gibbet.[^13] "The rock," he says, "was Christ"; that is, that rock mystically figured Christ. Yet it is to be noted that such locutions can be soundly understood in two ways: namely, that such a [figure]{g:figura} figured the thing figured of it; or, leaving aside the literal sense of the figure, to understand [equivocally]{g:equivoce} the thing figured; and in both ways the sense will be catholic, without scruple of falsehood.[^14]
