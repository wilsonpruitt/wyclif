## Doubtful, left as they are

- **02-e, p. 42**: *Item, Deus potest auferre licenciam … pro delicto, sed quando auferret graciosus dominus si non quando …?*, rendered "but when would a gracious lord". Could be (a) ("now, when would …"): the rhetorical question supplies the second premiss of the *Item* argument. But that argument's conclusion is left implicit, and the following *ideo* draws a different conclusion (Gregory speaks equivocally), so the *ergo* test doesn't settle it. "But" is defensible.
- **02-d, p. 39**: *Sed organa quinque spirituum … figurant secundum* and *sed in regione pectoralium cor … correspondent secundo trinario*, both rendered "But" / "but". These run through a list of correspondences (brain, head organs, neck, breast, belly) and aren't really contrastive; they could be read as next-step markers. Left as they are, as in wave 1 (*Sed quia*, 01-a).
- **02-d, p. 38**: *Sed alie due habent virtutem opitulandi*, rendered "But the other two". Same point: it either turns from the two outer senses to the middle ones (b), or simply carries the list on.
- **02-e, p. 41**: *Sed quantum ad ultimum scilicet perdicionem potencie*, rendered "But as to the last [point]". Either (b) or (a): it moves to the last head, and *Confirmatur secundo* follows (cf. 02-d p. 38, *Sed quoad terciam yerarchiam*, which reads "Now as to"). "But" is defensible.
- **02-e, p. 43**: *Potestas autem, quam Deus dat beato*, rendered "And the power". Either (c) or (b), setting the power of the blessed against the greatest power given to the wayfarer.
- **03-a, pp. 45**: *Sed secundo, ut cognoscat ecclesiam …* and *Sed tercio … limitat prelacie officium*, rendered "But secondly" / "But thirdly". These are steps in expounding Luke 22:31–32, and "Now secondly / thirdly" is possible. "But" is defensible, as at 02-d p. 39.
- **03-c, p. 51**: *Cum enim scriptura sacra exprimit … ecclesia autem generaliter credat hoc de Petro, Deus … permitteret*, rendered "and the church generally believes". Functionally this is the minor premiss inside the *cum* period. The period's construction carries it (both premisses sit under "since", and the conclusion follows), so "and" stands. Compare wave 1, 01-b p. 8.
- **03-c, p. 52**: *Petrus autem surrexit armatus virtutibus*, rendered "Now Peter arose". Could be (c), carrying on the exposition of the vision. "Now" is defensible as the step from what the sheet signifies to what Peter did.
- **03-e, p. 61**: *veritatem autem contemplacionis … minus diligimus*, rendered "and the truth of contemplation … we love less". Either (c) or (b), set against "we love God's mercy more". "More … less" carries the contrast in the English.
