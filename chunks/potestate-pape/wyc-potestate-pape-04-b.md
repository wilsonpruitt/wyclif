# *De potestate pape*, chapter 4, part 2: election by lot, the apostles and Matthias

## ¶1 · 66.19–68.7

> [p. 66] Sed secundo replicatur per hoc quod oportet pro salvando cursu et statu ecclesie incidere in tales errores absconditos, ne ecclesia destituatur regimine. Sed iste est communior lapsus in heresim, qua dogmatizatur peccandum esse ut bonum eveniat. Constat quidem ex fide quod pro toto mundo non consentiret fidelis mendacio electorum, sicut nec electores contra veritatem eligerent unum inhabilem quem Deus prius non elegerit et habilitaverit. Et patet quod sentencia electivarum legum debet supponere cetera paria et conformia in preeleccione divina, quia aliter est dicta maxima summe impossibilis et fidelium seductiva. Nec aliquis fidelis ad tantum infatuatur, ut credat Deum ex pacto electoribus assistere et suam eleccionem qualiscumque fuerit confirmare; [p. 67] tunc enim forent electores inpeccabiles, cum in eligendo facilius errare potuerunt. Nec tunc convinci poterit error eleccionis nec esset eius confirmacio ab homine expectanda. Ideo nichil fideli nocius quam quod in eleccionibus huiusmodi contingit errare crebrius; quod regulariter contingit, quando electores eligunt personam quam Deus non approbat vel habilitat ad tale officium. Et hec racio quare apostoli elegerunt Mathiam per sortem, ut patet Actuum Ubi sunt notanda misteria, primo quomodo beatus Petrus ex illo Psalmo CVIII, 8 episcopatum eius accipiat alter concludit quod oportet ex hiis viris, qui viderunt conversacionem apostolorum cum Christo; unum institui loco Jude et, quia nondum habuerunt revelacionem de persona; constituerunt duos plus habiles scilicet Barsabam et Mathiam, relinquentes divino iudicio quem illorum voluerit per sortem ostendere electum penes eum; cuius arbitrio oportet in eleccionibus dare fidem. Unde triginta electores sequentes dixerunt verba notabilia sepe dicte sentencie expressiva. Tu Domine, inquiunt, qui nosti corda hominum, ostende quem elegeris ex hiis duobus unum accipere locum ministerii huius, ubi patet quod oportet Deum eligere et secundum alios ritus eligentes valde ecquivoce eleccionem Dei fideliter promulgare, ideo dicit, ostende quem elegeris non ad dignitatem sed ministerium. Sed tercio obicitur, cum eleccio nostra debet exemplari et autorizari ab apostolis; a quibus trahimus ritus alios, videtur quod debemus in nostris eleccionibus sequi illos; nam magis autenticam [p. 68] formam eligendi non possumus cogitare; et obiectus eque moverent contra apostolos. Et iterum videtur quod non plus in isto quam in illis que sonant commodum et honorem licet a ritu apostolorum recedere, quia sic pertinenter imponeretur nobis quod deserentes fidem scripture exequimur infideliter ritus gentilium.

But secondly it is rejoined, by appeal to this, that to save the course and state of the [church]{g:ecclesia} one must fall into such hidden errors, lest the church be left destitute of government.[^1] But this is the commoner lapse into the heresy by which it is taught as dogma that "one must sin, that good may come".[^2] It is evident indeed, [as faith teaches]{g:ex-fide}, that a believer would not, for the whole world, consent to the lie of the electors, just as electors would not, against the truth, elect an [unqualified]{g:inhabilis} man whom God has not first elected and [qualified]{g:habilitare}.[^3] And it is clear that the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of the laws on election ought to suppose all else equal and conformed to the divine pre-election, since otherwise the said maxim is supremely impossible and a seducer of the faithful. Nor is any believer so far infatuated as to believe that God by covenant assists electors and confirms their election, whatever it may be; [67] for then electors would be impeccable, although in electing they could the more easily err. Nor then could the error of an election be proved, nor would its confirmation have to be awaited from man. Therefore nothing is more harmful to the believer than that in elections of this kind error happens rather often; which happens regularly when electors elect a person whom God does not approve or qualify for such an office.[^4] And this is the reason why the apostles elected Matthias by lot, as is clear from Acts [1]; where mysteries are to be noted: first, how blessed Peter, from that [verse of] Psalm 108:8, "his bishopric let another take", concludes that one must be instituted in the place of Judas from these men who saw the [manner of life]{g:conversacio} of the apostles with Christ; and, because they did not yet have revelation about the person, they appointed two more [qualified]{g:habilis} [men], namely Barsabas and Matthias, leaving to the divine judgement which of them he would by lot show to be elected in his sight, to whose decision faith must be given in elections.[^5] Hence thirty electors, following, said notable words expressive of the teaching often stated: "Thou, Lord," they say, "who knowest the hearts of men, shew which one of these two thou hast chosen to take the place of this ministry"; where it is clear that God must elect, and that those electing according to other [rites]{g:ritus} must, very equivocally, faithfully promulgate God's election; therefore he says, "shew whom thou hast chosen", not to a dignity but to ministry.[^6] But thirdly it is objected: since our election ought to be modelled on and [authorized]{g:autorizare} by the apostles, from whom we draw other rites, it seems that we ought to follow them in our elections; for we cannot think of a more [authentic]{g:autenticus} [68] form of electing; and the objections would tell equally against the apostles.[^7] And again it seems that it is no more lawful in this than in those things that sound of advantage and honour to depart from the rite of the apostles, for so it would pertinently be charged against us that, deserting the faith of scripture, we faithlessly carry out the rites of the gentiles.

## ¶2 · 68.8–68.34

> [p. 68] Quantum ad istam materiam diffuse tractat eam dominus Ardmacanus libro VII De questionibus Armenorum, recitans quatuor vias constituendi pontifices: una via est per sortem, secunda via per episcopi institucionem, tercia via per hereditariam successionem et quarta via per eleccionem: quod variatur secundum diversitatem variam electorum. Nec movet triplex obiectus istius materie. Primo quod sic temptaretur Deus, si non habentibus ad hec revelacionem liceret sortes mittere, quia ista argumenta procederent contra factum apostolorum et contra sortem veteris testamenti. Nec est Dei temptacio, ubi devotus Dei orator ignorat quid sit Dei voluntati conformius; qualis ignorancia communiter est in electoribus. Secundus obiectus est quod apostoli elegerunt duos scilicet Barsabam et Mathiam, ideo nos debemus operari conformiter. Quantum ad illud non contendo si ordinare multitudinem ut Deus de illa digniorem eligat vocetur eleccio, sed scriptura sic loquitur: Statuerunt duos etc. Sed rogo sic arguentem ut consenciat quod utrobique fiat conformiter nisi subsit racio diversitatis; statuant igitur de humilioribus, quantum adest illis certitudo, et de incerto residuo mittant sortem.

As to this matter, the [lord of Armagh]{g:dominus-ardmacanus} treats it at length in book 7 of *De questionibus Armenorum*, reciting four ways of constituting pontiffs: one way is by lot, the second way by a bishop's institution, the third way by hereditary succession, and the fourth way by election, which varies according to the varied diversity of the electors.[^8] Nor does the threefold objection on this matter move [me]. First, that God would be tempted in this way if it were lawful for those who have no revelation for these things to cast lots; for these arguments would proceed against the deed of the apostles and against the lot of the old testament. Nor is it a tempting of God where a devout [suppliant]{g:orator} of God [is ignorant of]{g:ignorare} what is more conformed to God's will; and such ignorance is commonly in electors.[^9] The second objection is that the apostles chose two, namely Barsabas and Matthias; therefore we ought to act in conformity. As to that, I do not dispute whether ordering a multitude, so that God may choose the worthier from it, be called election; but scripture speaks thus: "They appointed two", etc. But I ask the one who argues thus to agree that it be done in conformity in both cases unless there is a ground for difference; let them therefore appoint from the humbler, so far as they have certainty, and on the uncertain remainder let them cast lots.[^10]

## ¶3 · 69.1–69.11

> [p. 69] Et per hoc patet racio ad tercium obiectum, quo arguitur sorte sepe contingeret ineptum eligi; non nominentur, inquam, primo aliqui nisi apti et tunc indubie sors communius indicabit apciorem quem Deus elegerit quam faciunt elecciones hodie celebrate, et preter hoc tradiciones humane de eleccionibus, occupaciones superflue ad illos regulandum et conspiraciones, contenciones nec non deviaciones a divino arbitrio delete multum exonerarent et prosperarent ecclesiam.

[69] And by this the answer to the third objection is clear, by which it is argued [that] by lot it would often happen that an unfit man is elected: let none, I say, be named first except fit men, and then without doubt the lot will more commonly indicate the fitter man whom God has elected than do the elections held today; and besides this, human traditions about elections, superfluous occupations in regulating them, and conspiracies, contentions, and also deviations from the divine decision, once deleted, would greatly unburden and prosper the church.[^11]

## ¶4 · 69.12–69.29

> [p. 69] Secunda via est probabilis subducto errore instituentis, cum Petrus sic instituit Marcum et Clementem Antiochie et Rome, Paulus autem Thimoteum et Tytum. Ceteri vero apostoli ordinarunt episcopos super populos quos evangelizando converterunt, modo autem superadduntur sicut in ordinibus clericorum, de eleccionibus, de presentacionibus, de institucionibus; de induccionibus et confirmacionibus, que pharisei tantum ponderant ut sacramenta ecclesie; et certum est quod totum est tradicio gentilis superflua nisi forte sit medium ducens ad exequendum divinum arbitrium, sic quod rite exequens opus talis officii sine tali cerimonia introducta, sicut dicit Crisostomus, est tam autentice a Deo electus, sicut aliquis cum quotlibet tradicionibus phariseicis quantumlibet sollempniter celebratis.

The second way is probable, error on the part of the one instituting being excluded, since Peter thus instituted Mark and Clement at Antioch and Rome, and Paul Timothy and Titus.[^12] And the other apostles ordained bishops over the peoples whom they converted by evangelizing; but now [ceremonies] are added, as in the orders of clerics: of elections, of presentations, of institutions, of inductions and confirmations, which the pharisees weigh as much as sacraments of the church; and it is certain that the whole is a superfluous gentile tradition, unless perhaps it is a means leading to the carrying out of the divine decision; so that one who duly carries out the work of such an office without such a ceremony introduced, as Chrysostom says, is as authentically elected by God as anyone with however many pharisaic traditions, however solemnly celebrated.[^13]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 66.19: It is argued that without elections order would fail in the Church.
- 66.26: This is doing evil that good may come.
- 66.32: (;od has not engaged to confirm any election made.
- 67.4: Electors often err.
- 67.10: The apostles elected Matthias by lot.
- 68.8: Four ways of choice: 1. Lot. 2. Bishop's appointment. 3. Hereditary succession; 4. Election as as now practised.
- 68.24: It may be well to select to the best of our judgement and then cast lots, as did the apostles.
- 69.1: This would prevent the objection that the lot might fall on one quite unfit.
- 69.12: Appointment by bishops might be good, but is now ruined by all kinds of traditions.

## Notes

[^1]: **C/R.** *Replicatur per*, "it is rejoined, by appeal to", parallel to *arguitur per* (GLOSSARY §D): the second objection to the teaching of 63.27–65.7 (the first is at 65.28). A in marg.: *2. Eleccionis leges quomodo sunt intelligende. Eleccionis modus in prelatum* (66.19).

[^2]: **S.** Rom. 3:8 (DR "let us do evil that there may come good"), named as a heresy.

[^3]: **T.** *Habilitaverit* is Loserth's correction; the manuscripts read *humilitaverit* (66.28). The same confusion recurs: *habilitat*, MSS *humilitat* (67.8); *habiles*, MSS *humiles* (67.17).

[^4]: **T/C.** B reads *irregulariter* (67.6–7). The harm is that error is frequent where no one may question the election once made.

[^5]: **T/S.** *Actuum* stands without a chapter number; we supply "[1]" (Acts 1:15–26). Ps. 108:8 (DR), quoted by Peter at Acts 1:20; the eyewitnesses, Acts 1:21–22; the two appointed, Acts 1:23. *Petrus* is Loserth's expansion of the manuscripts' *P.* (67.12). *Penes eum*, "in his sight": with God, by his judgement.

[^6]: **T/S/C.** *Triginta*, "thirty", is the print, confirmed on the plate (67.21); Acts 1:15 gives about a hundred and twenty, and the number is unexplained. Acts 1:24–25: Wyclif reads *corda hominum* (Loserth: "rightly *corda omnium*", 67.23–24; DR "the heart of all men") and stops before *et apostolatus* (DR "To take the place of this ministry and apostleship"). *Valde ecquivoce*: what men do is called "election" only equivocally, since God is the one who elects.

[^7]: **C.** The third objection argues from the apostles' example for the lot; *obiectus eque moverent contra apostolos*: the objections to the lot would tell equally against the apostles' own practice.

[^8]: **S/R.** Richard FitzRalph, *Summa in quaestionibus Armenorum*, book 7; Loserth gives no reference (LEDGER P04b-1, to be checked). A in marg.: *Eleccio pontificis quatuor modis*; B in marg.: *Quatuor vie eligendi pontifices* (68.8).

[^9]: **C/G/R.** *Quia ista argumenta procederent*: Wyclif's answer to the first objection follows at once, without a formula. *Orator* is here one who prays to God over the lot ("suppliant", GLOSSARY, CONTEXTUAL), not a *bedesman* praying for a benefactor. A in marg.: *1* (68.16).

[^10]: **T/S/R.** Acts 1:23 (DR "And they appointed two"). A in marg.: *2* (68.24). A reads *nos debemus nos* (68.25–26). *De humilioribus*, "from the humbler", is the print; given the *habil-*/*humil-* confusion at 66.28, 67.8 and 67.17, *habilioribus*, "the more qualified", may be meant.

[^11]: **C/R.** A in marg.: *3* (69.1). *Delete … exonerarent*: the participle carries the condition, "once deleted, would unburden". *Ad illos regulandum* refers to the elections (*illos* as printed).

[^12]: **C/R.** A in marg.: *Secunda via* (69.12). The pairs run in order, Mark at Antioch and Clement at Rome, as printed; the common tradition puts Mark at Alexandria (LEDGER P04b-2, to be checked).

[^13]: **T/S.** Loserth notes that a word is missing after *superadduntur* ("deest verbum", 69.17); we supply "[ceremonies]" from *tali cerimonia* below. *Sicut dicit Crisostomus*: the passage from *De laudibus sancti Pauli* at 65.17–20, where the unordained man who fulfils the care of many is most worthy of honour.
