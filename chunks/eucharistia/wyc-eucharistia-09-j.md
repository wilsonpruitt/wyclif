# *De eucharistia*, chapter 9, part 10: the motion and eating of the body of Christ, and the three senses of bread

## ¶1 · 307.24–309.16

> [p. 307] Quantum ad mocionem corporis Christi videtur homines in ista materia loqui dupliciter vel tropice ad sensum quo concedunt illa que contingunt sacramentum corporis Christi contingere (ut loquitur decretum Ego Beringarius) vel sine figura ydemptice, modo quo dicitur Deum esse ubique et Petrum solum esse ubi est diffinitive; et sic videtur corpus Christi post ascensionem nedum non moveri in terris nec esse, sed virtus sua est per totum mundum, cum perficit [p. 308] humanitus quamlibet creaturam. Sed propter innovacionem vel incepcionem virtutis non oportet corpus Christi correspondenter moveri, sicut non oportet regem moveri correspondenter ad motum baiuli suas litteras deferentis. Quamvis enim corpus Christi sit ubique, eciam per totum mundum sublunarem, tamen est quadam singularitate in sacramento altaris; sed sicut non alteratur vel movetur in quantitate ad motum hostie, ita non movetur localiter correspondenter ut ipsa movetur localiter, quia non acquirit sibi ex motu hostie quantitatem aut qualitatem, sicut nec ex delacione hostie sibi acquirit novum, quod sicut corpus Christi non conburitur conbusta pixide, sic nec elevatur nec submittitur presbitero hostiam sic movente. Et patet quod corpus Christi non evomitur vel screatur. Et quoad manducacionem patet ex dictis dupliciter potest intelligi, vel tropice vel ydemptice; ydemptice non manducatur corporaliter sed spiritualiter a fideli, dum mens sua pascitur ex memoria corporis Christi; et sic non potest manducari a bestia nec manducatur sic a simpliciter fideli, sed indignus manducat sacramentum et accipit corpus Christi. Unde aliud est capere, aliud manducare; nam multi accipiunt Spiritum Sanctum qui non est mobilis nec corporaliter manducabilis; aer eciam secundum sic loquentes accipit corpus Christi, sicut os hominis; intestina eciam bestiarum in tantum quod nedum corpus Christi est ad omnem punctum mundi sed sacramentaliter est in interioribus bestiarum, cum posset ab illis forma panis extrahi; et quamdiu remanet forma panis aut vini consecrata, dicunt adversarii corpus Christi eciam quantumcunque modica [p. 309] particula sacramentaliter remanere. Et isto modo videtur Thomam dicere super distinccione IX, questione IIᵃ, quod creatura irracionalis sumit corpus Christi. Wilelmus autem in De Sacramento Altaris videtur dicere quod quinque modis accipitur corpus Christi et sic mus (ut inquit) comedit corpus Christi realiter sed non sacramentaliter neque spiritualiter. Sed absit, licet omnes isti (eciam papa) dixerint, quod bestia et omne manducans hostiam manducat propterea corpus Christi, sicut exemplificatum est de anima, ymmo loyci dicunt quod, sicut fons, lacus vel bestia accipit aquam que de facto est sacramentum baptismi, et tamen non baptizatur vel accipit baptismum, sic multa accipiunt illum panem vel illa accidencia, que de facto sunt sacramentum, et tamen non accipiunt sacramentum quia non sub racione sacramenti.

As to the [motion]{g:mocio} of the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}, it seems that men speak in this matter in two ways: either [tropically]{g:tropice}, in the sense in which they grant that the things which befall the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} of the body of Christ befall [the body] (as the [decree]{g:decretum} *Ego Berengarius* speaks),[^1] or without figure, [identically]{g:ydemptice}, in the way in which God is said to be everywhere and Peter to be only where he is [definitively]{g:diffinitive}. And so it seems that the body of Christ after the ascension [not only]{g:nedum} is not moved on earth, nor is [there], but its power is through the whole world, since it perfects [308] [humanly]{g:humanitus} every [creature]{g:creatura}. But because of a renewal or [beginning]{g:incepcio} of the power the body of Christ need not be moved correspondingly, just as a king need not be moved correspondingly to the motion of the porter who carries his letters. For although the body of Christ is everywhere, even through the whole sublunar world, yet it is with a certain singularity in the [sacrament of the altar]{g:sacramentum-altaris}; but just as it is not altered or moved in [quantity]{g:quantitas} at the motion of the [host]{g:hostia}, so it is not moved [locally]{g:localiter} correspondingly as the host itself is moved locally, because it does not acquire for itself from the motion of the host quantity or [quality]{g:qualitas}, just as it acquires nothing new for itself from the carrying of the host either, in that, just as the body of Christ is not burned when the pyx is burned, so neither is it raised or lowered when the [presbyter]{g:presbiter} so moves the host. And it is clear that the body of Christ is not vomited or spat out. And as to the [eating]{g:manducacio}, it is clear from what has been said [that] it can be understood in two ways, either tropically or identically.[^2] Identically, it is not eaten [bodily]{g:corporaliter} but [spiritually]{g:spiritualiter} by the faithful man, while his mind is fed from the memory of the body of Christ; and so it cannot be eaten by a beast, nor is it so eaten by one who is faithful [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}, but the unworthy man eats the sacrament and receives the body of Christ.[^3] Hence it is one thing to take, another to eat;[^4] for many receive the Holy Spirit, who is not [movable]{g:mobilis} nor bodily eatable. The air too, according to those who speak so, receives the body of Christ, as does a man's mouth; the intestines of beasts too, so far that not only is the body of Christ at every point of the world, but it is [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} in the inward parts of beasts, since the form of [bread]{g:panis} could be drawn out of them; and as long as the consecrated form of bread or [wine]{g:vinum} remains, the adversaries say that the body of Christ remains sacramentally even in however tiny a [309] particle. And in this way Thomas seems to say, on distinction 9, question 2, that "an irrational creature receives the body of Christ."[^5] And William, in *On the Sacrament of the Altar*, seems to say that the body of Christ is received in five ways, and so "a mouse" (as he says) "eats the body of Christ [really]{g:realiter} but not sacramentally nor spiritually."[^6] But far be it, although all these (even the [pope]{g:papa}) have said it, that a beast and everything that eats the host therefore eats the body of Christ, as was illustrated of the soul;[^7] indeed the logicians say that, just as a fountain, a lake or a beast receives water which is in fact the sacrament of baptism, and yet is not baptized or receives baptism, so many things receive that bread or those [accidents]{g:accidens} which are in fact the sacrament, and yet do not receive the sacrament, because [they do] not [receive it] under the aspect of the sacrament.

## ¶2 · 309.17–311.15

> [p. 309] Sed dimissa ista altercacione loycis concedo cum glossa communi super distinccione secunda, capitulo Non iste, quod corpus Christi non descendit in stomachum, et usque ad quem locum ducitur per gulam nescio. Unde textus vere dicit: Non iste panis est qui vadit in corpus. Tripliciter enim accipitur panis ad propositum in scriptura sacra et sanctis doctoribus, scilicet pro pane usuali quem vulgus comedit, pro pane consecrato qui est sacramentum; quem Augustinus vocat panem celestem, et pro pane ultimo figurato qui est Christus Deus noster. Et istam equivocacionem sicut equivocam istorum predicacionem oportet theologum notare. Si enim panis qui est corpus Christi [p. 310] gloriosum in celo foret in sua persona nobiscum, ut sentimus de hostia, nullus vivens hanc vitam sufficeret suffere gloriam et extasim quam inferret; nam Petrus in transfiguracione cadebat ut mortuus. Sed in pane et vino figuratur nobis corpus Christi gloriosum, ut allegavi superius Augustinum et Rabanum et Damascenum. Scribit enim iste Grecus IIIᵒ Sentenciarum suarum, capitulo VIIIᵛᵒ: Quemadmodum consuetudo est homini aqua lavari et oleo ungi quo coniuncta gracia Spiritus Sancti facit lavacrum regeneracionis, sic quia consuetudo est hominibus panem comedere, vinum et aquam bibere, coniugavit Deus ipsis sui ipsius deitatem et fecit ipsa corpus et sanguinem sui ipsius. Nec repugnat sed consonat quod dicitur eodem capitulo quod panis et vinum transit et transsubstanciatur in corpus Christi et sanguinem. Per hoc enim quod dicit Deum fecisse ea corpus suum et sanguinem, patet quod intelligit ipsa esse tropice corpus Christi et sanguinem ad modum loquendi scripture; nam ut dicit Scotus: Eodem spiritu exponende sunt scripture quo condite erant; et sic ecclesia katholica ad illum sensum exposuit ad quem docuit. [p. 311] Sed absit fidelem ex illo concludere quod si Innocencius IIIᵘˢ cum suis satrapis sic diffinit, tunc ecclesia katholica ita sensit; verumptamen (ut dixi) presumpcio est quod katholice sensierunt de transsubstancione et accidentibus modo quo supra exposui, et non contrariabantur sanctis qui dicunt quantitatem et qualitatem non posse per se esse; sed generaliter modo scripture vocant ea panem et vinum, et sic intelligunt ipsi bene per sua accidencia, quia XXV, questione prima, Sunt quidam, dicit Urbanus papa katholice: ubi vero aperte Dominus vel eius apostoli et eos sequentes sancti patres sentencialiter aliquit diffinierunt, ibi non novam legem Romanus dare potest sed pocius quod predicatum est usque ad animam et sanguinem confirmare, quia aliter nimium oberraret.

But, leaving this wrangling to the logicians, I grant with the common [gloss]{g:glossa} on distinction 2, chapter *Non iste*, that "the body of Christ does not descend into the stomach," and "up to what place it is led through the gullet I do not know."[^8] Hence the text says [truly]{g:vere}: "This is not the bread which goes into the body." For bread is taken in three ways for the purpose in holy scripture and the holy doctors: namely for the common bread which the people eat; for the consecrated bread which is the sacrament, which Augustine calls "heavenly bread";[^9] and for the bread [figured]{g:figuratum} last of all, which is Christ our God. And this equivocation, like the equivocal predication of these, the theologian must note. For if the bread which is the body of Christ, [310] glorious in heaven, were with us in its own person, as we [perceive]{g:sentire} of the host, no one living this life would be able to bear the glory and ecstasy it would bring; for Peter at the transfiguration fell as dead.[^10] But in the bread and wine the glorious body of Christ is figured to us, as I [alleged]{g:allegare} Augustine and Rabanus and Damascene above. For this Greek writes in [book] 3 of his *Sentences*, chapter 8: "As it is the custom for man to be washed with water and anointed with oil, with which the grace of the Holy Spirit, joined, makes the laver of regeneration, so, because it is the custom for men to eat bread, to drink wine and water, God conjoined to them the [Godhead]{g:deitas} of himself and made them the body and blood of himself."[^11] Nor is it [incompatible]{g:repugnare}, but it accords, that it is said in the same chapter that "the bread and wine pass over and are transubstantiated into the body and blood of Christ." For by his saying that God made them his body and blood, it is clear that he understands them to be tropically the body and blood of Christ, after scripture's manner of speaking; for, as Scotus says, "the scriptures are to be expounded in the same spirit in which they were composed";[^12] and so the catholic [church]{g:ecclesia} expounded [them] to that [meaning]{g:sensus} to which it taught [them]. [311] But far be it from a faithful man to [infer]{g:concludere} from that, that if Innocent III with his [satraps]{g:satrapa} so defines, then the catholic church so [held]{g:sentire}; nevertheless (as I said) the presumption is that they held in catholic fashion about [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio} and the accidents in the way I expounded above, and did not contradict the [saints]{g:sancti}, who say that quantity and quality cannot be [of themselves]{g:per-se}; but generally, after the manner of scripture, they call them bread and wine, and so they themselves understand [them] well by "its accidents".[^13] For in [Causa] 25, question 1, *Sunt quidam*, Pope Urban says in catholic fashion: "But where the Lord or his apostles, and the holy fathers following them, have openly defined something by way of teaching, there the Roman [pontiff] cannot give a new law, but rather confirm, even unto soul and blood, what has been preached; because otherwise he would go [exceedingly]{g:nimis} astray."[^14]

## ¶3 · 311.16–311.30

> [p. 311] Cum ergo auctores scripture et sancti doctores concorditer vocant sacramentum panem quem dicunt esse corpus, grave esset illa verba vel sensum illum impetere; scio tamen quod pontifices eciam Romani possunt seduci ad tantum et amplius, cum possunt errare in articulo plus necessario ad viacionem ecclesie, scilicet quod expedicius et perfeccius est eis dotari cesarie quam sequi statum exproprietarium quem Christus verbo et facto docuit et servandum suis discipulis dereliquit. Iste enim error est maior quam foret credere non esse eukaristie sacramentum; et cum error unus ducit in reliquum, creditur quod in penam erroris prioris ecclesia cadebat in reliquum exponens scripturam sacram et sanctos doctores yronice per suum contradictorium.

Since, therefore, the authors of scripture and the holy doctors with one accord call the sacrament bread, [the bread] which they say is the body,[^15] it would be a grave thing to attack those words or that meaning; yet I know that even [Roman pontiffs]{g:romanus-pontifex} can be led astray so far and further, since they can err in an article more necessary to the [wayfaring]{g:viacio} of the church: namely, that it is more expedient and more perfect for them to be endowed [in caesarean fashion]{g:cesaree} than to follow the [propertyless]{g:exproprietarii} state which Christ taught by word and deed and left to his disciples to be kept.[^16] For this error is greater than it would be to believe that there is no sacrament of [the Eucharist]{g:eukaristia}; and since one error leads into the other, it is believed that in punishment of the prior error the church fell into the other, expounding holy scripture and the holy doctors [ironically]{g:yronice} by their contradictory.

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 308.12: Christ's body is not subject to be burned, eaten or vomited.
- 308.26: According to our opponents a beast's belly receives Christ.
- 309.9: To take the elements is not to take the sacrament, unless they are taken as the sacrament.
- 311.16: We know the Roman pontiffs can err, as in the matter of endowment.

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** *De cons.* D. 2 c. 42, *Ego Berengarius* (the form of reference is ours), whose oath says the body is handled and broken by the priests' hands and ground by the teeth of the faithful; Wyclif takes its predicates as said of the sacrament tropically. (LEDGER K09j-1)
[^2]: **T.** The print *patet ex dictis* is Loserth's correction (the MSS *et dictis*), and he notes "adde: *quod*" before *dupliciter*: the bracketed "that" follows him.
[^3]: **C.** *Nec manducatur sic a simpliciter fideli* is translated as printed. The sense expected by the contrast that follows (the unworthy eat the sacrament) is "by one who is *not* faithful without qualification"; a word may be lost. D reads *a simplici* ("by a simple man"); ABC *nec sic*.
[^4]: **C.** *Capere* "take" and *accipere* "receive" are kept apart from *manducare* "eat": Wyclif's point is that receiving (as the air or a beast's gut receives) is not eating.
[^5]: **S.** Thomas, *In IV Sent.* d. 9; Loserth corrects the reference to d. 9 q. 1 a. 2 (*recte*), against the print's "question 2". Parma d. 9 q. 1 a. 2 qc. 3, where the true body remains under the species *a brutis ore acceptis, et in ventrem trajectis*, and brutes receive it *per accidens* (ad 1) (LEDGER K09j-2).
[^6]: **S.** Loserth identifies this William as William of Saint-Amour; but William of Saint-Amour's *Opera omnia* (1632) has no *De sacramento altaris*, and the mouse and the five ways are not found there or in the works of that title by William of Champeaux, William of Saint-Thierry or Ockham; the William is unidentified. (LEDGER K09j-3)
[^7]: **C.** *Sicut exemplificatum est de anima*: no illustration from the soul stands in this chunk (the Holy Spirit and the air do). Translated as printed; *anima* may stand for another word.
[^8]: **S.** Gratian, *De cons.* D. 2 c. 56 *Non iste panis* (Loserth; the form is ours), and the ordinary gloss at those words, which Loserth cites for the two quoted clauses. (LEDGER K09j-4)
[^9]: **S.** Augustine's "heavenly bread": Loserth gives several places in the Maurist *Opera* (III.2 494; V 326, 334, 339, 344, *et passim*), none named. One such place is *In Ioh. tr.* 26.11 (PL 35, 1611): *panem coelestem spiritualiter manducate, innocentiam ad altare apportate*. (LEDGER K09j-5)
[^10]: **S.** Cf. Matt. 17:6, "the disciples hearing fell upon their face, and were very much afraid": the gospel does not say "as dead" (the phrase of Apoc. 1:17); Wyclif's wording is given as his.
[^11]: **S.** Not the *Sentences* but John Damascene, *De fide orthodoxa* IV.13 (Loserth, who prints the Greek Father's Latin and refers to James of Mies, *De vera existencia corporis Christi*, in von der Hardt II, 906). The second quotation is from the same chapter. The chapter is confirmed (LEDGER Kprolc-5); the James of Mies reference is Loserth's. (LEDGER K09j-6)
[^12]: **S.** Scotus, *Reportata Parisiensia* III d. 24 schol. 4 (Loserth, "*et alibi*"). Loserth's locus is not checked. (LEDGER K09j-7)
[^13]: **C.** *Sic intelligunt ipsi bene per sua accidencia*: likelier, that in saying "its accidents" (the bread's) they mean well, since they call the accidents bread; alternatively "they understand [the bread] well through its accidents". The print spells *transsubstancione* (for *transsubstanciacione*).
[^14]: **S/T.** Gratian, C. 25 q. 1 c. 6 *Sunt quidam* (Loserth; the form is ours), under Pope Urban's name. PL 187, 1321A: *ibi non novam legem Romanus Pontifex dare, sed potius quod praedicatum est usque ad animam et sanguinem confirmare debet*. (LEDGER K09j-8) The bracketed "pontiff" follows Loserth's "adde: *pontifex*" (as in the decretal); *sentencialiter* "by way of teaching" is the adverb of *sentencia*.
[^15]: **C.** The relative *quem* can take *panem* ("call the sacrament bread, the bread they say is the body") or *sacramentum* ("call bread the sacrament which they say is the body"); the first is taken.
[^16]: **T.** *Scilicet quod* is the print; the MSS read *sed quod* (Loserth). D reads *cesariem*, ABC *cesarie* as printed.
