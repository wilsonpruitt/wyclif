# *De potestate pape*, chapter 11, part 9: custom, human tradition and the law of God; no secular gradation in the clergy

## ¶1 · 294.3–299.5

> [p. 294] Conceditur tamen, quod ex illo, sicut ex quolibet malo quedam propria utilitas succedit ecclesie. Sed (ut utar verbis Apostoli) absit ut illa mala sint propterea facienda. Ulterius conceditur quod observanda est laudabilis consuetudo ecclesie, licet non sit explicita sed implicita in scriptura, quia minus diffusa et onerosa foret lex Christi, si omnes tales ritus, usus vel consuetudines forent in ea expresse. Ideo credendum est quod Spiritus Sanctus necessariora ecclesie expressit, et leviora sub quodam confuso involucro simul texit, ut patet prima Cor. XIV 40; dicit spiritus per suum Apostolum: Omnia honeste et secundum ordinem fiant. In quo includuntur omnes usus ecclesie in dicendo servicium, omnes ritus ordinum privatorum, et breviter omnis consuetudo clericalis vel laycalis laudabilis. Et in isto puncto surrepit maior irreligiositas populi christiani, quod postponendo preponderanciora expressa in lege Domini, preponderantur tradiciones et adinvenciones humane, quedam heretice et quedam in suo ordine observande. Sicut enim scriptura docet nos ista preponderanter cognoscere, sic minimum expressum in lege Domini observare sub incursu maioris pene quam aliquid quod est expressum singulariter in usu sacrum vel ritus aliquos eciam ordinate in religiosis vel laicis preter fidem Christi expressam noviter introductos. Et in isto cecatur ac oneratur ecclesia ultra modum, quia vel introducuntur irracionalia vel racionalia sunt nimium ponderata. Unde decretum XII distinccione sub [p. 295] auctoritate Augustini ad inquisiciones Januarii sic loquitur (ut sepe repecii): Omnia talia que neque scripturarum sanctarum auctoritatibus continentur nec in consiliis episcoporum statuta inveniuntur nec consuetudine universalis ecclesie roborata sunt, sed diversorum locorum diversis moribus innumerabiliter variantur, ita ut vix aut nunquam inveniri possunt cause, quas in eis constituendis homines secuti sunt, ubi facultas tribuitur sine aliqua dubitacione resecanda estimo. Quamvis enim hoc non inveniri possit, quomodo contra fidem sint, ipsam tamen religionem quam paucissimis et manifestissimis celebracionum sacramentis misericordia Domini voluit esse liberam, servilibus oneribus adeo premunt, ut tollerabilior sit condicio Iudeorum, qui etsi tempus libertatis non cogno verunt, legalibus tamen sacramentis, non humanis presumcionibus subiciuntur. Ex istis videtur, primo quod infidelius nostri pharisei premunt ecclesiam tradicionibus, sectis et exaccionibus quam fecerant pharisei legis veteris. Ipsi enim undique parcius onerarunt; moralia legis preponderarunt et veniente figurato viderunt quod figure cessarunt. Nostri autem pontifices, scribe et pharisei magnificant infideles tradiciones hominum, eciam ultra tradiciones quas Deus instituit, cum illas dicunt oportere cessare, si onus sapiunt et non lucrum; tradiciones vero suas, que honorem vel lucrum temporalem sapiunt, dicunt non posse cessare, licet sint tradicionibus et vite Christi contrarie; cum per illas consecrantur res, a qua consecracione non licet recedere; deminuere autem libertatem, que est verum privilegium ecclesie, per [p. 296] falsos dico non clericos sed idiotas, vocem propriam ignorantes, foret nota infidelitatis discipulo Antichristi. Quando enim queritur ab eis racio facti, vel balbuciunt, dicentes machometice quod non debet dari vel disputari racio sue tradicionis, cum per se sufficit voluntas capitis, vel arguunt ex insufficienti similitudine de lege veteri, vel allegant infundabiliter leges hominis, ut quod iurisconsultus vel satrapa sic diffinit, nec licet (ut inquiunt) racionem querere ut in lege Dei sed ad ipsos ut primum principium est sistendum. Seculares autem provident quod leges eorum consonent legi Dei, quia utilitati et racioni, cum profitentur catholice quod omnis lex vel ritus hominis que non habet ab ipsa principium est heretica et fugibilis ut venenum. Ex quo patet quod lex Dei debet primo disci a quocumque catholico. Quid inquam fideli de lege alia? Unde lex Dei cum tradicionibus humanis sophistice est commixta; scilicet ut tradicio pallietur et coloracius observetur. Observet igitur fidelis iusticiam legis et paret se ad sufferendum iniurias et penas pro omissione legum hominum. Hoc enim directe accelerat viatorem ad terminum et oppositum deviat vel retardat. Conceditur igitur quod ritus et leges honeste ex lege Domini eliquate sunt in suo ordine observande. Sed caveat christianus de nimietate studii eorum et observancie; suspensus itaque in lege Dei sine ulteriori sollicitudine sit contentus de flatu spiritus sine istis extravagantibus dirigentis. Quid queso fideli de gentili ritu vel phariseico nisi de quanto modeste facilitat ad observanciam legis Dei? Unde graviter peccarunt, qui introduxerunt [p. 297] sectas et distincciones legum ecclesie, per quas diminuitur meditacio legis Dei, cum melius esset quod pure observaretur vita apostolica, et dimissa difformitate tenderetur ad uniformitatem status innocencie, licet graduacio in seculari dominio sit licita ac necessaria peccato supposito. Unde nichil infidelius quam ex graduacione secularis dominii concludere graduacionem dominacionis et honoris secularis in clericis. Cum enim status ille sit alcior, statui Christi et sanctorum conformior, oportet quod tendat ad maiorem unitatem, regulans statum laycalem et non sit a statu tam difformi quem regulat exemplatus. Conceditur tamen quod sunt gradus in illo statu apostolico; sed contrarie ad statum secularem; nam hic maior est pauperior, servitivior et ad currendum per peregrinacionem cum paucioribus temporalibus sufficiencior. Unde omnes presbiteri debent esse socii, ut docet Apostolus cum decreto de ipso et Petro, verumtamen servus ex maiori humilitate fit alcior. Unde (ut sepe dixi) pars laycalis dividitur in popularem et partem secularem illi prepositam quarum prior est bona et secunda melior. Sed pars clericalis propter socialem uniformitatem remanet indivisa et optima, quam elegit Maria; unde periculose balbuciunt, qui dicunt quod reges non sunt domini regnorum, sed nudi defensores, vel aliter (ut inquiunt) oportet dare in clericis gradus talis dominii. Scrutentur isti scripturas et videre possunt, quam spissim autor scripture tam verbo quam opere concedit illis de medio statu dominium, cum ipse obedienter dedit tributum cesari, a se ipso autem ac suis apostolis et eorum successoribus [p. 298] exclusit dominium, ut patet superius. Iudicent igitur inter evidencias; Deus interdicit sacerdoti Christi dominium et dat seculari dominium, igitur sic debet esse. Et cesar vel lex papalis negat seculari adesse dominium et dicit quod pape ac episcopis debet adesse quodcumque seculare dominium, igitur verum. Et sic de istis locis a simili: Christus exclusit se et suos apostolos a seculari dominio, igitur per idem hoc debet a suis vicariis observari, et seculares domini ac prelati vendicant sibi dominacionem civilem, primi legittime et secundi falsa usurpacione, igitur per idem omnes clerici debent taliter dominari. In tales argucias arundineas prorumpunt qui siciunt seculare dominium; et necesse est ut multiplicetur in clero Christi, sicut in parte multiplicabatur in clero legis veteris, talis confusio, quousque Antichristus destruxerat membra sua. Ideo cum secundum variacionem loci et temporis agendum est; ut congruit, patet quod regnante cupiditatis malicia sequenda est nobis via Christi sollercius, nam imminente maiori periculo debet periclitans magis inniti suffragio. Sic enim fecit sacerdos Christus veniens in summo gradu expropriacionis, quando summe regnavit cupiditas sacerdotum. Et hec est causa apostoli, ut prius exposui. Redimentes, inquit, tempus, quoniam dies mali sunt, unde nimis blasfemant degeneres qui dicunt quod tam utile et tam conveniens est statui cleri modo ditari amplius quam necessaria fuit potestas faciendi miracula in ecclesia primitiva. Revera tunc nec clerus possessionatus nec laycus daret corporalem elemosinam mendicanti, sicut apostoli non diminuerent de potestate faciendi [p. 299] miracula, ymo potestas, quam Evangelista habuit ad mutandum littoria saxorum fragmenta in gemmas et silvestres frondes in aurum, foret mundo utilior quam quantacumque temporalis possessio, sicut testaretur eciam Symon magus.

Yet it is granted that from that, as from any evil, a certain proper benefit accrues to the [church]{g:ecclesia}. But (to use the Apostle's words) God forbid that those evils should therefore be done.[^1] Further, it is granted that the praiseworthy [custom]{g:consuetudo} of the church is to be observed, although it is not explicit but implicit in scripture; for the law of Christ would be less diffuse and burdensome if all such rites, usages or customs were expressly in it.[^2] Therefore it is to be believed that the Holy Spirit expressed the things more necessary to the church, and covered the lighter things together under a certain [indistinct]{g:confusus} wrapping, as is clear in 1 Cor. 14:40; the Spirit says through his Apostle: "Let all things be done decently and according to order." In this are included all the usages of the church in saying service, all the rites of the private orders, and, in brief, every praiseworthy custom, clerical or lay. And at this point the greater [irreligion]{g:irreligiositas} of the Christian people creeps in: that, setting aside the more preponderant things expressed in the law of the Lord, human traditions and inventions are made to preponderate, some of them heretical and some to be observed in their order. For as scripture teaches us to know these things preponderantly, so [it teaches us] to observe the least thing expressed in the law of the Lord under incurring a greater penalty than anything that is expressed singly in a sacred usage, or any rites, even introduced in orderly fashion among the [religious]{g:religiosi} or lay people, newly, beyond the expressed faith of Christ.[^3] And in this the church is blinded and burdened beyond measure, because either irrational things are introduced or rational things are weighted too much. Hence the Decretum, in distinction 12, under the [295] authority of Augustine, *To the Inquiries of Januarius*, speaks thus (as I have often repeated): "All such things as are neither contained in the authorities of the holy scriptures, nor found established in the councils of bishops, nor strengthened by the custom of the universal church, but vary innumerably according to the diverse manners of diverse places, so that the causes which men followed in establishing them can scarcely or never be found — where the opportunity is given, I judge that they are to be cut off without any hesitation. For although it cannot be found how they are against the faith, yet they so weigh down with servile burdens religion itself, which the mercy of the Lord willed to be free, with very few and most manifest sacraments of celebration, that the condition of the Jews is more tolerable: for they, even though they did not know the time of liberty, are yet subject to the sacraments of the law, not to human presumptions."[^4] From these things it seems, first, that our pharisees weigh down the church with traditions, [sects]{g:secta} and exactions more faithlessly than the pharisees of the old law had done. For they burdened more sparingly on every side; they gave preponderance to the moral precepts of the law, and when the thing figured came they saw that the figures ceased. But our pontiffs, scribes and pharisees magnify faithless traditions of men even beyond the traditions which God instituted, since they say that these must cease if they savour of burden and not of gain; but their own traditions, which savour of honour or temporal gain, they say cannot cease, although they are contrary to the traditions and to the life of Christ, since by them things are consecrated, and from that consecration it is not lawful to withdraw; and yet to diminish the liberty which is the true [privilege]{g:privilegium} of the church, through false men — I say, not clerics but [unlearned men]{g:idiota}, [ignorant of]{g:ignorare} their own word — [296] would be a mark of [unbelief]{g:infidelitas} in a disciple of Antichrist.[^5] For when the reason for the deed is asked of them, either they stammer, saying in Mahomet's manner that the reason for their tradition ought not to be given or disputed, since the will of the [head]{g:caput} suffices by itself;[^6] or they argue from an insufficient likeness to the old law; or they [allege]{g:allegare} [without foundation]{g:infundabilis} the laws of man, as that a jurisconsult or a [satrap]{g:satrapa} so defines; and (as they say) it is not lawful to seek a reason, as in God's law, but one must stop at them as at a first principle. But [the seculars]{g:secularis} see to it that their laws accord with God's law, because [they accord] with utility and reason, since they profess in catholic fashion that every law or rite of man which does not have its beginning from it is heretical and to be fled like poison.[^7] From which it is clear that God's law ought to be learned first by every catholic. What, I say, is another law to the faithful man? Hence God's law has been sophistically mixed with human traditions, namely so that the tradition may be [cloaked]{g:palliare} and observed more colourably. Let the faithful man, therefore, observe the justice of the law, and prepare himself to suffer wrongs and penalties for the omission of the laws of men. For this speeds the [wayfarer]{g:viator} directly to his end, and the opposite leads him astray or delays him. It is granted, therefore, that rites and laws decently drawn off from the law of the Lord are to be observed in their order. But let the Christian beware of excess in the study and observance of them; [suspended]{g:suspendere}, then, on the law of God, without further anxiety, let him be content with the breath of the Spirit, which directs without these extravagants.[^8] What, I ask, is a gentile or pharisaic [rite]{g:ritus} to the faithful man, except in so far as it modestly makes easier the observance of God's law? Hence those sinned gravely who introduced [297] sects and distinctions of the laws of the church, by which the meditation of God's law is diminished; since it would be better that the apostolic life were purely observed, and that, difformity set aside, men tended to the uniformity of the state of innocence, although gradation in secular [lordship]{g:dominium} is lawful and necessary, sin being supposed.[^9] Hence nothing is more faithless than [to infer]{g:concludere} from the gradation of secular lordship a gradation of secular [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio} and honour among clerics. For since that state is higher, more conformed to the state of Christ and of the saints, it must tend to greater unity, ruling the lay state, and not be modelled on a state so difform as the one it rules. Yet it is granted that there are degrees in that apostolic state, but contrary to the secular state; for here the greater is the poorer, the more [serviceable]{g:servitivus}, and the better fitted to run on pilgrimage with fewer [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}. Hence all [presbyters]{g:presbiter} ought to be fellows, as the Apostle teaches, together with the decree, concerning himself and Peter; nevertheless the servant becomes higher out of greater humility.[^10] Hence (as I have often said) the lay part is divided into the popular part and the secular part set over it, of which the first is good and the second better. But the clerical part, because of its fellowly uniformity, remains undivided and the best, which "Mary hath chosen";[^11] hence those stammer dangerously who say that kings are not lords of their kingdoms but bare defenders, or otherwise (as they say) one would have to grant degrees of such lordship among clerics. Let these men search the scriptures, and they can see how often the author of scripture, by word as well as by deed, grants lordship to those of the middle state, since he himself obediently gave tribute to Caesar, but excluded lordship from himself and his apostles and their successors, [298] as is clear above.[^12] Let them judge, therefore, between the [evidences]{g:evidencia}: "God forbids lordship to the priest of Christ and gives lordship to the secular; therefore so it ought to be." And: "Caesar, or the papal law, denies that lordship belongs to the secular, and says that any secular lordship whatever ought to belong to the pope and the bishops; therefore it is true."[^13] And so with these arguments from likeness: "Christ excluded himself and his apostles from secular lordship; therefore by the same token this ought to be observed by his vicars"; and "Secular lords and [prelates]{g:prelatus} claim civil exercise of lordship for themselves, the first lawfully and the second by false usurpation; therefore by the same token all clerics ought to [exercise lordship]{g:dominari} in this way." Into such reedy [quibbles]{g:argucie} break out those who thirst for secular lordship; and such confusion must be multiplied in Christ's [clergy]{g:clerus}, as it was in part multiplied in the clergy of the old law, until Antichrist has destroyed his members.[^14] Therefore, since one must act as is fitting according to the variation of place and time, it is clear that while the malice of covetousness reigns we must follow Christ's way the more diligently; for when a greater danger threatens, the one in peril ought to lean the more on aid. For so did Christ the priest, coming in the highest degree of [renunciation of property]{g:expropriacio}, when the covetousness of the priests reigned most. And this is the Apostle's reason, as I expounded before: "Redeeming," he says, "the time, because the days are evil."[^15] Hence the degenerate blaspheme excessively who say that it is as useful and as fitting for the state of the clergy now to [be enriched]{g:ditare} further as the power of working miracles was necessary in the primitive church. In truth, then, neither the [possessioned]{g:possessionatus} clergy nor a layman would give bodily [alms]{g:elemosina} to a beggar, just as the apostles would not diminish anything of the power of working [299] miracles; indeed, the power which the Evangelist had "to change fragments of the stones of the shore into gems and the leaves of the woods into gold" would be more useful to the world than any temporal possession whatever, as even Simon Magus would testify.[^16]

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** Allusion to Rom. 3:8 ("let us do evil that there may come good? Whose damnation is just"), not a quotation; *absit* is Paul's formula ("God forbid", e.g. Rom. 6:2).
[^2]: **T.** The argument requires "too diffuse and burdensome" (*nimis*); the print has *minus* "less" (the plate confirms it), translated as it stands; Loserth gives no variant.
[^3]: **T.** At 294.26 Loserth marks *sacrum* "ita cod." (so the manuscript): he doubted the word but printed it. **C.** The construal of *expressum singulariter in usu sacrum* is uncertain; it is taken as "set out singly in a sacred usage", against the least thing expressed in the law of the Lord.
[^4]: **S.** Decretum D.12 c.12 (*Omnia talia*), from Augustine, *Ep.* 55.19.35 to Januarius; the identification is ours, to be checked (LEDGER P11i-1). Loserth's italics bound the quotation, as here.
[^5]: **C.** The clause *deminuere autem libertatem … foret nota infidelitatis discipulo Antichristi* is taken as Wyclif's own judgement: to diminish the church's true liberty (Augustine's "free" religion) marks a disciple of Antichrist. It could also continue the pharisees' reasoning ("it would be, they say, a mark of unbelief"). *Vocem propriam ignorantes*: they do not know what their own name (*clericus*) means; perhaps "their own language".
[^6]: **C.** *Machometice*, "in Mahomet's manner": Wyclif's charge that Islam forbids its law to be disputed. *Voluntas capitis*: the head is the pope.
[^7]: **C.** *Seculares* are here the secular lords and their lawmakers (GLOSSARY *secularis*: the referent matters). *Ab ipsa* refers to *lex Dei*.
[^8]: **C.** *Extravagantibus* plays on the *Extravagantes*, the papal decretals "wandering outside" the collections. *Suspensus* is "hanging on" the law of God, intent on it.
[^9]: **C.** *Distincciones* may glance at the distinctions of the Decretum. *Graduacio* is rendered "gradation" (rank), not GLOSSARY's academic "graduation"; *difformitas, difformis* "difformity, difform", the want of uniformity, against *uniformitas*.
[^10]: **S.** Paul on himself and Peter: Gal. 2:9–11 (the right hands of fellowship; Paul's resistance to Peter); the decree is not named and is to be identified (LEDGER P11i-2). "The servant … higher": cf. Matt. 23:11.
[^11]: **S.** Luke 10:42, "Mary hath chosen the best part".
[^12]: **S.** Tribute to Caesar: Matt. 17:26 and 22:21. *Medius status*: the secular lords, between the clergy and the commons.
[^13]: **C.** The two arguments are set side by side for judgement; the second is ironic (*igitur verum*).
[^14]: **C.** *Locis a simili*: the topical place "from likeness". *Destruxerat* is pluperfect where a future perfect is meant; *membra sua* are Antichrist's own members.
[^15]: **S.** Eph. 5:16; Loserth's reference reads "Eph. V, 10" in the OCR of the page foot.
[^16]: **S.** The Evangelist is John: the legend that he turned rods of the woods into gold and pebbles of the shore into gems (Isidore, *De ortu et obitu patrum* 72, and the *Legenda aurea*; to be checked, LEDGER P11i-3); Loserth's italics bound the phrase as a quotation, and he refers to *De ecclesia* p. 189 and p. 81 above (LEDGER P11i-4). Simon Magus: Acts 8:18, who offered money for the apostles' power.
