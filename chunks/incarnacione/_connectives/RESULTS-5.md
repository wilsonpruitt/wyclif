# Connective check: *sed* / *autem*, De benedicta incarnacione, wave 5 (chunks 05-a – 06-a)

Classes: (a) minor premiss / next step, "now" · (b) adversative, "but" · (c) continuative *autem*, "and";
*non … sed* (incl. *nedum … sed*), "but".

## Changes

| chunk | page | Latin phrase | was | now | reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 06-a | 87 | *ergo est homo univoce. Maior patet … Minor autem argumenti patet ex fide* | And the minor of the argument is clear | Now the minor of the argument is clear | a: the syllogism (*omne suppositum … ; Christus est suppositum …; ergo*) has its major proved, and *Minor autem* brings the minor to proof; the card reserves "now" for exactly this, and 05-a p. 66 (*Minor autem argumenti sic probatur*) is already "Now the minor of the argument" |

## Doubtful, left as they are

- **05-a, p. 67**: *Quod autem dimissio foret dampnosa humanitati Christi ex hoc evidet*, rendered "And". Either (c), the second half of the proof of the minor already begun with "Now the minor … is proved" (no demerit, then damage), or (a), the next premiss of that proof. "And" is defensible as extending the same step. (Contrast 06-a p. 86, *Quod autem humanitates … evidet*, "Now": there the proof moves from the consequence to the antecedent, a new step.)
- **06-a, p. 89**: *Christus autem sepe dixit se esse filium hominis*, rendered "Now". (a), the facts that make good the minor (*sic fecisset Christus*) after the major's authorities, or (c), a continuation. "Now" is defensible.
- **05-e, p. 81** *Sed et Sabelliani erraverunt* and **06-a, p. 88** *Sed et multi non ponentes universalia*, both rendered "But … too / also". *Sed et* is additive ("and also, moreover") as much as adversative; "But … also" keeps the connective and the *et*, and reads acceptably.
- **06-a, p. 85**: *Sed in qua, rogo, si non fuerit in humana*, rendered "But in which, I ask". Could be (a), the step that presses to the conclusion, but no *ergo* follows; "But" (pressing the opponent) stands.
- **05-e, p. 80**: *aliqui ponebant … aliqui autem e contra dixerunt*, rendered "some held …; others, on the contrary, said": *autem* not rendered by a word, but carried by the "some … others" correlative (the *aliqui … aliqui autem* construction), with *e contra* "on the contrary". Not an omission.
