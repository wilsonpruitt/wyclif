## Doubtful, left as they are

- **02-c, p. 21**: *Christus nedum est univoce homo cum aliis **sed** non est homo simpliciter*, rendered "Christ, let alone being [univocally]{g:univoce} a man with others, is not a man [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}". No "but" in the English: the *nedum … sed* correlation is carried by "let alone" (the stronger denial is stated, the weaker one subordinated), and it untangles Wyclif's elliptical *nedum est* (= "not only is he not") correctly. A card-literal alternative, if wanted: "Christ not only is not [univocally]{g:univoce} a man with others, but is not a man [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}".
- **02-c, p. 22**: *Non est **autem** homo perfectus si vel anima carni vel anime ipsi mens humana defuerit* (Augustine to Dardanus), rendered "And a man is not perfect". Either (c), extending the definition of "perfect man" just given (the whole nature), or (a), the premiss brought to bear against the heretics just named (a soulless or mindless Christ is no perfect man; the conclusion left unstated). No *ergo* follows. "And" is defensible; "Now a man is not perfect" would be the (a) reading.
- **02-c, p. 24**: *si dico—Christus est creatura—**sed** et Arrius dixit Christum esse creaturam*, rendered "if I say "Christ is a creature", and Arius too said "Christ is a creature"". *Sed et* is intensive-additive here ("and also"), fitting none of the classes cleanly (cf. *sed precipue*, De ecclesia RESULTS-01, 01-d); "too" carries the *et*, "and" the *sed*. Card-literal alternative: "but Arius too said".
- **02-d, p. 25**: *Nam illa creatura non foret melior creato angelo: de Christo **autem** dicit Apostolus …*, rendered "but of Christ". Either (b), Arius's creature set against Christ, or (a), the premiss (Christ better than the angels, Heb 1) supporting the conclusion already drawn (*per consequens melior illa creatura*). No *ergo* follows; "but" is the plainer reading.
- **02-d, p. 25**: *secundum **autem** naturam corpoream est 'paululum minoratus ab angelis'*, rendered "but as to the bodily nature". Either (b), against the excelling *secundum creatum spiritum* that follows, or (c), the first member of the partition. "But" defensible.

Not counted (not in bold): 02-b p. 21 *non mutando quod erat, sed³ assumendo* is rendered "but by assuming"; 02-c p. 21 *Tercio antem* (OCR for *autem*, continuative) is rendered "And third". Both correct.
