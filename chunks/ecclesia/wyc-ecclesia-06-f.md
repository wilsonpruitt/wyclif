# *De ecclesia*, chapter 6, part 6: whether each member of the church is Christ; unity of person rightly understood

## ¶1 · 133.26–136.8

> [p. 133] dubium est tactum in quarto argumento de unitate numerali ecclesie, utrum Christus et quodlibet membrum ecclesie sit eadem ecclesia in numero, et videtur quod sic ex triplici conveniencia numerali. Primo, inquam, videtur quod quantumcunque partem anima hominis adequate actuaverit, quantumcunque materia corporea [p. 134] varietur, manet continue idem homo, ut hic supponitur. Quare ergo non sic de homine ecclesie cuius forma est Christus manens in eternum? Secundum exemplum naturale est quod anima cuicunque membro copulata fuerit remanet tam anima quam membrum eadem persona hominis. Quare ergo non sic de Christo et ecclesia, sic quod quodlibet membrum eius sit Christus, ut sonant multe auctoritates? Tercio videtur quod sicut Christus mansit eadem persona in numero pro tempore mortis sue, tam in sepulchro mortuus quam in inferno vivus, ita videtur eandem personam ecclesie disiunctive regnantem in celo et peregrinantem in mundo, et sic Christus assumpsit ypostatice personam ecclesie peregrinantis in beata virgine. Hic oportet prudenter intelligere unitatem persone quam sancti exprimunt ex scriptura, ut patet de octo generibus unitatum quas exprimit beatus Bernhardus, ut recitatur in proximo capitulo in responsione ad tercium argumentum. Et tunc loquendo ad sensum doctorum concedenda videtur sentencia cuiuslibet horum trium argumentorum. Nam primo concedi potest quod manet continue eadem ecclesia que est collectim omnes predestinati et singillatim singuli, ac singulus eorundem omnes uniti in Christo capite, sed tunc oportet intelligere personam ut caput, basim tocius compositi et quodlibet membrum suum confuse tamquam absorptum in Christo absconditum, sic quod ecclesia Christi dicat Christum cum quocunque fideli sibi incorporatum. Si enim papa dicitur ecclesia propter virtutem quam influit in ecclesiam, [p. 135] ut dicitur XXIVª, q. prima: Quodcunque, et VIIª, q. prima Scire debes, multo magis primus papa admirabilis dominus Jesus Christus. Iterum, si procedentes carnaliter ab Adam primo dicuntur fuisse ipse in quo originaliter peccaverunt, multo evidencius descendentes a secundo Adam dicentur fore secundus Adam in quo finaliter requiescent. Nam semen fidei quod est veritas est magis communicativum quam semen corporeum. Iterum, si combustibile vel ignibile ut lignum, carbo vel ferrum dicitur post ignicionem vulgariter esse ignis, cum alium ignem non percipimus hic nobiscum, multo magis spiritualiter comestus a Christo et plene digestus ab eo usque ad quartam digestionem que est glorificacio; ille ergo cui vivere Christus est, sicut sunt omnes sancti qui vivunt Deum: manifestum videtur ad hunc sensum quod sunt vita eorum que Christus est. Et patet quomodo verum est quod benefaciendo vel persequendo membra Christi, ut sic, taliter respicitur ipse Christus (ut patet Matthei XXV°, et Act. IX°, 5). Patet secundo quod martires nunquam possent sustinuisse tot passiones et fecisse tot mirabilia nisi per incorporantem et assimilantem membra capiti loquentem et operantem in eis dominum Jesum Christum. Et patet tercio causa quare Christus precepit suis declinare a mundo et sensibilibus et proicere totam scienciam, curam et affeccionem in ipsum, quia aliter non assimilantur sibi, quia nichil plus obstat assimilacioni cordi, capiti et corpori ecclesie quod est Christus, quam mundana affeccio. Ipse enim sepe in evangelio vocatur regnum celorum, corpus quod sequuntur aquile, et vita omnibus ipsum viventibus. Et patet [p. 136] quomodo dicendum est ad secundam racionem et terciam, concedendo sentenciam catholicam, in qua manuducunt, et cavendo diligenter ab errore intellectus et scandalo auditorii. Posset enim seductus credere quod omnis christianus foret idem personaliter cum Christo secundum corpus et animam, ita quod non forent dispares persone essencie vel nature, quod foret nimis erroneum.

There is a difficulty, touched on in the fourth argument, about the numerical unity of the [church]{g:ecclesia}: whether Christ and each member of the church are the same church in number. And it seems that they are, from a threefold numerical agreement.[^1] First, I say, it seems that, however great the part that the soul of a man has adequately actuated, and however much the bodily matter [134] varies, the same man remains continuously, as is supposed here. Why then is it not so with the man of the church, whose form is Christ, abiding for ever? The second natural example is that, to whatever member the soul has been joined, both the soul and the member remain the same person of the man. Why then is it not so with Christ and the church, so that each member of it is Christ, as many authorities sound?[^2] Thirdly, it seems that, just as Christ remained the same person in number for the time of his death, both dead in the sepulchre and alive in hell, so it seems that the same person of the church [remains], disjunctively reigning in heaven and [on pilgrimage]{g:peregrinari} in the world; and so Christ assumed hypostatically the person of the pilgrim church in the blessed Virgin.[^3] Here one must understand prudently the unity of person which the saints express from scripture, as is clear from the eight kinds of unity which blessed Bernard expresses, as is recited in the preceding chapter in the reply to the third argument.[^4] And then, speaking according to the sense of the doctors, the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of each of these three arguments seems to be granted. For, first, it can be granted that there remains continuously the same church, which is collectively all the [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} and severally each one, and each one of them [is] all, united in Christ the [head]{g:caput}; but then one must understand the person as the head, the base of the whole composite, and each of its members [indistinctly]{g:confuse}, as absorbed, hidden in Christ, so that "the church of Christ" means Christ with every faithful [person] incorporated in him.[^5] For if the [pope]{g:papa} is called the church on account of the power that he makes [flow]{g:influere} into the church, [135] as is said in [Causa] 24, question 1, *Quodcunque*, and [Causa] 7, question 1, *Scire debes*, much more the first pope, the wonderful Lord Jesus Christ.[^6] Again, if those who proceed carnally from the first Adam are said to have been him in whom they originally sinned, much more evidently will those descending from the second Adam be said to be the second Adam, in whom they will finally rest. For the seed of faith, which is truth, is more communicative than bodily seed.[^7] Again, if a combustible or ignitable thing, such as wood, coal or iron, is commonly said after ignition to be fire, since we perceive no other fire here with us, much more [is he Christ who is] spiritually eaten by Christ and fully digested by him, up to the fourth [digestion]{g:digestio}, which is glorification; he, therefore, for whom to live is Christ, as are all the saints who live God: … it seems manifest in this sense that they are their life, which is Christ.[^8] And it is clear how it is true that, in doing good to or persecuting the members of Christ, as such, Christ himself is thus regarded (as is clear, Matthew 25, and Acts 9:5). It is clear, secondly, that the martyrs could never have endured so many sufferings and done so many wonders except through the Lord Jesus Christ, incorporating and assimilating the members to the head, speaking and working in them. And it is clear, thirdly, why Christ commanded his own to turn aside from the world and from [perceptible things]{g:sensibilis}, and to cast all their knowledge, care and affection upon him: because otherwise they are not assimilated to him; for nothing hinders assimilation to the heart, the head and the body of the church, which is Christ, more than worldly affection.[^9] For he is often called in the gospel the [kingdom]{g:regnum} of heaven, the body which the eagles follow, and the life for all who live him. And it is clear [136] how one must answer the second and the third reason: by granting the catholic teaching to which they lead by the hand, and by guarding diligently against error of understanding and the scandal of the audience. For one led astray could believe that every Christian would be the same in person with Christ according to body and soul, so that they would not be disparate persons, [essences]{g:essencia} or natures; which would be too erroneous.[^10]

## ¶2 · 136.9–136.30

> [p. 136] Unde ne darem scandalum scole, distuli istam materiam. Aliquis enim est participative Christus ut quodlibet membrum eius, et aliquis personaliter ut solum ipsum caput, et illi qui non capiunt istam distinccionem, non capiunt sensum huius scripture Qui adheret Deo, unus spiritus est, et quomodo vinculum caritatis vel gracie est forcius quam vinculum naturale, ut dicit Augustinus in libro De Vera Religione XCI°, et tactum est cap. proximo in solucione tercii argumenti. Et patet quomodo intelligendi sunt doctores sancti, concedentes istam sentenciam, ut Augustinus, Crisostomus et Bernardus cum aliis qui sapiunt Deum et legem scripture. Et patet secundo quomodo tolli debent argumenta sophistica in ista materia, ut hii instant, si beati singuli sunt persona Christi, tunc sunt personaliter ipse Christus, et sic tantum Christus est homo, et omnis homo est omnis homo, cum aliis arguciis multipliciter in materia de trinitate. Sed hii non considerant quod multa possunt esse eadem persona communicative, participative vel secundum esse intelligibile, licet non personaliter, et patet quod consequencie multiplicate non procedunt.

Hence, so as not to give scandal to the schools, I put off this matter. For someone is Christ by participation, as each member of his, and someone personally, as the head itself alone; and those who do not grasp this distinction do not grasp the sense of this scripture, "He who is joined to God is one spirit", nor how the bond of [charity]{g:caritas} or of grace is stronger than the natural bond, as Augustine says in the book *On True Religion*, 91, and as was touched on in the preceding chapter in the solution of the third argument.[^11] And it is clear how the holy doctors are to be understood who grant this teaching, such as Augustine, Chrysostom and Bernard, with others who savour God and the law of scripture. And it is clear, secondly, how the sophistical arguments in this matter must be removed, as these [men] [object]{g:instatur}: if the blessed, each of them, are the person of Christ, then they are personally Christ himself, and so only Christ is a man, and every man is every man, with other [quibbles]{g:argucie} in manifold ways in the matter of the Trinity. But these do not consider that many things can be the same person communicatively, by participation, or according to intelligible [being]{g:esse}, though not personally; and it is clear that the multiplied [inferences]{g:consequencia} do not proceed.[^12]

## ¶3 · 136.31–137.11

> [p. 136] Ultimo patet quod non est repugnancia in equivoce concedentibus et negantibus eandem proposicionem [p. 137] sensibilem, ut unus catholice negat quod ipse est Christus, sicut fecit Baptista, quia non fuit personaliter ipse Christus, et alius concedit quod omnis qui Christum induit est Christus (ut patet de sanctis predictis). Unde captandum est auditorium cui sic varie est loquendum, nec videtur esse concedendum quod omnis christianus sit Deus, licet omnis res sit Deus, et omnis substancia secundum esse intelligibile est Deus (ut patet in materia De Ideis), ut omnis homo est idem Christo, quia eadem species, et Christus est deitas, et tamen non est idem deitati.

Lastly, it is clear that there is no repugnance in those who equivocally grant and deny the same [137] perceptible proposition: as one, in a catholic way, denies that he is Christ, as the Baptist did, because he was not personally Christ himself, and another grants that everyone who has put on Christ is Christ (as is clear of the saints aforesaid).[^13] Hence one must take the measure of the audience to which one is to speak so variously; nor does it seem that it is to be granted that every Christian is God, although every thing is God, and every substance according to intelligible being is God (as is clear in the matter *On Ideas*); just as every man is the same as Christ, because [of] the same species, and Christ is the [Godhead]{g:deitas}, and yet [a man] is not the same as the Godhead.[^14]

## Notes

[^1]: **T/R/C.** The *Sed* that opens this sentence stands at the end of 06-e (133.25). A margin *Dubium*; A and B margin *Utrum Christus et quodlibet membrum ecclesie sit eadem ecclesia in numero* (A lacks *in*; in B by a later hand) (133.26). *Conveniencia numeralis*: agreement bearing on numerical identity; three analogies follow, which the answer grants in a sense (134.18 ff.).

[^2]: **T/C.** B lacks *anima* (133.30). *Quantumcunque partem … adequate actuaverit*: however much matter the soul informs as its adequate subject, the man is the same though the matter changes. *De homine ecclesie*: the church taken as one man, whose form is Christ (cf. 132.30–133.3, 06-e). *Persona hominis*: the man's one person, which soul and member alike are said to be. *Ut sonant*: as the words of many authorities run.

[^3]: **T/C.** Loserth (134.11): a word seems to have dropped out; we supply "[remains]" from *mansit* before. *Disiunctive*: as either reigning in heaven or on pilgrimage in the world. *Ypostatice*: in the unity of the hypostasis (person); the claim is the objector's, and the answer guards it (136.3–8).

[^4]: **T/R/S.** The tokens *be* before *Hic* and *j.* after *persone* (134.16–17) are not Wyclif's text and are not translated. A margin *Responsio. Nota quomodo omnia membra Christi sunt Christus cum correspondenciis* (134.16). *Hic* opens the answer. Bernard's eight kinds of unity: *De consideratione* V.8.18 (PL 182:799C–D; LEDGER E06f-1). The back-reference is to ch. 5, the reply to its third argument (cf. Loserth's "above pag. 109" at 136.17).

[^5]: **C/S.** *Collectim … singillatim*: the church is all the predestinate taken together and each taken singly. *Ac singulus eorundem omnes uniti in Christo capite*: we take *omnes uniti* as predicate, "each of them [is] all, united in Christ the head"; "and each of them, all being united in Christ the head" is possible; doubtful. *Basim tocius compositi*: the head as the base of the whole composite. *Absconditum*: cf. Col. 3:3, "your life is hid with Christ in God" (DR).

[^6]: **S/R.** A margin *Papa quomodo dicitur ecclesia* (135.1). Loserth (135.1): Decretum II, C.24 q.1 c.6, *Quodcumque*, and C.7 q.1 c.7, *Scire debes* (the form is ours; LEDGER E06f-2): in the first (Augustine, *In Ioh.* 50) Peter, receiving the keys, signified the church; in the second (Cyprian) the bishop is in the church and the church in the bishop (PL 187:1267A–B, 746B). *Virtutem quam influit*: the power he makes flow in (GLOSSARY *influere*). "The first pope" is Wyclif's title for Christ.

[^7]: **T/S.** B lacks *ab* (135.4). *In quo originaliter peccaverunt*: cf. Rom. 5:12 and the first and second Adam of 1 Cor. 15:45 (cited by sense only). *Communicativum*: apt to communicate itself.

[^8]: **T/S/C.** The sentence after *multo magis* has no verb; we supply "[is he Christ who is]". Loserth (135.16): after *Deum* some words seem to have fallen out; we mark the gap "…" and translate what stands. *Quartam digestionem … glorificacio*: the fourth of the four digestions (GLOSSARY; Rom. 8:30). *Cui vivere Christus est*: Phil. 1:21, "For to me, to live is Christ" (DR; Loserth 135.15). *Vivunt Deum*: "live God", transitive, as printed (so *ipsum viventibus*, 135.30).

[^9]: **S/T/C.** Matt. 25 (no verse printed): "as long as you did it to one of these my least brethren, you did it to me" (25:40, DR); Acts 9:5, "I am Jesus whom thou persecutest" (DR). *Respicitur*: it is Christ himself who is regarded, that is, touched. B *assimulantur … assimulacioni* (135.28–29). *Assimilacioni cordi, capiti et corpori ecclesie, quod est Christus*: datives of what one is assimilated to; the relative takes the whole.

[^10]: **S/R/C.** *Corpus quod sequuntur aquile*: cf. Matt. 24:28, "Wheresoever the body shall be, there shall the eagles also be gathered together" (DR). *In qua manuducunt*: the second and third arguments lead by the hand to a catholic teaching, which is granted. B margin *Error intellectus et scandalum sunt cavenda* (136.3). *Dispares persone essencie vel nature*: printed without commas; we take three nouns.

[^11]: **T/S/R.** A margin *Christus dupliciter dicitur* (136.9). *Scandalum scole*: offence to the schools (the university). *Solum ipsum caput*: A and B *Salomon* for *solum*; A₁ corrects in the margin to *solum* (136.12); Loserth prints *solum*, translated. 1 Cor. 6:17, Vulgate *Qui autem adhæret Domino*, DR "But he who is joined to the Lord is one spirit"; Wyclif's *Deo* translated (Loserth 136.14). *De vera religione* "XCI": B *de una religione* (136.17); §91 (PL 34:163): the union of minds outweighs ties of place and birth, which Wyclif paraphrases loosely (LEDGER E06f-3).

[^12]: **T/R/C.** A margin *Contra dicta* (136.21); B *Sed hiis* (136.27). *Sapiunt*: savour, have a taste for. The sophisms run from "the blessed are the person of Christ" to "only Christ is a man" and "every man is every man". *Secundum esse intelligibile*: in intelligible being, as a thing is in God's idea (cf. 137.6–8). *Consequencie multiplicate non procedunt*: the inferences so multiplied do not hold.

[^13]: **S/C.** John 1:20, "I am not the Christ" (DR), cited by sense; *omnis qui Christum induit*: cf. Gal. 3:27, "have put on Christ" (DR). *Eandem proposicionem sensibilem*: the same perceptible proposition, the sentence spoken or written, granted by one in one sense and denied by another in another; hence "equivocally".

[^14]: **R/C.** *Captandum est auditorium*: one must take the measure of the audience. *In materia De Ideis*: Wyclif's own treatment, the treatise *De ideis* (*Summa de ente* II, tract. 5; not read), which Wyclif cites for this same thesis in *De dominio divino* II c. 5 (197) and *De civili dominio* I c. 43 (369) (LEDGER E06f-4). *Ut omnis homo …*: we take *ut* as "just as", illustrating the *licet*: every man is the same as Christ in species, and Christ is the Godhead, yet a man is not the same as the Godhead; doubtful.
