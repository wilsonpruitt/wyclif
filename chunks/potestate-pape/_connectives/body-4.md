# sed/autem extract, wave 4: chunks 05-e 05-f 06-a 06-b 06-c 06-d

### 05-e ¶1 · 93.31–96.20  (11 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 93] Et quantum ad beatos Silvestrem, Gregorium et similes dicitur quod cum pena habuerunt tantum tumultum temporalium exproprietarie per [p. 94] medias personas distribuendo bona ecclesie, ipsi **autem** servabant vitam humilem et pauperem in se ipsis, quod si scivissent peccatores sequentes sic obtinuisse illas divicias, firmiter credo quod ipsas pro se et posteris reliquissent. Et stultus est qui sompniat et defendit quod non in hoc venialiter peccaverunt. Non enim repugnat quod remisse in gracia occuparunt illud officium sicut meritorie, **sed** defectu veniali occuparunt temporalia et in posteris propter profundiorem maliciam in occupacionem mortalem et simpliciter Deo odibilem est perversa. Periculum igitur foret trahere in exemplum vel consequenciam modum agendi venialiter culpabilem, quia sic ex pietate doceremus cum Petro gentes iudaizare, propter quod fuit a Paulo, ut patet capitulo II ad Gal., reprehensus. Et credo quod longe maius peccatum fuit in dotatis episcopis ex hoc quod dotacionem huiusmodi admiserunt, Caveamus igitur exempla capere sanctorum ab operibus eorum mixtim laudabilibus et venialiter culpabilibus secundum disparem racionem, quia nescimus si in nobis sit predestinacionis gracia vel excusandi racio a mortali. Ideo debemus sequi secure sanctum sanctorum et virtutes in membris suis, nec non in operibus sic mixtis debemus colare racionem laudabilem et illi inniti racione alia separata. Et credo quod ignorancia huius sentencie fecit multos a via Domini declinare. Noverunt sophiste quomodo eadem res est simul laudabilis et culpabilis secundum disparem racionem, ut Petrus et operacio sua est laudabilis, quia sub aliquo gradu bona moraliter, et est cum hoc culpabilis et reprobabilis propter defectum habundancie [p. 95] virtutis et fervorum amoris. Sic enim sumus omnes in vita continue increpandi; et talis tepiditas, ut dicitur Apocalipsis III, 16 causat vomitum, quia indubie inducit plurimos in mortale. **Sed** tercio arguitur contra dicta per hoc quod iuxta sepe et publice dicta a me alia est potestas iurisdicionis et alia ordinis; sicut declarat dominus Ardmacanus libro VII De Questionibus Armenorum VI capitulo, ubi dicitur, quod potestates ordinis in omnibus sacerdotibus sunt equales. **Sed** potestates regiminis variantur, cum Petro et suis successoribus qui soli habent universalem potestatem regiminis data est sic potestas super universalem ecclesiam; et consonat factum ecclesie cum multis legibus de regimine vel iurisdicione loquentibus; oportet igitur docere quod officium Petro conveniat et quid sit ab eo vetitum. Quantum ad illud non discucio, quid fiat de facto, **sed** quid auctoritate scripture vel racione debet fieri. Unde Deum contestor, nec ex scriptura nec ex sanctis doctoribus fundari video istius iurisdicionis extensionem tam vanam, **sed** ex ritu gentilium sicut institucionem pape et cardinalium introductam. Concedo igitur distinccionem predictam. Pro quo notandum quod multi sacerdotes et episcopi habent dispares potestates. **Sed** potestas ordinis, que est par in quolibet sacerdote, est potestas quam clericus habet in quantum est Christi sacerdos, quam oportet esse communem et quodlibet eius iudicium est potestas sic remissa, qua sacerdos non potest carere manente suo sacerdocio; et illa potestas non potest maius et [p. 96] minus suscipere. Et preter illam est dare aliam maneriem potestatis iurisdicionis sive regiminis, que fuit dispar in diversis apostolis et continue est dispar in diversis episcopis. **Sed** illa non attenditur penes institucionem humanam, **sed** penes hoc quod Deus ordinavit episcopum intensius vel remissius debere regere maiorem partem ecclesie vel minorem. Et non attenditur in isto ad humanum primatum **sed** ad quantitatem potestatis quam Deus attribuit ad edificandum suam ecclesiam, et (ut dictum est) omnes habuerunt commixtam potenciam super eodem populo sine repugnancia, Petrus primatum non propter amplitudinem iurisdiccionis **sed** propter fervorem dileccionis Christi et sue ecclesie. Nam ut credo ceteri extensius laborarunt, ita quod turpissime et inevidentissime arguitur quod, si Petrus habuit primatum inter apostolos, tunc ille habuit iurisdiccionem universalem tocius ecclesie, et alii particularem a Petro et eius vicariis limitatam.

ENGLISH: And as to the blessed Silvester, Gregory and the like, the answer is that, since they had with pain so great a turmoil of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, distributing the goods of the [church]{g:ecclesia} [without property]{g:exproprietarie} through [94] intermediate persons, while they themselves kept a humble and poor life in themselves, [it follows] that, if they had known that the sinners following would so obtain those riches, I firmly believe that they would have left them, for themselves and their successors.[^1] And he is a fool who dreams and maintains that they did not sin venially in this.[^2] For it is not [incompatible]{g:repugnare} that they held that office slackly in grace, as [they did] meritoriously, but held the temporal goods with a venial defect; and in their successors, because of a deeper malice, [the holding] was perverted into a mortal holding, hateful to God [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}.[^3] It would therefore be a danger to draw into an example or a precedent a way of acting that is venially culpable, for so, out of [piety]{g:pietas}, we should teach the gentiles with Peter to judaize, for which he was rebuked by Paul, as is clear in chapter 2 of Galatians.[^4] And I believe that the sin was far greater in the endowed [bishops]{g:episcopus} in that they admitted an [endowment]{g:dotacio} of this kind. Let us therefore beware of taking examples of the saints from their works that are, in mixed fashion, praiseworthy and venially culpable under different aspects, for we do not know whether there is in us the grace of predestination or a reason for being excused from mortal [sin]. Therefore we ought to follow securely the holy of holies and the virtues in his [members]{g:membrum}; and in works so mixed we ought to strain out the praiseworthy aspect and lean on it, the other aspect being separated. And I believe that ignorance of this [teaching]{g:sentencia} has made many turn aside from the way of the Lord. The [sophists]{g:sophista} know how the same thing is at once praiseworthy and culpable under different aspects: as Peter and his working is praiseworthy, because morally good in some degree, and is with this culpable and reprovable for want of an [abundance]{g:habundancia} [95] of [virtue]{g:virtus} and of the fervours of love.[^5] For so we are all, in this life, continually to be rebuked; and such lukewarmness, as is said in Revelation 3:16, causes vomiting, for without doubt it leads very many into mortal [sin].[^6] But, thirdly, [it is argued]{g:arguitur-per} against what has been said, by appeal to the fact that, according to what I have often and publicly said, the [power of jurisdiction]{g:potestas-iurisdiccionis} is one thing and that [of order]{g:potestas-ordinis} another; as [the lord of Armagh]{g:dominus-ardmacanus} declares in book 7 of the *Questions of the Armenians*, chapter 6, where it is said "that the powers of order are equal in all priests".[^7] But the powers of government vary, since to Peter and his successors, who alone have universal power of government, power was thus given over the universal church; and the practice of the church agrees with many laws that speak of government or [jurisdiction]{g:iurisdiccio}; it is therefore necessary to teach what office befits Peter and what is forbidden him.[^8] As to that, I do not discuss what is done in fact, but what ought to be done by the [authority]{g:auctoritas} of scripture or by reason. Hence I call God to witness: I see so vain an extension of this jurisdiction grounded neither in scripture nor in the holy doctors, but introduced from the rite of the gentiles, as [was] the institution of the [pope]{g:papa} and the cardinals. I grant, therefore, the aforesaid distinction. For which it is to be noted that many [priests]{g:sacerdos} and bishops have unequal powers. But the power of order, which is equal in every priest, is the power that a cleric has inasmuch as he is a priest of Christ, which must be common, and every judgement of it is a power so remitted, which a priest cannot lack while his priesthood remains; and that power cannot admit more and [96] less.[^9] And besides it there is another [kind]{g:maneries} of power, of jurisdiction or government, which was unequal in different apostles and is continually unequal in different bishops. But that [power] is not reckoned according to human institution, but according to God's having ordained that a bishop should rule, more intensely or more slackly, a greater or a lesser part of the church.[^10] And regard is paid in this not to human [primacy]{g:primatus} but to the quantity of power that God assigned for building up his church; and (as has been said) all had a mingled [power]{g:potencia} over the same people without [incompatibility]{g:repugnancia}, Peter [having] the primacy not because of the breadth of his jurisdiction but because of the fervour of his love of Christ and of his church.[^11] For, as I believe, the others laboured more extensively, so that it is most shamefully and with the least evidence argued that, if Peter had the primacy among the apostles, then he had universal jurisdiction over the whole church, and the others a particular one [assigned]{g:limitare} by Peter and his vicars.

### 05-f ¶1 · 97.13–98.26  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 97] Quantum ad illud, patet quod turpiter petitur quod probaretur, quia, ut patet in parte ex dictis, propter spiritualem primatum, quo ceteris fuit humilior, servicior et ecclesie Christi amancior, et non propter aliquam dignitatem mundanam Deus dedit sibi primatum ecclesie, et sic fuit ad laborem et sollicitudinem suam, quod habuit illam preeminenciam, et hoc fuit obligare obedire minoribus. Et hinc sumitur racio, quare sic meruit paciendo humiliter reprobacionem Apostoli, de qua Gal. II, ut declarat Augustinus Epistola IX. Quilibet igitur apostolus habuit regimen quodammodo universale viantis ecclesie, **sed** Paulus Petro extensius. Et quantum ad illud dictum Petro Mar. XVI Tibi dabo claves regni celorum, patet quod fuit dictum in persona ecclesie sacerdotum, quod aliter nos non gauderemus hoc privilegio virtute horum verborum Domini; unde miror quod aliquis habens fidem scripture retorqueret ex isto verbo singulare privilegium Romani pontificis in solvendo. Hoc enim fuit singulariter [p. 98] dictum Petro tamquam generali procuratori ecclesie sacerdotum et non tamquam singulari recipienti pro se solum hoc privilegium, **sed** limitatum et diffinitum fuit a Christo preter ordinacionem humanam quomodo sacerdotes sibi habiles privilegio isti participent. Et ista est sentencia Augustini super X, Omelia CXXIV: Petrus, inquit, apostolus propter apostolatus sui primatum gerebat figurata generalitate personam ecclesie; quod enim ad ipsum proprie pertinet, natura unus homo erat gracia christianus, habundanciori gracia unus idemque primus apostolus. **Sed** quando ei dictum est: Tibi dabo claves regni celorum, et quodcumque ligaveris super terram, erit ligatum et in celis, et quodcumque solveris super terram, erit solutum et in celis, universam signat ecclesiam, que in hoc seculo diversis temptacionibus ut ymbribus et tempestatibus quatitur, et non cadit, quoniam fundatur super petram; unde Petrus nomen accepit, que fundatur in Christo. Horrenda itaque et infidelis seduccio simplicium laycorum foret ex isto textu evangelii episcopum irracionabiliter reservare sibi potenciam ad ligandum et cecare populum cum laxacione potestatis proprie et suspensione potestatis alterius, ut patet in materia de sacramento penitencie.

ENGLISH: As to that, it is clear that what was to be proved is shamefully begged; for, as is clear in part from what has been said, it was because of a spiritual [primacy]{g:primatus}, by which he was humbler than the others, more serviceable and more loving of the [church]{g:ecclesia} of Christ, and not because of any worldly dignity, that God gave him the primacy of the church; and so it was for his labour and solicitude that he had that preeminence, and this was to bind [him] to obey lesser men.[^1] And hence is taken the reason why he so merited, by humbly suffering the reproof of the Apostle, of which Galatians 2 [speaks], as Augustine declares in Letter 9.[^2] Every apostle, therefore, had a government in a way universal over the church [on the road]{g:viantes}, but Paul more extensively than Peter.[^3] And as to that saying to Peter, Mark 16, "I will give to thee the keys of the kingdom of heaven", it is clear that it was said in the person of the church of priests, since otherwise we should not enjoy this [privilege]{g:privilegium} by virtue of these words of the Lord; hence I marvel that anyone having the faith of scripture should twist out of this word a singular privilege of the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex} in loosing.[^4] For this was said singularly [98] to Peter as to the general [proctor]{g:procurator} of the church of priests, and not as to a singular recipient of this privilege for himself alone; but it was [assigned]{g:limitare} and defined by Christ, apart from human [ordinance]{g:ordinacio}, how [priests]{g:sacerdos} [qualified]{g:habilis} for it should share in this privilege.[^5] And this is the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of Augustine on [John], Homily 124: "Peter the apostle," he says, "because of the primacy of his apostleship, bore in a figurative generality the person of the church; for as to what belongs to him properly, by nature he was one man, by grace a Christian, by more abundant grace one and the same first apostle. But when it was said to him, 'I will give to thee the keys of the kingdom of heaven. And whatsoever thou shalt bind upon earth, it shall be bound also in heaven: and whatsoever thou shalt loose on earth, it shall be loosed also in heaven', he signified the whole church, which in this world is shaken by divers temptations as by rains and storms, and does not fall, because it is founded upon the rock, whence Peter received his name"; which is founded in Christ.[^6] It would therefore be a horrible and [faithless]{g:infidelis} seduction of simple laymen, from this text of the gospel, for a [bishop]{g:episcopus} unreasonably to reserve to himself the [power]{g:potencia} of binding, and to blind the people by relaxing his own [power]{g:potestas} and [suspending]{g:suspendere} the power of another, as is clear in the matter on the sacrament of penance.[^7]

### 05-f ¶2 · 98.28–99.34  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 98] **Sed** tercio nimis leviter sollempnisatur quod Paulus accepit autoritatem et licenciam a Petro ad predicandum in gentibus ex textu Gal. II, 9: Cum cognovissent, inquit, graciam que data est [p. 99] michi, Jacobus et Cephas et Johannes, qui videbantur esse columpne, dexteras dederunt michi et Barnabe societatis, ut nos in gentes, ipsi vero in circumcisionem. **Sed** notet rogo arguens ex isto textu, quomodo Paulus triennio ante illud tempus sine eorum auctoritate predicavit in gentibus, de quo non cepit penitenciam vel reprobacionem a Petro, quia Paulus cicius illud insereret quam reprehensionem Petri. Notet secundo quomodo communi consilio et auctoritate Christi, ut dicit Jeronimus, locuti sunt apostoli in ecclesia primitiva. Petrus **autem** ex decreto cetus apostolici fuit paracior ad laborem, ut patet Act. VIII, 14 ubi dicitur, quod apostoli, qui erant Jerusolimis, miserunt Petrum et Johannem in Samariam ad confirmandum populum, qui prius receperat verbum Dei; ubi non notatur Petro humana dignitas, quod apostoli eum miserunt ad illud officium, sicut papa non acceptaret sibi dignitatem et honorem, quod cardinales mitterent eum et unum episcopum socium ad convertendum unam gentem. Et patet quod minus inevidenter eliceretur ex isto textu quod Petrus et Johannes cepissent autoritatem ab apostolis qui residebant Jerusolimis; non enim est magna auctoritas cursorem mitti ad operam. **Sed** tercio notet falsarius verba textus Apostoli et videat si sequatur: Jacobus, Petrus et Johannes dederunt Paulo et Barnabe dextram societatis, igitur dederunt eis autoritatem sive licenciam; dederunt, inquam, eis bonam societatem, gaudentes quod dedit eis Deus graciam predicandi gentibus, sicut Petro Judeis, ymmo precepit separare sibi Paulum et Barnabam ad illud officium.

ENGLISH: But thirdly, it is too lightly made a solemn matter that Paul received [authority]{g:auctoritas} and [licence]{g:licencia} from Peter to preach among the gentiles, from the text of Galatians 2:9: "When they had known," he says, "the grace that was given [99] to me, James and Cephas and John, who seemed to be pillars, gave to me and Barnabas the right hands of fellowship: that we should go unto the Gentiles, and they unto the circumcision."[^8] But let him who argues from this text note, I pray, how Paul for three years before that time preached among the gentiles without their authority, for which he received no penance or reproof from Peter; for Paul would sooner have inserted that than the rebuke of Peter.[^9] Let him note secondly how, by common counsel and the authority of Christ, as Jerome says, the apostles spoke in the primitive church.[^10] And Peter, by decree of the apostolic company, was the readier for labour, as is clear from Acts 8:14, where it is said that "the apostles, who were in Jerusalem", "sent Peter and John" into Samaria to confirm the people, who had earlier received the word of God; where no human dignity is marked in Peter in that the apostles sent him to that office, just as the [pope]{g:papa} would not take it as dignity and honour to himself that the cardinals should send him, with one bishop as companion, to convert one nation. And it is clear that it would be drawn from this text with less want of evidence that Peter and John had received authority from the apostles who stayed in Jerusalem; for it is no great authority for a runner to be sent to a task. But thirdly let the [falsifier]{g:falsarius} note the words of the Apostle's text and see whether [it follows]{g:sequitur}: "James, Peter and John gave Paul and Barnabas the right hand of fellowship; therefore they gave them authority or licence". They gave them, I say, good fellowship, rejoicing that God had given them the grace of preaching to the gentiles, as to Peter [that of preaching] to the Jews; indeed, he commanded that Paul and Barnabas be separated to him for that office.[^11]

### 05-f ¶4 · 100.23–101.12  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 100] Ex istis colligitur, quod sicut omnes apostoli preter Scarioth erant socii sibi obedientes, honore invicem prevenientes et superiores invicem arbitrantes, qui inmediate a Deo potestatem et licenciam ad evangelizandum acceperunt; sic debet esse de sacerdotibus et episcopis ecclesie iam instantis, ita quod disparitas ditacionis societatem et amiciciam evangelicam non dissolvet, quia per idem Christus per possibile reconversans nobiscum, observans scolam suam ex integro sicut olim, non haberet papam vel dotatos episcopos socios vel amicos **sed** dominos, quibus solveret tributum sicut olim fecit cesari. Non enim consonat [p. 101] scole sapientis habere familiam tam discordem a capite quod monstruose et contra policiam humanam servicium ab eis exigeret, et in propria familia doctrine capitis repugnaret, quod si veniens in mundum illum non invenerit episcopos in conversacione sibi consentaneos et amicos, quomodo laudaret eos ut fideles vicarios, nec ut fideles negociatores in die iudicii beatitudine premiandos; a multis creditur quod nullos vel paucos inveniet, in quibus scintilla sue conversacionis sit propter antichristianam declinacionem ad seculum exemplata.

ENGLISH: From these things it is gathered that, just as all the apostles except Iscariot were companions obedient to one another, "with honour preventing one another" and esteeming one another superiors, who received power and licence to evangelize immediately from God, so it ought to be with the priests and bishops of the church now present, so that disparity of enrichment shall not dissolve fellowship and evangelical friendship.[^15] For by the same token Christ, if, as is possible, he were living among us again and keeping his school entire as of old, would have the pope or the endowed bishops not as companions or friends but as [lords]{g:dominus}, to whom he would pay tribute as once he did to Caesar.[^16] For it does not befit [101] the school of a wise man to have a [household]{g:familia} so discordant from its [head]{g:caput} that, monstrously and against human polity, it should exact service from them, and in his own household be [incompatible]{g:repugnare} with the [doctrine]{g:doctrina} of the head; so that if, coming into the world, he does not find bishops agreeing with him in [manner of life]{g:conversacio}, and friends, how would he praise them as faithful vicars, or as faithful [dealers]{g:negociatores} to be rewarded with blessedness on the day of judgement? It is believed by many that he will find none or few in whom a spark of his manner of life is copied, because of the antichristian decline towards the world.[^17]

### 05-f ¶5 · 101.13–101.22  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 101] Si **autem** queritur quid nos sacerdotes debemus facere, dicitur indubie quod debemus in conversacione sequi ipsum quantum sufficimus. Sic quod primo abraderemus quantum sufficimus omnem occupacionem mentis aut corporis, que fuerit inpertinens aut contraria officio sacerdotis. Secundo vero poneremus omnem sacerdotalem potestatem quantum sufficeremus efficaciter in effectum, et omnium istorum exemplar et specula debet esse dominus Jhesus Christus.

ENGLISH: And if it is asked what we priests ought to do, [the answer is]{g:dicitur}, without doubt, that we ought to follow him in manner of life as far as we are able: so that, first, we should shave away, as far as we are able, every occupation of mind or body that is irrelevant or contrary to the office of priest; and secondly, we should put every priestly power, as far as we are able, effectually into effect; and of all these the exemplar and mirror must be the Lord Jesus Christ.[^18]

### 05-f ¶6 · 101.23–102.24  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 101] Ex istis sequerentur tres conclusiones graciose ecclesie, primo quod papa non intromitteret se iudicando de negocio seculari, quia est alienum a sacerdotali officio iuxta illud II Thim. II, 4: Nemo militans Deo implicat se negociis secularibus, Secundo quod renueret omnem dotacionem cesaream, cum ex facto Christi et Petri patet quod foret impertinens papali officio; et per consequens omnes alii episcopi ut sit conformitas ad caput, et revera necessarior foret conformitas paparum sequencium in ista pauperie ad Christum, sicut [p. 102] Petrus et alii sub quibus crevit ecclesia se Domino conformarunt. Tercio quod papa et omnes alii episcopi debent evangelizacione, oracione et sancte vite exemplacione assidue pascere oves Christi; patet ex officio ter limitato Petro Johannis ultimo. Unde Augustinus Epistola CXLI ad Paulinum notat, quomodo in textu Apostoli Ephe. IV, ii officium pastoris et doctoris iunguntur: Pastores, inquit, et doctores quos maxime discernere voluisti eosdem puto esse et non alios pastores et alios doctores intelligamus; et ideo cum predixisset pastores, subiunxit doctores, ut pastores intelligerent ad officium suum pertinere doctrinam. Ideo vero non ait: quosdam vero pastores et quosdam doctores, ut superiori locucionis genere, **sed** quosdam pastores et doctores, tamquam unum aliquid duobus nominibus amplexus est, quosdam, inquit, pastores et doctores. Et sic spoliatis superfluis, pateret quis episcopus remaneret habilis ex thesauro Domini et quis remaneret vacuus prescisso organo et officio laycali; et forte reperirentur plures pastores cesarei quam fideles vicarii domini Jhesu Christi. Et utinam talis discernendi monstracio foret facta.

ENGLISH: From these would follow three [conclusions]{g:conclusio} gracious to the church.[^19] First, that the pope would not meddle in judging a secular business, for it is alien to the priestly office, according to 2 Timothy 2:4: "No man, being a soldier to God, entangleth himself with secular businesses." Secondly, that he would renounce every caesarean [endowment]{g:dotacio}, since from the deed of Christ and of Peter it is clear that it would be irrelevant to the papal office; and in consequence all the other bishops [would], that there may be conformity to the head; and in truth the conformity of the popes who follow to Christ in this poverty would be more necessary, as [102] Peter and the others under whom the church grew conformed themselves to the Lord.[^20] Thirdly, that the pope and all the other bishops ought to feed the sheep of Christ assiduously by evangelizing, by prayer, and by the example of a holy life; this is clear from the office thrice assigned to Peter in the last [chapter] of John.[^21] Hence Augustine, Letter 141 to Paulinus, notes how in the Apostle's text, Ephesians 4:11, the office of pastor and of doctor are joined: "Pastors," he says, "and doctors, whom you most wished me to distinguish, I think are the same, and let us not understand some to be pastors and others doctors; and therefore, when he had spoken of pastors, he added doctors, that pastors might understand that doctrine belongs to their office. And therefore he does not say: 'and some pastors, and some doctors', as in the earlier kind of expression, but 'some pastors and doctors', as though he embraced one thing under two names: 'some', he says, 'pastors and doctors'."[^22] And so, the superfluities being stripped away, it would be clear which bishop remained qualified from the treasury of the Lord, and which remained empty, the instrument and the lay office being cut off; and perhaps more caesarean pastors would be found than faithful vicars of the Lord Jesus Christ. And would that such a [muster]{g:monstracio} for discerning had been made.[^23]

### 06-a ¶2 · 103.8–104.8  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 103] Item, Christus habuit potestatem ponendi animam suam et iterum sumendi eam, ut patet Johan. X, et sic non potuit aliquis eius vicarius, ut declarat Augustinus Omelia XLVII, ergo conclusio. Si enim Petrus haberet potestatem iterum sumendi animam suam prius positam, oporteret Petrum esse naturam terciam a corpore et anima cui inesset potestas resumendi animam sic positam, quod soli Deo potest competere, quia oportet dare subiectum cui competeret sic assumere, corpus vero exanime, non habet huiusmodi potestatem nec anima potest se ipsam assumere. Item, Christus habet per se potestatem ad redimendum genus humanum et ad principiandum graciam cuilibet alteri de suo genere, **sed** sic non habet aliquis eius vicarius, ergo conclusio. Antecedens patet per illud Johan. I, 14: Vidimus gloriam eius quasi unigeniti a patre plenum gracie et veritatis; de cuius plenitudine nos omnes accipimus, graciam pro Gracia. Sic igitur nullus alius potest mutari in Deum et hominem, ut sit per se fons gracie, a quo oportet omnem graciam datam ecclesie derivari, quia sic nec potest alius homo vel angelus Jhesu nostro in potencia, in plenitudine gracie vel preeminencia coequari. [p. 104] Nec hoc verti debet fideli in dubium, qui credit, quod nullus alius potest idemptificari personaliter Jhesu nostro, et quod natura divina ac humana idemptificantur in persona filii, licet ille nature infinite distinguantur, quodammodo necesse est divinitatem Christi in humanitatem eius ex vi unionis supereminenter incomunicabiliter aliis personis influere.

ENGLISH: Again, Christ had power to lay down his soul and to take it up again, as is clear from John 10, and so no vicar of his could, as Augustine declares in Homily 47; therefore the [conclusion]{g:conclusio}.[^3] For if Peter had the power of taking up again his soul once laid down, Peter would have to be a third nature, apart from body and soul, in which the power of resuming the soul so laid down would inhere, which can belong to God alone; for a subject must be given to which it would belong so to take [it] up, and the lifeless body has no such power, nor can the soul take itself up.[^4] Again, Christ has of himself the power to redeem the human race and to originate grace for any other of his kind; now no vicar of his has [it] so; therefore the conclusion.[^5] The [antecedent]{g:antecedens} is clear from John 1:14: "We saw his glory as of the only begotten of the Father, full of grace and truth; of whose fulness we all receive, grace for grace."[^6] So therefore no other can be changed into God and man, so as to be of himself the fount of grace, from which all grace given to the [church]{g:ecclesia} must be derived; for so neither can another man or angel be made equal to our Jesus in [power]{g:potencia}, in plenitude of grace or in preeminence.[^7] [104] Nor ought this to be brought into doubt by the faithful man who believes that no other can be made identical in person with our Jesus, and that the divine and human nature are made identical in the person of the Son, although those natures are infinitely distinguished; in a certain way it is necessary that the divinity of Christ [flow]{g:influere} into his humanity, by force of the union, supereminently, incommunicably to other persons.[^8]

### 06-a ¶3 · 104.9–106.10  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 104] Nec sunt obiectus, quos ignari obiciunt digni memoria. Arguunt enim quidam: cum gracia et potestas date Christo humanitus sint tantum finite, derogaretur infinite divine potencie nisi posset eandem vel tantam ponere in quocumque. Et confirmacio est quod Christus sicut est eiusdem speciei nobiscum sic et quelibet gracia vel potestas quam habet humanitus. Stat igitur Deum ex omnipotencia sua augere potestatem vel graciam in Petro, quousque parificata fuerit qualitati in Christo. Tercio confirmatur per hoc quod omnem qualitatem, potest per se ponere et per consequens in quocumque subiecto voluerit et cum anima Petri sit tante capacitatis, ut anima Christi, Deus potest facere qualitates tales positas in aliis spiritibus informare. **Sed** et istud arguunt ex hoc quod verbum Dei potest quamcumque naturam humanam seorsum vel simul assumere; quo facto inessent sibi tanta gracia et potestas sicut Christus habet humanitus, igitur non posita illa unione Deus potest faciliter facere qualitates huiusmodi informare hominem non assumptum. **Sed** benedictus dominus qui nos liberavit de istis arguciis. Dictum est **autem** in materia de [p. 105] incarnacione, quod Christus non potuit aliam humanitatem assumere, verumtamen potuit facillime si voluisset, dictum est eciam quod ipse tam secundum corpus quam animam est eiusdem speciei cum quolibet alio fratre suo. Et dictum est tercio quod ex vi unionis ipostatice gracia, potestas et pulchritudo vel speciositas in Christo est alterius speciei quam aliqua qualitas consimilis nominis in alio homine potest esse. Et ideo incarnacio Christi perfecit totam universitatem creatam tam secundum individua quam universalia, cum multa talia aliter non fuissent. Sicut igitur individuum unius speciei licet infinitum creverit, non potest in toto parificari individuo alterius speciei, sic est de gracia Christi, de potestate, speciositate sue humanitatis. Qualitates **autem** multe, sicut patet de corporali pulchritudine, principiantur a relacione, ideo idem esset querere istas qualitates in alio homine et querere alium hominem qui simul sit Deus et homo. Ideo errant, qui nude arguunt idemptitatem specificam qualitatum ex idemptitate specifica subiectorum. Oportet enim principia rerum tendere ad maiorem unitatem quam sit in suis effectibus, cum summa unitas sit in primo principio et summa diversitas in sua universitate creata. Nec est michi sermo de qualitate que potest per se esse, quia certum est quod est dare unam naturam qualitatis, que non potest esse nisi informet substanciam, cum non sit nisi substanciam accidentaliter esse qualem. Et qui sompniant aliam ex vanitate subtilitatis circa sacramentum eukaristie, ultra hoc quod sciunt fundare in scriptura sacra, in antiquis sanctis doctoribus vel racionis efficacia, debilitant scolam Christi. Paratus sum tamen eis annuere si illud probaverint et non ante. Et credo quod scola possit aliunde occupari [p. 106] circa fidem scripture ut olim usque ad tubam novissimam, antequam rima ad fundandum illud in scriptura sacra in qua est omnis veritas sit reperta; et tamen, si foret utile, autor scripture non omississet illud inserere nec quatuor sancti doctores illud detegere neglexissent; unde oportet ecclesiam cavere ab huiusmodi novitate. Et colligi potest solucio ad tres predictas argucias; quomodo vel peccant in materia vel in forma. Et patet prima pars predicte sentencie.

ENGLISH: Nor are the [objections]{g:objectum} which the ignorant raise worthy of memory. For some argue: since the grace and power given to Christ in his humanity are only finite, it would derogate from the infinite divine power unless it could place the same, or as great, in anyone whatever.[^9] And the confirmation is that, just as Christ is of the same species with us, so too is any grace or power that he has in his humanity. It stands, therefore, that God by his omnipotence [can] increase power or grace in Peter until it has been made equal to the quality in Christ. Thirdly, it is confirmed by the fact that he can place any quality by itself, and in consequence in whatever subject he will; and since the soul of Peter is of as great capacity as the soul of Christ, God can make such qualities, placed in other spirits, inform [them].[^10] And they also argue this from the fact that the Word of God can assume any human nature whatever, separately or together; and when that is done there would be in it as much grace and power as Christ has in his humanity; therefore, without that union being posited, God can easily make qualities of this kind inform a man not assumed. But blessed be the Lord, who has freed us from these [quibbles]{g:argucia}. Now it has been said in the matter of the [105] incarnation that Christ could not assume another humanity, though he could most easily have done so had he willed; it has also been said that he, as much according to body as to soul, is of the same species with any other brother of his.[^11] And it has been said, thirdly, that by force of the hypostatic union the grace, power, and beauty or comeliness in Christ is of another species than any quality of like name can be in another man. And therefore the incarnation of Christ perfected the whole created universe, as much in individuals as in [universals]{g:universale}, since many such would not otherwise have been.[^12] As, therefore, an individual of one species, although it should grow infinitely, cannot be made wholly equal to an individual of another species, so it is with the grace of Christ, with the power [and] comeliness of his humanity. Now many qualities, as is clear of bodily beauty, originate from relation; therefore it would be the same to seek these qualities in another man and to seek another man who is at once God and man. Therefore they err who barely argue the specific identity of qualities from the specific identity of their subjects. For the principles of things must tend to greater unity than there is in their effects, since the highest unity is in the first principle and the highest diversity in its created universe. Nor am I speaking of a quality that can exist by itself; for it is certain that there is a nature of quality that cannot be unless it informs a substance, since it is nothing but a substance's being accidentally of such a kind.[^13] And those who dream of another [kind], out of the vanity of subtlety concerning the sacrament of the eucharist, beyond what they know how to ground in holy scripture, in the ancient holy doctors or in the force of reason, weaken the school of Christ. Yet I am ready to assent to them if they prove it, and not before. And I believe that the school could be otherwise occupied [106] about the faith of scripture, as of old, until the last trumpet, before a chink for grounding that in holy scripture, in which is all truth, would be found; and yet, if it were useful, the author of scripture would not have omitted to insert it, nor would the four holy doctors have neglected to [expose]{g:detegere} it; hence the church must beware of novelty of this kind.[^14] And the solution to the three aforesaid quibbles can be gathered: how they sin either in matter or in form. And the first part of the aforesaid teaching is clear.[^15]

### 06-a ¶5 · 106.14–106.28  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 106] Quantum ad miracula, patet quod cessant hodie in nostris episcopis, cum satis est post fidem in evangelio divulgatam ipsam impressam sanctis exhortacionibus confirmare. Cum igitur Deus non dat quicquam superflue, sequitur quod Deus non dat nostris prepositis, ut olim dedit discipulis, potestatem faciendi miracula; et per consequens nunc non habent; hec **autem** fuit racio; quare Apostolus non miraculose **sed** per viam naturalis medicine sanavit Thimoteum, bonum regimen iniungendo. Utere, inquit; modico vino propter stomachum tuum et frequentes tuas infirmitates prima Thim. V. **Sed** communicando cum infidelibus convertendis per miracula exercuit illam potestatem; ut patet Actuum ultimo copiose.

ENGLISH: As to miracles, it is clear that they cease today in our [bishops]{g:episcopus}, since it is enough, after the faith has been spread abroad in the gospel, to confirm it, once impressed, with holy exhortations. Since therefore God gives nothing superfluously, it follows that God does not give to our [superiors]{g:prepositi}, as he once gave to the disciples, the power of working miracles; and in consequence they do not now have [it]; and this was the reason why the Apostle healed Timothy not miraculously but by way of natural medicine, enjoining a good regimen: "Use," he says, "a little wine for thy stomach's sake and thy frequent infirmities", 1 Timothy 5.[^17] But in communicating with [unbelievers]{g:infidelis} who were to be converted he exercised that power through miracles, as is clear abundantly in the last [chapter] of Acts.[^18]

### 06-b ¶1 · 106.29–107.16  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 106] Item, si nostri episcopi habent tantam potestatem faciendi miracula eciam primitiva ut olim, cum sepe foret necessarium uti huiusmodi potestate, culpandi sunt ex ocio potestatis et discredendi tamquam fictores pompatici, cum nesciunt [p. 107] racione vel facto ostendere huiusmodi potestatem. Nam Mar. ultimo dicit Christus: Signa **autem** eos qui crediderint, hec sequentur: In nomine meo demonia eiicient, linguis loquentur novis, serpentes tollent, et si mortiferum quid biberint non eis nocebit. Et patet in cronicis quod apostoli habuerunt huiusmodi potestates, ut patet de Paulo Act. ultimo; et quod apostoli habuerunt potestatem tradendi perversos sathane, patet Act. V de Anania et Saphira, et prima Cor. V de fornicario, qui cognovit uxorem patris et prima Thim. primo de Ymeneo et Alexandro, quos Apostolus tradidit sathane propter blasphemiam.

ENGLISH: Again, if our [bishops]{g:episcopus} have so great a [power]{g:potestas} of working miracles, even the miracles of the beginning, as of old, then, since it would often be necessary to use such power, they are to be blamed for the idleness of their power and disbelieved as pompous pretenders, since they cannot [107] show such power by reason or by deed. For the apostles, just as they had power to free those vexed by Satan, so also had power to deliver to Satan sinners who infected the [church]{g:ecclesia}. For in the last [chapter] of Mark Christ says: "And these signs shall follow them that believe: In my name they shall cast out devils. They shall speak with new tongues. They shall take up serpents: and if they shall drink any deadly thing, it shall not hurt them."[^1] And it is clear in the chronicles that the apostles had such powers, as is clear of Paul in the last [chapter] of Acts;[^2] and that the apostles had the power to deliver the perverse to Satan is clear in Acts 5 of Ananias and Sapphira, and in 1 Corinthians 5 of the fornicator who knew his father's wife, and in 1 Timothy 1 of Hymenaeus and Alexander, whom the Apostle delivered to Satan on account of blasphemy.[^3]

### 06-b ¶2 · 107.18–108.24  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 107] Item, secundum Apostolum prima Cor. XII, 4 divisiones graciarum sunt, quas Dominus dividit singulis prout vult; et ille gracie vel sunt potestates vel ad potestates consequentes, igitur sicut non omnes parificantur in graciis, sic nec in potestate. Nec valet dicere quod nostri prelati in potestate precipua sunt pares apostolis, quia summa potestas concessa christiano stat in hoc, quod sit Dei filius adoptivus, cum aliis inde sequentibus, ut puta quod constituatur super omnia bona Dei. **Sed** ex dubio cuiquam prelato nostro simplex ydiota sibi subiectus in hoc ipsum exuperat, igitur non in hoc debet pompare de preeminencia potestatis, ymo si parificatur apostolis in potestate ministrandi sacramenta et aliunde apostoli ipsum excesserant, videtur quod [p. 108] tota aggregata potestas in apostolis fuit maior, ymo cum apostoli mediate eadem potestate suffecerunt instituere sacramenta et tam miraculose ac tam efficaciter ministrare eadem sacramenta, videtur quod apostoli habuerunt ampliorem et efficaciorem potestatem ad dispensandum ecclesie sacramenta. Nec ostendunt in signis efficacibus, quod habeant vel absconditam potestatem; cum igitur posset esse error in institucione et error in potestatis abusione, racione cuius Deus non conferat **sed** auferat huiusmodi potestatem, videtur signum luciferinum gloriari sine efficacia operis de huiusmodi potestate. Opus inquam meritorium de celo ecclesie instanter tractum in preteritum gigneret consolacionem et gaudium potentatus et daret fidem subditis quod Deus sibi communicat potestatem. Status **autem** et potestas magna viancium incuteret eis ex altitudine casus formidinem. Et patet ex secundo capitulo per multa media quomodo in qualibet potestatum novem patres in primitiva ecclesia excesserunt. Aliter enim prelati nostri nimis illuderent subtrahendo usum huiusmodi potestatum.

ENGLISH: Again, according to the Apostle, 1 Corinthians 12:4, "there are diversities of graces", which the Lord divides "to every one according as he will";[^4] and those graces either are powers or are consequent upon powers; therefore, just as not all are made equal in graces, so neither in power. Nor does it avail to say that our [prelates]{g:prelatus} are equal to the apostles in the principal power, because the highest power granted to a Christian consists in this, that he be an adopted son of God, with the other things that follow from it, for instance that he be set "over all the goods" of God.[^5] Now, for all anyone knows, a simple [unlettered man]{g:ydiota} subject to any prelate of ours surpasses him in this very thing;[^6] therefore he ought not to vaunt himself on preeminence of power in this; indeed, if he is made equal to the apostles in the power of ministering the sacraments, and the apostles had exceeded him in other respects, it seems that [108] the whole aggregated power in the apostles was greater; indeed, since by means of the same power the apostles sufficed to institute sacraments and to minister those same sacraments so miraculously and so effectively, it seems that the apostles had an ampler and more effective power for dispensing the sacraments to the church. Nor do they show by effective signs that they have even a hidden power; since, therefore, there could be error in the institution and error in the abuse of the power, by reason of which God would not confer but take away such power, it seems a Luciferian sign to glory in such power without the effect of the work. A meritorious work, I say, drawn down from heaven for the church at urgent asking, as in time past, would beget consolation and joy for the [potentate]{g:potentatus}, and would give faith to his subjects that God communicates power to him.[^7] But the great state and power of wayfarers would strike [fear]{g:formido} into them from the height of the fall.[^8] And it is clear from the second chapter, by many middle terms, how in each of the nine powers the fathers in the primitive church excelled.[^9] For otherwise our prelates would mock [us] too much by withholding the use of such powers.

### 06-b ¶3 · 108.25–110.19  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 108] Quantum ad auctorizacionem scripture sacre patet ex modo loquendi beati Augustini IV De Doctrina Christiana VI cap.; quod apostoli et evangeliste qui scripserunt libros canonicos erant eorum autores, licet solus Deus fuerit autor summus. ENGLISH: As to the [authorizing]{g:autorizare} of holy scripture, it is clear from the manner of speaking of blessed Augustine, *On Christian Doctrine* book 4, chapter 6, that the apostles and evangelists who wrote the canonical books were their authors, although God alone was the highest author.[^10] For otherwise no creature would have [authority]{g:auctoritas} to do anything. And that the priests of our time do not have so great authority or power of [canonizing]{g:canonizare} books such as are the books of the Bible appears from the general discourse given [109] *On the Truth and Authority of Scripture*.[^11] And it is clear from this: that to make their sayings equal to the authority of scripture would be the same as to assert that the Holy Spirit and our Jesus speaks these things in them. Now in Wisdom 1:5 it is said that "the Holy Spirit of discipline will flee from the deceitful"; therefore the [evidence]{g:evidencia} of authority perishes for them, [the evidence] that the Holy Spirit speaks his teachings in them. And since in Christ there was not "It is and It is not", as is clear in 2 Corinthians 1:17–18,[^12] now they often contradict the gospel and even themselves; therefore Christ's authority perishes for them as to those things in which they stray from it: for often the later contradicts the earlier. Let them therefore show these powers, conversions of [unbelievers]{g:infidelis} or healings of the sick, through their epistles, as was done with the writings of the apostles; and that is evidence that Christ speaks in them; indeed, as was said above, power is in general withdrawn for mortal [sin]. Now many of them fall into mortal [sin] through [ingratitude]{g:ingratitudo}; therefore it is necessary that the power, if they had any in the beginning, has been withdrawn for ingratitude. Again, if they are of equal authority with our authors, then, since they continually multiply their laws, [we should have] to learn [them] like the law of the faith of the scriptures, which is against many laws of the church, which prefer theology and [privilege]{g:privilegiare} its students;[^17] likewise the canon [laws]{g:ius} would then be destroyed, because that whole faculty would be pure theology, because [it would be] the word that God speaks in them as evidently as he spoke of old in the scribes of the faith of the scriptures.

Item, Apoc. ultimo propter auctoritatem libri propheta sic loquitur: Si quis apposuerit ad hec, apponet Deus super illum plagas scriptas in libro [p. 112] isto, et si quis diminuerit de verbis libri prophecie huius, auferet Deus partem eius de libro vite et de civitate sancta. In veteri vero testamento est aliud duplex testimonium: primo Deutero. IV, 2 per sanctum Moysen Deus precipit Israeli: Non addetis ad verbum quod vobis loquor neque auferetis ex eo. Et Proverb. XXX dixit vir cum quo Deus est ne addas quicquam verbis illius et arguaris et inveniaris mendax. Cum igitur nostri non doceant quod hec dicit Dominus, illud videretur nimis magna presumcio parificare dicta illa legi scripture. In cuius confirmacionem unus Papa revocat, suspendit vel abrogat epistolas et bullas alterius, **sed** facta sua falsificat. Ideo vel oportet falsificare totam fidem scripture vel omnes satrapas sequentes in auctoritate postponere. Hoc tamen papa habet pre aliis, quod sibi ut pape plus credi debet propter vite sanctitatem, cum debet ut sic conformius sequi Christum, quod si manifeste declinat ab hac semita, nullus est simulacior, mendacior et quidquid tunc locutus fuerit de mendacio plus suspectus. Ideo absit michi plus credere cuicumque Romano pontifici, quam quatuor sanctis doctoribus vel eis similibus, primo quia facta et scripta multorum paparum plus discordant a lege Dei; secundo quia non debemus credere verbis hominis nisi de quanto debemus credere quod prima veritas in ipso loquitur. Fides inquam est, si quid sit veritas, verbum Domini ipsam loquitur, libencius **autem** loquitur sapiencia in iusto sibi benivolo, quam in mendace sibi contrario. Tercio quia criminosus mentitur quicquid dixerit, non auctoritate [p. 113] vel racione sui, **sed** racione sermonis primi dicentis, credenda est veritas quam ipse balbuciet menciendo. Revera qui convictus est mendax in opere vel sermone, antequam purificetur quicquid dixerit est suspectum.

ENGLISH: Likewise, we are no more bound to believe those epistles than we are bound to believe that their author is [head]{g:caput} of the [church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans}; now we are not bound to believe this without [misgiving]{g:formido}, not only because we do not know whether he is dead, but because we do not know whether he has been [foreknown]{g:prescitus} or a [member]{g:membrum} of holy mother church.[^2] For so, when two popes have been elected contending over the [primacy]{g:primatus}, Christendom would be [perplexed]{g:perplexus}, and it would not suffice to believe in Christ;[^3] for I am not bound to believe a man's saying more than I am bound to believe holiness of him, or that God speaks in him; now I am not bound to believe holiness of life in our [superiors]{g:prepositi} equally as in the holy apostles; therefore I am not bound to believe their writings so much; for then I would be bound to believe that they are confirmed in the grace of predestination. Likewise, this [teaching]{g:sentencia} would infect the whole of holy scripture, since the Apostle, Galatians 1:8, boldly asserts that if even "an angel from heaven" should preach to them "besides that which" has been preached, "let him be anathema".[^4] Now many nations are "removed", with the Galatians, from the doctrine of the Apostle through the decretal epistles.[^5] Since, therefore, according to the [false writer]{g:falsigraphus} one must believe them as much as the apostle, it remains that one must disbelieve the apostle in this, since it is lawful for them, as they report, to dispense against the apostle, because they themselves [authorize]{g:autorizare} the writings of the apostle.[^6] Again, in the last [chapter] of the Apocalypse, for the sake of the [authority]{g:auctoritas} of the book, the prophet speaks thus: "If any man shall add to these things, God shall add unto him the plagues written in [112] this book. And if any man shall take away from the words of the book of this prophecy, God shall take away his part out of the book of life, and out of the holy city."[^7] And in the old testament there is another, twofold, testimony: first, Deuteronomy 4:2, through holy Moses God commands Israel: "You shall not add to the word that I speak to you, neither shall you take away from it." And in Proverbs 30 "the man" said, "with whom God is": "Add not any thing to his words, lest thou be reproved and found a liar."[^8] Since, therefore, our [prelates] do not teach that "thus saith the Lord", it would seem too great a presumption to make those sayings equal to the law of scripture. In confirmation of this, one [pope]{g:papa} revokes, [suspends]{g:suspendere} or abrogates the epistles and bulls of another, but [so] falsifies his acts.[^9] Therefore either one must falsify the whole faith of scripture, or put all the [satraps]{g:satrapa} who follow after [it] in authority.[^10] Yet the pope has this before others: that he, as pope, ought to be believed more on account of holiness of life, since as such he ought to follow Christ more conformably; but if he manifestly declines from this path, no one is more dissembling, more lying, and whatever he has then said is the more suspect of lying. Therefore far be it from me to believe any [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex} more than the four holy doctors or those like them: first, because the deeds and writings of many popes are more at odds with [God's law]{g:lex-dei}; second, because we ought not to believe the words of a man except in so far as we ought to believe that the first truth speaks in him. It is of faith, I say, that if anything is truth, the word of the Lord speaks it; and wisdom speaks more willingly in a just man well-disposed to her than in a liar contrary to her. Third, because a criminous man lies whatever he says; not by the authority [113] or by reason of him, but by reason of the word of the first speaker, is the truth to be believed which he stammers out while lying. Truly, he who has been convicted a liar in deed or word, whatever he says before he is purified is suspect.

### 06-c ¶3 · 113.6–114.12  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 113] Item, si quilibet papa haberet tantam potestatem edificandi ecclesiam et quicquid aliud faciendi cum sanctis apostolis et cum non excederet Mattheum, Johannem et ceteros citra Petrum, videtur quod, sicut omnes alii apostoli, ut fingitur, erant minoris auctoritatis quam Petrus, sic et quilibet papa ipsum succedens. Si igitur papa habet auctoritatem canonizandi epistolas Pauli, quod ipse non habuit, quia tunc foret per se autenticus, videtur quod quilibet papa sequens omnem apostolum citra Petrum auctoritate excesserat, et sic decretales essent maioris autoritatis quam quatuor evangelia et paris auctoritatis cum Petri epistolis. **Sed** deliramenta sunt ista, cum tota scriptura capit parilitatem auctoritatis a Spiritu Sancto, quem credimus eius autorem. Nec credo beatum Petrum fuisse maioris auctoritatis quam ceteros, quia ore, digito et auctoritate Domini locuti sunt et fecerant facta sua. Ideo auctoritas Dei non cepit magis vel minus in ipsis. Nec aliquis apostolus in quantum illa privata persona **sed** in quantum homo divinus; ut loquitur Augustinus IV De Doctrina christiana cap. VI; vel in quantum Christus participative, ut loquuntur multi sancti, ut sic (inquam) sunt omnes paris auctoritatis, unus ad unum et alius ad aliud, secundum quod Deus eis diviserat dona sua, et quicquid aliquis apostolus ut talis dixerit est paris auctoritatis cum [p. 114] quolibet. Et hinc Petrus ut solidum membrum sancte matris ecclesie gaudebat de Pauli iusta correpcione; non enim spectat ad membra corporis usurpare vel remurmurare contra aliena officia. Et sic videtur michi quod quilibet sacerdos debet esse sub non gradu potestatis et auctoritatis secularis, et pure in nomine Domini precipere sic vel sic facere; quod si sit antichristus, membrum dyaboli vel aliter Scarioth, non habet aliquam huiusmodi potestatem. Unde ut sepe repecii, pure de tanto debet christianus implere quod precipit.

ENGLISH: Again, if every pope had as great a [power]{g:potestas} of building the church, and of doing whatever else, as the holy apostles had, and since he would not exceed Matthew, John and the others short of Peter, it seems that, just as all the other apostles, as is feigned, were of less authority than Peter, so also is every pope who succeeds him.[^11] If then the pope has authority to [canonize]{g:canonizare} the epistles of Paul, which [Paul] himself did not have, because then he would be [authentic]{g:autenticus} of himself, it seems that every following pope had exceeded in authority every apostle short of Peter, and so the decretals would be of greater authority than the four gospels and of equal authority with Peter's epistles. But these are ravings, since the whole of scripture takes equality of authority from the Holy Spirit, whom we believe to be its author. Nor do I believe that blessed Peter was of greater authority than the rest, because they spoke and did their deeds by the mouth, the finger and the authority of the Lord. Therefore God's authority did not admit of more or less in them. Nor [does] any apostle [speak] as that private person, but as a divine man, as Augustine says, *On Christian Doctrine* book 4, chapter 6; or as Christ [does], [participatively]{g:participative}, as many saints say;[^12] as such, I say, they are all of equal authority, one for one thing and another for another, according as God had divided his gifts to them; and whatever any apostle as such has said is of equal authority with [114] [that of] any other. And hence Peter, as a solid member of holy mother church, rejoiced at Paul's just rebuke; for it does not belong to the members of the body to usurp or murmur against the offices of others.[^13] And so it seems to me that every [priest]{g:sacerdos} ought to be under no degree of secular power and authority, and to command purely in the name of the Lord that this or that be done;[^14] but if he is an antichrist, a member of the devil, or otherwise a Scarioth, he has no such power at all. Hence, as I have often repeated, a Christian ought to fulfil what he commands purely to that extent.

### 06-c ¶4 · 114.13–114.29  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 114] **Sed** contra illud arguitur per hoc quod iuxta dicta nullus apostolus vel predestinatus citra Christum est maioris auctoritatis quam quilibet, quod confunderet gradus ecclesie et iura papalia, cum sicut Petrus et ceteri apostoli fuerunt paris auctoritatis, sic debet esse papa et quilibet sanctus episcopus, ymo quilibet predestinatus. Ubi igitur foret papalis sentencia de maioritate et obediencia? Similiter, iuxta dicta de sanctitate debet capi auctoritas, **sed** unus est reliquo sanccior, igitur maioris auctoritatis quam evangelium. Nec valet referre omnem auctoritatem pure ad Deum, quia sunt gradus auctoritatum, cum unus constitutus est sub alterius potestate, aliter enim periret regula obediencie humane et maioritas auctoritatis ecclesie.

ENGLISH: But against this [it is argued by appeal to]{g:arguitur-per} the fact that, according to what has been said, no apostle or [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} [person] short of Christ is of greater authority than any other, which would confound the grades of the church and the papal [laws]{g:ius}, since, as Peter and the other apostles were of equal authority, so must the pope and every holy bishop be, indeed every predestinate [person]. Where, then, would the papal teaching on [greatness]{g:majoritas} and obedience be?[^15] Likewise, according to what has been said, authority must be taken from holiness; now one is holier than another; therefore of greater authority than the gospel.[^16] Nor does it avail to refer all authority purely to God, because there are grades of authorities, since one is set under the power of another; for otherwise the rule of human obedience and the greatness of the church's authority would perish.

### 06-d ¶2 · 115.11–117.15  (9 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 115] Ad primum videtur michi, ut sepe tetigi, quod omnis clericus debet pure inniti auctoritati divine, dimittendo auctoritatem gentilem adinventam regibus gencium; qui infideliter blasfemaverant quod sunt Deus, ut patet de Nabugodonosor Danielis IV; et hinc sumpserunt sibi nomen auctoritatis et capitalis dominii, Christus **autem** docet suos apostolos gentilitatem istam aufugere, ut patet Luce XXII et Matth. XX. Unde quia aliquid licencie remansit in ista gentilitate, ideo remansit cum civilitate verbum auctoritatis, per quod ego intelligo superioritatem potestatis civilis. **Sed** postquam dotata est ecclesia, introducta sunt irreligiose nomen iurisdicionis et auctoritatis cum similibus, que civilitatem sapiunt. Et sic conceditur quod unus episcopus est reliquo maioris potestatis quam quidam vocant auctoritatem. Referendo **autem** auctoritatem ad Deum et negando auctoritatem **sed** promulgacionem inesse formaliter creature neganda foret in apostolis auctoritatis maioritas. Secundo vero intelligendo auctoritatem in quantum dicit Dei preconem habere potestatem ad promulgandum eius sentenciam, sic omnes apostoli vel evangeliste [p. 116] sunt paris auctoritatis, cum ipsa non potest maius aut minus suscipere. Ad quem sensum scriptura sacra est paris auctoritatis utrobique. **Sed** tercio michi infundabiliter loquuntur quidam de auctoritate, ut dicit maioritatem sanctitatis vel carismatum multitudinis, et quia illud est nobis incognitum, transferunt auctoritatem gentiliter ad ordinacionem humanam, ut quotlibet gradus ponunt in papa, archiepiscopo, rectore et simplici sacerdote, cum quotlibet intercisionibus, que quia auctoritate divina bene possunt fieri secundum divisiones graduum, discrecionis et potestatis quam ipse distribuit, ideo sollempnizantur instituciones humane, quamvis ordinacioni divine contrarie, et observantur amplius quam lex Dei. **Sed** loquatur sic de auctoritate, qui in scriptura sacra sciverit hoc fundare. Leges **autem** papales de maioritate et obediencia possunt intelligi, quando institucio humana de preposituris hominum correspondet divino iudicio, sic videtur quod proporcionaliter ut quis ponitur in maiori preeminencia quoad homines, sic ponatur realiter in maiori preeminencia virtutum et gracie quoad Deum; quod quia nunquam vel raro contingit, nec superest (ut credo) ad illud fundandum scripture auctoritas, ideo taceo quoad leges et facta, dicens quod illud bene potest fieri de Dei potencia absoluta, sciens quod sic non fuit inter Christi sacerdotes in ecclesia primitiva. Et patet in parte quomodo dicendum est ad primum obiectum. Ad secundum patet ex dictis, quomodo ex sanctitate ut signo remoto debet capi duplex auctoritas, et non ut de equata mensura, quia tunc diminueretur vel extingueretur ut sanctitas [p. 117] promulgantis, quod est falsum, cum infideles ut Balaam, Cayfas et ceteri dampnabiles prophetarunt. Sanctitas tamen est signum auctoritatis divine et firmitatis inaugmentabilis auctoritatis humane, et sic gradus prepositure et auctoritatis civilis nuncupate surrepserunt cum dotacione et temporalium cupiditate non solum in seculares clericos **sed** in fratres, cum ipsi constituunt tribunos, centuriones, decanos et alios sicut Judei, qui servarunt cerimonias veteris testamenti, ut notatum est Exod. XVIII. **Sed** servata vera humilitate octuplici, de qua superius, omnia ista superfluerent sicut superfluissent in sanctis apostolis, et sic defectus instruccionis virtutis est causa, quare ista sunt modo utilia.

ENGLISH: To the first it seems to me, as I have often touched on, that every [cleric]{g:clericus} ought to rely purely on divine [authority]{g:auctoritas}, leaving aside the gentile authority devised for the kings of the gentiles, who [unbelievingly]{g:infidelis} blasphemed that they are God, as is clear of Nebuchadnezzar in Daniel 4;[^3] and hence they took to themselves the name of authority and of chief [lordship]{g:dominium}. But Christ teaches his apostles to flee that [paganism]{g:gentilitas}, as is clear in Luke 22 and Matthew 20.[^4] Hence, because something of [licence]{g:licencia} remained in that paganism, therefore the word "authority" remained with [civil standing]{g:civilitas}, by which I understand the superiority of civil power. But after the church was endowed, the names of jurisdiction and of authority, with others like them that savour of civil standing, were irreligiously brought in. And so it is granted that one [bishop]{g:episcopus} is of greater power than another, which some call authority. But, referring authority to God, and denying that authority, rather than promulgation, is formally in a creature, greatness of authority would have to be denied in the apostles.[^5] And secondly, understanding authority as it signifies that God's herald has power to promulgate his [teaching]{g:sentencia}, in this way all the apostles or evangelists [116] are of equal authority, since it cannot admit of greater or less. In this sense holy scripture is of equal authority on both sides.[^6] But thirdly some speak of authority, groundlessly in my view, as it signifies [greatness]{g:majoritas} of holiness or a multitude of charisms; and because that is unknown to us, they transfer authority in gentile fashion to human [ordinance]{g:ordinacio}, so that they posit any number of grades in pope, archbishop, rector and simple priest, with any number of subdivisions; and because these can well be made by divine authority according to the divisions of the grades of discernment and of power which he distributes, therefore human institutions are solemnized, although contrary to divine ordinance, and are observed more than [God's law]{g:lex-dei}.[^7] But let him speak so of authority who knows how to ground it in holy scripture. And the papal [laws]{g:ius} on greatness and obedience can be understood [to hold] when the human institution of men's [offices of superior]{g:prepositura} corresponds to the divine judgement; [then] it seems that, in proportion as someone is set in greater preeminence as regards men, so he should really be set in greater preeminence of virtues and of grace as regards God.[^8] And because this never or rarely happens, and there is not, as I believe, any authority of scripture left to ground it, therefore I am silent as to the laws and the deeds, saying that this can well be done by God's [absolute power]{g:potencia-absoluta}, knowing that it was not so among Christ's priests in the primitive church. And so it is clear in part how one must answer the first [objection]{g:objectum}. To the second it is clear from what has been said how a twofold authority must be taken from holiness as from a remote sign, and not as from an equal measure; because then [authority] would be diminished or extinguished as the holiness [117] of the one who promulgates [is], which is false, since unbelievers such as Balaam, Caiaphas and other damnable men prophesied.[^9] Yet holiness is a sign of divine authority and of the firmness, which cannot be increased, of human authority;[^10] and so the grades of the office of superior and of what is called civil authority crept in, with the endowment and the cupidity for [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, not only among secular clerics but among the friars, since they appoint tribunes, centurions, deans and others, like the Jews who kept the ceremonies of the old testament, as is noted in Exodus 18.[^11] But if the true eightfold humility, of which [I spoke] above, were kept, all these things would be superfluous, as they would have been superfluous in the holy apostles; and so the lack of instruction in virtue is the cause why these things are now useful.[^12]

### 06-d ¶3 · 117.16–118.5  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 117] Ad tercium patet quod potestas ordinis est potestas regiminis, quam quilibet habet in quantum presbiter; quam solum cognoscunt qui noverunt universalia, et illa distinguitur a potestate ordinis sive regiminis excedente gradum remississimum potestatis. Et sic idem episcopus habet multas potestates ordinis sive regiminis, ut patet superius. Nec prelatus dat curato novam potestatem regiminis nisi sit fidelis preco Dei prius dantis; ymo videtur michi quod sacerdos prius fuit debitor eidem populo, cui per episcopum suum preponitur, cum apostolus Rom. I, 14 confitetur se esse Grecis Latinis et barbaris debitorem, verumtamen ex novo voto et munere recepto est amplius obligatus. Ritus **autem** gentilis ecclesie in hac parte videtur dare licenciam ad congregandum commodum temporale et inhabili quoad Deum, et talis iurisdicio non frenata per scripturam maxime induceret sectam Antichristi. Si igitur nostri habent potestates episcopales pares [p. 118] cum apostolis, ostendunt se dampnabiles, quod ociando tantum illis potestatibus abutuntur. Si **autem** potestates terrenas habuerint supra apostolos, hoc false et antichristiane habent a principe mundi huius.

ENGLISH: To the third it is clear that the power of order is a [power]{g:potestas} of rule, which each has inasmuch as he is a [presbyter]{g:presbiter}; which only those recognize who have known [universals]{g:universale}; and that power is distinguished from the power of order or of rule that exceeds the most remiss degree of power.[^13] And so the same bishop has many powers of order or of rule, as is clear above. Nor does a [prelate]{g:prelatus} give a [curate]{g:curatus} a new power of rule unless he is a faithful herald of God, who gave [it] before; indeed, it seems to me that the priest was already a debtor to the same people over whom he is set by his bishop, since the apostle, Romans 1:14, confesses himself a debtor to Greeks, Latins and barbarians;[^14] nevertheless, by the new vow and the charge received, he is the more bound. But the [rite]{g:ritus} of the gentile church in this matter seems to give licence for gathering temporal advantage even to one [unqualified]{g:inhabilis} as regards God; and such jurisdiction, not bridled by scripture, would bring in the [sect]{g:secta} of Antichrist above all.[^15] If, therefore, our [bishops] have episcopal powers equal [118] with the apostles, they show themselves damnable, in that by idling they so far abuse those powers. But if they have had earthly powers above the apostles, they have this falsely and antichristianly from "the prince of this world".[^16]
