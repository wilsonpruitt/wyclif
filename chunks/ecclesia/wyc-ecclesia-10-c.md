# *De ecclesia*, chapter 10, part 3: the answers from the Lord's prayer, the parable and the jubilee; the clergy as ravens

## ¶1 · 212.1–212.19

> [p. 212] Ex istis patet tercio, cum erga omnem punitum oportet punientem servare caritatis amorem ac difficile vel impossibile est viantem caritative persequi fratrem suum contencione, excommunicacione vel occisione in visceribus caritatis, lex Christi est certissima qui, inquit, aufert que tua sunt, ne repetas, Luce VI°, 30 (quod alias diffuse exposui); Benedicite persequentibus vos, benedicite et nolite maledicere, ad Rom. XII°, 14, et si quis te percusserit ad maxillam dexteram, prebe ei et alteram Matthei V°, 39. Ille autem qui nescit erga inimicum quem cruciat servare regulas caritatis, non licet declinare ab istis consiliis. Unde Archidiaconus super XXXIII, q. V. Excomunicatorum, dicit quod non licet quemquam occidere nisi ex dileccione occisi preponderante dileccionem cuiuslibet temporalis, multo magis hoc astrueret purus theologus. Unde desidia predicacionis illius veritatis catholice fuit causa occisionis duplicis multi populi; remittat Deus episcopis qui ipsam dominis intrepide predicarent.

From these it is clear, thirdly (since toward everyone who is punished the one punishing must keep the love of [charity]{g:caritas}, and it is difficult or impossible for [one on the road]{g:viantes} charitably to pursue his brother by litigation, excommunication or killing in the bowels of charity), [that] the law of Christ is most certain: "of him that taketh away thy goods, ask them not again", Luke 6:30 (which I have expounded at length elsewhere); "Bless them that persecute you: bless, and curse not", Romans 12:14; and "if one strike thee on thy right cheek, turn to him also the other", Matthew 5:39.[^1] And he who does not know how to keep the rules of charity toward the enemy whom he torments may not turn aside from these counsels. Hence the Archdeacon, on C. 33 q. 5, *Excommunicatorum*, says that it is not lawful to kill anyone except out of love for the one killed outweighing the love of any temporal thing; much more would a pure theologian assert this.[^2] Hence sloth in preaching that catholic truth has been the cause of the double killing of much people; may God forgive the bishops, who should have preached it fearlessly to the lords.[^3]

## ¶2 · 212.20–214.3

> [p. 212] Ex istis patet solucio ad primum obiectum de quinta peticione oracionis dominice; nam secundum Augustinum, libro II°, De Sermone Domini in monte c° X°, peticio intelligitur de spirituali debito (ut alias diffuse exposui): Debitum, inquit, peccatum dici manifestum est. Non ergo quisquis hic urgetur pecuniam debitori dimittere, sed quecunque alius in eum peccaverit, quia tunc non reciperet a volente dare debitum, nec peteret a potente avaro qui inops animo inexcusabiliter detinet alienum. Et tum conclusio Augustini est: Si modeste ac leniter omnimodo quis egerit, ut [p. 213] sibi restituatur a tali, non tam intendens fructum pecunie, quam ut hominem corrigat cui indubie perniciosum est detinere, cum habeat unde reddat; non solum non peccabit sed proderit plurimum eciam debitori; aliter enim dum alienam pecuniam lucrum vult facere, dampnum fidei pacietur. Cum ergo sensus pretensus huius privilegii excludit hanc elemosinam, cum protegit indifferenter potentem et nolentem aliena reddere quam eciam inpotentem, manifestum est quod ponit obstaculum regulis caritatis. Per hec patet responsio ad secundum, cum dictum Christi intelligitur de debito culpe cuius satisfactoriam solucionem omnis christianus tenetur humiliter iudici supremo remittere. Si enim intelligeretur de corporali debito, tunc omnes religiosi possessionati et clerici tenerentur ex privilegio Christi quo amplius obligantur eius consiliis et preceptis, remittere et non repetere omne eis debitum corporale; et tamen dicitur quod sine misericordia exigunt eciam ab inpotentibus tale debitum per exacciones seculares et ecclesiasticas censuras ardencius quam domini seculares. Non ergo est eis privilegium fovere hostes ecclesie, ne sibi satisfaciant, cum tamen sufficiant, quia (ut alias dixi) Deus non potest licenciare hominem, ut servet debitum sine hoc quod satisfaciat saltem Deo, quia nisi satisfaciat restitucione vel meritoria punicione, est dampnandus. Deus autem non potest licenciare hominem ad peccandum; hoc autem foret privilegium tali ecclesie quod haberet a suis patronis seculares divicias ad ministrandum fugitivis impotentibus se iuvare, et (quantum facultas suppetit) pro ipsis restituere, quia hoc sonaret utrimque in opus misericordie, non autem defensio cuiuscunque fugitivi quantumlibet excomunicati, [p. 214] quantumcunque defensio cadat fugitivo ad dampnum anime et iniuriatis ad detrimentum, nec non ad perturbacionem regni atque ecclesie.

From these the solution to the first [objection]{g:objectum}, about the fifth petition of the Lord's prayer, is clear; for according to Augustine, *On the Lord's Sermon on the Mount*, book 2, chapter 10, the petition is understood of the spiritual debt (as I have expounded at length elsewhere): "That debt", he says, "is called sin is manifest. No one, therefore, is here pressed to forgive money to a debtor, but whatever another has sinned against him"; for then he would not receive the debt from one willing to pay it, nor seek it from a miser who is able [to pay], who, needy in spirit, inexcusably keeps back what is another's.[^4] And then Augustine's conclusion is: "If anyone acts in every way modestly and gently that it be [213] restored to him by such a man, not so much aiming at the fruit of the money as to correct the man, for whom it is without doubt ruinous to keep it back when he has the means to repay, he will not only not sin but will greatly profit even the debtor; for otherwise, while he wishes to make another's money his gain, he will suffer the loss of faith."[^5] Since, therefore, the pretended sense of this [privilege]{g:privilegium} excludes this [alms]{g:elemosina}, since it protects without distinction the man who is able and unwilling to return what is another's as well as the man who is unable, it is manifest that it puts an obstacle to the rules of charity.[^6] By these the answer to the second is clear, since Christ's saying is understood of the debt of guilt, whose satisfactory payment every Christian is bound humbly to remit to the supreme judge. For if it were understood of bodily debt, then all the [possessioner]{g:possessionati} [religious]{g:religiosi} and the clerics would be bound, by the privilege of Christ by which they are the more bound to his counsels and precepts, to remit and not to reclaim every bodily debt owed them; and yet it is said that without mercy they exact such debt even from those unable [to pay], by secular exactions and ecclesiastical [censures]{g:censura}, more ardently than the secular lords.[^7] It is not, therefore, a privilege for them to shelter the enemies of the church so that they do not satisfy them, although they are able; for (as I have said elsewhere) God cannot license a man to keep back a debt without his making satisfaction at least to God, because unless he makes satisfaction by restitution or by meritorious punishment, he is to be damned.[^8] Now God cannot license a man to sin. But this would be a privilege for such a church: that it should have from its [patrons]{g:patronus} secular riches to minister to fugitives unable to help themselves, and (as far as its means allow) to make restitution for them, because this would tend on both sides to a work of mercy; but not the defence of any fugitive whatever, however excommunicated, [214] however much the defence falls out to the fugitive's loss of soul and to the detriment of those he has injured, and also to the disturbance of the [kingdom]{g:regnum} and of the [church]{g:ecclesia}.[^9]

## ¶3 · 214.4–214.22

> [p. 214] Ad tercium dicitur quod tam sabbatizacio pro anno septimo quam remissio pro anno iubileo fuit valde racionabilis pro tempore suo, nunc autem pro tempore legis gracie quando colimus octonarium, debemus habere amorem fertiliorem: sicut enim septenarius numerus virginitatis habet solum unitatem pro parte aliquota, octonarius autem habet unum, duo et quattuor que septem constituunt, sic patres veteris testamenti erant in caritate plus steriles quam patres sub lege gracie qui colunt ex integro septenarium; qui signat unici Dei cultum et preter hoc unionem Christi et ecclesie que est unitas septenario superaddita in numerum priorem, redundans quoad partes aliquotas. Et sic debemus nos tempore legis gracie habere diffusiorem amorem et ampliorem continenciam, quam habuerunt patres veteris testamenti, quia nedum habemus contribules, sed fratres germanos singulos christianos, cum omnes nati sumus spiritualiter de eodem patre Christo et eadem matre ecclesia.

To the third the answer is that both the sabbath-keeping for the seventh year and the remission for the jubilee year were very reasonable for their time; but now, for the time of the [law of grace]{g:lex gracie}, when we keep the eighth, we must have a more fruitful love. For as the number seven, [the number] of virginity, has only unity as an aliquot part, while the eight has one, two and four, which make up seven, so the fathers of the old testament were more barren in charity than the fathers under the law of grace, who keep the seven in its entirety; which signifies the worship of the one God and, besides this, the union of Christ and the church, which is a unity added to the seven into a prior number, overflowing as regards its aliquot parts.[^10] And so we, in the time of the law of grace, ought to have a more widespread love and a larger continence than the fathers of the old testament had; for we have not only countrymen, but every single Christian as a full brother, since we are all born spiritually of the same father, Christ, and the same mother, the church.[^11]

## ¶4 · 214.23–215.10

> [p. 214] Et rogo innitentem isti evidencie notare, primo quomodo nos sacerdotes Christi debemus esse magis misericordes et magis religiosi quam sacerdotes veteris testamenti. Notet secundo quomodo non licuit sacerdotibus Aaronitis acquirere sibi redditus vel seculare dominium, sed in partem dominii forent de porcione sibi limitata a domino contentati (ut patet Ezechielis XLIV°, 28); alie autem tribus impetrantes possessiones a suis contribulibus non exheredarent eos perpetuo, sed in anno iubileo restituerent diutissime impetrata. Et notet tercio per iuxtaposicionem [p. 215] conversacionis cleri nostri eciam religiosi, si sit proporcionalis correspondencia utrobique. Dicitur enim quod clerus eciam vocatus religiosus exheredat multos de fratribus eciam pro suo perpetuo, vocantes hoc perquisitum Christi patrimonium, cum tamen Christus omnino exproprietarie pendebat nudus in cruce, scisso velo templi, ad denotandum Judeis et gentibus quod spiritualis hereditas est simpliciter appetenda et terrena possessio contempnenda (ut expositum est... cap. libri sexti).

And I ask the one who relies on this [evidence]{g:evidencia} to note, first, how we [priests]{g:sacerdos} of Christ ought to be more merciful and more religious than the priests of the old testament. Let him note, secondly, how it was not lawful for the Aaronite priests to acquire for themselves [revenues]{g:reditus} or secular [lordship]{g:dominium}, but for their share of lordship they were to be content with the portion [assigned]{g:limitare} them by the Lord (as is clear from Ezekiel 44:28); and the other tribes, [obtaining]{g:impetrare} possessions from their countrymen, were not to disinherit them in perpetuity, but in the jubilee year were to restore what had been obtained, however long before.[^12] And let him note, thirdly, by setting beside [these] the [215] [manner of life]{g:conversacio} of our [clergy]{g:clerus}, the religious too, whether there is a proportional correspondence on both sides. For it is said that the clergy, even the [so-called]{g:vocatus} religious, disinherit many of their brethren, even in perpetuity for themselves, calling this acquisition Christ's patrimony; although Christ hung naked on the cross wholly [without property]{g:exproprietarie}, the veil of the temple being rent, to signify to Jews and gentiles that the spiritual inheritance is to be sought [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} and earthly possession despised (as has been expounded in chapter [ ] of the sixth book).[^13]

## ¶5 · 215.11–216.11

> [p. 215] Tunc enim essemus altilia de puro frumento, pasta ad exemplariter nutriendum filios, ut fecerunt sancti patres, vice quorum fungimur saltem in nomine, ut congregemus pecunias, non ut sanemus animas. Modo autem pervertimur in corvos perfidos, pastos de cadavere extra navem ecclesie, et tamquam aves rapacissime in altum ascendimus, non ad pericula et commoda spiritualia ecclesie speculandum sed lumina habemus acutissima, membra velocissima et rostra retentissima ad predandum; et cum beatus Gregorius, pastor precipuus, dicit XXI° Moralium, cap. IV°, satis asperum verbum atque terrificum: Festina, inquit, consolacio bonorum est consideratus finis malorum. Dum enim ex eorum interitu malum conspiciunt quod evadunt, bene existimant quidquid adversum in hac vita paciuntur. Eant ergo nunc reprobi et delectacionum suarum desideria compleant: in finis sui dampnacione sensuri sunt, quia mortem male viventes amaverunt. Transitorio autem verbere affligantur electi ut a pravitate flagella corrigant quos paterna pietas ad hereditatem servat. Modo etenim flagellatur iustus, [p. 216] et verbere discipline corrigitur, quia ad eterne hereditatis patrimonium preparatur. In suis autem voluptatibus relaxatur iniustus, et quanto eis temporalia bona suppetunt, tanto eterna denegantur. Vituli, inquit, qui mactandi sunt in liberioribus pastibus relinquuntur; non quin stat vere divitem, in temporalibus affluentem, regnum celorum acquirere, sed tunc oportet Deum miraculose agere quod aput homines est impossibile, convertendo mundo divitem ab affeccione temporalium et carnalium voluptate, et omnino in extremis suis finire in evangelica paupertate (ut patet Luce XIV°).

For then we should be fatlings of pure wheat, fed so as to nourish the children by example, as the holy fathers did, in whose stead we act, at least in name, to gather money, not to heal souls.[^14] But now we are perverted into faithless ravens, fed on carrion outside the ship of the church, and like most rapacious birds we mount on high, not to watch for the dangers and spiritual advantages of the church; but we have the sharpest eyes, the swiftest limbs and the most retentive beaks for preying. And this while blessed Gregory, the chief shepherd, says in *Moralia* 21, chapter 4, a word harsh and terrifying enough: "A swift consolation of the good," he says, "is the end of the wicked, considered. For when from their destruction they see the evil that they escape, they think well of whatever adversity they suffer in this life. Let the reprobate go now, then, and fulfil the desires of their delights: in the damnation of their end they will feel that, living ill, they loved death. But let the elect be afflicted with a passing stroke, so that the scourges may correct from wickedness those whom fatherly kindness keeps for the inheritance. For now the just man is scourged, [216] and corrected by the stroke of discipline, because he is being prepared for the patrimony of the eternal inheritance. But the unjust man is let loose in his pleasures, and the more temporal goods are at hand for them, the more the eternal are denied. The calves," he says, "that are to be slaughtered are left in freer pastures."[^15] Not that it is impossible for a man truly rich, abounding in temporal things, to acquire the kingdom of heaven; but then God must act miraculously [to do] what with men is impossible, converting the rich man from the world, from affection for temporal things and from carnal pleasure, and [making him] in his last days end altogether in evangelical poverty (as is clear from Luke 14).[^16]

## ¶6 · 216.12–216.26

> [p. 216] Unde quia probabile est paucos mundi divites sic facere, ideo argumentum topicum est quod multi tales moriuntur in peccato finalis inpenitencie. Ideo dicit Gregorius (ubi supra): Negantur electis in hac vita bona terrena, quia et egris quibus spes vivendi est nequaquam a medico cuncta que appetunt conceduntur. Dantur autem reprobis bona que in hac vita appetunt, quia desperatis egris omne quod desiderant non negatur. Attendamus ergo ad doctrinam Christi in opere et sermone et videbimus quod filios suos carissimos non sic dotat, et tum nos faciendo elemosinas debemus capere exemplum a Christo. Et patet quod dicte raciones super pietate milvina pretense non concludunt nec rectificant sed excludunt privilegia supradicta.

Hence, because it is probable that few of the world's rich men do so, it is therefore a [topical argument]{g:argumentum topicum} that many such die in the sin of final impenitence. Therefore Gregory says (in the place cited above): "Earthly goods are denied to the elect in this life, because to the sick, too, who have hope of living, by no means is everything they crave granted by the physician. But to the reprobate are given the goods they crave in this life, because to the sick who are despaired of, nothing that they desire is denied."[^17] Let us attend, then, to the [doctrine]{g:doctrina} of Christ in deed and word, and we shall see that he does not so endow his dearest sons; and then we, in giving alms, ought to take example from Christ. And it is clear that the said arguments, alleged on the score of a kite's piety, do not [infer]{g:concludere} nor justify but exclude the privileges mentioned above.[^18]

## Notes

[^1]: **C/S.** *Patet tercio*: the third conclusion drawn (after 210.5 and 211.4); A in the margin *Conclusio* (212.1); side-note 212.1. The *cum* clause is causal; what is clear is that the law of Christ is most certain. *Viantem*: GLOSSARY *viantes*. Luke 6:30 and Romans 12:14 are quoted as the Vulgate; at Matthew 5:39 Wyclif has *ad maxillam dexteram, prebe ei* (Vulgate *in dexteram maxillam tuam, praebe illi*; B *in maxilla dextera*, 212.9), with DR's sense. "Elsewhere": not identified (LEDGER E10c-4).

[^2]: **S/T.** "The Archdeacon": the canonists' name for Guido de Baysio, author of the *Rosarium* on the Decretum; to be checked (LEDGER E10c-1). *Excomunicatorum* is Loserth's correction (codd. *Excommunicantes*, 212.13). The canon is C. 23 q. 5 c. 47 (Urban II to Godfrey of Lucca; Friedberg I; PL 187), not C. 33 as printed (LEDGER E10c-2). AA₁ read *preponderantem* (212.15). *Purus theologus*: a theologian pure and simple, as against a canonist.

[^3]: **C.** *Occisionis duplicis*: perhaps the killing of body and of soul, of the slain and of the slayers; the text does not say. *Predicarent*: what they ought to have done, "who should have preached". The bloodshed meant is presumably that at Westminster (10-a); to be checked (LEDGER E10a-5).

[^4]: **S/C.** Augustine, *De sermone Domini in monte* II, cited by Wyclif as chapter 10: the place is II.8.28 (PL 34:1281–82), which Wyclif condenses; Loserth: Opp. III, pt. 2, pp. 214, 211 (LEDGER E10c-3). A in the margin *Triplex solucio* (212.20), *Augustinus* (212.22), *Dimittere debita quid est?* (212.24); side-note 212.21. *Primum obiectum*: the first confirmation, from Matthew 6:12 (10-b, 207.10). *Non ergo quisquis … urgetur*: "no one is pressed"; the quotation may run on to *alienum*; we close it at *peccaverit*. *Potente avaro qui inops animo*: a miser who can pay and is poor only in spirit. "Elsewhere": not identified (LEDGER E10c-4).

[^5]: **S/T/C.** *Omnimodo* is Loserth's correction from Augustine (codd. *omnino quis*, 212.30). A reads *intendes*, A₁ *intendendo* (213.1). AA₁ lack *aliter enim … pacietur* (213.5); the print is translated. *Dampnum fidei pacietur*: we take the subject to be the debtor who keeps another's money as his gain.

[^6]: **C.** *Hanc elemosinam*: the correction of the debtor, which is a spiritual alms. *Sensus pretensus*: the sense claimed for the privilege. Side-note 213.11.

[^7]: **C/G.** *Ad secundum*: the parable of Matthew 18 (10-b, 207.16). *Satisfactoriam solucionem … remittere*: to leave its satisfying payment to God the judge. *Religiosi possessionati*: the endowed orders (GLOSSARY). *Ex privilegio Christi*: Christ's own "privilege", his counsels and precepts; Wyclif turns the word against the privilege. A in the margin *Conclusio* (213.21).

[^8]: **C.** *Ne sibi satisfaciant, cum tamen sufficiant*: we take the fugitives as the subject, "so that they do not satisfy them [their creditors], though they can"; *sibi* could instead be the monks. "As I have said elsewhere": not identified (LEDGER E10c-7).

[^9]: **T/C.** *Deus autem*: Loserth suggests *Deus enim* (213.27); the print is translated, *autem* bringing a new premiss ("now"). *Utrimque*: for fugitive and creditor alike.

[^10]: **C.** A in the margin, on the sabbatical and jubilee years, that they were valid in their time and are not now (214.4); side-note 214.4. *Octonarium*: the eight, set against the seven of the old law. *Pars aliquota*: an exact divisor; seven has only one, eight has one, two and four, whose sum is seven. *Qui signat*: the antecedent may be the seven or the eight. *In numerum priorem*: so printed; the sense looks for a greater number (eight), and "prior" is translated as it stands; to be weighed.

[^11]: **T/C.** *Continenciam* is Loserth's emendation (codd. *continuum*, 214.18); "continence" may here mean capacity, what love holds. A reads *contribulos* (214.20). *Contribules*: "countrymen", as DR at Leviticus 25:17. *Fratres germanos*: full brothers, of one father and one mother.

[^12]: **S/C.** A in the margin *Status cleri. Nota tria* (214.23); B, second hand, *Nota pro sacerdotibus*; side-note 214.23. *Innitentem isti evidencie*: the one who leans on this evidence (the Levitical argument). Ezekiel 44:28 is cited, not quoted: "they shall have no inheritance, I am their inheritance: neither shall you give them any possession in Israel". *Diutissime impetrata*: things obtained, however long held.

[^13]: **T/C/S.** *Vocatus*: "so-called" (GLOSSARY). *Pro suo perpetuo*: as their own for ever. *Perquisitum*: an acquisition, property gained otherwise than by inheritance, set against the disinherited. B reads *ex proprietate* for *exproprietarie* (215.6). The veil: Matthew 27:51. The chapter number is wanting in the codices, which leave a gap (Loserth, 215.9–10); "the sixth book" may be the sixth book of Wyclif's *Summa*; to be checked (LEDGER E10c-5). Side-notes 214.32, 215.3.

[^14]: **C/S.** *Tunc enim*: then, if the correspondence held. *Altilia de puro frumento*: the fatlings of Gregory's figure (10-b, 208–09), fattened on pure wheat. *Vice quorum fungimur … ut congregemus pecunias*: we hold their place in name only, and for money. The ravens outside the ship: the raven of the ark, "which went forth and did not return" (Genesis 8:7). *Speculandum*: to keep watch, as the watchman does.

[^15]: **S/C.** Gregory, *Moralia* 21.4, as Wyclif cites it; Loserth: Opp. I, p. 681 (LEDGER E10c-6). A in the margin *Gregorius* (215.20) and *Nota de tribulacione bonorum et prosperitate malorum in hac vita* (215.22). *Reprobi* "the reprobate" and *electi* "the elect" are Gregory's words, not *presciti* and *predestinati*.

[^16]: **S/C.** *Quod aput homines est impossibile*: compare Luke 18:27, "The things that are impossible with men are possible with God". *Convertendo mundo divitem*: *mundo* is taken as "from the world"; it may stand for *mundi*, "the world's rich man" (cf. *mundi divites*, 216.12). "Luke 14": perhaps 14:33, "every one of you that doth not renounce all that he possesseth cannot be my disciple".

[^17]: **C/S.** *Argumentum topicum*: a probable, not a demonstrative, argument (GLOSSARY): probable because few rich men are so converted. Gregory, *Moralia* 21.4, the same place (LEDGER E10c-6). A in the margin *Gregorius* (216.15); B in the margin *Bona terrena quare electis negantur*; side-note 216.12.

[^18]: **R/T/C.** AA₁ read *videamus* (216.21), "let us see"; the print is translated. *Pietate milvina*: the kite's piety, a bird of prey's show of mercy; B, second hand, in the margin *Pietas Milvina* (216.24). *Raciones … pretense*: the arguments for the privilege (10-b, 207.1 ff.), alleged on the ground of mercy. *Non concludunt*: do not infer [the privileges] (GLOSSARY *concludere*).
