# *De eucharistia*, chapter 4, part 1: the force of the sacramental words and Christ's modes of being

## ¶1 · 83.10–83.13

> [p. 83] Sed restat videndum ulterius quam efficaciam habent verba sacramentalia et quomodo corpus Christi habet non esse dimensionale sed sacramentale in signis illis absconditum.

But it remains to see further what efficacy the sacramental words have, and how the body of Christ has not dimensional but [sacramental being]{g:esse-sacramentale}, hidden in those [signs]{g:signum}.

## ¶2 · 83.14–84.6

> [p. 83] Quantum ad verba sacramentalia, videtur quod Christus in illis loquitur figurative vel tropice, sicut prius allegatum est legem novam sub habitudine ad veterem sepius loqui. Sic enim dicit Christus quod Johannes est Helias Math. XIº, 14. Sic enim dicit Math. XIIIº, 37—40: Qui seminat bonum semen est filius hominis; ager autem est mundus; bonum vero semen sunt filii regni, zizania autem sunt filii nequam; inimicus autem qui seminavit ea est diabolus, messis vero est consummacio seculi, messores autem angeli sunt. Sicut igitur Veritas loquitur in istis parabolice ut dicit evangelium, sic videtur loqui figurative in quatuor evangeliis, scilicet Math. XXVIº, 26, Marci XIVº, 22, Luce XXIIº, 19, 20, Iᵃ Cor. XIº, 24, quando dicit panem et vinum esse corpus suum et sanguinem, sicut docet Apostolus prima Cor. XIº, 24 (ut patet [p. 84] posterius). Differencia tamen est in modis loquendi, cum unus sit tropologicus vel moralis et alius allegoricus vel sacramentalis; habet enim efficaciam faciendi corpus Christi et sanguinem esse de facto sub sacramentalibus speciebus, a quo defecerunt alie figure tam nove legis quam veteris.

As to the sacramental words, it seems that Christ speaks in them [figuratively]{g:figurative} or [tropically]{g:tropice}, as it was alleged before that the new law, in its [respect]{g:habitudo} to the old, often speaks [so].[^1] For so Christ says that John is Elias, Matt. 11:14. For so he says, Matt. 13:37–40: "He that soweth the good seed is the Son of man. And the field is the world. And the good seed are the children of the kingdom. And the [cockle]{g:zizania} are the children of the wicked one. And the enemy that sowed them, is the devil. But the harvest is the end of the world. And the reapers are the angels."[^2] As, therefore, the Truth speaks in these in parables, as the gospel says, so he seems to speak figuratively in the four gospels, namely Matt. 26:26, Mark 14:22, Luke 22:19–20, 1 Cor. 11:24, when he says that the [bread]{g:panis} and [wine]{g:vinum} are his body and blood, as the Apostle teaches in 1 Cor. 11:24 (as will appear [84] later).[^3] Yet there is a difference in the manners of speaking, since one is tropological or moral and the other allegorical or sacramental; for it has the efficacy of making [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} and blood to be in fact under the sacramental [species]{g:species}, which the other [figures]{g:figura}, both of the new law and of the old, fell short of.[^4]

## ¶3 · 84.7–86.20

> [p. 84] Sed ulterius notandum quod sicut rex habet triplex esse in regno, scilicet situale, intencionale et potenciale, correspondenter videtur Christum habere humanitus in mundo modum essendi triplicem; habet enim in celo ubi sedet ad dextram Dei modum essendi situalem, cum ibi situatur loco per quem diffunditur coequatus. Modum autem essendi potencialem videtur habere eciam humanitus, cum sit rex regum et dominus dominancium ad quamlibet partem mundi; verumptamen (ut dicunt multorum sanctorum testimonia) non habet humanitus esse situale vel dimensionale per totum mundum, sed limitate in celo ubi ponitur, sed per totum mundum habet esse essenciale divinitus; quoad medium autem esse sunt in Christo ultra alios homines divisiones multiplices; habet autem per tantum celi per quantum multiplicatur species sui corporis esse presens intencionale quo (quantum ad corpus suum) pascuntur oculi beatorum; et ista specierum multiplicacio corporis Christi secundum naturales est striccior et secundum magicos et perspectivos diffusior; limites autem istius diffusionis expedit nobis viantibus ignorare. Sed illud credimus quod beati in celo post diem iudicii ex elongacione a corpore Christi non pacientur angustias, sed beaciores non obstante maioritate [p. 85] distancie videbunt corpus Christi oculo corporali beacius; stat enim sensibile in maiori distancia videri clarius, quanto magis in beatitudine, ubi sensualis noticia ultra presenciam sensibilis secundum aliam legem emanabit ab anima. Unde pie credimus ordinem posicionis beatorum quoad corpus Christi esse regulatissimam in qua omnes beati mutuo gaudio quietantur. Sed preter istum modum essendi intencionalem quoad humanitatem Christi videtur quod secundo alii probabiliter sunt ponendi, scilicet modus essendi supernaturalis et modus essendi sacramentalis. Primo modo potest intelligi illud Math. ultimo 20: Ecce ego vobiscum sum omnibus diebus usque ad consumacionem seculi. Cum enim secundum fidem scripture Hebr. IIº, 17 Christus sit frater noster, videtur quod spectat caritati sue quod sit saltem in mente cum fratribus iuxta illud Math. XVIIIº, 20: Ubi duo vel tres congregati sunt in nomine meo, ibi sum ego in medio eorum. Si enim secundum evangelium Apostoli conversacio apostolorum in terra viancium est in celis (ut dicitur Phil. IIIº, 20, et a sanctis exponitur), evidencius anima Christi est cum fratribus quibus afficitur. Sic enim legitur IV. Reg. Vº, 26 de Elizeo multum distante a Gezi et Naaman: Nonne cor meum in presenti erat, quando reversus est homo de curru suo in occursum tui? Talia sunt multa dicta et argumenta ex quibus tam fideles quam infideles convincunt quod anima hominis est nedum ubi afficitur, sed eciam ubi a Deo ponitur ad aliquid singulariter cognoscendum. Modus autem essendi sacramentalis licet varietur in Christo secundum [p. 86] distinccionem sacramentorum, tamen specialiter est in hostia consecrata, quia omnes quatuor evangeliste Matthaeus, Marcus, Lucas et Paulus commemorant quomodo Christus dixit panem et vinum esse corpus suum et sanguinem; que locucio ex solempnitate loquentis et circumstancie captate ac testimonio sanctorum oportet quod habeat aliquam insolitam efficaciam ultra alia signa novi vel veteris testamenti; nam crux muta, benediccio laycalis et alia signa que facimus, sed specialiter in die Parasceues, significant atque commemorant corpus Christi et sanguinem. Sed magister optimus et Cesar semper Augustus in cena ultima qua prandebat vians cum suis apostolis, post esum pure figuralem agni paschalis introduxit sollempniter hoc sacramentum; ex quo patet, cum oportet Christum meliorando procedere, quod hoc sacramentum sit efficacius et rei sacramenti propinquius quam sacramenta antiqua. Aliter enim non foret racio quare illa cessarent novo non excedente superflue introducta.

But further it is to be noted that, as a king has a threefold [being]{g:esse} in a kingdom, namely [situal]{g:esse-situale}, intentional and potential, correspondingly it seems that Christ has, [humanly]{g:humanitus}, a threefold mode of being in the world.[^5] For in heaven, where he sits at the right hand of God, he has a situal mode of being, since there he is situated in a place through which he is spread out, made equal [to it]. And he seems to have a potential mode of being, also humanly, since he is "King of kings and Lord of lords" to every part of the world.[^6] Nevertheless (as the testimonies of many [saints]{g:sancti} say) he does not have, humanly, situal or dimensional being throughout the whole world, but limitedly in heaven, where he is placed; but throughout the whole world he has essential being, [divinely]{g:divinitus}.[^7] And as to the middle being, there are in Christ, beyond other men, manifold divisions. And he has, through as much of heaven as the species of his body is multiplied through, a present intentional being, by which (as to his body) the eyes of the blessed are fed; and this multiplication of the species of the body of Christ is narrower according to the natural philosophers, and more diffuse according to the magicians and [the perspectivists]{g:perspectivi}.[^8] But the [limits]{g:limites} of this diffusion it is expedient for us, [who are on the road]{g:viantes}, [to be ignorant of]{g:ignorare}. But this we believe: that the blessed in heaven after the day of judgement will not suffer distress from their distance from the body of Christ, but the more blessed, notwithstanding the [greatness]{g:majoritas} [85] of the distance, will see the body of Christ more blessedly with the bodily eye. For it can stand that a [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} thing is seen more clearly at a greater distance; how much more in blessedness, where sensual knowledge, beyond the presence of the perceptible thing, will flow from the soul by another law. Hence we [piously]{g:pie} believe the order of the position of the blessed with respect to the body of Christ to be most regulated, in which all the blessed are set at rest in mutual joy.[^9] But besides this intentional mode of being, as to the [humanity]{g:humanitas} of Christ, it seems that secondly others are to be posited [with probability]{g:probabiliter}, namely a supernatural mode of being and a sacramental mode of being. In the first way that [word] of Matthew, the last [chapter], 20, can be understood: "Behold I am with you all days, even to the consummation of the world." For since, according to the faith of scripture, Heb. 2:17, Christ is our brother, it seems that it belongs to his [charity]{g:caritas} that he be at least in mind with his brothers, according to that of Matt. 18:20: "Where there are two or three gathered together in my name, there am I in the midst of them."[^10] For if, according to the Apostle's gospel, the "[manner of life]{g:conversacio}" of the apostles, while on the road on earth, "is in heaven" (as is said in Phil. 3:20, and expounded by the saints), more evidently is the soul of Christ with the brothers toward whom he is moved in affection.[^11] For so it is read in 2 Kings 5:26 of Eliseus, far distant from Giezi and Naaman: "Was not my heart present, when the man turned back, from his chariot, to meet thee?"[^12] Such are many sayings and arguments from which both believers and [unbelievers]{g:infidelis} prove conclusively that the soul of a man is not only where it is moved in affection, but also where it is placed by God to know something [in a singular way]{g:singulariter}.[^13] And the sacramental mode of being, although it varies in Christ according to the [86] distinction of the [sacraments]{g:sacramentum}, yet is especially in the consecrated [host]{g:hostia}; for all four evangelists, Matthew, Mark, Luke and Paul, record how Christ said that the bread and wine are his body and blood; and this speech, from the solemnity of the speaker and of the circumstance chosen, and from the testimony of the saints, must have some unwonted efficacy beyond the other signs of the new or the old testament. For the dumb cross, the lay blessing, and the other signs that we make, but especially on the day of Parasceve, signify and commemorate the body of Christ and his blood.[^14] But the best master, and Caesar ever August, at the last supper, at which he dined [as a wayfarer]{g:vians} with his apostles, after the purely figural [eating]{g:esus} of the paschal lamb, solemnly introduced this sacrament;[^15] from which it is clear, since Christ must proceed by bettering, that this sacrament is more efficacious and nearer to the [reality of the sacrament]{g:res-sacramenti} than the ancient sacraments. For otherwise there would be no reason why those should cease, the new, introduced superfluously, not exceeding [them].[^16]

## ¶4 · 86.21–87.28

> [p. 86] Nec dubium fideli quin Christus reliquit istud ut sacramentum sacerdotibus celebrandum, quia Luce XXIIº, 19 mandat generaliter episcopis scole sue: Hoc facite in meam commemoracionem. Cum ergo figure corporis Christi in lege veteri profuerunt suis servantibus ad salutem (ut patet prima Cor. Xº), videtur quod oportet figuram novam huiusmodi corporis ipsas excedere; quod foret efficax et consonum scripture ponere in presencia corporis et sanguinis figurati, cum humanitas Christi sit quodammodo in aliis sacramentis, ergo multo magis in isto, cum verba huiusmodi sacramenti efficaciter illud vocant; et hoc confirmant facta discipulorum Christi memoriam eius in [p. 87] isto sacramento celebrancium, sentencie sanctorum doctorum de fundacione istius sacramenti ex verbis scripture scribencium et multiplices leges ecclesie. Unde dicitur communiter quod hoc sacramentum excedit alia in hiis tribus: Primo quod, ubi alia sacramenta multum distant a re sacramenti tam loco quam tempore, hoc sacramentum habet rem suam tam loco quam tempore concomitantem. Nam agnus paschalis legis veteris (de quo Exodi XIIº) multum distitit loco et tempore a corpore Christi; hoc autem sacramentum habet totam humanitatem Christi necessario comitantem. Secundo ubi alie figure veteris testamenti defecerunt ab efficacia conficiendi concomitanciam rei sacramenti, figura huius rei habet efficaciam efficienter causandi non corpus Christi sed quandam eius presenciam sacramentum sensibile concomitantem; unde non solum verba sed panis et vinum cum suis accidentibus dicuntur coniunctim et divisim sacramentum corporis Christi, et sic quamvis omnia ista sint unum sacramentum, tamen sunt valde multa sacramenta, que omnia reducuntur, ut spiritus aqua et sanguis, ad unum in effectu. Unde hoc sacramentum quoad eius permanenciam cum comitancia rei sue excedit sacramenta alia legis nove. Et tercio videtur quod hoc sacramentum excedit alia nedum quoad graciam unionis membrorum ecclesie cum Christo, sed rite celebratum prodest ministranti super alia sacramenta.

Nor is it doubtful to a faithful man that Christ left this as a sacrament to be celebrated by priests, since in Luke 22:19 he commands the [bishops]{g:episcopus} of his school generally: "Do this for a commemoration of me." Since, therefore, the figures of the body of Christ in the old law profited those who kept them unto salvation (as is clear from 1 Cor. 10), it seems that the new figure of this body must exceed them; and it would be effective and consonant with scripture to place it in the presence of the body and blood [figured]{g:figuratum}; since the humanity of Christ is in some manner in the other sacraments, therefore much more in this one, since the words of this sacrament efficaciously call it [there].[^17] And this is confirmed by the deeds of the disciples of Christ, celebrating his memory in [87] this sacrament, by the [teachings]{g:sentencia} of the holy doctors, writing of the founding of this sacrament from the words of scripture, and by the manifold [laws of the church]{g:leges-ecclesie}.[^18] Hence it is commonly said that this sacrament exceeds the others in these three [respects]. First, that whereas the other sacraments are far distant from the reality of the sacrament both in place and in time, this sacrament has its reality concomitant with it both in place and in time. For the paschal lamb of the old law (of which Exod. 12) was far distant in place and time from the body of Christ; but this sacrament has the whole humanity of Christ necessarily accompanying it. Second, whereas the other figures of the old testament fell short of the efficacy of making the [concomitance]{g:concomitancia} of the reality of the sacrament, the figure of this reality has the efficacy of efficiently causing, not the body of Christ, but a certain presence of it accompanying the perceptible sacrament;[^19] hence not only the words but the bread and wine with their accidents are called, jointly and severally, the sacrament of the body of Christ; and so, although all these are one sacrament, yet they are very many sacraments, which are all reduced, as the spirit, the water and the blood, to one [in actual fact]{g:in-effectu}.[^20] Hence this sacrament, as to its permanence together with the accompaniment of its reality, exceeds the other sacraments of the new law. And third, it seems that this sacrament exceeds the others not only as to [the grace of union]{g:gracia-unionis} of the [members]{g:membrum} of the [church]{g:ecclesia} with Christ, but, rightly celebrated, it profits the one ministering above the other sacraments.[^21]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 83.10: The force of the sacramental words and the nature of Christ's presence.
- 83.14: In the sacramental words Christ speaks figuratively, but with a special efficacy not found in other figurative speech.
- 84.7: Three kinds of presence: local, intentional, potential. Christ's local being is in heaven, His potencial being throughout the world, of which He is Lord. His intentional being is manifold: visible, to the saints in heaven;
- 85.10: supernatural, as with the faithful assembled in His name;
- 85.30: sacramental, specially in the host.
- 86.4: The solemnity of the words of institution shew the exceptional efficacy of this sacrament.
- 86.21: Christ ordered His priests to celebrate this sacrament, which must therefore exceed the figures of the old law.
- 87.4: It excels others in three points: 1. in the actual presence of the res sacramenti;
- 87.12: 2. in its unfailing efficacy to produce the concomitance of the res sacramenti;
- 87.24: 3. in not only uniting the members of the Church with Christ, but also in profiting the minister.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Habitudo*, CONTEXTUAL: here "respect", the relation of the new law to the old. *Sepius* (83.17) is C's marginal reading for *sequitur* in its text; ABD omit *prius* (83.16).
[^2]: **S.** Matt. 13:37–39, Douay. Wyclif omits *hi* before *sunt filii regni* and writes *zizania autem sunt filii nequam* and *messis vero est consummacio seculi* for the Vulgate's *filii sunt nequam* and *consummatio saeculi est*, without change of sense. Loserth's range runs to verse 40; the quotation ends with verse 39.
[^3]: **S/C.** *Ut dicit evangelium*: Matt. 13:34, that Jesus spoke all these things in parables. *Veritas* is Christ (John 14:6). The "four gospels" count Paul's account with the three, as the "four evangelists" do at 86.1–2.
[^4]: **C.** The subject of *habet enim efficaciam* (84.3) is taken as the sacramental manner of speaking just named. D reads *sacramentaliter*, and AB *habet esse*; AB read *modalis* for *moralis* (84.2).
[^5]: **C.** *Situale, intencionale, potenciale*: a king is in his kingdom by place, by the species of him that reach his subjects, and by power. *Situalis* is rendered "situal", being in a place (*situs*); Loserth's side-note has "local". *Intencionalis* is "intentional", of being in the species.
[^6]: **S.** "Sits at the right hand of God": Mark 16:19 (*sedet a dextris Dei*), not quoted; D reads *a dextris* and C adds *patris* (84.11). "King of kings and Lord of lords": Apoc. 19:16, Douay (cf. 1 Tim. 6:15).
[^7]: **S.** The saints' testimonies that Christ's body is in one place in heaven (LEDGER K04a-1: among them Augustine, *Ep.* 187.13.41 to Dardanus, PL 33, 848, *in loco aliquo coeli propter veri corporis modum*; *In Ioh.* tr. 30.1, PL 35, 1632, *uno loco esse potest: veritas ejus ubique diffusa est*). *Divinitus*, "divinely", is the pair of *humanitus*.
[^8]: **C.** The middle being is the intentional, the second of the three: Christ's body is present wherever its visible species are multiplied. *Naturales* are the natural philosophers, *magici* the magicians, *perspectivi* the writers on optics.
[^9]: **T/C.** *Regulatissimam* (85.6) is feminine beside *ordinem*; it and *in qua* agree with *posicionis*, and the English keeps the print's subject. A reads *oracionem*, D *ordinacionem* (85.5); ABCE read *et qua*, and D corrects to *in* (85.7).
[^10]: **S.** Matt. 28:20 and 18:20, Douay. Wyclif omits *et* before *ecce* (28:20); at 18:20 he omits *enim* and adds *ego* (*ibi sum ego*), which the Douay's "there am I" already renders. ABCE read *fuerint* for *sunt* (85.18).
[^11]: **S/G.** Phil. 3:20, Douay "our conversation is in heaven". *Conversacio* takes its row, "manner of life", inside the quotation. Wyclif makes it the apostles' (*conversacio apostolorum*); the Vulgate has *nostra*.
[^12]: **S.** 2 Kings 5:26 (Vulgate 4 Kings), Douay; the names are given in the Douay's forms.
[^13]: **C.** *Singulariter cognoscendum* (85.28–29): "in a singular way", of a knowledge given by God to one soul; "alone, only" does not construe here, so the CONTEXTUAL row's second sense. D omits *Talia — convincunt* (85.26–28).
[^14]: **C.** *Dies Parasceues*: Good Friday, the day of the Preparation. *Crux muta*: the cross that says nothing, set against the spoken words of institution.
[^15]: **C.** *Cesar semper Augustus*: the emperor's title, given to Christ. *Vians*: Christ before his passion, at the supper, as a wayfarer.
[^16]: **C.** *Novo non excedente superflue introducta* (86.20) is taken as "the new, introduced superfluously, not exceeding [them]"; *introducta* does not agree with *novo*. ABDE read *introducit* for *introduxit* (86.14).
[^17]: **C.** *Quod foret efficax ... ponere* is taken as "and it would be effective ... to place [the new figure]" in the presence of what it figures. *Illud vocant*: the words call the body there.
[^18]: **C.** *Sentencia*, CONTEXTUAL: here "teachings", the positions of the holy doctors. C has *sentencie — scribencium* (87.1–3) in the margin, in another hand.
[^19]: **C.** *Conficiendi concomitanciam*: "making", the general sense of *conficere*, not "consecrate"; untagged. The sign causes a presence, not the body.
[^20]: **S.** The spirit, the water and the blood: 1 John 5:8, "and these three are one". D reads *divise* for *divisim* (87.18).
[^21]: **T/C.** *Excedit* (87.25) is Loserth's: the manuscripts read *excessit*. *Gracia unionis* is here the union of the church's members with Christ, not the union of the natures in Christ.
