# *De ecclesia*, chapter 12, part 5: custom yields to truth; the basilica is not the bride; satisfaction cannot be remitted; fifth objection: the Westminster dream

## ¶1 · 268.1–268.19

> [p. 268] Mala autem consuetudo propter maioritatem periculi est pocius extirpanda, dicente decreto d. VIIIª: Mala consuetudo non minus quam perniciosa corruptela est abicienda; que nisi cicius radicitus evellatur in privilegiorum ius ab impiis assumitur, et incipiunt prevaricaciones et varie presumpciones, celeberrime non compresse, pro legibus venerari et privilegiorum more perpetuo celebrari. Et sentencia huius decreti verificata est nimium hiis diebus, unde ad interpretandum rectitudinem consuetudinis et privilegii subiungit decretum sub auctoritate Augustini: Veritate manifestata cedat consuetudo veritati. Nemo consuetudinem racioni et veritati preponat, quia consuetudinem racio et veritas semper excludunt. Cum ergo racio naturalis et veritas scripture patenter convincunt quod dictum privilegium non debet intelligi nude ad literam, sed racione et veritate limitante condicionem sepe expositam, aptari debet singulare pertinens secundum distribucionem accomodam.

And an evil [custom]{g:consuetudo}, because of the greatness of the danger, is rather to be rooted out, as the decree says (D.8 c.3): "An evil custom is to be cast away no less than a pernicious corruption; for unless it is quickly torn out by the roots, it is taken up by the wicked into the right of [privileges]{g:privilegium}, and [transgressions]{g:prevaricator} and various presumptions, most notoriously unchecked, begin to be venerated as laws and to be celebrated perpetually in the manner of privileges."[^1] And the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of this decree has been verified all too much in these days; hence, to interpret the rightness of custom and privilege, the decree adds, under the authority of Augustine (D.8 c.4): "When truth has been made manifest, let custom yield to truth. Let no one put custom before reason and truth, for reason and truth always exclude custom."[^2] Since, then, natural reason and the truth of scripture plainly prove that the said privilege ought not to be understood barely according to the letter, but with reason and truth limiting [it by] the condition often expounded, the pertinent singular must be fitted [to it] according to an [accommodated distribution]{g:distribucio-accomoda}.[^3]

## ¶2 · 268.20–269.11

> [p. 268] Per hec patet quod forma argumenti quod primo innuitur non procedit, tum quia multi sancti potuerunt consuetudinem predictam improvide approbasse et post penituisse, vel potuit fuisse tunc consuetudo laudabilis propter usum modestum, sed modo versum fuisse in corruptelam fidei contrariam propter malam consuetudinem superadditam gracia cupidinis usurpatam. Unde si racio ista procederet, nunquam impugnaret catholicus malum usum, sed ex consuetudine peccandi prescriberet membrum diaboli contra Deum, cuius oppositum docuit Christus consuetudines phariseicas acutissime reprobando, mundana vero prosperitas tam in personis quam in regnis est sepe kalende preparatorie ad ruinam. [p. 269] Quantum ad violacionem sancte matris ecclesie, dictum est quod ipsa non violatur nisi per defectum observancie legis sponsi; unde non est maior ydolatria credere truncum putridum esse dominum Jesum Christum quam credere domum materialem sive basilicam esse sanctam matrem ecclesiam, sponsam Christi. Unde quia in festis dedicacionis basilice per episcopum laudat ecclesia Deum in hoc quod ecclesiam suam sponsam vocare dignatus est, instruendi sunt simplices sacerdotes ne credant infideliter quod illa basilica sit ecclesia sponsa Christi.

By these things it is clear that the form of the argument that is first hinted at does not hold good: first, because many saints could have approved the aforesaid custom improvidently and afterwards repented; or the custom could then have been praiseworthy because of a moderate use, but now have been turned into a corruption contrary to faith because of an evil custom added on top, usurped for the sake of greed.[^4] Hence, if that reasoning held good, a catholic would never attack an evil usage, but a [member]{g:membrum} of the devil would gain title by [prescription]{g:prescripcio} against God from the custom of sinning; the opposite of which Christ taught by reproving most sharply the customs of the Pharisees. But worldly prosperity, both in persons and in [kingdoms]{g:regnum}, is often the kalends that prepare for ruin.[^5] [269] As to the violation of holy mother [church]{g:ecclesia}, it has been said that she is not violated except through a failure to observe the law of the [bridegroom]{g:sponsus}; hence it is no greater idolatry to believe a rotten tree-trunk to be the Lord Jesus Christ than to believe a material house or basilica to be holy mother church, the [bride]{g:sponsa} of Christ.[^6] Hence, since on the feasts of the dedication of a basilica by the bishop the church praises God in this, that he has deigned to call his church [his] bride, simple priests are to be instructed not to believe, faithlessly, that that basilica is the church, the bride of Christ.[^7]

## ¶3 · 269.12–270.15

> [p. 269] Ulterius quantum ad remissionem debiti satisfaccionis, dicitur quod nec temporalis dominus nec Deus ipse potest ipsum omnino remittere, tunc enim Christus frustra fuisset mortuus ad satisfaciendum pro peccato humani generis. Necesse est ergo quod pro quocunque crimine satisfiat in virtute meriti Jesu Christi, vel quod illud crimen eternaliter puniatur. Deus autem non potest concedere licenciam alicui ut eternaliter duret in crimine, et per consequens non potest concedere criminoso licenciam ut non satisfaciat pro delicto. Verumptamen homo potest cum circumspeccione sagaci remittere fratri iniuriam et debitum corporale et Deus remittet culpam postquam misericorditer invenerit peccatorem satisfacere pro debito. Nemo enim posset satisfacere pro minima offensa in tantum dominum, nisi ipse misericorditer levem penitenciam, baptismum vel aliquod opus penale acceptaverit graciose: iusticia, inquam, sua requirit quod solum premiet promerentem. Et patet quantum periculum est occasionare debitoribus fugitivis audaciam, ut non satisfaciant Deo vel homini, cum secundum principia Augustini et iuris [p. 270] canonici non dimittitur peccatum nisi restituatur ablatum, non quod oportet spoliantem restituere idem in numero fratri suo, sed quod satisfaciat omnimode domino capitali. Ipse autem vult ex inflexibilitate sue iusticie quod debitor satisfaciat servo suo debitum corporale, aut sibi equivalens restituendo, vel in pena inferni perpetuo serviendo. Illud autem servicium non est meritorium, sicut meritum quo impotens satisfacit creditori in via, sed est demeritorium prodessens creditori beato, et crucians perpetuo creditorem dampnatum; ideo non satisfaccio sed satispassio nominatur. Ulterius non oportet theologum omnes leges hominum de servitute et manucapcione discutere, sed satis est dicere quod precise de tanto sunt racionabiles de quanto consonant cum sacra scriptura.

Further, as to the remission of the debt of [satisfaction]{g:satisfaccio}, [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that neither a [temporal lord]{g:temporales domini} nor God himself can remit it wholly; for then Christ would have died in vain to make satisfaction for the sin of the human race.[^8] It is necessary, therefore, that for every crime satisfaction be made in virtue of the merit of Jesus Christ, or that that crime be punished eternally. Now God cannot grant anyone licence to endure eternally in crime, and consequently he cannot grant a criminal licence not to make satisfaction for his offence. Nevertheless a man can, with shrewd circumspection, remit to his brother an injury and a bodily debt; and God will remit the fault after he has mercifully found the sinner making satisfaction for the debt.[^9] For no one could make satisfaction for the least offence against so great a lord unless he himself had mercifully and graciously accepted a light penance, baptism, or some penal work: his justice, I say, requires that he reward only one who deserves it. And it is clear how great a danger it is to give fugitive debtors occasion of boldness, so that they do not make satisfaction to God or to man, since, according to the principles of Augustine and of [270] canon [law]{g:ius}, "the sin is not forgiven unless what was taken is restored": not that the despoiler must restore to his brother the numerically same thing, but that he make satisfaction in every way to the [chief lord]{g:capitalis dominus}.[^10] And he wills, from the inflexibility of his justice, that the debtor make satisfaction to his servant for the bodily debt, either by restoring to him an equivalent, or by serving perpetually in the pain of hell.[^11] But that [service]{g:servicium} is not meritorious, like the merit by which one who has not the means makes satisfaction to a creditor in the [way]{g:via}; rather it is demeritorious, profiting the blessed creditor and perpetually tormenting the damned creditor; therefore it is called not satisfaction but "[satispassion]{g:satispassio}".[^12] Further, the theologian need not discuss all the laws of men on servitude and [mainprise]{g:manucapcio}, but it is enough to say that they are reasonable precisely so far as they accord with holy scripture.[^13]

## ¶4 · 270.16–272.5

> [p. 270] Quinto et ultimo accusamur a vulgo per sompnia; fingunt enim filii patris mendacii quomodo revelatum est regnum Anglie transire impune, quousque sub quodam rege Ricardo basilica Westmonasterii sit polluta. Alias autem variaciones quas false fabricant non oportet hic recitare, cum mendacium dissonat sibi ipsi. Unde quicquid infortunii regno nostro acciderit applicant ad vindictam Dei de illo homicidio in sanctuario perpetrato, sed nesciunt applicare sequelam nisi per mendacia sompniorum. Hic non contendo si fuerit tale sompnium, quia scimus ex fide scripture quod pater mendacii agitat fantasias fidelium in sompnis, ut eos subvertat vel impediat profectum ecclesie. Nam Matthei XXVII°, 69, legitur uxorem Pilati fuisse illusam a sathana ad impediendum profectum ecclesie ex merito mortis Christi. Nichil, inquit, tibi et iusto illi: multa enim passa sum hodie per visum propter eum. Ulterius [p. 271] videtur multis verisimile quod regnum nostrum pacietur in brevi; sed cum non sum propheta cui facta est illa visio, non presumo illud sompnium revelare. Credo autem quod in occisione istorum hominum commissa erant horrenda facinora, sed comparacionem gravedinis ipsorum ad alia difficile est taxare, cum verisimile est quod alie polluciones per occisiones et verberaciones sacerdotum ibi et alibi in Anglia induxerunt in regnum crimina graviora. Ideo vellem quod omnia illa crimina sint correcta, quia certus sum ex fide scripture quod purgato regno a crimine et reconciliato Deo suo non est compossibilis tribulacio ab extrinseco vel intrinseco nisi sibi cesserit ad profectum, quia in oracione ecclesie sic habetur: Nulla ei nocebit adversitas, si nulla dominetur iniquitas. Ulterius tamen plus credendum est vive voci spiritus sancti, quam verbis vel sompniis patris mendacii, cum quandoque inmiscet verum, ut magis fraudulenter decipiat. Videndum est ex verbis scripture pro quibus peccatis contingit regnorum destruccio, et patet ex dicto sapientis Ecclesiastici X° quod iniusticie quoad Deum et iniurie quoad proximum sunt causa precipua. Et cum ad regem pertinet in regno suo utrumque extinguere, patet quod omissio regis in corrigendo clerum suis elemosinis abutentem et in corrigendo secularem miliciam pacem ecclesie perturbantem, est in rege causa precipua. Sed ve speculatoribus qui illud dicerent regi suo; ipsi enim sicut sacerdotes Judee sunt causa precipua persecucionis nostre ecclesie, si contingat, cum in quorum profectu stat regni prosperitas, in eorum defectu stat regni adversitas. Sacerdotes autem Dei debent autonomatice esse induti iusticiam, ideo declinantes ad iniusticiam gravius inficiunt se et suos, [p. 272] nam per absenciam vite in membris mors eorum inducitur. Et de isto conqueritur Isaie LVI°, cap. X°: Speculatores eius ceci omnes. Quod si fuerit, credenda est consequencia Christi Matthei XV°, 14°: Cecus si ceco ducatum prestet, ambo in foveam cadunt.

Fifth and last, we are accused by the common people through dreams; for the sons of the father of lies make up how it was revealed that the kingdom of England would pass on unpunished until, under a certain King Richard, the basilica of Westminster should be polluted.[^14] And the other variations which they falsely fabricate need not be recited here, since a lie is out of tune with itself. Hence whatever misfortune befalls our kingdom they apply to God's vengeance for that homicide perpetrated in the sanctuary; but they do not know how to apply [what follows]{g:sequela} except through the lies of dreams. Here I do not dispute whether there was such a dream, for we know [from the faith of scripture]{g:ex fide} that the father of lies stirs up the fantasies of the faithful in dreams, to overthrow them or to hinder the progress of the church.[^15] For in Matthew 27:19 we read that Pilate's wife was deluded by Satan to hinder the progress of the church [that came] from the merit of Christ's death: "Have thou nothing to do with that just man," she says, "for I have suffered many things this day in a dream because of him." Further, [271] it seems likely to many that our kingdom will suffer shortly; but since I am not the prophet to whom that vision came, I do not presume to reveal that dream.[^16] And I believe that in the killing of these men horrible [misdeeds]{g:facinus} were committed; but it is difficult to assess the comparison of their gravity with others, since it is likely that other pollutions, through the killings and beatings of priests there and elsewhere in England, have brought graver crimes into the kingdom.[^17] Therefore I would wish all those crimes to be corrected; for I am certain from the faith of scripture that, once the kingdom has been purged of crime and reconciled to its God, no tribulation from without or from within is [compossible]{g:compossibilis} [with that state] unless it turn out to its profit; for in the prayer of the church it is put thus: "No adversity shall harm it, if no iniquity [lords it]{g:dominari}."[^18] Further, nevertheless, more credence is to be given to the living voice of the Holy Spirit than to the words or dreams of the father of lies, since he sometimes mixes in what is true, the more fraudulently to deceive. We must see from the words of scripture for which sins the destruction of kingdoms comes about; and it is clear from the saying of the wise man, Ecclesiasticus 10:8, that injustices as regards God and injuries as regards neighbour are the chief cause.[^19] And since it belongs to the king to extinguish both in his kingdom, it is clear that the king's omission in correcting the [clergy]{g:clerus} that abuses its [alms]{g:elemosina}, and in correcting the secular [knighthood]{g:milicia} that disturbs the peace of the church, is in the king the chief cause. But woe to the [watchmen]{g:speculator} who were to say that to their king! For they, like the priests of Judea, are the chief cause of the persecution of our church, if it comes about; since on the failure of those on whose progress the prosperity of the kingdom stands, stands the adversity of the kingdom.[^20] Now the [priests]{g:sacerdos} of God ought [by antonomasia]{g:autonomatice} to be clothed with justice; therefore, when they turn aside to injustice, they infect themselves and their own more gravely, [272] for through the absence of life in the members their death is brought on.[^21] And of this [the prophet] complains in Isaiah 56:10: "His watchmen are all blind." And if this be so, Christ's [inference]{g:consequencia} in Matthew 15:14 is to be believed: "If the blind lead the blind, both fall into the pit."[^22]

## ¶5 · 272.6–272.14

> [p. 272] Et quoad correccionem brachii secularis, patet quod perturbacio qua iniuste perturbant forinsecus, evidencia topica prenosticat perturbacionem futuram illis per quos staret eorum correccio; Matthei VII°, 2, scribitur: Eadem mensura qua mensi fueritis, remecietur vobis. Et ista est causa quare Salomon ad pacificacionem regni sui ex mandato patris fecit Joab occidi ad cornu altaris, ut patet III°, Regum II° cap., quia Isaie XXXIII° 1, scribitur: Ve qui predaris nonne et ipse predaberis?

And as to the correction of the [secular arm]{g:brachium seculare}, it is clear that the disturbance by which they unjustly cause disturbance outwardly foretells, by topical [evidence]{g:evidencia}, a future disturbance for those through whom their correction would have stood; in Matthew 7:2 it is written: "With the same measure that you shall mete withal, it shall be measured to you again." And this is the reason why Solomon, for the pacifying of his kingdom, at his father's command had Joab killed at the horn of the altar, as is clear from 1 Kings 2, because in Isaiah 33:1 it is written: "Woe to thee that spoilest, shalt not thou thyself also be spoiled?"[^23]

## ¶6 · 272.15–272.21

> [p. 272] Non autem intendo quod milites regis occidi debeant, sed arguebam per locum a maiori: Si pro tempore veteris testamenti licuit militem ad cornu altaris occidere, multo magis licet maleficum pro pacificacione regni et mundacione ecclesie salvatum in vita et membris extrahere (ut patet in primo argumento capituli septimi).

But I do not [maintain]{g:intendere} that the king's [knights]{g:milites} ought to be killed; rather I was arguing by [the topic from the greater]{g:locus a maiori}: if in the time of the old testament it was lawful to kill a knight at the horn of the altar, much more is it lawful, for the pacifying of the kingdom and the cleansing of the church, to drag out an evildoer, saved in life and limb (as is clear in the first argument of the seventh chapter).[^24]
