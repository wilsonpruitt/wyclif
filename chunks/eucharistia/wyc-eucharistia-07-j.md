# *De eucharistia*, chapter 7, part 10: against the glossators' reading and against impanation; the first two ways of the logic of the faithful

## ¶1 · 226.1–226.16

> [p. 226] O quam detestanda glossa que tociens exponit textum per suum contradictorium; et in eadem proposicione mutat sensum eiusdem termini contra regulam propriam. Recoleret, inquam, de textu Augustini, ubi dicit de omnibus modis contendere approbandum quod caro est carnis et sanguis est sanguinis sacramentum; et illam carnem vocat iste sanctus celestem panem qui vere est corpus Christi suo modo; tunc enim verecundaretur de glossa quam hic addicit, hoc est: inproprie dicitur corpus Christi suo modo, sed non rei veritate. Beatus autem Augustinus exponit se ipsum quod ille panis sacratus videtur esse corpus Christi modo signi, cum tropice et sacramentaliter vere significat corpus Christi. Nec dubium quin Augustinus intendit per panem naturam panis, non accidens, quod tam expresse asserit non posse per se esse.

O how detestable a gloss, which so often expounds the text by its contradictory, and in the same proposition changes the [meaning]{g:sensus} of the same term against its own rule![^1] He should recall, I say, the text of Augustine, where he speaks of contending in every way to prove that "the flesh is the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} of the flesh and the blood is the sacrament of the blood"; and that flesh this saint calls "the heavenly [bread]{g:panis} which [truly]{g:vere} is [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} in its own manner";[^2] for then he would be ashamed of the gloss which he adds here, that is: "it is improperly called the body of Christ in its own manner, but not in the truth of the thing." But blessed Augustine expounds himself: that that [hallowed]{g:sacrare} bread appears to be the body of Christ in the manner of a [sign]{g:signum}, since [tropically]{g:tropice} and [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} it truly signifies the body of Christ.[^3] Nor is there doubt that Augustine means by bread the [nature]{g:natura} of bread, not an [accident]{g:accidens}, which he so expressly asserts "cannot be [of itself]{g:per-se}".[^4]

## ¶2 · 226.17–227.4

> [p. 226] Et conformiter intelligit beatus Ambrosius illud decretum: Panis est in altari; non enim est bona glossa, beato Ambrosio probante et asserente quod panis potest esse corpus Christi; quam glossator sibi addicit dicens: Inpossibile est quod panis sit corpus Christi, sed talia sunt ad sanum sensum intelligenda. Nec dubium quin glossator iste non tantum insanivisset, nisi fuisset seductus per glossatores theologos. Unde signum prolixitatis et dubitacionis sue in ista materia est quod in principio illius distinccionis recitat tanquam opinionem probabilem quod panis et vinum remanent post consecracionem; quod probat ex illo decreto Ego Berengarius et non reprobat. [p. 227] Foret autem immemor sensus proprii, nisi appareret sibi sensum illius facere pro hac via. Oportet ergo intelligere sanctos cum scriptura quod figurative et vere vocant signum nomine signati.

And in conformity [with this] blessed Ambrose understands that [decree]{g:decretum}, "Bread is on the altar";[^5] for it is not a good gloss (blessed Ambrose proving and asserting that "bread can be the body of Christ") which the [glossator]{g:glossa} adds to it, saying: "It is impossible that bread should be the body of Christ, but such things are to be understood in a sound meaning."[^6] Nor is there doubt that this glossator would not have raved so much, had he not been led astray by the theologian glossators. Hence a sign of his prolixity and doubting in this matter is that at the beginning of that distinction he recites as a probable [opinion]{g:opinio} that the bread and the [wine]{g:vinum} remain after [consecration]{g:consecracio}; which he proves from that decree *Ego Berengarius*, and does not reprove.[^7] [227] And he would be unmindful of his own meaning, unless the meaning of that [decree] appeared to him to make for this way.[^8] We must therefore understand [the saints]{g:sancti}, together with scripture, [as saying] that [figuratively]{g:figurative} and truly they call the sign by the name of the [thing signified]{g:signatum}.

## ¶3 · 227.5–227.25

> [p. 227] Habito ergo sensu decreti magni Augustini et aliorum sanctorum inveho contra istam sentenciam, primo per hoc quod iuxta sanctorum sentenciam panis materialis per benediccionem fit corpus Christi; quod non foret verum iuxta hanc viam: ergo illa repugnat sanctorum sentenciis. Assumptum licet sit contra regulas modernorum doctorum, patet per beatum Augustinum, Sermone LXIᵒ pro feria IIᵃ Pasche, et ponitur in De consecracione, distinccione IIᵃ: Mementote, inquit, carissimi quemadmodum dominus Jesus ab eis quorum oculi tenebantur ne illum agnoscerent in fraccione panis voluit se agnosci. Non, inquit, omnis panis sed accipiens benediccionem Christi fit corpus Christi. Nec dubium quin loquitur de pane materiali, ut IVᵗᵒ de Trinitate: Corpus et sanguinem Christi dicimus illud quod ex terre fructibus acceptum et prece mistica consecratum recte sumimus ad salutem in memoriam dominice passionis. Nulli dubium quin non intelligit ignotum accidens acceptum ex terre fructibus et sacramentaliter benedictum sed (ut verba sonant) verum panem usualem taliter consecratum.

Having therefore the meaning of the decree of the great Augustine and of the other saints, I inveigh against this [teaching]{g:sentencia},[^9] first by this: that according to the saints' teaching material bread by blessing becomes the body of Christ; which would not be true according to this way: therefore it [is incompatible with]{g:repugnare} the saints' teachings. [The assumption]{g:assumptum}, although it is against the rules of [the modern doctors]{g:moderni-doctores}, is clear from blessed Augustine, Sermon 61, for the second [day of the week]{g:feria} of Easter, and it is placed in *De consecracione*, distinction 2: "Remember," he says, "dearly beloved, how the Lord Jesus willed to be known in the [breaking]{g:fractio} of bread by those whose eyes were held that they should not know him." "Not every bread," he says, "but [the bread] receiving the blessing of Christ becomes the body of Christ."[^10] Nor is there doubt that he speaks of material bread, as in [*On the Trinity*] 4: "We call the body and blood of Christ that which, taken from the fruits of the earth and consecrated by mystical prayer, we rightly receive for salvation in memory of the Lord's passion."[^11] To no one is there doubt that he does not mean an unknown accident taken from the fruits of the earth and sacramentally blessed, but (as the words sound) true ordinary bread so consecrated.

## ¶4 · 227.26–228.23

> [p. 227] Item, omnis natura vel quidditas corporis est suppositum eiusdem corporis, ut patet alibi; ergo si natura panis et vini ydemptificata sit corpori Christi [p. 228] et sanguini, videtur quod panis et vinum ydemptificatur corpori Christi et sanguini, et per idem Deus ydemptificare quidlibet posset cuilibet; quod est contra dicta de ydemptificacione. Est enim questio isti vie: Si tam materia quam forma panis et vini remanet post consecracionem vel non. Si non, tollitur inpanacio sicut in transsubstanciacione, ubi dicitur totam substanciam annichilari. Non enim fit panis caro Christi qui secundum se totum destruitur, antequam fieret caro Christi; nec natura eius convertitur in suppositum partis Christi, cuius utraque natura desinit esse secundum se totam. Si vero tam materia quam forma panis et vini remanet, videtur quod integre suppositum panis et vini remanet, et cum neutra illarum fit natura corporis et sanguinis, videtur quod remanent corpora omnia distincta ut in principio, preter hoc quod per consecracionem habent habitudinem ut signum et signatum, sicut dicit antiqua sanctorum sentencia; nec evadet inpanans difficultatem, querendo sollicite quid evenit de elementis panis et vini. Non enim accidit quidditas corpori naturali nec fingi potest quod accrescit ex consecracione corpori Christi in celo pars materialis aliqua vel formalis.

Again, every nature or [quiddity]{g:quiditas} of a body is the [supposit]{g:suppositum} of the same body, as is clear elsewhere; therefore if the nature of the bread and wine were [identified]{g:ydemptificare} with the body of Christ [228] and with his blood, it seems that the bread and wine are identified with the body and blood of Christ, and by the same token God could identify anything with anything; which is against what was said of [identification]{g:ydemptificacio}.[^12] For there is a question for this way: whether both the matter and the form of the bread and wine remain after consecration, or not. If not, [impanation]{g:inpanacio} is taken away, as in [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio}, where the whole [substance]{g:substancia} is said to be [annihilated]{g:anichilacio}. For the bread, which is destroyed according to its whole self before it became the flesh of Christ, does not become the flesh of Christ; nor is its nature converted into the supposit of a part of Christ, since each nature of it ceases to be according to its whole self.[^13] But if both the matter and the form of the bread and wine remain, it seems that the supposit of the bread and wine remains entire; and since neither of them becomes the nature of the body and blood, it seems that all the bodies remain distinct as at the beginning, except that through consecration they have the [respect]{g:habitudo} of sign and thing signified, as the ancient teaching of the saints says;[^14] nor will [the impanator]{g:inpanans} escape the difficulty, when one asks carefully what becomes of the elements of bread and wine. For a quiddity does not befall a natural body, nor can it be feigned that from consecration some material or formal part accrues to the body of Christ in heaven.

## ¶5 · 228.24–230.3

> [p. 228] Item (ut sepe asserui), non est sompniandum mendacium in ista materia, ne ydolatra false immolet patri mendacii. Apostolus I. Cor. Xᵒ, 14 incipiens tractare de hoc sacramento hortatur sibi carissimos fugere ab ydolorum cultura. Cum ergo ista inpanacio non habet evidenciam ex scriptura, ex racione vel ex testimonio dante fidem, videtur quod katholicus pro evitando periculo non debet inpanacionem talem asserere. Et ista regula usus sum contra transsubstanciacionem [p. 229] ecclesie Avinonice et contra omnem novitatem preter scripturam sacram de ista materia sompniatam. Notum est (inquam) istam ecclesiam eciam in materia fidei sepius deviasse. Cum ergo lex scripture tradidit nobis de eukaristia sufficiens ad credendum, videtur quod sit presumptuosa stulticia preter eius fundacionem superaddere novitates; iuxtaponamus itaque exposiciones sanctorum et videamus ubi inpanacio sit fundata; ymmo non videbimus per locum a simili, ubi eius possibilitas fundaretur. Verbum enim infinite potencie assumpsit personaliter humanitatem redimendam; sed quid hoc ut caro Christi et sanguis assumant sibi panem et vinum? Et iterum, illa humanitas non fuit, antequam fiebat Deus, quia aliter foret ydemptificacio, nec potest amitti; quorum utriusque oppositum contingit in sacramento altaris. Et iterum, idem suppositum est persona veri hominis et Dei, quod si foret evidenter simile in inpanacione, idem suppositum foret panis in inpanacione qui prefuit et vera caro Christi. Quod negatur, quia tunc foret ydemptificacio et non mutacio; quod negant leges ecclesie cum scriptura; ymmo sicut Christus assumendo multas humanitates foret multi homines, sic caro Christi et sanguinis forent multi panes et vina quantumcunque varie accidentata; et sic Christus moveretur eodem motu quo movetur aliud tale corpus; et reciperet mediante tali pane predicaciones multas superfluas et inhonestas, ut tactum est in materia de incarnacione, ubi inprobatur ista via. Sequitur enim panis quem fecit Petrus pistor est Deus, et alius panis quem pinsuit Paulus pistor est idem Deus, ergo multe substancie create forent Deus, et sic Deus foret facillime factibilis a pistore et inhonestissime ac crudelissime [p. 230] tractabilis nedum ab homine sed a bestia, sicut tractabatur secundum humanitatem suam in via. Sed nichil plus hereticum, impium et insane.

Again (as I have often asserted), a lie is not to be dreamed up in this matter, lest the idolater falsely immolate to the father of lies. The Apostle, 1 Cor. 10:14, beginning to treat of this sacrament, exhorts "his dearly beloved" to "fly from the service of idols".[^15] Since therefore this impanation has no [evidence]{g:evidencia} from scripture, from reason, or from testimony giving faith, it seems that a catholic, to avoid the danger, ought not to assert such impanation. And this rule I have used against the transubstantiation [229] of the Avignon [church]{g:ecclesia}, and against every novelty dreamed up beyond holy scripture concerning this matter. It is known (I say) that this church has quite often gone astray even in a matter of faith. Since therefore the law of scripture has handed down to us concerning [the Eucharist]{g:eukaristia} what suffices for believing, it seems that it is presumptuous folly to add novelties over and above, beyond its foundation; let us therefore set side by side the expositions of the saints and see where impanation is founded; indeed we shall not see, by the topic [from likeness]{g:a-simili}, where its possibility would be founded. For [the Word]{g:verbum} of infinite [power]{g:potencia} [assumed]{g:assumere} personally [the humanity]{g:humanitas} that was to be redeemed; but what is this to the flesh and blood of Christ assuming bread and wine to themselves? And again, that humanity was not before it became God, because otherwise it would be identification; nor can it be lost; the opposite of each of which happens in [the sacrament of the altar]{g:sacramentum-altaris}.[^16] And again, the same supposit is the [person]{g:persona} of true man and of God; and if this were evidently alike in impanation, the same supposit would be, in impanation, the bread which was there before and the true flesh of Christ. This is denied, because then it would be identification and not change; which [the laws of the church]{g:leges-ecclesie} deny together with scripture. Indeed, just as Christ by assuming many humanities would be many men, so the flesh and blood of Christ would be many breads and wines, however variously [subject to accidents]{g:accidentatus};[^17] and so Christ would be moved with the same motion with which another such body is moved; and he would receive, by means of such bread, many superfluous and unseemly [predications]{g:predicacio}, as was touched on in the matter of the incarnation, where this way is disproved.[^18] For [it follows]{g:sequitur}: "the bread which Peter the baker made is God, and another bread which Paul the baker kneaded is the same God"; therefore many [created]{g:creatus} substances would be God; and so God would be most easily makeable by a baker, and most unseemly and most cruelly [230] handleable, [not only]{g:nedum} by a man but by a beast, as he was handled [as to his humanity]{g:secundum-humanitatem} in [the way]{g:via}. But nothing [is] more heretical, impious and insane.

## ¶6 · 230.4–230.18

> [p. 230] Et sic intelligi debet confessio Berengarii qui primo negavit predicacionem tropicam, quomodo referri debet ad signum quod teritur et non Christus. Notandum quod multiplex est variacio loyce fidelium in ista materia, ut aliqui fideliter concedunt proposiciones Christi et tocius scripture esse verissimas et tamen negant quod panis quem Christus cepit et sacravit est vel fuit corpus suum, et a pari nullus panis sacerdotum sequencium; nec proposicio scripture sacre illud significat sed significat quod panis sacramentaliter fiat corpus Christi. Proposicionibus autem nostris non debemus dare talem sensum tropicum, cum Christus concessit hoc: Baptista est Helias, et Baptista negavit ipsum esse Heliam, reservando tropum magistro.

And so the confession of Berengar must be understood, who first denied the [tropical predication]{g:predicacio-tropica}: how it must be referred to the sign which, and not Christ, is ground.[^19] It is to be noted that the variation of the [logic]{g:loyca} of the faithful in this matter is manifold: so that some faithfully grant the propositions of Christ and of all scripture to be most true, and yet deny "the bread which Christ took and hallowed is or was his body", and by parity [say] that no bread of the priests who follow [is]; nor does the [proposition]{g:proposicio} of holy scripture signify that, but it signifies "the bread sacramentally becomes the body of Christ".[^20] But to our propositions we ought not to give such a [tropical meaning]{g:sensus-tropicus}, since Christ granted this, "the Baptist is Elias", and the Baptist denied himself to be Elias, reserving the [trope]{g:tropus} to the master.[^21]

## ¶7 · 230.19–230.31

> [p. 230] Secunda via concedit generaliter quod panis sacramentaliter consecratus est corpus Christi, quia sacramentaliter ipsum figurat et erit ac fuit et per consequens potest esse corpus Christi; sed non concedit plus cum aliis verbis adiectivis, ut quod homo videt, terit vel sentit corpus Christi oculo corporali. Et pro utraque parte istius loyce sunt multa dicta sanctorum et determinaciones ecclesie; et nisi sic diceretur, oporteret negare dicta sanctorum et ecclesie tanquam heretica, et non esset michi efficax testimonium vie mee. Et quantum ad sensum eorum haberem mille glossatores michi contrarios, et negando dicta sanctorum tanquam heretica deficeret michi efficacia replicandi.

The second way grants generally that "the bread sacramentally consecrated is the body of Christ", because it sacramentally [figures]{g:figurare} it, and will be and was, and consequently can be, the body of Christ; but it does not grant more with other [adjectival verbs]{g:verba-adiectiva}, as that a man sees, grinds or [perceives]{g:sentire} the body of Christ with the bodily eye.[^22] And for each part of this logic there are many sayings of the saints and [the church's determinations]{g:determinaciones-ecclesie}; and unless it were so said, one would have to deny the sayings of the saints and of the church as heretical, and the testimony of my way would not be effective for me. And as to their meaning I should have a thousand glossators contrary to me, and by denying the sayings of the saints as heretical I should lack the effectiveness of [rejoining]{g:replicatur}.

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 226.1: This gloss contradicted by Augustine,
- 226.17: and Ambrose.
- 227.5: The saints declare that material bread becomes by benediction the body of Christ.
- 227.26: If matter and form pass away there is no bread to be changed,
- 228.12: if they remain the bread remains.
- 228.24: Scripture says nothing of impanation and we should not add to the requirements of Scripture.
- 229.11: The parallel with Christ's assumption of manhood does not hold.
- 230.4: The faithful take this matter variously: 1. Some say the bread is sacramentally Christ's body.
- 230.19: 2. Others that it figures Christ's body.
