# *De eucharistia*, De eucharistia et penitencia, chapter 5: the evils that such confession promotes

## ¶1 · 338.19–339.26

> [p. 338] Videtur tangendum leviter ulterius que mala ex confessione tali per homines introducta poterint faciliter promoveri. Unum autem malum ad quod diabolus sepe seducit ecclesiam est quod homines non debite credunt in Deum Jesum Christum et sic errant fide qua crederent in Spiritum Sanctum, cum credunt remissionem peccatorum stare regulariter in iudicio prescitorum. Et sic talis confitens est maledictus qui confidit in homine, capiens sepe audaciam ad peccata talia non timendum; et sic non veretur Deum nec timet delictum in proximum et predicacio ac hortacio [p. 339] per Christum et apostolos precepte nimium sunt soporate, cum papa potest credere quod remissio peccatorum est singulariter suo iudicio reservata; et sic potest mercari cum hominibus servando sibi soli delecionem peccati a crimine. Sic optant et confessores proprii quos ipse specialiter limitat, possunt maiores et minores subvertere et servitutem diaboli introducere ac libertatem Christi paulative excludere. Et sic fratres possent mercari symoniace et observanciam mandatorum Dei tam in se quam in suis confessis perdere, ymmo possent sub sigillo confessionis ad sanctimoniales et alias mulieres accedere et sub forma confessionis seratis clausuris ipsas feminas impregnare, cum olfactus mutuus inter virum et feminam in locis absconditis ex cautela dyaboli provocat ad peccatum. Et hinc Yob pepigit fedus cum oculis suis, ut non cogitaret de virgine. Multo magis lubricus non ipsam olfaceret aut tangeret sic solitarie in loco abscondito, ymmo breviter cautela diaboli et istud medium sunt ad omnia genera criminum inductiva, ut ad peccatum diaboli, ut superbiam, invidiam atque iram, cum confessor post confessionem non audet culpam confessam prodere nec confessum suum private vel publice ad virtutem oppositam inclinare; et sic necessitatur a lege Domini Matthei XVIIIᵒ, 15 Si peccaverit in te frater tuus consenciendo crimini declinare.

It seems we should touch lightly, further, on what evils could easily be promoted by such [confession]{g:confessio} introduced by men.[^1] And one evil to which the devil often seduces the [church]{g:ecclesia} is that men do not duly believe in God, Jesus Christ, and so they err in the faith by which they should believe in the Holy Spirit, since they believe that the [remission]{g:remissio} of sins stands, [as a rule]{g:regulariter}, in the judgement of [the foreknown]{g:prescitus}.[^2] And so such a one confessing is "cursed", the one "that trusteth in man",[^3] often taking boldness not to fear such sins; and so he does not reverence God nor fear an offence against his neighbour, and the preaching and exhortation [339] enjoined by Christ and the apostles are lulled too far asleep, since the [pope]{g:papa} can believe that the remission of sins is reserved to his judgement [alone]{g:singulariter};[^4] and so he can trade with men, keeping to himself alone the [blotting out]{g:delecio} of sin from the crime. So the confessors of their own, too, whom he specially [assigns]{g:limitare}, choose [it]; they can subvert great and small, bring in the [servitude]{g:servitus} of the devil and little by little shut out the freedom of Christ.[^5] And so the [friars]{g:frater} could trade simoniacally and destroy the observance of God's commandments both in themselves and in those who have confessed to them; indeed they could, under the seal of confession, approach nuns and other women and, under the form of confession, behind locked enclosures, get these women with child, since the mutual scent of man and woman in hidden places provokes to sin by the [wile]{g:cautela} of the devil.[^6] And hence Job "made a covenant with his eyes, that he would not think upon a virgin."[^7] Much more should a man prone to slip not scent her or touch her, alone like this in a hidden place; indeed, in short, the wile of the devil and this means are such as lead on to every kind of crime: as to the sin of the devil, as pride, envy and wrath, since the confessor after confession dares not disclose the fault confessed nor incline his penitent, privately or publicly, to the opposite virtue; and so, by consenting to the crime, he is compelled to turn aside from the law of the Lord, Matthew 18:15, "If thy brother shall offend against thee."[^8]

## ¶2 · 339.27–339.31

> [p. 339] Et quantum ad peccata carnalia, notum est quod istud est medium demonum ad introducendum tam gulam quam luxuriam non solum in confessores et confessos sed in personas alias per cautelas quas diabolus machinatur.

And as to [carnal]{g:carnalis} sins, it is well known that this is the demons' means of bringing in both gluttony and lechery, not only into confessors and those confessed but into other persons, through the wiles which the devil contrives.

## ¶3 · 340.1–340.28

> [p. 340] Et quantum ad peccatum mundi sive cupidinem, patet quod sacramentum istud est plenum cupidine, cum confessores tales nedum accipiunt de confessis suis pecuniam sed disponunt occulta media per que ipsam vel sibi equivalens accipient in futurum. Et una cautela novella per diabolum et Anglicos recencius hodie practisatur quod primo reservata absolucione notati criminis Romane curie mittuntur bulle quod homines possent domi absolvi, sic tamen quod fideliter calcularent quantum expenderent eundo a patria sua usque ad Romam et econtra. Et illam pecuniam darent personis mediis per quas poterit ipsis confessoribus devenire; et iustificatur per hoc istud graciosum commercium quod caritative laxatur labor peccantis quem haberet ad curiam et econtra. Et tamen hec immensa indulgencia ex plenitudine potestatis summi pontificis est promissa. Sed monoculis est satis cognitum quod ista reservacio absolucionis ad curiam est contra caritatis regulas non fundata; est insuper satis cognitum quod sequens mercacio est plena cupidine atque superbia, cum idem esset sic facere et breviter confesso dicere da mihi tantum peccuniam et securabo te quod eris apud Deum absolutus. Et revera sic in sacramento eucaristie regnat ignorancia sicut in isto; sed hic expressior infidelis cupiditas est adiuncta. Istam ergo infidelitatem fideles ecclesie Christi detegerent licet pro eius deteccione penaliter paterentur.

And as to the sin of the world, or cupidity, it is clear that this [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} is full of cupidity,[^9] since such confessors [not only]{g:nedum} take money from those who confess to them but arrange hidden means through which they will receive it, or its equivalent, in future.[^10] And one new wile is [more freshly]{g:recencius} [put into practice]{g:practizare} today by the devil and the English: that first, absolution for a noted crime being reserved to the [Roman curia]{g:curia-romana}, bulls are sent [saying] that men may be [absolved]{g:absolvere} at home, yet on the condition that they faithfully calculate how much they would spend going from their [homeland]{g:patria} to Rome and back. And that money they are to give to intermediate persons through whom it can come to the confessors themselves; and this gracious [trade]{g:commercium} is justified by this, that the sinner's labour, which he would have in going to the curia and back, is charitably relaxed. And yet this immense [indulgence]{g:indulgencie} is promised out of the [plenitude of power]{g:plenitudo-potestatis} of the [highest pontiff]{g:summi-pontifices}. But it is well enough known to the one-eyed that this reservation of absolution to the curia is against the rules of [charity]{g:caritas}, not founded [on them];[^11] it is moreover well enough known that the trading that follows is full of cupidity and pride, since to do so would be the same as to say briefly to the one confessed, "Give me so much money, and I will assure you that you will be absolved before God." And [in truth]{g:revera} ignorance reigns in the sacrament of the eucharist as it does in this one; but here a more express [faithless]{g:infidelis} cupidity is joined to it.[^12] This [unbelief]{g:infidelitas}, therefore, the faithful of Christ's church should [expose]{g:detegere}, though they should suffer [in hardship]{g:penaliter} for exposing it.

## Notes

[^1]: **R.** D has in its margin the chapter's heading as a question: *Que mala ex confessione tali poterint promoveri* ("what evils may be promoted by such confession"). A, D and E lack the chapter heading; C numbers it 5.
[^2]: **G/C.** "The foreknown" (*presciti*) is Wyclif's term for those God foreknows will be damned, not "the reprobate". The error is in the faith "by which" (*qua*) men believe in the Holy Spirit: the article of the creed that follows it, the remission of sins, is put in the hands of men who may be damned. One manuscript (D or E; the OCR gives "U") reads *stare essencialiter*, "stands essentially", for *regulariter*.
[^3]: **S/T.** Jer. 17:5, Vulgate *Maledictus homo qui confidit in homine*, DR "Cursed be the man that trusteth in man"; Wyclif drops *homo* and makes the man confessing the subject, so the Douay wording is given in pieces. B reads *maledicens*, D *maledictus hic*.
[^4]: **C/T.** *Singulariter* in the sense "alone" (the row's first sense), set beside *sibi soli* "to himself alone" in the next clause. A reads *separate* for *soporate* ("lulled asleep"), apparently "set apart".
[^5]: **C.** The sentence is loose: *Sic optant et confessores proprii … possunt …* has two verbs with no connective. We take *et* as "too" and *optant* as "choose [it]" (the pope's reservation), with *possunt* opening a fresh clause. One manuscript (the OCR gives "H") reads *aptant*, "fit [themselves to it]". The "confessors of their own" (*proprii*) are those the pope assigns (*limitat*) outside the parish priest. A word may be lost here.
[^6]: **T/R.** B, D, E and F read *impugnare*, "assail", for the print's *impregnare*; we follow Loserth. B has in its margin the clause *Olfactus mutuus inter virum et feminam in locis absconditis provocat ad peccatum*.
[^7]: **S.** Job 31:1, Vulgate *Pepigi foedus cum oculis meis, ut ne cogitarem quidem de virgine*; Wyclif puts it in the third person and drops *quidem*, so the Douay wording ("I made a covenant with my eyes, that I would not so much as think upon a virgin") is adapted to his.
[^8]: **S/R.** Matt. 18:15, Vulgate *Si autem peccaverit in te frater tuus*: the law of fraternal correction, which the seal of confession, as Wyclif argues, keeps the confessor from obeying. D's margin: *Confessor necessitatur a lege Domini declinare*, which confirms the construal of *necessitatur … declinare*.
[^9]: **C.** The three sins run in order: the sin of the devil (pride, envy, wrath, ¶1), the sins of the flesh (¶2), the sin of the world (¶3). *Sacramentum istud* here is confession, the penitential sacrament, not the eucharist (which is named apart at the end of the paragraph).
[^10]: **T.** The manuscripts read *nondum*, "not yet"; *nedum* "not only" is Loserth's emendation, which the *sed* requires.
[^11]: **C/R.** *Est contra caritatis regulas non fundata*: we take *non fundata* as a second predicate ("is against the rules of charity, [and] not founded"); A's margin reads *Absolucionis reservacio ad Romanam curiam est contra regulas caritatis*, without it.
[^12]: **C/T.** *Infidelis* as an adjective: the cupidity is "faithless", which the next sentence names as *infidelitas*, "unbelief". One manuscript (siglum lost in the OCR) reads *expressior et infidelis*, "more express and faithless".
