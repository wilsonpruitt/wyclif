# *De ecclesia*, chapter 8, part 5: no true privilege outside scripture; the Westminster case; poverty the highest privilege

## ¶1 · 172.25–177.30

> [p. 172] Unde ad pacificandum discipulos et instruendum eos quale privilegium et prioritatem eligerent, dicit quod qui voluerit inter eos prior vel maior fieri in celis, oportet quod sit servus humilior atque diligencior hic in terris (ut alias ostendi modo opposito) quoad primatum vel honorem seculi. Ideo dicit Crisostomus: Fructum humilitatis posuit primatum [p. 173] celestem, ut quicunque desiderat primatum celestem, sequatur hic humilitatem celestem; quicunque autem desiderant primatum in terra invenient confusionem in celo. Ergo, inquit, iam non sit inter servos Christi de primatu certamen, nec festinet unusquisque eorum quomodo alio maior appareat, sed quomodo omnibus inferior videatur. Et ista doctrina sufficeret toti ecclesie ad eleccionem privilegii vel primatus, nec est possibile quod vere pacificetur ecclesia, antequam ista regula practice sit edocta. Ex istis elicitur quod nullum est verum privilegium ecclesie, nisi de quanto fundatur, docetur vel elicitur ex scriptura. Patet sic: Omnis lex utilis sancte matri ecclesie docetur explicite vel implicite in scriptura, omne privilegium est huiusmodi ex dictis: ergo conclusio. Confirmatur ex sepe dictis: Omnis veritas est in scriptura sacra, omne privilegium est veritas quia lex, ergo conclusio. Unde Augustinus, II° De Doctrina Christiana, in fine: Quicquid homo extra scripturam sacram didicerit, si noxium est ibi dampnatur, si utile est ibi invenitur; et idem dicit epistola quarta ad Volusianum (ut alibi diffuse exposui). Sequitur ergo, si quis quidquid utile extra scripturam sacram didicerit, ibi invenitur, omne privilegium concessum in cartis vel bullis est quiddam utile (ut patet ex diffinicione), ergo omne privilegium ecclesie est in scriptura sacra; ideo cum illa sit maioris auctoritatis et accepcionis quam bulle vel carte (ut dixi alias), ille ut plurimum in clero superfluunt. Nam illis ablatis inniteretur privilegiatus ministerio ecclesie quod est per se causa privilegii, et non sic confideret in [p. 174] cartis dimisso officio. Talis enim securitas confundit ecclesiam, cum telis sophisticis super equivocacione que est ypocrisis et mendacium, diabolus et membra sua concludunt ecclesie. Quis queso non decipitur hodie in adherendo parti ecclesie malignancium et colendo ipsam ut membrum ecclesie? Quis insuper non aspirat ad fastum seculi vel primatum vel privilegium, cum tamen sit sceleratissima corruptela, in tantum quod sepe incidimus in illud ve propheticum Ysaie V°, 20: Ve qui dicitis bonum malum et malum bonum. Nonne diligencia nostra ostendit quod plus appreciamus cartas et privilegia humana quam scripturam sacram et privilegia que Christus suis instituit? Et indubie ut sic cadimus a quibuscunque veris privilegiis per abusum, quia iuxta beatum Gregorium (et ponitur in decretis XI, q. III. Privilegium) privilegium debet amittere qui sua abutitur potestate. Cum ergo omne racionabile vel utile ecclesie Christi prius ab ipso conceditur et in carta vel libro suo inscribitur, patet quod scrutinio illius libri et condicioni sub qua privilegiavit debemus primo attendere. Non enim sequitur: Datus potentatus seculi concessit hoc ut privilegium date ecclesie, ergo hoc est rationabile sive verum. Ideo oportet ultra concessionem scrutari regulam per quam racio privilegii debeat iudicari. Esto, inquam, quod Petrus potentatus seculi concedat privilegium Westmonasterio sub hac forma quod quicunque quantumcunque maleficus intraverit septa sua habebit immunitatem omnimodam, sic tamen quod in prima conclusione in qua post introitum a preposito loci [p. 175] sibi opponitur dicat verum. Casus autem videtur possibilis et honestus, cum pro mendacio et pro quocunque scelere posset uti privilegio ad hoc sibi donato, ut dicitur; ergo multo magis pro confessione humili veritatis. Pono insuper quod Petrus maleficus intrans Westmonasterium pro immunitate habenda sit primo interrogatus a preposito Westmonasterii si habebit immunitatem in dicta ecclesia et pono quod Petrus conscius sibi ex dignitate contracta de crimine dicat desperando de venia vel vere reputando se dignum morte temporali, quod non habebit immunitatem in dicta ecclesia; tunc isto casu deducto ad placitum, patet quod ex casu et aliis circumstanciis positis et ponendis utraque pars contradiccionis sequitur, ut noverunt sophiste in materia insolubilium. Et revera quilibet intrans limina ecclesie, ut foveatur in crimine, incidit culpabilius in materia insolubili cuiusmodi est peccatum, quam sequitur Petrum incidere. Unde ego nescio aliter solvere nec credo alium quemquam scire, nisi quod dictum vocatum privilegium non est privilegium, quia non est fundabile in scriptura; et ita generaliter omnis lex privata non fundabilis in scriptura non est privilegium, quia non cedit ad utilitatem ecclesie, cum (ut dicit Augustinus) quicquid utile est ibi invenitur. Et si claudit contradiccionem quod hoc sit privilegium, multo magis omnis casus in quo ponitur dari privilegium loco, ut quicunque intraverit cum quocunque crimine, gaudeat immunitate date ecclesie; illud enim est impossibile plus elongatum ab apparencia quam precedens. Cum multi intrant sanctuarium cum illo peccato, racione cuius tenentur amittere libertatem, ymo cum principium Gregorii sit verum quod omnis et specialiter prescitus [p. 176] privilegium tenetur amittere qui sua abutitur potestate, et omnis criminosus ut sic sua abutitur potestate, cum racione mortalis peccati inficientis abutitur bonis nature: sequitur quod omnis criminose intrans septa ecclesie tenetur ut sic privilegiis ecclesie pro illo tempore non gaudere. Et hoc est notum illis qui sciunt quod quicquid criminosus fecerit racione peccati inficientis peccat mortaliter. Fundet ergo homo racionabilitatem privilegii ex scriptura et aliter nichil valet. Illa autem privilegia que debemus magis attendere plus relucent in speculo scripture, et minus necessaria plus confuse, quia Deus qui non potuit in privilegiis et scriptis ipsa testantibus sponse sue deficere, scivit quod palea plus diverteret ad terrena quam celestia, ideo ut gradus notificacionis scripture sit nobis pro regula, expressit privilegia spiritualia de corporalibus que adducerentur confusam adiciens disciplinam. Ex istis tercio colligitur quod summum privilegium pro statu vie foret ecclesiam esse exoneratam possessione civili temporalium, currendo libere post Christum in evangelica paupertate. Patet ex duabus conclusionibus precedentibus, nam ut sic disponitur fidelis Christo similius et paracius ad conquirendum regnum celorum. Unde et hoc privilegium notatur in ecclesia expressius, alcius et crebrius carioribus Christi discipulis (ut patet Matthei X°, Luce XIV° et Iª, Thymotei VI°); ad hoc enim copiosius sequebantur bona gracie. Confirmatur primo sic: Christus posuit vicarios suos in maxima libertate et privilegio in hoc quod regulam illam iniunxit singulariter suis apostolis, ergo conclusio. Non enim licet negare quin Christus privilegiavit suum collegium, cum dedit eis leges singulares, [p. 177] ut consilia evangelica ad maximam utilitatem sui atque ecclesie, sicut patet ex indole que inde provenerat. Ergo ex diffinicione privilegii in hoc privilegiavit suam ecclesiam. Confirmatur secundo: Ille status est perfectissimus, ut patet in VI° decretalium, cap. Exiit qui seminat, per Nicolaum III., et omne ponere viatorem in statu tam alto est proporcionaliter privilegiare ipsum, ergo conclusio. Si enim dare bona fortune que sunt abiectissima sit privilegiare datarium, multo magis dare sibi bona gracie. Item, quicunque vians exoneratus fuerit sarcina temporalium, ad propinquius, paracius atque laudabilius sequendum hynnulum cervorum salientem in montibus, ut sic est privilegiatus uberius, sed sic fuit de apostolis renunciantibus quoad civilitatem omnibus bonis suis, ergo conclusio. Non enim negabit fidelis quin possessio talis temporalium sit pondus anime, ut docet beatus Gregorius XXXI° Moralium (sicut declaravi XII° cap. huius). Unde apostoli vere reputarunt eis magnum privilegium, quod exonerati fuerunt a ministerio cotidiano in mensis fidelium (ut patet Actorum VI°), ergo multo magis exonerare eos a statu seculari plus abiecto. In hoc enim consistit excellencia status cleri super dominos seculares (ut hic supponitur), non autem in caractere ordinis, cum stat cum illo servitus abiectissima (ut patet de Scarioth), sed in hoc quod deputati sunt ut milites plus strenui ad plus meritorie peragendum bella domini et conquirendum regnum celorum. Nam Matthei V°, 3 dicitur: Beati pauperes spiritu, quoniam ipsorum est regnum celorum.

Hence, to pacify the disciples and instruct them what privilege and [priority]{g:prioritas} they should choose, he says that whoever will be prior or greater among them in heaven must be a humbler and more diligent servant here on earth (as I have shown elsewhere), in the way opposite to primacy or the honour of the world.[^1] Therefore Chrysostom says: "He has set heavenly primacy as the fruit of humility, [173] so that whoever desires heavenly primacy may follow heavenly humility here; but those who desire primacy on earth will find confusion in heaven. Therefore," he says, "let there now be no contest about primacy among the servants of Christ, nor let each of them hasten after how he may appear greater than another, but after how he may seem lower than all."[^2] And this [doctrine]{g:doctrina} would suffice the whole [church]{g:ecclesia} for the choice of privilege or primacy; nor is it possible that the church be truly pacified before this rule has been taught in practice. From these things it is drawn out that there is no true privilege of the church except in so far as it is founded on, taught by or drawn out of scripture.[^3] It is clear thus: every law useful to holy mother church is taught explicitly or implicitly in scripture; every privilege is of this kind, from what has been said; therefore the [conclusion]{g:conclusio}. It is confirmed from what has often been said: all truth is in sacred scripture; every privilege is a truth, because it is a law; therefore the conclusion. Hence Augustine, De doctrina christiana II, at the end: "Whatever a man has learned outside sacred scripture, if it is harmful it is condemned there; if it is useful it is found there"; and he says the same in the fourth letter to Volusianus (as I have expounded at length elsewhere).[^4] It follows, then: if whatever useful thing anyone has learned outside sacred scripture is found there, [and] every privilege granted in charters or bulls is something useful (as is clear from the definition), therefore every privilege of the church is in sacred scripture; therefore, since scripture is of greater authority and acceptance than bulls or charters (as I have said elsewhere), these are [for the most part]{g:ut plurimum} superfluous among the [clergy]{g:clerus}.[^5] For if they were taken away, the one privileged would lean on the ministry of the church, which is of itself the cause of the privilege, and would not so trust in [174] charters, leaving his office aside. For such security confounds the church, since with sophistical darts, upon an equivocation which is hypocrisy and a lie, the devil and his members close in upon the church.[^6] Who, I ask, is not deceived today in adhering to a part of the church of the malignant and cultivating it as a member of the church? Who, moreover, does not aspire to the pomp of the world, or to primacy or privilege, although it is a most wicked corruption, insomuch that we often fall into that prophetic woe, Isaiah 5:20: "Woe to you that call good evil, and evil good"?[^7] Does not our diligence show that we value charters and human privileges more than sacred scripture and the privileges which Christ instituted for his own? And undoubtedly, as such, we fall by abuse from whatever true privileges [we have], because according to blessed Gregory (and it is placed in the Decretum, C. 11 q. 3 c. Privilegium) "he ought to lose a privilege who abuses his power".[^8] Since, therefore, everything reasonable or useful to the church of Christ is first granted by him and inscribed in his charter or book, it is clear that we ought first to attend to the scrutiny of that book and to the condition under which he privileged. For it does not follow: a given potentate of the world granted this as a privilege to a given church, therefore it is reasonable or true. Therefore beyond the grant one must scrutinize the rule by which the ground of the privilege ought to be judged.[^9] Suppose, I say, that Peter, a potentate of the world, grants a privilege to Westminster in this form: that whoever, however much a malefactor, has entered its precincts shall have immunity of every kind; provided, however, that in the first conclusion which is put to him after his entry by the [superior]{g:prepositi} of the place [175] he speaks the truth.[^10] Now the case seems possible and honest, since he could use the privilege given him for this for a lie and for any crime whatever, as is said; therefore much more for the humble confession of the truth. I suppose, moreover, that Peter, a malefactor entering Westminster to have immunity, is first asked by the superior of Westminster whether he will have immunity in the said church; and I suppose that Peter, conscious of the desert he has contracted by his crime, says, despairing of pardon or truly reckoning himself worthy of temporal death, that he will not have immunity in the said church. Then, this case having been carried through at will, it is clear that from the case and the other circumstances posited and to be posited each part of the contradiction follows, as the sophists know in the matter of insolubles.[^11] And in truth anyone who enters the threshold of the church in order to be harboured in crime falls more culpably into an insoluble matter, of which kind is sin, than it follows that Peter falls.[^12] Hence I do not know how to solve it otherwise, nor do I believe that anyone else knows, except that the said so-called privilege is not a privilege, because it cannot be founded in scripture; and so, generally, every private law not foundable in scripture is not a privilege, because it does not turn to the profit of the church, since (as Augustine says) whatever is useful is found there. And if [it implies a contradiction]{g:claudit contradictionem} that this should be a privilege, much more every case in which a privilege is posited to be given to a place, that whoever has entered with whatever crime should enjoy immunity in a given church; for that is an impossibility further removed from appearance than the preceding.[^13] Since many enter sanctuary with that sin by reason of which they are bound to lose the liberty, indeed, since the principle of Gregory is true, that everyone, and especially one [foreknown]{g:prescitus}, [176] "is bound to lose a privilege who abuses his power", and every criminal as such abuses his power, since by reason of the mortal sin infecting him he abuses the goods of nature: it follows that everyone entering the precincts of the church criminally is bound, as such, not to enjoy the privileges of the church for that time.[^14] And this is known to those who know that whatever a criminal does, by reason of the sin infecting him he sins mortally. Let a man, therefore, found the reasonableness of a privilege on scripture, and otherwise it is worth nothing. Now those privileges to which we ought to attend more shine out more in the mirror of scripture, and the less necessary ones more [indistinctly]{g:confuse}; because God, who could not fail his [bride]{g:sponsa} in privileges and in writings that witness to them, knew that the chaff would turn aside more to earthly than to heavenly things; therefore, so that the degree of scripture's making known might be a rule for us, he expressed the spiritual privileges, adding an indistinct teaching about the bodily ones which would be adduced.[^15] From these things, thirdly, it is gathered that the highest privilege for the state of [the way]{g:via} would be for the church to be discharged of the civil possession of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, running freely after Christ in evangelical poverty.[^16] It is clear from the two preceding conclusions, for so the believer is disposed more like Christ and more ready for conquering the [kingdom]{g:regnum} of heaven. Hence also this privilege is noted in the church more expressly, more highly and more often for the dearer disciples of Christ (as is clear, Matthew 10, Luke 14 and 1 Timothy 6); for upon this the goods of grace followed more copiously.[^17] It is confirmed, first, thus: Christ placed his vicars in the greatest liberty and privilege in this, that he enjoined that rule singularly on his apostles; therefore the conclusion. For it is not lawful to deny that Christ privileged his college when he gave them singular laws, [177] such as the evangelical counsels, for the greatest profit of himself and of the church, as is clear from the [promise]{g:indoles} which came of it. Therefore, by the definition of privilege, he privileged his church in this.[^18] It is confirmed, secondly: that state is the most perfect, as is clear in the Sext, chapter Exiit qui seminat, by Nicholas III; and to place a wayfarer in so high a state is proportionally to privilege him; therefore the conclusion. For if to give the goods of fortune, which are the most abject, is to privilege the recipient, much more to give him the goods of grace.[^19] Again, whatever [wayfarer]{g:viator} has been discharged of the burden of temporal goods, to follow more nearly, more readily and more laudably the young hart leaping upon the mountains, is as such more richly privileged; now so it was with the apostles, who renounced, as to civil possession, all their goods; therefore the conclusion.[^20] For a believer will not deny that such possession of temporal goods is a weight on the soul, as blessed Gregory teaches, Moralia XXXI (as I declared in chapter 12 of this [work]). Hence the apostles truly reckoned it a great privilege to themselves that they were discharged of the daily ministry at the tables of the faithful (as is clear, Acts 6); therefore much more [is it a privilege] to discharge them from the secular state, which is more abject.[^21] For in this consists the excellence of the state of the clergy over the secular lords (as is here supposed): not in the character of order, since the most abject servitude stands with that (as is clear of Iscariot), but in this, that they are deputed as more vigorous [knights]{g:milites} to wage more meritoriously the wars of the Lord and to conquer the kingdom of heaven. For in Matthew 5:3 it is said: "Blessed are the poor in spirit: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven."[^22]

## Notes

[^1]: **S/C.** A paraphrase of Matt. 20:26–27. *Prioritatem*: GLOSSARY "priority", paired with *prior*. *(ut alias ostendi modo opposito) quoad primatum vel honorem seculi*: we take *modo opposito* with what follows: humility here is the way opposite to worldly primacy. Read the other way, it gives "as I have shown elsewhere in the opposite manner".

[^2]: **S/R.** Pseudo-Chrysostom, *Opus imperfectum in Matthaeum*, hom. 35 (Loserth to 172.30: "l.c., pag. CLIII C"; LEDGER E08e-1). A margin *Crisostomus* (172.30). B's second-hand gloss at 172.9, *Primatus est fructus humilitatis*, takes up this sentence.

[^3]: **T.** B lacks *inter* (173.4). A has two margin notes: *Honor scripture, quia omnis veritas in ea continetur* (173.13) and *Secunda conclusio* (173.16). *De quanto*: "in so far as". Side-note 173.11.

[^4]: **S.** Augustine, *De doctrina christiana* II.42 (II.42.63, PL 34:65: *quidquid homo extra didicerit, si noxium est, ibi damnatur; si utile est, ibi
    invenitur*; Loserth: "cap. XLII, Opp. III, 1, 44"; LEDGER E08e-2); *scripturam sacram* is Wyclif's gloss. "The fourth letter to Volusianus": Loserth refers to the letter to Volusianus without locating it. The passage is not in *Ep.* 132, 135 or 137 (PL 33); untraced (LEDGER E08e-3). "Elsewhere" is not identified. A margin *Augustinus* (173.18).

[^5]: **T/C.** B reads *si quis est, quis est, quid utile* (173.18–19). The sentence is loosely built: we take *si … invenitur* as the major premiss, and we supply [and] before the minor. *Illa*: scripture. *Ille*: the bulls and charters. B reads *acceptacionis* (173.28). A margin *Correlarium* (173.28).

[^6]: **T/C.** Loserth queries *ecclesie* at 174.4 and suggests *confundunt ecclesiam*. The print, *concludunt ecclesie*, is translated: *concludere* is used here in its physical sense, "to shut in", not its logical one. A margin *Conclusio* (174.1). Side-note 174.1.

[^7]: **S/T.** *Ecclesie malignancium*: Ps. 25:5 (DR "the assembly of the malignant"). We keep "church" for *ecclesia*. Isa. 5:20: Wyclif has *bonum malum et malum bonum*, the reverse of the Vulgate's order (*malum bonum, et bonum malum*). His order is translated. B reads *Cum tamen scit* (174.8).

[^8]: **S/T.** *Decretum* C.11 q.3 c.63 (Loserth: "Dec. sec. pars, causa XI, q. III, c. LXIII"; the modern form is ours; LEDGER E08e-4). Whether the canon's wording matches Wyclif's is to be checked. A margin *Conclusio* (174.15).

[^9]: **T/C.** The codices read *condicione*; Loserth prints *condicioni* (174.20–21), translated. *Datus … date*: "a given", the logician's placeholder. *Racio privilegii*: the ground of the privilege. A margin *De privilegiis monasteriorum et ecclesiarum* (174.22).

[^10]: **C/R.** The case is set out in the manner of the *obligationes*. *In prima conclusione … sibi opponitur*: the first proposition put to him for his answer. "Peter" is the logicians' placeholder name. Whether Westminster is chosen because of the "case which lately befell" of 165.15 (08-c), a contemporary dispute over its sanctuary, is to be checked (LEDGER E08e-5). A margin *Casus* (174.27). Side-note 174.27.

[^11]: **T/C.** A₁ would correct to *ex gravitate contracta* (175.6). The print's *dignitate*, which construes as "desert" (what he deserves, *dignum morte*), is translated. A and A₁ lack *casu et* (175.13). *Deducto ad placitum*: "carried through at will", the obligational formula. *Insolubilia*: the paradoxes of self-reference: if Peter's answer is true he has immunity, so it is false, and so on.

[^12]: **T/C.** Loserth judges *in materia insolubili* (175.17) superfluous, a margin gloss fallen into the text, but he prints it; it is translated. B reads *peccatum incidere* (175.18). *Quam sequitur Petrum incidere*: "than Peter, by the inference, falls [into the insoluble]".

[^13]: **T.** A margin *Conclusio* (175.23). B lacks *immunitate … elongatum* (175.28–30). *Impossibile … elongatum ab apparencia*: an impossibility that does not even look possible. Side-note 175.21.

[^14]: **T/S.** A₁ would correct *criminose* to *criminosus* (176.4). The print is translated: the adverb, "criminally". A has the margin notes *Responsio* (176.4) and *Nota* (176.8). Gregory's principle is the canon of n. 8 (LEDGER E08e-4).

[^15]: **T/C.** A and A₁ read *Non ut* (176.15). A and A₁ read *adicerentur* for *adducerentur* (176.17). *Expressit … disciplinam*: we take *de corporalibus* with *confusam … disciplinam*. It could instead go with *expressit*, "he expressed the spiritual privileges by means of bodily ones". *Palea*: the chaff, the worldly part of the church.

[^16]: **T.** A has the margin notes *Tercia conclusio* (176.19) and *Responsio. Paupertas cleri* (176.22). *Currendo … post Christum*: cf. Song 1:3. Side-note 176.19.

[^17]: **S/C.** The chapters only, as printed: Matt. 10 (the sending of the twelve), Luke 14 (cf. 14:33), 1 Tim. 6 (cf. 6:8). *Carioribus … discipulis*: a dative, "for (or to) the dearer disciples".

[^18]: **C.** *Vicarios suos*: the apostles. *Indoles*: the promise or growth that came of it (GLOSSARY, as at 164.1). A margin *Optimum privilegium ecclesie est paupertas cleri* (177.5).

[^19]: **T/S.** Loserth prints *alto est*; the codices read *alto ut* (177.7–8). *Sext* 5.12.3, *Exiit qui seminat* (Loserth: "Lib. V. tit. XII, cap. III"; the modern form is ours; LEDGER E08e-6). Side-note 177.5.

[^20]: **S.** *Hynnulum cervorum salientem in montibus* joins Song 2:17 ("a young hart upon the mountains", Loserth's reference) with 2:8 ("leaping upon the mountains"), both DR. A margin *Sequela Christi* (177.11). *Quoad civilitatem*: as to civil possession.

[^21]: **S/T.** Gregory, *Moralia* XXXI (Loserth: "cap. XIII, Opp. tom. I, 1006"; LEDGER E08e-7). Loserth notes that "chapter 12 of this [work]" should rather be chapter 3 (pp. 50–54 above). The print is translated. A margin *Gregorius* (177.18). Acts 6:2. B lacks *a* (177.22).

[^22]: **T/C.** *Caracter ordinis*: the sacramental character of orders, which Judas also had. B reads *belli domini et conquirendo* (177.28). *Milites* is the estate's word, "knights" (GLOSSARY), in keeping with the battle figure of 169.15–28. Matt. 5:3 DR.
