# *De potestate pape*, Compendium, part 2: the compendium, chapters eight to eleven (breaks off)

## ¶1 · 402.34–403.23

> [p. 402] Capitulum octavum declarat primo de nomine pape, quomodo inolevit, secundo declarat tripliciter [p. 403] quod non propter capitalitatem Rome oportet generaliter eius episcopum esse papam. Tercio probat tripliciter quod non propter mutuum iuvamen cleri et milicie oportet papam esse cum cesare, et convincit triplici testimonio mendacium, fingens quod non a cesare sed Christo vel Petro ecclesia est dotata. Quarto probat tripliciter, quod non ex loci sanctitate oportet Romanum pontificem esse papam. Quinto tollit tripliciter secundum fucum, quo fingitur quod per institucionem Petri Clemens et generaliter Romani pontifices forent pape. Sexto tollitur tripliciter tercia ficticia, qua fingitur quod eleccione humana oportet Romanum pontificem esse papam. Septimo narrat ex cronicis quomodo regula et preeminencia pape originabatur a cesare et irreligiose ac fraudulenter continuabatur a brachio seculari. Octavo narrat tres condiciones Antichristi ex testimonio Augustini. Nono narrat quomodo tres pape occisi sunt a beatis et tercius papatu deposito gloriose occiditur a tyranno. Decimo narrando pericula Romani pontificis exponit quomodo debemus tempus redimere.

The eighth chapter declares, first, about the name of [pope]{g:papa}, how it grew up; second, it declares in three ways [403] that it is not on account of Rome's [capital standing]{g:capitalitas} that its [bishop]{g:episcopus} must as a rule be pope.[^1] Third, it proves in three ways that it is not on account of the mutual help of the [clergy]{g:clerus} and the [knighthood]{g:milicia} that the pope must be with Caesar; and it convicts by a threefold testimony the lie that feigns that the church was endowed not by Caesar but by Christ or Peter.[^2] Fourth, it proves in three ways that it is not from the holiness of the place that the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex} must be pope. Fifth, it removes in three ways the second pretence, by which it is feigned that by Peter's institution Clement, and the Roman pontiffs generally, would be popes. Sixth, the third [fiction]{g:ficticia} is removed in three ways, by which it is feigned that by human election the Roman pontiff must be pope. Seventh, it recounts from the chronicles how the rule and [preeminence]{g:preeminencia} of the pope took its origin from Caesar, and was continued irreligiously and fraudulently by [the secular arm]{g:brachium-seculare}. Eighth, it recounts three conditions of Antichrist from the testimony of Augustine.[^3] Ninth, it recounts how three popes were slain by the blessed, and the third, having laid down the [papacy]{g:papatus}, is gloriously slain by a tyrant.[^4] Tenth, recounting the perils of the Roman pontiff, it expounds how we ought to "redeem the time".[^5]

## ¶2 · 403.24–404.10

> [p. 403] Capitulum nonum declarat primo quomodo erit necessario unus papa, et declarat tripliciter, quomodo ritus eligendi papam cesaree introductus est nocivus ecclesie: primo racione novelli et infundabilis cetus cardinalium, secundo per hoc quod non licet recedere a ritu quem Christus instituit, in quo declarat sextupliciter quod idem est episcopus et sacerdos, et tercio per hoc quod occasione eleccionis officium predicandi suspenditur, in quo narrando de quantitate potestatis episcopi declarat primo quam vanum est pompare de preeminencia huiusmodi potestatis, secundo [p. 404] quod stul tum est pompare de dignitate status, et tercio quod non est pompacio ex titulo sanctitatis, et exponens diffuse descripcionem sextuplicem pseudochristi ex epistola Yude exponit tripliciter quid dicit Crysostomus de primatu, et confirmando per legem triplicem quod humana eleccio non facit membrum ecclesie, inculcat quomodo sedes mutantur, quomodo oportet adorare Deum in spiritu et veritate et quomodo abhominacio desolacionis catholice potest intelligi.

The ninth chapter declares, first, how there will of necessity be one pope; and it declares in three ways how the [rite]{g:ritus} of electing a pope, introduced in caesarean fashion, is harmful to the [church]{g:ecclesia}: first, by reason of the novel college of cardinals, which is [without foundation]{g:infundabilis}; second, by appeal to this, that it is not lawful to depart from the rite which Christ instituted, in which it declares in six ways that bishop and [priest]{g:sacerdos} are the same; and third, by appeal to this, that by occasion of the election the office of preaching is [suspended]{g:suspendere}; in which, recounting the extent of a bishop's [power]{g:potestas}, it declares, first, how vain it is to [boast]{g:pompare} of the preeminence of such power; second, [404] that it is foolish to boast of the dignity of [one's] [state]{g:status}; and third, that there is no [ground for] boasting from a [title]{g:titulus} of holiness.[^6] And, expounding at length the sixfold [definition]{g:descripcio} of a [false christ]{g:pseudochristus} from the epistle of Jude, it expounds in three ways what Chrysostom says of the [primacy]{g:primatus};[^7] and, confirming by a threefold law that human election does not make a [member]{g:membrum} of the church, it impresses how sees are changed, how one "must adore" God "in spirit and in truth", and how the "abomination of desolation" can be understood in a catholic sense.[^8]

## ¶3 · 404.12–405.22

> [p. 404] Capitulum decimum arguit sextupliciter contra dicta: primo per hoc, quod sine tali dominio non sufficeret quis ad papale officium, et narrando cronicam de irregulari peticione subsidii cuiusdam pape de ecclesia Anglicana respondet detegendo versuciam. Secundo obicit per hoc quod papa habet illum primatum immediate a Deo, ut allegat decretum XXII distinccione; et respondet declarans quomodo papa ex dotacione insolubiliter involvitur et quomodo papa excidit a privilegio conferendi dignitates ecclesie per hoc quod preficit indignos, et tercio narrans de absolucione a pena et a culpa declarat quomodo in isto credi debet doctoribus, et obiciendo dissolvit. Tercio obicit principaliter per hoc quod multi sancti episcopi et seculares approbarunt dotacionem cesaream; et respondet, dicens quod in hoc peccaverant plus vel minus; ideo cum solus Christus vel christianus secundum racionem qua sequitur Christum est sequendus patet, quod non sequitur, si sancti qui peccaverunt continue sic fecerunt, tunc in hoc est licitum sequi illos, et respondet ad obiectum quo fingitur quod sancti non penituerunt de isto delicto, et ad obiectum [p. 405] quo fingitur quod melius foret corrigere cleri cupidinem, et declarat primo quomodo debemus in Deo confidere, secundo quomodo Deus dat dupliciter bona sua et tercio quomodo est dare duplicem clericorum ordinem, scilicet cesareum et divinum, Quartum argumentum tangit de iuramento facto Romane ecclesie et dissolvens tangit perplexitatem ecclesie atque remedium istorum paparum. Quintum argumentum obicit per decretum Jeronymi de excellencia Romane ecclesie, et declarans quid est Romana ecclesia docet quod Jeronymus credibiliter et reputative locutus est, et narrando de novellis heresibus et fatuis earum inposicionibus ponit quid est et qualiter senciendum de istis papis. Sextum argumentum est dictum floridis Lynconiensis de preeminencia pape et cardinalium quod primo exponit, secundo docet tripliciter quod non est tam necessarius articulus fidei credere isti pape, in quo declarat quid est fides. Tercio probat tripliciter quod licet subditis papam corripere; et tollit obiectum triplicem.

The tenth chapter argues in six ways against what has been said: first, by appeal to this, that without such [lordship]{g:dominium} no one would suffice for the papal office; and, recounting a chronicle of a certain pope's irregular petition for a subsidy from the English church, it answers by [exposing]{g:detegere} the cunning.[^9] Second, it objects by appeal to this, that the pope has that primacy immediately from God, as it [alleges]{g:allegare} the Decretum, distinction 22; and it answers, declaring how the pope is inextricably entangled by the [endowment]{g:dotacio}, and how the pope falls from the [privilege]{g:privilegium} of conferring the dignities of the church by the fact that he sets the unworthy over [it]; and third, recounting [the matter] of absolution from penalty and from fault, it declares how in this the doctors ought to be believed; and, raising objections, it resolves them.[^10] Third, as a main head, it objects by appeal to this, that many holy bishops and [seculars]{g:secularis} approved the caesarean endowment; and it answers, saying that in this they had sinned more or less. Therefore, since Christ alone, or a Christian in the respect in which he follows Christ, is to be followed, it is clear that it does not [follow]{g:sequitur}: "if the saints who sinned did so continually, then in this it is lawful to follow them".[^11] And it answers the [objection]{g:objectum} by which it is feigned that the saints did not repent of this offence, and the objection [405] by which it is feigned that it would be better to correct the greed of the clergy; and it declares, first, how we ought to trust in God; second, how God gives his goods in two ways; and third, how one may posit a twofold order of [clerics]{g:clericus}, namely the [caesarean and the divine]{g:ordo-cesareus}.[^12] The fourth argument touches on the oath made to the Roman church; and, resolving it, it touches on the [perplexity]{g:perplexitas} of the church and the remedy for these popes.[^13] The fifth argument objects by appeal to the decree of Jerome on the excellence of the Roman church; and, declaring what the Roman church is, it teaches that Jerome spoke credibly and [by estimation]{g:reputative}; and, recounting the novel heresies and their foolish impositions, it sets out what is [the case] and how one should think about these popes.[^14] The sixth argument is the saying of Lincoln, from the *Flores*, on the preeminence of the pope and the cardinals; which it first expounds; second, it teaches in three ways that to believe this pope is not so necessary an [article of faith]{g:articulus-fidei}, in which it declares what faith is.[^15] Third, it proves in three ways that it is lawful for subjects to [reprove]{g:corripere} the pope; and it removes a threefold objection.

## ¶4 · 405.23–405.32

> [p. 405] Capitulum undecimum obicit primo per hoc quod de lege nature et lege veteri oportet unicum esse rectorem principalem ecclesie, et dissolvit concedendo conclusionem. Sed pro materia docet primo quomodo necesse est esse papam cesareum, et distinguens de duplici peccato ordinis, adducit triplex testimonium Bernhardi qualis debeat esse papa, et describens sacerdotem cesareum, dicit quod foret expediens ecclesie nullum talem esse.

The eleventh chapter objects, first, by appeal to this, that by the law of nature and the old law there must be one sole principal ruler of the church; and it resolves it by granting the [conclusion]{g:conclusio}. But as to the matter, it teaches, first, how it is necessary that there be a [caesarean pope]{g:papa-cesareus}; and, distinguishing a twofold sin of order, it adduces a threefold testimony of Bernard as to what the pope ought to be; and, describing the [caesarean priest]{g:sacerdos-cesarius}, it says that it would be expedient for the church that there be none such.[^16]

## ¶5 · 405.33–406.10

> [p. 405] Et obiciendo contra descripcionem datam de papa, declarat quomodo oportet rite electum ab homine a Deo preeligi, et declarat tripliciter quomodo [p. 406] turpatur argucia concludendo eleccionem pape cesarei ex elleccione summi sacerdotis veteris testamenti, primo racione libertatis ecclesie, qua legalia cessare debuerant, secundo quomodo potestates ordinum in cunctis sacerdotibus sunt equales, et tercio quomodo potestas iurisdicionis racionabiliter restringi poterat et tam restringi quam laxari irracionabiliter ad onus ecclesie, secundo principaliter arguit per hoc quod ex officiis et ornamentis sacerdotum

And, objecting against the definition given of the pope, it declares how one duly elected by man must be pre-elected by God; and it declares in three ways how [406] the [quibble]{g:argucia} is disgraced that [infers]{g:concludere} the election of the caesarean pope from the election of the high priest of the old testament: first, by reason of the liberty of the church, by which the [legal precepts]{g:legalia} ought to have ceased; second, how the powers of orders are equal in all priests; and third, how the [power of jurisdiction]{g:potestas-iurisdiccionis} could reasonably be restricted, and could be both restricted and [loosed]{g:laxare} unreasonably, to the burden of the church. Second, as a main head, it argues by appeal to this, that from the offices and ornaments of the priests …[^17]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** The subject throughout is the chapter ("it declares"). *Generaliter*: "as a rule", i.e. in every case. *Capitalitas*: Rome's standing as capital city.
[^2]: **C.** *Mendacium, fingens*: the nominative participle has no fitting noun; we take it with *mendacium*, "the lie that feigns" (as *quo fingitur* below). The print has no stop before *Quarto*.
[^3]: **S.** Augustine, *Sermo* 129 on John 5 (*De verbis Domini* 45 in Wyclif's numbering; PL 38, 720–25), from which the treatise's ch. 8 draws its testimonies to Antichrist's disciples (187–189; LEDGER P08f-2, P08f-3, P08g-1; P13b-1).
[^4]: **C.** *Occisi sunt a beatis*: "slain by the blessed", i.e. by saints, as the treatise tells it (ch. 8, 190–191): Sabinian, struck down in a vision by Gregory (Higden, *Polychronicon* V, 412), and Innocent IV, struck by the dead Grosseteste (Higden VIII, 240–42), whom Wyclif excuses of irregularity *ex percussione letali summorum pontificum*. The third is Marcellinus, who laid down the papacy and was martyred under Diocletian (Decretum D.21 c.7 *Nunc autem*) (LEDGER P08g-5, P08g-6, P08g-7; P13b-2).
[^5]: **S.** Eph. 5:16 (DR "redeeming the time, because the days are evil").
[^6]: **C.** *Cesaree introductus*: "introduced in caesarean fashion", i.e. by the emperor's order, the *cesareus* family (pejorative). *Occasione eleccionis*: the occasion, not the cause. *Quantitate potestatis*: the extent, not a measured quantity.
[^7]: **S/T.** Jude 12–13 (DR "clouds without water … trees of the autumn, unfruitful, twice dead"), the figures of the false teachers. Pseudo-Chrysostom, *Opus imperfectum in Matthaeum*, hom. 35 on Matt. 20:27 (PG 56, 829–30; Wyclif's "Homily 28"), quoted in the treatise's ch. 9 at 210–213 (LEDGER P09f-2, P09f-4; P13b-3). For *ex epistola* (404.4) B appears to read *et epistola*; the print is followed.
[^8]: **S/C.** John 4:24 (DR "they that adore him must adore him in spirit and in truth"); John 4:21 on the place of worship may lie behind *sedes mutantur*. Matt. 24:15 (DR "the abomination of desolation"). *Catholice*: "in a catholic sense".
[^9]: **T/S.** The manuscripts read *irregularia*; Loserth prints *irregulari* (apparatus 404.15). The chronicle is the *Flores historiarum*, s.a. 1226 (ed. Luard, II, 184): the nuncio Otto's letters asking a subsidy of the English church, as told in the treatise's ch. 10 at 223–224 (LEDGER P10b-1; P13b-4). *Detegere*: "expose".
[^10]: **S/C.** *Ut allegat decretum*: we take *decretum* as the object, "as it cites the Decretum", the objection being the subject; "as the decree alleges" is possible. Gratian, *Decretum* D. 22 c. 2 *Sacrosancta* (Anacletus, Pseudo-Isidore: *non ab apostolis sed ab ipso Domino primatum obtinuit*; PL 187, 123A–124A), the canon the treatise's ch. 10 cites at 228 (modern form ours; LEDGER P10c-1; P13b-5). *A pena et a culpa*: the formula of the plenary indulgence.
[^11]: **C.** The print's *patet; quod non sequitur;* is read as one clause, *patet quod non sequitur*: what does not follow is the inference that follows the colon. *Secundum racionem qua*: "in the respect in which".
[^12]: **C.** *Est dare*: the scholastic "one may posit", "there is to be granted". *Ordo cesareus / divinus*: the caesarean / divine order of clerics.
[^13]: **C.** *Remedium istorum paparum*: the genitive may be objective, "the remedy for (against) these popes", or subjective, "the remedy these popes offer"; we take the first.
[^14]: **S/G.** Jerome in the Decretum, C. 24 q. 1 c. 14 (*Haec est fides*, PL 187, 1269B; under Jerome's name, though the text is Pelagius' *Libellus fidei*), cited in the treatise's ch. 10 at 250 (LEDGER P10h-2, P10h-3; P13b-6). *Reputative*: "by estimation", as opposed to by revelation.
[^15]: **T/S.** *Floridis*: Loserth, "Rectius: Florum Historiarum. Vide supra p. 223", i.e. the *Flores historiarum*, the chronicle the treatise names at 223. But the treatise draws Lincoln's saying on the pope and the cardinals from Grosseteste's own letters 36 and 35 (ed. Luard, pp. 125, 123), and at 258–259 says he flattered the pope *verbis floridis*, "with flowery words", just before *tam necessarius articulus fidei*. *Dictum floridis* may rather echo that phrase, "the flowery saying" (as if *floridum*); we keep Loserth's correction (LEDGER P10j-5, P10k-3; P13b-7).
[^16]: **S/C.** *Sed* is adversative here: the chapter grants the conclusion but turns to its own matter. Bernard, *De consideratione* IV.7.23, II.7.15 and III.1.1–2 (PL 182, 788A, 750C, 758B–C), the three passages quoted in the treatise's ch. 11 at 266–268 (LEDGER P11a-1, P11a-2, P11a-4; P13b-8). *Quomodo necesse est*: "how", i.e. in what sense, it is necessary.
[^17]: **T.** The compendium breaks off here. B: *sacerdotum etc. Cetera desunt*, "the rest is wanting" (apparatus 406.10).
