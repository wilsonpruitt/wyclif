# sed/autem extract, wave 10: chunks 04-h 04-i 05-a

### 04-h ¶1 · 110.22–111.3  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 110] In cuius evidenciam glosa ordinaria in De Consecracione, distinccione IIᵃ, super capitulo Sacerdotes dicit quod hostia ista est ponderositas **sed** non est ponderosum. **Sed** omnes tres vie predicte dicunt sacramentum aliquid esse ponderosum; et patet quod false intelligentes de hoc sacramento equivocant et [p. 111] errant tanquam ydolatre false senciendo de Eukaristia, cum false adorant hoc ut Deum in signo, ac si homo adoret dyabolum in similitudine crucifixi.

ENGLISH: As evidence of this, the ordinary [gloss]{g:glosa} on *De consecratione*, distinction 2, on the chapter *Sacerdotes*, says that this [host]{g:hostia} is [heaviness]{g:ponderositas} but is not heavy.[^1][^2] But all three ways aforesaid say that the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} is something heavy; and it is clear that those who understand falsely about this sacrament equivocate and [111] err like idolaters, [holding]{g:sentire} falsely about the eucharist, since they falsely worship this as God in the [sign]{g:signum}, as if a man were to worship the devil in the likeness of the crucified.

### 04-h ¶2 · 111.4–112.3  (6 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 111] Nec dubium quin error in talibus accusat false adorantem; et patet quod necessarium est tractare materiam istam de eukaristia, quia indubie altera istarum viarum est manifeste heretica, cum in precipua scriptura, quia in verbis Domini quibus sacratur hostia tenet altera istarum viarum valde pertinaciter sensum autori scripture contrarium et dimittit doctrinam Christi in valde necessariis ad salutem et imponit sibi implicite auctorisacionem abhominacionis pessime, licet doctores famosi legum ecclesie parvipendentes recitent inter opiniones quod panis et vinum remanent post consecracionem; ad quod arguunt **sed** non inprobant, ut non possunt, licet parti false magis faveant. Si ergo illi precipui doctores legum ecclesie non condempnant tamquam hereticum quod panis remanet, **sed** probant quod hoc sequitur ex lege predicta Ego Beringarius, verisimile est (ut prius) quod doctores impericiores non temptabunt hereticare istam sentenciam; **sed** quomodocunque temptaverunt scio quod non possunt, cum non possunt probare istam sentenciam esse falsam. Non ergo refert in quo vase servatis moribus sacerdos sanctificet, **sed** (ut dictum est) standum est appropriate consuetudini patrie et servata honestate de Christo virtuose recolere, hoc enim est infinitum melius quam celebrare. Nec dissonat quod Christus sit sacramentaliter in aqua vel alio mixto vino, ymmo in medio aereo **sed** preeminenter in anima, cum finis huius sacramenti sit quod Christus inhabitet animam per virtutes, sic quod laycus memorans [p. 112] corpus Christi in celo, melius quam iste sacerdos conficiens facit efficacius et eque vere **sed** modo alio corpus Christi esse cum illo.

ENGLISH: Nor is there doubt that error in such things accuses the one who worships falsely; and it is clear that it is necessary to treat this matter of the eucharist, because without doubt one of these ways is manifestly heretical, since in a principal scripture, namely in the words of the Lord by which the host is [hallowed]{g:sacrare}, one of these ways holds very obstinately a sense contrary to the author of scripture, and lays aside the [doctrine]{g:doctrina} of Christ in things very necessary to salvation, and lays upon itself implicitly the authorization of a most wicked abomination; although famous doctors of the [laws of the church]{g:leges-ecclesie}, making little of it, [recite]{g:recitare} among the [opinions]{g:opinio} that [bread]{g:panis} and [wine]{g:vinum} remain after [consecration]{g:consecracio}; they argue towards it but do not disprove it, as they cannot, although they favour the false side more.[^3] If therefore those leading doctors of the laws of the church do not condemn as heretical that the bread remains, but prove that this [follows]{g:sequitur} from the aforesaid law *Ego Berengarius*, it is likely (as before) that less skilled doctors will not attempt to [pronounce heretical]{g:hereticare} this [teaching]{g:sentencia};[^4] but however they have attempted it, I know that they cannot, since they cannot prove this teaching to be false.[^5] Therefore it does not matter in what vessel, good conduct being kept, the [priest]{g:sacerdos} sanctifies; but (as has been said) one must stand by the appropriate [custom]{g:consuetudo} of the [homeland]{g:patria} and, decency being kept, call Christ to mind virtuously, for this is infinitely better than to celebrate.[^6] Nor is it out of tune that Christ should be [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} in water or in another [liquid] mixed with wine, indeed in the medium of the air, but preeminently in the soul, since the end of this sacrament is that Christ should dwell in the soul through the [virtues]{g:virtus};[^7] so that a layman calling to mind [112] the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} in heaven, better than such a priest [consecrating]{g:conficere}, makes, more efficaciously and equally [truly]{g:vere} but in another mode, the body of Christ to be with him.[^8]

### 04-h ¶3 · 112.4–112.7  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 112] **Sed** vulgus credit nimis infideliter et blaspheme quod hoc signum sacramentale corporis Christi sit realiter ipse Christus. Et in ista heresi involvuntur clerici et prelati.

ENGLISH: But the common people believe, [exceedingly]{g:nimis} unfaithfully and blasphemously, that this sacramental sign of the body of Christ is [really]{g:realiter} Christ himself. And in this heresy the [clerics]{g:clericus} and [prelates]{g:prelatus} are entangled.

### 04-h ¶4 · 112.8–112.10  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 112] Et alii racione victi hoc esse blasphemum dicunt quod non est populo tangendum, **sed** summe inpossibile est ad excitandum devocionem populo predicandum.

ENGLISH: And others, overcome by reason, say that this is blasphemous, a thing not to be touched on before the people, but that what is supremely impossible is to be preached to the people to stir up devotion.[^9]

### 04-h ¶5 · 112.12–112.18  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 112] **Sed** veritas novit si cupiditas honoris et lucri a laycis sit in causa huius blasphemie. **Sed** nec istis obstat **sed** consonat et expedit devotis celebrare frequenter, cum ille sit valde indispositus, cui non ex celebracione plus et devocius imprimitur memoria Christi et per consequens ut oracio sua prosit ecclesie; quod si deest expedit quod talis tunc non conficiat.

ENGLISH: But the truth knows whether greed for honour and gain from the laity is the cause of this blasphemy. But it does not stand against these things, rather it accords with them, that it is expedient for the devout to celebrate often, since that man is very [unfit]{g:indispositus} in whom the memory of Christ is not, from celebrating, imprinted more and more devoutly, and in consequence so that his prayer may profit the [church]{g:ecclesia}; and if this is lacking, it is expedient that such a one should not then consecrate.

### 04-h ¶6 · 112.19–112.30  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 112] Unde expedit populo habere multos devotos presbiteros, **sed** nocet eis multum habere presbiteros viciosos; unde falsum est quod tantum valet missa cuiuscunque presbiteri, ut missa alicuius, cum missa sit aggregatum ex toto ministerio presbiteri celebrantis a signo usque ad signum; quod vulgus mensurat penes tempus quo sacerdos plene vestitur usque ad tempus quo casulam est exutus. Patet sic: Una talis missa est dampnabilis et Deo odibilis et alia est meritoria et Deo acceptabilis; ergo sunt valde dispares in valore; patet ex hoc quod ut sacerdos est Deo deaccepcior est ministracio sua iniustior.

ENGLISH: Hence it is expedient for the people to have many devout [presbyters]{g:presbiter}, but it harms them much to have vicious presbyters; hence it is false that the mass of any presbyter whatever is worth as much as the mass of anyone [else], since the mass is the aggregate of the whole ministry of the celebrating presbyter from sign to sign; which the common people measure by the time from when the priest is fully vested to the time when he has put off the chasuble. It is clear thus: one such mass is damnable and hateful to God, and another is meritorious and acceptable to God; therefore they are very unequal in value. This is clear from the fact that the more [disowned]{g:deacceptus} by God the priest is, the more unjust is his ministration.

### 04-h ¶7 · 112.31–113.10  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 112] Nec valet argucia qua arguitur quod missa illa est Christus vel eius pars qui est utrobique eque bonus, [p. 113] quia nimis equivocatur in quidditate misse. Non enim habet quilibet presbiter auctoritatem mittendi corpus Christi ad celum vel trahendi ipsum ad populum nec facit corpus Christi **sed** missam celebrat post et ante, ymmo tantum valet Christus existens in medio laycorum qui in suo nomine congregantur sicut ipse Christus per quemcunque sacerdotem ubilibet factus presens; ideo nimis leve foret dicere quod bonitas misse attenditur penes bonitatem Christi vel partis sue, quia tunc nimis bona foret generaliter missa presbiteri.

ENGLISH: Nor does the [quibble]{g:argucia} hold by which it is argued that that mass is Christ, or his part, who is equally good in both places, [113] for there is exceeding equivocation about the quiddity of the mass. For not every presbyter has [authority]{g:auctoritas} to send the body of Christ to heaven or to draw him to the people, nor does he make the body of Christ, but he celebrates the mass after and before; indeed Christ existing in the midst of laymen who are gathered in his name is worth as much as Christ himself made present anywhere by any priest whatever; therefore it would be exceedingly frivolous to say that the goodness of the mass is reckoned by the goodness of Christ or of his part, because then the mass of a presbyter would in general be exceedingly good.[^10]

### 04-h ¶8 · 113.11–113.21  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 113] **Sed** de paritate bonitatis hostie est difficultas, cum ipsa sit signum Christi et non pars eius; cum itaque illa hostia significat inter alia unionem Christi ad ecclesiam et illam efficacius significat signum unius facientis illam unionem quam signum illud quod impedit unionem, ergo efficacius est sacramentum huiusmodi in sacerdote bono quam malo; et sic intelliguntur decreta multa que mandant quod homo non audiat missam presbiteri notorie fornicantis; ideo, inquam, non est audienda, quia est Deo et homini odibilis et nociva ecclesie.

ENGLISH: But about the equality of the host's goodness there is a difficulty, since it is the sign of Christ and not his part; since therefore that host signifies, among other things, the [union]{g:unio} of Christ to the church, and the sign of one who makes that union signifies it more efficaciously than that sign which hinders the union, therefore a sacrament of this kind is more efficacious in a good priest than in a bad one; and so are understood many [decrees]{g:decretum} which command that a man should not hear the mass of a notoriously fornicating presbyter; therefore, I say, it is not to be heard, because it is hateful to God and man and harmful to the church.[^11]

### 04-h ¶9 · 113.22–113.28  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 113] Ex quibus videtur quod nedum missa **sed** hostia est melior in uno presbitero et in alio minus bona; nam quam efficax signum est ad memorandum memorie vitam Christi tam bonum sacramentum est, **sed** in uno sacerdote est ad hoc efficacius signum et in alio minus efficax: ergo sic est proporcionaliter melius sacramentum.

ENGLISH: From which it seems that [not only]{g:nedum} the mass but the host is better in one presbyter and less good in another; for as efficacious a sign as it is for calling the life of Christ to memory, so good a sacrament it is; now in one priest it is a more efficacious sign for this, and in another less efficacious: therefore in this way it is proportionally a better sacrament.

### 04-i ¶1 · 113.29–115.4  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 113] Distinguat, inquam, katholicus inter bonitatem naturalem hostie que est par in alio pane non sacrato ac bonitatem eius moralem, et videbit quod illam potest suscipere magis et minus secundum meritum et sanctitatem vite presbiteri; non enim per se capit [p. 114] sanctitatem ex presencia humanitatis Christi, quia sic Scarioth cum Christo comedens et presbiter iniquus sacramentaliter Christum in hostia accipiens foret sanctus; ipse enim est capax maioris sanctitatis quam res inanimata que habet in se equivoce sanctitatem, sicut aqua baptismi habet in se graciam tamquam signum vel occasionaliter efficiens graciam in persona que communicat sacramento, sic quod sanctitas quam habet ex presencia corporis Christi videtur esse par in cunctis eukaristiis, **sed** sanctitas orta ex benediccione presbiteri videtur correspondenter ad suum meritum variari, et per distinccionem intelligantur leges que videntur contrarie. Sicut enim oracio dominica quoad eius naturam est eque bona, a quocunque dicatur, sic est de sacramento, **sed** ista habet efficaciam et bonitatem. Bona enim inanimata quibus homo utitur ex virtuoso usu contrahunt bonitatem; quomodo ergo sacramentum quo iustus utitur ut instrumento merendi super aliis non habet bonitatem respectivam ultra sacramentum quo dampnabiliter iniustus abutitur? Sicut enim tota universitas prodest iusto et iniustus facit iniuriam toti mundo, quia debet communicare sibi perfeccionem secundam a qua deficit. Dicunt enim quod denarius est melior in manu prudentis quam fatui, et tamen negant sacramentum esse melius in ministerio iusti qui facit cum illo fructum centuplum quam in ministerio reprobi qui cum illo dampnificat se ipsum et populum. Unde ad tantum clerus illudit populo quod credit et docet quod sacramentum non sit nisi accidens abiectum, et tamen offenditur illi qui vulgum docet credere quod illud sacramentum eukaristie non sit Deus. Et tamen secundum Apostolum Ephes. IVᵒ, 5 debet esse [p. 115] unus dominus et una fides cuilibet christiano. Ille ergo est invidus qui bibit dulcem potum fidei orthodoxe et consentit quod populus cuius debet habere curam intoxicetur veneno.

ENGLISH: Let the catholic, I say, distinguish between the natural goodness of the [host]{g:hostia}, which is equal in another bread not [hallowed]{g:sacrare}, and its moral goodness, and he will see that it can admit the latter more and less according to the merit and holiness of life of the [presbyter]{g:presbiter};[^1] for it does not [of itself]{g:per-se} take [114] holiness from the presence of the [humanity]{g:humanitas} of Christ, because then Iscariot eating with Christ, and a wicked presbyter receiving Christ [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} in the host, would be holy;[^2] for he is capable of greater holiness than an inanimate thing, which has holiness in it [equivocally]{g:equivoce}, as the water of baptism has grace in it as a sign, or as [by occasion]{g:occasionaliter} effecting grace in the person who partakes of the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}; so that the holiness which it has from the presence of the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} seems to be equal in all eucharists, but the holiness arising from the blessing of the presbyter seems to vary correspondingly to his merit; and by this distinction let the laws that seem contrary be understood. For as the Lord's prayer, as to its nature, is equally good by whomever it is said, so it is with the sacrament; but this has efficacy and goodness.[^3] For inanimate goods which a man uses gain goodness from virtuous use; how then does the sacrament which a just man uses as an instrument of meriting above others not have a [relational]{g:respectivus} goodness beyond the sacrament which an unjust man damnably abuses? For as the whole totality profits the just man, and the unjust man does injury to the whole world, because he ought to communicate to it the [second perfection]{g:perfeccio-secunda} from which he falls short.[^4] For they say that a [penny]{g:denarius} is better in the hand of a prudent man than of a fool, and yet they deny that the sacrament is better in the ministry of a just man, who brings forth with it a hundredfold fruit, than in the ministry of a reprobate, who with it [damages]{g:dampnificare} himself and the people. Hence the [clergy]{g:clerus} deludes the people to such a point that it believes and teaches that the sacrament is nothing but an abject [accident]{g:accidens}, and yet is offended at the one who teaches the common people to believe that that sacrament of the eucharist is not God. And yet according to the Apostle, Ephesians 4:5, there must be [115] one Lord and one faith for every Christian. He therefore is envious who drinks the sweet drink of orthodox faith and consents that the people whose care he ought to have be poisoned with venom.[^5]

### 05-a ¶1 · 115.6–115.22  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 115] Quantum ad duas difficultates in parte expeditum est, scilicet de virtute et veritate verborum sacramentalium et quid de hostia est credendum. **Sed** antequam tangam terciam difficultatem obmissam, restat replicare contra sentenciam istam. Nam graviter ferunt doctores et cultores signorum quod locucio effectiva sacramenti est tropica. Ponit enim Scotus super quartum in principio distinccione Xᵃ quod hec non est figurativa locucio: Hoc est corpus meum; quod probat ex hoc quod secundum Augustinum LXXXIIIᵘᵐ questionum questione LXIXᵃ ex parte scripture precedenti vel sequenti aut alibi posita potest cognosci que sit figurativa. Cum ergo Christus dicit Luce XXIIᵒ, 19: Hoc est corpus meum, statim subiungit quod pro vobis tradetur, et Joh. VIᵒ, 56 dicit: Caro mea vere est cibus. Ex quibus concludit quod omnino est contra intencionem Salvatoris loqui hic figurative.

ENGLISH: As to two difficulties, the matter has in part been settled, namely about the power and truth of the sacramental words, and what is to be believed about the [host]{g:hostia}. But before I touch the third difficulty, which was left aside, it remains to [rejoin]{g:replicatur} against this [teaching]{g:sentencia}.[^1] For the doctors and [worshippers of signs]{g:cultores-signorum} take it hard that the [effective speech]{g:locucio-effectiva} of the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} is [tropical]{g:locucio-tropica}.[^2] For Scotus lays down on the fourth [book], at the beginning of distinction 10, that this is not figurative speech: "This is my body"; which he proves from this, that according to Augustine, *Eighty-three Questions*, question 69, it can be known from a part of scripture preceding or following, or set elsewhere, which [speech] is figurative.[^3] Since therefore Christ says in Luke 22:19, "This is my body", he at once adds "which shall be delivered for you"; and in John 6:56 he says: "My flesh is meat indeed."[^4] From which he [infers]{g:concludere} that it is wholly against the intention of the Saviour to speak [figuratively]{g:figurative} here.

### 05-a ¶2 · 115.23–117.8  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 115] **Sed** non satis subtiliter, ymmo nimis superficialiter videtur doctor in isto fuisse locutus, tum quia ex verbis Christi tam de sacramento panis quam calicis patet ipsum locutum fuisse figurative. Nam non dubium quin panem materialem accepit, benedixit et fregit et ex illo manducare precepit, quem demonstravit dicens: Hoc est corpus meum, quod oportet omnino figurative intelligi sicut et verba de calice. [p. 116] Nec dubium quin, sicut panem et vinum materiale assumpsit, sic ipsum sumi tamquam sacramentum mandavit; aliter enim illusorie equivocasset cum ecclesia. Et sic indubie figurative locutus est Joh. VIᵒ (ut patet per Augustinum); unde miror quomodo aliqua subtilitas potest ex relacione ydemptitatis quod pro vobis tradetur excludere locucionem figurativam, cum antecedens locucio foret ad hoc efficacior; ut in isto dicto Joh. XVᵒ, 1: Ego sum vitis vera foret evidencius quod excludit figuram loquendo ydemptice quam dicendo, sicut dixit implicite quod apostoli sunt palmites que inseruntur in hac vite. Idem enim est dicere: Hoc est corpus meum quod pro vobis tradetur, et: Hoc efficaciter et sacramentaliter figurat corpus meum quod pro vobis tradetur. Et tales sunt multe locuciones sanctorum (ut patet in De Consecracione, distinccione IIᵃ). Istud **autem** est quod doctor abhorret tamquam hereticum quod corpus Christi et sanguis non sint veraciter et realiter in sacramento altaris, et sic intelligitur Henricus Gaunt quodlibeto IXᵒ, questione IXᵃ cum multis aliis testibus. Et evidenter colligitur quod non in toto est conveniens illorum testimonium, eo quod quilibet eorum alteri in aliquo contradicit. Cum ergo nec testificacioni Augustini nec Jeronimi nec alicuius sancti debet credi nisi de quanto se fundaverint in scriptura, patet quod oportet istos doctores transcendere et commenta scripture vel racionem vivacem proponere, et multo magis de doctoribus decretorum. Convenio **autem** cum illis quantum sumunt testimonium illud superius, cui testimonio ipsis contrariantibus ego illis intrepide contradico et specialiter in isto quod panis et vinum [p. 117] pro instanti confeccionis desinunt esse secundum se tota et remanet solum accidens sacramentum; hoc enim repugnat decreto Romane ecclesie, De Consecracione, distinccione IIᵃ, capitulo: Ego Beringarius et sentencie sancti Augustini cum aliis sanctis et racionibus. Nec scit tota pars adversa facere scintillam evidencie que istud vel loyco suaderet. Quare ergo onerarentur discipuli Veritatis hoc credere?

ENGLISH: But the doctor seems to have spoken in this not subtly enough, indeed [exceedingly]{g:nimis} superficially: first, because from the words of Christ, both about the sacrament of the [bread]{g:panis} and about that of the [chalice]{g:calix}, it is clear that he spoke figuratively. For there is no doubt that he took material bread, blessed and broke [it], and commanded [them] to eat of it, which he pointed out, saying: "This is my body"; which must wholly be understood figuratively, as must the words about the chalice.[^5] [116] Nor is there doubt that, as he took material bread and [wine]{g:vinum}, so he commanded it to be [received]{g:sumpcio} as a sacrament; for otherwise he would have equivocated deceptively with the [church]{g:ecclesia}. And so without doubt he spoke figuratively in John 6 (as is clear through Augustine); hence I wonder how any subtlety can, from the [relation of identity]{g:relacio-ydemptitatis} "which shall be delivered for you", exclude figurative speech, since the antecedent speech would be more efficacious for this; as in this saying, John 15:1, "I am the true vine", it would be more evident that it excludes a figure, speaking [identically]{g:ydemptice}, than by saying, as he said implicitly, that the apostles are branches which are grafted into this vine.[^6] For it is the same to say "This is my body which shall be delivered for you" and "This efficaciously and [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} [figures]{g:figurare} my body which shall be delivered for you." And such are many speeches of [the saints]{g:sancti} (as is clear in *De consecratione*, distinction 2). And this is what the doctor abhors as heretical: that the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} and blood should not be [verily]{g:veraciter} and [really]{g:realiter} in the sacrament of the altar; and so Henry of Ghent is understood, *Quodlibet* 9, question 9, with many other witnesses.[^7] And it is evidently gathered that their testimony is not in agreement throughout, because each of them contradicts the other in something. Since therefore neither the testimony of Augustine nor of Jerome nor of any saint ought to be believed except so far as they have founded themselves in scripture, it is clear that one must go beyond these doctors and put forward the [commentaries on scripture]{g:commenta-scripture} or living reason, and much more so with the doctors of the [decrees]{g:decretum}. And I [agree]{g:convenire} with them so far as they take up that testimony above; but where they contradict that testimony I fearlessly contradict them, and especially in this, that bread and wine [117] at the instant of [consecration]{g:confeccio} cease to be in their whole selves, and only an [accident]{g:accidens} remains, the sacrament;[^8] for this [is incompatible with]{g:repugnare} the decree of the Roman church, *De consecratione*, distinction 2, chapter *Ego Berengarius*, and with the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of Saint Augustine, with other saints and with reasons.[^9] Nor can the whole opposing party make a spark of [evidence]{g:evidencia} which would [make the case]{g:suadere} for it even to a logician. Why then should the disciples of the Truth be burdened with believing it?

### 05-a ¶3 · 117.9–118.12  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 117] Nec valet dicere quod abrogatur illud decretum, sicut communiter dicitur, quando aliqua lex ecclesie non placet satrapis, primo quia tam glossa Beringarii quam glossa ordinaria decretorum allegat illud capitulum, ut patet alibi, secundo quia Vᵒ Decretalium De Penitencia, capitulo primo, docetur per hoc capitulum, quod qui publice peccat debet publice penitere. Et tercio quia cum tangit fidem ecclesie et fulcitur scriptura sacra, sanctis doctoribus et evidencia racionis, oportet docere, quando et qualiter abrogatur. Aliter enim non pocius crederetur hoc dicenti quam cuicunque ydiote omnem legem ecclesie per istum fucum sophisticum deneganti. Nam editor decretalis non potest sentenciam istam destruere nisi docto quod sit contraria legi Christi; **sed** cum hoc non potest, remanet secura, et quecunque decretalis de quanto fuerit illi contraria abroganda. Penset, rogo, fidelis quomodo fides de eukaristia debet servari conformius scripture sacre, racioni et sanctis doctoribus, ponendo quod panis et vinum post benediccionem remanent sacramenta et videat quare hodie sentencia ista Romane ecclesie est neganda. Numquid ascribendum [p. 118] est miraculo Christi magnifico quod sine causa destruit creatas substancias, dampnificat suam universitatem, tollit figuras quibus in pane et vino (ut dicunt doctores) edificaretur ecclesia, inducit decepcionem sensus hominum, facit accidens abiectum coli ut Deum et cum dubiis inutilibus fatigat ecclesiam? Omnes enim in isto conveniunt quod eque honorifice, eque religiose et pie posset Christus coli sub specie panis, sicut sub illo globo accidencium ignotorum quibus diffamatur ecclesia, ymmo religiosius, quia in signo plus honorabili, plus fundabili et plus defensibili contra infideles presumpcionis nostre fatue irrisores.

ENGLISH: Nor does it avail to say that that decree is abrogated, as is commonly said when some law of the church does not please the [satraps]{g:satrapa}: first, because both the gloss of Bernard and the ordinary gloss of the decrees [allege]{g:allegare} that chapter, as is clear elsewhere; secondly, because in the fifth [book] of the [Decretals]{g:decretalis}, *De penitencia*, chapter one, it is taught through this chapter that he who sins publicly must do penance publicly.[^10] And thirdly because, since it touches the faith of the church and is propped up by holy scripture, by the holy doctors and by the evidence of reason, they must teach when and how it is abrogated. For otherwise one would not believe the man who says this more than any [unlettered man]{g:ydiota} who denies every law of the church by this sophistical disguise. For the maker of a decretal cannot destroy this teaching unless it is shown that it is contrary to the law of Christ; but since this cannot [be done], it remains secure, and any decretal whatever, so far as it is contrary to it, is to be abrogated. Let the faithful man weigh, I ask, how the faith concerning the eucharist ought to be kept more in conformity with holy scripture, with reason and with the holy doctors, by laying down that bread and wine remain sacraments after the blessing; and let him see why today this teaching of the Roman church is to be denied.[^11] Is it to be ascribed [118] to a magnificent miracle of Christ that without cause he destroys created [substances]{g:substancia}, [damages]{g:dampnificare} his totality, takes away the [figures]{g:figura} by which (as the doctors say) the church would be edified in the bread and wine, brings in deception of men's senses, makes an abject accident to be worshipped as God, and wearies the church with useless doubts? For all agree in this, that Christ could be worshipped equally honourably, equally religiously and piously under the [species]{g:species} of bread as under that heap of unknown accidents by which the church is defamed; indeed more religiously, because in a sign more honourable, more able to be grounded and more defensible against unbelievers, the mockers of our foolish presumption.

### 05-a ¶6 · 118.23–119.9  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 118] Quoad primum quare nunc seminatur ultra fidem antiquam quod accidencia manent sine subiecto et sic sacramentum illud est accidens, ecce quod respondetur per duplex mendacium et nimis confuse per analogum vulgo incognitum; nam vulgus et multi qui ista balbuciunt ignorant quid est accidens, sicut ignorant quod accidens est eukaristia de novem generibus accidentis. Fideles **autem** dicunt quod eukaristia est panis aut vinum verbo Domini consecratum; ecce hic modo antique ecclesie descenditur [p. 119] ad speciem vulgo satis noscibilem; voluit enim Deus iugum legis sue esse leve et aput quemcunque fidelem noscibile, ne in angulis latebre mendacium abscondatur. Sic enim discipuli cognoverunt Christum in fraccione panis, quem Augustinus dicit esse sacramentum, sic eciam fideles fregerunt panem circa domos in primitiva ecclesia, ut dicitur Act. IIᵒ; quod dominus Armachanus libro IX capitulo... declarat dici de hostia consecrata.

ENGLISH: As to the first, why it is now sown beyond the ancient faith that accidents remain without a subject, and so that that sacrament is an accident: see, the reply is made by a twofold lie, and exceedingly [indistinctly]{g:confuse}, by an [analogous term]{g:analogum} unknown to the common people; for the common people, and many who stammer these things, do not know what an accident is, just as they do not know which accident, of the nine genera of accident, the eucharist is.[^13] But the faithful say that the eucharist is bread or wine consecrated by the word of the Lord; see, here, in the manner of the [ancient church]{g:antiqua-ecclesia}, one descends [119] to a species well enough knowable to the common people; for God willed the yoke of his law to be light and knowable to any faithful man, lest a lie should be hidden in the corners of a hiding-place.[^14] For so the disciples knew Christ in the breaking of bread, which [bread] Augustine says is the sacrament; so also the faithful broke bread from house to house in the primitive church, as is said in Acts 2[:46]; which the lord of Armagh, book 9, chapter …, makes clear is said of the [consecrated host]{g:hostia-consecrata}.[^15]

### 05-a ¶7 · 119.10–119.26  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 119] Quantum ad aliam sentenciam, patet quod est communis cuilibet fideli; quilibet enim fidelis dicit sectam suam esse katholicam et communitatem suam esse sanctam ecclesiam et sic credere sicut ecclesia sancta credit. Et iterum si fides ista sufficiat, quare oneratur ecclesia ad concedendum insolite quod eukaristia non est substancia **sed** res infinitum abieccior de genere accidentis? Et tercio suppono quod fiat interrogacio a periciori huius scole vel a layco subtilis ingenii qui ultra questionem ‘si est’ de sacramento languet circa questionem ‘quid est’ de sacramento altaris. Dictum, inquam, est sibi quod sunt septem ecclesie sacramenta et specificantur aliqua; quare ergo non specificaretur et distincte cognosceretur sacramentum altaris communissimum, necessariissimum atque dignissimum? oportet enim cognoscere per quid distinguitur ab aliis sacramentis.

ENGLISH: As to the other teaching, it is clear that it is common to every faithful man; for every faithful man says that his [sect]{g:secta} is catholic and his community holy church, and that he believes as holy church believes. And again, if this faith suffices, why is the church burdened to grant, in an unwonted way, that the eucharist is not a substance but a thing infinitely more abject, of the genus of accident? And thirdly I suppose that the question is put by the more skilled of this school, or by a layman of subtle wit, who, beyond the question "whether it is" about the sacrament, languishes over the question "what it is" about the sacrament of the altar. He has been told, I say, that there are seven sacraments of the church, and some of them are specified; why then should not the sacrament of the altar, the most common, most necessary and most worthy, be specified and distinctly known? For one must know by what it is distinguished from the other sacraments.[^16]
