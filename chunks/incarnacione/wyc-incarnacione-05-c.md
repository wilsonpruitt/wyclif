# *De benedicta incarnacione*, chapter 5, part 3: "the scripture cannot be broken"; the objection from annihilation

## ¶1 · 72.19–73.3

> [p. 72] Ex istis colligitur quod, si beatus alius tantum adheret Deo quod non potest post beatitudinem dissolvi unio, qua est unus spiritus cum eo;⁹ multo magis natura Christi, que ultra hoc ypostatice est unita: ut si homo, qui potest peccare, habet per graciam quod sit impeccabilis, immortalis, et sic de aliis que peioracionem sapiunt: quanto magis natura humana in fine bonitatis possibilis exaltata, que omnino peccare non poterit, ab illa gracia dimitti non poterit. Unde, quamvis Mt. 27 secundum naturam completam creatam clamet Christus; ‘Deus meus, Deus meus, utquid me dereliquisti?’ non tamen dimisit ypostaticam unionem, sed dereliquit illud ‘infirmum Dei,’ quod est suppositum ‘forcius’ ad tempus, inconservatum a¹⁰ persecucionibus et flagellacionibus Iudeorum; sed¹⁰ nunquam [p. 73] dimisit vel simpliciter dereliquit, quia dimissio dicit existenciam dimissi et separacionem ypostaticam ab eodem, quod non fuit in triduo.

From these it is gathered that, if another blessed one so cleaves to God that after blessedness the [union]{g:unio} by which he is one spirit with him cannot be dissolved, much more [so] the [nature]{g:natura} of Christ, which beyond this is [hypostatically]{g:ypostatice} united: as, if a man, who can sin, has by grace that he is impeccable, immortal, and so of the other things that savour of [worsening]{g:peioracio}, how much more the human nature, exalted to the limit of possible goodness, which can in no way sin, cannot be [laid aside]{g:dimittere} from that grace.[^1] Hence, although in Matthew 27, as to the complete [created]{g:creatus} nature, Christ cries: "My God, My God, why hast thou forsaken me?", yet he did not lay aside the hypostatic union, but forsook that "weakness of God", which is the "stronger" [supposit]{g:suppositum}, for a time unprotected from the persecutions and scourgings of the Jews;[^2] but he never [73] laid [it] aside or forsook [it] [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}, because laying-aside [signifies]{g:dicere} the existence of what is laid aside and its hypostatic separation from the same, which was not [so] in [the three days]{g:triduum}.

## ¶2 · 73.4–75.5

> [p. 73] Quod si quis in evidenciam huius veritatis olim famose expectat testimonium scripture, ecce Veritas Ioh. 10. alloquitur Phariseos: ‘non potest, inquit, solvi scriptura, quem Pater sanctificavit et misit in mundum.’ Pro cuius intellectu sunt tria notanda per ordinem.¹ Primo quod Veritas non loquitur de scriptura nostra artificiali aggregata ex ficturis² atramenti et pellibus mortuorum; tum quia talis scriptura cottidie per artifices potest solvi; tum eciam quia illi non competit sanctificacio et missio in mundum: sed illa scriptura est ‘liber vite’ cui inscribuntur omnia, cum sit intrinsecus Sermo Dei. Et ad illum sensum exponit Augustitinus Ome. 44.³ ‘Forte, inquit, aliquis dicit⁴: Si pater eum sanctificavit, ergo⁵ aliquando non erat sanctus; sed si sic eum sanctificavit,⁵ quomodo genuit? ut enim sanctus esset, gignendo ei⁶ dedit, quia sanctum eum genuit.’ Et respondet posterius ad obiectum. ‘Si, inquit, quod sanctificatur aliquando, non erat sanctum; quomodo dicimus Deo Patri —“sanctificetur nomen tuum”—?’ Ille ergo, qui est absolute necessario in se sanctus, fit sanctus⁷ in nobis per prevenientem graciam, noticiam, et operacionem, et confessionem meritoriam.⁸ Unde Spiritus Sanctus ad relinquendum nobis exemplar, quod loquitur de scriptura vitali, subdit in genere masculino— ‘quem Pater sanctificavit’— innuendo nobis relacionem faciendam ad Filium. Mos⁹ enim est scripture et Augustini per adiectivos¹⁰ masculinos personas concipere et per neutros¹⁰ naturas. Et isti modo¹¹ loquendi alludit Apostolus ad Gall. 3°; ‘non, inquit,¹² dicit seminibus quasi in multis, sed semini tuo, qui est Christus.’ Indubie, sicut sanctus Apostolus notavit¹³ numerum, sic et genus. [p. 74] 2°. supponitur quod Veritas non loquitur de solucione mentis a corpore vel Patris a Filio in hoc dicto: de prima non, quia supra Ioh. 2°. dicit Veritas Iudeis prophetice quod solverent templum corporis sui ab anima et ipse in tribus diebus excitaret illud. Cum ergo ‘non fuit in eo EST et NON,’ patet quod hic non intellexit corpus non posse solvi ab anima. Nec alius sensus est pertinens; tum quia ligacio¹ et solucio est duarum naturarum ad invicem et non personarum eiusdem summe indivisibilis essencie; tum eciam, quia ille sensus fuisset dictis impertinens, non eis ambiguus. Ideo relinquitur 3°. per locum a sufficienti divisione quod intellexit insolubilitatem deitatis ab humanitate, quas expressit nomine ‘scripture,’ ut vel sic scrutarentur scripturas, que testimonium perhibent² de istis duabus naturis sic ypostatice copulatis. Unde et Ioh. 5. dicitur³ ‘scrutamini⁴ scripturas in quibus vos putatis vitam eternam habere, et ille sunt que testimonium perhibent² de me.’ Non enim haberent evidenciam dicere quod blasfemat dicendo se esse Dei Filium naturalem apposito sufficienti scrutinio scripturarum. Nam si in lege scriptum sit de Messia vel Christo futuro in duabus naturis et una ypostasi—quod⁵ vinculum propter unionem⁶ ypostaticam ad infinitam personam non potest includi⁷ fine temporis, sicut potest unio participative deificatorum per Sermonem Dei⁸ factum ad eos et non eis personaliter copulatum—quomodo est color concludendi blasfemiam⁹ ex assercione filiacionis naturalis? Medium autem per quod tenet ista inveccio est veritas quod ‘non potest solvi’ hec ‘scriptura’ ad sensum expositum. Nam ex illo¹⁰ sequitur quod sit natura increata in supposito Verbi, et cum¹¹ hoc natura creata temporaliter sed¹² insolubiliter copulata. Unde signantissime Veritas dicit negativam quod ‘non potest solvi scriptura;’ quia, licet sancti in patria sint Deo inseparabiliter copulati, tamen contingentissime possunt solvi, cum sint pro [p. 75] tempore suo viantes¹ peccabiles. Homo autem assumptus a Verbo Dominus Iesus Christus pro nullo tempore peccare poterit. Et² patet conclusio.² Nec³ aliter haberent sancti doctores evidenciam imprecandi anathema eis qui concedunt dimissionem vel solucionem huiusmodi naturarum.

And if anyone, for [evidence]{g:evidencia} of this truth, once famous, awaits the testimony of scripture, behold, the Truth, John 10, addresses the Pharisees: "the scripture," he says, "cannot be broken, whom the Father hath sanctified and sent into the world."[^3] For the understanding of this, three things are to be noted in order. First, that the Truth does not speak of our artificial scripture, put together out of the shapings of ink and the skins of the dead; both because such scripture can daily be loosed by craftsmen, and also because sanctification and sending into the world do not belong to it: but that scripture is the "book of life", in which all things are written, since it is the inward [Speech]{g:sermo} of God.[^4] And in that sense Augustine expounds, Homily 48: "Perhaps," he says, "someone says: If the Father sanctified him, then at some time he was not holy; but if he so sanctified him, how did he beget him? For that he should be holy, he gave it him in begetting, because he begot him holy." And he answers the [objection]{g:objectum} afterwards: "If," he says, "what is sanctified was at some time not holy, how do we say to God the Father, 'hallowed be thy name'?"[^5] He, therefore, who is absolutely necessarily holy in himself becomes holy in us through [prevenient grace]{g:gracia-preveniens}, knowledge, and working, and meritorious confession. Hence the Holy Spirit, to leave us a pattern [showing] that he speaks of the living scripture, adds in the masculine gender, "whom the Father hath sanctified", intimating to us that the reference is to be made to the Son. For it is the custom of scripture and of Augustine to conceive persons through masculine adjectives, and natures through neuters.[^6] And to this manner of speaking the Apostle alludes, Galatians 3: "He saith not," he says, "to seeds, as of many: but to thy seed, which is Christ."[^7] Undoubtedly, as the holy Apostle noted the number, so also the gender. [74] Second, it is supposed that the Truth does not speak in this saying of the [loosing]{g:solucio} of the mind from the body, or of the Father from the Son: not of the first, because above, John 2, the Truth says to the Jews prophetically that they would [loose]{g:solvere} the temple of his body from the soul, and he in three days would raise it up. Since, therefore, "there was not in him, It is and It is not", it is clear that here he did not mean "the body cannot be loosed from the soul".[^8] Nor is the other sense pertinent; both because binding and loosing is of two natures to one another, and not of persons of the same supremely indivisible [essence]{g:essencia}; and also because that sense would have been [irrelevant]{g:impertinens} to those addressed, not a matter of doubt to them.[^9] So it remains, third, by the topic from sufficient division, that he meant the [inextricability]{g:insolubilitas} of [the Godhead]{g:deitas} from [the humanity]{g:humanitas}, which he expressed by the name "scripture", so that at least thus they might search the scriptures, which give testimony of these two natures thus hypostatically joined. Hence in John 5 too it is said: "Search the scriptures, in which you think to have life everlasting. And the same are they that give testimony of me."[^10] For they would not have had evidence to say "he blasphemes in saying he is the natural Son of God", had a sufficient searching of the scriptures been applied. For if it is written in the law of the Messiah or Christ to come in two natures and one [hypostasis]{g:ypostasis} (which bond, because of the hypostatic union to an infinite [person]{g:persona}, cannot be closed by an end of time, as can the union of those [participatively]{g:participative} deified through the Speech of God made to them and not joined to them personally), what [colour]{g:color} is there for [inferring]{g:concludere} blasphemy from the assertion of natural sonship?[^11] And the middle [term] through which this invective holds is the truth that this "scripture" "cannot be broken", in the sense expounded. For from it [it follows]{g:sequitur} that there is an uncreated nature in the supposit of [the Word]{g:verbum}, and together with this a created nature joined in time but [inextricably]{g:insolubiliter}. Hence most pointedly the Truth speaks the negative, "the scripture cannot be broken"; because, although [the saints]{g:sancti} in [the homeland]{g:patria} are inseparably joined to God, yet most contingently they can be loosed, since for [75] their time they are [those on the road]{g:viantes}, [able to sin]{g:peccabilis}. But [the man assumed by the Word]{g:homo-assumptus}, the Lord Jesus Christ, could at no time sin. And the [conclusion]{g:conclusio} is clear.[^12] Nor otherwise would the holy doctors have evidence for invoking anathema on those who grant the [laying-aside]{g:dimissio} or loosing of such natures.

## ¶3 · 75.6–75.10

> [p. 75] Sed contra illud obicitur. Quicquid Deus contingenter et libere contradictorie facit, potest alias eciam adnichilando⁴ destruere: sed libere contradictorie assumpsit hominem: ergo potest nedum ipsum dimittere sed adnichilando⁴ destruere.

But against this [it is objected]{g:obicitur}. Whatever God does contingently and [freely, as between contradictories]{g:libere-contradictorie}, he can at another time also destroy by annihilating: now he freely, as between contradictories, [assumed]{g:assumere} the man: therefore he can [not only]{g:nedum} lay him aside but destroy [him] by annihilating.[^13]

## ¶4 · 75.11–75.22

> [p. 75] Confirmatur tripliciter. Primo ex hoc, quod idem est iudicium de una creatura et qualibet, cum tanta sit utrobique divina libertas: sed aliquam creaturam Deus potest adnichilare: ergo⁵ quamlibet. 2°. per hoc quod aliter Deus esset necessitatus ad conservandam dictam unionem, et sic non libere contradictorie eam servat. 3°. ex hoc quod Deus non esset omnipotens, nisi posset tam contingentem et fragilem⁶ nedum dissolvere,⁷ sed et adnichilare.⁷ Tales⁸ sunt multe⁸ instancie, que omnes currunt sub uno circulo quod Deus non esset omnipotens nisi posset in talia. Vel taliter⁹ arguunt per locum a simili;— Si Deus creare poterit et utrobique tanta¹⁰ est¹⁰ distancia; ergo per idem¹¹ adnichilare poterit.

This is confirmed in three ways. First from this, that the judgement is the same of one [creature]{g:creatura} and of any, since the divine freedom is as great on both sides: now God can [annihilate]{g:adnichilare} some creature: therefore any. Second, by this, that otherwise God would be necessitated to conserve the said union, and so he does not keep it freely, as between contradictories. Third, from this, that God would not be omnipotent unless he could not only dissolve [a union] so contingent and frail, but also annihilate [it].[^14] Such are many [objections]{g:instancia}, which all run within one circle: that God would not be omnipotent unless he had power over such things. Or thus they argue by the topic from likeness: If God can create, and on both sides the distance is as great; therefore by the same token he can annihilate.[^15]

## ¶5 · 75.23–76.2

> [p. 75] Pro isto dico sicut alias sepe dixi quod ad omnipotenciam Dei consequitur perficere construere et non destruere, creare¹² et non adnichilare; et sic de actibus positivis privacionibus repugnantibus separatis. Unde sicut repugnat¹³ pure creaturam esse et non posse deficere, sic repugnat omnipotencie Creatoris posse adnichilare peiorare dampnificare vel de¹⁴ se destruere creaturam. Peccatum autem¹⁵ quod non est creatura Deus destruit, peccantes solum per accidens racione peccati punit vel meliorando consumit.¹⁶ [p. 76] Et sic ex omnipotencia Dei sequitur oppositum predictorum et patet quod assumptum est inpossibile.

To this I say, as I have often said elsewhere, that upon the omnipotence of God there follows [the power] to perfect, to build and not to destroy, to create and not to annihilate; and so of positive acts set apart from the privations incompatible [with them].[^16] Hence, as it [is incompatible]{g:repugnare} [for a thing] to be purely a creature and not to be able to fail, so it is incompatible with the omnipotence of the [Creator]{g:creator} to be able to annihilate, make worse, [damage]{g:dampnificare}, or [of itself]{g:per-se} destroy a creature. But sin, which is not a creature, God destroys; sinners he punishes only [by accident]{g:per-accidens}, by reason of the sin, or consumes [them] by bettering [them].[^17] [76] And so from the omnipotence of God there follows the opposite of the aforesaid, and it is clear that [the assumption]{g:assumptum} is impossible.[^18]

## ¶6 · 76.3–76.14

> [p. 76] Pro cuius¹ intellectu pono triplex exemplum ut alias posui. Primum logicum. Nam Deus produxit tempus perpetuum, sicut testatur scriptura et sepe meminit Augustinus. Nam Ps. 73.² dicitur ‘estatem et ver tu plasmasti ea.’³ Et idem est iudicium de toto⁴ tempore,⁴ et signanter dicit Veritas Marc. 2ᶜ· quod ‘Filius hominis est dominus sabbati’ et sic Deus. Et tamen notum est quod Deus de potencia absoluta non potest tempus perpetuum⁵ adnichilare sive destruere, ymo, quod plus est, tempus vel motum finire ante finem sue periodi naturalis. Modicus ergo color est concludere, si Deus libere contradictorie datum effectum produxerit, quod potest ipsum destruere.

For the understanding of this I set out a threefold example, as I have set out elsewhere. The first, logical. For God produced perpetual time, as scripture testifies and Augustine often recalls. For in Psalm 73 it is said: "the summer and the spring were formed by thee."[^19] And the judgement is the same of the whole of time; and pointedly the Truth says, Mark 2, that "the Son of man is Lord of the sabbath", and so God [is].[^20] And yet it is known that God by [absolute power]{g:potencia-absoluta} cannot annihilate or destroy perpetual time; indeed, what is more, [he cannot] end time or motion before the end of its natural period. Small colour, therefore, is there for inferring, if God freely, as between contradictories, produced a given effect, that he can destroy it.

## ¶7 · 76.15–76.19

> [p. 76] 2ᵐ exemplum naturale⁶ est de hoc⁶ quod Deus libere condictorie produxit mundum ex nichilo et tamen non potest ipsum destruere nec aliam substancialem essenciam ut patet in materia De Adnichilacione, ne fiat vacuum vel defectus in Deo formaliter.

The second example, natural, is of this: that God freely, as between contradictories, produced the world from nothing, and yet cannot destroy it, or any other substantial essence, as is clear in the matter *On Annihilation*, lest there be a void or a defect in God formally.[^21]

## ¶8 · 76.20–76.23

> [p. 76] 3ᵐ exemplum est methaphisicum. Nam Deus produxit veritatem talem, de preterito me fuisse, et hoc liberrime contingenter; et tamen notum⁷ est quod⁷ de potencia sua absoluta non potest ipsam destruere vel eius desicionem⁸ permittere.

The third example is metaphysical. For God produced such a truth about the past, "I have been", and this most freely, contingently; and yet it is known that by his absolute power he cannot destroy it or permit its ceasing.[^22]

## ¶9 · 76.24–76.31

> [p. 76] Talia quotlibet exempla reliquit nobis Deus, ut consideremus eius bonitatem et naturalem condicionem ad positive posse bene agere et non male. Sicut enim est actus purus non admixtus⁹ potencia ante⁹ actum, sic potest per se proficiendo tendere in esse positivum, et non deficiendo per se tendere in¹⁰ non esse, ut, licet modicum sit, mentiri vel aliter peccando male agere; Deus tamen ex omnipotencia hoc non potest.

Any number of such examples God has left us, that we may consider his goodness and his natural condition toward being able positively to act well and not ill. For as he is pure act, not mixed with [potency]{g:potencia} before act, so he can of himself, by advancing, tend into positive [being]{g:esse}, and not, by failing, tend of himself into non-being, as, though it be a small thing, to lie or otherwise to act ill by sinning: God, nevertheless, from omnipotence cannot do this.[^23]

O *pro ordine* (73.8).

[^5]: **S/T.** "Homily 48", with O and the source, against the print's *44*: the words on John 10:36 and Matt. 6:9 (DR "hallowed be thy name") belong to *Tract. in Ioh.* 48.9, PL 35, 1745, the number O reads (*verified*, LEDGER I05c-1; Harris's index gives the same place). [^6]: **C.** 73.24–29: *quem* is masculine where *scriptura* is feminine: the relative points to a person, the Son. *Relacionem*: the grammatical reference of the relative. A B C *adiecciones masculinas … neutras* (73.28–29); O *mous*.
[^7]: **S.** Gal. 3:16 (DR "He saith not: And to his seeds as of many. But as of one: And to thy seed, which is Christ"): Wyclif's text drops the Vulgate's *Et* and *sed quasi in uno*. A B C *enim* for *inquit*; C M *numeravit* for *notavit* (73.32).
[^8]: **S.** John 2:19 (DR "Destroy this temple; and in three days I will raise it up"): the Vulgate's verb is *solvite*, which Wyclif's "loose" keeps; and 2 Cor. 1:19 (DR "was not: It is and It is not. But, It is, was in him").
[^9]: **C.** 74.9–10 *dictis*: those to whom the words were said; *ambiguus*: in doubt, open to question. O *ligamentum* for *ligacio* (74.7).
[^10]: **S.** John 5:39 (DR "Search the scriptures: for you think in them to have life everlasting. And the same are they that give testimony of me"): Wyclif reads *in quibus* for the Vulgate's *quia … in ipsis*. A B C *dicit* (74.15); O *prohibent* for *perhibent* (74.14, 74.17).
[^11]: **C.** 74.20–26: *Sermonem Dei factum ad eos* takes up John 10:35, "to whom the word of God was spoken": the "gods" of that verse, deified by participation, whose union can end. *Color* is the row's "colour", a plausible ground. A B C *concludi* for *includi* (74.22), *domini* for *Dei* (74.24), *blasfemia* (74.25); B *ypostasis* (74.21).
[^12]: **C.** 74.31–75.3: even the blessed can be "loosed" in that their union was given to wayfarers who could sin; the assumed man never could. A B C *ut patet oculo* for *Et patet conclusio*; O *euantes* (75.1); C *nam* for *nec*.
[^13]: **C.** 75.7 *libere contradictorie*: free with the freedom of contradiction, able to do a thing or not do it; rendered "freely, as between contradictories". *Adnichilare*: "annihilate" (A B C *anichilando*, as often below).

[^21]: **S.** *De adnichilacione*: Wyclif's own treatise on annihilation (Harris's index, *De Adnichilacione, 76, 78*): *De potencia productiva Dei ad extra* cc. 12–14, ed. A B C *est naturale hoc*.
[^22]: **T.** 76.23 *desicionem*, "ceasing" (A B C M); O *designacionem*; Harris asks whether *destruccionem* should be read. The print is kept. A B omit *notum … quod* (76.22).
[^23]: **C.** 76.26–31: *potencia* is the CONTEXTUAL row, here "potency", Aristotle's pair with act; A B C *potencia ad actum* "potency toward act".
