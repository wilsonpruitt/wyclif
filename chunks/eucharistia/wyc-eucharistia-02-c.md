# *De eucharistia*, chapter 2, part 3: scripture's bread, and when the church may vary its logic

## ¶1 · 36.24–38.21

> [p. 36] Secundum exemplum est illud Apoc. XIXº, 8: Byssinum enim iustificaciones sunt sanctorum, ut alias diffuse exposui; et tercium exemplum est illud I. Cor. XIº, 25: Hic calix novum testamentum est in meo sanguine; figuravit enim vinum in calice novum testamentum, sicut sanguis vituli fuit signum confirmacionis veteris testamenti, ut patet Exod. XXXIVº. Sic igitur videtur sanctos ecclesie primitive et suos filios post eos intelligere per panem et vinum figurative corpus Christi et sanguinem. Unde Iª Cor. XIº, 21 narrans Apostolus quomodo Corinthi conveniebant ad manducandum cenam dominicam [p. 37] unoquoque portante secum proprium panem et vinum, sic quod unus fuit ebrius et alius esuriens post hanc cenam, increpat eos leniter in hoc facto et addit instruccionem quoad hoc sacramentum, que tunc suffecit ecclesie; videlicet quod conveniant manducantes in ecclesia ordinate vel aliter domi infirmi manducent nec alii darent scandalum fratribus in copiosis, in sumptuosis et in deliciosis preparacionibus panis et vini. Unde pro plena instruccione quoad hoc sacramentum sic loquitur: Ego enim accepi a Domino quod et tradidi vobis; quoniam dominus Jesus in qua nocte tradebatur accepit panem et gracias agens fregit et dixit: Accipite et manducate, hoc est corpus meum quod pro vobis tradetur, hoc facite in meam commemoracionem. Quocienscunque, inquit, manducabitis panem hunc et calicem bibetis, mortem Domini annunciabitis donec veniat; ubi patet quod loquitur quadrupliciter de pane et vino materiali et per consequens dicit hoc esse corpus eius et sanguinem in figura, sicut dicit hunc calicem esse novum testamentum et hoc fieri in eius commemoracionem. Quod autem alii dicuntur in medio prandii accipere instar Christi hostiam a sacerdotibus consecratam, videtur esse sompnium, sed sacramenta et ritus adiecti videntur michi esse racionabiles, sic tamen quod non sint nimium ponderati. Aliqui enim stulte plus ponderant ritus adiectos quam sentenciam Apostoli, dum sic dicit: Itaque quicunque manducaverit panem hunc vel biberit calicem Domini indigne, reus erit corporis [p. 38] et sanguinis Domini. Intelligit enim quod panis et vinum non debent sumi in memoriam corporis Christi et sanguinis nisi digne, hoc est, nisi mundo corde et pura intencione; et sic videtur michi quod nulli ritus honesti de Eukaristia debent contempni nec plus virtutibus ponderari; et utrobique videtur quod scriptura vocat hoc sacramentum panem et vinum, quomodo quidam intelligunt illud Act. IIº, 46 quod dicitur de conversis: Cotidie autem perdurantes unanimiter in templo et frangentes circa domos panem sumebant cibum cum exultacione et simplicitate cordis. Panis itaque vocatur utrobique hoc venerabile sacramentum; quod si sit verum, patet cum paribus quod graviter peccaverunt qui onerarunt ecclesiam tam speculative quam practice tot perplexitatibus quibus difficultatur circa hoc sacramentum. Manet ergo iuxta sentenciam ecclesie in Beringario, iuxta sanctos doctores et formam verborum scripture tam panis quam vinum post benediccionem et sacramentum et corpus Christi in signo et non in essencia quod sic frangitur et tractatur.

The second example is that of Apoc. 19:8: "For the fine linen are the justifications of saints", as I have set out at length elsewhere;[^1] and the third example is that of 1 Cor. 11:25: "This [chalice]{g:calix} is the new testament in my blood"; for the [wine]{g:vinum} in the chalice [figured]{g:figurare} the new testament, as the blood of the calf was the sign of the confirmation of the old testament, as is clear from Exod. 34.[^2] So therefore it seems that [the saints]{g:sancti} of the primitive [church]{g:ecclesia} and their sons after them understood by the [bread]{g:panis} and wine [figuratively]{g:figurative} [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} and [his] blood. Hence in 1 Cor. 11:21 the Apostle, relating how the Corinthians came together to eat the Lord's supper, [37] each one bringing with him his own bread and wine, so that one was drunk and another hungry after this supper, rebukes them gently in this deed and adds an instruction concerning this [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}, which then sufficed the church: namely, that they come together to eat in the church in order, or else that the weak eat at home, and that the others should not give scandal to the brethren in plentiful, sumptuous and delicious preparations of bread and wine. Hence for full instruction concerning this sacrament he speaks thus: "For I have received of the Lord that which also I delivered unto you, that the Lord Jesus, the same night in which he was betrayed, took bread, and giving thanks, broke, and said: Take ye and eat: This is my body, which shall be delivered for you. This do for the commemoration of me." "As often", he says, "as you shall eat this bread and drink the chalice, you shall shew the death of the Lord, until he come."[^3] Where it is clear that he speaks four times of material bread and wine, and consequently says this to be his body and blood in figure, as he says this chalice to be the new testament and this to be done in his commemoration.[^4] But that others are said, in the middle of a dinner, to receive after the manner of Christ a [host]{g:hostia} consecrated by priests, seems to be a dream;[^5] but the sacraments and the [rites]{g:ritus} added seem to me to be reasonable, yet so that they are not weighed too much. For some foolishly weigh the added rites more than the [sense]{g:sentencia-verborum} of the Apostle, when he says thus: "Therefore, whosoever shall eat this bread,[^6] or drink the chalice of the Lord unworthily, shall be guilty of the body [38] and of the blood of the Lord." For he understands that the bread and wine ought not to be taken in memory of the body and blood of Christ except worthily, that is, except with a clean heart and pure intention; and so it seems to me that no honest rites of the Eucharist ought to be despised, nor weighed more than the virtues; and in both places it seems that scripture calls this sacrament bread and wine,[^7] as some understand that of Acts 2:46 which is said of the converts: "And continuing daily with one accord in the temple and breaking bread from house to house, they took their meat with gladness and simplicity of heart." So this venerable sacrament is called bread in both places; and if this is true, it is clear, with like cases, that those sinned gravely who burdened the church, both speculatively and practically, with so many [perplexities]{g:perplexitas} by which it is made difficult concerning this sacrament. There remains therefore, according to the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of the church in Berengar, according to the holy doctors and the form of the words of scripture, both the bread and the wine after the blessing, both sacrament and body of Christ, in sign and not in essence, which is thus broken and handled.[^8]

## ¶2 · 38.22–38.29

> [p. 38] Et patet solucio obiectus quo dicitur sentenciam ecclesie in Beringario contradicere sentencie supradicte, sed replicatur secundo per hoc quod scriptura sacra in sex locis predictis vocat sacramentum Eukaristie panem, et idem dicunt sancti doctores cum decreto ecclesie in Beringario. Quare ergo non teneremus nos eandem loycam, cum non licet in materia fidei secundum loycam variare?

And the [resolution]{g:solvere} of the objection is clear, by which it is said that the teaching of the church in Berengar contradicts the teaching stated above. But [it is rejoined]{g:replicatur} secondly, on the ground that sacred scripture in the six places aforesaid calls the sacrament of the Eucharist bread, and the holy doctors say the same, together with the [decree]{g:decretum} of the church in Berengar. Why therefore should we not hold the same [logic]{g:loyca}, since it is not lawful in a matter of faith to vary as to logic?[^9]

## ¶3 · 38.30–40.3

> [p. 38] Hic dicitur quod sensus supradictus est satis equivocus et vulgo satis extraneus ac occasio unde multi accipiunt fidem ad credendum panem et vinum post [p. 39] consecracionem esse ydemptice corpus Christi et sanguinem: ideo sicut ecclesia variavit in aliis accidentalibus preter fidem, sic debet ex causa variare in loyca stante sentencia et sic fide. Variarunt enim observanciam sabbati a feria septima usque ad feriam quintam et abhinc usque ad feriam primam, quod hodie observat ecclesia stante fide non variata, et ita de multis similibus preter fidem; quare ergo non licet ecclesie variare in loyca, servando fidem et obserando infidelitatis aditum, sicut custos castri ex causa racionabili mutat portas, nec hoc licet nisi cum sufficienti ac racionabili decreto custodum. Aliqua ergo sunt loca in quibus expedit variare in loyca propter periculum evitandum et aliqua sunt loca in quibus non convenit variare; et patet quod non oportet, si sancti doctores ecclesie katholice sic locuntur, tunc licet cuilibet katholico ita loqui, ut beatus Bernhardus in sermone De Exaltacione sancte crucis, qui sic incipit De virga Jesse sic loquitur: Manus ille que fundaverunt celos extense sunt in cruce. Et multa similia dicunt iste sanctus et usus ecclesie que non sunt nobis generaliter trahendi in consequenciam, quia nec manus corporales Christi eterne fuerant crucifixe nec manus spirituales que sunt potencia operandi; nam manus corporales non habuit antequam fuerat incarnatus, quia tunc fuisset Deus corpus antequam fuit mundus, nec manus spirituales, quia omnes ille sunt divina essencia, et sic Trinitas que non potest extendi vel pati nisi incidatur in heresim [p. 40] Patripassianorum, quos Augustinus recitat in libro suo de heresibus dixisse naturam divinam et per consequens patrem pati.

Here [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that the sense stated above is quite equivocal and quite strange to the common people, and an occasion whence many take up the faith of believing the bread and wine after [39] consecration to be [identically]{g:ydemptice} the body and blood of Christ: therefore, as the church has varied in other accidental things outside the faith, so it ought for cause to vary in logic, the teaching, and so the faith, standing. For they varied the observance of the sabbath from the seventh [day of the week]{g:feria} to the fifth, and thence to the first, which the church observes today, the faith standing unvaried;[^10] and so of many like things outside the faith. Why therefore is it not lawful for the church to vary in logic, keeping the faith and barring the entrance of [unbelief]{g:infidelitas}, as the keeper of a castle for a reasonable cause changes the gates? Nor is this lawful except with a sufficient and reasonable decree of the keepers. There are therefore some places in which it is expedient to vary in logic for the avoiding of danger, and some places in which it is not fitting to vary; and it is clear that it does not follow, if the holy doctors of the catholic church speak thus, that then it is lawful for any catholic so to speak; as blessed Bernard, in the sermon *On the Exaltation of the Holy Cross*, which begins thus, *De virga Jesse*, speaks thus: "The hands that founded the heavens were stretched out on the cross."[^11] And this saint and the usage of the church say many like things which are not to be [drawn]{g:trahere-in-consequenciam} by us generally into a precedent, because neither had the bodily hands of Christ, as eternal hands, been crucified, nor the spiritual hands, which are the [power]{g:potencia} of working;[^12] for he did not have bodily hands before he had been incarnate, since then God would have been a body before the world was; nor spiritual hands, since all these are the divine essence, and so the Trinity, which cannot be stretched out or suffer unless one falls into the heresy [40] of the Patripassians, whom Augustine [recites]{g:recitare} in his book on heresies as having said that the divine [nature]{g:natura}, and consequently the Father, suffered.[^13]

## ¶4 · 40.4–40.11

> [p. 40] Cum enim hec predicacio sit formalis, manus eterne Dei erant passe in cruce, patet quod consequenter concedi oportet quod virtus ymmo digitus Dei qui est Spiritus Sanctus extendebatur et paciebatur, et sic natura increata, potencia, virtus et bonitas, raciones exemplares et ydee forent palpabiles, et sic foret confusio personarum et naturarum, cum omnis natura foret sensibilis.

For since this predication, "the eternal hands of God suffered on the cross", is [formal]{g:predicacio-formalis},[^14] it is clear that consequently it must be granted that the [virtue]{g:virtus}, indeed the finger of God, which is the Holy Spirit, was stretched out and suffered, and so the uncreated nature, power, virtue and goodness, the [exemplary reasons]{g:raciones-exemplares} and [ideas]{g:ydea}, would be palpable, and so there would be [confusion]{g:confusio-naturarum} of the [persons]{g:persona} and of the natures, since every nature would be [perceptible]{g:sensibilis}.[^15]

## ¶5 · 40.12–40.26

> [p. 40] Item, si ex auctoritate scripture licet huiusmodi conclusiones extorquere, per idem concedendum foret quod Deus ante mundi constitucionem et sic eternaliter infinitum plus in corde de valde bono doluit quam dolere potuit aliqua creatura, quia Genes. VIº, 6 dicitur quod Deus tactus dolore cordis intrinsecus dixit quod penituit eum fecisse hominem; et sic habuisset infinita membra infinite et eternaliter dolorosa; nam sicut eternaliter novit peccatum hominis, sic eternaliter in corde doluit de eodem, quia in corde tactus fuit dolore; et cum per cor intelligitur Deus Pater iuxta illud Psalmi XLIVº, 2: Eructavit cor meum verbum bonum, videtur quod eternaliter doluit Deus Pater; et constat quod nulla creatura potest parificari sibi in dolore, sicut nec in noticia vel affeccione.

Again, if on the [authority]{g:auctoritas} of scripture it is lawful to extort [conclusions]{g:conclusio} of this kind, by the same token it would have to be granted that God, before the founding of the world, and so eternally, grieved in heart over a very good thing infinitely more than any [creature]{g:creatura} could grieve,[^16] because in Gen. 6:6 it is said that God, touched inwardly with sorrow of heart, said that "it repented him that he had made man";[^17] and so he would have had infinite members, infinitely and eternally sorrowful; for as he eternally knew the sin of man, so he eternally grieved in heart over it, since he was touched in heart with sorrow; and since by the heart God the Father is understood, according to that of Psalm 44:2, "My heart hath uttered a good word", it seems that God the Father grieved eternally; and it is evident that no creature can be made equal to him in sorrow, as neither in knowledge or affection.[^18]

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** Wyclif does not name his earlier exposition. The one printed is *De potestate pape* c. 11 (ed. Loserth, p. 279.1–15), which quotes Apoc. 19:8 and expounds the byssus as purity won by grace; his unprinted *Postilla* on the Apocalypse is not checked (LEDGER K02c-1).
[^2]: **S/T.** The print reads *Exod. XXXIVº*; D reads *Exod. XXIV*. The blood that confirmed the covenant, sprinkled after the sacrifice of calves, is Exod. 24:5–8 (DR 24:8 "the blood of the covenant"); the printed reference is translated as it stands.
[^3]: **S.** 1 Cor. 11:23–24, 26, as Loserth identifies them; Wyclif agrees with the Vulgate except that he drops *enim* in v. 26 and inserts *inquit*. Douay–Rheims wording.
[^4]: **C.** *Quadrupliciter*: "four times", Wyclif's count of the Apostle's references to the bread and wine in the passage just quoted. *Dicit hoc esse corpus eius*: the accusative-and-infinitive is kept ("says this to be"), so that the proposition is not asserted in the translator's voice.
[^5]: **T.** 37.22 *alii dicuntur* is Loserth's correction ("Correxi"). The manuscripts read AC *dicunt Cor[inthios]*, B *dicunteorum*, D *dicunt Corinthii*, E *Corinthios*: with them, "that others say the Corinthians, in the middle of a dinner, received ...". The print is translated.
[^6]: **T/S.** *Panem hunc* is lacking in all the manuscripts; Loserth added it ("Addidi") from the Vulgate of 1 Cor. 11:27. ABC read *cum sic* for *dum sic*.
[^7]: **C.** *Sentencia* here is the sense of the Apostle's words (the row *sentencia verborum*); at 38.19 and 38.23 it is the church's teaching (the CONTEXTUAL row, "teaching"). *Utrobique*: "in both places", 1 Cor. 11 and Acts 2:46, which follows.
[^8]: **C.** The subject of *manet* is *tam panis quam vinum*: the bread and wine "remain" after the blessing (*manere*, the verb of *remanencia*), and the same subject is then both sacrament and body of Christ "in sign and not in essence". *Quod sic frangitur et tractatur* is neuter singular and takes up what remains, answering the oath's words on the breaking and handling. *In Beringario*: in the decree *Ego Berengarius* (*De cons.* D. 2 c. 42; the form is ours) (LEDGER K02c-2).
[^9]: **C.** *Loyca*, "logic", is the church's manner of speaking about the sacrament (the form of words), set against *sentencia* and *fides*, which stand unchanged (38.30–39.4). *Secundum loycam variare*: "to vary as to logic". ABE read *solvendo obiectus* for *solucio obiectus* (38.22), and AB lack *secundo*.
[^10]: **C.** *Feria septima* is Saturday, *feria prima* Sunday, in the church's numbering of the week; the middle stage, *feria quinta* (Thursday), is as the print gives it. The subject of *variarunt* is unexpressed.
[^11]: **S.** The sermon *De exaltacione sancte crucis*, incipit *De virga Jesse*, is as Wyclif names it. It is not Bernard's: it is printed among Peter Damian's sermons (*Sermo* 47, PL 144, 761C–766B, inc. *De virga Jesse devenimus ad virgam crucis*). Loserth (39.19) proposed instead Bernard's *De duobus discipulis euntibus in Emaus* (*Opp.*, p. 1555) (LEDGER K02c-3). The quoted sentence stands verbatim at PL 144, 763D: *Manus illae, quae fundaverunt coelos, extensae sunt in cruce* (LEDGER K02c-4).
[^12]: **C.** *Eterne* is taken as the adjective with *manus* ("as eternal hands"), as *manus eterne Dei* at 40.5, not as an adverb ("eternally"). *Potencia* (CONTEXTUAL) is "power" here, a power of working, not Aristotle's potency. E reads *potencie*.
[^13]: **S.** Augustine, *De haeresibus* 41 (PL 42, 32), on the Sabellians: *sic innotuerint dicere Patrem passum, ut Patripassiani quam Sabelliani crebrius nuncupentur*. Loserth, who did not find the passage there, compares *Sermo* 52.6 (*Opp.* V.1, 304; PL 38, 357): *Excidit nobis quosdam male intelligentes vocari Patripassianos, qui dicunt ipsum Patrem natum ex femina, ipsum Patrem passum* (LEDGER K02c-5). E reads *recita*.
[^14]: **C.** *Cum ... sit formalis*: the opponent's taking of Bernard's sentence as a formal predication (one in which the predicate belongs to the subject as its form), from which Wyclif draws the absurd consequences; it is not his own claim. AB lack *ymmo*; C reads *concedi debet*, D *concedendum*.
[^15]: **C.** *Virtus* is rendered "virtue" in its old sense of strength or power (as DR Luke 8:46 "virtue is gone out from me"), to keep it apart from *potencia* "power" in the same list. The "finger of God" which is the Holy Spirit: compare Luke 11:20 with Matt. 12:28. In A *raciones exemplares* is in the margin in another hand.
[^16]: **C.** *De valde bono*: "over a very good thing", that is, over man, made among the things God saw to be "very good" (Gen. 1:31, *valde bona*); the neuter is Wyclif's. D reads *alia creatura*.
[^17]: **S.** Not a quotation of the Vulgate's order: Gen. 6:6 reads *paenituit eum quod hominem fecisset in terra et tactus dolore cordis intrinsecus*, and the *dixit* comes from 6:7 (*Delebo, inquit ... paenitet enim me fecisse eos*). Wyclif's paraphrase is translated; only Loserth's italic run is quoted. AB lack *dixit*; E reads *dixit quod penituisset*.
[^18]: **T/S.** ABCD read *si eternaliter* for *sic eternaliter* (40.19–20); the print is translated. Ps. 44:2 agrees with the Vulgate; C lacks *bonum*.
