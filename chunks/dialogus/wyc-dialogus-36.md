# *Dialogus*, chapter 36: the lords' remedy: seven years of poverty

## Truth · 84.2–85.29

> [p. 84] Cum ista sentencia sit tam multipliciter confirmata quod clerus debet servare pauperiem evangelicam instar Christi et apostolorum, sicut patet ex fide duplicis testamenti, et in contrario jacet tantum periculum tam futurum in anima quam temporale sensibile pro presenti, quelibet trium parcium ecclesie, sed specialiter temporales domini, debent istud periculum evitare, et primo in communi interloquio dicere istis prelatis cesariis quod nolunt propter amorem eorum, ymmo pocius propter eorum odium condempnari: et dictum est illis a catholicis quod si continuant in isto facinore clerum sic contra Christi regulam ditando, et non fructuose de ista stulticia penitendo, tunc sunt inexcusabiliter condempnandi, nec experimentum vel humana noticia de prosperitatibus vel aliis mundialibus docet oppositum: ideo volunt exonerare clerum de istis dominiis, sicut clerici fingunt se exonerasse per tempus brachium seculare: non dicunt quod per mille annos sed per septem vel tantum temporis in quanto clare doceant quod ista vita cleri sit fundata ex lege Christi et non cedat seculari brachio in periculum animarum. Et videtur quod istud sit racionabile cum tam clerus quam brachium seculare debet memorare solicite illud quod tantum concernit salutem sue anime; et tam opera Christi quam sua doctrina videntur illis expresse sonare in istam sentenciam. Clerus autem contra ipsam remurmurans in hoc innuit ipsum reum. Quomodo, rogo, domini temporales continuarent hoc opus misericordie per mille annos et clerici ipsum per tantum temporis acceptarunt, et tamen clerici remurmurarent quod [p. 85] per septem annos domini temporales exonerarent eos in quo tempore vivere possent facilius et conformius legi Christi! Numquid plus consonat racioni et fidei scripture quod domini temporales subeant istud onus quam quod clerus Christi sit per ipsum a Christi ministerio impeditus? Fratres volunt in isto puncto saltem ex sua professione et regula pro racionabilitate dictorum dominorum temporalium attestari. Et ista commutacio videtur posse prodesse cuilibet trium parcium ecclesie militantis. Prodesset enim clero cum ipsum poneret in numero et mensura quem deus disposuit, et excussis aliis secularibus officiis et vitis pompaticis, clerum ad officium assignatum a Christo pure et integre limitaret. prodesset 2° brachio seculari. Dimitto autem profectum sensibilem mundialem et considero profectum alium spiritalem, scilicet quod domini temporales non tunc necessitarentur indebitare se tercie parti ecclesie, nec esse suis tenentibus, ut dicitur modo, dotatos claustrales et alios onerosi. Quando enim innaturale dominium diabolice introducitur plus vexat subditos quam dominium a domino limitatum. Ideo creditur quod domini temporales forent plus misericordes et plus suis tenentibus graciosi quam clerici, qui contra Christi monita sunt dotati. Et sic prosperaretur tercia pars ecclesie tam in corporalibus quam in spiritualibus. Et totum corpus ecclesie foret secundum legem domini temperatum. Unde videtur quibusdam quod ille est infidelis domino, et cuicunque parti ecclesie tanquam proditor atque hereticus, qui isti sentencie contradicit. Rogo deum quod moveat cunctas tres partes ecclesie ut veritati fructuose consenciant in hac parte. Explicit speculum ecclesie militantis.

TRUTH. Since this [teaching]{g:sentencia} has been confirmed in so many ways, that the [clergy]{g:clerus} ought to keep evangelical poverty after the pattern of Christ and the apostles, as is clear [from the faith of the twofold testament]{g:ex-fide}, and on the contrary side lies so great a peril, both future, in the soul, and temporal and [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} for the present, each of the three parts of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, but especially the temporal [lords]{g:dominus}, ought to avoid this peril,[^1] and first, in common talk, to say to these [caesarean prelates]{g:prelatus} that they do not wish to be damned for love of them, indeed rather for hatred of them.[^2] And it has been told them by catholics that if they continue in this crime, [enriching]{g:ditare} the clergy thus against Christ's rule and not repenting fruitfully of this folly, then they are to be condemned beyond excuse; nor does experience or human knowledge of prosperities or other worldly things teach the opposite. Therefore they wish to relieve the clergy of these [lordships]{g:dominium}, as the [clerics]{g:clericus} pretend to have relieved the [secular arm]{g:brachium-seculare} for a time. They do not say for a thousand years, but for seven, or for so much time as it takes them to show clearly that this life of the clergy is founded on the [law of Christ]{g:lex-christi} and does not result, for the secular arm, in the peril of souls.[^3] And it seems that this is reasonable, since both the clergy and the secular arm ought to call to mind with care that which so greatly concerns the salvation of his soul; and both the works of Christ and his [doctrine]{g:doctrina} seem to them expressly to sound in favour of this teaching. But the clergy, murmuring against it, in this signals itself guilty. How, I ask, should the [temporal lords]{g:temporales-domini} have continued this work of mercy for a thousand years, and the clerics have accepted it for so long a time, and yet the clerics murmur that [85] for seven years the temporal lords should relieve them, a time in which they could live more easily and more in conformity with the law of Christ![^4] Does it not accord more with reason and with the faith of scripture that the temporal lords should take up this burden than that Christ's clergy should be hindered by it from Christ's ministry? The [friars]{g:frater}, on this point at least, by their profession and rule, are willing to bear witness to the reasonableness of the said temporal lords.[^5] And this [exchange]{g:commutacio} seems able to profit each of the three parts of the [church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans}. For it would profit the clergy, since it would put it in the number and measure that God disposed, and, other secular offices and pompous lives being shaken off, would [assign]{g:limitare} the clergy purely and wholly to the office appointed by Christ. Secondly, it would profit the secular arm. But I set aside the perceptible worldly profit and consider another, spiritual profit: namely, that the temporal lords would not then be driven to make themselves debtors to the [third part of the church]{g:tercia-pars-ecclesie},[^6] nor would [endowed]{g:dotacio} [cloisterers]{g:claustrales} and others have to be burdensome to their tenants, as is said now.[^7] For when an unnatural lordship is brought in by the devil's working, it vexes subjects more than a lordship assigned by the Lord. Therefore it is believed that the temporal lords would be more merciful and more gracious to their tenants than clerics, who are endowed against Christ's admonitions. And so the third part of the church would prosper both in bodily things and in spiritual. And the whole body of the church would be tempered according to the law of the Lord. Hence it seems to some that he is [faithless]{g:infidelis} to the Lord, and to every part of the church, as a traitor and heretic, who contradicts this teaching.[^8] I pray God that he move all three parts of the church to consent fruitfully to the truth in this matter. Here ends the Mirror of the Church Militant.[^9]

## Pollard's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 84.5: The obligation of poverty on the clergy being proved, all the three orders must beware of their peril.
- 84.8: Lords should tell the clergy 'we will not be damned for your sake.'
- 84.11: 'If we continue enriching you we are condemned beyond excuse.'
- 84.16: 'We are therefore willing to relieve you of your burdens.'
- 84.18: 'And this, not for a thousand years, but till you can show that you ought to hold property.'
- 84.26: The clergy cannot complain if the lords, after a thousand years of alms, give them seven years of poverty in conformance with Christ's law.
- 85.5: The friars may be applied to, to support the duty of poverty.
- 85.7: All three orders will be benefited.
- 85.9: The clergy, by being brought under Christ's rule.
- 85.14: The lords, by being relieved from the need of oppressing labourers.
- 85.21: The labourers, by having better masters.
- 85.25: God grant that the three orders may unite in adherence to the truth in this matter.

## Notes

[^1]: **C/R.** No speaker is named in this chapter: the voice continues Truth's from cap. 35, and
    at the close (85.26–28) it becomes the author's own prayer and explicit. *Quelibet trium parcium
    ecclesie … debent*: a singular subject with a plural verb, construed by sense. The three parts
    are the clergy, the temporal lords (the secular arm) and the commons, the *tercia pars ecclesie*
    of 85.15 and 85.22.

[^2]: **C.** *Propter amorem eorum, ymmo pocius propter eorum odium*: *eorum* is the prelates.
    *Ymmo* corrects upward: to go on enriching them would be not love of them but hatred, since it
    ruins them. The alternative takes *eorum odium* as subjective ("for their hatred"), which fits
    the correction less well.

[^3]: **C/R.** *Sicut clerici fingunt se exonerasse per tempus brachium seculare*: the clergy's
    claim, rehearsed in cap. 35 (p. 82), that the pope relieves the emperor by bearing "the burden
    of earthly lordship". The lords offer the relief back, not *per mille annos* (the span of the
    endowed church in cap. 35) but for seven years, or *tantum temporis in quanto clare doceant*:
    "so much time as it takes them to show clearly"; Pollard's side-note: "till you can show that
    you ought to hold property". *Non cedat seculari brachio in periculum animarum*: we take *cedat*
    as "result", with a dative of the party affected; "does not yield to the secular arm" is also
    possible.

[^4]: **T.** 84.28 *et tamen clerici remurmurarent*: B reads *cum* (corrected from *tamen*), C
    *cum*, B¹ *tamen* (corrected from *cum*): with *cum*, "since the clerics murmur". We translate
    the print. The sentence is printed with an exclamation mark: an indignant question.

[^5]: **C.** *Pro racionabilitate dictorum dominorum temporalium*: "of the said temporal lords";
    *dictorum* could also be the noun, "the reasonableness of the temporal lords' sayings". The
    friars' profession of poverty is itself the witness.

[^6]: **C.** *Indebitare se tercie parti ecclesie*: "to make themselves debtors to the third part",
    i.e. to lie under obligations to (or to exact dues from) the commons. Pollard's side-note at
    85.14 reads it as "the need of oppressing labourers".

[^7]: **T/C.** Printed *onerosi*, agreeing with *domini* ("nor [would they need] to be burdensome
    to their tenants"), leaves *dotatos claustrales et alios* without construction. B, B¹, C read
    *onerosos*, which makes the cloisterers the accusative subject of *esse*; we translate it. *Suis
    tenentibus* may then be the cloisterers' tenants or the lords'. *Ut dicitur modo*: "as is said
    now", i.e. by common report.

[^8]: **G/C.** *Infidelis domino*: with a dative, "faithless to the Lord", not the row's
    "unbeliever", which would lose the construction; the sense is disloyalty, matched by *proditor*.
    *Cuicunque parti ecclesie*: literally "to whatever part of the church", i.e. to every part.

[^9]: **T.** 85.26: B, B¹ read *Rogo igitur*, C *Rogo ergo*; B, C read *istas* for *cunctas*; B¹
    omits *veritati*. After *parte* the apparatus records, without siglum, "To whom be honour and
    imperial power for infinite ages of ages. Amen etc." (then in red letters); B: "Corrected in the
    year of the Lord 1414, the Friday after the feast of George"; B¹: "Here ends the Dialogue of
    Master John Wic."; and, again without siglum, "And so is the end of the little treatise of
    M. Jo. W., namely the dialogue".
