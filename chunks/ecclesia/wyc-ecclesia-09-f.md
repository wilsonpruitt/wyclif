# *De ecclesia*, chapter 9, part 6: the hidden judgment; the sure way of temporal alms; the answer to the sixth objection

## ¶1 · 202.30–204.26

> [p. 202] Et raciones omnium istorum sunt nobis abscondite, (ut dictum est.... capitulo). Nam secretum divine ordinacionis dies nostre mortis et dies finalis divisionis ideo sunt a mortalibus occultati, ut nec presumamus [p. 203] nec despiciamus, sed timentes senciamus humiliter et amemus generaliter; et quoad duo sequencia, ut vigilemus a peccato continue et obviam sponso cum oleo et lumine sollicitissime nos paremus. Et ista ignorancia est racionalis et salubris. Ideo pertinenter dicit canon sub auctoritate Augustini XXIIIª, q. IV: Quantus arrogancie tumor est, quanta humilitatis et lenitatis oblivio quantaque arrogancie iactancia, ut quis se credat posse facere quod nec apostolis concessit dominus, ut zizannia videlicet a frumento posse se putet discernere. Ex istis formari potest talis racio: Quilibet christianus debet in ambiguis viam perplexam abicere et viam securam eligere, sed omnis dominus secularis dotans vel privilegians ecclesiam est christianus cui talis duplex via exponitur, ergo debet eligere viam certam: sed certum est quod dare elemosinas temporales est via certa, dare autem elemosinas perpetuas est via ambigua, ergo prima via facilior et cercior est eligenda, neglecta alia. Quis queso presumeret viam eligere per quam verisimiliter credere posset Deum offendere? Quis insuper dubitat quin ex perpetuacione elemosinarum fundantur lites et bella per que subtrahitur religio christiana, originantur utrimque superbia et cetera vicia, in tantum quod elemosinarius insurgit saltem contencione cauterizante contra elemosinantem, et (quod est pessimum) surrepentibus multis discolis loco veri cleri cui donata sunt temporalia, aspiratur ad statum propter temporale lucrum, contempto vero officio? Omnia autem ista vel extinguerentur vel mitigarentur data elemosina temporaliter probabiliter egenis qui fideliter peragerent incumbens ministerium, et non data cece ad securandum [p. 204] discolos futuros quos datarius ignorat. Hoc enim plus consonat legi Christi qui nec suscepit nec docuit elemosinas tales fieri, sed pocius oppositum. Nec dubium quin temporalis elemosina de decimis, de oblacionibus et sponte oblatis sufficeret ad numerum tanti cleri quanto eget ecclesia. Non ergo ex veteri testamento vel novo fundari potest tanta et talis dotacio. Unde sophisma diaboli procedens nunc ex equivocis, nunc per locum ab insufficiente similitudine conturbat ecclesiam dicentem Canticorum VI°, 11, Anima mea turbata est propter currus Aminadab. Nunc enim ecclesiastici procedunt currati cum multitudine cliencium quasi reges et reputant privilegium quod ceci et inhabiles et ignari preficiantur in altis statibus quoad mundum (de qua materia patet alibi). Sed redeundo ad difficultatem dicitur quod potentatuum seculi est privilegia terrena (quantum in eis est) concedere, et Dei virtute et gracia confirmare sine quibus non recipit privilegium ultimum complementum. Unde si hoc moveret, tunc nullus sacerdos posset conficere vel dare sacramenta, nullus fidelis posset facere opera meritoria, nec aliquis homo preter Christum dare alteri quevis dona. Omnia enim ista implicant graciam Dei, ideo non fiunt, nisi Deus approbando preveniat et det graciam; quod solus Deus potest facere.

And the reasons of all these are hidden from us (as was said in chapter [ ]).[^1] For the secret of the divine [ordinance]{g:ordinacio}, the day of our death and the day of the final division are hidden from mortals for this reason: that we neither presume [203] nor despise, but, fearing, think humbly and love generally; and, as to the two that follow, that we watch continually against sin and most carefully make ourselves ready to meet the [bridegroom]{g:sponsus} with oil and light. And this ignorance is reasonable and wholesome.[^2] Therefore the canon says pertinently, under the authority of Augustine, C. 23 q. 4: "How great is the swelling of arrogance, how great the forgetfulness of humility and gentleness, and how great the boasting of arrogance, that anyone should believe he can do what the Lord did not grant even to the apostles, namely should think that he can distinguish the [cockle]{g:zizania} from the wheat."[^3] From these such an argument can be formed: Every Christian ought in doubtful things to reject the [perplexed]{g:perplexus} way and choose the safe way; now every secular lord who endows or [privileges]{g:privilegiare} the [church]{g:ecclesia} is a Christian to whom such a double way is set forth; therefore he ought to choose the certain way. Now it is certain that to give temporal [alms]{g:elemosina} is a certain way, but to give perpetual alms is a doubtful way; therefore the first way, easier and more certain, is to be chosen, the other being set aside.[^4] Who, I ask, would presume to choose a way by which he could in all likelihood believe that he would offend God? Who, moreover, doubts that from the perpetuation of alms there are founded lawsuits and wars, through which the Christian religion is withdrawn; that pride and the other vices arise on both sides, so far that the [almsman]{g:elemosinarius} rises up, at least with a searing contention, against the almsgiver; and (what is worst) that, many [froward]{g:discoli} men creeping in in place of the true [clergy]{g:clerus} to whom the [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} were given, men aspire to the state for temporal gain, the true office being despised?[^5] But all these things would either be extinguished or mitigated if alms were given for a time to those in probable need, who would faithfully carry out the ministry incumbent on them, and not given blindly to secure [204] future froward men whom the giver does not know.[^6] For this accords more with the law of Christ, who neither received nor taught that such alms be made, but rather the opposite. Nor is there doubt that temporal alms from [tithes]{g:decime}, from oblations and from things freely offered would suffice for the number of so great a clergy as the church needs. Therefore so great and such an [endowment]{g:dotacio} cannot be founded on the old testament or the new. Hence the devil's [sophism]{g:sophisma}, proceeding now from equivocals, now by the topic from insufficient likeness, troubles the church, which says, Song of Songs 6:11: "My soul is troubled for the chariots of Aminadab."[^7] For now [churchmen]{g:ecclesiastici} go forth in chariots with a multitude of retainers, like kings, and count it a [privilege]{g:privilegium} that the blind, the [unqualified]{g:inhabilis} and the ignorant are set over high states as regards the world (on which matter it is clear elsewhere).[^8] But returning to the difficulty, the answer is that it belongs to the potentates of the world to grant earthly privileges (as far as in them lies), and to God to confirm them by power and grace, without which a privilege does not receive its final completion. Hence if this moved [us], then no priest could consecrate or give the sacraments, no faithful man could do meritorious works, nor could any man except Christ give another any gifts whatever. For all these imply the grace of God; therefore they are not done unless God, approving, goes before and gives grace; which God alone can do.[^9]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 203.12: A christian is bound: to take the sure way.
- 203.17: Giving temporal alms is sure; giving perpetual alms uncertain,
- 203.23: since it leads to quarrels,
- 203.27: and induces unfit men to take clerical office.
- 204.16: To sum up: rulers have the power of giving privileges so far as they are confirmed by God.

## Notes

[^1]: **T.** The chapter number is wanting in the codices, which leave a gap (Loserth, 202.31); the English leaves the gap. *Istorum*: the divisions of each yoke into the saved and the damned (202.1–29, 09-e). B reads *Nam decretum* (202.31) for *Nam secretum*, "the decree of the divine ordinance"; the print is translated.

[^2]: **C/T.** *Nec presumamus nec despiciamus*: that we neither presume [on our own salvation] nor despise [others]; *despiciamus* is translated as printed, though the usual pair is presumption and despair. *Senciamus humiliter*: cf. Romans 11:20, "Be not highminded, but fear". *Quoad duo sequencia*: the day of death and the day of the final division call for watchfulness. The oil and the light: the wise virgins (Matthew 25). The codices read *solitissime* (203.4); Loserth's *sollicitissime* is translated.

[^3]: **S.** Loserth: Decretum, *Decr.* II, causa XXII, q. IV, c. XV; the text above reads XXIIIª, and Loserth's "XXII" is evidently a misprint for C. 23 (the modern form is ours). But the passage is c. 14 (*Boni a malis numquam in hac vita penitus possunt separari*; PL 187:1178; Friedberg I, 904–05), inscribed Augustine, *De baptismo* IV.12, who quotes Cyprian, *Ep.* 55; c. 15 is the Gregory passage at 201.6–9 (09-e) (LEDGER E09f-1). The cockle and the wheat: Matthew 13:24–30.

[^4]: **C/T.** A margin *Racio* (203.12), and *Nota contra perpetuacionem elemosine* (203.17). The first *sed* brings the minor premiss ("now"), the second a new premiss ("now"). *Elemosinas temporales*: alms given for a time, against alms given in perpetuity. Side-notes 203.12, 203.17.

[^5]: **C/T.** B reads *contenciose* (203.20). *Contencione cauterizante*: with a contention that burns like a cautery. *Aspiratur*, impersonal: men aspire. *Elemosinarius … elemosinantem*: the almsman, who receives, against the almsgiver, who gives (GLOSSARY). Side-notes 203.23, 203.27.

[^6]: **C.** *Data elemosina temporaliter probabiliter egenis*: ablative absolute with conditional force; we take *probabiliter* with *egenis*, "to those in probable need", as far as the giver can judge, set against *cece*, "blindly", below. *Datarius*: the giver.

[^7]: **S/C.** Song of Songs 6:11: the Vulgate has *anima mea conturbavit me propter quadrigas Aminadab* (DR "my soul troubled me for the chariots of Aminadab"); Wyclif's *anima mea turbata est propter currus Aminadab* is translated. Loserth refers it to Song of Songs 6:11 (*propter quadrigas*, 204.11). *Per locum ab insufficiente similitudine*: the counterpart of the topic *a sufficienti similitudine* (195.27, 09-d).

[^8]: **G/C/T.** *Ecclesiastici*: GLOSSARY CONTEXTUAL; here the clergy, rendered "churchmen". *Currati*: riding in chariots, answering Aminadab's chariots. B reads *altis stautibus* (204.15). "Elsewhere": not identified (LEDGER E09f-2).

[^9]: **C/T.** *Potentatuum seculi est … et Dei … confirmare*: we take *Dei* as a second genitive of what belongs to someone, "and [it belongs] to God to confirm"; it could also be read "and to confirm them by God's power and grace". Side-note 204.16 supports the first. *Si hoc moveret*: if this argument (the sixth, 199.20, 09-e) were sound. *Conficere*: to consecrate the eucharist. *Preveniat*: goes before, as prevenient grace (GLOSSARY *gracia preveniens*). A margin *Nota de privilegio Westermonasterii* (204.28) falls after this chunk.
