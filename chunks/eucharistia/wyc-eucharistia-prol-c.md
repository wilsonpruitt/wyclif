# *De eucharistia*, prologue, part 3: chapter nine, the arguments from authority and the seven objections

## ¶1 · 8.30–10.18

> [p. 8] Capitulum nonum arguit multipliciter contra dicta, primo per hoc quod Innocencius IIIᵘˢ cum mille trecentis [p. 9] et quindecim diffinierunt hodiernam sentenciam, et respondet tripliciter, primo per hoc quod non docetur quod aliquis eorum fuit membrum ecclesie, secundo quod illorum sentencia est glosanda, et tercio quod non est credendum homini in materia fidei nisi de quanto dictum suum docuerit ex scriptura. Et narrat causam multiplicem quare diffinicio Innocencii est suspecta. Secundo arguit per hoc quod decretum Nicholai est in eadem dampnacione, et respondet assignando causam multiplicem quare testimonium illud est pocius, et declarat quomodo in materia fidei credi debet doctoribus et quomodo Augustinus ex fide scripture invexit contra Donatistas. Qua sentencia servata hodie non foret ecclesia tot creditorum novitatibus onerata. Et describit opera huius Innocencii, ut eius auctoritas plus patescat. Tercio arguit ex testimonio doctorum, legis Dei et legis ecclesie; et respondet declarando quomodo nemo debet accipi nisi ut testis veritatis. Et exhinc variantur gradus credendi hominibus adiecta causa quare non debet credi isti sentencie; et descendit ad tres evidencias Lanfranci qui videtur fuisse unus de primis cultoribus accidentis. Quarta racio obicit de multitudine sanctorum et famosorum doctorum istius sentencie et retorquet ad oppositum, quod sancciores et famosiores illis tenuerunt contrarium; quare ergo non credimus illis? Et declarat primo auctoritatem Romani episcopi et quomodo ex lege propria est infirma et quomodo pape instarent pro declaracione istius fidei, cum [p. 10] videtur scriptura vocare Eukaristiam panem et quid figurat quod cultores signorum tanquam ydolatre contendunt infundabiliter circa hoc signum. Quinto obicit sextupliciter quod corpus Christi non est quantum in Eukaristia et respondet solvendo tres obiectus narrat quomodo Deus habet esse per loca differenter a creaturis et quomodo dissonat claritati corporis Christi esse in hostia. Sexta obieccio tractat quomodo corpus Christi manducatur et movetur in hostia et recitando dictum Damasceni narrat periculum huius erroris et quomodo maius miraculum est cognacio Christi cum membris suis quam Eukaristia. Septima obieccio declarat qua adoracione est hostia adoranda et quomodo sacerdotes debent communicare bona ecclesie que iacent in manu eorum mortua ad defensionem regnorum, quod probatur tripliciter regno Anglie pertinere; et recitat tres sophisticaciones cleri in quibus populus est illusus.

The ninth chapter argues in many ways against what has been said: first, on the ground that Innocent III with one thousand three hundred [9] and fifteen defined today's [teaching]{g:sentencia};[^1] and it replies in three ways: first, that it is not taught that any of them was a [member]{g:membrum} of the [church]{g:ecclesia}; second, that their teaching is to be [glossed]{g:glosare}; and third, that a man is not to be believed in a matter of faith except so far as he has taught his saying from scripture. And it [relates]{g:narrare} the manifold cause why Innocent's [definition]{g:diffinicio} is suspect. Second, it argues on the ground that the [decree]{g:decretum} of Nicholas is in the same [condemnation]{g:dampnacio}, and it replies by assigning a manifold cause why that testimony is the weightier;[^2] and it [makes clear]{g:declarare} how the doctors ought to be believed in a matter of faith, and how Augustine inveighed against the Donatists [from the faith of scripture]{g:ex-fide}.[^3] Were this teaching kept, the church would not today be burdened with so many novelties of things believed. And it describes the works of this Innocent, so that his [authority]{g:auctoritas} may be the more apparent. Third, it argues from the testimony of the doctors, of the law of God and of the law of the church; and it replies by making clear how no one ought to be accepted except as a witness of the truth. And from this the [degrees]{g:gradus} of believing men are varied, with the cause added why this teaching ought not to be believed; and it comes down to three [evidences]{g:evidencia} of Lanfranc, who seems to have been one of the first [worshippers]{g:cultores-accidencium} of the [accident]{g:accidens}.[^4] The fourth argument objects from the multitude of saints and famous doctors of this teaching, and it turns [it] back to the opposite: that men holier and more famous than they held the contrary; why then do we not believe them? And it makes clear, first, the authority of [the Roman bishop]{g:romanus-episcopus}, and how by its own law it is weak, and how the popes should press for the declaration of this faith, since [10] scripture seems to call the Eucharist [bread]{g:panis}; and what it [figures]{g:figurare} that the worshippers of signs, as idolaters, contend [without foundation]{g:infundabiliter} about this [sign]{g:signum}.[^5] Fifth, it objects in six ways that the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} is not [quantified]{g:quantus} in the Eucharist; and it replies: in resolving three objections it relates how God has [being]{g:esse} through places differently from [creatures]{g:creatura}, and how it is out of keeping with the [clarity]{g:claritas} of the body of Christ to be in the [host]{g:hostia}.[^6] The sixth objection treats how the body of Christ is eaten and moved in the host, and, [reciting]{g:recitare} a saying of Damascene, it relates the danger of this error, and how the [kinship]{g:cognacio} of Christ with his members is a greater miracle than the Eucharist.[^7] The seventh objection makes clear with what adoration the host is to be adored, and how [priests]{g:sacerdos} ought to share the goods of the church which lie in their [dead hand]{g:manus-mortua} for the defence of kingdoms, which is proved in three ways to pertain to the realm of England; and it recites three [sophistries]{g:sophisticacio} of the [clergy]{g:clerus} by which the people has been deceived.[^8]

## Notes

[^1]: **S/C.** Innocent III's definition is *Firmiter* (Lateran IV, 1215, c. 1); the number "one thousand three hundred and fifteen" is the Latin's, and recurs in ch. 9 (272.17–19, *mille CCC et XV prelati*); *Firmiter* is X 1.1.1, whose *transsubstantiatis pane in corpus* is the definition in view (LEDGER Kprolc-1). The plural *diffinierunt* takes Innocent with the council as its subject (AC *qui diffiniunt*, B *qui diffinierunt*, 9.1). *Sentencia* (CONTEXTUAL): the position taught, "teaching", here the opponents' (*hodiernam*, "today's").

[^2]: **C/S.** *Dampnacio* (CONTEXTUAL): "condemnation", of a teaching; the decree of Nicholas (*Ego Berengarius*, *De cons.* D. 2 c. 42, modern form ours) stands with Innocent's definition against the same teaching. *Pocius*: "the weightier", of more account. So named in ch. 9 (279.11–15) (LEDGER Kprolc-2).

[^3]: **S.** Augustine against the Donatists: ch. 9 (280.17–282.6) appeals to his ground that the church is not to be burdened beyond scripture; Loserth (282.4) gives *De baptismo* II.3 (*Quis autem nesciat sanctam Scripturam canonicam … omnibus posterioribus episcoporum litteris ita praeponi*) and *Contra Faustum* XIII.5 (LEDGER Kprolc-3). C has *ex fide* in the margin in another hand (9.13).

[^4]: **C/S.** *Gradus credendi hominibus*: the degrees in which belief is given to men. *Accidentis*: the accident without a subject. Lanfranc's three evidences (283.27–284.2) are from his *De corpore et sanguine Domini*: the first two (the manna; foretelling the future more divine than narrating the past) are c. 22, the third the decrees he alleges (LEDGER Kprolc-4).

[^5]: **C.** *Et quid figurat quod …*: likeliest "what it figures (betokens) that the worshippers of signs contend …"; or *quid figurat* ends the clause on scripture ("what [the bread] figures"), with *quod* opening a new statement. *Ex lege propria*: by its own law, the canon law. B *vacari*, D *vocari* (10.1).

[^6]: **C/T.** *Respondet solvendo tres obiectus narrat*: two finite verbs with no *et*; we construe the second as the content of the reply. A word may have been lost (10.5–6). *Claritas*: "clarity", the dowry of the glorified body (with agility, subtlety and impassibility), its radiance. B omits *per loca — esse* (10.6–8).

[^7]: **T/S.** *Cognacio Christi cum membris suis* is Loserth's emendation; the manuscripts read *cognacio Christi et vino* (M: *viam*) *Christi*, and he cites the passage in chapter 9 it summarises (*Ista quidem cognacio …*, 314.21–22), where the kinship is with those who do the Father's will (Matt 12:50) (10.11–12). A reads *inanis miraculum*, B *maius periculum*, B *cognicio*. Damascene's saying is *De fide orthodoxa* IV.13 (Wyclif *III Sentenciarum*, 310.7–16; Loserth corrects) (LEDGER Kprolc-5).

[^8]: **C.** *In manu eorum mortua*: "in their dead hand", the mortmain of land held by the church. *Communicare*: to share (BD *communicari*, 10.14).
