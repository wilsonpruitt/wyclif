## Doubtful, left as they are

- **01-c, p. 21**: *Sed quid magis paganicum quam quod nos possumus figurare ad libitum Deum nostrum?*, rendered "But". The same case as 01-a p. 15 in wave 1: either (a), the last step of the reductio (*now* what is more pagan?), or (b), the turn against the absurdity just drawn out. "But" is defensible.
- **01-c, p. 21**: *in hostia una sit albius, in alia **autem** fuscius*, rendered "but in another darker". A correlative *una … alia autem*; one could call it continuative ("and"), but the contrast whiter/darker is real and the next pair (*maius … minus*) runs without a connective. "But" is defensible.
- **01-b, p. 18**: *comestus **autem** spiritualiter superat comedentem*, rendered "and the one eaten …". It could be read as adversative (eater vs. eaten), but both clauses unfold the single *econtra* introduced by *vero*, so it is continuative (c). Probably right; noted only.
- **01-c, p. 22**: *Ad Jesum **autem** cum venissent* (Jn 19:33, quoted), rendered "But after they were come". It is scripture, and it follows the KJV wording; left as it is.
