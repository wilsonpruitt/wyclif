# *De potestate pape*, chapter 11, part 5: the vestments of the priesthood: girdle, breeches, inner tunic, superhumeral

## ¶1 · 279.16–281.17

> [p. 279] Secunda vestis estzona, baltheus vel cingulum, latitudinis quasi quatuor digitorum, reticulatam, quasi pellis vipperea vel stricta tunica linea circa renes, per cuius fortem tinccionem notatur, ut castitas sacerdotis nullo incentivorum estu, cogitacione velopere dissolvatur, iuxta illud LuceXII, 35: Sint lumbi vestri precincti. Et sic candor vestis signat mundiciam, zona vero continenciam, ut illic non sordescat, hic non effluat conversacio sacerdotis. De zona legitur Exod. XXVII: distincta quatuor coloribus, scilicet bisso et purpura, iacincto et cocco. Per que iuxta signacionem predictam duplicem duplex quaternarius designatur: Primo notantur quatuor virtutes cardinales, scilicet iusticia, fortitudo, temperancia et prudencia, per quas cinctus animus servat albedinem vestis, per quam sit digna, ut dicitur Apoc. XIV, 1 sequi agnum quocumque ierit. Et est correspondencia electorum in istis quatuor coloratis ad istas quatuor virtutes. [p. 280] cum bissus terre admodo, ut patet de combustis cineribus, ossibus et ceteris albis siccis notat iusticiam, que instar terre solide stat in omni temptacione, ut Aristoteles dicit I Ethicorum sicut tetragonus sine vituperio. Purpuravero ignem pretendens, racione ruboris signat fortitudinem purgatam, ut aurum in camino tribulacionis, ut loquitur scriptura, laccinctus autem celestis coloris, aquam induratam signans, notat temperanciam que massam corporis humani sic temperat, ut in via morum propter amorem celestium non effluat terminis alienis. Coccus vero bis tinctus est genus tincture medius inter rubeum et croceum, quem nos dicimus vermiculum vel subrubeum colorem ex silvestribus frondibus, vel (ut aliis placet) ex quodam verme decoctum; et signat prudenciam vigentem in sanguineis instar aeris temperatis et specialiter si tincti fuerint preter virtutes morales in prudencia conversacionis penalis domini Ihesu Christi. Secundo notantur quatuor humores, scilicet melancolica et colerica, flegma et sanguis, quibus nature sagacitate commixtis humana complexio denotatur. Sic inquam istis quatuor moribus continencia figuratur. Sicut enim electa in mixtis, sic humores in animalibus et proprietates spirituales in mentibus habent sua officia. Terra enim facit mixto constanciam, aqua continuacionem, aer capacitatem celestis influencie et ignis est via recipiendi peregrinas impressiones et quasi luna instrumentalis, mediante qua mitigantur frigiditas et humiditas inferioris duplicis elementi et confortatur qualitas aeris, ut puta raritas atque caliditas. Sic est de complexione hominis corporali [p. 281] et de continencia mentis spirituali, dum memoria proprie fragilitatis ut terra, continuacio dulcis devocionis ut aqua, tenuitas corporalis cibacionis ut aer et raritas muliebris conversacionis ut ignis, faciunt sacerdotem Domini continentem. Nichil enim tantum valet ad domandum carnis desideria, sicut pensare quomodo incinerabitur post mortem hominis facta terra; continuacio vero devocionis, extinguens ocium, precavet omne crimen et per consequens carnis luxum. Ieiunium vero cavens crapulam cum adiutorio celestis radii extinguit ignem libidinis, et caritativa fuga muliebris consorcii et profugienda luxuria supereminens medicina. Evanescas igitur et non expectes in cogitacione vel corpore muliebre consorcium instar ignis, quia nisi habueris ignem amoris, non calesces supernaturaliter.

The second vestment is the girdle, belt or cincture, about four fingers broad, netted[^1] like a viper's skin, or "a close linen tunic about the loins, by whose firm binding[^2] it is signified that the chastity of the priest is not to be loosened by any heat of incitements, in thought or in deed, according to that of Luke 12:35: 'Let your loins be girt.' And so the whiteness of the vestment signifies cleanness, and the girdle continence, so that there the [manner of life]{g:conversacio} of the priest may not grow foul, and here may not flow away. Of the girdle we read in Exodus 27: distinguished with four colours, namely fine linen and purple, violet and scarlet."[^3] By these, according to the twofold signification already stated, a twofold [four]{g:quaternarius} is designated. First, the four cardinal virtues are signified, namely justice, fortitude, temperance and prudence; girt with these the mind keeps the whiteness of the vestment, by which it is worthy, as is said in Apocalypse 14:1, "to follow the Lamb whithersoever he goeth."[^4] And there is a correspondence of the elements[^5] in these four coloured things to these four virtues, [280] since fine linen, after the manner of earth[^6] (as is clear from burnt ashes, bones and other white, dry things), signifies justice, which like solid earth stands in every temptation, as Aristotle says in *Ethics* I, "like a square [man] without reproach."[^7] And purple, representing fire, by reason of its redness signifies fortitude purged, like "gold in the furnace of tribulation", as scripture says.[^8] And violet, of heavenly colour, signifying hardened water, denotes temperance, which so tempers the mass of the human body that on the way of morals, for love of heavenly things, it does not flow out into alien bounds. And scarlet twice dyed is a kind of dye midway between red and saffron, which we call vermilion or a reddish colour, boiled out of woodland leaves or (as others prefer) out of a certain worm; and it signifies prudence flourishing in the sanguine, who are tempered like air, and especially if they have been dyed, beyond the moral virtues, in the prudence of the hard manner of life of the Lord Jesus Christ.[^9] Second, the four humours are signified, namely the melancholic and the choleric, phlegm and blood, by whose mixture through the skill of nature the human complexion is denoted. So, I say, by these four habits[^10] continence is figured. For as the elements in mixed bodies, so the humours in animals and spiritual properties in minds have their offices. For earth gives the mixed body stability, water continuity, air a capacity for [celestial influence]{g:influencia}, and fire is the way of receiving foreign impressions and, as it were, an instrumental moon,[^11] by means of which the coldness and moisture of the lower twofold element are mitigated and the quality of air is strengthened, namely rarity and heat. So it is with the bodily complexion of man [281] and with the spiritual continence of the mind: memory of one's own frailty as earth, continuity of sweet devotion as water, slenderness of bodily feeding as air, and rareness of converse with women as fire,[^12] make the priest of the Lord continent. For nothing avails so much for taming the desires of the flesh as to ponder how, become earth after the man's death, [it] will be turned to ashes; and continuity of devotion, putting out idleness, guards against every crime and consequently against the wantonness of the flesh. And fasting, guarding against surfeit, with the help of the heavenly ray puts out the fire of lust; and charitable flight from the company of women [is], for the lechery that must be fled, the supereminent medicine.[^13] Vanish, therefore, and do not wait, in thought or in body, for the company of women, which is like fire; for unless you have the fire of love, you will not grow warm supernaturally.

## ¶2 · 281.18–282.2

> [p. 281] Tercia vestis sunt femoralia, que preter braccas com munes sacerdos induet m signum castitatis eciam interioris, dum vacat divinis officiis. Non enim credi debet sacerdotes veteris testamenti alias continue discooperta carne pudoris incedere, ut solum intrando in sancta verenda tegere iubeantur, sed servando continue castitatem coniugalem voluit sermo Dei, ut sacerdotem fungentem officio sacri ministerii cooperiat castitas singularis; unde quia sacerdotes novi testamenti inconiugati servare debent castitatem virginalem continue, ideo tantum utuntur femoralibus communibus ad denotandum, quod continue non licet eis ut sacerdotibus legis veteris post completum officium, laxare ministerium carnalis copule; eis enim pro servanda castitate [p. 282] est cottidiana necessitas, quibus continue offerrendi summum sacrificium concessa est potestas.

The third vestment is the breeches, which the [priest]{g:sacerdos} puts on besides the common drawers as a sign of chastity, inward chastity too, while he is free for the divine offices. "For it must not be believed that the priests of the old testament otherwise went about continually with the flesh of shame uncovered, so that only on entering the holy place were they bidden to cover their privy parts; but, by keeping conjugal chastity continually, the word of God willed that a singular chastity should cover the priest performing the office of the sacred ministry.[^14] Hence, because the unmarried priests of the new testament must keep virginal chastity continually, therefore they use only the common breeches, to denote that it is not lawful for them, as it was for the priests of the old law after their office was completed, to [loose]{g:laxare} themselves continually to the ministry of carnal union; for they have [282] a daily necessity of keeping chastity, to whom the [power]{g:potestas} of continually offering the highest sacrifice has been granted."[^15]

## ¶3 · 282.3–282.15

> [p. 282] Quarta vestis est tunica interior vel iacinctina, qua in utroque testamento utuntur soli pontifices ad denotandum, quod in illis debet precipue duplex castitas scilicet mentis et corporis observari. Pontifex quidem, qui de thesauro suo debet proferre nova et vetera, cum carnalis copula distrahit a spirituali noticia duplicis testamenti, debet observare duplicem castitatem. Ista igitur tunica priori superaddita signat quod dissimilis terrestribus debet de celestibus cogitare. Ideo vocatur tunica interior iacinctina, ut significet cordis mundiciam, et de celestibus cogitatum. Phil. III. 20 dicit Apostolus: nostra conversacio in celo est.

The fourth vestment is the inner or violet tunic, which in both testaments the pontiffs alone use, to denote that in them a twofold chastity, namely of mind and of body, ought especially to be observed. For the pontiff, who must bring forth out of his treasure new things and old, must observe a twofold chastity, since carnal union draws [a man] away from the spiritual knowledge of the twofold testament.[^16] This tunic, therefore, added over the former, signifies that, unlike earthly men, he must think of heavenly things. Therefore it is called the inner violet tunic, to signify cleanness of heart and thought of heavenly things. In Philippians 3:20 the Apostle says: "our manner of life is in heaven."[^17]

## ¶4 · 282.16–282.33

> [p. 282] Quinta uestis est superhumerale, que hebrayce ephot dicitur quasi casula vel planeta; et contextum erat pilis de auro ac coloribus supradictis. In veteri vero testamento soli pontifices usi sunt hac veste quatuor distincta coloribus, ad sensum de zona expositum; tempore vere gracie utuntur ea quilibet sacerdotes. Signat autem superhumerale fortitudinem operum et pacienciam laborum, per duos humeros denotatas, unde altrinsecus super humeros fibulabantur duo lapides onichini, in quorum dextro inscripta erant sex nomina seniorum filiorum Ysrahel, et sex in alio, denotando quod sacerdos debet recolere de patriarcharum constancia tam in prosperis quam in adversis. Nostrum autem superhumerale non sic inscribitur, quia tempore gracie dimissis cerimoniis antiquis, veniente unico patriarcha, debemus plus inniti simplici veritati.

The fifth vestment is the superhumeral,[^18] which in Hebrew is called ephod, like a chasuble or planeta; and it was woven of strands of gold[^19] and of the colours aforesaid. And in the old testament the pontiffs alone used this vestment, distinguished with four colours, in the sense expounded for the girdle; in the time of true grace[^20] any priests whatever use it. "And the superhumeral signifies the fortitude of works and the patience of labours, denoted by the two shoulders; hence on either side two onyx stones were clasped on the shoulders, on the right one of which were inscribed six names of the elder sons of Israel, and six on the other, denoting that the priest ought to recall the constancy of the patriarchs in prosperity as in adversity. But our superhumeral is not so inscribed, because in the time of grace, the ancient ceremonies having been dismissed and the one patriarch having come, we ought to lean more on the simple truth."

## Notes

[^1]: **T.** The print's *reticulatam* (so on the plate) agrees with nothing in the sentence (*zona*, *pellis*, *tunica* are nominative); we translate as if *reticulata*.
[^2]: **T.** The print reads *tinccionem*, "dyeing", which does not suit a girdle that keeps chastity from being "loosened" (*dissolvatur*); we translate *cinccionem*, "girding" (our emendation; *t* and *c* are readily confused). Loserth italicises 279.18–27 (*stricta tunica … iacincto et cocco*) as a quotation; its source is not given (to be checked, LEDGER P11e-1).
[^3]: **S.** The four-coloured girdle is Exod. 28:39 and 39:28 (Vulgate 39:28 *cingulum vero de bysso retorta, hyacintho, purpura, ac vermiculo bis tincto*), not ch. 27; the words given are not the Vulgate's. Chapter numbers in references are Wyclif's, verse numbers Loserth's.
[^4]: **S.** Apoc. 14:4 (DR); the print has "XIV, 1".
[^5]: **T.** The print reads *electorum* (and at 280.25 *electa in mixtis*), both confirmed on the plate; neither construes. The pairing that follows (linen with earth, purple with fire, violet with water, scarlet with air) and *elementi* at 280.32 require *elementorum*, *elementa* (our emendation, both places).
[^6]: **C.** The plate has a comma after *virtutes* (279.34), so *cum bissus* continues the sentence: "since". *Terre admodo* is taken as *ad modum terre*, "after the manner of earth".
[^7]: **S.** Aristotle, *Nic. Eth.* 1.10, 1100b21–22, the good man as "four-square without reproach" (to be checked, LEDGER P11e-2). Loserth notes the same example in *De civili dominio* I, 54, l. 26 (LEDGER P11e-3).
[^8]: **S.** Loserth refers to Prov. 17:3 ("gold in the furnace", DR); the wording is nearer Ecclus. 2:5, "in the furnace of humiliation" (LEDGER P11e-4).
[^9]: **C.** *Tincti fuerint* has the sanguine (*sanguineis*) as its subject. *Penalis*, the adjective beside *penalitas* "hardship", is rendered "hard": Christ's life of hardship.
[^10]: **C.** The plate reads *moribus*, "habits [of conduct]"; we take it forward, to the four practices of continence set out at 281.1–4. *Humoribus* would make it point back to the humours; the print is kept.
[^11]: **C.** The plate reads *luna*; why fire should be "an instrumental moon" is not explained, and the word may be corrupt.
[^12]: **G.** *Conversacio* here is converse, dealings with women, not the priest's "manner of life" of 279.25.
[^13]: **T.** *Profugienda* is read as two words, *pro fugienda*; "is" is supplied, the sentence having no verb.
[^14]: **C.** *Servando* has no expressed subject: the old priests kept conjugal chastity at all times, and the word of God required a singular chastity in addition while they ministered.
[^15]: **S.** Loserth italicises 281.19–282.2 and refers to Hugh of St Victor, *De sacramentis* ("l. c., p. 460"), "very loosely quoted" (LEDGER P11e-5).
[^16]: **S.** Matt. 13:52, "who bringeth forth out of his treasure new things and old" (DR), said there of the scribe instructed in the kingdom.
[^17]: **S.** Wyclif's form; the Vulgate reads *Nostra autem conversatio in caelis est*, DR "But our conversation is in heaven". *Conversacio* keeps the glossary's "manner of life".
[^18]: **G.** *Superhumerale* is the Vulgate's word for the ephod (DR "ephod"); "superhumeral" keeps Wyclif's word visible, as he gives *ephot* beside it. The two onyx stones are Exod. 28:9–12.
[^19]: **T.** *Pilis*, "hairs, strands"; *filis*, "threads" (cf. *filis iacinctivis*, 285.5), may be the reading.
[^20]: **C.** *Vere* may stand for *vero*, "but in the time of grace"; the print gives "true grace".
