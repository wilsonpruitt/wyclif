# *De eucharistia*, chapter 9, part 2: the two lights, the bread of the Apostle, and the decree of Nicholas II

## ¶1 · 276.21–277.14

> [p. 276] Tercio vero extorquet ex illo Gen. Iᵒ, 16: Fecit Deus duo luminaria, luminare maius, ut preesset diei et luminare minus ut preesset nocti, quomodo Spiritus Sanctus per illud intenderat ipsum et omnes Romanos pontifices super omnes cesares preeminere. Que omnia si sint vera, quomodo pendebit sensus scripture derivandus toti ecclesie a tali capite? Unde solebam sciscitari a satrapis sensum huius Iᵃ Cor. XI, 28: Probet autem se ipsum homo et sic de pane illo edat et de calice bibat. Manifestum (inquam) videtur quod loquitur [p. 277] de hoc sacramento corporis et sanguinis Domini, sicut ante: Panis quem frangimus, nonne participacio corporis Domini est? Et quocienscunque manducabitis panem hunc etc. Quicunque manducaverit panem vel biberit calicem etc. Omnia enim ista intelliguntur de pane in cena Domini manducato. Non autem audemus asserere illum esse sensum Spiritus Sancti: De pane illo edat, hoc est, de accidentibus illis edat, frangimus vel quod panis quem frangimus, hoc est, accidencia que frangimus hoc figurant, quia oporteret specificare illa accidencia quod nesciunt; nam securius est loqui vocando illud panem, ut facit evangelium Apostoli, quousque habuerimus revelacionem vel racionem de sensu extraneo.

And thirdly he wrests from that [text] of Genesis 1:16, "God made two lights: a greater light to rule the day; and a lesser light to rule the night",[^1] [the claim] that the Holy Spirit by it had meant him and all [Roman pontiffs]{g:romanus-pontifex} to be preeminent over all caesars.[^2] If all these things are true, how will the [meaning]{g:sensus} of scripture, which is to be derived to the whole [church]{g:ecclesia}, hang on such a head? Hence I used to inquire of the [satraps]{g:satrapa} the meaning of this [text] of 1 Corinthians 11:28: "But let a man prove himself: and so let him eat of that bread and drink of the chalice." It seems manifest (I say) that he speaks [277] of this [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} of the body and blood of the Lord, as before: "The bread which we break, is it not the partaking of the [body of the Lord]{g:corpus-domini}?" And: "As often as you shall eat this bread" etc. "Whosoever shall eat the bread, or drink the chalice" etc.[^3] For all these are understood of the [bread]{g:panis} eaten at the supper of the Lord. But we dare not assert that this is the meaning of the Holy Spirit: "Let him eat of that bread", that is, "let him eat of those [accidents]{g:accidens}"; "we break"; or that "the bread which we break", that is, "the accidents which we break", [figure]{g:figurare} this;[^4] for it would be necessary to specify those accidents, which they do not know [how to do]; for it is safer to speak calling it bread, as the gospel of the Apostle does, until we have a revelation or a reason for the strange meaning.

## ¶2 · 277.15–277.29

> [p. 277] Et confirmacio illius est quod beatus Augustinus sepe respondet in talibus quod ideo sic loquitur, quia sacra scriptura sic loquitur. Quare ergo debemus nos pocius quam doctores sancti modum loquendi scripture sacre deserere? Et specialiter propter tam extraneam sentenciam ex aliis lapsibus tam suspectam. Unde inter alia valde suspectum est quod decretalis nec racionem nec scripturam sacram allegat pro sensu quem exprimit. Ideo cum testificacio non sit maior quam quatuor magnorum doctorum, ut docet decretum, illi autem prohibuerunt credi dictis suis, nisi de quanto scriptura sacra vel racio ipsa docent, que ergo racio credendi vel capiti in hoc parte? Honorare tamen debemus et glossare dicta papalia quantum permittit religio christiana.

And the confirmation of this is that blessed Augustine often answers in such cases that he speaks so because holy scripture speaks so.[^5] Why, then, ought we, rather than the holy doctors, to desert the manner of speaking of holy scripture? And especially for so strange a [teaching]{g:sentencia},[^6] so suspect from other lapses. Hence, among other things, it is highly suspect that the [decretal]{g:decretalis} [alleges]{g:allegare} neither reason nor holy scripture for the meaning it expresses. Therefore, since no testimony is greater than that of the four great doctors, as the [decree]{g:decretum} teaches; now they forbade their sayings to be believed except so far as holy scripture or reason itself teaches,[^7] what reason, then, is there for believing even the head in this part? Yet we ought to honour and [gloss]{g:glossa} the papal sayings as far as the Christian religion permits.[^8]

## ¶3 · 278.1–278.24

> [p. 278] Nec contendo quod iste Innocencius irreligiose subiugavit sibi Angliam obligacione nimis civili in nongentis marcis solvendis curie annuatim, seminavit discordiam inter Angliam et Franciam, contra imperatorem et alios seculares dominos, contra abbatem Joachim et alios irreligiose processit, ut narrat Cestrensis libro septimo, capitulo XXIV et infra; temporeque suo duo ordines Fratrum inceperant, sed multa mala passa fuit ecclesia quod figuratum est portento multiplici. Nam crescente secta signa colencium ultra religionem quam Christus instituit crescere oportet irreligiositatem fictam in signis. Multa autem opera insignia dereliquit, ut tractatum De Miseria condicionis humane et magnam partem sentencie quinque librorum decretalium; sed non ceperunt auctoritatem ab eo, cum successit post eum Honorius tercius qui dicitur confirmasse ordines fratrum Predicatorum et Minorum, et post eum successit Gregorius IX qui quinque libros decretalium per fratrem Raymundum dicitur compilasse. Et utrobique dicuntur opera eius laudanda primo de quanto consonant legi Dei. Nec dicet aliquis quod dicta sua (in quantum sua) sunt magis autentica quam glossa Innocencii quarti quam ecclesia per se acceptat, in quantum consonat veritati.

Nor do I contend that this Innocent irreligiously subjugated England to himself by an [exceedingly]{g:nimis} civil obligation, of nine hundred marks to be paid to the curia every year; sowed discord between England and France; proceeded irreligiously against the emperor and other [secular lords]{g:seculares-domini}, against Abbot Joachim and others, as [the Chester]{g:cestrensis} [chronicler] relates in book seven, chapter 24 and following;[^9] and in his time the two orders of Friars had begun, but the church suffered many evils, which was figured by a manifold portent. For as the sect of those who worship signs grows beyond the religion which Christ instituted, the [irreligion]{g:irreligiositas} feigned in signs must grow.[^10] But he left many notable works, such as the treatise *On the Misery of the Human Condition* and a great part of the teaching of the five books of the Decretals;[^11] but they did not take [their] [authority]{g:auctoritas} from him, since after him succeeded Honorius III, who is said to have confirmed the orders of the friars Preachers and Minors, and after him succeeded Gregory IX, who is said to have compiled the five books of the Decretals through Brother Raymond.[^12] And [on both sides]{g:utrobique} his works are said to be praiseworthy first so far as they accord with the law of God. Nor will anyone say that his sayings (insofar as [they are] his) are more [authentic]{g:autenticus} than the gloss of Innocent IV, which the church accepts [of itself]{g:per-se}, [insofar as]{g:in-quantum} it accords with the truth.[^13]

## ¶4 · 278.25–279.10

> [p. 278] Quinta autem responsio in qua senex fideliter potest quiescere stat in isto quod, cum videtur esse scrupulus in determinacione ecclesie, quiescendum est humiliter in sentencia antiqua magis probabili, quousque ecclesia declaraverit veritatem. Ponit enim synodus sub Nicholao IIᵒ expresse quod panis et vinum que in altari [p. 279] ponuntur sunt post consecracionem non solum sacramentum sed corpus Christi et sanguis. In nulla autem decretali sequenti tam expresse ponitur quod post consecracionem non remanet panis aut vinum eukaristia, et interim volo parate acquiescere racioni, quia scio quod in nullius pape potestate est cum millesies mille satrapis determinare quidquam de fide katholica, nisi quod reperire poterunt in scriptura, quia aliter indubie foret Antichristus et plus quam Deus, potens totam fidem quam credit christianismus subvertere.

And the fifth reply, in which an old man may faithfully rest, stands in this: that when there seems to be a scruple in the [determination of the church]{g:determinaciones-ecclesie}, one must rest humbly in the ancient teaching, the more probable, until the church has [made clear]{g:declarare} the truth.[^14] For the synod under Nicholas II lays down expressly that "the bread and the [wine]{g:vinum} which are placed on the altar [279] are after [consecration]{g:consecracio} not only the sacrament but the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} and [his] blood".[^15] But in no later decretal is it laid down so expressly that after consecration the [Eucharist]{g:eukaristia} does not remain bread or wine;[^16] and meanwhile I am willing readily to acquiesce in reason, because I know that it is in the [power]{g:potestas} of no [pope]{g:papa}, with a thousand times a thousand satraps, to determine anything concerning the catholic faith except what they can find in scripture; for otherwise he would without doubt be Antichrist and more than God, able to overturn the whole faith that Christendom believes.

## ¶5 · 279.11–279.17

> [p. 279] Secundo arguitur contra dicta per hoc, quod potissima racio cui via ista innititur est Nicholai IIⁱ testimonium et sinodus quam ipse congregavit pro ista materia (ut patet De Consecracione, distinccione IIᵃ, Ego Beringarius). Cum ergo hoc argumentum sit in eadem dampnacione qua testimonium decretalis quod spernitur, videtur quod ista sentencia sit suspecta.

Secondly, against what has been said, [it is argued by appeal to]{g:arguitur-per} this: that the [foremost]{g:potissimus} reason on which this way rests is the testimony of Nicholas II and the synod which he gathered for this matter (as is clear in *De consecratione*, distinction 2, *Ego Berengarius*). Since, then, this argument is under the same [condemnation]{g:dampnacio} as the testimony of the decretal, which is spurned, it seems that this teaching is suspect.[^17]

## ¶6 · 279.18–279.30

> [p. 279] Hic dictum est quod decretum Nicholai IIⁱ est probabilius, non solum quia ibi fuerunt plures episcopi, vel quia fuit antiquius, fonti fidei christiane propinquius, sed eo potissime quod fonti fidei scripture est similius et sanctis doctoribus conformius. Unde non propter loquelam illius synodi sed propter veritatem fidei scripture debet pocius credi priori sentencie; nec solum debet credi cuicunque parti scripture plus quam millies mille episcopis eciam Romanis vel Avinonicis sub racione qua tales, verum eciam plus beato Augustino et aliis sanctis doctoribus quoad materiam fidei quam papis et cardinalibus qui non sunt in fide scripture adeo approbati.

Here it has been said that the decree of Nicholas II is more probable, not only because more [bishops]{g:episcopus} were there, or because it was more ancient, nearer the fountain of the Christian faith, but most of all because it is more like the fountain of the faith of scripture and more conformable to the holy doctors.[^18] Hence not on account of the speech of that synod but on account of the truth of the faith of scripture ought the earlier teaching rather to be believed; and not only ought any part of scripture to be believed more than a thousand times a thousand bishops, even Roman or Avignonese, considered as such, but also blessed Augustine and the other holy doctors [ought to be believed] more, as to the matter of faith, than popes and cardinals who are not so approved in the faith of scripture.[^19]

## ¶7 · 279.31–280.16

> [p. 279] In hiis autem que concernunt leges quas fabricant sunt boni iudices, quia ut dicit Jeronimus, quod medicorum [p. 280] est medici tractant, fabrilia fabri. Et ista est sentencia legis ecclesie distinccione XXᵃ in principio. Si autem simea in tecto se simulat scire quod nescit, bonum est temptare, si opere, vita et auctoritate scripture confirmat quod fingit; lex namque nature quam philosophi detegunt immediate et testimonia Augustini et aliorum sanctorum recitata superius satis docent quod accidencia de quibus loquebantur philosophi non possunt esse sine subiecto. Qua ergo presumpcione seminarent isti contrariam infidelitatem quam nec sciunt colorare racionibus nec scriptura? Quo ergo colore debet fidelis hoc credere? Cum ergo secundum Augustinum credere non potest nisi volens, patet quod preceptum eciam Romani pontificis non est ad hoc efficax nisi supra preceptum adduxerit in testem vel naturalem evidenciam vel scripturam.

But in those things which concern the laws they forge they are good judges, for, as Jerome says, "what is [280] the physicians', physicians handle; smiths' work, smiths".[^20] And this is the teaching of the [law of the church]{g:leges-ecclesie} in Distinction 20, at the beginning.[^21] But if the ape on the roof pretends to know what it does not know, it is good to test whether by work, life and the authority of scripture it confirms what it feigns;[^22] for the law of nature, which the philosophers [expose]{g:detegere} [without intermediary]{g:immediate},[^23] and the testimonies of Augustine and the other [saints]{g:sancti} recited above, teach sufficiently that the accidents of which the philosophers spoke cannot be without a [subject]{g:subiectum}. With what presumption, then, would these men sow the contrary [unbelief]{g:infidelitas}, which they know how to [give colour to]{g:colorare} neither by reasons nor by scripture? By what [colour]{g:color}, then, ought the faithful man to believe this?[^24] Since, then, according to Augustine, [a man] cannot believe unless willing,[^25] it is clear that the command even of the Roman pontiff is not effective for this unless, over and above the command, he has brought in as witness either natural [evidence]{g:evidencia} or scripture.

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** Gen. 1:16; the Vulgate has *duo luminaria magna*, DR "two great lights"; Wyclif's Latin lacks *magna*, and so the English. The decretal is still *Solitae* (X 1.33.6); Loserth (276.24) cites it with Innocent III, *Registers* I, ep. 401 (LEDGER K09b-1).
[^2]: **C/T.** *Ipsum*: the pope himself; the *quomodo* clause gives what he wrests from the text. D reads *ex isto*.
[^3]: **S/T.** 1 Cor. 10:16; 11:26; 11:27. In 11:27 Wyclif's Latin has *panem* without *hunc* (Vulgate *panem hunc*, DR "this bread"), so "the bread". ABDE lack *et sanguinis* (277.1) and *calicem* (277.5).
[^4]: **C/T.** The Latin is compressed and may be damaged: the bare *frangimus* (277.8–9) stands without construction. Taken as citing the verb of 1 Cor. 10:16 as a second text the opponents would gloss ("we break" the accidents). *Hoc figurant*: the accidents "figure this", the body. C reads *illo etc.* and lacks *edat*.
[^5]: **S/T.** Augustine is not located by the Latin; one instance of the habit is *In Ioh.* tr. 21.9 (PL 35, 1569): *Res mira est et magna, sed Scriptura sic loquitur* (LEDGER K09b-2). AB read *lapsis* (277.20); C corrects.
[^6]: **C.** *Sentencia*: the position taught, "teaching", the first sense of the CONTEXTUAL row; so throughout the chunk.
[^7]: **S/C.** The decree is not named: Gratian D. 20 d.a.c. 1, cited at 280.2, for the doctors' precedence, and D. 9 cc. 3–10 (Augustine, *Noli meis litteris*, *Neque quorumlibet*) for their bar on being believed beyond scripture (LEDGER K09b-3). *Illi autem* continues the *cum* clause, "and they". *Capiti*: the pope as head (276.25); *vel* "even".
[^8]: **T.** AB read *dicta probabilia*; C has *probabilia* in the text and *papalia* in the margin in another hand; the print follows C's margin.
[^9]: **C/S.** *Nec contendo quod*: Wyclif names these charges without pressing them. *Obligacione nimis civili*: a secular, feudal bond. *Cestrensis*: Ranulf Higden, *Polychronicon* VII.24 ff. as Wyclif numbers it; Rolls Series VII.31–33 (vol. VIII, 162, Joachim and *De miseria*; 190–192, the tribute, 700 marks for England and 200 for Ireland, Wyclif's nine hundred) (LEDGER K09b-4). For Joachim of Fiore Loserth refers to Jöcher, *Gelehrten-Lexikon* II, 1803, and Fabricius, *Bibl. lat. med. aet.* IV, 328 (LEDGER K09b-5); for the tribute he compares Wyclif, *Sermones* II, 424 (LEDGER K09b-6). D reads *abbothem*.
[^10]: **C.** *Secta signa colencium*: "the sect of those who worship signs", Wyclif's *secta signorum* and *cultores signorum* in another form; *in signis* takes up the same word.
[^11]: **S/T.** Innocent III (Lothar of Segni), *De miseria humane condicionis*; his decretals make up much of the *Liber Extra* (LEDGER K09b-7). C has *quinque* in the margin in another hand.
[^12]: **S.** Honorius III confirmed the Dominicans (1216) and the Franciscan rule (1223); Gregory IX had the *Liber Extra* compiled by Raymond of Peñafort (1234). The Latin says only *dicitur* (LEDGER K09b-8).
[^13]: **C/T.** *Per se*, "of itself": the church accepts Innocent IV's gloss (his commentary on the Decretals, LEDGER K09b-9) on its own account, and only insofar as it is true. D reads *prime*. DE read *dicit* for *dicet*, C has *dubitet* in the margin; ABCE read *dicta sentencia*, C *sit*.
[^14]: **C.** *Quinta responsio*: the fifth answer in the series of *instancie* opened at 272.23. *Magis probabili*: "more probable", more worthy of credence, as GLOSSARY's *probabiles doctores*.
[^15]: **S.** *Ego Berengarius*, *De cons.* D. 2 c. 42 (Loserth, 278.30: "Decret. Tert. Pars, De Consecr. dist. II, cap. XLII"; the modern form is ours) (LEDGER K09b-10). Wyclif's wording is shorter than Friedberg's (*non solum sacramentum, sed etiam uerum corpus et sanguinem Domini nostri Iesu Christi esse*): he lacks *etiam verum* (LEDGER K09b-11).
[^16]: **C/T.** *Eukaristia* is the subject: "the Eucharist does not remain bread or wine". B reads *eucaristie*, which would give "the bread or wine of the Eucharist does not remain". ABCE read *poterit* for *poterunt* (279.8), "what he can find".
[^17]: **C/T.** *Dampnacio*: "condemnation", the sense of the CONTEXTUAL row for a judgement passed on a testimony. ABCE read *quem* (279.13); DE *assumptum* for *argumentum* (279.15).
[^18]: **C.** *Hic dictum est*: the answer, in the perfect, pointing back to the fifth reply (278.25–279.10). The decree's age brings it nearer the fountain of the faith; its content makes it like the fountain of scripture.
[^19]: **C/T.** *Sub racione qua tales*: "considered as such", that is, as bishops. C has *fidei* (279.24) and *parti* (279.25) in the margin in another hand; D reads *millesies* (279.26).
[^20]: **C/S.** The subject of *sunt boni iudices* is the popes and cardinals of 279.29. Jerome, *Ep.* 53.6 (PL 22, 544), quoting Horace, *Ep.* II.1.115–116 (*quod medicorum est promittunt medici; tractant fabrilia fabri*) (LEDGER K09b-12); E's *promittunt medici tractant* (280.1) is nearer that form. *Fabricant … fabri*: the Latin plays on "forge" and "smiths".
[^21]: **S.** Gratian, D. 20 d.a.c. 1, which Loserth quotes: decretal letters are equal to the canons of councils, but in the exposition of scripture the sayings of Augustine, Jerome and others seem to be preferred to the constitutions of some pontiffs (LEDGER K09b-13). C has *legis* in the margin in another hand (280.2).
[^22]: **C/T.** *Simea in tecto*: the ape on the roof, a figure for one who pretends to knowledge; from Bernard, *De consideratione* II.7 (PL 182, 750C), of an unworthy prelate: *Simia in tecto, rex fatuus in solio sedens* (LEDGER K09b-14). ACDE read *temperare* for *temptare*; ABE *scilicet* for *si*, C corrected in the margin; E *opere et*.
[^23]: **T.** At 280.6 Loserth reports *Codd.: quo (C: ymo) mediante est*, apparently the manuscripts' reading where the print has *immediate*; the print is translated.
[^24]: **T.** C reads *sumpcionem* with *pre* added in the margin, ABCE *presumpcionem*; AB *contra*, C *contraria* in the margin in another hand (280.10). E reads *fideles*, C *fidelis* in the margin (280.12).
[^25]: **S/T.** Augustine, *In Ioh. tract.* 26.2 (*credere non potest nisi volens*) (LEDGER K09b-15). B reads *adduxerint* (280.15).
