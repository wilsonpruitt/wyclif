# sed/autem extract, wave 15: chunks 06-c 06-d 06-e

### 06-c ¶1 · 165.17–168.8  (7 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 165] **Sed** sola redemptoris nostri percepta sacramenta ad veram sollempnitatem mentis non sufficiunt nisi eis quoque et bona opera iungantur. Quid enim prodest corpus et sanguinem illius ore percipere et e perversis moribus contraire? Unde bene ad comedendum subditur: Et azimos panes cum lactucis aggrestibus. Panes quippe sine fermento comedit qui recta opera sine corrupcione vane glorie exercet, qui mandata misericordie sine admixtione peccati exhibet, ne perverse diripiat quod quasi recte dispensat. Hoc quoque peccati fermentum miscuerant bone sue accioni quibus prophete voce per increpacionem Dominus [p. 166] dicebat: Venite ad Bethel et impie agite. Atque post pauca: Et sacrificate de fermentato laudem. De fermentato namque laudem immolat qui Deo sacrificium de rapina parat. Lactuce vero aggrestes valde amare sunt. Carnes **autem** agni cum lactucis aggrestibus sunt edende, ut cum corpus Redemptoris accipimus, nos pro peccatis nostris in fletibus affligamus, quatenus ipsa amaritudo penitencie abstergat a mentis stomacho perverse humorem vite. Ubi et subditur: Non comedetis ex eo crudum quid neque coctum aqua. Ecce iam nos ipsa verba historie ab intellectu historiaco repellunt. Numquid, fratres karissimi, Israeliticus ille populus in Egypto constitutus comedere agnum crudum consueverat, ut ei lex dicat, non comedetis ex eo crudum quid? Ubi et additur: Nec coctum aqua. **Sed** quid aqua nisi humanam scienciam designat iuxta hoc quod per Salomonem dicitur sub hereticorum voce: Aque furtive dulciores sunt. Quid crude agni carnes, nisi inconsideratam ac sine reverencia cogitacionis relictam illius humanitatem. Omne enim quod subtiliter cogitamus quasi mente coquimus. **Sed** agni caro nec cruda edenda est nec aqua cocta, quia redemptor noster nec purus homo estimandus est neque per humanam sapienciam qualiter Deus incarnari potuit cogitandus. Omnis enim qui redemptorem nostrum purum hominem credit quid iste aliud quam crudas agni carnes comedit quas videlicet coquere per divinitatis eius intelligenciam noluit? Omnis vero qui incarnacionis eius misteria iuxta humanam sapienciam discutere conatur carnes agni [p. 167] aqua vult coquere, id est, dispensacionis eius misterium per dissolutam vult scienciam penetrare. Qui ergo paschalis gaudii sollempnitatem celebrare desiderat agnum nec aqua coquat nec crudum comedat, ut neque per humanam sapienciam profunditatem incarnacionis illius penetrare appetat neque in eum tamquam in hominem purum credat, **sed** assas igne carnes comedat, ut dispensari omnia per Sancti Spiritus potenciam sciat. De quo adhuc recte subiungitur: Caput cum pedibus et intestinis vorabitis, quia Redemptor noster est alpha et o, Deus videlicet ante secula et homo in fine seculorum. Et sicut iam prediximus, fratres, Paulo attestante didicimus quod caput Christi Deus; caput ergo agni vorare est divinitatis illius fidem percipere, pedes vero agni vorare est vestigia humanitatis illius amando et imitando perquirere. Quid vero sunt intestina nisi verborum illius occulta et mistica mandata? Que tunc voramus, cum verba vite cum aviditate sumimus. In quo devoracionis verbo quid aliud quam pigricie nostre torpor deprehenditur? Qui eius verba atque misteria per nosmetipsos non requirimus et dicta ab aliis audimus inviti. Non remanebit ex eo quidquam usque mane, quia eius dicta magna sunt sollicitudine discucienda, quatenus prius quam dies resurreccionis appareat in hac presentis vite nocte omnia mandata eius intelligendo et operando penetrentur. **Sed** quia valde difficile est, ut omne sacrum eloquium possit [p. 168] intelligi et omne eius misterium penetrari, recte subiungitur: Si quid remanserit, igni conburetis. Quod ex agno remanet igni conburimus, quando hoc quod de misterio incarnacionis eius intelligere et penetrare non possumus, potestati Sancti Spiritus humiliter reservamus, ut non superbe quis audeat vel contempnere vel denunciare quod non intelligit: **sed** hoc igni tradat, cum Spiritui Sancto reservat.

ENGLISH: "But the [sacraments]{g:sacramentum} of our Redeemer, received alone, do not suffice for a true solemnity of the mind unless good works also are joined to them.[^1] For what does it profit to receive his body and blood with the mouth and to oppose him by perverse ways? Hence it is well added to the eating: 'and unleavened bread with wild lettuce.' For he eats loaves without leaven who does right works without the corruption of vainglory, who performs the commandments of mercy without the admixture of sin, lest he perversely plunder what he dispenses as though rightly. This leaven of sin too they had mixed into their good action to whom the Lord[^2] [166] said by the voice of the prophet, in rebuke: 'Come ye to Bethel, and do wickedly.' And a little after: 'And offer a sacrifice of praise with leaven.'[^3] For he offers praise with leaven who prepares a sacrifice for God out of plunder. And the wild lettuces are very bitter. And the flesh of the lamb is to be eaten with wild lettuces, so that when we receive the body of the Redeemer we may afflict ourselves with weeping for our sins, so that the very bitterness of penance may wipe from the stomach of the mind the humour of a perverse life. Where it is also added: 'You shall not eat thereof any thing raw, nor boiled in water.' Behold, the very words of the narrative now drive us back from a historical understanding.[^4] Did that Israelite people, settled in Egypt, dearly beloved brethren, use to eat the lamb raw, that the law should say to it, 'You shall not eat thereof any thing raw'? Where it is also added: 'nor boiled in water.' But what does water designate but human knowledge, according to what is said by Solomon in the voice of the heretics: 'Stolen waters are sweeter'? What [is] the raw flesh of the lamb but his [humanity]{g:humanitas}, left unconsidered and without the reverence of thought?[^5] For all that we think out subtly we as it were cook in the mind. But the flesh of the lamb is to be eaten neither raw nor boiled in water, because our Redeemer is neither to be reckoned a mere man nor to be thought out by human wisdom, as to how God could be incarnate. For everyone who believes our Redeemer a mere man, what else does he eat but the raw flesh of the lamb, which, that is, he would not cook by understanding his [divinity]{g:divinitas}? And everyone who tries to discuss the [mysteries]{g:misterium} of his incarnation according to human wisdom wishes to cook the lamb's flesh [167] in water, that is, wishes to penetrate the mystery of his [dispensation]{g:dispensacio} by a dissolute knowledge. Therefore whoever desires to celebrate the solemnity of the paschal joy, let him neither cook the lamb in water nor eat it raw, so that he neither seeks to penetrate the depth of his incarnation by human wisdom nor believes in him as in a mere man, but let him eat the flesh roasted with fire, so that he may know that all things are dispensed by the [power]{g:potencia} of the Holy Spirit.[^6] Of which it is further rightly subjoined: 'you shall eat the head with the feet and entrails thereof,'[^7] because our Redeemer is alpha and o,[^8] that is, God before the ages and man at the end of the ages. And as we said before, brethren, we have learned on Paul's witness that 'the head of Christ is God'; so to devour the head of the lamb is to receive faith in his divinity, and to devour the feet of the lamb is to seek out the footprints of his humanity by loving and imitating. And what are the entrails but the hidden and mystical commandments of his words? These we devour when we take the words of life with avidity. In which word 'devouring' what else is caught out but the torpor of our sloth, who do not seek out his words and mysteries for ourselves, and hear unwillingly what is said by others? 'Neither shall there remain any thing of it until morning,' because his sayings are to be discussed with great care, so that before the day of the resurrection appears, all his commandments may be penetrated, by understanding and by doing, in this night of the present life.[^9] But because it is very difficult that all sacred eloquence can [168] be understood and all its mystery penetrated, it is rightly subjoined: 'If there be any thing left, you shall burn it with fire.'[^10] What remains of the lamb we burn with fire when what we cannot understand and penetrate of the mystery of his incarnation we humbly reserve to the [power]{g:potestas} of the Holy Spirit, so that no one should proudly dare either to despise or to denounce what he does not understand, but let him hand it over to the fire, when he reserves it to the Holy Spirit.

### 06-c ¶2 · 168.9–169.13  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 168] Quia igitur qualiter edendum sit pasca agnovimus, nunc **autem** a qualibus edi debeat agnoscamus. Sequitur: Sic **autem** comedetis illum: Renes vestros accingetis. Quid in renibus nisi delectacio carnis accipitur? Unde et Psalmista postulat dicens: Ure renes meos. Si enim voluptatem in renibus esse nesciret, eos uri minime petisset. Unde quia potestas dyaboli in humano genere maxime per luxuriam prevaluit, de illo voce dominica dicitur: Potestas eius in lumbis eius. Qui ergo pasca comedit habere renes succinctos debet, ut qui sollempnitatem resurreccionis atque incorrupcionis agit corrupcioni iam per nulla vicia subiaceat, voluptates edomet, carnem a luxuria restringat. Neque enim cognovit que sit sollempnitas incorrupcionis qui adhuc corrupcioni per incontinenciam subiacet. Hec quibusdam dura sunt, **sed** angusta est via que ducit ad celum, et habuimus iam multa exempla continencium. Unde et adhuc subditur: Calceamenta habebitis in pedibus. Quid etenim sunt pedes nostri nisi opera? Quid vero calceamenta nisi pelles mortuorum animalium; calceamenta **autem** pedes muniunt. Que [p. 169] vero sunt mortua animalia, ex quorum pellibus nostri muniantur pedes, nisi antiqui patres qui nos ad eternam patriam precesserunt. Quorum dum exempla conspicimus, nostri operis pedes munimus. Calceamenta ergo in pedibus habere est mortuorum vitam conspicere et nostra vestigia a peccati vulnere custodire. Tenentes baculos in manibus. Quid lex per baculum nisi pastoralem custodiam designat? Et notandum quod prius precipimur renes accingere, postmodum baculos tenere, quia illi debent curam pastoralem suscipere, quia iam sciunt in suo corpore fluxa luxurie edomare, ut cum aliis forcia predicant, ipsi desideriis mollibus et voluptatibus non succumbant.

ENGLISH: "Since, then, we have learned how the pasch is to be eaten, let us now learn by what sort of people it ought to be eaten. It follows: 'And thus you shall eat it: you shall gird your reins.' What is understood by the reins but the delight of the flesh? Hence the Psalmist too asks, saying: 'Burn my reins.' For if he did not know that pleasure is in the reins, he would by no means have asked that they be burned. Hence, because the power of the devil prevailed in the human race chiefly through lust, it is said of him by the Lord's voice: 'His power is in his loins.'[^11] He therefore who eats the pasch ought to have his reins girded, so that he who keeps the solemnity of the resurrection and of incorruption may no longer be subject to corruption through any vices, may tame his pleasures, may restrain the flesh from lust. For he does not know what the solemnity of incorruption is who is still subject to corruption through incontinence. These things are hard to some, but narrow is the way that leads to heaven,[^12] and we have had already many examples of the continent. Hence it is further added: 'and you shall have shoes on your feet.' For what are our feet but works? And what are shoes but the skins of dead animals? And shoes protect the feet. And what [169] are the dead animals from whose skins our feet are protected but the ancient fathers who have gone before us to the eternal [homeland]{g:patria}? While we look on their examples we protect the feet of our work. To have shoes on the feet, therefore, is to look on the life of the dead and to guard our footsteps from the wound of sin. 'Holding staves in your hands.' What does the law designate by the staff but pastoral guardianship? And it is to be noted that we are commanded first to gird the reins, afterwards to hold the staves, because those ought to take up pastoral care who already know how to tame the flux of lust in their own body,[^13] so that when they preach strong things to others, they themselves may not succumb to soft desires and pleasures.

### 06-c ¶3 · 169.14–169.26  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 169] Bene **autem** subditur: Et comedetis festinanter. Notate, fratres, notate quod dicitur festinantes. Mandata Dei, misteria Redemptoris, celestis patrie gaudia cum festinacione cognoscite et precepta vite cum festinacione implere curate. Quia enim adhuc hodie licet bene agere scimus: utrum cras liceat ignoramus. Festinantes **autem** pascha comedite, id est, ad sollempnitatem patrie celestis anhelate. Nemo in huius vite itinere torpeat, ne in patria locum perdat. Nemo moras ad appetenda studia innectat **sed** cepta perficiat, ne minime liceat implere quod inchoat. Si ad amorem Dei pigri non sumus, adiuvat ipse quem amamus.

ENGLISH: "And it is well added: 'and you shall eat in haste.' Note, brethren, note that it says 'hastening.'[^14] Learn with haste the commandments of God, the mysteries of the Redeemer, the joys of the heavenly homeland, and take care to fulfil with haste the precepts of life. For we know that today it is still permitted to do well; whether it will be permitted tomorrow we do not know. And eat the pasch hastening, that is, pant after the solemnity of the heavenly homeland. Let no one be sluggish on the journey of this life, lest he lose his place in the homeland. Let no one weave delays into the pursuits that are to be sought, but let him finish what he has begun, lest it be by no means permitted him to complete what he begins. If we are not slothful toward the love of God, he whom we love himself helps us."

### 06-d ¶1 · 169.27–171.11  (7 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 169] Sic ergo tota disposicio hominis ad comedendum hostiam stat in sincera et grata dileccione Christi et Dei. Est, inquam, sincera dileccio, quando amor Dei supereminet et amor cuiuslibet creature in ordine ad ipsum supponitur. Qui enim plus diligit aliquid [p. 170] Domino est ingratus et per consequens non ipso dignus. Omnes **autem** istam dileccionem **sed** nimis mendaciter profitemur; quod discerni potest ex hoc quod nos sacerdotes misse a summo usque ad infimum statim post missam propter superbiam, carnem vel similia dimittimus Christi servicium, legem suam, monita sua, et conversacionem suam abicimus in contemptum. Quis, inquam, nostrum nedum post missam, **sed** in missa non plus nititur servire mundo vel homini quam auctori? Inclinaciones **autem**, occupaciones et operaciones istud diiudicant, cum nedum facillime possemus ad delectaciones corporis vel commodi offendere veritatem **sed** de facto sic facimus: ut, inquam, dimittam maiora facinora, quis nostrum non est tam fragilis quin propter affeccionem carnalem sive pecuniam diceret testimonium contra veritatem vel prorumpat in assercionem sentencie, antequam secundum Dei regulas examinet veritatem, vel tercio ubi ad honorem Dei posset prodesse ecclesie, taceat vecorditer veritatem? Et omnium istorum causa est preponderans dileccio terrenorum. Et talis indigne capit corpus Christi et sanguinem Jesu Christi; et nisi hoc esset sacerdotes Christi non occuparentur in seculari negocio, non aspirarent ad temporalia **sed** pauper vita Christi foret eis amena; nec dimittendo certum et melius prorumperent in verba et opera minus utilia. Et bene esset illi sacerdoti qui sciret ab ista macula triplici se servare. Oportet enim quod ob amorem Christi quem comedit moderet intellectum, quia aliter prandium istud quod ordinatum est propter hanc medicinam, foret tam sumenti quam toti ecclesie venenosum; quanto magis si demonium meridianum [p. 171] commixtum fuerit in istis ministerio sacerdotum? Utile quidem foret ecclesie non habere elemosinarios sic celebrantes dotatos nec ypocritas proditores veritatis super falso fundamento religionis et ficcione mendacii sic orantes, **sed** evangelisacione Christi plus in suis iustis operibus confidentes. Tunc enim digne manducarent corpus Christi et sanguinem eius biberent, que in celis sunt, subducto omni conversante nobiscum presbytero celebrante. Ymmo efficacius et verius forent corpus Christi et sanguis tam in corpore nostro quam anima hic nobiscum.

ENGLISH: So, therefore, the whole disposition of a man for eating the [host]{g:hostia} stands in a sincere and grateful love of Christ and of God. It is, I say, sincere love when the love of God is pre-eminent and the love of any creature whatever is placed beneath it in order to him. For he who loves anything [170] more than the Lord is [displeasing]{g:ingratus} to the Lord,[^1] and consequently not worthy of him. And we all profess this love, but exceedingly falsely; which can be discerned from this, that we [priests]{g:sacerdos} of the mass, from the highest to the lowest, immediately after mass, for pride, the flesh or the like, lay aside the [service]{g:servicium} of Christ, and cast his law, his admonitions and his manner of life into contempt. Who of us, I say, not only after mass but in the mass, does not strive more to serve the world or man than the author? And our inclinations, occupations and operations judge this, since not only could we very easily offend against the truth for the delights of the body or for advantage, but in fact we do so: so that, I say, to pass over greater [misdeeds]{g:facinus}, who of us is not so frail that, for carnal affection or for money, he would bear witness against the truth, or burst out into the assertion of a [sentence]{g:sentencia} before he examines the truth according to God's rules,[^2] or, thirdly, where he could benefit the [church]{g:ecclesia} to the honour of God, would faint-heartedly keep silent about the truth? And the cause of all these is the preponderant love of earthly things. And such a man unworthily takes the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} and the blood of Jesus Christ; and were it not for this, the priests of Christ would not be occupied in secular business, would not aspire to [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, but the poor life of Christ would be pleasant to them; nor, laying aside what is certain and better, would they burst out into less useful words and works. And it would be well with that priest who knew how to keep himself from this threefold stain. For it is necessary that, for the love of Christ whom he eats, he should moderate his understanding, because otherwise this meal, which is ordained for the sake of this medicine, would be poisonous both to the one who takes it and to the whole church; how much more if the noonday demon [171] has been mingled in this ministry of priests?[^3] It would indeed be useful to the church not to have endowed [almsmen]{g:elemosinarius} celebrating thus, nor hypocrites, [traitors]{g:proditor} to the truth, praying thus on the false foundation of religion and the fiction of falsehood, but [men] trusting more, by the preaching of the gospel of Christ, in their own just works.[^4] For then they would worthily eat the body of Christ and drink his blood, which are in heaven, with every celebrating [presbyter]{g:presbiter} who [lives his life]{g:conversari} among us taken away. Indeed, the body and blood of Christ would be more efficaciously and more [truly]{g:vere} here with us, both in our body and in our soul.

### 06-d ¶2 · 171.12–171.19  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 171] **Sed** obicitur primo per hoc quod nedum in legendis et serviciis huius festi accidencia asseruntur esse sine subiecto, **sed** decretalis libro III hoc videtur expresse innuere, ut patet De Celebracione Missarum, capitulo Cum Marthe et in Clementinis capitulo... de hoc festo dicuntur multa similia. Negando itaque hoc posse fieri contradiceretur decretis et glosse ecclesie **sed** blaspheme contradiceretur divine potencie.

ENGLISH: But [it is objected, by appeal to]{g:obicitur-per} this, first: that not only in the legends and services of this feast are [accidents]{g:accidens} asserted to be [without a subject]{g:accidens-sine-subiecto},[^5] but the [decretal]{g:decretalis} in book III seems to intimate this expressly, as is clear in *De celebratione missarum*, the chapter *Cum Marthe*; and in the Clementines, in the chapter … many like things are said of this feast.[^6] And so, by denying that this can be done, one would contradict the [decrees]{g:decretum} and the gloss of the church, but, what is more, would blasphemously contradict the divine [power]{g:potencia}.[^7]

### 06-d ¶3 · 171.20–172.7  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 171] Quantum ad legendas patet quod nedum sunt collecta apocrifa **sed** sepe mendacia. Unde Augustinus in De Sermone Domini in Monte libro primo declarans quomodo Petrus et Paulus excommunicaverunt homines pro amore, sicut patet Act. V°, 3 de Anania et Saphira, et prima Cor. V°, 5, quem Paulus tradidit Sathane, ut spiritus salvus fiat: ymmo in libris illis quibus adversarii magnam auctoritatem tribuunt. Advertant inquit, quod dicimus ubi scriptum est apostolum [p. 172] Thomam inprecatum cuidam a quo palma percussus esset atrocissime mortis supplicium, animam tamen eius commendat ut in futuro seculo ei parceretur: cuius a leone occisi a cetero corpore discerptam manum canis intulit mensis convivarum, in quibus convivabatur Apostolus. Cui scripture, inquit, licet nobis non credere; non enim est in katholico canone.

ENGLISH: As to the legends, it is clear that they are not only [apocryphal]{g:apocrifa} collections but often falsehoods. Hence Augustine, in *De sermone Domini in monte*, book one, making clear how Peter and Paul excommunicated men out of love, as is clear in Acts 5:3 of Ananias and Sapphira, and in 1 Cor. 5:5, whom Paul delivered to Satan "that the spirit may be saved": indeed, in those books to which the adversaries attribute great authority.[^8] "Let them take note," he says, "of what we say, where it is written that the apostle [172] Thomas imprecated on someone by whom he had been struck with the palm the punishment of a most atrocious death, yet commends his soul, that he might be spared in the world to come: whose hand, torn from the rest of his body when he had been killed by a lion, a dog brought in to the tables of the banqueters, among whom the apostle was feasting." "This scripture," he says, "we are permitted not to believe; for it is not in the catholic canon."[^9]

### 06-d ¶5 · 172.14–173.29  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 172] Quantum ad decretales, videtur primo ex dicto Augustini quod non oportet ipsas credere, cum non sint in katholico canone, **sed** (ut sepe glossavi) potest dici quod per accidencia panis et vini intelliguntur panis et vinum, ut ecclesia sepe per signa intelligit sua signata. Sicut ergo scriptura sacra, sancti doctores et leges ecclesie vocant illa que dicimus accidencia panem et vinum, sic nos ad imitacionem eorum vocamus ea panem et vinum; nec habent auctoritatem vel evidenciam ad impugnandum nominacionem nostri baptismatis pocius quam econtra. Nec probare possunt quod interim sit miraculosa variacio pocius quam fuit tempore istorum auctorum. **Sed** maiori auctoritate vel evidencia licuit eis vocare illum panem et vinum accidencia quantitatis cum cognomine. Nec dubium quin Apostolus vocavit illam rem panem prima Cor. X et XI°, sicut generaliter scriptura et beatus Ambrosius, ut patet de consecracione, distinccione IIᵃ, [p. 173] Panis est, et Augustinus eadem distinccione, Hoc est, et Sermone XXVI (ut sepe recitavi); et pari evidencia qua glossatores glossant eos quod intelligunt per panem et vinum non substanciam **sed** accidencia, nos econtra glossamus eos quod non intelligunt per accidencia aliquas res novem generum accidentis, quia nemo scit quam; **sed** intelligunt per accidencia substanciam panis et vini et non accidens, quia (ut testatur Augustinus in isto katholicus) nullum accidens poterit per se esse. Nec videtur quod ecclesia sit oneranda ut fide eorum testimonio, cum cotidie fiant ista miracula que Augustinus dicit non posse fieri, sicut tempore Augustini Spiritus Sanctus noluit onerari ecclesiam dictis suis vel alterius preter auctores scripture tanquam fide katholica (ut sepe testatur iste sanctus); quomodo ergo oneretur contra dicta sanctorum et contra racionem, ut credat accidens esse ibi sine subiecto? Nec in isto contradicitur divine potencie, sicut nec Apostolus contradixit IIᵃ Thym. II°, quando asseruit Deus verax est, se ipsum negare non potest. Quid ergo obest quod longitudo, latitudo et profunditas materialis substancie (sicut Augustinus testatur) non poterunt per se esse? Leges itaque quotlibet constituit Deus in natura quarum opposita implicant contradiccionem, in quam Deus non potest, cum tunc foret contrarius sibi ipsi, cuiusmodi est lex de inpossibilitate substancie sine accidente; et sic de inpossibilitate ydemptificacionis generum, specierum et differenciarum.

ENGLISH: As to the decretals, it seems first, from Augustine's saying, that there is no need to believe them, since they are not in the catholic canon; but (as I have often glossed) it can be said that by "the accidents of bread and wine" are understood [bread]{g:panis} and [wine]{g:vinum}, as the church often understands by [signs]{g:signum} the [things they signify]{g:signatum}. As, therefore, holy scripture, the holy doctors and the [laws of the church]{g:leges-ecclesie} call those things which we call accidents bread and wine, so we, in imitation of them, call them bread and wine; nor have they authority or [evidence]{g:evidencia} for impugning the [naming]{g:nominacio} of our baptism rather than the reverse.[^10] Nor can they prove that in the meantime there is a miraculous variation rather than there was in the time of these authors. But with greater authority or evidence it was permitted to them to call that bread and wine "accidents of quantity", with a surname.[^11] Nor is there doubt that the Apostle called that thing bread, 1 Cor. 10 and 11, as scripture does generally, and blessed Ambrose, as is clear in *De consecratione*, distinction 2, [173] *Panis est*, and Augustine in the same distinction, *Hoc est*, and in Sermon 26 (as I have often recited);[^12] and with equal evidence as that with which the glossators gloss them, that they understand by bread and wine not the [substance]{g:substancia} but the accidents, we on the contrary gloss them, that they do not understand by accidents any things of the nine genera of accident, since no one knows which; but they understand by accidents the substance of bread and wine and not an accident, since (as Augustine, catholic in this, testifies) no accident can be [by itself]{g:per-se}.[^13] Nor does it seem that the church is to be burdened, as with faith, by their testimony, since these miracles happen daily which Augustine says cannot happen, just as in Augustine's time the Holy Spirit would not have the church burdened with his sayings or another's, apart from the authors of scripture, as with catholic faith (as this saint often testifies);[^14] how then should it be burdened against the sayings of the [saints]{g:sancti} and against reason, so as to believe "an accident is there without a subject"? Nor in this is the divine power contradicted, as neither did the Apostle contradict [it], 2 Tim. 2, when he asserted "God is true", "he cannot deny himself".[^15] What harm is it, then, that the length, breadth and depth of material substance (as Augustine testifies) cannot be by themselves? So God has established in nature any number of laws whose opposites imply a contradiction, of which God is not capable, since then he would be contrary to himself;[^16] of which kind is the law of the impossibility of substance without accident; and so of the impossibility of the [identification]{g:ydemptificacio} of genera, species and [differences]{g:differencia}.

### 06-e ¶1 · 174.1–174.32  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 174] Ideo fabulantes talia sompnia viderent primo quomodo sancti et philosophi loquuntur de descripcione et de distinccione substancie et accidentis. Multi enim locuntur de signis huiusmodi sine sensu, et multi iudaice fabricant allegaciones corruptas, ut allegant fundacionem transubstanciacionis sue in illo Math. VI°, 11: Panem nostrum supersubstancialem da nobis hodie, **sed** hii non minus delirant quam falsi reportatores Christi qui loco istius dicti Johannis II°, 19: Solvite templum hoc et in tribus diebus excitabo illud. Hic dixit, inquiunt, possum destruere templum Dei et post triduum reedificare illud Math. XXVI°, 61. Nam longe plus differunt panis ille substancialis et sua transubstanciacio quam differant solvere et destruere, excitare et reedificare. Pictores **autem** vocant colores corpora liquida que illiniunt corporibus solidis, que debent ex hoc colorari. Et ita videtur decretalem grosse loqui de accidentibus vocando grave liquidum inebriatum generativum vini quod inmiscet se vino aut aqua post consecracionem accidenter; quod alii specificant per quantitatem quam dicunt ternam dimensionem que potest esse vanum. Que quantitas foret eque activa ut vinum, sive fuerit per se sive in subiecto. **Sed** philosophi non sic locuntur de quantitate quod sit de genere activorum racione qualitatis active quod subiectet, sicut nec qualitates prime agunt acciones qualitatis secunde quam causant, nec potest quantitas secundum diffinicionem Augustini sic per se variari; nec umquam conceperunt priores sancti illud insensibile cotidianum miraculum, ymmo pocius vocatur irracionabilis Dei crudelitas sanctificare per se accidencia destruendo substanciam.

ENGLISH: Therefore those who tell such dreams as fables should see first how the [saints]{g:sancti} and the philosophers speak of the [definition]{g:descripcio} and the distinction of [substance]{g:substancia} and [accident]{g:accidens}. For many speak of signs of this kind without sense, and many in Jewish fashion fabricate corrupt [allegations]{g:allegacio}, as they allege the foundation of their [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio} in that [text], Matt. 6:11: "Give us this day our supersubstantial bread";[^1] but these rave no less than the false reporters of Christ who, in place of that saying, John 2:19, "Destroy this temple; and in three days I will raise it up," [said]: "This man said," they say, "I am able to destroy the temple of God and after three days to rebuild it," Matt. 26:61.[^2] For that substantial bread and their transubstantiation differ far more than *solvere* and *destruere*, "raise up" and "rebuild", differ. Now painters call "colours" the liquid bodies which they smear on solid bodies that are to be coloured by this. And so the [decretal]{g:decretalis} seems to speak grossly of accidents, calling [accidents] a heavy, intoxicated liquid, generative of wine, which mingles itself with the wine or water after [consecration]{g:consecracio} accidentally;[^3] which others specify by [quantity]{g:quantitas}, which they say is the threefold dimension that can be a void. And that quantity would be as active as wine, whether it were [of itself]{g:per-se} or in a [subject]{g:subiectum}. But philosophers do not speak of quantity thus, as though it were of the genus of active things by reason of an active [quality]{g:qualitas} which it subjects, just as neither do the primary qualities perform the actions of the secondary quality which they cause; nor can quantity, according to Augustine's definition, vary thus of itself;[^4] nor did the earlier saints ever conceive that imperceptible daily miracle; indeed it is rather called an irrational cruelty of God, to sanctify accidents of themselves by destroying the substance.

### 06-e ¶4 · 175.11–175.24  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 175] Tercia solucio est quod omnes adversarii veritatis intelligunt accidens esse sine subiecto condicionaliter, si Deus voluerit. Et illam condicionem oportet adversarium (ut docet beatus Jacobus) velit nolit intelligere. Nec habet dictum eorum in hoc efficacius testimonium, nam si per inpossibile Deus posset sic facere, adhuc non dicetur per hoc sic esse de facto; nec displiceret illis dicta glossa sancti Apostoli, cum sic glossatur Augustinus in dicto suo ad maximum hereticum, quando dicit quod Deus filius potuit produxisse ad intra alium filium. Non, inquit, non potuit **sed** non decuit, hoc est, potuit, si voluerit; et sic non plus quam sancti primitive ecclesie contradicimus in hoc divine potencie.

ENGLISH: The third resolution is that all the adversaries of the truth understand "an [accident without a subject]{g:accidens-sine-subiecto}" conditionally: if God should will. And that condition the adversary (as blessed James teaches) must understand, whether he will or not.[^7] Nor has their saying a more efficacious testimony in this, for if, by an impossible supposition, God could do so, still it will not on that account be said to be so in fact; nor would the said gloss of the holy Apostle displease them, since Augustine glosses thus in his saying to Maximinus the heretic, when he says that God the Son could have produced another son within. "No," he says, "he could not, but it was not fitting; that is, he could, if he should will";[^8] and so we no more than the saints of the primitive [church]{g:ecclesia} contradict the divine [power]{g:potencia} in this.[^9]

### 06-e ¶6 · 176.3–176.22  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 176] Hic sunt multa dicenda; aliqua enim indisponunt hominem simpliciter et aliqua secundum quid, ut communicet cum infidelibus in sacramento altaris; nam peccatum mortale vel infidelitas presciti indisponit eum simpliciter et peccata venialia indisponunt secundum quid; unde quia quilibet nostrum debet presupponere quod sit predestinatus, debet supponere quod hoc sacramentum sibi proficiet, et licet sit indispositus secundum quid per veniale peccatum, tamen est simpliciter dispositus per graciam predestinacionis et per triplicem graciam consequentem (ut notatum est in principio huius VIᵗⁱ capituli). Loquor **autem** de veniali peccato ut alias; sine cuius noticia vanum est dubitare in ista materia; et sic nemo est infinitum fragilis, cum non attenditur penes hoc quod cicius ant facilius homo posset peccare. **Sed** multi positi in adopcione eligerent virtutem dimisso vicio; et ita non sufficit carencia mortalis peccati actualis cum malus habitus nimis indisponit hominem ad cultum huius sacramenti.

ENGLISH: Here many things are to be said; for some things make a man unfit [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} and some [in a certain respect]{g:secundum-quid}, so that he communicates with [unbelievers]{g:infidelis} in the [sacrament of the altar]{g:sacramentum-altaris};[^11] for mortal sin, or the unbelief of the [foreknown]{g:prescitus}, makes him unfit without qualification, and venial sins make unfit in a certain respect; hence, because each of us ought to presuppose that he is [predestinate]{g:predestinatus}, he ought to suppose that this sacrament will profit him, and though he be unfit in a certain respect through venial sin, yet he is fit without qualification through the grace of predestination and through the threefold consequent grace (as was noted at the beginning of this sixth chapter).[^12] And I speak of venial sin as elsewhere; without knowledge of which it is vain to doubt in this matter; and so no one is infinitely frail, since that is not reckoned by this, that a man could sin more quickly or more easily.[^13] But many, placed in the choice, would choose virtue, leaving vice; and so the lack of actual mortal sin does not suffice, since a bad habit makes a man exceedingly unfit for the worship of this sacrament.

### 06-e ¶7 · 176.23–178.30  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 176] Tria ergo sunt disponencia, scilicet Christi dileccio, pax cum proximo et actualis devocio; que tria exprimuntur in illo magno sermone Christi post dacionem huius sacramenti a capitulo XIII Joh. usque ad capitulum XVIII. Quoad dileccionem Christi et pacem proximi dicit Joh. XIII°, 34: Mandatum novum do vobis: ut diligatis invicem, sicut dilexi vos. Et quia illa dileccio convertitur cum dileccione et pace proximi, ideo capitulo XIV°, 21 explicat hanc dileccionem: [p. 177] Si, inquit, diligitis me, mandata mea servate. Et sequitur: Pacem relinquo vobis, pacem meam do vobis; ubi non dubium dat doctrinam tenerrimam ad colendum hoc sacramentum quod paulo ante dedit apostolis: Oportet, inquam, cultorem huius sacramenti radicari et fundari in caritate, sic quod habitualiter Deum amet pre omnibus et omnia in ordine ad ipsum. Unde nimis indisponitur qui peccato invidie, peccato avaricie vel carnis proponunt inordinate creaturas diligere; nam cultus eukaristie nocet tali et facit iniuriam cuilibet creature. Unde in signum plene mundicie observancie huius sacramenti creditur Christum post eius ministerium lavisse humillime pedes discipulorum quod significat mundiciam affeccionum. Unde Augustinus Omelia LVI dicit quod quantumcunque homo mundus fuerit in baptismo, verumtamen cum in rebus humanis postea vivitur et terra pede fedata calcatur: Ipsi, inquit, humani affectus sine quibus in hac mortalitate non vivitur quasi pedes sunt quibus humanis rebus afficimur, et sic afficimur quod si dixerimus quia peccatum non habemus nos ipsos seducimus et veritas in nobis non est. Ideo debemus lavare pedes mentis cotidie, cum oramus: Dimitte nobis debita nostra. Qui ergo est mundus totus secundum partes superiores anime carens mortali crimine indiget ut pedes lavet, cum solum beati sine macula sive ruga qui non calcant hanc terram peregrinancium et incolunt terram vivencium laverunt pedes simpliciter, ut dicitur Cant. Vᵗᵒ, 3. Valet ergo usus eukaristie ad memorandum gesta Christi, ut sit efficacius imitatus, sic quod quicunque celebrat missam vel [p. 178] audit cum oleo devocionis ungit devote corpus Christi cum Magdalena. Unde signanter dicit Christus Matth. XXVI°, 10 contra imitatores Scarioth qui remurmurant contra devotos quos vocant paplardos vel ypocritas, quod poterunt apcius prodesse pauperibus. Quid, inquit, molesti estis huic mulieri? Opus enim bonum operata est in me. Et sequitur: Amen dico vobis, ubicunque predicatum fuerit hoc evangelium, in toto mundo dicetur quod et hoc fecit in memoriam eius. Quicunque, inquam, sic ungit Dominum in sacramento altaris. Nichil debet esse in mundo quoad actus nostros famosius quam quod hoc debet facere in memoriam imitatoriam Jesu Christi; nam subvenire pauperibus est opus predicto inferius, ad quod semper est oportunitas temporis, dum hic vivitur. **Sed** oportet captare tempus ad sic singulariter celebrandum, quia solum dum est dolor ingratitudinis precedentis et devocio ad colendum corpus Christi pro nobis sic passi mortui et sepulti. Et hec racio quare nostri dicunt quod requiritur intencio consecrandi; requiritur, inquam, devota intencio ad memorandum sic sacramentaliter Salvatorem, quod si defuerit, est Christi irrisio et despectiva super Christum humoris fetentis infusio. Digne ergo manducatum corpus Christi valet pro vivis et mortuis, ut pena pro peccatis debita remittatur, ut vivus ex devocione amplius mereatur, et ut sanctum merendi propositum et per consequens ut gracia firmius et perseverancius habeatur, et sic utrobique delentur culpe tam veniales quam mortales occasione huius sacramenti.

ENGLISH: There are therefore three things that dispose, namely the love of Christ, peace with one's neighbour, and actual devotion; which three are expressed in that great sermon of Christ after the giving of this sacrament, from chapter 13 of John to chapter 18. As to the love of Christ and peace with the neighbour, he says, John 13:34: "A new commandment I give unto you: That you love one another, as I have loved you." And because that love [is convertible]{g:converti} with the love of the neighbour and peace with him, therefore in chapter 14:21 he explains this love: [177] "If you love me," he says, "keep my commandments."[^14] And it follows: "Peace I leave with you: my peace I give unto you"; where without doubt he gives a most tender doctrine for worshipping this sacrament which a little before he had given to the apostles. The [worshipper]{g:cultor} of this sacrament, I say, must be rooted and founded in [charity]{g:caritas},[^15] so that habitually he loves God before all things and all things in order to him. Hence he is exceedingly unfit who, through the sin of envy, the sin of avarice or of the flesh, purpose to love creatures inordinately;[^16] for the worship of the eucharist harms such a man and does injury to every creature. Hence, in sign of the full cleanness of the observance of this sacrament, it is believed that Christ, after its ministry, most humbly washed the feet of the disciples, which signifies cleanness of the affections. Hence Augustine, Homily 56, says that however clean a man has been in baptism, nevertheless when one lives afterwards among human things, the earth too is trodden, [and] fouled by the foot:[^17] "The human affections themselves," he says, "without which one does not live in this mortality, are as it were feet by which we are affected toward human things, and we are so affected that 'if we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves and the truth is not in us.'" Therefore we ought to wash the feet of the mind daily, when we pray: "Forgive us our debts." He, therefore, who is wholly clean according to the higher parts of the soul, lacking mortal crime, needs to wash his feet, since only the blessed, without spot or wrinkle, who do not tread this earth of pilgrims and inhabit the land of the living, have washed their feet without qualification, as is said, Cant. 5:3.[^18] The use of the eucharist therefore avails for remembering the deeds of Christ, so that he may be more efficaciously imitated, so that whoever celebrates mass or [178] hears it with the oil of devotion devoutly anoints the body of Christ with the Magdalene. Hence Christ says pointedly, Matt. 26:10, against the imitators of Iscariot who murmur against the devout, whom they call [paplards]{g:paplardus} or hypocrites,[^19] [saying] that they could more fitly benefit the poor: "Why," he says, "do you trouble this woman? For she hath wrought a good work upon me." And it follows: "Amen I say to you, wheresoever this gospel shall be preached in the whole world, that also which she hath done shall be told for a memory of her."[^20] Whoever, I say, thus anoints the Lord in the sacrament of the altar. Nothing ought to be [better known]{g:famosius} in the world, as to our acts, than that one ought to do this in imitative memory of Jesus Christ;[^21] for to relieve the poor is a work inferior to the aforesaid, for which there is always opportunity of time while one lives here. But one must seize the time to celebrate thus singularly, because [it is the time] only while there is sorrow for the preceding ingratitude and devotion to worship the body of Christ, who thus suffered, died and was buried for us. And this is the reason why ours say that the intention of consecrating is required;[^22] there is required, I say, a devout intention to remember the Saviour thus [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter}, which if it be lacking, it is a mockery of Christ and a contemptuous pouring of stinking humour over Christ. The body of Christ worthily eaten, therefore, avails for the living and the dead, that the penalty owed for sins may be remitted, that the living may merit more from devotion, and that the holy purpose of meriting, and consequently grace, may be held more firmly and more perseveringly; and so [on both sides]{g:utrobique} faults both venial and mortal are blotted out on the occasion of this sacrament.
