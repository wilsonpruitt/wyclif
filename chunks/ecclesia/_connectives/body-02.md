# sed/autem extract 02: chunks 03-d–06-c

### 03-d ¶1 · 58.17–60.4  (7 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 58] Non repugnat ergo equivocando vocare corpus diaboli ecclesiam Christi, ut posito quod una communitas nominetenus clericorum prescita credatur esse particularis ecclesia, licet committat peccata quantumcunque enormia. Tunc vulgus reputat eam esse ecclesiam, ymo quilibet eorum asserit quod propter ordinem est vir sancte matris ecclesie, racione cuius tituli excecat populum, in fide, officio et ypocrisi indubie menciendo, quia sic idem foret simul membrum Christi et membrum diaboli, ecclesia sanctorum et ecclesia malignancium. Tales enim dicit Thomas eciam membra ecclesie in potencia, licet nunquam reducantur ad actum. Ac si diceretur illud habet potenciam ex sufficiencia passionis Christi et potestate sui arbitrii esse membrum Christi, cum tamen nude sit membrum congregacionis habencium fidem informem, licet nunquam sit membrum corporis Christi mistici. **Sed** ne ego laborem in istis [p. 59] equivocis, limitavi nomen ecclesie, ut signat sponsam Christi. Tres tamen evidencie fiunt ad probandum hanc sentenciam. Primo dictum apostoli I. Timothei IV°, 10 Salvator est omnium et maxime fidelium; secundo per illud I. Johannis, II° cap., Ipse propiciacio pro peccatis nostris, non **autem** pro nostris tantum est, **sed** eciam pro tocius mundi. **Sed** in quantum salvat et propiciatur hominibus, est caput eorum: ergo Christus est caput omnium hominum. Tercio per hoc quod omnes homines sunt fideles, quia habent fidem de Deo. Patet quod ista argumenta procedunt ex equivoco: nam concedi potest cum sancto Thoma quod Christus quoad esse naturale est caput eciam diaboli, et per consequens, caput membrorum suorum, non solum quoad esse naturale, **sed** eciam quia sunt fratres Christi quoad naturam specificam, quorum omnia peccata ad eorum dampnacionem et penam cedunt, sanctis vero ad gloriam. Et sic misericorditer punit fratres suos ad bonum eorum (ut alias declaravi). Loqui **autem** sic generaliter atque equivoce est preter propositum nostrum, quia loquimur de capite et membris unius hominis ex sponso et sponsa, de quo Joh. III°, 13 dicitur quod nemo ascendit in celum, nisi qui descendit de celo (ut Augustinus exponit, Joh. Omelia XIIª). Et quantum ad dictum apostoli, dicitur communiter quod esse salvatorem vel salvare est equivocum ad quatuor: primo generaliter accipitur pro apponere medicinam ad infirmum cuius virtute egrotus sanari poterit, et isto modo salvavit Christus totum mundum humani generis, cum apposuit medicinam passionis, que suffecit redimere multos mundos. Istam **autem** [p. 60] medicinam apposuit Christus vel obtulit humano generi, cuius septima pars nunquam suscepit eam, sicut semper infideles quos Christus dicit Joh. III°, 18, iam iudicatos, et illos dicit sanctus Thomas esse membra

equivocal [senses], I have [assigned]{g:limitare} the name church [to it] as it signifies the

### 03-d ¶2 · 60.5–60.10  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 60] Christi in potencia, non in actu. Alii **autem** recipiunt medicinam **sed** finaliter recidivant, ut presciti. Tercii vero receperunt medicinam et post lapsum ad sanctam matrem ecclesiam revertuntur, ut peccatores predestinati. ENGLISH: Christ in potency, not in act. And others receive the medicine but finally relapse, as the
[foreknown]{g:prescitus}. But the third have received the medicine and after a fall return to holy
mother church, as [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} sinners. The fourth, indeed, are healed by virtue of
Christ's merit and never afterwards fall into mortal [sin].[^6]

### 03-e ¶1 · 60.11–63.21  (16 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 60] Et sic vere dicit apostolus quod Christus est salvator maxime fidelium in tercio et quarto gradu, et intelligunt quidam dictum apostoli I ad Thimoteum II°, 4, directe ad litteram, quod Deus vult omnes homines salvos fieri. Ad secundum obiectum ex dicto beati Johannis est eadem sentencia. Christus enim est quoad sufficienciam propiciacio, hoc est, medicus vel medicina propiciativa tocius humani generis, quod habet ultra diabolum post ruinam angelorum libertatem arbitrii, ut salvetur, cum totum genus humanum sit homo quidam qui descendendo ab Jerusalem in Jericho incidit in latrones; qui relictus secundum totum semivivus ex gracia Samaritani habuit medicinam dispositam et ministros mercede et instruccione duplicis testamenti provide dispensantes, secundum quam totus ille homo sanari poterit. **Sed** preter illum hominem naturalem oportet secundum Adam generare spiritualiter unam generacionem priori permixtam que est sancta mater ecclesia. Et in illo corpore sunt aliqui vere membra ut predestinati, quarte digestioni assimilati, sive sint ad tempus discrasiati ut lapsi, sive [p. 61] solide post primam sanacionem in sanitate perpetua stabiliti. Illi **autem** qui sunt intra dictam ecclesiam non ut partes, sunt in gradu duplici, ut quidam in sua infidelitati perpetuo permanentes qui nunquam receperunt caritatem vel graciam. Et hii sunt tamquam aer aut ventus in homine, in quem nunquam egit suus calor naturalis. Hii **autem** sive commixti sint localiter sive solum in genere humano, prosunt ad refrigerium sancte matris ecclesie. Hii **autem** sunt tamquam superfluitates cibi sponsi ecclesie qui spiritualiter digerit predestinatos et demum assimilat. Et iste est cibus de quo loquitur Christus Johannis IV°, 32: Ego cibum habeo manducare quem vos nescitis. Unde superflua illius cibi sunt infecta duplici, ut aliqui sunt in stomacho sancte matris ecclesie recepti per graciam secundum presentem iusticiam, **sed** ex eorum ypocrisi vel heresi non sunt propter abhominacionem caritatis operantis digesti, **sed** in hac vita sensibiliter evomendi; de quibus loquitur spiritus sanctus Apocalypsis III°, 16: Neque calidus es neque frigidus, ideo incipiam te evomere de ore meo. Tales **autem** sunt sive clerici sive laici pretendentes se operari ad edificacionem sancte matris ecclesie quidquid agunt, **sed** tamen quia habent intenciones frigidas temporalium propter quas principaliter agunt, ideo sunt ypocrite Deo abhominabiles ut aqua tepida que provocat ad vomitum. Et hii sunt Deo maxime abhominabiles racione falsitatis in duplicitate latentis que opponitur veritati. Unde sicut iuxta Aristotelem primo Metyororum VI° cap. [p. 62] aqua sic calida cicius congelatur propter exalaciones frigidas quas pori caloris recipiunt: sic pretendentes sanctitatem huiusmodi sunt periculosissimi hostes ecclesie, per quos ut proditorie aperientes portas ecclesie subintrant frigidi fide et virtutibus; qui plus quam inter fideles extinguunt caritatem vocate ecclesie et congelant. Debet, inquam, ecclesia fervere pura caritate ex simplici participacione Christi qui est ignis per se calidus caritate, et matrem omnium malorum cupiditatem postponere, et per consequens, non adinvenciones lucrativas plus quam legem Dei diligere. Sunt **autem** intra ecclesiam alii presciti magis absconditi, evadentes evomicionem huiusmodi, ut hii tamquam superfluitas sicca digestionis prime, hii tamquam superfluitas humida digestionis secunde, et hii ut apostemata vel vermes digestionis tercie. Et patet quod non sequitur: Si quicunque viantes sunt in ecclesia, tunc sunt de ecclesia, **sed** econtra. Nam scimus zizania crescere inter frumenta, corvum pasci in eadem area cum columba et paleam inorreari pro tempore inter grana, et tamen est incommunicans distinccio inter ista, sicut exemplatum est in corpore humano. Ita ymaginari debemus de sancta matre ecclesia, et sic intelligendus est textus I. Joh. II°, 18, cum dicitur: Nunc Antichristi multi facti sunt; ex nobis prodierunt, **sed** non erant ex nobis, nam si fuissent ex nobis, permansissent utique nobiscum. Sicut enim superfluitas procedit ex cibo et membris solidis, dum tamen non sit ex eis, sic purgamenta ecclesie procedunt ex ea, non tamen erant ex ea ut partes, cum nulla pars eius potest ab ea excidere. Unde ad discernendum distinccionem puri et superfluorum huiusmodi dicit beatus Johannes epistola secunda canonica: Omnis, inquit, qui recedit et non permanet in doctrina Christi, Deum [p. 63] non habet. Doctrina **autem** Christi sunt humilitas, caritas et paupertas. Quicunque ergo oderit ista, non imitando Christum ut aquila, scito quod non sit de sua ecclesia. Et sic tres gentes excluduntur a membris ecclesie qui omnino peccant in fide scilicet omnino infideles ut pagani; heretici ut sacerdotes et pharisei cum eis similibus qui nunquam gustaverunt dulcedinem spei beatitudinis ex imitacione sapiencie incarnate, **sed** resident facietenus in ecclesia gracia affeccionis primatuum temporalium et carnalium voluptatum; tercii vero qui gustaverunt paululum digestionis divine, **sed** propter defectum caritatis perfecte aut finalis perseverancie excidunt tamquam inhabilia, ut formaliter induant caput Christum; et hii peccant in trinitatem et tres virtutes catholicas (ut dixi superius). Omnes **autem** tales, dicit Augustinus, non esse de membris ecclesie, sicut in corpore hominis naturali nemo dicit proporcionalia esse membra. Sanctus **autem** Thomas videtur loqui generaliter, equivocando de membro, scilicet de membro temporaliter vivo et de membro omnino mortuo putativo.

ENGLISH: And so the apostle says truly that Christ is the saviour especially of the faithful in the third and
fourth degree; and some understand the saying of the apostle, 1 Timothy 2:4, directly according to the
letter, that God "will have all men to be saved".[^1] To the second [objection]{g:objectum}, from the
saying of blessed John, the [teaching]{g:sentencia} is the same. For Christ is, as to sufficiency, the
propitiation, that is, the physician or propitiatory medicine, of the whole human race, which has,
beyond the devil, after the fall of the angels, freedom of choice, so that it may be saved; since the
whole human race is "a certain man" who, going down "from Jerusalem to Jericho, fell among robbers";
who, left half dead in his whole [being], had by the grace of the Samaritan medicine prepared, and
ministers providently dispensing it with the wages and instruction of the twofold testament, according
to which that whole man could be healed.[^2] But besides that natural man, the second Adam must
spiritually beget one generation mingled with the former, which is holy mother [church]{g:ecclesia}. And
in that body some are truly members, as the [predestinate]{g:predestinatus}, assimilated in the fourth
digestion, whether they are for a time distempered, as the lapsed, or [61] firmly established in
perpetual health after the first healing.[^3] And those who are within the said church not as parts are
in a twofold degree: as some remaining perpetually in their [unbelief]{g:infidelitas}, who never
received [charity]{g:caritas} or grace. And these are like air or wind in a man, on which his natural
heat never acted. And these, whether they are mingled [with the church] in place or only in the human
race, serve for the cooling of holy mother church. And these [others] are like the superfluities of the
food of the church's [bridegroom]{g:sponsus}, who spiritually digests the predestinate and at last
assimilates [them]. And this is the food of which Christ speaks, John 4:32: "I have meat to eat which
you know not."[^4] Hence the superfluities of that food are tainted in two ways: as some are received
into the stomach of holy mother church through grace, according to present justice, but because of
their hypocrisy or heresy are not digested, on account of the abhorrence of working charity, but are to
be [perceptibly]{g:sensibilis} vomited up in this life; of whom the Holy Spirit speaks, Apocalypse 3:16:
"Thou art neither hot nor cold; therefore I will begin to vomit thee out of my mouth."[^5] And such are,
whether clerics or laymen, those who make a show of working for the edification of holy mother church
in whatever they do, but yet, because they have cold intentions of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, for
which they principally act, are therefore hypocrites abominable to God, like lukewarm water that
provokes to vomiting. And these are most abominable to God by reason of the falsehood lurking in
duplicity, which is opposed to truth. Hence, just as, according to Aristotle, *Meteorologica* 1,
chapter 6, [62] water thus heated freezes more quickly because of the cold exhalations which the pores
of heat receive, so those who make a show of sanctity of this kind are the most dangerous enemies of the
church; through whom, as through men treacherously opening the gates of the church, those cold in faith
and virtues creep in; who, more than [happens] among the faithful, extinguish and freeze the charity of
the so-called church.[^6] The church, I say, ought to glow with pure charity from simple participation in
Christ, who is a fire hot of itself with charity, and to put after [it] cupidity, the mother of all
evils, and consequently not to love lucrative inventions more than [God's law]{g:lex dei}. And there are
within the church other [foreknown]{g:prescitus}, more hidden, escaping such vomiting: some like the dry
superfluity of the first digestion, some like the moist superfluity of the second digestion, and some
like [abscesses]{g:apostema} or worms of the third digestion.[^7] And it is clear that this does not
[follow]{g:sequitur}: "If any [persons] [on the road]{g:viantes} are in the church, then they are of the
church"; but the converse [does]. For we know that [cockle]{g:zizania} grows among the wheat, that the
raven feeds on the same threshing-floor as the dove, and that chaff is laid up in the barn for a time
among the grain; and yet there is an incommunicable distinction between these, as has been exemplified
in the human body. So we must imagine of holy mother church; and so is to be understood the text of 1
John 2:18, where it is said: "Even now there are become many Antichrists. They went out from us, but they
were not of us. For if they had been of us, they would no doubt have remained with us."[^8] For just as
superfluity proceeds from the food and from the solid members, while yet it is not of them, so the
purgings of the church proceed from her, yet they were not of her as parts, since no part of her can
fall away from her. Hence, to discern the distinction between the pure and such superfluities, blessed
John says in the second canonical epistle: "Whosoever," he says, "revolteth and continueth not in the
[doctrine]{g:doctrina} of Christ hath [63] not God." Now the doctrine of Christ is humility, charity and
poverty. Whoever, therefore, hates these, not imitating Christ as an eagle [does], know that he is not of
his church.[^9] And so three peoples are excluded from the members of the church, who sin wholly in
faith: namely, the wholly unbelieving, as pagans; heretics, as priests and Pharisees with those like
them, who never tasted the sweetness of the hope of blessedness from the imitation of incarnate wisdom,
but sit in the church in outward face for the sake of an affection for temporal primacies and carnal
pleasures; and, third, those who have tasted a little of the divine digestion, but, for want of perfect
charity or of final perseverance, fall away as [unqualified]{g:inhabilis} to put on formally Christ the
head; and these sin against the Trinity and the three catholic virtues (as I said above).[^10] And all
such, Augustine says, are not of the members of the church, just as in the natural body of a man no one
says that the proportionals are members. But St Thomas seems to speak generally, equivocating on
"member": namely, of a member alive for a time and of a member wholly dead, a putative one.[^11]

### 03-e ¶2 · 63.22–64.10  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 63] Ad tercium negatur ad sensum scripture quod omnes homines sunt fideles. Conceditur tamen cum Damasceno quod omnis homo habet fidem saltem informem cum naturaliter prima impressione entis cognoscit saltem confuse primo omnium Deum esse et omnem articulum fidei. **Sed** ista fides informis non sufficit denominare hominem fidelem vel partem ecclesie, **sed** oportet excludere omnem infidelitatem finalem. Et de tali fideli loquitur Christus Johannis X° 28, promittens, ut omnis qui credit in filium non pereat, supple morte perpetua; ille **autem** credit in [p. 64] filium qui credendo ipsum inheret sibi finaliter per amorem. Unde quia de tali finali inherencia est nobis dubium, ideo docet Augustinus super Johannem, Omelia XIIª, quomodo membrum ecclesie est Deo et cui ipse voluerit revelare satis cognitum. Novit, inquit, dominus qui sunt eius, novit qui permaneant ad coronam, qui permaneant ad flammam, novit in area sua triticum, novit paleam, novit segetem, novit zizania. Ceteris **autem** est illud incognitum qui sunt columbe et qui corvi.

ENGLISH: To the third, it is denied, in the sense of scripture, that all men are faithful. Yet it is granted with
Damascene that every man has faith, at least [unformed]{g:fides informis}, since naturally, by the first
impression of [being]{g:ens}, he knows, at least [indistinctly]{g:confuse}, first of all, that God is,
and every [article of faith]{g:articulus fidei}.[^12] But this unformed faith does not suffice to name a
man faithful, or a part of the church; rather, all final [unbelief]{g:infidelitas} must be excluded. And
of such a faithful man Christ speaks, John 10:28, promising that everyone who believes in the Son may not
perish (supply: by everlasting death); now he believes in [64] the Son who, by believing him, adheres to
him finally through love.[^13] Hence, since about such final adherence we are in doubt, Augustine on
John, homily 12, teaches how a member of the church is well enough known to God, and to whom he wishes to
reveal [it]. "The Lord knoweth," he says, "who are his; he knows who will remain for the crown, who will
remain for the flame; he knows in his threshing-floor the wheat, he knows the chaff; he knows the crop,
he knows the [cockle]{g:zizania}." But to the rest it is unknown who are doves and who ravens.[^14]

### 03-f ¶1 · 64.11–66.2  (9 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 64] Unde Augustinus super Joh. Omelia VIa, exponens illud Johannis primo: Vidi spiritum descendentem quasi columbam de celo et manentem super eum, notat primo quomodo descensus columbe signat mistice spiritum sanctum, quia columba habet gemitum amorosum pro cantu. Spiritus **autem** sanctus secundum apostolum Romanorum VIII°, 26, postulat pro nobis gemitibus inenarrabilibus. Secundo quia columba sine felle vel laceracione unguium communicat pacis seculum, et tercio quia pascitur puris granis, econtra de corvis et minutis avibus que pascuntur carnibus vel muscas capiunt ut pascantur. Et propter istam similitudinem ad proprietates spiritus sancti precepit Christus suis apostolis Matthei X°, 16, quod sint simplices ut columbe. Secundo notat Augustinus quod descensus singularis columbe super Christum cum voce patris docuit Baptistam quod Christus capitaliter baptizat in spiritu sancto primo cuius principii est unitas in baptismo, qui non suscipit magis et minus secundum merita baptizantum, [p. 65] eo quod Christus non potuit communicare hanc primiciam potestatis suis discipulis. Tercio notat quomodo columba ad sensum allegoricum signat matrem ecclesiam que est una (ut dicitur Canticorum, V° capitulo, de columba). Unde sicut in diluvio columba egressa de archa reversa est, deferens ramum virentis olive, corvo pretextu affectus cadaveris extincto diluvio, sic in sancta matre ecclesia hii exeunt ad tempus et penitencia ducti redeunt, non solum cum verborum foliis, **sed** fructu bonorum operum, et omnino cum oleo caritatis que supereminenter super alia carismata instar olei supernatat. Ideo signanter dicit apostolus Iª Cor. XII°, 31: Adhuc eminenciorem viam demonstrabo vobis. Presciti **autem** exeunt de archa ut corvi, **sed** propter amorem temporalium pro quibus instanter placitant demerguntur. Quarto notari potest secundum logicam scripture quomodo spiritus sanctus movetur, cum descendit et postulat gemitibus inexplicabilibus et mane consurgit ad instruendum ecclesiam (ut dicitur Isaie VII°). **Sed** hoc non facit in natura propria, **sed** in creatura quam assumit, non ypostatice, **sed** singulariter ad sui noticiam et caritatis officium peragendum, nec solum in simplicitate columbina apparuit, **sed** in linguis igneis dispertitis ad docendum quod nedum debemus esse simplices, **sed** in operibus eciam caritatis ferventes, ut, sicut superbia divisit linguas in sensu, non in loco, tempore edificacionis turris Babel, Genesis XI°, sic econtra humilitas spiritus sancti congreget linguas uniformes quoad sensum, quantumlibet dispares quoad locum. Quinto colligitur ex dictis beati Augustini quod solum illi salvantur ut partes matris ecclesie qui proprietatem deserentes [p. 66] querunt finaliter res communes ecclesie que sunt capitaliter domini Jesu Christi.

ENGLISH: Hence Augustine on John, homily 6, expounding that [text] of John 1[:32], "I saw the Spirit coming
down, as a dove from heaven, and remaining upon him," notes, first, how the descent of the dove
mystically signifies the Holy Spirit: because the dove has a loving moan for its song. And the Holy
Spirit, according to the apostle, Romans 8:26, "asketh for us with unspeakable groanings."[^1] Second,
because the dove, without gall and without tearing with its claws, communicates the age [?] of peace;
and third, because it feeds on clean grains, the contrary [being true] of ravens and small birds, which
feed on flesh or catch flies to feed themselves.[^2] And because of this likeness to the properties of
the Holy Spirit, Christ commanded his apostles, Matthew 10:16, that they be "simple as doves." Second,
Augustine notes that the descent of a single dove upon Christ, with the voice of the Father, taught the
Baptist that Christ baptizes as head in the Holy Spirit, as first [principle], of which principle is
the unity in baptism, which does not admit more and less according to the merits of those who baptize,
[65] because Christ could not communicate this first-fruit of power to his disciples.[^3] Third, he
notes how the dove, in the allegorical sense, signifies mother [church]{g:ecclesia}, which is one (as is
said in the Canticle, chapter 5, of the dove). Hence, just as in the flood the dove that went out of the
ark returned, carrying a branch of green olive, while the raven, on the pretext of its craving for a
carcass, was destroyed by the flood, so in holy mother church these go out for a time and, led by
penance, return, not only with the leaves of words but with the fruit of good works, and above all with
the oil of [charity]{g:caritas}, which floats above the other gifts preeminently, as oil does.[^4]
Therefore the apostle says pointedly, 1 Corinthians 12:31: "Yet I will shew you a more eminent way."
But the [foreknown]{g:prescitus} go out of the ark like ravens, and because of the love of
[temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, for which they plead insistently, they are drowned.[^5] Fourth, it can be
noted according to the logic of scripture how the Holy Spirit is moved, since he comes down, and asks
with inexpressible groanings, and rises early to instruct the church (as is said in Isaiah 7). But he
does not do this in his own nature, but in the creature which he assumes, not hypostatically, but
singularly, to make himself known and to carry out the office of charity; nor did he appear only in
dovelike simplicity, but in parted tongues of fire, to teach that we ought not only to be simple but
also fervent in works of charity: so that, just as pride divided tongues in sense, not in place, at the
time of the building of the tower of Babel, Genesis 11, so, on the contrary, the humility of the Holy
Spirit may gather tongues uniform as to sense, however disparate as to place.[^6] Fifth, it is gathered
from the sayings of blessed Augustine that only those are saved as parts of mother church who,
forsaking property, [66] seek finally the common things of the church, which are the Lord Jesus
Christ's as chief [lord].[^7]

### 03-g ¶1 · 66.3–69.3  (9 sed/autem)

### 04-c ¶4 · 79.4–79.29  (2 sed/autem)

this way but obscure, since to be so joined to the devil [103] does not sound directly of mystery but
of the scourge;[^11] and so the body of the devil has natural [being]{g:esse}, since according to
Augustine in the book *On the Nature of the Good* … every evil must be founded in a good: therefore
every evil of conduct is founded in a good of nature.[^12] Nor is it an
[absurdity]{g:inconveniens}, but in harmony with the manner of speaking of the saints and of the
common folk, to call any created substance composed of quantitative parts, whether material or
formal, a true body; for thus the aggregate of spiritual and indivisible laws is called the body of
[law]{g:ius}. Such natural being, therefore, any aggregate substance has that is united in what it
has in common, as in a species or genus; and besides natural being the rational creature has the
being of blessedness, of virtue or of vice, as is the case with the members of Christ and of the
devil.[^13] And speaking of such union, or being of a body, Augustine denies in *On Christian
Doctrine* III, chapter 32, that one body is composed of the members of Christ and of the devil.[^14]
And further, when the form uniting the body of the devil is touched on, the answer is that there is
to be given an extrinsic form and an intrinsic deformity. And the extrinsic form is God's eternal
[foreknowledge]{g:presciencia}, by which he knows and ordains that all such are bound to perpetual
punishment; and the intrinsic deformity is final disobedience, or pride, a contumacy which the saints
call the guilt of final impenitence, or the sin against the Holy Spirit.[^15] And so the same sin
joins and separates. For it joins the members of the devil, binding them in hell in their malice, and
segregates them from the fellowship of the blessed; just as heat, dissolving a mixed body, gathers
together things of one kind, since it makes each element [104] of the dissolved mixture seek its own
place, but segregates things of different kinds, since it dissolves the harmonious complexion,
resolving the parts of the mixture into their simple, separated natures.[^16] For on the day of
judgement, from the contrariety of the cold of the body of the devil and the heat of charity of the
body of Christ, the bipartite body must, according to the law of the final form, be dissolved, when
the light parts will fly up with their head, which is a consuming fire, to their natural mansions
above. But the leaden parts, by earthly affection, go down to the lower regions; and so these two are
not only two bodies, but two persons (as I touched on in chapter 43 of the third book);[^17] on which
it is enough to adduce one testimony of blessed Gregory, *Moralia* IV, chapter 11: "Just as," he
says, "our Redeemer is one person with the congregation of the good (for he is the head of the body,
and we the body of this head), so the old enemy is one person with the whole collection of the
reprobate."[^18]

### 05-e ¶1 · 108.18–110.8  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 108] Hec **autem** fecit abbas noster, ut doceat nos tenere medium virtutis in nostris operibus. Et ex II° Ethicorum extrema ab illo medio in quo virtus consistit deviancia corrumpunt virtutem; nec video quin, si quantitas mathematica continuat elementa, si humor aqueus continuat iuxta singenia, si armonia corporis animalis faciat membra adnata esse continua, quasi longe plus latitudo, longitudo, sublimitas et profundum (de quibus loquitur apostolus Eph. III° 18) facit unanimes vere unum. Illa eciam aqua sapiencie (de qua Ecclesiastici XV, 3) continuabit unius moris in domo, et demum armonia in trinitate fundata illapsa universali ecclesie ipsam ex membris compaginatam faciet unam sponsam. [p. 109] Unde Bernhardus, De Consideracione, libro V°, ad Eugenium papam, ponit octo modos unitatum. Est, inquit, unitas que collectiva potest dici, ut multi lapides faciunt acervum unum. Est unitas constitutiva, cum multa membra unum corpus vel multe partes unum quodque totum constituunt. Est et coniugativa qua fit, ut duo iam non duo sint **sed** una caro. Est et nativa qua anima et caro unus nascitur homo. Est unitas potestativa qua homo virtutis non instabilis non dissimilis **sed** unus sibimet semper nititur inveniri. Est consentanea, cum per caritatem multorum hominum est cor unum et anima una. Est et votiva, cum anima votis omnibus adherens Deo unus spiritus est. Et est dignativa unitas qua limus noster a Dei verbo in unam assumptus est personam. Et super istas octo unitates dicit in quodam sermone quod est unitas principalis qua tres persone divine sunt una indivisibilis essencia. Et sic omnis unitas potest dici supernaturalis, naturalis vel virtualis. Duo posteriora membra recipiunt divisiones multiplices et cum in equivocis non sit contradiccio, patet quod non repugnat corpus ecclesie non continuari, loquendo de continuacione naturali sensibili, cum quelibet pars eius quantitativa completa in actu non sic continuatur cum alia naturaliter incommunicante, et tamen continuatur alia continuacione spirituali plus forti per spiritum Dei diffusum in cordibus omnium membrorum ecclesie habencium omnia in communi. Quarto principaliter arguitur per hoc quod ad univocam racionem ecclesie requiritur univoca racio suarum [p. 110] parcium, **sed** non est talis univocacio in peregrinantibus, dormientibus et beatis. Ergo non univoce constituunt Christi sanctam matrem ecclesiam que non potest dici etherogena, nam si fides ponatur forma constituens membrum ecclesie, videtur quod beati quibus inest clara visio, evacuata fide non sint de membris ecclesie, si caritas adhuc caritas patrie et caritas vie sunt disparium racionum.

ENGLISH: And these things our abbot did to teach us to hold the mean of virtue in our works. And from
*Ethics* II, the extremes that deviate from that mean in which virtue consists corrupt virtue;[^1] nor
do I see why, if mathematical quantity makes the elements continuous, if watery humour makes things
continuous with their kin, if the harmony of an animal body makes the members grown on it to be
continuous, the breadth, length, height and depth (of which the apostle speaks, Ephesians 3:18) should
not, as it were far more, make those of one mind truly one.[^2] That "water of wisdom" too (of which
Ecclesiasticus 15:3 [speaks]) will make continuous [those] "of one manner in a house"; and finally the
harmony founded in the Trinity, flowing into the universal [church]{g:ecclesia}, will make it, joined
together of members, one [bride]{g:sponsa}.[^3] [109] Hence Bernard, *On Consideration*, book 5, to
[Pope]{g:papa} Eugenius, sets out eight modes of unity. "There is," he says, "a unity that can be
called collective, as many stones make one heap. There is a constitutive unity, when many members
constitute one body, or many parts any one whole. There is also a conjugal [unity], by which it comes
about that two are no longer two, but one flesh. There is also a native [unity], by which soul and
flesh are born one man.[^4] There is a potestative unity, by which a man of virtue strives always to
be found not unstable, not unlike himself, but one with himself. There is a consentient [unity], when
through [charity]{g:caritas} many men have one heart and one soul. There is also a votive [unity], when
the soul, cleaving to God with all its desires, is one spirit [with him]. And there is a condescending
unity, by which our clay was assumed by the Word of God into one person."[^5] And above these eight
unities he says in a certain sermon that there is a principal unity, by which the three divine persons
are one indivisible essence.[^6] And so every unity can be called supernatural, natural or virtual. The
two latter members admit manifold divisions; and since there is no contradiction in equivocal [terms],
it is clear that it is not repugnant that the body of the church is not continuous, speaking of
natural, [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} continuity, since each quantitative part of it, complete in act,
is not so continuous with another that naturally does not communicate [with it]; and yet it is
continuous by another, spiritual continuity, stronger, through the Spirit of God poured forth in the
hearts of all the members of the church, who have all things in common.[^7] Fourthly and principally,
[it is argued, by appeal to]{g:arguitur per} this: that for a univocal account of the church there is
required a univocal account of its [110] parts; now there is no such univocity in those who are
[pilgrims]{g:peregrinari}, the sleeping and the blessed. Therefore they do not univocally constitute
Christ's holy mother church, which cannot be called heterogeneous; for if faith is posited as the form
constituting a member of the church, it seems that the blessed, in whom is clear vision, faith being
done away, are not of the members of the church; if charity, still the charity of the
[homeland]{g:patria} and the charity of the [way]{g:via} are of disparate accounts.[^8]

### 05-e ¶2 · 110.9–110.17  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 110] **Sed** hic dicitur (ut supra) quod forma exemplaris eternaliter formans ab extra quodcunque membrum ecclesie est predestinacio Dei eterna. Forma **autem** per se et ab intrinseco constituens membrum ecclesie est predestinacio passiva. Ipsa enim non potest inesse multiplicative, nisi assit ecclesia. ENGLISH: But here [the answer is]{g:dicitur} (as above) that the exemplar form, eternally forming from without
any member of the church, is God's eternal predestination. And the form constituting a member of the
church of itself and from within is passive predestination. For it cannot be present
multiplicatively unless the church is there. And so every [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} [person]

### 05-e ¶3 · 110.18–112.9  (12 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 110] Unde sicut crementum et profectus persone non variat eius speciem, quia sic homo secundum variaciones etatum transiret de specie ad speciem, sic etas persone sancte matris ecclesie non variat eius speciem, **sed** corroborat per accidens racionem ecclesie. Ymmo manet eadem fides in mundo, in purgatorio et in celo. **Sed** imperfeccio que ex parte est hic evacuabitur in beatis; manebit, inquam, eadem fides que creditur, cum sit veritas eterna, et manebit eadem fides qua et secundum quam creditur, quia manebunt iidem actus et habitus. Est enim prima adhesio quam creatura habet de veritate credenda actus fidei cui accidit obscuritas enigmatica pro via que evacuatur in patria veniente intuicione clara, caritas **autem** eadem habens in patria confirmacionem loco incertitudinis. [p. 111] Dissensio **autem** est aput doctores nostros, eo quod aliqui intelligunt fidem aggregative pro adhesione prima cum imperfeccione sequente. Aliqui **autem** intelligunt fidem personaliter pro illa adhesione cui inest per accidens intuicio vel enigma. Illa **autem** prima adhesio precedens racionem est eiusdem racionis hic et in patria, **sed** ibi validior (sicut alibi diffuse exposui). Et sic omnes predestinati habent continue fidem gracia predestinacionis formatam, et sic non cadit a membris ecclesie per mortale, ut patet III°, De Doctrina Christiana XXXII°, et epistola XXXIII ad Bonifacium. Explicat hoc de David et Petro; de David **autem** explanat hoc in libro De Octo Questionibus ad Dulcicium amicum suum; quorum omnium racio est quia Deus non potest quemquam noviter diligere vel odire, **sed** sub quocumque gradu amat vel odit aliquem, sub eodem gradu eternaliter sic amat vel odit. Nec est detestabile quod Deus semper odit beatos in celo, nam semper odit peccata eorum preterita, non pro semper, ideo semper amat eos pro sempiterna glorificacione. Unde ecclesia excommunicat tales cum, ipsis existentibus in mortali, notificat eos privari communione sanctorum secundum presentem iusticiam, cum humidum fluens ad tempus eis deficiat, nunquam **autem** anathematizat tales vel omnino dividit a corpore Christi nec potest. Et ista est plerumque sentencia Augustini, si bene inspicitur, non in dolo. Et patet ex solucione proximi argumenti et huius, quomodo intelliguntur sancti Ysidorus, De Summo Bono. [p. 112] cap. XIX° et Augustinus, epistola XXXIIIª ad Bonifacium, quomodo ecclesia fidelium est ubique diffusa, ecclesia **autem** hereticorum est in parvo angulo coartata. Predestinati nempe dilatant more celestium fructus suos, alii **autem** licet sint hinc plures in numero, more terrestrium constringuntur. Christus enim caput prioris ecclesie cui oportet ut secunda subserviat, est potencior patre mendacii quem sic ligat, hoc est, ipsum in membris suis penaliter sic constringit.

ENGLISH: Hence, just as the growth and progress of a person does not vary his species (for so a man, according
to the variations of his ages, would pass from species to species), so the age of the person of holy
mother church does not vary its species, but strengthens by accident the account of the church.
Indeed, the same faith remains in the world, in purgatory and in heaven. But the imperfection that is
"in part" here will be done away in the blessed; there will remain, I say, the same faith which is
believed, since it is eternal truth; and there will remain the same faith by which and according to
which [one] believes, since the same acts and habits will remain. For the first adhesion which the
creature has to the truth to be believed is the act of faith, to which, for the way, there befalls an
enigmatic obscurity, which is done away in the homeland when clear intuition comes; and charity
[remains] the same, having in the homeland confirmation in place of uncertainty.[^10] [111] And there
is disagreement among our doctors, in that some understand faith [aggregatively]{g:aggregative}, for
the first adhesion together with the imperfection that follows; but others understand faith
personally, for that adhesion in which intuition or enigma is present by accident. Now that first
adhesion, preceding reason, is of the same account here and in the homeland, but stronger there (as I
have expounded at length elsewhere).[^11] And so all the predestinate continually have faith formed by
the grace of predestination, and so [such a one] does not fall from the members of the church through
mortal [sin], as is clear from *On Christian Doctrine* III, 32, and the letter 33 to Boniface. He
explains this of David and Peter; and of David he explains it in the book *On Eight Questions* to his
friend Dulcitius;[^12] and the reason of all these is that God cannot love or hate anyone newly, but
under whatever degree he loves or hates someone, under the same degree he eternally so loves or hates.
Nor is it detestable that God always hates the blessed in heaven, for he always hates their past sins,
[though] not for always; therefore he always loves them for everlasting glorification.[^13] Hence the
church excommunicates such [persons] when, while they are in mortal [sin], it makes known that they are
deprived of the communion of saints according to present justice, since the [flowing
moisture]{g:humidum fluens} fails them for a time; but it never anathematizes such, or wholly divides
[them] from the body of Christ, nor can it. And this is for the most part the
[teaching]{g:sentencia} of Augustine, if it is looked at well, not with guile.[^14] And it is clear,
from the solution of the last argument and of this one, how the saints are to be understood, Isidore,
*On the Highest Good*, [112] chapter 19, and Augustine, letter 33 to Boniface, [when they say] that
the church of the faithful is spread everywhere, but the church of the heretics is confined in a small
corner. For the predestinate spread their fruits after the manner of heavenly things; but the others,
although they are on this side more in number, are confined after the manner of earthly things. For
Christ, [head]{g:caput} of the former church, which the second must serve, is mightier than the father
of lies, whom he thus binds; that is, he thus confines him [in hardship]{g:penaliter} in his
members.[^15]

### 06-b ¶1 · 120.14–121.28  (8 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 120] Hic dicitur concedendo assumptum, cum non possunt esse plures quam duo homines, et ita conceditur quod uterque istorum duorum hominum habet Deum pro capite, non pro parte eius, **sed** principaliter extrinsecus influente, et sic unus eorum est corpus domini quod est sancta ecclesia, **sed** alter non est corpus domini, **sed** corpus diaboli. Et sic non sequitur: Hoc corpus est domini, ergo hoc est corpus domini. Et hoc intendit Augustinus, III° De Doctrina Christiana, XXXII° cap., increpans Ticonium, quia vocavit prescitos corpus dominicum. Non enim est totum corpus naturale corpus domini bipertitum, nisi utraque pars fuerit corpus domini. Et cum Augustinus XXXII° postquam ostendit quod Christus et corpus suum quod est ecclesia sunt una persona, increpat eum in secunda regula qua vocat totum genus hominum corpus domini bipartitum. Non, inquit, sic debuit appellare. Non enim revera domini corpus est quod cum ipso non [p. 121] erit in eternum, **sed** dicendum fuitde corpore domini vero atque permixto, aut vero atque simulato, quia non solum in eternum, verum eciam nunc ypocrite non cum illo esse dicendi sunt. Ecce quam plane iste sanctus diffinit quod presciti non sunt de Christi ecclesia. Unde De Consecracione, d. IIª Hoc sacramentum, dicit canon quod per cibacionem eucaristie societatem vult intelligi corporis et membrorum quod est ecclesia in predestinatis. Et De Penitencia, d. IVª, Si ex bono; et sepe infra habetur quod nemo est de regno Christi quod est ecclesia, nisi vere filius quem pater dedit sibi. De quo dicit Joh. 111°, 16, quod oportet ut non pereat, **sed** habeat vitam eternam. Et sic multi sunt in ecclesia qui vocantur filii nuncupatim qui non sunt de ecclesia. Conceditur ergo quod homo qui est humanum genus est partim prescitus et partim predestinatus, et sic tempore suo partim salvatus et partim dampnatus, **sed** ille non est ecclesia, quamvis faciat quidquid facit aliquis homo, quia ad unitatem requiritur unitas capitis, **sed** ut dicit apostolus IIª Cor. VI°, 15. Que convencio Christi ad Belial? Non ergo sunt duo capita ex equo vel subordinata, cum secundum apostolum que a Deo sunt ordinata, sunt. Et sic homo humani generis est corpus naturale **sed** non corpus ecclesie propter diversitatem contrarietatis capitum et equivocacionem ordinacionis divine ad gloriam et ad penam.

ENGLISH: Here [the answer is]{g:dicitur} by granting the [assumption]{g:assumptum}, since there cannot be more
than two men; and so it is granted that each of these two men has God for [head]{g:caput}, not as a
part of it, but as principally [flowing in]{g:influere} from without; and so one of them is the body
of the Lord, which is holy [church]{g:ecclesia}, but the other is not the body of the Lord, but the
body of the devil.[^1] And so it does not follow: "This body is the Lord's, therefore this is the body
of the Lord." And this is what Augustine means, *On Christian Doctrine* III, chapter 32, rebuking
Tyconius because he called the [foreknown]{g:prescitus} the Lord's body. For the whole natural body
is not the bipartite body of the Lord unless each part were the body of the Lord.[^2] And Augustine,
in [chapter] 32, after he has shown that Christ and his body, which is the church, are one person,
rebukes him in the second rule, in which he calls the whole race of men the bipartite body of the
Lord. "He ought not," he says, "so to have called it. For that is not truly the Lord's body which will
not [121] be with him for ever; but he should have spoken of the Lord's body true and mixed, or true
and feigned; for not only for ever, but even now hypocrites are not to be said to be with him."[^3]
See how plainly this saint defines that the foreknown are not of Christ's church. Hence in *De
consecratione*, distinction 2, *Hoc sacramentum*, the canon says that by the eating of the eucharist
he wishes to be understood the fellowship of the body and members, which is the church in the
[predestinate]{g:predestinatus}.[^4] And in *De penitencia*, distinction 4, *Si ex bono*, and often
below, it is held that no one is of the kingdom of Christ, which is the church, unless [he is] truly
a son whom the Father has given him; of whom John 3:16 says that he must "not perish, but have life
everlasting". And so there are many in the church, called sons by name, who are not of the
church.[^5] It is granted, therefore, that the man who is the human race is partly foreknown and
partly predestinate, and so in his time partly saved and partly damned; but he is not the church,
although he does whatever any man does; for unity requires unity of the head; but, as the apostle
says, 2 Corinthians 6:15, "What concord hath Christ with Belial?" There are not, therefore, two heads
on an equal footing or subordinated, since according to the apostle the things that are from God are
ordered.[^6] And so the man of the human race is a natural body, but not the body of the church,
because of the diversity of contrariety of the heads and the equivocation of the divine
[ordinance]{g:ordinacio} to glory and to punishment.[^7]

### 06-b ¶3 · 122.7–123.10  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 122] Unde videtur mihi quod illi non minus heretice quam blaspheme scandalizant sanctam matrem ecclesiam qui dicunt quod habet ut partes componencia tot membra diaboli, quia indubie tunc foret turpissima et a Deo maxime odita, quia secundum magnam partem. ### 06-b ¶4 · 123.11–123.27  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 123] Quarto arguitur per hoc quod iuxta dicta ecclesia foret per totum tempus veteris testamenti acephala, eo quod non potest habere caput nisi Christum, **sed** tunc non habuit Christum caput, ergo tunc non habuit caput. Et per idem videtur sequi quod eadem ecclesia fuit pro tempore crementi quotlibet membra sua, quia pro nullo instanti fuit nisi fideles qui tunc fuerunt. Unde in exemplo tacto in fine secundi argumenti videtur quod non manet eadem domus, quacunque parte ablata vel noviter veniente, quia per idem ablata iterum tanta et alia noviter veniente, et sic qualibet parte domus corrupta et una secundum se totam noviter generata, maneret eadem domus, sicut est de domo ecclesie. Hic sunt tres difficultates. Prima, utrum Christus fuit ante incarnacionem, quia certum est quod eternaliter fuit verbum Dei et semper caput ecclesie. **Sed** videtur non fuisse caput eius intrinsecum antequam fuit homo.

ENGLISH: Fourthly, [it is argued, by appeal to]{g:arguitur per} this: that according to what has been said the
church would have been headless through the whole time of the old testament, in that it cannot have a
head except Christ; but then it did not have Christ as head; therefore then it had no head. And by the
same token it seems to follow that the same church was, for the time of its growth, any number of its
members, because at no instant was it anything but the faithful who then were.[^15] Hence in the
example touched on at the end of the second argument, it seems that the same house does not remain
when any part is taken away or newly comes; for by the same token, when as much again is taken away
and another newly comes, and so every part of the house is corrupted and one [house] is newly
generated as a whole, the same house would remain, as is the case with the house of the church.[^16]
Here there are three difficulties. The first, whether Christ existed before the incarnation; for it is
certain that he was eternally the Word of God and always head of the church. But he seems not to have
been its intrinsic head before he was man.[^17]

### 06-c ¶1 · 123.28–125.32  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 123] Ad illud **autem** videtur dicendum iuxta sepe dicta quod Christus in primo instanti mundi fuit sponsus ecclesie predestinacione, in confirmacione **autem** angelorum dedit dotem parti sponse sue et sic in confirmacione [p. 124] Abel iusti et aliorum sanctorum usque ad incarnacionem, manente continue eadem desponsacione, cum Cesar semper Augustus continue auget dotem. In incarnacione vero fecit secundas nupcias, creando quandam reginam partem tocius ecclesie que quadam proprietate dicitur ecclesia christiana. Tunc enim dux et legifer noster familiarius alloquens sponsam suam (ut dicit apostolus Hebr. 1°) per assumpcionem humanitatis inducit arma nostra et ut gigas superavit hostes ecclesie, redimens partem sponse incarceratam et docens aliam quomodo sequeretur eius vestigia. Unde tota doctrina christiana stat in ista oracione ecclesie qua rogamus sponsum per adventum eius in carnem, ut doceat nos terrena despicere et amare celestia. Despicere, id est, in affeccione postponere. Ille enim despicit temporalia qui a summa sede anime ipsa abicit et facit ipsa intencioni celestium abiectissime ministrare. Et bene foret sibi qui perfecte didicerit istam regulam religionis, tunc enim foret in recta via sequendi sponsum et assumptam iuvenculam in regnum celorum. Tercio vero in ultimo adventu faciet agnus nupcias consummatas, coronando et confirmando reginam integram que est una columba et universalis ecclesia, non pars alterius ecclesie (ut patet Apoc. XXI°). Et super ista consideracione dependet magna pars sensus scripture et servicii ecclesie. Nam ex fide erit una totalis ecclesia glorificata post diem iudicii, et illa secundum magnam partem est modo sponsa in celo, in purgatorio et in mundo. Ideo illa est continue una universalis et sancta mater ecclesia, et illa est quam [p. 125] debent leges intelligere et omnis christianus reverenter intendere; illa, inquam, figurata est in Salomone et templo, ut canit ecclesia. Frustra namque ordinaretur vel legeretur quod Salomon haberet tot reginas, tot concubinas et tot adolescentulas, nisi ut figurando Christum significet nobis suum coniugium cum spiritibus celestibus, tam angelis quam animabus, quam cum adolescentibus viantibus; ex quibus omnibus componitur una columba que est universalis ecclesia, eadem que coronabitur in patria, quia aliter foret continue alia et alia universalis ecclesia. Frustra eciam ordinaretur et legeretur construccio templi Salomonis (de quo III° Reg. VI° cap.) ad figurandum Christum et ecclesiam, que est templum suum (ut dicit apostolus Iª Cor. III° 17). Sicut enim fuerunt tres partes templi: prima in quam totum vulgus et alienigene intraverunt, quam sacerdotes in tempore Christi negociacionibus occuparunt (ut patet Matthei XXI°, 12). Et illa signat militantem ecclesiam in qua sunt eciam alienigene ut presciti. Secunda vero fuit corpus ecclesie populo Israhelitico deputata, signans perfecciores pure Israhelitas a rugis in purgatorio depurandos. Tercia vero fuit sancta sanctorum in quam soli sacerdotes intrarunt solempniter semel in anno. Et illa signat triumphantes, ut exponit apostolus Hebr. IX°, 11: Christus assistens pontifex etc. Sic, inquam, tota fabrica templi signat universalem ecclesiam iuxta fidem. Unde istum articulum fidei tenentur episcopi docere inferiores sacerdotes et populum, quia aliter ignorarent usum servicii ecclesie ad cuius noticiam obligantur, et specialiter illum articulum fidei ecclesiam sanctam catholicam quem omnis catholicus debet scire.

ENGLISH: Now to that, it seems, the answer must be, according to what has often been said, that Christ at the
first instant of the world was the [bridegroom]{g:sponsus} of the [church]{g:ecclesia} by
predestination; and at the confirmation of the angels he gave a dowry to part of his
[bride]{g:sponsa}, and so at the confirmation of [124] Abel the just and of the other saints up to the
incarnation, the same espousal remaining continuously, since Caesar ever Augustus continually
increases the dowry.[^1] And at the incarnation he held a second wedding, creating a certain part of
the whole church a queen, which by a certain propriety is called the christian church.[^2] For then
our leader and lawgiver, speaking more familiarly to his bride (as the apostle says, Hebrews 1),
through the assumption of humanity puts on our armour, and as a giant overcame the enemies of the
church, redeeming the imprisoned part of the bride and teaching the other how it should follow his
footsteps.[^3] Hence the whole christian [doctrine]{g:doctrina} stands in this prayer of the church,
in which we ask the bridegroom, by his coming into the flesh, to teach us to despise earthly things
and to love heavenly. To despise, that is, to put after in affection. For he despises [temporal
goods]{g:temporalia} who casts them away from the highest seat of the soul and makes them serve, most
abjectly, the intention of heavenly things.[^4] And it would be well with him who had perfectly
learned this rule of religion; for then he would be on the right way of following the bridegroom and
the [young girl]{g:iuvencula} he has taken to himself into the [kingdom]{g:regnum} of heaven.[^5] And
thirdly, at the last coming the Lamb will make the nuptials consummate, crowning and confirming the
queen entire, which is one dove and the universal church, not part of another church (as is clear,
Apocalypse 21).[^6] And on this consideration depends a great part of the sense of scripture and of
the church's service. For [as faith teaches]{g:ex fide} there will be one total church glorified
after the day of judgment, and that [church] is now, in great part, the bride in heaven, in purgatory
and in the world. Therefore she is continuously one universal and holy mother church, and she it is
whom [125] the laws ought to understand and every christian reverently to attend to; she, I say, was
figured in Solomon and the temple, as the church sings.[^7] For in vain would it be ordained or read
that Solomon had so many queens, so many concubines and so many [young maidens]{g:adolescentula},
unless it were that, by figuring Christ, he signifies to us his marriage with the heavenly spirits,
angels as well as souls, as well as with the young ones who are [on the road]{g:viantes}; of all of
whom is composed one dove, which is the universal church, the same that will be crowned in the
[homeland]{g:patria}; for otherwise there would continually be one universal church after
another.[^8] In vain, too, would the building of Solomon's temple (of which 3 Kings, chapter 6) be
ordained and read, [were it not] to figure Christ and the church, which is his temple (as the apostle
says, 1 Corinthians 3:17).[^9] For just as there were three parts of the temple: the first, into
which the whole common people and the foreigners entered, which the priests in Christ's time occupied
with their trafficking (as is clear, Matthew 21:12). And that signifies the [church
militant]{g:ecclesia militans}, in which there are foreigners too, as the [foreknown]{g:prescitus}.[^10]
And the second was the body of the church, assigned to the people of Israel, signifying the more
perfect, purely Israelites, who are to be cleansed of their wrinkles in purgatory. And the third was
the holy of holies, into which the priests alone entered solemnly once a year. And that signifies the
triumphant, as the apostle expounds, Hebrews 9:11: "Christ, being come an high priest" etc.[^11] So, I
say, the whole fabric of the temple signifies the universal church, according to faith. Hence the
[bishops]{g:episcopus} are bound to teach this [article of faith]{g:articulus fidei} to the lower
[priests]{g:sacerdos} and to the people, since otherwise they would [be ignorant of]{g:ignorare} the
use of the church's service, to the knowledge of which they are obliged; and especially that article
of faith, "the holy catholic church", which every catholic ought to know.[^12]

### 06-c ¶2 · 126.1–126.13  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 126] Et patet quod Christus semper est caput ecclesie. Unde ego credo quod, quicunque episcopus vere presidet in particulari ecclesia, remanet post mortem suam caput vel patronus eiusdem ecclesie. Quamvis enim moriatur in corpore, procurat tamen in spiritu pro subditis eciam fervencius quam in via, et post diem iudicii habebit quandam preeminenciam et illuminacionem, sicut in angelis longe alterius racionis quam prepositura viancium. Ideo rogamus, ut sanctos episcopos semper habeamus patronos in celis. **Sed** illi non contendebant circa primatus, adulterantes scripturam sacram, ut populus astringatur in via credere quod ipsi sunt capita sancte matris ecclesie.

ENGLISH: And it is clear that Christ is always [head]{g:caput} of the church. Hence I believe that whatever
bishop truly presides in a particular church remains after his death head or [patron]{g:patronus} of
that same church.[^13] For although he dies in the body, yet in spirit he acts for his subjects even
more fervently than on [the way]{g:via}, and after the day of judgment he will have a certain
pre-eminence and illumination, as among the angels, of a far other kind than the [office of
superior]{g:prepositura} of those on the road. Therefore we pray that we may always have holy bishops
as patrons in heaven. But they did not contend about primacies, adulterating holy scripture, so that
the people on the way should be bound to believe that they themselves are heads of holy mother
church.[^14]
