# *De potestate pape*, chapter 12, part 7: the law of christ suffices for the church without men's laws

## ¶1 · 338.9–339.25

> [p. 338] Ideo talis evangelizans predicaret vitam et doctrinam Christi, in qua non est periculum. Secundo exponeret quomodo sanctus, cui innititur non in lege quam Christus instituit, declinavit, tercio consideraret si modum ita laudabilem sicut fecerunt patroni, quibus innitimur, observamus, quia cum Deus remunerator est adverbiorum, modus est maxime attendendus; quarto vero consideraremus in vitis patrum, si patres nostri ordinis spissius vixerunt et sanccius, quando viventes exproprietarie et nobis contrarie secuti sunt facilius et similius salvatorem. Modicus enim error, qui est insensibilis ex pravitate peccati, est ex continuo incremento sensibile et intolerabile peccatum, a tota ecclesia destruendum, sicut specialiter patet in dotacione ecclesie, que a nuda elemosina crevit in venenosum dominium. Sic igitur duplex illud morale principium, practizatum in via morum, retrocedere est illicitum et de duobus bonis est pocius et facilius eligendum, rectificaret totam ecclesiam; ideo sine excusacione debemus omnes effectualiter illud attendere. Sed redeundo quantum ad secundam sentenciam de necessitate legum ecclesie, patet ex sepe dictis, quod melius regeretur ecclesia pura [p. 339] lege Domini quam per se vel cum adiectis legi domini legibus hominum adinventis. Patet ex hoc quod primo modo honori Dei conformius, ad serviendum sibi direccius et inducendum pacem ecclesie copiosius, regularetur ecclesia. Unde antichristiana heresis videtur quod lex Dei non est per se sufficiens nec eque sufficiens ut leges hominum extinguere iniurias in ecclesia germinantes, nam ad confundendum tales infidelitates de Antichristo dicitur Psalmo IX, 21: Constitue Domine legislatorem super eos, ut sciant gentes, quoniam homines sunt ad imaginem Dei, quibus creavit legem in reputacione sua completam condere; ideo debent secundum illam conversari ut homines et non ut bruta. Deus enim ex summa sapiencia legem condidit et dedit ecclesie ad hunc finem. Ideo si lex humana foret ad hoc lege Dei complecior, Antichristus in potencia, in sapiencia, et in providencia excederet Jhesum Christum; et tunc vera foret discipulorum eius sentencia, quod expedicius foret ecclesie quod legerentur in missis epistole decretales quam epistole apostolorum vel evangelium; nam secundum quod lex est necessarior et expedicior est magis venerabilis et plus meritorie commendanda.

Therefore such a preacher of the gospel would preach the life and doctrine of Christ, in which there is no danger. Second, he would expound how the saint on whom he relies, [where he relies on him] not in the law which Christ instituted, turned aside;[^1] third, he would consider whether we observe a manner as praiseworthy as the [patrons]{g:patronus} on whom we rely did, since, because God is a rewarder of adverbs, the manner is most of all to be attended to;[^2] and fourth we would consider in the lives of the fathers whether the fathers of our order lived more closely and more holily when, living [without property]{g:exproprietarie} and contrary to us, they followed the saviour more easily and more like him.[^3] For a small error, which is [imperceptible]{g:insensibilis} by the depravity of sin, becomes by continual increase a [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} and intolerable sin, to be destroyed by the whole [church]{g:ecclesia}, as is especially clear in the [endowment]{g:dotacio} of the church, which grew from bare [alms]{g:elemosina} into poisonous [lordship]{g:dominium}.[^4] So, therefore, that twofold moral principle, [put into practice]{g:practizare} in the way of conduct (to go backward is unlawful, and of two goods the better and the easier is to be chosen), would set right the whole church; therefore all of us, without excuse, must attend to it in effect. But, returning to the second [teaching]{g:sentencia}, on the necessity of the laws of the church, it is clear from what has often been said that the church would be better ruled by the pure [339] law of the Lord than by [the laws of the church] by themselves, or with laws devised by men added to the law of the Lord.[^5] This is clear from the fact that in the first way the church would be ruled more conformably to God's honour, more directly to serving him, and more abundantly to bringing in the peace of the church. Hence it seems an antichristian heresy that God's law is not sufficient by itself, nor as sufficient as men's laws, to extinguish the injuries sprouting in the church;[^6] for to confound such [unbeliefs]{g:infidelitas} it is said of Antichrist in Psalm 9:21: "Appoint, O Lord, a lawgiver over them: that the Gentiles may know themselves to be but men", men to the image of God, for whom he created [it] to establish a law complete in his reckoning; therefore they ought to live according to it as men and not as brute beasts.[^7] For God out of his highest wisdom established the law and gave it to the church for this end. Therefore if human law were more complete for this than God's law, Antichrist would exceed Jesus Christ in [power]{g:potencia}, in wisdom and in providence; and then the teaching of his disciples would be true, that it would be more expedient for the church that the decretal epistles were read at masses than the epistles of the apostles or the gospel; for the more necessary and more expedient a law is, the more it is to be venerated and the more meritoriously commended.

## ¶2 · 339.27–340.14

> [p. 339] Item, lex Christi plus pacificat cum Deo et plus disponit ad salutem anime, quam tradicio humana, sed in quantum lex hec fecerit, est honorabilior ac necessarior, igitur lex Christi excellit proporcionabiliter tradicionem humanam. Et cum pax quam habet homo cum Deo infinitum excedit pacem aliam, sicut salus anime et spiritualis prosperitas infinitum excedunt bona alia, que [p. 340] per leges hominum acquiruntur, patet quod lex Dei infinitum excedit leges hominum; et ex eodem sequitur quod sit infinitum prestancior ad placendum Deo et paciendum cum populo, nam per observanciam illius legis et non aliter placeretur, et per consequens pacificaretur homo cum Deo; sed nacta pace cum Deo, acquiritur pax cum qualibet creatura. Quomodo rogo, non obediret et proficeret quelibet creatura homini, cui videret Deum suum amicabiliter obedire, igitur etsi membra dyaboli tribulant amicos Christi, tamen prosunt illis, ut obedienciarii et amici; sic enim Scarioth fuit amicus Christo, Joh. XVIII, et procuravit licet invite serviens pacem Christi.

Again, the law of Christ does more to bring peace with God, and disposes more to the salvation of the soul, than human tradition; now, in so far as a law does this, it is more honourable and more necessary; therefore the law of Christ excels human tradition in proportion. And since the peace which a man has with God infinitely exceeds any other peace, just as the salvation of the soul and spiritual prosperity infinitely exceed the other goods which [340] are acquired through men's laws, it is clear that God's law infinitely exceeds men's laws; and from the same [premiss] [it follows]{g:sequitur} that it is infinitely more excellent for pleasing God and for suffering with the people;[^8] for by the observance of that law, and not otherwise, would [God] be pleased, and consequently man would be at peace with God; now, once peace with God has been gained, peace is acquired with every creature. How, I ask, would every creature not obey and profit a man whom it saw its God obeying in friendship?[^9] Therefore, even if the members of the devil trouble the friends of Christ, yet they profit them, as [ones who obey]{g:obedienciarius} and as friends; for so Iscariot was a friend to Christ (John 18), and, though serving unwillingly, procured the peace of Christ.[^10]

## ¶3 · 340.15–340.26

> [p. 340] Item, lex Christi per se gigneret copiosius caritatem in populo quam leges hominum per se vel commixte legi Domini. Et patet conclusio corollaria. Assumptum patet ex hoc, quod lex Domini precipit quoad bona nature prebere percucienti maxillam sinistram Matth. V 39, 40, quoad bona fortune volenti auferre tunicam dare et pallium, ymo volenti tecum in iudicio contendere et tunicam tollere dare debitum; unde Luce VI, 30 dicitur: Qui aufert que tua sunt ne repetas. Et ista triplex paciencia per se induceret plenam pacem, extinguendo discordias.

Again, the law of Christ by itself would breed [charity]{g:caritas} in the people more abundantly than men's laws by themselves or mingled with the law of the Lord. And the [conclusion]{g:conclusio}, a corollary, is clear. [The assumption]{g:assumptum} is clear from this, that the law of the Lord commands, as to the goods of nature, "to offer to the one who strikes the left cheek" (Matthew 5:39–40); as to the goods of fortune, to one "who wishes to take away the coat, to give the cloak as well"; indeed, to one "who wishes to contend with you in judgment and take away your coat", to give what is owed;[^11] hence it is said in Luke 6:30: "Of him that taketh away thy goods, ask them not again." And this threefold patience would by itself bring in full peace, by extinguishing discords.

## ¶4 · 340.27–340.35

> [p. 340] Nec valet infidelis obiectus, quo dicitur quod tunc extingueretur complementum iusticie, quia Deus foret necessitatus ad providendum sue ecclesie ex hinc ad melius, ymo sic accederet ad statum exproprietarium plus perfectum. Ideo idem foret sic dicere et blasfeme imponere Deo inpotenciam, negligenciam sive invidiam, et per consequens quod legifer nonnullus extollitur super omne, quod dicitur Deus.

Nor does the [unbelieving]{g:infidelis} objection hold, by which it is said that then the fulfilment of justice would be extinguished; for God would be bound to provide for his church from then on for the better; indeed, so it would draw near to the more perfect propertyless [state]{g:status}.[^12] Therefore to say this would be the same as blasphemously to impute to God impotence, negligence or envy, and consequently [to say] that some lawgiver "is lifted up above all that is called God".[^13]

## ¶5 · 341.1–342.7

> [p. 341] Unde consequenter profundantur in heresim, qui dicunt quod status dotacionis sit perfeccior quam status expropriacionis, et per consequens lex papalis qua stabilitur dotacio est perfeccior quam lex Christi, qua suadetur expropriacio; nam ut inquiunt iste due leges et isti duo status convertibiliter se sequuntur, quia aliter omnino superflueret lex humana et venenose pasceret populum postposito verbo vite. Oportet enim populum illum regi magis intendere, cuius legem preponderat; quia lex et status quem stabilit, se invicem consequuntur. Unde videtur michi nota infidelitatis et dolosa calliditas Antichristi, quod domini temporales statuunt sibi tales pastores et prophetas, qui et artem pascendi ignorent et preparent vel inordinate vel toxice cibum mentis. Constat autem ex fide scripture quod anima pascitur verbo Dei: verba autem alia sunt venenum anime; cum autem non sic ordinant pro coco et cibo corporis, et secundum Crysostomum prelati debent esse stomachus corporis ecclesie et cocus membrorum eius, patet quanta infidelitate laborant mundi principes, qui plus appreciantur atque preponderant cocum corporis coco mentis. Illi autem, quorum Deus venter est, non sunt filii pacis, cum pax prima cum Deo dissolvitur. Et in isto notabiliter deficit doctor fidei christiane. Oportet enim primo rectificare in ecclesia apostolos et prophetas et consequenter residuum corporis ecclesie erit sanum. Illi autem stabilirent hoc necessarium principium fidei modo discreditum propter excecacionem pulveris temporalium, quod quilibet christianus [p. 342] debet infinitum plus amare virtutes quam honorem, voluptatem vel commodum temporale, sicut debet infinitum plus odire peccatum eciam veniale quam penam aliquam corporalem, et idem principium fidei per hoc quod, ut aliquid est peius vel melius, est ipsum fugiendum proporcionabiliter vel amandum.

Hence those sink, as a consequence, deeper into heresy who say that the state of endowment is more perfect than the state of [renunciation of property]{g:expropriacio}, and consequently that the papal law by which endowment is established is more perfect than the law of Christ, by which renunciation of property is urged; for, as they say, these two laws and these two states follow one another convertibly, since otherwise human law would be altogether superfluous and would feed the people poisonously, the word of life being set aside. For a people must attend more to that king whose law it weighs more heavily; for a law and the state that it establishes follow upon each other.[^14] Hence it seems to me a mark of unbelief and a deceitful cunning of Antichrist that the [temporal lords]{g:temporales-domini} set up for themselves such shepherds and prophets as both [are ignorant of]{g:ignorare} the art of feeding and prepare the food of the mind either without order or poisonously. Now it is evident [from the faith of scripture]{g:ex-fide} that the soul is fed by the word of God, and other words are poison to the soul; and since they do not ordain so for the cook and the food of the body, and according to Chrysostom "[prelates]{g:prelatus} ought to be the stomach of the body of the church and the cook of its members", it is clear with how great unbelief the princes of the world labour, who value more, and weigh more heavily, the cook of the body than the cook of the mind.[^15] And those "whose God is their belly" are not "sons of peace", since the first peace, with God, is dissolved.[^16] And in this the teacher of the Christian faith notably falls short.[^17] For one must first set right the apostles and prophets in the church, and as a consequence the rest of the body of the church will be sound. And they would establish this necessary principle of faith, now discredited because of the blinding by the dust of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}: that every Christian [342] ought to love the virtues infinitely more than honour, pleasure or temporal advantage, just as he ought to hate sin, even venial sin, infinitely more than any bodily penalty; and the same principle of faith by this, that as a thing is worse or better, so it is to be fled or loved in proportion.[^18]

## ¶6 · 342.8–342.18

> [p. 342] Ex quibus sequitur quod lex Christi eciam secundum consilia sit generaliter servanda et lex hominum postponenda. Exhinc enim accrescit bonum anime infinitum preciosius quam lucrum aliquod temporale. Et hec racio moveret eciam infidelem, nam melius, facilius et brevius foret observare Christi consilia et precepta, igitur non debemus inniti suis contrariis prestancius omittendo. Peccamus igitur graviter, dum facimus nobis inexpediens, et honorem Dei diminuens, utilitatem ecclesie subtrahendo.

From which it follows that the law of Christ is to be kept generally, even as to the counsels, and the law of men put after it. For from this there accrues a good of the soul infinitely more precious than any temporal gain. And this reason would move even an unbeliever: for it would be better, easier and shorter to observe Christ's counsels and precepts; therefore we ought not to rely on their contraries, omitting what is more excellent. We sin gravely, therefore, when we do what is inexpedient for us and diminishes the honour of God, by withdrawing the benefit of the church.[^19]

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Cui innititur non in lege quam Christus instituit*: the *non in lege* is taken with the relative ("relies on him, not in Christ's law"); it could instead go with *declinavit*, "turned aside, not in the law Christ instituted". Either way the preacher is to show where the saint departed from Christ's law. The margin of both manuscripts heads the passage *Predicacionis modus de sanctis* (338.9). The apparatus at 338.12 records *hoc* for *lege* in a manuscript.
[^2]: **S.** The maxim that God rewards adverbs (the manner of the deed), not nouns, comes from the *Glossa ordinaria* to the Decretals on X 5.22.1 (*Deus non est remunerator nominum sed adverbiorum*), which Wyclif elsewhere cites as the "glossa Bernhardi" (*De civili dominio* II.12, ed. Loserth p. 139); Wyclif here gives only the first half. **G.** *Patroni*: the saints taken as patrons, as in 12-f.
[^3]: **C.** *Spissius*, literally "more densely", taken as "more closely" (with less slackening of the rule). The speaker shifts to the first person plural (*consideraremus*, *nostri ordinis*): the preacher is imagined as a member of a religious order.
[^4]: **C.** *Insensibilis ex pravitate peccati*: "imperceptible by the depravity of sin", i.e. too small to be perceived as sin; Wyclif sets *insensibilis* against *sensibile* in the same sentence.
[^5]: **C.** *Quam per se vel cum adiectis … legibus hominum adinventis*: the subject of *per se* is unexpressed; we take it as the laws of the church just named. **G.** *Sentencia*: the second position under discussion in the chapter, "teaching", not a judicial sentence.
[^6]: **T.** At 339.11 the manuscripts read *sed ut* (the apparatus gives the reading without its lemma in the OCR); the print's *nec eque sufficiens ut* is translated.
[^7]: **S.** Psalm 9:21 (Vulgate numbering), quoted as in the Vulgate. Wyclif's gloss runs on from *homines sunt*, which Douay renders "to be but men". **C.** *Quibus creavit legem … condere*: the infinitive has no clear subject; we take it as God's creating men so that a law complete in his reckoning should be established for them. The construction is doubtful.
[^8]: **T.** *Paciendum cum populo*, "suffering with the people", is the print; the sense of the argument (peace with God and with creatures) suggests *pacificandum* or a like word, "making peace with the people". We translate the print.
[^9]: **C.** *Cui videret Deum suum amicabiliter obedire*: *obedire* takes the dative, so *cui* is the one obeyed and *Deum* the subject: God himself obeys the man (cf. Joshua 10:14). *Suum* may refer to the creature or to the man.
[^10]: **S.** John 18 (the betrayal); Christ calls Judas "Friend" at Matthew 26:50.
[^11]: **S.** Matthew 5:39–40, paraphrased: the Vulgate has the right cheek (*dexteram*) and "turn to him also the other"; *dare et pallium* answers to *dimitte ei et pallium*; the order of coat and cloak follows Luke 6:29. **C.** *Dare debitum*, "to give what is owed": the third clause, against Matthew's "let go thy cloak also", is taken as yielding the coat as though it were owed. B reads *tunicam contendere* at 340.23.
[^12]: **C.** The objection's ground is answered in the *quia* clause: if men's laws were extinguished, God would not leave justice wanting but would provide better for the church. *Ex hinc*, "from then on". The propertyless state is that of the church without endowment.
[^13]: **S.** 2 Thessalonians 2:4; Wyclif has *super* for the Vulgate's *supra*.
[^14]: **T.** At 341.10 B reads *legi*, and at 341.11 *preponderant*. **C.** *Populum illum regi magis intendere*: *regi* is taken as the dative of *rex*, "attend to that king"; it could also be the passive infinitive, "that people must be ruled", which leaves *magis intendere* without a construction.
[^15]: **S.** Ps.-Chrysostom, *Opus imperfectum in Matthaeum*, hom. 38 (on Matthew 21:12; PG 56.839–840), cited as Chrysostom: priests are the stomach of the church, which takes in the food, "cooks it in itself" and distributes it through the whole body; Wyclif paraphrases. No reference in Loserth. Both manuscripts note in the margin (341.20) that those who prefer the cook of the body to the cook of the mind sin gravely.
[^16]: **S.** Philippians 3:19; Luke 10:6 ("the son of peace").
[^17]: **C.** *Doctor fidei christiane* is not identified; it may be the prelate as teacher of the faith in general. B reads *defecit* (341.27).
[^18]: **C.** *Illi autem stabilirent*: *illi* is taken as the apostles and prophets once set right. *Et idem principium fidei per hoc quod …* has no verb of its own; we take *stabilirent* as understood: they would establish the same principle by the rule of proportion. Both manuscripts mark the two principles in the margin (342.1, 342.3).
[^19]: **C.** *Facimus nobis inexpediens, et honorem Dei diminuens*: the participle is taken in parallel with the adjective, "what is inexpedient … and diminishes"; *utilitatem ecclesie subtrahendo* gives the means.
