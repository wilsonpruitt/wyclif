## Doubtful, left as they are

- **11-a, p. 227–8**: *cum illius sit interpretari leges … cuius est condere …, rex autem Anglie debet concedere et condere leges tales …: ideo …*, rendered "and [since] the king of England must grant". This is the minor of the syllogism, so (a) "now". But it sits inside the *cum* clause, and the English carries it as the second "since" premiss before "therefore". Putting "now" there would break the sentence. Left, and flagged for a recast if the sentence is ever reworked.
- **11-c, p. 240**: *si quilibet criminosus sit indignus …, tunc lex illud exprimeret, sed facit oppositum*, rendered "but it does the opposite". This is the minor of a modus tollens with the conclusion left unstated, so (a) "now" is possible. *facit oppositum* is also a plain contrast, so "but" (b) is defensible.
- **11-d, p. 243**: *a suis creditoribus et specialiter a mundi divitibus … dimittantur liberi …; egenis autem creditoribus satisfaciant*, rendered "and to needy creditors". It is either (c), the next provision, or (b), rich creditors against needy ones ("but to needy creditors"). Left.
- **11-d, p. 244**: *Si autem placeret regi … gauderem*, rendered "If, however,". This is adversative (b). "however" carries the contrast, though the card rendering would be "But if". Left as a construction that carries it.
- **11-e, p. 248**: *Talia autem privilegia instrumentaliter et ministratorie concedi possunt a domino temporali; sed est dare spiritualia privilegia pociora*, rendered "Now such privileges". It is either (a), the step that answers the objection (temporal lords can grant these), or (c), a remark answered by the *sed* that follows ("And such privileges"; cf. dialogus 05). "Now" is defensible.
- **11-e, p. 250**: *sancti plus contentantur de tribulacione …, dampnati autem habent quoddam bonum*, rendered "and the damned have". It is either (c), or (b), saints against damned ("but the damned"). Left.
- **12-f, p. 272**: *eo quod dicta ecclesia fuit miraculose consecrata, sed nec templum nec altare … fuit taliter consecratum*, rendered "whereas neither the temple". This is adversative (b). "whereas" carries the contrast. Left, as at 08-c in extract 03.
- **13-e, p. 290**: *instantaneum seculare desiderium inducit dampnacionem perpetuam, brevis autem mundi contemptus preparat …*, rendered "while a brief contempt". This is adversative (b), and "while" carries the contrast. Left.
- **13-e, p. 291**: *sic autem est in corpore ecclesie cuius virtus regitiva est patris sapiencia*, rendered "and so it is in the body of the church". It is either (a), the application of the analogy (the step that brings it to bear), or (c). "and" is defensible.
- **13-e, p. 292**: *Tota autem contrariacio stat in hoc quod bona temporalia plus bonis perpetuis sunt amata. Ideo ve illis …*, rendered "And the whole contrariety". It is either (a), the premiss behind *Ideo*, or (c), a remark extending the last sentence. Left.
