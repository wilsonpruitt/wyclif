# *De ecclesia*, chapter 13, part 2: no heir is bound to continue his forefathers' alms, and the pope may claim no tribute

## ¶1 · 278.33–279.27

> [p. 278] Religiosior ergo foret concessio expressa condicione quod persone sint habiles et quod Deo sit placitum; [p. 279] quam condicionem licet oportet intelligere, tamen illa expressa foret contencionum plurium sedativa et disposicionis plus vigilis causativa et sic racione humilitatis foret magis autentica et racione declinacionis a ritu gentili ad religionem Christi foret plus meritoria et racione pluris adiutorii plus secura. Et ita videtur presumpcio quod quis obliget heredes suos simpliciter ad continuacionem talis elemosine, cum non subiacet potestati sue dignificare quoscunque tales elemosinarios, nec potest obligare se ipsum vel alium ad premiandum indignos, cum heres plus obligatur veritati a qua per se capit hereditatem, quam patri carnali a quo capit solum per accidens. Et hec est racio quare catholice dicitur regem Anglie semper esse infra etatem, cum debet corrigere errores tam sui quam progenitorum suorum, sive ex ignorancia sive ex inadvertencia deceptus fuerit; aliter enim attemptaret defendere iniusticias contra Deum. Ideo absit nos inponere regi et regno tantam demenciam ut inveteratus dierum malorum defenderet antiquum errorem tractum in consuetudinem, cum secundum decretum, allegatum XI° cap°, error cui non resistitur approbatur. Et patet consideranti attencius quomodo omnis elemosina humana vocata perpetua est realiter ex lege Dei invincibili temporalis, cum oportet omnino condicionem quam Deus apponit subintelligi, quamdiu ditarius manserit dignus participare elemosina quoad Deum.

Therefore the [grant]{g:concessio} would be more religious with the condition expressed, that the persons be qualified and that it be pleasing to God; [279] and although this condition must be understood, yet, expressed, it would allay many contentions and cause a more watchful disposition; and so by reason of humility it would be more [authentic]{g:autenticus}, by reason of its turning aside from the gentile rite to the religion of Christ it would be more meritorious, and by reason of greater help it would be more secure.[^1] And so it seems presumption that anyone should bind his heirs [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} to the continuation of such [alms]{g:elemosina}, since it does not lie in his power to make worthy any such [almsmen]{g:elemosinarius} whatever, nor can he bind himself or another to reward the unworthy; since the heir is more bound to the truth, from which he takes his inheritance of itself, than to his fleshly father, from whom he takes it only accidentally.[^2] And this is the reason why it is said catholically that the king of England is always under age: since he ought to correct the errors both of himself and of his forefathers, whether he was deceived from ignorance or from inadvertence; for otherwise he would attempt to defend injustices against God. Therefore God forbid that we should impute to the king and the [kingdom]{g:regnum} such madness that, "grown old in evil days", he should defend an ancient error drawn into [custom]{g:consuetudo}, since according to the decree alleged in the eleventh chapter, "an error that is not resisted is approved".[^3] And it is clear to one who considers more attentively how every [so-called]{g:vocatus} perpetual human alms is in reality, by the invincible law of God, [temporary]{g:elemosina temporalis}, since the condition which God attaches must by all means be [understood as implied]{g:subintelligere}: as long as the one enriched remains worthy to share in the alms as regards God.[^4]

## ¶2 · 279.28–280.10

> [p. 279] Unde talis attencio et expressio hominis elemosinantis ex maiori humiliacione, ex maiori Deo confirmacione, et maiori errorum exclusione magis disponeret ad perpetuacionem elemosine per Christum quam elemosina humana irreligiose vocata perpetua. [p. 280] Nam perpetuare elemosinas est Christo proprium, eo quod est dominus, simpliciter assistens omni tempori, potens iustificare elemosinarium, a qua iustificacione (iuxta dicta proximo capitulo) dependet humana donacio; et sic est de successione hereditaria de qua minus videtur. Deo enim est proprium heredes in perpetuitate successionis hereditarie limitare. Unde creditur quod multi fundantes elemosinas nominetenus perpetuas in penam sue superbie a perpetuitate generis hereditarie defecerunt.

Hence such attention and expression on the part of the man who is [almsgiver]{g:elemosinans}, from greater humbling, from greater confirmation to God, and from greater exclusion of errors, would dispose more toward the perpetuation of the alms through Christ than a human alms irreligiously called perpetual.[^5] [280] For to perpetuate alms belongs properly to Christ, in that he is [lord]{g:dominus}, present without qualification to every time, able to justify the almsman, on which justification (according to what was said in the last chapter) human giving depends; and so it is with hereditary succession, in which this is less apparent. For it belongs properly to God to [assign]{g:limitare} heirs in the perpetuity of hereditary succession. Hence it is believed that many who founded alms perpetual in name have, in punishment of their pride, failed of the perpetuity of their hereditary line.[^6]

## ¶3 · 280.11–280.16

> [p. 280] In talibus ergo elemosinis unus princeps concedit pro suo tempore subintellecta supradicta condicione et imprecatur suum heredem sic facere, et ex meritoriis donis multorum sibi succedencium cum graciosa disposicione materie facit Deus unam elemosinam perpetuam, si sibi sit placitum.

In such alms, therefore, one prince grants for his own time, the condition stated above being understood as implied, and prays that his heir may do likewise; and from the meritorious gifts of many who succeed him, with a gracious disposing of the matter, God makes one perpetual alms, if it pleases him.[^7]

## ¶4 · 280.17–280.33

> [p. 280] Unde hic instatur sophistice quod temporalis dominus meritorie facit elemosinam perpetuam, cum meritorie faciat partem eius. Sed iste obiectus non vadit contra sensum expositum, ymmo si procedit de faccione elemosine perpetue secundum partem, eque probaret quod una vidua paupercula plus faceret in casu elemosinam talem perpetuam, posito quod plus mereatur ut Deus elemosinam utrimque perficiat; nam 1° Corinthiorum III°, 6, scribitur: Ego plantavi, Apollo rigavit, sed Deus incrementum dedit. Unde patet consideranti subtiliter quod nullus heres procedens post progenitorem dotantem ecclesiam tenetur approbare fundatam elemosinam nisi ex libera voluntate. Patet primo sic: Non tenetur facere aliquod opus meritorium nisi ex libera voluntate, non tenetur sic approbare nisi secundum racionem operis meritorii, ergo non tenetur sic approbare nisi ex libera voluntate.

Hence here [it is objected]{g:instatur} sophistically that a [temporal lord]{g:temporales domini} meritoriously makes a perpetual alms, since he meritoriously makes a part of it. But that [objection]{g:objectum} does not go against the sense set out; indeed, if it proceeds from the making of a perpetual alms in respect of a part, it would equally prove that one poor little widow would in some case do more to make such an alms perpetual, supposing that she merits more that God should complete the alms on both sides; for in 1 Corinthians 3:6 it is written: "I have planted, Apollo watered: but God gave the increase."[^8] Hence it is clear to one who considers subtly that no heir coming after a forefather who [endowed]{g:dotacio} the [church]{g:ecclesia} is bound to approve the founded alms except of free will. It is clear, first, thus: he is not bound to do any meritorious work except of free will; he is not bound so to approve except in the character of a meritorious work; therefore he is not bound so to approve except of free will.[^9]

## ¶5 · 281.1–281.28

> [p. 281] Et hoc est notum intelligentibus textum Apostoli Philipp. IIIo, 3, et Gal. VI°, 14°, dicentis: Michi autem absit gloriari nisi in cruce domini Jesu Christi. Item, ex precepto Dei et eius successione hereditaria filii adoptivi non tenetur heres continuare aliquod bonum opus nisi ex libera voluntate; sed Deus, pater heredis plus obligat quam pater suus carnalis, ergo multo magis ex obligacione patris carnalis non tenetur sic facere nisi ex libera voluntate. Deus enim posuit hominem in tanta libertate, quod non potest sibi quicquam absolute precipere nisi meritorium, et per consequens adiecta condicione, ut libere illud perficiat. Unde Augustinus super Psalmum CXLVIIIᵘᵐ: In tuo, inquit, arbitrio Deus te esse voluit, cui pares locum, Deo aut diabolo, cum paraveris, qui possidebit ipse imperabit. Sed absit ut aliquis reportet me quod dem occasionem dominis temporalibus ad spoliandum sanctam ecclesiam vel ad continuandum elemosinas quas progenitores eorum inceperant, cum ex conclusione sequatur formaliter quod tenentur elemosinas tales perficere. Scit enim logicus quod si non sic elemosinare nisi libere tunc tenentur, quis queso urgeret regem Anglie coaccione civili continuare elemosinas patris sui? Non rex preteritus, tum quia par in parem non habet imperium, tum eciam quia ipsis in regno incompossibilibus est plenum ius ad regem posterum derivatum, nec imperatori pertinet, cum regnum nostrum non sit imperio sic subiugatum.

[281] And this is known to those who understand the text of the Apostle, Philippians 3:3 and Galatians 6:14, where he says: "But God forbid that I should glory, save in the cross of the Lord Jesus Christ."[^10] Again, by the precept of God and by the heir's hereditary succession to him as an adopted son, the heir is not bound to continue any good work except of free will; now God, the heir's father, binds more than his fleshly father; therefore much more, by the obligation of his fleshly father, he is not bound so to act except of free will. For God has placed man in such liberty that he cannot command him anything absolutely except what is meritorious, and consequently with the condition added, that he perform it freely. Hence Augustine on Psalm 148: "God willed," he says, "that it should lie in your choice for whom you prepare the place, for God or for the devil; when you have prepared it, he who possesses it will rule."[^11] But God forbid that anyone should report me as giving occasion to temporal lords to despoil holy church, or to discontinue the alms which their forefathers began, since it [follows]{g:sequitur} formally from the [conclusion]{g:conclusio} that they are bound to complete such alms.[^12] For the logician knows that if they are not bound so [to give alms]{g:elemosinare} except freely, then they are bound [so to give them]. Who, I ask, would compel the king of England by civil coercion to continue the alms of his father? Not the past king, both because "an equal has no command over an equal", and also because, the two being incompossible in the kingdom, the full [right]{g:ius} has passed to the later king; nor does it belong to the emperor, since our kingdom is not so subjected to the empire.[^13]

## ¶6 · 281.29–282.34

> [p. 281] Ex quibus sequitur quod non pertinet ad papam propter obligacionem regis et regni antiquis temporibus [p. 282] pensionem vel elemosinam annualem ab istis requirere, nam in lege Christi talis perpetuus redditus non habetur nec ex lege imperiali post dotacionem factam a cesare, quia integrum imperium non habet ius, unde liceat sibi exaccionem huiusmodi vendicare. Requireretur ergo ut papa in hoc humilis elemosinarius regis petat ratificacionem talis elemosine, cum omnia que habet in Anglia tenet de rege, tum quia dominium regni nostri non est subiectum imperio, et per consequens non parti imperii decise ad Romanum pontificem, tum eciam quia sic regnum nostrum foret per mortificacionem temporalium nimium diminutum et per consequens virtute brevis regii; ad quod dampnum rex statim revocaret quod tam improvide est concessum. Rex vero ex vocali promissione, ex scripti personali obligacione, vel ex paternali affeccione potest esse obligacior ad progenitorum suorum elemosinas gratis continuandum, sed potissime racione obligacionis qua Christo tenetur sub obtentu beatitudinis impertiri opera misericordie membris suis (ut patet Matthei XXV°); et cum maius opus misericordie sit fraterna coreccio, patet quod heredes sunt secundum leges Dei et hominum ad ipsam amplius obligati. Unde (ut dixi superius) plus faceret in casu ad continuacionem elemosine progenitorum, auferendo ipsam a discolis et convertendo eam in alios pios usus quam quantumlibet multiplicando collacionem temporalium minus provide. Et ex talibus operibus misericordie sibi succedentibus eciam diversis in genere fit unum opus misericordie successivum, cum tota ecclesia a principio mundi usque ad novissimum electum habet unum opus misericordie secundum quod ecclesia plene complacet sponso suo.

From which it follows that it does not belong to the [pope]{g:papa}, on account of an obligation of the king and the kingdom in ancient times, [282] to require from them a pension or annual alms; for in the law of Christ such a perpetual [revenue]{g:reditus} is not found, nor by imperial law after the endowment made by Caesar, because the whole empire has no right whereby it might be lawful for him to claim an exaction of this kind.[^14] It would be required, then, that the pope, in this the king's humble almsman, should ask for the ratification of such an alms, since all that he has in England he [holds of]{g:tenere de} the king: both because the [lordship]{g:dominium} of our kingdom is not subject to the empire, and consequently not to a part of the empire cut off for the Roman pontiff; and also because our kingdom would in this way be too much diminished by the [amortization]{g:mortificacio} of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, and consequently, by virtue of the king's writ *ad quod damnum*, the king would at once revoke what has been so improvidently granted.[^15] But the king, by a spoken promise, by the personal obligation of a writing, or by fatherly affection, can be more bound to continue freely the alms of his forefathers; but most of all by reason of the obligation by which he is bound to Christ, as the condition of obtaining blessedness, to bestow works of mercy on his [members]{g:membrum} (as is clear from Matthew 25); and since the greater work of mercy is fraternal correction, it is clear that heirs are, according to the laws of God and of men, more bound to it.[^16] Hence (as I said above) he would in some case do more toward the continuation of his forefathers' alms by taking it from the [froward]{g:discoli} and turning it to other pious uses than by multiplying, however much, the conferring of temporal goods less providently. And from such works of mercy succeeding one another, even though diverse in kind, there comes to be one [successive]{g:successivum} work of mercy, since the whole church, from the beginning of the world to the last elect, has one work of mercy, according to which the church fully pleases her [bridegroom]{g:sponsa}.[^17]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 279.1: Such conditions must be always understood, but would be better expressed.
- 279.28: Temporary alms would probably endure longer than those called perpetual.
- 280.11: The giver's example would be followed and the succession of gifts kept up.
- 280.27: An heir is not bound to approve of his ancestor's gifts.
- 281.9: God ordains all good works to be done of free will.
- 281.16: (I must not be supposed to mean that lords should cease their payments to the Church.)
- 282.1: The Pope has no power to enforce payments from the King,
- 282.16: but the King is bound to continue his ancestor's alms,
- 282.26: though he may vary their application.

## Notes

[^1]: **C/T.** Side-note 279.1; A in the margin *Conclusio* (279.6). *Religiosior*: more religious, more fitting to the religion of Christ, set against the *ritus gentilis*, the pagan rite of giving without condition. *Habiles*: qualified. *Sedativa … causativa*: allaying, causing. *Autentica*: carrying Christ's approval (13-a, n. 1).

[^2]: **C.** *Dignificare*: make worthy. *Per se … per accidens*: the heir has his inheritance of itself from truth (God's grant), from his father only accidentally; so his first obligation runs to truth.

[^3]: **R/S.** A and A₁ read *Et hec racio* (279.13), A in the margin *Conclusio*. *Regem Anglie semper esse infra etatem*: a maxim of English law (source to be checked, LEDGER E13b-2). *Inveteratus dierum malorum*: Daniel's words to the elder, Daniel 13:52 ("O thou that art grown old in evil days", DR). The decree is Gratian, D.83 c.3, per Loserth (*Dec. I pars, dist. LXXXIII, c. III*; the modern form is ours), who refers back to p. 235 (cap. 11); its attribution in Gratian is not vouched for here (LEDGER E13b-1).

[^4]: **G/C.** *Temporalis*: temporary, the GLOSSARY's *elemosina temporalis*, against *perpetua*. *Ditarius*: the one enriched by the alms (cf. *ditare*, GLOSSARY §B), the almsman. *Quoad Deum*: in God's sight.

[^5]: **C/T.** Side-note 279.28; B reads *elemosinant* (279.28–29). *Ex maiori Deo confirmacione*: we read a greater confirming [of the alms] to God, its being made fast in God's will; "greater conformity to God" would be *conformacione*, which the print does not have. *Per Christum* goes with *perpetuacionem*: Christ alone perpetuates.

[^6]: **C.** A in the margin *Conclusio* (280.1, 280.7). *Simpliciter assistens omni tempori*: present without qualification to every time, as lord of time (13-a, 277.24). *Iuxta dicta proximo capitulo*: cap. 12 (12-b, 256–57), on the grant that depends on the grantee's worthiness (LEDGER E13b-7). *De qua minus videtur*: hereditary succession depends on God in the same way, though it is less apparent. *Limitare*: assign (GLOSSARY §D).

[^7]: **T/C.** Side-note 280.11. B reads *interpretatur* for *imprecatur* (280.13), and *graciose* (280.14). *Imprecatur*: prays for, invokes. *Materie*: the matter of the alms, the succession of gifts out of which God makes one perpetual alms.

[^8]: **C/T.** A and B in the margin *Instancia* (280.17). *Obiectus*: the objection (GLOSSARY §C, *objectum*). B reads *ex faccione* (280.21). The reply turns the objection: if contributing a part made one a maker of the perpetual alms, a poor widow (cf. Luke 21:2–3) who merited more would contribute more. *Utrimque*: on both sides, of the lord's gift and of the widow's merit; "in either case" is possible. 1 Corinthians 3:6 is the Douay–Rheims.

[^9]: **C/T.** Side-note 280.27; A in the margin *Conclusio* (280.27). B lacks *nisi … meritorii* (280.31). The minor premiss stands without a connective in the Latin. *Secundum racionem operis meritorii*: in the character of a meritorious work.

[^10]: **S/T.** Loserth: after *Philipp.* the manuscripts leave a gap, and he supplied *III, 3* (281.1). Philippians 3:3 ("glory in Christ Jesus", DR); Galatians 6:14 is the Douay–Rheims, but Wyclif lacks *nostri* ("our Lord"). *Hoc* points back to the proof just given (280.29–33).

[^11]: **S/C.** Side-note 281.9. *Eius successione hereditaria filii adoptivi*: the heir succeeds to God as an adopted son (cf. Romans 8:15–17); the construal of *eius* is ours. The argument runs from the greater obligation (God's) to the lesser (the fleshly father's). The Augustine is *Enarrationes in Psalmos* 148.2 (PL 37, 1938–39; Loserth: *Opp.* IV.ii, p. 1673), quoted freely: Augustine has *In tuo autem arbitrio Deus esse voluit … Deo, an diabolo* (LEDGER E13b-3). *Arbitrio*: choice.

[^12]: **T/C.** Side-note 281.16; A in the margin *Nota solucionem obiectus*. The print has *ad continuandum*; Loserth queries *discontinuandum (?)* (281.18). We translate "discontinue", which the argument needs: Wyclif is disclaiming any encouragement to lords to withdraw their alms; "or to continue the alms" would say the opposite. *Formaliter*: by the form of the inference. *Perficere*: complete.

[^13]: **T/C/R.** A and A₁ read *quod si non tenentur* (281.21); we translate with their *tenentur*, which the print's sentence needs, and supply the bracket. B reads *urget* (281.22–23); A in the margin *Non tenetur filius continuare dotacionem elemosine patris sui*. *Par in parem non habet imperium*: the canonists' maxim, drawn from Innocent III's *quum non habeat imperium par in parem* (X 1.6.20, *Innotuit*; again Clem. 5.11.1) (LEDGER E13b-4). *Incompossibilibus*: the past and the present king cannot reign together. B reads *non sit imperatori* (281.28).

[^14]: **R/C.** Side-note 282.1; A in the margin *Conclusio* (281.29). *Obligacionem regis et regni antiquis temporibus*: presumably the tribute King John promised the pope in 1213 (to be checked, LEDGER E13b-5). *Dotacionem factam a cesare*: Constantine's endowment of the church. *Sibi*: the pope; the empire itself, whole, has no such right to hand on.

[^15]: **C/T.** B reads *artificacionem* (282.7); A in the margin *Conclusio*. *Parti imperii decise ad Romanum pontificem*: the part of the empire cut off for the pope by Constantine's gift. A and A₁ read *dirimitum* (282.12–13); *regii* is Loserth's correction (A A₁ *regie*, B *regei*, 282.13). We construe *virtute brevis regii ad quod dampnum* across the scaffold's semicolon as the royal writ *ad quod damnum*, the inquiry into the damage of a grant in mortmain (to be checked, LEDGER E13b-6); taken apart, *ad quod dampnum* would be "at which loss".

[^16]: **S/C.** Side-note 282.16; A in the margin *Conclusio* (282.16). *Vocali promissione … scripti personali obligacione*: a promise by word, a personal bond in writing. *Sub obtentu beatitudinis*: as the condition of obtaining blessedness. Matthew 25:35–40, the works of mercy done to Christ's members. *Fraterna coreccio*: fraternal correction (cf. Matthew 18:15); *ad ipsam*: to it.

[^17]: **C/G.** Side-note 282.26. *Ut dixi superius*: the place is not identified here (LEDGER E13b-8). *Successivum*: a successive entity, one whose parts exist one after another (GLOSSARY §C). *Novissimum electum*: the last of the elect.
