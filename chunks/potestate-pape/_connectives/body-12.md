### 11-h ¶4 · 292.3–292.28  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 292] Cum **autem** signa ista non sint nisi gracia signatorum, signata per se sufficiunt sine signis, **sed** signa sine signatorum correspondencia sunt venenum; nec morarer circa hec signa in novo testamento ad onus ecclesie introducta nisi ad detegendum sensum misticum ornamentorum sacerdotalium legis veteris, quibus omnia in figura contigerant ad ostendendum sensum, quem nos utentes talibus signis debemus preponderanter attendere et ad convincendum mendacium philosophice signa ista gerencium. Ad tantum enim quidam in istis vane subtiliant, quod totum officium misse et sacerdotalis officii cum signis suis ac eciam partes basilice cum suis accidentibus mistice moralizant, ut ventilogium in summitate campanilis ecclesie dicunt signare curatum illius ecclesie, qui debet esse in conversacione superior, contemplacione celesti ecclesie triumphanti propinquior et contra omnes ventos temptacionum ecclesie tamquam gallus ad predicandum paracior. **Sed** Antichristus cum magistro suo Lucifero habet sensum contrarium in hiis signis, ut quod prelatus in fastu sit alcior, ad omnem ventum temptacionis vertibilior et tamquam canis mutus non valens latrare, ymo ut nuda galli statua ad fallendum subiectos mendacior.

ENGLISH: And since these signs exist only for the sake of the things signified, the things signified suffice by themselves without the signs, but signs without the correspondence of the things signified are poison; nor would I dwell on these signs, introduced in the new testament to the burden of the church, except to [expose]{g:detegere} the mystical sense of the priestly ornaments of the old law, to whom "all things happened in figure",[^10] in order to show the sense to which we who use such signs ought to attend preponderantly, and to convict philosophically of lying those who wear these signs. For some [refine]{g:subtiliare} so vainly on these things that they moralize mystically the whole office of the mass and of the priestly office with its signs, and even the parts of the church building with their accidents: thus they say that the weathervane at the top of the church's bell-tower signifies the [curate]{g:curatus} of that church, who ought to be higher in [manner of life]{g:conversacio}, nearer in heavenly contemplation to the [church triumphant]{g:ecclesia-triumphans}, and, like a cock, readier to preach against all the winds of the church's temptations.[^11] But Antichrist with his master Lucifer has the contrary sense in these signs: that the prelate is higher in pride, more apt to turn with every wind of temptation, and like "a dumb dog not able to bark",[^12] indeed, like the bare statue of a cock, more lying for deceiving his subjects.

### 11-h ¶5 · 292.29–293.11  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 292] Tercio principaliter arguitur per hoc quod nisi papa haberet plenitudinem potestatis et alii episcopi gradatim, ut in ecclesia ordinantur, periret ordo confessionis et timor committendi peccata, ymo per idem non observaretur ritus vel usus alicuius ecclesie, nisi qui foret expresse fundabilis [p. 293] in scriptura, et per consequens nemo observaret aliquem modum in serviendo Deo, **sed** longe evidencius tolleretur ordo secularium dominorum, sic quod non oportet esse cesarem, cum necessarius foret quod esset dignitas et distinccio clericalis. Ad hec igitur oportet elecciones procedere, sicut dictant leges et usus ecclesie. Non igitur sequitur: Christus et apostoli sic sequuntur, ergo non licet nobis sequentibus facere contrarium, cum pro variacione loci et temporis diversimode est agendum.

ENGLISH: Thirdly and principally, [it is argued by appeal to]{g:arguitur-per} this: that unless the [pope]{g:papa} had [plenitude of power]{g:plenitudo-potestatis}, and the other [bishops]{g:episcopus} by degrees, as they are ordered in the church, the order of confession would perish, and the fear of committing sins; indeed, by the same token, no [rite]{g:ritus} or usage of any church would be observed except one that could be expressly founded [293] in scripture, and consequently no one would observe any mode in serving God; but far more evidently the order of [secular lords]{g:seculares-domini} would be done away, so that there need be no Caesar, since it would be more necessary that there be clerical dignity and distinction.[^13] To these ends, therefore, elections must proceed as the laws and usages of the church dictate. Therefore [it does not follow]{g:sequitur}: "Christ and the apostles proceed in this way, therefore it is not lawful for us who follow them to do the contrary", since one must act in different ways according to the variation of place and time.[^14]

### 11-h ¶6 · 293.12–294.2  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 293] Quantum ad illud patet quod est expediens quod sit papa, **sed** ad aggravandum difficultatem loquamur limitate de papa cesareo. Et tunc videtur michi quod multum officit esse talem, ut patet inferius. Diceret **autem** logicus quod quilibet apostolus et quilibet sacerdos apostolorum vicarius habet in gradu suo plenitudinem potestatis. **Sed** decretiste videntur intelligere quod quilibet papa habet in infinitum maiorem potestatem iurisdicionis sive regiminis quam aliquis alius episcopus non Romanus, quia (ut sepe dictum est) cum sit infundabile mendacium in materia fidei est nimis hereticum. Petrus enim non habuit tantum aut talem excessum super alios apostolos sibi consocios; nec imperatoris est talem potestatem concedere. Quomodo igitur foret utile tale mendax et monstruosum ydolum ducem tocius corporis Christi fingere? Nec periret ex defectu talis monstri ordo confitendi, ordo regiminis vel alterius boni ecclesie, quia sicut in primitiva ecclesia regulabatur sine tali graduacione dominii in clero ex communi sacerdotum officio, qui omnes erant episcopi, quod exemplatum est [p. 294] in ecclesia apostolica, et tunc crevit ecclesia, sic posset esse hodie ad maiorem utilitatem ecclesie.

ENGLISH: As to that, it is clear that it is expedient that there be a pope; but, to make the difficulty heavier, let us speak restrictedly of the [caesarean pope]{g:papa-cesareus}. And then it seems to me that it does much harm that there be such a one, as is clear below. And a logician would say that every apostle, and every [priest]{g:sacerdos} who is a vicar of the apostles, has in his degree plenitude of power. But the [decretists]{g:decretiste} seem to understand that every pope has infinitely greater [power of jurisdiction]{g:potestas-iurisdiccionis} or [of rule]{g:potestas-regiminis} than any other bishop not Roman; for (as has often been said), since this is a lie [without foundation]{g:infundabilis} in a matter of faith, it is exceedingly heretical.[^15] For Peter did not have so great or such an excess over the other apostles, his fellows; nor is it the emperor's part to grant such power. How then would it be useful to feign such a lying and monstrous idol as leader of the whole body of Christ? Nor would the order of confessing, the order of rule, or that of any other good of the church perish for want of such a monster; for as in the primitive church it was regulated without such a gradation of [lordship]{g:dominium} in the [clergy]{g:clerus}, by the common office of priests, who were all bishops (which was exemplified [294] in the apostolic church, and then the church grew), so it could be today, to the greater benefit of the church.[^16]

### 11-i ¶1 · 294.3–299.5  (13 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 294] Conceditur tamen, quod ex illo, sicut ex quolibet malo quedam propria utilitas succedit ecclesie. **Sed** (ut utar verbis Apostoli) absit ut illa mala sint propterea facienda. Ulterius conceditur quod observanda est laudabilis consuetudo ecclesie, licet non sit explicita **sed** implicita in scriptura, quia minus diffusa et onerosa foret lex Christi, si omnes tales ritus, usus vel consuetudines forent in ea expresse. Ideo credendum est quod Spiritus Sanctus necessariora ecclesie expressit, et leviora sub quodam confuso involucro simul texit, ut patet prima Cor. XIV 40; dicit spiritus per suum Apostolum: Omnia honeste et secundum ordinem fiant. In quo includuntur omnes usus ecclesie in dicendo servicium, omnes ritus ordinum privatorum, et breviter omnis consuetudo clericalis vel laycalis laudabilis. Et in isto puncto surrepit maior irreligiositas populi christiani, quod postponendo preponderanciora expressa in lege Domini, preponderantur tradiciones et adinvenciones humane, quedam heretice et quedam in suo ordine observande. Sicut enim scriptura docet nos ista preponderanter cognoscere, sic minimum expressum in lege Domini observare sub incursu maioris pene quam aliquid quod est expressum singulariter in usu sacrum vel ritus aliquos eciam ordinate in religiosis vel laicis preter fidem Christi expressam noviter introductos. Et in isto cecatur ac oneratur ecclesia ultra modum, quia vel introducuntur irracionalia vel racionalia sunt nimium ponderata. Unde decretum XII distinccione sub [p. 295] auctoritate Augustini ad inquisiciones Januarii sic loquitur (ut sepe repecii): Omnia talia que neque scripturarum sanctarum auctoritatibus continentur nec in consiliis episcoporum statuta inveniuntur nec consuetudine universalis ecclesie roborata sunt, **sed** diversorum locorum diversis moribus innumerabiliter variantur, ita ut vix aut nunquam inveniri possunt cause, quas in eis constituendis homines secuti sunt, ubi facultas tribuitur sine aliqua dubitacione resecanda estimo. Quamvis enim hoc non inveniri possit, quomodo contra fidem sint, ipsam tamen religionem quam paucissimis et manifestissimis celebracionum sacramentis misericordia Domini voluit esse liberam, servilibus oneribus adeo premunt, ut tollerabilior sit condicio Iudeorum, qui etsi tempus libertatis non cogno verunt, legalibus tamen sacramentis, non humanis presumcionibus subiciuntur. Ex istis videtur, primo quod infidelius nostri pharisei premunt ecclesiam tradicionibus, sectis et exaccionibus quam fecerant pharisei legis veteris. Ipsi enim undique parcius onerarunt; moralia legis preponderarunt et veniente figurato viderunt quod figure cessarunt. Nostri **autem** pontifices, scribe et pharisei magnificant infideles tradiciones hominum, eciam ultra tradiciones quas Deus instituit, cum illas dicunt oportere cessare, si onus sapiunt et non lucrum; tradiciones vero suas, que honorem vel lucrum temporalem sapiunt, dicunt non posse cessare, licet sint tradicionibus et vite Christi contrarie; cum per illas consecrantur res, a qua consecracione non licet recedere; deminuere **autem** libertatem, que est verum privilegium ecclesie, per [p. 296] falsos dico non clericos **sed** idiotas, vocem propriam ignorantes, foret nota infidelitatis discipulo Antichristi. Quando enim queritur ab eis racio facti, vel balbuciunt, dicentes machometice quod non debet dari vel disputari racio sue tradicionis, cum per se sufficit voluntas capitis, vel arguunt ex insufficienti similitudine de lege veteri, vel allegant infundabiliter leges hominis, ut quod iurisconsultus vel satrapa sic diffinit, nec licet (ut inquiunt) racionem querere ut in lege Dei **sed** ad ipsos ut primum principium est sistendum. Seculares **autem** provident quod leges eorum consonent legi Dei, quia utilitati et racioni, cum profitentur catholice quod omnis lex vel ritus hominis que non habet ab ipsa principium est heretica et fugibilis ut venenum. Ex quo patet quod lex Dei debet primo disci a quocumque catholico. Quid inquam fideli de lege alia? Unde lex Dei cum tradicionibus humanis sophistice est commixta; scilicet ut tradicio pallietur et coloracius observetur. Observet igitur fidelis iusticiam legis et paret se ad sufferendum iniurias et penas pro omissione legum hominum. Hoc enim directe accelerat viatorem ad terminum et oppositum deviat vel retardat. Conceditur igitur quod ritus et leges honeste ex lege Domini eliquate sunt in suo ordine observande. **Sed** caveat christianus de nimietate studii eorum et observancie; suspensus itaque in lege Dei sine ulteriori sollicitudine sit contentus de flatu spiritus sine istis extravagantibus dirigentis. Quid queso fideli de gentili ritu vel phariseico nisi de quanto modeste facilitat ad observanciam legis Dei? Unde graviter peccarunt, qui introduxerunt [p. 297] sectas et distincciones legum ecclesie, per quas diminuitur meditacio legis Dei, cum melius esset quod pure observaretur vita apostolica, et dimissa difformitate tenderetur ad uniformitatem status innocencie, licet graduacio in seculari dominio sit licita ac necessaria peccato supposito. Unde nichil infidelius quam ex graduacione secularis dominii concludere graduacionem dominacionis et honoris secularis in clericis. Cum enim status ille sit alcior, statui Christi et sanctorum conformior, oportet quod tendat ad maiorem unitatem, regulans statum laycalem et non sit a statu tam difformi quem regulat exemplatus. Conceditur tamen quod sunt gradus in illo statu apostolico; **sed** contrarie ad statum secularem; nam hic maior est pauperior, servitivior et ad currendum per peregrinacionem cum paucioribus temporalibus sufficiencior. Unde omnes presbiteri debent esse socii, ut docet Apostolus cum decreto de ipso et Petro, verumtamen servus ex maiori humilitate fit alcior. Unde (ut sepe dixi) pars laycalis dividitur in popularem et partem secularem illi prepositam quarum prior est bona et secunda melior. **Sed** pars clericalis propter socialem uniformitatem remanet indivisa et optima, quam elegit Maria; unde periculose balbuciunt, qui dicunt quod reges non sunt domini regnorum, **sed** nudi defensores, vel aliter (ut inquiunt) oportet dare in clericis gradus talis dominii. Scrutentur isti scripturas et videre possunt, quam spissim autor scripture tam verbo quam opere concedit illis de medio statu dominium, cum ipse obedienter dedit tributum cesari, a se ipso **autem** ac suis apostolis et eorum successoribus [p. 298] exclusit dominium, ut patet superius. Iudicent igitur inter evidencias; Deus interdicit sacerdoti Christi dominium et dat seculari dominium, igitur sic debet esse. Et cesar vel lex papalis negat seculari adesse dominium et dicit quod pape ac episcopis debet adesse quodcumque seculare dominium, igitur verum. Et sic de istis locis a simili: Christus exclusit se et suos apostolos a seculari dominio, igitur per idem hoc debet a suis vicariis observari, et seculares domini ac prelati vendicant sibi dominacionem civilem, primi legittime et secundi falsa usurpacione, igitur per idem omnes clerici debent taliter dominari. In tales argucias arundineas prorumpunt qui siciunt seculare dominium; et necesse est ut multiplicetur in clero Christi, sicut in parte multiplicabatur in clero legis veteris, talis confusio, quousque Antichristus destruxerat membra sua. Ideo cum secundum variacionem loci et temporis agendum est; ut congruit, patet quod regnante cupiditatis malicia sequenda est nobis via Christi sollercius, nam imminente maiori periculo debet periclitans magis inniti suffragio. Sic enim fecit sacerdos Christus veniens in summo gradu expropriacionis, quando summe regnavit cupiditas sacerdotum. Et hec est causa apostoli, ut prius exposui. Redimentes, inquit, tempus, quoniam dies mali sunt, unde nimis blasfemant degeneres qui dicunt quod tam utile et tam conveniens est statui cleri modo ditari amplius quam necessaria fuit potestas faciendi miracula in ecclesia primitiva. Revera tunc nec clerus possessionatus nec laycus daret corporalem elemosinam mendicanti, sicut apostoli non diminuerent de potestate faciendi [p. 299] miracula, ymo potestas, quam Evangelista habuit ad mutandum littoria saxorum fragmenta in gemmas et silvestres frondes in aurum, foret mundo utilior quam quantacumque temporalis possessio, sicut testaretur eciam Symon magus.

ENGLISH: Yet it is granted that from that, as from any evil, a certain proper benefit accrues to the [church]{g:ecclesia}. But (to use the Apostle's words) God forbid that those evils should therefore be done.[^1] Further, it is granted that the praiseworthy [custom]{g:consuetudo} of the church is to be observed, although it is not explicit but implicit in scripture; for the law of Christ would be less diffuse and burdensome if all such rites, usages or customs were expressly in it.[^2] Therefore it is to be believed that the Holy Spirit expressed the things more necessary to the church, and covered the lighter things together under a certain [indistinct]{g:confusus} wrapping, as is clear in 1 Cor. 14:40; the Spirit says through his Apostle: "Let all things be done decently and according to order." In this are included all the usages of the church in saying service, all the rites of the private orders, and, in brief, every praiseworthy custom, clerical or lay. And at this point the greater [irreligion]{g:irreligiositas} of the Christian people creeps in: that, setting aside the more preponderant things expressed in the law of the Lord, human traditions and inventions are made to preponderate, some of them heretical and some to be observed in their order. For as scripture teaches us to know these things preponderantly, so [it teaches us] to observe the least thing expressed in the law of the Lord under incurring a greater penalty than anything that is expressed singly in a sacred usage, or any rites, even introduced in orderly fashion among the [religious]{g:religiosi} or lay people, newly, beyond the expressed faith of Christ.[^3] And in this the church is blinded and burdened beyond measure, because either irrational things are introduced or rational things are weighted too much. Hence the Decretum, in distinction 12, under the [295] authority of Augustine, *To the Inquiries of Januarius*, speaks thus (as I have often repeated): "All such things as are neither contained in the authorities of the holy scriptures, nor found established in the councils of bishops, nor strengthened by the custom of the universal church, but vary innumerably according to the diverse manners of diverse places, so that the causes which men followed in establishing them can scarcely or never be found — where the opportunity is given, I judge that they are to be cut off without any hesitation. For although it cannot be found how they are against the faith, yet they so weigh down with servile burdens religion itself, which the mercy of the Lord willed to be free, with very few and most manifest sacraments of celebration, that the condition of the Jews is more tolerable: for they, even though they did not know the time of liberty, are yet subject to the sacraments of the law, not to human presumptions."[^4] From these things it seems, first, that our pharisees weigh down the church with traditions, [sects]{g:secta} and exactions more faithlessly than the pharisees of the old law had done. For they burdened more sparingly on every side; they gave preponderance to the moral precepts of the law, and when the thing figured came they saw that the figures ceased. But our pontiffs, scribes and pharisees magnify faithless traditions of men even beyond the traditions which God instituted, since they say that these must cease if they savour of burden and not of gain; but their own traditions, which savour of honour or temporal gain, they say cannot cease, although they are contrary to the traditions and to the life of Christ, since by them things are consecrated, and from that consecration it is not lawful to withdraw; and yet to diminish the liberty which is the true [privilege]{g:privilegium} of the church, through false men — I say, not clerics but [unlearned men]{g:idiota}, [ignorant of]{g:ignorare} their own word — [296] would be a mark of [unbelief]{g:infidelitas} in a disciple of Antichrist.[^5] For when the reason for the deed is asked of them, either they stammer, saying in Mahomet's manner that the reason for their tradition ought not to be given or disputed, since the will of the [head]{g:caput} suffices by itself;[^6] or they argue from an insufficient likeness to the old law; or they [allege]{g:allegare} [without foundation]{g:infundabilis} the laws of man, as that a jurisconsult or a [satrap]{g:satrapa} so defines; and (as they say) it is not lawful to seek a reason, as in God's law, but one must stop at them as at a first principle. But [the seculars]{g:secularis} see to it that their laws accord with God's law, because [they accord] with utility and reason, since they profess in catholic fashion that every law or rite of man which does not have its beginning from it is heretical and to be fled like poison.[^7] From which it is clear that God's law ought to be learned first by every catholic. What, I say, is another law to the faithful man? Hence God's law has been sophistically mixed with human traditions, namely so that the tradition may be [cloaked]{g:palliare} and observed more colourably. Let the faithful man, therefore, observe the justice of the law, and prepare himself to suffer wrongs and penalties for the omission of the laws of men. For this speeds the [wayfarer]{g:viator} directly to his end, and the opposite leads him astray or delays him. It is granted, therefore, that rites and laws decently drawn off from the law of the Lord are to be observed in their order. But let the Christian beware of excess in the study and observance of them; [suspended]{g:suspendere}, then, on the law of God, without further anxiety, let him be content with the breath of the Spirit, which directs without these extravagants.[^8] What, I ask, is a gentile or pharisaic [rite]{g:ritus} to the faithful man, except in so far as it modestly makes easier the observance of God's law? Hence those sinned gravely who introduced [297] sects and distinctions of the laws of the church, by which the meditation of God's law is diminished; since it would be better that the apostolic life were purely observed, and that, difformity set aside, men tended to the uniformity of the state of innocence, although gradation in secular [lordship]{g:dominium} is lawful and necessary, sin being supposed.[^9] Hence nothing is more faithless than [to infer]{g:concludere} from the gradation of secular lordship a gradation of secular [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio} and honour among clerics. For since that state is higher, more conformed to the state of Christ and of the saints, it must tend to greater unity, ruling the lay state, and not be modelled on a state so difform as the one it rules. Yet it is granted that there are degrees in that apostolic state, but contrary to the secular state; for here the greater is the poorer, the more [serviceable]{g:servitivus}, and the better fitted to run on pilgrimage with fewer [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}. Hence all [presbyters]{g:presbiter} ought to be fellows, as the Apostle teaches, together with the decree, concerning himself and Peter; nevertheless the servant becomes higher out of greater humility.[^10] Hence (as I have often said) the lay part is divided into the popular part and the secular part set over it, of which the first is good and the second better. But the clerical part, because of its fellowly uniformity, remains undivided and the best, which "Mary hath chosen";[^11] hence those stammer dangerously who say that kings are not lords of their kingdoms but bare defenders, or otherwise (as they say) one would have to grant degrees of such lordship among clerics. Let these men search the scriptures, and they can see how often the author of scripture, by word as well as by deed, grants lordship to those of the middle state, since he himself obediently gave tribute to Caesar, but excluded lordship from himself and his apostles and their successors, [298] as is clear above.[^12] Let them judge, therefore, between the [evidences]{g:evidencia}: "God forbids lordship to the priest of Christ and gives lordship to the secular; therefore so it ought to be." ### 11-k ¶9 · 307.11–307.28  (1 sed/autem)

Sic enim propter necessitatem baptismi sufficit laycus in defectu cleri baptizare. Sic eciam sufficit quicumque sacerdos simplex eque plene cum papa absolvere in mortis articulo, quare igitur non eque sufficit deficiente superiori prelato ineque necessario sacramento? Leges enim vel constituciones humane non obviant legi Dei nec sunt credende nisi de quanto consonant racioni.

ENGLISH: It is confirmed thirdly by this, that [from the faith of scripture]{g:ex-fide} Christ would stand by His church until the end of the world;[^16] now He would be unreasonable and to be rebuked if He required the office from His [clergy]{g:clerus} and, while the [power]{g:potestas} of the presbyter stood by to do this, hindered that presbyter himself; therefore, to supply [the want], out of the [plenitude of power]{g:plenitudo-potestatis} of Christ, a simple priest in such a case would have the [power]{g:potencia} and sufficiency to dispense so necessary a ministry.[^17] For so, because of the necessity of baptism, a layman suffices to baptize in default of the clergy. So also any simple priest whatever suffices, as fully as the [pope]{g:papa}, to absolve at the point of death; why then does he not equally suffice, the superior prelate failing, for a sacrament not equally necessary?[^18] For human laws or constitutions do not oppose the law of God, nor are they to be believed except so far as they accord with reason.

### 11-l ¶1 · 307.29–308.14  (1 sed/autem)

[p. 308] cum racio diversitatis sit necessarioritas baptismi supra alia sacramenta. Verumtamen subtiliantes in Dei potencia absoluta dicunt quod sicut Deus potest communicare nedum layco **sed** femine ac alii irracionali potenciam conficiendi ac ministrandi quelibet sacramenta, sic et ipsum potest ministrare huiusmodi sacramenta. Nec credo quod aliquis impugnabit illum articulum, quia cum multa absolute sunt necessaria, que est inefficax populo predicare; et grosse procedo in ista materia limitate loquendo de posse legali et Dei potencia ordinata, ideo dimitto scrutinium talium conclusionum detegens sentencias catholicas, quas credo prodesse ecclesie.

### 11-l ¶2 · 308.15–309.27  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 308] Videtur igitur michi quod licet et expedit in casu layco baptizare, et cum non sit de necessitate salutis cuiuscumque predestinati recipere corporaliter alia sacramenta, videtur michi quod non debet presumere ministrare alia sacramenta, et sic reservacio ac restriccio potestatis potest tam bene quam male fieri, sicut reus potest tam stulte quam pie adire Romanum pontificem pro absolucionis beneficio obtinendo. Et debet supponi, quod utrobique bene, nisi sit patens evidencia contradicens et specialiter superbia ac avaricia prelati et omnino utrimque preponderancia sacramenti. Videtur enim quod plus obesset monstruositas peccati in prescito masculo ad recipiendum vel ministrandum legittime quelibet sacramenta quam foret quantumcumque corporalis monstruositas vel distinccio sexus in femina predestinata et virtutibus perornata, sive ad recipiendum sive ministrandum quelibet sacramenta, et tamen in penam peccati multi de [p. 309] generacione adultera sic phariseice seducuntur, quod preponderant signum visibile humanitus institutum signato invisibili quod Deus preponderat, ut in casu et facto Romane ecclesie, quod narrat Cestrensis cap. Ill libri V scilicet quod quedam femina sedit in papatu duobus annis et quinque mensibus post Leonem. Hec dicitur fuisse puella Agnes vocata nacione Moguntina, a suo amasio Athenis in habitu virili adducta, et Johannes Anglicus nominata que in diversis scienciis sic profecit, quod Romam veniens magnos magistros habens auditores trivium legit, demum in papam electa, per suum amasium impregnatur, et dum Lateranensem ecclesiam ab ecclesia Petri pergeret, angustiata pariendi doloribus, inter Collosseum et sanctum Clementem peperit, et postmodum ibi fuit mortua et sepulta. Et hinc dicitur papam hanc viam communiter obliquare, unde non ponitur in cathalogo paparum. In isto casu saltem possibili dicunt leges hominum ipsam non fecisse actus papales nec fuisse electam legittime propter indisposicionem corporis, licet fuerit iam beata. **Sed** grave est hoc fundare vel fugere, quin generaliter desit eleccio quando eligitur inhabilis quoad Deum; unde angustiatur institucio humana describere verum papam.

ENGLISH: It seems to me, therefore, that it is lawful and expedient for a layman to baptize in a [given] case; and since it is not of necessity of salvation for any [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} [person] to receive the other sacraments bodily, it seems to me that he ought not to presume to minister the other sacraments.[^4] And so the reservation and restriction of [power]{g:potestas} can be done as well as badly, just as a guilty man can go to the Roman pontiff as foolishly as piously to obtain the benefit of absolution. And it ought to be supposed that [it is done] well on both sides, unless there is patent [evidence]{g:evidencia} to the contrary, and especially the pride and avarice of the [prelate]{g:prelatus}, and, on each side, the preponderance of the sacrament in every respect.[^5] For it seems that the monstrousness of sin in a [foreknown]{g:prescitus} male would be more of an obstacle to receiving or ministering lawfully any sacraments whatever than would be any bodily monstrousness however great, or the distinction of sex in a predestinate woman adorned with virtues, whether for receiving or for ministering any sacraments whatever.[^6] And yet, in punishment of sin, many of [9] an adulterous generation are so pharisaically led astray that they set the visible sign humanly instituted above the invisible thing signified, which God sets above it,[^7] as in the case and deed of the Roman church which [the Chester chronicler]{g:cestrensis} narrates in ch. 3 of book 5, namely that a certain woman sat in the papacy two years and five months after Leo.[^8] She is said to have been a girl called Agnes, "of Mainz by nation, brought to Athens by her lover in man's dress, and named John the Englishman, who so advanced in diverse sciences that, coming to Rome and having great masters for her hearers, she lectured on the trivium; at length, elected pope, she is made pregnant by her lover, and while she was going from the church of Peter to the Lateran church, gripped by the pains of childbirth, she gave birth between the Colosseum and Saint Clement's, and afterwards died there and was buried. And hence the pope is said commonly to turn aside from this road; and so she is not placed in the catalogue of popes."[^9] In this case, possible at least, the laws of men say that she did not perform papal acts and was not lawfully elected, because of the [unfitness]{g:indisposicio} of her body, even though she were already blessed. But it is hard to ground this, or to escape [the consequence] that election generally fails whenever one [unqualified]{g:inhabilis} as regards God is elected; hence human institution is hard put to define the true [pope]{g:papa}.[^10]

### 11-l ¶4 · 310.7–311.2  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 310] Hic dicitur quod materia ista requirit fundabilem declaracionem sacramentorum cum suis passionibus. Ideo opportet leviter et breviter in istis transcurrere. Videtur enim quod baptismus habet maiorem auctoritatem scripture quam nostra penitencia humanitus circumstancionata. Unde videtur michi, quod sicut non est possibile peccatorem salvari nisi baptizetur baptismo flaminis, sic nec est possibile peccatorem salvari nisi mente peniteat; verumtamen videtur quod nulla scriptura obviat **sed** consonat, quod quantumcumque gravis peccator in fine contritus fuerit, et si nullum sacerdotem secum conversantem consuluerit aut peccata sua auriculariter ei ostenderit et dicendo verba humanitus inventa manum super caput penitentis imposuerit, non eo minus per absolucionem Christi quem opportet prius absolvere est vere de numero salvandorum, quia communiter in contricione precedenti peccatum deletur a Domino. Verumtamen meritorium atque legittimum est confiteri secundum formam iam currentem presbitero, **sed** preternecessarium quoad salvacionem, et licet posterius sufficiens et negligens confiteri proprio sacerdoti incurrit offensam Domini, tamen illa offensa per contricionem anime potest tolli, unde officium prelati foret ostendere libertatem Christi ecclesie et non [p. 311] propter fastum aut questum subditos infundabiliter in servitutem redigere.

ENGLISH: Here [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that this matter requires a well-founded declaration of the sacraments with their [attributes]{g:passio}. Therefore one must run over these things lightly and briefly. For it seems that baptism has greater [authority]{g:auctoritas} of scripture than our penance with its humanly devised circumstances. Hence it seems to me that, just as it is not possible for a sinner to be saved unless he is baptized with the [baptism of the Spirit]{g:baptismus-flaminis}, so neither is it possible for a sinner to be saved unless he repents in mind;[^11] nevertheless it seems that no scripture opposes, but rather accords with this: that however grave a sinner, if he is contrite at the end, even if he has not consulted any [priest]{g:sacerdos} living with him, nor shown him his sins in the ear, nor has [the priest], saying words humanly invented, laid his hand upon the head of the penitent, he is none the less, through the absolution of Christ, who must absolve first, truly of the number of those to be saved; for commonly in the preceding contrition the sin is blotted out by the Lord.[^12] Nevertheless it is meritorious and lawful to confess to a [presbyter]{g:presbiter} according to the form now current, but [beyond necessity]{g:preternecessarius} as regards [salvation]{g:salvacio}; and although one who is afterwards able, and neglects to confess to his own priest, incurs the Lord's offence, yet that offence can be removed by contrition of soul;[^13] hence the office of the prelate would be to show the church the freedom of Christ, and not, [11] for pomp or gain, to reduce subjects [without foundation]{g:infundabiliter} into [servitude]{g:servitus}.

### 11-l ¶5 · 311.3–311.33  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 311] Unde soleo dicere frequenter in ista materia, quod talia possunt bene fieri, **sed** si bene fiunt, trepido vel non dico. Et quando queritur si oportet sic fieri, detego libertates ecclesie. Unde credunt multi quod reservantes absoluciones ab excomunicacione, ab alio crimine vel alia censura imposita stulte sepe imponitur ad gravamen ecclesie, ut licet homo possit meritorie petere Romanam curiam, et talis ordinacio per accidens prosit multis, faciens eos timidos ad peccandum, verumtamen communius sapit peccatum in prelato et sollicitudinem preternecessariam in subdito. Sic quod melius foret multis domi satisfaciendo conteri, et quicumque dixerit quod cum hoc non potest salvari propter obedienciam faciendam ordinacioni prepositi, negando libertatem ecclesie transgreditur legem Dei. In votis quidem et obligacionibus stulte factis oportet penitere de stulticia et spiritum consilii flagitare. Unde videtur illis quod non est color concludere quod tale decretum reservandi est licitum, si subiectis timorem incutit ad peccandum; omne enim malum per accidens facit bonum, unde timor filialis debet incuti per sanctam predicacionem prelati, et non timor servilis iudicii per exaccionem prelati cesarei. Et sic videtur michi, quod in quocumque casu christianus non debet accipere dicta sacramenta a layco, cum hoc foret christianismum serviliter altrinsecus onerare. Dignitas **autem** sacramentorum pensanda consistit in dignitate rei, sacramenti et sua efficacia.

ENGLISH: Hence I am accustomed to say often in this matter that such things can be done well; but whether they are done well, I tremble, or do not say. And when it is asked whether it must be done so, I expose the liberties of the church. Hence many believe that those reserving absolutions from excommunication, from another crime or from another [censure]{g:censura} imposed, often impose it foolishly, to the burden of the church;[^14] so that, although a man may meritoriously make for [the Roman curia]{g:curia-romana}, and such an [ordinance]{g:ordinacio} may by accident profit many, making them fearful of sinning, nevertheless it more commonly savours of sin in the prelate and of a care beyond necessity in the subject. So that it would be better for many to be contrite at home, making satisfaction; and whoever says that with this he cannot be saved, because of the obedience to be paid to the ordinance of a [superior]{g:prepositi}, by denying the liberty of the church transgresses [the law of God]{g:lex-dei}.[^15] In vows indeed and obligations foolishly made, one must repent of the folly and beg for the spirit of counsel. Hence it seems to them that there is no [colour]{g:color} for [inferring]{g:concludere} that such a decree of reservation is lawful if it strikes fear of sinning into the subjects; for every evil makes good by accident;[^16] hence filial fear ought to be struck by the holy preaching of the prelate, and not the servile fear of judgement by the exaction of a [caesarean prelate]{g:prelatus-cesarius}.[^17] And so it seems to me that in no case whatever ought a Christian to receive the said sacraments from a layman, since this would be to burden Christianity servilely from the other side. And the dignity of the sacraments, as it is to be weighed, consists in the dignity of the reality, of the sacrament, and its efficacy.[^18]

### 11-m ¶4 · 312.27–314.29  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 312] Patet secundo ex dictis quod sicut omnes sancti viri et femine membra Christi sunt sacerdotes, quia sacramentum dantes ex vi communionis sanctorum, sic solum predestinati sunt membra Christi vel pars sancte matris ecclesie et [p. 313] proprie christiani, ut pulcre docet Crysostomus in Imperfecto Omelia XIX: Solum enim continuatos corpori Christi dicit esse christianos; cum itaque omnia que evenient de necessitate evenient, patet quod necesse est esse hos sacerdotes triplices eciam sacerdotes Baal. **Sed** quantum ad intentum conceditur quod sine quocumque sacerdote cesareo ecclesia posset viare prosperius quam nunc viat. Gravissime **autem** qui prava intencione menciuntur in talia, dum tamen non sint infra sacros ordines ecclesiastice constituti. [p. 314] Unde notando peccata sic ordinancium et dicta sanctorum de symonia episcopi ordinantis pauci vel nulli forent clerici a prelato cesareo ordinati. Deus enim tunc supplet, quia aliter quilibet recipiens sacramentum de suo preposito fluctuaret. Nec convenit aliquem presumere quod immediate recipit ordinem sacerdotalem a Deo, sicut fecerunt Moyses et apostoli, licet concedi debeat, quod taliter potest esse, quia sine revelacione sic presumere foret stulticia scandalosa; ymo videtur quod homo pocius pateretur symoniam prepositi, dicens tamen ordinanti periculum qui sic mercatur cum ministerio sacramenti, quam temere confideret in insensibili Dei ordinacione mundo abscondita.

ENGLISH: It is clear, secondly, from what has been said, that just as all holy men and women, members of Christ, are priests, because they give a sacrament by virtue of the communion of saints, so only [the predestinate]{g:predestinatus} are members of Christ, or part of holy mother church, and [13] properly Christians, as Chrysostom finely teaches in the *Opus imperfectum*, homily 19: for he says that "only those joined to the body of Christ are Christians".[^5] Since, then, all things that will come about will come about of necessity, it is clear that these threefold priests must exist, even priests of Baal.[^6] But as to the point at issue, it is granted that without any caesarean priest whatever the church could [be a wayfarer]{g:viator} more prosperously than it now is.[^7] Therefore let us not believe those who swear that unless this bishop were pope all the ecclesiastical sacraments would perish; for they would stand better than now, even if there were no [caesarean pope]{g:papa-cesareus}, since after the day of judgement the body of Christ will remain, and so the sacrament and the [reality of the sacrament]{g:res-sacramenti}, the other burdensome [rites]{g:ritus} ceasing together with these sacraments of [those on the road]{g:viantes}.[^8] As to the second difficulty, I believe that often both those who ordain clerics sin, and the [clerics]{g:clericus} themselves, and the people as well; for those who go to [the Roman curia]{g:curia-romana} so as to be bishops, even [bishops "of Nowhere"]{g:episcopus-nullatensis}, but, living the suffragan life of a caesarean [prelate]{g:prelatus}, ordain clerics, bless churches, and live apart a luxurious life, labouring for temporal advantage, sin gravely, and many are apostates.[^9] But they sin more gravely who with wicked intention lie that they have been [deputed]{g:deputacio} by the pope to this office. And most gravely those who with wicked intention falsely take on such things, while they are not established within the holy orders of the church. And correspondingly the clerics so ordained sin, while they remain in them; with God humility does no harm, but it accords [with reason] that those [ordained] by simple priests who presume, and so sin in ordaining, are lawfully ordained.[^10] [14] Hence, noting the sins of those who so ordain, and the sayings of the saints on the simony of the ordaining bishop, few or no clerics would be ordained by a caesarean prelate.[^11] Therefore we ought not to fear about such things, so long as, according to a pure conscience about just works, we keep the clerical order; for then God supplies the place of the bishop, and all things that [follow]{g:sequitur} from these are to be granted fearlessly: as that one who has confessed in grave infirmity a sin for which the apostolic see would have to be sought, provided he was contrite and making satisfaction, need not, on recovering, seek the Roman curia or a prelate above a simple priest. And again, one ordained by a single simple priest need not fear about his presbyterate, or be ordained otherwise, provided he has duly performed the clerical office, for prudent estimation with diligent office suffices. For God then supplies, because otherwise anyone receiving a sacrament would be wavering about his [superior]{g:prepositi}. Nor is it fitting for anyone to presume that he receives the priestly order immediately from God, as Moses and the apostles did, although it must be granted that it can be so; for without revelation so to presume would be a scandalous folly; indeed it seems that a man should rather suffer the simony of the superior, telling the ordainer, however, the danger [to one] who so trades with the ministry of a sacrament, than rashly trust in an [imperceptible]{g:insensibilis} [ordination]{g:ordinacio} of God hidden from the world.[^12]

### 11-m ¶6 · 315.6–315.27  (1 sed/autem)

Sicut enim omnis christianus et specialiter bonus presbiter est sacerdos, sic est spiritualiter hostiarius, ceroferarius, lector, exorcista, subdyaconus, dyaconus et sacerdos, ut alias diffuse exposui. Et sic videtur quod ecclesia posset viare facilius, contenta de duobus ordinibus, in quibus residua spiritualiter includuntur. Nec obest, quod Augustinus dicit in libello suo De Heresibus, quod Heriani ab Herio quodam sunt dicti, qui, cum esset presbiter, doluit fortiter quod episcopus non potuit ordinari, et in Arrianorum heresim lapsus propria queque doctrinata addidisse, nonnulla dicens offerri pro dormientibus non oportere, nec statuta sollempniter celebranda esse ieiunia, **sed** cum quibus voluerit esse ieiunandum, ne videatur esse sub lege. Dicebat eciam presbiterum ab episcopo nulla differencia debere discerni.

ENGLISH: But from the old and new testament there ought to be seven orders; therefore the choice of the middle five seems to signify to the faithful that the first proud number, namely five, makes our superiors vary in this way.[^15] For just as every Christian, and especially a good presbyter, is a priest, so he is spiritually doorkeeper, taper-bearer, lector, exorcist, subdeacon, deacon and priest, as I have expounded at length elsewhere.[^16] And so it seems that the church could journey more easily, content with two orders, in which the rest are spiritually included. Nor is it an obstacle that Augustine says in his little book *On Heresies* that "the Herians are named from a certain Herius, who, when he was a presbyter, grieved greatly that he could not be ordained bishop, and, having fallen into the heresy of the Arians, [is said] to have added certain doctrines of his own, saying that one ought not to offer for the sleeping, nor that the appointed fasts should be solemnly kept, but that one should fast with those with whom one will, lest one seem to be under the law. He said also that a presbyter ought to be distinguished from a bishop by no difference."[^17]

### 11-m ¶7 · 315.28–316.23  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 315] In capitulo Herrii videntur michi esse Fratres, qui sumptuose, laboriose et periculose aquirunt episcopatus officia, ut seculariter voluptuose et ociose dimissa prius commendata obediencia conversentur. Non enim sonat directe ad utilitatem ecclesie vel religionis augmentum quod sic vivunt, quia per idem quilibet frater aptus ad regimen [p. 316] sic dissolveret claustrale vinculum. **Sed** iuxta principia fidei dimitteret dotacionem et conversacionem cesaream proficiendo subiectis episcopaliter, sicut fecerunt apostoli. Hoc enim plus congrueret professioni sue primeve. **Sed** iste sanctus dicit quod Herius doluit, quod episcopus non potuit ordinari, quia noluit esse sub lege; et sic incidit in istum errorem, quod presbiterum simplicem et episcopum non oportet discrimen per potestatem iurisdicionis atque scienciam, quia tunc iste Herius propter inhabilitatem ad officium episcopale pateretur repulsam. **Sed** absit logicum istud credere illud dictum Augustini repugnare decreto Jeronymi: Quod olim idem fuit episcopus et sacerdos, olim enim erant pauci episcopi atque ydonei, qui seclusi a seculo non propter mundum, **sed** propter Deum et amorem ecclesie curam pastoralis officii perfecerunt; et sic error esset hodie negare presbiterum ab episcopo nulla differencia discerni, cum multi presbiteri instructi in lege Domini vivunt sancte, et multi episcopi ignari legis Dei vivunt contrarie. Ideo necesse est esse differenciam sacerdotum.

ENGLISH: Under the heading of Herius, it seems to me, are the [friars]{g:frater}, who sumptuously, laboriously and dangerously acquire the offices of bishoprics, so that, having first abandoned the obedience committed to them, they may live in a worldly, voluptuous and idle way.[^18] For that they live so does not tend directly to the usefulness of the church or the increase of religion; for by the same token any friar fit for rule [16] would so dissolve the cloistral bond. But according to the principles of faith he would give up the [endowment]{g:dotacio} and the caesarean [manner of life]{g:conversacio}, profiting his subjects in the manner of a bishop, as the apostles did. For this would better suit his first profession. But that saint says that Herius grieved that he could not be ordained bishop, because he would not be under the law; and so he fell into this error, that a simple presbyter and a bishop need not be distinguished by the [power of jurisdiction]{g:potestas-iurisdiccionis} and by knowledge; for then this Herius, because of his lack of qualification for the episcopal office, would suffer rejection.[^19] But far be it from a logician to believe that saying of Augustine to [be incompatible]{g:repugnare} with the decree of Jerome, "that once bishop and priest were the same";[^20] for once there were few bishops, and suitable ones, who, secluded from the world, not for the world's sake but for God's and for love of the church, carried out the care of the pastoral office; and so it would be an error today to deny that a presbyter is distinguished from a bishop by no difference, since many presbyters instructed in the law of the Lord live holily, and many bishops ignorant of the law of God live the contrary way.[^21] Therefore there must be a difference among priests.
