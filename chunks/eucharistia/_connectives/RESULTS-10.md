## Doubtful, left as they are

- **04-h, p. 113**: ***Sed** de paritate bonitatis hostie est difficultas*, rendered "But about the equality of the host's goodness there is a difficulty". The mass has been dealt with; the host is set against it as the harder case: (b). The *ergo* that follows (*ergo efficacius est sacramentum*) closes the argument inside this sentence, not one the *sed* supplies a premiss to, so it is not (a). Left.
- **05-a, p. 116**: *Istud **autem** est quod doctor abhorret tamquam hereticum*, rendered "And this is what the doctor abhors". Read as (c): it carries on to say what Scotus is really guarding against. A contrast reading (b, "But what the doctor abhors is this") is possible, setting his real fear against the weak argument just refuted. Left.
- **05-a, p. 116**: *Convenio **autem** cum illis quantum sumunt testimonium illud superius*, rendered "And I agree with them so far as". After "one must go beyond these doctors", a concession could be (b) "But I agree with them". (c) is defensible as the next step of Wyclif's position. Left. The English goes on "but where they contradict that testimony": that "but" has no *sed* behind it (it renders the ablative absolute *cui testimonio ipsis contrariantibus*). It is a fair construal and does not touch the connective count.
- **04-h, p. 111**: ***sed** quomodocunque temptaverunt scio quod non possunt*, rendered "but however they have attempted it". "however" here is *quomodocunque* (in whatever way), not the connective; *sed* is "but". Left.
