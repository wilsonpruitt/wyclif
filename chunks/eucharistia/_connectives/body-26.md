# sed/autem extract, wave 26: chunks 09-g 09-h 09-i

### 09-g ¶1 · 296.23–298.4  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 296] Et minus tropicat nostra sentencia illud dictum quam sentencia contraria que intelligit quod accidencia panis erunt figura corporis Christi, quia illud infundabiliter tropicat utrumque extremum; **sed** nos tantum alterum sicut facit Spiritus Sanctus in Paulo in eodem sacramento, quando dicit prima Cor. XI, 25: Hic calix novum testamentum est in meo sanguine, ubi patet quod Paulus tropat predicatum ultra sacramentum [p. 297] et rem et addit in signum quod non demonstrat naturam sanguinis **sed** naturam vini, quia ponit tantum in meo sanguine in obliquo. Nec credo quod adversarii invenient tantum fundamentum ad tam compendiose tropandum dicta sua. Scio quidem quod adversarius posset infideliter tropare totam historiam scripture, ut omnia gesta Christi posset intelligere tropice, quod non Christus in persona propria **sed** per se accidens illa fecit, ut in assimili tangit Augustinus in libello suo De Heresibus. Scio quidem quod nos qui irreligiose contendimus circa temporalia et signa adinvencionis nostre in penam peccati cecati sumus in sensu quem habuit ecclesia primitiva iuxta prophetiam senis Symeonis, Luc. IIᵒ, 34, positus est hic in signum cui contradicetur. Sicut enim in conversacione contradicimus gestis Christi, sic in speculacione contradicimus realibus verbis Christi, ideo in istis paratus sum stare decreto sancte matris ecclesie; horreo **autem** inducere hunc tantum extraneum accidens vel quantitas quod Spiritus Sanctus per panem aut vinum in verbis sacramentalibus ipsum intenderat, sicut beatus Jeronimus detestatur nomen ypostasis, ne sub melle sanctitatis venenum quod horruerunt pure per hec sit absconsum. Et sic donemus ipsis iniuriam qui nituntur extinguendo falsam devocionem nostram destruere in nobis ydolatriam, quia revera nimis malum foret tales ydolatras domi decorticare canabem quam sic ydolatriam geminare; ideo Christus contra excusaciones universales ydolatrarum precipit suis discipulis quod habeant prudenciam [p. 298] serpentinam; nam Paulus committendo blasphemiam credidit se prestare obsequium meritorium Deo suo. Veritas itaque fidei est instancia necessaria cuilibet viatori.

ENGLISH: And our [teaching]{g:sentencia} takes that saying less tropically than the contrary teaching, which understands that the [accidents]{g:accidens} of the [bread]{g:panis} will be a [figure]{g:figura} of the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}; for that [teaching] tropes both extremes [without foundation]{g:infundabiliter}, but we only the one, as the Holy Spirit does in Paul in the same [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}, when he says, 1 Cor. 11:25: "This [chalice]{g:calix} is the new testament in my blood";[^1] where it is clear that Paul tropes the predicate beyond the sacrament [297] and the reality, and adds, as a sign that [the pronoun] [demonstrates]{g:demonstrare} not the nature of blood but the nature of [wine]{g:vinum}, that he puts "in my blood" only [in an oblique case]{g:in-obliquo}.[^2] Nor do I believe that the adversaries will find so great a foundation for troping their sayings so economically. I know indeed that an adversary could faithlessly trope the whole history of scripture, so that he could understand all the deeds of Christ [tropically]{g:tropice}: that not Christ in his own person but an accident [by itself]{g:per-se} did them, as Augustine touches on in a like case in his little book *On Heresies*.[^3] I know indeed that we who irreligiously contend about [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} and signs of our own invention have been blinded, in punishment of sin, in the sense which the primitive [church]{g:ecclesia} had, according to the prophecy of the old man Simeon, Luke 2:34: "this [child] is set for a sign which shall be contradicted".[^4] For as in our [manner of life]{g:conversacio} we contradict the deeds of Christ, so in speculation we contradict the real words of Christ; therefore in these matters I am ready to stand by the [decree]{g:decretum} of holy mother church. But I shudder to bring in this so foreign [sense]: that the Holy Spirit by "bread" or "wine" in the sacramental words had meant an accident or quantity,[^5] just as blessed Jerome detests the name [hypostasis]{g:ypostasis}, lest under the honey of holiness the poison which they abhorred, purely, be hidden by these [words].[^6] And so let us forgive the wrong to those who strive, by extinguishing our false devotion, to destroy idolatry in us; for [in truth]{g:revera} it would be less bad for such idolaters to strip hemp at home than thus to double idolatry.[^7] Therefore Christ, against the universal excuses of idolaters, commands his disciples that they "have the prudence [298] of serpents"; for Paul, in committing blasphemy, believed that he was offering a meritorious [duty]{g:obsequium} to his God.[^8] The truth of faith, therefore, is a necessary [objection]{g:instancia} for every [wayfarer]{g:viator}.[^9]

### 09-g ¶3 · 298.11–298.21  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 298] Hic dicitur communiter quod omnia accidencia absoluta corporis Christi que sunt in celo commultiplicantur cum ipso corpore in ipsa hostia concomitanter; et ita nedum tota quantitas et qualitas, ymmo totum corpus Christi et anima et sic tota eius humanitas et deitas indubie est ubicunque fuerit aliqua pars Christi; **sed** de septem accidentibus respectivis non oportet. Et ita posicio que est differencia quantitatis continue permanentis **sed** non posicio que est penultimum predicamentum commultiplicatur concomitanter ad corpus Christi in omni puncto hostie.

ENGLISH: Here [the answer]{g:dicitur} commonly given is that all the [absolute accidents]{g:accidens-absolutum} of the body of Christ which are in heaven are co-multiplied with the body itself in the host itself [concomitantly]{g:concomitanter}; and so [not only]{g:nedum} the whole [quantity]{g:quantitas} and [quality]{g:qualitas}, indeed the whole body of Christ and the soul, and so his whole [humanity]{g:humanitas} and [Godhead]{g:deitas}, is without doubt wherever any part of Christ is; but of the seven [relational]{g:respectivus} accidents it need not be so. And so [position]{g:posicio} which is a [difference]{g:differencia} of continuous permanent quantity, but not position which is the penultimate [category]{g:predicamentum}, is co-multiplied concomitantly with the body of Christ at every point of the host.[^11]

### 09-g ¶5 · 299.6–299.20  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 299] Item, non sunt fingenda miracula sine patencia racionis, **sed** foret summum miraculum intrudere tantam molem, sicut est tota corporeitas corporis Christi in infinitum modico loco; ergo cum non subsit racio isti miraculo, videtur ipsum non esse fingendum. Quis inquam non diceret quod esset inopinatum miraculum ponere totam mundi machinam non minoratam in nucleo in parvo loculo, sic quod idem corpus potest deperdere de loco usque ad non quantum et ita nullibi locum acquirere cum hoc quod non minoraretur **sed** in infinitum maioraretur? et sic pedale infinitum magnum locum posset successive occupare, cum hoc quod non diminuatur ad non quantum, et periret certitudo motus localis (ut dictum est de multiplicacione).

ENGLISH: Again, miracles are not to be [feigned]{g:fingere} without the plainness of a reason; now it would be the highest miracle to thrust so great a mass, such as is the whole corporeity of the body of Christ, into an infinitely small place; therefore, since no reason underlies this miracle, it seems that it is not to be feigned. Who, I say, would not say that it would be an unheard-of miracle to put the whole [frame]{g:machina} of the world, not lessened, into a kernel, in a little space, so that the same body can lose place down to [being] not quantified, and so acquire place nowhere, together with this, that it would not be lessened but infinitely enlarged? And so a foot-long [body] could successively occupy an infinitely great place, together with this, that it is not diminished to not quantified; and the certainty of local motion would perish (as has been said of multiplication).

### 09-h ¶1 · 300.6–301.3  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 300] Item, omne accidens substancie multiplicate independens ab extrinseco, cuiusmodi sunt quantitas et qualitas, commultiplicatur cum ea, **sed** extensio, locacio et multa accidencia respectiva non dependent in corpore Christi ab extrinseco; ergo omnia talia commultiplicantur cum illo; nam staret de Deo possibili quod omne corpus vel quantitas foret per se sine maiori substancia quod concedunt ut fidem. Ex quo videtur quod extensio quantitatis non dependet ab aliquo extrinseco, sicut nec figura ad quam requiritur extensio. Et ex istis videtur quod corpus multiplicatum commultiplicat locum secum, quia Deo annichilante totum residuum creature foret corpus tantum in loco suo; et sequitur locorum confusio, et de posicione que est sessio, stacio vel iacere videtur quod inest ab intrinseco (sicut dicit Gilbertus Porretanus) quia stante Petro, sedente Paulo et iacente Lino annichilatoque residuo mundi usque ad illos continue servatos inmotos foret fingere grave, quomodo in illis variaretur posicio, cum conservatur continue moto subiecto in curru vel annichilatis polis mundi vel centro. Et idem videtur de distancia, de inmediacione et aliis respectibus quos foret maxime evidens non servari; nam stat corpora maxime distancia esse in infinitum propinqua, cum infinitum longa linea recta multiplicata posset intercipi inter quantumcunque [p. 301] propinqua, sicut patet de longitudine corporis Christi intercepta inter quamcunque partem eukaristie et aliam.

ENGLISH: Again, every [accident]{g:accidens} of a multiplied [substance]{g:substancia} that is independent of anything extrinsic, of which kind are [quantity]{g:quantitas} and [quality]{g:qualitas}, is co-multiplied with it; now extension, location and many [relational]{g:respectivus} accidents do not depend, in the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}, on anything extrinsic; therefore all such are co-multiplied with it. For it would stand, as possible to God, that every body or quantity should be [by itself]{g:per-se} without a greater substance, which they grant as faith.[^1] From which it seems that the extension of a quantity does not depend on anything extrinsic, as neither does [figure]{g:figura}, for which extension is required. And from these it seems that a multiplied body co-multiplies its place with it; for if God annihilated the whole remainder of [creature]{g:creatura}, the body would be only in its own place; and there follows a confusion of places. And of [position]{g:posicio}, which is sitting, standing or lying, it seems that it is in [its subject] from within (as Gilbert of Poitiers says);[^2] for, Peter standing, Paul sitting and Linus lying, and the rest of the world annihilated down to them, they being continuously preserved unmoved, it would be hard to [feign]{g:fingere} how position would vary in them, since it is continuously preserved when the subject is moved in a cart, or when the poles of the world or its centre are annihilated. And the same seems [to hold] of distance, of immediacy and of the other [relations]{g:respectus}, which it would be most evident are not preserved; for it may be that bodies most distant are infinitely near, since an infinitely long straight line, multiplied, could be intercepted between [things] however [301] near, as is clear of the length of the body of Christ intercepted between any part of [the Eucharist]{g:eukaristia} and another.

### 09-h ¶2 · 301.4–301.27  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 301] Item, ubicunque est quantitas vel qualitas corpori Christi, ibi est quod ipsum est sic quantum et quale et per consequens per totam hostiam est quod corpus Christi est septipedale et gloriose qualificatum ut in celo. Et hic mirantur philosophi, cum qualitas sine subiecto habet operacionem suam naturalem eque efficacem sicut substancia sic qualificata, quod celestis qualitas in nobili subiecto corpore Christi ab accione suspenditur, et unio qua uniretur qualitas celestis in hostia fortificaret accionem, quia secundum philosophos omnis virtus unita est forcior se dispersa, ymmo cum secundum perspectivorum sentenciam forma collacionis iuvat ad luminis accionem ac in corpore Christi si est ibidem est claritas sole splendidior et ad omnem punctum figura Mukephi que secundum Vitilonem est figura efficacissima possibilis ad lumina congreganda, videtur quod valde sensibiliter ageret, et hoc forte movet eos qui dicunt quod vident in hostia corpus Christi oculo corporali, **sed** evangelium dicit quod fuit singulariter concessum privatis apostolis videre scilicet gloriam transfiguracionis corporis Domini ante mortem; ideo cum non dicunt cum Petro: bonum est nos hic esse, creditur quod de visione quatuor dotium corporis Christi in hostia menciuntur.

ENGLISH: Again, wherever there is quantity or quality to the body of Christ, there it is [the case] that it is so [quantified]{g:quantus} and qualified; and consequently, throughout the whole [host]{g:hostia} it is [the case] that the body of Christ is seven feet long and gloriously qualified, as in heaven. And here the philosophers wonder (since a quality without a subject has its natural operation as efficacious as a substance so qualified) that the heavenly quality in the noble subject, the body of Christ, is [suspended]{g:suspendere} from action; and the [union]{g:unio} by which the heavenly quality would be united in the host would strengthen the action, for according to the philosophers every [power]{g:virtus} united is stronger than itself dispersed;[^3] indeed, since according to the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of the [perspectivists]{g:perspectivi} the form of the [collection]{g:collacio} helps toward the action of light,[^4] and in the body of Christ, if it is there, there is a [clarity]{g:claritas} more splendid than the sun, and at every point the figure *Mukephi*, which according to Witelo is the most efficacious figure possible for gathering lights,[^5] it seems that it would act very [perceptibly]{g:sensibiliter}. And this perhaps moves those who say that they see in the host the body of Christ with the bodily eye; now the gospel says that it was granted [alone]{g:singulariter} to apostles taken apart to see, namely, the glory of the transfiguration of the [body of the Lord]{g:corpus-domini} before [his] death; therefore, since they do not say with Peter, "It is good for us to be here", it is believed that they lie about the vision of the four dowries of the body of Christ in the host.[^6]

### 09-h ¶5 · 302.24–302.32  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 302] **Sed** absit fidelem de Deo suo concedere inconveniencia ad que heresis ista ducit; cum enim corpus Christi habet ibi partes quantitativas distinctas a partibus qualitativis, oportet quod utrumque genus parcium, sicut habet ibi nomen, sic habeat ibi racionem nominis. Et confirmacio est quod corpus Christi foret ibi, solummodo (quod est adversariis summe possibile) foret magnum, quantum ergo foret ibi, si non tantum quanta est sua quantitas.

ENGLISH: But far be it from a faithful man to grant of his God the [absurdities]{g:inconveniens} to which this heresy leads.[^9] For since the body of Christ has there [quantitative parts]{g:pars-quantitativa} distinct from [qualitative parts]{g:pars-qualitativa}, it must be that each kind of parts, as it has the name there, so has there the account of the name. And the confirmation is that the body of Christ would be there; merely (which is to the adversaries supremely possible) it would be great; how great, then, would it be there, if not as great as its quantity is?[^10]

### 09-h ¶6 · 303.1–303.7  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 303] Similiter, eque spiritualis res est quantificacio sicut quantitas; cum ergo ibi sit quantitas cum figura, quod sua informacio sit ibidem, aliter enim oportet blasphemare in Deum, dicendo quod potest alicubi servare quantitatem in subiecto cuius informacionem bene suspendit, **sed** non potest suspensionem illam dissolvere.

ENGLISH: Likewise, quantification is as spiritual a thing as quantity; since, therefore, quantity is there with figure, [it must be] that its [informing]{g:informacio} is there too; for otherwise one must blaspheme against God, saying that he can somewhere preserve quantity in a subject whose informing he indeed suspends, but cannot undo that suspension.[^11]

### 09-h ¶7 · 303.8–303.15  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 303] In istis **autem** suppono ex loyca quod, si alicubi erit quantificacio, tunc est res quanta ibi, quia aliter motus et omnis respectus multiplicarentur per totum mundum. Infinita sunt talia argumenta propter que dicit katholicus quod corpus Christi est ibi virtualiter et in signo, non corpus Christi ut est in celo, **sed** signum eius vicarium, ut dictum est in fine proximi capituli.

ENGLISH: And in these I suppose from [logic]{g:loyca} that, if quantification will be anywhere, then there is a quantified thing there; for otherwise motion and every relation would be multiplied through the whole world. Infinite are such arguments, on account of which the catholic says that the body of Christ is there [virtually]{g:virtualiter} and in [sign]{g:signum}: not the body of Christ as it is in heaven, but its vicarial sign, as was said at the end of the preceding chapter.[^12]

### 09-h ¶8 · 303.16–303.21  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 303] **Sed** contra istud instatur loyce: Videtur enim primo quod corpus sit in hostia extensum, quia est ibi panis extensus et sic extensum, quia res extensa et sic sensibile sacramentum foret res sacramenti, quod est contra leges ecclesie, et sequeretur magna confusio et repugnancia predictorum.

ENGLISH: But against this [it is objected]{g:instatur} logically: for it seems, first, that the body is extended in the host, because the bread is there extended, and so [something] extended, because a thing extended; and so the [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} would be the [reality of the sacrament]{g:res-sacramenti}, which is against [the laws of the church]{g:leges-ecclesie}; and there would follow great confusion and [incompatibility]{g:repugnancia} of the things said before.

### 09-h ¶9 · 303.22–304.7  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 303] Quantum ad dictum, apparet quomodo oportet in ista materia notare equivocaciones que excludunt contradiccionem et elucidant responsionis variacionem et specialiter de equivocacione predicacionis et corporis Christi; nam secundum predicacionem tropicam concedunt sancti cum scriptura quod corpus Christi est panis vel signum sacramentaliter ipsum significans; et ad conformem sensum concedunt quod corpus Christi est in altari et mistice ille panis et sic corpus Christi quod est in celo natum de virgine et crucifixum est in altari non ipsum corpus nec aliquid [p. 304] ydemptice **sed** est tropice signum sacramentaliter ipsum significans; et ita intelligunt leges et dicta sanctorum quod sacramentum sensibile non est ydemptice corpus Christi; et sicut sophista replicat corpus Christi est in altari extensum equivocando predicacione, ut accipiendo predicatum in neutro genere substanciato, et tamen corpus Christi non ibi extenditur.

ENGLISH: As to what has been said, it appears how one must in this matter note the [equivocations]{g:equivocacio} which exclude contradiction and throw light on the variation of the answer, and especially the equivocation of [predication]{g:predicacio} and of "the body of Christ". For according to [tropical predication]{g:predicacio-tropica} [the saints]{g:sancti}, with scripture, grant "the body of Christ is bread", or "a sign [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} signifying it"; and in a conforming [meaning]{g:sensus} they grant "the body of Christ is on the altar", and "[mystically]{g:mistice}, that bread", and so "the body of Christ which is in heaven, born of the virgin and crucified, is on the altar": not [as] that body itself, nor anything [304] [identically]{g:ydemptice}, but it is [tropically]{g:tropice} a sign sacramentally signifying it. And so they understand the laws and the sayings of the saints: that the perceptible sacrament is not identically the body of Christ.[^13] And just as the [sophist]{g:sophista} [rejoins]{g:replicatur}, "the body of Christ is on the altar extended", by equivocating in the predication, as by taking the predicate in the neuter gender made a substantive, and yet the body of Christ is not extended there.[^14]

### 09-h ¶10 · 304.8–304.14  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 304] **Sed** secundo replicat sophista si hoc ibi extenditur et hoc est corpus Christi, tunc corpus Christi ibi extenditur; et per idem sequitur quod corpus in altari sit corpus Christi, quia est ibidem panis qui ubicunque fuerit est corpus Christi. Aliter enim sequeretur confusa multiplicacio inconveniencium et nimia intricacio, sophismatum.

ENGLISH: But secondly the sophist rejoins: "If this is extended there and this is the body of Christ, then the body of Christ is extended there"; and by the same token [it follows]{g:sequitur} that the body on the altar is the body of Christ, since the bread is there which, wherever it is, is the body of Christ. For otherwise there would follow a confused multiplication of absurdities and an [exceeding]{g:nimis} entanglement of [sophisms]{g:sophisma}.

### 09-i ¶1 · 304.15–305.9  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 304] Hic dicitur ut supra quod illud (false vocatur sylogismus expositorius) propter equivocacionem manifeste deficit, sicut patet exprimendo sensum minoris: Hic panis ibidem extenditur et ille figurat sacramentaliter corpus Christi, ergo corpus Christi ibidem extenditur; nam in consequente intelligitur corpus Christi personaliter pro illo supposito quod creabatur ex virgine, paciebatur et sedet in celo in pocioribus donis Dei; et ita equivocant sancti in secunda argucia, concedentes quod corpus Christi est in altari corpus Christi, sicut beatus Jeronimus dicit quod corpus Christi intelligi potest dupliciter; et ita panem illum post conversionem concedunt secundum argumentum expositorium esse delatum in celum ante oculos Dei et angelorum. **Sed** in omnibus istis est solamen fidelibus quod cognoscunt sensum katholicum et possunt licite ac meritorie [p. 305] sine contradiccionis repugnancia nunc loqui uno modo nunc alio secundum capacitatem auditorii, cui loquuntur, ut aliter loquendum est sophistis et aliter piis simplicibus. Ego **autem** elegi laicis loqui planius quod illud sacramentum figurat corpus Christi et conficitur, colitur et manducatur intencione memorandi et imitandi Christum; in quo quia contingit tanta duplicitas ac sophisticacio, permittit Deus in ista materia sophistas infructuose contendere.

ENGLISH: Here [the answer]{g:dicitur} is, as above, that that [argument] (falsely called an [expository syllogism]{g:sillogismus-expositorius}) manifestly fails through [equivocation]{g:equivocacio}, as is clear by expressing the [meaning]{g:sensus} of the minor: "This [bread]{g:panis} is extended there, and it [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} [figures]{g:figurare} the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}; therefore the body of Christ is extended there." For in the [consequent]{g:consequens} "the body of Christ" is understood personally, for that [supposit]{g:suppositum} which was created from the virgin, suffered, and sits in heaven in the greater gifts of God.[^1] And so [the saints]{g:sancti} equivocate in the second [quibble]{g:argucia}, granting "the body of Christ is on the altar the body of Christ", as blessed Jerome says that the body of Christ can be understood in two ways;[^2] and so they grant, according to an expository argument, that that bread after the [conversion]{g:conversio} is carried into heaven before the eyes of God and of the angels.[^3] But in all these there is comfort for the faithful, that they know the catholic meaning and can lawfully and meritoriously, [305] without the [incompatibility]{g:repugnancia} of contradiction, speak now in one way, now in another, according to the capacity of the audience to which they speak, as one must speak otherwise to sophists and otherwise to the pious simple. But I have chosen to speak more plainly to lay people: that that [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} figures the body of Christ, and is [consecrated]{g:conficere}, worshipped and eaten with the intention of remembering and imitating Christ. And because in this so much doubleness and [sophistry]{g:sophisticacio} happens, God permits sophists to contend fruitlessly in this matter.

### 09-i ¶2 · 305.10–307.17  (8 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 305] Tercio **autem** replicatur per hoc quod non efficaciter docetur corpus Christi esse in altari sacramentaliter; et super isto dependet tota contencio. Hic non oportet suadere istam conclusionem fidelibus, cum christiani pocius excedant in ista quam distrahunt veritatem. **Sed** alloquendo infideles hortandi sunt layci, ut in ista parte credant fidei scripture superius allegate. Infideles **autem** philosophi instruendi sunt quomodo eadem res habet esse multiplex. Deus enim qui est basis tocius universitatis create est ubique per se primo essencialiter, virtualiter et potencialiter; et quia in eo idem est essencia, virtus et potencia (ut declarat Augustinus in De Cognicione Vere Vite), ideo ipse est ubique indivisibilis secundum essenciam, potenciam et virtutem. In creaturis triplex esse precedit suam existenciam; primum est esse intelligibile eternum quod secundum raciones seminales habet esse in Deo; secundum est esse potenciale in causis secundis secundum raciones seminales, et tercium est esse propinquum quod creatura habet in suo efficiente secundum applicacionem cause superioris, ut omnis [p. 306] homo secundus est in suo parente; et in istis sunt multi gradus. Secundum vero genus essendi subsequitur existenciam creature et specialiter hominis qui cum fit ad ymaginem Dei, habet esse existencie vel esse actuale; secundo habet esse presenciale et tercio habet esse virtuale, ut exemplificat Magister in principio Historiarum de rege. Unde Augustinus ubi supra: Deus, inquit, iusticia est et ubicunque iusticia exercetur, Deus ibi creditur. Sic denique adest imperator latroni dum eum dampnat, sicut militi dum eum remunerat. Et nemo enim opinetur sordes abhominari **sed** solas peccatorum sordes eum noverit execrari: nec eum posse ulla re maculari, sicut nec radium solis ulla inmundicia sordidari. Nec umquam creditur istum sanctum vel aliquem ex hoc katholicum corpus Christi habuisse in eukaristia existenciam singularem in sua essencia, dicente Augustino ubi supra: Ultimus, inquit, in regno celorum ut sol fulgebit, qui tunc sepcies clarior quam nunc erit. Et in hac claritate tantum quisque ab alio pro meritis differt quantum stella a stella in claritate differt; nos **autem** omnes tantum corpus Christi in claritate excedit quantum creator creaturas transcendit; igitur talis visio talis glorie regnum celorum dicitur, quia soli celi, id est iusti, hoc fruuntur. Ideo humanitatem Christi solo oculo mentis non corporis nunc, videmus sicut et beatus Stephanus fide vidit, dicente Augustino [p. 307] ubi supra: Stephanus, inquit, non corporeis oculis Deum in celo ultra firmamentum vidit, cum nullius hominis visus ullomodo usque ad celum pertingere possit. **Sed** et concio illa in qua Stephanus stans Christum conspexit in domo aliqua fuit cuius tecti culmen ipsius corporeus oculus minime penetrare potuit. Constat igitur, inquit, quod nec oculo corporis **sed** intuitu interioris hominis Christum non in corporeo **sed** intellectuali celo viderit. Quod si sophista recalcitrat quod homo sit in loco a quo distat, studeat philosophos dicentes res esse in anima, videat theologos dicentes homines ante suam existenciam esse in suis parentibus, in quibus ex scripture testimonio fuerant decimati, et (ut loyci locuntur) homo post sui corrupcionem generat vel occidit et sic post mortem habet esse in suo effectu et evidencius corpus Christi in hostia.

ENGLISH: And thirdly [it is rejoined]{g:replicatur} by appeal to this: that it is not effectively taught that the body of Christ is on the altar sacramentally; and on this the whole dispute depends. Here there is no need to [make the case]{g:suadere} for this [conclusion]{g:conclusio} to the faithful, since christians rather exceed in this than detract from the truth. But in addressing [unbelievers]{g:infidelis}, lay people are to be exhorted to believe, in this part, the faith of scripture [alleged]{g:allegare} above. And unbelieving philosophers are to be instructed how the same thing has manifold [being]{g:esse}. For God, who is the basis of the whole [created totality]{g:universitas-creata}, is everywhere [of himself]{g:per-se} and first, [essentially]{g:essencialiter}, [virtually]{g:virtualiter} and potentially; and because in him [essence]{g:essencia}, power and [potency]{g:potencia} are the same (as Augustine [makes clear]{g:declarare} in *On the Knowledge of the True Life*), therefore he is everywhere indivisible as to essence, potency and power.[^4] In [creatures]{g:creatura} a threefold being precedes their existence: the first is eternal [intelligible being]{g:esse-intelligibile}, which according to [exemplary reasons]{g:raciones-exemplares} has being in God;[^5] the second is potential being in secondary causes according to seminal reasons; and the third is proximate being, which the creature has in its efficient [cause] according to the application of the higher cause, as every [306] second man is in his parent;[^6] and in these there are many degrees. And the second kind of being follows upon the existence of the creature, and especially of man, who, since he is made to the image of God, has the being of existence, or actual being; secondly he has presential being, and thirdly he has virtual being, as the Master exemplifies at the beginning of the *Histories* with a king.[^7] Hence Augustine, [in the place cited above]{g:ubi-supra}: "God," he says, "is justice, and wherever justice is exercised, God is believed to be there. Thus, in the end, the emperor is present to the robber while he condemns him, as to the soldier while he rewards him. And let no one think that he abhors filth, but let him know that he execrates only the filth of sins; nor can he be stained by anything, as neither is the ray of the sun soiled by any uncleanness."[^8] Nor is it ever believed that this saint, or anyone catholic, held on this ground that the body of Christ had in [the Eucharist]{g:eukaristia} a singular existence in its essence,[^9] as Augustine says in the place cited above: "The last," he says, "in the [kingdom]{g:regnum} of heaven shall shine as the sun, which will then be seven times brighter than now. And in this [clarity]{g:claritas} each differs from another according to merits as much as star differs from star in clarity; but the body of Christ exceeds all of us in clarity as much as the creator transcends creatures; therefore such a vision of such glory is called the kingdom of heaven, because the heavens alone, that is, the just, enjoy this."[^10] Therefore we now see the [humanity]{g:humanitas} of Christ with the eye of the mind alone, not of the body, as blessed Stephen also saw by faith, as Augustine says [307] in the place cited above: "Stephen," he says, "did not see God in heaven beyond the firmament with bodily eyes, since no man's sight can in any way reach as far as heaven. And besides, that assembly in which Stephen, standing, beheld Christ was in some house, the peak of whose roof his bodily eye could by no means penetrate. It is evident, therefore," he says, "that not with the eye of the body but with the gaze of the inner man he saw Christ, not in a bodily but in an intellectual heaven."[^11] And if the sophist kicks against [it], that a man should be in a place from which he is distant, let him study the philosophers who say that things are in the soul; let him look at the theologians who say that men before their existence are in their parents, in whom, by the testimony of scripture, they had been tithed; and (as the logicians speak) a man after his corruption begets or kills, and so after death has being in his effect; and more evidently the body of Christ in the [host]{g:hostia}.[^12]

### 09-i ¶3 · 307.18–307.23  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 307] **Sed** sexto principaliter arguitur de mocione et manducacione corporis Christi in hostia et tenetur communiter quod corpus Christi movetur cum hostia et sacramentaliter manducatur et sic in casu exscreatur, evomitur, et a sorice manducatur non solum in suo signo **sed** in sua essencia.

ENGLISH: But sixthly, [as a main head]{g:principaliter}, it is argued about the [motion]{g:mocio} and [eating]{g:manducacio} of the body of Christ in the host; and it is commonly held that the body of Christ is moved with the host and is sacramentally eaten, and so, in a given case, is spat out, vomited up, and eaten by a mouse, not only in its [sign]{g:signum} but in its essence.[^13]
