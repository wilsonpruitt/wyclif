# *De eucharistia*, chapter 5, part 9: one body in many places without conversion, and the gloss imputed to Thomas

## ¶1 · 147.9–148.29

> [p. 147] Item, maximum inconveniens ad quod deducit ex isto foret quod idem corpus moveretur simul et semel ad diversa loca, sed possibilitatem huius oportet viam istam concedere, nam positis sex sacerdotibus quorum quatuor deferant hostiam consecratam ad quatuor differencias posicionis, quintus vero elevet hostiam in manibus et sextus deprimat, patet quod corpus Christi movetur in illa hostia ad sex differencias posicionis simul et semel. Sicut enim anima movetur ad motum corporis, sic et corpus Christi in hostia consecrata, quia est continue noviter per diversa loca sine conversione ad hoc pertinente. Prima enim conversio Domini fuit ante hoc consumptum, ideo pro nunc non facit quod corpus Christi non movetur, quia sic non posset corpus Christi moveri in hostia; nec valet dicere quod non habet motum sibi proprium, quia hoc non requiritur, sed est possibile, tum quia sic posset fingi de possibili quod corpus Christi assumeret in celo diversa loca cum quibus sic moveatur per accidens ut anima, tum eciam quia corpus potest de Dei omnipotencia esse per diversa loca distancia cum hoc tamen quod non sit in loco medio, ut patet ex multiplici evidencia. [p. 148] Item, queratur de mendicacione istius termini excludentis motum localem conversio corporis acquirentis locum; patet, inquam, quod non est terminus philosophicus ad sensum illum, cum philosophi et subtiliores theologi negarent omnem talem conversionem de possibili. Et patet quod petitur descripcio motus; non enim est racio quare panis et vinum convertitur in corpus Christi vel sanguinem vel aliquam eius partem quin per idem in quamlibet et quidlibet assignandum. Videtur enim multis quod subducto omni motu locali vel conversione unius in aliud corpus posset quotlibet loca acquirere et alias perdere. Ideo inconveniens videtur descripcio mendicata. Si enim essent quotlibet loca occupata corporibus contiguis omnino quietis que subito continuentur anima Petri actuante eorum quotlibet, tunc Petrus esset per quotlibet loca de novo sine hoc quod moveretur ad aliquem eorum vel alicuius rei fiat in aliam conversio. Nam motus localis in Petro utrobique subducitur, et idem conceditur perditis partibus per discontinuacionem sine motu locali. Et eodem modo diceret pius philosophus pertinenter quod corpus habet esse intencionale per medium, capiens suam speciem, sicut habet esse spirituale per anime consideracionem in ipsa anima sine hoc quod corpus moveatur ad illa loca; quare non posset sic esse de esse sacramentali, quod habet corpus Christi in hostia per virtutem datam hostie sine hoc quod corpus Christi illinc localiter moveatur.

Again, the greatest [absurdity]{g:inconveniens}[^1] to which he leads from this would be that the same body would be moved at one and the same time to different places. But this way must grant the possibility of this. For if six [priests]{g:sacerdos} are posited, four of whom carry a [consecrated host]{g:hostia} toward the four differences of position, while the fifth elevates the host in his hands and the sixth lowers it, it is clear that the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} is moved in that host to six differences of position at one and the same time.[^2] For just as the soul is moved at the motion of the body, so also is the body of Christ in the consecrated host, since it is continually and newly through different places without a [conversion]{g:conversio} pertaining to this. For the first conversion of the Lord was completed before this; therefore for the present it does not make the body of Christ not be moved, since in that way the body of Christ could not be moved in the host.[^3] Nor does it avail to say that it does not have a motion proper to itself, because this is not required, but it is possible: both because one could in this way feign, as a possibility, that the body of Christ took on different places in heaven, with which it would so be moved [by accident]{g:per-accidens}, like the soul; and also because a body can, by the omnipotence of God, be through different distant places, with this however, that it is not in the middle place, as is clear from manifold [evidence]{g:evidencia}.[^4] [148] Again, let the question be put about the [begging]{g:mendicacio} of this term that excludes local motion, "conversion of a body acquiring place".[^5] It is clear, I say, that it is not a philosophical term in that [meaning]{g:sensus}, since the philosophers and the subtler theologians would deny every such conversion as possible. And it is clear that the [definition]{g:descripcio} of motion is [begged]{g:petitur}. For there is no reason why the [bread]{g:panis} and [wine]{g:vinum} are converted into the body of Christ or [his] blood or any part of it, but that, [by the same token]{g:per-idem}, [they are converted] into any [part] whatever and anything whatever that might be assigned.[^6] For it seems to many that, with all local motion or conversion of one thing into another taken away, a body could acquire any number of places and at other times lose them. Therefore the begged definition seems absurd.[^7] For if there were any number of places occupied by contiguous bodies wholly at rest, which were suddenly [made continuous]{g:continuare},[^8] the soul of Peter actuating any number of them, then Peter would be through any number of places anew, without his being moved to any of them or any conversion being made of any thing into another. For local motion in Peter is taken away [on both sides]{g:utrobique}, and the same is granted when parts are lost through [discontinuation]{g:discontinuacio} without local motion. And in the same way a loving philosopher would fittingly say that a body has [intentional being]{g:esse-intencionale} through the medium, as it takes its species,[^9] just as it has spiritual [being]{g:esse} through the soul's consideration in the soul itself, without the body's being moved to those places. Why then could it not be so of the [sacramental being]{g:esse-sacramentale} which the body of Christ has in the host, through the [power]{g:virtus} given to the host, without the body of Christ being moved [locally]{g:localiter} from there?[^10]

## ¶2 · 148.30–150.2

> [p. 148] Iterum, secundum Doctorem Subtilem et suam sectam Deus posset subito multiplicare idem corpus per quotlibet loca et alias demultiplicare; quod non [p. 149] posset esse, nisi idem corpus posset acquirere quotlibet loca sine motu vel conversione ad hoc requisita; ergo descripcio non est sana secundum eos. Quamvis autem hoc sit argumentum ad hominem et (ut dicetur posterius) procedit ex falso principio, grave tamen et ultra vires prioris secte foret hereticare in hoc omnes de secta secunda. Constat quidem quod si hoc sit simpliciter inpossibile in materia fidei, tunc est summe hereticum toti sacre scripture contrarium, cum omne tale non posset esse nisi Deus et omnis veritas non esset. Nec dubium quin innitens huic principio ad inprobandum panis substanciam remanere nitatur inpossibilitare et sic hereticare omnem viam que ponit multiplicacionem corpoream; ideo indubie altera istarum viarum in materia de existencia accidencium sine subiecto est inpossibilis atque heretica, sicut vel via Augustini que ponit generaliter distinccionem quantitatis a subiecto (ut recitavi superius) vel via contraria negans distinccionem quantitatis a subiecto. Unde illi qui inponunt scandalose sancto Thome super Tercium Sentenciarum quod pertinaciter viam que dicit Christum esse creaturam omnino esse inpossibilem sed non hereticam mirabiliter defamant ipsum, cum implicant ipsum in materia fidei esse contradictorium sibi ipsi et per consequens ante purgacionem nullibi esse credendum, et sic ponendo possibilitatem vacui non crederetur sibi, quia tunc terra pure posita in concavo orbis lune posset per vacuum subito acquirere locum terre quiescendo continue vel in celo vel in terra et tunc acquisicio situs foret formaliter pertinens motui vel in vacuo [p. 150] foret situs acquisicio vel duo instancia inmediata vel indivisibile moveretur in vacuo successive.

Again, according to the [Subtle Doctor]{g:doctor-subtilis} and his sect,[^11] God could suddenly multiply the same body through any number of places and at other times [demultiply]{g:demultiplicare} it; which could not [149] be, unless the same body could acquire any number of places without the motion or conversion required for this. Therefore the definition is not sound according to them. But although this is an [argument to the man]{g:argumentum-ad-hominem} and (as will be said later) proceeds from a false principle, yet it would be grave, and beyond the strength of the former sect, to [pronounce heretical]{g:hereticare} on this point all those of the second sect.[^12] It is evident indeed that if this is impossible [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} in a matter of faith, then it is supremely heretical, contrary to the whole of holy scripture, since every such thing could not be unless God and all truth were not. Nor is there doubt that one who relies on this principle to disprove that the [substance]{g:substancia} of bread remains strives to [make out as impossible]{g:inpossibilitare}, and so to pronounce heretical, every way that posits bodily multiplication.[^13] Therefore without doubt one or other of these ways, in the matter of the existence of [accidents without a subject]{g:accidens-sine-subiecto}, is impossible and heretical: either the way of Augustine, which posits generally a distinction of [quantity]{g:quantitas} from its subject (as I [recited]{g:recitare} above), or the contrary way, denying the distinction of quantity from the subject.[^14] Hence those who scandalously impute to Saint Thomas, on the Third [book] of the *Sentences*, that [he holds] obstinately that the way which says "Christ is a [creature]{g:creatura}" is wholly impossible but not heretical, wonderfully defame him,[^15] since they imply that in a matter of faith he is contradictory to himself, and consequently, before purgation, is nowhere to be believed.[^16] And so, when he posits the possibility of a vacuum, he would not be believed; for then earth placed purely in the concave of the orb of the moon could, through a vacuum, suddenly acquire the place of the earth while continuously at rest either in the heaven or on earth; and then either the acquiring of situation would formally pertain to motion, or in a vacuum [150] there would be an acquiring of situation, or two immediate instants, or an indivisible would be moved successively in a vacuum.[^17]

## ¶3 · 150.3–150.17

> [p. 150] Tales ficticias hereticas expedit fideli tangere, quia certum est quod omne simpliciter impossibile est summe hereticum; et iterum certum est quod summe inpossibile est, quod quantitas, qualitas vel aliquod accidens posset esse sine subiecto. Et quantum ad allegandum in oppositum, dicitur, ut ipsi leviter glossant magnum Augustinum, quando dicit quantitatem et qualitatem non posse esse sine subiecto, hoc est, inquiunt, nisi fiat miraculum. Sic, inquam, de testimoniis suis que allegant vera sunt nisi Deus velit contrarium. Nec sunt auctores sui ita graves et prudentis eloquii quod excedant in hoc auctores scripture vel ecclesiam primitivam. Nec obviat ista pictacia directe nostre sentencie, cum panis convertitur in corpus Christi.

It is expedient for the faithful man to touch on such heretical [fictions]{g:ficticia}, because it is certain that everything impossible without qualification is supremely heretical; and again it is certain that it is supremely impossible that quantity, [quality]{g:qualitas} or any [accident]{g:accidens} could be without a subject. And as to [what is alleged]{g:allegare} to the contrary, the answer is: as they lightly [gloss]{g:glosare} the great Augustine, when he says that quantity and quality cannot be without a subject, "that is," they say, "unless a miracle is done",[^18] so, I say, of their testimonies which they allege: they are true unless God wills the contrary. Nor are their authors so weighty and of such prudent speech that they exceed in this the authors of scripture or the primitive [church]{g:ecclesia}. Nor does this [patchwork]{g:pictacia} [meet]{g:obviare} our [teaching]{g:sentencia} directly,[^19] since the bread is converted into the body of Christ.

## ¶4 · 150.18–151.23

> [p. 150] Quantum ad ultimum Sancto impositum quod supradicta sentencia est heretica, quia scripture sacre contraria, cum tunc non diceretur: Hoc est corpus meum sed hic est corpus meum, frequenter musitavi si aliquis sciolus potuit istam stulticiam tam nudam asserere et demum credidi quod aliquis de pseudofratribus finxit istam demenciam contra Sanctum, quia (ut dicit Apostolus) periculum in falsis fratribus. Ponit enim scriptura sacra regulariter sacramentum istud esse panem, sicut sancti doctores sequentes et decretum Romane ecclesie. Sed falsa glossa adulatorum subintroeuncium maculavit istam sentenciam. Nam super IV Sentenciarum, distinccione XIᵃ, imponitur [p. 151] sancto Thome quod recitat Damascenum dicentem: Quia consuetudo est hominibus comedere panem et vinum, Christus coniugavit eis deitatem et fecit ea corpus suum et sanguinem. Et hoc dictum glossat consequenter: Dicendum, inquit, quod verbum Damasceni intelligendum est quantum ad species quibus corpus Christi divinitati coniunctum modo ineffabili coniungitur. Et ista glossa ad tantum dilatata est per patrem mendacii quod maculavit doctores decretorum precipuos, ut patet de glossa super additis, distinccione IIᵃ, De Consecracione primo; ergo patet quod ista glossa est contraria sibi ipsi, nam questione IVᵃ eiusdem distinccionis obicit per beatum Ambrosium dicentem: quod erat panis ante consecracionem est corpus Christi post consecracionem; hoc est, inquit, quod est sub speciebus panis, primo fuit panis et postea corpus Christi; ubi videtur quod loquitur de illo ante consecracionem quod ipsa substancia erit postea corpus Christi; quod est valde contrarium eidem sentencie, cum in fine eiusdem questionis dicit quod nichil panis umquam erit aliquid corporis Christi. Quis ergo crederet glossam istam que tam patenter se ipsam inficit?

As to the last [point] imputed to the Saint,[^20] that the aforesaid teaching is heretical, because contrary to holy scripture, since then it would not be said "This is my body" but "This [one] is my body":[^21] I have often pondered whether any [knowing]{g:sciolus} man could assert a folly so bare, and at last I came to believe that one of the [false friars]{g:pseudofrater} feigned this madness against the Saint, because (as the Apostle says) "peril from false brethren".[^22] For holy scripture [as a rule]{g:regulariter} lays it down that "this [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} is bread", as do the holy doctors who follow it and the [decree]{g:decretum} of the Roman church.[^23] But the false gloss of flatterers creeping in[^24] has stained this teaching. For on the Fourth [book] of the *Sentences*, distinction 11, it is imputed [151] to Saint Thomas that he recites Damascene saying: "Because it is the custom of men to eat bread and wine, Christ joined the [Godhead]{g:deitas} to them and made them his body and blood."[^25] And he glosses this saying afterwards: "It is to be said," he says, "that the word of Damascene is to be understood as to the [species]{g:species}, to which the body of Christ, joined to the [divinity]{g:divinitas}, is joined in an ineffable way." And this gloss has been spread so far by the father of lies[^26] that it has stained the chief doctors of the decrees, as is clear from the gloss on the additions, distinction 2, *De consecratione*, first.[^27] Therefore it is clear that this gloss is contrary to itself.[^28] For in question 4 of the same distinction he objects, by appeal to blessed Ambrose, who says: "What was bread before the [consecration]{g:consecracio} is the body of Christ after the consecration"; "that is," he says, "what is under the species of bread was first bread and afterwards the body of Christ". Here it seems that he speaks of that [which was] before the consecration, that the substance itself will afterwards be the body of Christ; which is very contrary to the same teaching, since at the end of the same question he says that "nothing of the bread will ever be anything of the body of Christ".[^29] Who then would believe this gloss, which so patently infects itself?

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 147.9: One body may be subjected to various motions at one time, as is shewn by Christ's body in different hosts.
- 148.1: The term 'conversion' is a mere subterfuge without any proper meaning, whereas esse intencionale admits all the required conditions.
- 148.30: Errors and inconsistency of Duns Scotus.
- 150.3: Foolish glosses on St. Augustine.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Inconveniens* "absurdity": the unfitting consequence of an argument, as the row's first sense. *Deducit*: the adversary of the preceding objections.

[^2]: **C.** The *differencie posicionis* are the six directions (up, down, before, behind, right, left): four carried, one raised, one lowered. D reads *defuerunt*, E *deferunt* (147.13); A *depremat*, D *deprimit* (147.15). C has *consecratam … hostiam* in the margin in another hand (147.13–15).

[^3]: **C.** *Consumptum* "completed" (D *consumata*; E *consumptim*, 147.21; E also *conversio diu*). The sense is that the conversion is past, so the present motion of the body in the host is not a conversion; if it were required, the body could not be moved in the host at all. AB read *quod*, E *potest* (147.23); C *quia* (147.24).

[^4]: **T.** *Cum hoc tamen* is Loserth's from the margin of C: the manuscripts lack *cum hoc* in the text (147.30); D reads *cum quod*. At 147.25 ABDE lack *est* in *sed est possibile* (C adds it in another hand); D reads *secundum* for *sed*. D has *diversa corpora*, B *cum omnibus* (147.27).

[^5]: **C/R.** *Mendicacio* "begging": the term is borrowed without title, as in begging the question (*petitur*, next sentence). E reads *mendicancium* at 148.13, "the definition of the mendicants", which makes the play on the friars explicit. AB read *victum laicalem* at 148.2, which C corrects.

[^6]: **C.** *Quin per idem in quamlibet et quidlibet assignandum*: the verb is understood from *convertitur*; *quamlibet* takes up *partem*. E reads *et sanguinem* for *vel sanguinem* (148.8); D *aliam* (148.9).

[^7]: **C.** *Alias* is the adverb "at other times", as at 148.30–149.1 (*alias demultiplicare*). E reads *et antecedens perdere* (148.12–13).

[^8]: **G.** *Continuare*: to make several contiguous bodies into one continuum, the act, not "carry on"; so *discontinuacio* below. B reads *accidente* (148.16); C in the margin *ipse* (148.17).

[^9]: **G.** *Species* here is the species of perception that the medium carries, not the sacramental species. **C.** *Capiens* is nominative and goes grammatically with *corpus*; the medium is what receives the species, and the rendering leaves the subject open. *Pius philosophus*: *pius* as in the row *pius pater*, "loving" (a philosopher of good will). C has *de esse* in the margin (148.26–27).

[^10]: **C.** *Quare non posset* is taken as a question, though the print closes with a full stop. *Illinc*, "from there" (from its place in heaven); C reads *illic* "there" (148.29).

[^11]: **S.** Loserth: the Subtle Doctor is Duns Scotus (148.30). Scotus on God's power to multiply one body in many places, *Ordinatio* IV d. 10 q. 2 (Vivès XVII, 190 ff.): *Deo simpliciter est possibile idem corpus simul facere in diversis locis localiter* (LEDGER K05i-1).

[^12]: **C.** *Prior secta* and *secta secunda*: those who deny and those who grant a body's multiplication without motion; Wyclif's point is that the first cannot condemn the second as heretics. AB read *in manere fidei* (149.8).

[^13]: **G.** *Inpossibilitare*, "make out as impossible": no row; formed like *hereticare*. E reads *Et hoc dubium* (149.11); ABCE *nitens*, ABD *nititur*, D *impossibiliter* (149.11–13).

[^14]: **S.** Augustine's teaching that quantity is distinct from its subject, as Wyclif recited it above: the place is 132–133 above, *De Trinitate* V.8 and V.10.11 (PL 42, 918), where without that distinction the creature's magnitude would make it God (LEDGER K05i-2). B omits *ut … subiecto* (149.18–20).

[^15]: **T/S.** "[He holds]" supplies Loserth's gloss on *pertinaciter*, "scilicet: teneat" (149.21). Loserth refers to Thomas, *In III Sent.* d. 2 q. 3 a. 3 "and elsewhere" (149.21: *Cf. Dist. II, q. III, art. III, pag. 13, 14 (et alibi)*, the book supplied from the text's *super Tercium*; not matched in the Parma numbering, where d. 2 has two questions; LEDGER K05i-3). E reads *implicat* (149.24).

[^16]: **C.** *Ante purgacionem*: before he is cleared of the contradiction, as one cleared by canonical purgation. D reads *nulli* for *nullibi* (149.26).

[^17]: **C.** The argument runs through the absurdities of motion in a vacuum: if earth could pass through a void to its place while resting, either gaining position would itself be motion, or a void would give position, or there would be two immediate instants, or an indivisible would move successively. ACDE read *pura* for *pure* (149.28); D *vel secundo* (150.1).

[^18]: **S.** Augustine that quantity and quality cannot be without a subject, the places of ch. 3, 72.3–73.18: *De quantitate animae* 4.6 (PL 32, 1038–1039), *Soliloquia* II.12.22–13.23 (PL 32, 895–896) and *Ep.* 187.6.18 (PL 33, 838), where the gloss *nisi fiat miraculum* is already refused (LEDGER K05i-4). *Dicitur* opens Wyclif's answer: their own way with Augustine, turned on their authorities. ABC read *impossibile summe* (150.4); D *quantitas et*, E omits *qualitas*, B *vel aliud* (150.6); D *allegandam*, E *alleganda*, B *opposicionem* (150.8); B *sed inquam* (150.11).

[^19]: **T.** *Pictacia*, "patchwork" (*pittacium*, a patch); BCD read *pictancia*, "pittance" (150.15). The print is translated. **C.** *Sentencia* "teaching", Wyclif's position. *Cum panis convertitur* is taken as causal, "since" (our teaching also says that the bread is converted); it may be temporal, "when [we say that] the bread is converted".

[^20]: **C.** The Saint is Thomas, to whom the opponents ascribe the argument (so *imponitur sancto Thome* at 150.30–151.1); Wyclif suspects the attribution. D reads *imposita* (150.18).

[^21]: **C.** *Hoc* is neuter, *hic* masculine: if the bread remained, the argument runs, the pronoun would point to the bread and Christ would have had to say "this one is my body". Thomas, *ST* III q. 75 a. 2 co. (*Sed potius esset dicendum, hic est corpus meum*) (LEDGER K05i-5); Loserth's foot at 151.5, which also names this article, belongs to the Damascene gloss below.

[^22]: **S.** 2 Cor. 11:26, in the singular *periculum*: the Vulgate has *periculis in falsis fratribus*, Douay–Rheims "in perils from false brethren". The "false brethren" answer to the *pseudofratres* of the sentence. E reads *enim usitavi* (150.21); D *periclitent* (150.25).

[^23]: **S.** The decree of the Roman church is *Ego Berengarius* (*De cons.* D. 2 c. 42), as Wyclif names it at 117.1–3: *decreto Romane ecclesie, De Consecracione, distinccione IIa, capitulo: Ego Beringarius* (LEDGER K05i-6). E reads *irregulariter* (150.26).

[^24]: **S.** *Subintroeuncium* recalls the *subintroductos falsos fratres* of Gal. 2:4. E reads *adulatores*, D *maculat* (150.28–29).

[^25]: **S.** John Damascene, *De fide orthodoxa* IV.13 (PG 94.1147), as recited in Thomas, *In IV Sent.* d. 11 q. 1 a. 1 qc. 1 arg. 1 and glossed at ad 1 (*verbum Damasceni intelligendum est quantum ad species*); Loserth refers also to *ST* III q. 75 a. 2 and Wyclif's *De apostasia* (LEDGER K05i-7, K05i-8). E reads *recitant* (151.1).

[^26]: **S.** John 8:44, the devil, "a liar, and the father thereof".

[^27]: **S.** Loserth: the passage is not in the Gloss on *De cons.* D. 2 in Jenson's edition of 1477 (151.10; LEDGER K05i-9). *Super additis … primo* is translated as printed; E reads *de consecracione distinccione II* (151.10–11).

[^28]: **C.** *Ista glossa* now means the gloss imputed to Thomas on *IV Sent.* d. 11, set against the same commentary's q. 4.

[^29]: **S.** Ambrose as in *De cons.* D. 2 c. 55 *Panis est in altari* (Loserth's reference at 151.14, 27; the form of the reference is ours), and the commentary's "question 4", which is Thomas, *In IV Sent.* d. 11 q. 1 a. 4, qc. 1 ad 1 (*quod est sub speciebus panis, primo fuit panis, et postea corpus Christi*) and, at its end, qc. 4 co. (*nihil panis unquam erit aliquid corporis Christi*) (LEDGER K05i-10). B reads *eiusdem* (151.20); D *nunquam*, ABC *corpus* (151.21); B *Quid ergo* (151.22).
