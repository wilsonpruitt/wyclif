# *De ecclesia*, chapter 4, part 5: the Roman church is the whole church militant, not the pope and his college

## ¶1 · 84.23–86.2

> [p. 84] Non ergo est nova ficticia sic loqui de unitate ecclesie, nec dubium quin sit illa que post diem iudicii erit regnum celorum, celestis Jerusalem et sponsa Christi (ut supra meminit Augustinus). Ex istis sequitur ut sepe repecii) quod nimis magna foret presumpcio quemquam sine formidine vel revelacione asserere quod sit membrum illius ecclesie. Nam nemo nisi predestinatus et sanctus tempore suo sine macula vel ruga est [p. 85] membrum illius ecclesie. Sed nemo sine formidine vel revelacione assereret quod sit predestinatus et sanctus sine macula vel ruga: ergo conclusio. Unde miror qua fronte magis contendentes pro proprietate bonorum ecclesie, magis seculariter viventes, elongati a Christi conversacione et plus steriles ab execucione Christi consilii et precepti plus vendicant titulo participii illius ecclesie bona fortunii per accidens adiacencia illi ecclesie, quam fecerunt apostoli vel sancti patres priores. Numquid credimus quod sic placitantes sunt sancti sine macula mortalis vel ruga venialis? Si dicunt quod sic, desercio Christi consilii cum ocio sancti officii hos condempnat. Jus enim humanum non dat eis titulum taliter vendicandi, et ius divinum docet quod rapinam bonorum cum gaudio susciperent et que sua sunt non sic repeterent (ut patet cap. III° ex testimonio beati Gregorii). Quo iure ergo sic vendicant, cum nitendo sic illi titulo ab eodem se privant? Considerarent ergo condiciones sancte matris ecclesie, antequam redarguti ex proprio opere contra Christi consilia sic instarent. Et quantum ad dictum de Romana ecclesia, suppono quod illa dicatur sancta mater ecclesia catholica, sponsa Christi. Et patet quod illa diffunditur, ubicunque sunt fideles predestinati; unde (ut tetigi superius) Augustinus in epistola ad Dardanum cap. XI°, exponens illud Johannis XVIII°, 14, Expedit ut unus homo moriatur pro populo etc. Cayphas, inquit, prophetavit quod Deus filius suos congregaret in unum. Non, inquit, in aliquem unum locum corporalem, sed congregavit in unum spiritum et unum corpus cuius unum caput est Christus. Ex quo patet quod unitas sancte matris ecclesie stat in [p. 86] unitate fidei et virtutum, licet fideles quantumcunque varie localiter sint diffusi.

It is not, therefore, a new fiction to speak thus of the unity of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, nor is
there doubt that it is that [church] which after the day of judgement will be the
[kingdom]{g:regnum} of heaven, the heavenly Jerusalem and the [bride]{g:sponsa} of Christ (as Augustine
mentioned above). From these [it follows]{g:sequitur} (as I have often repeated) that it would be too
great a presumption for anyone to assert without [misgiving]{g:formido} or revelation that he is a
member of that church. For no one except one [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} and holy in his time,
without spot or wrinkle, is [85] a member of that church. Now no one would assert without misgiving or
revelation that he is predestinate and holy without spot or wrinkle: therefore the
conclusion.[^1] Hence I wonder with what face those who contend the more for the
ownership of the goods of the church, living the more worldly, removed far from Christ's [manner of
life]{g:conversacio} and the more barren of the carrying out of Christ's counsel and precept, claim
the more, by [title]{g:titulus} of participation in that church, the goods of fortune that by
accident lie next to that church, than the apostles or the earlier holy fathers did.[^2] Do we believe
that those who plead [at law] in this way are saints without the spot of mortal [sin] or the wrinkle
of venial? If they say so, the desertion of Christ's counsel, with idleness in the holy office,
condemns them. For human [law]{g:ius} does not give them a title to claim in such a way, and divine
law teaches that they should take the plundering of their goods with joy, and not so ask back what is
theirs (as is clear in chapter 3 from the testimony of blessed Gregory).[^3] By what right, then, do
they so claim, since by striving thus for that title they deprive themselves of it? Let them consider,
then, the conditions of holy mother church, before, [refuted]{g:redarguere} by their own work, they
press [their case] thus against Christ's counsels. And as to what was said of the Roman church, I
suppose that it is called holy mother catholic church, the bride of Christ.[^4] And it is clear that
it is spread abroad wherever the predestinate faithful are; hence (as I touched on above) Augustine,
in the letter to Dardanus, chapter 11, expounding that [text] of John 18:14, "It is expedient that one
man should die for the people," etc.: "Caiaphas," he says, "prophesied that God would gather his sons
into one. Not," he says, "into some one bodily place, but he gathered [them] into one spirit and one
body, whose one [head]{g:caput} is Christ."[^5] From which it is clear that the unity of holy mother
church stands in [86] the unity of faith and of the virtues, although the faithful are spread abroad
in place, however variously.

## ¶2 · 86.3–88.19

> [p. 86] Unde beatus Isidorus, libro primo De Summo Bono cap. XIX°, ita scribit: Sancta ecclesia ideo dicitur catholica, eo quod universaliter per omnem sit mundum diffusa. Et Hugo, libro II° De Sacramentis parte cap. II°: Sancta ecclesia, id est, universitas fidelium corpus Christi vocatur. Et istam sentenciam innuunt decreta XXIª d.: Quamvis universe per orbem catholice ecclesie unus thalamus sit Christi, tamen sancta Romana ecclesia catholica et apostolica multis sinodicis institutis ceteris ecclesiis prelata est. Quod probat ex illo Matthei XVI°: Tu es Petrus et super hanc petram edificabo ecclesiam meam. Et illam ecclesiam vocat postmodum ecclesiam Romanam, primam apostoli Petri sedem, non habentem maculam neque rugam. Ista ecclesia non potest intelligi dumtaxat iste papa cum istis cardinalibus ac sua familia, cum ipsi cotidie recedunt et veniunt et crebrius quam alius fidelis populus sunt rugosi. Unde super isto textu sic dicit glosa: Argumentum, inquit, est quod ubicunque sunt boni ibi est Romana ecclesia. Et hoc argumentum daret plenam fidem fidelibus. Et sic intelligo decretum XXIVª, q. 1 A recta, ubi canon de Romana ecclesia sic loquitur: Hec est sancta et apostolica mater omnium ecclesiarum Christi ecclesia que per Dei omnipotentis graciam a tramite apostolice tradicionis nunquam errasse probatur nec hereticis succubuit. Ubi notum est hoc [p. 87] non posse intelligi de quolibet papa et suis domesticis. Ideo glosa sic loquitur: Quero, inquit, de qua ecclesia hic loquitur, quia non potest intelligi de papa qui dicitur ecclesia, ut supra eodem, Quodcunque, et VIIª q. prima: Scire debes. Sed certum est quod papa errare potest (ut XIX dist. Anastasius et XL dist. Si papa). Ideo nec ipse nec eius familia est illa ecclesia de qua hic dicitur quod errare non potest. Unde ipsa congregacio fidelium dicitur hec ecclesia (ut De Consecracione d. Iª, Ecclesia). Et sic intelligitur dictum beati Jeronymi positum XXIVª, q. i. Hec est fides. Sancta, inquit, est Romana ecclesia que semper immaculata permansit domino providente et beato Petro apostolo opem ferente in futurum manebit sine ulla hereticorum insultacione atque firma et immobilis omni tempore persistet. Hic non potest intelligi quicunque papa cum suo collegio. Illi enim sunt sepius maculati et insultacione hereticorum tam ab intra quam ab extra tamquam fragilis viator concussi, ut oculata fide patet diebus nostris, cum due partes cardinalium inponunt papa nostro et fratribus eum foventibus hereticam pravitatem, cum (ut inquiunt) nedum nituntur auferre privilegia Romane ecclesie, sed quantum in eis est ipsam destruere. Et omnis talis est hereticus (ut patet XXIIª dist. Omnes, ut patet libro VI°, tractatu de heresi). Cum ergo iuxta decreta Romana ecclesia habet primatum et dignitatem quoad Deum super omnes alias, patet quod illa est totalis ecclesia militans quam Deus plus diligit quam aliam eius partem. Et sic manifeste sequitur ex fide quod non illud collegium sed tota mater in omni gente et lingua dispersa [p. 88] sit illa sancta Romana ecclesia de qua iura loquuntur cum sanctis doctoribus. Unde ad imprimendum in nobis istam sentenciam in cantu ecclesie per beatos Ambrosium et Augustinum composito mater nostra sic Deum alloquitur: Te per orbem terrarum sancta confitetur ecclesia. Et in canone misse primo et principaliter offerimus pro sancta ecclesia catholica, ut Deus dignetur pacificare, custodire et adiuvare eam toto orbe terrarum. Unde non dubium oratur pro principalissima ecclesia militante quam suppono esse Romanam ecclesiam, verumptamen inter partes eius in comparacione ad quantitatem sunt papa et suum collegium pars precipua dignitate, dum tamen sequantur Christum propinquius et deserendo fastum atque primatum serviant matri sue efficacius atque humilius. Nam faciendo oppositum sunt nidus hereticorum, apostema putridum et ydolum desolacionis cum aliis monstrosis nominibus in sacra pagina prophetatis.

Hence blessed Isidore, in the first book *On the Highest Good*, chapter 19, writes thus: "The holy
church is called catholic for this reason, that it is spread universally through the whole world."
And Hugh, in book 2 *On the Sacraments*, part [2], chapter 2: "The holy church, that is, [the whole
body of the faithful]{g:universitas fidelium}, is called the body of Christ."[^6] And the decrees
intimate this [teaching]{g:sentencia}, distinction 21: "Although all the catholic churches throughout
the world are the one bridal chamber of Christ, yet the holy Roman church, catholic and apostolic, has
been set over the other churches by many synodal decrees." Which it proves from that [text] of
Matthew 16: "Thou art Peter; and upon this rock I will build my church." And afterwards it calls
that church the Roman church, the first see of the apostle Peter, "not having spot or wrinkle."[^7]
This church cannot be understood as only this [pope]{g:papa} with these cardinals and his
[household]{g:familia}, since they daily depart and come, and are wrinkled more often than the rest of
the faithful people. Hence on this text the [gloss]{g:glosa} says thus: "It is an argument," it says,
"that wherever the good are, there is the Roman church." And this argument would give full faith to
the faithful.[^8] And so I understand the decree 24 q. 1, *A recta*, where the canon speaks thus of the
Roman church: "This is the holy and apostolic church, mother of all the churches of Christ, which by
the grace of almighty God is proved never to have erred from the path of apostolic tradition, nor has
it succumbed to heretics." Where it is known that this [87] cannot be understood of any pope whatever
and his domestics.[^9] Therefore the gloss speaks thus: "I ask," it says, "of which church it speaks
here; because it cannot be understood of the pope, who is called the church (as above in the same
[question], *Quodcunque*, and 7 q. 1, *Scire debes*). But it is certain that the pope can err (as dist.
19, *Anastasius*, and dist. 40, *Si papa*). Therefore neither he nor his household is that church of
which it is said here that it cannot err. Hence the congregation of the faithful itself is called this
church (as *De consecratione*, dist. 1, *Ecclesia*)."[^10] And so is understood the saying of blessed
Jerome placed at 24 q. 1, *Hec est fides*: "Holy," he says, "is the Roman church, which has always
remained unspotted, and, the Lord providing and blessed Peter the apostle bringing help, will remain in
the future without any assault of heretics, and will persist firm and immovable for all time."[^11]
Here cannot be understood any pope whatever with his college. For they are rather often spotted, and
shaken by the assault of heretics from within as well as from without, as a frail
[wayfarer]{g:viator} [is], as is clear to eyewitness faith in our days, since two parts of the
cardinals impute [heretical depravity]{g:heretica pravitas} to our pope and to the brethren who support
him, since (as they say) they strive not only to take away the privileges of the Roman church but, as
far as in them lies, to destroy it.[^12] And every such [man] is a heretic (as is clear from dist. 22,
*Omnes*; as is clear in the Sixth Book, in the title on heresy).[^13] Since, therefore, according to the
decrees the Roman church has the primacy and dignity with respect to God over all others, it is clear
that it is the total [church militant]{g:ecclesia militans}, which God loves more than any other part
of it. And so it follows manifestly from faith that not that college but the whole mother, dispersed
in every nation and tongue, [88] is that holy Roman church of which the laws speak, with the holy
doctors.[^14] Hence, to imprint this teaching in us, in the church's chant composed by blessed Ambrose
and Augustine, [our mother]{g:mater nostra} thus addresses God: "Thee the holy church throughout the
world doth acknowledge." And in the canon of the mass we offer first and chiefly for the holy catholic
church, that God may vouchsafe to give it peace, to guard and to help it throughout the whole
world.[^15] Hence without doubt prayer is made for the most principal church militant, which I suppose
to be the Roman church; nevertheless, among its parts, in comparison with respect to quantity, the
pope and his college are the chief part in dignity, provided however that they follow Christ more
closely and, deserting pride and primacy, serve their mother more effectively and more humbly. For,
doing the opposite, they are a nest of heretics, a putrid [abscess]{g:apostema} and the idol of
desolation, with other monstrous names prophesied in the sacred page.[^16]

## ¶3 · 88.20–88.30

> [p. 88] Et ne fingam istam sentenciam, ecce decretum ecclesie De Penitencia d. Iª: Ecclesia, inquit, Christi non habet maculam neque rugam aut aliquid huiusmodi; qui ergo peccatorum sorde aliqua maculatus est, de ecclesia Christi non potest appellari. Maculam, inquit glosa, id est, heresim vel mortale peccatum, neque rugam, idest, scisma vel veniale. Sed hoc credo ego cum sancto Thoma debere modificari ad statum patrie, et hic quicunque est in macula mortalis peccati, protunc deficit ab humido fluent continuante, hoc est, gracia secundum presentem iusticiam.

And lest I feign this teaching, behold the decree of the church, *De penitencia*, dist. 1: "The church
of Christ," it says, "has not spot or wrinkle or any such thing; he therefore who is spotted with any
filth of sins cannot be called of the church of Christ." "Spot," says the gloss, "that is, heresy or
mortal sin; nor wrinkle, that is, schism or venial [sin]."[^17] But this, I believe with St Thomas,
ought to be modified to the state of the [homeland]{g:patria}; and here whoever is in the spot of
mortal sin is, for that time, lacking in the continuing [flowing moisture]{g:humidum fluens}, that is,
grace according to present justice.[^18]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 84.25: Hence it is presumption to assert oneself to be a member of the Church.
- 85.4: Yet this is done by prelates, whose lives are most unlike Christ's.
- 85.22: The Roman Church may be used as a name of the Catholic Church, including all the faithful.
- 86.17: It cannot be only the Pope and the Curia;
- 87.10: since Jerome's description of the Roman Church applies to them only by contrast;
- 87.28: but must be the whole Church militant.
- 88.12: The Pope and his college are chief in dignity if they follow Christ;
- 88.17: otherwise they are a nest of heretics.
