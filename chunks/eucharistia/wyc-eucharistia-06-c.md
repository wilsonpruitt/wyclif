# *De eucharistia*, chapter 6, part 3: Gregory on the paschal lamb, how and by whom it is eaten

## ¶1 · 165.17–168.8

> [p. 165] Sed sola redemptoris nostri percepta sacramenta ad veram sollempnitatem mentis non sufficiunt nisi eis quoque et bona opera iungantur. Quid enim prodest corpus et sanguinem illius ore percipere et e perversis moribus contraire? Unde bene ad comedendum subditur: Et azimos panes cum lactucis aggrestibus. Panes quippe sine fermento comedit qui recta opera sine corrupcione vane glorie exercet, qui mandata misericordie sine admixtione peccati exhibet, ne perverse diripiat quod quasi recte dispensat. Hoc quoque peccati fermentum miscuerant bone sue accioni quibus prophete voce per increpacionem Dominus [p. 166] dicebat: Venite ad Bethel et impie agite. Atque post pauca: Et sacrificate de fermentato laudem. De fermentato namque laudem immolat qui Deo sacrificium de rapina parat. Lactuce vero aggrestes valde amare sunt. Carnes autem agni cum lactucis aggrestibus sunt edende, ut cum corpus Redemptoris accipimus, nos pro peccatis nostris in fletibus affligamus, quatenus ipsa amaritudo penitencie abstergat a mentis stomacho perverse humorem vite. Ubi et subditur: Non comedetis ex eo crudum quid neque coctum aqua. Ecce iam nos ipsa verba historie ab intellectu historiaco repellunt. Numquid, fratres karissimi, Israeliticus ille populus in Egypto constitutus comedere agnum crudum consueverat, ut ei lex dicat, non comedetis ex eo crudum quid? Ubi et additur: Nec coctum aqua. Sed quid aqua nisi humanam scienciam designat iuxta hoc quod per Salomonem dicitur sub hereticorum voce: Aque furtive dulciores sunt. Quid crude agni carnes, nisi inconsideratam ac sine reverencia cogitacionis relictam illius humanitatem. Omne enim quod subtiliter cogitamus quasi mente coquimus. Sed agni caro nec cruda edenda est nec aqua cocta, quia redemptor noster nec purus homo estimandus est neque per humanam sapienciam qualiter Deus incarnari potuit cogitandus. Omnis enim qui redemptorem nostrum purum hominem credit quid iste aliud quam crudas agni carnes comedit quas videlicet coquere per divinitatis eius intelligenciam noluit? Omnis vero qui incarnacionis eius misteria iuxta humanam sapienciam discutere conatur carnes agni [p. 167] aqua vult coquere, id est, dispensacionis eius misterium per dissolutam vult scienciam penetrare. Qui ergo paschalis gaudii sollempnitatem celebrare desiderat agnum nec aqua coquat nec crudum comedat, ut neque per humanam sapienciam profunditatem incarnacionis illius penetrare appetat neque in eum tamquam in hominem purum credat, sed assas igne carnes comedat, ut dispensari omnia per Sancti Spiritus potenciam sciat. De quo adhuc recte subiungitur: Caput cum pedibus et intestinis vorabitis, quia Redemptor noster est alpha et o, Deus videlicet ante secula et homo in fine seculorum. Et sicut iam prediximus, fratres, Paulo attestante didicimus quod caput Christi Deus; caput ergo agni vorare est divinitatis illius fidem percipere, pedes vero agni vorare est vestigia humanitatis illius amando et imitando perquirere. Quid vero sunt intestina nisi verborum illius occulta et mistica mandata? Que tunc voramus, cum verba vite cum aviditate sumimus. In quo devoracionis verbo quid aliud quam pigricie nostre torpor deprehenditur? Qui eius verba atque misteria per nosmetipsos non requirimus et dicta ab aliis audimus inviti. Non remanebit ex eo quidquam usque mane, quia eius dicta magna sunt sollicitudine discucienda, quatenus prius quam dies resurreccionis appareat in hac presentis vite nocte omnia mandata eius intelligendo et operando penetrentur. Sed quia valde difficile est, ut omne sacrum eloquium possit [p. 168] intelligi et omne eius misterium penetrari, recte subiungitur: Si quid remanserit, igni conburetis. Quod ex agno remanet igni conburimus, quando hoc quod de misterio incarnacionis eius intelligere et penetrare non possumus, potestati Sancti Spiritus humiliter reservamus, ut non superbe quis audeat vel contempnere vel denunciare quod non intelligit: sed hoc igni tradat, cum Spiritui Sancto reservat.

"But the [sacraments]{g:sacramentum} of our Redeemer, received alone, do not suffice for a true solemnity of the mind unless good works also are joined to them.[^1] For what does it profit to receive his body and blood with the mouth and to oppose him by perverse ways? Hence it is well added to the eating: 'and unleavened bread with wild lettuce.' For he eats loaves without leaven who does right works without the corruption of vainglory, who performs the commandments of mercy without the admixture of sin, lest he perversely plunder what he dispenses as though rightly. This leaven of sin too they had mixed into their good action to whom the Lord[^2] [166] said by the voice of the prophet, in rebuke: 'Come ye to Bethel, and do wickedly.' And a little after: 'And offer a sacrifice of praise with leaven.'[^3] For he offers praise with leaven who prepares a sacrifice for God out of plunder. And the wild lettuces are very bitter. And the flesh of the lamb is to be eaten with wild lettuces, so that when we receive the body of the Redeemer we may afflict ourselves with weeping for our sins, so that the very bitterness of penance may wipe from the stomach of the mind the humour of a perverse life. Where it is also added: 'You shall not eat thereof any thing raw, nor boiled in water.' Behold, the very words of the narrative now drive us back from a historical understanding.[^4] Did that Israelite people, settled in Egypt, dearly beloved brethren, use to eat the lamb raw, that the law should say to it, 'You shall not eat thereof any thing raw'? Where it is also added: 'nor boiled in water.' But what does water designate but human knowledge, according to what is said by Solomon in the voice of the heretics: 'Stolen waters are sweeter'? What [is] the raw flesh of the lamb but his [humanity]{g:humanitas}, left unconsidered and without the reverence of thought?[^5] For all that we think out subtly we as it were cook in the mind. But the flesh of the lamb is to be eaten neither raw nor boiled in water, because our Redeemer is neither to be reckoned a mere man nor to be thought out by human wisdom, as to how God could be incarnate. For everyone who believes our Redeemer a mere man, what else does he eat but the raw flesh of the lamb, which, that is, he would not cook by understanding his [divinity]{g:divinitas}? And everyone who tries to discuss the [mysteries]{g:misterium} of his incarnation according to human wisdom wishes to cook the lamb's flesh [167] in water, that is, wishes to penetrate the mystery of his [dispensation]{g:dispensacio} by a dissolute knowledge. Therefore whoever desires to celebrate the solemnity of the paschal joy, let him neither cook the lamb in water nor eat it raw, so that he neither seeks to penetrate the depth of his incarnation by human wisdom nor believes in him as in a mere man, but let him eat the flesh roasted with fire, so that he may know that all things are dispensed by the [power]{g:potencia} of the Holy Spirit.[^6] Of which it is further rightly subjoined: 'you shall eat the head with the feet and entrails thereof,'[^7] because our Redeemer is alpha and o,[^8] that is, God before the ages and man at the end of the ages. And as we said before, brethren, we have learned on Paul's witness that 'the head of Christ is God'; so to devour the head of the lamb is to receive faith in his divinity, and to devour the feet of the lamb is to seek out the footprints of his humanity by loving and imitating. And what are the entrails but the hidden and mystical commandments of his words? These we devour when we take the words of life with avidity. In which word 'devouring' what else is caught out but the torpor of our sloth, who do not seek out his words and mysteries for ourselves, and hear unwillingly what is said by others? 'Neither shall there remain any thing of it until morning,' because his sayings are to be discussed with great care, so that before the day of the resurrection appears, all his commandments may be penetrated, by understanding and by doing, in this night of the present life.[^9] But because it is very difficult that all sacred eloquence can [168] be understood and all its mystery penetrated, it is rightly subjoined: 'If there be any thing left, you shall burn it with fire.'[^10] What remains of the lamb we burn with fire when what we cannot understand and penetrate of the mystery of his incarnation we humbly reserve to the [power]{g:potestas} of the Holy Spirit, so that no one should proudly dare either to despise or to denounce what he does not understand, but let him hand it over to the fire, when he reserves it to the Holy Spirit.

## ¶2 · 168.9–169.13

> [p. 168] Quia igitur qualiter edendum sit pasca agnovimus, nunc autem a qualibus edi debeat agnoscamus. Sequitur: Sic autem comedetis illum: Renes vestros accingetis. Quid in renibus nisi delectacio carnis accipitur? Unde et Psalmista postulat dicens: Ure renes meos. Si enim voluptatem in renibus esse nesciret, eos uri minime petisset. Unde quia potestas dyaboli in humano genere maxime per luxuriam prevaluit, de illo voce dominica dicitur: Potestas eius in lumbis eius. Qui ergo pasca comedit habere renes succinctos debet, ut qui sollempnitatem resurreccionis atque incorrupcionis agit corrupcioni iam per nulla vicia subiaceat, voluptates edomet, carnem a luxuria restringat. Neque enim cognovit que sit sollempnitas incorrupcionis qui adhuc corrupcioni per incontinenciam subiacet. Hec quibusdam dura sunt, sed angusta est via que ducit ad celum, et habuimus iam multa exempla continencium. Unde et adhuc subditur: Calceamenta habebitis in pedibus. Quid etenim sunt pedes nostri nisi opera? Quid vero calceamenta nisi pelles mortuorum animalium; calceamenta autem pedes muniunt. Que [p. 169] vero sunt mortua animalia, ex quorum pellibus nostri muniantur pedes, nisi antiqui patres qui nos ad eternam patriam precesserunt. Quorum dum exempla conspicimus, nostri operis pedes munimus. Calceamenta ergo in pedibus habere est mortuorum vitam conspicere et nostra vestigia a peccati vulnere custodire. Tenentes baculos in manibus. Quid lex per baculum nisi pastoralem custodiam designat? Et notandum quod prius precipimur renes accingere, postmodum baculos tenere, quia illi debent curam pastoralem suscipere, quia iam sciunt in suo corpore fluxa luxurie edomare, ut cum aliis forcia predicant, ipsi desideriis mollibus et voluptatibus non succumbant.

"Since, then, we have learned how the pasch is to be eaten, let us now learn by what sort of people it ought to be eaten. It follows: 'And thus you shall eat it: you shall gird your reins.' What is understood by the reins but the delight of the flesh? Hence the Psalmist too asks, saying: 'Burn my reins.' For if he did not know that pleasure is in the reins, he would by no means have asked that they be burned. Hence, because the power of the devil prevailed in the human race chiefly through lust, it is said of him by the Lord's voice: 'His power is in his loins.'[^11] He therefore who eats the pasch ought to have his reins girded, so that he who keeps the solemnity of the resurrection and of incorruption may no longer be subject to corruption through any vices, may tame his pleasures, may restrain the flesh from lust. For he does not know what the solemnity of incorruption is who is still subject to corruption through incontinence. These things are hard to some, but narrow is the way that leads to heaven,[^12] and we have had already many examples of the continent. Hence it is further added: 'and you shall have shoes on your feet.' For what are our feet but works? And what are shoes but the skins of dead animals? And shoes protect the feet. And what [169] are the dead animals from whose skins our feet are protected but the ancient fathers who have gone before us to the eternal [homeland]{g:patria}? While we look on their examples we protect the feet of our work. To have shoes on the feet, therefore, is to look on the life of the dead and to guard our footsteps from the wound of sin. 'Holding staves in your hands.' What does the law designate by the staff but pastoral guardianship? And it is to be noted that we are commanded first to gird the reins, afterwards to hold the staves, because those ought to take up pastoral care who already know how to tame the flux of lust in their own body,[^13] so that when they preach strong things to others, they themselves may not succumb to soft desires and pleasures.

## ¶3 · 169.14–169.26

> [p. 169] Bene autem subditur: Et comedetis festinanter. Notate, fratres, notate quod dicitur festinantes. Mandata Dei, misteria Redemptoris, celestis patrie gaudia cum festinacione cognoscite et precepta vite cum festinacione implere curate. Quia enim adhuc hodie licet bene agere scimus: utrum cras liceat ignoramus. Festinantes autem pascha comedite, id est, ad sollempnitatem patrie celestis anhelate. Nemo in huius vite itinere torpeat, ne in patria locum perdat. Nemo moras ad appetenda studia innectat sed cepta perficiat, ne minime liceat implere quod inchoat. Si ad amorem Dei pigri non sumus, adiuvat ipse quem amamus.

"And it is well added: 'and you shall eat in haste.' Note, brethren, note that it says 'hastening.'[^14] Learn with haste the commandments of God, the mysteries of the Redeemer, the joys of the heavenly homeland, and take care to fulfil with haste the precepts of life. For we know that today it is still permitted to do well; whether it will be permitted tomorrow we do not know. And eat the pasch hastening, that is, pant after the solemnity of the heavenly homeland. Let no one be sluggish on the journey of this life, lest he lose his place in the homeland. Let no one weave delays into the pursuits that are to be sought, but let him finish what he has begun, lest it be by no means permitted him to complete what he begins. If we are not slothful toward the love of God, he whom we love himself helps us."

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** The whole of this part continues the passage of Gregory the Great's Easter homily on Exod. 12 (*Hom. in Evang.* II.22, §§7–9 as we take it) that Wyclif quotes at length; Loserth's reference at 168.17 is "S. Gregorii l. c. pag. 1536, 1537" (LEDGER K06c-1). The quotation opened in the previous part; Gregory's words are therefore not set in quotation marks here, and only the scripture inside them is.
[^2]: **T.** 165.28 *Dominus*: absent in all the manuscripts; C adds it in the margin over an erasure. Loserth prints it, and we translate his print.
[^3]: **S.** Amos 4:4 and 4:5, both in the Vulgate's words (Loserth 166.1).
[^4]: **T.** 166.10 *historiaco*: so the print, for the expected *historico*; translated as "historical".
[^5]: **T.** 166.19 *inconsideratam*: the manuscripts read *inconsiderata*; Loserth's print gives the accusative that the construction with *humanitatem* requires.
[^6]: **T/C.** 167.8 *dispensari*: the manuscripts read *dispensare*; Loserth corrected it "from the text of St Gregory". **C.** *Potencia* is the CONTEXTUAL row potency / power: here the Holy Spirit's power.
[^7]: **S/C.** Exod. 12:9, in the Vulgate's words; Douay–Rheims "you shall eat" renders *vorabitis*, which is literally "you shall devour", and Gregory's gloss that follows turns on that verb (*vorare*, *devoracio*), so the gloss is rendered "devour".
[^8]: **T/S.** *Alpha et o*: so the print, the Greek letter *omega* given by its Latin letter (cf. Apoc. 1:8, Vulgate *alpha et omega*). 167.12 *Et* before *sicut* is Loserth's addition; the manuscripts lack it. The citation that follows is 1 Cor. 11:3.
[^9]: **T.** 167.26 *in hac presentis vite nocte omnia*: read on the plate. The manuscripts are confused here: ABE *vite vocacione communia*, D *vocacione omnia*, and C corrects in the margin to *omnia nocte*, which Loserth prints.
[^10]: **S.** Exod. 12:10. Gregory's *Si quid remanserit, igni conburetis* departs from the Vulgate *si quid residuum fuerit, igne comburetis*; the sense is the same, and the Douay–Rheims wording serves for both.
[^11]: **S.** Ps. 25:2 (Vulgate numbering), *ure renes meos*; Job 40:11, where the Vulgate has *Fortitudo ejus in lumbis ejus* (DR "His strength is in his loins") and Gregory *Potestas eius*: translated from his Latin.
[^12]: **S.** Cf. Matt. 7:14, where the Vulgate's way "leadeth to life" (*ad vitam*); Gregory has *ad celum*, "to heaven". The editor cites Matt. 7:13.
[^13]: **C.** 169.10 *quia iam sciunt*: the second *quia* is taken as introducing the qualification of *illi* ("those ... who already know"); read strictly causal it would give "because those ought to take up pastoral care, since they already know". A has *ille* in the text, *illi* above the line; C adds *illi debent* in the margin. At 169.12 ABCE read *sarcinam predicant ipsi desidiis* for the print's *forcia predicant ipsi desideriis*.
[^14]: **C/T.** Gregory comments on the participle *festinantes*, "hastening", though the verse as he gives it reads the adverb *festinanter*, "in haste" (Exod. 12:11; 169.15 E reads *festinanter* in the comment as well).
