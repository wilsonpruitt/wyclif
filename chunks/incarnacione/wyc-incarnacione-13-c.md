# *De benedicta incarnacione*, chapter 13, part 3: the moderns' three created substances; why the Word was incarnate; the doctors reconciled

## ¶1 · 223.17–225.5

> [p. 223] Unde miror quomodo moderni negantes Christum esse creaturam, et cum hoc admittentes tres personas divinas eandem humanitatem vel tres omnimode separatas assumere, dicunt quod substancia divina est tres homines et per consequens tres substancie; non enim tres substancie increate, cum tunc eternaliter ita essent. Si autem sit⁶ tres create substancie, tunc tres creature forent substancia divina per conversionem; et sic aliquid quod⁷ prius non fuerat. Si enim iste tres substancie differunt personaliter, tunc tres persone divine sunt tres substancie, quas notum est oportere distingui a communi divina substancia. Et si dicatur quod, postquam eternaliter erant persone et non substancie, facte sunt substancie, hoc est equivoce ad eternam substanciam vel personam; quia aliter unica substancia et non plures foret Trinitas increata. Et multo evidencius si tantum una humanitas sit assumpta a tota Trinitate, non essent tres [p. 224] materiales substancie, ut puta tria animalia vel tria corpora; et sic de ceteris passionibus humanis cum ceteris accidentibus separabilibus; quia adequatum corpus, compositum ex isto corpore et ista anima, est omne animal quod est homo in dato situ, eo quod eadem sunt principia singulorum, ut deductum est superius. Ymo videtur quod quilibet illorum trium hominum habeat peccatum maximum naturale, cum quilibet caret proprio capite, et sic de ceteris organis quibuscunque; et per idem sunt unum¹ corpus tantum. Licet autem tres persone assumendo tres homines forent indubie tres create substancie; tamen inconveniens est quod increata substancia sic plurificetur² in multas substancias, cum divina substancia non in plura dispergitur, sed in ipsam omnis pluralitas creature unite³ colligitur; quamvis enim Verbum sit due substancie, divina scilicet et humana, divina tamen substancia est tantum una substancia, cum non sit substancia creata: accipiendo enim huiusmodi substanciam pro natura et substanciam personaliter, sic non ponitur⁴ in numerum cum divina,⁵ cum sit ipsa. Unde videtur quod subiectum abstractum contrahat predicatum ad supponendum simpliciter pro natura, quando sic dicitur—substancia divina est substancia creata—et per consequens proposicio est neganda. Sed cum subiecto personali conceditur quod Verbum est substancia creata, quia homo et humanitas, quod non est deitas. Cuius racionis diversitas est variacio a supposicione simplici in personalem secundum limitacionem personalis termini vel abstracti. Et tunc non oportet concedere quod substancia increata sit creata substancia; licet Verbum, quod est increata substancia, sit creata substancia. Trinitas ergo foret in casu illo tres create substancie; et per consequens necessaria⁶ esset ad⁷ finem attingendum⁷ tanta in Deo pluralitas naturarum; quod est impossibile; cum ad redempcionem humani generis, ad beatitudinem hominis secundum [p. 225] corpus et animam, et quemlibet alium effectum eque sufficit Christi incarnacio, sicut incarnacio horum trium. Sicut ergo non sunt ponendi plures dii propter impossibilitatem superflui, sic nec plurium personarum incarnacio propter causam consimilem.

Hence I wonder how [the moderns]{g:moderni}, denying "Christ is a [creature]{g:creatura}", and with this admitting "three divine [persons]{g:persona} [assume]{g:assumere} the same [humanity]{g:humanitas}, or three wholly separate ones", say "the divine substance is three men", and consequently three substances; for not three uncreated substances, since then they would be so eternally. But if it is three created substances, then three creatures would be the divine substance by [conversion]{g:conversio};[^1] and so [it would be] something it had not been before. For if these three substances differ personally, then the three divine persons are three substances, which, it is known, must be distinguished from the common divine substance. And if it is said that, after they were eternally persons and not substances, they were made substances, this is [said] [equivocally]{g:equivoce} with respect to eternal substance or person; because otherwise the uncreated Trinity would be a single substance and not several. And much more evidently, if only one humanity is assumed by the whole Trinity, there would not be three [224] material substances, for example three animals or three bodies; and so of the other human [attributes]{g:passio} with the other separable [accidents]{g:accidens}; because the adequate body, composed of this body and this soul, is every animal that is a man in a given place, since the principles of the singulars are the same, as was deduced above. Indeed it seems that each of those three men would have the greatest natural defect, since each lacks a head of his own, and so of any other organs whatever;[^2] and by the same token they are one body only. And although three persons, by assuming three men, would undoubtedly be three created substances, yet it is [an absurdity]{g:inconveniens}[^3] that the uncreated substance should be thus multiplied into many substances, since the divine substance is not dispersed into many things, but into it all the plurality of the united creature is gathered; for although the [Word]{g:verbum} is two substances, namely divine and human, yet the divine substance is only one substance, since it is not a created substance: for, taking substance of this kind for the [nature]{g:natura}, and substance personally, it is thus not put into number with the divine, since it is it.[^4] Hence it seems that an [abstract]{g:abstractum} subject restricts the predicate to stand with [simple supposition]{g:supposicio-simplex} for the nature,[^5] when it is said thus: "the divine substance is a created substance"; and consequently the [proposition]{g:proposicio} is to be denied. But with a personal subject it is granted that "the Word is a created substance", because [he is] a man and a humanity, which is not the [Godhead]{g:deitas}. The diversity of this reasoning is a variation from simple supposition to personal, according to the limitation of a personal or an abstract term.[^6] And then it is not necessary to grant "the uncreated substance is a created substance", although the Word, which is uncreated substance, is created substance. The Trinity, then, would in that case be three created substances; and consequently so great a plurality of natures in God would be necessary for attaining the end;[^7] which is impossible, since for the redemption of the human race, for the blessedness of man as to [225] body and soul, and for any other effect whatever, the incarnation of Christ suffices equally, as would the incarnation of these three. Just as, then, several gods are not to be posited because of the impossibility of the superfluous, so neither is the incarnation of several persons, for a like cause.

## ¶2 · 225.6–225.20

> [p. 225] Ponunt autem doctores congruenciam¹ in Christo quare incarnatus est et nec Pater nec Spiritus sanctus. Primo quia² Verbum,² quod est naturalis ymago Patris; per quod factus est homo ad Dei ymaginem, per eandem ymaginem debuit reformari. Unde ex proprietate ymaginis fuit consonancius quod ymago ymaginem assumeret, quam potencia vel benevolencia alterius persone. Forme enim est reformare deformitatem; sapiencie vincere peccati maliciam remissibilem ex ignorancia contractam; et ymaginis exemplaris fabricam turpatam ad dictam ymaginem prius factam [revocare].³ Verumptamen sicut vir non peccavit nisi excitante muliere et dyabolo, et sic iuvamine alterius⁴ debuit relevari:⁵ sic nec persona Verbi fecit redempcionis misterium sine coefficiencia Trinitatis, aliter tamen Verbum quam duo alii.

And the doctors posit a [fittingness]{g:congruencia} in Christ, why he was incarnate and neither the Father nor the Holy Spirit.[^8] First, because the Word is the natural image of the Father; through him man was made to the image of God, [and] through the same image he had to be reformed.[^9] Hence from the property of an image it was more consonant that the image should assume the image than the [power]{g:potencia} or benevolence of another person. For it belongs to form to reform deformity; to wisdom to overcome the malice of sin, remissible [as] contracted from ignorance; and to the exemplar image to [restore] the defaced workmanship to the said image after which it was first made.[^10] Nevertheless, just as the [man]{g:vir} did not sin except at the instigation of the woman and the devil, and so had to be relieved by the help of another, other than the devil,[^11] so neither did the person of the Word perform the [mystery]{g:misterium} of redemption without the co-working of the Trinity, though the Word [did so] otherwise than the two others.

## ¶3 · 225.21–225.31

> [p. 225] 2ᵈᵒ cum Verbum sit Dei Filius naturalis, habet in⁶ Trinitate⁶ principium a quo mittatur, in cuius auctoritate vel virtute operetur obedienter humanitus. Unde filiacio temporalis habet proprietates analogas in Filio naturali aliter quam haberet in Patre vel Spiritu. Nam utrobique nascitur ex suo principio, et manet Filius faciens nos filios adoptivos⁷ et⁸ heredes, ut ipse est primogenitus;⁸ et hoc sine confusione filiorum⁹ cognacionis in Deo, quod Patri innascibili non potest competere. Et si iste conveniencie videntur nobis ignaris modice, tamen sunt apud Deum, qui ponderat cuncta, ad regulam infringibiliter perficiende.

Secondly, since the Word is the natural Son of God, he has in the Trinity a principle by which he may be sent, in whose [authority]{g:auctoritas} or power he may work obediently, [humanly]{g:humanitus}.[^12] Hence temporal [sonship]{g:filiacio} has analogous properties in the natural Son, otherwise than it would have in the Father or the Spirit. For in both places he is born from his principle, and remains the Son, making us adoptive sons and heirs, as he himself is the firstborn;[^13] and this without a confusion of sons or of kinship in God, which could not befit the unbegotten Father. And if these [agreements]{g:conveniencia} seem slight to us in our ignorance, yet with God, who weighs all things, they are to be perfected unbreakably to the rule.

## ¶4 · 226.1–226.25

> [p. 226] 3° concludendo¹ breviter, non est aliqua Verbi proprietas quin limitat Verbo² congruere² incarnacionis misterium ultra quam aliis. Mediam quidem³ personam oportet esse mediatorem Dei et hominum; Verbum eternaliter de corde ingenito eructatum decet verbis predicacionis ostendere Patrem suum Ioh. 17°. ‘Pater, manifestavi nomen tuum;’ ‘Dei virtus atque Dei sapiencia,’ prima ad Cor. 1°, debuit vicium ex ignorancia superare;⁴ ut homo, qui⁴ in sapiencia conditus est per insipienciam perditus appetendo equalitatem increate sapiencie, per illam sapienciam restauretur, et dyabolus usurpando acceptam virtutem et potenciam⁵ per dantem⁶ virtutem et⁵ bracchium deprimeretur, Luc 1°. ‘fecit potenciam in bracchio suo.’ Et sicut secundum Augustinum est equalitas immediate consequens ad suum principium, sic ab eo specialiter omnis morbi inequalitas ad temperanciam reducatur: ut inequalitas sanitatis in qua dyabolus cecidit, appetendo equalitatem divine auctoritatis sive potencie, secundum iudicium datum Filio per impotenciam puniatur, qua non possit a peccato in Spiritum⁷ sanctum resurgere; inequalitas autem, in qua homo ignoranter peccando in sapienciam, stulte appetendo eius equalitatem, ceciderat, est⁸ precise equalitatis terminus,⁹ cum sit sapiencia, verbum, virtus, pulcritudo, et species reducenda; et ita de ceteris sacramentis que scriptura de Christo commemorat.

Thirdly, concluding briefly, there is no property of the Word that does not [assign]{g:limitare} to the Word that the mystery of the incarnation befits [him] beyond the others. The middle person, indeed, must be the mediator of God and men;[^14] it befits the Word, eternally uttered from the unbegotten heart, to show his Father by words of preaching, John 17[:6]: "Father, I have manifested thy name";[^15] "the power of God and the wisdom of God", 1 Corinthians 1[:24], had to overcome the vice [that comes] from ignorance; so that man, who was established in wisdom [and] was lost through folly by desiring equality with the uncreated wisdom, might be restored through that wisdom; and that the devil, by usurping the power and might he had received, might be pressed down through the power and arm of the one who gave [them], Luke 1[:51]: "He hath shewed might in his arm."[^16] And as, according to Augustine, equality follows [without intermediary]{g:immediate} upon its principle,[^17] so by him in particular every inequality of disease should be brought back to [temperance]{g:temperancia}: so that the inequality of health in which the devil fell, desiring equality of divine authority or power, should be punished, according to the judgement given to the Son, by an impotence through which he cannot rise again from the sin against the Holy Spirit;[^18] and the inequality in which man, sinning ignorantly against wisdom by foolishly desiring its equality, had fallen, is to be brought back through precisely the term of equality, since he is wisdom, word, power, beauty, and form;[^19] and so of the other [sacraments]{g:sacramentum}[^20] which scripture records of Christ.

## ¶5 · 226.26–226.30

> [p. 226] Redeundo igitur ad propositum ex dictis colligitur quod quelibet trium predictarum opinionum sentenciat¹⁰ fidei consonum quoad superius recitatum. Et sic concordande sunt apparentes doctorum repugnancie in tam alta materia fidei Christiane.

Returning, therefore, to the purpose, it is gathered from what has been said that each of the three aforesaid [opinions]{g:opinio} [pronounces]{g:sentenciare} what is consonant with the faith as to what was rehearsed above. And so the apparent [incompatibilities]{g:repugnancia} of the doctors are to be brought into agreement in so high a matter of the Christian faith.[^21]

## ¶6 · 226.31–227.26

> [p. 226] Quod si obicitur omnes tres sectas concorditer admittere quod quelibet persona¹¹ divina eandem naturam assumat [p. 227] vel quotlibet¹ separatim; dicitur tripliciter concordando istam radicem. Primo quod in ista materia opinative locuti sunt nichil temere asserendo, ut patet ex scriptis eorum. 2ᵈᵒ quod ipsi intelligunt ipsam possibilitatem condicionaliter, si Deus voluerit; ad modum loquendi Anselmi. Vel 3° si replicando inutiliter de sensibus eorum argueretur, quod ipsi sic simpliciter sencierunt, potest dici quod non ad laudem eorum opinio eorum forsitan fuit talis. Et taliter glosant ipsi superiores doctores, quorum sensus pocius debet credi. Sed non valet—si isti sic senciunt opinando; ergo verum—cum per idem contradiccio sequeretur; ipsi enim discrepant a seipsis. Sed et sanctus Thomas super Dist. 6. 3ⁱⁱ Sentenciarum Questione....,² dicit quod sentencia concedens humanitatem assumptam esse Christum non est heretica, licet sit impossibilis in precipua materia fidei. De quo miror, cum tot sancti doctores qui in aliis materiis ambigue sunt locuti, illam sentenciam in materia fidei affirmaverant tam constanter. Cum ergo pium et salubre foret de fide Christiana sentenciam impossibilem tocius fidei corruptivam investigando excludere, videtur quod ecclesia sollicite discuteret quid in ista materia foret catholice sustinendum; quia certum est quod una pars est formaliter impossibilis; ex qua sequitur oppositum tocius fidei, ymo ut noverunt logici, quidlibet inferendum; et talis latens error in principio per vias inopinatas infirmat partes fidei consequenter.

But if it is objected that all three [sects]{g:secta}[^22] with one accord admit "each divine person assumes the same nature, [227] or any number separately", the answer is, bringing this root into agreement in three ways: first, that in this matter they spoke by way of opinion, asserting nothing rashly, as is clear from their writings. Secondly, that they understand that possibility conditionally, "if God should will", after Anselm's manner of speaking.[^23] Or thirdly, if, by rejoining uselessly about their meanings, it were argued that they thought so [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}, it can be said that perhaps their opinion was such, not to their praise. And in this way the superior doctors themselves [gloss]{g:glosare} [it], whose sense ought rather to be believed. But it does not hold: "if these think so by way of opinion, therefore it is true", since by the same token a contradiction would follow; for they disagree with themselves. Moreover, Saint Thomas too, on distinction 6 of the third [book] of the *Sentences*, question ...,[^24] says that the [teaching]{g:sentencia} granting "the assumed humanity is Christ" is not heretical, although it is impossible in a principal matter of faith. At which I wonder, since so many holy doctors, who in other matters have spoken ambiguously, affirmed that teaching in a matter of faith so constantly. Since, then, it would be pious and salutary to exclude from the Christian faith, by investigation, an impossible teaching, corruptive of the whole faith, it seems that the [church]{g:ecclesia} should carefully discuss what in this matter should be held as catholic; because it is certain that one side is formally impossible; from which follows the opposite of the whole faith, indeed, as the logicians know, anything whatever is to be inferred; and such a hidden error in the principle, by unexpected ways, consequently weakens parts of the faith.

## ¶7 · 227.27–227.32

> [p. 227] Sed forte preter instancias factas superius arguitur possibilitas trium casuum abiectorum, scilicet quod quelibet persona divina indifferenter poterit incarnari; quia eadem persona multas humanitates simul vel successive potest assumere et quocienslibet potest dimittere; ac 3° quod multe possunt eandem humanitatem assumere.

But perhaps, beyond the [objections]{g:instancia} made above, the possibility of the three rejected cases is argued: namely that each divine person indifferently could be incarnated; that the same person can assume many humanities at once or successively, and can [lay them aside]{g:dimittere} as often as he pleases; and thirdly that many [persons] can assume the same humanity.[^25]

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** "By conversion": the proposition "the divine substance is three created substances" converted gives "three created substances [three creatures] are the divine substance". C reads *sint* "they are" for *sit* (223.22).

[^2]: **C/S.** "The greatest natural defect": *peccatum … naturale*, a fault of nature (the "sin" of nature in Aristotle, *Physics* II.8, the monster), not a moral sin: each of the three would lack a head of his own, since the one body is all three (there monsters are failures, ἁμαρτήματα, of nature's purposive effort; *verified*, LEDGER I13c-1). O reads *unicum* for *unum* (224.9).

[^3]: **C.** "An absurdity": *inconveniens*, in the sense of argument (GLOSSARY, CONTEXTUAL). A B C read *plurificaretur* (224.12) and *univoce* "univocally" for *unite* (224.14).

[^4]: **C/T.** 224.17–19: "it" is the human substance taken personally, i.e. the Word, which is not counted with the divine substance because it is the divine substance. A B C read *potest* "can" for *ponitur* (224.18) and add *substancia* after *divina* (224.19).

[^5]: **C.** "Stand with simple supposition" (*supponendum simpliciter*): the theory of supposition (GLOSSARY *supposicio simplex*), not the pair *simpliciter / secundum quid*. The abstract subject *substancia divina* makes the predicate stand for the nature, so the proposition is false; the personal subject *Verbum* lets it stand for the person, so "the Word is a created substance" is true.

[^6]: **C.** *Cuius racionis diversitas*: "the diversity of this reasoning"; or, taking *cuius* with *diversitas*, "the reason for this diversity". "Limitation": the restriction of the predicate's supposition by the kind of subject term.

[^7]: **T.** "For attaining the end": *ad finem attingendum* (224.31), with A B; C *ad finem a…* with a gap, O *ad finem aliquem* "for some end"; O also *necessarium* (224.31).

[^8]: **S.** The doctors' reasons why the Son rather than the Father or the Spirit was incarnate: cf. Anselm, *De incarnatione Verbi* c. 10 (Schmitt; PL 158, c. V; c. 6 in Wyclif's numbering), on the image, the confusion of sons and the Father as "grandson"; Peter Lombard, *Sent.* III d. 1; Thomas, *Summa theologiae* III q. 3 a. 8; closest to Wyclif's list is Thomas, *Scriptum* III d. 1 q. 2 a. 2 co., *quod filius est, quod verbum est, quod imago, quod media in Trinitate persona*; Lombard's place is d. 1 c. 2, PL 192, 757 (*ne alius in divinitate esset Filius, alius in humanitate*) (*verified*, LEDGER I13c-2). "Fittingness": *congruencia*; A B C *convenienciam* "agreement" (225.6).

[^9]: **C/T.** 225.8–10: the sentence changes construction after *Patris* (*per quod … per eandem ymaginem debuit reformari*); the subject of *debuit reformari* is man. O omits *quia Verbum*.

[^10]: **T.** "[Restore]": *[revocare]*, Harris's supplement, which no manuscript has ("*revocare* or something like it is needed for the sense"); the clause has no verb without it.

[^11]: **T.** "Other than the devil": *alter quam dyabolus*, with O; A B C *aliter quam dyabolus* "otherwise than the devil". All four manuscripts have the words after *alterius*; Harris drops both readings as a gloss (225.17). O reads *revelari* for *relevari* (225.18).

[^12]: **T.** "In the Trinity": *in Trinitate*, with O; A B C *intrinsece* "intrinsically" (225.21).

[^13]: **S/T.** "Adoptive sons and heirs … firstborn": cf. Romans 8:17, 29 and Galatians 4:5. O reads *adopcionis* (225.26) and writes *et heredes … primogenitus* twice. "Of sons or of kinship": *filiorum vel cognacionis*, with C O, against the print's *filiorum cognacionis* "of sons of kinship" (225.28).

[^14]: **S.** 1 Timothy 2:5, "one mediator of God and men" (quoted at 231.17). "Indeed": *quidem*; O *quippe*. O reads *concludo* (226.1) and *congrue* without *Verbo* (226.2).

[^15]: **S.** John 17:6 has "I have manifested thy name" without "Father", which Wyclif takes from the vocatives of the same prayer (17:1, 5, 11). "Eternally uttered from the unbegotten heart": cf. Psalm 44:2, "My heart hath uttered a good word".

[^16]: **C/T.** "Might": *potencia*, given the Douay word of Luke 1:51 so that the verse answers to the devil's "power and might" (*virtutem et potenciam*); *virtus* is "power" throughout, as in "the power of God". B omits *potenciam … et* (226.11–12); A B C read *datam* "the given [power]" for *dantem* (226.12). A B read *homo autem*, C *ut autem*, O *ut qui* (226.8).

[^17]: **S.** Augustine, *De doctrina christiana* I.5.5, "in the Father unity, in the Son equality, in the Holy Spirit the concord of unity and equality" (PL 34, 21; *verified*, LEDGER I13c-3).

[^18]: **S.** "The judgement given to the Son": John 5:22, "hath given all judgment to the Son". "The sin against the Holy Spirit": Matthew 12:32. C reads *peccatum* for *Spiritum* (226.19).

[^19]: **T/S.** "Is to be brought back through precisely the term of equality": *est per precise equalitatis terminum … reducenda*, with O, against the print's *est precise equalitatis terminus* (226.22), which leaves *reducenda* without construction. "Beauty, and form": *species* appropriated to the Son, as in Hilary's triad quoted by Augustine, *De Trinitate* VI.10.11 (PL 42, 931: *species in Imagine*, which Augustine glosses *propter pulchritudinem, ubi jam est … prima aequalitas*; *verified*, LEDGER I13c-4).

[^20]: **G.** *Sacramentum*: in the Augustinian sense, "mystery" (1 Timothy 3:16).

[^21]: **T.** "Pronounces": *sentenciat*; O *senciat fide* "thinks by faith" (226.27).

[^22]: **C.** "Sects": the three schools of opinion (Thomas's, the moderns', the Subtle Doctor's), GLOSSARY *secta*. B reads *natura* for *persona* (226.32); for *quotlibet* (227.1) A has *t* over an erasure by another hand, B *quidlibet*, C *quodlibet*.

[^23]: **S.** "If God should will": Harris italicizes the words as Anselm's formula; Anselm's way of speaking is *Cur Deus homo* II.10 (PL 158, 409A), *Possumus itaque dicere de Christo, quia potuit mentiri, si subaudiatur, si vellet*: a possibility granted only under the condition of a will; Thomas uses the same *sub conditione, scilicet si vellet* on this question (*Scriptum* III d. 1 q. 2 a. 3 co.) (*verified* in sense, LEDGER I13c-5).

[^24]: **T/S.** "Question ...": the number is wanting in all the manuscripts, and Harris prints points. Thomas, *Scriptum super Sententiis* III d. 6 q. 1 a. 2 co., on the first opinion (the assumed man): *non est haeretica … immo impossibile est … et ideo non sustinetur* (*verified*, LEDGER I13c-6).

[^25]: **C.** *Quia eadem persona* (227.29): *quia* here introduces the second case, in the sense of *quod* "that", parallel to *quod quelibet* and *quod multe*.
