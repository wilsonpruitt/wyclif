# sed/autem extract 05: chunks 13-f–15-g

### 13-f ¶1 · 294.11–295.8  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 294] Ulterius consideret Christi discipulus si ista vocata mundana securitas assecurat altrinsecus elemosinatores ac elemosinatos de accepcione operum misericordie quoad Deum. Nonne ista mundialitas retardat Christi presbiteros, ut ascendant ad speculam scripture in qua possent omnia pericula ecclesie a mundi principio usque in finem premuniendo ecclesiam speculari (ut notat scriptura Jeremie XXXI° et Ezechiel XXXIII°)? Notet secundo si ista copiosa mundana securitas facit istos speculatores sompniferos et ignaros ad ista pericula ecclesie previdendum, illos **autem** facit timidos ad pericula dampnacionis populi predicendum, et alios qui forent altilia facit reptilia, indispositos ad orandum pro subditorum salute, inter Deum et populum mediando, vel ad merendum sibi et aliis sacramenta ministrando vel evangelium predicando. Unde notaremus parabolam Christi, Luce XII°, ubi precipit cavere ab omni avaricia, quia non in diviciis cuiusquam vita eius est, sicut probat de quodam divite, sollicitante se circa inhorreacionem bonorum suorum, et eadem nocte fuit anima eius rapta per demones. Cogitaremus insuper illud Ezechielis XVI°, 49: Hec fuit iniquitas Sodome, sororis tue, superbia, saturitas panis et habundancia et ocium, que quatuor [p. 295] si sint in clero occasione dotacionis, imminet exinde periculum. Si **autem** occasio sit data vel accepta per accidens ex tali dotacione perpetua, ubi temporalis elemosinacio foret securior, patet periculum tam in elemosinante quam elemosinato isti seculari ritui innitendo. Deus **autem** necessitat divites huius seculi, ut continue ministrent clero in vite necessariis, quantum de clero est necessarium ecclesie militanti.

ENGLISH: Further, let Christ's disciple consider whether this [so-called]{g:vocatus} worldly security assures, on either side, the almsgivers and the [almsmen]{g:elemosinatus} of the acceptance of their works of mercy as regards God.[^1] Does not this [mundanity]{g:mundialitas} hold back Christ's [presbyters]{g:presbiter} from climbing to the watchtower of scripture, from which they could watch for all the dangers of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, from the beginning of the world to the end, forewarning the church (as scripture notes in Jeremiah 31 and Ezekiel 33)?[^2] Let him note secondly whether this copious worldly security makes these [watchmen]{g:speculator} drowsy and ignorant for foreseeing these dangers of the church, and makes those others fearful of foretelling the dangers of the people's damnation, and makes others, who would be fatted fowl, creeping things,[^3] [unfit]{g:indispositus} to pray for the salvation of their subjects, mediating between God and the people, or to merit for themselves and others by ministering the sacraments or preaching the gospel. Hence we should note Christ's parable in Luke 12, where he commands us to beware of all covetousness, because a man's life is not in his riches, as he proves of a certain rich man who busied himself about storing his goods in barns, and that same night his soul was snatched away by demons.[^4] We should consider moreover that word of Ezekiel 16:49: "This was the iniquity of Sodom thy sister, pride, fulness of bread, and abundance, and idleness"; and if these four [295] are in the [clergy]{g:clerus} by occasion of [endowment]{g:dotacio}, danger hangs over [them] from it.[^5] And if the occasion is given or taken by accident from such perpetual endowment, where temporary almsgiving would be more secure, the danger is clear both in the [almsgiver]{g:elemosinans} and in the almsman who leans upon this secular rite.[^6] But God constrains the rich of this world to minister continually to the clergy in the necessaries of life, as much of the clergy as is necessary to the [church militant]{g:ecclesia militans}.

### 13-f ¶2 · 295.9–296.6  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 295] Et patet quod non est securitas domino seculari elemosinam suam taliter impertiri, cum veritas precipit sibi Luce XVI, 9, ut faciat sibi amicos de mammona iniquitatis, non **autem** spirituales et periculosissimos inimicos. **Sed** Matthei X, 36, dicit Christus: Inimici eius, domestici eius. Nulli enim plus subdole faciunt scisma in sancta matre ecclesia tamquam magis periculosi heretici quam illi qui videntur esse capitales ecclesie induti vestibus ovium. Unde quamvis tam de laicis quam de clero sint multi heretici, ut patet de tribus personis prescitis que relinquentur in finali iudicio (de quibus Matthei XXIV, 1), periculosior et communior heresis pululat in clero, cuius (ut alias exposui) sunt tres gradus. Primus est quando in clero quis aspirat ad dignitatem ecclesiasticam symoniace pocius propter questum et fastum cum eis sequentibus quam meritorium officium spiritualiter serviendi. De ista heresi loquitur decretum Ia, q. I, sub auctoritate Gregorii sic dicentis: Fertur symoniaca heresis que prima contra ecclesiam Dei diabolica supplantacione surrepsit et in ipso ortu suo zelo apostolice ulcionis percussa atque dampnata est, in regni nostri finibus dominari. [p. 296] Tales enim hereticos debet superior in ecclesia et precipue verus Christi vicarius extirpare; omnis enim symoniacus est hereticus (ut patet Iª, q. I. Quisquis). Nec videtur apcior destruccio huius heresis quam tollere proprietatem temporalium gracia cuius est talis dignitas symoniace affectata.

ENGLISH: And it is clear that there is no security for a secular [lord]{g:dominus} in bestowing his [alms]{g:elemosina} in such a way, since the Truth commands him in Luke 16:9 to "make unto [him] friends of the mammon of iniquity," not spiritual and most dangerous enemies. But in Matthew 10:36 Christ says: "His enemies, they of his own household."[^7] For none make schism in holy mother church more craftily, as more dangerous heretics, than those who seem to be the chief men of the church, clothed in the garments of sheep. Hence, although there are many heretics among the laity as well as among the clergy, as is clear of the three [foreknown]{g:prescitus} persons who will be left at the final judgment (of whom Matthew 24:1), a more dangerous and commoner heresy sprouts in the clergy, of which (as I have set out elsewhere) there are three degrees.[^8] The first is when someone in the clergy aspires to an ecclesiastical dignity [simoniacally]{g:simonia}, rather for gain and pomp, with what follows on them, than for the meritorious office of serving spiritually. Of this heresy the Decretum speaks at 1 q. 1, under the authority of Gregory, who says thus: "The simoniac heresy, which first crept in against the church of God by the devil's supplanting, and at its very birth was struck and condemned by the zeal of apostolic vengeance, is reported to [lord it]{g:dominari} within the borders of our kingdom."[^9] [296] For such heretics the superior in the church, and especially the true [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius Christi}, must root out; for every simoniac is a heretic (as is clear from 1 q. 1, *Quisquis*).[^10] Nor does there seem a more apt destruction of this heresy than to take away the ownership of the [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} for whose sake such dignity is simoniacally [desired]{g:affectare}.

### 13-f ¶3 · 296.7–298.24  (7 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 296] Secundus gradus heresis a prelatis venandus et ab utroque brachio destruendus est, cum persona dotata ecclesie, que debet esse procurator, non dominus elemosinarum datarum pauperibus, ut patet in principio capituli huius et libro III Decretalium, De Donacionibus, cap°: Fraternitatem, cum, inquam, talis expendit bona ecclesie prodigaliter in pompa seculi vel consumit ipsa in cognacione vel affeccione carnali, aut tercio detinet thesaurum pauperum avare affeccione terreni commodi, tunc est periculosus hereticus **sed** periculosissimus persona conventualis istis condicionibus heresis maculata. Patet ista heresis, ymmo antechristiana vesania ex dicto beati Gregorii posito in decretis XVIª, q. VII, sic dicentis: Pervenit ad nos fama sinistra quod quidam episcoporum non sacerdotibus proprie diocesis decimas atque christianorum oblaciones conferant, **sed** pocius laicalibus personis, militum videlicet sive servitorum vel ( quod gravius est) eciam consanguineis. Unde si quis amodo episcopus inventus fuerit huius divini precepti transgressor, inter maximos hereticos et pessimos antichristos non minimus habeatur, et sicut Nicena synodus de simoniacis censuit, et qui dat episcopus [p. 297] et qui recipiunt ab eo laici, sive pacto sive beneficio, eterni incendii ignibus deputentur. Et multo magis communitas que debet esse religiosior et est in isto peccato pertinacior et antichristus profundior, cuiusmodi forent religiosi, viventes seculariter, distribuentes bona pauperum cognacioni sue, ut fiat mundo inclitus, militaribus atque iuridicis, ut defendant suos redditus, et (quod foret pessimum) si darent cognacioni sue carnalibus et famulis inhabilibus presentacionem ad beneficia ecclesiastica virtute iuris patronatus. Et omnia ista innuit beatus Gregorius. Tercius gradus clericalis heresis est, cum sacerdos occupat statum et bona ecclesie sine hoc quod rependat debitum ministerium ad quod pretextu elemosine et acceptacionis solempnis officii obligatur; talis **autem** ut sic excommunicatur a domino, quia si laicus excommunicatur ab homine, eo quod iniuste detinet decimas vel elemosinas debitas sacerdoti, multo plus sacerdos excommunicatur a domino qui iniuste post maiorem obligacionem detinet spirituales elemosinas subditorum. Obligacio, inquam, est maior, cum non licet sibi elemosinas spirituales subtrahere eciam subdito subtrahente elemosinas corporales. Sacerdotes enim Christi debent benefacere persequentibus et diligere inimicos, et iterum subtraccio spiritualis elemosine est periculosior, cum minatur perpetuam perdicionem corporis et anime, **sed** neutrum tam evidenter subtraccio elemosine corporalis. Unde Jeremie XLVIII°, 10 dicitur: Maledictus qui facit opus domini fraudulenter; et multo magis est maledictus qui recipit onus ducatus gregis dominici, quem vel proditorie ducit in precipicium, vel fraudulenter omnino subtrahit ministerium, de quo dictum est libro VI°, [p. 298] cap. XX° et tribus sequentibus. Omnis **autem** excommunicatus eciam rite ab homine est hereticus, et evidencius omnis excommunicatus a Deo. Unde IVª, q. I Quod **autem**, sub auctoritate secunde synodi Nicolaus papa sic loquitur: Hereticos, inquiunt, dicimus tam eos qui ab ecclesia olim proiecti sunt, quam qui post hec a nobis anathematizati sunt. Porro si adhuc nec sic credatis, audite. Preter hos **autem** aiunt et eos qui fidem simulant confiteri scismaticos esse et eos qui seorsum a comunicantibus nobis episcopis collectas faciunt. Demum vero, et si quidam ab ecclesia super causis quibusdam reprehensi sunt et proiecti et excommunicati, sive ex clero sive ex laicali ordine. Dicimus omnes huiusmodi hereticos atque scismaticos. Unde glosa dicit ibidem quod omnis excommunicatus est hereticus, et allegat decretum, XXIIIª, q. IVª, Ipsa pietas, et XXIVª, q. III, Illi. Et concordant dicta de heresi libro VI°. Omnis enim vel peccans mortaliter est ut sic divisus a communione sancte matris ecclesie quod sonat hereticus. Item, omnis qui nititur auferre privilegium Romane ecclesie est ut sic hereticus; quod dissipantes legem de sacerdotum officio maxime faciunt in effectu.

ENGLISH: The second degree of heresy, to be hunted down by [prelates]{g:prelatus} and destroyed by both arms, is when the endowed person of the church, who ought to be the [proctor]{g:procurator}, not the lord, of the alms given to the poor (as is clear at the beginning of this chapter and in book III of the Decretals, *De donationibus*, chapter *Fraternitatem*) — when, I say, such a one spends the goods of the church prodigally in the pomp of the world, or consumes them on kindred or carnal affection, or thirdly withholds the treasure of the poor through avaricious affection for earthly advantage: then he is a dangerous heretic, but most dangerous is a conventual person stained with these conditions of heresy.[^11] This heresy, indeed this antichristian madness, is clear from the saying of blessed Gregory placed in the Decretum at 16 q. 7, who says thus: "An ill report has reached us that certain of the bishops confer the [tithes]{g:decime} and the oblations of christians not on the [priests]{g:sacerdos} of their own diocese but rather on lay persons, namely [knights]{g:milites} or servants, or (what is graver) even on kinsmen. Hence if any [bishop]{g:episcopus} henceforth shall be found a transgressor of this divine precept, let him be held not the least among the greatest heretics and worst antichrists; and as the Nicene synod decreed concerning simoniacs, both the bishop who gives [297] and the laymen who receive from him, whether by bargain or by favour, are to be assigned to the fires of eternal burning."[^12] And much more [is] a community [a heretic] which ought to be more religious and is more pertinacious in this sin and a deeper antichrist: such would be [the religious]{g:religiosi} living in a secular manner, distributing the goods of the poor to their kindred, so that it may become renowned to the world, [and] to military men and lawyers, so that they may defend their [revenues]{g:reditus}, and (what would be worst) if they gave to their carnal kindred and to [unqualified]{g:inhabilis} servants presentation to [ecclesiastical benefices]{g:beneficium ecclesiasticum} by virtue of the [right of patronage]{g:ius patronatus}.[^13] And blessed Gregory hints at all these things. The third degree of clerical heresy is when a priest occupies the estate and goods of the church without repaying the due ministry to which he is bound by reason of the alms and of his acceptance of a solemn office; and such a one, as such, is excommunicated by the Lord; for if a layman is excommunicated by man because he unjustly withholds tithes or alms due to the priest, much more is the priest excommunicated by the Lord who unjustly, after a greater obligation, withholds the spiritual alms of his subjects. The obligation, I say, is greater, since it is not lawful for him to withdraw spiritual alms even when the subject withdraws bodily alms. For the priests of Christ must do good to those who persecute [them] and love their enemies; and again, the withdrawal of spiritual alms is more dangerous, since it threatens perpetual perdition of body and soul, but the withdrawal of bodily alms [threatens] neither so evidently.[^14] Hence it is said in Jeremiah 48:10: "Cursed be he that doth the work of the Lord deceitfully"; and much more cursed is he who receives the burden of leading the Lord's flock, which he either treacherously leads to the precipice or from which he fraudulently withdraws [his] ministry altogether, of which it was said in book VI, [298] chapter 20 and the three following.[^15] Now everyone excommunicated by man, even duly, is a heretic, and more evidently everyone excommunicated by God. Hence at 4 q. 1, *Quod autem*, under the authority of the second synod, [pope]{g:papa} Nicholas speaks thus: "'We call heretics,' they say, 'both those who were long ago cast out of the church and those who after this were anathematized by us.' Further, if even so you do not yet believe, hear. 'Besides these,' they say, 'those also who feign to confess the faith are schismatics, and those who hold assemblies apart from the bishops in communion with us. Lastly, also if some have been reproved by the church over certain causes and cast out and excommunicated, whether of the clergy or of the lay order. We call all such heretics and schismatics.'"[^16] Hence the [gloss]{g:glosa} says there that everyone excommunicated is a heretic, and [alleges]{g:allegare} the Decretum, 23 q. 4, *Ipsa pietas*, and 24 q. 3, *Illi*.[^17] And what was said of heresy in book VI agrees. For everyone [excommunicated], or sinning mortally, is as such divided from the communion of holy mother church, which is what "heretic" signifies.[^18] Again, everyone who strives to take away the [privilege]{g:privilegium} of the Roman church is as such a heretic; and those who scatter the law on the office of priests do this most of all in effect.[^19]

### 13-f ¶4 · 298.25–299.8  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 298] Item, tales periuri sunt nedum, quia negant fidem quam Christo promiserant in baptismo, in recepcione sacerdotalis ordinis et in recepcione illius officii, **sed** iurati sunt specialiter ad defendendum et retinendum (quantum sufficiunt) iura Romane ecclesie et regulas sanctorum patrum (ut patet III° Decretalium De Iure [p. 299] Iurando. Ego episcopus). Quod si in isto contra Romanam ecclesiam incurrunt periurium, quomodo non papa primus divideret eos a sua ecclesia cui tam proditorie sic illudunt? Heresis ergo symoniacorum in ingressu non per hostium, in progressu quo prodigaliter consumunt bona pauperum, et maxime in omissione debiti officii curatorum videtur inficere tam ecclesiam quam Romanum imperium.

ENGLISH: Again, such men are perjurers,[^20] not only because they deny the faith which they had promised to Christ in baptism, in receiving the priestly order and in receiving that office, but [because] they are specially sworn to defend and retain (as far as they are able) the [rights]{g:ius} of the Roman church and the rules of the holy fathers (as is clear from book III of the Decretals, *De iure [299] iurando*, *Ego episcopus*).[^21] And if in this they incur perjury against the Roman church, how would the pope not be the first to divide them from his church, which they so treacherously mock? The heresy of simoniacs, therefore — in their entry, not by the door; in their progress, in which they prodigally consume the goods of the poor; and above all in the omission of the due office of [curates]{g:curatus} — seems to infect both the church and the Roman empire.[^22]

### 14-a ¶1 · 299.10–300.6  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 299] Antequam respondeam ad magistrales obiectus, sunt duo dubia breviter transcurrenda. Primo, quomodo stat cum fide scripture dotacio Romane ecclesie. Videtur enim ex multis dictis superius expresse innui quod non sine gravi peccato altrinsecus potest tale privilegium temporale contra Christi privilegia introduci. **Sed** videtur probabile (ut alias sepe dixi) quod illa dotacio sicut et particularium ecclesiarum possunt sic racionabiliter colorari, quod seculares domini videntes quod vita exproprietaria qualem Christus et sui apostoli vixerant est seculari ac civili conversacione perfeccior, et sic ad exonerandum eos, ad accumulandum eis meritum, ad multiplicandum milites Christi qui distrahant mundum a vita mundana, cum talis sit ad multiplicandum religionem Christi habilior, dederunt titulo elemosine suum seculare dominium, non ut sacerdotes Christi civiliter aut seculariter dominentur, **sed** ut servantes humilitatem, paupertatem et penalitatem priorem fideliter ministrent pauperibus elemosinas ad hoc datas, ut vel sic distraheretur mundus a conversacione civili et ritu quem [p. 300] ante Christum observaverant mundiales. Nec credo quod ista condicione dimissa nec secularium dotacio nec cleri Christi admissio cum religione Christi sit compossibiliter sustinenda; talis **autem** dotacio, sonans in puram et perpetuam elemosinam, utrimque est tota trinitate auctorizabilis et fundabilis in scriptura.

ENGLISH: Before I reply to the master's [objections]{g:objectum}, two doubts are to be run through briefly.[^1] First, how the [endowment]{g:dotacio} of the Roman [church]{g:ecclesia} stands with the faith of scripture. For from many things said above it seems to be expressly implied that such a temporal [privilege]{g:privilegium} cannot be brought in against Christ's privileges without grave sin on either side. But it seems probable (as I have often said elsewhere) that that endowment, as also that of particular churches, can [be given colour]{g:colorare} reasonably in this way:[^2] that secular [lords]{g:dominus}, seeing that the [propertyless]{g:exproprietarii} life such as Christ and his apostles had lived is more perfect than a secular and civil [manner of life]{g:conversacio}, and so, to unburden them, to heap up merit for them, to multiply the [soldiers]{g:milites} of Christ, who would draw the world away from worldly life (since such [a life] is more apt for multiplying the religion of Christ), gave their secular [lordship]{g:dominium} by [title]{g:titulus} of [alms]{g:elemosina},[^3] not that the [priests]{g:sacerdos} of Christ should [exercise lordship]{g:dominari} civilly or secularly, but that, keeping their former humility, poverty and [hardship]{g:penalitas}, they should faithfully minister to the poor the alms given for this, so that at least in this way the world might be drawn away from the civil manner of life and the rite which [300] worldly men had observed before Christ. Nor do I believe that, once this condition is dropped, either the endowment by [seculars]{g:secularis} or its acceptance by the [clergy]{g:clerus} of Christ can be [compossibly]{g:compossibilis} upheld with the religion of Christ; but such endowment, sounding in pure and [perpetual alms]{g:elemosina perpetua}, is on both sides authorizable by the whole Trinity and capable of being founded in scripture.[^4]

### 14-a ¶2 · 300.7–300.17  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 300] **Sed** hic recalcitrant moderni prepositi, dicentes quod licet eis civiliter ac seculariter dominari, et quod secularis dominacio fuit iure poli debita Christi vicariis, cum Christus dominatus fuit seculariter toti mundo. Unde (ut inquiunt) Constantinus non dotavit ecclesiam, **sed** illud dominium quod ipse ac predecessores sic occuparunt tyrannice, humiliter et debite diviserunt, unde Christus, Petrus, Clemens et alii precedentes beatum Silvestrum habuerunt ius plenum ad totum imperium, **sed** non erant in possessione ante concessionem humilem Constantini.

ENGLISH: But here the modern [superiors]{g:prepositi} kick back, saying that it is lawful for them to exercise lordship civilly and secularly, and that secular [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio} was due to Christ's [vicars]{g:vicarius Christi} by the law of heaven, since Christ exercised lordship secularly over the whole world.[^5] Hence (as they say) Constantine did not endow the church; rather, the lordship which he and his predecessors had thus occupied tyrannically they humbly and duly divided; hence Christ, Peter, Clement and the others who preceded blessed Silvester had full [right]{g:ius} to the whole empire, but were not in possession before the humble [grant]{g:concessio} of Constantine.[^6]

### 14-a ¶3 · 300.18–302.2  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 300] Et inter omnes hereses dissipantes religionem christianam et fidem scripture hec est una precipua. Primo, inquam, claudicat in hoc quod dicit Christum habuisse ius ad dominandum civiliter, cum conversacione et sermone secundum testimonia sanctorum doctorum exponencium fidem evangelii docebat oppositum. Ipse enim dicit Luce, IX°, 58: Vulpes foveas habent et volucres celi nidos, filius **autem** hominis non habet ubi caput suum reclinet. Super quo dicit venerabilis Beda super Lucam lib. II°, c. IV°: Quid me propter divicias et seculi lucra cupis sequi, cum tante sim paupertatis ut nec hospiciolum quidem habeam et non meo utar tecto? Et ad istum sensum illud exponitur a beato Jeronymo nec contrarie ab aliquo sancto (ut alias explanavi). [p. 301] Item, si Christus et sui apostoli habuissent ius ad civiliter dominandum, et faciliter ex oblacione mundi potentum possent fuisse positi in possessione pacifica, ut patet de Evangelista et aliis sanctis: sequitur quod ex desidia fuissent valde culpabiles, cum possent ita faciliter quesivisse ius sibi et sue ecclesie et excusasse sibi iniuriantes a sua intrusione. Hoc **autem** est contra evangelium Luce XXII°, ubi decidens questionem de maioritate inter apostolos dicit quod opposito modo se habet ad ritum gentilem, cum maioritas inter eos attenditur penes maioritatem servicii ex humilitate maiori. Ex quo Iª Petri V°, 3, capit ipse pro principio quod sui clerici non sint dominantes in clero. Item, illud est expresse contra iura canonica, cum sub auctoritate Augustini, distinccione VIIIª canon sic loquitur: Quo iure defendis villas ecclesie divino vel humano? Divinum ius in divinis scripturis habemus, humanum ius in legibus regum. Unde quisquis possidet quod possidet? Nonne jure humano? Nam iure divino domini est terra et plenitudo eius. Pauperes et divites una terra supportat. Iure ergo humano dicitur: Villa hec mea est. Tolle, inquit, iura imperatoris et quis audet dicere: Villa hec mea est, meus est iste servus, mea est hec domus? Si **autem** ut teneantur ista ab hominibus, regum iura fecerunt, vultis reticeamus leges ut gaudeatis? Unde infra, XCVIª distinccione, Constantinus, incorporat ecclesia cartam cesaris sub forma qua dotavit ecclesiam. Cum ergo lege scripture, lege sanctorum doctorum et iuris canonici non sit fundabilis secularis dominacio cleri [p. 302] Christi, qua temeritate sompniarent discoli istam blasphemiam?

ENGLISH: And among all the heresies that scatter the christian religion and the faith of scripture, this is one of the chief. First, I say, it limps in this, that it says Christ had a right to exercise lordship civilly, since in manner of life and in speech, according to the testimonies of the holy doctors who expound the faith of the gospel, he taught the opposite. For he himself says in Luke 9:58: "The foxes have holes, and the birds of the air nests: but the Son of man hath not where to lay his head." On which the venerable Bede says, on Luke, book II, chapter 4: "Why do you desire to follow me for the sake of riches and the gains of the world, when I am of such poverty that I have not even a little lodging and use no roof of my own?"[^7] And it is expounded in this sense by blessed Jerome, and not otherwise by any saint (as I have explained elsewhere).[^8] [301] Again, if Christ and his apostles had had a right to exercise lordship civilly, and could easily have been put in peaceful possession by the offering of the mighty of the world, as is clear of the Evangelist and other saints, [it follows]{g:sequitur} that they would have been very culpable through sloth, since they could so easily have sought the right for themselves and for their church, and have excused those who wronged them from their intrusion.[^9] But this is against the gospel, Luke 22, where, deciding the question of [greatness]{g:majoritas} among the apostles, he says that it stands with them in the opposite way to the gentile rite, since greatness among them is reckoned by greatness of [service]{g:servicium} out of greater humility.[^10] From this, in 1 Peter 5:3, he takes it as a principle that his clerics should not be "lording it over the clergy." Again, that is expressly against the canon laws, since, under the authority of Augustine, the canon in distinction 8 speaks thus: "By what right do you defend the estates of the church, divine or human? The divine right we have in the divine scriptures, the human right in the laws of kings. Whence does each one possess what he possesses? Is it not by human right? For by divine right 'the earth is the Lord's and the fulness thereof.' The poor and the rich one earth supports. By human right, therefore, it is said: This estate is mine. Take away, he says, the rights of the emperor, and who dares to say: This estate is mine, this servant is mine, this house is mine? But if the rights of kings have brought it about that these things are held by men, do you want us to keep silent about the laws, so that you may rejoice?"[^11] Hence below, in distinction 96, *Constantinus*, the church incorporates the charter of Caesar in the form in which he endowed the church.[^12] Since therefore by the law of scripture, by the law of the holy doctors and of canon law, the secular exercise of lordship by the clergy [302] of Christ cannot be founded, with what rashness would the [froward]{g:discoli} dream up this blasphemy?

ENGLISH: Therefore (as I have often set out at length) Christ could not [exercise civil lordship]{g:dominari}; how then would he teach his servants, his chief imitators, to exercise lordship in that way? For if it can be founded, tell me where. And since there is no contradiction in holy scripture, one would have to set aside the laws of the old testament and of the new which sound to the opposite, as is clear of the laws [alleged]{g:allegare} here and in the preceding chapter. Indeed, since laws, as being more necessary, are more clearly expressed in holy scripture (as is clear from book 4 of *On Christian Doctrine*),[^4] whoever held this opinion would impute a great defect to the author of scripture, that he nowhere expresses that exercise of lordship, so necessary to his [church]{g:ecclesia}. Again, Christ could not exercise civil lordship according to his [Godhead]{g:deitas} nor according to his humanity; therefore he could not exercise civil lordship as to the Godhead. There is no doubt for a believer,[^5] since according to it he cannot be an active [wayfarer]{g:viator}, a proprietor, or part of a people; nor according to his humanity, whether he had been man alone, or in the pure state of innocence with his whole race, or after the fall, he himself being set as [head]{g:caput} of the church as he is now: in the two former ways there would be no civil condition, nor in the third way would he be a proprietor as such.[^6] For the [303] declaration of this I suppose the saying of blessed Gregory, *Moralia* 33, chapter 1, on that text of Job 19:25, "I know that my Redeemer liveth":[^7] "It must be known," he says, "that the Redeemer showed himself one person with his church, which he assumed; for of him it is said, 'Who is the head, Christ'; and again of his church it is written, 'And the body of Christ, which is the church.'"[^8] And it goes on: as we believe Christ and the church one person, so we may see this signified also by the actions of one person.[^9] With this faith supposed, I argue thus: it is not possible that Christ, the [bridegroom]{g:sponsus} of the church, should do or have anything humanly except in sharing with his whole church, because otherwise he would be a bridegroom [displeasing]{g:ingratus} to his bride, since he is personally identified with her;[^10] now any sharing so great will exclude civil property; therefore it is not possible that Christ, the bridegroom of the church, should live according to civil property. And this is the reason why Christ loved common goods, and the Apostle so greatly reproves those who seek the things that are their own. Hence Christ never ate, slept, or bore himself humanly in any way, without first intending the profit of the whole church militant, sleeping and triumphant,[^11] even more than the proper profit of his own humanity. But who would call the person of a man, as such, a civil proprietor? And the apostles at first [were ignorant of]{g:ignorare} this [article of faith]{g:articulus fidei}, when they said, "Master, eat," and he: "I have meat to eat which you know not," John 4:31–32.[^12] And on this reason the primitive religion of the apostles was founded, living [without property]{g:exproprietarie}, secular or civil life and occupation set aside. Again, supposing the truth of the aforesaid [opinion]{g:opinio}, [it follows]{g:sequitur}, with truths, that Constantine after the [endowment]{g:dotacio} of the church remained a heretic, as did all the secular lords succeeding him; which is repugnant to the church's approval.[^13] And [304] the [inference]{g:consequencia} is proved, supposing as the [false writer's]{g:falsigraphus} given that by right the whole civil exercise of lordship of this world is owed to the [pope]{g:papa}, the [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius Christi}, and to his clergy. And I argue thus: for a debtor, or one who unjustly withholds what is another's, to be absolved as regards God, he must make full satisfaction; now Constantine did not do so, since he kept half of the empire for himself. Since therefore that half was as much owed as the western half that was restored,[^14] it seems that he remained in debt as before; for according to Augustine, Epistle 37, the sin is not forgiven unless what was taken away is restored.[^15] Hence (as I said in the matter *On Privileges*)[^16] God cannot release or license a debtor not to make satisfaction; and hence I said it is dangerous that anyone should authorize or shelter a fugitive who has the means, so that he does not pay his creditor a bodily debt, as Augustine says in the aforesaid letter to Macedonius: "This," he says, "I would say most confidently: that he who intervenes for a man to this end, that he may not restore what was wrongly taken, and who does not compel one fleeing to him to restore, as far as he honourably can, is a partner in the fraud and the crime."[^17] Do we suppose that the [superior]{g:prepositi} of a place of refuge can honourably compel a fugitive who has the means either to leave the place as its violator or to make satisfaction according to the amount brought in or the means of the fugitive?[^18] For that would be honourableness in accord with God's law; but favour that moves to unjust detention, and especially for the sake of [participation]{g:participium}, would be devilish trickery.[^19] Therefore (as I have often said) one must either deny the faith of scripture, reason and the holy doctors, or say that such a defence savours of [heretical depravity]{g:heretica pravitas}. And the same seems [to follow] when the aforesaid opinion on the [lordship]{g:dominium} of Christ's clergy is supposed; for since silence [305] about this is consent, and such consent, when speaking is easy, is equally damnable, it seems that those who hold this opinion, from the fact that they are thus silent about stating their [right]{g:ius} and the wrong in which the [temporal lords]{g:temporales domini} are damnably hardened, sin mortally.[^20] Nor does it avail them to pretend that blessed Silvester remitted the remainder to Constantine, because (as this [sect]{g:secta} believes as a principle) it is not lawful for Christ's vicar or his clergy either to alienate or to give up the right of the patrimony of the crucified, namely in such a way that both it and its equivalent should be [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} outside the civil possession of Christ's clergy. Again, there is neither [privilege]{g:privilegium} nor dowry nor possession acquired for Christ's vicar except that for which Christ the head had been crucified; now Christ did not suffer such things for the civil or secular endowment of his church of clerics;[^21] therefore such endowment or civil exercise of lordship is not purely befitting Christ's clergy. [The assumption]{g:assumptum} is clear from this, that Christ merited for the human race all the grace, and everything that follows upon it, that it would have; now according to what is said elsewhere, without grace and [charity]{g:caritas} there is no just possession without qualification; therefore neither dowry nor privilege belongs to Christ's clergy except what Christ merited for it by suffering. Nor do I believe that a believer contradicts this [teaching]{g:sentencia};[^22] for all the fathers of the old testament, all the gentiles after and before who were either justified or saved, had obtained this by the merit of faith and of the passion of Christ, since otherwise they would have been damned and never cleansed of original sin, and consequently never justified morally.

### 14-b ¶2 · 305.29–306.14  (1 sed/autem)

ENGLISH: Nor is there doubt for a theologian who knows how a man intends, merits or buys some things principally and some secondarily, accessorily or [indistinctly]{g:confuse}, that Christ, as he suffered for the redemption of the whole church, so also [suffered] for the redemption of each of his [members]{g:membrum} and for every good useful to it to this end. ### 14-c ¶2 · 307.3–307.33  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 307] Cum ergo in facto movit apostolos suos ad facto et conversacione interpretandum hec mandata secundum vitam exproprietariam et penalem, nec unquam innuit eis dominacionem civilem, fuisset nimis magnus seductor, si cum istis paribus dominacionem civilem pro statu cleri moderni intendebat ipsis adicere. ### 14-c ¶3 · 308.1–309.7  (6 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 308] Christus enim est plus obligatus creature sue quam econtra, cum ex lege sua eterna et titulo gracie prevenientis constituit se debitorem suis servis fidelibus, non ut solum daret eis bonum aliquod temporale, **sed** ut daret eis se ipsum in premium sempiternum; tantum **autem** non potest creatura beata domino obligari, et sic tenetur sacerdos rependere subditis spirituales elemosinas. Nec feramus graviter quod quantumcunque nobiles quoad mundum ad tantum se humilient pro Christo quod deveniant elemosinarii pauperum subditorum, tum quia Christus nobilissimus recepit ad sui egenciam relevandam tam a viris secularibus quam a devotis feminis elemosinas corporales, tum eciam quia ex tali humiliacione et ministerii complecione crescit prepositi maioritas quoad Deum. Sic ergo non est quoad hoc distinccio inter fratres, inter possessionatos et inter curatos, viventes de decimis et oblacionibus, quin omnes debemus vivere exproprietarie de elemosinis laicorum et omnes debemus esse procuratores, non civiles possessores bonorum pauperum, iuxta preceptum Christi Matthei X°, nec potest conventualis conversacio vel solitaria aut prelacia maior vel minor tollere hoc debitum, **sed** omnes tenemur esse fideles dispensatores thesauri spiritualis vel corporalis domini secundum mensuram donacionis Christi; illi **autem** sunt superiores quoad Deum qui instar apostolorum plus deserentes mundum seminant efficacius verbum Dei. Unde ritus moderni [p. 309] isti maxime religioni supperadditi alios iuvando facilitant et alios retardando difficultant, **sed** utrobique qui ex maiori caritate plus defenderit et docuerit legem domini accepcior erit illi; non enim est personarum accepcio apud Deum, **sed** quicunque ex maiori caritate pro illo plus passus fuerit, magis penaliter vixerit et sponse sue magis profecerit, sibi accepcior erit.

ENGLISH: For Christ is more bound to his creature than the reverse, since by his eternal law and by [title]{g:titulus} of [prevenient grace]{g:gracia preveniens} he made himself debtor to his faithful servants, not only to give them some temporal good, but to give them himself as an everlasting reward; but a blessed creature cannot be bound to the Lord to so great an extent; and so the [priest]{g:sacerdos} is bound to repay spiritual alms to his subjects. Therefore (as I said in the preceding chapter)[^8] one who fails in the satisfaction of that debt is more gravely excommunicated by the Lord, and consequently falls more dangerously into heresy, so much so that if God, to suppose the impossible, failed in the debt to which he bound himself by graciously promising and by making merit profitable,[^9] he would be the supreme heretic. Nor let us take it ill that men, however noble as regards the world, humble themselves for Christ so far as to become [almsmen]{g:elemosinarius} of their poor subjects: both because Christ, most noble, received bodily alms to relieve his need, from secular men as well as from devout women; and also because from such humbling and fulfilment of ministry the greatness of the [superior]{g:prepositi} grows as regards God. But the total alms of a foreknown [person] concurs as a whole to mitigating the perpetual punishment.[^12] If [temporal lords]{g:temporales domini} (as the [almsmen]{g:elemosinatus} claim) are bound to continue the alms of the fathers whose heirs they are, then they are bound to everything that follows from that formally; because otherwise, against the possibility of divine obligation, they would be obliged to contradictories, namely to making them be alms and to defending their abuse in the [froward]{g:discoli}, in which respect they lack the form of alms.[^13] Therefore, since the kings of England are bound to keep the alms of their forebears in their perpetuity, are bound further by the law of spiritual alms to chastise their lieges, and are bound, third, by the obligation by which they are bound to do justice to their lieges and to extinguish wrongs which tend most dangerously to the detriment of their people, it is clear that they are bound in a given case to restore their goods to holy church and to take them away from those who hold their God and the kingdom in contempt. For this was the compact of the first [endowment]{g:dotacio}. Therefore I boldly assert: if, these things being equal, our king does not have power to do in his [clergy]{g:clerus} the work of mercifully chastising, he does not have sufficiency to [exercise lordship]{g:dominari} politically over our whole kingdom.[^14] Indeed, if we attend well to holy scripture and to what has been said of the privileges of the church, it both makes for the privilege of the king that he has power so to [coerce]{g:cohercere} clerics, and the clerics have [345] a privilege in that they have such a [superior]{g:prepositi} to whom they can thus meritoriously be subject. Those, therefore, who say that the king has an incomprehensible power in chastising lions and in performing bodily healings, with other things which they call miracles, should grant a portion of his power for regulating his kingdom in the clergy, since this and its act would be more excellent and more founded in scripture.[^15] Would it not be more excellent to chastise the frenzied, the sinners and the froward of Christ's school than to tame wild beasts, which do not harm the church so much? Again, would it not be a work of greater power to beget virtues in a [teachable]{g:disciplinabilis} mind by occasion of wholesome chastisement (for when that is taken away from the church, the virtues are withdrawn from the untamed) than to induce bodily health by power?[^16] For in both cases the king is the bare instrument of God, who, giving the king a sub-authentic power, works as the principal [agent]. And so (as I have often said) the king would do a work of greater power in chastising his subjects not only than in doing any wondrous bodily acts whatever, but also than in heaping up any number of temporal goods for the clergy of Christ; for such powers of producing bodily healings can be in beasts, in things growing from the earth and in stones, but not the power of wholesome correction. And again, the addition of temporal goods is commonly not so near to the ultimate necessity of present bodily salvation as the withdrawal of what is abused is near to the ultimate necessity of perpetual salvation, of body as well as soul.[^17] For just as it is a work of greater mercy to take away a sword from a frenzied man who wants to kill himself than to give a sword to a man pursued, to defend himself from one who wants to kill him, because it would be worse for a man to be killed thus by himself than to be killed by another, since the first is damnable and the second always meritorious or [346] just;[^18] and to this there are many laws of the church, as I [alleged]{g:allegare} above in chapter 11 of this [work] from the sayings of Augustine placed in [C.] 23 q. 4, *Nimium*, *Ipsa pietas* and *Quid faciet*.[^19] And this is clear in [C.] 5 q. 5, where it is written under the authority of Augustine: "Do not judge yourselves to be malevolent when you point out another's crime; for you are rather guilty in not pointing out the crime than if you let your brothers, whom you could correct by pointing it out, perish by keeping silent about the crime. For if your brother has a wound in his body which he wants hidden while he fears the knife, is it not cruelly kept silent by you and mercifully pointed out? How much more, then, ought you to make it known, lest it fester worse in the heart?"[^20] And it continues: "Not everyone who spares is a friend, nor everyone who strikes an enemy; for better are the wounds of a friend than the deceitful kisses of an enemy; it is better to love with severity than to deceive with gentleness."[^21] And it continues, to the purpose: "It is more useful for bread to be taken from a hungry man, if, secure of his food, he neglects justice, than for bread to be broken for a hungry man so that, led astray, he acquiesces in injustice. And he who binds the frenzied man and he who rouses the lethargic man is troublesome to both and loves both. Who can love [us] more than God? And yet he does not cease not only to teach us gently but also to terrify us wholesomely," as the Apostle teaches, Hebrews 12.[^22] "To the gentle poultices with which God consoles all he often adds also the most biting medicine of tribulation; he exercises with famine the patriarchs, pious and religious men, but harries the contumacious people with heavier punishments; he does not take away from the Apostle the sting of the flesh, though asked a third time, so that he may perfect power in infirmity."[^23] From any number of [347] sayings of the saints expounding the faith of scripture it is clear that, by the law of charity and of spiritual alms, under pain of damnable consent, a superior is bound to rebuke his subjects. Hence among all the sins which I fear most in the superiors of our kingdom are blind [piety]{g:pietas}, false mercy and consenting omission, whether from negligence or (what is most horrible) when consent to crime is sold, and Christ's enemy is unjustly and treasonably defended by means of Christ's patrimony. Hence Augustine, letter 37 to Macedonius: "We more mercifully withdraw our help from such men than bestow it. For he does not bring help who assists toward sinning, and does not rather overturn and suppress."[^24] But against what has been said [it is objected, by appeal to]{g:obicitur per} this, first: that no one ought to defame his neighbour, and especially the anointed of God.[^25] But those who object this consider too little how a liar (as such) defames himself; and again how he who, from these general words of the Lord's law, thinks himself defamed shows himself, as such, to be an adversary of God's law; and again he considers too little how the prophets of the old testament, Christ and the laws of the church press most sharply against those who are priests in name only; and the reason is that in their subversion a greater danger threatens mother church.[^26] And the decree mentions this, dist. 83, where it is written thus: "For no one in the church does more harm than he who, acting perversely, has the name or the order of holiness or of priest. For no one presumes to [refute]{g:redarguere} him when he offends, and the fault [348] is vehemently extended as an example when the sinner is honoured out of reverence for his order. So a bishop who does not correct the crimes of such men is rather to be called a shameless dog than a bishop."[^27] These laws of the saints, I say, have been incorporated in the canon for this reason: not that they should be kept silent forever like chaff, but that they should be [exposed]{g:detegere} with moderation to the praise of the good.[^28] And I say "with moderation", because it happens that a man, in speaking the truth, exceeds measure, as is noted in the decrees, [C.] 5 q. 5, *Qui ambulat*, where, in resolving an apparent contradiction in this matter, it is written thus: "It is one thing out of charity to report the crimes of others, so that those whom we cannot correct by secret admonition the judge's [sentence]{g:sentencia} may rebuke when convicted; and another thing by lying in wait to object falsehoods, or by insulting to reproach readily with truths. The former is the office of charity, the latter of impiety."[^29] Hence it is said in Proverbs 12:6: "The words of the wicked lie in wait for blood: the mouth of the just delivereth them." And below: "He that speaketh that which he knoweth is a judge of justice: but he that lieth, is a deceitful witness."[^30] An accuser, therefore, must keep integrity of truth and rightness of intention. But I keep myself from descending to particulars, resting, after the manner of the philosophers, in a general teaching.[^31]

### 15-f ¶2 · 349.17–350.11  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 349] Unde miror quomodo quidam innituntur exemplo de celesti ierarchia, putando quod clerici non debent corrigi a laycis, nam stabilito ordine militantis ierarchie, sicut est in ierarchia celesti, procederet racio a similitudine quod clerici non forent subiecti laicis, **sed** si deficit eis virtus illuminandi et expurgandi, sic quod sint ceciores et criminosiores quam layci, quomodo staret in illis perturbantibus ordinem ecclesie exemplum ierarchie celestis? Ideo in defectu illorum stat quod pervertitur ordo ecclesiastice ierarchie; ordo dico non simpliciter **sed** ordo individuus qui foret in illis. Non enim est fas fideli credere quod datus clerus servabit ordinem superiorem in ecclesia quantumcunque peccaverit, cum primus angelus cum suis complicibus propter apostasiam ab ordine deiectus [p. 350] est de ecclesia triumphante (ut notatur Is. XIV°). Viator ergo extra ordinem debet habere subsidium a fratre inferioris ordinis, cum pes iuvat oculum, eciam utroque servante integritatem corporis. Et ita videtur Lincolniensi quod angelus superioris ordinis illuminatur ab angelo inferioris ordinis, cum omnes beati spiritus sese iuvant reciproce, ymmo (ut alias dixi): Michael et omnes angeli inferioris ordinis confirmati pugnarunt contra draconem Luciferum et contra complices suos apostatantes, quare ergo non debet esse proporcionaliter in ecclesiastica ierarchia?

ENGLISH: Hence I wonder how some rely on the example of the celestial hierarchy, thinking that clerics ought not to be corrected by lay people; for, once the order of the militant hierarchy is established as it is in the celestial hierarchy, the argument from likeness would proceed that clerics would not be subject to lay people; but if the [power]{g:virtus} of illuminating and purging fails in them,[^5] so that they are blinder and more criminal than lay people, how would the example of the celestial hierarchy hold in those who disturb the order of the church? Therefore, in their failure, it holds that the order of the ecclesiastical hierarchy is perverted: the order, I say, not [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}, but the individual order that would be in them. For it is not right for a believer to believe that a given clergy will keep the superior order in the church however much it has sinned, since the first angel with his accomplices was thrown down from his order for apostasy [350] from the church triumphant (as is noted in Isaiah 14).[^6] Therefore a wayfarer outside his order ought to have help from a brother of a lower order, since the foot helps the eye, each also preserving the integrity of the body. And so it seems to [the bishop of] Lincoln that an angel of a higher order is illuminated by an angel of a lower order,[^7] since all the blessed spirits help one another reciprocally; indeed (as I have said elsewhere) Michael and all the angels of the lower order, confirmed [in grace], fought against the dragon Lucifer and against his apostate accomplices:[^8] why then should it not be so, proportionally, in the ecclesiastical hierarchy?

### 15-f ¶3 · 350.12–351.19  (8 sed/autem)

ENGLISH: But elsewhere I raised the objection,[^9] by appeal to this: that no civil punitive power extends further than the power of [exercising lordship]{g:dominari} civilly; but it is not lawful for a civil [lord]{g:dominus} to exercise lordship civilly over a cleric, since a cleric for whom it were lawful to serve civilly could with more [colour]{g:color} exercise civil lordship.[^10] But here I have often said that by the law of nature and by the law of the gospel, by which we are bound to fraternal correction, the king of England ought to correct his clergy; nor do I see why it is not consistent that a cleric serve civilly, because to exercise lordship civilly implies pride. But to serve civilly can be without sin. Nor need a man serve civilly in such a way that he is a civil serf, since every lord of England under the king serves him civilly, being his servant [holding of]{g:tenere de} the king [in chief]{g:in capite} by the [services]{g:servicium} owed and customary to his lord. Now a civil serf is wholly without civil liberty; and so the clerics who hold of him in chief serve the king freely; and so it seems to me that our king has over his [liege]{g:legii} clergy, as to the goods of nature and the goods of fortune, [in a certain respect]{g:secundum quid} [351] [civil lordship]{g:dominium civile}.[^11] But if they lived a wholly [propertyless]{g:exproprietarii} life, as Christ and the Baptist lived, they would not be so subject; and so, by reason of the goods of fortune and the goods of nature, in a case where notable abuse or contempt occurs in them, it is lawful for the king to [coerce]{g:cohercere} them civilly, not inasmuch as they are priests, but inasmuch as they are the king's [almsmen]{g:elemosinarius} or his liege men who hold him in contempt. And so they are subject to their king in body and goods in many ways. Conversely, the king ought to be subject to them in the things that pertain to God, so that there is thus a mutual connexion of charity and of order on both sides. Thirdly [it is argued by appeal to]{g:arguitur per} this: that the clergy ought never to be chastised by a layman except in default of the spiritual [superior]{g:prepositi}, and consequently never before the [pope]{g:papa} has failed; therefore the subject must be corrected [first] by the diocesan, secondly by the metropolitan, and, if they have failed, then by the pope. And it is not to be presumed that at these three stages the church of Christ will fail.[^12]

ENGLISH: But as to this caesarean jurisdiction or primacy, it is clear, since our [kingdom]{g:regnum} is exempt from caesarean jurisdiction, that not even the [coactive]{g:coactivus} jurisdiction of his bishop binds us,[^1] except in so far as it can be founded on holy scripture. But let the adversary turn and turn again the faith of scripture, and he will not find such jurisdiction. But whatever priest has more expressly taught [the law of Christ]{g:lex Christi}, Christ is to be obeyed in him more devoutly, since such primacy was introduced from caesarean [rite]{g:ritus}, so that, whether the [superior]{g:prepositi} be fit or not, the faithful people obey Christ. If therefore we English obey our [pope]{g:papa} Urban VI freely, only as a humble servant of Christ, while the schismatics obey Clement for the sake of [lordship]{g:dominium} and secular power, who doubts that as such we have the ground of greater merit?[^2] And we could have seen with our own eyes the possibility of all these five in Gregory XI and his accomplices.[^3] For it is said that he with his pseudo-cardinals condemned four [353] catholic truths and approved four cardinal heresies, as I saw noted in a certain letter, cursed by a man but reproved by no one.[^4] Nor let us shudder at a curse of this kind, since the truth itself was cursed, hanging on the cross (as the Apostle notes, Galatians 3, and Ambrose expounds in letter 38).[^5] For by many it is said to be catholic, on his authority, that by the very fact that he or another Roman pope claims in any way to loose or bind, by that very fact he so looses or binds, since its opposite has been condemned as most wicked.[^6] And I dare say that from the time of the first foundation of the Roman [church]{g:ecclesia} there has not been so manifest an antichristian heresy so publicly proclaimed; for not only holy scripture but the papal laws testify that Peter and all his successors could err, both in the exercise of the keys and also in the way of morals; and if the said Gregory cannot, he is blasphemously lifted up above all that is called God.[^7] Nor do I see to what end that should be published as a new [article of faith]{g:articulus fidei}, except to acquire pomp and gain for the bishop Iscariot.[^8] For, given that article, he could free all souls, even against God's will, both from purgatory and from hell, since he could fall into such madness as to claim to do these things, as those speak who say that he is God on earth. And as to gain, it is clear that, with this article [declared catholic]{g:catholicare}, he could easily acquire all the kingdoms of the world, because (suppose the opposite, in the case of the kingdom of England) against [it]: if he claims to bind our king and kingdom, as part of salutary penance, to give in perpetuity the whole fruit of our kingdom, holding of the Roman bishop [in chief]{g:in capite} as supreme secular [lord]{g:dominus}, then by that very fact they are so bound; now he can claim so to bind; therefore by virtue of that power of the keys he can easily [354] acquire our kingdom.[^9] And the same judgment holds of all the other kingdoms of the world. And the possibility of the minor is clear in three ways. First, from the fact that King John was so bound with his kingdom (as it is said) to the amount of a thousand marks; and the same judgment holds of the whole.[^10] Second, from the fact that many believe that the pope ought to exercise lordship secularly and [as head]{g:capitaliter} over the whole world; nor should it be called in doubt that king and kingdom could oblige themselves to so great a [perpetual alms]{g:elemosina perpetua}. But to whom, if not to the pope, who can reserve absolution and dispensation in this matter to the Roman pontiff alone? For so he says he can restrict the powers of the keys from [plenitude of power]{g:plenitudo potestatis}. Third, it is confirmed by the fact that he has an incomprehensible power, on which it is not lawful to set bounds; because then there would be cause to fear that he exceeds the [limits]{g:limites} of his power,[^11] and faith about the power of the keys would waver, and the opposite of the newly declared catholic article would manifestly follow, namely that it is not by the very fact that he claims in any way to loose or bind that by that very fact he so looses or binds.

### 15-g ¶2 · 354.20–356.16  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 354] In tales insanias inducuntur ignari episcopi et doctores facientes ydiotam credere quod potest tantum facere in ecclesia sicut Christus. Unde episcopus Roffensis dixit mihi in publico parlamento stomachante spiritu quod conclusiones mee sunt dampnate, sicut testificatum est sibi de curia per instrumentum notarii. Et visum est multis quod fuit assercio indiscreta, primo quia vergebat ad curie Romane defamacionem, secundo quia implicabat regis nostri et regni scandalizacionem, et tercio quia ostendebat sui et fratrum suorum consentaneam suspicionem. Non enim tam signanter mitterent fratres eius sibi dictam dampnacionem, nisi applaudendo de facto cuius utraque pars foret auctor vel fautor, nec dubium quin stante iusticia dicte dampnacionis cum facto [p. 355] regis et iure quod vendicat, rex et regnum forent expresse heretici, et sic iuxta leges ecclesie nedum exheredandi, **sed** a toto christianismo funditus destruendi. Nam hoc est hereticatum ut dampnatissimum quod aliqui domini temporales possunt auferre temporalia ab ecclesia delinquente. Unde videtur zelantes pro dampnacione ista intendere quod papa debet super regno nostro imperatorie civiliter dominari (ut sepe tetigi), primo quia procurarunt quod inconsulto rege vel suo consilio virtute bullarum papalium legius homo regis ubicunque in Anglia non convictus super pravitate heretica arrestetur et papali carceri mancipetur, et (ut videtur prudentibus) non posset esse evidencius signum capitalis civilis dominii pape super Angliam. Secundo quia dampnavit ex procuracione quorundam qui videntur esse de nostris quod alique carte humanitus adinvente sunt impossibiles, et per consequens catholicavit oppositum, et cum impossibile et necessarium dicantur condicionaliter et simpliciter (ut patet XXIII, q. IVª, Due sunt) necessitates, sequitur cum veris quod catholicavit omnes cartas cleri Anglie de elemosina perpetua, et per consequens non foret necesse ad regem recurrere pro ratificacione cartarum huiusmodi, cum sint per superiorem dominum nedum confirmate **sed** catholicate. Et tercio potissime, quia vendicat quod non liceat regi punire clerum suum legium quantumcunque in regnum deliquerit nec subtrahere elemosinas cleri quantumcunque contempserit nisi petita ab eo licencia et obtenta, et cum hoc non habet ex lege Christi (sicut patet scrutanti), ab omnibus eius fautoribus relinquitur quod hoc habet a cesare. Unde vellem quod rex faceret diligenter inquiri a clero suo legio istas quatuor questiones: primo si [p. 356] rex licite potest auferre temporalia a clero suo legio pro contemptu; secundo si rex dominatur capitaliter super regno Anglie et papa sic civiliter subdominans vel econtra; tercio si forme dampnacionum et factorum Romani pontificis hic recitate sint iuri divino consone, et si clericus regis qui vel expresse vel mutescendo consenserit videtur in contemptum regis et regni incidere; quarto **autem** queritur a plano parliamento regis, cum propter maiorem contemptum sit contemptor gravius puniendus, utrum defendens ista expresse vel tacite contra regem et regnum debeat cum hoc participare elemosinis regni nostri. Videtur enim quod, si susurrones huiusmodi non expectant nisi subdole premittendo preambulam horam illis congruam, quando expulsis secularibus dominis possint dominari ex integro toti regno.

ENGLISH: Into such madnesses ignorant bishops and doctors are led, making the [unlettered man]{g:ydiota} believe that he can do as much in the church as Christ. Hence the bishop of Rochester said to me in open parliament, with an angry spirit, that my conclusions are condemned, as had been attested to him from the curia by a notary's instrument.[^12] And it seemed to many that it was an indiscreet assertion: first because it tended to the defamation of the [Roman curia]{g:curia romana}; second because it implicated our king and kingdom in scandal; and third because it showed a suspicion of collusion on his part and his [brothers']{g:frater}. For his brothers would not so pointedly send him the said condemnation unless applauding a deed of which each party would be author or abettor; nor is there doubt that, if the justice of the said condemnation stands together with the deed [355] of the king and the right that he claims, king and kingdom would be expressly heretics,[^13] and so, according to the laws of the church, not only to be disinherited but to be utterly destroyed from all Christendom. For this has been [pronounced heretical]{g:hereticare} as most damnable: that some [temporal lords]{g:temporales domini} can take away [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} from a delinquent church.[^14] Hence those zealous for that condemnation seem to [maintain]{g:intendere} that the pope ought to exercise lordship over our kingdom imperially and civilly (as I have often touched on): first, because they procured that, without consulting the king or his council, by virtue of papal bulls a [liege]{g:legii} man of the king, anywhere in England, not convicted of [heretical depravity]{g:heretica pravitas}, be arrested and committed to the papal prison; and (as it seems to prudent men) there could not be a more evident sign of the pope's chief civil lordship over England.[^15] Second, because, at the procurement of certain men who seem to be of our own, he condemned [the proposition] that some charters humanly devised are impossible, and consequently [declared] the opposite catholic;[^16] and since "impossible" and "necessary" are said, as necessities, conditionally and [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} (as is clear in Cause 23, question 4, *Due sunt*),[^17] it follows, with true [premisses], that he declared catholic all the charters of the clergy of England concerning perpetual alms, and consequently it would not be necessary to have recourse to the king for the ratification of such charters, since they are by a superior lord not only confirmed but declared catholic.[^18] And third, and most of all, because he claims that it is not lawful for the king to punish his liege clergy however much they offend against the kingdom, nor to withdraw the [alms]{g:elemosina} of the clergy however much it has shown contempt, unless leave has been asked of him and obtained; and since he does not have this from the law of Christ (as is clear to one who examines it), it is left to all his supporters [to say] that he has it from Caesar. Hence I would wish the king to have diligent inquiry made of his liege clergy on these four questions: first, whether [356] the king can lawfully take away temporal goods from his liege clergy for contempt; second, whether the king exercises lordship as head over the kingdom of England, and the pope thus civilly [holding lordship under]{g:subdominans} [him], or conversely; third, whether the forms of the condemnations and of the deeds of the Roman pontiff recited here are consonant with divine [law]{g:ius}, and whether the king's cleric who has consented, either expressly or by keeping silent, seems to fall into contempt of the king and kingdom; and fourth, it is asked of the king's full parliament, since by reason of greater contempt the contemner is to be punished more gravely, whether one who defends these things expressly or tacitly against king and kingdom ought, with this, to share in the alms of our kingdom. For it seems that whisperers of this kind are waiting only for an hour suited to them, which they cunningly arrange beforehand as a preamble,[^19] when, the secular lords being expelled, they could exercise lordship entirely over the whole kingdom.[^20]
