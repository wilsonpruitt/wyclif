# *De eucharistia*, chapter 6, part 7: the bishops' three uses: the host as heaviness, as quantity, as quality

## ¶1 · 182.28–183.3

> [p. 182] Unde nota nimis magne infidelitatis foret in illis quod figuram pecunie et eius quidditatem cognoscerent sed formam et quidditatem huius hostie penitus [p. 183] ignorarent. Quid enim plus scandalizaret prelatum, quam quod diligenter curet de karactere sigilli vel carte et non curet de quidditate hostie consecrate.

Hence it would be a mark of [exceedingly]{g:nimis} great [unbelief]{g:infidelitas} in them that they should know the figure of money and its quiddity but be wholly [183] [ignorant of]{g:ignorare} the form and quiddity of this [host]{g:hostia}. For what should scandalize a [prelate]{g:prelatus} more than that he should care diligently about the stamp of a seal or a charter and not care about the quiddity of the consecrated host?

## ¶2 · 183.4–184.15

> [p. 183] Ex istis colligitur primo quod illi qui ex ista ignorancia infideli sine causa accusant prelatos ecclesie sunt culpandi. Secundo quod illi prelati religionum qui in suis capitulis accusant precipue fratres suos, quia predicant in patria quod sacramentum altaris nec est corpus Christi nec sanguis, sed efficax eius signum; tales enim prelati tanquam infideles heretici inhabilitant se ad quascunque dotaciones regis et alia suffragia regni nostri. Et tercio creditur quod episcopi acute prosecuntur errores dogmatisantes in ista materia, cum instanter persecuti sunt evangelisantes quod domini temporales debent exonerare eos a temporalibus auferendis in casu quo eorum ponderacio ceca undique sit nociva. Ille enim qui preponderat signum fallax et fetidum prosperitatis mundane signo vero et venerabili corporis domini Jesu Christi nedum est indignus possidere predia sed ut sit incola terre nostre: ideo timendo istam persecucionem severam protestatus sum publice quod volo humiliter corrigi per quoscunque et specialiter per episcopos qui docuerint in ista materia veritatem. Sed volo querere signum, unde doctrine quam asserunt fideliter possum confidere. Possunt autem omnes usus ecclesiarum regis nostri ad istam triplicitatem reduci: primo dicitur secundum illos episcopos qui innituntur legi cesaree quod sacramentum altaris est ponderositas: utinam non libret suos usurarios ad infernum. Fundatur autem istud super dicto glosse ordinarie cuiusdam [p. 184] cesarei in De Consecracione, distinccione IIᵃ, capitulo Sacramentum, ubi movet dubium si hoc sacramentum potest cadere, cum sit accidens et non pondus: Dicit, inquit, quod potest cadere et dicit quod ponderositas remanet cum accidentibus aliis et dicit quod illa non est aliquid ponderosum, cum sacramentum quod per se movetur non sit ponderosum, relinquitur quod sit ponderositas in abstracto. Et sic maior pars Anglicane ecclesie ad ponderositatem hostie declinabit, cum quasi per totam Cantuariensem provinciam servabitur iste usus. Sed constat quod auctor huius sentencie ignorat vocem propriam, cum hoc sacramentum potest ad omnem eius punctum ponderositatem deperdere; oportet ergo dare aliquid ponderosum quod fiat continue remissius ponderosum.

From these things it is gathered, first, that those who from this unbelieving ignorance accuse the prelates of the [church]{g:ecclesia} without cause are to be blamed. Secondly, that those prelates of the religious [orders] who in their chapters accuse their brothers chiefly because they preach in the [homeland]{g:patria} that the [sacrament of the altar]{g:sacramentum-altaris} is neither the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} nor the blood, but its efficacious [sign]{g:signum}:[^1] for such prelates, as unbelieving heretics, [disqualify]{g:inhabilitare} themselves for any [endowments]{g:dotacio} of the king and the other [aids]{g:suffragium} of our [kingdom]{g:regnum}. And thirdly, it is believed that the bishops sharply prosecute the errors of those who [teach as dogma]{g:dogmatisare} in this matter, since they have urgently persecuted those [preaching the gospel]{g:evangelizans} that the [temporal lords]{g:temporales-domini} ought to unburden them of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, which are to be taken away in the case where their blind [weighing]{g:ponderacio} [of them] is harmful on every side.[^2] For he who weighs the false and fetid sign of worldly prosperity above the true and venerable sign of the body of the Lord Jesus Christ is unworthy [not only]{g:nedum} to possess estates but even to be a dweller in our land: therefore, fearing this severe persecution, I have publicly [declared solemnly]{g:protestari} that I wish to be humbly corrected by anyone whatever, and especially by the bishops who shall teach the truth in this matter.[^3] But I wish to seek a sign by which I can faithfully trust the [doctrine]{g:doctrina} that they assert. And all the [uses]{g:usus} of the churches of our king can be reduced to this threefold: first it is said, according to those bishops who lean on the [caesarean law]{g:lex-cesarea}, that the sacrament of the altar is [heaviness]{g:ponderositas}: would that it may not weigh their usurers down to hell.[^4] And this is founded on a saying of the ordinary gloss of a certain [184] caesarean on *De consecratione*, distinction 2, chapter *Sacramentum*, where it raises the doubt whether this sacrament can fall, since it is an [accident]{g:accidens} and not a weight. It says, he says, that it can fall, and it says that heaviness remains with the other accidents, and it says that that is not anything heavy;[^5] since the sacrament, which is moved [of itself]{g:per-se}, is not heavy, it remains that it is heaviness in the abstract. And so the greater part of the English church will incline to the heaviness of the host, since this use will be kept through almost the whole province of Canterbury. But it is evident that the author of this [teaching]{g:sentencia} is ignorant of the proper word,[^6] since this sacrament can lose heaviness at every point of it; one must therefore posit something heavy which becomes continually more remissly heavy.

## ¶3 · 184.16–185.11

> [p. 184] Secundus usus sparsus in multis ecclesiis est quod hostia altaris sit quantitas; sed iste usus est non minus a katholicis abrogandus, cum stat sacramentum istud per totum rarefieri vel condensari et per consequens ipsum sacramentum sic moveretur, quod non potest competere quantitati, quia sic remaneret antiqua quantitas acquirens ad omnem punctum quantitatem novam, et per consequens immediate postquam maiorari inceperit, fieret infinitum, quia infinitas quantitates secundum se totas acquireret, quarum quelibet foret maior quam sacramentum in principio assignatum; ideo difficultatur iste usus quomodo [p. 185] hoc sacramentum potest tales quantitates acquirere tanquam partes, cum nec sint partes eius quantitative nec qualitative; nec quantitas talis potest esse accidens quantitati nec sacramentum istud sic subito deperdi; unde quamvis diocesis Linconiensis cui dicitur iste usus competere sit ampla et rara, tamen oportet ipsam inniti magis stabili fundamento, nam superficies vel linea non potest sic qualificari in hostia, sed sanctitas sacramentalis videtur esse in qualitate que est de genere activorum; constancior enim videtur esse duricies quantitate.

The second use, spread in many churches, is that the host of the altar is [quantity]{g:quantitas}; but this use is no less to be abrogated by catholics, since it can be that this sacrament is rarefied or condensed throughout, and consequently the sacrament itself would be so moved, which cannot belong to quantity: because thus the old quantity would remain, acquiring at every point a new quantity, and consequently [immediately]{g:immediate} after it had begun to be increased it would become infinite, because it would acquire infinitely many quantities, each in its whole self, any one of which would be greater than the sacrament assigned at the beginning.[^7] Therefore this use is put in difficulty [over] how [185] this sacrament can acquire such quantities as parts, since they are neither its [quantitative parts]{g:pars-quantitativa} nor its [qualitative]{g:pars-qualitativa} ones; nor can such a quantity be an accident to quantity; nor can this sacrament be so suddenly lost. Hence, although the diocese of Lincoln, to which this use is said to belong, is ample and rare, yet it must lean on a more stable foundation;[^8] for a surface or a line cannot be so qualified in the host, but sacramental holiness seems to be in a [quality]{g:qualitas} which is of the genus of the active [qualities]; for hardness seems to be more constant than quantity.

## ¶4 · 185.12–185.17

> [p. 185] Ideo tercius usus est credere quod sacramentum altaris sit qualitas. Unde illi qui vident acucius eligunt quod hoc sacramentum de omnibus qualitatibus sit albedo; ipsa enim plus inter alios colores suscitat visionem; et hic usus in Vallia montuosa et Hibernia ubi vident mortuos est famosus.

Therefore the third use is to believe that the sacrament of the altar is a quality. Hence those who see more sharply choose that this sacrament is, of all qualities, whiteness; for it, more than the other colours, arouses vision; and this use is well known in mountainous Wales and in Ireland, where they see the dead.[^9]

## ¶5 · 185.18–186.19

> [p. 185] Sed patet error in isto usu ex hoc quod nec variatur hoc sacramentum in specie ut color hostie, nec qualitas talis potest exspectare intencionem et remissionem in colore ut hostia, quia nec potest sic alterari nec componi ex suis partibus intensivis, sic quod una medietas intensiva sit sacramentum et alia non sacramentum. Sic enim posset rarefieri et condensari, et per consequens tanquam prius natura posset sibi subicere quantitatem. Potest enim successive fieri maius aut minus et per consequens acquirere vel deperdere magnitudinem. Et tunc nimis errarunt philosophi ponentes quantitatem secundum genus precedere qualitatem. Si ergo hoc sacramentum sit sic quantum, tunc est coloratum et per consequens non est color, sed distinguuntur ut accidens et subiectum. Si, inquam, [p. 186] hoc sacramentum sit quantum accidentatum et quale intensum, tunc quantitas sibi accidentalis quam acquirit est alterius racionis et est dignius quantitate. Et istud videtur decretalis sentire libro III, capitulo Cum Marthe, que ponit colorem vini sine subiecto posse profundere se per aquam maiorem, quod non potest competere quantitati; quantitas ergo que potest per se occupare locum maiorem quam nunc occupat et sic acquirere ac perdere quantitatem potest rarefieri et condensari. Et iuxta hoc quot sunt ecclesie tot possunt esse errores mendaces de quidditate hostie, ut unus dicit quod est odor, alius quod est duricies, tercius quod est sapor et sic in infinitum secundum quod afficiunt qualitati quam faciunt. Et omnes isti senciunt minus erronee quam illi qui ponunt quod sacramentum est quantitas vel fasciculus omnis generis accidentis. Omnes tamen isti in hoc conveniunt quod hoc sacramentum est infinitum abieccioris nature quam aliqua vilis substancia corporea assignanda.

But the error in this use is clear from this: that neither is this sacrament varied in species, as the colour of the host is, nor can such a quality await intension and remission in colour, as the host can; because it can neither be so altered nor be composed of its intensive parts, so that one intensive half would be the sacrament and the other not the sacrament.[^10] For thus it could be rarefied and condensed, and consequently, as prior by nature, could subject quantity to itself. For it can successively become greater or less, and consequently acquire or lose magnitude. And then the philosophers erred exceedingly in positing that quantity precedes quality in genus. If therefore this sacrament is thus [quantified]{g:quantus}, then it is coloured, and consequently it is not colour, but they are distinguished as accident and [subject]{g:subiectum}. If, I say, [186] this sacrament is a quantified thing [subject to accidents]{g:accidentatus} and an intensified qualified thing, then the quantity accidental to it which it acquires is [of another kind]{g:alterius-racionis} and is worthier than quantity. And this the [decretal]{g:decretalis} seems to [hold]{g:sentire}, book 3, chapter *Cum Marthe*, which posits that the colour of wine [without a subject]{g:accidens-sine-subiecto} can pour itself out through a greater [quantity of] water, which cannot belong to quantity;[^11] therefore the quantity which can of itself occupy a greater place than it now occupies, and so acquire and lose quantity, can be rarefied and condensed. And according to this, as many churches as there are, so many lying errors can there be about the quiddity of the host: as one says that it is odour, another that it is hardness, a third that it is savour, and so to infinity, according as they are attached to the quality which they make [the sacrament].[^12] And all these hold less erroneously than those who posit that the sacrament is quantity, or a [bundle of accident]{g:fasciculus-accidentis} of every genus. Yet all these agree in this: that this sacrament is of an infinitely more abject nature than any vile corporeal [substance]{g:substancia} that could be assigned.

## ¶6 · 186.20–186.28

> [p. 186] Et quantum ad mores attinet, modicum honoramus hoc sacramentum, cum honor ille stat in imitacione Christi in moribus, in vita pauperi et penali et in mundi abieccione pro ritu iusticie; quod licet sit infinitum melius hostia parum attendimus sed mendaciter fingimus indumentum abiectissimum corpori Christi competere et accumulamus mendacium, dicentes Christum signum istius nature ad cultum suum statuere.

And as far as morals are concerned, we honour this sacrament little, since that honour consists in the imitation of Christ in morals, in a poor and [hard]{g:penalis} life, and in the casting-off of the world for the [rite]{g:ritus} of justice; which, though it is infinitely better than the host, we attend to little, but we lyingly feign that a most abject garment belongs to the body of Christ, and we heap up the lie, saying that Christ appointed a sign of this nature for his [worship]{g:cultus}.[^13]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 183.4: They are to blame who accuse prelates wrongly. Those prelates are false heretics who prosecute their brethren for preaching the symbolic nature of the sacrament.
- 183.11: They shew themselves only too ready to prosecute.
- 183.21: Wyclif is ready to be corrected but desires some reason for trusting in the bishops' teaching.
- 183.26: Three beliefs common in the Church: 1. That the host is weight.
- 184.16: 2. The Sacrament is quantity.
- 185.4: This view prevails in Lincoln diocese.
- 185.12: 3. The Sacrament is quality, say whiteness. White is the most visible colour, so this notion is naturally prevalent in the welsh mountains and in Ireland where men see the dead.
- 186.10: Other views as the host: that it is odour, hardness etc.

## Notes

[^1]: **T/C.** Loserth notes at 183.6 (*quod illi prelati religionum*) that "some words have fallen out here": the second corollary has no main verb (it wants e.g. "are heretics", which the *tales enim prelati … heretici* clause then grounds). Translated as printed, without supplying. *Religionum*: of the religious orders (*religio privata*).

[^2]: **C.** *Ponderacio* ("weighing", valuation) answers the *ponderositas* of the first use below and *preponderat* in the next sentence. The clause is ironic: the bishops' zeal against the gospel preachers is the ground for "believing" they prosecute errors on the sacrament. *Auferendis* is taken as a gerundive with *temporalibus*.

[^3]: **C.** The *protestacio*: a solemn offer to be corrected, not a defiance.

[^4]: **C.** *Usus* is also the term for a church's liturgical Use; the regional spread Wyclif gives (Canterbury province, Lincoln, Wales and Ireland) suggests the play, and *libret* ("weigh down") plays on *ponderositas*. Rendered "use" throughout (LEDGER K06g-1).

[^5]: **S/T.** Loserth: "more rightly" the chapter *Si per negligenciam*, *De cons.* D. 2 c. 27 (modern form ours), gloss *ad v.* *In terram*: "Say that heaviness still remains with the other accidents, and yet nothing there is heavy" (LEDGER K06g-2). The gloss has the imperative *Dic quod*; the print's *Dicit, inquit, … dicit … dicit* may answer it. At 184.6–7 ABC lack *cum sacramentum … ponderosum*, which C adds in the margin by another hand; the inference that follows is Wyclif's, not the gloss's.

[^6]: **C.** *Sentencia* (CONTEXTUAL): "teaching", the position taught. *Vocem propriam*: the proper word for the thing (heaviness against heavy, the abstract against the concrete).

[^7]: **C.** *Immediate postquam* in the scholastic sense: at no intermediate instant after the increase begins. *Cum stat* "since it can be". The argument: if the host were its quantity, every increase would add a new whole quantity while the old remained.

[^8]: **C.** *Ampla et rara* is a pun: the diocese of Lincoln is large and thinly peopled, and *rara* echoes *rarefieri*. "Rare" keeps both senses.

[^9]: **C.** The second sight of the Welsh and Irish is Wyclif's jibe at a usage that takes the host for whiteness, the colour that most "arouses vision".

[^10]: **C.** *In specie* is taken in the logician's sense (in kind, as a colour passes from one species of colour to another), not the sacramental species. *Exspectare intencionem et remissionem*: admit of being intensified and remitted.

[^11]: **S.** Loserth: *Decretales* Gregory IX, book 3, tit. 41, cap. 4 (*Cum Marthe*), modern form ours (LEDGER K06g-3).

[^12]: **C.** *Afficiunt qualitati quam faciunt* is compressed: they are attached to the quality that each makes the sacrament to be. D reads *afficiuntur* (186.14).

[^13]: **C.** The "most abject garment" is the accident without a subject, which the newer doctors make the clothing of Christ's body; the "sign of this nature" is that accident as sign.
