# *De ecclesia*, chapter 8, part 4: corollaries of the definition of privilege; the sons of Zebedee and the cup

## ¶1 · 168.4–169.28

> [p. 168] Ex istis patet primo non equivocantibus quod proprietates que naturaliter competunt trinitati non sunt earum privilegia, cum omne privilegium principaliter ab extrinseco pro utilitate inest privilegiato, et sic solum creatura racionalis est capax privilegii. Patet secundo quod privilegiante aut privilegiato decepto in credendo privilegium vergere privilegiando ad commodum, dum tamen concessum privilegiando cedat ad dampnum tamquam utilitati sue contrarium, non est privilegium sed pocius corruptela. Patet ex hoc quod privilegium (in quantum huiusmodi) est bonum utile, ut non est privilegium infidelibus quod liberati a iusticia exonerati sunt a iugo domini et effrenes ad peccandum subiecti iugo diaboli, nisi forte equivocetur, vocando privilegium quodlibet privans legem. Tercio sequitur quod non est aliquod privilegium, nisi quod princeps pacis concesserit, caput universalis ecclesie, dominus Jesus Christus. Patet ex hoc quod non potest esse bonum ecclesie, nisi primo venerit per derivacionem a capite. Si enim primas privatus concedit aliquid et Deus illud non concedit, tunc vel oportet concedere quod Deus non prevenit ipsum in agendo, et per consequens non est causa essencialis cuiuslibet creature, vel aliter quod non est bonum vel rationale quod privilegiatus illud habeat, quia si esset sapiencia Dei, eternaliter ordinaret ut habeat, et cum clemencia spiritus sancti graciose illud donaret; cum Johannis XV°, 5, 4, dicit caput: Sine me nichil potestis facere, sicut palmes non potest facere fructum, nisi manserit in vite, sic [p. 169] enim privilegium concessum domesticis non est ratum, dum tamen ab essenciali patrefamilias omnipotente et omnisciente indultum non fuerit et optenta eius licencia confirmatum. Ex istis colligitur quod summum privilegium concessum christiano pro via foret quod, exproprietarie vivendo, Christo similius foret anterior in exercitu christiano. Patet primo sic: Iuxta proximum corellarium nullum est privilegium nisi concessum a Christo, ipse autem non potest concedere maius privilegium quam plus assimilando subditum sibi ipsi, ergo etc. Unde Matthei XVIII°, 1, quando discipuli quesierunt a Christo: Quis putas maior est in regno celorum, ipse advocans parvulum sine malicia statuit eum in medio illorum dixitque re et sermone quod qui sic plus se humiliaverit hic maior est in ecclesia militante. Item, cuicunque militi pro statu milicie foret aptacius quod armatus decencius et adiutus specialius ponatur in acie belli, ubi forcior est conflictus, in hoc enim consistit militaris strenuitas. Sed ut dicitur Job VII°, 1: Milicia est tota vita hominis super terram, et iuxta Apostolum Eph. VI°, 12: Non est nobis colluctacio adversus carnem et sanguinem, sed adversus principes et potestates, adversus mundi rectores tenebrarum harum, contra spiritualia nequicie. Ideo patet quod, quanto quis fuerit sic positus anterius in dicto exercitu, indultum est sibi privilegium eo maius quo amplius coronabitur. Et patet ista sentencia de matre

From these things it is clear, first, to those who do not equivocate, that the properties which belong naturally to the Trinity are not its privileges, since every privilege is in the one privileged principally from without, for his profit; and so only a rational creature is capable of privilege.[^1] It is clear, secondly, that when the one granting or the one privileged is deceived in believing that the privilege tends to the benefit of the one being privileged, while yet what is granted to the one being privileged turns to his harm, as contrary to his profit, it is not a privilege but rather a corruption.[^2] This is clear from the fact that a privilege (in so far as it is such) is a useful good; so it is no privilege for [unbelievers]{g:infidelis} that, set free from justice, they are discharged from the yoke of the [Lord]{g:dominus} and, unbridled for sinning, made subject to the yoke of the devil, unless perhaps the word is used equivocally, calling a privilege anything that deprives of law.[^3] Thirdly, it follows that there is no privilege except what the Prince of Peace has granted, the [head]{g:caput} of the universal [church]{g:ecclesia}, the Lord Jesus Christ. This is clear from the fact that there cannot be a good of the church unless it has first come by derivation from the head. For if a primate on his own grants something and God does not grant it, then either one must grant that God does not go before him in acting, and consequently is not the essential cause of every creature, or else that it is not good or reasonable that the one privileged should have it; because if it were the wisdom of God, he would eternally ordain that he should have it, and with the clemency of the Holy Spirit would graciously give it;[^4] since, John 15:5, 4, the head says: "Without me you can do nothing"; for as "the branch cannot bear fruit, unless it abide in the vine", so [169] a privilege granted to the members of the household is not valid so long as it has not been granted by the essential father of the household, almighty and all-knowing, and confirmed by his licence obtained.[^5] From these things it is gathered that the highest privilege granted to a Christian for [the way]{g:via} would be that, [living without property]{g:exproprietarii}, he should, being more like Christ, be further forward in the Christian army.[^6] It is clear, first, thus: by the next corollary there is no privilege except one granted by Christ; now he cannot grant a greater privilege than by making the subject more like himself; therefore etc. Hence, Matthew 18:1, when the disciples asked Christ, "Who, thinkest thou, is the greater in the [kingdom]{g:regnum} of heaven?", he, calling a little child without malice, set him in the midst of them, and said in deed and in word that he who has humbled himself more in this way, he is the greater in the [church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans}.[^7] Again, for any knight it would be more fitting, for the state of [knighthood]{g:milicia}, that, armed more becomingly and aided more specially, he should be placed in the line of battle where the fight is fiercer; for in this consists military vigour.[^8] Now, as is said, Job 7:1: "The whole life of man upon earth is a warfare"; and according to the Apostle, Ephesians 6:12: "Our wrestling is not against flesh and blood; but against principalities and powers, against the rulers of the world of this darkness, against the spirits of wickedness." Therefore it is clear that the further forward anyone has been placed thus in the said army, the greater the privilege granted him, by as much as he will be the more fully crowned.[^9] And this [teaching]{g:sentencia} is clear from the mother

## ¶3 · 170.5–170.20

> [p. 170] Super quo Crisostomus, Omelia XXXV° Operis In-, perfecti notat primo quod diabolus qui seduxit primum virum per feminam movit ut dicti apostoli seducerentur per feminam. Notat secundo quomodo Christus dirigens sermonem ad illos discipulos docet quod illi ex ceca presumpcione atque superba primatum culpabiliter pecierunt, aliter enim non direxisset omnisciens increpativum sermonem ad illos discipulos dimissa femina procurante: Nescitis, inquit, quid petatis. Ideo dicit Cristostomus: O error salutaris qui tocius mundi solvit errorem. Si enim apostoli non sic errassent, unde nos cognoscere poteramus quod non omne quod videtur bonum desiderare bonum est? Cum enim scimus quod aliud est opus bonum et aliud primatus honoris. Et opus quidem bonum desiderare bonum est, primatum autem honoris vanitas est.

On this Chrysostom, in Homily 35 of the Opus imperfectum, notes, first, that the devil, who seduced the first man through a woman, contrived that the said apostles should be seduced through a woman. He notes, secondly, how Christ, directing his speech to those disciples, teaches that they culpably sought the primacy out of blind and proud presumption; for otherwise the all-knowing one would not have directed a rebuking speech to those disciples, passing over the woman who was acting on their behalf: "You know not," he says, "what you ask." Therefore Chrysostom says: "O salutary error, which loosed the error of the whole world! For if the apostles had not erred thus, whence could we have known that not everything that seems good is good to desire? For we know that a good work is one thing and the primacy of honour another. And to desire a good work is good, but [to desire] the primacy of honour is vanity,"[^11]

## ¶4 · 170.21–172.7

> [p. 170] Tercio notandum quod sive apostoli errore seducti pecierunt primatum in regno temporali sive in regno celorum, sicut videtur ex textu convinci, stulta fuit eorum peticio, cum debuerunt laborem certaminis humiliter petivisse, et ad hoc sine iactancia primatus honor beatitudinis secutus fuisset; honor enim fugientem se sequitur et refugit affectantem. Unde dicit Crisostomus: Desiderium primatus ex iactancia nascitur cordis; qui, inquit, humilis est indigniorem se aliis arbitratur [p. 171] iuxta apostolicum preceptum: Alter alterum existimans superiorem. Et hec racio (ut tactum est superius), quare membra ecclesie et gradus quibus Deus ipsos diligit sunt nobis abscondita. Quarto notandum quod Christus videns alienum spiritum in cordibus templi sui, non more conquirencium castra sua corporalia, disrupit muros castri, sed hostem expulit alliciens eos ad humilitatem qua ipsimet superent tyrannum qui eos turpiter superavit. Potestis, inquit, bibere calicem quem ego bibiturus sum? Ex qua interrogacione mulcente docemur, primo quod oportet victoriam esse voluntariam, si debet esse meritoria, ideo querit Christus ab eis modeste, si possunt pati voluntarie instar sui. Secundo docentur ceteri apostoli non indignari contra duos fratres, cum dominus eorum, contra quem principaliter usurpata fuit iniuria, tam urbane optulit eis idem medium quo ipse meruit dignitatem. Tercio docentur omnes christiani quod pati propter iusticiam sit necessarium cuilibet volenti beatifice premiari, cum dominus incessit per summum gradum illius medii; ideo vocat Christus hoc singulariter suum calicem. Quinto docemur quomodo Christus non potest suis deficere in concedendo privilegia, et consequenter mercedem ex eorum usu sequentem, cum statim post humilem consensum fraternum quo dixerunt possumus, statim concessit: calicem quidem meum bibetis, ubi non licet fideli ambigere quin concessit duobus fratribus quos prius gratificavit privilegium singulare. Si enim terreni patres noverunt bona data dare filiis suis, quanto plus pater primus post tam gloriosam victoriam quam per alios fecit in principem huius mundi? Lex ergo privata quam princeps pacis, et per consequens tocius ecclesie concessit hiis fratribus ad commodum eorum maximum [p. 172] quod possint regnum celorum rapere armis suis, fuit privilegium singulare. Et ex isto eliciunt doctores quod est dare duplex genus martirii, scilicet affeccione et corporali passione; cum beatus Evangelista non fuit sic passus corporaliter usque ad mortem ut Jacobus, sed uterque iuxta vocem predicti Christi veridicam bibit calicem passionis.

Thirdly, it is to be noted that whether the apostles, seduced by error, sought primacy in a temporal kingdom or in the kingdom of heaven, as the text seems to establish, their petition was foolish, since they ought to have asked humbly for the labour of the contest, and upon this the primacy of honour of blessedness would have followed without boasting; for honour follows the one who flees it and flees the one who [desires]{g:affectare} it.[^12] Hence Chrysostom says: "The desire of primacy is born of boasting of heart; he who is humble," he says, "judges himself less worthy than others, [171] according to the apostolic precept: Each esteeming the other superior." And this is the reason (as was touched on above) why the members of the church, and the degrees in which God loves them, are hidden from us.[^13] Fourthly, it is to be noted that Christ, seeing an alien spirit in the hearts of his temple, did not, in the manner of those who conquer their bodily castles, break down the walls of the castle, but drove out the enemy, enticing them to the humility by which they themselves might overcome the tyrant who had foully overcome them. "Can you," he says, "drink the chalice that I shall drink?"[^14] From this soothing question we are taught, first, that the victory must be voluntary if it is to be meritorious; therefore Christ asks them modestly whether they can suffer voluntarily after his likeness. Secondly, the other apostles are taught not to be indignant against the two brothers, since their Lord, against whom chiefly the wrong had been usurped, so courteously offered them the same means by which he himself merited dignity. Thirdly, all Christians are taught that to suffer for justice is necessary for anyone who wishes to be rewarded with blessedness, since the Lord went by the highest degree of that means; therefore Christ calls this, singularly, his chalice.[^15] Fifthly, we are taught how Christ cannot fail his own in granting privileges, and consequently the reward following from their use, since immediately after the humble brotherly consent by which they said "We can", he at once granted: "My chalice indeed you shall drink"; where it is not lawful for a believer to doubt that he granted to the two brothers, whom he had first favoured, a singular privilege. For if earthly fathers "know how to give good gifts to their children", how much more the first Father, after so glorious a victory as he has won through others over the prince of this world?[^16] The private law, therefore, which the Prince of Peace, and consequently [Prince] of the whole church, granted to these brothers for their greatest benefit, [172] that they might seize the kingdom of heaven by their arms, was a singular privilege. And from this the doctors draw out that there is a twofold kind of martyrdom, namely by affection and by bodily suffering; since the blessed Evangelist did not suffer thus bodily unto death, as James did, but each, according to the truthful word of the aforesaid Christ, drank the chalice of the passion.[^17]

## ¶5 · 172.8–172.24

> [p. 172] Sexto docentur omnes fideles et specialiter prelati non pompare vel alta sapere de gradus excellencia vel primatu, cum Christus coniungit: Sedere autem ad dexteram vel ad sinistram non est meum dare vobis, sed quibus paratum est a patre meo. Quamvis enim Christus sit omnipotens et eiusdem auctoritatis et operacionis cum patre, pater ex se et ipse per se, tamen Christus more suo loquens humanitus prefert patrem, dicens quod nullum membrum ecclesie habebit mansionem in celo, nisi qui ex auctoritate patris eternaliter inscribitur libro vite; quod licet eleccione eterna sit factum per filium, quia tamen discipuli considerarunt Christum secundum humanitatem, ideo univocando cum eis fuit pertinens Christum vere atque humiliter ostendere minoritatem suam secundum humanitatem, cum non sit menciendum propter devocionem.

Sixthly, all the faithful, and especially [prelates]{g:prelatus}, are taught not to make a show, nor to mind high things, about excellence of degree or primacy, since Christ adds: "But to sit on the right or on the left hand is not mine to give to you, but to them for whom it is prepared by my Father." For although Christ is almighty and of the same authority and operation with the Father, the Father from himself and he through himself, yet Christ, speaking after his manner as a man, puts the Father first, saying that no member of the church will have a mansion in heaven except one who by the authority of the Father is eternally written in the book of life; and although this was done by eternal election through the Son, yet because the disciples considered Christ according to his humanity, it was therefore fitting, speaking univocally with them, that Christ should truly and humbly show his lesser standing according to his humanity, since one must not lie for the sake of devotion.[^18]

## Notes

[^1]: **C/T.** These are corollaries of the definition of privilege at 167.32–168.3 (08-c): a special licence granted to a creature. A margin *Nota de privilegiis ecclesie continue infra* (168.7). *Non equivocantibus*: for those who use the word in its proper sense, not equivocally. *Earum*: plural, of the divine persons. Side-note 168.4.

[^2]: **C.** *Privilegiante aut privilegiato decepto …*: an ablative absolute. We take *privilegiando* (twice) as a dative gerundive, "to the one being privileged". It could also be an ablative gerund, "in the privileging". Side-note 168.9.

[^3]: **T/C.** B lacks *ut* (168.15). *Privans legem*: the wordplay on *privi-legium* as "depriving of law" sets it against the true sense, a private law (08-c, 167.18).

[^4]: **T/C.** *Primas privatus*: a primate acting on his own authority, without God. *Prevenit*: God goes before every agent as first cause (cf. *gracia preveniens*). The codices read *racio'*; Loserth prints *racionale* (168.28), translated "reasonable".

[^5]: **S.** John 15:5, then 15:4. Wyclif has *facere fructum* where the Vulgate has *ferre fructum a semetipso*, and he lacks "of itself". *Domesticis*: the members of the household (Eph. 2:19), that is, the church. *Dum tamen … non fuerit*: "so long as it has not been".

[^6]: **T/G.** A margin *Prima conclusio* (169.5). A reads *anteterior* (169.7). *Exproprietarie*: the adverb of *exproprietarii*, "the propertyless" (GLOSSARY). *Anterior in exercitu*: further forward in the battle line, the figure worked out in the next sentences. Side-note 169.5.

[^7]: **S/C.** Matt. 18:1–4: the question is in the DR's words. "Without malice", "in deed and in word" and "in the church militant" are Wyclif's own glosses. Where the Vulgate has *in regno caelorum* at 18:4, Wyclif reads it of the church militant.

[^8]: **T.** A reads *optacius* for *aptacius* (169.17). A and A₁ read *specialiter* for *specialius* (169.18). *Milicia* here is the estate of knighthood (GLOSSARY). In Job 7:1, which follows, the same word is the DR's "warfare".

[^9]: **S.** Job 7:1: Wyclif adds *tota*, "the whole", which the Vulgate lacks. Eph. 6:12 DR: he stops before *in caelestibus*, "in the high places". *Coronabitur*: the crown of the one who strives lawfully (cf. 2 Tim. 2:5).

[^10]: **S/T.** Matt. 20:20–23 DR. The petition is 20:21, but the print cites XX°, 22. *Quo concesso*: once they had granted it, that is, once they had answered "We can" (20:22), which Wyclif does not quote here but recalls at 171.25. B reads *patet ex ista* (169.28). A reads *Zebei* (169.29) and *Noscitis* (169.33). B reads *consessit illis privilegium in* (170.1). Side-note 169.28.

[^11]: **S/T.** Pseudo-Chrysostom, *Opus imperfectum in Matthaeum*, hom. 35. Loserth gives "l.c., CLII E" and compares Richard FitzRalph, *Defensio curatorum* (Goldast, *Monarchia* II.ii, p. 1403) (LEDGER E08d-1, E08d-2). The codices read *Omelia XXVIII*; Loserth prints XXXV, translated. Three of his corrections from the *Opus imperfectum* are translated: *poteramus* where the codices have *poterimus* (170.16); *omne quod videtur bonum* where they have *omne bonum* (170.17); and *opus quidem bonum desiderare* where they lack *bonum* (170.19). B lacks *illos* (170.9). *Femina procurante*: the mother, acting as their agent. "To desire a good work is good" recalls 1 Tim. 3:1.

[^12]: **S/C.** *Honor … fugientem se sequitur*: a proverbial saying. No source found; cf. the *Vita Bernardi* III (PL 185:304A), *prosequebatur gloria fugientem* (LEDGER E08d-3). *Sicut videtur ex textu convinci*: Matt. 20:21 has "in thy kingdom". Side-note 170.21.

[^13]: **S.** Chrysostom: the same homily, *Opus imperfectum* hom. 35 (PG 56:822–32): *desiderium primatus ex jactantia nascitur cordis … Alter alterum aestimans superiorem se* (LEDGER E08d-4). The non-Vulgate form of Phil. 2:3 comes from the homily; it is not the Vulgate (*superiores sibi invicem arbitrantes*, DR "let each esteem others better than themselves"); his Latin is translated. "As was touched on above": the hiddenness of the church's members is a theme of the earlier chapters.

[^14]: **T/C.** A and A₁ read *turpiter superant*, "who foully overcome them" (171.9). *In cordibus templi sui*: the apostles are his temple. *Castra … corporalia* is set against the spiritual conquest. Side-note 171.5.

[^15]: **C.** *Medium*: the means, suffering, by which Christ merited exaltation (cf. Phil. 2:8–9). *Singulariter suum*: "my chalice" (Matt. 20:23). Side-note 171.23.

[^16]: **T/S.** A and A₁ read *assensum* for *consensum* (171.26). Matt. 20:22–23 DR. "Know how to give good gifts to their children": Matt. 7:11 DR. Wyclif's *terreni patres* stands where the Vulgate has *vos, cum sitis mali*. *Quam per alios fecit in principem huius mundi*: the victory over the devil (John 12:31) won through others, here the brothers' humble consent.

[^17]: **C/S/R.** *Et per consequens tocius ecclesie*: we supply [Prince]. The genitive could instead go with *lex privata*, "the private law … of the whole church". *Regnum celorum rapere*: cf. Matt. 11:12. A margin *Martirium*. A margin note *Martir affeccione*, with a second hand's *passione corporali* (172.3). "The doctors" on the twofold martyrdom are not identified; to be checked (LEDGER E08d-5). James: Acts 12:2. The Evangelist is John, his brother. Side-note 172.3.

[^18]: **T/S/R.** A margin *humilitas* (172.9). A second hand in B's margin: *Primatus est fructus humilitatis* (172.9). Loserth prints *tamen*; the codices read *tum* (172.19). His reading is translated. A second hand in B's margin: *Menciendum non est in causa devocionis* (172.23). *Alta sapere*: cf. Rom. 12:16, 11:20. Matt. 20:23: Wyclif lacks *meam*. *Mansionem*: cf. John 14:2. *Univocando cum eis*: using words in the sense the disciples gave them. *Minoritatem*: his being less than the Father according to his humanity (cf. John 14:28). Side-note 172.8.
