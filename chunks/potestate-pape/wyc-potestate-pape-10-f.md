# *De potestate pape*, chapter 10, part 6: the answer: the saints who approved the endowment sinned

## ¶1 · 237.28–241.3

> [p. 237] Quantum ad istud sunt multa dicenda: primo quod argumentum non valet, cum omnes peccaverunt et egent gracia Dei, ideo docet decretum distinccione VIII: Si solus Christus, quod debemus solum sequi Christum, qui errare non potuit [p. 238] vel alios de quanto sequamur ipsum, ipse autem summus medicus non autorizavit dotacionem huiusmodi, sed verbo et opere reprobavit; et sic videtur michi quod omnes approbantes dotacionem illam peccaverant in hoc mortaliter vel venialiter plus vel minus; nec feramus hoc graviter de beatis, quia beatus Evangelista, qui detestatus est approbacionem huiusmodi fuit sanccior omnibus illis, et tamen ipse dicit: Si dicimus quia peccatum non habemus, nos ipsos seducimus et veritas in nobis non est I. Joh. I, 8. Nec est color si multa ecclesie utilia ex hoc processerant, ideo non fuit in illa peccatum; nam multa mala ex illa processerant, cum ex hoc deiecta est ecclesia cleri a statu perfecciori ad statum minus perfectum sine equivalencia recompense. Secundo seminavit discordiam in ecclesia, ut homines plus appetant preposituram propter fastum et questum quam propter meritum, ut patet superius VIII capitulo. Et tercio facit errores in eleccionibus, in legibus et omnino in officii cleri execucionibus. Aliter enim non est facile fingere, quare indigni tam frontose, tam impetuose et tam ociose ultra quam ante dotacionem aspirant ad pinguiores ecclesiasticas prelacias. Unde vellem eos qui sompniant quod quicquid mali regno contigerit est propter parcitatem vel subtraccionem dotacionis aut privilegiacionis cesaree discere et docere clare suas causales, et quare post dotacionem circa papatus tales magis contenditur quam perante; cuius causa experiencia doceri poterit, si subtracta dotacione cessaret aspiracio ad huiusmodi dignitates. Causa enim secundum philosophos est qua posita vel subtracta correspondenter [p. 239] effectus, posicio vel subtraccio consequetur. Cum igitur ante dotacionem non fuit talis divisio nec adhuc est, ubi non est ecclesia sic dotata, evidentissimum est, quod litura temporalium et voluptas honoris mundani est causa. Cum igitur omnia ista pericula evacuantur per expropriacionem et observanciam status quem Christus instituit, manifestum est (ut sepe repecii) quod elemosina foret expergefacere illum statum. Unde magna infidelitas est qua Antichristi discipuli excecant laycos, quod instar phariseorum Matth. V predicant esse sacrilegium auferre bona consecrata ecclesie, et sic sollempnizant facta que layci cecati stulte et superbe donaverant, sed decretum Christi non curant; et tamen luce clarius patet quod seculares debent postposito excommunicacionis timore in hoc errata corrigere, quia ut docet glossa ordinaria super decreto XXIV questio I cap. I: Sacerdos hereticus vel excommunicatus non excommunicat nisi se ipsum, et indubie omnis excommunicans propter restitucionem istius perfeccionis status sancte matris ecclesie est ut sic excommunicatus. Ideo non obstante malediccione sua bonum ecclesie est constanter et celeriter consummandum, ymo (ut patet alibi) sive excommunicatus sive insons maledixerit homini, non est timendum sibi, nisi de quanto declinaverit a Christi consiliis vel preceptis. Ideo (ut dicit glossa) non obstante excommunicacione tali homo debet saltem abscondite celebrare et facere quecumque bona de genere inturbate. Quod si pars Antichristi ad tantum prevaleat, quod sit Christiano plurior, christianus debet publice reclamare, quia talis foret gloriosa causa [p. 240] martyrii. Quod si queratur de gravedine peccati sanctorum episcoporum primitive ecclesie qui dotacionem receperant, certum est quod peccarunt graviter sed venialiter ex ingrata negligencia et consensu, ut patet ex decreto beati Jeronymi posito in De Consecracione, distinccione V: Non mediocriter. Et ista sciunt modo Silvestri, Augustini, Gregorii et consimiles, qui in hoc graviter peccaverunt. Sed secundo notanda est cecitas, quam sepe repecii, quod per locum ab insufficientissima similitudine cadimus paulative a statu apostolico, qui fuit status viantis perfectissimus, usque ad statum cleri cesareum qui est status cleri abiectissimus; capimus enim illud quod consonat peccatum in gestis sanctorum patrum, et illud extendimus, sed illud quod sonat virtutem omittimus. Ut sophistice arguitur, si dicti sancti receperunt temporalia supereffluenter ex pura secularium elemosina et totum residuum pauperibus erogarunt, igitur per idem licet nobis quantumcumque ample extorquere dominia tamquam sine elemosina secularium primo nobis debita, et dimisso eorum usu eis abuti quantumcumque voluptuose et seculariter conversando. Sic enim concludimus in effectu et, amplificando culpam ac dimittendo perfeccionem laudabilem, augemur nimirum difformiter ambobus partibus in voraginem viciorum. Ideo perfeccius atque securius foret nobis tenere limites quos Deus instituit, vivendo de decimis, oblacionibus et privatis elemosinis, quia transgrediendo terminos quos Christus instituit damus nobis et posteris evidenciam corruendi. Et hec fuit causa quare [p. 241] beatus Gregorius percussit iam mortuus successorem suum usque ad mortem, ut recitatum est VIII cap.

[237] As to this, there are many things to be said.[^1] First, that the argument is not valid, since all have sinned and need the grace of God;[^2] therefore the decree teaches, in distinction 8, *Si solus Christus*, that we ought to follow Christ alone, who could not err, [238] or others to the extent that we follow him [in them];[^3] now he, the supreme physician, did not [authorize]{g:autorizare} an [endowment]{g:dotacio} of this kind, but reproved it by word and deed; and so it seems to me that all who approved that endowment had sinned in this, mortally or venially, more or less. Nor let us take this hard of the blessed, for the blessed Evangelist, who detested approval of this kind, was holier than all of them, and yet he says: "If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us" (1 John 1:8).[^4] Nor is there [colour]{g:color} [in arguing]: "Many things useful to the [church]{g:ecclesia} had proceeded from this, therefore there was no sin in it";[^5] for many evils had proceeded from it, since by it the church of the clergy was cast down from a more perfect [state]{g:status} to a less perfect state without an equivalent recompense. Secondly, it sowed discord in the church, so that men seek the [office of superior]{g:prepositura} more for pomp and gain than for merit, as is clear above in chapter 8. And thirdly, it causes errors in elections, in laws, and altogether in the executions of the clergy's office. For otherwise it is not easy to imagine why the unworthy aspire so brazenly, so impetuously and so idly, beyond what they did before the endowment, to the fatter ecclesiastical prelacies. Hence I should like those who dream that whatever evil befalls the realm is on account of the sparingness or withdrawal of the endowment or of caesarean [privileging]{g:privilegiacio} to learn and teach clearly their causal [propositions],[^6] and why after the endowment there is more contention about such papacies than before; the cause of which could be taught by experience, if, the endowment being withdrawn, the aspiring to such dignities ceased. For a cause, according to the philosophers, is that which being posited or withdrawn, the positing or withdrawal of the [239] effect correspondingly follows.[^7] Since, therefore, before the endowment there was no such division, nor is there still where the church is not so endowed, it is most evident that the stain of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} and the pleasure of worldly honour is the cause.[^8] Since, therefore, all these dangers are emptied out by the [renunciation of property]{g:expropriacio} and the observance of the state which Christ instituted, it is manifest (as I have often repeated) that it would be an [alms]{g:elemosina} to awaken that state.[^9] Hence it is a great [unbelief]{g:infidelitas} by which the disciples of Antichrist blind lay people, in that, after the manner of the Pharisees (Matt. 5), they preach that it is sacrilege to take away goods consecrated to the church; and so they solemnize the deeds which blinded lay people had foolishly and proudly given, but they do not care for the decree of Christ.[^10] And yet it is clearer than light that [seculars]{g:secularis} ought, setting aside the fear of excommunication, to correct the errors in this, because, as the ordinary gloss on the decree, C. 24 q. 1 c. 1, teaches: "A heretical or excommunicated priest excommunicates none but himself";[^11] and without doubt everyone who excommunicates on account of the restoration of this perfection of the state of holy mother church is, as such, excommunicated. Therefore, notwithstanding his curse, the good of the church is to be accomplished steadfastly and swiftly; indeed (as is clear elsewhere), whether an excommunicate or an innocent man has cursed a man, there is nothing for that man to fear, except to the extent that he has turned aside from Christ's counsels or precepts. Therefore (as the gloss says), notwithstanding such an excommunication, a man ought at least secretly to celebrate and to do, undisturbed, whatever things are [good in kind]{g:bona-de-genere}. But if Antichrist's party should prevail so far that it is more numerous than the Christian [party], a Christian ought to protest publicly, because such would be a glorious cause of [240] martyrdom.[^12] And if it is asked about the gravity of the sin of the holy bishops of the primitive church who had received the endowment, it is certain that they sinned gravely but venially, by [displeasing]{g:ingratus} negligence and consent, as is clear from the decree of blessed Jerome placed in *De consecratione*, distinction 5, *Non mediocriter*.[^13] And the Silvesters, Augustines, Gregories and the like, who sinned gravely in this, know these things now.[^14] Now, secondly, there is to be noted the blindness, which I have often repeated, that by the topic from a most insufficient likeness we fall little by little from the apostolic state, which was the most perfect state of [one on the road]{g:viantes}, down to the caesarean state of the clergy, which is the most abject state of the clergy;[^15] for we take what accords with sin in the deeds of the holy fathers, and extend it, but what sounds of virtue we omit. As it is sophistically argued: "If the said saints received temporal goods overflowingly from the pure alms of seculars, and paid out all the residue to the poor, then by the same token it is lawful for us to extort lordships however amply, as though they were owed to us in the first place, without alms from seculars, and, having abandoned the use of them, to abuse them, living however voluptuously and worldlily."[^16] For so we [infer]{g:concludere} in effect, and, by amplifying the fault and abandoning the praiseworthy perfection, we are, no wonder, swollen deformedly on both counts into the whirlpool of vices.[^17] Therefore it would be more perfect and safer for us to keep the [limits]{g:limites} which God instituted, living on [tithes]{g:decime}, offerings and private alms, because by overstepping the bounds which Christ instituted we give to ourselves and to our posterity [evidence]{g:evidencia} for falling. And this was the reason why [241] blessed Gregory, already dead, struck his successor to death, as was recounted in chapter 8.[^18]

## ¶2 · 241.4–241.6

> [p. 241] Sed instatur quod dicti sancti non penitebant de istis peccatis finaliter nec revocarunt errorem, sed pocius tamquam meritoriam auxerunt dotacionem.

[241] But [it is objected]{g:instatur} that the said saints did not repent of these sins at the end, nor revoke the error, but rather increased the endowment as meritorious.[^19]

## ¶3 · 241.8–241.33

> [p. 241] Hic dicitur quod false assumitur nec umquam probabitur, ymo sancti ut remisse perceperunt errorem sic remisse laborarunt ad eius extinccionem, ut beatus Possidonius narrat de beato Augustino, quomodo rogavit cives suos, ut caperent possessiones quas sibi et sociis suis contulerunt, ut possint pure vivere de sorte Domini, tamquam clerici, sicut vixerunt in veteri testamento. Et sic videtur michi quod clerici et religiosi possessionati in ista appropriacione et proposito decedentes sine fructuosa penitencia sunt damnandi; et hanc reor racionem, quare Christus Matth. XXIV. 21- dicit: hoc tempus esse tantum horribile, non propter tribulacionem corporis sed anime infinitum maiorem, unde Apostolus II Thym. III, 1, 2 dicens, quod instabunt tempora periculosa, annectit tamquam signum et causam precipuam: Erunt, inquit, homines se ipsos amantes. Dyabolus enim ex cupiditate temporalium trahit plurimos ad infernum. Sancti igitur percipientes saltem confuse huius peccati maliciam, saltem confuse finaliter penitebant. Nos autem qui oculata fide percipimus huius avaricie invalescentis gravedinem, oportet penitere distinccius de tali avaricia et omnino de consensu et correpcionis negligencia, si volumus esse salvi.

[241] To this [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that it is falsely assumed and will never be proved; indeed the saints, as they perceived the error faintly, so faintly laboured for its extinction, as blessed Possidius relates of blessed Augustine: how he asked his citizens to take [back] the possessions which they had conferred on him and his companions, so that they might live purely on the Lord's lot, as clerics, as they lived in the old testament.[^20] And so it seems to me that [clerics]{g:clericus} and [possessioned]{g:possessionatus} [religious]{g:religiosi} who die in this [appropriation]{g:appropriacio} and purpose without fruitful penance are to be damned;[^21] and this I reckon the reason why Christ (Matt. 24:21) says that this time is so horrible, not on account of tribulation of the body but [on account of tribulation] of the soul, infinitely greater; hence the Apostle (2 Tim. 3:1–2), saying that "dangerous times shall come", adds as the sign and chief cause: "Men", he says, "shall be lovers of themselves."[^22] For the devil, by covetousness of temporal goods, draws very many to hell. The saints, therefore, perceiving at least [indistinctly]{g:confuse} the malice of this sin, at least indistinctly repented at the end. But we, who perceive [with our own eyes]{g:oculata-fide} the gravity of this growing avarice, must repent more distinctly of such avarice, and altogether of consent and of negligence in correction, if we wish to be saved.

## Notes

[^1]: **R.** The answer to the third rejoinder (237.15). At 237.31 both manuscripts note in the margin *Dotacionem approbantes sancti peccaverunt* ("the saints who approved the endowment sinned"); at 238.11 *Dotacio quomodo prodest [et] nocet* ("how the endowment profits and harms"); at 240.9 *Dotationem recipientes sancti quomodo peccaverunt* ("how the saints who received the endowment sinned").

[^2]: **S.** Rom. 3:23; Wyclif reads *gracia Dei* where the Vulgate has *gloria Dei* (DR "the glory of God").

[^3]: **S/C.** Gratian, *Decretum* D. 8 c. 9 (*Si solus Christus*), from Cyprian, *Ep.* 63 to Caecilius (Gratian's inscription *Idem … ad Caecilium*, PL 187, 48B); Loserth's foot gives only "Cap. IX" (LEDGER P10f-1). *Vel alios de quanto sequamur ipsum*: literally "or others in so far as we follow him", i.e. other teachers are to be followed only as far as following them is following Christ.

[^4]: **S.** 1 John 1:8. Wyclif's *Si dicimus quia … nos ipsos* departs slightly from the Vulgate (*Si dixerimus quoniam … ipsi nos*) without changing the sense; the Douay–Rheims wording is kept.

[^5]: **C.** *Nec est color*: "colour" is a plausible ground (as in "colour of title"). The rejected inference is quoted as an argument.

[^6]: **C.** *Suas causales*: the logicians' *propositiones causales*, propositions of the form "this happens because of that". Wyclif asks his opponents to state and prove their claim that the realm's misfortunes are caused by the withdrawal of endowment.

[^7]: **S.** The philosophers' rule that a cause is that whose positing or removal is followed by the positing or removal of the effect; Loserth gives no reference (LEDGER P10f-2).

[^8]: **T/C.** *Litura*: Loserth glosses it *i. e. macula*, "stain" (239.4), and we follow him; the word is properly an erasure or smear.

[^9]: **C.** *Elemosina foret expergefacere illum statum*: to rouse the clergy back to Christ's state would itself be a work of alms, the kind of alms the laity owe the clergy.

[^10]: **S/T.** *Instar phariseorum Matth. V*: so the print. The Pharisees' rule that a gift vowed to the temple frees a man from his duty to his parents stands at Matt. 15:5 (DR "The gift whatsoever proceedeth from me, shall profit thee"), the passage meant: Wyclif cites it as *Matth. XV* for the same point at 245.2, so *V* has lost an *X* (LEDGER P10f-3). *Facta* are the acts or deeds of gift. At 239.15 the manuscripts read *non curant postponere*; Loserth prints *non curant*, which we translate.

[^11]: **S.** Glossa ordinaria on Gratian C. 24 q. 1 c. 1 (modern form ours); Loserth cites the Venice 1595 edition, vol. I, p. 1295 (LEDGER P10f-4). "As the gloss says", later in the paragraph, is taken to refer to the same gloss; this has not been checked against the gloss (LEDGER P10f-5).

[^12]: **C.** *Quod sit Christiano plurior*: *Christiano* is a dative of comparison standing for the Christian party, "more numerous than the Christian [party]". *Celebrare* (239.28) is to say mass: the case is a priest under an unjust sentence.

[^13]: **S.** Gratian, *De consecratione* D. 5 c. 24 (*Non mediocriter*), from Jerome; Loserth's "Cap. XXIV", modern form ours (LEDGER P10f-6).

[^14]: **T/C.** 240.7 *modo*: Loserth's reading; the manuscripts read *modi*. *Silvestri, Augustini, Gregorii* are nominative plurals ("the Silvesters, Augustines, Gregories"), subject of *sciunt* with *consimiles*: the saints who accepted endowment now, in the other life, know their error (cf. the side-note at 240.6).

[^15]: **G.** *Per locum ab … similitudine*: the dialectical topic from likeness (*locus a simili*), here "a most insufficient likeness". *Clerus cesareus*, the clergy endowed by Caesar, gives "the caesarean state of the clergy".

[^16]: **C.** *Dimisso eorum usu eis abuti*: *eorum* and *eis* both take up *dominia*, so the contrast is use against abuse of the lordships. *Tamquam … primo nobis debita* "as though owed to us in the first place", i.e. by right and not by the laity's alms.

[^17]: **T.** 240.27 *augemur*: Loserth marks it *Ita codd.* ("so the manuscripts"), signalling doubt; we translate it ("we are swollen"). *Ambobus partibus* is taken as the two moves just named, amplifying the fault and abandoning the perfection.

[^18]: **S.** The story that Gregory the Great, after his death, appeared to his successor Sabinian and struck him for his avarice, so that he died; told at chapter 8. Wyclif there follows Higden, *Polychronicon* V, 412; the story is in Paul the Deacon's *Life of Gregory* 29 (PL 75, 58C–D) and Sigebert of Gembloux, *Chronica*, a. 607 (PL 160, 114B) (LEDGER P10f-7).

[^19]: **R.** 241.4: the manuscripts in the margin, *Dotacionis argumentum* ("an argument for endowment").

[^20]: **T/S.** *Possidonius*: so the print; Loserth notes *Recte: Possidius*, and the English gives the right name. Possidius, *Life of Augustine*, where Augustine offers to give back the church's estates to the people of Hippo (chapter 23, PL 32, 53: *paratum se esse illis cedere … Sed nunquam id laici suscipere voluerunt*; LEDGER P10f-8); Loserth compares *De civili dominio* III, 225 (LEDGER P10f-9). *De sorte Domini*: the Lord's lot (*kleros*), the root of the word "cleric", and the Levites' portion in the old law.

[^21]: **G.** *Appropriacione* here is the clergy's making the endowment their own, not only the canonists' appropriation of a parish church.

[^22]: **S/T.** Matt. 24:21 is paraphrased, not quoted (Vg *erit enim tunc tribulatio magna*). 2 Tim. 3:1–2 is quoted in the Douay–Rheims wording. At 241.22 the manuscripts read *Tim. II* (an omitted margin reference in the print, line 33).
