### 09-d ¶1 · 285.1–285.19  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 285] Et quantum ad tercium credo quod ante Lanfrancum et Wimundum non erant aliqua decreta ecclesie contraria huic sentencie. Unde quarta synodus Ephesina, que statim post mortem beati Augustini tempore Celestini pape celebrata est, dampnat unam stulticiam hereticam que dicit quod non vera Christi caro vivificata **sed** quelibet caro unius iusti virtute verborum sacramenti sit ibi vel terminus conversionis, et vel hoc vel aliud ibi dictum ad inferendum proposicionem. Nec obest quod Gregorius in epistola ad patriarchas acceptat hec quatuor consilia ut quatuor evangelia, quia nullum eorum contradicit isti sentencie. Unde creditur religiosos qui nimis magnificant sua signa introduxisse illum errorem in ecclesiam in penam peccati prioris. In cuius signum Augustinus et alii patres in quibus viguit sapiencia vel asseruerunt expresse oppositum et negant hoc tanquam hereticum quia inpossibile, vel tacuerunt illud tanquam inauditam stulticiam in quam credebant nullos sane mentis prorumpere.

ENGLISH: And as to the third, I believe that before Lanfranc and Guitmund[^1] there were no [decrees]{g:decretum} of the [church]{g:ecclesia} contrary to this [teaching]{g:sentencia}.[^2] Hence the fourth synod, of Ephesus, which was held immediately after the death of blessed Augustine in the time of Pope Celestine, condemns a heretical folly which says that not the true flesh of Christ, made living, but any flesh of some just man is there by the power of the words of the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}, or is the term of the [conversion]{g:conversio}; and either this or something else said there [serves] to infer the [proposition]{g:proposicio}.[^3] Nor does it stand in the way that Gregory, in his letter to the patriarchs, accepts these four councils as the four gospels,[^4] for none of them contradicts this teaching. Hence it is believed that the [religious]{g:religiosi} who [exceedingly]{g:nimis} magnify their signs brought that error into the church in punishment of an earlier sin. In sign of which Augustine and other fathers in whom wisdom flourished either asserted the opposite expressly and deny this as heretical because impossible, or kept silent about it as an unheard-of folly into which they believed no one of sound mind would break out.

### 09-d ¶2 · 285.21–287.12  (6 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 285] Unde quando arguitur contra Lanfrancum et sectam suam in isto, sepe prorumpunt in excusaciones istas inpossibiles in peccatis. Hoc (inquiunt) salubriter credi potest, investigari non potest, nam (ut sepe dixi) una pars nimis heretice contradicit alteri, quia absolute necessario omne credibile et specialiter fides katholica potest investigari salubriter, cum beatus Augustinus sub obtentu sue beatitudinis investigavit sollicite materiam de Trinitate que est infinitum superior sacramento [p. 286] eukaristie; ideo nullum velamen hereticum posset magis cecare ecclesiam; quia, inducta quacunque novitate heretica sine fundacione scripture si doctores incipiant idem examinare sicut sancti examinarunt alios articulos fidei, statim proponitur hoc principium Machometi: Hoc investigari non potest. Et meretricantur scripture ad colorandum hoc dictum, que docent quod nemo scrutaretur inprovide sine fide et humilitate priori aliquam katholicam veritatem. **Sed** pro distinccione doctorum in ista materia notandum quod Sathan pater mendacii ligatus est mille annis post ascensionem Domini (ut dicitur Apok. XX°, 2). Tempore **autem** huius ligacionis successerunt doctores veridici, recti loyci atque philosophi conformes fidei scripture, ut patet de quatuor magnis doctoribus et multis eis similibus; et illos sanctos oportet defendere in loyca et theologia tanquam pugiles veritatis. **Sed** tempore solucionis patris mendacii inter sanctos doctores surrepserunt nimis multi, **sed** plus vel minus discipuli Antichristi plus declinantes a loyca fidei scripture ex suggestione dyaboli, et licet omnibus istis doctoribus eciam Augustino qui fuit in loyca et theologia precipuus non debemus credere nisi de quanto se fundaverit in scriptura, ut patet distinccione IX Noli meis, et capitulo Negare non possum, et capitulo Ego solum, tamen testimonium priorum longe plus valet ad fundandum argumentum topicum quam sequencium. Quotquot ergo duodene allegate fuerint contra me de secta secunda non solum non [p. 287] valent, nisi de quanto se fundaverint in scriptura, **sed** non valent in testimonium fidei, nisi de quanto concordaverint in se ipsis. Duodena enim admittitur in causa politica, nisi prius concordaverit cum se ipsa. Oportet ergo concordare recentes doctores cum prioribus et exemplare fidei scripture et post est eorum testimonium admittendum. Nunc **autem** conturbant reciproce sentencias suas; ideo infidelis foret qui gustaret aquam sic turbidam, dum a prioribus potare poterit aquam claram, nam illis existentibus nobis contrariis, plures sunt nobiscum quam cum illis, quia totus exercitus ecclesie triumphantis.

ENGLISH: Hence when it is argued against Lanfranc and his sect on this point, they often break out into these impossible "excuses in sins":[^5] "This (they say) can wholesomely be believed; it cannot be investigated." For (as I have often said) the one part contradicts the other exceedingly heretically, since by absolute necessity everything believable, and especially the catholic faith, can wholesomely be investigated; since blessed Augustine, as he hoped for his blessedness,[^6] carefully investigated the matter of the Trinity, which is infinitely higher than the sacrament [286] of the [Eucharist]{g:eukaristia}. So no heretical veil could blind the church more; for, once any heretical novelty has been brought in without foundation in scripture, if doctors begin to examine it just as the [saints]{g:sancti} examined other [articles of faith]{g:articulus-fidei}, at once this principle of Mahomet is put forward: "This cannot be investigated." And they prostitute[^7] the scriptures to [give colour to]{g:colorare} this saying, [scriptures] which teach that no one should search improvidently, without prior faith and humility, into any catholic truth. But for the distinction of doctors in this matter it is to be noted that Satan, the father of lies, "was bound a thousand years" after the ascension of the Lord (as is said in Apoc. 20:2).[^8] And in the time of this binding there followed truthful doctors, right logicians, and philosophers conformed to the faith of scripture, as is clear of the four great doctors and many like them; and those saints must be defended in [logic]{g:loyca} and theology as champions of the truth. But in the time of the loosing of the father of lies there crept in among the holy doctors exceedingly many, but more or less disciples of Antichrist,[^9] declining further from the logic of the faith of scripture at the suggestion of the devil; and although to all these doctors, even Augustine, who was foremost in logic and theology, we ought not to give credence except in so far as he has founded himself in scripture, as is clear in distinction 9, *Noli meis*, and the chapter *Negare non possum*, and the chapter *Ego solum*,[^10] yet the testimony of the earlier is worth far more for founding a [topical argument]{g:argumentum-topicum} than that of those who follow. However many juries of twelve,[^11] then, are [alleged]{g:allegare} against me from the second sect, not only are they [287] of no worth, except in so far as they have founded themselves in scripture, but they are of no worth as testimony to the faith, except in so far as they have agreed among themselves. For a jury of twelve is [not] admitted in a civil cause unless it has first agreed with itself.[^12] The recent doctors must therefore agree with the earlier and with the exemplar of the faith of scripture,[^13] and afterwards their testimony is to be admitted. Now in fact they throw one another's teachings into confusion; so he would be an [unbeliever]{g:infidelis} who tasted water so muddied while he could drink clear water from the earlier; for, though those are contrary to us, "there are more with us than with them",[^14] since the whole army of the [church triumphant]{g:ecclesia-triumphans} [is with us].

### 09-d ¶3 · 287.13–287.19  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 287] **Sed** quarto obicitur quod multi sancti asserebant istam sentenciam. Qui ergo sumus nos qui presumimus inducere sentenciam istam novellam contra decretum? Ymmo contra scripturam de veritate verborum sacramentalium et contra devocionem populi in hac parte; unde deficiente evidencia clamant contra nos quod ex dictis nostris devocio populi est extincta.

ENGLISH: But fourthly [it is objected]{g:obicitur} that many saints asserted this teaching. Who then are we, who presume to bring in this newer teaching against the decree? Indeed, against scripture on the truth of the sacramental words, and against the devotion of the people in this regard;[^15] hence, for lack of [evidence]{g:evidencia}, they cry out against us that from our sayings the devotion of the people is extinguished.

### 09-d ¶4 · 287.20–288.10  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 287] Ad illud sepe dixi quod illud porizoma dyaboli excecat plurimos; nam obiecta sunt nobis scripta sacra et sancti doctores prestanciores et prudenciores cum dictis suis que floruerunt in primitiva ecclesia, et in alio genere sunt oblata nobis facta, dicta et scripta novorum doctorum recenciumque qui venerunt. Et vitam, facta scriptaque priorum contempnimus, **sed** posteriorum sentencias et facta amplexamur. Nec dubium, cum ista pars sit infirmior et ad peccatum proclivior, quin peccati cecitas sit in causa admittere istam quod multi sancti asserebant sentenciam nobis contrariam, sicut multi tam in speculativis quam practicis [p. 288] peccaverunt **sed** sancciores et antiquiores tenuerunt nostram sentenciam. Quare ergo non debemus sine presumpcione humiliter sequi priores, potissime cum aliis nec ex alia evidencia pro sua sentencia asserunt raciones? Nam omnes auctores scripture qui illud sacramentum meminerant, sicut et omnes sancti doctores ecclesie usque ad tempus dotacionis et diu post eciam (si non fallor) usque ad tempus Nicholai IIⁱ qui fuit anno domini MLIX vocaverunt hoc sacramentum panem et non accidens.

ENGLISH: To that I have often said that this apron of the devil[^16] blinds very many; for there are set before us the sacred writings and the more eminent and more prudent holy doctors, with their sayings, which flourished in the primitive church; and in another kind there are offered us the deeds, sayings and writings of the new and recent doctors who have come. And we despise the life, deeds and writings of the earlier, but embrace the teachings and deeds of the later.[^17] Nor is there doubt, since this part is the weaker and the more prone to sin, that the blindness of sin is the cause of admitting this, that many saints asserted the teaching contrary to us; just as many have sinned in speculative as well as in practical [288] matters; but the holier and more ancient held our teaching. Why, then, ought we not, without presumption, humbly follow the earlier, most of all since they put forward reasons for their teaching neither with others nor from any other evidence?[^18] For all the authors of scripture who made mention of that sacrament, as also all the holy doctors of the church up to the time of the [endowment]{g:dotacio}, and long after too (if I am not mistaken), up to the time of Nicholas II, who was in the year of the Lord 1059,[^19] called this sacrament [bread]{g:panis} and not [accident]{g:accidens}.

### 09-e ¶1 · 288.11–290.3  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 288] **Sed** multi dixerunt quod inpossibile est istam sentenciam de accidentibus esse veram. Et ita (ut utar verbis reprehensoris) quero qui sunt illi presumentes seminare istam infundabilem et novellam sentenciam contra diffiniciones sanctorum patrum quas Spiritus Sanctus in eis posuit. Recurrendum est (inquam) ad unum primum quod sit metrum omnium aliorum, cum illi non sunt credibiles, quia illi, nec potest doceri quod illi ut sic sentenciantes a Spiritu Sancto fuerunt illustrati. Ideo absit a nobis presumpcio ut mendicemus quod credatur nobis tanquam nobis in ista antiqua sentencia propter peccatum quod Deus novit ad tempus sopita **sed** tamquam preconibus scripture et sanctorum ac antiquorum doctorum illud dicencium. Unde Lanfrancus in libello suo contra Beringarium (velit nolit) fatetur quod sancti doctores ut Ambrosius, Jeronimus et specialiter Augustinus vocant generaliter hoc sacramentum panem et nunquam accidens, quia (ut vere dicit) hoc sacramentum habet signa que naturaliter significant formam panis. [p. 289] Cum ergo scriptura sacra et sancti doctores cum lege ecclesie sic locuntur, nos debemus indubie sic loqui ad sensum eorum et glossare eos quod per accidens panis intelligunt ipsum panem, sicut ipsi econtra de nobis asserunt; et potest fundari modus loquendi in hoc quod mundi divicie que sunt in suis naturis substancie vocantur accidencia, quia accidunt vel adiacent ipsis divitibus, sicut pani accidit quod sit signum corporis; ideo illud signum dicitur sibi accidens. Nec dubium quin non sit accidens univoce ut accidens limitatur ad novem genera, vel ut locuntur philosophi, Augustinus et sancti alii. Unde solebam exponere adversarios quod tale accidens significat naturaliter tam subiectum quam se ipsum, et prius causaliter **sed** non quoad consequenciam se ipsum significat; ideo subiectum mediante tali significacione sui accidentis suscipit equivoce predicaciones amborum; et quia vigili significacio accidentis est insopibilis **sed** significacio distincta substancie est racione corporis Christi sopita, ideo dicitur quod accidens manet sine subiecto suo in actu; unde philosophi vocant universale tempus et sensibile encia in actu per animam, quando sunt actualiter apprehensa; et aliter dicuntur encia in potencia. **Sed** illud dictum licet fuerit quoad sentenciam adversam plus coloratum, nescio tamen si ipsum intenderant; unde quoad primum de decreto ecclesie quoad fidem miror multum quomodo non vident quod nullus episcopus (in quantum Avinonicus vel Romanus) habet potestatem ad hoc decidendum, cum nec (in quantum talis) habet supereminenter scienciam fidei scripture vel vivacitatem racionis; nec [p. 290] habet ut sic supereminenter graciam aut virtutem ad regendum se ipsum et populum in fide domini nostri Jesu Christi.

ENGLISH: But many have said that it is impossible that this [teaching]{g:sentencia} about accidents is true. And so (to use the words of the critic)[^1] I ask who are those who presume to sow this teaching, [without foundation]{g:infundabilis} and newer, against the definitions of the holy fathers which the Holy Spirit put in them. One must have recourse (I say) to one first [thing] which is to be the measure of all the others, since those are not to be believed because they are those,[^2] nor can it be taught that they, pronouncing so as such, were enlightened by the Holy Spirit. So far from us be the presumption of [begging]{g:mendicare} that we be believed as ourselves in this ancient teaching, laid asleep for a time on account of a sin which God knows; but [that we be believed] as [heralds]{g:preco} of scripture and of the [saints]{g:sancti} and ancient doctors who say it. Hence Lanfranc, in his little book against Berengar, confesses willy-nilly that the holy doctors, such as Ambrose, Jerome and especially Augustine, generally call this [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} [bread]{g:panis} and never [accident]{g:accidens}, because (as he [truly]{g:vere} says) this sacrament has signs which naturally signify the form of bread.[^3] [289] Since, then, sacred scripture and the holy doctors with the law of the [church]{g:ecclesia} speak thus, we ought without doubt to speak so, in their sense, and to gloss them[^4] [as meaning] that by "accident of bread" they understand the bread itself, just as they on the other hand assert of us; and the manner of speaking can be founded on this, that the riches of the world, which in their natures are [substances]{g:substancia}, are called accidents, because they befall or lie beside the rich themselves, just as it befalls bread that it is a sign of the body; so that sign is called its accident. Nor is there doubt that it is not an accident [univocally]{g:univoce}, as "accident" is restricted to the nine genera, or as the philosophers, Augustine and other saints speak. Hence I used to expound the adversaries [as meaning] that such an accident naturally signifies both the subject and itself, and signifies itself first causally but not as to [inference]{g:consequencia};[^5] so the subject, by means of such signification of its accident, admits [equivocally]{g:equivoce} the predications of both; and since to one awake the signification of the accident cannot be laid asleep,[^6] but the distinct signification of the substance is laid asleep by reason of the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}, so it is said that the accident remains without its subject in act; hence the philosophers call the universal, time and the perceptible beings in act through the soul, when they are actually apprehended, and otherwise they are called beings in [potency]{g:potencia}.[^7] But although that saying, as to the adverse teaching, was [given more colour]{g:colorare}, yet I do not know whether they had meant it. Hence, as to the first, on the [decree]{g:decretum} of the church as to the faith,[^8] I wonder greatly how they do not see that no bishop ([insofar as]{g:in-quantum} he is of Avignon or of Rome) has [power]{g:potestas} to decide this, since he does not (insofar as he is such) have pre-eminently the knowledge of the faith of scripture or liveliness of reason; nor [290] does he have, as such, pre-eminently grace or [virtue]{g:virtus} to rule himself and the people in the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ.

### 09-e ¶2 · 290.4–291.2  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 290] Nec dubium quin ad faciendum hoc opus alterum istorum requiritur vel utrumque. Et assumptum patet de multis episcopis talibus post dotacionem ecclesie qui erant ydiote heretici et precipui Antichristi; ymmo licet concedatur quod quicunque locus quem incolunt fuerit plenus virtute et gracia per se stante (sic continue ipsas inhamarant), adhuc foret totum hoc istis inpertinens, quia facere qualitates istas informare, sine quo nichil valent in opus Domini singulare. Videant ergo si inter viantes simillime secuntur Christum et de isto sine pompa congaudeant, considerantes quod secundum tradiciones proprias, XXᵃ distinccione, capitulo primo, quoad causas secundum legem suam decidendas supereminet potestas sua cesarea, **sed** quoad veritatem scripture et mores est Augustino et ceteris doctoribus plus credendum. Unde infidelis videtur sentencia Graciani XXV, questione prima, Ideo permittente Domino quod, sicut Christus fuit dominus legis sue et tamen illi obtemperare voluit et ipsam implere, sic prima sedes, sic papa tribuit ius et auctoritatem canonibus et quandoque ostendit se dominum canonum sive suorum sive aliorum, iubendo, diffiniendo seu agendo contrarium, licet (inquit) sibi contra generalia decreta specialia privilegia indulgere. Ymmo in omni canone subintelligitur condicio nisi [p. 291] auctoritas Romane ecclesie aliter fieri mandaverit aut permiserit.

ENGLISH: Nor is there doubt that for doing this work one or the other of these is required, or both. And the [assumption]{g:assumptum} is clear from many such bishops after the [endowment]{g:dotacio} of the church, who were [unlettered]{g:ydiota} heretics and chief Antichrists; indeed, though it be granted that whatever place they inhabit were full of virtue and grace standing [of itself]{g:per-se} (so continually do they adulterate them),[^9] still all this would be irrelevant to them, since [what is needed is] to make these qualities [inform]{g:informare}, without which they avail nothing for the singular work of the Lord.[^10] Let them see, then, whether among [those on the road]{g:viantes} they follow Christ most closely, and of this let them rejoice together without [boasting]{g:pompa}, considering that according to their own traditions, distinction 20, chapter 1,[^11] as to causes to be decided according to their law their caesarean power is pre-eminent, but as to the truth of scripture and morals more credence is to be given to Augustine and the other doctors. Hence the teaching of Gratian in [Causa] 25, question 1, *Ideo permittente Domino*,[^12] seems [unbelieving]{g:infidelis}: that, "just as Christ was the lord of his law, and yet willed to obey it and to fulfil it, so the first see, so the pope, gives [right]{g:ius} and [authority]{g:auctoritas} to the canons, and sometimes shows himself lord of the canons, whether his own or others', by commanding, defining or acting to the contrary"; "it is lawful" (he says) "for him to grant special [privileges]{g:privilegium} against general decrees. Indeed in every canon the condition is [understood as implied]{g:subintelligere}: unless [291] the authority of the Roman church has commanded or permitted it to be done otherwise."

### 09-e ¶3 · 291.3–291.17  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 291] Ista **autem** sentencia clare docet omnes decretales et decreta Romane ecclesie de quanto non fundantur in scriptura esse superflua et iniusta. Superflua quidem, quia dependent ex voluntate persone nimis vertibilis ad omnia genera peccatorum. Intencionem itaque suam que est origo legis debet legista consulere, quia non valet lex sua nisi de quanto fuerit ab ipso approbata; ymmo cum (velit papa nolit) permittit fieri contrarium legi sue, cassata est eius efficacia et superfluitas declarata. Est secundo iniusta, quia quandocunque pape placuerit revocanda, et non est revocanda nisi fuerit iniusta, ergo est continue parate iniusta; quia ergo nescimus voluntatem vel factum illius pape vel priorum, ideo quiescimus in fide scripture, cuius auctoritatem scimus immobilem.

ENGLISH: And this teaching clearly teaches that all the [decretals]{g:decretalis} and decrees of the Roman church, insofar as they are not founded in scripture, are superfluous and unjust. Superfluous indeed, because they depend on the will of a person [exceedingly]{g:nimis} turnable to every kind of sin. The [legist]{g:legiste} therefore must consult his intention, which is the origin of the law, for his law is of no worth except insofar as it has been approved by him; indeed, since (whether the pope will or no) he permits the contrary of his law to be done, its efficacy is annulled and its superfluity declared. It is, secondly, unjust, because it is to be revoked whenever it pleases the pope, and it is not to be revoked unless it is unjust; therefore it is continually unjust in readiness.[^13] Since, then, we do not know the will or deed of that pope or of the earlier ones, therefore we rest in the faith of scripture, whose authority we know to be [immovable]{g:immobilis}.

### 09-e ¶4 · 291.18–291.23  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 291] Nec alii pape quoad fidem credimus, nisi de quanto se fundaverit in scriptura in qua scimus nec Petrum nec alium apostolum tantum dominium usurpasse **sed** consuluisse Deum per sortem, quando defecit eis inspiracio singularis. Patet Act. primo de eleccione Mathie.

ENGLISH: Nor do we believe any other pope as to the faith, except insofar as he has founded himself in scripture, in which we know that neither Peter nor any other apostle usurped so great a [lordship]{g:dominium}, but that they consulted God by lot when singular inspiration failed them. It is clear in Acts 1 of the election of Matthias.[^14]

### 09-e ¶5 · 291.24–292.14  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 291] Quoad secundum de veritate verborum sacramenti patet quod ipsa sunt verissima, cum sunt constans et salubris sentencia domini veritatis; et cum panis aut vinum demonstratur ubique nec dicta transsubstanciacio nec ydemptificacio nec inpanacio potest esse, non restat nisi sensus tropicus scilicet quod hoc figurat sacramentaliter corpus Christi. Quod **autem** demonstratur panis aut vinum, sonat quadruplex [p. 292] evangelium (ut recitavi sepius); et idem dicunt sancti doctores concorditer, scilicet Ambrosius, Jeronimus et specialiter Augustinus et alii sequentes eos, ut usus et leges ecclesie cum postillantibus super ipsos, ut Rabanus De Sacramento Altaris et alii qui omnes dicunt quod panis potest esse corpus Christi, erit et est corpus Christi, quod orant ut fiat. Illud **autem** esset stultissimum, nisi virtute verborum sacramentalium hoc fieret, et per consequens nisi in verbis consecracionis demonstraretur panis, et consequenter necesse est predictum sensum verbis Christi addicere; unde non solum doctores novelli **sed** et communitas artis humane clamat nedum quod de pane **sed** ex verbi Dei omnipotencia ipse panis fiet corpus Christi.

ENGLISH: As to the second, on the truth of the words of the sacrament, it is clear that they are most true, since they are the constant and wholesome teaching of the Lord of truth; and since bread or wine is demonstrated everywhere,[^15] and neither the said [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio} nor [identification]{g:ydemptificacio} nor [impanation]{g:inpanacio} can be, there remains only the [tropical meaning]{g:sensus-tropicus}, namely that this [figures]{g:figurare} [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} the body of Christ. And that bread or wine is demonstrated, the fourfold [292] gospel sounds (as I have [recited]{g:recitare} often); and the holy doctors say the same in accord, namely Ambrose, Jerome and especially Augustine, and others following them, as do the [use]{g:usus} and [laws of the church]{g:leges-ecclesie} with those who write postils upon them, such as Rabanus, *On the Sacrament of the Altar*,[^16] and others, who all say that bread can be the body of Christ, will be and is the body of Christ, which they pray may come about.[^17] Now that would be most foolish unless this came about by the power of the sacramental words, and consequently unless bread were demonstrated in the words of [consecration]{g:consecracio}; and consequently it is necessary to add the aforesaid meaning to the words of Christ. Hence not only the [newer doctors]{g:novelli} but also the community of human art cry out [not only]{g:nedum} that [the body is made] of bread, but that by the omnipotence of the word of God the bread itself will become the body of Christ.[^18]

### 09-f ¶1 · 292.15–293.32  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 292] Unde Doctor Communis super illud distinccione XI, questione III, videtur dicere quod mutacio ista differt ab aliis, cum ibi sit corrupcio et nullius substancie generacio vel subiectum mutacionem illam suscipiens, et cum hoc verbum substantivum est dicit quandam mutacionem, quia aliter non inplicaretur sacramentalis confeccio, unde ipse videtur dicere quod per Lyncolniensem hoc demonstratur corpus Christi, ut sub illis accidentibus Doctor Subtilis super distinccione VIII videtur dicere quod demonstratur ens analogum sub quadam contraccione. Glossa decreti super distinccione II, De Consecracione, capitulo Timorem, dicit quod nichil demonstratur, quia proposicio illa sumitur materialiter, cum aliter foret falsa. **Sed** sanctus Richardus, Armachanus et alii sancti dicunt cum [p. 293] evangelio quod demonstratur panis, cum aliter foret locucio illa inpertinens. Admisso ergo quod panis demonstratur in proposicione illa conversiva: Hoc est corpus meum, manifestum est quod supposita eius veritate sequitur panem remanere post consecracionem; nam post consecracionem est verum quod ille panis est corpus Christi, et si sic ille panis est panis, ideo post consecracionem remanet quod ille panis est panis. Confirmatur ex hoc quod Christus inmediate ante benedixit illi pani quem mandavit ab apostolis comedi, quod non est signum desicionis panis **sed** pocius melioracionis. Nam si ille panis benedictus secundum se totum destruatur, illa benediccio foret crudelior sive severior quam malediccio qua Christus maledixit ficui, Math. XXIᵒ, quia post malediccionem ficus, substancia eius remansit arida, hic **autem** dicitur nichil de panis substancia remanere et conversio nichil facit ad bonitatem panis sive sue essencie. Nec dubium quin Christus non mandavit suis manducare ex hoc, nisi post eius consecracionem, et per consequens Veritas que tantum odivit duplicitatem sophisticam voluit hoc remanere post consecracionem et fraccionem ab apostolis comedendum. Unde nimis longum a veritate scripture videtur michi concedere ydemptificacionem vel inpanacionem quam transsubstanciacionem predictam et propinquius loyce sanctorum, quos non licet scandalizare quod ignorarunt logicam seminantes verba heretica, cum ipsi loquuntur scripture similius nec sciunt fundare glossam quam illi fingunt.

The [Subtle Doctor]{g:doctor-subtilis}, on distinction 8, seems to say that there is demonstrated an [analogous]{g:analogum} being under a certain contraction.[^3] The gloss of the decree on distinction 2, *De consecratione*, chapter *Timorem*, says that "nothing is demonstrated, because that proposition is taken materially", since otherwise it would be false.[^4] But Saint Richard [Armachanus]{g:ardmacanus} and other [saints]{g:sancti} say with [293] the gospel that bread is demonstrated, since otherwise that utterance would be irrelevant.[^5] It being admitted, then, that bread is demonstrated in that [converting]{g:conversivus} [proposition]{g:proposicio}, "This is my body", it is manifest that, its truth being supposed, it follows that bread remains after consecration; for after consecration it is true that that bread is the body of Christ, and if so, that bread is bread; so after consecration it remains [true] that that bread is bread. This is confirmed from the fact that Christ, immediately before, blessed that bread which he commanded to be eaten by the apostles, which is a sign not of the [ceasing]{g:desicio} of the bread but rather of its betterment. For if that blessed bread were destroyed according to its whole self, that blessing would be crueller or more severe than the curse by which Christ cursed the fig tree, Matt. 21, since after the cursing of the fig tree its substance remained, withered;[^6] but here it is said that nothing of the bread's substance remains, and the [conversion]{g:conversio} does nothing toward the goodness of the bread or of its [essence]{g:essencia}. Nor is there doubt that Christ did not command his own to eat of this except after its consecration; and consequently the Truth, which so hated sophistical doubleness, willed this to remain after consecration and breaking, to be eaten by the apostles. Those, then, who shudder at the truth of scripture and those who love it as faith should be solicitous about the catholic [meaning]{g:sensus} of this principle. Hence it seems to me [exceedingly]{g:nimis} [less] far from the truth of scripture to grant [identification]{g:ydemptificacio} or [impanation]{g:inpanacio} than the aforesaid [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio},[^7] and nearer to the [logic]{g:loyca} of the saints, whom it is not lawful to defame [by saying] that they were [ignorant of]{g:ignorare} logic, sowing heretical words, since they speak more like scripture; nor can they found the gloss which those [feign]{g:fingere}.[^8]

LATIN: [p. 293] Quoad tercium verbum patet quod adversarii destruunt mendaciter devocionem populi, cum dicunt [p. 294] illud sentitum in manibus sacerdotis non posse esse corpus Christi vel partem eius, cum sit unum ignotum accidens; quod nec sciunt nec audent detegere; nam (ut sophiste arguunt) non potest esse terna dimensio, quia latitudo est alterius speciei infinitum maior quam eukaristie longitudo, et profunditas est alterius speciei quam altera priorum, infinitum maior quam hostie latitudo, maior in profundo calice et minor in minus repleto. Devocio itaque populi foret maior secundum sentenciam veridicam dicentem quod sacer panis remanet habens vere ad omnem punctum sui corpus Domini, ymmo est quodammodo ipsum corpus, quam fingendo quod solum remanet ibi sacrum **sed** ignotum accidens quod non potest esse corpus Domini vel pars eius. Suspecta quidem foret populo ista ficticia; **sed** utentes istis clamorosis ficticiis notarent scripturam Act. XIXᵒ, quomodo Demetrius quidam faciens edes argenteas Dyane commovit populum contra Paulum. Unde, inquit, scitis quod de hoc artificio est nobis acquisicio, et videtis quia non solum Ephesi **sed** pene tocius Asie Paulus hic disuadens avertit multam turbam, dicens quoniam non sunt dii qui manibus fiunt. Non solum **autem** hoc **sed** et magne Dyane templum in nichilum reputabitur et destrui incipiet maiestas eius quam tota Asia et orbis colit. Et hiis dictis clamaverunt: Magna Dyana Ephesiorum. Nam non solum in ymagine argentea vel trunco putrido colunt Christum **sed** fingunt mendaciter edes accidentales quas faciunt illi populum credere esse Christum, et ipsi colunt tanquam Deum, ut creditur propter questum. Et quando [p. 295] quis rimatur veritatem fidei in ista materia commovent populum contra eum. Et illi tantum adorant quod nesciunt nec cognoscunt quod genus accidentis est sacramentum nec quid per verba sacramentalia demonstratur. Non enim est color in glossa Thome super distinccione VIII, questione Iᵃ, IVⁱ Sentenciarum quod demonstratur corpus Christi ut sub illis accidentibus, primo quia pro toto tempore prolacionis pronominis usque in finem foret sensus ille falsus, et sic tota proposicio nunquam foret vera. Secundo quia Christus non cogitavit singulariter de accidentibus panis tui aut mei quod contentum sub illis accidentibus sit corpus suum, quod sic foret sensus proposicionis nimis longus et inexplicabilis Christi discipulis. Et hec creditur racio Scoti quod pronomen significat confuse omne accidens et evidencius omnem substanciam corporalem vel spiritualem. **Sed** nimis confusa foret huius sensus Christi expressio. Et tercio quia iste sensus fidei scripture latebat maiores sanctos ut Augustinum, Ambrosium, Jeronimum et eis similes eciam per mille annos et amplius; quos hereticare propter sensum huic contrarium foret nimis difficile. Multi enim erant sancti qui complecius laborarunt circa fidem sensus scripture qui nunquam cognoverunt quod Christus intellexit corpus suum sic repetere sub illis accidentibus. Nec dubium quin hereticum sit fingere tante scripture sensum alium quam Spiritus Sanctus flagitat, ut dicit decretum Jeronimi positum XXIV questione III Heresis. Sensum itaque illius scripture scrutarentur doctores antequam cece arguerent [p. 296] quod, si panis remaneat, non diceretur: Hoc est corpus meum, **sed**: Hic est corpus meum. Coloracius enim emendaretur scriptura sic: Hoc quod continetur sub istis accidentibus est corpus meum; aliud enim posset laycus verificare. Ideo videtur dicendum (ut docet processus evangelii) quod per illam proposicionem demonstratur et significatur principaliter panem quem Christus porrexerat esse corpus suum. Ad quem sensum sacerdos in persona Christi vere asserit: Hoc est corpus meum. Et demonstracionem ac sensum secundarium habet proposicio sacerdotis quod ille panis quem ipse habet in manibus sit corpus Christi. Egena quidem foret scriptura Domini, nisi ebulire posset in talem sensum vicarium. Radix itaque dissensionis illius materie stat in intellectu proposicionis sacramentalis; et videtur quod demonstratur panis (ut dictum est quinto capitulo), quia de pane dixit Christus: Manducate ex hoc omnes. Et animando eos ad fructum huius manducacionis subiunxit pertinenter: Hoc est corpus meum. Aliter enim nimis sine causa dixissent Ambrosius et alii sancti quod panis erit corpus Christi.

ENGLISH: As to the third word,[^9] it is clear that the adversaries lyingly destroy the devotion of the people when they say [294] that that thing [perceived]{g:sentire} in the hands of the priest cannot be the body of Christ or a part of it, since it is an unknown accident; which they neither know nor dare to [expose]{g:detegere}; for (as the [sophists]{g:sophista} argue) it cannot be the threefold dimension, since the breadth is of another species, infinitely greater than the length of the [Eucharist]{g:eukaristia}, and the depth is of another species than either of the former, infinitely greater than the breadth of the [host]{g:hostia}, greater in a deep chalice and less in one less filled.[^10] The devotion of the people, therefore, would be greater according to the truthful [teaching]{g:sentencia} that says that the [sacred]{g:sacer} bread remains, having [truly]{g:vere} at every point of itself the [body of the Lord]{g:corpus-domini}, indeed is in a manner the body itself, than by feigning that there remains there only a sacred but unknown accident which cannot be the body of the Lord or a part of it.[^11] This [fiction]{g:ficticia} would indeed be suspect to the people; but those who use these clamorous fictions should mark the scripture, Acts 19, how "a certain Demetrius, who made silver temples for Diana", stirred up the people against Paul. "Hence," he says, "you know that our gain is by this trade; and you see that not only of Ephesus, but almost of all Asia, this Paul, by dissuading, hath drawn away a great multitude, saying: They are not gods which are made by hands. And when these things were said, they cried out: Great is Diana of the Ephesians."[^12] For not only in a silver image or a rotten trunk do they worship Christ, but they lyingly feign accidental temples, which they make the people believe to be Christ, and they themselves worship [them] as God, for gain, as is believed.[^13] And when [295] anyone searches into the truth of the faith in this matter, they stir up the people against him. And they so adore what they know not,[^14] nor do they recognise what genus of accident the sacrament is, nor what is demonstrated by the sacramental words. For there is no [colour]{g:color} in the gloss of Thomas on distinction 8, question 1, of the fourth [book] of the *Sentences*, that the body of Christ is demonstrated as under those accidents:[^15] first, because for the whole time of the uttering of the pronoun, up to the end, that meaning would be false, and so the whole proposition would never be true. And this is believed to be Scotus's reason, that the pronoun signifies [indistinctly]{g:confuse} every accident, and more evidently every substance, bodily or spiritual. But Christ's expression of this meaning would be exceedingly indistinct. And third, because this meaning of the faith of scripture lay hidden from the greater saints, such as Augustine, Ambrose, Jerome and those like them, even for a thousand years and more; and to [pronounce them heretical]{g:hereticare} for a meaning contrary to this would be exceedingly hard. For there were many saints who laboured more completely about the faith of the meaning of scripture who never knew that Christ meant his body to recur so under those accidents.[^17] Nor is there doubt that it is heretical to feign for so great a scripture a meaning other than the Holy Spirit demands, as the decree of Jerome placed in [Causa] 24, question 3, *Heresis*, says.[^18] The doctors, therefore, should search out the meaning of that scripture before they argued blindly [296] that, if bread remained, it would not be said "This (*hoc*) is my body" but "This (*hic*) is my body".[^19] For scripture would be emended with more colour thus: "This which is contained under these accidents is my body"; for a layman could make the other true.[^20] So it seems it must be said (as the [course]{g:processus} of the gospel teaches) that by that proposition is demonstrated and signified [principally]{g:principaliter} that the bread which Christ had held out is his body. In this meaning the priest, in the person of Christ, truly asserts: "This is my body." And the priest's proposition has a secondary demonstration and meaning: that the bread which he himself has in his hands is the body of Christ. Needy indeed would the scripture of the Lord be, unless it could bubble up into such a vicarial meaning. The root, therefore, of the dissension in that matter stands in the understanding of the sacramental proposition; and it seems that bread is demonstrated (as was said in the fifth chapter), because of the bread Christ said: "Eat ye all of this."[^21] And, encouraging them to the fruit of this [eating]{g:manducacio}, he added [fittingly]{g:pertinenter}: "This is my body." For otherwise Ambrose and the other saints would have said exceedingly without cause that the bread will be the body of Christ.
