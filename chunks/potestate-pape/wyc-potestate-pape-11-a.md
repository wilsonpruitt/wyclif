# *De potestate pape*, chapter 11, part 1: order in the priesthood, and Bernard against papal lordship

## ¶1 · 265.18–266.3

> [p. 265] Contra dicta arguitur per hoc quod Deus posuit quamlibet universitatem suam in mensura, numero et pondere, ut dicitur Sapiens XI, 31 et per consequens oportet esse ordinem in sacerdotali potestate sacramenta et sacramentalia ministrandi. Sic enim in lege veteri fuit unicus summus sacerdos, cui erant excellenciora officia limitata, in cuius signum Deus ordinavit unicam vestem sacerdotalem pro Aaron, ut patet Exod. XXVIII, 4; unde et consequenter dicitur: Vestem autem sanctam qua utitur Aaron habebunt filii eius post eum, ut ungantur in ea et consecrentur manus eorum. Summo autem sacerdoti appropriata sunt multa officia, ut patet Exod. XXIX, [p. 266] XXX et Levitici VIII, multo magis igitur in novo testamento, ubi debet esse in perfecciori ordine maior distinccio.

Against what has been said, [it is argued by appeal to]{g:arguitur-per} this, that God has set each whole of his "in measure, and number, and weight", as is said in Wisdom 11:31; and consequently there must be order in the priestly [power]{g:potestas} of ministering the sacraments and [sacramentals]{g:sacramentalia}.[^1] For so in the old law there was a single high priest, to whom the more excellent offices were [assigned]{g:limitare}; in sign of which God ordained a single priestly vestment for Aaron, as is clear in Exod. 28:4; hence it is also said in consequence: "And the holy vesture, which Aaron useth, his sons shall have after him, that they may be anointed, and their hands consecrated in it."[^2] Now to the high priest many offices are appropriated, as is clear in Exod. 29, [266] 30, and Lev. 8; much more, therefore, in the new testament, where in a more perfect order there must be a greater distinction.

## ¶2 · 266.4–266.8

> [p. 266] Hic dicitur quod prima conclusio est concedenda. Omne enim quod erit contingenter oportet esse necessitate ex supposicione, cum Deus necessario eternaliter noscit illud et ordinat vel ipsum vel de ipso bonum.

To this [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that the first [conclusion]{g:conclusio} is to be granted. For everything that will be contingently must be by necessity [on supposition]{g:ex-supposicione}, since God necessarily and eternally knows it, and ordains either it or some good from it.[^3]

## ¶3 · 266.9–268.27

> [p. 266] Sed pro materia argumenti notandum quod aliquis pretenditur esse ordo, in quo propter defectum ordinis homo peccat et aliquis ordo est, circa quem propter defectum preponderancie homo peccat. Pretenditur autem esse ordo, quod papa sit mundo ditissimus, et tamen est abusus ordinis abiectissimus, cum Christus cuius ordini strictissime obligatur fuit mundo pauperrimus; potest tamen titulo elemosine accipere benediccionem ab homine, sic quod nec sit exinde fastu vel avaricia soporatus nec a papali officio plus abstractus. Quod si elemosinacio perversa est in dominacionem, sancta predicacio in litis secularis defensionem et affeccio spiritualis lucri ecclesie in mundialium affeccionem, tunc est plena abhominacio desolacionis Dan. IX et Matth. XXIV capitulis prophetata. Et talem vocant quidam papam cesareum et alium condicionis opposite papam Christi. Unde beatus Bernhardus ad Eugenium destruit multas hereses istius materie, nam in fine libri IV loquitur contra dominium ecclesie sub hiis verbis: Consideres ante omnia sanctam Romanam ecclesiam, cui Deo autore prees, [p. 267] ecclesiarum matrem esse non dominam: te vero non dominum esse episcoporum sed unum ex ipsis, qualem de cetero oportet te esse. Considera formam iusticie, sanctimonie speculum, pietatis exemplar, assertorem veritatis, fidei defensorem, doctorem gencium, christianorum ducem, amicum sponsi, sponse paranimfum, cleri ordinatorem, pastorem plebium, magistrum insipiencium refugium oppressorum, pauperum advocatum, miserorum spem, tutorem pupillorum, iudicem viduarum, oculum cecorum, linguam mutorum, baculum senum, ultorem scelerum, malorum metum, bonorum gloriam, virgam potentum, malleum criminosorum, regum patrem, legum moderatorem, canonum dispensatorem, sal terre orbis lumen, sacerdotem altissimi, vicarium Christi, christum Domini postremo Deum Pharaonis. Talis debet esse papa: quod si in istis triginta quatuor deficiat induens vestem lupi oppositam, nullum est in ecclesia horribilius monstrum, ut docet in fine III libri et libro II: Nunc audi canticum meum minus suave sed salutare. Monstruosa res gradus summus et animus infimus, sedes prima et vita yma, lingua magniloqua et manus ociosa, sermo multus et fructus nullus, vultus gravis et actus levis, ingens auctoritas et nutans subtilitas. Unde II in principio libri III declarat et modificat: potestatem, quam papa debet habere si vitam novam inculpabiliter recompensat et incipiens ab apostolis, a quibus oportet exemplari papalem potenciam Occumbebant, inquit, fortissimi bellatores: et non succumbebant triumphabant eciam mortui; nimis confortatus est principatus eorum; constituti sunt [p. 268] principes super omnem terram; eis tu successisti in hereditatem; ita tu heres et orbis hereditas, ac quamvis hec porcio te contingat, id sobria consideracione pensandum; non enim reor per omnem modum, sed sane quodamtenus; dispensacio, inquit, super illum tibi credita est, non data possessio. Et post detegit heresim, qua Antichristus extollit se super omne quod dicitur Deus. Si quis, inquit, usurpat hanc potestatem, contradicit illi, qui dicit: Meus est orbis terre et plenitudo eius. Non tu ille, de quo propheta: Et erit omnis terra possessio eius. Christus hic est, qui possessionem sibi vendicat et iure creacionis et merito redemcionis et dono Patris. Cui enim alteri dictum est: Postula a me et dabo tibi gentes, hereditatem tuam et possessionem tuam, terminos terre, possessionem et dominium, cede huic, tu curam illius habe, pars tua hec, ultra ne extendas manum. Et post obicit per hoc quod idem est preesse et dominari, sed hoc negat, cum bene preest, qui preest in sollicitudine, villa eciam subiecta est villico et dominus parvulus pedagogo. Ita et tu, inquit, presis, ut provideas, ut consulas, ut procures et serves, presis ut prosis, presis ut fidelis servus et prudens, ut des subiectis escam in tempore, hoc est, ut dispenses, non ut imperes; hoc fac et dominari te non affectes hominum homo, ut non dominetur tui iniusticia.

But for the matter of the argument it is to be noted that something is pretended to be an order in which, through a defect of order, a man sins; and there is some order about which a man sins through a defect of preponderance.[^4] And it is pretended to be an order that the [pope]{g:papa} should be the richest in the world, and yet it is the most abject abuse of order, since Christ, to whose order he is most strictly bound, was the poorest in the world; yet he can, by [title]{g:titulus} of [alms]{g:elemosina}, accept a blessing from a man, provided that he is neither lulled to sleep by it with pride or avarice nor drawn further away from the papal office.[^5] But if [almsgiving]{g:elemosinacio} is perverted into [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio}, holy preaching into the defence of secular litigation, and the affection for the spiritual gain of the [church]{g:ecclesia} into affection for worldly things, then there is the full "abomination of desolation" prophesied in the ninth chapter of Daniel and the twenty-fourth of Matthew.[^6] And such a one some call a caesarean pope, and the other, of the opposite condition, a pope of Christ. Hence blessed Bernard, [writing] to Eugenius, destroys many heresies on this matter; for at the end of book 4 he speaks against the [lordship]{g:dominium} of the church in these words: "Consider before all things that the holy Roman church, over which by God's authorship you preside, [267] is the mother of the churches, not their mistress; and that you are not lord of the [bishops]{g:episcopus} but one of them, such as henceforth you must be.[^7] Consider [that you are] the form of justice, the mirror of holiness, the exemplar of [piety]{g:pietas}, the asserter of truth, the defender of the faith, the teacher of the nations, the leader of Christians, the friend of the bridegroom, the bridesman of the [bride]{g:sponsa}, the ordainer of the clergy, the shepherd of the peoples, the master of the foolish, the refuge of the oppressed, the advocate of the poor, the hope of the wretched, the guardian of orphans, the judge of widows, the eye of the blind, the tongue of the dumb, the staff of the old, the avenger of crimes, the terror of the wicked, the glory of the good, the rod of the powerful, the hammer of [criminous men]{g:criminosus}, the father of kings, the moderator of laws, the [dispenser]{g:dispensator} of the canons, the salt of the earth, the light of the world, the priest of the Most High, the [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius-christi}, the anointed of the Lord, lastly the God of Pharaoh."[^8] Such ought the pope to be; and if he fails in these thirty-four, putting on the opposite garment of the wolf, there is no more horrible monster in the church, as he teaches at the end of book 3 and in book 2: "Now hear my song, less sweet but wholesome. A monstrous thing: the highest rank and the lowest mind, the first seat and the basest life, a grandiloquent tongue and idle hands, much speech and no fruit, a grave countenance and light conduct, immense [authority]{g:auctoritas} and wavering subtlety."[^9] Hence at the beginning of book 3 he declares and qualifies the power which the pope ought to have, if he blamelessly repays [it] with a new life, and [he does so] beginning from the apostles, from whom the papal [power]{g:potencia} must take its pattern:[^10] "There fell," he says, "the bravest warriors, and they did not succumb; they triumphed even when dead; 'their principality is exceedingly strengthened'; they were made [268] 'princes over all the earth'. To them you have succeeded in inheritance; so you are the heir, and the world the inheritance; and although this portion falls to you, this is to be weighed with sober consideration; for I do not think [it falls to you] in every way, but soundly, up to a point. A dispensation over it," he says, "is entrusted to you, not a possession given."[^11] And afterwards he [exposes]{g:detegere} the heresy by which Antichrist lifts himself up "above all that is called God".[^12] "If anyone," he says, "usurps this power, he contradicts him who says: 'The world is mine, and the fulness thereof.' You are not he of whom the prophet [says]: 'And all the earth shall be his possession.' This is Christ, who claims the possession for himself both by [right]{g:ius} of creation, and by merit of redemption, and by gift of the Father.[^13] For to what other was it said: 'Ask of me, and I will give thee the Gentiles for thy inheritance, and the utmost parts of the earth for thy possession'? The possession and the lordship, yield them to him; you, have the care of it: this is your part; do not stretch out your hand beyond it."[^14] And afterwards he objects, by appeal to this, that to preside and [to exercise lordship]{g:dominari} are the same; but he denies this, since he presides well who presides "with carefulness"; the estate too is subject to the steward, and the little lord to his tutor.[^15] "So you too," he says, "preside in order to provide, to counsel, to manage and to preserve; preside to profit; preside as 'a faithful and wise servant', to give your subjects 'meat in season', that is, to dispense, not to command. Do this, and do not [desire]{g:affectare} to lord it, a man over men, so that no injustice may lord it over you."[^16]

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** Wis. 11:21 in the Vulgate (Wyclif "XI, 31"), "thou hast ordered all things in measure, and number, and weight"; *posuit quamlibet universitatem suam* is Wyclif's own phrasing. **C.** *universitas*: each whole, each ordered totality God has made.
[^2]: **S.** Exod. 29:29; the Vulgate has *utetur* (DR "shall use") and *consecrantur*, Wyclif *utitur* and *consecrentur*. Exod. 28:4 names "the holy vestments for thy brother Aaron and his sons". **T.** B reads *Si* for *Sic* (265.23). **G.** *summus sacerdos*: the Old Testament "high priest" (DR), kept apart from *summus pontifex* "highest pontiff".
[^3]: **C.** "The first conclusion": that God has set everything in order. It is granted, as every contingent thing is necessary on supposition of God's knowing and ordaining it; the *sed* of 266.9 then turns to the kind of order the argument wants.
[^4]: **C.** *preponderancia*: taken as a failure of due weighting, an order rightly held but outweighed by something preferred to it; the first kind is a false order. The sense is not certain.
[^5]: **C.** *benediccio*: a gift, in the biblical sense (cf. 2 Cor. 9:5, *benedictionem*). *sic quod*: "provided that".
[^6]: **S.** Dan. 9:27; Matt. 24:15, "the abomination of desolation". **C.** *mundialium* is taken as neuter, "worldly things"; masculine, "worldly men" (GLOSSARY *mundialis*), is possible.
[^7]: **S.** Bernard, *De consideratione* IV.7.23 (LEDGER P11a-1, to be checked). **T.** For *unum ex ipsis* the manuscripts have another reading, illegible in the OCR of Loserth's foot (267.2); the print is translated.
[^8]: **C.** *Considera* governs the accusatives that follow, with *te esse* understood; Wyclif counts the titles as thirty-four. **S.** "The salt of the earth", "the light of the world": Matt. 5:13–14; "the god of Pharao": Exod. 7:1. *christum Domini*: "the anointed of the Lord".
[^9]: **S.** Bernard, *De consideratione* II.7.15; the "monster" at the end of book 3 (LEDGER P11a-2, to be checked). **T.** The print's *nutans subtilitas* is translated; Bernard's text is commonly given as *nutans stabilitas*, "wavering stability" (LEDGER P11a-3, to be checked). **R.** Both manuscripts note in the margin (267): *Monstruosa res*.
[^10]: **C.** *potenciam*: "power" here (GLOSSARY *potencia*, CONTEXTUAL), the papal power in the ordinary sense, beside *potestatem* earlier in the sentence; Wyclif uses the two words interchangeably. *si vitam novam inculpabiliter recompensat*: "if he blamelessly repays [it] with a new life"; the clause is compressed.
[^11]: **S.** Bernard, *De consideratione* III.1.1 (LEDGER P11a-4, to be checked), with Ps. 138:17 and Ps. 44:17. **T.** The print's *ac quamvis* is translated, "and although"; Bernard's text is usually given as *sed quatenus*, "but how far" (checked with P11a-4).
[^12]: **S.** 2 Thess. 2:4, "is lifted up above all that is called God"; Wyclif's *extollit se* is active.
[^13]: **S.** Ps. 49:12 (Loserth: Ps. XLIX, 11), "the world is mine, and the fulness thereof". For the prophet's words Loserth gives Ps. CIII (verse illegible in the OCR); they are not the Vulgate's Ps. 103:24, and are translated as quoted (LEDGER P11a-5, to be checked). **T.** For *et iure creacionis* the manuscripts read *opere creacionis*, and for *Patris*, *potestatis* (268.12–13); the print governs. **R.** Both manuscripts at 268.8: *Dominacio pape interdicitur universaliter*.
[^14]: **S.** Ps. 2:8 (Loserth).
[^15]: **S.** Rom. 12:8, "he that ruleth, with carefulness". **G.** *dominari*: "to exercise lordship" where Bernard denies it is the same as presiding; in his closing words (*dominari te non affectes*, *non dominetur*) the pejorative "lord it".
[^16]: **S.** Matt. 24:45, "a faithful and wise servant … to give them meat in season"; the closing words echo Ps. 118:133, "let no iniquity have dominion over me".
