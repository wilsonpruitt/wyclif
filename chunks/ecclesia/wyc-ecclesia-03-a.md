# *De ecclesia*, chapter 3, part 1: third objection: church dues; Gregory on the hypocrite's love of temporal goods

## ¶1 · 47.7–47.13

> [p. 47] Sed tercio principaliter obicitur per hoc quod, data ista sentencia, tolleretur omnis exaccio debiti ecclesie et extingueretur omnis lex contencionis in clero, cum debitor posset fingere ecclesiasticum non esse dignum vel talem qui sic bona ecclesie vendicaret, et quantum ad excommunicacionem que est ultimum refugium, diceret quod non ligat.

But third, as a principal [objection, it is objected, by appeal to]{g:obicitur per} this: that, granted
this [teaching]{g:sentencia}, every exaction of what is owed to the [church]{g:ecclesia} would be taken
away, and every law of suit among the clergy would be extinguished, since a debtor could pretend that
the churchman was not worthy, or not such as might so claim the goods of the church; and as to
excommunication, which is the last refuge, he would say that it does not bind.[^1]

## ¶2 · 47.14–48.22

> [p. 47] Hic dicitur quod, si lex Dei debite scrutaretur nobis, non tantum contenderemus circa bona ecclesie, cum manifestum sit quod prescitus vel quicunque clericus sine peraccione sui ministerii compensante qui plus propter honorem, voluptatem vel questum seculi contendit, bona ecclesie occupat vel acceptat, quam propter honorem Dei et edificacionem ecclesie, ut sic false aliena vendicat et consumit, et per consequens, licet versucias talium detegere et subtrahendo bona pauperum resistere (ut diffuse dictum est lib. VI, cap....), unde sub auctoritate beati Augustini scribitur in decretis VIIIª, q. 1: Sunt inquit quidam in ecclesia prepositi de quibus apostolus Paulus dicit: Sua querentes, non que Jesu Christi. Quid est: Sua querentes [p. 48] non Christum gratis diligentes, non Deum propter ipsum Deum querentes, temporalia consequentes, lucris inhiantes, honores ab hominibus appetentes. Hec quando amantur a preposito et propter hec servitur Deo, quisquis talis est, mercenarius est, inter filios se non computet. Unde quia omnes confitemur nos principaliter propter honorem Dei et spiritualem profectum ecclesie tales preposituras appetere, ad discernendum si sumus ypocrite, oportet credere operibus nostris, speculo scripture et testimonio sanctorum. Unde Gregorius XXXI°, Moral. cap. IX, et infra, satis explicat istam materiam in tribus conclusionibus; supponens primo quod mens iusti vel veri christiani debet esse tam affectu quam intellectu collecta intrinsecus, sic quod nichil suum exterius derelinquat. Patet ex hoc quod aliter peccaret contra hoc mandatum: Diliges dominum Deum tuum ex toto corde, ex tota anima et ex tota mente. Cum ergo non debet peccare, sequitur quod non debet esse fracta sic in intellectu et affectu circa temporalia, quia ut sic amor uni debitus dispergitur circa multa.

To this [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that, if God's [law]{g:lex dei} were duly searched by us, we should
not contend so much about the goods of the church; since it is manifest that a
[foreknown]{g:prescitus} man, or any [cleric]{g:clericus} without a performance of his ministry to
compensate, who contends for, occupies or accepts the goods of the church more for the honour,
pleasure or gain of the world than for the honour of God and the edification of the church, thus
falsely claims and consumes what belongs to others; and consequently it is lawful to
[expose]{g:detegere} the cunning of such men and to resist [them] by withdrawing the goods of the poor
(as was said at length in book 6, chapter ...).[^2] Hence, under the authority of blessed Augustine, it
is written in the Decrees, [causa] 8, q. 1: "There are", he says, "certain [superiors]{g:prepositi} in
the church of whom the apostle Paul says: Seeking their own, not the things that are Jesus Christ's.
What is 'seeking their own'? [48] Not loving Christ freely, not seeking God for God's own sake, pursuing
[temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, gaping after gains, coveting honours from men. When these things are
loved by a superior, and God is served for these things, whoever is such a one is a hireling; let him
not count himself among the sons."[^3] Hence, since we all confess that we covet such superiorships
principally for the honour of God and the spiritual profit of the church, in order to discern whether
we are hypocrites we must give credence to our works, to the mirror of scripture and to the testimony of
the saints.[^4] Hence Gregory, *Moralia* 31, chapter 9 and following, explains this matter well enough
in three conclusions, supposing first that the mind of a just man, or of a true Christian, ought to be
gathered within, in affection as much as in intellect, so that it leaves nothing of its own outside.
This is clear from this: that otherwise it would sin against this commandment: "Thou shalt love the
Lord thy God with thy whole heart, and with thy whole soul, and with thy whole mind."[^5] Since,
therefore, it ought not to sin, [it follows]{g:sequitur} that it ought not to be so broken up, in
intellect and affection, about temporal goods; for in that way the love owed to one is dispersed about
many things.

## ¶3 · 48.23–50.7

> [p. 48] Isto supposito patet, primo quod sancti dum se intra semet ipsos sic colligunt, occulta tam de statu proprio quam de statu proximi comprehendunt. Patet ex hoc quod lux vera que non potest personas accipere, necessario secundum capacitatem irradiat talem mentem. Et hoc figuratum est Ezechiel VIII, 3, ubi sic legitur: [p. 49] Emissa similitudo manus apprehendit me in cincinno capitis mei et elevavit me spiritus inter terram et celum et adduxit me in Jerusalem in visione Dei iuxta ostium interius quod respiciebat ad aquilonem, ubi erat statutum ydolum zeli ad provocandum emulacionem. Manus missa est operativa volucio divina, cincinnus capitis est collecte cogitaciones mentis quibus habitis mens incomplete trahitur a terrenis, cum nec habet animum omnino celestem nec omnino in terrestribus soporatum, sed inter terram et celum in visione Dei in Jerusalem ducitur, quia zelo caritatis qualis debet esse mater ecclesia contemplatur. Et signanter animus talis adducitur ad ostium interius quod respicit ad aquilonem, quia interna contemplacione intra ecclesiam plus prava quam recta fieri deprehendunt, dum clerici in aquilonis pariete, id est, ad solis sinistrum oculos flectunt et elongata austerna caritate habundant frigore viciorum; cuius causa est globus temporalium quem ecclesia sola specie fidelis adorat; ex hinc enim zelus sponsi ecclesie ad feriendum ypocritas provocatur. Secundo patet ex quo signo perpenditur, si ecclesiastici flagrant terrenis, celestibus pretermissis: cum enim amor sit pondus animi, nec sit possibile hominem moveri libere quo affeccio mentis ipsum non tulerit, patet quod ex operibus debet mentis affeccio iudicari. Si ergo vides quod ecclesiasticus quantum ad perpetuacionem, quantum ad defensionem et quantum ad amissionem temporalium plus insudat, quam facit pro spiritualibus, quis dubitat quin eis magis afficitur? Unde quantum ad perpetuacionem temporalium sic [p. 50] loquitur iste sanctus: postposita quippe cura filiorum sepe eos videas se contra pericula immensi laboris accingere, maria transmeare, adire iudicia, pulsare principatus, palacia irrumpere, iurgantibus populorum cuneis interesse. Cum autem pro spiritualibus acquirendis non apponunt tantam diligenciam, quis dubitat quin ista temporalia magis amant?

This supposed, it is clear, first, that the saints, while they so gather themselves within themselves,
grasp hidden things, as much of their own state as of their neighbour's. It is clear from this: that the
true light, which cannot respect persons, of necessity shines upon such a mind according to its
capacity.[^6] And this is figured in Ezekiel 8:3, where it reads thus: [49] "The likeness of a hand was
put forth and took me by a lock of my head: and the spirit lifted me up between the earth and the
heaven, and brought me in the vision of God into Jerusalem, near the inner gate, that looked toward the
north, where was set the idol of jealousy to provoke to jealousy." The hand put forth is the divine
operative will; the lock of the head is the gathered thoughts of the mind, which once had, the mind is
drawn incompletely from earthly things, since it has a soul neither wholly heavenly nor wholly asleep
in earthly things, but is led between earth and heaven in the vision of God into Jerusalem, because with
the zeal of [charity]{g:caritas} it contemplates what mother church ought to be.[^7] And significantly
such a soul is brought to the inner gate that looks toward the north, because by inward contemplation
they discover that within the church more crooked things than right are done, while clerics on the
north wall, that is, to the left of the sun, turn their eyes, and, [southern] charity removed far off,
abound in the cold of vices; the cause of which is the mass of temporal goods, which the church,
faithful in appearance only, worships; for hence the zeal of the church's [bridegroom]{g:sponsus} is
provoked to strike the hypocrites.[^8] Second, it is clear by what sign it is weighed whether churchmen
burn for earthly things, heavenly things being passed over: for since love is the weight of the soul,
and it is not possible for a man to be moved freely where the affection of his mind has not carried
him, it is clear that the affection of the mind must be judged from works.[^9] If, therefore, you see
that a churchman sweats more over the perpetuating, over the defence and over the loss of temporal goods
than he does for spiritual ones, who doubts that he is more attached to them? Hence, as to the
perpetuating of temporal goods, thus [50] speaks this saint: "For, the care of their sons put aside,
you may often see them gird themselves against the dangers of immense labour, cross the seas, go to the
courts, knock at the doors of princes, break into palaces, take part in the wrangling throngs of the
peoples." Since, though, they do not lay out so much diligence for acquiring spiritual things, who
doubts that they love these temporal ones more?[^10]

## ¶4 · 50.8–51.7

> [p. 50] Et quantum ad retencionem, patet idem ex maioritate diligencie quam apponimus pro temporalibus quam pro spiritualibus defendendis. Ypocrita, inquit Gregorius, assumpto sancte conversacionis habitu filiorum custodiam deserit et temporalia queque defendere eciam iurgiis querit. Exemplo suo eorum corda perdere non trepidat et quasi per negligenciam amittere patrimonia terrena formidat. In errorem discipulus labitur et tamen cor ypocrite nullo dolore sauciatur. Ire in iniquitatis voraginem commissos sibi conspicit et hec quasi inaudita pertransit; at si quod sibi leviter inferri temporale dampnum senserit, in ulcionis iram repente ab intimis inardescit. Et mox paciencia rumpitur, mox dolor cordis in vocibus effrenatur, quia dum animarum dampna equanimiter tollerat, iacturam vero rerum temporalium repellere eciam cum spiritus commocione festinat, cunctis veraciter indicat teste mentis perturbacione quid amat. Ibi quippe est grande studium defensionis, ubi est grandior vis amoris. Et quoad amissionem bonorum hominis, est idem indicium, quia indubie quicunque plus dolet de amissione boni fortune quam boni virtutis, ut sic, indicat se plus diligere temporalia quam virtutes, cum sit maxima apud philosophos quod dolor amissi et amor [p. 51] habiti suis temporibus correspondent. Unde Gregorius ubi supra: Quanto magis terrena quis diligit, tanto privari eis vehemencius pertimescit. Qua enim mente in hoc mundo aliquid possidemus, non docemur nisi cum amittimus. Sine dolore namque amittitur quidquid sine amore possidetur, que vero ardenter diligimus habita, graviter suspiramus ablata.

And as to retention, the same is clear from the [greatness]{g:majoritas} of the diligence we lay out
for defending temporal rather than spiritual things. "The hypocrite", says Gregory, "having put on the
habit of a holy [manner of life]{g:conversacio}, deserts the keeping of his sons, and seeks to defend
all temporal things, even with quarrels. He does not tremble to destroy their hearts by his example,
and he fears to lose earthly patrimonies as if by negligence. The disciple slips into error, and yet the
hypocrite's heart is wounded by no grief. He sees those committed to him go into the whirlpool of
iniquity, and passes these things by as if unheard; but if he feels some temporal loss lightly inflicted
on himself, he suddenly blazes up from his inmost parts into the anger of revenge. And at once his
patience breaks, at once the grief of his heart runs unbridled into words; because, while he bears the
losses of souls with equanimity, but hastens, even with commotion of spirit, to repel the loss of
temporal things, he truly shows to all, by the witness of his mind's disturbance, what he loves. For
there is great zeal for defence where there is the greater force of love."[^11] And as to the loss of a
man's goods, there is the same indication: for without doubt whoever grieves more over the loss of a
good of fortune than of a good of virtue shows himself, as such, to love temporal goods more than
virtues; since it is a maxim among the philosophers that grief for what is lost and love for [51] what
is had correspond in their times.[^12] Hence Gregory in the place cited: "The more anyone loves earthly
things, the more vehemently he dreads being deprived of them. For with what mind we possess anything
in this world, we are not taught except when we lose it. For whatever is possessed without love is lost
without grief; but the things we love ardently when had, we sigh for heavily when taken away."[^13]

## Notes

[^1]: **C/R.** The third of the objections announced at 38.25 (the second, 42.1). *Ista sentencia*:
    that faith in a superior's membership of the church is owed only on the evidence of his works. *Lex
    contencionis*: legal action, the right of suit by which dues were recovered; *in clero* (print *m
    clero*, 47.8) is read as *in*. A's margin: *Contra dicta arguitur*.

[^2]: **C/T.** *Hic dicitur*: "To this the answer is" (GLOSSARY §C). *Prescitus*: one God foreknows
    will be damned (GLOSSARY §A). Construal: *qui … contendit, bona ecclesie occupat vel acceptat* is one
    relative clause with three verbs, governed by *plus … quam*; the main verb is *vendicat et consumit*.
    *Sine peraccione sui ministerii compensante*: without doing the ministry that would earn the
    revenue. *Subtrahendo bona pauperum*: withholding tithes and offerings, which are the goods of the
    poor. *Lib. VI, cap. ...*: the chapter number is wanting (Loserth: *capit. seq. lacuna*, 47.24); the
    place in *De veritate* is unidentified (LEDGER E03a-1). A's margin: *Conclusio*.

[^3]: **S.** *Decretum* C.8 q.1 c.19, Loserth's reference, form ours (LEDGER E03a-2), from Augustine,
    *In Iohannis evangelium tractatus* 46.5, on John 10:12, "the hireling" (LEDGER E03a-3). The apostle's
    words: Phil. 2:21, DR "For all seek the things that are their own, not the things that are Jesus
    Christ's", here in the participle. *Consequentes*: Augustine's *temporalia commoda consectantes*, "pursuing" (PL 35:1729; Wyclif lacks
    *commoda*). A's
    margins: *Augustinus*; *Contra clerum pravum et avarum*.

[^4]: **T.** *Speculo scripture* (48.10), "the mirror of scripture", as at 41.13 (*optimum speculum*):
    the print's reading (plate), which the OCR had cut to *specu*. B *confitemur nec principaliter* (48.7);
    the MSS *preposito propter* (48.4).

[^5]: **S.** Gregory, *Moralia in Iob* 31, Wyclif's chapter 9; Loserth: 31, chapter 12 (or 9), *Opp.*
    I, 1005; on Job 39: PL 76:583D–585C, §§19–21, Migne chapters XII–XIII (LEDGER E03a-4). Matt. 22:37 (Loserth's "XIII, 37" is an OCR error), DR; Wyclif
    has *ex … ex … ex* for the Vulgate's *ex … in … in* (cf. Mark 12:30). A's margin: *Gregorius*.

[^6]: **S/R.** *Lux vera*: John 1:9. *Personas accipere*: to respect persons (Acts 10:34). Loserth
    gives Gregory's words for *colligunt*: *mira ac penetrabili acie occulta eciam aliena delicta
    deprehendunt* (48.24). AB margin: *Prima conclusio*; B *statum* (48.24).

[^7]: **S/T/C.** Ezek. 8:3 (DR), Wyclif's text the Vulgate's but *provocandum*. Loserth corrects the
    MSS' *respicit* to *respiciebat* (49.4). *Incomplete*: not wholly, as the next clause explains. AA₁
    *colore cogitacionis* (49.7); AA₁ *inter* (49.15). *Qualis debet esse mater ecclesia contemplatur*:
    we take *qualis* as an indirect question, "contemplates what mother church ought to be"; it may be
    "contemplates [with such zeal] as mother church ought to have". A's margin: *cincinnus*.

[^8]: **T/G.** *Austerna* (49.18), as printed (plate), is not a word, and Gregory has no such clause
    (PL 76:584B: the sentence is Wyclif's paraphrase); we translate *austrina*, "southern", opposed to the
    north (our emendation); *eterna*, "eternal", is the alternative. *Globus*: here "mass" (of temporal
    goods), not the glossary's derogatory "band" of persons. *Sola specie fidelis*: the church faithful
    in outward show only. A's margin: *Pastor negligens continue infra* (49.18).

[^9]: **S/R.** *Amor … pondus animi*: Augustine, *Confessions* 13.9.10, *pondus meum amor meus* (LEDGER
    E03a-5). AB margin: *Secunda conclusio*; B: *Nota optime quomodo vere discernendum est, quis appetit
    preesse in ecclesia propter honorem Dei et spiritualem profectum animarum*. B *affecto* (49.25).

[^10]: **S/C.** Gregory, *l.c.* (Loserth, 49.7, 49.11, 50.1) (LEDGER E03a-4). *Perpetuacio*: securing
    temporal goods in perpetuity (Loserth's side-note: "eagerness to obtain wealth"). *Filiorum*: the
    spiritual sons in the hypocrite's charge.

[^11]: **S/T.** Gregory, *Moralia* 31, chapter 13 (Loserth, 50.10) (LEDGER E03a-4). *Conversacio*:
    "manner of life" (GLOSSARY §D); *formidat* is the plain fear of the emotion. B *rei in* (50.16–17);
    AA₁ *at sibi quid*, B *at si quid sibi* (50.18). AB margin: *Gregorius*.

[^12]: **C/R.** *Maioritas*: "greatness" (GLOSSARY §D). *Boni fortune … boni virtutis*: the
    philosophers' goods of fortune against goods of soul. *Maxima*: a maxim; the source is unidentified
    (LEDGER E03a-6). A's margin: *Amissio temporalium est minus dolenda quam amissio virtutis* (50.28).

[^13]: **S/T.** Gregory, *Moralia* 31, chapter 13 (Loserth, 51.1) (LEDGER E03a-4). A *in terrena*
