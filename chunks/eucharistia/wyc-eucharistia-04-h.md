# *De eucharistia*, chapter 4, part 8: the heaviness of the host, the vessel of the celebration, and the worth of the mass

## ¶1 · 110.22–111.3

> [p. 110] In cuius evidenciam glosa ordinaria in De Consecracione, distinccione IIᵃ, super capitulo Sacerdotes dicit quod hostia ista est ponderositas sed non est ponderosum. Sed omnes tres vie predicte dicunt sacramentum aliquid esse ponderosum; et patet quod false intelligentes de hoc sacramento equivocant et [p. 111] errant tanquam ydolatre false senciendo de Eukaristia, cum false adorant hoc ut Deum in signo, ac si homo adoret dyabolum in similitudine crucifixi.

As evidence of this, the ordinary [gloss]{g:glosa} on *De consecratione*, distinction 2, on the chapter *Sacerdotes*, says that this [host]{g:hostia} is [heaviness]{g:ponderositas} but is not heavy.[^1][^2] But all three ways aforesaid say that the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} is something heavy; and it is clear that those who understand falsely about this sacrament equivocate and [111] err like idolaters, [holding]{g:sentire} falsely about the eucharist, since they falsely worship this as God in the [sign]{g:signum}, as if a man were to worship the devil in the likeness of the crucified.

## ¶2 · 111.4–112.3

> [p. 111] Nec dubium quin error in talibus accusat false adorantem; et patet quod necessarium est tractare materiam istam de eukaristia, quia indubie altera istarum viarum est manifeste heretica, cum in precipua scriptura, quia in verbis Domini quibus sacratur hostia tenet altera istarum viarum valde pertinaciter sensum autori scripture contrarium et dimittit doctrinam Christi in valde necessariis ad salutem et imponit sibi implicite auctorisacionem abhominacionis pessime, licet doctores famosi legum ecclesie parvipendentes recitent inter opiniones quod panis et vinum remanent post consecracionem; ad quod arguunt sed non inprobant, ut non possunt, licet parti false magis faveant. Si ergo illi precipui doctores legum ecclesie non condempnant tamquam hereticum quod panis remanet, sed probant quod hoc sequitur ex lege predicta Ego Beringarius, verisimile est (ut prius) quod doctores impericiores non temptabunt hereticare istam sentenciam; sed quomodocunque temptaverunt scio quod non possunt, cum non possunt probare istam sentenciam esse falsam. Non ergo refert in quo vase servatis moribus sacerdos sanctificet, sed (ut dictum est) standum est appropriate consuetudini patrie et servata honestate de Christo virtuose recolere, hoc enim est infinitum melius quam celebrare. Nec dissonat quod Christus sit sacramentaliter in aqua vel alio mixto vino, ymmo in medio aereo sed preeminenter in anima, cum finis huius sacramenti sit quod Christus inhabitet animam per virtutes, sic quod laycus memorans [p. 112] corpus Christi in celo, melius quam iste sacerdos conficiens facit efficacius et eque vere sed modo alio corpus Christi esse cum illo.

Nor is there doubt that error in such things accuses the one who worships falsely; and it is clear that it is necessary to treat this matter of the eucharist, because without doubt one of these ways is manifestly heretical, since in a principal scripture, namely in the words of the Lord by which the host is [hallowed]{g:sacrare}, one of these ways holds very obstinately a sense contrary to the author of scripture, and lays aside the [doctrine]{g:doctrina} of Christ in things very necessary to salvation, and lays upon itself implicitly the authorization of a most wicked abomination; although famous doctors of the [laws of the church]{g:leges-ecclesie}, making little of it, [recite]{g:recitare} among the [opinions]{g:opinio} that [bread]{g:panis} and [wine]{g:vinum} remain after [consecration]{g:consecracio}; they argue towards it but do not disprove it, as they cannot, although they favour the false side more.[^3] If therefore those leading doctors of the laws of the church do not condemn as heretical that the bread remains, but prove that this [follows]{g:sequitur} from the aforesaid law *Ego Berengarius*, it is likely (as before) that less skilled doctors will not attempt to [pronounce heretical]{g:hereticare} this [teaching]{g:sentencia};[^4] but however they have attempted it, I know that they cannot, since they cannot prove this teaching to be false.[^5] Therefore it does not matter in what vessel, good conduct being kept, the [priest]{g:sacerdos} sanctifies; but (as has been said) one must stand by the appropriate [custom]{g:consuetudo} of the [homeland]{g:patria} and, decency being kept, call Christ to mind virtuously, for this is infinitely better than to celebrate.[^6] Nor is it out of tune that Christ should be [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} in water or in another [liquid] mixed with wine, indeed in the medium of the air, but preeminently in the soul, since the end of this sacrament is that Christ should dwell in the soul through the [virtues]{g:virtus};[^7] so that a layman calling to mind [112] the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} in heaven, better than such a priest [consecrating]{g:conficere}, makes, more efficaciously and equally [truly]{g:vere} but in another mode, the body of Christ to be with him.[^8]

## ¶3 · 112.4–112.7

> [p. 112] Sed vulgus credit nimis infideliter et blaspheme quod hoc signum sacramentale corporis Christi sit realiter ipse Christus. Et in ista heresi involvuntur clerici et prelati.

But the common people believe, [exceedingly]{g:nimis} unfaithfully and blasphemously, that this sacramental sign of the body of Christ is [really]{g:realiter} Christ himself. And in this heresy the [clerics]{g:clericus} and [prelates]{g:prelatus} are entangled.

## ¶4 · 112.8–112.10

> [p. 112] Et alii racione victi hoc esse blasphemum dicunt quod non est populo tangendum, sed summe inpossibile est ad excitandum devocionem populo predicandum.

And others, overcome by reason, say that this is blasphemous, a thing not to be touched on before the people, but that what is supremely impossible is to be preached to the people to stir up devotion.[^9]

## ¶5 · 112.12–112.18

> [p. 112] Sed veritas novit si cupiditas honoris et lucri a laycis sit in causa huius blasphemie. Sed nec istis obstat sed consonat et expedit devotis celebrare frequenter, cum ille sit valde indispositus, cui non ex celebracione plus et devocius imprimitur memoria Christi et per consequens ut oracio sua prosit ecclesie; quod si deest expedit quod talis tunc non conficiat.

But the truth knows whether greed for honour and gain from the laity is the cause of this blasphemy. But it does not stand against these things but accords with them, that it is expedient for the devout to celebrate often, since that man is very [unfit]{g:indispositus} in whom the memory of Christ is not, from celebrating, imprinted more and more devoutly, and in consequence so that his prayer may profit the [church]{g:ecclesia}; and if this is lacking, it is expedient that such a one should not then consecrate.

## ¶6 · 112.19–112.30

> [p. 112] Unde expedit populo habere multos devotos presbiteros, sed nocet eis multum habere presbiteros viciosos; unde falsum est quod tantum valet missa cuiuscunque presbiteri, ut missa alicuius, cum missa sit aggregatum ex toto ministerio presbiteri celebrantis a signo usque ad signum; quod vulgus mensurat penes tempus quo sacerdos plene vestitur usque ad tempus quo casulam est exutus. Patet sic: Una talis missa est dampnabilis et Deo odibilis et alia est meritoria et Deo acceptabilis; ergo sunt valde dispares in valore; patet ex hoc quod ut sacerdos est Deo deaccepcior est ministracio sua iniustior.

Hence it is expedient for the people to have many devout [presbyters]{g:presbiter}, but it harms them much to have vicious presbyters; hence it is false that the mass of any presbyter whatever is worth as much as the mass of anyone [else], since the mass is the aggregate of the whole ministry of the celebrating presbyter from sign to sign; which the common people measure by the time from when the priest is fully vested to the time when he has put off the chasuble. It is clear thus: one such mass is damnable and hateful to God, and another is meritorious and acceptable to God; therefore they are very unequal in value. This is clear from the fact that the more [disowned]{g:deacceptus} by God the priest is, the more unjust is his ministration.

## ¶7 · 112.31–113.10

> [p. 112] Nec valet argucia qua arguitur quod missa illa est Christus vel eius pars qui est utrobique eque bonus, [p. 113] quia nimis equivocatur in quidditate misse. Non enim habet quilibet presbiter auctoritatem mittendi corpus Christi ad celum vel trahendi ipsum ad populum nec facit corpus Christi sed missam celebrat post et ante, ymmo tantum valet Christus existens in medio laycorum qui in suo nomine congregantur sicut ipse Christus per quemcunque sacerdotem ubilibet factus presens; ideo nimis leve foret dicere quod bonitas misse attenditur penes bonitatem Christi vel partis sue, quia tunc nimis bona foret generaliter missa presbiteri.

Nor does the [quibble]{g:argucia} hold by which it is argued that that mass is Christ, or his part, who is equally good in both places, [113] for there is exceeding equivocation about the quiddity of the mass. For not every presbyter has [authority]{g:auctoritas} to send the body of Christ to heaven or to draw him to the people, nor does he make the body of Christ, but he celebrates the mass after and before; indeed Christ existing in the midst of laymen who are gathered in his name is worth as much as Christ himself made present anywhere by any priest whatever; therefore it would be exceedingly frivolous to say that the goodness of the mass is reckoned by the goodness of Christ or of his part, because then the mass of a presbyter would in general be exceedingly good.[^10]

## ¶8 · 113.11–113.21

> [p. 113] Sed de paritate bonitatis hostie est difficultas, cum ipsa sit signum Christi et non pars eius; cum itaque illa hostia significat inter alia unionem Christi ad ecclesiam et illam efficacius significat signum unius facientis illam unionem quam signum illud quod impedit unionem, ergo efficacius est sacramentum huiusmodi in sacerdote bono quam malo; et sic intelliguntur decreta multa que mandant quod homo non audiat missam presbiteri notorie fornicantis; ideo, inquam, non est audienda, quia est Deo et homini odibilis et nociva ecclesie.

But about the equality of the host's goodness there is a difficulty, since it is the sign of Christ and not his part; since therefore that host signifies, among other things, the [union]{g:unio} of Christ to the church, and the sign of one who makes that union signifies it more efficaciously than that sign which hinders the union, therefore a sacrament of this kind is more efficacious in a good priest than in a bad one; and so are understood many [decrees]{g:decretum} which command that a man should not hear the mass of a notoriously fornicating presbyter; therefore, I say, it is not to be heard, because it is hateful to God and man and harmful to the church.[^11]

## ¶9 · 113.22–113.28

> [p. 113] Ex quibus videtur quod nedum missa sed hostia est melior in uno presbitero et in alio minus bona; nam quam efficax signum est ad memorandum memorie vitam Christi tam bonum sacramentum est, sed in uno sacerdote est ad hoc efficacius signum et in alio minus efficax: ergo sic est proporcionaliter melius sacramentum.

From which it seems that [not only]{g:nedum} the mass but the host is better in one presbyter and less good in another; for as efficacious a sign as it is for calling the life of Christ to memory, so good a sacrament it is; now in one priest it is a more efficacious sign for this, and in another less efficacious: therefore in this way it is proportionally a better sacrament.

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 110.22: False notions of the host lead to idolatry.
- 111.4: Hence the need of dealing with the subject.
- 111.30: The best presence of Christ is in the soul.
- 112.12: Need of good priests.
- 113.11: The value of the mass varies with the character of the priest.

## Notes

[^1]: **S/T.** Loserth notes that the sentence does not stand in the chapter named, and refers to *De cons.* D. 2 c. 30; the right chapter, as C reads (over an erasure, another hand), is *Si per negligenciam*, c. 27, with the ordinary gloss at the word *In terram* (modern form ours), which Loserth quotes at 183.1: *Dic quod ponderositas adhuc manet cum accidentibus aliis, et tamen nihil est ibi ponderosum*. D and E read *Sacerdotum* (LEDGER K04h-1).

[^2]: **C.** The abstract *ponderositas* "heaviness" against the concrete *ponderosum* "heavy [thing]": the gloss gives the host the quality and denies that it is the thing that has it. The next sentence turns on the concrete term.

[^3]: **C/S.** *Ad quod arguunt*: they bring arguments bearing on the remaining of the bread, without disproving it; "the false side" is the side Wyclif holds false. The canonists who recite the remaining among the opinions are not named here; they are the ordinary glosses cited at 107.22–108.1, e.g. that on *X* 3.41.6 *Cum Marthe* at *transubstantiatis*: *Tertia dicit quod remanet substantia panis et vini et sub eadem specie est corpus Christi* (Rome 1582) (LEDGER K04h-2).

[^4]: **C.** *Sentencia* (CONTEXTUAL): the position taught, that the bread remains; "teaching", not a judicial sentence. *Ego Berengarius* is *De cons.* D. 2 c. 42 (modern form ours) (LEDGER K04h-3).

[^5]: **T.** AB omit *non possunt* before *probare*; C supplies it in the margin in another hand. A has *sed … sentenciam* in the margin.

[^6]: **C/T.** *Servatis moribus* is taken of the priest's good conduct; it may instead mean "the usages being kept", which the next clause on custom would echo. *Appropriate* is the dative *appropriatae* with *consuetudini*, "the custom appropriate [to the place]"; ABCD read *approbate*, "approved". *Patria* is the earthly homeland here.

[^7]: **T/C.** *Alio mixto vino* is construed "another [liquid] mixed with wine"; ABC read *vel aliquo*, "or in something". D reads *animo* for *anima*. C has *sit … virtutes* (111.31–32) in the margin in another hand.

[^8]: **C.** Two comparatives stand side by side: *melius quam iste sacerdos* (the layman does it better than such a priest) and *efficacius* (the mode of the body's being with him); *eque vere sed modo alio* sets equal truth against a different mode. *Iste* is taken as "such a [consecrating but undevout] priest".

[^9]: **C.** The two gerundives are parallel: *non est populo tangendum* / *est … populo predicandum*, with *summe inpossibile* the subject of the second ("what is supremely impossible is to be preached"). Alternatively *summe inpossibile est* is a main clause, "it is supremely impossible [for it] to be preached to the people to stir up devotion".

[^10]: **C/S.** *Mittendi*: a play on *missa* and *mittere* "send". *Post et ante*: before and after the consecration, which the presbyter does not make. *In suo nomine congregantur* recalls Matt. 18:20 ("gathered together in my name, there am I in the midst of them"). D reads *mens equivocatur*; ABCD *in de quidditate*.

[^11]: **S/T.** The decrees are not named; the best-known is the canon *Nullus missam audiat presbyteri quem scit concubinam indubitanter habere* (Gratian D. 32 c. 5, Nicholas II, repeated by Alexander II in c. 6; modern form ours) (LEDGER K04h-4). At 113.15 E reads *sacerdotis* for *facientis*; at 113.20 ABCE read *inquit* for *inquam*.
