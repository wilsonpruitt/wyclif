# *Dialogus*, chapter 30: perpetual alms and the loaves and fishes

## Truth · 63.13–66.17

> [p. 63] VERITAS. Item suppono quod clerus propter cupidinem horum temporalium velit dotacionem dimittere et sub forma elemosine perpetue bona ista accipere et sic redit idem clericis quo ad fructus. Istud autem est multis inevidens cum tunc caderet a titulo quem silvester a cesare acceptavit, nec veritas perpetue elemosine in anglia salvaretur cum defendere non posset quin propter multa et magna peccata que perpetraverat sepius cadebat a titulo elemosine, cum non sit elemosina taliter apostatas enutrire. Immo non docetur vel evidenter ostenditur quod talis donacio unquam fuit elemosina voluntati divine conformata, quomodo ergo fingerent quod sit pura et perpetua elemosina? Numquid credimus quod sit in potestate istorum hominum perpetuare [p. 64] talem elemosinam cleri sui ac si dicerent contra Christum, ego volo quod sit elemosina quomodocunque vixerint et fuerint contrarie legi tue, vel ego volo quod vivat ad edificacionem ecclesie Christi utiliter, quia indubie sic vult deus, ergo mea volicio est impleta. Stephanus quidem Act. 7° negavit hanc consequenciam cum dixit Judeos specialiter religiosos atque episcopos sepe voluntati divine restitisse, quis ergo color quod sic ditantes ecclesiam benefecerant cum habuerunt huiusmodi voluntatem, deus enim non obstante voluntate huiusmodi inculpabili auffert collata istis clericis cum sic delinquunt, quare ergo non debent sic domini se dei bene placito conformantes, cum in hoc non iacet evidens periculum sed conformitas legi dei, quia non dubium quin clerus in apostolis et Chrysti discipulis vixit securius atque perfeccius carendo talibus diutinis elemosinis quam vivit vel vixerit clerus noster. Quis ergo fructus spiritualis vel vita miraculosa daret evidenciam ad continuandum tales elemosinas sic inceptas? Et patet defectus argucie consequentis, quando arguitur quod sic servari possent elemosine perpetue a progenitoribus concesse per generacionem ex dei ordinancia succedentem. Nec enim docetur quod iste sunt elemosine perpetue fundate a progenitoribus, nec quod illi exinde a dampnacione perpetua sunt exempti, nec quod esset meritorium generacioni illorum dictam elemosinam servare pro morula in hac forma, nec video per hoc evidenciam, nisi quod isti domini in hoc excedunt et declinant a regula domini Jesu Christi. Cum ergo in talibus pars securior sit tenenda, videtur quod foret securius talem ad ewangelicam paupertatem restringere et non excedere limites Jesu Christi. Et concedi posset quod seculares domini conservent et colligant ad usus cleri omnes redditus istos sive fructus et [p. 65] expendant residuum suorum redituum circa colleccionem istam servilem. Sed non sequitur quod tunc bene, quia talis servitus istis clericis non est in lege domini exemplata, et ipsimet clerici propter zelum quem habent ad dominacionem secularem non tale servicium secularium acceptarent. Sed evidenter infertur quod episcopi vel ewangelisantes fideliter dyaconos limitarent ad moderatam elemosinam ministrandam. Sed quid hoc ad perpetuacionem sive dominium, cum secundum legem domini insecuritas in tali elemosina sit meritoria et ad non confidendum in temporalibus sed in vita et spe ad beatitudinem inductiva? Numquid credimus quod tales per castra sua caynitica possunt subvertere legem dei et facere hic manentem civitatem et prosperitatem mundanam premium laboris et mercedem ultimam viatoris? Sed arguitur quod hoc officium pertineat sacerdoti quia Math. 6° et 8° legitur quo modo Christus fecit suos apostolos de panibus et piscibus quos miraculose multiplicaverat populo ministrare; ergo licet nostris episcopis habere ultra vite necessaria ad pauperibus parciendum. Sed hic concedi debet conclusio cum episcopi debent esse hospitales secundum apostolum. Sed iste locus simealis a simili videtur pocius concludere quod ipsi debent in persona propria ministrare egentibus regis elemosinas eis datas. Sed ex isto non sequitur quod thesaurisare debent vel perdere regis elemosinas pauperibus parciendas. Nec sequitur ex isto quod possunt per diem vel septimanam et multo magis per centum annos elemosinas totidem domini conservare. Et claret in facto quod nec manibus propriis ministrant egenis fideliter ista bona, nec per ministros ipsa faciunt fideliter ministrare, quia deus scit si voluntati sue eterne sit contrarium ut sic fiat. Ideo argucia ista est similis illi qua concluditur, quod episcopi debent pati Christum vel papam lavare sibi pedes, quia apostoli cum domino sic fecerunt. Si [p. 66] enim ipsi debent sic facere, tunc vel papa peccat non veniendo sic facere hoc ministerium vel episcopi peccant non veniendo ad ipsum ut hoc debitum ministerium compleatur. Et sic concedi debet quod stat servari dominium in manu principalis domini secularis et genus sacerdotum per quantumcunque tempus racionabile percipere puros fructus, quia hoc verificaretur ipsis percipientibus fructus quantumcunque modicos vel per horam. Sed quod debent per totidem annos fructus percipere, ut iam de facto percipiunt, non est evidencia asinina. Et sic concedi debet conclusio illata ulterius, scilicet quod episcopi possunt vivere continue in paupertate ewangelica et pauperibus distribuere fideliter quod superest de elemosinis sibi datis, quia a probabili sic fecerunt apostoli, qui erant episcopi, et multi alii episcopi in illo centenario in quo vixerunt exproprietarie ante dotacionem ecclesie. Sed quid ista conclusio ad iustificandum dotacionem vel elemosinam cleri a tempore Silvestri continue decurrentem?

TRUTH. Again, I suppose that the [clergy]{g:clerus}, out of desire for these [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, should be willing to give up the [endowment]{g:dotacio} and to receive these goods under the form of perpetual [alms]{g:elemosina}, and so the same comes back to the clerics as regards the fruits. But this is not evident to many, since then [the clergy] would fall from the [title]{g:titulus} which Silvester accepted from Caesar.[^1] Nor would the truth of perpetual alms be preserved in England, since [the clergy] could not deny that, because of the many and great sins it had committed, it often fell from the title of alms, since it is not alms to nourish apostates in such a way.[^2] Indeed, it is not taught, or evidently shown, that such a donation was ever alms conformed to the divine will; how then would they feign that it is pure and perpetual alms? Do we believe that it is in the power of these men to perpetuate [64] such alms of their clergy, as if they said against Christ: "I will that it be alms however they may have lived and been contrary to thy law"; or: "I will that it [the clergy] live profitably to the edification of Christ's [church]{g:ecclesia}, because undoubtedly God so wills; therefore my volition is fulfilled"? Stephen indeed, in Acts 7, denied this [inference]{g:consequencia} when he said that the Jews, especially the [religious]{g:religiosi} and the [bishops]{g:episcopus}, often resisted the divine will.[^3] What colour is there, then, [for saying] that those who so [enriched]{g:ditare} the church did well, in that they had such a will? For God, notwithstanding such a blameless will, takes away what was conferred on these clerics when they so transgress. Why then ought not the [lords]{g:dominus} so [to take it away],[^4] conforming themselves to God's good pleasure, since in this there lies no evident danger but conformity to [God's law]{g:lex-dei}? Because there is no doubt that the clergy, in the apostles and Christ's disciples, lived more securely and more perfectly by lacking such long-lasting alms than our clergy lives or has lived. What spiritual fruit, then, or what miraculous life would give [evidence]{g:evidencia} for continuing such alms so begun? And the defect of the following [quibble]{g:argucie} is clear, when it is argued that perpetual alms granted by forebears could be kept in this way by the generation succeeding by God's [ordinance]{g:ordinacio}. For it is not taught that these are perpetual alms founded by the forebears, nor that those [forebears] are thereby exempt from perpetual damnation, nor that it would be meritorious for their generation to keep the said alms[^5] for a little while in this form. Nor do I see evidence in this, except that these lords in this exceed and turn aside from the rule of the Lord Jesus Christ. Since therefore in such matters the safer part is to be held, it seems that it would be safer to restrict such [a clergy][^6] to evangelical poverty and not to exceed the limits of Jesus Christ. And it could be granted that secular lords should keep and collect for the clergy's uses all these [revenues]{g:reditus} or fruits, and [65] spend the remainder of their own revenues on this servile collection. But it does not [follow]{g:sequitur} that it would then be well, because such servitude to these clerics is not exemplified in the Lord's law. And the clerics themselves, for the zeal they have for secular [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio}, would not accept such [service]{g:servicium} from [seculars]{g:secularis}. But it is evidently inferred that bishops, or those who faithfully preach the gospel, would assign deacons to minister moderate alms. But what has this to do with perpetuation or [lordship]{g:dominium}, since according to the Lord's law insecurity in such alms is meritorious, and leads [men] not to trust in temporal goods but in life and in hope of blessedness?[^7] Do we believe that such men, by their [Caim's castles]{g:castra-caynitica}, can subvert God's law and make here a lasting city, and make worldly prosperity the prize of labour and the final wage of the [wayfarer]{g:viator}? But it is argued that this office pertains to the [priest]{g:sacerdos}, because in Matt. 6 and 8[^8] we read how Christ made his apostles minister to the people from the loaves and fishes which he had miraculously multiplied; therefore it is lawful for our bishops to have beyond the necessaries of life for sharing out to the poor. But here the conclusion must be granted, since bishops ought to be hospitable according to the apostle. But this apish [topic from likeness]{g:locus-a-simili} seems rather to [infer]{g:concludere} that they ought in their own person to minister to the needy the king's alms given to them. But from this it does not follow that they ought to hoard or waste the king's alms that are to be shared out to the poor. Nor does it follow from this that they can keep so many of the lord's alms[^9] for a day or a week, and much less for a hundred years. And it is plain in fact that they neither minister these goods faithfully to the needy with their own hands, nor have them faithfully ministered by ministers; for God knows whether it is contrary to his eternal will that it be done so. Therefore this quibble is like the one by which it is inferred that bishops ought to suffer Christ or the [pope]{g:papa} to wash their feet, because the apostles did so with the Lord. [66] For if[^10] they ought to do so, then either the pope sins by not coming to perform this ministry thus, or the bishops sin by not coming to him so that this due ministry may be fulfilled. And so it must be granted that it is consistent[^11] that lordship be kept in the hand of the principal secular lord and that the race of priests receive the mere fruits for any reasonable length of time whatever, because this would be made true by their receiving fruits however small, or for an hour. But that they ought to receive fruits for so many years, as they now in fact receive them, there is no evidence, [not even] an asinine one.[^12] And so the conclusion inferred further must be granted, namely that bishops can live continually in evangelical poverty and faithfully distribute to the poor what remains over of the alms given them, because in all probability the apostles, who were bishops, did so, and many other bishops in that century[^13] in which they lived without property before the endowment of the church. But what has this conclusion to do with justifying the endowment or alms of the clergy running on continuously from the time of Silvester?

## Truth · 66.18–69.18

> [p. 66] Sed quia quidam qui videntur esse aliquid multum ponderant hanc evidenciam sicut sepe verum innuit, ideo aliqui necessitantur particulatim ipsum dissolvere seriose. Assumitur ergo in evidencia cum clerici possent vivere continue in paupertate ewangelica et pauperibus fideliter distribuere quod superest de elemosinis sibi datis, quare non licet dominis sic dare suas elemosinas, exonerando se et suos posteros, et ex parte altera licet clericis prudenter distribuere has elemosinas sicut apostoli et dyaconi perfecerunt ut patet Actuum vi°? Hic dicitur quod hoc ideo quia clerus debet peccandi occasionem tollere et dare evidenciam aliis ad peccata mundialia fugiendum et omnino capere exemplum a Christo et suis apostolis ad ipsos in vita et moribus et pauperie imitandum. Ille enim fuit finis, quare Christus vixit vitam tam pauperem [p. 67] et penalem; iuxta hanc fidem prima Petri secundo Christus passus est pro nobis, nobis relinquens exemplum ut sequamur vestigia eius. Si ergo tam patens evidencia et salubris evidet clero ut sequatur vestigia antichristi, potest Christum deserere et huius dyaboli vestigia imitari. Immo iuxta istam stulticiam clerus qui vovit virginitatem potest cottidie dormire cum femina et cum hoc ab incontinencia per dei graciam preservari, quare ergo non licet clero sic facere ad meritum augmentandum. Ulterius dicitur quod superiores clerici non debent continue cum distribucionibus huiusmodi occupari ut patet ex eleccione apostolorum, qui deserentes istud officium elegerunt septem dyaconos Actuum vi°. Et Stephanus propter eius inperfeccionem officium deseruit. Que ergo religio antichristi obligaret papam, episcopos et superiores ecclesie ad tales corporales elemosinas parciendum cum spiritualis elemosina infinitum melior, scilicet ewangelii predicacio, sit a Christo et suis apostolis exemplata? Domini ergo superhabundantes diviciis possunt per fidelem subministracionem sibi subserviencium subtiliare redditus suorum pauperum tenencium, vel alios fideles laycos ad istud officium limitare, licet non onerent ad istud abiectum officium sacerdotes, cum patet experiencia satis certa, quod ipsi sacerdotes infra tempus satis modicum peccabiliter et infideliter ministrarent. Quis ergo episcopus monachus vel alius dotatus clericus staret in isto officio cum superbia cupidine vel gulosa libidine propter purum premium pauperibus ministrandi? Et sic nostri clerici renuerunt indubie formam dyaconorum satis similem in hac parte. Nec pertinet superioribus sacerdotibus officium istud tam abiectum facere et taliter cum ipso se et suos posteros desponsare. Et quantum ad consequentem arguciam quod apostoli collegerunt fragmenta ut dicitur Math. xiiii. et xv. quare ergo non licet episcopis [p. 68] nostris sic facere, evidencia ista plena est dolositate, quia hoc factum apostolorum non arguit quod papa vel episcopi debent taliter dominari, sed quod possunt in facto laudabili fragmenta ad pascendum pauperes humiliter congregare, nec scio ex ewangelio docere quod apostoli distribuebant fragmenta ista pauperibus, sed potuerunt, si Christus voluerit, post istam collectionem limitare egenis ut ipsa tollerent, et apostoli intenderunt interim ministerio digniori. Domini ergo seculares debent exonerare clericos ab isto vili officio et ipsos ad ewangelium, oracionem vel exercicium aliud salubrius secundum formam ewangelii limitare, et subtilient, ut supra dictum est, redditus in suis pauperibus per ministros, et non sic extorqueant peccuniam de suis egenis tenentibus ad conventum dyaboli sic ditandum. Lapsus ergo infidelis cleri in hoc ministerio daret occasionem Christianis dominis ad hos redditus confiscandum. Cum rex Anglie cum cleri repugnancia dicitur confiscasse bona episcopi quia episcopus ille noluit secundum formam ewangelii suum curatum officium regis dimittere et in sua ecclesia residere. Et re vera videtur multis quod ista vecors taciturnitas in tantum periculum regis et regni foret causa sufficiens ad confiscandum omnes redditus huiusmodi clericorum. Rex vero debet dominanter secundum formam ewangelii imitacionem domini practisare, et clerus dotatus potest, sicud sciverit, istud opus regis ex racione vel ewangelio inpugnare. Videat ergo fidelis theologus, quod clerus cupidus non deficiat in argumentis ex forma ewangelii fabricandis, et fundet se in completa similitudine, capiendo ut fidem quod Christus ab instanti sue concepcionis usque ad horam sue mortis fuit homo pauperrimus ad pauperiem suis ovibus exemplandam, et ista exemplacio debet esse preciosior quam decreta omnium paparum, eciam Iohannis 22ⁱ, nisi in fide ewangelii sint fundata. Et pauperies in [p. 69] vita apostolica est plus a fidelibus attendenda quam omnes vite episcoporum divitum vel cleri alterius, licet sompniati fuerint nunc in beatitudine residere: fides enim docet, quod apostoli pauperes sunt beati, sed non docet illud de istis divitibus, sed pocius docet practice quod canonisaciones istorum care pro pecunia erant empte. Iudas enim, quem Christus vocat filium perdicionis, adhuc in fine vite sue proiecit argenteos et sic, licet desperavit, abiit satis pauper; fratres autem videntur superare Scarioth, eo quod incollunt manentem civitatem, ac si de celesti Ierusalem desperarent, et sic vocatas elemosinas statim inpossibilitant ut iuvent pauperes vel quantumcunque fratres derelinquerant ab elemosinantibus revocentur, cum preter exemplacionem Christi faciunt sibi basilicas sumptuosas et procurant ypocritice quod maiores domini et domine in eorum basilica sint humati, ut vel sic perpetuacio habitacionis loci sui in terris per istorum sepultorum progeniem defendantur. Nec dubium quin melius foret hiis mortuis in campo inter bestias sepeliri.

[66] But because certain men who seem to be something set great weight on this evidence, as it often hints at the truth, therefore some are compelled to take it apart point by point, in order. It is assumed, then, in the evidence: since clerics could live continually in evangelical poverty and faithfully distribute to the poor what remains over of the alms given them, why is it not lawful for lords so to give their alms, discharging themselves and their posterity? And on the other side, it is lawful for clerics to distribute these alms prudently, as the apostles and deacons carried it out, as is clear in Acts 6. Here it is said that this is so for this reason: because the clergy ought to remove the occasion of sinning, and to give others evidence for fleeing worldly sins, and altogether to take example from Christ and his apostles, so as to imitate them in life and morals and poverty. For that was the end for which Christ lived so poor [67] and hard a life; according to this faith, 1 Pet. 2:21: "Christ suffered for us, leaving us an example that we should follow his steps."[^14] If therefore such plain and wholesome evidence makes it evident[^15] to the clergy that it should follow the steps of antichrist, it may desert Christ and imitate the steps of this devil. Indeed, according to this folly, the clergy that has vowed virginity can sleep every day with a woman and at the same time be preserved from incontinence by God's grace; why then is it not lawful for the clergy to do so, to increase merit? Further, it is said that the higher clerics ought not to be continually occupied with distributions of this kind, as is clear from the election made by the apostles, who, leaving this office, chose seven deacons (Acts 6:2–4). And Stephen left the office because of its imperfection.[^16] What religion of antichrist, then, would bind the pope, the bishops and the higher [clergy][^17] of the church to share out such bodily alms, since spiritual alms, infinitely better, namely the preaching of the gospel, has been set as an example by Christ and his apostles? The lords, therefore, who superabound in riches can, through the faithful ministration of those who serve under them, lighten[^18] the revenues of their poor tenants, or assign other faithful laymen to this office, though they should not burden priests with this abject office. For it is clear by experience certain enough that the priests themselves would, within a fairly short time, minister sinfully and unfaithfully. What bishop, then, or monk or other endowed cleric would stand in this office, with pride, covetousness or gluttonous lust, for the mere reward of ministering to the poor? And so our clerics have undoubtedly refused the form of the deacons, which is similar enough in this respect. Nor does it pertain to the higher priests to do this so abject office, and in such wise to wed themselves and their posterity to it. And as to the following quibble, that the apostles gathered the fragments, as is said in Matt. 14:20 and 15:37, "why then is it not lawful for our [68] bishops to do so?": this evidence is full of guile. For this deed of the apostles does not show that the pope or bishops ought so to exercise lordship, but that they can, in a praiseworthy deed, humbly gather fragments to feed the poor. Nor do I know how to teach from the gospel that the apostles distributed these fragments to the poor; but they could, if Christ willed, after this collection assign them to the needy to take away, and the apostles meanwhile attended to a worthier ministry. The secular lords, therefore, ought to discharge clerics from this vile office and assign them to the gospel, to prayer, or to other more wholesome exercise according to the form of the gospel. And let them lighten, as was said above, the revenues [taken] from their poor, through ministers, and not so extort money from their needy tenants to enrich the devil's convent in this way. The lapse, then, of the [faithless]{g:infidelis}[^19] clergy in this ministry would give Christian lords occasion to confiscate these revenues, since the king of England, against the resistance of the clergy, is said to have confiscated the goods of a bishop because that bishop would not, according to the form of the gospel, give up his office as the king's [curate]{g:curatus} and reside in his church.[^20] And in truth it seems to many that this faint-hearted silence, to so great a danger of the king and the [kingdom]{g:regnum}, would be sufficient cause for confiscating all the revenues of such clerics. But the king ought, with a lord's authority, to [put into practice]{g:practizare} the imitation of the Lord according to the form of the gospel, and the endowed clergy can, if it knows how, attack this work of the king from reason or the gospel. Let the faithful theologian therefore see that the covetous clergy does not fall short in the arguments to be fashioned from the form of the gospel, and let him ground himself in a complete likeness.[^21] Let him take as faith that Christ, from the instant of his conception to the hour of his death, was the poorest of men, to set an example of poverty to his sheep. And this example ought to be more precious than the decrees of all the popes, even of John XXII,[^22] unless they are founded in the faith of the gospel. And poverty in [69] the apostolic life is more to be heeded by the faithful than all the lives of rich bishops or of other clergy, although it has been dreamed that they now dwell in blessedness. For faith teaches that the poor apostles are blessed, but it does not teach that of these rich men; but rather it teaches in practice that the [canonizations]{g:canonizare} of these men were dearly bought for money. For Judas, whom Christ calls the son of perdition, still at the end of his life cast down the pieces of silver, and so, although he despaired, departed poor enough. But the [friars]{g:frater} seem to surpass Scarioth, in that they inhabit a lasting city,[^23] as if they despaired of the heavenly Jerusalem. And so they straightway make it impossible for the alms so called either to help the poor or, however much the friars have left [of them],[^24] to be recalled by [those who gave the alms]{g:elemosinare}. For, beyond the example of Christ, they make themselves sumptuous churches, and hypocritically contrive that the greater lords and ladies be buried in their church, so that at least in this way the perpetuation of the dwelling of their place on earth may be defended[^25] by the offspring of those buried. Nor is there doubt that it would be better for these dead to be buried in the field among the beasts.

## Pollard's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 63.13: Supposing the clergy ready to exchange endowments for a perpetual alms, they would lose their title, and yet the alms would be no true alms.
- 63.24: Have men the power of investing any donation they please with the character of alms?
- 64.7: Despite the good intention of the donor, God takes away alms from priests when they sin, and secular lords should conform to His decree.
- 64.17: The argument that perpetual alms can be maintained by renewals from successive heirs is bad, for (i) these are not perpetual alms, (ii) they have not the merit ascribed to such alms, (iii) the heirs would do no good work in renewing them.
- 64.30: Granting that temporal lords would be willing to act as rent-gatherers for the clergy, they would be acting unscripturally, and the clergy would refuse their services.
- 65.8: The essence of dependence on alms is that it should be precarious.
- 65.12: Are we to believe that by their 'Caim's Castles' they can set up an abiding city here?
- 65.16: It is argued that as the Apostles distributed Christ's loaves and fishes, so Bishops should have superfluities to give to the poor.
- 65.20: But by this text bishops should personally distribute the king's alms, not hoard or squander them. It certainly does not sanction the retention of alms for centuries.
- 65.28: As a fact they minister to the needy neither themselves nor by others.
- 65.31: Compare the argument that Bishops should have their feet washed by the Pope.
- 66.1: Which convicts either the Pope or Bishops of sin in disobedience.
- 66.4: We must allow that the clergy may receive mere fruits, if given in moderation, and for the needs of the moment.
- 66.8: But not continuously, as at present.
- 66.10: We allow further that bishops living in poverty may distribute of their superfluity.
- 66.15: But this does not justify endowments held since the time of Silvester.
- 66.22: It is argued, why may not lords compound for their own and their descendants' alms, and why may not the clergy distribute, as did the apostles and deacons?
- 66.28: The answer is that the clergy should avoid all occasion of sin, and follow Christ.
- 67.5: If their risking temptation is meritorious, why should they not do so as to purity?
- 67.11: The apostles left the distribution of alms to the deacons.
- 67.14: Why then should the heads of the Church distribute temporal alms instead of spiritual?
- 67.18: Lords should find their own almoners, not burden the clergy and tempt them to sin.
- 68.2: That the apostles gathered up the fragments cannot justify clerical lordship: I do not even know that they themselves distributed the fragments.
- 68.9: Let lords leave clergy to their proper work, and not rack tenants to endow a convent of the devil.
- 68.14: The unfaithful ministry of the clergy would justify the confiscation of their revenues, according to the precedent of an English king.
- 68.22: The king should obey Christ, and confiscate their goods, and the clergy show reason against it, if they can.
- 68.28: The example of Christ's poverty must outweigh the decrees of all the popes, even of John XXII.
- 69.1: It outweighs, too, the example of saints, whose canonisations were probably purchased.
- 69.11: The friars squander the alms given them for the poor on sumptuous buildings, and get great lords to be buried in them, so as to gain the protection of their descendants.

## Notes

[^1]: **R.** The title Silvester accepted from Caesar is the endowment conferred on Pope Silvester I by the emperor Constantine, as told in the Donation of Constantine. This is Wyclif's usual starting point for the church's endowment (cf. 66.16–17) (LEDGER D30-1).

[^2]: **C/T.** *Cum defendere non posset quin … cadebat*: "since it could not deny that … it fell". The subject is the clergy (*clerus*), which is also the subject of *perpetraverat*. Apparatus: B and C omit *propter* (63.19), which B¹ has added in the margin by another hand. For *taliter apostatas* (63.21), "apostates in such a way", B (corrected) and C read *tales apostatas*, "such apostates".

[^3]: **S.** Acts 7:51, "you always resist the Holy Ghost" (DR), paraphrased. For *sepe*, "often", C reads *semper*, "always", as the Vulgate does. B and C read *et specialiter*, "and especially".

[^4]: **C/T.** *Cum habuerunt huiusmodi voluntatem* (64.8) is taken as causal ("in that they had"), since the donors' blameless will is the ground alleged. "Although they had" is possible. *Debent sic* (64.11) has no infinitive. Beside it, a later hand in B's margin supplies *aufferre*, "take away", and that sense is supplied here. In B¹ the *non* is itself a marginal addition by another hand.

[^5]: **T.** 64.24 *elemosinam*: the scans' text read *elemosmam*; the plate has *elemosinam* (Transcription notes).

[^6]: **C.** *Talem* (64.29) is taken with *clerum* understood. It could instead be *talem elemosinam*: "to restrict such alms to evangelical poverty".

[^7]: **C.** *Inductiva* is taken as nominative, agreeing with *insecuritas* and governing *ad non confidendum*, with *ad beatitudinem* taken with *spe* ("hope of blessedness"). If *inductiva* is ablative with *vita et spe*, the clause reads "… but in a life and hope that lead to blessedness".

[^8]: **T/S.** *Math.* 6° et 8° is so printed and is not emended. The two feedings in which Christ gives the loaves to the disciples to set before the people are at Mark 6:41 and 8:6. Matthew's are at 14:19 and 15:36, cited as Matt. 14 and 15 at 67.32. "The apostle" (65.20) refers to 1 Tim. 3:2 and Titus 1:8, "given to hospitality" (DR).

[^9]: **C.** *Regis elemosinas* (65.22), "the king's alms": in the figure these are Christ's loaves, and in the application the goods given to bishops. *Elemosinas totidem domini* (65.26) is taken as "so many of the lord's alms". *Totidem* may instead be elliptical, "for as many [years]", as with *per totidem annos* at 66.8. *Deus scit si* (65.29), "God knows whether", is probably an asseveration: God knows how contrary it is.

[^10]: **T.** 65.33 *Si*: lost at the page turn in the scans and restored from the plate (Transcription notes). For *ipsi* in the next clause, B reads *episcopi*.

[^11]: **C.** *Stat* is the scholastic "it is consistent (possible) that". *Puros fructus* means the mere fruits without the lordship, which stays with the secular lord. *Hoc verificaretur*: the proposition would be made true by any receipt of fruits, however small or brief.

[^12]: **C.** *Non est evidencia asinina* (66.10) is confirmed on the plate and literally means "is not asinine evidence". It is taken here as "there is no evidence, not even an ass's". A word such as *nisi* ("no evidence but an asinine one") may have dropped out; nothing is supplied in the Latin.

[^13]: **T.** For *in illo centenario*, "in that century", B and C read *tercentenario*, "in those three hundred years", the span before Silvester. The print is translated.

[^14]: **S.** 1 Pet. 2:21. The Vulgate reads *vobis relinquens exemplum ut sequamini*, "leaving you an example that you should follow his steps" (DR). Wyclif's *nobis … sequamur* ("us … we") is translated.

[^15]: **T/C.** *Evidet* (67.3) is confirmed on the plate. It is apparently formed from *evidens* and is taken as "makes it evident". The clause is ironic, turning the opponents' "evidence" (66.18–26) against them.

[^16]: **C.** *Propter eius inperfeccionem* is taken as "its" (the office's); "his" is grammatically possible. Stephen, one of the seven (Acts 6:5), is next shown preaching and disputing (Acts 6:8–10). Apparatus: for *superiores clerici* (67.9) B¹ reads *pauperiores*.

[^17]: **T.** 67.14–15 *superiores*: the scans' text was broken (*su" periores*); the plate has *superiores* (Transcription notes). The word is rendered "the higher [clergy]" to keep it apart from *prepositi*, "superiors".

[^18]: **C.** The sense of *subtiliare redditus* (here and 68.11, *subtilient*) is doubtful (GLOSSARY, provisional). It is rendered "lighten", i.e. reduce what is exacted from poor tenants. The words that follow at 68.12–13 (*non sic extorqueant peccuniam de suis egenis tenentibus*) support this. The alternative is "refine", i.e. manage the revenues carefully for the poor through almoners, as Pollard's side-note at 67.18 takes it. *Licet non onerent* is taken as "though they should not burden".

[^19]: **G.** *Infidelis cleri*: the adjective describes the clergy's unfaithful ministry (cf. *infideliter ministrarent*, 67.22). It is therefore rendered "faithless", not "unbelieving".

[^20]: **R/C.** The incident is not identified here; Pollard's side-note says only "the precedent of an English king" (LEDGER D30-2). *Cum rex …* has no main clause of its own and is joined to the preceding sentence. The construal of *suum curatum officium regis* is uncertain. It is taken as an office held in the king's service, which the bishop would not give up in order to reside.

[^21]: **C.** *Videat … quod clerus cupidus non deficiat in argumentis* is taken to mean that the theologian should see that the covetous clergy is met on the gospel's own ground; the sense is uncertain. *Fundet se* is taken as jussive, with the theologian as subject. It could instead depend on *quod*, with the clergy as subject ("and that it ground itself"). *Completa similitudo* answers the "apish topic from likeness" of 65.21: here the likeness is carried through, to Christ's poverty.

[^22]: **R.** John XXII's decrees on the poverty of Christ, chiefly *Cum inter nonnullos* (1323), which declared heretical the assertion that Christ and the apostles had nothing either individually or in common (LEDGER D30-3).

[^23]: **S.** Judas is called "the son of perdition" at John 17:12; Matt. 27:5 reads "casting down the pieces of silver in the temple, he departed" (DR). The "lasting city" (here and at 65.13) is from Heb. 13:14, "For we have not here a lasting city, but we seek one that is to come" (DR). At 69.9 B and C add *hic*, "here", which is the Vulgate's word.

[^24]: **T/C.** *Derelinquerant* (69.12) is taken as "had left (over)", with *quantumcunque* as its object. The apparatus gives *deliquerant*, "however much the friars had transgressed", under the siglum printed *b* in the OCR. The print is translated. At 69.14 a stray *is* in the scans has been removed on the plate's authority (Transcription notes).

[^25]: **T.** The plural *defendantur* has the singular *perpetuacio* as its subject; B and C read *defendatur*. The sense is the same either way.
