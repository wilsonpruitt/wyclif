# *De eucharistia*, chapter 3, part 6: Hugh's words drawn to the ancient teaching

## ¶1 · 73.19–75.4

> [p. 73] Quantum ad glosas et opiniones modernorum doctorum in ista materia, patet quod ut plurimum sunt frivole; ipsi enim vix corticem verborum sanctorum suorum priorum conceperant; et ideo deficiente intelligencia hii negant et imponunt erronee sanctis stulticiam et hii ad ostensionem maioris stulticie fingunt eis glosas nimis extraneas, ut patet de glosa Doctoris Communis ad dicta Augustini de potenciis anime, quomodo memoria, racio et voluntas non sunt essencia anime inseparabiles qualitates, Augustinus [p. 74] autem ponit in utroque oppositum. Sed movet me dictum illius magni doctoris et sciencioris modernis Hugonis, IIº De Sacramentis, Parte VIIIª in cuius IXº capitulo ita scribit: Per verba sanctificacionis vera panis et vini substancia in verum corpus Christi et sanguinem convertitur, sola specie panis et vini remanente et substancia in substanciam transeunte. Conversio autem ipsa non secundum unionem sed secundum transicionem credenda est: quoniam nequaquam essencia in augmentum essencie accidit ut per id quod accedit id ad quod accedit cum illo ad quod accedit unum fiat. Nec sic in pane corpus Christi consecrari dicimus, ut de pane corpus Christi esse accipiat, nec quasi novum corpus subito factum de mutata essencia, sed in ipsum corpus verum mutatam essenciam; nec ipsam substanciam panis et vini in nichilum redactam, quia desiit esse quod fuit, sed mutatam pocius, quia incepit esse aliud quod non fuit et ipsum quod cepit esse ex ea esse non accipiat, quia panis fuit, sed ipsa eius esse accepit quando desiit esse quod fuit. Hoc expressius distinximus propter eos qui ex sua racione fidei preiudicium faciunt et sensu suo incedentes asserere nituntur vel hoc esse solum quod cernitur vel tale esse quod creditur; hoc est, quia sola panis et vini species cernitur solam ibi esse panis et vini substanciam vel quia ibi substancia corporis et sanguinis esse [p. 75] creditur possibiliter ei inesse speciem et qualitatem panis et vini que cernitur quasi non possit species apparere cuius non assit substancia vel substancia latere cuius non appareat forma.

As to the [glosses]{g:glosa} and [opinions]{g:opinio} of [the modern doctors]{g:moderni-doctores} in this matter, it is clear that [for the most part]{g:ut-plurimum} they are frivolous; for they had scarcely grasped the bark of the words of their own earlier [saints]{g:sancti}; and therefore, understanding failing them, some deny and erroneously impute folly to the saints, and others, to the display of greater folly, feign for them glosses [exceedingly]{g:nimis} strange; as is clear of the gloss of [the Common Doctor]{g:doctor-communis} on Augustine's sayings about the powers of the soul, how memory, reason and will are not the [essence]{g:essencia} of the soul [but] inseparable qualities,[^1] [74] but Augustine posits the opposite on both points. But I am moved by the saying of that great doctor, more knowing than the moderns, Hugh, in *De sacramentis* II, part 8, in whose ninth chapter he writes thus:[^2] "Through the words of sanctification the true [substance]{g:substancia} of [bread and wine]{g:panis} is converted into the true body of Christ and blood, the [species]{g:species} of bread and wine alone remaining, and substance passing into substance. And the [conversion]{g:conversio} itself is to be believed to be not by [union]{g:unio} but by transition: since in no way does essence come to the increase of essence so that, through that which accedes, that to which it accedes becomes one with that to which it accedes.[^3] Nor do we say that the body of Christ is consecrated in the bread in such a way that [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} receives its [being]{g:esse} from the bread, nor as if a new body were suddenly made from the changed essence, but [we say that] the changed essence [passes] into the true body itself; nor [do we say] that the substance itself of bread and wine is reduced to nothing, because it has ceased to be what it was, but rather that it is changed, because it has begun to be another thing which it was not; and that which it began to be does not receive being from it, because it was bread, but it itself received its being when it ceased to be what it was. This we have distinguished more expressly on account of those who out of their own reason prejudice the faith, and, going by their own sense, strive to assert either that this alone is which is seen, or that what is believed is such [as is seen]; that is: because only the species of bread and wine is seen, [they assert] that only the substance of bread and wine is there; or, because the substance of the body and blood is believed to be there, [75] [they assert] that the species and quality of bread and wine which is seen can possibly be in it; as if a species could not appear whose substance is not present, or a substance lie hidden whose form does not appear."[^4]

## ¶2 · 75.5–75.26

> [p. 75] Ista verba doctoris incusserunt michi formidinem nec video quin doctor in illis intelligit quod fidelis suscitet intellectum ad credendum quod solum corpus Christi sit post consecracionem ibi principaliter pane et vino in principalitate illa corpori Christi cedentibus, unde propter amissionem illius principalitatis dicit nec panem nec vinum remanere sed in corpus Christi converti. Accidencia autem habent ibi principalem existenciam ad expergefaciendum sensus fidelium sine subiecto suo ibi principaliter existente; verumtamen ipsa informant panem et vinum, non autem corpus Christi vel sanguinem, sed illa informacio propter presencialitatem dignioris corporis est sopita, sicut Petro tenente in domo principalitatem dominii post successionem Christi, in eadem domo cui Petrus omnino succedit in servum nomen Petri tacetur et nomen Christi in hiis que sunt dignitatis vel principalitatis ibi erigitur. Sic est quodammodo de corpore Christi et pane qui post conversionem, licet remaneat secundum essenciam, tamen esse principalitatis ab eo tollitur, cum remanet secundarie serviens superiori corpori ut figura.

These words of the doctor struck [misgiving]{g:formido} into me; nor do I see but that the doctor means in them that the faithful man should rouse his understanding to believe that the body of Christ alone is there [principally]{g:principaliter} after the consecration,[^5] the bread and wine [giving way]{g:cedere} to the body of Christ in that principality;[^6] hence, because of the loss of that principality, he says that neither bread nor wine remains, but [that it is] converted into the body of Christ. And the [accidents]{g:accidens} have there a principal existence, to awaken the senses of the faithful, without their subject existing there principally; nevertheless they [inform]{g:informare} the bread and wine, but not the body or blood of Christ; but that informing is lulled asleep because of the presence of the worthier body. So, when Peter holds the principality of [lordship]{g:dominium} in a house after succeeding Christ, in the same house, in which Peter wholly passes into [the place of] a servant,[^7] the name of Peter is silent, and the name of Christ is raised up there in the things that belong to dignity or principality. So it is in a certain way with the body of Christ and the bread, which after the conversion, although it remains as to its essence, yet has the being of principality taken from it, since it remains, secondarily, serving the superior body as a [figure]{g:figura}.

## ¶3 · 75.27–76.5

> [p. 75] Et possunt dicta verba doctoris trahi ad istam sentenciam, et per hoc potest intelligi dicta conversio vel transsubstanciacio et ecclesia novella concordari cum ecclesia antiqua, et potest labor in ista equivocacione [p. 76] colorari ex modo loquendi quo servus recipiens dominum in domum suam inferiorat se quantum sufficit, dando sibi in domo totum nomen honoris vel reverencie ac si ipse foret in huiusmodi anullatus.

And the said words of the doctor can be drawn to this [teaching]{g:sentencia},[^8] and by this the said conversion or [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio} can be understood, and [the newer church]{g:novella-ecclesia} brought into concord with [the ancient church]{g:antiqua-ecclesia}; and the labour in this [equivocation]{g:equivocacio} [76] can be [given colour]{g:colorare} from the manner of speaking by which a servant, receiving his lord into his house, lowers himself as far as suffices, giving him in the house the whole name of honour or reverence, as if he himself were annulled in such [matters].

## ¶4 · 76.6–76.18

> [p. 76] Et iterum, attribuimus in aliis terminacionem actus corpori principali, accessorio dimisso, ut dicimus nos emere vel vendere equum vel asinum et non materiam eius vel formam, sicut legiste dicunt se non excommunicare propter pecuniam sed propter inobedienciam consumatam; et cadit utrimque ex equivocacione illusio, potest tamen modus loquendi fundari ex illo Joh. IVº, 42: Mulieri dicebant quia iam non propter tuam loquelam credimus, ipsi enim audivimus et scimus quia hic est vere salvator mundi, ubi oportet intelligere negacionem super principalitatem muliebris evidencie; et tales sunt multi modi loquendi in scriptura.

And again, in other things we attribute the terminus of an act to the principal body, the accessory being set aside: as we say that we buy or sell a horse or an ass, and not its matter or its form; just as the [legists]{g:legiste} say that they excommunicate not on account of money but on account of consummated disobedience;[^9] and on both sides [illusion]{g:illusio} falls out of equivocation. Yet the manner of speaking can be grounded on that of John 4:42: "They said to the woman: We now believe, not for thy saying: for we ourselves have heard him and know that this is indeed the Saviour of the world";[^10] where one must understand the negation [as falling] on the principality of the woman's [evidence]{g:evidencia}; and such are many manners of speaking in scripture.

## ¶5 · 76.19–77.28

> [p. 76] Sic ergo possunt concordari dicta gravium personarum ecclesie quod est melius quam invenire sensum et verba ab antiquis sanctis extranea, et ex illis ut in materia calculatoria ex falso fundamento deducere conclusiones et sentencias mundo mirabiles; que de facto sunt deliramenta fidelibus onerosa: ut adversarii concludunt in casu eis possibili quod iste homo fuit temporaliter generatus univoce et non ab animali aliquo, licet parentes eius conversantes cum eo in fide ipsum instruxerint; qui licet a Deo perpetue dampnabuntur, non possunt tamen peccare vel puniri in corpore aut anima, sicut non possunt in altero horum pati, posito quod ex duobus spiritibus [p. 77] qui possunt esse anime humane et omnibus accidentibus absolutis corporeis (que sunt in Petro et Martha) optime complexionatis constituantur duo supposita subducta in eis omni materiali substancia et quod ista que sunt A et B peccent dampnabiliter, procreando Paulum compositum ex corpore et anima, de quo Paulo verificant consequenciam; nam sicut A et B mediantibus aliquibus suis accidentibus corporeis possunt operari eque efficaciter et satis univoce, ut patet de operibus nutritivis et sensitivis: ita videtur de accionibus generacionis, et per consequens sicut A et B habent potenciam gignendi et aliter operandi secundum quascunque qualitates quas habent, sic possunt esse parentes et coniuges hominem procreantes. Et patet cum casu prima pars conclusionis; et secunda pars probatur per hoc quod nec A nec B est substancia animata sensibilis quia non corpus, ymmo pari evidencia qua foret substancia foret tam quantitas quam qualitas; et patet tercia pars ex casu cum tam A quam B potest exercere omnes operaciones tam organicas quam non organicas. Et patet quomodo tam virtus quam vicium potest inesse illis spiritibus, et sic tam A quam B potest dampnari perpetuo, cum habet libertatem flexibilitatis arbitrii, sicut ponitur de inesse. Ultima vero particula videtur per hoc, quod nec A nec B potest esse animatum sive corporeum, quia tunc foret aliene nature omnino a natura cuius est materia.

So, then, the sayings of weighty persons of the [church]{g:ecclesia} can be brought into concord; which is better than to invent a sense and words foreign to the ancient saints, and from them, as in a [calculatory]{g:calculatorius} matter,[^11] to deduce from a false foundation [conclusions]{g:conclusio} and teachings marvellous to the world, which in fact are [ravings]{g:deliramenta} burdensome to the faithful: as the adversaries [infer]{g:concludere}, in a case possible for them, that this man was generated in time, [univocally]{g:univoce}, and not from any animal, although his parents, [living]{g:conversari} with him in the faith, instructed him; who, although they will be perpetually damned by God, yet cannot sin or be punished in body or soul, just as they cannot suffer in either of these. [This is] supposing that from two spirits [77] which can be human souls, and all the [absolute]{g:accidens-absolutum} bodily accidents (which are in Peter and Martha) of the best [complexion]{g:complexio}, two [supposits]{g:suppositum} are constituted, all material substance in them being withdrawn, and that these, which are A and B, sin damnably by procreating Paul, composed of body and soul; and of this Paul they [make]{g:verificari} the [inference]{g:consequencia} true. For as A and B, by means of some of their bodily accidents, can operate equally effectively and univocally enough, as is clear of the works of nutrition and sense, so it seems of the actions of generation; and consequently, as A and B have the power of begetting and of otherwise operating according to whatever qualities they have, so they can be parents and spouses procreating a man. And the first part of the conclusion is clear with the case; and the second part is proved by this, that neither A nor B is an animate [sentient]{g:sensibilis} substance,[^12] because [it is] not a body; indeed, by the same [evidence]{g:evidencia} by which it would be a substance it would be as much a quantity as a quality; and the third part is clear from the case, since both A and B can exercise all operations, organic as well as non-organic. And it is clear how both virtue and vice can be in those spirits, and so both A and B can be damned perpetually, since each has the freedom of the flexibility of [choice]{g:libertas-arbitrii}, as is posited [as a matter of fact]{g:de-inesse}. And the last particle appears by this, that neither A nor B can be [animate]{g:animare} or bodily, since then it would be of a nature wholly foreign to the nature of which there is matter.[^13]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 73.19: The comments and opinions of modern doctors are frivolous.
- 74.1: The words of Hugo give us pause, since he seems to say that after consecration the substance is of the Body.
- 75.5: Perhaps he only means that the Body is the object on which our attention should be fixed.
- 76.6: Examples of such use of language.
- 76.19: It is better to strain the later doctors into agreement with the early church than to accept absurdities: e. g. that human beings may live and act without substance.

## Notes

[^1]: **S/T.** Loserth: *Scil. Thomae Aquinatis*. The gloss is on Augustine's memory, understanding and will (*De Trinitate* X); Thomas makes the powers distinct from the soul's essence, probably *ST* I q. 77 a. 1 ad 1 (LEDGER K03f-1, to be checked). The print *non sunt essencia anime inseparabiles qualitates* (73.27–28) lacks a connective; "[but]" is supplied.
[^2]: **S.** Hugh of St Victor, *De sacramentis* II p. 8 c. 9; Loserth's reference: ed. Mainz 1617, p. 463 (LEDGER K03f-2; the PL column to be checked).
[^3]: **T.** *Accidit* (74.10) is the print; the manuscripts read *accedit*, as the next clause does, rendered "comes". Loserth notes that Hugh's text reads *ut per id quod accedit, id ad quod accedit maius fiat*, "so that ... becomes greater"; the print's *cum illo ... unum fiat* is translated.
[^4]: **C.** Hugh's two errors: those who trust sight say only bread is there; those who trust faith say the visible species inhere in the body of Christ. *Tale esse quod creditur* is taken as "that what is believed is such as is seen".
[^5]: **C/G.** *Principaliter* here means "as the principal thing", the standing that the bread yields to the body; not the row *principaliter* "as a main head" (the compendium's divisions). "Principally" and "principality" are used for the family through the paragraph.
[^6]: **T.** *Cedentibus* (75.10) is Loserth's correction; the manuscripts read *credentibus*.
[^7]: **C.** *Cui Petrus omnino succedit in servum*: Peter, lord of the house after Christ, becomes wholly a servant there when Christ is present, and the name of honour passes to Christ. The comparison is the servant's courtesy of 75.30–76.5.
[^8]: **C.** *Sentencia*, CONTEXTUAL: here "teaching", the position just stated, to which Hugh's words can be drawn.
[^9]: **C.** The legists' formula: an excommunication for unpaid money is said to be for the contumacy, not the debt; the name falls on the principal thing, as in buying a horse.
[^10]: **S.** John 4:42, Douay. Wyclif omits the opening *Et*; *vere* is the Douay's "indeed" inside the quotation.
[^11]: **C.** *Materia calculatoria*: the matter of the calculators, the mathematical physics of the Oxford schools, where conclusions are deduced from an assumed case.
[^12]: **G.** *Sensibilis* in the definition of animal (*substancia animata sensibilis*) is "sentient", not the usual "perceptible".
[^13]: **C.** *Natura cuius est materia*: taken as the bodily nature, which has matter as a part; A and B, spirits with accidents only, would have to be animate without it. The construal is doubtful.
