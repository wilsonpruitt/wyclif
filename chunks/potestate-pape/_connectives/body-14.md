# sed/autem extract, wave 14: chunks 12-g 12-h 12-i 12-j 12-k 12-l

### 12-g ¶1 · 338.9–339.25  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 338] Ideo talis evangelizans predicaret vitam et doctrinam Christi, in qua non est periculum. Secundo exponeret quomodo sanctus, cui innititur non in lege quam Christus instituit, declinavit, tercio consideraret si modum ita laudabilem sicut fecerunt patroni, quibus innitimur, observamus, quia cum Deus remunerator est adverbiorum, modus est maxime attendendus; quarto vero consideraremus in vitis patrum, si patres nostri ordinis spissius vixerunt et sanccius, quando viventes exproprietarie et nobis contrarie secuti sunt facilius et similius salvatorem. Modicus enim error, qui est insensibilis ex pravitate peccati, est ex continuo incremento sensibile et intolerabile peccatum, a tota ecclesia destruendum, sicut specialiter patet in dotacione ecclesie, que a nuda elemosina crevit in venenosum dominium. Sic igitur duplex illud morale principium, practizatum in via morum, retrocedere est illicitum et de duobus bonis est pocius et facilius eligendum, rectificaret totam ecclesiam; ideo sine excusacione debemus omnes effectualiter illud attendere. **Sed** redeundo quantum ad secundam sentenciam de necessitate legum ecclesie, patet ex sepe dictis, quod melius regeretur ecclesia pura [p. 339] lege Domini quam per se vel cum adiectis legi domini legibus hominum adinventis. Patet ex hoc quod primo modo honori Dei conformius, ad serviendum sibi direccius et inducendum pacem ecclesie copiosius, regularetur ecclesia. Unde antichristiana heresis videtur quod lex Dei non est per se sufficiens nec eque sufficiens ut leges hominum extinguere iniurias in ecclesia germinantes, nam ad confundendum tales infidelitates de Antichristo dicitur Psalmo IX, 21: Constitue Domine legislatorem super eos, ut sciant gentes, quoniam homines sunt ad imaginem Dei, quibus creavit legem in reputacione sua completam condere; ideo debent secundum illam conversari ut homines et non ut bruta. Deus enim ex summa sapiencia legem condidit et dedit ecclesie ad hunc finem. Ideo si lex humana foret ad hoc lege Dei complecior, Antichristus in potencia, in sapiencia, et in providencia excederet Jhesum Christum; et tunc vera foret discipulorum eius sentencia, quod expedicius foret ecclesie quod legerentur in missis epistole decretales quam epistole apostolorum vel evangelium; nam secundum quod lex est necessarior et expedicior est magis venerabilis et plus meritorie commendanda.

ENGLISH: Therefore such a preacher of the gospel would preach the life and doctrine of Christ, in which there is no danger. Second, he would expound how the saint on whom he relies, [where he relies on him] not in the law which Christ instituted, turned aside;[^1] third, he would consider whether we observe a manner as praiseworthy as the [patrons]{g:patronus} on whom we rely did, since, because God is a rewarder of adverbs, the manner is most of all to be attended to;[^2] and fourth we would consider in the lives of the fathers whether the fathers of our order lived more closely and more holily when, living [without property]{g:exproprietarie} and contrary to us, they followed the saviour more easily and more like him.[^3] For a small error, which is [imperceptible]{g:insensibilis} by the depravity of sin, becomes by continual increase a [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} and intolerable sin, to be destroyed by the whole [church]{g:ecclesia}, as is especially clear in the [endowment]{g:dotacio} of the church, which grew from bare [alms]{g:elemosina} into poisonous [lordship]{g:dominium}.[^4] So, therefore, that twofold moral principle, [put into practice]{g:practizare} in the way of conduct (to go backward is unlawful, and of two goods the better and the easier is to be chosen), would set right the whole church; therefore all of us, without excuse, must attend to it in effect. But, returning to the second [teaching]{g:sentencia}, on the necessity of the laws of the church, it is clear from what has often been said that the church would be better ruled by the pure [339] law of the Lord than by [the laws of the church] by themselves, or with laws devised by men added to the law of the Lord.[^5] This is clear from the fact that in the first way the church would be ruled more conformably to God's honour, more directly to serving him, and more abundantly to bringing in the peace of the church. Hence it seems an antichristian heresy that God's law is not sufficient by itself, nor as sufficient as men's laws, to extinguish the injuries sprouting in the church;[^6] for to confound such [unbeliefs]{g:infidelitas} it is said of Antichrist in Psalm 9:21: "Appoint, O Lord, a lawgiver over them: that the Gentiles may know themselves to be but men", men to the image of God, for whom he created [it] to establish a law complete in his reckoning; therefore they ought to live according to it as men and not as brute beasts.[^7] For God out of his highest wisdom established the law and gave it to the church for this end. Therefore if human law were more complete for this than God's law, Antichrist would exceed Jesus Christ in [power]{g:potencia}, in wisdom and in providence; and then the teaching of his disciples would be true, that it would be more expedient for the church that the decretal epistles were read at masses than the epistles of the apostles or the gospel; for the more necessary and more expedient a law is, the more it is to be venerated and the more meritoriously commended.

### 12-g ¶2 · 339.27–340.14  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 339] Item, lex Christi plus pacificat cum Deo et plus disponit ad salutem anime, quam tradicio humana, **sed** in quantum lex hec fecerit, est honorabilior ac necessarior, igitur lex Christi excellit proporcionabiliter tradicionem humanam. Et cum pax quam habet homo cum Deo infinitum excedit pacem aliam, sicut salus anime et spiritualis prosperitas infinitum excedunt bona alia, que [p. 340] per leges hominum acquiruntur, patet quod lex Dei infinitum excedit leges hominum; et ex eodem sequitur quod sit infinitum prestancior ad placendum Deo et paciendum cum populo, nam per observanciam illius legis et non aliter placeretur, et per consequens pacificaretur homo cum Deo; **sed** nacta pace cum Deo, acquiritur pax cum qualibet creatura. Quomodo rogo, non obediret et proficeret quelibet creatura homini, cui videret Deum suum amicabiliter obedire, igitur etsi membra dyaboli tribulant amicos Christi, tamen prosunt illis, ut obedienciarii et amici; sic enim Scarioth fuit amicus Christo, Joh. XVIII, et procuravit licet invite serviens pacem Christi.

ENGLISH: Again, the law of Christ does more to bring peace with God, and disposes more to the salvation of the soul, than human tradition; now, in so far as a law does this, it is more honourable and more necessary; therefore the law of Christ excels human tradition in proportion. And since the peace which a man has with God infinitely exceeds any other peace, just as the salvation of the soul and spiritual prosperity infinitely exceed the other goods which [340] are acquired through men's laws, it is clear that God's law infinitely exceeds men's laws; and from the same [premiss] [it follows]{g:sequitur} that it is infinitely more excellent for pleasing God and for suffering with the people;[^8] for by the observance of that law, and not otherwise, would [God] be pleased, and consequently man would be at peace with God; now, once peace with God has been gained, peace is acquired with every creature. How, I ask, would every creature not obey and profit a man whom it saw its God obeying in friendship?[^9] Therefore, even if the members of the devil trouble the friends of Christ, yet they profit them, as [ones who obey]{g:obedienciarius} and as friends; for so Iscariot was a friend to Christ (John 18), and, though serving unwillingly, procured the peace of Christ.[^10]

### 12-g ¶5 · 341.1–342.7  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 341] Unde consequenter profundantur in heresim, qui dicunt quod status dotacionis sit perfeccior quam status expropriacionis, et per consequens lex papalis qua stabilitur dotacio est perfeccior quam lex Christi, qua suadetur expropriacio; nam ut inquiunt iste due leges et isti duo status convertibiliter se sequuntur, quia aliter omnino superflueret lex humana et venenose pasceret populum postposito verbo vite. Oportet enim populum illum regi magis intendere, cuius legem preponderat; quia lex et status quem stabilit, se invicem consequuntur. Unde videtur michi nota infidelitatis et dolosa calliditas Antichristi, quod domini temporales statuunt sibi tales pastores et prophetas, qui et artem pascendi ignorent et preparent vel inordinate vel toxice cibum mentis. Constat **autem** ex fide scripture quod anima pascitur verbo Dei: verba **autem** alia sunt venenum anime; cum **autem** non sic ordinant pro coco et cibo corporis, et secundum Crysostomum prelati debent esse stomachus corporis ecclesie et cocus membrorum eius, patet quanta infidelitate laborant mundi principes, qui plus appreciantur atque preponderant cocum corporis coco mentis. Illi **autem**, quorum Deus venter est, non sunt filii pacis, cum pax prima cum Deo dissolvitur. Et in isto notabiliter deficit doctor fidei christiane. Oportet enim primo rectificare in ecclesia apostolos et prophetas et consequenter residuum corporis ecclesie erit sanum. Illi **autem** stabilirent hoc necessarium principium fidei modo discreditum propter excecacionem pulveris temporalium, quod quilibet christianus [p. 342] debet infinitum plus amare virtutes quam honorem, voluptatem vel commodum temporale, sicut debet infinitum plus odire peccatum eciam veniale quam penam aliquam corporalem, et idem principium fidei per hoc quod, ut aliquid est peius vel melius, est ipsum fugiendum proporcionabiliter vel amandum.

ENGLISH: Hence those sink, as a consequence, deeper into heresy who say that the state of endowment is more perfect than the state of [renunciation of property]{g:expropriacio}, and consequently that the papal law by which endowment is established is more perfect than the law of Christ, by which renunciation of property is urged; for, as they say, these two laws and these two states follow one another convertibly, since otherwise human law would be altogether superfluous and would feed the people poisonously, the word of life being set aside. For a people must attend more to that king whose law it weighs more heavily; for a law and the state that it establishes follow upon each other.[^14] Hence it seems to me a mark of unbelief and a deceitful cunning of Antichrist that the [temporal lords]{g:temporales-domini} set up for themselves such shepherds and prophets as both [are ignorant of]{g:ignorare} the art of feeding and prepare the food of the mind either without order or poisonously. Now it is evident [from the faith of scripture]{g:ex-fide} that the soul is fed by the word of God, and other words are poison to the soul; and since they do not ordain so for the cook and the food of the body, and according to Chrysostom "[prelates]{g:prelatus} ought to be the stomach of the body of the church and the cook of its members", it is clear with how great unbelief the princes of the world labour, who value more, and weigh more heavily, the cook of the body than the cook of the mind.[^15] And those "whose God is their belly" are not "sons of peace", since the first peace, with God, is dissolved.[^16] And in this the teacher of the Christian faith notably falls short.[^17] For one must first set right the apostles and prophets in the church, and as a consequence the rest of the body of the church will be sound. And they would establish this necessary principle of faith, now discredited because of the blinding by the dust of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}: that every Christian [342] ought to love the virtues infinitely more than honour, pleasure or temporal advantage, just as he ought to hate sin, even venial sin, infinitely more than any bodily penalty; and the same principle of faith by this, that as a thing is worse or better, so it is to be fled or loved in proportion.[^18]

### 12-h ¶1 · 342.19–343.31  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 342] Et de ista subtraccione a libertate legis Christi post multiplicitatem legum Antichristi conquesti sunt sancti; quod patet in exemplo triplici, primo ex decreto beati Augustini ad inquisiciones Januarii superius recitato et ponitur in decretis XII distinccione: Omnia; secundo ex dicto beati Jeronymi epistola CXVIII ad Domicianum de morte Fabiole, ubi exponens illud evangelium Matth. XIX, 9, ubi dominus precepit viris uxorem non debere dimittere, excepta causa fornicacionis, et dimissam manere innuptam sic loquitur: Quicquid viris iubetur, hoc consequenter redundat ad feminas, neque enim adultera uxor dimittenda est [p. 343] et vir mechus tenendus. Si quis meretrici coniungitur unum corpus efficitur, igitur que scortatori impuroque sociatur, unum cum eo corpus efficitur. Alie, inquit, sunt leges cesarum, alie Ihesu Christi, aliud Papinianus, aliud Paulus noster precipit. Apud illos viris inpudicicie frena laxantur, et solo stupro atque adulterio condempnato, passim per lupanaria atque ancillulas libido permittitur, quasi culpam dignitas faciat, non voluptas; apud nos quod non licet feminis, eque non licet viris. Igitur in lege Christi oportet fidelem quiescere, et alias leges, nisi de quanto consonant legi Christi, contempnere, quod sepe oportet de vocata lege ecclesie. Unde beatus Bernhardus libro primo ad Eugenium sic effatur: Quid servilius indigniusque presertim summo pontifici, quem non dico omni die, **sed** pene omni hora insudare talibus rebus et pro talibus. Deinde quando oramus, quando docemus populos, quando edificamus ecclesiam, quando meditamur in lege? Et quidem cottidie perstrepunt in pallacio leges, **sed** Iustiniani non Domini. Si iustum istud, tu videris, nam certe lex Domini immaculata convertens animas. Hec **autem** non tam leges quam lites sunt et cavillaciones subvertentes iudicium. Tu igitur pastor et episcopus animarum, qua mente obsecro sustines coram te, semper silere illam, garrire istas? Fallor, si non movet tibi scrupulum perversitas hec, puto quod interdum compellat te clamor ad Dominum cum propheta: Narraverunt michi iniqui fabulaciones, **sed** non ut lex tua.

ENGLISH: And of this withdrawal from the freedom of the law of Christ, in following after the multitude of Antichrist's laws, the saints have complained;[^1] as is clear in a threefold example: first from the decree of blessed Augustine to the inquiries of Januarius, recited above, which is placed in the Decretum, distinction 12, [the canon] *Omnia*;[^2] second from the saying of blessed Jerome in letter 118 to Domitianus on the death of Fabiola, where, expounding that gospel, Matthew 19:9, where the Lord commanded husbands that a wife ought not to be put away except for the cause of fornication, and that one put away should remain unmarried, he speaks thus:[^3] "Whatever is commanded to men redounds consequently to women; for neither is an adulterous wife to be put away [343] and an adulterous husband to be kept. If anyone is joined to a harlot, he is made one body; therefore she who is united to a fornicator and unclean man is made one body with him.[^4] The laws of the Caesars," he says, "are one thing, those of Jesus Christ another; Papinian commands one thing, our Paul another. Among them the reins of unchastity are loosened for men, and, with only seduction and adultery condemned, lust is permitted everywhere through brothels and slave-girls, as if rank, not pleasure, made the fault; with us what is not lawful for women is equally not lawful for men."[^5] Therefore the faithful man must rest in the law of Christ and despise other laws, except in so far as they accord with the law of Christ; which must often be done with the [so-called]{g:vocatus} law of the [church]{g:ecclesia}. Hence blessed Bernard, in the first book to Eugenius, speaks thus:[^6] "What is more servile and more unworthy, especially for the supreme pontiff, than to sweat, I do not say every day, but almost every hour, over such matters and for such [people]?[^7] Then when do we pray, when do we teach the peoples, when do we build up the church, when do we meditate on the law? And indeed every day laws resound in the palace, but Justinian's, not the Lord's. Whether this is just, you must see; for certainly 'the law of the Lord is unspotted, converting souls'. But these are not so much laws as lawsuits and cavils subverting judgment. You then, shepherd and bishop of souls, with what mind, I beseech you, do you bear it before you that the one is always silent and these chatter? I am mistaken if this perversity does not stir a scruple in you; I think that at times it compels you to cry out to the Lord with the prophet: 'The wicked have told me fables: but not as thy law.'"[^8]

### 12-h ¶2 · 344.1–344.25  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 344] Ista **autem** secundum fidem scripture sunt a papa et quolibet episcopo meditanda, et postpositis cunctis legibus extraneis effectualiter exequenda. In extraneis enim legibus iacet a lege Christi et per consequens a Deo distraccio, utriusque testamenti offensio et periculosa ac latens iniuriacio. Quis (queso) dubitat, quin occupacio circa leges hominum, que omnes sunt conclusio ad viacionem superflue, de tanto distrahunt pusillam capacitatem anime a lege Domini? Quis insuper hesitat de legislatore Christo, quem oportet omnem salvandum sequi? Psalmus primo, 2 scribitur: In lege eius meditabitur die ac nocte, ymo Deutronomio VIII, 1 dicitur: Omne mandatum quod ego precipio tibi hodie, cave diligenter ut facias. Et quia ad hoc requiritur mandatorum noticia; ideo Deutronomio VI, 6, precipitur post precepcionem observancie omnium mandatorum erunt verba hec, que ego precipio tibi hodie, in corde tuo, et narrabis ea filiis tuis et meditaberis sedens in domo tua et ambulans in itinere, dormiens atque consurgens. Et Joh. XIV, 15 ab equo dicitur: Si quis diligit me, sermonem meum servabit. Nichil enim attencius mandatur a Domino, quam iugis meditacio legis Dei.

ENGLISH: And these things, according to the faith of scripture, are to be meditated on by the [pope]{g:papa} and by every [bishop]{g:episcopus}, and, all foreign laws being set aside, carried out in effect. For in foreign laws lies a distraction from the law of Christ, and consequently from God, an offence against both testaments, and a dangerous and hidden wronging. Who, I ask, doubts that busying oneself with men's laws, which are all [*conclusio*] superfluous to [wayfaring]{g:viacio}, draws the small capacity of the soul away from the law of the Lord by so much?[^9] Who, moreover, hesitates about Christ the lawgiver, whom everyone who is to be saved must follow?[^10] In the first Psalm, [verse] 2, it is written: "on his law he shall meditate day and night"; indeed in Deuteronomy 8:1 it is said: "All the commandments, that I command thee this day, take great care to observe." And because for this a knowledge of the commandments is required, therefore in Deuteronomy 6:6, after the commanding of the observance of all the commandments, it is commanded: "these words which I command thee this day, shall be in thy heart: and thou shalt tell them to thy children, and thou shalt meditate sitting in thy house, and walking on thy journey, sleeping and rising."[^11] And in John 14[:23] it is said: "If any one love me, he will keep my word."[^12] For nothing is more carefully commanded by the Lord than continual meditation on the law of God.

### 12-h ¶3 · 344.26–345.10  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 344] Et quantum ad tercium patet, quod in legibus humanis est fraudis et iniurie plenitudo. Patet hoc de principiis vel maximis legum ecclesie, et multo magis de conclusionibus ex illis sequentibus. Nam libro VI de constitucionibus datur pro regula, quod duobus super eadem provisione litteras a papa habentibus una die, is, cui papa [p. 345] canonicatum contulit preferendus est ei cui non dedit canonicatum, ignorante executore quis prius impetraverit. Si **autem** sint pares in gracia, quoad formam stili papalis, is qui prius presentavit erit pocior in prebenda. Et tercio si equales fuerint in hiis tribus, tunc canonici, quibus spectat collacio, vel maior pars eorum tenetur eligere reliquo carente fructu gracie nisi ex tenore papalium litterarum expresse appareat, quod papa utrique illorum voluerit providere.

ENGLISH: And as to the third, it is clear that in human laws there is a fullness of fraud and injury. This is clear of the principles or maxims of the laws of the church, and much more of the conclusions following from them. For in the Sixth Book, [under] "On constitutions", it is given as a rule that "when two have letters from the pope on the same provision on one day, he on whom the pope [345] conferred a canonry is to be preferred to him on whom he did not bestow a canonry, if the executor does not know which [obtained]{g:impetrare} [his letters] first. But if they are equal in the grace, as to the form of the papal style, he who presented first shall have the stronger right in the prebend. And third, if they are equal in these three, then the canons to whom the [conferral]{g:collacio} belongs, or the greater part of them, are bound to choose, the other lacking the fruit of the grace, unless it appears expressly from the tenor of the papal letters that the pope wished to provide for both of them."[^13]

### 12-h ¶4 · 345.11–345.18  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 345] Ista **autem** trimembris maxima videtur in toto esse principium contra Christum, quia supposito sicut in maiori parte contingit, quod prepositus in canonicatu vel tempore, in presentacione vel ostensione gracie, vel tercio in capitali eleccione sit reliquo quantumlibet indignior quoad Deum, tunc ex ista maxima Antichristi debet iudicari contra Christi iudicium.

ENGLISH: Now this three-membered maxim seems to be wholly a principle against Christ; for supposing, as happens for the greater part, that the one preferred in canonry or in time, in presentation or in showing the grace, or, third, in the chapter's election, is ever so much more unworthy than the other as regards God, then by this maxim of Antichrist judgment must be given against the judgment of Christ.[^14]

### 12-i ¶1 · 345.22–347.25  (8 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 345] Ex quo patet ulterius, quod sit a fidelibus tamquam hereticum abrogandum, et per consequens papa non debet pro toto mundo secundum ipsum generaliter causas ecclesiasticas terminare. Et idem est argumentum de generalibus legum paparum principiis, et specialiter de maximis Bonifacii VIII in Sexto, que manifeste obviant legi Dei. Non enim preferunt nec sciunt preferre quem Deus preelegit, **sed** communiter preeligunt maximum Antichristum, quia cum Judas non dormit avaricia debriatus sepe contingit quod membrum dyaboli preferatur; calliditas [p. 346] enim vulpina sepe preficit minus dignos. Unde apostoli non audebant nec potuerunt sicut nec Christus talem legem statuere. Ideo ista vocata lex est Antichristi, qui extollitur super omne quod dicitur Deus. Christus enim non potest ex nuda prelacione momenti temporis, ex callida presentacione priori collacionis papalis nec ex iterata eleccione sepe frivola capituli acceptantis, ineptum virtute preponere, **sed** oportet omnino, quod ille quem Christus vel lex sua preeligit, sit quoad Deum virtute prestancior. Et hinc apostoli non presumpserunt vel legem eleccionis statuere vel Mathiam eligere, **sed** commiserunt hec divino iudicio, ut patet Act. I. Sic **autem** faceret papa, cum pro toto mundo non statueret legem iniustam vel committeret peccatum eciam veniale. Est **autem** maior blasfemia constanter asserrere quod eo ipso quo Papa hoc statuit eciam contra decretum Domini, tunc est iustum, vel quod oportet quod Deus condescendat pape et legem quamcumque pape iustificet. Vel tercio quod levem iniuriam homo debet committere propter maius commodum inde sequens. Cum igitur quoad omnes causas terminandas per se sufficit lex scripture, quomodo presumeret Christi vicarius legem assumere tam iniquam quoad iniurias, commoveatur utraque pars pati iniurias et propter periculum tempus sibi redimere. Hoc **autem** ex fide est melius quam iudicium quod sentenciat lex humana, quod si neutra pars voluerit decreto Domini in talibus humiliter consentire, compromittant ponendo se in arbitrio boni viri vel ad minimum iudicentur secundum leges civiles intra ecclesiam, vel si omnia ista renuerint, fugiantur tamquam ethnici et publicani [p. 347] et discipuli Antichristi. Et nullomodo trahat Christi vicarius huiusmodi causas sibi, **sed** effugiat ut venenum iuxta exemplaria legum civilium condere novas leges, **sed** abnuentem finaliter hanc viam triplicem, excommunicet ut membrum dyaboli. Excommunicanten im quandoque simplices; quia dissentire renuunt legi. Et patet quomodo preter papam oportet esse, sicut fuit in primitiva ecclesia, unum caput ad causas ecclesiasticas terminandum. Quoad secundum patet quod oportet esse unum caput ad beneficia ecclesiastica parciendum, nam lex Christi est ad illud sufficiens, et persona populi, cui preficeretur talis prepositus, foret optimus iudex ad discernendum talem prepositum episcopo presentandum. Sic enim fuit in primitiva ecclesia; nec cessat racio, quare non sic foret hodie. Nam illa persona tenerrime provideret de mediis ad salutem anime necessariis cuiusmodi est pastor discretus; si enim providet de mediis corporis nutritivis, fides necessitat quod infinitum plus provideat de mediis anime nutritivis. Ideo Ecclesiastici XXX, 24 dicitur: Miserere anime tue placens Deo. Istam igitur providenciam non est Christi et per consequens nullius creature vetare.

ENGLISH: From this it is further clear that it is to be abrogated by the faithful as heretical, and consequently the [pope]{g:papa} ought not to determine ecclesiastical causes generally for the whole world according to it. And the argument is the same about the general principles of the popes' laws, and especially about the maxims of Boniface VIII in the Sext, which plainly run counter to God's law. For they do not prefer, nor know how to prefer, the one whom God has chosen beforehand, but commonly choose beforehand the greatest Antichrist; for since Judas, drunk with avarice, does not sleep, it often happens that a [member]{g:membrum} of the devil is preferred; for foxlike cunning [346] often sets the less worthy over [others]. Hence the apostles did not dare and were not able, just as Christ was not, to establish such a law. Therefore this [so-called]{g:vocatus} law is Antichrist's, who "is lifted up above all that is called God".[^1] For Christ cannot, on the bare priority of a moment of time, on a crafty earlier presentation of the papal [conferral]{g:collacio}, or on the repeated and often frivolous election of the accepting chapter, set over [others] one unfit in [virtue]{g:virtus}; but it must altogether be that the one whom Christ or his law chooses beforehand is more excellent in virtue as regards God. And hence the apostles did not presume either to establish a law of election or to elect Matthias, but committed these things to divine judgment, as is clear from Acts 1.[^2] And so the pope would do, since for the whole world he would not establish an unjust law or commit even a venial sin.[^3] And it is a greater blasphemy to assert steadfastly that by the very fact that the pope has established this, even against the decree of the Lord, it is then just; or that God must condescend to the pope and justify any law of the pope whatever; or, third, that a man ought to commit a slight injury for the sake of a greater advantage following from it. Since, therefore, the law of scripture by itself suffices for determining all causes, how would the [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius-christi} presume to take up a law so wicked? ### 12-k ¶3 · 355.4–356.20  (8 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 355] **Sed** hic dicitur negando contrarium; quia omnia temporalia data clerico ut huiusmodi debent dari secundum racionem elemosine, que debet esse voluntaria et discreta; redditus **autem** dati seculari domino ut tali debent dari secundum racionem contractus et subieccionis civilis, que non respicit dignitatem prepositi quoad Deum; nec est tenens culpandus, licet coacte det temporalia tyranno, quem credit esse indignum, ymo propter pacem emendam debet gratis dare et servire domino infideli, ut docet Apostolus; ne nomen Domini blasfemetur; et si crediderit per subtraccionem servitutis vel redditus convertere dominum infidelem debet ea intencione subtrahere salva fide; talem **autem** fidem non fecit legibus Antichristi. Quamvis **autem** leges civiles erant diu ante incarnacionem Domini et sint secundum Augustinum mixte multis nequiciis, tamen sunt plurimum iuste, cum pati rapinas bonorum et ceteras iniurias sit meritorium; lex **autem** Antichristi sub specie sanctitatis et legis Domini est heretica et blasfema, cui omnes fideles debent resistere et execucioni sue secundum formam ipocrisis; quia si vocatus clerus velit rapere bona subditi per modum tyrannidis est meritorie tollerandus. **Sed** cum defert talem civilem tyrannidem et blasfeme palliat ac sub colore legis civilis exaccionem bonorum fortune per viam excommunicacionis vel alterius censure, que nec cogit corporaliter nec nocet anime impetiti in contemptum legis, debent servari temporalia, cum nec sit fides nec elemosina [p. 356] sic dare blasfemis bona pauperum, ideo quod ea racione debetur caret colore meriti. **Sed** dimittendo hanc formam et innitendo pugnis et spoliacioni civili, ut modo faciunt discipuli Antichristi, videtur michi quod via meritorior atque securior foret pati iniurias, confidenter expectando divinum iudicium, **sed** non propter timorem fulminis quod pretendunt. Sic igitur verificata est de nomine christianis et paganis vel clero et layco prophecia Ezechielis XXIII de duabus sororibus Oolla et Oolliba; nam Oolliba christiana plus fornicata est spiritualiter cum tradicionibus infidelium quam Oolla, quia plus insanivit sophisticando legem, et excanduit in temporalium voluptatem, ideo secundum diffinicionem Dei tradendi sunt in manus gencium puniendi; deseruerunt enim viam, quam Christus docuit, ad intrinsecus se regendum et exteros conquirendum; nam mamme pubertatis sunt fracte, dum leges infancium sunt dirupte.

ENGLISH: But here [the answer is]{g:dicitur} [given] by denying the contrary;[^10] because all [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} given to a cleric as such ought to be given according to the nature of alms, which ought to be voluntary and discreet; but revenues given to a secular lord as such ought to be given according to the nature of a contract and of civil subjection, which does not regard the worthiness of the superior as regards God; nor is the tenant to be blamed, although under compulsion he gives temporal goods to a tyrant whom he believes to be unworthy; indeed, for the sake of buying peace he ought to give freely and serve an unbelieving lord, as the Apostle teaches, lest the name of the Lord be blasphemed;[^11] and if he believes that by withdrawing [servitude]{g:servitus} or revenue he can convert the unbelieving lord, he ought to withdraw them with that intention, saving faith; but such faith he did not pledge to the laws of Antichrist.[^12] And although the civil laws existed long before the incarnation of the Lord, and are, according to Augustine, mixed with many wickednesses, yet they are for the most part just, since to suffer the plundering of one's goods and other wrongs is meritorious;[^13] but the law of Antichrist, under the appearance of holiness and of the law of the Lord, is heretical and blasphemous, and all the faithful ought to resist it and its execution according to the form of hypocrisy; because if the [so-called]{g:vocatus} clergy wishes to seize the goods of a subject by way of tyranny, it is to be meritoriously tolerated. But when it sets aside such civil tyranny, and blasphemously cloaks, and under [colour]{g:color} of civil law [pursues], the exaction of goods of fortune by way of excommunication or another censure, which neither coerces bodily nor harms the soul of the one assailed in contempt of the law, the temporal goods ought to be kept, since it is neither faith nor alms [356] to give the goods of the poor thus to blasphemers, because what is owed on that ground lacks the colour of merit.[^14] But, leaving this form and relying on fists and civil despoiling, as the disciples of Antichrist now do, it seems to me that the more meritorious and safer way would be to suffer the wrongs, confidently awaiting the divine judgment, but not for fear of the thunderbolt which they hold out. Thus, then, the prophecy of Ezekiel 23 about the two sisters Oolla and Ooliba has been verified of Christians and pagans in name, or of clergy and laity;[^15] for the Christian Ooliba has committed spiritual fornication with the traditions of unbelievers more than Oolla, because she went more mad in [counterfeiting]{g:sophisticare} the law, and burned with desire for the pleasure of temporal goods; therefore according to the [definition]{g:diffinicio} of God they are to be delivered into the hands of the gentiles to be punished; for they have deserted the way which Christ taught for ruling themselves inwardly and winning those outside; for the teats of [their] virginity are bruised, while the laws of the infants are torn apart.[^16]

### 12-k ¶4 · 356.21–356.26  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 356] **Sed** secundo obicitur per hoc quod de lege Dei evangelizantibus debentur stipendia, ut docet Apostolus prima Cor. IX et Luce X; oportet igitur esse aliquam legem que iniuriantes coherceat, cuiusmodi est lex ecclesie, quam oportet credere in hac parte.

ENGLISH: But, secondly, [it is objected, by appeal to]{g:obicitur-per} this: that by God's law stipends are owed to [preachers of the gospel]{g:evangelizans}, as the Apostle teaches, 1 Corinthians 9 and Luke 10; there must therefore be some law that [coerces]{g:cohercere} those who do [them] wrong, of which kind is the law of the church, which must be believed in this respect.[^17]

### 12-l ¶1 · 356.27–358.3  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 356] Hic conceditur totum, cum utrobique veritas confuse tegitur; nam Christo et apostolis debebantur de lege Dei stipendia officio spiritualis ministerii necessaria, et lex Dei atque suum iudicium plene excuciunt istorum iniurias, quia faciat sacerdos spirituale officium sibi debitum; et incompossibile est quod desint sibi vite necessaria ad dampnum anime. Patet de prophetis legis veteris, de Christo et suis apostolis et de [p. 357] vitis patrum ut sanctis monachis. Si **autem** talis patitur penam vel defectum corporis, ut sepe contigit de Christo et suis apostolis, hoc servata paciencia redundat in lucrum anime, ideo Christus non tradidit legem in talibus reluctandi, **sed** humiliter iniurias paciendi, ut patet Luce IX: **sed** infideliter istam legem Christi contempnimus. Cum igitur discolis non desunt hodie temporalia, quanto magis Deus non deficiet in necessariis servo suo. **Sed** oportet in isto confuso involucro quod christianus caveat de triplici periculo, primo quod clericus Christi non ocietur accipiendo avide stipendium sine ministerio; hoc enim secundum legem Dei et hominum est iniustum; nec ad illud extinguendum ordinatur aliquod suffragium. Secundo quod in mensura et non in superhabundancia accipiatur de fimo temporalium, iuxta doctrinam Apostoli prima Thym. VI. 8 habentes, inquit, alimenta et quibus tegamur hiis contenti simus. Unde beatus Crysostomus super primo cap. ad Hebreos Hom. II dicit, quod christianus debet sapere in penuria, non in diviciis, quoniam qui in paucis potest vivere, eo qui non potest multo maior et melior est. Ut si vocatorum ad regalem civitatem hii nec subiugalibus egent nec famulis nec palliis nec deversoriis nec calligis nec vasis, **sed** sufficiat eis panem habere et ex fontibus aquam accipere; illi vero dicunt nisi vehicula, stramenta mollia, numerosam familiam habeamus, non possumus, et adhuc remissis brevibus et interpolatis passibus, civitatem attingere. Si insuper dum civitatem attigerint, sint secundum celeritatem et expedicionem ad civitatem [p. 358] cum ipsam intraverint premiandi, quis dubitat, quin primi excedent secundos? Correspondenter est de viantibus ad patriam senciendum.

ENGLISH: Here the whole is conceded, since on both sides the truth is covered [indistinctly]{g:confuse};[^1] for to Christ and the apostles were owed, by the law of God, the stipends necessary to the office of spiritual ministry, and God's law and his judgment fully shake off the wrongs of those [men], provided the [priest]{g:sacerdos} does the spiritual office owed by him; and it is [incompossible]{g:incompossibilis} that the necessaries of life should be lacking to him to the harm of his soul. It is clear from the prophets of the old law, from Christ and his apostles, and from the [357] lives of the fathers, as [those of] holy monks.[^2] And if such a one suffers pain or want of body, as often happened with Christ and his apostles, this, patience being kept, redounds to the gain of the soul; therefore Christ did not give a law of struggling back in such things but of humbly suffering wrongs, as is clear in Luke 9; but we unfaithfully despise this law of Christ.[^3] Since, then, [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} are not lacking today to the [froward]{g:discoli}, how much more will God not fail his servant in necessaries. But in this indistinct wrapping a Christian must beware of a threefold danger: first, that the [cleric]{g:clericus} of Christ be not idle, greedily accepting a stipend without ministry; for this is unjust according to the law of God and of men; nor is any [aid]{g:suffragium} ordained for extinguishing it.[^4] Second, that he take of the dung of temporal goods in measure and not in superabundance, according to the [doctrine]{g:doctrina} of the Apostle, 1 Timothy 6:8: "Having," he says, "food and wherewith to be covered, with these we are content."[^5] Hence blessed Chrysostom, on the first chapter to the Hebrews, Homily 2, says that "a Christian ought to be wise in penury, not in riches, since he who can live on little is much greater and better than he who cannot."[^6] As if, of those called to the royal city, these need neither beasts of burden nor servants nor cloaks nor inns nor boots nor vessels, but it suffices them to have bread and to take water from the springs; but those say: "Unless we have carriages, soft bedding and a numerous [household]{g:familia}, we cannot, even with slack, short and halting steps, reach the city." If, moreover, when they have reached the city, they are to be rewarded according to their speed and dispatch to the city [358] when they have entered it, who doubts that the first will surpass the second? Correspondingly one must judge of [those on the road]{g:viantes} to the [homeland]{g:patria}.[^7]

### 12-l ¶2 · 358.4–358.24  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 358] **Sed** caveatur tercio ne ad querendum nobis bona superflua et itineris ad patriam tardativa menciamur blasfeme de scriptura, dicentes quod de lege Domini debemus anathematizare strictissime pro temporalium negacione, et sic sollicitemur sollicite exequi tradiciones ad hoc conditas, quia hoc nimis ex Antichristi dogmate tardaret a patria, patet destruccio huius heresis Luce IX et per legem pape Gregorii, XXIII, questione IV, Inter querelas, ubi docetur non sic pro temporalibus vindicandum. **Sed** tercio obicitur per hoc quod iuxta hanc viam nedum detinerentur decime et proventus ecclesie a cardinalibus et alienigenis indignis nec solum ab ecclesiis appropriatis religiosis subtrahentibus debitum rectoris officium, **sed** ab assiduis rectoribus vigilanter residentibus, cum aperta sit via laycis ad iudicandum de ministerio sui prepositi et subtrahendum de stipendio secundum suum iudicium diminuerentur proventus ecclesie ad non quantum.

ENGLISH: But, third, let care be taken lest, to seek for ourselves superfluous goods that slow the journey to the homeland, we blasphemously lie about scripture, saying that by the law of the Lord we ought to anathematize most strictly for the denial of temporal goods, and so be anxiously solicited to carry out the traditions made for this purpose; because this, out of Antichrist's dogma, would hold [us] back too much from the homeland. The destruction of this heresy is clear from Luke 9 and by the law of Pope Gregory, [Causa] 23, question 4, *Inter querelas*, where it is taught that one is not thus to take vengeance for temporal things.[^8] But, third, [it is objected, by appeal to]{g:obicitur-per} this: that on this way the [tithes]{g:decime} and proceeds of the [church]{g:ecclesia} would be withheld not only from cardinals and unworthy foreigners, nor only from churches [appropriated]{g:appropriare} to the [religious]{g:religiosi}, who withdraw the office owed by a rector, but from assiduous rectors vigilantly resident; since the way is open to laymen to judge of the ministry of their [superior]{g:prepositi} and to withdraw from his stipend according to their own judgment, the proceeds of the church would be diminished to nothing.[^9]

### 12-l ¶5 · 359.23–360.18  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 359] Et quantum ad ultimum membrum, in quo iacet difficultas, conceditur quod occasione male accepta decimantes erranter excederent. **Sed** in parte altera iacet incomparabiliter maius periculum ut, posito quod tollatur a clero decimacio, sicut tollebatur a Christo et suis apostolis, cum fuit legale legis veteris, cum non debuit tunc remanere nisi secundum moralitatem inclusam, adhuc viveret clerus secundum privatas elemosinas ut tempore Christi, et postmodum magis fructificans nec tot episcopis egerent, ut modo propter penuriam; et sic concesso maximo inconvenienti huius partis foret modicum in comparacione [p. 360] ad inconveniens quod insurgit ex legibus et practicis circa ecclesiasticas porciones. Quamvis **autem** oportet quod senex sacerdos sit sumptuosior, tamen non vidi senem talem sine pena peccati sui a Domino quoad temporale subsidium derelictum. Ille enim, qui non derelinquit in bonis maximis non potest deficere in bonis minimis. Presbiter igitur talis decrepitus debet cavere de avaricia, de omni assistencia familiari vel ministeriali superflua, et dare se oracioni, contemplacioni et omni modo quo sufficit prodesse ecclesie; et sic vite sanctitas ac ecclesie edificacio erit carta, procuratorium ac medium interpellans ad Deum et populum, propter temporale subsidium acquirendum, sic quod sacerdos moriatur clericus, deserto seculo in spe spiritualis porcionis dominice, sicut vixit corporaliter ex porcione domini temporali.

ENGLISH: And as to the last member, in which the difficulty lies, it is conceded that, on an occasion wrongly taken, tithe-payers might err and go too far. But on the other side lies an incomparably greater danger; as, supposing that tithing were taken away from the clergy, as it was taken away from Christ and his apostles, since it was a [legal precept]{g:legalia} of the old law, since it ought not then to remain except according to the morality included [in it], the clergy would still live on private alms, as in the time of Christ, and afterwards more fruitfully; nor would they need so many bishops, as now [they do] because of penury;[^14] and so, granting the greatest [ill consequence]{g:inconveniens} on this side, it would be small in comparison [360] with the ill consequence that arises from the laws and practices concerning ecclesiastical portions.[^15] And although an old priest must be more costly, yet I have not seen such an old man, without the penalty of his own sin, forsaken by the Lord as to temporal support.[^16] For he who does not forsake [us] in the greatest goods cannot fail in the least. Such a decrepit [presbyter]{g:presbiter}, therefore, ought to beware of avarice, of all superfluous attendance of household or ministers, and give himself to prayer, contemplation and every way in which he is able to profit the church; and so holiness of life and the building up of the church will be his charter, [proctor's mandate]{g:procuratorium} and mediating means, interceding with God and the people for acquiring temporal support, so that the priest may die a cleric, having forsaken the world, in hope of the Lord's spiritual portion, as he lived bodily from the Lord's temporal portion.[^17]
