# *De ecclesia*, chapter 18, part 3: the church spread through the world; the devil head of his members; avarice and the death of Christ

## ¶1 · 422.19–424.10

> [p. 422] Ad octavam raciunculam conceditur quod universalis ecclesia est diffusa per totum orbem terrarum, cum corpus Christi quod est ecclesia sit diffinitum loco, et allegatur Augustinus XVIII° De Civitate Dei, cap. L°, et XXII° De Civitate Dei, cap. XXVIII°. Hic miror quare de virtute sermonis ex auctoritate Augustini conceditur quod ecclesia est diffusa per totum orbem terrarum et tamen non conceduntur alia que eque asserit Augustinus de ipsa ecclesia; nam per maiorem partem orbis terrarum non est aliquis christianus, nullibi est ecclesia [p. 423] nisi ubi est aliquis christianus, ergo per maiorem partem orbis terrarum non est ecclesia. Et si dicitur quod est ibi secundum potestatem, sic revera quelibet persona sancte matris ecclesie est ubique, et sic concederet cum beato Augustino quod tam ecclesia militans quam triumphans est pars tocius ecclesie. Hoc enim persepe dicunt sancti doctores, licet mendacissime dicatur quod nullibi dicit aliquis doctor unam esse ecclesiam ex istis. Nam (ut sepe allegavi) Augustinus, Encheridion LXIV° cap. ponit illud et in De Questionibus veteris et nove legis cap. XLVII° sic scribit: Non solum autem nos sed et superni cives ecclesie membra sunt. Aliter enim ipsa non esset corpus vel sponsa Christi et persona manens continue una a principio mundi usque in eternum. Non enim est racio quare ex omnibus christianis quantumcunque localiter distantibus componitur unum corpus, quin per idem ex beatis et viantibus; nec potest colorari racio, quomodo ex tota multitudine fidei christianorum, licet fuit valde disparis speciei, resultat una fides que est formale quo constituitur ecclesia, quin a pari ex omnibus predestinatis sit una sponsa Christi. Sic enim ex beatis viantibus et dampnatis fit unum genus humanum, unde quod dicitur ecclesiam militantem non esse circumscriptive alicubi falsum est, cum sit corpus habens unum locum circumscribentem, sicut habet unam fidem, et sic est corpus animatum racionale, nam est corpus sensibile exercens actus vitales, sicut multitudinis credencium erat cor unum et anima una, ut dicitur Act. IV°, 32. Ideo valde remota est dissimilitudo ad probandum quod non est animata, licet vivat, sicut spiritus vivunt, et non sunt animati. Si enim spiritus forent organici [p. 424] sensibiles et gressibiles, sicut sunt militantes ecclesie, tunc oporteret concedendo ipsos vivere consequenter concedere quod sunt animati, sed hoc concedit doctor de ecclesia. Dicit enim in responsione ad novam raciunculam quod ecclesia est aliquid, quia substancia, cum totum sit sue partes, et habet dicta ecclesia tam partes quantitativas quam qualitativas, nec fingetur habere partes qualitativas, si non animas, et per consequens, sicut ecclesia est vivens multis vitis, sic est animata multis animabus.

To the eighth [little argument]{g:raciuncula} it is granted that the universal [church]{g:ecclesia} is spread throughout the whole world, although the body of Christ, which is the church, is bounded by place; and Augustine is [alleged]{g:allegare}, *City of God* book 18, chapter 50, and *City of God* book 22, chapter 28.[^1] Here I wonder why, [in the proper sense of the words]{g:de-virtute-sermonis}, it is granted on Augustine's authority that the church is spread throughout the whole world, and yet the other things which Augustine asserts equally of the church herself are not granted; for through the greater part of the world there is no Christian; nowhere is there church [423] except where there is some Christian; therefore through the greater part of the world there is no church.[^2] And if it is said that it is there according to power, in that way truly any person of holy mother church is everywhere; and so he would grant with blessed Augustine that the [church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans} as well as the church triumphant is a part of the whole church. For the holy doctors say this very often, although it is said most mendaciously that no doctor anywhere says that one church is made of these. For (as I have often alleged) Augustine lays it down in the *Enchiridion*, chapter 64, and in *On the Questions of the Old and New Law*, chapter 47, writes thus: "And not only we, but the citizens above too, are members of the church."[^3] For otherwise she would not be the body or [bride]{g:sponsa} of Christ, and one person remaining continuously from the beginning of the world for ever. For there is no reason why one body is composed out of all Christians, however distant in place, but that by the same token [one is composed] out of the blessed and [those on the road]{g:viantes}; nor can a reason be [given colour]{g:colorare} how out of the whole multitude of the faith of Christians, although it was of very unlike species, there results one faith, which is the formal [principle] by which the church is constituted, but that equally out of all the [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} there is one bride of Christ.[^4] For so out of the blessed, those on the road and the damned there comes one human race; whence what is said, that the church militant is not anywhere [circumscriptively]{g:circumscriptive}, is false, since it is a body having one circumscribing place, as it has one faith; and so it is an ensouled rational body, for it is a [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} body exercising vital acts, as the multitude of believers had but one heart and one soul, as it is said, Acts 4:32.[^5] Therefore the unlikeness [brought] to prove that it is not ensouled, although it lives, as spirits live and are not ensouled, is very remote. For if spirits were organic, [424] perceptible and able to walk, as the militants of the church are, then in granting that they live one would have consequently to grant that they are ensouled; now the doctor grants this of the church. For he says in his answer to the ninth little argument that the church is something, because a substance, since the whole is its parts, and the said church has [quantitative parts]{g:pars-quantitativa} as well as qualitative ones; nor will it be feigned to have qualitative parts, if not souls; and consequently, as the church is living with many lives, so it is ensouled with many souls.[^6]

## ¶2 · 424.11–426.30

> [p. 424] Ulterius quando negatur dyabolum caput esse membrorum suorum, expresse contradicitur sanctis doctoribus, cum beatus Gregorius, Omelia XVIª, super illud Matthei IV°, 1: Ductus est Jesus in desertum a spiritu, ita scribit: Certe iniquorum omnium dyabolus caput est, et huius capitis membra sunt omnes iniqui. An non dyaboli membrum fuit Pylatus? Sicut ergo est corpus iniquorum, sic est caput eorum, sicut multi qui censentur summi pontifices sunt capita particularium membrorum diaboli; nullus autem prescitus est caput sancte matris ecclesie nec alicuius membri eius, sed sicut Petrus gessit typum omnium predestinatorum pastorum ipsum sequencium post Christum, sic Scarioth gessit typum omnium episcoporum prescitorum ipsum sequencium post dyabolum, non quod ante istos non erant priores in sancta matre ecclesia quorum ipsi erant filii nati pro patribus, sed quod in tempore novi testamenti isti erant capitales post caput ecclesie et principem huius [p. 425] mundi. Differencia autem est inter istos capitaneos, quia boni siciunt precipue virtutes subditorum et lucra animarum suarum, mali autem econtra precipue siciunt temporalia subditorum. Unde Scarioth maximus symoniacus et proprietarius ferens loculos dixit, quasi continuando avariciam pontificum veteris testamenti: Quid vultis mihi dare et ego eum vobis tradam, Matthei XXVI°, 15. Nec dubium quin avaricia quam pontifices habebant circa lucrum templi fecit eos intendere mortem Christi; Johannis XI°, 47, 48, sic scribitur: Collegerunt pontifices et pharisei consilium adversus Jesum et dicebant: Quid faciemus, quia hic homo multa signa facit? si dimittimus eum sic, omnes credent in eum et venient Romani et tollent locum nostrum et gentem. Super quo Augustinus Omelia XLIXª notat primo quomodo invalescente avaricia sacerdotum tempore Christi fuit officium venale nedum pluribus existentibus sacerdotibus simul, ut elicit de Zacharia, Luce I, sed eodem anno per circumvencionem avaram versati fuerunt summi pontifices, et ista avaricia excecavit et excitavit eos ad blaspheme conspirandum in mortem domini. Plus, inquit Augustinus, perditi homines cogitabant quomodo nocerent ut perderent, quam quomodo sibi consulerent ne perirent; ideo collegerunt consilium contra salutem anime sue, quia contra signa veritatis salutaris seviebant. Secundo notat Augustinus quomodo timor perdicionis questus templi fuit causa precipua mortis Christi: Temporalia, inquit, perdere timuerunt et vitam eternam non cogitaverunt et sic utrumque amiserunt. [p. 426] Hec autem timuerant ne, si omnes homines in Christum crederent, nemo remaneret qui adversus Romanos civitatem et templum defenderet. Conceperant enim Christum esse hostem templi et ministrorum eius, et per consequens inimicum ecclesie capitalem, eo quod tradiciones avaricie detexit (Matthei XV°), contra scelera eorum multiplex ve invexit (Matthei XXIII°), ymmo de templo eorum instrumenta avaricie dominanter evexit (Matthei XXI°, 12 et Johannis II°). Ideo accusarunt eum reportacione falsa de potestate ruine templi minata, quod potuit destruere templum illud et in tribus diebus reedificare illud (ut patet Matthei XXVI° ex dicto Christi Johannis II°, 19: Solvite templum hoc et in tribus diebus excito illud). Unde tamquam habentes hoc plus cordi inproperabant domino crucifixo: Vach, inquiunt, qui destruit templum Dei et in tribus diebus reedificat illud, Matthei XXVII°, 40. Contra templum, inquit Augustinus, et contra suas paternas leges esse totam doctrinam Christi temere senciebant, et ideo iusto Dei iudicio evenit eis finis quem prepostere sic timebant. Nam quadragesimo secundo anno post ascensionem domini per Tytum et Vespasianum tam gentem quam locum perpetuo deperdebant, et sic verificatum est de eis illud Matthei VIII°, 12: Filii autem regni huius eicientur in tenebras exteriores. Unde imposuerunt sibi horribiliorem speciem heresis que est blasphemia, per quam coloracius possent eum extinguere. Johannis XIX°, 7 scribitur: Nos legem habemus et secundum legem debet mori, quia filium Dei se fecit. De lege autem occidendi blasphemum patet Levitici XXIV°, 16.

Further, when it is denied that the devil is [head]{g:caput} of his [members]{g:membrum}, the holy doctors are expressly contradicted, since blessed Gregory, homily 16, on that text of Matthew 4:1, "Jesus was led by the spirit into the desert," writes thus: "Surely the devil is head of all the wicked, and all the wicked are members of this head. Was not Pilate a member of the devil?"[^7] As, therefore, there is a body of the wicked, so there is a head of them, just as many who are reckoned [highest pontiffs]{g:summi-pontifices} are heads of particular members of the devil; but no [foreknown]{g:prescitus} [man] is head of holy mother church or of any member of her; but as Peter bore the type of all the predestinate pastors following him after Christ, so Iscariot bore the type of all the foreknown [bishops]{g:episcopus} following him after the devil; not that before these there were not earlier ones in holy mother church, whose sons they were, born instead of the fathers, but that in the time of the new testament these were the chiefs after the head of the church and the prince of this [425] world.[^8] And the difference between these [captains]{g:capitaneus} is that the good thirst chiefly for the virtues of their subjects and the gain of their souls, but the bad, on the contrary, thirst chiefly for the [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} of their subjects. Whence Iscariot, the greatest simoniac and a proprietary, carrying the purse, said, as if continuing the avarice of the pontiffs of the old testament: "What will you give me, and I will deliver him unto you?" Matthew 26:15.[^9] Nor is there doubt that the avarice which the pontiffs had about the gain of the temple made them aim at the death of Christ; in John 11:47–48 it is written thus: "The chief priests and the Pharisees gathered a council against Jesus and said: What shall we do, for this man doth many miracles? If we let him alone so, all will believe in him; and the Romans will come, and take away our place and nation."[^10] On which Augustine, homily 49, notes first how, as the avarice of the priests grew strong in Christ's time, the office was for sale: not only were there several priests at once, as he draws out of Zachary, Luke 1, but in the same year, by avaricious circumvention, the high priests were changed; and this avarice blinded them and stirred them to conspire blasphemously in the death of the Lord. "Lost men," says Augustine, "thought more how they might do harm, so as to destroy, than how they might take counsel for themselves, so as not to perish"; therefore they gathered a council against the salvation of their own soul, because they raged against the signs of saving truth.[^11] Secondly, Augustine notes how fear of losing the gain of the temple was the chief cause of the death of Christ: "They feared," he says, "to lose temporal things, and did not think of eternal life, and so they lost both. [426] And they had feared this, lest, if all men believed in Christ, no one would remain to defend the city and the temple against the Romans." For they had conceived Christ to be an enemy of the temple and of its ministers, and consequently the chief enemy of the church, because he [exposed]{g:detegere} the traditions of avarice (Matthew 15), brought in a manifold woe against their crimes (Matthew 23), indeed carried the instruments of avarice out of their temple as a lord (Matthew 21:12 and John 2).[^12] Therefore they accused him with a false report about a threatened power of ruin to the temple, that he could destroy that temple and in three days rebuild it (as is clear in Matthew 26, from Christ's saying, John 2:19: "Destroy this temple, and in three days I raise it up").[^13] Whence, as though they had this more at heart, they reproached the Lord crucified: "Vah," they say, "he that destroyeth the temple of God and in three days rebuildeth it," Matthew 27:40. "They thought rashly," says Augustine, "that the whole [doctrine]{g:doctrina} of Christ was against the temple and against their fathers' laws"; and therefore by the just judgment of God there befell them the end which they so perversely feared. For in the forty-second year after the ascension of the Lord, through Titus and Vespasian, they lost both nation and place for ever, and so that text of Matthew 8:12 was verified of them: "But the children of this kingdom shall be cast out into the exterior darkness."[^14] Whence they laid on him the more horrible species of heresy, which is blasphemy, by which they could extinguish him with more colour. In John 19:7 it is written: "We have a law; and according to the law he ought to die, because he made himself the Son of God." And the law of killing a blasphemer is clear from Leviticus 24:16.[^15]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 422.19: 8. My opponent grants that the Church is spread throughout the world, and cites Augustine.
- 423.5: Why does he not admit with Augustine that militant are triumphant and one Church?
- 423.24: The militant Church is circumscribed as to place.
- 424.4: 9. My opponent allows that the Church has substance and parts.
- 424.11: In denying that the devil is head of his members, he goes against holy doctors.
- 424.24: Iscariot is the type of foreknown bishops, as Peter is of the predestinate.
- 425.15: Augustine remarks that it was avarice that led the High Priests to conspire against Christ;
- 426.20: and to bring about the ruin that they feared.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Cum ... sit diffinitum loco* is taken as concessive ("although"): the doctor grants the church's diffusion although the body is bounded by place; causal "since" is also possible. **S.** Augustine, *De civitate Dei* 18.50, *primum se ab Jerusalem diffudit Ecclesia* (PL 41); Wyclif's 22.28 (on Plato, Labeo and Varro and the resurrection) does not fit and is given as he cites it. Loserth gives no reference (LEDGER E18c-1). **R.** A in the margin at 422.19: *Tercia responsio*. Loserth compares p. 115.

[^2]: **C.** A syllogism: no Christian through most of the world; no church where there is no Christian; therefore no church through most of the world. **R.** A in the margin at 422.25: *Ecclesia est diffusa per totum orbem et per maiorem partem orbis non est ecclesia*.

[^3]: **C.** *Secundum potestatem*: present by power, not in place; then every person of the church would be everywhere. *Unam esse ecclesiam ex istis*: that one church is made of the militant and the triumphant. **S.** Augustine, *Enchiridion* 64 (PL 40; LEDGER E18c-2); *Quaestiones Veteris et Novi Testamenti* 47, the work of Ambrosiaster under Augustine's name (Loserth: Aug. *Opp.* III, Append. p. 60; LEDGER E18c-3, editor). Both were cited at 418.4–5.

[^4]: **C.** *Non est racio quare ... quin*: there is no reason why the one holds and not the other. *Formale quo*: the formal principle by which the church is constituted. *Licet fuit valde disparis speciei*: the faith of the many believers, though unlike in species, yields one faith; so the predestinate, though unlike, make one bride. **G.** *Colorari*: "be given colour", a plausible ground (GLOSSARY *color*, *colorare*).

[^5]: **C.** *Circumscriptive*: in a place as a body is, bounded by it. *Corpus animatum racionale*: the definition of man, here applied to the church. **S.** Acts 4:32, an allusion, roman in the print. **R.** B in the margin at 423.27: *Ecclesia est corpus animatum racionale*.

[^6]: **C.** *Militantes ecclesie*: the church's members in the warfare. *Nec fingetur habere partes qualitativas, si non animas*: no qualitative parts can be supposed in the church unless they are souls. *Totum sit sue partes*: the whole is its parts. **R.** A in the margin at 424.4: *Quarta responsio*. Loserth compares p. 115. **T.** The print reads *novam* (plate n0461, 424.4; no variant). It does not fit the count of the opponent's arguments (side-note 424.4: '9.'), and 'ninth' follows *nonam* (our emendation).

[^7]: **S.** Gregory, *Homiliae in Evangelia* 1.16.1 (Loserth: *XL Homil. in Evang.* lib. I, hom. XVI, 1, *Opp.* I, p. 1492; LEDGER E18c-4): *iniquorum omnium caput diabolus est, et hujus capitis membra sunt omnes iniqui* (PL 76); Matthew 4:1, where Wyclif gives only the opening words. **R.** A in the margin at 424.11: *Diabolus est caput omnium suorum membrorum* (B: *omnium iniquorum*); at 424.13: *Gregorius*.

[^8]: **C.** *Summi pontifices*: "highest pontiffs" here, of the popes reckoned as such; of the Jewish priesthood at 425.20 the same words are "high priests". *Capitales*: "the chiefs", Peter after Christ, Iscariot after the devil, the prince of this world. **S.** Psalm 44:17 (DR "Instead of thy fathers, sons are born to thee"); John 12:31. **T.** At 424.25 B omits *prescitorum*. **R.** A in the margin at 424.22: *Sicut Petrus gessit typum omnium predestinatorum pastorum, sic Judas omnium prescitorum pastorum ipsum in avaricia sequencium*; B: *Petrus gessit typum pastorum predestinatorum et Scarioth pastorum iniquorum*.

[^9]: **C.** *Proprietarius*: one who holds property as his own, set against the propertyless. **S.** John 12:6 (the purse); Matthew 26:15. **T.** At 425.2 and 425.4 A reads *sciciunt* (corrected in the margin at 425.4 to *siciunt*).

[^10]: **S.** John 11:47–48: Wyclif adds *adversus Jesum* and reads *faciemus* for the Vulgate's *facimus* (DR "What do we"), and *consilium* for *concilium*. **R.** A in the margin at 425.8: *Avaricia sacerdotum fecit mortem Cristi*. **T.** At 425.9 B reads *lucra*.

[^11]: **S.** Augustine, *Tractatus in Iohannem* 49.27 (Loserth: *Opp.* III, ii, p. 629; LEDGER E18c-5, editor); Wyclif calls the tractate a homily. Zachary's course: Luke 1:5–9. **C.** *Nedum ... sed*: "not only ... but". *Versati fuerunt*: the high priests were turned over, changed within the year. **T.** At 425.20 B reads *variati fuerunt*. **R.** B in the margin at 425.18: *Nota bene quare pontifices et pharisei Christum fuerant persecuti*.

[^12]: **C.** The quotation is taken to run to *defenderet* (426.2); *Conceperant enim* is Wyclif's. *Dominanter*: "as a lord". **S.** Matthew 15:1–9; 23:13–33; 21:12; John 2:15. **T.** At 425.30 B reads *utraque*.

[^13]: **C.** *Minata* is ablative and agrees with *potestate* (or with *reportacione*), not with the genitive *ruine*: the power of destroying the temple that he was said to have threatened. **S.** Matthew 26:61; John 2:19, where the Vulgate reads *excitabo* (DR "I will raise it up"); Wyclif has the present *excito*. **T.** At 426.9 A adds *Ideo accusarunt ... potestate* in the last line of the page, and B sets it in the margin; at 426.10 B omits *de potestate*; B in the margin at 426.15: *de potestate*.

[^14]: **S.** Matthew 27:40: the Vulgate reads *Vah qui destruis templum Dei et in triduo illud reaedificas* (DR "Vah, thou that destroyest the temple of God and in three days dost rebuild it"); Wyclif gives it in the third person. Augustine again *Tract. in Ioh.* 49 (LEDGER E18c-5). Matthew 8:12: Wyclif adds *huius*. **T.** At 426.22 AA₁ read *Vaspasianum*, B *Vaspesianum*.

[^15]: **C.** *Imposuerunt sibi*: *sibi* for *ei*, "on him". *Coloracius*: "with more colour", a more plausible ground (GLOSSARY *color*). **S.** John 19:7; Leviticus 24:16. **R.** A in the margin at 426.26: *Christo fuit imposita blasphemia et heresis, ut cicius extingueretur*.
