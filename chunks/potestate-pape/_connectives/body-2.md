### 02-d ¶2 · 37.12–37.18  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 37] In tercio **autem** gradu sunt doctores qui seorsum ad partem sive scolastice sive private docent evangelizatum vel prophetizatum publice. Et illis correspondet donum fidei, cum debent constanter docere rudes instar Christi, qui domi docebat suas predicatas parabolas, ut patet Marc. IV.

ENGLISH: And in the third degree are the doctors, who apart, to one side, whether in the schools or privately, teach what has been preached as gospel or prophesied publicly. And to them corresponds the gift of faith, since they must steadfastly teach the unlearned after the likeness of Christ, who at home taught the parables he had preached, as is clear from Mark 4.[^3]

### 02-d ¶3 · 37.19–38.20  (7 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 37] Habito itaque pastu triplici secundum primam yerarchiam recipientem a Deo sibi tres gradus exemplariter ad primam yerarchiam celestem, consequenter ponit quomodo suffragatur virtus inferior. Et omnia ista manifestant quomodo Deus qui est spiritus rutilat varie in hominibus donis suis. Sicut enim fumus indicat ignem a quo efficitur, sic quelibet creatura habens variabiliter lumen virtutis sicut radius dicit lucem. Ideo Apostolus vocat hec dona manifestacionem spiritus. Alii **autem** habent virtutem ut Deus faciat per eos mirabilia; per hoc enim spiritus pascitur et virtus anime roboratur, **sed** hoc est modicum in comparacione ad priora, sicut modicum sit quod Spiritus Sanctus apparuit apostolis in linguis igneis, nisi eos illustrasset interius; [p. 38] talem **autem** virtutem habuerunt patres in primitiva ecclesia plus vel minus. **Sed** non ascribebant sibi virtutes nisi ut organis ex humilitate magis capacibus. In secundo vero gradu secunde yerarchie est gracia curandi corporaliter infirmos, et sic duo sensus extremi, scilicet visus et tactus, in ordine ad animam erant pasti. Unus **autem** sensus quoad ordinem dignitatis et propinquitatem ad spiritum est prior, **sed** alius sensus genere, generalitate et carnis propinquitate preponitur. **Sed** alie due habent virtutem opitulandi egentibus, pascendi medios cunctos sensus; et hoc fit firmitate fidei, qua auditur, adoratur et gustatur Christus in membris. Apostolus enim corporalis elemosyne pascit tres sensus medios quo auditur vox Christi Matth. XXV, 40: Quamdiu uni de minimis meis fecistis, mihi fecistis, tunc enim Christi bonus odor sumus Deo, si gustamus internam Christi dulcedinem.

ENGLISH: Having thus [treated] the threefold feeding according to the first hierarchy, which receives from God three degrees on the pattern of the first heavenly hierarchy, he next sets out how the lower [power]{g:virtus} [aids]{g:suffragari}.[^4] And all these show how God, who is a spirit, shines variously in men by his gifts. For as smoke indicates the fire by which it is produced, so every creature that has the light of power in varying measure declares the light, as a ray does. Therefore the Apostle calls these gifts "the manifestation of the Spirit".[^5] And others have the power that God works wonders through them; for by this the spirit is fed and the power of the soul strengthened, but this is little in comparison with the former, just as it would be little that the Holy Spirit appeared to the apostles in tongues of fire, had he not enlightened them inwardly; [38] and such power the fathers in the primitive [church]{g:ecclesia} had, more or less. But they ascribed the powers to themselves only as to instruments made more capable by humility. And in the second degree of the second hierarchy is the grace of healing the sick bodily, and so the two outermost senses, namely sight and touch, were fed in their order to the soul. And the one sense, as to the order of dignity and nearness to the spirit, is prior, but the other sense is put before it in kind, in generality and in nearness to the flesh. But the other two have the power of helping the needy, of feeding all the middle senses; and this is done by the firmness of faith, by which Christ is heard, worshipped and tasted in his members.[^6] For the apostle of bodily alms feeds the three middle senses, in that the voice of Christ is heard, Matthew 25:40: "As long as you did it to one of my least, you did it to me"; for then "we are the good odour of Christ unto God" (2 Corinthians 2:15), if we taste the inward sweetness of Christ.[^7]

### 02-d ¶4 · 38.21–39.5  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 38] **Sed** quoad terciam yerarchiam, qua populus a prepositis regulatur, Deus dedit quibusdam gubernaciones sicut prepositis utriusque membrorum ecclesie; ad quod requiritur discrecio donorum spiritualium et per consequens spirituum movencium ad illud officium, ponendo; servando et protegendo quemlibet in illo gradu, ad quem Deus per dona sua disposuit; hoc enim maxime pertinet ad officium prelatorum. Et quia homo est naturaliter animal communicativum in sermonibus et civile, ideo oportet secundo quod talis prepositure curatus habeat genera linguarum, [p. 39] quibus communicabit cum subditis, quia aliter foret eis barbarus. Nec hoc sufficit, cum avis posset voces formare, ideo requiritur ultimo interpretacio sermonum, nam viva vox habet ex cooperacione spiritus multum latentis energie.

ENGLISH: Now as to the third hierarchy, by which the people is ruled by [superiors]{g:prepositi}, God gave to some governments, as to the superiors of both [kinds] of the church's [members]{g:membrum}; for this is required the discerning of spiritual gifts, and consequently of the spirits that move [men] to that office, by placing, keeping and protecting each one in that degree to which God has disposed him by his gifts; for this pertains most of all to the office of [prelates]{g:prelatus}.[^8] And since man is by nature an animal that communicates in speech, and a civil one, therefore it is needful secondly that the [curate]{g:curatus} of such an [office of superior]{g:prepositura} have kinds of tongues, [39] by which he will communicate with his subjects, since otherwise he would be a barbarian to them. Nor is this enough, since a bird could form sounds; therefore lastly the interpretation of speeches is required, for the living voice has, from the cooperation of the spirit, much hidden energy.[^9]

### 02-d ¶5 · 39.6–39.23  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 39] Et aptantur hec novem dona spiritualia ad membra humani corporis, ita quod trinarius prime yerarchie correspondeat cerebro, in quo sunt tres ventriculi vasa lucidorum spirituum, et quinque sensuum interiorum, quibus sapiencia, id est Dei noticia, tamquam sol vel dies ad intra percipitur. **Sed** organa quinque spirituum situatorum in capite figurant secundum spiritum dicentem scienciam mundanorum, collum vero quod est organum vociferandi signat donum fidei ex auditu, **sed** in regione pectoralium cor, stomachus et pulmo ex proprietate correspondent secundo trinario. In regione vero nutritivorum splen et intestina correspondent tercie yerarchie, ymo sicut Apostolus factus est omnia omnibus, ut omnia lucrifaceret; sic non est membrum ecclesie, quin pertinenter possit cuicunque membro corporis cooperari.

ENGLISH: And these nine spiritual gifts are fitted to the members of the human body, so that the triad of the first hierarchy corresponds to the brain, in which are three ventricles, vessels of the lucid spirits and of the five inner senses, by which wisdom, that is, the knowledge of God, is perceived inwardly as the sun or the day. But the organs of the five spirits situated in the head figure the second, the spirit that speaks the knowledge of worldly things; and the neck, which is the organ of uttering, signifies the gift of faith that comes by hearing; but in the region of the breast the heart, stomach and lung correspond by their property to the second triad.[^10] And in the region of the nutritive [members] the spleen and intestines correspond to the third hierarchy; indeed, as the Apostle "became all things to all", that he might gain all, so there is no member of the church that cannot fittingly cooperate with any member of the body whatever.[^11]

### 02-d ¶6 · 39.24–40.17  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 39] Triplex tamen conveniencia et triplex differencia est notanda. Sicut enim membra constituunt unum corpus cui anima copulatur, et iterum sicut omne membrum est cuicunque necessarium, cum iuvant se reciproce in suis officiis, sic est de membris ecclesie ex vi communionis et vinculo caritatis. Tercio vero sicut membra se habent in suo regimine, sic et membra ecclesie, cum secundum Crist. Omel. XXII Operis Imperfecti homo sit liber quidam, cui tota religio christiana [p. 40] inscribitur. Sicut igitur est affinitas capitis ad pedes, sic racio et affeccio connectuntur, sicut eciam omne membrum nobile vel ignobile sine contencione servit spiritui, sic omne membrum ecclesie sine contencione minoritatis et obediencie servit Christo, et sicut membra preeminencia non de sua nobilitate superbiunt, **sed** faciunt suum officium secundum regimen anime ad suffragium singulorum, sic debet esse de membris ecclesie; oculi quidem et facies carent tali tegimine, ne velatum deturpent et preparent ad ruinam: sic Christus et apostoli ex fervore caritatis et alienacione a fervore cupiditatis non erant temporalibus seculariter involuti. **Sed** membra minus nobilia ut pudenda sunt absconsius, multiplicius et tenerius cooperta, et sic est de contemptibilibus per quos feces ecclesie expurgantur.

ENGLISH: Yet a threefold agreement and a threefold difference must be noted. For as the members make up one body to which the soul is joined, and again as every member is necessary to every other, since they help one another in their offices, so it is with the members of the church by force of communion and the bond of [charity]{g:caritas}. And thirdly, as the members behave in their governance, so too do the members of the church, since according to Chrysostom, homily 22 of the *Opus imperfectum*, "man is a kind of book in which the whole Christian religion [40] is written".[^12] As therefore there is an affinity of the head to the feet, so reason and affection are joined; and as every member, noble or ignoble, serves the spirit without contention, so every member of the church serves Christ without contention over [lesser standing]{g:minoritas} and obedience; and as the members of preeminence do not grow proud of their nobility but do their office according to the governance of the soul for the [aid]{g:suffragium} of each, so it should be with the members of the church.[^13] The eyes indeed and the face lack such covering, lest a veil disfigure them and prepare them for ruin: so Christ and the apostles, from the fervour of charity and estrangement from the fervour of covetousness, were not entangled in [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} in a worldly way. But the less noble members, like the private parts, are covered more hiddenly, more manifoldly and more tenderly, and so it is with the contemptible persons by whom the dregs of the church are purged.[^14]

### 02-d ¶7 · 40.18–40.30  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 40] Alii **autem** dividunt membra ecclesie in spiritualia vel dextera sicut clerum et membra corporalia vel sinistra ut laycos, et prima pars que debet esse ut tectum domus ecclesie, eo quod non capit in se incisionem dominii, remanet indivisa. **Sed** secunda pars dividitur in prepositos et operarios ut parietes et fundamentum ecclesie, et sic habemus tres yerarchias ecclesie cum suis subdivisionibus, in partem infimam, mediam et supremam. Alii **autem** ex textu apostoli trahunt novem partes ecclesie ad novem partes simplices et spermaticas in humano corpore et alii aptant ipsas ad organa propter conveniencias naturales.

ENGLISH: And others divide the members of the church into spiritual or right-hand, such as the [clergy]{g:clerus}, and bodily or left-hand, such as the laity; and the first part, which ought to be like the roof of the house of the church, because it does not admit into itself the cutting up of [lordship]{g:dominium}, remains undivided. But the second part is divided into superiors and workers, like the walls and foundation of the church, and so we have three hierarchies of the church with their subdivisions, into a lowest, a middle and a highest part. And others draw from the Apostle's text nine parts of the church [answering] to the nine simple and spermatic parts in the human body, and others fit them to the organs on account of natural correspondences.[^15]

### 02-e ¶1 · 40.31–42.5  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 40] **Sed** quomodocunque loquimur, est notanda diversitas inter membra istorum corporum. Prima, cum partes ecclesie continuantur per graciam, non respiciunt posicionem vel ubicacionem situs [p. 41] corporei ut membra hominis; secunda cum membra sint mistica, non repugnat **sed** convenit quod unum membrum habeat diversorum generum officia; homo enim est quasi universitas, ideo sibi convenit instanter agere quantum potest. Tercia diversitas est, licet membra ecclesie habeant virtutes influxas a Christo, sicut membra corporis habent virtutes influxas ab anima, a quibus ponuntur in esse membrorum, tamen influencia prior et membrorum operacio est voluntaria et graciosa et meritoria, et secundo influencia anime et membrorum operacio sunt naturales et permanencius perdurantes; licet enim membrum ecclesie posset facillime mutari ex sua vertibilitate ab uno membro in aliud vel ex variacione operis desinere esse membrum vivum, hoc tamen non potest contingere ex differencia spiritus influentis, unde quia tota materia ista est insensibilis, est plena illusione zophistica. **Sed** quantum ad ultimum scilicet perdicionem potencie, videtur, cum potestas illa sit licencia operandi, quod generaliter perditur per mortale, cum sit illicitum tali aliquid operari. Et ista videtur sentencia beati Gregorii posita in decretis XI, q. III: Ipse, inquit, ligandi atque solvendi potestate se privat, qui hanc pro suis voluntatibus et non subditorum moribus exercet. Et sequitur: Iudicare digne de subditis nequeunt, qui in subditorum causis sua vel odio vel gracia sequuntur. Tunc, inquit vera est absolucio presidentis, cum interni sequitur arbitrium iudicis, quod bene quadriduani mortui resuscitacio illa signat, quia prius Dominus mortuum suscitavit [p. 42] et vivificavit dicens: Lazare, veni foras et postmodum is, qui vivus fuerat, egressus a discipulis est solutus. Et patet cum cursu ecclesie quomodo maior pars ecclesie tam in foro penitencie quam iuris caret potestate iudicii.

ENGLISH: But however we speak, a diversity must be noted between the members of these bodies.[^1] The first: since the parts of the [church]{g:ecclesia} are continuous by grace, they do not regard the position or location of bodily [41] place, as the members of a man do; the second: since the members are mystical, it is not repugnant but fitting that one [member]{g:membrum} have offices of diverse kinds; for man is as it were a universe, therefore it befits him to act urgently as much as he can. The third diversity is that, although the members of the church have powers [flowed in]{g:influere} from Christ, as the members of the body have powers flowed in from the soul, by which they are set in the being of members, yet the former influence and the members' operation is voluntary, gracious and meritorious, and in the second the influence of the soul and the operation of the members are natural and endure more permanently; for although a member of the church could very easily be changed, by its changeability, from one member into another, or by a variation of its work cease to be a living member, yet this cannot happen from a difference in the spirit flowing in; hence, since this whole matter is [imperceptible]{g:insensibilis}, it is full of sophistical illusion.[^2] But as to the last [point], namely the loss of [power]{g:potencia}, it seems, since that [power]{g:potestas} is a licence to act, that it is generally lost by mortal [sin], since it is unlawful for such a one to do anything.[^3] And this seems to be the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of blessed Gregory placed in the Decretum, C. 11 q. 3: "He deprives himself," he says, "of the power of binding and loosing, who exercises it for his own wishes and not for the conduct of his subjects." And it follows: "They cannot judge worthily of their subjects, who in their subjects' cases follow their own hatred or favour." "Then," he says, "is the absolution of the one presiding true, when it follows the judgement of the inward judge; which that raising of the man four days dead well signifies, for the Lord first raised the dead man [42] and gave him life, saying, 'Lazarus, come forth', and afterwards he who had come out alive was loosed by the disciples."[^4] And it is clear from the course of the church how the greater part of the church, both in the [court of penance]{g:forum-penitencie} and in that of law, lacks the power of judgement.[^5]

### 02-e ¶2 · 42.6–42.16  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 42] Confirmatur secundo ex hoc quod potestas talis cum dativo a Deo ad tam excellens ministerium, est quoddam singulare privilegium. **Sed** secundum beatum Gregorium ibidem: privilegium omnino meretur amittere qui permissa sibi abutitur potestate. Cum igitur quilibet potentatus abutitur sua potestate, dum illam exercuerit in mortali, videtur quod meretur potestatem talem amittere, et cum Deus qui dat insensibiliter potestatem non defert meritum sive dignum, videtur quod eo ipso Deus aufert ab homine potestatem.

ENGLISH: It is confirmed secondly from this, that such power, [given] by God for so excellent a ministry, is a certain singular [privilege]{g:privilegium}.[^6] Now according to blessed Gregory in the same place, "he deserves to lose his privilege altogether, who abuses the power permitted him". Since therefore every [potentate]{g:potentatus} abuses his power while he exercises it in mortal [sin], it seems that he deserves to lose such power; and since God, who gives power imperceptibly, does not defer what is merited or deserved, it seems that by that very fact God takes the power away from the man.[^7]

### 02-e ¶3 · 42.17–42.33  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 42] Item, Deus potest auferre licenciam vel potestatem ab homine pro delicto, **sed** quando auferret graciosus dominus si non quando se ipsum dampnificat per abusum ad quod non habet licenciam? ideo cum nemo potest abuti tali licencia nec virtute, videtur quod Gregorius equivocat vocando potestatem, posse vel potenciam aut liberum arbitrium, et alias vocat potestatem amissam ipsam licenciam. Et idem videtur ex decreto beati Augustini posito XXIV, q. prima, ubi dicit omnes hereticos, id est, peccato mortali ab ecclesia divisos, non habere aliquid potestatis. **Sed** notandum est; ut alias declaravi, quod opus heretici vertitur dignis ad commodum, sicut Deus per se supplet, quando prepositi excommunicati a Deo subditis suis deficiunt.

ENGLISH: Again, God can take away a licence or power from a man for a fault; but when would a gracious lord take it away, if not when the man harms himself by an abuse for which he has no licence? Therefore, since no one can abuse such a licence or [power]{g:virtus}, it seems that Gregory speaks [equivocally]{g:equivoce}, calling "power" the capacity or power or [free choice]{g:liberum-arbitrium}, and elsewhere calling the power that is lost the licence itself.[^8] And the same seems [to follow] from the decree of blessed Augustine placed at C. 24 q. 1, where he says that "all heretics", "that is, those divided from the church by mortal sin, have nothing of power".[^9] But it must be noted, as I have declared elsewhere, that the work of a heretic is turned to the profit of the worthy, as God supplies by himself when [superiors]{g:prepositi} excommunicated by God fail their subjects.[^10]

### 02-e ¶4 · 43.1–43.9  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 43] Et ex istis videtur quod potestas predestinacionis (de qua Joh. primo et sepe alibi) est maxima potestas, quam Deus communicat viatori. Potestas **autem**, quam Deus dat beato ad plene fruendum dominio, et in ipso qualis creatura est potestas hominis consumata, de qua quidam intelligunt illud Matth. ult: Data est mihi omnis potestas in celo et in terra, beati enim habent fructum cuiuslibet potestatis.

ENGLISH: And from these things it seems that the power of predestination (of which John 1 and often elsewhere) is the greatest power that God communicates to the [wayfarer]{g:viator}.[^11] And the power which God gives to the blessed for the full enjoyment of [lordship]{g:dominium}, and in him, such as a creature is, is the consummated power of man, of which some understand that of the last chapter of Matthew: "All power is given to me in heaven and in earth"; for the blessed have the fruit of every power.[^12]

### 03-a ¶1 · 43.11–45.12  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 43] Habito generali sermone de potestate membrorum ecclesie cum suis subditis et eorum singulis superest videre de potestate summi sacerdotis, quem alii vocant papam et alii Romanum pontificem. Et quia tota moderna ecclesia exemplata est ab ecclesia peregrinante pro tempore Christi, ideo restat videre in isto explicari, quis apostolorum post ascensionem Christi primatum tenuit; et arguit dominus Ardmacanus VII libro De Questionibus Armenorum viginti racionibus quod Petrus inter omnes habuit preeminenciam capitalem, primo per illud Math. XX, quando facta est contencio inter discipulos Jesu, quis illorum videtur esse maior, in determinando questionem docet ut qui maior est eorum fiat ut minor; quod dictum implicat aliquem esse inter eos maiorem ceteris. Unde postquam ostenderit quod maioritas, quam ipse definitive diligit [p. 44] attenter penes maioritatem mitis et humilis ministerii vertitur verbum ad Symonem: Ecce, inquit, sathanas expetivit vos, ut cribraret sicut triticum, ego **autem** rogavi pro te, ut non deficiat fides tua, et tu aliquando conversus confirma fratres tuos. Ecce post generalem solucionem facta est particularis applicacio preminencie ad Petrum, cum non aliter pertinenter magister optimus decideret questionem sedando brigam et instruendo eos in regulis religionis sue, quas in posterum observaverunt; unde relinquens exemplum suis; ne superbiant de prepositura ecclesiastica, **sed** pocius timeant propter annexum periculum, sub quodam velamine includente periculum adiutorium atque officium insinuat Petro primatum. Per cribrum intelligunt doctores ad sensum misticum naturam lapsam corpoream. Ipsa enim est quasi caminus temptacionis, in qua spiritus humanus colliditur, sicut in cribro triticum ventilatur. Curvitas ligni signat declinacionem hominis a rectitudine originalis iusticie per peccatum. Tegule dicto ligno infixe que se intersecant ex adverso sunt versucie diaboli et fraus mundi, quibus sensualitati infixis spiritus temptacionibus periculosis concutitur, et eo timorosius quo gradus prepositure est alcior; foramina vero spatulis intercepta sunt genera virtutum, quibus temptatus cognoscit humiliter vitam suam esse ventum aut vacuum. Per istas siquidem rimas patet temptati subterfugium, ut ex gracia et gravedine humilitatis evadat collisionis hostilis periculum, si fuerit horreandus in terra vivencium, quia aliter ventus superbie exsufflat [p. 45] ipsum ab area virtutum comburendum ut paleam vel bestiis civitatis dyaboli devorandum. Expetivit igitur sathanas ut post percussionem pastoris posset eos temptando concutere vento superbie, perplexitate diffidencie et aliis collusionibus spiritualibus, non ad expurgacionem predestinatorum, licet hoc sciverit, **sed** ad dampnificandum prescitos relictos ut palea, quod obtinuit in Juda. Et sic licencia concessa a Deo sathane ad temptandum apostolos preter eius propositum sicut in beato Job cessit eis ad commodum, cum erant cribrati sicut triticum.

ENGLISH: Having had a general discourse on the [power]{g:potestas} of the [members]{g:membrum} of the [church]{g:ecclesia} with their subjects, and of each of them, it remains to see about the power of the highest priest, whom some call [pope]{g:papa} and others [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex}. And since the whole modern church is patterned on the church as it was a pilgrim in the time of Christ, it therefore remains to see set out in this [place] which of the apostles held the primacy after Christ's ascension; and [the lord of Armagh]{g:ardmacanus} argues in book 7 of the *De questionibus Armenorum*, by twenty reasons, that Peter had among all a preeminence as head:[^1] first by that of Matthew 20, when a contention arose among the disciples of Jesus which of them seemed to be the greater, in settling the question he teaches "that he who is the greater among them should become as the lesser"; which saying implies that someone among them was greater than the rest.[^2] Hence, after he had shown that the [greatness]{g:majoritas} which he definitively loves [44] [is weighed] attentively by the greatness of a meek and humble ministry, the word is turned to Simon: "Behold," he says, "Satan hath desired to have you, that he may sift you as wheat. But I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not: and thou, being once converted, confirm thy brethren."[^3] Behold, after the general solution a particular application of preeminence to Peter was made, since otherwise the best master would not have decided the question fittingly, settling the quarrel and instructing them in the rules of his religion, which they observed afterwards; hence, leaving an example to his own, that they should not grow proud of an ecclesiastical [office of superior]{g:prepositura} but rather fear on account of the peril annexed to it, under a certain veil that includes peril, help and office he intimates the primacy to Peter. By the sieve the doctors understand, in the mystical sense, fallen bodily nature. For it is as it were a furnace of temptation, in which the human spirit is dashed about, as wheat is tossed in a sieve. The curve of the wood signifies man's turning aside from the rectitude of original justice through sin. The laths fixed in the said wood, which cross one another from opposite sides, are the wiles of the devil and the fraud of the world; with these fixed in sensuality the spirit is shaken by perilous temptations, and the more fearfully the higher the degree of the office of superior; and the holes caught between the slats are the kinds of virtues, by which the tempted man knows humbly that his life is wind or emptiness.[^4] Through these chinks, indeed, a way of escape lies open to the tempted, so that by grace and the weight of humility he may escape the peril of the enemy's collision, if he is to be gathered into the barn in the land of the living, since otherwise the wind of pride blows [45] him off the threshing floor of virtues, to be burned like chaff or devoured by the beasts of the devil's city.[^5] Satan therefore desired that after the striking of the shepherd he might shake them, by tempting, with the wind of pride, the [perplexity]{g:perplexitas} of distrust and other spiritual collusions, not for the purging of [the predestinate]{g:predestinatus}, though he knew this [would follow], but to harm [the foreknown]{g:prescitus} left behind like chaff, which he obtained in Judas.[^6] And so the licence granted by God to Satan to tempt the apostles turned, beyond his purpose, to their profit, as in blessed Job, since they were sifted as wheat.[^7]

### 03-a ¶2 · 45.13–47.8  (7 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 45] **Sed** secundo, ut cognoscat ecclesiam non a se hoc habere, **sed** a fide et gracia domini Jesu Christi, ut vel sic postposita superbia celebret eius laudem, statim subiungit: Ego **autem** rogavi pro te, ut non deficiat fides tua in quo videtur innuere primatum Petri in hoc quod nomine suo exprimit totam ecclesiam militantem; semper enim manet fides ecclesie in una persona vel in alia, sicut in sancto sabbato, quando defecit in Petro; servata fuerat in Maria. Ista igitur negacio accomoda, verificans dictum Christi, docet quod nomine suo tamquam capitanei expressit ecclesiam. **Sed** tercio (ut exposui in capitulo V huius) limitat prelacie officium, cum subinfert: Et tu aliquando conversus confirma fratres tuos quod officium post Joh. XXI, 17 exponit expressius, cum sibi singulariter iniungit tribus vicibus pascere oves suas; unde Joh. XX, 1 9 dicitur quod apostoli audito a Magdalena sublatum esse corpus Domini Petrus et Johannes, in quibus fides remissius est extincta, cucurrerunt, ut haberent experienciam [p. 46] rei gestae. Et quamvis Johannes, virgo et sterilis, synagogam singnificans, precucurrit citius Petro, tamen Petrus ex fervore amoris in effectu posito introivit et ab experiencia cum inspiracione interna edoctus resurreccionem credidit, et sic Johannem qui fuit post eum precipuus confirmavit. Unde sequitur in textu: Tunc inquit, introivit et ille discipulus, qui venerat primus ad monumentum, et vidit et credidit; nondum enim sciebant scripturam, quia oportuit eum a mortuis resurgere. Quamvis enim Johannes quoad devocionem amoris contemplantis fervencius Petro Christum dilexerit, tamen Petrus quantum ad amorem practicum plus ceteris apostolis diligebat. In cuius signum Johannes, tamquam speculativus ad percipiendum archana Dei magis dispositus; de Jesu stante in littore ad signandum cursum mundane viacionis sibi transactum, prius percepit quam Petrus, quia dominus est, ut dicitur Joh. ultimo, verumtamen Petrus, audito quia dominus est, misit se fervencius in mare. Et propter magnitudinem istius amoris practici et experienciam fragilitatis humane, cum ipsemet ter negavit Christum Math. XXVI, fuit racioni consonum, ut ipse preficeretur caput ecclesie, Johannes **autem** virgo et contemplativus a mundo abstractus habuit custodiam matris et virginis que fuit locale sponsi preciosissimum. Et sic habuit signum preeminencie amoris in hoc, quod de tam prope quietus factus est celestis coniugii paranymphus, et preter hec habuit cum ceteris apostolis generalem custodiam matris ecclesie; **sed** quadam prerogativa Petrus. Et sic secundum unam racionem amandi videtur catholicum quod Johannes plus Petro dilexit celeste coniugium [p. 47] et sine huiusmodi repugnancia quoad aliam racionem amandi Petrus plus hiis diligens plus dilexit Christum atque ecclesiam quam Johannes; et proporcionaliter erant a sponso ecclesie readamati; racione cuius Johannes tam crebro dicit, non quod Jesus eum maxime diligebat; ut patet Joh. XIX, XX et XXI, **sed** quod eum quodam amoris privilegio precipue diligebat.

ENGLISH: But secondly, that he may know that the church has this not of itself but from the faith and grace of the Lord Jesus Christ, so that thus at least, setting pride aside, he may celebrate his praise, he immediately adds: "But I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not"; in which he seems to hint at Peter's primacy in this, that under his name he expresses the whole [church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans}; for the faith of the church always remains in one person or another, as on Holy Saturday, when it failed in Peter, it had been kept in Mary.[^8] This fitting negation, therefore, verifying Christ's saying, teaches that he expressed the church under his name as under a [captain]{g:capitaneus}'s.[^9] But thirdly (as I set out in chapter 5 of this [work]) he [assigns]{g:limitare} the office of prelacy, when he adds: "And thou, being once converted, confirm thy brethren"; which office he sets out more expressly afterwards, John 21:17, when he enjoins on him singly, three times, "to feed his sheep";[^10] hence in John 20:1–9 it is said that, when the apostles had heard from Magdalene that "the body of the Lord" had been "taken away", Peter and John, in whom faith was less fully extinguished, ran, that they might have experience [46] of what had been done.[^11] And although John, virgin and barren, signifying the synagogue, "did outrun" Peter "more swiftly", yet Peter, from the fervour of love put into effect, went in, and, taught by experience together with inward inspiration, believed the resurrection, and so confirmed John, who was the chief after him. Hence it follows in the text: "Then," he says, "that disciple also went in, who came first to the sepulchre: and he saw and believed. For as yet they knew not the scripture, that he must rise again from the dead." For although John, as to the devotion of contemplative love, loved Christ more fervently than Peter, yet Peter, as to practical love, loved more than the other apostles. In sign of which John, as one speculative and more disposed to perceive the secrets of God, perceived sooner than Peter, of "Jesus standing on the shore" (to signify that the course of worldly [wayfaring]{g:viacio} was over for him), that it is the Lord, as is said in the last chapter of John; nevertheless Peter, having heard that it is the Lord, cast himself more fervently into the sea.[^12] And on account of the greatness of this practical love and his experience of human frailty, since he himself thrice denied Christ (Matthew 26), it was consonant with reason that he should be set over [it] as [head]{g:caput} of the church; and John, virgin and contemplative, withdrawn from the world, had the keeping of the mother and virgin, who was the bridegroom's most precious dwelling. And so he had a sign of preeminence of love in this, that, from resting so near, he was made groomsman of the heavenly marriage; and besides these things he had, with the other apostles, the general keeping of mother church; but Peter [had it] by a certain prerogative.[^13] And so, according to one way of loving, it seems catholic that John loved the heavenly marriage more than Peter, [47] and, without any such conflict, as to another way of loving, Peter, loving "more than these", loved Christ and the church more than John; and in proportion they were loved in return by the bridegroom of the church; by reason of which John says so often, not that Jesus loved him most, as is clear from John 19, 20 and 21, but that he loved him especially by a certain privilege of love.[^14]

### 03-a ¶3 · 47.9–47.26  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 47] Ex ista ranga potest pius theologus tamquam fundamento colligere quod Petrus habuit quendam primatum apostolice dignitatis; secundo confirmatur idem ex serie verborum sacre scripture et prerogativa pre ceteris data Petro. Nam Math. X, 2 sic dicitur: Duodecim apostolorum nomina sunt hec Primus Symon, qui dicitur Petrus, et Andreas frater eius. Cum **autem** nec ordo verborum nec sillaba in scriptura ponatur, quin sit omissa sentencia, quid pertinencius daretur intelligi; quod Petrus dicitur primus apostolorum, nisi quod ipse singulariter tenet primatum apostolice dignitatis; cum vocacione Andreas Petrum precesserit, ut patet Joh. I. Non igitur ex indifferencia preposuit Christus Petrum in vocacione; tercio; ut patet Math. X et Luce VI, ymo communiter connumerando apostolos Petrus preponitur ab eo, qui dixit et facta sunt.

ENGLISH: From this ranking a pious theologian can gather, as from a foundation, that Peter had a certain primacy of apostolic dignity; secondly the same is confirmed from the order of the words of holy scripture and the prerogative given to Peter before the rest.[^15] For in Matthew 10:2 it is said thus: "The names of the twelve apostles are these: The first, Simon who is called Peter, and Andrew his brother." And since neither an order of words nor a syllable is placed in scripture without some teaching being put forth in it, what more fitting could be given to be understood by Peter's being called the first of the apostles, than that he singly holds the primacy of apostolic dignity, since in the calling Andrew preceded Peter, as is clear from John 1?[^16] Not without distinction, therefore, did Christ put Peter first in the naming; thirdly, as is clear from Matthew 10 and Luke 6, indeed in commonly enumerating the apostles Peter is put first by him who "spoke, and they were made".[^17]

### 03-b ¶1 · 47.27–49.31  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 47] Quantum ad privilegia dignitatis ecclesie Math. XVI; 18— 19 post confessionem Petri de filiacione Christi naturali, respondit Christus, quasi pro arris mercedis fidei: Et ego dico tibi quia tu es Petrus, et super hanc petram edificabo ecclesiam meam et porte inferi non prevalebunt adversus eam, et tibi dabo claves regni celorum, et quodcunque ligaveris super terram erit ligatum et in [p. 48] celis, et quodcunque solveris super terram, erit solutum et in celis. **Sed** post Math. XVIII et Joh. XX dat apostolis communiter potestatem. **Sed** nusquam legitur in scriptura quod alteri quam Petro dedit dominus claves celi; per hoc igitur quod Petrus ex approbacione Christi respondit pro omnibus in precipua materia fidei, que est fundamentum spirituali preposito, et per hoc quod Christus mutato nomine a Symone in Petrum quod nomen contraxit a se ipso, qui est Petra et fundamentum ecclesie, ut patet I. Corinth. X et I. Corinth. Ill, videtur concedere Petro quandam prerogativam super ceteros apostolos ad regendam suam ecclesiam, ac si diceret secundum exposicionem Augustini De Verbis Domini sermone XIII: Ego sum petra, (quia Christus) a qua tu es Petrus, id est christianus in fide firmus, inmediatus mei vicarius, super quam petram, quam confessus es, per te ut proximum eius capitaneum fundabo ecclesiam meam. Ideo, dicit Augustinus, a petra petrus non a petro petra, sicut a Christo Christianus, non a Christiano Christus. Ideo super me edificabo ecclesiam meam, non super te, nam volentes edificare super homines dicebant: Ego quidem sum Pauli, ego quidem Apollo, ego vero Cephe, **sed** apostolus dicit se esse lapidis, qui est Christus. Cum igitur Christus nominat Petrum primo nomine suo; videtur concedere primitatem, ideo dicit Augustinus quod apostolatus tenuit principatum in signum cuius possessionis et preeminencie subdit Christus: Dabo tibi post mortem meam claves ecclesie, quam fortificabo [p. 49] super ecclesiam malignancium, dando tibi potestatem ligandi atque solvendi; ut non superflue occupes claves ecclesie, quas dedi tibi racione confessionis meritorie humanitatis et deitatis qua a Spiritu Sancto edoctus dixisti, tu es Christus et per consequens verus homo filius Dei vivi; quod non fuisset pertinenter additum, nisi intelligeretur de filio naturali. Et ista confessio est maxime pertinens christiano preposito, ut patet Joh. IV; eo quod maxime obviat Antichristo, cum ipse negabit divinitatem et infatuabit; quantum in ipso erit, Christi pauperiem et voluntariam passionem. Ubi enim nos christiani docemur a Christo et ecclesia expetere constanciam, prospera mundi despicere et nulla eius adversa formidare, Antichristus econtra cum multis suis docet homines ad mundi prospera aspirare et penalitates contempnere, unde Petrus, quia dixit Christo; absit hoc a te Domine ut vadas Jerusalem crucifigi, vocatus est ab eo Sathanas paulo post hoc privilegium singulare, ut patet Math. XVI; 23, ad denotandum quod ipse est potissime Christo adversans tamquam alter Antichristus, qui in paupertate et penalitate abnuit sequi Christum. Unde isto scuto fidei armatus est Petrus postmodum, cum pro Christo reliquit uxorem et omnia, sequendo ipsum Math. XIX, 27, pro Christo promisit se mori percuciendo servum pontificis Joh. XVIII, 10; pro Christo exposuit se in aquam Math. XIV et Joh. ultimo et demum in capite mundi Roma pro Christo pertulit mortem acerbam.

ENGLISH: As to the privileges of the dignity of the [church]{g:ecclesia}: in Matthew 16:18–19, after Peter's confession of Christ's natural sonship, Christ answered, as if in earnest of the wages of faith: "And I say to thee: That thou art Peter; and upon this rock I will build my church, and the gates of hell shall not prevail against it. And I will give to thee the keys of the kingdom of heaven. And whatsoever thou shalt bind upon earth, it shall be bound also in [48] heaven: and whatsoever thou shalt loose upon earth, it shall be loosed also in heaven."[^1] But afterwards, in Matthew 18 and John 20, he gives [power]{g:potestas} to the apostles in common. Now nowhere is it read in scripture that the Lord gave the keys of heaven to another than Peter; by this, therefore, that Peter with Christ's approval answered for all in the chief matter of faith, which is the foundation for a spiritual [superior]{g:prepositi}, and by this, that Christ changed his name from Simon to Peter, a name he drew from himself, who is the Rock and foundation of the church, as is clear from 1 Corinthians 10 and 1 Corinthians 3, he seems to grant Peter a certain prerogative over the other apostles for ruling his church, as if he said, according to the exposition of Augustine, *De verbis Domini*, sermon 13: "I am the rock (since [I am] Christ), from which you are Peter, that is, a Christian firm in faith, my immediate [vicar]{g:vicarius-christi}, upon which rock, which you have confessed, I will found my church through you as its nearest [captain]{g:capitaneus}."[^2] Therefore, says Augustine, "from *petra* comes *Petrus*, not *petra* from *Petrus*, as Christian from Christ, not Christ from Christian. Therefore upon me will I build my church, not upon thee; for those who wished to build upon men said: I indeed am of Paul, I indeed of Apollo, and I of Cephas; but the apostle says that he is of the stone, which is Christ."[^3] Since therefore Christ names Peter first by his own name, he seems to grant him firstness; therefore Augustine says that "he held the principate of the apostolate", in sign of which possession and preeminence Christ adds: "I will give thee", after my death, "the keys of the church", which I will fortify [49] over the church of the malignant, giving you the power of binding and loosing; so that you may not hold idly the keys of the church which I have given you by reason of the meritorious confession of humanity and [Godhead]{g:deitas}, by which, taught by the Holy Spirit, you said, "Thou art Christ", and consequently true man, "the Son of the living God"; which would not have been fittingly added unless it were understood of the natural son.[^4] And this confession pertains most of all to a Christian superior, as is clear from John 4, because it most opposes Antichrist, since he will deny the divinity and will make foolish, as far as in him lies, Christ's poverty and voluntary passion.[^5] For where we Christians are taught by Christ and the church to seek constancy, to despise the prosperity of the world and to fear none of its adversities, Antichrist on the contrary, with many of his own, teaches men to aspire to the world's prosperity and to scorn [hardships]{g:penalitas}; hence Peter, because he said to Christ, "Lord, be it far from thee" that thou go to Jerusalem to be crucified, was called Satan by him shortly after this singular privilege, as is clear from Matthew 16:23, to denote that he is most of all adverse to Christ, as another Antichrist, who refuses to follow Christ in poverty and hardship.[^6] Hence Peter was afterwards armed with this shield of faith, when for Christ he left his "wife and all things", following him (Matthew 19:27); for Christ he promised to die, "striking the servant of the high priest" (John 18:10); for Christ he exposed himself in the water (Matthew 14 and the last chapter of John); and finally in Rome, the head of the world, he suffered for Christ a bitter death.[^7]

### 03-b ¶2 · 49.32–50.27  (1 sed/autem)

Hoc eciam figuratur per hoc, quod soli tempore passionis Salvator dixerit Marc. XIV, 37: Simon dormis? Non potuisti una hora vigilare mecum? per hoc innuens quod ipse solus pre ceteris et pro ceteris haberet capitaliter in cura pastoralis officii vigilare. Et hoc idem figuratur per hoc, quod ipse solus in secunda piscacione traxit ad litus rete plenum centum quinquaginta tribus magnis piscibus, ut dicitur Joh. ult; per hoc enim figuratur quod ipse solus inter apostolos trahit capitaliter sponsam Christi ad terram vivencium in retibus legis Dei. Magnum itaque privilegium vicarii, qui potest trahere animas ad beatitudinem, cum ista secunda piscacio significat capcionem hominum ad ecclesiam triumphantem, sicut prima piscacio signat capcionem hominum ad ecclesiam militantem.

ENGLISH: What, then, is given to be understood by this, that Peter alone was seen by the Lord (Matthew 14) coming to him upon the sea, except that, helped by God and presiding singly over the little ship that signifies the church of Christ, he should bring under servitude the present world, [50] signified by the sea? And this sense Augustine sets out in Sermon 4.[^8] And what is given us to understand by the second casting into the sea, of which the last chapter of John speaks, when, "the other disciples" coming to Christ "in the ship", he alone, girt with his coat, boldly cast himself into the sea, unless truly that all the other disciples of Christ, under Peter's leadership, were adorned with the coat of virtues and the girdle of the [power]{g:potencia} of binding, on the way that tends to the goal of ruling?[^9] This too is figured by this, that in the time of the passion the Saviour said to him alone, Mark 14:37: "Simon, sleepest thou? Couldst thou not watch one hour with me?", thereby intimating that he alone, before the rest and for the rest, should keep watch [as head]{g:capitaliter} in the care of the pastoral office.[^10] And the same is figured by this, that he alone, in the second fishing, drew to land the net "full of a hundred and fifty-three great fishes", as is said in the last chapter of John; for by this it is figured that he alone among the apostles draws, as head, the [bride]{g:sponsa} of Christ to the land of the living in the nets of [God's law]{g:lex-dei}. Great, then, is the privilege of the vicar, who can draw souls to blessedness, since this second fishing signifies the catching of men for the church triumphant, as the first fishing signifies the catching of men for the [church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans}.[^11]

### 03-c ¶1 · 51.11–52.2  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 51] Quamvis enim sensus misticus scripture non sit de se evidens ignaris ad probandum scolastice figuratum, tamen si probari potest ex racione vel scriptura alia quod sensus iste misticus intentus fuerat ab autore; tunc sensus ille est equivalens literali; et sic sepe probat Apostolus ex figuris veteris testamenti figuratum allegorice in novo, ut patet I. Cor. X. Gal. IV et Heb. VII. Cum igitur non dicetur quid pertinencius figurarunt ista singulariter facta Petro quam prerogativam Petri super alios; videtur quod iste suasiones quietarent fidelem theologum ad fidem questioni proposite adhibendam. Dicat igitur adversarius istam sentenciam, vel quod aliquid est a Christo inpertinenter factum; aut ex indifferencia positum in scriptura; vel aliter dicat quid ordo et singularitas prenarratorum operum quoad Petrum pertinencius quam eius preeminenciam super alios figuraret. Cum enim scriptura sacra exprimit et racio necessitat oportere aliquem apostolum esse talem, ecclesia **autem** generaliter credat hoc de Petro, Deus notabiliter permitteret [p. 52] ecclesiam suam a principio oberasse, si isti fidei falsitas sit substrata.

ENGLISH: For although the mystical sense of scripture is not of itself evident to the unlearned for proving scholastically the thing figured, yet if it can be proved from reason or from another scripture that this mystical sense was intended by the author, then that sense is equivalent to the literal; and so the Apostle often proves from figures of the old testament what is figured allegorically in the new, as is clear from 1 Corinthians 10, Galatians 4 and Hebrews 7.[^1] Since therefore it will not be said what these things done singularly to Peter figured more fittingly than Peter's prerogative over the others, it seems that these persuasions would set a faithful theologian at rest so as to lend faith to the question proposed.[^2] Let the adversary therefore either affirm this [teaching]{g:sentencia}, or say that something was done by Christ unfittingly, or put in scripture indifferently; or else let him say what the order and singularity of the works narrated above, as regards Peter, would figure more fittingly than his preeminence over the others.[^3] For since holy scripture expresses, and reason necessitates, that some apostle must be such, and the [church]{g:ecclesia} generally believes this of Peter, God would notably have permitted [52] his church to have erred from the beginning, if falsehood underlay this faith.

### 03-c ¶2 · 52.3–55.26  (4 sed/autem)

ENGLISH: Again, secondly, from Peter's opinions after the Ascension the same truth could probably be established; for to Peter alone was shown the sheet let down from heaven, in which were all four-footed beasts and creeping things of the earth and fowls of the air, as is clear from Acts 10:13 and 11:7, and to him alone was it said of all those animals: "Arise, Peter. Kill and eat."[^4] Now the sheet let down by four corners signifies the surface of the earth habitable by men, whose plane is woven together and worn like a sheet, the influence of heaven descending from east, west, north and south to temper the plane of the soil for the use of men. And those three kinds of animals let down in the sheet signify in order the secular lords as the four-footed beasts, the common people as the creeping things of the earth, and the [clergy]{g:clerus} as the fowls, flying up into the contemplative consideration of the times.[^5] These three kinds of men indeed make up the inhabitants of this world. Now Peter arose armed with [virtues]{g:virtus} and killed of these three kinds, when he extinguished the [mundanity]{g:mundialitas} in them and made them not men as regards the world. For so "man" is often taken in scripture, as is clear from 1 Corinthians 3:3: "Whereas there is among you envying and contention, are you not carnal and walk you not according to man?"[^6] Hence blessed Peter interpreted the imaginative vision by word and deed to this sense: that his eating was to cut away the perverse morals in them and to incorporate them personally into the church; for so Christ says, John 4:32: "I have meat to eat which you know not." For when the preacher, who in Canticles 4:2 is called the "teeth" of the bride, kills carnality with the sword of the spirit and with the mouth of the church grinds those who are to be converted to penance, then he spiritually eats the converted, assimilating them into [53] members of mother church; and this office belonged to Peter primarily.[^7] Hence in Acts 1:15 it is read that Peter, "rising up in the midst of the brethren", showed by the scripture of the old testament that in the place of Judas the traitor another bishop must be chosen, to fill his place; and so, according to [priority]{g:prioritas} of dignity, he conducted the election of Matthias.[^8] Again, in Acts 2:14 it is read how Peter with the eleven "lifted up his voice", speaking for all against those who charged them with drunkenness, showing from scripture that this is the spiritual filling by the Holy Spirit foretold in Joel 3.[^9] Again, in Acts 2:31 he next preached to the Jews "of the resurrection", and foretold to them, before all the apostles, what was necessary for them for salvation; and below, in chapter 3:1–2, when Peter and John went up into the temple, Peter held the [office of superior]{g:prepositura} in speaking at the healing of the lame man, as he holds also the first naming in the sequence of scripture; Peter also answered for all to the magistrates of the temple, as is clear from Acts chapter 4, and for all pronounced the sentence of death on Ananias and Sapphira, as is clear from chapter 5, and so on Simon Magus, Acts 8.[^10] Again, in the general council of which Acts 15 speaks, Peter pronounced for all the sentence that converts from the gentiles ought not to be circumcised nor to observe the ceremonies of the law.[^11] Now so holy an apostle would not presume to usurp such an office of superior after the sending of the Holy Spirit, who taught him all truth and confirmed in him all the humility instructed by Christ, unless he knew that it belonged to his office to accept such a dignity magnanimously; and this dignity seems to be specified by Christ in Matthew [54] 17:23, 26, when the collectors of the didrachma, in the presence of the apostles, say singularly to Peter, as to the greatest of them: "Doth not your master pay the didrachma?" This the Saviour taught in effect when he joined Peter alone to himself in the payment of the tribute: "Go," he says, "to the sea, and cast in a hook: and that fish which shall first come up, take: and when thou hast opened its mouth, thou shalt find a stater: take that, and give it to them for me and for thee."[^12] Thirdly, this teaching is confirmed from the testimony of the saints. For blessed Dionysius, after the authors of scripture the chief witness, in his book *On the Divine Names*, chapter 3, calls blessed Peter the summit (*verticalis*), that is, chief or [captain]{g:capitaneus}, moved by Christ's saying in John 1, where at Peter's first calling he names him Cephas, which means "head".[^13] And in his book *On the Ecclesiastical Hierarchy*, chapter 5, he also calls blessed Peter "the summit of the disciples", which he shows from this, that he principally conducted the election of Matthias in the place of Judas, as is clear from Acts 1.[^14] Hence this saint, blessed Paul's chief disciple, who stood by him at his death, in the little book he wrote *On the Death of the Apostles* addresses Timothy thus: "When they were being separated from each other, led to the places of execution, Paul addresses Peter in these words: Peace be with thee, foundation of the churches and shepherd of the sheep and lambs of Christ."[^15] So holy and so subtle a theologian would not testify that he had heard these testimonies with his senses and noted them so diligently, unless he believed that blessed Peter was [head]{g:caput} of the college of the Apostles after [55] Christ's death, as the church often inculcates in the service of this saint. And the laws of the church hold the same teaching; for in *Causa* 24, question 1, *Rogamus*, Pope Marcellus speaks thus: "We ask you, brethren, teach nothing other than what you received from blessed Peter and from the other apostles; for he is head of the whole church, to whom the Lord said: Thou art Peter."[^16] And the same is clear from Distinction 21, chapter *In novo*, and Distinction 22, chapters *Omnes* and *Sacrosancta*. Again, *Causa* 24, question 3, *De illicita*.[^17] Again, blessed Augustine, in *On the Questions of the Old and New Law*, chapter 79, says that "Peter was first among the apostles". Therefore Christ says that Peter should pay the didrachma for himself: "him", says Augustine, "he appointed their head, that he might be shepherd of the Lord's flock".[^18] And he says the same in the last chapter, and in the little book *To the Holy Spirit* he says: "Thou madest the apostate weep and didst ordain him supreme pontiff."[^19] Nor would it be needful to dwell so long on the declaration of this teaching, were it not for three things: first, for the declaration of the authorities of scripture by which this question is shown; second, that the sense of the church may be known, according to which it grants that Peter has a preeminence of dignity over the other apostles; and third, that the vicars of Peter may know the form according to which the [power]{g:potestas} of Peter is derived to the pastors.[^20]

### 03-d ¶1 · 55.27–58.7  (9 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 55] Quantum ad primum, expeditum est in parte, **sed** pro secundo notandum quod racio quare Petrus ordinatus fuit a Christo post eum caput ecclesie fuit preeminencia virtutum ad regendum [p. 56] ecclesiam; aliter enim sapiencia patris inprovide eum constituisset ecclesie sue episcopum. Et cum omnes virtutes in genere sint connexe, patet quod Petrus habuit quandam preeminenciam in omni genere virtutum. Tres **autem** erant virtutes in quibus pertinencius Petrus precellerat, scilicet fides, humilitas, caritas; fides, quam oportet esse petram ecclesie, precellebat in Petro, propter quod ordinavit magister Matth. XVI, 16 quod ad questionem, qua quesivit de se ipso, quem dicunt homines esse filium hominis, Petrus pro omnibus responderet: Tu es, inquit; filius Dei vivi. Ubi orthodoxe confitetur humanitatem in Christo, quia signat illum prophetam promissum patribus vel Messiam. Et secunda pars confitetur Christum Dei filium naturalem, quia aliter impertinens foret confessio. Et inter omnes articulos fidei nullus est ad edificandum ecclesiam Christi pertinencior quam secundum beatum Joh. I. Joh. V, 4 Dei filius vincit mundum: Quis est inquit, qui vincit mundum, nisi qui credit quoniam Ihesus est filius Dei. Nam hoc fundamento habito consequenter credendum, quod omnia, que fecit vel docuit; sunt sine calumpnia possibili a tota ecclesia acceptanda. Ideo audivit Petrus a Domino: Beatus es Symon Baryona, quia caro et sanguis non revelavit tibi, **sed** pater meus, qui est in celis. Et ex ista fide accepit arras prefecture ecclesie. Et ego dico tibi, inquit, quia tu es Petrus, et super hanc petram edificabo ecclesiam meam. De quo August, super Johannem sermone ultimo sic loquitur: Ecclesia quatitur **sed** non cadit, quando fundatur super petram, unde Petrus nomen accepit, [p. 57] non enim a Petro petra, **sed** Petrus a petra, petra **autem** erat Christus. Et propter istas raciones tenentur superiores prepositi predicare fidem ecclesie, unde Luce XXII, 32: Ego **autem** rogavi pro te, ut non deficiat fides tua, et tu aliquando conversus confirma fratres tuos. Ecce quomodo Christus exauditus pro sua reverencia singulariter rogavit pro fide Petri; et secundo iniunxit sibi primatum officii et per hoc fundamentum operatus est Petrus singulariter supra ceteros apostolos; per hanc; ut supra dixi, fidem extraxit Petrus gladium et percussit servum pontificis auferendo dextram eius auriculam, ut patet Joh. XVIII; per hanc eciam introivit in monumentum Evangelista, qui prius venerat stante foras, per hanc audito ab Evangelista, quia dominus est, misit se in mare, aliis discipulis venientibus navigio ad littus, ubi Christus stabat, ut patet Joh. ultimo, et sic de aliis superexcessivis operibus, que ex vehemencia sue fidei processerunt. Primum **autem** allegorizatur a sancto Gregorio, ut patet XXIV, q. I, Si Petrus: Primo inquit, docetur quod aurem in specie habere non debent, quam in ministerio non habebant. **Sed** bonus dominus ipsi refundit auditum demonstrans et ipsos, si convertantur, posse sanari qui in passione Christi vulnerati sunt; tollit eciam gladio spirituali interiorem auriculam male intelligentis de passione Domini, dum vel prescindit errorem quo putant esse dexterum non pati pro Christo, vel quod eius divinitas paciatur. Per secundum allegorizatur quod Petrus, gentes signans, licet tardius venit ad thesaurum fidei quam Johannes ex virginitate [p. 58] sterilis, signans synagogam, tamen prius plenius introivit copiosius ad credendum in Christum. Per tercium vero signatur anagogice quod imitatores Petri et specialiter martyres in sermone fidei citius veniunt ad Christum stantem in littore eternitatis quam alii navigio, qui arte confessorum prudenter certant diu in vita cum hostibus.

ENGLISH: As to the first, it has been dealt with in part; but for the second it is to be noted that the reason why Peter was ordained by Christ [head]{g:caput} of the [church]{g:ecclesia} after him was his preeminence in [virtues]{g:virtus} for ruling [56] the church; for otherwise the wisdom of the Father would have improvidently appointed him bishop of his church.[^1] And since all virtues are connected in their kind, it is clear that Peter had a certain preeminence in every kind of virtue. And there were three virtues in which Peter more fittingly excelled, namely faith, humility, [charity]{g:caritas}. Faith, which must be the rock of the church, excelled in Peter; on account of which the master ordained, Matthew 16:16, that to the question he asked about himself, "Whom do men say that the Son of man is?", Peter should answer for all: "Thou art," he says, "[Christ,] the Son of the living God."[^2] Here he confesses, as the orthodox do, the humanity in Christ, since [the name] signifies that prophet promised to the fathers, or the Messiah. And the second part confesses Christ the natural Son of God, since otherwise the confession would be beside the point. And among all the [articles of faith]{g:articulus-fidei} none is more fitting for building the church of Christ than [that] the Son of God overcomes the world, according to blessed John, 1 John 5:4: "Who is he," he says, "that overcometh the world, but he that believeth that Jesus is the Son of God?"[^3] For with this foundation held, it is consequently to be believed that all things which he did or taught are to be accepted by the whole church without possible [challenge]{g:calumpnia}. Therefore Peter heard from the Lord: "Blessed art thou, Simon Bar-Jona: because flesh and blood hath not revealed it to thee, but my Father who is in heaven." And from this faith he received the earnest of charge over the church: "And I say to thee," he says, "That thou art Peter; and upon this rock I will build my church." Of which Augustine, on John, in the last sermon, speaks thus: "The church is shaken but does not fall, since it is founded upon the rock from which Peter received his name; [57] for the rock is not from Peter, but Peter from the rock; and the rock was Christ."[^4] And for these reasons the higher [superiors]{g:prepositi} are bound to preach the faith of the church; hence Luke 22:32: "But I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not: and thou, being once converted, confirm thy brethren." Behold how Christ, "heard for his reverence", prayed singularly for Peter's faith; and secondly enjoined on him the [primacy]{g:primatus} of office; and by this foundation Peter worked singularly above the other apostles.[^5] By this faith, as I said above, Peter drew the sword and struck the servant of the high priest, cutting off his right ear, as is clear from John 18; by this also he went into the sepulchre, while the Evangelist, who had come first, stood outside; by this, having heard from the Evangelist "that it is the Lord", he cast himself into the sea, while the other disciples came in the ship to the shore where Christ stood, as is clear from the last chapter of John; and so of the other surpassing works which proceeded from the vehemence of his faith.[^6] And the first is allegorized by St Gregory, as is clear from *Causa* 24, question 1, *Si Petrus*: "First," he says, "it is taught that they ought not to have in outward form the ear which they did not have in ministry. But the good Lord restores to him his hearing, showing that they too can be healed, if they are converted, who were wounded in the passion of Christ." He also takes away with the spiritual sword the inner ear of one who understands the Lord's passion wrongly, when he cuts off either the error by which they think it the right thing not to suffer for Christ, or [the error] that his [divinity]{g:divinitas} suffers.[^7] By the second it is allegorized that Peter, signifying the gentiles, although he came later to the treasure of faith than John, barren from [58] virginity, signifying the synagogue, yet went in first, more fully and more abundantly, to believe in Christ. And by the third it is signified anagogically that the imitators of Peter, and especially the martyrs in the word of faith, come more quickly to Christ standing on the shore of eternity than the others in the ship, who by the art of confessors contend prudently and long in life with the enemies.[^8]

### 03-e ¶1 · 58.8–60.9  (6 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 58] Quantum ad secundam virtutem que est humilitas, patet quod necesse est cementem fidei coniungi lapidi ad construccionem celestis edificii, unde Math. XVIII. 1, 2, quando mota est questio, quis maior est in regno celorum magister decreverat, quod parvulus in mente humilior; ideo cum Petrus sit post Christum fundamentum ecclesie, necesse est quod sit quodammodo fratribus suis humilior. Et istam leccionem didicerunt apostoli a magistro Math. XI, 29: Discite, inquit, a me, quia mitis sum et humilis corde, quod Petrus quia eminenter didicerat constitutus est fundamentum primum inter apostolos; unde Augustinus in De Verbis Domini Sermone X: Discite a me non mundum fabricare, non creare, non miracula facere, nec mortuos suscitare, **sed** quoniam mitis sum et humilis corde. Quamvis **autem** istam leccionem convenit et oportet quemlibet salvandum advertere, oportet tamen Petrum, si debet esse fundamentum substratum aliis, profundius illam cognoscere, oportet enim fundamentum ecclesie esse humillimum, quia aliter tanta fabrica cuius fundus in terra capitur et cacumen usque ad summum celum erigitur non stabit solidum; fabrica igitur tam gravis in moribus requirit fundacionem [p. 59] in summo gradu humilitatis possibili. Et hec racio quare Christus noluit fundare suam ecclesiam in mundi diviciis tamquam stercoribus vel aquis labilibus nec in mundi gloria vel fastu seculi tamquam ventis, **sed** in stabili paupertate, que securius, brevius et statui innocencie conformius facit humiles regnum celorum acquirere. Unde, ut declarat Lincolniensis in quodam sermone de humilitate, quod vere humilem iste octo condiciones inseparabiliter consequuntur: Discutiat, inquit, unusquisque se ipsum diligenter, an vere humilitatis signa ipsum sequuntur. Vere namque humilis hiis signis dinoscitur: Si correptus a minori non dedignatur, si non insolescat honoribus, si non elevetur vel delectetur in suis laudibus, si non reddat malum pro malo, nec maledictum pro maledicto **sed** e contrario benedicit. Si inter equales primus est in ope et ultimus in ordine, primus in labore et ultimus in requie, primus in ministrando et ultimus in recumbendo. Si gracias agit divine benevolencie, si tam libenter obedit quam precipit, si in potestate constitutus prius se noscit subiectum quam prepositum, sicut fecit centurio, de quo Luce VII. Et inter omnes scolas pertinentes preposito et Petri vicario non est alia pertinencior, sicut enim Petrus acute correptus a Paulo, ut patet Gal. II, humiliter toleravit, quando eciam missum est pro illo ad confirmandum centurionem Act. X. non petiit constipacionem comitive elegantis equestrium **sed** humiliter resedit in Yoppen pedester et requisitus in domo Simonis coriarii iuxta mare venit sibi humilius et instruxit, non expectans ab eo retribucionem temporalium vel [p. 60] donorum, et quando crebro se exposuit ante alios apostolos, non sic fecit gracia mundani honoris vel commodi, **sed** humiliter, obedienter et serviliter pro supportando onere Christi. Et sic quicquid de ipso legimus sonans in eius maioritatem super alios apostolos dicit maioritatem sue humilitatis atque servicii, quia iuxta hoc debet in eis maioritas attendi, ut patet Math. XVII et Luce XXII ex diffinicione magistri.

ENGLISH: As to the second [virtue]{g:virtus}, which is humility, it is clear that the cement of faith must be joined to the stone for the construction of the heavenly building; hence in Matthew 18:1–2, when the question was raised who "is the greater in the kingdom of heaven", the master had decided that [it is] the "little child", humbler in mind; therefore, since Peter is, after Christ, the foundation of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, he must be in a certain way humbler than his brethren.[^1] And the apostles learned this lesson from the master, Matthew 11:29: "Learn," he says, "of me, because I am meek, and humble of heart"; and because Peter had learned it eminently, he was appointed the first foundation among the apostles; hence Augustine, *De verbis Domini*, sermon 10: "Learn of me, not to fabricate the world, not to create, not to do miracles, nor to raise the dead, but that I am meek and humble of heart."[^2] And although it is fitting and needful for everyone who is to be saved to heed this lesson, yet Peter, if he is to be the foundation laid beneath the others, must know it more deeply; for the foundation of the church must be the humblest, since otherwise so great a fabric, whose base is taken in the earth and whose summit is raised up to the highest heaven, will not stand firm; therefore a fabric so weighty in morals requires a foundation [59] in the highest degree of humility possible. And this is the reason why Christ would not found his church on the riches of the world, as on dung or slipping waters, nor on the glory of the world or the pomp of the age, as on winds, but on stable poverty, which makes the humble acquire the [kingdom]{g:regnum} of heaven more securely, more quickly, and more in conformity with the state of innocence.[^3] Hence, as Lincoln declares in a certain sermon on humility, these eight conditions follow the truly humble man inseparably: "Let each one," he says, "examine himself diligently, whether the signs of true humility follow him. For the truly humble man is discerned by these signs: if, rebuked by a lesser man, he does not disdain it; if he does not grow insolent with honours; if he is not lifted up or delighted in his own praises; if he does not render evil for evil, nor railing for railing, but contrariwise blesses; if among equals he is first in help and last in rank, first in labour and last in rest, first in serving and last in reclining at table; if he gives thanks to the divine benevolence; if he obeys as willingly as he commands; if, set in [power]{g:potestas}, he knows himself a subject before [he knows himself] a [superior]{g:prepositi}, as the centurion did, of whom Luke 7 speaks."[^4] And among all the schools pertinent to a superior and [vicar of Peter]{g:vicarius-petri} there is none more pertinent. For as Peter, sharply rebuked by Paul, as is clear from Galatians 2, humbly bore it, [so] also when he was sent for to confirm the centurion, Acts 10, he did not seek the press of an elegant mounted company, but humbly stayed on foot at Joppa, and, sought out in the house of Simon the tanner by the sea, he came to him the more humbly and instructed him, not expecting from him any return of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} or [60] gifts; and when he often put himself forward before the other apostles, he did not do so for the sake of worldly honour or advantage, but humbly, obediently and as a servant, to bear the burden of Christ.[^5] And so whatever we read of him that sounds toward his [greatness]{g:majoritas} over the other apostles speaks of the greatness of his humility and [service]{g:servicium}, since it is according to this that greatness ought to be regarded among them, as is clear from Matthew 17 and Luke 22, from the master's [definition]{g:diffinicio}.[^6]

### 03-e ¶3 · 60.23–61.12  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 60] **Sed** notandum (ut supra tetigi) quod multiplex est racio amandi: quidam enim plus amant Christum secundum racionem divinitatis, ut creditur de Evangelista, quidam vero plus amant eum secundum racionem sue humanitatis, ut creditur de Philippo, et quidam secundum racionem dileccionis sui corporis, quod est ecclesia; et sic de infinitis aliis racionibus et istorum subdivisionibus, pro quibus de quolibet sancto dicunt illud Ecclesiastici XLIV, 20 verificari: Non est [p. 61] inventus similis illi. Et sic quilibet humilior est caritativior, et sic de aliis virtutibus; et quoad aliquam earum eminenciam. Videtur tamen Augustinum super Joh. Omeliam CXXIV difficultare istam materiam in hoc, quod inpertinenter tam crebro diceret Johannes de se ipso quod ipsum diligebat Ihesus, nisi eum ultra alios preeminenter diligeret. Et certum est quod proporcionaliter ut Deus quicquam prius diligit est ipsum melius et e contra, et ex alio latere videtur quod si Petrus plus diligit Ihesum suum est ipse melior, et per consequens plus amatur.

ENGLISH: But it is to be noted (as I touched on above) that the ground of loving is manifold: for some love Christ more under the aspect of his [divinity]{g:divinitas}, as is believed of the Evangelist; some love him more under the aspect of his humanity, as is believed of Philip; and some under the aspect of love of his body, which is the church; and so of infinitely many other aspects and their subdivisions, on account of which they say of every saint that the word of Ecclesiasticus 44:20 is verified: "There was not [61] found the like to him."[^9] And so anyone who is humbler is more charitable, and so of the other virtues; and [this] as regards some eminence among them.[^10] Yet Augustine, on John, homily 124, seems to make this matter difficult in this, that John would say of himself so often, beside the point, that Jesus loved him, unless he loved him preeminently beyond the others. And it is certain that in proportion as God loves anything sooner, it is the better, and conversely; and on the other side it seems that if Peter loves his Jesus more, he is himself better, and consequently is loved more.[^11]

### 03-e ¶4 · 61.13–62.8  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 61] In ista materia videtur sanctum sic dicere, quod isti duo apostoli signant duas partes ecclesie, Petrus activam et Johannes contemplativam, quarum prima imperfecta finitur in via; et secunda perfeccior consumatur in patria, et sic ad sensum anagogicum Christus plus diligit Johannem dum plus amat vitam in patria, **sed** Petrus plus amat Christum, quia senciendo miseriam nostram plus diligimus Dei misericordiam, veritatem **autem** contemplacionis, quia nondum apparet nobis, minus diligimus. Et ad istum sensum Christus plus amavit Johannem, quia minus, inquit Augustinus, amat nos Christus miseros quam beatos, **sed** quam personam de illis plus diligit in celo non innotescit. Ex isto dicto videtur tamen quod in vita activa plus amoris Christi presbiter hic indicat in effectu, et sic Deus eternaliter plus amat membrum suum esse in beatitudine quam in via; et tamen quidquid amaverit eternaliter eque amat; et sic non est nostrum modo cognoscere utrum Deus simpliciter plus amat Petrum [p. 62] in beatitudine quam Johannem. Non, inquit Augustinus, quantum ab alio quolibet Christus diligitur scire poterat, qui cor alterius videre non poterat, et ideo prudenter non dicit Petrus: Domine, Tu scis, quia amo te plus hiis **sed** asseruit: Domine, inquit, Tu scis, quia amo te; caritatem itaque et alias virtutes Petrus habuit in via secundum aliquam racionem ceteris apostolis plus perfectas.

ENGLISH: In this matter the saint seems to say thus: that these two apostles signify two parts of the church, Peter the active and John the contemplative, of which the first, imperfect, is ended in the [way]{g:via}, and the second, more perfect, is consummated in the [homeland]{g:patria}; and so in the anagogical sense Christ loves John more, inasmuch as he loves more the life in the homeland, but Peter loves Christ more, because, feeling our misery, we love God's mercy more, and the truth of contemplation, because it does not yet appear to us, we love less. And in this sense Christ loved John more, "because", says Augustine, "Christ loves us less wretched than blessed"; but which person of those he loves more in heaven is not made known.[^12] From this saying, however, it seems that in the active life the [presbyter]{g:presbiter} here shows in effect more of the love of Christ; and so God eternally loves more that his member be in blessedness than in the way; and yet whatever he has loved, he loves eternally and equally; and so it is not ours now to know whether God [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} loves Peter [62] more in blessedness than John.[^13] "For he could not know," says Augustine, "how much Christ is loved by any other, since he could not see another's heart; and therefore Peter prudently does not say: Lord, thou knowest that I love thee more than these, but asserted: Lord," he says, "thou knowest that I love thee." Peter, therefore, had charity and the other virtues in the way, in some respect, more perfect than the other apostles.[^14]
