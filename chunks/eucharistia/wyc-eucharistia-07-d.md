# *De eucharistia*, chapter 7, part 4: the three kinds of naming

## ¶1 · 200.1–202.8

> [p. 200] Sed limitatum est nobis pro fugandis heresibus quod loquamur de predicacione ydemptica, et illam oportet scire distinguere a predicacione tropica ad intelligendum sanctos. Scribit enim Jeronimus in epistola ad Helbidiam De XII Questionibus questione IIᵃ: Nos, inquit, audiamus panem quem fregit Dominus deditque discipulis suis esse corpus Domini salvatoris, ipso dicente ad eos: Accipite et comedite; hoc est corpus meum. Ecce quod iste sanctus cui mille doctores et pape non equiparantur in fide scripture asserit quod panis demonstratur pronomine, et usque hodie omnes eciam infideles satrape non audebant hereticare huius sancti sentenciam. Unde Augustinus exponit eam expressius epistola XII ad Bonifacium episcopum: Si, inquit sacramenta quandam similitudinem rerum earum quarum sacramenta sunt non haberent, omnino sacramenta non essent. Ex hac autem similitudine plerumque eciam ipsarum rerum nomina accipiunt; sicut ergo secundum quendam modum sacramentum corporis Christi corpus Christi est, sacramentum sanguinis Christi sanguis Christi est, ita et sacramentum fidei fides est. Ideo docet Augustinus Sermone LV° quomodo non debemus contemptibiliter vel irracionabiliter accipere sacramentum propter suum signatum: Non tibi videatur vile, inquit, quod vides; quod vides transit, sed quod signatur in ipso visibili non transit sed permanet; ecce signum accipitur, comeditur, consumitur: numquid [p. 201] corpus Christi consumitur? Absit. Distinguunt enim sancti inter hoc signum et corpus Christi ut inter Deum et creaturam signantem, nec umquam sciverunt concipere quod qualitas sit sine subiecto, ita quod omnes vires anime plene virtute, beatitudine et gracia sint per se in patria adorande tanquam considerantes et compacientes de nostra miseria si tamen per se forent, pocius hoc facerent qualitates: fides, spes, karitas, felicitas, virgines quas oramus, quia de sua natura haberent perfecciones, quas per accidens accomodant sanctis quasi fines suarum perfeccionum, quas in se habent perfeccius, cum nichil accomodat alteri perfeccionem ulteriorem quam ex se habeat; virtus autem talis cum sit intuitiva, abstractiva et quomodocunque natura humana est secundum animam operativa, foret valde perfecta ex illa operacione sibi propria et advocatrix valde compaciens et miseri iuvativa. Nec dicetur racio quare qualitates corporee possunt per se habere omnem accionem quam possunt habere in corpore quin a pari vel evidencius qualitates anime ita possunt; nec est facile fingere quomodo possunt communicare acciones suas corporibus quibus per accidens sunt linite, quin evidencius Deus posset communicare potenciam creandi et omnem accionem suam ad intra cuilibet creature; et ita sicut ille qualitates anime insensibiles communicant corpori denominaciones quas per se sibi retinent, ita possent de Dei omnipotencia communicare ligno vel lapidi, et sic omnis materialis substancia foret de Dei omnipotencia intellectiva subtilissime et beatificabilis de obedienciali potencia. Ille enim qui potest [p. 202] suspendere informacionem qua qualitas spiritualis informat corpus et alias facere quod informet eciam efficacius corpus ex natura sua minus dispositum, ipse posset extendere potenciam informativam virtutis et potenciam passivam subiecti; et sic quelibet materialis substancia foret de Dei potencia quomodocunque activa et per consequens nulla foret substancialis differencia.

But it has been [assigned]{g:limitare} to us, for the putting to flight of heresies, that we speak of [identical predication]{g:predicacio-ydemptica}, and one must know how to distinguish it from tropical predication in order to understand [the saints]{g:sancti}.[^1] For Jerome writes in the letter to Hedibia, *On Twelve Questions*, question 2: "Let us," he says, "hear that the [bread]{g:panis} which the Lord broke and gave to his disciples is the body of the Lord the Saviour, since he himself says to them: Take ye and eat. This is my body."[^2] See: this saint, to whom a thousand doctors and popes are not equal in the faith of scripture, asserts that the bread is demonstrated by the pronoun; and to this day even all the unbelieving [satraps]{g:satrapa} have not dared [to pronounce]{g:hereticare} this saint's [teaching]{g:sentencia} heretical.[^3] Hence Augustine expounds it more expressly in letter 12 to Bishop Boniface: "If [sacraments]{g:sacramentum}," he says, "did not have a certain likeness to the things of which they are sacraments, they would not be sacraments at all. And from this likeness they for the most part also receive the names of the things themselves; as therefore in a certain mode the sacrament of the body of Christ is [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}, the sacrament of the blood of Christ is the blood of Christ, so also the sacrament of faith is faith."[^4] Therefore Augustine teaches in Sermon 55 how we ought not to receive the sacrament contemptibly or irrationally on account of the [thing it signifies]{g:signatum}: "Let not what you see," he says, "seem cheap to you; what you see passes, but what is signified in the visible thing itself does not pass but remains. See, the sign is received, eaten, consumed: is [201] the body of Christ consumed? Far from it."[^5] For the saints distinguish between this sign and the body of Christ as between God and the signifying [creature]{g:creatura}, nor did they ever know how to conceive that a [quality]{g:qualitas} is without a subject; so that [on that view] all the powers of the soul, full in virtue, blessedness and grace, would [of themselves]{g:per-se} be adored in [the homeland]{g:patria} as considering and having compassion on our misery. If indeed they existed of themselves, the qualities would rather do this: faith, hope, charity, happiness, the virgins to whom we pray;[^6] for of their own nature they would have perfections, which [by accident]{g:per-accidens} they lend to the saints as the ends of their perfections, which they have more perfectly in themselves, since nothing lends to another a perfection further than it has of itself. And such a [virtue]{g:virtus}, since it is intuitive, abstractive, and operative in whatever way human [nature]{g:natura} is operative as to the soul, would be very perfect from that operation proper to it, and a very compassionate advocate, and helpful to the wretched.[^7] Nor will a reason be given why bodily qualities can of themselves have every action which they can have in a body, without the qualities of the soul being able to do so equally or more evidently; nor is it easy to feign how they can communicate their actions to the bodies on which they are by accident [smeared]{g:linire}, without God's being able, more evidently, to communicate the power of creating and every action of his *ad intra* to any creature whatever; and so, just as those [imperceptible]{g:insensibilis} qualities of the soul communicate to the body [namings]{g:denominacio} which of themselves they retain for themselves, so they could by God's omnipotence communicate [them] to a log or a stone, and so every material [substance]{g:substancia} would by God's omnipotence be most subtly intellective, and beatifiable by obediential [potency]{g:potencia}.[^8] For he who can [202] [suspend]{g:suspendere} the [informing]{g:informacio} by which a spiritual quality informs the body, and otherwise bring it about that it inform, even more efficaciously, a body less disposed by its nature, could himself extend the informative power of the virtue and the passive potency of the subject; and so any material substance whatever would by God's power be active in any way whatever, and consequently there would be no substantial difference.

## ¶2 · 202.9–202.19

> [p. 202] Sed deliramenta sunt ista, cum nulla qualitas sit nisi substanciam esse qualem, nec aliqua forma est nisi illa que est substanciam esse formatam vel formosam; ideo idem est ponere quod qualitas sit per se sine substancia et ponere quod sit ista quod substancia sit qualis sine hoc quod sit substancia; et sic includit formalem contradiccionem quod sit accidens sine substancia subiecta, in tantum quod forma substancialis qua corpus hominis est formaliter vivum est vita que est accidentalis tam corpori quam illi spiritui. Et illi uniuntur in eadem persona hominis, ut hic supponitur.

But these are [ravings]{g:deliramenta}, since no quality is anything but substance's being qualified, nor is any form anything but that which is substance's being formed or shapely;[^9] therefore it is the same to posit that a quality is of itself without substance and to posit that it is this: that substance is qualified without its being substance; and so it includes a formal contradiction that there be an [accident]{g:accidens} without a subject substance, insofar that the [substantial form]{g:forma-substancialis} by which a man's body is formally alive is a life which is accidental both to the body and to that spirit. And these are united in the same [person]{g:persona} of the man, as is here supposed.

## ¶3 · 202.21–202.28

> [p. 202] Quantum ad triplicem evidenciam tactam in principio argumenti patet quod decretum loquitur tropice quod panis sacramentaliter fiat corpus Christi et non ydemptice quod natura panis sit natura corporis Christi, et sic licet albedo sit sacramentum, non tamen est per se sacramentum, sed sacramento illi inherens; et illud subiectum est infinitum perfeccius quam quantitas vel albedo.

As to the threefold [evidence]{g:evidencia} touched on at the beginning of the argument, it is clear that the [decree]{g:decretum} speaks [tropically]{g:tropice}, "the bread [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} becomes the body of Christ", and not [identically]{g:ydemptice}, "the nature of bread is the nature of the body of Christ";[^10] and so, although whiteness is a sacrament, yet it is not of itself a sacrament, but inhering in that sacrament; and that subject is infinitely more perfect than [quantity]{g:quantitas} or whiteness.

## ¶4 · 202.29–202.30

> [p. 202] Quantum ad secundum argumentum patet quod illud sacramentum est album quia natura panis.

As to the second argument, it is clear that that sacrament is white because [it is] the nature of bread.

## ¶5 · 202.31–203.17

> [p. 202] Ulterius pro denominacione accidentis notandum quod triplex est denominacio, scilicet subiectiva, [p. 203] respectiva et intrinseca. Prima denominacione sola substancia denominatur, cum omne accidens sit substanciam esse accidentatam, ita quod nichil est subiectum accidentis nisi substancia; accidens autem est fundamentum et causa alteri sed non subiectum. Denominacio autem respectiva non exigit denominatum illud esse subiectum accidentis denominantis, quod satis est quod sit fundamentum ad quod accidens absolutum respiciat, causando in ipso habitudinem respectivam, ut patet de septem respectibus fundatis in quantitate et qualitate. Et isto modo dicunt Augustinus V De Trinitate in fine et Anshelmus in Monologion XXVII quod Deus est fundamentum habitudinis relative, que quia non requirit fundamentum suum per eius acquisicionem moveri, vocatur ab aliquibus relacio racionis. Non enim requirit in illius subiecto fundamentum esse accidens absolutum.

Further, for the naming of an accident it is to be noted that naming is threefold, namely subjective, [203] relational, and intrinsic. By the first naming substance alone is named, since every accident is substance's being [subject to accident]{g:accidentatus}, so that nothing is the subject of an accident but substance; but an accident is a foundation and a cause to another, but not a subject. And [relational naming]{g:denominacio-respectiva} does not require that the thing named be the subject of the naming accident, since it is enough that it be a foundation to which an [absolute accident]{g:accidens-absolutum} looks, causing in it a [relational]{g:respectivus} [respect]{g:habitudo}, as is clear of the seven relations founded in quantity and quality.[^11] And in this way Augustine, *On the Trinity* V at the end, and Anselm in *Monologion* 27 say that God is the foundation of a [relative respect]{g:habitudo-relativa}, which, because it does not require its foundation to be moved by its acquisition, is called by some a [relation of reason]{g:relacio-racionis}. For it does not require that the foundation in its subject be an absolute accident.[^12]

## ¶6 · 203.18–204.8

> [p. 203] Tercia autem denominacio intrinseca est qua idem denominat se per se, quod potest esse multipliciter; nam Deus per se excludendo omnem aliam causam denominat se sapientem, iustum, potentem etc.: ideo ista non sunt in Deo accidencia. Quantitas eciam dicitur per se quanta, licet equivoce a substancia; sic eciam albedo alba et ita de aliis accidentibus eciam respectivis. Aliter enim foret processus in infinitum, ut qualitas foret distincta intensione intensa, et sic in infinitum, nisi foret dare unum per se intensum. Non tamen video quod quantitas denominatur subiective albedine vel econtra, sed sicut superficies est alba vel albata, ut locuntur philosophi, ita qualitas [p. 204] est mole magna, ita quod neutrum istorum accidencium est subiectum alterius, sed denominant se reciproce respective, quia unum concomitatur reliquum in eodem subiecto, ut quia albedo est per partem substancie superficialem aut tenuem; ideo dicitur albedo superficialis, et quia qualitas est per pedalem quantitatem subiecti, ideo dicitur pedalis, sic quod nec quantitas subiectat qualitatem nec econtra.

And the third, intrinsic, naming is that by which the same thing names itself of itself, which can be in many ways; for God, of himself, excluding every other cause, names himself wise, just, powerful and so on: therefore these are not accidents in God. Quantity, too, is said to be of itself [quantified]{g:quantus}, though [equivocally]{g:equivoce} with substance; so too whiteness [is] white, and so of the other accidents, even the relational ones.[^13] For otherwise there would be a process to infinity, as that a quality would be intense by a distinct intension, and so to infinity, unless [one may posit]{g:est-dare} one thing intense of itself. Yet I do not see that quantity is named subjectively by whiteness or the reverse; but just as a surface is white or whitened, as the philosophers speak, so a quality [204] is great in bulk, so that neither of these accidents is the subject of the other, but they name each other reciprocally, relationally, because the one accompanies the other in the same subject: as, because whiteness is through the superficial or thin part of the substance, therefore it is called superficial whiteness; and because a quality is through the foot-long quantity of the subject, therefore it is called foot-long; so that neither [is]{g:subiectare} quantity the subject of quality nor the reverse.

## ¶7 · 204.9–204.12

> [p. 204] Sed qualitas multiplicius denominatur intensa, activa, quanta etc., quam denominatur ipsa quantitas, licet sit secundum speciem et non individuum fundamentum et basis in subiecto qualitati corporee.

But quality is named intense, active, quantified and so on, in more ways than quantity itself is named, although [quantity] is, according to species and not [according to] the [individual]{g:individuum}, the foundation and base in the subject for bodily quality.[^14]

## ¶8 · 204.13–204.32

> [p. 204] Sacramentum autem est per se subiectum tam quantitati quam qualitati, cum adversarii concedunt quod accidente albi vini infuso in accidens rubei vini consecrati utraque quantitas et qualitas movetur, cum sacramentum erit maius et alterum; ideo necesse est ponere ibi subiectum prius quantitate et qualitate, quia certum est quod fasciculus accidencium est posterius utroque; et patet in parte quomodo accidencia in denominacione sese respiciunt. Et per hec patet responsio ad tercium argumentum. Nam qualitates secunde denominant qualitates primas, materiam et formam, eciam Deum denominacione respectiva. Sed denominacione subiectiva solum denominant materialem substanciam per quam extenduntur. Denominacionem autem respectivam facit accidens in quantumcunque distante in quo non est subiective, ut albedo Petri Rome facit ipsum esse similem Johanni Anglie, ideo causat ad omnem punctum propinquitatem atque distanciam et quodammodo facit quod Deus est causa eius.

Now the sacrament is of itself the subject both of quantity and of quality, since the adversaries grant that when the accident of white [wine]{g:vinum} is poured into the accident of consecrated red wine, both the quantity and the quality are moved, since the sacrament will be greater and other; therefore it is necessary to posit there a subject prior to quantity and quality, for it is certain that the [bundle of accidents]{g:fasciculus-accidentis} is posterior to both;[^15] and it is clear in part how accidents look to each other in naming. And by these things the reply to the third argument is clear. For second qualities name the [first qualities]{g:qualitas-prima}, matter and form, even God, by relational naming. But by subjective naming they name only the material substance through which they are extended. And an accident makes relational naming in a thing however distant in which it is not subjectively: as the whiteness of Peter at Rome makes him like John in England; therefore it causes nearness and distance to every point, and in a manner brings it about that God is its cause.[^16]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 200.1: -We must distinguish between identical and tropical statements.
- 201.1: If qualities could exist separately, we ought to pray to the virtues rather than to the Saints.
- 201.27: Intelligence might house itself in a log of wood.
- 202.9: This is mere madness: neither quality nor form can exist without substance.
- 203.1: Three kinds of naming: 1. Subjective, which applies only to substance.
- 203.7: 2. Respective, which does no require the thing named to be the subject of the accident.
- 203.18: 3. Intrinsic, which applies in many ways.
- 204.13: The sacrament a subject for both quality and quantity.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Limitatum est nobis*: the impersonal passive does not say who assigned it. "Tropical predication" renders *predicacione tropica*, the pair of *predicacio ydemptica*.

[^2]: **S/T.** Jerome, *Ep.* 120 *ad Hedibiam*, q. 2; Loserth: *Ep. ad Hedibiam*, vol. I, 824 (LEDGER K07d-1). The print's *Helbidiam*; A and B read *Albidiam* (A's margin, B's *alias*: *Helbidiam*). The words of institution are Matt. 26:26.

[^3]: **C/G.** "Demonstrated by the pronoun": the *hoc* of *hoc est corpus meum* points to the bread. *Satrape*: Wyclif's word for the officials of the church courts. *Sentencia* is CONTEXTUAL; here the position taught, "teaching".

[^4]: **S.** Augustine, *Ep.* 98 *ad Bonifacium* (Wyclif "letter 12"); Loserth: *Ep.* XCVIII, Opp. II, 267 (LEDGER K07d-2).

[^5]: **S/T.** Wyclif's "Sermon 55"; Loserth: not *Sermo* LV but LXXXIII *De diversis*, in the Maurist numbering *Sermo* 227, *In die Paschae* IV, Opp. V, 973 (LEDGER K07d-3). Loserth notes *significatur* as the correct reading for the print's *signatur* (200.27); the sense is the same. ABC omit "passes … eaten" (200.26–28), which C's margin supplies in another hand.

[^6]: **C/T.** *Virgines quas oramus* stands in apposition to *fides, spes, karitas, felicitas*: Wyclif plays on the virgin martyrs who bear those names (Faith, Hope and Charity, and Felicity), to whom prayer is made (LEDGER K07d-4). The point: if qualities could be without a subject, the qualities themselves, not the saints, would be the ones to pray to. D reads *quam fides* ("rather than faith …").

[^7]: **C.** *Virtus* here is the quality just named (faith, hope, charity) imagined as an agent; "power" (the faculty) is possible, since the predicates *intuitiva, abstractiva* belong to a cognitive power.

[^8]: **C.** *Potencia* is CONTEXTUAL: "obediential potency" (what a creature can receive from God beyond its nature) and the "passive potency of the subject" take "potency"; *potencia creandi*, *potencia informativa* and *de Dei potencia* take "power".

[^9]: **C.** *Substanciam esse qualem*: "substance's being of a quality (*qualis*)"; so *formatam vel formosam*, "formed or shapely", a play on *forma*.

[^10]: **T/C.** ABCD read *figurat* for *fiat*: "the bread sacramentally figures the body of Christ". The print's *fiat* construes and is kept. The two propositions are given as the decree's sense and the sense it does not have.

[^11]: **C.** *Habitudinem respectivam*: the "respect" sense of the CONTEXTUAL *habitudo*, a relation founded in the accident's foundation.

[^12]: **S.** Augustine, *De Trin.* V.16; Anselm, *Monologion* (Wyclif "27"; Loserth: cap. XXVIII, ed. Maur. p. 14) (LEDGER K07d-5, K07d-6).

[^13]: **C.** *Equivoce a substancia*: quantity is called quantified in another sense than substance is called quantified.

[^14]: **C.** The subject of *sit* is supplied from *ipsa quantitas*: quantity, taken by species, is the base of bodily quality in the subject.

[^15]: **C.** The adversaries' case: white wine poured into consecrated red wine. If the mixture's quantity and quality both change, something prior to both must be their subject, and Wyclif says that subject is the sacrament.

[^16]: **C.** Peter's whiteness makes John "like" Peter (a relational naming in John), and in the same way names God its cause.
