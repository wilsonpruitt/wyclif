Classes: (a) minor premiss / next step, "now" · (b) adversative, "but" · (c) continuative *autem*, "and";
*non … sed* (incl. *nec solum … sed*), "but".

## Doubtful, left as they are

- **01-a, p. 3**: *corporalis autem potestas est delebilis*, rendered "while corporeal power is delible". Adversative (b), set against the indelible spiritual power; "while" carries the contrast in the English construction (a "but" here would follow hard on "but restrictable"). Could be changed to "but corporeal power" if the card is read strictly.
- **01-a, p. 2**: *Sed quia iuxta Aristotelem potestas equivocatur ad quatuor*, rendered "But since". Either (b), turning from the general active sense to the fourfold division, or (a), the next step of the exposition ("Now since"). "But" defensible.
- **01-d, p. 12**: *sed et sacerdotalis potestas spiritualis superaddit*, rendered "and priestly spiritual power indeed adds". Intensive-additive *sed et* ("and moreover") fits no class cleanly (cf. *De ecclesia* 01-d *sed precipue*); "and … indeed" carries it.
- **02-b, p. 29**: *Ista autem blasfema gentilitas … maculat matrem ecclesiam*, rendered "And". Either (c), continuing the account of priests' limits, or (b), turning from what Christ's priests rightly have to their usurpation ("But this blasphemous paganism").
- **02-b, p. 30**: *laudem autem creature … oportet esse aput Deum absconditam*, rendered "and the praise of the creature". Either (c) or (b) against "the praise of the work is to be given to him"; the contrast is clear from the sentence as rendered.
