# *De eucharistia*, chapter 1, part 3: feigned miracles and the five senses

## ¶1 · 19.19–20.17

> [p. 19] Quantum ad istud patet quod fingitur assumptum, sicut nunc finguntur miracula et nunc ex veris miraculis propter ignoranciam commune falsum creditur, ut audivi quendam fingere quomodo hostia de altari paulatim descendit ad ventrem ecclesie et ingressum est cor cuiusdam infirmi qui devote et publice professus est sic inquiens: Tu Deus nosti quod reverenter subducta infirmitate te sumerem, sed non infirmitas mentis, ymmo infirmitas corporis me retardat. Hostia vero fisso pectore cum corde infirmi surrepsit in cordis ventriculum, et sic infirmus constitutus est [p. 20] subito totus sanus. Et postquam narrator iste ex narracione et populi devocione fuit ad partem a quodam familiari socio commendatus, confessus est mendacium hoc turpe: Os finxit, inquit, hoc pulchrum mendacium. Sed esto quod fiant miracula circa hostiam consecratam (ut narrat beatus Gregorius), adhuc foret nimis extraneum noscenti consequencias ex istis inferre quod corpus Domini sit illa hostia consecrata. Correspondenter dico quod, si sompniator iste noverit fidem infirmi credentis quod sentit modo dicto corpus dominicum quod non potest ab experimento vel scriptura convincere, adhuc dico quod iste credens non tunc vel propterea fuit sanctus sed, sicut sunt nimis multi, fuit ex cultu signorum Antichristi ydolatra manifestus. Revera sic posset ex signis seduci populus quod coleret talpas et vespertiliones et creaturas magis abhominabiles tanquam Deum.

As to this, it is clear that the [assumption]{g:assumptum} is [feigned]{g:fingere},[^1] just as miracles are now feigned, and now, out of true miracles, a common falsehood is believed through ignorance; as I heard a certain man feign how the [host]{g:hostia} came down little by little from the altar to the body of the [church]{g:ecclesia}[^2] and entered the heart of a certain sick man, who had devoutly and publicly professed, saying thus: "Thou, God, knowest that I would receive thee reverently, were my infirmity withdrawn; but not infirmity of mind, rather infirmity of body holds me back." And the host, the breast being split open along with the sick man's heart, crept into the ventricle of the heart, and so the sick man was made [20] suddenly wholly sound.[^3] And after this narrator, for his narration and the people's devotion, had been commended aside by a certain familiar companion, he confessed this foul lie: "The mouth," he said, "feigned this fine lie." But granted that miracles are done about the consecrated host (as blessed Gregory [relates]{g:narrare}),[^4] still it would be [exceedingly]{g:nimis} strange to one who knows [inferences]{g:consequencia} to infer from these that [the body of the Lord]{g:corpus-domini} is that consecrated host. Correspondingly I say that, if this dreamer knew the faith of the sick man, believing that he [perceived]{g:sentire} the Lord's body in the way described, which he cannot prove from experience or from scripture, still I say that this believer was not then, or on that account, holy, but, as exceedingly many are, was by the worship of the signs of Antichrist a manifest idolater.[^5] In truth the people could thus be seduced by signs so as to worship moles and bats, and creatures more abominable, as God.[^6]

## ¶2 · 20.18–20.28

> [p. 20] Quantum ad quinque sensaciones predictas patet quod moderniores doctores negant hoc de visu, quia (ut inquiunt) nichil videtur ab oculo nisi secundum formam trianguli cuius conus est in oculo et basis in ipso visibili; sed corpus Domini cum sit multiplicatum ad omnem punctum hostie, non terminat talem basem, ideo secundum vera perspectivorum principia non sic videtur oculo corporali, in tantum quod sancti tenent nec oculum corporis glorificati nec eciam oculum Christi posse carnaliter videre corpus Christi vel sanguinem in hostia consecrata.

As to the five [sensations]{g:sensacio} aforesaid, it is clear that [the more modern doctors]{g:moderniores-doctores} deny this of sight, because (as they say) nothing is seen by the eye except according to the form of a triangle whose [apex]{g:conus} is in the eye and its base in the visible thing itself; now the body of the Lord, since it is multiplied to every point of the host, does not terminate such a base; therefore, according to the true principles of [the perspectivists]{g:perspectivi}, it is not seen thus by the bodily eye, so much so that [the saints]{g:sancti} hold that neither the eye of a glorified body nor even the eye of Christ can carnally see [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} or the blood in the consecrated host.[^7]

## ¶3 · 21.1–21.11

> [p. 21] Item, quererem ab istis fictoribus, si in sacramento vident sensibiliter corpus Christi, utrum inibi stat vel sedet; sic enim Stephanus vidit Jesum stantem a dextris virtutis Dei (ut patet Act. VIIº, 55), nec sciunt hoc dicere ut nec primum; ideo sicut in examinacione testium sunt de mendacio convincendi. Sessio enim et alie circumstancie visibiles eque viderentur ut Christus in hostia consecrata ab oculo corporali. Sicut igitur deficiunt in circumstancia satis sensibili, sic in sensacione corporis Christi quam fingunt.

Again, I would ask of these feigners, if they see the body of Christ [perceptibly]{g:sensibiliter} in the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}, whether it stands or sits there; for so Stephen saw "Jesus standing on the right hand of the power of God" (as is clear in Acts 7:55);[^8] and they cannot say this, as neither [can they say] the first; therefore, as in the examination of witnesses, they are to be convicted of lying. For the sitting and the other visible circumstances would be seen by the bodily eye equally with Christ in the consecrated host. As, therefore, they fail in a circumstance that is [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} enough, so [they fail] in the sensation of the body of Christ which they feign.

## ¶4 · 21.12–21.29

> [p. 21] Item, si tam clare intuentur corpus Christi in sacramento altaris (ut inquiunt), queratur cuius coloris aut quantitatis et figure est corpus Christi ibidem; et oportet eos dicere quod in hostia una sit albius, in alia autem fuscius, in una maius, in alia minus, secundum proprietates accidentales hostie consecrate; et sic Deus noster foret in una hostia plene rotundus et integer, et in alia hostia oblongus et fractus, et cum corpus non videtur sic accidentatum nisi per accidens quod in eo fuerit subiective, oportet dicere quod corpus Christi subiacet cuilibet huiusmodi accidentalitati, et sic Deus noster foret cotidie quantumcunque varie contrarie et inhonorifice transmutatus, quia sacerdos alteraret eum a colore celesti, a figura humana et ceteris accidentibus que decent Christum in gloria ad turpia accidencia absoluta. Sed quid magis paganicum quam quod nos possumus figurare ad libitum Deum nostrum?

Again, if they behold the body of Christ so clearly in [the sacrament of the altar]{g:sacramentum-altaris} (as they say), let it be asked of what colour, or [quantity]{g:quantitas} and figure, the body of Christ is there; and they must say that in one host it is whiter, but in another darker, in one larger, in another smaller, according to the accidental properties of the consecrated host; and so our God would be in one host fully round and whole, and in another host oblong and broken. And since a body is not seen so [subject to accidents]{g:accidentatus} except by an [accident]{g:accidens} that was in it as in a [subject]{g:subiectum}, it must be said that the body of Christ underlies every accidentality of this kind; and so our God would be daily transmuted in whatever way, variously, contrarily and dishonourably, because the [priest]{g:sacerdos} would alter him from the heavenly colour, from the human figure and the other accidents which befit Christ in glory, to foul [absolute accidents]{g:accidens-absolutum}. But what is more pagan than that we can [figure]{g:figurare} our God at our pleasure?[^9]

## ¶5 · 21.30–22.20

> [p. 21] Et conformiter obicitur de auditu: Si enim audimus Dominum in fractura hostie, cum ipsa non sit nisi fraccio corporis quod auditur, oportet concedere quod [p. 22] Christus frangitur; quod est horribilius quam opus militum, quia ut dicitur Joh. XIXº, 33: Ad Jesum autem cum venissent et viderent eum iam mortuum, non fregerunt eius crura, quia Exod. XIIº, 46 de agno pascali figurante Christum precipitur: Os non comminuetis ex eo; multo magis ergo de corpore Christi vivo et glorificato christiani non frangerent eius membra. Sicut ergo facta collisione membrorum vel lapidum exhinc non auditur Deus vel anima, sic senciendum est de corpore Christi fracta hostia. Aliter enim sonus articulatus per organa dignissima foret nimis raucus et illiteratus. Oportet ergo dare duriciem in hostia cum aliis accidentibus non subiectatis in corpore Christi, ex quibus cum aere reverberante efficitur talis sonus; et sic senciendum est de tribus aliis sensibus, scilicet olfactu, gustu et tactu. Nichil enim paganius quam quod putrefacta hostia olet Deus, acefacto sacramento calicis acesceret Deus, et sic Deus noster in ecclesiis ruralibus fetet et acet, sed in civitatibus est sapidus atque recens.

And in like manner [it is objected]{g:obicitur} about hearing: for if we hear the Lord in the [breaking]{g:fractio} of the host, since that is nothing but a breaking of the body that is heard, one must grant that [22] Christ is broken; which is more horrible than the work of the soldiers, because, as is said in John 19:33: "But after they were come to Jesus, when they saw that he was already dead, they did not break his legs", because in Exod. 12:46 it is commanded of the paschal lamb that [figured]{g:figura} Christ: "You shall not break a bone of him";[^10] much more, therefore, would Christians not break the members of the body of Christ, living and glorified. As, therefore, when a collision of members or of stones takes place, God or the soul is not thereby heard, so must one judge of the body of Christ when the host is broken.[^11] For otherwise a sound articulated through the most worthy organs would be exceedingly hoarse and unlettered. One must therefore posit hardness in the host, with the other accidents not subjected in the body of Christ, from which, with the reverberating air, such a sound is produced; and so must one judge of the three other senses, namely smell, taste and touch. For nothing is more pagan than that when the host is putrefied God smells, when the sacrament of the chalice turns sour God would turn sour; and so our God in country churches stinks and is sour, but in cities is savoury and fresh.

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 19.19: Fabled miracles concerning the host.
- 20.1: One such fiction confessed in private by the narrator.
- 20.18: Proof from optics that the body cannot be seen in the host.
- 21.1: Those who say they see it should tell us whether Christ is sitting or standing,
- 21.12: what colour the body is, and whether it varies in different hosts.
- 21.30: So too of the hearing,
- 22.15: and of the other senses.

## Notes

[^1]: **C/T.** The *assumptum* is the dreamers' premiss at 19.9–10, that many saints held they perceived the body of Christ with the five senses. ABC read *argumentum* (19.19).

[^2]: **C.** *Ventrem ecclesie*, literally "the belly of the church": the nave, the body of the building where the people stand.

[^3]: **C.** *Fisso pectore cum corde infirmi*: ablative absolute, "the breast, with the sick man's heart, being split", the host passing into the ventricle; *cum corde* could instead go with *surrepsit* ("crept, with the heart, into …"), which makes less sense. The manuscripts are rough here (19.28: ABC *fissa peccatoris*, D *pectoris cum*, E *fisso peccatori*). *Ingressum est* (neuter, of the feminine *hostia*) is translated as it stands.

[^4]: **S.** Gregory relates a miracle about the consecrated host; the text names no work. Loserth (20.9): *Notae et observ. in Librum Sacramentorum S. Gregorii*, Nota 972, *Opp.* III, 563 (plate); to be checked (LEDGER K01c-1).

[^5]: **C.** *Signa* here are the miracles taken as signs, and *cultus signorum* is the worship of them; Wyclif's word also points at his name for his adversaries, *cultores signorum*.

[^6]: **S.** Isa. 2:20, "moles and bats", the idols cast away (Vulgate *talpas et vespertiliones*); an allusion.

[^7]: **S.** The visual triangle (*conus*, the apex, in the eye; the base in the object) is the perspectivists' doctrine; the claim that no bodily eye, glorified or Christ's own, sees Christ's body in the sacrament is attributed to "the saints" unnamed; Thomas, *ST* III q. 76 a. 7, holds it; to be checked (LEDGER K01c-2).

[^8]: **S.** Acts 7:55 has "standing on the right hand of God" (Vulgate *a dextris Dei*); *virtutis Dei*, "of the power of God", is from Luke 22:69, where the Son of man is "sitting". Wyclif's question, standing or sitting, sets the two verses side by side.

[^9]: **C.** *Figurare*: to give shape; "figure" keeps the word that elsewhere means the sacramental figure, and the pun is Wyclif's. *Figura* earlier in the sentence and paragraph is the shape of a body.

[^10]: **S.** The words quoted are John 19:36, *Os non comminuetis ex eo* (DR "You shall not break a bone of him"), which applies Exod. 12:46 (Vulgate *nec os illius confringetis*, DR "neither shall you break a bone thereof"). The second *quia* gives the reason behind the soldiers' act, as John 19:36 does.

[^11]: **C.** *Senciendum est*: "one must judge", the opinion to be held, not perception by sense, though the verb is *sentire* and the matter is perception.
