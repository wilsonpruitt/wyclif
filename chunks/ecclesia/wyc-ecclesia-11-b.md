# *De ecclesia*, chapter 11, part 2: three corollaries: privilege implies reason; punishment as the fugitive's privilege; no courts or fairs in holy places

## ¶1 · 233.1–237.33

> [p. 233] Ex istis patet triplex correlarium: primum declaracio et confirmacio descripcionis supradicti privilegii; secundo patet, cum actus privilegiandi regum terre exemplari debet a privilegiacione sancte Romane ecclesie, quam oportet subintelligere condicionem sicut oportet et Deum a quo est omnis privilegiacio exemplata: necesse est reges terre subintelligere racionabilitatem privilegii, sic quod non sonet in alicuius iniuriam; quod si ignoranter fecerit, error perceptus debet statim ex mandato Christi et eius pura licencia revocari. Et patet tercio stultum esse ex nudis verbis privilegii aliqua vocata privilegia que sunt manifesti errores concludere, quia (ut patet V° Ethicorum cap. ultimo De Epykeya) necesse est in talibus subintelligere condicionem captam a fine; aliter enim preter triplex inconveniens (deductum in proximo capitulo) sequeretur manifesta blasphemia, ut puta, rex Anglie concedit cuilibet tali fugitivo omnimodam libertatem, sed libertas a peccato est maxima, sine qua non est aliqua vera libertas, ergo rex concedit cuilibet tali ipsum esse liberum a peccato. Quod cum sonat blasphemiam, manifestum est quod hec non fuit regis intencio sed quod haberent quodcunque terrenum privilegium tam in bonis nature quam in bonis fortune quod sue subiacet potestati. Ex istis tercio colligitur quod virtute dicti privilegii multi fugitivi ad dictum locum debent in casu acute puniri, bonis alienis que occupant privari et aliis medicinis tam corporalibus quam spiritualibus ad salutem anime sue tendentibus expurgari. Patet ex hoc quod ibi virtute privilegii habebunt quod [p. 234] nocius vergit ad sui commodum, sed hoc foret in casu huiusmodi medicina; ergo conclusio. Unde movet ad hoc privilegiacio regis, finis, gracia cuius privilegium conceditur illi loco, et utilitas quam caritative rex debet intendere fugitivo. Si enim rex erranter intenderit finem illi oppositum, tunc si ab illo errore purgatus non fuerit, pena sua ex quacunque malicia que directe exinde sequitur, aggravatur, et nedum hoc sed peccatum regum dominorum et prelatorum superstitum, qui correpcionem illam quam debent ex accidia pretermittunt. Nam LXXXIIIª distinccione sic scribitur: Error cui non resistitur approbatur, et veritas cum minime defensatur opprimitur. Negligere quippe, cum possis perturbare perversos, non est aliud quam fovere. Nec caret scrupulo societatis occulte qui manifesto facinori desinit obviare. Et utinam nostri domini, nostri prelati et nostri scolastici attenderent illud dictum; nam totus labor scolasticus debet tendere ad hunc finem propter zelum ad dominum veritatis in quem offenditur. Et ad idem sonat quies monachorum ibi degencium; cum enim omnis nociva perturbacio trahit originem a peccato, patet quod monachi non quietarentur sed turbarentur ex favore et assistencia peccatorum. Et si dicitur quod est eis lucrum, cum fugitivi condividunt temporalia que apportant, superadditur accusans gravacio quod tam sancti viri, tam alienati a mundo, sint socii furum, communicantes cum excommunicatis, impedientibus suam contemplacionem et quietem in domino, quia Isaie IX°, 5, scribitur: Omnis violenta predacio cum tumultu. Ymmo cum loci sanctitas [p. 235] precipue trahitur ab inhabitantibus illum locum, patet quod talis monachorum infeccio derogat sanctitati et reverencie loci quam rex privilegians intendebat. Nec valet dicere (ut audivi quosdam fingere) quod infra locum haberent iniuriati contra profugos respondentes ibidem iusticie complementum. Primo quia ritus regni laudabilis in habendo forum contenciosum in loco prophano non debet in domum oracionis vel locum sanctum propter tale maleficium transmutari. Secundo quia monachis pro quorum quiete rex tam tenere procuravit inferretur dampnum anime, quia inquietacio a contemplacione distrahens cuius oppositum privilegians intendebat. Tercio quia contra ius divinum et ius ecclesie est violare sponsam Christi vel locum eius. Unde VI° Decretalium, De immunitate ecclesie, cap. Decet, postquam ostenditur ex scriptura quod multipliciter decet sanctitas domum Dei, sic infertur: Nullus in locis eisdem in quibus cum pace ac quiete vota convenit celebrari, sedicionem excitet, conclamacionem moveat, impetumve committat; cessent in illis universitatum et societatum quelibet consilia, concertaciones et publica parliamenta; cessent vana et multo forcius feda et prophana colloquia, cessent confabulaciones quelibet, sed postremo quecunque alia que divinum possent turbare officium aut oculos divine maiestatis offendere sint ab ipsis prorsus extranea, ne ubi peccatorum est venia postulanda, ibi peccandi detur occasio aut deprehendantur peccata committi. cessent in ecclesiis earumque cimiteriis negociaciones et precipue nundinarum ac fori cuiuscunque [p. 236] tumultus. Omnis in eis secularium iudiciorum strepitus conquiescat. Nulla inibi causa per laycos criminalis maxime agitetur; sint loca eadem a laicorum congregacionibus aliena. Hec ibi. Quomodo queso foret ecclesia fomentum criminum? vel quoscunque in ipsam committentes defenderet? Unde super verbo Sedicionem dicit glossa quod committens furtum in ecclesia non habebit inmunitatem in ea et hoc tenet Hostiensis, et bene, cum frustra invocat loci suffragium qui fedat eundem. Cum ergo quicunque offendit in uno factus est omnium reus, manifestum est quod omnis peccans mortaliter intra ecclesiam furatur spirituale suffragium, quo Deus plus offenditur quam pro furto temporalium, ymmo cum omnis occupans temporalia in mortali, deficit a iure utendi quocunque, pro tunc manifestum est quod furatur bona ecclesie in qua pausat. Si ergo ille sit prevaricator precepti decimi: quicunque iniuste cupierit aliena, multo magis ille est prevaricator decalogi qui fraudulenter et iniuste detinet aliena. Si ergo non licet alicui dispensare cum aliquo preceptorum domini, manifestum videtur quod non subiacet potestati humane privilegiare locum vel hominem, ut liceat sibi vivere contra mandatum dominicum. Et hic mirantur nonnulli, cum sit ad privilegium locis sanctis quod eximantur a quocunque tumultu secularis negocii, quomodo superiores prepositi ecclesie, scilicet episcopi, priores et abbates nedum permittunt sed vendicant ut privilegium et pertinaciter defendunt, quod in ecclesiis suis ac cimiteriis suis sint nundine et secularia negocia que nunquam sine dolo et crimine multiplici exercentur. Cum autem non est possibile ut duo contraria sint simul privilegium eidem ecclesie, manifestum est quod vel oportet negare privilegium iuris divini ac iuris canonici de exempcione [p. 237] ecclesie a tumultu negocii secularis vel concedere hoc esse privilegium impediens bonos mores. Et credo quod nostri ecclesiastici isti sentencie annuerent, si non essent lucro temporalium excecati, cuius probacio foret, si princeps tolleret questum, renunciaretur privilegium; sed sceleratissimum videtur atque sacrilegum pro pecuniis vendere divinum privilegium. Et per idem tollitur quod leges Anglie non mutarentur pretextu erroris in hoc privilegio, introducendo bancum regis, eius iudicem vel placitum seculare in domum domini; tunc enim foret novissimus error peior priore, cum error ignorancie factus in Deum augeretur supplecius per supinum contemptum; nam unum peccatum illaqueando profundius trahit ad aliud. Quoad terciam personam, scilicet fugientes, patet quod privilegians intendit commodum fugitivi, quia aliter non esset sibi elemosina vel opus misericordie sed omnino contrarium, si intenderet dampnificare proximum. Quod autem esset in casu fugitivo privilegium sic puniri, patet supponendo aliquos occasione male accepta ex securitate talis refugii iniuriari proximis et sic dampnificare animas proprias per peccatum. Suppono secundo quod maius opus misericordie foret impedire occidentem sic animam propriam quam occidere volentem nudum corpus. Et ex istis tercio sic arguitur: Omne maius bonum opus misericordie est pocius inpendendum, sed maius bonum opus misericordie est subtrahere bonum a tali in casu signato et castigare freneticos ad salutem eorum perpetuam, quam egentem solum in bonis corporalibus taliter relevare; ergo illam elemosinam potens pocius debet inpendere. Supposito ergo casu de fugitivis, sic alibi nocentibus, patet conclusio.

From these a threefold corollary is clear. First, the declaration and confirmation of the [definition]{g:descripcio} of the aforesaid [privilege]{g:privilegium}.[^1] It is clear, second (since the act of privileging by the kings of the earth must be copied from the [privileging]{g:privilegiacio} of the holy Roman [church]{g:ecclesia}, which must tacitly understand a condition, as God too must, from whom all privileging is copied), that the kings of the earth must tacitly understand the reasonableness of the privilege, so that it may not tend to anyone's injury; and if [a king] has done so in ignorance, the error, once perceived, must at once be revoked, by Christ's command and by his pure licence.[^2] And it is clear, third, that it is foolish to [infer]{g:concludere} from the bare words of a privilege some [so-called]{g:vocatus} privileges which are manifest errors; because (as is clear from the fifth [book] of the *Ethics*, last chapter, on *epieikeia*) in such things one must tacitly understand a condition taken from the end. For otherwise, besides the threefold [absurdity]{g:inconveniens} (drawn out in the preceding chapter), a manifest blasphemy would follow: for example, the king of England grants to every such fugitive liberty of every kind; but freedom from sin is the greatest, without which there is no true liberty at all; therefore the king grants to every such man that he be free from sin.[^3] Since this tends to blasphemy, it is manifest that this was not the king's intention, but that they should have whatever earthly privilege lies within his power, both in goods of nature and in goods of fortune.

From these, third, it is gathered that by virtue of the said privilege many fugitives to the said place ought in [such a] case to be sharply punished, deprived of the goods of others that they occupy, and purged by other medicines, both bodily and spiritual, tending to the health of their souls. This is clear from the fact that there, by virtue of the privilege, they will have what [234] rather turns to their advantage; but [now] in such a case this would be medicine; therefore the conclusion.[^4] Hence three things move to this: the king's privileging; the end for the sake of which the privilege is granted to that place; and the benefit which the king, in charity, ought to intend for the fugitive. For if the king in error intended an end opposed to that, then, if he has not been purged of that error, his punishment is aggravated by whatever malice follows directly from it; and not only this, but [so is] the sin of the surviving kings, lords and prelates who out of sloth omit the correction they owe.[^5] For in distinction 83 it is written thus: "An error that is not resisted is approved, and truth, when it is not defended, is crushed. For to neglect to disturb the perverse, when you can, is nothing other than to foster them. Nor is he free from the scruple of a hidden fellowship who ceases to oppose a manifest crime." And would that our lords, our prelates and our schoolmen attended to that saying! For the whole labour of the schools ought to tend to this end, out of zeal for the Lord of truth, against whom offence is given.[^6]

And the quiet of the monks living there tends to the same. For since every hurtful disturbance draws its origin from sin, it is clear that the monks would not be quieted but disturbed by favour to and support of sinners. And if it is said that it is gain for them, since the fugitives share out the [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} they bring, an accusing aggravation is added: that men so holy, so estranged from the world, should be companions of thieves, communicating with the excommunicate, who hinder their contemplation and quiet in the Lord; for in Isaiah 9:5 it is written: "Every violent taking of spoils [is] with tumult." Indeed, since the holiness of a place [235] is drawn chiefly from those who dwell in it, it is clear that such an infection of the monks detracts from the holiness and reverence of the place which the privileging king intended.[^7]

Nor does it avail to say (as I have heard some feign) that within the place those injured would have the full measure of justice against the refugees, who would answer [at law] there.[^8] First, because the praiseworthy usage of the kingdom, of holding the contentious court in a profane place, ought not, for such wrongdoing, to be transferred into the house of prayer or a holy place. Second, because harm of soul would be inflicted on the monks, for whose quiet the king provided so tenderly, since [there would be] disquiet drawing them away from contemplation, the opposite of which the privileger intended. Third, because it is against divine [law]{g:ius} and the law of the church to violate the [bride]{g:sponsa} of Christ or her place. Hence in the Sext, *De immunitate ecclesiarum*, chapter *Decet*, after it is shown from scripture that holiness becomes the house of God in many ways, it is inferred thus: "Let no one, in the same places in which it is fitting that vows be celebrated with peace and quiet, stir up sedition, raise an outcry, or commit violence; let there cease in them all councils of corporations and societies, disputes and public parliaments; let vain talk cease, and much more foul and profane talk; let all chatter cease; but, finally, let whatever else could disturb the divine office or offend the eyes of the divine majesty be wholly foreign to them, lest where pardon for sins is to be asked, there occasion of sinning be given, or sins be found committed. Let there cease in churches and their cemeteries trading, and especially the tumult of fairs and of any market [236]. Let all the din of secular judgments be quiet in them. Let no cause, especially a criminal one, be pleaded there by laymen; let the same places be strangers to gatherings of laymen." Thus far there.[^9] How, I ask, could the church be a fomenter of crimes, or defend any who offend against it? Hence on the word *Sedicionem* the [gloss]{g:glosa} says that one who commits theft in a church shall not have immunity in it; and Hostiensis holds this, and rightly, since he invokes the [aid]{g:suffragium} of a place in vain who defiles it.[^10]

Since, then, whoever offends in one point is become guilty of all, it is manifest that everyone who sins mortally within a church steals [its] spiritual aid, by which God is offended more than by a theft of temporal goods. Indeed, since everyone who occupies temporal goods in mortal [sin] falls short of the right of using anything, it is then manifest that he steals the goods of the church in which he rests.[^11] If, then, he is a [transgressor]{g:prevaricator} of the tenth commandment who unjustly covets the goods of others, much more is he a transgressor of the decalogue who fraudulently and unjustly withholds the goods of others. If, then, it is not lawful for anyone to dispense with any of the Lord's commandments, it seems manifest that it is not within human power to privilege a place or a man so that it may be lawful for him to live against the Lord's command.[^12]

And here some wonder how, since it is a privilege for holy places that they be exempted from every tumult of secular business, the higher [superiors]{g:prepositi} of the church, namely bishops, priors and abbots, not only permit but claim as a privilege, and obstinately defend, that in their churches and their cemeteries there be fairs and secular business, which are never carried on without fraud and manifold crime. Now, since it is not possible that two contraries be at once a privilege to the same church, it is manifest that one must either deny the privilege of divine law and canon law concerning the [exemption]{g:exempcio} [237] of the church from the tumult of secular business, or grant that this is a privilege that hinders good morals.[^13] And I believe that our [clergy]{g:ecclesiastici} would assent to this [teaching]{g:sentencia} if they were not blinded by the gain of temporal goods; the proof of which would be [this]: if the prince took away the profit, the privilege would be renounced. But it seems most wicked and sacrilegious to sell a divine privilege for money.[^14] And by the same token [that reply] is [disposed of]{g:tollere}, so that the laws of England should not be changed, on the pretext of an error in this privilege, by bringing the King's Bench, its judge, or secular pleading into the house of the Lord; for then "the last error would be worse than the first", since an error of ignorance committed against God would be more fully increased by supine contempt; for one sin, ensnaring, draws [a man] more deeply to another.[^15]

As to the third person, namely those who flee, it is clear that the privileger intends the advantage of the fugitive; for otherwise it would not be alms or a work of mercy to him, but wholly the contrary, if he intended to harm his neighbour. Now, that in [such a] case it would be a privilege to a fugitive to be so punished is clear, supposing that some, taking occasion amiss from the security of such a refuge, injure their neighbours and so harm their own souls by sin.[^16] I suppose, second, that it would be a greater work of mercy to hinder one who thus kills his own soul than one who wishes to kill a bare body. And from these, third, it is argued thus: every greater good work of mercy is rather to be bestowed; now it is a greater good work of mercy to withdraw a good from such a man in the case specified, and to chastise the frenzied for their everlasting health, than in this way to relieve one who is needy only in bodily goods; therefore one who is able ought rather to bestow that alms. Supposing, then, the case of fugitives who have thus done harm elsewhere, the conclusion is clear.[^17]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 233.1: All privileges must be reasonable and not injurious,
- 233.11: and this condition must always be understood, however the words of the privilege run,
- 233.26: By virtue of this very privilege many fugitives should be punished;
- 234.1: since it would be to their advantage.
- 234.7: If the King had a wrong purpose his mistake should be corrected.
- 234.14: Not to punish the wicked is to support them.
- 234.24: The monks cannot have quiet, if they are disturbed by sin.
- 235.4: Redress cannot be had within the Sanctuary; a) because it is not a proper court of justice;
- 235.10: b) to hold trials there would disturb the monks;
- 235.13: c) to use a church as a court of law is to violate it.
- 236.10: Whoever sins mortally in a Church steals its protection.
- 236.26: Church dignitaries should not allow fairs etc. to be held in Churchyards;
- 237.8: nor the King's court to sit in the house of the Lord.
- 237.15: As to the fugitives it is not a privilege to them to be encouraged in their sin.
