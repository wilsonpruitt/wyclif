# *De potestate pape*, chapter 11, part 6: the pontiff's vestments: rational, mitre, golden plate, bells, and what they figure

## ¶1 · 283.1–284.16

> [p. 283] Sexta vestis est racionale vel logyon, quod pectori pontificis in foramine superhumeralis contexitur. Et fuit vestis conformis texture cum superhumerali, habens nomina duodecim filiorum Ysrahel inscripta secundum quatuor ordines in duodecim lapidibus, ut recolendo de sanctis patribus non degenerent ab eis in moribus. In medio autem fuit lapis sardonicus, cui inscribebantur hec duo nomina: iudicium vel doctrina et veritas. Unde secundum Yosephum, dum populus fuit domino placabilis sacerdosque daret populo secundum inspiracionem Dei responsum prosperum, lapis in signum placacionis divine in solito splendore emicuit. Sed ducentis annis antequam scripsit librum Antiquitatum, quod fuit tempore Tyti et Vaspasiani cessavit dictus splendor, quod fuit prenosticum reprobacionis populi ex peccato. Rasa dicit, quod urin et thyrin Hebreum, quod sonat doctrina et veritas, fuit nomen Domini tetragramaton, virtute cuius responsum et splendor populo innuebant et signat mistice, quod pontifex medians inter Deum et populum debet docere doctrinam inhaustam secundum Dei iudicium; et sicut non docebit aliam quam veritas suggerit, sic doctrinam suam opere et racione confirmare non desistit. Concathenatur autem racionale superhumerali [p. 284] in signum, quod habitus et opera sibi debeant coherere, sic quod totum corpus operum a caritate pectoris procederet. Que caritas licet sit modica in sua essencia, tamen extenditur in quadratum, quia secundum lineam protensam a: summo in infimum a dextris amicorum ad sinistrum inimicorum, et ab antiquo ad patres preteritos ad retro quoad filios successores, latitudo vero in operibus debet effectualiter practisari correspondenter ad istam quadraturam, unde ad denotandum istum ordinem prius recitatur racionale in Exodo XXVIII ante superhumerale, quia oportet thesaurum habitus procedere, cum nemo doceat antequam sciat. Sed in ordini docendi oportet opus precedere iuxta illud Act. primo, Cepit Ihesus facere et docere.

"The sixth vestment is the rational or logion, which is woven onto the breast of the pontiff in the opening of the superhumeral."[^1] And it was a vestment conformed in weaving to the superhumeral, having the names of the twelve sons of Israel inscribed in four rows on twelve stones, so that, recalling the holy fathers, they might not degenerate from them in morals. And in the middle was a sardonyx stone, on which these two names were inscribed: judgment or [doctrine]{g:doctrina}, and truth.[^2] Hence, according to Josephus, while the people was pleasing to the Lord and the priest gave the people a favourable answer according to the inspiration of God, the stone shone out with its accustomed splendour as a sign that God was appeased. But two hundred years before he wrote the book of the *Antiquities*, which was in the time of Titus and Vespasian, the said splendour ceased, which was a presage of the people's rejection for sin.[^3] Rasa says that *urin* and *thyrin* in Hebrew, which sounds "doctrine and truth", was the name of the Lord, the tetragrammaton, by whose power they gave the people answer and splendour;[^4] and it signifies mystically that the pontiff, mediating between God and the people, must teach the doctrine he has drawn in, according to the judgment of God; and as he will teach no other than truth suggests, so he does not cease to confirm his doctrine by work and by reason. And "the rational is chained to the superhumeral [284] as a sign that habit and works ought to cohere with each other, so that the whole body of works should proceed from the [charity]{g:caritas} of the breast."[^5] And this charity, although it is small in its essence, is yet extended into a square: for along a line stretched from the highest to the lowest, from the right hand of friends to the left of enemies, and from of old to the fathers gone by, [and] backward as regards the sons who succeed, its breadth in works must be effectually [put into practice]{g:practizare} in correspondence with this square.[^6] Hence, to denote this order, the rational is recited in Exodus 28 before the superhumeral,[^7] because the treasure of habit must go first, "since no one teaches before he knows." But in the order of teaching the work must go first, according to that of Acts 1: "Jesus began to do and to teach."[^8]

## ¶2 · 284.17–285.3

> [p. 284] Septimum ornamentum est mitra, cydaris vel thyara, que imposita capiti, in quo viget sensus, signat quod illis debet committi cura regendi alios, qui ad sensum preexpositum sciunt preeminenter se ipsos regere. Sic enim loquiturApostolus prima Cor. XI 3 de capite ponens caput uxoris virum, caput autem viri Christum et caput Christi Deum; ex quo sequitur, quod Christus est creatura, cum secundum humanitatem regitur a se Deo. Dicitur autem mitra fuisse duplicata, cuius pars interior est bissina, pars vero exterior iacinctiva habens in parte inferiori semicirculum aureum ab auribus occupat amplectentem; per hanc igitur signatur vite mundicia, cum bissus supponitur, celestis conversacio, cum iacinctivus color eminet; donum autem caritatis et sapiencie connectens hec in capitaneo signatur per semicirculum [p. 285] aureum transeuntem per mitram capiti conectentem. Semicirculus autem signat, quod semiplene in via suscipimus ista dona.

The seventh ornament is the mitre, cidaris or tiara, which, placed on the head, in which sense flourishes, signifies that the care of ruling others ought to be committed to those who, in the sense set out above, know how to rule themselves preeminently. For so the Apostle speaks of the head in 1 Corinthians 11:3, setting down: "the head of the wife [is] the man, and the head of the man Christ, and the head of Christ God";[^9] from which [it follows]{g:sequitur} that Christ is a creature, since according to his humanity he is ruled by himself as God. And the mitre is said to have been doubled, its inner part of fine linen and its outer part violet, having on its lower part a golden semicircle embracing the back of the head[^10] from the ears; by this, then, cleanness of life is signified, since the fine linen is placed beneath, [and] a heavenly [manner of life]{g:conversacio}, since the violet colour stands out; and the gift of charity and wisdom, which connects these in the [captain]{g:capitaneus},[^11] is signified by the golden semicircle [285] passing across the mitre and connecting it to the head. And the semicircle signifies that on the [way]{g:via} we receive these gifts half-fully.

## ¶3 · 285.4–285.19

> [p. 285] Octavum ornamentum est lamina aurea, que transiens ab aure m aurem per frontem iuncta est; cum mitra filis iacinctivis, cui insculpebatur nomen Domini tetragramaton, quod in Hebreo ineffabile nominatur; per quod consignatur Deum omnium conditorem et rectorem secundum suam sapienciam esse precipue a pontificibus ostendendum tam opere quam sermone, ita quod nomen inscriptum significet essenciam, aurum sapienciam, semicirculus patens in fronte significet eius lucernam ad extra in fabrica quam creavit accione eius ad intra a nobis abscondita: ora autem huius lamine supposita signat opus creacionis, ora vero superior signat opus gubernacionis, et connexio huius lamine cum mitra signat, quod officio spiritualis prepositure coniungi debeat hec doctrina.

The eighth ornament is the golden plate which, passing from ear to ear across the forehead, was joined to the mitre with violet threads; on it was engraved the name of the Lord, the tetragrammaton, which in Hebrew is called ineffable. By this it is signified that God, the maker and ruler of all things according to his wisdom, is to be shown forth chiefly by the pontiffs, as much by work as by word; so that the name inscribed signifies [his] essence, the gold [his] wisdom, and the semicircle open on the forehead signifies his lantern outwardly in the fabric which he created, his action inwardly being hidden from us; and the lower edge of this plate signifies the work of creation, the upper edge the work of governance, and the joining of this plate with the mitre signifies that this doctrine ought to be joined to the duty of the spiritual [office of superior]{g:prepositura}.[^12]

## ¶4 · 285.21–285.30

> [p. 285] Preter hec octo ornamento sunt anuli, vitte vel fibule et tyntinabula, que computantur ut partes vestium predictarum. Debet enim pontifex in suo officio habere tunicam iacinctinam, que poderis dicitur, in cuius margine inferiori forent septuaginta tyntinabula aurea et tot vasculi vel noduli quasi mala punica commixti, ut ingradiendo sanctuarium sonitus audiatur. Sic sub pena mortis debet pontifex sonare verbum sapiencie predicando et opera fructifera commiscendo.

Besides these eight ornaments there are rings, bands or clasps, and bells, which are counted as parts of the vestments aforesaid. For the pontiff in his office must have a violet tunic, which is called the poderis, on whose lower hem there were to be seventy golden bells and as many little vessels or knots like pomegranates mixed among them, so that the sound might be heard on entering the sanctuary. So, under pain of death, the pontiff must sound the word of wisdom by preaching, and mix in fruitful works.[^13]

## ¶5 · 285.31–286.29

> [p. 285] Alia autem confibulancia has vestes ad invicem signat cathenam auream, qua caritas connectit omnia opera grata Deo. Unde quatuor causas in [p. 286] genere ponunt doctores talis ornatus pontificis, prima ut excellencia Dei consideretur affectuosius dum eius abiectus vicarius sic ornatur. Secunda ut sacerdotes medii inter Deum et populum tractentur honorabilius. Tercia ut populus intendat cultui divino delectabilius. Et quarta ut divina excellencia consideretur in fabrica sua expressius. Per tunicam quidem lineam dicunt doctores offigurari terram cum suis ornamentis: Baltheus ligans hanc tunicam figuratur occeanum. Tunica iacinctina racione coloris signat aerem. Et tyntinabula cum malogranatis notant tonitrua cum aliis aeris impressionibus. Quidam autem ponunt baltheum cingentem hanc tunicam figurare speram ignis cingentem aerem, superhumerale cum racionali sibi iuncto signat celum ethereum, et multitudo colorum diversitatem stellarum. Duo lapides onichini signant duo luminaria magna celi, duodecim gemme in racionali signant duodecim signa zodiaci, in quibus sunt raciones istorum sublunarium; unde Yob XXXVIII: Numquid nosti ordinem celi et pones racionem eius in terra? Per mitram vero figuratur celum empireum et per laminam auream dominus presidens omnibus creaturis. Et hinc nomen Domini magnum sibi inscribitur. Et hinc creditur Alexandrum magnum adorare Deum pronum in terram, dum vidit formam pontificis sic vestiti, ut narrat Magister Historioarum in historia Machabeorum primo cap.

And the other clasping pieces, which bind these vestments to one another, signify the golden chain by which charity connects all works pleasing to God. Hence the doctors set down four causes, [286] in general, of such adornment of the pontiff: first, that the excellence of God may be considered more affectionately while his lowly vicar is so adorned; second, that the priests, as mediators between God and the people, may be treated more honourably; third, that the people may attend to divine worship with more delight; and fourth, that the divine excellence may be considered more expressly in his fabric. By the linen tunic, the doctors say, the earth with its ornaments is figured; the belt binding this tunic figures the ocean; the violet tunic by reason of its colour signifies the air; and the bells with the pomegranates denote thunders with the other impressions of the air. But some hold that the belt girding this tunic figures the sphere of fire girding the air; the superhumeral with the rational joined to it signifies the ethereal heaven, and the multitude of colours the diversity of the stars; the two onyx stones signify the two great lights of heaven; the twelve gems in the rational signify the twelve signs of the zodiac, in which are the reasons of these sublunar things;[^14] hence Job 38: "Dost thou know the order of heaven, and canst thou set down the reason thereof on the earth?" And by the mitre the empyrean heaven is figured, and by the golden plate the Lord presiding over all creatures; and hence the great name of the Lord is inscribed on it. And hence it is believed that Alexander the Great worshipped God, prostrate on the ground, when he saw the form of the pontiff so vested, as the Master of the Histories relates in the history of the Maccabees, chapter 1.[^15]

## Notes

[^1]: **G.** *Racionale* is the Vulgate's name for the breastplate (DR "the rational of judgment", Exod. 28:15, made "according to the workmanship of the ephod"); *logyon* is the Greek name. *Superhumerale* is "superhumeral", the ephod, as at 282.16. Loserth italicises 283.1–3 and 283.21–284.3 as quotation; the source is not given (to be checked, LEDGER P11f-1).
[^2]: **S.** Exod. 28:30 puts "doctrine and truth" (DR) in the rational; the sardonyx in the middle is not in Exodus but in Josephus (note 3).
[^3]: **S.** Josephus, *Antiquities* III, 9 (Loserth, citing the Frankfurt 1588 edition, p. 38b, and quoting it at the page foot): the stones ceased to shine two hundred years before Josephus wrote (LEDGER P11f-2); modern form *Ant.* 3.215–218 [our form, to be checked, LEDGER P11f-3].
[^4]: **S.** "Rasa" is Rashi (R. Solomon ben Isaac), very likely known to Wyclif through Nicholas of Lyra on Exod. 28:30 (to be checked, LEDGER P11f-4). *Urin* and *thyrin* are Urim and Thummim; the plural verb *innuebant* has the two names for its subject.
[^5]: **C.** *Habitus* is the habit of knowledge (the "treasure of habit" below), set against works: knowing and doing.
[^6]: **C.** The sentence names the lines of the square without a verb for each: height (highest to lowest), width (friends to enemies), and time (the fathers before, the sons after), then "its breadth in works". A word may be lost (a *longitudo* answering *latitudo*); the print is translated as it stands. The colon after *a* at 284.5 is not on the plate.
[^7]: **S.** In Exod. 28 the superhumeral (28:6) comes before the rational (28:15). Loserth refers to Exod. 25 (OCR "XXV, 28") and 29:5 (LEDGER P11f-5).
[^8]: **S.** Acts 1:1 (DR); the plate reads "Act. primo, 1:". *In ordini* is the print's form for *in ordine*.
[^9]: **S.** Wyclif's order and wording; the Vulgate reads *omnis viri caput, Christus est: caput autem mulieris, vir: caput vero Christi, Deus* (DR "the head of every man is Christ: and the head of the woman is the man: and the head of Christ is God"). Christ "ruled by himself as God": by himself in his divinity.
[^10]: **T.** The print reads *occupat* (so on the plate), which does not construe; we translate *occiput*, "the back of the head" (our emendation). The manuscripts have *per occiput mitram* at 285.1, where Loserth prints *per mitram*.
[^11]: **C.** *Capitaneus* is the wearer, the pontiff as captain of the people; "in the head" (*capite*) would also suit the mitre, but the print has *capitaneo*.
[^12]: **C.** *Connexio* is the plain joining of plate and mitre, not the logicians' "conditional". *Doctrina* is the teaching of God as maker and ruler just described.
[^13]: **S.** Exod. 28:33–35: "that the sound may be heard, when he goeth in and cometh out of the sanctuary … and that he may not die" (DR). Exodus gives no number of bells.
[^14]: **S.** The reading of the vestments as the universe is Wisd. 18:24 and Josephus, *Antiquities* 3.7.7; Wyclif's "doctors" are not named (to be checked, LEDGER P11f-6). The *raciones* of sublunar things are their causes in the heavens, answering *racionem eius* in Job 38:33 (DR; Loserth's foot reference reads "Job XVIII, 33").
[^15]: **S.** Peter Comestor, *Historia scholastica*, *Historia libri Machabaeorum* c. 1 (Loserth, "Petr. Comest. l. c."; LEDGER P11f-7); the story is from Josephus, *Antiquities* 11.8.5 (to be checked, LEDGER P11f-8).
