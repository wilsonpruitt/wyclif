# *De eucharistia*, chapter 5, part 3: why the bread remains: sense, reason, the saints and scripture

## ¶1 · 123.29–124.19

> [p. 123] Nec credo quod plus excellencie sacramenti huius potest dici ex racionibus vel scriptura. Unde stulte multiplicant quidam argumenta in ista materia quod ad magnificandum Dei potestatem et graciosam pietatem, [p. 124] ac ad augendum nostram meritoriam credendi difficultatem sunt talia ardua ac difficilia credenda de hostia, ut scola hodie dogmatisat quia lex Christi debet esse patens et facilis secundum formam scripture et non difficultata ultra eius limites; ymmo infinitum honorabilius posset credi de hoc sacramento, cum posset credi et baptismatis signis ac logice defendi magis extranee istorum opposita ac magis difficilia; illa ergo propter causas consimiles sunt credenda. Posset enim fingi quod forma substancialis per se sine quantitate vel qualitate supplet officium in hostia cuiuscunque experiencie quam sentimus; quod minus claudicaret a veritate quam hodierna ficticia: ideo ne blasphememus, oportet quod secure teneamus nos in illis limitibus quos sancti ex scriptura sacra posuerant, ne forte ponamus blaspheme Deum auctorisare mendacium, quod non potest; quod creditur fieri in multis quoad hoc sacramentum infundabiliter adinventis.

Nor do I believe that more of the excellence of this [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} can be said from reasons or from scripture. Hence certain [men] foolishly multiply arguments in this matter, that, to magnify the [power]{g:potestas} of God and his gracious [piety]{g:pietas}, [124] and to increase our meritorious difficulty in believing, such hard and difficult things are to be believed about the [host]{g:hostia} as the school today [teaches as dogma]{g:dogmatisare}; [foolishly,] because [the law of Christ]{g:lex-christi} ought to be open and easy according to the form of scripture, and not made difficult beyond its [limits]{g:limites}. Indeed, infinitely more honourable things could be believed of this sacrament, since the opposites of these, and more difficult things, could be believed of the signs of baptism too, and be defended in logic more strangely; therefore those [things] are, for like causes, to be believed.[^1] For it could be feigned that the [substantial form]{g:forma-substancialis} [of itself]{g:per-se}, without [quantity]{g:quantitas} or [quality]{g:qualitas}, supplies in the host the office of whatever experience we [perceive]{g:sentire}; which would limp less from the truth than today's [fiction]{g:ficticia}.[^2] Therefore, lest we blaspheme, we must keep ourselves securely within those limits which [the saints]{g:sancti} had set from holy scripture, lest perhaps we blasphemously posit that God [authorizes]{g:autorizare} a lie, which he cannot do; which is believed to happen in many things devised [without foundation]{g:infundabiliter} concerning this sacrament.[^3]

## ¶2 · 124.20–125.2

> [p. 124] Et si queratur quomodo fundabitur quod panis et vinum remanent hostia consecrata, dicitur (ut sepe superius) quod ad hoc movent sensus et racio sanctorum, doctorum testificacio et fidei scripture informacio. Nam eque certe movet sensus ad credendum ibi esse panem et vinum, sicut movet ad credendum ibi esse quantitatem aut qualitatem, sicut prior est illorum sensitiva noticia. Racio autem videtur triplex: prima quod omnes disposiciones previe requisite ad formas elementi vel mixti sunt posite, sicut et omnes potencie agendi ex quibus naturalis concluderet formas tales, et tercio quod Deus non sine causa necessaria [p. 125] dampnificaret suam universitatem subtrahendo naturam substancie.

And if it is asked how it will be grounded that [bread]{g:panis} and [wine]{g:vinum} remain, the host consecrated, [the answer is]{g:dicitur} (as often above) that sense and reason move [us] to this, the testimony of the saints [and] doctors, and the [informing]{g:informacio} of the faith of scripture.[^4] For sense moves [us] to believe that bread and wine are there just as certainly as it moves [us] to believe that quantity or quality is there, as the sense-knowledge of those is prior.[^5] And the reason seems threefold: first, that all the dispositions [required beforehand]{g:previe-requisitum} for the forms of an element or of a mixed [body] are posited, as are all the [powers]{g:potencia} of acting from which a natural [philosopher] would [infer]{g:concludere} such forms; and thirdly, that God would not without necessary cause [125] [damage]{g:dampnificare} [his universe]{g:universitas} by withdrawing the nature of [substance]{g:substancia}.[^6]

## ¶3 · 125.3–125.9

> [p. 125] Triplex autem ponitur testificacio sanctorum secundum dicta superius. Primum est dictum Augustini positum in De Consecracione, distinccione IIª, Qui manducant, ubi sic dicitur: quod videtur panis est, et calix quod oculi renunciant, quod autem fides postulat instruenda, panis est corpus Christi et calix sanguis.

And the testimony of the saints is laid down as threefold, according to what was said above. The first is the saying of Augustine set in *De consecratione*, distinction 2, *Qui manducant*, where it is said thus: "What is seen is bread, and the [chalice]{g:calix}, as the eyes report; but as faith asks to be instructed, the bread is [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} and the chalice the blood."[^7]

## ¶4 · 125.10–125.14

> [p. 125] Item, eadem distinccione ex dicto Hilarii sic habetur: Corpus Christi quod sumitur de altari figura est, dum panis et vini natura extra videtur: veritas autem, dum corpus et sanguis Christi in veritate interius creditur.

Again, in the same distinction, from a saying of Hilary, it is had thus: "The body of Christ which is taken from the altar is a [figure]{g:figura}, while the [nature]{g:natura} of bread and wine is seen outwardly; but a truth, while the body and blood of Christ is believed inwardly in truth."[^8]

## ¶5 · 125.15–127.14

> [p. 125] Item, sanctus papa Nicholaus cum multis episcopis declarat ut fidem panem et vinum que in altari ponuntur remanere post consecracionem tam sacramentum quam carnem Christi et sanguinem, ut patet distinccione IIª Ego Berengarius. Et ex istis credo diffinicionem Urbani nostri cum suis episcopis tenere antiquam fidem Romane ecclesie, licet Robertus cum suis teneat ficticiam Avinone de transsubstanciacione; nec videtur alter eorum dignus in papam recipi, nisi declarare sciverit istam fidem, cum docere fidem katholicam sit precipuum illorum officium. Unde quidam credunt quod non concordabunt caritative antequam per alterum eorum hoc efficaciter sit edoctum, quia propter huius defectum et ipsi et eorum subditi sunt ydolatre et blasphemi, sic quod de sic errantibus in hiis signis potest dici illud [p. 126] Ysaie Iº, 11: Quo mihi multitudinem victimarum vestrarum? Plenus sum. Et sequitur: Cum extenderitis manus vestras, avertam oculos meos a vobis, et cum multiplicaveritis, oracionem non exaudiam: Quomodo ergo excusabuntur in isto peccato, qui ex officio tenentur in isto docere ecclesiam? Et ex fide scripture videtur quod panis et vinum post benediccionem remaneant sacramentum; nam (ut sepe repecii) quatuor evangeliste narrant quomodo Christus accepit panem et vinum et illa demonstrando dixit: Hoc est corpus meum vel sanguis. Secundo confirmatur ex evangelio Apostoli Iº, Cor. Xº, 14, 16, 17, ubi primo precipit: Fugite ab ydolorum cultura. Et adiungit: Calix benediccionis cui benedicimus nonne communicacio sanguinis Christi est? et panis quem frangimus nonne participacio corporis Domini est? quoniam unus panis et unum corpus multi sumus; ubi manifeste patet quod loquitur de pane et vino materialibus, que post benediccionem sunt hoc sacramentum; et iterum manifestum est quod hic utitur predicacione tropica et non ydemptica, dum intelligit panem et vinum figurare unionem ecclesie cum Christo qui est res huius sacramenti. Et patet quanta cecitate percutitur ecclesia que dimisso evangelio et sanctis doctoribus regulatur per stultam heresim decretistarum, qui tradunt pro regula quod generaliter in hiis dictis per panem et vinum intelliguntur accidencia non substancia vel natura. Numquid credimus sanctum Apostolum intelligere appropriate per panem quem frangimus illa accidencia que nescimus? Nonne beatus Hilarius explicat sensum quod sunt natura panis et [p. 127] vini? Nonne secundum decretum beati Jeronimi illi qui intelligunt scripturam sacram aliter quam Spiritus Sanctus flagitat sunt heretici? Et tam detestanda et iners est glossa quam dant dictis sanctorum; et quando Augustinus dicit superius quod videtur panis est, sed quod creditur panis est corpus Christi, glossant per oppositum; quod videtur non est panis sed accidens per se quod fuit in pane; sed certum est quod tam subtilis logicus, tam profundus methaphisicus et tam prudens theologus non sic oneraret verba fidei tam aliena sentencia, specialiter cum expresse tam crebro asserit quod accidens non potest esse sine subiecto, ut exemplificat de quantitate et qualitate.

Again, the holy [pope]{g:papa} Nicholas with many bishops [makes clear]{g:declarare} as faith that the bread and wine which are placed on the altar remain after the [consecration]{g:consecracio} both a sacrament and the flesh and blood of Christ, as is clear in distinction 2, *Ego Berengarius*.[^9] And from these I believe that the [definition]{g:diffinicio} of our Urban with his bishops holds the ancient faith of the Roman [church]{g:ecclesia}, although Robert with his [men] holds the fiction at Avignon about [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio}; nor does either of them seem worthy to be received as pope unless he knows how to make this faith clear, since to teach the catholic faith is their chief office.[^10] Hence some believe that they will not agree in [charity]{g:caritas} before this has been effectually taught by one of them, because for lack of this both they and their subjects are idolaters and blasphemers; so that of those who so err in these signs that [word] [126] of Isaiah 1:11 can be said: "To what purpose do you offer me the multitude of your victims? I am full." And it follows: "And when you stretch forth your hands, I will turn away my eyes from you: and when you multiply prayer, I will not hear."[^11] How then will they be excused in this sin, who are bound by their office to teach the church in this? And [from the faith of scripture]{g:ex-fide} it seems that the bread and wine remain the sacrament after the blessing; for (as I have often repeated) the four evangelists relate how Christ took bread and wine and, pointing to them, said: "This is my body" or "blood".[^12] Secondly, this is confirmed from the gospel of the Apostle, 1 Cor. 10:14, 16, 17, where he first commands: "Fly from the service of idols." And he adds: "The chalice of benediction which we bless, is it not the communion of the blood of Christ? And the bread which we break, is it not the partaking of [the body of the Lord]{g:corpus-domini}? For we, being many, are one bread and one body"; where it is plainly clear that he speaks of material bread and wine, which after the blessing are this sacrament; and again it is plain that here he uses [tropical predication]{g:predicacio-tropica} and not identical, while he understands the bread and wine to [figure]{g:figurare} the [union]{g:unio} of the church with Christ, who is the [reality of this sacrament]{g:res-sacramenti}.[^13] And it is clear with what blindness the church is struck which, having set aside the gospel and the holy doctors, is ruled by the foolish heresy of the [decretists]{g:decretiste}, who hand down as a rule that generally in these sayings by bread and wine are understood the [accidents]{g:accidens}, not the substance or nature.[^14] Do we believe that the holy Apostle means properly, by "the bread which we break", those accidents which we do not know? Does not blessed Hilary unfold the [meaning]{g:sensus}, that they are the nature of bread and [127] of wine? Are not, according to the [decree]{g:decretum} of blessed Jerome, those who understand holy scripture otherwise than the Holy Spirit demands heretics?[^15] And so detestable and inert is the gloss which they give to the sayings of the saints; and when Augustine says above, "What is seen is bread, but what is believed, the bread is the body of Christ", they gloss by the opposite: "what is seen is not bread but an accident by itself which was in the bread". But it is certain that so subtle a logician, so profound a metaphysician and so prudent a theologian would not so burden the words of the faith with so alien a [sense]{g:sentencia-verborum}, especially since he expressly and so often asserts that an accident cannot be without a subject, as he exemplifies of quantity and quality.[^16]

## Notes

[^1]: **C/T.** The foolish argument: hard beliefs magnify God and increase merit. Wyclif turns it: by that ground still harder things, the opposites of these, could be believed of the signs of baptism, so "those" would have to be believed "for like causes". *Baptismatis signis* is construed as "of the signs of baptism"; E reads *baptisalis*, AB *logico*, D *logica* (C corrected) for *logice* (124.7); D *argumenta*, E *opposito* for *opposita* (124.8); E *ille* (124.9). E reads *augendi* (124.1); D *compatens* for *patens* (124.4).

[^2]: **C.** *Per se*: "of itself", the substantial form alone doing what accidents do; the counter-fiction is offered as less false than the accident without a subject. *Sentimus* (GLOSSARY *sentire*, the sacrament sensed) "perceive".

[^3]: **T.** B reads *racione* at *ideo ne* (124.14); E omits *quo* (124.18). C has *ergo* in the margin in another hand (124.9).

[^4]: **C/T.** *Remanent hostia consecrata* is taken as "remain, the host [being] consecrated"; it could be "remain the consecrated host". The print's comma gives *racio sanctorum, doctorum testificacio*, "the reason of the saints, the testimony of the doctors"; the order of what follows (reason ¶2, the saints' testimony ¶3–¶5, scripture ¶5) is followed here. C has *hostia … movent* in the margin in another hand (124.21–22); AB repeat *et racio sanctorum … movet sensus* (124.21–24); D *manet*; ABC *mnvent* (C corrected) (124.24).

[^5]: **C/T.** *Sicut prior est illorum sensitiva noticia*: "since" or "as" the senses know bread and wine before their accidents; *illorum* is taken of the bread and wine. D omits *movet* (124.25); D *istorum* (124.26–27).

[^6]: **C/T.** Three reasons, numbered "first … and thirdly": the second is the clause on the active powers (*sicut et omnes potencie agendi*). *Potencia* (CONTEXTUAL) "powers" of acting, not potency. *Universitas sua*: God's universe, untagged. E reads *primo* (124.27); E omits *Deus non* (124.31).

[^7]: **S/T.** Gratian, *De cons.* D. 2 c. 58 (Loserth; the form is ours), the words quoted from Augustine, *Sermo* 272 (PL 38, 1246–1247); the canon's opening *Qui manducant* is *Sermo* 131.1 (LEDGER K05c-1). *Testificacio sanctorum*: D reads *testimonium* (125.3). CE read *Qui manducat* (125.6); ABC *est sanguis* (125.9).

[^8]: **S/T.** Gratian, *De cons.* D. 2 c. 79 (Loserth; the form is ours), under Hilary's name (Friedberg: *dum panis et uinum extra uidetur*; Wyclif's *natura*, on which he argues at 126.29–127.1, is not in the canon) (LEDGER K05c-2). *Autem* is Loserth's correction: ABDE read *aut*, C omits (125.12).

[^9]: **S/T.** *Ego Berengarius*, Gratian, *De cons.* D. 2 c. 42 (Loserth; the form is ours); Nicholas II, Rome 1059 (Friedberg: *Ex Concilio Romano hab. a. 1059*) (LEDGER K05c-3). Wyclif reports the decree's sense in indirect speech. AB omit *multis*, C adds it in the margin (125.15); E *Beringarius*, CE omit *Et* (125.19).

[^10]: **R/C.** The schism of 1378: Urban VI at Rome and Robert of Geneva, Clement VII, at Avignon (LEDGER K05c-4). *Diffinicio*, Urban's determination: what it was is not said. D omits *nostri* (125.20), reads *Avinonie* (125.22), omits *dignus* (125.23).

[^11]: **S/T.** Isa. 1:11, 15 (Loserth: 1:15 for the second); Wyclif leaves out the Vulgate's *dicit Dominus*, "saith the Lord", in 1:11, which is dropped from the Douay wording. B reads *concordabant* (125.26).

[^12]: **T.** B reads *excusabunt* (126.5); ABD *Sed ex*, C *Et* over an erasure (126.6); D *ita demonstrando* (126.10).

[^13]: **C/T.** *Res huius sacramenti*: here Christ, as the relative *qui* shows, with the church's union with him the thing figured. ABE read *signare* "signify" for *figurare* (126.22). AB read *post consecracionem benediccionem*, C struck *consecracionem* (126.19); ACD *panem illum* (126.21).

[^14]: **S/T.** The decretists' rule, of the ordinary gloss on *De cons.* D. 2; Loserth quotes it on c. 42 at *dentibus*: *omnia referas ad species ipsas* (foot 34.22); the gloss itself is not checked (LEDGER K05c-5). C has *regulatur* in the margin in another hand (126.25); ABCE *intelligunt* (126.27); C *substanciam vel naturam* (126.28).

[^15]: **S/T.** Hilary: ¶4 (Loserth: "vide supra pag. 125"). Jerome: Gratian, C. 24 q. 3 c. 27 (Loserth; the form is ours), from Jerome on Gal. 5: *Quicunque … aliter scripturam intelligit, quam sensus Spiritus sancti flagitat … haereticus appellari potest* (PL 187, 1306) (LEDGER K05c-6). B reads *a proprietate* for *appropriate* (126.29); A *flagigat* (127.3).

[^16]: **C/S/T.** *Sentencia* here is the sense given to words (GLOSSARY *sentencia verborum*). Augustine's quotation: ¶3. That he often asserts that an accident cannot be without a subject: see the places of ch. 3 (72.4–73.4), *De quantitate animae* 4 (quantity) and *Soliloquia* II.13.23 and *Ep.* 187.6.18 (*Tolle ipsa corpora qualitatibus corporum, non erit ubi sint*) (LEDGER K05c-7). D reads *auderet* "would not dare" for *oneraret* (127.10). DE *inhers* for *iners*, B omits *est* (127.4); D *ut quando* (127.4–5).
