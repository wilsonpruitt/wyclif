# *De eucharistia*, chapter 8, part 2: the absurdities that follow from multiplying one body

## ¶1 · 236.31–237.2

> [p. 236] Ex quibus omnibus infertur possibilitas existencie eiusdem corporis per diversa loca et quod idem [p. 237] corpus quocienscunque libuerit potest esse per eundem locum.

From all of which is inferred the possibility of the existence of the same body through different places,[^1] and that the same [237] body can be through the same place as many times as one pleases.

## ¶2 · 237.3–237.9

> [p. 237] Sed contra istud replicatur primo per hoc quod tale corpus est solum semel per datum locum, cum postquam semel intravit illum nunquam exivit, ut suppono. Sicut ergo non quociens comedisti, tociens fuisti homo (ut dicunt loyci), eo quod semel es homo continue, ita videtur dicendum quod solum semel es in isto loco.

But against this [it is rejoined]{g:replicatur}, first, by this: that such a body is only once through a given place, since after it once entered it, it never went out, as I suppose. Therefore, just as you were not a man as many times as you have eaten (as the logicians say), because you are a man once, continuously, so it seems it must be said that you are only once in this place.[^2]

## ¶3 · 237.10–237.16

> [p. 237] Confirmatur ex hoc quod aliter infinicies foret omne corpus in loco, quia infinicies posset extrahi de loco ipso remanente continue in eodem et tociens est in loco; quocienscunque namque Deus extraheret eum de loco aliquo, tociens inveniret eum in illo; quod non foret, nisi tociens Deus poneret illum in eodem.

This is confirmed from this, that otherwise every body would be in place [infinitely many times]{g:infinicies}, for it could be drawn out of the place infinitely many times while it remains continuously in the same,[^3] and so many times is it in place; for as often as God drew it out of any place, so often would he find it in that place; which would not be, unless God put it in the same so many times.

## ¶4 · 237.17–237.21

> [p. 237] Loquamur autem de aliquo modo, non ut dicit vicissitudinem temporis, sed ut dicit multitudinem ordinis naturalis; et tunc oportet dicere quod Deus non potest ponere creaturam alicubi nisi ponat infinicies eam ibi.

And let us speak of some mode [of "so many times"],[^4] not as it [signifies]{g:dicere} the alternation of time but as it signifies a multitude of natural order; and then one must say: "God cannot put a [creature]{g:creatura} anywhere unless he puts it there infinitely many times."

## ¶5 · 237.22–237.27

> [p. 237] Similiter, videtur quod omnis materialis substancia sit infinitum rara et cum hoc infinitum densa, quia tociens posset poni per locum datum quod foret infinitum densa, et ita densa est modo, et per idem foret infinitum rara in casu quo Deus infinicies ipsam extraheret.

Likewise, it seems that every material [substance]{g:substancia} is infinitely rare and with this infinitely dense, for it could be put through a given place so many times that it would be infinitely dense, and so dense is it now;[^5] and by the same token it would be infinitely rare in the case in which God drew it out infinitely many times.

## ¶6 · 237.28–237.32

> [p. 237] Similiter, videtur quod omne corpus qualitate intensum et remissum sit tam infinitum intensum quam remissum, quia per multitudinem et subtraccionem sui a se ipso posset precise cum eadem qualitate esse huius.

Likewise, it seems that every body intensified and remitted in [quality]{g:qualitas} is as infinitely [intensified]{g:intendi} as [remitted]{g:remitti}, for by multitude and subtraction of itself from itself it could, with precisely the same quality, be of this [degree].[^6]

## ¶7 · 238.1–239.14

> [p. 238] Item, videtur quod omne corpus sit infinitum in quacumque denominacione sua corporea; nam corpus potest esse infinitum sine acquisicione alicuius quantitatis preter illam quantam habet et quantumcunque magnum potest esse cum illis paribus, ita magnum est modo: ergo omne corpus est infinitum. Consequencia patet ex hoc quod omnem quantitatem quam potest habere cum illis paribus habet modo. Et assumptum probatur capiendo A cubum pedalem tunc multiplicato eo per sex loca in quibus tangat se secundum sex superficies varias et fiat continuacio, et patet quod A equivalet sextipedali quadrangulo sine hoc quod aliqua quantitas generetur secundum se totam; pono iterum quod quelibet secunda pars proporcionalis istius A cum toto residuo multiplicetur per duodecim loca et fiat continuacio secundum superficies dispares, et patet quod A equivalebit uni corpori duplo ad sextipedale, et sic in infinitum secundum omnem dimensionem, petito hoc principio: si sint duo quanta et unum superpositum reliquo, nec excedit nec exceditur ab eodem, tunc illa sunt equalia. A enim est equale loco vel situi per quem sic extenditur. Nec valet dicere quod generatur nova quantitas, quia nulla generatur secundum se totam, sicut non generatur in A superficies, punctus vel linea. Cum ergo penes multiplicacionem punctualium attenditur maioritas quantitatis, patet quod A non maioratur in casu posito, et per idem non minoraretur ipso iterum demultiplicato, ymmo coextensa una medietate A cum sua quantitate cum reliqua, et sic in infinitum usque ad situm punctale, videtur quod tam [p. 239] A quam sua quantitas manebit continue eque magna, quia nullam quantitatem deperdet sed aliquam per accidens acquiret. Ex quo videtur quod nullam magnitudinem acquiret vel perdet, et sic omne quantum foret infinitum magnum et infinitum parvum, ymmo tantus mundus ut est iste et infinitum maior posset constitui per punctale. Quis ergo scit utrum sit ita de facto, cum brevius cum punctali materia posset fieri quoad beatitudinem vel aliquem per se finem; nec racio vel experiencia convincit oppositum. Quomodo ergo poneretur a philosopho cui non sit revelacio tanta multitudo rerum superflua per verbum Dei abbreviatum quod conpendiosissime ordinat media ad fines quos intendit?

Again, it seems that every body is infinite in whatever bodily [naming]{g:denominacio} of it;[^7] for a body can be infinite without the acquisition of any [quantity]{g:quantitas} beyond that which it has, and however great it can be, other things being equal, so great is it now: therefore every body is infinite. The [inference]{g:consequencia} is clear from this, that every quantity it can have, other things being equal, it has now. And [the assumption]{g:assumptum} is proved by taking A, a [foot-long]{g:pedalitas} cube: then, it being multiplied through six places in which it touches itself along six different surfaces, and [continuation]{g:continuacio} being made, it is clear that A is equivalent to a six-foot quadrangle without any quantity being generated as a whole;[^8] I suppose again that each second [proportional part]{g:partes-proporcionales} of this A, with the whole residue, is multiplied through twelve places and continuation is made along unequal surfaces, and it is clear that A will be equivalent to one body double the six-foot one; and so to infinity in every dimension, this principle being asked for:[^9] "if there are two [quantified]{g:quantus} things and one is laid upon the other, and it neither exceeds nor is exceeded by it, then they are equal." For A is equal to the place or site through which it is so extended. Nor does it avail to say that a new quantity is generated, for none is generated as a whole, just as no surface, point or line is generated in A. Since, therefore, the [greatness]{g:majoritas} of a quantity is reckoned by the multiplication of points,[^10] it is clear that A is not made greater in the case posited; and by the same token it would not be made less when it is again [demultiplied]{g:demultiplicare}; indeed, one half of A with its quantity being coextended with the other, and so to infinity down to a point-site, it seems that both [239] A and its quantity will remain continuously equally great, for it will lose no quantity but will acquire some [by accident]{g:per-accidens}. From which it seems that it will acquire or lose no magnitude, and so every quantified thing would be infinitely great and infinitely small; indeed, a world as great as this one is, and infinitely greater, could be constituted through a point. Who then knows whether it is so in fact, since it could be done more briefly with point-sized matter as regards blessedness or any end [of itself]{g:per-se}? Nor does reason or experience [conclusively prove]{g:convincere} the opposite. How then would so great a multitude of things, superfluous by the abbreviated word of God, which orders means most compendiously to the ends it intends,[^11] be posited by a philosopher to whom there is no revelation?

## ¶8 · 239.15–239.29

> [p. 239] Item, per hanc viam tolleretur omnis mensura loci, sicut et omnis species figurarum, nam si Deus potest multiplicare sic generaliter idem corpus in numero, potest et multiplicare generaliter situm, locum et figuram. Nec scimus quando sic faciat, cum foret plus compendiosum, plus philosophicum et plus undique racioni consonum, si foret possibile; Deus ergo multiplicans situm punctalem per totum mundum non posset compendiosius locare aliquid, quia viso fundamento ipse foret locus cuilibet creature et sic posset esse ad terram omnia in omnibus. Concesso ergo isto videtur primo quod omnis locus sit indivisibilis et per consequens nullus maior reliquo; situs enim indivisibilis multiplicatus foret omnis locus. Ex quo videtur quod nichil sit maius reliquo quoad molem.

Again, by this way all measure of place would be taken away, as also every species of figures; for if God can so generally multiply the same body in number, he can also generally multiply site, place and figure. Nor do we know when he does so, since it would be more compendious, more philosophical and in every way more consonant with reason, if it were possible. God, therefore, multiplying a point-site through the whole world, could not locate anything more compendiously, for, the foundation granted, it would be the place for every creature, and so it could be, on earth,[^12] "all in all". This therefore being granted, it seems first that every place is indivisible and consequently none greater than another; for an indivisible site multiplied would be every place. From which it seems that nothing is greater than another as to bulk.

## ¶9 · 239.30–240.2

> [p. 239] Secundo videtur sequi quod nichil posset cum illis paribus moveri localiter et foret plena illusio sensuum quoad omnem sensacionem et specialiter quoad visum; corpora enim naturalia haberent locacionem [p. 240] suam naturalem et visum secundum quem causarent apparenciam motus.

Secondly, it seems to follow that nothing could, other things being equal, be moved [locally]{g:localiter}, and there would be a full [illusion]{g:illusio} of the senses as to every [sensation]{g:sensacio}, and especially as to sight; for natural bodies would have their natural location [240] and the sight according to which they would cause the appearance of motion.[^13]

## ¶10 · 240.3–240.10

> [p. 240] Tercio videtur sequi repugnancia in terminis, cum solum per situm individualem non multiplicatum sit posicio cuiuscunque. Quomodo ergo multiplicatur situs per totum mundum, cum omnis situs sit ubique, quia in isto situ indivisibili? et tolleretur omnis differencia posicionis et per consequens omne signum diei vel noctis, omnis figura hominis et consequentur infiniti errores in illis; quorum opposita implicantur.

Thirdly, there seems to follow an [incompatibility]{g:repugnancia} in terms, since it is only through an individual site not multiplied that anything whatever has its [position]{g:posicio}. How then is a site multiplied through the whole world, since every site would be everywhere, because in this indivisible site? And every difference of position would be taken away, and consequently every sign of day or night and every figure of a man, and infinitely many errors about them would follow; whose opposites are implied.[^14]

## ¶11 · 240.12–240.30

> [p. 240] Quantum ad differencias figurarum patet quod ille presupponunt differenciam situs que tollitur; nam signato A triangulo qui sit medietas quadrati et multiplicato ipso per duo loca immediata sic quod applicetur unum extremum basis A ad aliud extremum eiusdem basis, ita quod fiat ad omnem eius punctum continuacio, patet quod annichilata reliqua medietate fieret unum quadratum, et sic quantumcunque magna figura et cuiuscunque speciei potest esse eciam per solum punctum multiplicatum; nam multiplicato A puncto per omnem situm punctalem qui iam est in mundo, et annichilato toto residuo, patet quod nullibi deficiet situs vel figura. Et patet inanitas responsionis qua dicitur quod non constituitur figura nisi per quantitatem secundum se totam non multiplicatam, quia si eadem persona potest multiplicari, replendo situs eque sicut tot homines, per idem eadem quantitas et figura, quia aliter non foret multiplicacio sed persone per unum situm posicio.

As to the differences of figures, it is clear that they presuppose the difference of site, which is taken away; for, A being designated a triangle that is half of a square, and it being multiplied through two [immediate]{g:immediatus} places[^15] so that one end of A's base is applied to the other end of the same base, so that continuation is made at its every point, it is clear that, the other half being annihilated, one square would come to be; and so a figure however great and of whatever species can be even through a single point multiplied; for, A, a point, being multiplied through every point-site that now is in the world, and the whole residue annihilated, it is clear that nowhere will site or figure be lacking. And the emptiness is clear of the response by which it is said that a figure is constituted only through quantity as a whole not multiplied; for if the same [person]{g:persona} can be multiplied, filling sites equally as so many men [would], by the same token the same quantity and figure [can], for otherwise it would not be multiplication but the position of the person through one site.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Per diversa loca*, *per eundem locum*: "through" keeps Wyclif's *per*, a body extended through the place it fills; the same idiom runs through the chapter.

[^2]: **C.** The rejoinder turns on the logicians' adverbs of number (*quociens … tociens*): being in a place, like being a man, is held to be had once and continuously, not counted afresh. *Comedisti* is perfect, "you have eaten".

[^3]: **C.** *De loco ipso remanente continue in eodem*: taken as an ablative absolute on the body ("while it remains"); *ipso* could instead go with *loco* ("out of the place itself, [the body] remaining …"). The sense is the same.

[^4]: **C/T.** *De aliquo modo* is obscure; ABC read *de alio modo* ("of another mode"). Taken as a mode of the count *tociens*, which *dicit* (signifies) either succession in time or a multitude in the order of nature. Flagged as a doubt.

[^5]: **C.** *Et ita densa est modo*: on the natural-order count of ¶4 the body is already put through its place infinitely many times, so it is already infinitely dense.

[^6]: **T.** *Esse huius* ends the sentence without a noun; a word (*gradus*, "degree", or the like) may have been lost at 237.32. Supplied in brackets. ABCD read *precipue* for *precise* (237.31).

[^7]: **C.** *Denominacio* is the naming of a body from its accidents (great, long, heavy); the side-note gives "attributes". *Cum illis paribus*: "other things being equal", here and below.

[^8]: **C.** *Secundum se totam* "as a whole": no quantity comes to be that is new in every part; the cube's six copies, joined (*continuacio*), cover six times the place.

[^9]: **C.** *Petito hoc principio*: the principle is asked to be granted (postulated), not the fallacy of begging the question (GLOSSARY *peticio principii*).

[^10]: **T.** *Punctualium* (238.26), "points"; ABCD read *multitudinem* for *multiplicacionem*. *Minoraretur* is the print (C in an erasure); ABD *minoratur* (238.28).

[^11]: **S/C.** *Verbum Dei abbreviatum* recalls Rom. 9:28, "a short word shall the Lord make upon the earth" (Vulg. *verbum breviatum*; cf. Isa. 10:23). *Per verbum* taken as "by [the standard of]" the word that does nothing superfluous; C has *per … Dei* in another hand (239.12–13), E *superflue*.

[^12]: **S/T.** "All in all", 1 Cor. 15:28, "that God may be all in all", here of the multiplied point. *Ad terram* is odd ("on earth" is a guess; perhaps from *super terram* of Rom. 9:28 above); flagged.

[^13]: **C.** The bodies would keep their natural location, and sight would show them a motion that is not there. B reads *quem carent* (240.1).

[^14]: **C.** *Quorum opposita implicantur*: the opposites of those errors are implied (are true), so the errors are false; taken as the close of the *reductio*.

[^15]: **C.** *Loca immediata*: places with nothing between them, adjoining (cf. *immediate* "without intermediary").
