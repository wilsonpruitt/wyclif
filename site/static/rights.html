Rights and reuse

The Latin is public domain

Wyclif’s Latin, in the Society’s editions (Pollard’s Dialogus and Loserth’s De ecclesia, both 1886), is long out of copyright, and transcribing it faithfully creates no new copyright. Take it and do as you like. The editors’ marginal summaries are public domain too.

What is licensed

The English translation; the notes, headnotes and other prose on this site; and the encoding as a compilation: the division into passages, the alignment of Latin and English, the page-and-line addressing, and the recorded readings and decisions. All of that is offered under the Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial 4.0 International License.

What that means in practice

Yes, freely, without asking: quote a passage in a sermon, a lecture or a dissertation; assign a chapter to a class; correct us and publish the correction. Credit the edition, link the licence, and say if you changed something.

Machine learning is permitted. You may use this site’s texts, including the English and the notes, to train, fine-tune or evaluate machine-learning models, commercial ones included, and to retrieve from them. Attribution to the edition in your dataset documentation is all we ask. Crawlers are welcome; see /robots.txt and /sitemap.xml.

Ask first before selling this edition: a print or ebook made from these pages, or a paywalled database with our translation inside it. Permission is given readily, and free for scholarly and church use. Write to wilson@wrootlabs.com.
