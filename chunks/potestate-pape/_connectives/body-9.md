# sed/autem extract, wave 9: chunks 10-e 10-f 10-g 10-h 10-i 10-j

### 10-e ¶1 · 236.1–237.14  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 236] De indulgenciis vero quas ipsi distribuebant per bullas non recolo, **sed** committebant illas sine bullis divino iudicio. Nec credo quod omnes falsigraphi fundabunt oppositum. Arguitur igitur communiter: quod Deus posset concessisse Petro et cuicumque Romano pontifici, ut communitas credit, preeminenciam potestatis, nec probari poterit quod non fecit; igitur cum ista fides multum intererat devocionis fidelium, videtur quod theologus debet ipsam defendere et non infundabiliter impugnare. Ista argucia est plena mendacio, nam communitas credit multa impossibilia et heretica de potestate vicaria, ut puta quod quilibet papa habet tantam potestatem sicut Christus habebat humanitus, quod quilibet Romanus pontifex in quantum talis habet infinitum maiorem potestatem quam habet alius in concedendo indulgencias, cum terminum potestatis ponunt in aliis non in illo, sic quod eo ipso quo pretendit se quovismodo solvere vel ligare, eo ipso sic solvit vel ligat; et sic de infinitis summe impossibilibus atque hereticis. De veritatibus **autem**, quas credere debet communitas de pape preeminencia, non dubium quin probari potest ex scripture testistimonio vel ex sensu. Loquendo **autem** de excessu ficte potestatis, patet quod minor est falsa. **Sed** si loquamur de gradu potestatis possibili, patet quod non est color in hoc concludere quod debemus illum concedere, quia sic concederemus in papa et quocumque presbitero omnem potestatem sibi possibilem. Ex ista blasfema evidencia potest habere istam potenciam, igitur habet. **Sed** quis tunc resisteret Antichristo, cum iuxta hoc debemus mendaciter credere falsissimam extollenciam [p. 237] potestatis? Est igitur argumentum sufficiens, si quis extollit se de preeminencia potestatis ecclesiastice, ultra quod scitur fundari ex scriptura verbo vel opere fecisse sanctos apostolos, tunc non debemus credere huiusmodi potestatem. Et sic aliquam potestatem vendicatam debemus non credere, ut ego non credo, non obstante concessione Cesaris quod quilibet papa habet potestatem iurisdicionis sive regiminis super totam Yndiam, Yudeam et alias naciones, ymo credo quod non habet, sicut nec credo quod Petrus superstes habuit tam ample singulariter tantam potenciam, quia tunc nimis graviter ex negligencia peccavisset.

ENGLISH: [236] And as for indulgences which they distributed by bulls, I do not recall [any]; rather, they committed those matters without bulls to the divine judgement.[^1] Nor do I believe that all the [false writers]{g:falsigraphus} will ground the opposite. It is argued, therefore, commonly: that God could have granted to Peter and to every [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex}, as the community believes, [preeminence]{g:preeminencia} of [power]{g:potestas}, and it cannot be proved that he did not; therefore, since this faith mattered greatly to the devotion of the faithful, it seems that a theologian ought to defend it and not attack it [without foundation]{g:infundabiliter}. This [quibble]{g:argucia} is full of lying, for the community believes many impossible and heretical things about the vicarial power: for instance, that every [pope]{g:papa} has as much power as Christ had in his humanity; that every Roman pontiff, in so far as he is such, has infinitely greater power than another has in granting indulgences, since they set the limit of power in others, not in him,[^2] so that by the very fact that he claims to loose or bind in any way whatever, by that very fact he so looses or binds; and so on for infinitely many things supremely impossible and heretical. But as for the truths which the community ought to believe about the pope's preeminence, there is no doubt that they can be proved from the testimony of scripture or from sense.[^3] And speaking of the excess of a feigned power, it is clear that the minor is false.[^4] But if we speak of a possible degree of power, it is clear that there is no [colour]{g:color} in [inferring]{g:concludere} from this that we ought to grant it,[^5] since so we would grant, in the pope and in any [presbyter]{g:presbiter}, every power possible to him. By this blasphemous [evidence]{g:evidencia}: "He can have this power, therefore he has it." But who then would resist Antichrist, since according to this we ought lyingly to believe a most false [exaltation]{g:extollencia} [237] of power?[^6] It is therefore a sufficient argument: if anyone exalts himself on the preeminence of ecclesiastical power beyond what the holy apostles are known, on the ground of scripture, to have done by word or deed, then we ought not to believe in power of this kind.[^7] And so some claimed power we ought to disbelieve, as I do not believe, notwithstanding the [grant]{g:concessio} of Caesar,[^8] that every pope has [power of jurisdiction]{g:potestas-iurisdiccionis} or [of rule]{g:potestas-regiminis} over all India, Judaea and other nations; indeed I believe that he does not have it, just as I do not believe either that Peter, while he lived, had singly so great a power so amply, for then he would have sinned too gravely by negligence.

### 10-f ¶1 · 237.28–241.3  (6 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 237] Quantum ad istud sunt multa dicenda: primo quod argumentum non valet, cum omnes peccaverunt et egent gracia Dei, ideo docet decretum distinccione VIII: Si solus Christus, quod debemus solum sequi Christum, qui errare non potuit [p. 238] vel alios de quanto sequamur ipsum, ipse **autem** summus medicus non autorizavit dotacionem huiusmodi, **sed** verbo et opere reprobavit; et sic videtur michi quod omnes approbantes dotacionem illam peccaverant in hoc mortaliter vel venialiter plus vel minus; nec feramus hoc graviter de beatis, quia beatus Evangelista, qui detestatus est approbacionem huiusmodi fuit sanccior omnibus illis, et tamen ipse dicit: Si dicimus quia peccatum non habemus, nos ipsos seducimus et veritas in nobis non est I. Joh. I, 8. Nec est color si multa ecclesie utilia ex hoc processerant, ideo non fuit in illa peccatum; nam multa mala ex illa processerant, cum ex hoc deiecta est ecclesia cleri a statu perfecciori ad statum minus perfectum sine equivalencia recompense. Secundo seminavit discordiam in ecclesia, ut homines plus appetant preposituram propter fastum et questum quam propter meritum, ut patet superius VIII capitulo. Et tercio facit errores in eleccionibus, in legibus et omnino in officii cleri execucionibus. Aliter enim non est facile fingere, quare indigni tam frontose, tam impetuose et tam ociose ultra quam ante dotacionem aspirant ad pinguiores ecclesiasticas prelacias. Unde vellem eos qui sompniant quod quicquid mali regno contigerit est propter parcitatem vel subtraccionem dotacionis aut privilegiacionis cesaree discere et docere clare suas causales, et quare post dotacionem circa papatus tales magis contenditur quam perante; cuius causa experiencia doceri poterit, si subtracta dotacione cessaret aspiracio ad huiusmodi dignitates. Causa enim secundum philosophos est qua posita vel subtracta correspondenter [p. 239] effectus, posicio vel subtraccio consequetur. Cum igitur ante dotacionem non fuit talis divisio nec adhuc est, ubi non est ecclesia sic dotata, evidentissimum est, quod litura temporalium et voluptas honoris mundani est causa. Cum igitur omnia ista pericula evacuantur per expropriacionem et observanciam status quem Christus instituit, manifestum est (ut sepe repecii) quod elemosina foret expergefacere illum statum. Unde magna infidelitas est qua Antichristi discipuli excecant laycos, quod instar phariseorum Matth. V predicant esse sacrilegium auferre bona consecrata ecclesie, et sic sollempnizant facta que layci cecati stulte et superbe donaverant, **sed** decretum Christi non curant; et tamen luce clarius patet quod seculares debent postposito excommunicacionis timore in hoc errata corrigere, quia ut docet glossa ordinaria super decreto XXIV questio I cap. I: Sacerdos hereticus vel excommunicatus non excommunicat nisi se ipsum, et indubie omnis excommunicans propter restitucionem istius perfeccionis status sancte matris ecclesie est ut sic excommunicatus. Ideo non obstante malediccione sua bonum ecclesie est constanter et celeriter consummandum, ymo (ut patet alibi) sive excommunicatus sive insons maledixerit homini, non est timendum sibi, nisi de quanto declinaverit a Christi consiliis vel preceptis. Ideo (ut dicit glossa) non obstante excommunicacione tali homo debet saltem abscondite celebrare et facere quecumque bona de genere inturbate. Quod si pars Antichristi ad tantum prevaleat, quod sit Christiano plurior, christianus debet publice reclamare, quia talis foret gloriosa causa [p. 240] martyrii. Quod si queratur de gravedine peccati sanctorum episcoporum primitive ecclesie qui dotacionem receperant, certum est quod peccarunt graviter **sed** venialiter ex ingrata negligencia et consensu, ut patet ex decreto beati Jeronymi posito in De Consecracione, distinccione V: Non mediocriter. Et ista sciunt modo Silvestri, Augustini, Gregorii et consimiles, qui in hoc graviter peccaverunt. **Sed** secundo notanda est cecitas, quam sepe repecii, quod per locum ab insufficientissima similitudine cadimus paulative a statu apostolico, qui fuit status viantis perfectissimus, usque ad statum cleri cesareum qui est status cleri abiectissimus; capimus enim illud quod consonat peccatum in gestis sanctorum patrum, et illud extendimus, **sed** illud quod sonat virtutem omittimus. Ut sophistice arguitur, si dicti sancti receperunt temporalia supereffluenter ex pura secularium elemosina et totum residuum pauperibus erogarunt, igitur per idem licet nobis quantumcumque ample extorquere dominia tamquam sine elemosina secularium primo nobis debita, et dimisso eorum usu eis abuti quantumcumque voluptuose et seculariter conversando. Sic enim concludimus in effectu et, amplificando culpam ac dimittendo perfeccionem laudabilem, augemur nimirum difformiter ambobus partibus in voraginem viciorum. Ideo perfeccius atque securius foret nobis tenere limites quos Deus instituit, vivendo de decimis, oblacionibus et privatis elemosinis, quia transgrediendo terminos quos Christus instituit damus nobis et posteris evidenciam corruendi. Et hec fuit causa quare [p. 241] beatus Gregorius percussit iam mortuus successorem suum usque ad mortem, ut recitatum est VIII cap.

ENGLISH: [237] As to this, there are many things to be said.[^1] First, that the argument is not valid, since all have sinned and need the grace of God;[^2] therefore the decree teaches, in distinction 8, *Si solus Christus*, that we ought to follow Christ alone, who could not err, [238] or others to the extent that we follow him [in them];[^3] now he, the supreme physician, did not [authorize]{g:autorizare} an [endowment]{g:dotacio} of this kind, but reproved it by word and deed; and so it seems to me that all who approved that endowment had sinned in this, mortally or venially, more or less. Nor let us take this hard of the blessed, for the blessed Evangelist, who detested approval of this kind, was holier than all of them, and yet he says: "If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us" (1 John 1:8).[^4] Nor is there [colour]{g:color} [in arguing]: "Many things useful to the [church]{g:ecclesia} had proceeded from this, therefore there was no sin in it";[^5] for many evils had proceeded from it, since by it the church of the clergy was cast down from a more perfect [state]{g:status} to a less perfect state without an equivalent recompense. Secondly, it sowed discord in the church, so that men seek the [office of superior]{g:prepositura} more for pomp and gain than for merit, as is clear above in chapter 8. And thirdly, it causes errors in elections, in laws, and altogether in the executions of the clergy's office. For otherwise it is not easy to imagine why the unworthy aspire so brazenly, so impetuously and so idly, beyond what they did before the endowment, to the fatter ecclesiastical prelacies. Hence I should like those who dream that whatever evil befalls the realm is on account of the sparingness or withdrawal of the endowment or of caesarean [privileging]{g:privilegiacio} to learn and teach clearly their causal [propositions],[^6] and why after the endowment there is more contention about such papacies than before; the cause of which could be taught by experience, if, the endowment being withdrawn, the aspiring to such dignities ceased. For a cause, according to the philosophers, is that which being posited or withdrawn, the positing or withdrawal of the [239] effect correspondingly follows.[^7] Since, therefore, before the endowment there was no such division, nor is there still where the church is not so endowed, it is most evident that the stain of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} and the pleasure of worldly honour is the cause.[^8] Since, therefore, all these dangers are emptied out by the [renunciation of property]{g:expropriacio} and the observance of the state which Christ instituted, it is manifest (as I have often repeated) that it would be an [alms]{g:elemosina} to awaken that state.[^9] Hence it is a great [unbelief]{g:infidelitas} by which the disciples of Antichrist blind lay people, in that, after the manner of the Pharisees (Matt. 5), they preach that it is sacrilege to take away goods consecrated to the church; and so they solemnize the deeds which blinded lay people had foolishly and proudly given, but they do not care for the decree of Christ.[^10] And yet it is clearer than light that [seculars]{g:secularis} ought, setting aside the fear of excommunication, to correct the errors in this, because, as the ordinary gloss on the decree, C. 24 q. 1 c. 1, teaches: "A heretical or excommunicated priest excommunicates none but himself";[^11] and without doubt everyone who excommunicates on account of the restoration of this perfection of the state of holy mother church is, as such, excommunicated. Therefore, notwithstanding his curse, the good of the church is to be accomplished steadfastly and swiftly; indeed (as is clear elsewhere), whether an excommunicate or an innocent man has cursed a man, there is nothing for that man to fear, except to the extent that he has turned aside from Christ's counsels or precepts. Therefore (as the gloss says), notwithstanding such an excommunication, a man ought at least secretly to celebrate and to do, undisturbed, whatever things are [good in kind]{g:bona-de-genere}. But if Antichrist's party should prevail so far that it is more numerous than the Christian [party], a Christian ought to protest publicly, because such would be a glorious cause of [240] martyrdom.[^12] And if it is asked about the gravity of the sin of the holy bishops of the primitive church who had received the endowment, it is certain that they sinned gravely but venially, by [displeasing]{g:ingratus} negligence and consent, as is clear from the decree of blessed Jerome placed in *De consecratione*, distinction 5, *Non mediocriter*.[^13] And the Silvesters, Augustines, Gregories and the like, who sinned gravely in this, know these things now.[^14] Now, secondly, there is to be noted the blindness, which I have often repeated, that by the topic from a most insufficient likeness we fall little by little from the apostolic state, which was the most perfect state of [one on the road]{g:viantes}, down to the caesarean state of the clergy, which is the most abject state of the clergy;[^15] for we take what accords with sin in the deeds of the holy fathers, and extend it, but what sounds of virtue we omit. As it is sophistically argued: "If the said saints received temporal goods overflowingly from the pure alms of seculars, and paid out all the residue to the poor, then by the same token it is lawful for us to extort lordships however amply, as though they were owed to us in the first place, without alms from seculars, and, having abandoned the use of them, to abuse them, living however voluptuously and worldlily."[^16] For so we [infer]{g:concludere} in effect, and, by amplifying the fault and abandoning the praiseworthy perfection, we are, no wonder, swollen deformedly on both counts into the whirlpool of vices.[^17] Therefore it would be more perfect and safer for us to keep the [limits]{g:limites} which God instituted, living on [tithes]{g:decime}, offerings and private alms, because by overstepping the bounds which Christ instituted we give to ourselves and to our posterity [evidence]{g:evidencia} for falling. And this was the reason why [241] blessed Gregory, already dead, struck his successor to death, as was recounted in chapter 8.[^18]

### 10-f ¶3 · 241.8–241.33  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 241] Hic dicitur quod false assumitur nec umquam probabitur, ymo sancti ut remisse perceperunt errorem sic remisse laborarunt ad eius extinccionem, ut beatus Possidonius narrat de beato Augustino, quomodo rogavit cives suos, ut caperent possessiones quas sibi et sociis suis contulerunt, ut possint pure vivere de sorte Domini, tamquam clerici, sicut vixerunt in veteri testamento. Et sic videtur michi quod clerici et religiosi possessionati in ista appropriacione et proposito decedentes sine fructuosa penitencia sunt damnandi; et hanc reor racionem, quare Christus Matth. XXIV. 21- dicit: hoc tempus esse tantum horribile, non propter tribulacionem corporis **sed** anime infinitum maiorem, unde Apostolus II Thym. III, 1, 2 dicens, quod instabunt tempora periculosa, annectit tamquam signum et causam precipuam: Erunt, inquit, homines se ipsos amantes. Dyabolus enim ex cupiditate temporalium trahit plurimos ad infernum. Sancti igitur percipientes saltem confuse huius peccati maliciam, saltem confuse finaliter penitebant. Nos **autem** qui oculata fide percipimus huius avaricie invalescentis gravedinem, oportet penitere distinccius de tali avaricia et omnino de consensu et correpcionis negligencia, si volumus esse salvi.

ENGLISH: [241] To this [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that it is falsely assumed and will never be proved; indeed the saints, as they perceived the error faintly, so faintly laboured for its extinction, as blessed Possidius relates of blessed Augustine: how he asked his citizens to take [back] the possessions which they had conferred on him and his companions, so that they might live purely on the Lord's lot, as clerics, as they lived in the old testament.[^20] And so it seems to me that [clerics]{g:clericus} and [possessioned]{g:possessionatus} [religious]{g:religiosi} who die in this [appropriation]{g:appropriacio} and purpose without fruitful penance are to be damned;[^21] and this I reckon the reason why Christ (Matt. 24:21) says that this time is so horrible, not on account of tribulation of the body but [on account of tribulation] of the soul, infinitely greater; hence the Apostle (2 Tim. 3:1–2), saying that "dangerous times shall come", adds as the sign and chief cause: "Men", he says, "shall be lovers of themselves."[^22] For the devil, by covetousness of temporal goods, draws very many to hell. The saints, therefore, perceiving at least [indistinctly]{g:confuse} the malice of this sin, at least indistinctly repented at the end. But we, who perceive [with our own eyes]{g:oculata-fide} the gravity of this growing avarice, must repent more distinctly of such avarice, and altogether of consent and of negligence in correction, if we wish to be saved.

### 10-g ¶2 · 242.6–242.30  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 242] **Sed** adhuc replicatur, quomodo melius foret corrigere cleri cupidinem quam devote collata auferre? cum ista possunt meritorie possideri et securitas ac copia temporalium confortat et auget clericos, ymo habent hoc, cum sit meritorium et iuste petitum ex titulo doni Dei; grave itaque foret cum tanta subtraccione perturbare ecclesiam. Hic videtur michi quod evidencia non procedit, cum melius foret clericos conversari ut clericos generaliter quoad quoscumque dotatos; optimum itaque foret radicitus corrigere cleri cupidinem, auferendo venenosam occasionem, sic quod clerus reductus ad perfeccionem status primevi allidat parvulos reatus ad petram iusticie. Ista itaque foret correccio cupiditatis cleri fructuosior, permanencior et complecior, quam blasfema extorsio humanitus palliata. Qui enim sanaret vulnus vel morbum hominis, destrueret radicalem causam; qua dimissa remaneret patens occasio frutices cupidinis producendi; oportet tamen prudenter auferre radicem istam pestiferam, quando ex peccato notorio datur occasio auferendi, specialiter incipiendo a fonte cupidinis producendi, quia non cessabunt cupiditatum litigia nisi ipse fuerit exsiccatus.

ENGLISH: [242] But it is further rejoined that it would be better to correct the cupidity of the [clergy]{g:clerus} than to take away what was devoutly conferred,[^2] since these things can be possessed meritoriously, and the security and [plenty]{g:copia} of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} strengthens and increases the clerics; indeed they have this, since it is meritorious and justly sought by the [title]{g:titulus} of God's gift; so it would be a grave thing to disturb the [church]{g:ecclesia} with so great a withdrawal. Here it seems to me that the [evidence]{g:evidencia} does not go through, since it would be better for clerics to live as clerics generally, as regards all who are endowed whatever;[^3] so it would be best to correct the clergy's cupidity at the root, by taking away the poisonous occasion, so that the clergy, brought back to the perfection of its primeval [state]{g:status}, may "dash the little ones" of guilt "against the rock" of justice.[^4] This, then, would be a correction of the clergy's cupidity more fruitful, more lasting and more complete than a blasphemous extortion [cloaked]{g:palliare} in human fashion. For whoever would heal a man's wound or disease would destroy the root cause; if that were left, there would remain an open occasion of producing shoots of cupidity. Yet this pestilent root must be taken away prudently, when occasion of taking it away is given by a notorious sin, beginning especially from the fount of cupidity that is to be produced, for the lawsuits of cupidities will not cease unless that fount has been dried up.[^5]

### 10-g ¶3 · 242.31–246.21  (14 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 242] Sunt **autem** mille occasiones sanandi ecclesiam, **sed** cautela dyaboli sparsi sunt favores et pax, quam Christus venit dissolvere, et sic dona mamone [p. 243] sunt inter legistas et clericos eciam exproprietarios et dominos seminata. **Sed** oportet quod Deus gladio vindicte quem venit mittere modo sibi abscondito, ut pestilencia, guerra vel alia pena racionali dissipet istas fraudes; tunc enim foret clerus proporcionatus in numero et virtute, ubi iam nocivus est undique vivis et mortuis: vivis non faciendo clericale officium peccati populi destructivum, et mortuis non mitigando penam defunctorum quam ex stultis donariis paciuntur. Et ista argucia: Hoc bene potest fieri, igitur non debet corrigi, multos cecat; securitas **autem** debet confidenter exspectari a Deo non seculo, et in hoc debet sperari tam nobis quam laycis vera securitas, et ideo precipit nobis Apostolus Hebre, ultimo, quod sumus contenti presentibus, ita quod confidenter dicamus: Dominus michi adiutor et non timebo, quid faciat michi homo. Et hinc petimus in oracione dominica: panem nostrum cotidianum da nobis hodie. **Sed** et nos sacerdotes innitentes ritibus legis veteris confidenter annuatim sine thaurizacione diutina porcionem Domini scilicet oblaciones et decimas expectamus. Quantum ad dacionem Domini, patet quod in penam peccati dat indignis, quod eis obedit, ut dicit Augustinus in De Verbis Domini Sermone LIII: Deus, inquit, iratus dat creature ad eius voluntatem et alias placidus dat creature ad eius utilitatem, eciam quod non appetit habere. Triplex **autem** ponitur exemplum primi, nam [p. 244] Yob primo legitur quomodo Deus dedit dyabolo potestatem ad temptandum beatum Yob in sui confusionem et sancti probacionem, sic eciam demones exauditi sunt petendo se mitti in porcos Mar. V, et sic Israhelite exauditi sunt secundum suum desiderium, scilicit confusionem in escis carnalibus, ut patet Psalmus LXXVII, 30: Adhuc esce eorum erant in ore ipsorum et ira Dei ascendit super eos. **Sed** Apostolus exauditus est in contrario illius, quod peciit. Nam II. Cor. XII, 8 ita dicit: Ter Dominum rogavi, ut discederet a me angelus sathane, et dixit mihi: Sufficit tibi gracia mea, nam virtus in infirmitate perficitur. Sunt enim nonnulli qui ex carencia temptacionis superbiunt, et sunt nonnulli qui ex fortitudine temptacionis senciunt de se ipsis humilius et gloriosius coronantur; et ideo preter beatitudinem et alia per se bona debemus condicionaliter petere moderate, ut petendo temporalia debemus modum attendere; et condicionaliter petere, si et quatenus nobis prosunt. Et hec racio, quare petimus sub forma panis cottidiani temporalia necessaria hodie nobis dari, que Apostolus vocat presencia. Et constat, quod nimis levis est evidencia, Deus concedit eciam sanctis copiam temporalium et gloriam mundanam, igitur eis expedit vel est licitum sic habere, quamvis sint aliunde apud Deum laudabiles, verumtamen non in illo. Unde videtur quod nostri episcopi sunt Christo ingratiores quam Scarioth et legis veteris sacerdotes; ipsi enim tenuerunt se superficietenus, quoad possessiones in limitibus legis Dei, **sed** [p. 245] adulterati sunt abscondite in adulacione voti aut elemosine, ut patet Matth. XV; et ideo remansit penes eos palliata clavis sciencie; unde et Christus Joh. V, 39 dicit eos putare vitam eternam habere in scripturis. Et Matth. XXIII, 3: Omnia quecumque dixerint vobis, facite, quod non diceret hodie de nostris episcopis propter ingratiorem defectum sentencie, ideo videtur quod sunt plus debriati avaricia et ingratitudine ignorancie, sapiencie prime opposite, quam irreligiositas Scariothis. Melius utique foret semel ad sanitatem perfectam perturbare ecclesiam quam quod remaneat continue periculosius, irreligiosius et lacius perturbata. Ulterius notandum est tercio, quod necesse est paparum et episcoporum ordinem esse in ecclesia militante. **Sed** duplicem ordinem talium ministrorum considera, scilicet cesareum et divinum. Quamvis **autem** oporteat ex malicia ordinem cesareum quem Christus approbat secundum totum bonum virtutis in eo bonum utile vel nature, necesse tamen est in eo esse malum culpe quod Deus reprobat, et sic dat Deus tyrannis et blasfemis bona que occupant sine eorum vero dominio. Alium **autem** ordinem divinum oportet esse integre secundum ordinacionem divinam, cum non per se sapit peccatum ut prior. Ipse **autem** ex institucione humana capit gradus suos, secundum quod variantur gradatim mundi divicie vel honores. Alius **autem** ordo hec respuens respicit pure et mundo abscondite ordinacionem divinam ad plus vel minus prodessendum ecclesie; quando **autem** ordinacio humana concordat in persona quam Deus ad gradum talem elegerit, [p. 246] sapit minus peccatum et aliter colitur Antichristus. Unde nichil maius hereticum quam quod tam necessarius est iste ordo cesareus, ut nemo posset sine papa istius ordinis esse salvus, primo quod multis pseudopapis non existentibus, ut patet ex dictis, sancta duravit predestinatorum ecclesia, secundo quia trecentis annis et amplius, quibus sine dotacione crevit ecclesia, regulabatur sine tali primatu cesareo communi consilio sacerdotum. Pro quo tempore idem fuit sacerdos et episcopus, ut patet ex dictis. Tercio quod totus ordo talis fuit, institutus a cesare preter Christum, ut patet ex dictis VIII cap. Et nichil infidelius quam quod nemo potest modo salvari sine suo decreto tam extraordinario preter fidem. Sicut igitur conversi in Yndia et Grecia et aliis locis, ubi papatus noster non colitur, sunt christiani salvandi, eciam qui religiosius nobis vivunt: sic in Europa stat esse perfectos christianos secundum fidem Christi, etsi non cognoscant preeminenciam pontificis Constantini.

ENGLISH: [242] And there are a thousand occasions of healing the church; but by the devil's [wile]{g:cautela} favours have been scattered, and the peace which Christ came to dissolve, and so the gifts of mammon [243] have been sown among [legists]{g:legiste} and clerics, even the [propertyless]{g:exproprietarii}, and lords.[^6] But God must, with the sword of vengeance which he came to send, in a way hidden with himself, as by pestilence, war or another reasonable penalty, scatter these frauds;[^7] for then the clergy would be proportioned in number and in [virtue]{g:virtus}, whereas now it is harmful on every side to the living and to the dead: to the living, by not doing the clerical office that destroys the people's sin; and to the dead, by not mitigating the punishment of the departed which they suffer for their foolish gifts.[^8] And this [quibble]{g:argucia}, "This can be well done, therefore it ought not to be corrected", blinds many. But security ought to be awaited confidently from God, not from the world, and in this true security is to be hoped for, by us as by the laity; and therefore the Apostle commands us (Hebrews, the last chapter) that we be content with present things, so that "we may confidently say: The Lord is my helper: [and] I will not fear what man shall do to me."[^9] And hence we ask in the Lord's prayer: "Give us this day our daily bread."[^10] And we priests too, relying on the rites of the old law, confidently await year by year, without long hoarding, the Lord's portion, namely offerings and tithes. As to the Lord's giving, it is clear that in punishment of sin he gives to the unworthy, in that he obeys them,[^11] as Augustine says in *On the Words of the Lord*, sermon 53: "God", he says, "when angry gives to a creature according to its will, and at other times, when pleased, gives to a creature according to its advantage, even what it does not desire to have."[^12] And a threefold example of the first is given: for [244] in Job 1 it is read how God gave the devil [power]{g:potestas} to tempt blessed Job, to the devil's confusion and the saint's proving; so also the demons were heard when they asked to be sent into the swine (Mark 5); and so the Israelites were heard according to their desire, namely to their confusion in fleshly foods, as is clear from Psalm 77:30: "As yet their meat was in their mouth: and the wrath of God came upon them."[^13] But the Apostle was heard in the contrary of what he asked. For in 2 Cor. 12:8 he says thus: "Thrice I besought the Lord that the angel of Satan might depart from me. And he said to me: My grace is sufficient for thee: for power is made perfect in infirmity."[^14] For there are some who grow proud from lack of temptation, and there are some who from the strength of temptation think more humbly of themselves and are more gloriously crowned; and therefore, apart from blessedness and the other goods in themselves, we ought to ask conditionally [and] moderately, so that in asking for temporal goods we ought to attend to measure, and to ask conditionally, if and in so far as they profit us.[^15] And this is the reason why we ask, under the form of daily bread, that the necessary temporal goods be given us today, which the Apostle calls "present things". And it is evident that this is too light an evidence: "God grants even to saints plenty of temporal goods and worldly glory, therefore it is expedient for them, or lawful, so to have them", although they are praiseworthy with God on other grounds; nevertheless not in that. Hence it seems that our [bishops]{g:episcopus} are more [ungrateful]{g:ingratus} to Christ than Iscariot and the priests of the old law;[^16] for these kept themselves outwardly, as to possessions, within the [limits]{g:limites} of God's law, but [245] were secretly adulterated in flattery of a vow or of alms, as is clear in Matt. 15; and therefore the key of knowledge remained with them, cloaked; hence Christ too (John 5:39) says that they think they have life everlasting in the scriptures.[^17] And Matt. 23:3: "All things whatsoever they shall say to you, do", which he would not say today of our bishops, because of the more displeasing defect of [teaching]{g:sentencia};[^18] therefore it seems that they are more drunk with avarice and with the [ingratitude]{g:ingratitudo} of ignorance, opposed to the first wisdom, than the [irreligion]{g:irreligiositas} of Iscariot [was].[^19] It would certainly be better to disturb the church once, to perfect health, than that it should remain continually disturbed, more dangerously, more irreligiously and more widely. Further, thirdly, it is to be noted that it is necessary that there be an order of popes and bishops in the [church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans}. But consider a twofold order of such ministers, namely the caesarean and the divine. Now although it is needful, out of malice, [that there be] the caesarean order, which Christ approves in respect of all the good of virtue in it, the useful good or [the good] of nature, yet it is necessary that there be in it the evil of fault, which God reproves; and so God gives to tyrants and blasphemers the goods which they occupy, without their true [lordship]{g:dominium}.[^20] But the other order, the divine, must be wholly according to the divine [ordinance]{g:ordinacio}, since it does not of itself savour of sin as the former does. And the former takes its grades from human institution, according as the riches or honours of the world vary by degrees.[^21] But the other order, spurning these things, looks purely, and hidden from the world, to the divine ordinance, for profiting the church more or less; and when the human ordinance agrees on a person whom God has chosen for such a grade, [246] it savours less of sin; and otherwise Antichrist is worshipped. Hence nothing is more heretical than [the claim] that this caesarean order is so necessary that no one could be saved without a [pope]{g:papa} of this order: first, because, with many [pseudo-popes]{g:pseudopapa} not being [popes], as is clear from what has been said, the holy church of the [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} endured;[^22] secondly, because for three hundred years and more, during which the church grew without [endowment]{g:dotacio}, it was ruled without such caesarean [primacy]{g:primatus}, by the common counsel of priests. For that time priest and bishop were the same, as is clear from what has been said. Thirdly, because the whole of such an order was instituted by Caesar, apart from Christ, as is clear from what was said in chapter 8. And nothing is more unbelieving than [the claim] that no one can now be saved without his decree, so extraordinary, beyond the faith.[^23] As, therefore, converts in India and Greece and other places, where our papacy is not honoured, are Christians to be saved, even those who live more religiously than we: so in Europe there may be perfect Christians according to the faith of Christ, even if they do not acknowledge the [preeminence]{g:preeminencia} of Constantine's pontiff.[^24]

### 10-h ¶1 · 246.22–247.3  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 246] Feci **autem** ad hoc raciones multiplices ex sufficiencia legis Christi et ex vertibilitate Romani pontificis; ymo patet ex dictis quomodo corpus Christi militaret securius atque perfeccius subducto tali ordine cesareo; nam vivendo omnino exproprietarie, sicut ante dotacionem tempore quo crevit ecclesia, quando pure regulabatur per legem Christi et regebatur per sacerdotes socios sine preeminencia humanitus instituta, melius et perfeccius vixit quam modo. Nec est racio quin per idem hodie viaret sic perfeccius quam nunc viat, igitur cum hoc sit possibile, patet conclusio. Tolle igitur dotacionem et tradiciones humanas, quibus preter scripturam [p. 247] sacram occupatur ecclesia et oriretur ecclesie prior status, qui foret indubie similior et disposicior ad ecclesiam triumphantem.

ENGLISH: And I have made many arguments to this end, from the sufficiency of [the law of Christ]{g:lex-christi} and from the changeableness of the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex}; indeed, it is clear from what has been said how the body of Christ would fight more securely and more perfectly if such a [caesarean order]{g:ordo-cesareus} were withdrawn.[^1] For by living altogether [without property]{g:exproprietarie}, as before the [endowment]{g:dotacio}, in the time when the [church]{g:ecclesia} grew, when it was ruled purely by the law of Christ and governed by [priests]{g:sacerdos} who were fellows, without a [preeminence]{g:preeminencia} instituted by men, it lived better and more perfectly than now. Nor is there any reason why, by the same token, it would not today [be a wayfarer]{g:viator} in this way more perfectly than it now is; therefore, since this is possible, the [conclusion]{g:conclusio} is clear. Take away, then, the endowment and the human traditions with which, beyond holy [247] scripture, the church is occupied, and the church's former [state]{g:status} would arise, which would without doubt be more like the [church triumphant]{g:ecclesia-triumphans} and more disposed toward it.

### 10-h ¶3 · 247.8–248.5  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 247] **Sed** constat quod lige tales forent infidelissime, nisi subintelligantur condicionaliter, de quanto sedes illa et persone compensant in facto imitando Christum similius et tenendo religionem suam perfeccius, ut verbaliter profitentur. Aliter quidem contingeret quod persone et ordines obligate forent tenere cum Antichristo et eius discipulis contra Christum, nam (ut patet ex dictis) est magna disposicio, ut ista comitiva sit realiter Antichristus. Unde ad tantum dicitur hodie perplexa ecclesia, quod fratres necessitantur Parisius iurare Roberto obedienciam tamquam capiti, quod sciunt vel credunt firmiter verum papam; in Anglia **autem** necessitantur iurare Urbano nostro forma consimili, licet illi sint directe contrarii, ubi oportet quod altera pars infideliter incurrat periurium sive perfidiam. Et cum ex famosis principiis legistarum oportet facere sub pena peccati mortalis quicquid papa mandaverit, oportet credere ut fidem, quicquid in excomunicacione et infidelitate papa decreverit. Et sic de iudicio pape ad propositum. Manifeste sequitur cum paribus quod oportet utramque partem imponere infidelitatem secte Antichristi alteri et relinquere comunicacionem cum reliqua tamquam excomunicata, heretica et omni possessione ac iure indigna; et sic de infinitis inconvenientibus, [p. 248] que ad nimis magnam equivocacionem fidei sequerentur. ENGLISH: But it is evident that such leagues would be most faithless unless they are [understood as implied]{g:subintelligere} conditionally: to the extent that that see and its persons make return in deed by imitating Christ more closely and keeping their religion more perfectly, as they profess in words. Otherwise, indeed, it would come about that the persons and orders so bound would be obliged to hold with Antichrist and his disciples against Christ; for (as is clear from what has been said) there is a great disposition for this company to be in reality Antichrist. Hence the church is said today to be [perplexed]{g:perplexus} to such a degree that the friars are compelled at Paris to swear obedience to Robert as [head]{g:caput}, whom they know or firmly believe [to be] the true pope; and in England they are compelled to swear to our Urban in a like form, although those two are directly contrary, where one party or the other must faithlessly incur perjury or perfidy.[^3] And since, by the well-known principles of the [legists]{g:legiste}, one must do on pain of mortal sin whatever the pope has commanded, one must believe as faith whatever the pope has decreed in [matters of] excommunication and [unbelief]{g:infidelitas}. And so with the pope's judgement on the matter in hand.[^4] It follows plainly, with like [premisses], that each party must impute the unbelief of the sect of Antichrist to the other, and abandon communion with the other as excommunicate, heretical, and unworthy of every possession and [right]{g:ius}; and so on with infinite [absurdities]{g:inconveniencia}[^5] [248] that would follow upon too great an equivocation of faith.[^6] Therefore both the [prelates]{g:prelatus} who laid it on men to swear thus and those who so solemnized this obedience should discuss and declare their [teaching]{g:sentencia}[^7] for the instruction of the church.

### 10-h ¶4 · 248.6–249.12  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 248] Sentencia **autem** catholica quam sepe repecii stat in isto quod nec est credendum nec obediendum pape, episcopo, abbati vel cuicumque spirituali preposito, nisi de quanto dixerit vel mandaverit legem Christi, et per consequens nec Urbano nec Roberto tenetur fidelis credere, nisi de quanto fidem scripture dixerit; nec est de substancia fidei explicite credere alterum eorum esse papam. Benedictus Deus veritatis, qui ordinavit istam dissensionem, ut veritas huius fidei elucescat. Nam ego fidelis philosophus extra utramque istarum parcium sic opinancium, unam istarum parcium hec negantem de reliqua sic arguo; nec credo quod evadet iuxta sua principia: Dicit enim quod subditus tenetur sub pena mortalis obedire papa suo in quibuscumque neutris que ipse mandaverit. Cum igitur iuxta sua principia omnis verus papa habens iurisdicionem super totam ecclesiam precipit vel debet precipere cuilibet membro eius, ut declinet reliquum ut excomunicandum Antichristum, patet quod utraque secta tenetur credere consequenter de reliqua, quod sit a fide Christi scismatica. Dicit enim utraque secta quod omnis subiectus pape sub pena peccati mortalis debet facere omne possibile sibi quod papa suus virtute sacre obediencie sibi mandaverit, et per consequens ipso mandante ut veniat sibi, ut det sibi medietatem [p. 249] sue substancie, et sic de neutris similibus tenetur sub pena mortalis illud perficere; et sic uno precipiente suis quod non veniant ad concilium generale, et alio precipiente suis quod veniant, diu foret antequam esset utrumque concilium congregatum. Unde medium empericum foret ad temptandum religiosos qui sic magnificant suam obedienciam, si loquuntur ex animo, quod papa suus precipiat ista neutra. Et revera ego verecundarer in Oxonia caput erigere, si istam sentenciam de obediencia in scolis publice seminassem.

ENGLISH: And the catholic teaching, which I have often repeated, consists in this: that one is neither to believe nor to obey pope, [bishop]{g:episcopus}, abbot or any spiritual [superior]{g:prepositi}, except to the extent that he has spoken or commanded the law of Christ; and consequently the faithful man is not bound to believe either Urban or Robert except to the extent that he has spoken the faith of scripture; nor is it of the substance of faith to believe explicitly that either of them is pope. Blessed be the God of truth, who ordained this dissension, that the truth of this faith might shine forth. For I, a faithful philosopher outside both of these parties that so [opine]{g:opinio}, argue thus against one of these parties, which denies these things of the other; and I do not think it will escape, by its own principles.[^8] For it says that a subject is bound, on pain of mortal [sin], to obey his pope in any neutral things whatever that he has commanded.[^9] Since, therefore, by its principles every true pope having [jurisdiction]{g:iurisdiccio} over the whole church commands, or ought to command, every [member]{g:membrum} of it to shun the other as an Antichrist to be excommunicated,[^10] it is clear that each sect is bound to believe, consequently, of the other that it is schismatic from the faith of Christ. For each sect says that every subject of the pope, on pain of mortal sin, must do everything possible to him that his pope has commanded him in virtue of holy obedience; and consequently, if he commands him to come to him, or to give him half [249] of his substance, and so with like neutral things, he is bound on pain of mortal [sin] to carry it out; and so, with one commanding his own not to come to the general council and the other commanding his own to come, it would be long before either council was assembled. Hence it would be an empirical means of testing [the religious]{g:religiosi} who so magnify their obedience, whether they speak from the heart, that their pope should command these neutral things. And in truth I would be ashamed to raise my head in Oxford if I had sown this teaching on obedience publicly in the schools.

### 10-h ¶5 · 249.13–249.35  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 249] Est igitur tractatus istius articuli cuicumque fideli necessarius et specialiter isti opinioni, cum dicit se esse membrum dyaboli et nisi resipiscat dampnandum, nisi adhereat vero pape; et revera pro querendo minori bono quam est regnum celorum, et profugiendo minori malo quam est dampnacio gehenne, daret operam ad scrutinium veritatis, ymo antequam scandalizaret tantum numerum de exercitu christiano, tum oportet eos dicere, quod maior pars vel quasi medietas christianismi relicta sit per unum Antichristum perdita. Noticiam igitur veri pape discuterent et post noticiam verum papam eligerent ac pro salvacione sui et populi veritatem catholicam publice predicarent; ideo vel sunt segnes infidelibus nequiores vel credunt sentenciam suam de obediencia esse ficticiam. Ego **autem** credo ex necessitate divina sentenciam quam dixi superius esse fidelibus instancius detegendam, ut si Deus voluerit, fideles moveantur dotacionem destruere, que est radix istius magne versucie vel saltem primatum cesareum minus attendere.

ENGLISH: The treatment of this article, then, is necessary to every faithful man, and especially to this opinion, since it says that [a man] is a member of the devil, and to be damned unless he comes to his senses, unless he adheres to the true pope; and in truth, for seeking a lesser good than the [kingdom]{g:regnum} of heaven, and for fleeing a lesser evil than the [damnation]{g:dampnacio} of gehenna, it would give its effort to the scrutiny of the truth, indeed before it scandalized so great a number of the Christian army; for then they must say that the greater part, or almost half, of Christendom has been left lost through one Antichrist.[^11] They should therefore discuss the knowledge of the true pope, and after the knowledge elect the true pope, and for the [salvation]{g:salvacio} of themselves and of the people preach the catholic truth publicly; therefore either they are sluggish, more wicked than unbelievers, or they believe their teaching on obedience to be a [fiction]{g:ficticia}. But I believe, from divine necessity, that the teaching I stated above must be [exposed]{g:detegere} to the faithful more urgently, so that, if God wills, the faithful may be moved to destroy the endowment, which is the root of this great craftiness, or at least to pay less heed to the caesarean [primacy]{g:primatus}.

### 10-h ¶6 · 249.36–250.6  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 249] Oportet igitur studere, ex quo signo debemus cognoscere verum papam, cum eleccio, opinio [p. 250] aut reprobacio humana sit huic impertinens. ### 10-i ¶1 · 250.22–253.27  (11 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 250] Multe sunt et leges et sanctorum sentencie de preeminencia Romane ecclesie. **Sed** ad intelligendum ista decreta sanctorum doctorum theologie oportet postponere glossas Johannis de Deo, Johannis Andree, Johannis Monachi et eis similium, qui logice, methaphisice, theologice sanctorum sentencie sunt ignari. Oportet (inquam) notare iuxta dicta quod beatus Jeronymus loquitur de genere predestinatorum fidelium, qui principium sumpserant a Romanis. Paulus enim fuit Romanus ex genere, ut patet Act. XVI et [p. 251] XXII, et Petrus ex occisione. Illam **autem** predestinatorum generacionem, cuius racio est insensibilis, oportet necessario semper stare fidelem ubicumque migraverit; nec oportet nunc aliquam eius partem Romam vel Avinonam incolere vel esse presbiterum, nisi forte necessitate ex supposicione, quod est nobis ambiguum; et de tali Romana ecclesia intelligit decretum Jeronymi, quia certum est ex VIII. capitulo quod pape plurimi Romani et Avinonici erant manifesti heretici et signantissimi Antichristi. Istam **autem** generacionem simpliciter et concrete intelligendo non est possibile peccare dampnabiliter, quia cum sit predestinata, necessario salvabitur, et quomodocumque peccaverit, habebit veniam. **Sed** de tali privilegio non est pompa, cum ignoramus, qui sunt de illa ecclesia. Beatus **autem** Jeronymus reputative locutus est et credibiliter ex fide quam sumpsit ex apostolicis operibus huius pape, sicut sancti reputative loquuntur, quando vident prelatos declinantes a Christi vestigiis, dicentes quod sunt dampnandi vel membra dyaboli. Utrum **autem** beatus Jeronymus habuit revelacionem de predestinacione huius pape et iusticia suorum operum, ignoratur. Subtilitas **autem** huius sensus est valde remota a noticia legistarum. Et patet quomodo intelligenda est condicionalis Jeronymi quod si illa sua confessio confirmatur illius pape iudicio, quicumque impugnare voluerit foret hereticus; reputative enim vel ex revelacione asseruit, quod ille papa non confirmaret aliud nisi verum. **Sed** dementis foret credere quod hoc debet trahi in consequenciam de quocumque Romano pontifice, quia certum est, quod multi eorum, quantum in eis fuit, ratificarunt multas hereses propter lucrum, [p. 252] ut Gregorius, qui excessit decalogum, vel saltem pseudocardinales, qui sunt hodie cum Roberto, dicuntur dampnasse tamquam valde hereticum quod domini temporales possunt auferre temporalia ab ecclesia delinquente; et tamen Urbanus noster succedens mercede conduxit temporales dominos ad practizandum illam catholicam veritatem; sicut prior ecclesia commendavit et privilegiavit imperatorem ex hoc, quod pseudopapas deposuit et tam temporalia quam insignia pape ab illis abstulit nec non papas quos ipse instituit, approbavit, ut patet XXIII distinccione: In nomine Domini, ubi textus narrat, quomodo papa conqueritur quod crebris tunsionibus symoniace heresis sedes illa sepius est percussa, ideo volendo providere de remedio in futurum decrevit, quod mortuo papa cardinales, religiosi, clerici et layci conveniant ad eligendum unum papam idoneum de illius ecclesie gremio vel alterius, ubi magis fuerit idoneus, salvo semper privilegio imperatoris Henrici, quod in eleccione pape habeat cum suis heredibus interesse. Electus **autem** tamquam verus papa habebit ante consecracionem suam instar beati Gregorii potestatem disponendi papaliter de bonis ecclesie, et quemcumque qui ordinem eligendi impedierit anathematizat tamquam pessimum antichristum. Glossa **autem** ordinaria dicit, quod hic tangitur latenter quod legitur in cronicis, quomodo Benedictus qui successit Stephano eiectus est de pontificatu et Johannes Sabinensis episcopus factus est papa pro pecunia; cui impositum est nomen [p. 253] Silvester. **Sed** iste deiectus est et restitutus Benedictus, qui iterum est eiectus; et datus est papatus Johanni archipresbitero ante portam Latinam, cui impositum est nomen Gregorius; et hic ab Henrico imperatore est deiectus et ultra montes translatus; et hoc totum uno anno factum est. Et propter hoc datum fuit hoc privilegium Henrico. Hec glossa decreti. Considerarent itaque clerici nostri imponentes heresim detegentibus has leges ecclesie, quomodo ipsi ex scandalo sunt econtra heretici. Sicut secta Roberti imponit nobis quod sumus heretici, nos econtra. Si enim iuxta illud decretum Jeronymi ille est hereticus, qui impugnat acrius, quod verus papa fecerit in se ipso, et sic secte iste dicunt altrinsecus se esse hereticos, ita quod ab origine mundi, ut credo, non fuit crebrior imposicio heretice pravitatis. Sicut enim sacerdotes imposuerunt Christo blasfemiam, que est peior pars heresis, sic ignari Antichristi discipuli imponunt heresim tamquam inimicis ecclesie eis qui plus zelant pro eius privilegiis que Christus concesserat conservandis, in tantum quod quidam doctor ignarus quia adeptus est dotacionem pontificis declamat acucius in locis plus publicis, quod talis est hereticus et hostis ecclesie. Nullus enim peior est apostata, quam qui post altiorem statum ecclesie ad mundum excidit.

ENGLISH: There are many laws and [teachings]{g:sentencia} of the saints on the [preeminence]{g:preeminencia} of the Roman [church]{g:ecclesia}.[^1] But to understand these decrees of the holy doctors of theology one must set aside the glosses of Johannes de Deo, Johannes Andreae, Johannes Monachus and those like them, who are ignorant, in logic, metaphysics and theology, of the teachings of the saints.[^2] One must (I say) note, in keeping with what has been said, that blessed Jerome speaks of the race of the [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} faithful, who had taken their beginning from the Romans. For Paul was a Roman by race, as is clear from Acts 16 and [251] 22, and Peter by his slaying.[^3] Now that generation of the predestinate, whose character is [imperceptible]{g:insensibilis},[^4] must necessarily always stand faithful wherever it has migrated; nor need any part of it now dwell in Rome or Avignon, or be a [presbyter]{g:presbiter}, except perhaps by [necessity on supposition]{g:ex-supposicione}, which is doubtful to us; and it is of such a Roman church that the decree of Jerome understands [it], for it is certain from chapter 8 that very many Roman and Avignonese popes were manifest heretics and most notable Antichrists. And understanding this generation [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} and concretely, it is not possible for it to sin damnably, since, being predestinate, it will necessarily be saved, and however it has sinned, it will have [forgiveness]{g:venia}.[^5] But there is no boasting over such a privilege, since we do not know who are of that church. And blessed Jerome spoke by estimation, and credibly, from the faith that he took from the apostolic works of this pope, as the saints speak by estimation when they see [prelates]{g:prelatus} turning aside from the footsteps of Christ, saying that they are to be damned, or members of the devil.[^6] But whether blessed Jerome had a revelation of the predestination of this [pope]{g:papa} and of the justice of his works is unknown. And the subtlety of this sense is very remote from the knowledge of the [legists]{g:legiste}. And it is clear how Jerome's conditional is to be understood, that if that confession of his is confirmed by that pope's judgement, whoever wishes to attack it would be a heretic: for he asserted, by estimation or from revelation, that that pope would confirm nothing but the true. But it would be a madman's part to believe that this must be drawn into an [inference]{g:consequencia} for any [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex} whatever,[^7] for it is certain that many of them, as far as in them lay, ratified many heresies for gain, [252] as Gregory, who exceeded the decalogue, or at least the [pseudo-cardinals]{g:pseudocardinales} who are today with Robert, are said to have condemned as very heretical [the proposition] that [temporal lords]{g:temporales-domini} can take away [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} from a delinquent church;[^8] and yet our Urban, succeeding, hired temporal lords for pay to [put into practice]{g:practizare} that catholic truth; just as the former church commended and [privileged]{g:privilegium} the emperor for having deposed [pseudo-popes]{g:pseudopapa} and taken from them both the temporal goods and the insignia of the pope, and approved the popes whom he himself instituted, as is clear in distinction 23, *In nomine Domini*, where the text tells how the pope complains that "by frequent blows of simoniacal heresy that see has been struck rather often", and therefore, wishing to provide a remedy for the future, he decreed that "on the death of the pope, cardinals, [religious]{g:religiosi}, clerics and laymen should come together to elect one suitable pope from the bosom of that church or of another, where a more suitable one may be found, saving always the privilege of the emperor Henry, that he with his heirs should have a part in the election of the pope. And the one elected, as true pope, shall have before his consecration, after the example of blessed Gregory, [power]{g:potestas} to dispose papally of the goods of the church; and whoever has impeded the order of electing he anathematizes as the worst antichrist."[^9] And the ordinary gloss says that "here is touched covertly what is read in the chronicles: how Benedict, who succeeded Stephen, was ejected from the pontificate, and John, bishop of Sabina, was made pope for money, to whom was given the name [253] Silvester. But he was cast out and Benedict restored, who was ejected again; and the papacy was given to John, archpriest at the Latin Gate, to whom was given the name Gregory; and he was cast out by the emperor Henry and taken across the mountains; and all this was done in one year. And for this reason this privilege was given to Henry."[^10] This is the gloss on the decree. Let our clerics therefore consider, who impute heresy to those who uncover these laws of the church, how they themselves, by the scandal, are on the contrary heretics.[^11] Just as the sect of Robert imputes to us that we are heretics, we [impute it to them] in return. For if, according to that decree of Jerome, he is a heretic who attacks too sharply what the true pope has done in himself, then these sects too say of one another that they are heretics, so that from the origin of the world, as I believe, there has not been a more frequent imputation of [heretical depravity]{g:heretica-pravitas}.[^12] For just as the priests imputed blasphemy to Christ, which is the worse part of heresy, so the ignorant disciples of Antichrist impute heresy, as to enemies of the church, to those who are more zealous for preserving its privileges, which Christ had granted; so much so that a certain ignorant doctor, because he has obtained the pontiff's [endowment]{g:dotacio}, declaims more sharply in the more public places that such a man is a heretic and an enemy of the church.[^13] For no apostate is worse than one who, after a higher [state]{g:status} of the church, has fallen away to the world.

### 10-j ¶1 · 253.28–255.20  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 253] Consolacio **autem** a Christo est in operando in causa ista fideliter et constanter, cum Matth. X, 25 dicat: Si patremfamilias Belzebub vocaverunt, quanto magis domesticos eius? Ideo ibant apostoli gaudentes quod digni habiti sunt pro nomine Jhesu contumeliam pati. **Sed** et consolacio quedam mundana exoritur, quod ad tantum insane prorumpunt satrape in suis sermonibus, quod imponunt [p. 254] principalem heresim regi suo. Rex, inquiunt, care conduxit stipendiarios contra sanctam ecclesiam; ex quo patenter implicant quod rex eorum sit heresiarcha non obstante ignorancia puerili. Apostolus enim non fuit excusatus a blasfemia, non obstante quod per ignoranciam stulte fecit, ut prima Timothei primo. Videtur itaque nimis magna presumpcio sic imponere heresim toti consilio tanti regni; predicarent enim iuxta suam opinionem atque scienciam de periculosiori heresi adversancium pape suo, et si umquam valet sciencia decretiste, valet ad cerciorandum ecclesiam in hac parte. Dicit enim quod ad illos singulariter pertinet cognoscere disposicionem veri pape et rectitudinem sue consuetudinis cum aliis annexis difficultatibus, sicut ad eos pertinet cognoscere quiditatem heresis et penam sui subditi. Doceant igitur in istis populum de fraude, quia (ut inquiunt) sine fide istius articuli non est salus; ostendant igitur artem suam in isto fideliter, quia maior et famosior est multitudo decretistarum eis contraria in hac parte et specialiter cuneus pseudocardinalium, quibus videbatur regi ecclesie tempore Gregorii excedentis decalogum. Ipsi enim in tradicionibus humanis periti, qui melius noverunt qualitatem eleccionis quam ipsimet celebrarunt, testantur nobis cartis et nunciis, quod eleccio eorum non fuit legitima, et sic (ut inquiunt) noster Urbanus, quem colimus infideliter, non est papa. Nostri **autem** theologi dicunt primo, quod licet eleccio eorum, ut vere creditur, fuerit maledicta, eleccio tamen passiva Urbani nostri ex eleccione activa Dei est valida, dum tamen fiat ab ipso electo in veris factis papalibus [p. 255] recompensa. Dicunt secundo quod nedum catholice **sed** melius quam modo conversaretur fidelis ecclesia quocumque papa cesareo subducto, quia Act. IV, 12 dicitur, quod non est aliud nomen sub celo, in quo oportet nos salvos fieri preter nomen domini Jhesu Christi. Et dicunt tercio quod pensando virtutum insignia probabiliter reputant Urbanum nostrum verum papam, cum omnes sancti episcopi erant pape, et Robertum non reputant explicite papam vel membrum sancte ecclesie, quia in De Verbis Domini sermone LIII sic scribitur: Caput humile et membrum superbum absit; non vult esse de corpore capitis Christi, quia amat superbiam. Unde cum secundum eundem Augustinum sermone L Christus vere loquens de quibuscumque pastoribus, cum in se habet omnes pastores bonos vere dicit: Ego sum unus, mecum omnes in unitate unum sunt. Prescitus **autem** cum sit membrum dyaboli, non compaginatur ordinate huic capiti.

ENGLISH: And consolation from Christ lies in working in this cause faithfully and constantly, since Matt. 10:25 says: "If they have called the good man of the house Beelzebub, how much more them of his household?" Therefore the apostles went "rejoicing that they were accounted worthy to suffer reproach for the name of Jesus."[^1] But a certain worldly consolation also arises, in that the [satraps]{g:satrapa} break out so madly in their sermons that they impute [254] principal heresy to their king. "The king," they say, "has dearly hired mercenaries against holy [church]{g:ecclesia}"; from which they plainly imply that their king is a heresiarch, notwithstanding his boyish ignorance.[^2] For the apostle was not excused from blasphemy, notwithstanding that he acted foolishly through ignorance, as in 1 Timothy 1.[^3] It seems, then, too great a presumption so to impute heresy to the whole council of so great a [kingdom]{g:regnum}; for they should preach, according to their [opinion]{g:opinio} and knowledge, about the more dangerous heresy of those opposing their pope; and if the knowledge of a decretist is ever worth anything, it is worth it for certifying the church in this matter. For it says that it pertains to them uniquely to know the disposition of the true [pope]{g:papa} and the rightness of his [custom]{g:consuetudo}, with the other difficulties annexed, just as it pertains to them to know the [quiddity]{g:quiditas} of heresy and the penalty of their subject. Let them therefore teach the people in these things about the fraud, since (as they say) without faith in this article there is no salvation; let them therefore show their art faithfully in this, since the multitude of [decretists]{g:decretiste} contrary to them in this matter is greater and [better known]{g:famosior}, and especially the wedge of [pseudo-cardinals]{g:pseudocardinales}, by whom the church seemed to be ruled in the time of Gregory, who exceeded the decalogue.[^4] For they, skilled in human traditions, who knew best the quality of the election that they themselves conducted, testify to us by charters and messengers that their election was not lawful, and so (as they say) our Urban, whom we faithlessly honour, is not pope.[^5] But our theologians say, first, that although their election, as is truly believed, was accursed, yet the passive election of our Urban, from the active election of God, is valid, provided that, in true papal deeds, it is [255] repaid by the one elected himself.[^6] They say, second, that the faithful church would live not only in a catholic manner but better than now with any caesarean pope whatever withdrawn, since Acts 4:12 says that "there is no other name under heaven" in which we must be saved, except the name of the Lord Jesus Christ.[^7] And they say, third, that, weighing the marks of the virtues, they reckon with probability that our Urban is the true pope, since all holy bishops were popes, and they do not reckon Robert explicitly pope or a [member]{g:membrum} of holy church, since in *On the Words of the Lord*, sermon 53, it is written thus: "Far be it [that there be] a humble [head]{g:caput} and a proud member; he does not wish to be of the body of the head, Christ, because he loves pride."[^8] Hence since, according to the same Augustine in sermon 50, Christ, speaking truly of all pastors whatever, since he has in himself all good pastors, truly says: "I am one; with me all are one in unity."[^9] And [the foreknown]{g:prescitus} man, since he is a member of the devil, is not fitted in due order to this head.[^10]

### 10-j ¶2 · 255.21–256.2  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 255] Istam **autem** negativam dico opinando, sicut secta altera possit catholice dicere conformiter de Urbano. Ideo oportet, quod opera iusta dent fidem papatus, cum nichil sit fallacius quam humana eleccio et tradicio phariseica cum suo iudicio. Nam easdem tradiciones habet secta Roberti et nostra, et tamen in iudicio quo maxime servirent ecclesie sunt directe opposite, et indubie neutra pars noscit evidenciam vie sue; theologus **autem** neutram sectam dampnat, **sed** instar libertatis christianorum primeve ecclesie dicit quod utraque pars potest viare satis catholice, et sive uter eorum credat explicite, et quicumque [p. 256] theologus dixerit huius contrarium, a facultate sua degenerat.

ENGLISH: And this negative I state as an opinion, just as the other sect could say the like of Urban in a catholic manner. Therefore it must be just works that give credence to a papacy, since nothing is more deceptive than human election and pharisaic tradition with its judgement.[^11] For the sect of Robert and ours have the same traditions, and yet in the judgement in which they would most serve the church they are directly opposed, and without doubt neither party knows the [evidence]{g:evidencia} for its way; but the theologian condemns neither sect, but, after the pattern of the freedom of the Christians of the primitive church, says that each party can [be a wayfarer]{g:viator} catholicly enough, whether or not either of them believes explicitly; and whatever [256] theologian says the contrary of this degenerates from his faculty.[^12]
