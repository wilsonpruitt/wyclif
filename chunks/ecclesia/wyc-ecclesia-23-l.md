# *De ecclesia*, chapter 23, part 12: the last conclusions; sin and punishment in purgatory; merit after death; the end of the treatise

## ¶1 · 584.23–586.17

> [p. 584] Dico septimo quod prelati ecclesie debent in ista veritate catholica subiectos instruere, ne laici infideliter occupati circa minus utilia attendant superbiam et avariciam sacerdotum? Patet in simili de lege questorum, quibus limitatum est quod non dicant populo nisi quod in literis episcoporum eis fuerit limitatum, ut patet libro septimo de Penitenciis et Remissionibus, capitulo Abusionibus. Multo magis ergo Christi vicarii debent docere populum secundum limites literarum spiritus sancti quas eis tradidit ad docendum. [p. 585] Dico octavo quod sacerdotes Christi, licet habeant potestatem ad absolvendum subditos a pena et culpa, non tamen debent absolvere sub hac forma nec subiecti illud expetere, nisi hoc eis specialiter fuerit revelatum. Prima pars patet ex hoc quod sacerdos potest sacramentaliter ostendere sibi confitentem taliter absolutum, qui ad tantum conteritur quod statim decedens sine pena purgatorii advolaret, et hoc est sacerdotem absolvere. Nec est potestas alicuius sacerdotis in casu ultime necessitatis sic ligata quin, quantum Deus revelans permiserit, possit absolvere; foret autem nimia presumpcio aliquem Christi vicarium absolucionem talem pretendere, nisi Deus hoc sibi revelaverit faciendum, ne forte incurrat blasphemum mendacium. Quid ergo valet subiectos inoportune absolucionem talem expetere, cum certe debent credere quod correspondenter ad sua merita vel demerita taxabuntur? Licet autem sufficiat apud Christum ubique presentem contricio, tamen sacramentum penitencie est valde necessarium, licet non proderit sine illa. Ideo foret stulticia sacerdotem cui non fit ad hoc revelacio diffinire quod penitencia vel aliud sacramentum suo suscipienti proderit ad salutem. Sic enim vetaret ut pro absoluto suo a pena et culpa in mortis articulo fieret suffragium ecclesie, sicut non facimus pro quocunque mortuo quem constat nobis iam esse beatum. Istam sentenciam de suffragiis et indulgenciis dixerim ad instruccionem inferiorum ecclesie, et exposicionem correccioni, supplecioni et approbacioni superiorum secundum regulas scripturarum et leges humanas ac decreta potentatuum precise ad tantum apprecior. Sed ad materiam de peccati purgacione quam tetigi in proximo argumento videtur mihi probabile quod quilibet purgandus tam diu habet peccatum [p. 586] in purgatorio, quam diu inibi subit penam. Nam sequitur: Hec est pena peccati, ergo est aliquid peccati, cum omnis pena sit res iusta per quam peccatum bonificatur, et per consequens illud peccatum est. Similiter, si quilibet in purgatorio purgatus est omnino sine peccato, tunc est ita mundus sicut beatus in celo, cum solum peccatum causat talem inmundiciam, et per consequens foret tam amabilis in purgatorio ab ecclesia triumphante, sicut ipso beato, quia tam pulcher. Similiter, quilibet existens in purgatorio propter peccatum suum preteritum deficit a plena dileccione Dei et ecclesie, ymmo a pleno profectu sibi et aliis quem debuit tunc habere. Ille ergo defectus purgandus est peccatum, ideo videtur penam purgare illum defectum et satisfacere pro defectu preterito, et quando lux amoris primo inducitur, tunc primo obscuritas peccati educitur.

I say seventh that the [prelates]{g:prelatus} of the [church]{g:ecclesia} ought to instruct their subjects in this catholic truth, lest the laity, faithlessly busied about less useful things, attend upon the pride and avarice of priests.[^1] This is clear by a like case from the law about questors, for whom it is [assigned]{g:limitare} that they say nothing to the people except what has been assigned to them in the bishops' letters, as is clear in the seventh book, *Of Penances and Remissions*, chapter *Abusionibus*. Much more, therefore, ought the [vicars of Christ]{g:vicarius-christi} to teach the people according to the [limits]{g:limites} of the letters of the Holy Spirit, which he has delivered to them to teach.[^2] [585] I say eighth that the [priests]{g:sacerdos} of Christ, although they have power to absolve their subjects from punishment and guilt, yet ought not to absolve under this form, nor ought the subjects to seek it, unless this has been specially revealed to them. The first part is clear from this: that a priest can sacramentally show the one who confesses to him to be so absolved, being contrite to such a degree that, dying at once, he would fly off without the punishment of purgatory; and this is for the priest to absolve. Nor is the power of any priest, in a case of last necessity, so bound that he cannot absolve, as far as God, revealing it, permits; but it would be excessive presumption for any vicar of Christ to put forward such an absolution unless God had revealed to him that it was to be done, lest perhaps he fall into a blasphemous lie.[^3] What does it avail, then, for subjects to seek such an absolution unseasonably, since they surely ought to believe that they will be [assessed]{g:taxare} correspondingly to their merits or demerits? And although contrition suffices with Christ, who is everywhere present, yet the sacrament of penance is very necessary, though it will not profit without that [contrition].[^4] So it would be folly for a priest to whom no revelation is made for this to define that penance, or any other sacrament, will profit its receiver to salvation. For so he would forbid that the [suffrage]{g:suffragia} of the church be made for the one he has absolved from punishment and guilt at the point of death, as we do not make it for any dead man whom we know to be already blessed.[^5] This [teaching]{g:sentencia} on suffrages and indulgences I would have spoken for the instruction of the lower [members] of the church; and the exposition [I submit] to the correction, supplementing and approval of my superiors according to the rules of the scriptures; and human laws and the decrees of the powerful I value precisely so far [as they agree with these].[^6] But as to the matter of the purging of sin, which I touched on in the last argument, it seems probable to me that anyone who is to be purged has sin [586] in purgatory for as long as he undergoes punishment there.[^7] For [it follows]{g:sequitur}: this is the punishment of sin, therefore it is something of sin; since every punishment is a just thing through which sin is made good, and consequently that sin exists.[^8] Likewise, if anyone in purgatory, being purged, is wholly without sin, then he is as clean as one blessed in heaven, since sin alone causes such uncleanness; and consequently he would be as lovable to the [church triumphant]{g:ecclesia-triumphans}, while in purgatory, as the blessed one himself, since he is as beautiful.[^9] Likewise, anyone who is in purgatory falls short, on account of his past sin, of the full love of God and of the church, indeed of the full profit to himself and to others that he ought then to have. That defect, then, which is to be purged, is sin; so it seems that punishment purges that defect and [makes satisfaction]{g:satisfacere} for the past defect; and when the light of love is first brought in, then first is the darkness of sin brought out.[^10]

## ¶2 · 586.18–586.30

> [p. 586] Videtur ulterius quod pro quolibet peccato in quo decedit predestinatus Deus statuit certam penam, cum quo non poterit illo peccato supposito dispensare. Nam per idem posset dispensare cum quolibet peccato in purgatorio vel in inferno, et dimittere homini peccatum sine hoc quod voluerit converti ad dominum, sine amore faciendo ipsum beatum, cum nulla pena essencialiter consequeretur peccatum sed foret tantum per accidens arbitraria quoad Deum. Sicut ergo pena essencialis est per se iusta, sic est tanta iusticia in pena accidentali, sed meritum ecclesie facit esse aliud peccatum totale in purgatorio quam foret merito illo dimisso.

It seems, further, that for every sin in which a [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} [person] dies God has established a certain punishment, with which, that sin being supposed, he will not be able to dispense. For by the same token he could dispense with any sin in purgatory or in hell, and remit sin to a man without his having willed to be converted to the Lord, making him blessed without love; since no punishment would follow sin essentially, but it would be only by accident, arbitrary as regards God.[^11] As, therefore, essential punishment is just of itself, so there is as much justice in accidental punishment; but the merit of the church makes the total sin in purgatory other than it would be were that merit set aside.[^12]

## ¶3 · 586.31–587.15

> [p. 586] Videtur tercio quod sicut beati in celo merentur sibi continue usque ad diem iudicii premium accidentale, aliis autem utrumque, sed existentes in [p. 587] purgatorio merentur sibi premium utrumque continue, sic dampnati usque in finem continue demerentur. Prima pars videtur: Eo quod beati, sicut continue merentur aliis in via, sic merentur ut post habeant consorcium eorum in patria; purgati vero merentur sibi nedum remissionem peccaminum, sed beatitudinem tam essencialem quam accidentalem futuram, voluntarie gracia huius ex Dei gracia paciendo; licet autem peccent continue, quia tunc sunt in gracia sine mortali, non video quomodo proprie demerentur, sicut nec libere contradictorie tunc merentur. Dampnati vero cum sint peccato mortali in malivolencia sua continue peiorati, continue demerentur, licet prosint per accidens. De isto alibi. Explicit tractatus de sancta matre ecclesia.

It seems, thirdly, that as the blessed in heaven merit for themselves continually, until the day of judgement, [accidental reward]{g:premium-accidentale}, but both [rewards] for others, but those who are in [587] purgatory merit both rewards for themselves continually, so the damned continually demerit until the end.[^13] The first part is evident thus: because the blessed, as they continually merit for others on the [way]{g:via}, so they merit that afterwards they may have their fellowship in the [homeland]{g:patria}; and those being purged merit for themselves not only remission of sins but future blessedness, both [essential and accidental]{g:beatitudo-essencialis}, suffering willingly for its sake out of God's grace; and although they sin continually, since they are then in grace without mortal [sin], I do not see how they properly demerit, as neither do they then merit with the freedom of contradiction.[^14] But the damned, since in mortal sin they are continually made worse in their malevolence, continually demerit, although they profit [others] by accident. Of this elsewhere. Here ends the treatise on holy mother church.[^15]

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Attendant*: taken as "attend upon", that is, serve and feed the priests' pride and avarice by buying indulgences; "give heed to" (and so be scandalised by) is possible. **T.** The print ends the sentence with a question mark, though it is a statement. **R.** Side-note 584.23.

[^2]: **S.** *Clem.* 5.9.2 *Abusionibus*: Loserth's reference ("Clementinarum lib. V, tit. IX, cap. II"); the form is ours (LEDGER E23l-1). Wyclif calls the Clementines the "seventh book" of the decretals (LEDGER E23l-2). **C.** *Questores*: the alms-collectors who published indulgences, whom the canon restricts to what their bishops' letters contain. *Limitatum*: "assigned", in both places (row *limitare*). *Christi vicarii*: priests at large, as vicars of Christ. *Litere spiritus sancti*: scripture.

[^3]: **C.** *Sub hac forma*: in the form "from punishment and guilt" (*a pena et culpa*). *Prima pars*: that priests have the power. *Ostendere … absolutum*: declare sacramentally that the penitent is absolved. *Advolaret*: would fly off, straight to heaven. *Pretendere*: put forward, claim. **R.** Side-note 585.1.

[^4]: **C.** *Inoportune*: out of season, without a revelation. *Illa*: contrition. **T.** B *afnciat* for *sufficiat* (585.18), a slip. **R.** Side-note 585.18.

[^5]: **C.** *Diffinire*: to determine (cf. row *diffinicio*). *Sic enim vetaret*: by defining that the dying man was absolved from punishment and guilt, the priest would in effect forbid the church's suffrage for him, since the church makes none for those known to be blessed. *In mortis articulo*: at the point of death.

[^6]: **C.** The sentence is Wyclif's submission. *Et exposicionem correccioni, supplecioni et approbacioni superiorum* has no verb; "[I submit]" is our supply, for a word such as *submitto* that may have been lost. *Secundum regulas scripturarum* is taken with the correction of superiors. *Precise ad tantum apprecior*: "I value precisely so far", with the measure, agreement with the rules of scripture, left unexpressed and here supplied in brackets (our supply). *Inferiorum*: the lower members of the church.

[^7]: **C.** *In proximo argumento*: the argument that punishment and sin go inseparably together (582.10–20). **T.** B *inpurgacione* (585.33). A notes *Conclusio* in the margin (586.1). **R.** Side-note 586.1.

[^8]: **C.** *Aliquid peccati*: something belonging to sin. *Bonificatur*: is made good, that is, set in just order by its punishment. *Illud peccatum est*: the sin still exists.

[^9]: **C.** *Ab ecclesia triumphante, sicut ipso beato*: taken as "as lovable to the church triumphant as [is] the blessed one himself"; the ablative may instead mean "loved by the blessed one himself". The argument is a reductio: the soul in purgatory is not yet as clean as the blessed.

[^10]: **C.** *Ille defectus purgandus*: the shortfall of love and profit, which remains to be purged, is itself sin. *Lux amoris … obscuritas peccati*: light brought in and darkness brought out (*inducitur … educitur*), the scholastic language of form induced and privation removed.

[^11]: **T.** A notes *Conclusio* in the margin (586.18). **C.** *Cum quo*: with which punishment (*quo* for *qua*). *Illo peccato supposito*: given that sin. *Arbitraria quoad Deum*: depending on God's mere choice. **R.** B in the margin at 586.21: *Apparet quod tenet predestinatum posse decedere in peccato*, "It appears that he holds that a predestinate [person] can die in sin." Side-note 586.18.

[^12]: **C.** *Pena essencialis … accidentali*: the punishment that follows sin of itself, and the punishment that comes by accident, which the merit of the church can change. *Merito illo dimisso*: that merit being set aside. **R.** B in the margin at 586.23: *Quam vocat penam essencialem et quam accidentalem?*, "Which does he call essential punishment and which accidental?"

[^13]: **C.** *Utrumque*: both rewards, the essential and the accidental. The blessed merit only accidental reward for themselves, since their essential reward is already had; for others still on the way they merit both. *Demerentur*: they deserve ill, the opposite of *merentur*. **R.** A notes *Conclusio* in the margin (586.31). Side-notes 586.31, 587.1.

[^14]: **C.** *Gracia huius*: for its sake. *Licet autem peccent continue*: Wyclif has just argued (585.33–586.17) that the souls in purgatory keep sin while they are punished. *Sine mortali*: without mortal sin. *Libere contradictorie*: with the freedom of contradiction, the power to act or not to act, which they no longer have; so they neither properly merit nor demerit in that way. **T.** A and A₁ read *quia tum* (587.9).

[^15]: **C.** *Prosint per accidens*: the damned profit others by accident, not by their will (for instance, in showing God's justice). *De isto alibi*: he treats it elsewhere; the place is not named. **T.** B *licet profuit* (587.14). B has *Explicit* alone; A₁ adds *Continet viginti tria capitula et sic est finis*, "It contains twenty-three chapters, and so is the end" (587.15).
