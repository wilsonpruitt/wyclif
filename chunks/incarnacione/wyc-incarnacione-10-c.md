# *De benedicta incarnacione*, chapter 10, part 3: what Christ became by the incarnation, and the decretal *Cum Christus*

## ¶1 · 167.23–169.5

> [p. 167] Contra primum verbale sophisticum non delector pro¹² nunc arguere, quia intencio loquencium de rebus univocis est cognoscere, si res similes in racione vel quiditate communicant, vel si de creata essencia Christi est quod ipse sit homo, sicut est de essencia cuiuscunque alterius singularis hominis quod sit homo. Aliter enim secundum verbales logicos non predicaretur iste terminus homo in quid de Verbo et secundum rectam methaphisicam; nisi Christus¹³ secundum humanitatem, vel secundum quod homo sit aliquid sit¹⁴ sic quod humanitas [p. 168] sit causa, quare ipse est idem.¹ Aliter non foret univoce homo cum aliis humanitate specifica. Sicut enim² quantitate est subiectum quantum, qualitate quale, et ita de ceteris; sic substancia vel quiditate est quid vel aliquid formaliter: et ita, sicut adveniente qualitate fit subiectum eo³ ipso quale quod prius³ non fuerat; sic adveniente⁴ substancia vel quiditate fit subiectum eo ipso aliquid quod prius non fuerat. Cum ergo humanitas st per consequens quiditas advenit Christo formaliter, sequitur quod secundum illam fit aliquid quod prius non fuerat, et per consequens est dare,⁵ quod aliquid est⁵ Christus per incarnacionem, in quo convenit cum homine plus quam ante: et cum quid dicit naturam ut quis dicit personam, sequitur quod post incarnacionem alia substancialis essencia est Christus, que⁶ prius non fuerat: quia aliter indubie in nullo esset univoce vel conveniens creature plus quam ante. Sicut ergo pertinenter queritur et solvitur—⁷ Qualis est Petrus post alteracionem,⁷ qualis prius non fuerat ?—sic pertinenter queritur et solvitur—Quid est Christus⁸ post incarnacionem, quod ante non fuerat?—⁹ et illud est substancia,⁹ natura, et essencia creata Dominus Iesus Christus, que¹⁰ est Verbum esse hominem; quia aliter indubie homo non predicaretur in quid de Verbo: nec Christus, in quantum homo esset aliquid, quod nedum contradicit principiis philosophicis, sed eciam Decretali*¹¹ Cum Christus¹² libro 7° De Hereticis. Quomodo queso dicerent¹³ doctores tam veteres quam novi quod aliquid cepit esse Christus, nisi aliquid per incarnacionem fuerit factum Christus? Aliter non oportuit Decretalem libro 7° De Hereticis sic scripsisse: ‘Cum Christus perfectus Deus [p. 169] et perfectus homo, mandamus quatenus¹ sub anathemate interdicas—Ne quis de ceteris² audeat dicere, Christum non esse aliquid secundum quod homo—quia sicut Christus verus est Deus, ita verus est homo ex anima racionali et humana carne³ subsistens.’³

Against the first, verbal and sophistical, [reply] I do not care to argue for now, since the intention of those who speak of univocal things is to know whether like things communicate in account or [quiddity]{g:quiditas}, or whether it is of the created [essence]{g:essencia} of Christ that he is a man, as it is of the essence of any other singular man that he is a man.[^1] For otherwise, according to the verbal logicians, this term "man" would not be predicated [in quid]{g:in-quid} of the [Word]{g:verbum}, and [neither would it] according to right metaphysics, unless Christ, [as to the humanity]{g:secundum-humanitatem}, or [insofar as]{g:secundum-quod} [he is] man, is something, in such a way that the [humanity]{g:humanitas} [168] is the cause why he is the same [thing].[^2] Otherwise he would not be [univocally]{g:univoce} a man with others by the humanity of the species. For as by quantity the [subject]{g:subiectum} is [quantified]{g:quantus}, by quality qualified, and so of the rest; so by substance or quiddity it is formally what or something: and so, as, with a quality supervening, the subject by that very fact becomes qualified, which it had not been before; so, with substance or quiddity supervening, the subject by that very fact becomes something which it had not been before. Since, then, the humanity, and consequently a quiddity, comes to Christ formally, it follows that as to it he becomes something which he had not been before; and consequently [one may posit]{g:est-dare} that something is Christ through the incarnation in which he agrees with man more than before: and since "what" [signifies]{g:dicere} a [nature]{g:natura} as "who" signifies a [person]{g:persona}, it follows that after the incarnation another substantial essence is Christ which had not been before: since otherwise, undoubtedly, he would in nothing be univocal with or agreeing with a [creature]{g:creatura} more than before.[^3] As, then, it is pertinently asked and solved, "Of what quality is Peter after alteration, which he had not been before?", so it is pertinently asked and solved, "What is Christ after the incarnation that he had not been before?"; and that is the substance, nature and [created]{g:creatus} essence, the Lord Jesus Christ, which is "the Word to be man";[^4] since otherwise undoubtedly "man" would not be predicated in quid of the Word, nor would Christ, [insofar as]{g:in-quantum} [he is] man, be something; which contradicts [not only]{g:nedum} philosophical principles but also the decretal *Cum Christus*, in book 7, *De haereticis*.[^5] How, I ask, would the doctors, old as well as new, say that something began to be Christ, unless something was made Christ through the incarnation? Otherwise the decretal in book 7, *De haereticis*, need not have written thus: "Since Christ [is] perfect God [169] and perfect man, we command that you forbid under anathema that any of the others dare to say 'Christ is not something insofar as he is man'; because, as Christ is true God, so he is true man, subsisting of a rational soul and human flesh."[^6]

## ¶2 · 169.6–170.15

> [p. 169] Ex qua Decretali patet primo quod Christus, quia homo, est aliquid quod ante non fuerat; 2° quod sit univoce homo cum aliis. Nam aliter non esset verus homo ex elementis⁴ univocis cum aliis principiis fratrum suorum. Aliter enim non esset vere homo, sicut nec Spiritus Sanctus est vere columba. Habito ergo quod sit univoce homo cum aliis, ut dicit Augustinus in quodam Sermone De Trinitate: patet quod est univoce animal, corpus, et substancia cum aliis; et per consequens substancia, que est Christus, est univoce substancia cum aliis substanciis creatis: et⁵ cum nulla natura divina sit univoce substancia cum aliis creatis substanciis⁵; sequitur quod creata substancia assumpta est univoce⁶ et Christus cum aliis⁶ substanciis. Confirmatur tripliciter. Omnis⁷ substancia unica univoce⁷ subiecta est accidentibus cum aliis creatis substanciis: sed illa non potest esse substancia, essencia, vel natura divina: ergo relinquitur quod alia substancia, ita quod sit gigas gemine substancie, ut canit ecclesia. Item Christus cum sit univoce homo, animal, corpus, et substancia cum aliis est species et genus hominis; et omne tale universale est creatum secundum se totum: ergo Christus est aliquod creatum. Item si Decretalis solum intelligit Christum esse aliquid secundum quod homo; quia omnis homo est aliquid, et, si Christus est homo, ipse est aliquid; tunc conceditur, quod Christus secundum quod quantus, qualis, vel quomodolibet aliter accidentatus foret aliquid; quod notum est repugnare logice, methaphisice, et sensui Decretalis. Sic enim dicerent quod omnis homo secundum quod peccator est aliquid placens: et Christus [p. 170] secundum quod homo natus ex virgine creavit mundum, cum ut sic inanitus sit equalis Deo¹ Patri; et per consequens secundum quod minor Patre est equalis Patri. Sed quis, rogo, hereticus negaret quin Christus, si est homo, est aliquid? Ideo certum est quod nec Decretalis nec hereticus intelligit sic reduplicativam; sed ut iste terminus secundum signat causam formalem specificam, que est causa subiectiva, unde Christus sit aliquid ut pugnarius substancie. Unde videtur michi indubie quod quicunque concedit de Christo communicacionem ydiomatum et denominacionem univocam accidencium cum creatura, habet concedere consequenter,² quod Christus sit duplex substancia et per consequens creatura. Nam increata non potest esse creato accidenti absoluto subiecta; quia tunc foret mole magna et specialis,³ ut alie create substancie.*⁴

From this decretal it is clear, first, that Christ, because [he is] man, is something which he had not been before; second, that he is univocally a man with others. For otherwise he would not be a true man from elements univocal with the other principles of his brothers. For otherwise he would not be truly a man, just as the Holy Spirit is not truly a dove either.[^7] Having established, then, that he is univocally a man with others, as Augustine says in a certain sermon on the Trinity, it is clear that he is univocally animal, body and substance with others; and consequently the substance which is Christ is univocally substance with other created substances: and since no divine nature is univocally substance with other created substances, it follows that the created substance assumed is univocally [substance], and [is] Christ, with the other substances.[^8] This is confirmed in three ways. Every single substance univocally is subject to [accidents]{g:accidens} with other created substances: now that cannot be the divine substance, essence or nature: therefore it remains that [it is] another substance, so that he is "a giant of twin substance", as the [church]{g:ecclesia} sings.[^9] Again, Christ, since he is univocally man, animal, body and substance with others, is the species and genus of man; and every such [universal]{g:universale} is created in its whole self: therefore Christ is something created. Again, if the decretal understands only "Christ is something insofar as he is man" [in this way], because every man is something, and, if Christ is a man, he is something; then it is granted that Christ, insofar as [he is] quantified, qualified, or in any other way [subject to accidents]{g:accidentatus}, would be something; which is known to be [incompatible]{g:repugnare} with logic, metaphysics and the sense of the decretal.[^10] For thus they would say that every man, insofar as [he is] a sinner, is something pleasing: and that Christ, [170] insofar as [he is] man born of a virgin, created the world, since as such, [emptied]{g:exinanire}, he is equal to God the Father; and consequently, insofar as [he is] less than the Father, he is equal to the Father.[^11] But what heretic, I ask, would deny that Christ, if he is a man, is something? Therefore it is certain that neither the decretal nor the heretic understands the [reduplicative]{g:reduplicativa} [proposition] so; but [it understands it] as this term *secundum* signifies the formal cause of the species, which is the subjective cause, whence Christ is something as a champion of substance.[^12] Hence it seems to me undoubted that whoever grants of Christ the [communication of idioms]{g:communicacio-ydiomatum} and the univocal [naming]{g:denominacio} of accidents with a creature must consequently grant that Christ is a twofold substance and consequently a creature. For an uncreated [substance] cannot be subject to a created absolute accident; since then it would be of great bulk and of a species, like other created substances.[^13]

## ¶3 · 170.16–170.27

> [p. 170] Confirmatur ex hoc, quod post incarnacionem fideles sciverunt quid est Verbum,. quod per ante nec Deus sciverat esse Verbum: ergo aliquid tunc fuit⁵ Verbum quod prius non fuerat. Infideles ergo nescientes ipsum esse Deum sciverunt quid⁶ fuerat: ergo fuit aliquid preter deitatem. Cum ergo aliqua racio substancialis post incarnacionem Verbo conveniat, que non prius convenerat, que non potest poni, nisi hoc quod Verbum est homo; sequitur quod hec sit racio substancialis non aliena a Christo, ut nunc fingitur. Nec sollicitor⁷ aptare verba, ut pertineret volenti contendere cum doctoribus signorum⁸; quia ex dictis⁹ patet materialiter sentencia et racio Augustini.

This is confirmed from this: after the incarnation the faithful knew what the Word is, which beforehand not even God had known to be the Word: therefore something was then the Word which had not been before.[^14] The [unbelievers]{g:infidelis}, therefore, not knowing him to be God, knew what he had been: therefore he was something besides [the Godhead]{g:deitas}. Since, then, some [substantial account]{g:racio-substancialis} belongs to the Word after the incarnation which had not belonged [to him] before, which cannot be posited except this, that the Word is man, it follows that this is a substantial account not alien from Christ, as is now feigned. Nor am I troubled to fit the words as would pertain to one wishing to contend with the doctors of [signs]{g:signum}; since from what has been said the [teaching]{g:sentencia} and the reasoning of Augustine are materially clear.[^15]

## ¶4 · 170.28–171.13

> [p. 170] Illud confirmari potest tripliciter. Primo ex hoc: Verbum assumendo hominem assumpsit aliquid; ergo cum omne assumere sit facere, sequitur, quod faciendo se esse¹⁰ hominem, fecit se esse aliquid; et per consequens per assumpcionem fuit aliquid quod prius non fuerat. 2° confirmatur. Posito quod¹¹ [p. 171] pro instanti incarnacionis Petrus fidelis cognoscat Verbum divinum nunc esse Deum et ignoret quod nunc sit homo; tunc patet quod sequitur—Verbum divinum nunc non est aliquid quin iste Petrus nunc¹ cognoscat ipsum esse aliquid: sed Verbum divinum nunc est homo: ergo Petrus cognoscit ipsum nunc esse hominem—. Hic enim non est fallacia accidentis, cum Christus, in quantum homo, sit aliquid: et consequens est falsum: ideo relinquitur quod maior: ex quo sequitur aliquid esse nunc Verbum, quod Petrus nunc ignorat esse Verbum, quod non potest poni deitas. 3° confirmatur ex hoc quod quantitas et cetera accidencia non sunt essencialiter distincta² a subiectis: ergo multo evidencius humanitas est homo.

This can be confirmed in three ways. First from this: the Word, by [assuming]{g:assumere} man, assumed something; therefore, since every assuming is making, it follows that, by making himself to be man, he made himself to be something; and consequently through the [assumption]{g:assumpcio} he was something which he had not been before. This is confirmed, second. Suppose that [171] at the instant of the incarnation the faithful Peter knows "the divine Word is now God" and [is ignorant of]{g:ignorare} "he is now man"; then it is clear that [it follows]{g:sequitur}: "The divine Word is not now anything without this Peter now knowing him to be it: now the divine Word is now man: therefore Peter knows him now to be man." For here there is no [fallacy]{g:fallacia} of accident, since Christ, insofar as [he is] man, is something: and the [consequent]{g:consequens} is false: therefore it remains [that] the major [is false]: from which it follows that something is now the Word which Peter now does not know to be the Word, which cannot be posited [to be] the Godhead.[^16] This is confirmed, third, from this: quantity and the other accidents are not essentially distinct from their subjects: therefore much more evidently the humanity is man.[^17]

## Notes

[^1]: **C/T.** The first reply of 167.16–19: univocation is only in signs. Wyclif sets it aside as a quarrel over words: those who speak of univocal things mean the things. A B C omit *pro* (167.23).

[^2]: **T/C.** "In such a way that": O omits the second *sit* (167.31), which the print doubles (*sit aliquid sit sic quod*); the English follows O. O omits *Christus* (167.30) and has *aliud* "another thing" over an erasure for *idem* (168.1). *Idem*: the same thing he is, a man.

[^3]: **C/T.** *Quid* and *quis*: "what" asks after a nature, "who" after a person; so a new "what" in Christ is a created substance, not a new person. The print's *st* (168.8) is read as *et*. A B C read *ergo* for *enim* (168.2); B *quale eo ipso quale prius* (168.5–6); A B *sic deveniente*, O *si adveniente* (168.6); C *dare quod est* (168.10–11); A B *qui* for *que* (168.14).

[^4]: **C/T.** *Que est Verbum esse hominem*: the created essence is the Word's being a man. O omits *Qualis … alteracionem* (168.17) and adds *et* after *Christus* (168.19); A B C read *et idem substancia* (168.19–20) and *qui* for *que* (168.21).

[^5]: **S/T.** The decretal of Alexander III, *Cum Christus*, in the *Liber Extra* 5.7.7, under the title *De haereticis* (LEDGER I10c-1, to be checked); Wyclif's "book 7" stands for the title. O omits everything from *Decretali* (168.24) to *substancie* (170.15), and so is no witness for the rest of this paragraph and the next. C reads *debent* for *dicerent* (168.26).

[^6]: **T.** *De ceteris* "of the others" (169.2) is the print with A; B C read *de cetero* "henceforth". C reads *carne assumpta vel subsistens* (169.5). A B C abbreviate *quatenus* variously (169.1).

[^7]: **S/T.** The dove: Matt. 3:16 ("as a dove"). The brothers: Heb. 2:17. C leaves a space for *elementis* (169.9).

[^8]: **S/C/T.** Augustine, "a certain sermon on the Trinity" (LEDGER I10c-2, to be checked). The final clause is construed as printed; Harris asks at the foot whether to read *univoce homo cum aliis* "univocally a man with the others" for *univoce et Christus cum aliis* (169.18). B omits *et … substanciis* (169.15–17).

[^9]: **S/C/T.** "A giant of twin substance": Ambrose's hymn *Intende qui regis Israel* (*Veni redemptor gentium*), after Ps. 18:6 (LEDGER I10c-3, to be checked). The major as printed, "every single substance", must mean the substance that is in Christ subject to accidents; C reads *ee substancia univoce* (169.19).

[^10]: **C.** The reading Wyclif rejects: that *secundum quod homo* only marks the condition "if he is a man", under which any accidental predicate would do as well.

[^11]: **S/T.** Phil. 2:6–7 (equal to God, emptied); John 14:28 (the Father greater). C omits *Deo* (170.2).

[^12]: **C.** *Secundum* in the reduplicative names the formal cause, so "insofar as man" gives what Christ is by his humanity. *Ut pugnarius substancie* (170.8) is the print and has no variant: "as a champion of substance", recalling the giant of twin substance (169.22); the word is doubtful.

[^13]: **C/T.** *Specialis* "of a species", belonging to a species as created substances do; B reads *speciabilis* (170.14). A has *communitur* corrected to *consequenter*, C *communiter* (170.11).

[^14]: **C/T.** The faithful knew after the incarnation a new "what" of the Word, the man, which before was not the Word and so was not known, even by God, to be the Word. A B C read *fuerat* for *fuit* (170.18); B *quod* for *quid* (170.20).

[^15]: **T.** O reads *Nolicitor* (170.25) and *terminorum* "of terms" for *signorum* (170.26); C *signis* for *dictis* (170.26).

[^16]: **C/T.** The fallacy of accident would excuse the inference if "man" were only an accident of the Word; Wyclif denies it, since by the humanity Christ is something. So the major, that Peter knows the Word to be everything it is, is false. O omits *esse* (170.30) and *quod* (170.32); B omits *nunc*, O reads *non* (171.4).

[^17]: **C/T.** Wyclif's own teaching that accidents are not essentially distinct from their subjects (cf. 165.31–166.1). O reads *distincte* (171.12).
