# *De ecclesia*, chapter 9, part 2: endowment as alms under condition; to be freed from it is a privilege

## ¶1 · 186.32–187.15

> [p. 186] Sed tercio obicitur, cum privilegia quibus Christus dotavit ecclesiam sunt perfecciora quam terrena privilegia [p. 187] succedencia, sicut et status vivencium secundum illa privilegia est statu moderno perfeccior atque ad servandum facilior, videtur quod non licuit a privilegiis illis et statu recedere; nam nulli persone licet magis bonum sibi in eleccione postponere: ecclesia, sponsa Christi est persona, cum sit virgo ex cap. VI°, ergo non licet sibi privilegia Christi et statum evangelice paupertatis relinquere. Confirmatur primo per decretum De Consecracione, dist. Vª, Non mediocriter, ubi dicitur quod graviter peccant qui eligunt minus bonum sibi maiori bono postposito. Confirmatur secundo ex dictis libro V°, cap., ubi docetur quod oportet viantem semper meliorando procedere. Et tercio confirmatur ex hoc quod consilia Christi sunt perpetua et sponse sue indispensabilia.

But thirdly it is objected: since the privileges with which Christ endowed the [church]{g:ecclesia} are more perfect than the earthly privileges [187] that succeeded them, as also the state of those living according to those privileges is more perfect than the modern state, and easier to keep, it seems that it was not lawful to depart from those privileges and that state; for it is lawful for no person, in choosing, to set the greater good behind [the lesser] for himself; the church, the [bride]{g:sponsa} of Christ, is a person, since she is a virgin, from chapter 6; therefore it is not lawful for her to abandon the privileges of Christ and the state of evangelical poverty.[^1] It is confirmed, first, by the decree De consecratione, D. 5 [c. 24] Non mediocriter, where it is said that they sin gravely who choose the lesser good for themselves, setting the greater good behind. It is confirmed, secondly, from what is said in book 5, chapter [ ], where it is taught that one [on the road]{g:viantes} must always proceed by growing better. And thirdly it is confirmed from this, that the counsels of Christ are perpetual and not to be dispensed for his bride.[^2]

## ¶2 · 187.16–188.13

> [p. 187] Hic videtur mihi quod unum concluditur ex assumpto necessario, cum omnis christianus debet sequi Christum in moribus quantum sufficit, unde videtur mihi quod beatus Silvester potest dici ex pietate suscepisse elemosinas Cesaris corporales ad exonerandum eum, ad excitandum eius devocionem et procurandum tenerius pro fidelibus quam faceret populus laycalis; quod, cum stat cum paupertate evangelica, patet quod clerus non debet exinde inniti proprietati vel fastui, sed ad sui tedium secundum partes plus abiectas ministrare pauperibus de collatis elemosinis. Unde tales elemosine non possunt donari in perpetuam elemosinam quomodocunque clerus vixerit, sed sub condicione saltem tacita quod clerus sit vere sequax apostolorum in vita et opere. Et ita credo quod oportet ut reputet sibi omne tediosum ministrare pauperibus et fore gaudiosum, dando operam contemplacioni [p. 188] et predicacioni, quod fideles layci accipiant illud onus (ut alias exemplificavi de Augustino et aliis sanctis). Unde quia fragilitas humana prona est ad relinquendum primam caritatem de elemosinis, superbiendo, se ipsam nimis laute pascendo et pastoris officium relinquendo, ideo apostoli et sancciores ecclesie talia reliquerunt. Unde mirum est si collacio rei temporalis sit clero privilegium; ymmo apostolis petentibus illam a Christo audivisset responsum superius: Nescitis quod petatis. Nonne docet experiencia quod dotacio talis subicit dotatos mundane sollicitudini, servicio et exaccioni, et per consequens distrahit ab amore Christi et ministerio sacerdotali?

Here it seems to me that one [thing] is [inferred]{g:concludere} from the [assumption]{g:assumptum} necessarily, since every Christian ought to follow Christ in manners as far as suffices.[^3] Hence it seems to me that blessed Silvester can be said to have received the bodily [alms]{g:elemosina} of Caesar out of piety: to unburden him, to stir up his devotion, and to provide more tenderly for the faithful than the lay people would; and since this stands with evangelical poverty, it is clear that the [clergy]{g:clerus} ought not on that account to rely on property or pomp, but, to its own weariness, to minister to the poor from the alms conferred, in the more abject parts [of the service].[^4] Hence such alms cannot be given as perpetual alms however the clergy may have lived, but under the condition, at least tacit, that the clergy be truly a follower of the apostles in life and work. And so I believe that it must count all ministering to the poor wearisome to itself, and count it joyful, while it gives its labour to contemplation [188] and preaching, that faithful laymen take up that burden (as I have shown elsewhere by the example of Augustine and other saints).[^5] Hence, because human frailty is prone to leave its first [charity]{g:caritas} over alms, by growing proud, by feeding itself too sumptuously and by leaving the office of shepherd, therefore the apostles and the holier [members] of the church left such things. Hence it is a wonder if the conferring of a temporal thing is a privilege to the clergy; indeed, had the apostles asked it of Christ, it would have heard the answer given above: "You know not what you ask."[^6] Does not experience teach that such an [endowment]{g:dotacio} subjects those endowed to worldly care, [service]{g:servicium} and exaction, and consequently draws them away from the love of Christ and from the priestly ministry?

## ¶3 · 188.14–190.12

> [p. 188] Quomodo ergo non foret privilegium ab incitamento huiusmodi liberari, unde decretum recitat XIª, q. Iª, ex epistola beati Petri ad Clementem scriptum sub hac forma: Te quidem oportet irreprehensibiliter vivere et summo studio niti, ut omnes vite huius occupaciones abicias, ne fideiussor existas nec advocatus licium fias neve in ulla alia occupacione prorsus inveniaris mundani negocii occasione perplexus. Neque enim iudicem neque cognitorem secularium negociorum hodie te vult ordinare Christus, ne prefocatus presentibus hominum curis non possis verbo Dei vacare. Hec vero opera que tibi minime congruere dicimus exibeant sibi invicem vacantes laici, et te nemo occupet ab hiis studiis per que salus hominibus datur. Quomodo ergo licet nobis ab ista regula apostolica recedere quibus relictum [p. 189] est a Deo ecclesiam gentilium ad perfectam legem Christi convertere? Non enim cadit auctoritas vel racio dispensandi; unde verisimile est quod radix tocius perturbacionis in seculo est cleri declinacio a lege Christi ad vitam et tradiciones civiles. Christus autem semper reliquit in una parte ecclesie sue vel alia aliquos fideles qui mundum deserant, et in illis forte abiectis primatibus stat fides et continuacio sancte matris ecclesie. Et patet quod sepe non est privilegium vocata dotacio cleri in temporalibus, et per consequens auferendo a clero ista non oportet quod auferantur ab eo privilegia, sed pocius amoto prohibente sit ecclesia apostolica restituta ad vera privilegia primitiva; quod ego credo dominos temporales in casu debere facere sub pena dampnacionis gravissime. Numquid credimus esse magnum privilegium episcopo vel abbati quod habeant furcas et puteum, in quibus dampnati regulariter trucidentur, et sic de carcere, de aliis mundanis privilegiis sive insigniis? Ymmo non foret privilegium prelato quod haberet efficaciam, ut quicquid tetigerit statim convertatur in aurum, quia tunc periret aer deferens inspirandus, periret vescibile et tegumentum corpori temperatum. Unde in detestacionem huius philargirie legitur beatum Evangelistam convertisse frondes silvestres in aurum et litoria saxa in gemmas. Iste sanctus statim potuit fuisse aput mundum ditissimus et secundum gloriam mundanam preclarissimus, dominatissimus et famosissimus, si non repugnasset perfeccioni apostolice et privilegio concesso a Christo; aliter enim peccasset graviter omittendo. Nam habens in potestate tam [p. 190] faciliter proficiendi sibi et aliis, glorificando ecclesiam plus quam Cesar, non haberet excusacionem de negligenti desidia, sed scitur demonstracione certissima quod omne spirituale privilegium superat quodcunque corporale privilegium in infinitum, et sic non est privilegium clerum stipari seculari milicia, vesci in auro, uti fibula vel habere secundum mundanam gloriam splendencia ornamenta. Omnia enim ista gentes exquirunt (ut patet I° Machabeorum IX° et XI°), Christus autem et eius discipuli cum habere possent facillime hec omnia, si non repugnarent sue perfeccioni, quia repugnant, ideo omnia ista penitus respuerunt.

How then would it not be a privilege to be freed from an incitement of this kind? Hence the decree, C. 11 q. 1 [c. 29], recites from the letter of blessed Peter to Clement, written in this form: "It behoves you indeed to live irreproachably and to strive with the utmost zeal to cast off all the occupations of this life, that you be not a surety, nor become an advocate in lawsuits, nor be found at all entangled in any other occupation by occasion of worldly business. For Christ does not wish to ordain you today a judge or an examiner of secular business, lest, choked by the present cares of men, you be unable to be free for the word of God. But these works, which we say do not at all befit you, let laymen who are free for them perform for one another; and let no one occupy you away from those studies through which salvation is given to men."[^7] How then is it lawful for us to depart from this apostolic rule, we to whom it has been left [189] by God to convert the church of the gentiles to the perfect [law of Christ]{g:lex Christi}? For there is no room for authority or reason for dispensing; hence it is likely that the root of all the disturbance in the world is the clergy's turning aside from the law of Christ to civil life and traditions. But Christ has always left, in one part of his church or another, some faithful who desert the world; and in them perhaps, who have cast primacies aside, stand the faith and the continuance of holy mother church.[^8] And it is clear that the so-called endowment of the clergy in [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} is often not a privilege; and consequently, in taking these things from the clergy, it is not necessary that privileges be taken from it, but rather that, the hindrance being removed, the apostolic church be restored to the true primitive privileges; which I believe the [temporal lords]{g:temporales domini} are bound in the case to do, under pain of most grievous damnation.[^9] Do we believe it a great privilege for a [bishop]{g:episcopus} or an abbot that they have gallows and pit, in which the condemned are regularly put to death, and so of the prison and of other worldly privileges or insignia? Indeed, it would not be a privilege for a [prelate]{g:prelatus} to have the power that whatever he touched should at once be turned into gold; for then the air that carries [breath], which must be breathed in, would perish, and food and covering fitted to the body would perish.[^10] Hence, in detestation of this love of money, it is read that the blessed Evangelist turned branches of the wood into gold and stones of the shore into gems. That saint could at once have been, in the world's eyes, the richest and, according to worldly glory, the most illustrious, the most lordly and the most famous, had it not been repugnant to apostolic perfection and to the privilege granted by Christ; for otherwise he would have sinned gravely by omitting it.[^11] For one having it in his power so [190] easily to profit himself and others, glorifying the church more than Caesar, would have no excuse for negligent sloth; but it is known by most certain demonstration that every spiritual privilege surpasses any bodily privilege infinitely; and so it is not a privilege for the clergy to be thronged about by secular [knighthood]{g:milicia}, to feed in gold, to wear a buckle, or to have shining ornaments according to worldly glory.[^12] For all these things the heathens seek (as is clear, 1 Maccabees 10 and 11); but Christ and his disciples, although they could most easily have had all these things if they were not repugnant to their perfection, because they are repugnant, therefore utterly rejected all these things.[^13]

## ¶4 · 190.13–190.16

> [p. 190] Sed quarto arguitur sequi ex dictis: quod nullum sit corporale privilegium, ymmo quod nedum licet secularibus sed debent sub pena excommunicacionis auferre bona a clericis quibus putantes se benefacere erraverunt.

But fourthly it is argued that it follows from what has been said that there is no bodily privilege; indeed, that it is not only lawful for [seculars]{g:secularis} but they are bound, under pain of excommunication, to take goods away from the clerics to whom, thinking they did them good, they erred [in giving].[^14]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 187.1: The Church ought not to have left the state (of poverty) appointed by Christ.
- 187.18: Silvester may have acted from pious motives.
- 187.29: The clergy should gladly leave laymen to minister to the poor
- 188.1: and devote themselves to preaching.
- 189.9: The removal of endowment may be a restoration of primitive privilege,
- 189.17: worth much more, than the right of gallows etc.
- 189.25: The apostles were poor by choice.
- 190.13: It is said that, if this be true, all temporal privileges are abolished.

## Notes

[^1]: **T/C.** A margin *Tercia obieccio* (186.33). *Statu moderno*: the present state of the endowed clergy. *Ex cap. VI°*: we take it as this work's chapter 6, on the church as virgin bride; the reference is to be checked (LEDGER E09b-1); cf. 2 Corinthians 11:2, "a chaste virgin to Christ". Side-note 187.1.

[^2]: **S/T/C.** Decretum, De cons. D. 5 c. 24 (the modern form is ours; Jerome; Friedberg I, 1418; LEDGER E09b-2). Loserth prints the canon as *Non mediocriter errat qui magno bono prefert mediocre bonum*: Wyclif's *graviter peccant qui eligunt minus bonum …* is his own restatement, translated. *Libro V°, cap.*: the chapter number is blank in the print, and the book is not identified (LEDGER E09b-3). *Viantem*: the one on the road (GLOSSARY *viantes*). *Indispensabilia*: not open to dispensation.

[^3]: **T/C.** A margin *Responsio* (187.16). Loserth prints *unum*; A₁ reads *verum*, A and B *um* (Loserth: "verum?"). The print is translated, "one [thing]"; with *verum* it would be "something true". *Necessario* may also go with *assumpto*: "from the necessary assumption". Wyclif grants part of the objection's conclusion.

[^4]: **C/R.** Silvester and Caesar: Constantine's endowment of the church under Pope Silvester (as in 08-f). *Ad exonerandum eum*: to unburden Caesar of his wealth. *Secundum partes plus abiectas*: in the more menial parts of the service of the poor. Side-note 187.18.

[^5]: **C/S.** *Reputet sibi omne tediosum … et fore gaudiosum … quod*: we take two complements: the clergy is to count ministering to the poor irksome, and to count it joyful that faithful laymen take over that burden. "Elsewhere": the place in Wyclif's works is to be checked (LEDGER E09b-4). Side-notes 187.29, 188.1.

[^6]: **S/T/C.** *Relinquendum primam caritatem*: Revelation 2:4, "thou hast left thy first charity". A and A₁ read *audivissent* (188.9), "they would have heard"; the print's singular is translated, with the clergy understood as subject ("it"). Matthew 20:22: Wyclif has *quod* for the Vulgate's *quid*, with the same sense. "Above": the request of the sons of Zebedee, 179 (08-f).

[^7]: **S/T.** Decretum C. 11 q. 1 c. 29 (the modern form is ours; Loserth's rubric *De secularibus negociis episcopus cognoscere non debet*; LEDGER E09b-5). The canon is from Clement's letter to James (Friedberg I, 634), but within it Peter speaks to Clement (c. 30, *tibi, o Clemens*), so Wyclif's ascription to Peter is defensible; translated. Loserth corrects the codices' *ne vel* from the decree (188.20); his correction is translated. *Perplexus*: entangled, not the moral-theology sense of GLOSSARY *perplexus*. A margins *Nota decretum super hoc quod clerus non debet se implicare mundo* (188.16) and *De ablacione temporalium et cleri paupertate* (188.28).

[^8]: **C/T.** *Non enim cadit*: there is no room for. *Abiectis primatibus*: we take *primatibus* from *primatus*, "primacy", in an ablative absolute: those who have cast primacies aside. It may be from *primas*: "the primates being cast off". A margin *Conclusio* (189.6); A₁ is corrected to *deserunt* (189.7).

[^9]: **T.** A and A₁ lack *amoto* (189.12). *In casu*: in the case stated (where the endowment hinders). Side-note 189.9.

[^10]: **C/T.** *Furcas et puteum*: the franchise of gallows and pit (the drowning-pit) held by lords of high justice. *Aer deferens inspirandus*: construed loosely, "the air that carries [breath]"; the phrase is compressed. A₁ reads *vestimentum* (189.23). B reads *Quod* (189.17). The golden touch is Midas's, unnamed. Side-note 189.17.

[^11]: **R/S/T.** B margin, another hand: *Evangelista frondem convertit in aurum* (189.26). The story of John the Evangelist turning branches and stones into gold and gems is in his legend; source and wording to be checked (LEDGER E09b-6). *Philargirie*: love of money (Greek *philargyria*). A and A₁ lack *et secundum — dominatissimus* (189.28–29). *Repugnasset*: the understood subject is that wealth. Side-note 189.25.

[^12]: **T/S.** A and A₁ read *in finitum* (190.5). B lacks *seculari* (190.6). Loserth suggests *vestiri*, "to be clothed", for *vesci*; the print's *vesci in auro* is translated, and construes: cf. 1 Maccabees 11:58, "leave to drink in gold".

[^13]: **S/T.** The golden buckle is at 1 Maccabees 10:89 and 11:58 (DR "buckle of gold"); Wyclif's *IX°* is a misnumbering for X. *Omnia enim ista gentes exquirunt*: Matthew 6:32, "for after all these things do the heathens seek" (Vulgate *inquirunt*). B reads *possint* and *repugnat*; A₁ is corrected to *sed quia* (190.10–11).

[^14]: **T/C.** A margin *Quarta obieccio* (190.13). *Quibus putantes se benefacere erraverunt*: the seculars erred in the clerics to whom they thought they did good, that is, in endowing them. *Debent*: are bound. Side-note 190.13.
