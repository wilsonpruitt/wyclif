# *De eucharistia*, chapter 7, part 8: figuring without identity, the sound sense of transubstantiation, and the argument for impanation

## ¶1 · 217.23–218.14

> [p. 217] Sed supponamus totam communitatem christianorum irregulariter cognoscere hunc sensum, non fingendo mendacium ex parte accidencium per se existencium et admittere talem modum loquendi: Hoc signum est corpus Christi ad sensum katholicum, ita quod esse significet sic sacramentaliter figurare, adhuc non sequitur quod panis ydemptificetur corpori Christi, ut patet attendenti ad sensus. Non enim [p. 218] sequitur, si panis sic figurabit corpus Christi quod tunc fiet idem numeraliter eidem corpori; nec subest tanta auctoritas ad concedendum locucionem unam ut aliam. Nunquam enim crediderunt patres vere ecclesie quod panis ille sit idem numeraliter cum corpore Christi, sicut nec credunt hodie sacerdotes qui conficiunt; tunc enim tangerent infirmi illum devocius quam mulier que tetigit fimbriam vestimenti Christi Luc. VIIIᵒ, 44; et infinitum honorabilius tractarent corpus dominicum vivum et gloriosum quam de facto tractant panem illum. Et patet quod nec fides scripture nec sancti doctores nec leges ecclesie dictant quod omnis talis panis consecratus fiet ydemptice corpus Christi.

But let us suppose that the whole community of Christians [irregularly]{g:irregulariter} knows this [meaning]{g:sensus},[^1] not feigning a lie on the part of [accidents]{g:accidens} existing [of themselves]{g:per-se}, and admits such a manner of speaking: "This sign is the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}" in the catholic meaning, so that "is" signifies "thus [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} to [figure]{g:figurare}"; still it does not [follow]{g:sequitur} that the [bread]{g:panis} is [identified]{g:ydemptificare} with the body of Christ, as is clear to one who attends to the meanings. For it does not [218] follow, if the bread will thus figure the body of Christ, that then it will become numerically the same with that same body; nor is there so great an authority underlying the granting of the one locution as of the other.[^2] For the fathers of the true [church]{g:ecclesia} never believed that that bread is numerically the same with the body of Christ, just as the priests who [consecrate]{g:conficere} do not believe it today; for then the sick would touch it more devoutly than the woman who "touched the hem of" Christ's "garment", Luke 8:44,[^3] and they would handle [the Lord's body]{g:corpus-dominicum}, living and glorious, infinitely more honourably than in fact they handle that bread. And it is clear that neither the faith of scripture nor the holy doctors nor the [laws of the church]{g:leges-ecclesie} dictate that every such consecrated bread will become [identically]{g:ydemptice} the body of Christ.

## ¶2 · 218.15–218.28

> [p. 218] Et si replicatur per locum a simili, si panis quem Christus accepit in manibus fiebat et fit corpus Christi per consecracionem, per idem panis quem sacerdos hodie rite consecrat, fieret et foret sic corpus Christi: patet quod doctores moderni negarent assumptum ut summe hereticum; ille autem qui fideliter concederet consequens quod panis ille est corpus Christi ad sensum expositum, negaret quod panis ille fiet vel erit idem numeraliter cum corpore Christi, quia illud esse quod est sic figurare non est sic ydemptificari; et talis fidelis crebro concederet sine repugnancia quod ille panis est et non est corpus Christi, ut equivocacio in qua non est contradiccio melius detegatur.

And if [it is rejoined]{g:replicatur} by [the topic from likeness]{g:a-simili} — if the bread which Christ took in his hands became and becomes the body of Christ by [consecration]{g:consecracio}, by the same token the bread which the priest today duly consecrates would become and would be thus the body of Christ — it is clear that the [modern doctors]{g:moderni-doctores} would deny [the assumption]{g:assumptum} as supremely heretical;[^4] but he who faithfully granted the [consequent]{g:consequens}, that that bread is the body of Christ in the meaning expounded, would deny that that bread will become or will be numerically the same with the body of Christ, because that [being]{g:esse} which is thus to figure is not thus to be identified; and such a faithful man would often grant without [incompatibility]{g:repugnancia} that that bread is and is not the body of Christ, so that the [equivocation]{g:equivocacio}, in which there is no contradiction, may be better [exposed]{g:detegere}.

## ¶3 · 218.29–218.31

> [p. 218] Sed difficultas est de virtute qua alteratur panis vel ut alii locuntur, convertitur, transsubstanciatur vel consecratur ut sit sacramentum.

But the difficulty is about the [power]{g:virtus} by which the bread is altered or, as others speak, is converted, transubstantiated or consecrated, so as to be a [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}.[^5]

## ¶4 · 218.32–219.26

> [p. 218] Hic dicitur primo quod multitudo talium terminorum introducta sine auctoritate scripture multum alteravit et perturbavit ecclesiam; secundo dicitur quod illa [p. 219] virtus quam habet panis est transitoria et nobis insensibilis, nec scimus pro quo instanti advenit vel recedit, cum tamquam species lucis fundatur in corpore Christi; ideo dicunt quidam philosophi quod panis non exinde alteratur, alii autem dicunt quod alteratur spiritualiter, extense loquendo de alteracione, et terminatur ad conversionem panis in corpus Christi, ut loquitur Ambrosius. Et per illam conversionem diversi diversimode intelligunt, illi minus bene quod panis secundum se totum destruitur, et illi quod panis fit corpus Christi ad sensum expositum; et sic potest intelligi Thomas quod corpus Christi est ibi presens per conversionem panis in ipsum; sic Christus est presens humanitus fidelibus congregatis in nomine suo per fidem et dileccionem conversis ad ipsum. Nec est alia sanctitas in sacramento, ut patet ex dictis; et illa sanctitas videtur esse subiective in corpore Christi et contentive vel diffinitive in sacramento non corporaliter sed causaliter, sicut sacramentum habet aliam virtutem ad movendum animam fidelem ad devote memorandum Christum, que virtus est subiective in anima et obiective in sacramento; nunquam tamen est nota fidelitas quod virtus talis sit res potens per se existere vel quod illa mutacio vel conversio terminatur ad ydemptitatem numeralem sacramenti et corporis Christi.

To this the answer is, first, that a multitude of such terms, introduced without the [authority]{g:auctoritas} of scripture, has much altered and disturbed the church; secondly it is said that that [219] power which the bread has is transitory and [imperceptible]{g:insensibilis} to us, nor do we know at what instant it comes or departs, since, like a species of light, it is founded in the body of Christ;[^6] therefore some philosophers say that the bread is not altered by it, but others say that it is altered [spiritually]{g:spiritualiter}, [speaking in an extended sense]{g:extense-loquendo} of alteration, and that it terminates at the [conversion]{g:conversio} of the bread into the body of Christ, as Ambrose speaks.[^7] And by that conversion different men understand different things: those less well, that the bread according to its whole self is destroyed; and these, that the bread becomes the body of Christ in the meaning expounded; and so Thomas can be understood, that the body of Christ is present there by the conversion of the bread into it;[^8] so Christ is present [humanly]{g:humanitus} to the faithful gathered in his name, converted to him by faith and [love]{g:dileccio}.[^9] Nor is there other holiness in the sacrament, as is clear from what has been said; and that holiness seems to be subjectively in the body of Christ and [by way of containing]{g:contentive}, or definitively, in the sacrament, not [bodily]{g:corporaliter} but causally,[^10] just as the sacrament has another power, to move the faithful soul to remember Christ devoutly, which power is subjectively in the soul and objectively in the sacrament; yet it is never a known [point of] fidelity that such a power is a thing able to exist of itself, or that that change or conversion terminates at the [numerical identity]{g:ydemptitas-numeralis} of the sacrament and the body of Christ.[^11]

## ¶5 · 219.27–220.7

> [p. 219] Sed pro istis mutacionibus notandum quod possunt tam bene quam male intelligi. Bene, ut transsubstanciacio sit conversio, quando una substancia transit in aliam vel fit alia. Contentarentur autem superiores ecclesie de sensu transsubstanciacionis manente transsubstanciati essencia, cum nullus alius sit fundandus. Ponunt enim papam in sua intronizacione creari [p. 220] mutato nomine et creare hos quos promovet; illud autem foret plus quam transsubstanciare, quod fit quando Deus mutat creaturam substancialem in melius. Unde Ambrosius IIᵒ libro De Penitencia: Interiit, inquit, caro, cum substancia eius transit in Christum. Ideo sepe dicunt doctores quod conversus sit ipse Christus.

But as for these changes it is to be noted that they can be understood well as well as badly. Well, so that [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio} is a conversion, when one [substance]{g:substancia} passes into another or becomes another. And the superiors of the church would be content with the meaning of transubstantiation in which the [essence]{g:essencia} of the transubstantiated thing remains, since no other is to be founded. For they hold that the [pope]{g:papa} at his enthronement is created [220] with his name changed, and creates those whom he promotes;[^12] and that would be more than to transubstantiate, which happens when God changes a substantial [creature]{g:creatura} for the better. Hence Ambrose in the second book *On Penance*: "The flesh," he says, "perished, when its substance passes into Christ."[^13] Therefore the doctors often say that the converted man is Christ himself.[^14]

## ¶6 · 220.8–220.12

> [p. 220] Item Augustinus De Penitencia: Nullus transit ad Christum, ut incipiat esse quod non erat nisi eum peniteat fuisse quod erat. Et sic sancti cum usu ecclesie ponunt talem conversionem vel transubstanciacionem essencie, licet ipsa maneat eadem in natura.

Again Augustine, *On Penance*: "No one passes over to Christ so as to begin to be what he was not, unless he repents of having been what he was."[^15] And so [the saints]{g:sancti}, with the [use]{g:usus} of the church, posit such a conversion or transubstantiation of the essence, although it remains the same in [nature]{g:natura}.

## ¶7 · 220.13–220.28

> [p. 220] Et patet ex principiis secte signorum conveniencia huius sensus. Ipsi enim ponunt propter difficultatem fidei et augmentum meriti multa que nesciunt fundare; quanto magis nos ponemus fundabiles et venerabiles veritates katholicas inconveniencium exclusivas. Per hoc enim possumus ponere essenciam panis subiectare transsubstanciacionem, quam ipsi ponunt esse sine subiecto, licet non possint hoc intelligere, sicut dicunt. Per hoc eciam possumus salvare dicta katholica sanctorum legum et usus ecclesie, que dicunt concorditer quod panis fiet caro Christi. Et tercio potissime, quia fides scripture in quinque evangelistis narrat Veritatem que mentiri non potest asserere quod panis est corpus suum. Unde videtur esse nimis hereticum quod virtute benediccionis Christi panis omnino destruitur, sicut patet ex diffinicione heresis.

And from the principles of [the sect of the signs]{g:secta-signorum} the [agreement]{g:conveniencia} of this meaning is clear.[^16] For they posit, on account of the difficulty of faith and the increase of merit, many things which they do not know how to found; how much more shall we posit catholic truths that can be founded and are venerable, which exclude [absurdities]{g:inconveniens}.[^17] For by this we can posit that the essence of the bread [is the subject of]{g:subiectare} the transubstantiation, which they posit to be without a subject, although they cannot understand this, as they say.[^18] By this also we can save the catholic sayings of the saints, of the laws and of the use of the church, which say with one accord that the bread will become the flesh of Christ. And third, most powerfully, because the faith of scripture in the five evangelists [relates]{g:narrare} that the Truth, who cannot lie, asserts that the bread is his body.[^19] Hence it seems to be [exceedingly]{g:nimis} heretical that by the power of Christ's blessing the bread is wholly destroyed, as is clear from the [definition]{g:diffinicio} of heresy.

## ¶8 · 220.29–221.9

> [p. 220] Et quantum ad decretalem que videtur dicere quod accidens est sine subiecto, dicitur quod hoc est inpossibile [p. 221] de virtute sermonis, ut patet ex determinacione Augustini; sed potest sic sane intelligi quod accidens sensibile in eukaristia est sine corpore sibi subiecto existente in actu consideracionis fidelis ad quod magis debet attendere. Nam (ut dictum est) cogitacio nostra principaliter debet esse de corpore Christi in celo et cogitacio de natura eukaristie est propterea suspendenda. Et ad istum sensum videntur sancti dicere quod in hostia solum remanent qualitates.

And as for the [decretal]{g:decretalis} which seems to say "an accident is without a subject",[^20] the answer is that this is impossible [221] [in the proper sense of the words]{g:de-virtute-sermonis}, as is clear from the determination of Augustine;[^21] but it can soundly be understood thus: that the [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} accident in [the Eucharist]{g:eukaristia} is without a body subject to it existing in the act of the faithful man's consideration, to which he ought rather to attend.[^22] For (as has been said) our thought ought [principally]{g:principaliter} to be of the body of Christ in heaven, and thought of the nature of the Eucharist is for that reason to be [suspended]{g:suspendere}. And in this meaning the saints seem to say that in the [host]{g:hostia} only [qualities]{g:qualitas} remain.

## ¶9 · 221.11–221.18

> [p. 221] Sed quarto principaliter circumpalpitat infidelis per aliam rimam vacuam, dum arguitur pro ydemptificacione per hoc quod magis consonus et devotus modus in eukaristia est ponendus; sed huiusmodi est inpanacio, ut dicit sic opinans quod sicut verbum assumpsit humanitatem faciendo ex deitate et humanitate idem suppositum, ita videtur in materia de eukaristia.

But fourth, as a main head, the [unbeliever]{g:infidelis} gropes about through another empty chink,[^23] when [it is argued]{g:arguitur-per} for [identification]{g:ydemptificacio} by appeal to this, that the more consonant and devout mode is to be posited in the Eucharist; now such is [impanation]{g:inpanacio}, as one who so opines says: that just as [the Word]{g:verbum} [assumed]{g:assumere} [the humanity]{g:humanitas}, making of [the Godhead]{g:deitas} and the humanity the same [supposit]{g:suppositum}, so it seems in the matter of the Eucharist.

## ¶10 · 221.19–221.30

> [p. 221] Unde quidam vocant inpanacionem qua panis fit caro Christi et invinacionem qua vinum fit sanguis Christi, sicut vocant incarnacionem qua caro fit Deus, ut dicitur Joh. primo. Et pro isto videtur sentencia Augustini in libro sentenciarum Prosperi, et ponitur in De Consecracione distinccione IIᵃ Hoc est, ubi sic dicitur quod sacrificium altaris duobus conficitur, et constat scilicet visibili elementorum specie et invisibili Christi carne et sanguine, scilicet sacramento et re sacramenti, sicut persona Christi constituitur ex homine et ex Deo, ideo sicut Christus est verus Deus et verus homo, sic est de isto sacrificio.

Hence some call impanation that by which the bread becomes the flesh of Christ, and [invination]{g:invinacio} that by which the [wine]{g:vinum} becomes the blood of Christ, just as they call incarnation that by which flesh becomes God, as is said in John 1.[^24] And for this seems to stand the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of Augustine in the book of Prosper's *Sentences*,[^25] and it is placed in *De consecratione*, distinction 2, *Hoc est*, where it is said thus, that the sacrifice of the altar "is made up of two things",[^26] and consists, namely, of "the visible [species]{g:species} of the elements and the invisible flesh and blood of Christ, namely of the sacrament and the [reality of the sacrament]{g:res-sacramenti}, just as the [person]{g:persona} of Christ is constituted of man and of God; therefore just as Christ is true God and true man, so it is with this sacrifice."

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 217.23: Taken thus, 'to be' does not imply identity.
- 218.29: The difficulty is as to the virtue by which the host becomes a sacrament.
- 219.1: Here we must beware of imposing unscriptural terms.
- 219.27: Even these terms e. g. transsubstantiation are capable of a right meaning.
- 220.13: This right meaning is in all ways more fitting. It allows us to find in the essence of bread a subject for transsubstantiation.
- 221.11: 4. argument: Impanation is the most devout theory; and it corresponds to the Word's assumption of manhood.

## Notes

[^1]: **T.** *Irregulariter*, against custom, stands in the print; ABD read *regulariter*, "as a rule", which gives the easier sense (the whole community regularly so understanding). The print construes as a supposition contrary to present usage and is kept.
[^2]: **C.** *Locucionem unam ut aliam*: "this sign is the body of Christ" (granted, sacramentally) against "this bread becomes numerically the same as the body" (not granted).
[^3]: **S/T.** Luke 8:44 Vulgate: *tetigit fimbriam vestimenti eius*; the print has *Christi*, and Loserth notes that E rightly reads *eius* (218.9). Douay wording, the print's "Christ's" kept.
[^4]: **C.** The rejoinder's antecedent (the bread Christ took became his body) is what the modern doctors deny; the faithful man grants the consequent in the tropical meaning and denies numerical identity. ABC omit *si* before *replicatur*; D reads *post consecracionem*.
[^5]: **G.** *Virtus* is the bread's power (the sense of *virtus* as a faculty), not a moral virtue; Loserth's side-note says "virtue".
[^6]: **C.** *Species lucis*: a species of light, the optical *species* by which light propagates, founded in its source; not the sacramental *species* (¶10).
[^7]: **S.** Ambrose on the conversion of the bread into the body (*De sacramentis* IV.4.14–16, PL 16, 440–441: *ut sint quae erant, et in aliud commutentur*; cf. *De mysteriis* 9.52, PL 16, 407) (LEDGER K07h-1).
[^8]: **S.** Thomas on the body's presence *per conversionem panis*: *ST* III q. 75 a. 2 co. (*non possit aliter corpus Christi incipere esse de novo in hoc sacramento nisi per conversionem substantiae panis in ipsum*), cf. a. 4 co. (LEDGER K07h-2).
[^9]: **S.** "Gathered … in his name": Matt. 18:20 ("gathered together in my name"). ABE read *conversetur* for *conversis* (219.15).
[^10]: **T.** *Non corporaliter*: C reads *corporaliter* in the text and *formaliter* in the margin (219.19).
[^11]: **C.** *Nota fidelitas*: a known point of fidelity, i.e. of the faith held by the faithful; the bracketed word supplies what the abstract implies. D reads *ydemptificacionem* for *ydemptitatem* (219.25).
[^12]: **C.** The pope is "created" with a new name at his enthronement and "creates" those he promotes: a change of status on the same essence, which the superiors already call more than a transubstantiation. D reads *tronisacione*.
[^13]: **S.** Ambrose, *De paenitentia* II; Loserth: "cap. VII (?)", "very loosely quoted". The words are not found in the work (II c. 7 is PL 16, 510–512, on Lazarus); the nearest is I.17.95 (PL 16, 496), where the flesh's *interitus* means *non consummatam absumptionem carnis … sed castigationem* (LEDGER K07h-3).
[^14]: **C.** *Conversus*: the man converted to Christ, a member of him, is said to be Christ himself; the alternative construal "that Christ himself is converted" does not fit the Ambrose quoted. E reads *fit*, and *sit* in the margin (220.6).
[^15]: **S.** Augustine, *De paenitentia*: Loserth's reference is *Opp.* V, 2, 1363 (a sermon on penance in the Maurist vol. V); it is *Sermo* 351.2.2, *De utilitate agendae poenitentiae* I (PL 39, 1537), verbatim: *nullus transit ad Christum, ut incipiat esse quod non erat, nisi eum poeniteat fuisse quod erat* (LEDGER K07h-4).
[^16]: **G/C.** *Conveniencia* "agreement" (its row): this meaning agrees with the signists' own principles, which is how the argument runs; Loserth's side-note reads it as "more fitting". *Secta signorum*: the sect of the signs; ABC read *huius signorum*.
[^17]: **C.** *Inconveniencium*: absurdities in argument, the CONTEXTUAL row's first sense.
[^18]: **C.** *Quam* takes up *transsubstanciacionem*: the signists posit the change with no subject that remains through it, which Wyclif gives the bread's essence. ABCE read *subiectari*.
[^19]: **C.** *In quinque evangelistis*: the four gospels with Paul (1 Cor. 11:24), who relates the institution. *Veritatem* is Christ.
[^20]: **S.** The decretal is not named; Innocent III's *Cum Marthae* (X 3.41.6), cited at 223.32, is the likely one: there the added wine is *accidentibus prioris vini commixtum corpori, quod sub eis latet*, and *cedit quippe natura miraculo* (Friedberg II, 640) (LEDGER K07h-5).
[^21]: **S.** Augustine's determination that an accident cannot be without a subject is not cited by place here; it is that of ch. 3 (72.3–73.18): *De quantitate animae* 4.6, *Soliloquia* II.12.22–13.23 and *Ep.* 187.6.18 (LEDGER K07h-6).
[^22]: **C.** *Existente* agrees with *corpore*: the bread's body is not present in the faithful man's consideration. *Ad quod* most likely takes up that act of consideration's object, Christ's body in heaven (next sentence); it may also be read of the *actus*. D reads *hostia sacramenti* at 221.9.
[^23]: **C.** *Circumpalpitat* (from *palpare*): gropes around. *Rimam vacuam*: an empty chink, the figure of 216.21; ABDE read *vacuandum*.
[^24]: **S/C.** John 1:14, "the Word was made flesh"; Wyclif's *caro fit Deus* turns the verse round, as the impanators' analogy needs (flesh into God, bread into flesh).
[^25]: **S.** Gratian's inscription of c. 48, *Augustinus in libro sententiarum Prosperi*; the sentence is not in Prosper's *Sententiae* (PL 51) but is Lanfranc's, *De corpore et sanguine Domini* 10 (PL 150, 421A: *Hoc est namque quod dicimus … sacrificium scilicet Ecclesiae duobus confici, duobus constare … sicut Christi persona … constat et conficitur Deo et homine*) (LEDGER K07h-7).
[^26]: **S/G.** *De cons.* D. 2 c. 48 *Hoc est* (Loserth: *Decreti tertia pars*, dist. II, cap. XLVIII; the modern form is ours) (LEDGER K07h-8). *Conficitur* here is "is made up", the general sense of *conficere*, not "consecrated". ABC read *sacramentum* (221.25) and *visibile* (221.26); D omits *et*, E reads *et Deo* (221.29).
