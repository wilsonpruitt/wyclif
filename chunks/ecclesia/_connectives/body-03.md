# sed/autem extract 03: chunks 06-e–09-f

### 06-e ¶1 · 130.18–131.28  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 130] Unde si quis zelans pro profectu sancte matris ecclesie, habens in animo scintillam virtutis constancie, posset dicere quod tyriaca est contra exactores huiusmodi, extorquentes sine recompensa plus debita bona pauperum sancte matris ecclesie ex titulo quod sunt membra principalia eiusdem matris sine macula vel ruga, qui possunt temptacionibus et tribulacionibus adversancium concuti **sed** non superari a membris diaboli vel submergi, ut dicunt iura canonica de sancta matre ecclesia: ideo ex ore proprio debent potentes mundi ab eis requirere quod ostendant secundum eadem iura procuratorium sufficiens in fructu operis quod sunt illi quibus bona sancte matris ecclesie sunt debita vel donata, quia dist. XLª scribitur: Non sanctorum filii sunt qui tenent loca sanctorum, **sed** [p. 131] qui exercent opera eorum. Et ista tyriaca obtunderet cauterium rubiginosum diaboli quod non gracia sanacionis **sed** gracia spoliacionis apponitur membris Christi. Conceditur ergo quod tam honor quam stipendium sunt impendenda Christi sacerdotibus, **sed** oportet eos ex operibus dare probabilem evidenciam quod sunt tales, cum nemo contra conscienciam daret bona sancte matris ecclesie membris diaboli; nam secundum leges humanas qui dat pseudoprocuratori debitum creditoris, tenetur in penam negligencie, et si perdiderit datum creditori tribuere quod est suum. Multo plus creditor matris sue, et specialiter si negligit discernere utrum exactor sit fidelis procurator sancte matris ecclesie, quod iudicabit secundum opera et regulas scripture. Nam liquido constat fidelibus quod decime, oblaciones et alie private elemosine debentur sancte matri ecclesie. Qua fronte ergo excomunicaret infructuosus operibus evangelicis subiectos simplices, nisi dent sibi bona sancte matris ecclesie, cum sit evidens ipsum non esse de illa, **sed** in illa, tamquam eius hostem precipuum et ypocrisi atque mendaciis eius infidelissimum proditorem. De isto inferius. Sexto arguitur contra dicta in racione secunda; videtur enim, si Christus desponsavit matrem ecclesiam cuius fuit filius, quod filius foret senior matre sua, sicut est honorabilior, melior et formalior quam est illa, et sic ydemptificaret sibi omne membrum ecclesie (ut sepe innuitur in sanctis doctoribus et scriptura).

ENGLISH: Hence, if anyone zealous for the advancement of holy mother [church]{g:ecclesia}, having in his mind
a spark of the virtue of constancy, could say that there is a theriac against exactors of this kind,
who extort without recompense, beyond what is due, the goods of the poor of holy mother church, on the
[title]{g:titulus} that they are principal members of the same mother, without spot or wrinkle, who
can be shaken by the temptations and tribulations of adversaries but not overcome by the members of
the devil, nor submerged, as the canon [laws]{g:ius} say of holy mother church:[^1] therefore, out of
their own mouth, the powerful of the world ought to require of them that they show, according to the
same laws, a sufficient [proctor's]{g:procurator} mandate in the fruit of their work, that they are
those to whom the goods of holy mother church are due or given; for in distinction 40 it is written:
"They are not the sons of the saints who hold the places of the saints, but [131] those who do their
works."[^2] And this theriac would blunt the devil's rusty cautery, which is applied to the members of
Christ not by way of healing but by way of despoiling.[^3] It is granted, therefore, that both honour
and stipend are to be paid to Christ's [priests]{g:sacerdos}; but they must give, from their works,
probable [evidence]{g:evidencia} that they are such, since no one against conscience would give the
goods of holy mother church to members of the devil; for according to human laws he who gives a
creditor's due to a false proctor is bound, as the penalty of negligence, even if he has lost what
was given, to render to the creditor what is his.[^4] Much more [is] the debtor of his mother [so
bound], and especially if he neglects to discern whether the exactor is a faithful proctor of holy
mother church, which he will judge according to works and the rules of scripture.[^5] For it is
plainly evident to the faithful that [tithes]{g:decime}, oblations and other private
[alms]{g:elemosina} are owed to holy mother church. With what face, then, would one who is unfruitful
in gospel works excommunicate simple subjects unless they give him the goods of holy mother church,
since it is evident that he is not of her but in her, as her chief enemy and, by his hypocrisy and
lies, her most faithless traitor?[^6] Of this below. Sixthly, it is argued against what was said in
the second reason; for it seems, if Christ espoused mother church, whose son he was, that the son
would be older than his mother, as he is more honourable, better and more formal than she is; and so
he would identify every member of the church with himself (as is often hinted in the holy doctors and
scripture).[^7]

### 06-e ¶2 · 131.29–132.29  (6 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 131] Hic dicitur quod Christus desponsavit matrem suam que est totalis ecclesia non secundum se totam, **sed** secundum synagogam ex qua Christus secundum carnem prodiit, et sic reliquit patrem et matrem (ut sancti doctores exponunt dictum suum Matthei XIX°, 5) [p. 132] et adhesit uxori sue, **sed** illud dictum oportet sane intelligi. Nam Christus assumens illud quod prius non erat non perdidit vel desiit esse illud quod erat, quia manet absolute necessario eternaliter plenus Deus, **sed** reliquit assumendo humanitatem esse solum Deus, cum fuit humanitas que est aliud quam deitas. Et sic ubi prius fuit summe beatus cum patre stante illa beatitudine cum patre assumpsit miseriam fraternam condicionis humane, et sic reliquit matrem synagogam, non deserendo membra predestinata in veteri testamento, **sed** cum eis assumendo ecclesiam gencium, (ut docet Apostolus Romanorum IX°, 24). Verumptamen multos carnaliter Iudeos reliquit tamquam ineptos ad fabricam sancte matris ecclesie et secundum dotem novam adhesit uxori sue que fit plurimum ex gentibus, et sic honorat matrem ecclesiam tamquam ipso priorem et superiorem eo quod est duarum naturarum creatarum utraque. Et secundum corpus erat subditus parentibus suis carnalibus, ergo multo magis parentibus suis spiritualibus, hoc est, sibi et sancte matri ecclesie. Sicut enim tam secundum corpus quam animam erat subditus trinitati, sic secundum corpus erat subditus sibi secundum animam. Verumptamen videtur quod non sit senior quam universalis ecclesia **sed** econtra, cum ipsa sit coetanea cum mundo et senium dicit rei mortalis periodum naturalem. **Sed** est antiquior quam creatura alia, cum sit antiquus dierum, et sic est honorabilior et melior quam mater ecclesia, proporcionaliter ut deitas excedit creaturam.

ENGLISH: Here [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that Christ espoused his mother, which is the total church, not as a
whole, but as to the synagogue, from which Christ came forth according to the flesh; and so he left
father and mother (as the holy doctors expound his saying, Matthew 19:5) [132] and cleaved to his
wife; but that saying must be soundly understood.[^8] For Christ, assuming what he was not before, did
not lose or cease to be what he was, for he remains with absolute necessity eternally full God; but by
assuming humanity he left being God alone, since he was humanity, which is other than the
[Godhead]{g:deitas}.[^9] And so, whereas before he was supremely blessed with the Father, that
blessedness with the Father remaining, he assumed the brotherly misery of the human condition; and so
he left his mother, the synagogue, not by deserting the [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} members in the
old testament, but by assuming with them the church of the gentiles (as the Apostle teaches, Romans
9:24).[^10] Nevertheless he left many carnal Jews as unfit for the building of holy mother church, and
according to the new dowry cleaved to his wife, who is made for the most part from the gentiles; and
so he honours mother church as prior to himself and superior, in that she is each of the two created
natures.[^11] And according to the body he was subject to his carnal parents; therefore much more to
his spiritual parents, that is, to himself and to holy mother church. For just as, both according to
body and according to soul, he was subject to the Trinity, so according to the body he was subject to
himself according to the soul.[^12] Nevertheless it seems that he is not older than the universal
church, but the reverse, since she is coeval with the world, and "old age" means the natural period
of a mortal thing. But he is more ancient than any other creature, since he is the ancient of days;
and so he is more honourable and better than mother church, in proportion as the Godhead exceeds the
creature.[^13]

### 06-e ¶3 · 132.30–133.25  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 132] **Sed** difficultas est, utrum Christus secundum humanitatem sit forma ecclesie. Et videtur mihi quod sic; nam sicut forma vivificat et movet corpus ad actus [p. 133] suos, limitans sibi et partibus suis crementum et alia accidentia naturalia, sic se habet nedum divinitas Christi extrinsecus **sed** humanitas eius intrinsecus ad sanctam matrem ecclesiam; unde blasphemum est cogitare quod subest potestati humane posse celebrare divorcium inter Christum et matrem suam, sicut carnaliter ymaginamur de defectu in carnali coniugio. Ecclesia enim est virgo spiritualiter intelligendo sicut tota multitudo celestium civium agnum sequencium (ut dicitur Apocalyps. XIV°, 1), unde tales virgines possunt post corrupcionem recuperare virginitatem, sicut non contingit de virginitate carnali, quamvis semper possunt esse virgines. Nam eo ipso, quo predestinatus habet celibatum, recipiens a sponso ecclesie semen fidei, gignendo filium Dei, est virgo, sponsa Christi, et per consequens, soror et frater eius germanus, et per idem, mater eius, cum gignit Christum spiritualiter ex semine sponsi in anima conversa ad Christum. Et istam materiam pulchre tangit decretum ex doctrina Augustini, ut patet XXVII q. prima: Nupciarum, ubi dicitur quod ecclesia tota virgo est, cum Apostolus IIª Corinthiorum XI°, 2 dicit: Aptavi vos uni viro virginem castam exhibere Christo. Et ab illo viro cum vivit perpetuo, non licet recedere spiritualiter fornicando, ideo coniugatus manet spiritualiter virgo. **Sed**

ENGLISH: But there is a difficulty, whether Christ according to his humanity is the form of the church. And it
seems to me that he is: for just as the form quickens the body and moves it to its [133] acts,
[assigning]{g:limitare} to it and its parts growth and the other natural accidents, so not only
Christ's [divinity]{g:divinitas} from without but his humanity from within stands to holy mother
church; hence it is blasphemous to think that it lies within human power to be able to celebrate a
divorce between Christ and his mother, as we carnally imagine [it] from a defect in carnal
marriage.[^14] For the church is a virgin, understanding it spiritually, as is the whole multitude of
the heavenly citizens following the Lamb (as is said, Apocalypse 14:1); hence such virgins can
recover virginity after corruption, which does not happen with carnal virginity, although they can
always be virgins.[^15] For by the very fact that a predestinate [person] has celibacy, receiving from
the [bridegroom]{g:sponsus} of the church the seed of faith, by begetting the Son of God he is a
virgin, a [bride]{g:sponsa} of Christ, and consequently his sister and own brother, and by the same
token his mother, since he begets Christ spiritually from the bridegroom's seed in a soul converted
to Christ.[^16] And this matter the decree touches finely, from the teaching of Augustine, as is clear
in [Causa] 27, question 1, *Nuptiarum*, where it is said that the whole church is a virgin, since the
Apostle, 2 Corinthians 11:2, says: "I have fitted you to one husband, that I may present you as a
chaste virgin to Christ."[^17] And since that husband lives for ever, it is not lawful to depart from
him by fornicating spiritually; therefore one who is married remains spiritually a virgin. But …[^18]

### 06-f ¶1 · 133.26–136.8  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 133] dubium est tactum in quarto argumento de unitate numerali ecclesie, utrum Christus et quodlibet membrum ecclesie sit eadem ecclesia in numero, et videtur quod sic ex triplici conveniencia numerali. Primo, inquam, videtur quod quantumcunque partem anima hominis adequate actuaverit, quantumcunque materia corporea [p. 134] varietur, manet continue idem homo, ut hic supponitur. Quare ergo non sic de homine ecclesie cuius forma est Christus manens in eternum? Secundum exemplum naturale est quod anima cuicunque membro copulata fuerit remanet tam anima quam membrum eadem persona hominis. Quare ergo non sic de Christo et ecclesia, sic quod quodlibet membrum eius sit Christus, ut sonant multe auctoritates? Tercio videtur quod sicut Christus mansit eadem persona in numero pro tempore mortis sue, tam in sepulchro mortuus quam in inferno vivus, ita videtur eandem personam ecclesie disiunctive regnantem in celo et peregrinantem in mundo, et sic Christus assumpsit ypostatice personam ecclesie peregrinantis in beata virgine. Hic oportet prudenter intelligere unitatem persone quam sancti exprimunt ex scriptura, ut patet de octo generibus unitatum quas exprimit beatus Bernhardus, ut recitatur in proximo capitulo in responsione ad tercium argumentum. Et tunc loquendo ad sensum doctorum concedenda videtur sentencia cuiuslibet horum trium argumentorum. Nam primo concedi potest quod manet continue eadem ecclesia que est collectim omnes predestinati et singillatim singuli, ac singulus eorundem omnes uniti in Christo capite, **sed** tunc oportet intelligere personam ut caput, basim tocius compositi et quodlibet membrum suum confuse tamquam absorptum in Christo absconditum, sic quod ecclesia Christi dicat Christum cum quocunque fideli sibi incorporatum. Si enim papa dicitur ecclesia propter virtutem quam influit in ecclesiam, [p. 135] ut dicitur XXIVª, q. prima: Quodcunque, et VIIª, q. prima Scire debes, multo magis primus papa admirabilis dominus Jesus Christus. Iterum, si procedentes carnaliter ab Adam primo dicuntur fuisse ipse in quo originaliter peccaverunt, multo evidencius descendentes a secundo Adam dicentur fore secundus Adam in quo finaliter requiescent. Nam semen fidei quod est veritas est magis communicativum quam semen corporeum. Iterum, si combustibile vel ignibile ut lignum, carbo vel ferrum dicitur post ignicionem vulgariter esse ignis, cum alium ignem non percipimus hic nobiscum, multo magis spiritualiter comestus a Christo et plene digestus ab eo usque ad quartam digestionem que est glorificacio; ille ergo cui vivere Christus est, sicut sunt omnes sancti qui vivunt Deum: manifestum videtur ad hunc sensum quod sunt vita eorum que Christus est. Et patet quomodo verum est quod benefaciendo vel persequendo membra Christi, ut sic, taliter respicitur ipse Christus (ut patet Matthei XXV°, et Act. IX°, 5). Patet secundo quod martires nunquam possent sustinuisse tot passiones et fecisse tot mirabilia nisi per incorporantem et assimilantem membra capiti loquentem et operantem in eis dominum Jesum Christum. Et patet tercio causa quare Christus precepit suis declinare a mundo et sensibilibus et proicere totam scienciam, curam et affeccionem in ipsum, quia aliter non assimilantur sibi, quia nichil plus obstat assimilacioni cordi, capiti et corpori ecclesie quod est Christus, quam mundana affeccio. Ipse enim sepe in evangelio vocatur regnum celorum, corpus quod sequuntur aquile, et vita omnibus ipsum viventibus. Et patet [p. 136] quomodo dicendum est ad secundam racionem et terciam, concedendo sentenciam catholicam, in qua manuducunt, et cavendo diligenter ab errore intellectus et scandalo auditorii. Posset enim seductus credere quod omnis christianus foret idem personaliter cum Christo secundum corpus et animam, ita quod non forent dispares persone essencie vel nature, quod foret nimis erroneum.

ENGLISH: There is a difficulty, touched on in the fourth argument, about the numerical unity of the [church]{g:ecclesia}: whether Christ and each member of the church are the same church in number. And it seems that they are, from a threefold numerical agreement.[^1] First, I say, it seems that, however great the part that the soul of a man has adequately actuated, and however much the bodily matter [134] varies, the same man remains continuously, as is supposed here. Why then is it not so with the man of the church, whose form is Christ, abiding for ever? The second natural example is that, to whatever member the soul has been joined, both the soul and the member remain the same person of the man. Why then is it not so with Christ and the church, so that each member of it is Christ, as many authorities sound?[^2] Thirdly, it seems that, just as Christ remained the same person in number for the time of his death, both dead in the sepulchre and alive in hell, so it seems that the same person of the church [remains], disjunctively reigning in heaven and [on pilgrimage]{g:peregrinari} in the world; and so Christ assumed hypostatically the person of the pilgrim church in the blessed Virgin.[^3] Here one must understand prudently the unity of person which the saints express from scripture, as is clear from the eight kinds of unity which blessed Bernard expresses, as is recited in the preceding chapter in the reply to the third argument.[^4] And then, speaking according to the sense of the doctors, the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of each of these three arguments seems to be granted. For, first, it can be granted that there remains continuously the same church, which is collectively all the [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} and severally each one, and each one of them [is] all, united in Christ the [head]{g:caput}; but then one must understand the person as the head, the base of the whole composite, and each of its members [indistinctly]{g:confuse}, as absorbed, hidden in Christ, so that "the church of Christ" means Christ with every faithful [person] incorporated in him.[^5] For if the [pope]{g:papa} is called the church on account of the power that he makes [flow]{g:influere} into the church, [135] as is said in [Causa] 24, question 1, *Quodcunque*, and [Causa] 7, question 1, *Scire debes*, much more the first pope, the wonderful Lord Jesus Christ.[^6] Again, if those who proceed carnally from the first Adam are said to have been him in whom they originally sinned, much more evidently will those descending from the second Adam be said to be the second Adam, in whom they will finally rest. For the seed of faith, which is truth, is more communicative than bodily seed.[^7] Again, if a combustible or ignitable thing, such as wood, coal or iron, is commonly said after ignition to be fire, since we perceive no other fire here with us, much more [is he Christ who is] spiritually eaten by Christ and fully digested by him, up to the fourth [digestion]{g:digestio}, which is glorification; he, therefore, for whom to live is Christ, as are all the saints who live God: … it seems manifest in this sense that they are their life, which is Christ.[^8] And it is clear how it is true that, in doing good to or persecuting the members of Christ, as such, Christ himself is thus regarded (as is clear, Matthew 25, and Acts 9:5). It is clear, secondly, that the martyrs could never have endured so many sufferings and done so many wonders except through the Lord Jesus Christ, incorporating and assimilating the members to the head, speaking and working in them. And it is clear, thirdly, why Christ commanded his own to turn aside from the world and from [perceptible things]{g:sensibilis}, and to cast all their knowledge, care and affection upon him: because otherwise they are not assimilated to him; for nothing hinders assimilation to the heart, the head and the body of the church, which is Christ, more than worldly affection.[^9] For he is often called in the gospel the [kingdom]{g:regnum} of heaven, the body which the eagles follow, and the life for all who live him. And it is clear [136] how one must answer the second and the third reason: by granting the catholic teaching to which they lead by the hand, and by guarding diligently against error of understanding and the scandal of the audience. For one led astray could believe that every Christian would be the same in person with Christ according to body and soul, so that they would not be disparate persons, [essences]{g:essencia} or natures; which would be too erroneous.[^10]

### 06-f ¶2 · 136.9–136.30  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 136] Unde ne darem scandalum scole, distuli istam materiam. Aliquis enim est participative Christus ut quodlibet membrum eius, et aliquis personaliter ut solum ipsum caput, et illi qui non capiunt istam distinccionem, non capiunt sensum huius scripture Qui adheret Deo, unus spiritus est, et quomodo vinculum caritatis vel gracie est forcius quam vinculum naturale, ut dicit Augustinus in libro De Vera Religione XCI°, et tactum est cap. proximo in solucione tercii argumenti. Et patet quomodo intelligendi sunt doctores sancti, concedentes istam sentenciam, ut Augustinus, Crisostomus et Bernardus cum aliis qui sapiunt Deum et legem scripture. Et patet secundo quomodo tolli debent argumenta sophistica in ista materia, ut hii instant, si beati singuli sunt persona Christi, tunc sunt personaliter ipse Christus, et sic tantum Christus est homo, et omnis homo est omnis homo, cum aliis arguciis multipliciter in materia de trinitate. **Sed** hii non considerant quod multa possunt esse eadem persona communicative, participative vel secundum esse intelligibile, licet non personaliter, et patet quod consequencie multiplicate non procedunt.

ENGLISH: Hence, so as not to give scandal to the schools, I put off this matter. For someone is Christ by participation, as each member of his, and someone personally, as the head itself alone; and those who do not grasp this distinction do not grasp the sense of this scripture, "He who is joined to God is one spirit", nor how the bond of [charity]{g:caritas} or of grace is stronger than the natural bond, as Augustine says in the book *On True Religion*, 91, and as was touched on in the preceding chapter in the solution of the third argument.[^11] And it is clear how the holy doctors are to be understood who grant this teaching, such as Augustine, Chrysostom and Bernard, with others who savour God and the law of scripture. And it is clear, secondly, how the sophistical arguments in this matter must be removed, as these [men] [object]{g:instatur}: if the blessed, each of them, are the person of Christ, then they are personally Christ himself, and so only Christ is a man, and every man is every man, with other [quibbles]{g:argucie} in manifold ways in the matter of the Trinity. But these do not consider that many things can be the same person communicatively, by participation, or according to intelligible [being]{g:esse}, though not personally; and it is clear that the multiplied [inferences]{g:consequencia} do not proceed.[^12]

### 07-a ¶1 · 142.3–143.30  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 142] Convenimus ex mandato domini regis ad dicendum secundum videre nostrum veritatem in casu nobis exposito ad honorem Dei, ad profectum ecclesie et ad stabilimentum prosperum regni nostri, et hec tria in tractatu nostro se invicem consequuntur. Casus **autem** nobis expositus in compendio fuit iste: Duo viri armorum, homines legii regis nostri fuerunt inobedientes regi et legibus suis, et per consequens regno, in redempcione unius captivi Hyspaniensis, filii comitis de Dene in cuius custodia et redempcione rex noster secundum leges suas debuit habere precipuum interesse. Unde quia noluerunt reddere regi nostro captivi custodiam per quem captivum regnum redimeret multos milites suos in Hispania captivatos, mandati sunt per regem et regnum tuciori regis carcere mancipari. **Sed** secundo superaddentes inobediencie priori exierunt contra mandatum regis violente de carcere et prostrato custode carceris intrarunt septa Westmonasterii, in quibus vendicarunt omnino eximi a subieccione regis ex illius ecclesie libertate. Et tercio, [p. 143] minando quod exirent de terra nostra coniuncti nostris capitalibus inimicis, dicuntur arma sibi parasse, literas salvi conductus ab hostibus procurasse et consilium domini nostri regis tamquam falsum publice difidasse. Istis suppositis cum multis aliis que nunc esset nimis longum disserere, queritur utrum licet regi et regno suo in casu isto facere ut dicti captivi de Westmonasterio extrahantur. Et dicunt canoniste et civiliste tam regni nostri quam imperii quod, supposita veritate casus, non obstante quocunque privilegio Westmonasterii licet eos extrahere. Theologi **autem** dicunt concorditer quod, supposita veritate casus expositi, licet eos extrahere, servando tamen eos in vita et membris incolumes. In isto **autem** articulo intendo condicionaliter ex supposicione veritatis casus narrati procedere, et nec invasionem occisi nec suam occisionem propter ignoranciam occulte circumstancie comprobare. Quibus suppositis conclusio theologorum suadetur tripliciter. Pro primo argumento suppono primo legem domini Exodi XXI° capitulo, 14, datam Moysi: Si quis de industria occiderit proximum suum et per insidias, ab altari meo evellas eum, ut occidatur, ubi notatur primo ex natura condicionalis quod lex ista debet esse perpetua. Secundo notatur quod mora talis in sanctuario non debet esse ex aliquo privilegio perpetuo constipata, ideo dicit evellas eum. Tercio notatur quod summus gradus sanctitatis loci non debet aliquem talem in immunitate ecclesiastica conservare, cum ab altari qui est locus dignissimus ecclesie talis homicida evelli debeat.

ENGLISH: We have come together at the command of our [lord]{g:dominus} the king to speak the truth, as we see it, on the case set before us, to the honour of God, to the profit of the [church]{g:ecclesia} and to the prosperous establishment of our [kingdom]{g:regnum}; and these three follow one another in our treatise.[^1] And the case set before us was, in brief, this: two men-at-arms, liege men of our king, were disobedient to the king and to his laws, and consequently to the kingdom, in the ransom of a Spanish captive, the son of the count of Dene, in whose custody and ransom our king, according to his laws, ought to have had the chief interest. Hence, because they would not render to our king the custody of the captive, through which captive the kingdom might ransom many of its [knights]{g:milites} taken captive in Spain, they were ordered by the king and the kingdom to be committed to the king's safer prison. But, secondly, adding to their former disobedience, they went out of prison violently, against the king's command, and, having struck down the keeper of the prison, entered the precincts of Westminster, in which they claimed to be wholly exempt from subjection to the king by the liberty of that church.[^2] And thirdly, [143] threatening that they would leave our land, joined to our chief enemies, they are said to have prepared arms for themselves, to have procured letters of safe-conduct from the enemy, and publicly to have defied the council of our lord the king as false. These things supposed, with many others which it would now be too long to discuss, it is asked whether it is lawful for the king and his kingdom, in this case, to cause the said captives to be taken out of Westminster.[^3] And the canonists and civilians, both of our kingdom and of the empire, say that, the truth of the case being supposed, notwithstanding any privilege of Westminster, it is lawful to take them out. But the theologians say with one accord that, the truth of the case set forth being supposed, it is lawful to take them out, yet keeping them unharmed in life and limb.[^4] And in this article I intend to proceed conditionally, [on supposition]{g:ex supposicione} of the truth of the case narrated, and, from ignorance of a hidden circumstance, to approve neither the assault on the man who was killed nor his killing.[^5] These things supposed, the [conclusion]{g:conclusio} of the theologians is argued for in three ways. For the first argument I suppose, first, the law of the Lord given to Moses, Exodus 21:14: "If a man kill his neighbour on set purpose and by lying in wait, take him away from my altar, that he may be killed"; where it is noted, first, from the nature of the conditional, that this law ought to be perpetual. Secondly, it is noted that such a stay in sanctuary ought not to be made fast by any perpetual privilege; hence it says "take him away". Thirdly, it is noted that the highest degree of the holiness of a place ought not to keep any such man in ecclesiastical immunity, since such a homicide must be taken away from the altar, which is the worthiest place of the church.[^6]

### 07-a ¶2 · 143.31–144.22  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 143] Secundo supponatur factum sapientis regis Salomonis III° Regum 2° cap., ubi fecit occidi Joab ad cornu [p. 144] altaris in tabernaculo domini, non obstante quod in exercitu patris sui David plus aliis militibus laboravit. Cuius occisionis doctores triplicem causam eliciunt ex scriptura. Primo, quia occidit Abner et Amasan tempore pacis ex vulpina industria, et tunc non gauderet immunitate ecclesie ex lege iam proximo allegata. Secundo, quia conspiravit cum Adonia et Abiathar contra regem. Et tercio precipue, quia rex timuit sibi quod Joab superstes in regno pacem populi perturbaret. Ideo subditur: David **autem** et semini eius et domui et trono eius sit pax in eternum a domino. Ex istis tercio sic arguitur ex lege Dei quam non est fas homini inpugnare, licet nedum de altari Dei propter timorem perturbacionis regni fugitivum ad occidendum extrahere, **sed** ibidem eum occidere, ut patet ex dictis; **sed** infinitum minus esset fugitivum salvum in vita et membris extrahere; ergo, posita vehementi suspicione regis et sui consilii de prodicione vel perturbacione regni per tales fugitivos futura, licet eos secundum formam predictam extrahere et in tuta custodia conservare.

ENGLISH: Secondly, let the deed of the wise king Solomon be supposed, 3 Kings 2, where he had Joab killed at the horn [144] of the altar in the tabernacle of the Lord, notwithstanding that in the army of his father David he had laboured more than the other knights.[^7] Of this killing the doctors draw a threefold cause from scripture. First, because he killed Abner and Amasa in time of peace with a fox's cunning, and so would not enjoy the immunity of the church, by the law just cited. Secondly, because he conspired with Adonias and Abiathar against the king. And thirdly and chiefly, because the king feared for himself that Joab, surviving in the kingdom, would disturb the peace of the people. Hence it is added: "But to David and his seed, and his house, and to his throne, be peace for ever from the Lord."[^8] From these, thirdly, it is argued thus, from the law of God, which it is not right for man to impugn: it is lawful not only to drag a fugitive from God's altar to be killed, for fear of disturbance of the kingdom, but to kill him there, as is clear from what has been said; now it would be infinitely less to take a fugitive out safe in life and limb; therefore, granted a vehement suspicion on the part of the king and his council of treason or disturbance of the kingdom to come through such fugitives, it is lawful to take them out in the aforesaid form and to keep them in safe custody.[^9]

### 07-a ¶3 · 144.23–145.18  (2 sed/autem)

[p. 145] Nec valet secundo quod Joab commisit peccata gravia, que plus movebant quam suspicio de perturbacione futura, tum quia sic est in casu nostro de superba eorum inobediencia, tum eciam quia certum videtur ex textu quod timor perturbacionis regni Salomonis fuit causa occidendi Joab precipua, quia ignoravit si priora crimina sint dimissa; ideo punicio quattuor personarum de quibus ibi fit mentio fuit precipue ad pacificandum in posterum regnum suum. Nec valet tercio dicere quod expectaretur quousque probata fuerit regni perturbacio sive prodicio, quia illa probacio foret nimis diutina contra prudenciam precavendi regno pericula; **sed** hoc videtur probabile quod non inferretur eis lesio corporalis, antequam culpa eorum fuerit notoria vel secundum leges patrie probata legitime. ENGLISH: Nor does it avail to say that the old law does not bind men of the new law: both because the laws of the church found their [rights]{g:ius} on this old law, as is clear of the four who shall not enjoy the immunity of the church (it is not lawful, therefore, to destroy the principles of the church's law); and because the justice of our deed is argued from the justice of the divine law by the topic from the greater; and also because then there would not remain to us [churchmen]{g:ecclesiastici} a [title]{g:titulus} for claiming [tithes]{g:decime}, since the payment of tithes is [authorized]{g:autorizare} to us not by the new law but by the old.[^10] [145] Nor does it avail, secondly, [to say] that Joab committed grave sins, which moved [Solomon] more than the suspicion of future disturbance: both because it is so in our case with their proud disobedience, and also because it seems certain from the text that fear of the disturbance of Solomon's kingdom was the chief cause of killing Joab, since he did not know whether the earlier crimes had been forgiven; hence the punishment of the four persons of whom mention is made there was chiefly to pacify his kingdom for the future.[^11] Nor does it avail, thirdly, to say that one should wait until the disturbance of the kingdom or the treason has been proved, for that proof would be too protracted, against the prudence of guarding the kingdom from dangers beforehand; but this seems probable, that bodily harm should not be inflicted on them before their guilt is notorious or legitimately proved according to the laws of the [homeland]{g:patria}. For it is one thing to condemn a man for his guilt, and another to keep him, vehemently suspected, in safe custody for the protection of the kingdom.[^12]

### 07-a ¶4 · 145.19–146.28  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 145] Quarta ficticia qua dicitur Salomonem in proposito illicite iudicasse non habet colorem, cum sic diceretur de opere legitimo aliorum quos sequimur ex auctoritate scripture. Cum enim scriptura tantum commendat sapienciam suam et non reprobat **sed** multiplici municione approbat illud factum, videtur ex sufficiencia scripture quod fuit factum legitimum. Sic enim supponunt leges humane bonitatem persone quousque probetur contrarium; immo omnes leges humane approbant iudicis inferioris iudicium quousque eius iniusticia convincatur. Secundo principaliter arguitur sic ad idem: Videtur quod quilibet legius homo regis Anglie tenetur tantum [p. 146] vel plus obedire sibi, sicut monachus abbati in quibuscunque que non sunt contraria legi Dei, **sed** monachus tenetur virtute obediencie et sub pena carceris obedire abbati in hiis que non sunt contraria legi Dei: ergo multo magis ad tantam obedienciam tenetur quilibet Anglicus suo regi specialiter in necessariis regimini regni sui. Ex quo sequitur quod servientes regis de sua privata familia tenentur parere sibi et suo sapienti consilio in huiusmodi licitis et honestis; **sed** mandatum est per regem et dictum consilium (sicut narratur) ut ad pacificandum regnum extrahant a Westmonasterio inobedienciarios predictos; ergo cum mandatum hoc vergit ad necessitatem regiminis regni sui, non obvians **sed** consonans legi Dei, sequitur quod dicti subditi tenentur in hoc parere monitis regis sui. Assumptum patet ex hoc quod obligacio regi est maior, cura regiminis regni est necessarior et pena delinquencium in regem est ex scriptura fundabilior. Nam beatus Petrus precipit christianis quod sint subiecti regibus Iª Petri II°, 13, et Apostolus precipit Rom. XIII°, 1 et Heb. XIII°, 17, Christi discipulis obedire prepositis, immo in veteri lege, quia Semei egressus est extra Jerusalem contra monita regis sui, ideo ex precepto sapientis Salomonis iustissime est occisus. Patet historia III° Reg. II° cap. Non **autem** inveniuntur tante evidencie ex scriptura quod monachus sub tanta pena parere debet abbati in monitis preter fidem.

ENGLISH: The fourth fiction, by which it is said that Solomon judged unlawfully in the matter in hand, has no [colour]{g:color}, since so it would be said of the lawful work of others whom we follow on the authority of scripture. For since scripture so commends his wisdom, and does not reprove but approves that deed with manifold defence, it seems from the sufficiency of scripture that it was a lawful deed. For so human laws presume the goodness of a person until the contrary is proved; indeed, all human laws approve the judgment of a lower judge until his injustice is proved against him.[^13] Secondly and principally, it is argued thus to the same [end]: it seems that any liege man of the king of England is bound to obey him as much [146] or more than a monk his abbot, in whatever things are not contrary to [God's law]{g:lex dei}; now a monk is bound, by virtue of obedience and under pain of prison, to obey his abbot in those things that are not contrary to God's law: therefore much more is every Englishman bound to such obedience to his king, especially in things necessary to the government of his kingdom.[^14] From which [it follows]{g:sequitur} that the king's servants of his private [household]{g:familia} are bound to obey him and his wise council in lawful and honest things of this kind; now it was commanded by the king and the said council (as is narrated) that, to pacify the kingdom, they should take the aforesaid disobedient men out of Westminster; therefore, since this command tends to the necessity of the government of his kingdom, not opposing but agreeing with God's law, it follows that the said subjects are bound in this to obey the [admonitions]{g:monita} of their king.[^15] [The assumption]{g:assumptum} is clear from this, that the obligation to the king is greater, the care of governing the kingdom is more necessary, and the punishment of offenders against the king is better grounded in scripture. For blessed Peter commands Christians to be subject to kings, 1 Peter 2:13, and the Apostle commands the disciples of Christ, Romans 13:1 and Hebrews 13:17, to obey [superiors]{g:prepositi}; indeed, in the old law, because Semei went out of Jerusalem against the admonitions of his king, therefore by the command of the wise Solomon he was most justly killed. The story is clear, 3 Kings 2. But such great [evidence]{g:evidencia} is not found in scripture that a monk must obey his abbot under so great a penalty in admonitions outside the faith.[^16]

### 07-b ¶1 · 146.29–147.24  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 146] Quod **autem** preceptum regis in hac parte non obviat legi Dei nec veris privilegiis Westmonasterii sic ostendo: Scrutetur homo totam scripturam et non inveniet quod pro debito, pro occisione iniusta, [p. 147] immo generaliter pro quocunque peccato debet pro fugus habere refugium, quia tunc Deus foveret proditorem proprium in suo crimine; quod non potest. Unde Deuteronomii XIX° et Josue XX°, ubi datur lex profugorum et constituuntur civitates refugii, limitatur quod solum occidentes nescii et inmunes criminis debent ibi habere refugium, ne a cognacione occisi effundatur sanguis innoxius, nec habitabant ibi perpetuo, **sed** Levite habebant custodiam sex civitatum refugii, nec acceptarent nisi insontes qui a casu preter intentum fratrem suum occiderint; illi **autem** non exibant civitatem nisi ad purgandum se in loco occisionis quod ignoranter occiderint fratrem suum usque ad mortem summi pontificis. Tunc enim redirent libere in domum propriam extra civitatem refugii; patet ista lex Numeri XXXV° et Josue XX°. Si ergo in tota lege nova et veteri (ubi diffuse tangitur ista materia) non limitatur locus refugii nisi insontibus qui casualiter fratrem suum occiderint, que foret in christianis ceca presumpcio sub colore pietatis defendere hostes Dei, regis et regni? Per hec patet responsio ad argumentum quod fundatur in privilegio concesso a rege monasterio supradicto.

ENGLISH: Now, that the king's command in this matter does not oppose [God's law]{g:lex dei}, nor the true privileges of Westminster, I show thus.[^1] Let a man search the whole of scripture, and he will not find that for debt, for unjust killing, [147] indeed in general for any sin whatever, a fugitive ought to have refuge; for then God would cherish his own traitor in his crime, which he cannot do.[^2] Hence in Deuteronomy 19 and Joshua 20, where the law of fugitives is given and the cities of refuge are appointed, it is [assigned]{g:limitare} that only those who kill unknowingly and are free of crime ought to have refuge there, lest innocent blood be shed by the kindred of the slain; nor did they dwell there for ever, but the Levites had the custody of the six cities of refuge, and were to accept none but the guiltless who had killed their brother by chance, beyond their intent; and these did not go out of the city, except to clear themselves at the place of the killing [of the charge, showing] that they had killed their brother unknowingly, until the death of the high priest. For then they would return freely to their own house outside the city of refuge; this law is clear in Numbers 35 and Joshua 20.[^3] If, therefore, in the whole law, new and old (where this matter is touched on at length), the place of refuge is assigned only to the guiltless who have killed their brother by chance, what blind presumption would it be in Christians, under [colour]{g:color} of piety, to defend the enemies of God, of the king and of the [kingdom]{g:regnum}? By these things the answer is clear to the argument that is founded on the privilege granted by the king to the aforesaid monastery.[^4]

### 07-b ¶2 · 147.25–148.24  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 147] Supponitur **autem** quod habeat privilegium sub isto tenore verborum quod liceat sibi tueri quoscunque fugitivos ex quacunque causa, eciam in causa lese regie maiestatis, adhuc totum istud est generalis concessio **sed** secundum leges hominum in generali concessione principis nunquam veniet speciale, nisi quod concederetur a principe in casu quo fuerit particulariter [p. 148] explicatum. Cum ergo non sit in potestate principis concedere quod vergeret ad Dei iniuriam, ad destruccionem regni sui, ad enervacionem regalie et legum per quas regeret regnum suum, patet quod illud suppositum in casu nobis exposito non includitur in illo privilegio generali. Quid queso foret magis ad Dei iniuriam quam facere domum suam speluncam latronum? aut quid foret magis destructivum regnorum quam protegere in nidis ad hoc deputatis hostes Dei ecclesie atque regni. Ecclesiastici X°, 8 scribitur: Regnum de gente in gentem transfertur propter iniusticias, iniurias, contumelias et diversos dolos. Cum ergo secundum sanctos qui emendare potest et negligit absque dubio participem criminis ut sic se constituit, ideo patet quam patenter minaretur ruinam regno talis erroris tolerancia sive defensio. Unde alias ostensum est quod monasterium, habens facultatem ad defendendum proditores Dei, cuiusmodi sunt singuli criminosi, non habet ut sic privilegium, **sed** pocius corruptelam. Unde sicut monti Ethne non sonat in privilegium quod includat ultra alios montes ignem suimet consumptivum, sic nec dicto monasterio foret privilegium facultas fovendi proditorem Dei tam sui quam totius Anglie destructivum.

ENGLISH: And suppose that it has a privilege in this form of words, that it may protect any fugitives whatever for any cause whatever, even in a case of injured royal majesty: still all this is a general [grant]{g:concessio}; but according to the laws of men a special case never comes under a prince's general grant, except what would be granted by the prince in a case that has been particularly [148] set out.[^5] Since, therefore, it is not in a prince's power to grant what would tend to the wrong of God, to the destruction of his kingdom, to the enervation of the regality and of the laws by which he should rule his kingdom, it is clear that what is supposed in the case set before us is not included in that general privilege. What, I ask, would be more to the wrong of God than to make his house "a den of thieves"? Or what would be more destructive of kingdoms than to protect, in nests appointed for this, the enemies of God, of the [church]{g:ecclesia} and of the kingdom?[^6] In Ecclesiasticus 10:8 it is written: "A kingdom is translated from one people to another, because of injustices, and wrongs, and injuries, and divers deceits." Since, therefore, according to the saints, he who can amend and neglects to do so without doubt makes himself, as such, a sharer in the crime, therefore it is clear how plainly the toleration or defence of such an error would threaten ruin to the kingdom.[^7] Hence it has been shown elsewhere that a monastery that has the power to defend traitors of God, such as every criminal is, does not, as such, have a privilege, but rather a corruption. Hence, just as it does not count as a privilege for Mount Etna that it contains, beyond other mountains, a fire that consumes itself, so neither would it be a privilege for the said monastery to have the power of cherishing a traitor of God, destructive both of itself and of all England.[^8]

### 07-c ¶1 · 148.25–151.33  (10 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 148] Item, nec regi licet privilegium tale concedere nec sacerdoti suo accipere nisi ex titulo elemosine vel misericordie et ad augendum in populo caritatem, **sed** constat quod non est elemosina vel opus misericordie dare abbathie licenciam, ut sustineat perpetuo debitorem, ne unquam satisfaciat, vel quod voluptuose vivendo aliena consumat, quousque factus fuerit inpotens ad solvendum. Cum enim secundum Augustinum non dimittitur peccatum, nisi restituatur ablatum, [p. 149] patet quod licenciare ad hoc foret licenciare hominem ad peccandum, quod Deus non potest, et per consequens non continetur sub opere misericordie privilegium tale concedere, nec tenderet ad augmentum amicicie vel caritatis in populo communi commune ius de satisfaccione debitoris precludere. Et patet tam ex parte donatoris quam receptoris quod ad tale privilegium est indisposicio utrobique. Nec moveat quod ex tali privilegio quandoque bonum contingeret, ut puta debitoris depredati misericors expectacio vel aliter criminosi ex tutela refugii finalis conversio, quia Deus non posset permittere peccatum fieri, nisi exinde occasionaliter bonum contingeret. Ideo absit, dicit Apostolus, ut faciamus mala, ut bona eveniant. Docto igitur quod libertas talis sonaret in caritatem et elemosinam, posset concedi singulariter illi loco privilegium sub ista condicione quam necesse est in omni donacione legitima habere tacitam vel expressam. Unde videtur quod prelati nostri debent humiliter pulsare dominos, ut tales errores nostre ecclesie previe corrigantur, antequam invadant hostes forinsecus, quia ex testimonio Christi Matth. X°, 36 nulli plus infestant matrem ecclesiam quam eius domestici inimici. Tercio principaliter arguitur sic ad idem de iure nature: Frustra invocat legis auxilium qui offendit in eam, **sed** dicti fugitivi rebellando offenderunt in legem Dei, ecclesie et in legem regni: ergo iustum est pro tempore sue obstinacie quod careant in omnibus istis legis suffragio. Maior **autem** capitur ut regula per se nota in iure canonico et demonstrari potest ex fide evangelii Matthei V°, 25, ubi Christus precipit: Esto consenciens adversario tuo cito, dum es in via cum eo, ne forte adversarius tuus tradat te iudici et iudex [p. 150] tradat te ministro et in carcerem mittaris, ubi secundum exposicionem beati Augustini oportet intelligere legem Dei adversantem cuilibet peccatori, quia nisi sibi consenserit hic in via, accusabit eum in finali iudicio cum demonibus detrudendum. Si ergo lex Dei mitissima non potest non adversari suo offendenti, quia tunc sequeretur quod falsitatis malicia superet veritatem, manifestum videtur quod omnis alia lex secundaria non excusat **sed** accusat, non iuvat **sed** reprobat, stante obstinacia offensoris. Dicti **autem** fugitivi iuxta nobis exposita offenderunt in legem Dei ex inobediencia suis prepositis, offenderant insuper in legem ecclesie racione multiplici, quia (ut dicunt docti in illis iuribus) inobedientes mandatis suorum principum committunt quoddam genus sacrilegii, et cum inobedientes remanserint legi Dei de obediendo legibus regis sui, que lex iusta illos protegeret? Et insuper secundum quod informati sumus occisus erat primus invasor et ecclesie pollutor. Quomodo ergo lex libertatis illius ecclesie defenderet tam ingratum et illegitimum eius pollutorem excommunicatum de iure? Non enim palliari potest defensio arrestati legittime et intenti salvari in vita et membris, ut invadat contra legem Anglie suos legitimos arrestantes. Cum enim arrestacio et occisio sunt contraria, patet quod miles arrestans sine extraccione gladii vel cultelli non ostendit occiso necessitatem se defendendi **sed** pocius legitime paciendi; et patet ex facto multiplici, supposita veritate narrati, quod erant inobedientes patriis legibus pro qua inobediencia continuanda querebant subterfugia [p. 151] dicti loci. Que queso maior inobediencia regi nostro quam nolle tractari secundum leges suas, **sed** proprias leges proponere secundum quas et non secundum alias velint tractari, et illis, non regni legibus, obedire? Revera sic hostes crudelissimi depopulantes regnum offerrent regnicolis quod si velint consentire legibus quas illi volunt statuere concordabunt, **sed** non exinde gauderent privilegiis regni nostri. Mirum itaque foret quod rex et regnum tales hostes intra se defenderent. Idem enim foret contradicere legibus regis per quas regeret regnum suum et contradicere regalie, cum rex non foret rex nisi propter preeminenciam et officium legis sue. Ideo dicit beatus Gregorius quod qui diligit regem diligit eius legem et demonstrari potest ex illo Johannis XIV°, 23. Si quis diligit me, sermonem meum servabit; idem enim est Deum diligere et sermonem vel legem eius servare, et sic indubie est de rege. Unde inobediencia legibus est radix tocius nequicie. Exhinc enim ceciderunt angeli de celo, expulsus fuit Adam de paradiso et breviter sine contumacia que est inobediencia non recte denunciatur heresis vel excommunicacio; nec est possibile regnum dissolvi nisi ex inobediencia legi sue. Si enim maior pars regni nostri vel prope nollet stare legibus regni nostri, **sed** sparsim acciperet municionem ecclesie commovendo sedicionem in populo, pretendendo utrobique libertatem ecclesie, quomodo staret regnum? Et si non licet regi talem hostem domesticum de castris ecclesie sue extrahere, quomodo liceret in regno non tantum subiecto ecclesias Dei invadere et remissius proditores vel hostes occidere? Nam ubi est maior falsitas atque iniuria coloracior, ibi est pena iustior.

ENGLISH: Again, it is neither lawful for the king to grant such a privilege nor for his priest to accept it, except by [title]{g:titulus} of [alms]{g:elemosina} or of mercy and to increase [charity]{g:caritas} in the people; but it is evident that it is no alms or work of mercy to give an abbey licence to maintain a debtor perpetually, so that he never pays, or so that by living in pleasure he consumes what is another's, until he has been made unable to pay. For since, according to Augustine, sin is not forgiven unless what was taken is restored, [149] it is clear that to license [anyone] to this would be to license a man to sin, which God cannot do; and consequently to grant such a privilege is not contained under a work of mercy, nor would it tend to the increase of friendship or charity in the common people to shut off the common [law]{g:ius} concerning a debtor's satisfaction.[^1] And it is clear, both on the side of the giver and on that of the receiver, that there is unfitness for such a privilege on both sides. Nor let it move [anyone] that from such a privilege some good would sometimes come about, as, for example, a merciful waiting on a debtor who has plundered, or otherwise the final conversion of a criminal through the protection of refuge; for God could not permit sin to be done unless some good came of it [by occasion]{g:occasionaliter}. Therefore "God forbid," says the Apostle, "that we should do evil, that good may come."[^2] Therefore, once it is shown that such a liberty would make for charity and alms, a privilege could be granted singly to that place under this condition, which in every lawful gift must be had, tacit or express. Hence it seems that our [prelates]{g:prelatus} ought humbly to press the [lords]{g:dominus}, so that such errors of our [church]{g:ecclesia} may be corrected beforehand, before enemies invade from outside; for by the testimony of Christ, Matthew 10:36, none harry mother church more than her enemies of her own household.[^3] Thirdly and principally, it is argued thus to the same [end], from the law of nature: he invokes the help of the law in vain who offends against it; now the said fugitives, by rebelling, have offended against the law of God, of the church, and against the law of the [kingdom]{g:regnum}: therefore it is just, for the time of their obstinacy, that they lack the aid of law in all of these.[^4] And the major is taken as a rule self-evident in canon law, and can be demonstrated [from the faith]{g:ex fide} of the gospel, Matthew 5:25, where Christ commands: "Be at agreement with thy adversary betimes, whilst thou art in the way with him: lest perhaps thy adversary deliver thee to the judge, and the judge [150] deliver thee to the officer, and thou be cast into prison"; where, according to the exposition of blessed Augustine, we must understand [God's law]{g:lex dei} as the adversary of every sinner; for unless he agree with it here, [in the way]{g:via}, it will accuse him at the final judgment, to be thrust down with the demons.[^5] If, then, God's most gentle law cannot but oppose him who offends against it (for then it would follow that the malice of falsity overcomes truth), it seems manifest that every other, secondary law does not excuse but accuses, does not help but condemns, while the offender's obstinacy stands.[^6] Now the said fugitives, by what has been set before us, offended against the law of God by disobedience to their [superiors]{g:prepositi}; they had besides offended against the law of the church for many reasons, because (as those learned in those laws say) those who disobey the commands of their princes commit a kind of sacrilege; and since they have remained disobedient to God's law on obeying the laws of their king, what just law would protect them? And besides, according to what we have been informed, the man who was killed was the first assailant and the polluter of the church. How then would the law of the liberty of that church defend so [displeasing]{g:ingratus} and unlawful a polluter of it, excommunicated by law?[^7] For the defence of one lawfully arrested, and meant to be kept safe in life and limb, cannot be cloaked so that he may assault, against the law of England, those who lawfully arrest him. For since arrest and killing are contraries, it is clear that a knight making an arrest without drawing sword or knife showed the slain man no necessity of defending himself, but rather of lawfully submitting; and it is clear from many deeds, the truth of the narrative being supposed, that they were disobedient to the laws of their [homeland]{g:patria}, to continue in which disobedience they sought the hiding-places [151] of the said place.[^8] What greater disobedience, I ask, [could there be] to our king than to refuse to be dealt with according to his laws, but to put forward their own laws, according to which, and not according to others, they would be dealt with, and to obey those and not the laws of the kingdom? In truth, so the cruellest enemies laying waste the kingdom would offer the inhabitants that, if they will consent to the laws which they choose to lay down, they will be at peace; but they would not on that account enjoy the privileges of our kingdom. It would therefore be a marvel that the king and the kingdom should defend such enemies within themselves. For it would be the same to gainsay the king's laws, by which he should rule his kingdom, and to gainsay the regality, since the king would not be king except for the pre-eminence and office of his law.[^9] Therefore blessed Gregory says that he who loves the king loves his law; and it can be demonstrated from that [text], John 14:23: "If any one love me, he will keep my word"; for it is the same to love God and to keep his word or law, and so beyond doubt it is with the king. Hence disobedience to laws is the root of all wickedness. For from this the angels fell from heaven, Adam was driven out of paradise, and, in short, without contumacy, which is disobedience, neither heresy nor excommunication is rightly declared; nor is it possible for a kingdom to be dissolved except by disobedience to its law.[^10] For if the greater part of our kingdom, or nearly so, would not stand by the laws of our kingdom, but here and there took the defence of the church, stirring up sedition in the people, pretending on both counts the liberty of the church, how would the kingdom stand? And if it is not lawful for the king to drag such a household enemy out of the strongholds of his church, how would it be lawful, in a kingdom not so subject [to him], to invade the churches of God and, on lighter grounds, to kill traitors or enemies? For where the falsity is greater and the wrong more [coloured]{g:color}, there the penalty is more just.[^11]

### 07-d ¶1 · 152.14–154.25  (8 sed/autem)

Est **autem** excommunicacio iniusta tripliciter: Primo quando sacerdos ex rancore et zelo vindicte excommunicat subditum licet reum. Secundo quando prelatus pocius pro temporalibus quam pro amore et correccione subditi eum excommunicat, licet excomunicandus fuerit bono modo. Et tercio quando indiscrete excommunicat subditum propter factum legitimum. Talis **autem** excommunicator videtur plus polluere sanctam ecclesiam quam corporaliter occidens hominem ad altare; pro quo notandum quod corporalis pollucio videtur esse excommunicacioni inpertinens, nisi de quanto sapit peccatum, tum quia pollucio per sputum vel alia pondera nature foret turpior quam pollucio per semen vel sanguinem, tum eciam quia illa pollucio deletur per locionem corpoream. Ex istis videtur, cum iniuste excommunicans fratrem suum (quantum in eo est) occidit animam et virtutes, **sed** corporalis homicida non occidit ut sic nisi corpus solummodo, sequitur quod, sicut anima excedit corpus, sic occisio in animam perpetrata excedit in malicia occisionem corporis. Ex quo videtur, cum peccatum non potest subiectari in lapide vel edificio corporali, quod locus per se non polluit suos incolas, **sed** [p. 154] econtra, cum II° Machab. Nec aliud volunt leges ecclesie debite intellecte. Plus eciam foret ad privilegium et iustificacionem ecclesie quod prevaricatores mandati Dei per censuras ecclesie sint correcti quam quod omnes ritus humani legi Christi superadditi sint quantumcunque multipliciter regulariter ac solempniter observati.

ENGLISH: From which it seems that those who publicly excommunicate the king's ministers without diligent examination of the offence, and especially if they are sworn to defend that very law, offend exceedingly. For in Genesis 18:20–21 the all-knowing [God], to give an example to judges how diligently they should examine a cause before they give judgment, says: "The cry," he says, "of the Sodomites is multiplied; I will go down and see whether they have fulfilled the cry in deed, or whether it be not so, that I may know."[^1] Nor is the notoriety of the substance of the deed enough, but it must be notorious in its quality; for it is known that one who excommunicates for a lawful act wrongs the excommunicate, excommunicating himself. Nor is there [colour]{g:color} for excommunicating the [captains]{g:capitaneus} of a lawful operation because, beyond their consent, authorization or occasion given, someone of the company did an undue deed; for so Christ would have to be excommunicated for the deed of [Is]cariot, and every leader of an army for any crime committed by a person of his army, and (what is more evident) every [prelate]{g:prelatus} [153] for any crime of a subject of his flock; and if these things were true, the power of excommunicating subjects would be taken away from prelates, since it is not lawful for one excommunicated to excommunicate anyone; indeed, since as such he is a heretic, it is not lawful for him to administer any sacraments.[^2] Nor does the excuse hold that the prelate does not excommunicate, but declares such men excommunicated by law; for such a declaration is a defamation to the least; and woe to him who so scandalizes his brother.[^3] And excommunication is unjust in three ways. First, when a [priest]{g:sacerdos} out of rancour and zeal for vengeance excommunicates a subject, though guilty. Secondly, when a prelate excommunicates a subject rather for [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} than for the love and correction of the subject, though he deserved excommunication in a good manner. For which it is to be noted that bodily pollution seems to be irrelevant to excommunication, except in so far as it savours of sin: both because pollution by spittle or other burdens of nature would be fouler than pollution by semen or blood, and also because that pollution is wiped out by a bodily washing.[^5] From these things it seems, since one who unjustly excommunicates his brother (as far as in him lies) kills the soul and the virtues, but the bodily homicide as such kills only the body, [that] it follows that, as the soul exceeds the body, so the killing perpetrated on the soul exceeds in malice the killing of the body. From which it seems, since sin cannot have its subject in a stone or a bodily building, that the place of itself does not pollute its inhabitants, but [154] the reverse; since in 2 Maccabees 5:19 it is written: "The Lord did not choose the people for the place's sake, but the place for the people's sake."[^6] And it is clear that the pollution of a place would not reasonably hinder the worship of God except in so far as it struck horror from the sin; and since a greater sin would strike a greater horror, it follows that a graver sin committed in the sanctuary would rather draw [men] back from divine service. Nor do the laws of the church, rightly understood, mean anything else. For it is not lawful for a cleric of Christ to be blinded in the Pharisaic folly (of which Matthew 15), valuing a bodily washing, from greater horror of bodily filth, more than a spiritual washing from the filth of the soul; since, according to Christ's definition, the things that come forth from the heart of the inner man, these are what preponderantly defile a man.[^7] And here the whole lay church wonders why the [clergy]{g:clerus} excommunicate with such urgency and fervour for their bodily debt, or for a [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} privilege brought in by the traditions of men; but they do not so excommunicate clerics for the repaying of a spiritual debt, although the sin is infinitely graver and that debt as regards God more binding.[^8] It would also be more to the privilege and justification of the church that [transgressors]{g:prevaricator} of God's command be corrected by the [censures]{g:censura} of the church than that all human rites added on to the law of Christ be observed, however manifoldly, regularly and solemnly.[^9]

### 08-a ¶1 · 157.26–160.12  (6 sed/autem)

For, according to what was said in chapter 15, a theologian would be all too ignorant of the reasons of scripture and of the orthodox faith who was wholly ignorant of the quiddity of holy mother church.[^2] Nor do I doubt that fruitful speculation is lacking to decretists, legists and philosophers who are wholly ignorant of what holy mother church is. For all human laws exist, as their end, to defend the justice of this mother and to purge her of the errors in which, for the time of [the way]{g:via}, she is entangled by the adversities of temptations. Since, then, the end imposes the greatest necessity on works, it is manifest from Physics II that all speculative doctors must set up [our mother]{g:mater nostra} as the rule for measuring the treatment of things that are objects of speculation; and by the same token no one applies himself to praiseworthy practice except in so far as he would tend to the honour or benefit of this mother; nor is there doubt that no one [is a wayfarer]{g:viator} meritoriously, according to the good pleasure of the bridegroom of the church, except in so far as he works for the honour and benefit of his [bride]{g:sponsa}.[^3] And hence it is commanded in the first commandment of the second table that the faithful man honour father and mother (Exodus 20:12), which is without doubt understood principally of Christ and his church. Nor does he honour or love faithfully her whom he does not know. Therefore it is necessary that every faithful man give effort to knowing his mother more directly.[^4] Secondly, it is supposed that one proceed in this treatise without the doubleness of lying. For lying is precisely the [wile]{g:cautela} by which the devil deludes the faithful, so that [159] they honour a harlot, adulterous with him, as the queen and mother of the faithful, the bride of Christ.[^5] Hence this teaching on the quiddity of holy mother church must be supposed as a foundation, so that it may be known who act on behalf of her true privileges and who degenerate; in what the true privilege and edification of the church consists, and in what its opposite stands, according to the faith of scripture; and [what is to be said] of the fittingness of the modern state in comparison with the flower of the church's age, according to scripture's manner of speaking. But in this matter there are certain modern doctors approving lying and saying that such truths of scripture are not to be spoken, both on account of the disturbance of the church and also on account of the crimes which the church's enemies would commit from their boldness.[^6] Hence it is argued and held that the doctors called by command of the lord king, and sworn to tell the truth about the case which lately befell at Westminster, for the honour of God, for the profit of the church and for the purging of errors in the kingdom, so that it might thenceforth stand more constantly in virtue, ought to have lied, for two reasons: first, to preserve their own reputation, they who hitherto have been reputed enemies of the church; now each man ought to preserve his own reputation more carefully than another's, for which [another's], in a given case, it is lawful to lie.[^7] Secondly, they ought to have lied to preserve the privileges of the church, which would otherwise have been destroyed, once the truth was [exposed]{g:detegere} to the [temporal lords]{g:temporales domini} and [patrons]{g:patronus}. Now since the said privileges are more excellent than the life of a man, to save which it is lawful to lie, much more ought they to have lied to preserve those privileges.[^8] As to the principle of that teaching, it is clear from the treatise On Lying that it is detestable to every faithful man, as heretical and blasphemous. For if some lie is meritorious, and some is superabundant [160] in the liar beyond what is fitting, and some, again, is in the deficient extreme in him who from a seared conscience lies too sparingly, it [follows]{g:sequitur} that the habit of lying, holding the mean between these two extremes, would be a virtue.[^9] The [inference]{g:consequencia} is clear from the definition of virtue laid down in Ethics II, chapter 6: "Virtue is an elective habit consisting in a mean relative to us, determined by reason."[^10] Yet the gentile philosopher Aristotle would abhor this, since in Ethics IV, chapter 4, treating of the virtue of the truthful man and of the twofold vice of the false-speaker, he writes thus: "And in itself the lie is base and to be shunned, but the true is good and praiseworthy."[^11]

### 08-a ¶2 · 160.13–160.24  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 160] Item, cum omnis virtus sit de genere tertii boni maximi, descendens desursum a patre luminum qua virtute nemo abuti potest (ut sepe meminit beatus Augustinus), sequitur quod omnis virtus mendacii sit a Deo, et cum omnis pater et auctor virtutis sit autonomatice virtuosus, sequitur quod Deus, pater et auctor mendacii, sit summe mendax, sicut Christus secundum humanitatem foret homo mendacissimus potens esse, et per consequens, nullus fidelis dubitat esse blasphemum, cum Manichei blasfemi dixerunt micius quod non Deus bonus, **sed** Deus malus sit origo et principium talis mali.

ENGLISH: Again, since every virtue is of the kind of the third and greatest good, coming down from above from the Father of lights, a virtue which no one can abuse (as blessed Augustine often recalls), it follows that every virtue of lying is from God; and since every father and author of virtue is [by antonomasia]{g:autonomatice} virtuous, it follows that God, the father and author of lying, is supremely a liar, just as Christ according to his humanity would be the most lying man that could be; and consequently [it follows that] which no faithful man doubts to be blasphemous, since the blasphemous Manichees said more mildly that not the good God but an evil God is the origin and principle of such evil.[^12]

### 08-b ¶1 · 160.25–162.5  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 160] Nec dubium quin non sit dare virtutem in homine, quin eius ydea vel exemplar sit in Deo et Deus virtute supra genus est taliter virtuosus, ut non posset exemplare iusticiam, misericordiam, patienciam, liberalitatem, et sic de et aliis, nisi ipse foret summe iustus, misericors, paciens et liberalis, et sic de principiis cuiuscunque generis virtutis. Et sic antecedenter ad virtutem mendacii [p. 161] oportet Deum esse summe mendacem. Non enim est fingendum quomodo pertinencius denominaret habitus mendacii subiectum suum quam mendax, cum mentiri sit opus virtutis. Item, suppono ex fide scripture quod Deus dicit quicquid creatura aliqua bene dicit; patet ex hoc quod omnis creatura ut sic est organum Dei principalius dicentis quam sua fistula, iuxta modum loquendi Psalmi LXXXIV, 9: Audiam quid loquatur in me dominus Deus. Et Apostolus IIª, Corinthiorum XIII°, 3: An experimentum queritis eius qui in me loquitur Christus. Ex quibus cum veris sequitur quod tam trinitas quam Christus humanitus facit in omni christiano mendace quodcunque mendacium virtuosum, et cum virtuosum non sit distrahens **sed** pocius viciosum, sequitur quod Deus simpliciter facit mendacium, et cum dicit falsum eadem intencione qua christianus pie menciens, sequitur quod Deus principalissime mentitur et per consequens mendax est, ut pater eius, ut Christus dicit Johannis VIII°, 44 de ipso diabolo. Cum enim Deus illo modo mendacium loquitur, ex propriis loquitur, cum sit fons et origo mendacii, et ita cum dicit angelis veritatem in verbo, nobis menciendo foret duplex animo, et forent in eo est et non contra Apostolum IIª, Cor. 1°, 17. Ymo, cum ipse sit omnis veritas, negando veritatem virtute mendacii, se ipsum negare posset, contra Apostolum IIª Thymotei II°, 13, et foret racione mendacis principii cateclismus tocius fidei christiane. Cum enim Deus mentiri potest iuxta sic opinantes nec potest habere denominacionem virtutis, nisi habeat ipsam in summo et quodam modo infinite, sequitur ex infinitate sue mendacitatis immense quod in tota fide sue scripture potuit totam ecclesiam decepisse [p. 162] dolose. Aliter enim esset dare finem sue mendacitatis immense, usque ad quem et non ultra potuit decepisse; quod credo non dabitur, cum dicitur communiter quod sit infinitum misericors **sed** finite solum modo miserator.

ENGLISH: Nor is there doubt that no virtue is to be found in man whose idea or exemplar is not in God; and God, by a virtue above every genus, is so virtuous that he could not be the exemplar of justice, mercy, patience, liberality, and so of the others, unless he were himself supremely just, merciful, patient and liberal; and so of the principles of every genus of virtue.[^1] And so, antecedently to the virtue of lying, [161] God must be supremely a liar. For it is not to be imagined how the habit of lying would more fittingly denominate its subject than as "liar", since to lie would be a work of virtue. Again, I suppose [from the faith]{g:ex fide} of scripture that God says whatever any creature says well. This is clear from the fact that every creature, as such, is the instrument of God, who speaks more principally than his pipe, according to the manner of speaking of Psalm 84:9: "I will hear what the Lord God will speak in me"; and the Apostle, 2 Corinthians 13:3: "Do you seek a proof of Christ that speaketh in me?"[^2] From which, together with true [premisses], it [follows]{g:sequitur} that both the Trinity and Christ in his humanity make, in every Christian liar, whatever virtuous lie [he tells]; and since "virtuous" is not a [diminishing qualifier]{g:distrahens}, but rather "vicious" is, it follows that God makes a lie [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}; and since he says what is false with the same intention as the Christian who lies piously, it follows that God lies most principally, and consequently is "a liar, and the father thereof", as Christ says of the devil himself, John 8:44.[^3] For when God speaks a lie in that way, "he speaketh of his own", since he is the fount and origin of lying; and so, when he tells the angels the truth in the Word, by lying to us he would be double-minded, and there would be in him "It is, and It is not", against the Apostle, 2 Corinthians 1:17. Indeed, since he himself is all truth, by denying the truth by virtue of lying he could deny himself, against the Apostle, 2 Timothy 2:13 ("he cannot deny himself"); and there would be, by reason of a lying principle, a cataclysm of the whole Christian faith.[^4] For since, according to those who hold this opinion, God can lie, and cannot have the denomination of a virtue unless he has it in the highest degree and in a certain way infinitely, it follows from the infinity of his immense mendacity that in the whole faith of his scripture he could have deceived the whole [church]{g:ecclesia} [162] guilefully. For otherwise an end would have to be assigned to his immense mendacity, up to which and not beyond he could have deceived; which I believe will not be granted, since it is commonly said that he is infinitely merciful, but only finitely one who shows mercy.[^5]

### 08-b ¶2 · 162.6–163.27  (6 sed/autem)

Ideo minus malum est sine dampno anime pacienter pati detracciones et scandala, quam menciendo et per consequens iniuriando toti universitati create, offendere dominum veritatis. Non enim ymaginari potest casus nec inveniri ficticia in quo vel qua licitum est mentiri, immo crescente mendacio eciam in clero plus solito esset opus doctoris proferre de thesauro scripture raciones et sanctorum testimonia in detestacionem mendacii et non fovere mendacium mendaciis allegacionum et sophisticis argumentis. Et hic notant allegantes hanc formam verborum quomodo aliquis est pius dolus quo licet nobis proximum menciendo decipere. **Sed** ego respexi multos libros Augustini in quibus dicit concorditer quod iste obstetrices in isto mendacio peccaverunt, licet peccatum fuerit Hebreis proficuum, unde cum nullum bonum sit irremuneratum, Deus pro pietate quam habuerant fecit eas taliter premiari. Unde Magister Historiarum super Exodi cap. III° quod sic incipit: De indole obstetricum, sic loquitur: Quia timuerunt Deum, edificavit illis domos, id est, locupletavit eas, cum essent pauperes, vel fecundavit eas, cum essent steriles. Augustinus vero dicit obstetrices venialiter peccasse, Gregorius vero videtur velle quod mortaliter; dicit enim quod merces benignitatis earum non potuit in eterna vita retribui, **sed** pro culpa mendacii in terrenam compensacionem declinata est.

ENGLISH: As to those two pieces of [evidence]{g:evidencia}, that we ought to have lied against our oath, so pious and lawful, before the council of our king, I call God and the divine powers to witness that among all the [doctrines]{g:doctrina} or counsels I have heard none has met me harder or more detestable.[^6] Therefore it seems to me that it could have been said less badly in private than thus publicly in the schools; for one lying thus would manifestly have incurred perjury and the infamy which he was avoiding, and so would culpably have [inferred]{g:concludere}, according to the goal of [refutation]{g:redarguere}, chiefly against himself.[^7] Therefore it is less evil, without damage to the soul, patiently to suffer detractions and scandals, than, by lying and consequently wronging the whole created universe, to offend the lord of truth. For no case can be imagined, nor [fiction]{g:ficticia} found, in which it is lawful to lie; rather, as lying grows, even among [the clergy]{g:clerus}, more than usual, it would be the doctor's work to bring forth from the treasury of scripture reasons and testimonies of the saints in detestation of lying, and not to foster lying with lies of allegation and with sophistical arguments.[^8] I for my part would shudder to bring in this school as a doctor of lying, since from frailty we are all too prone to it without being led further astray by a foreign science of lying; and so a doctor teaching that it is lawful to lie proceeds imperfectly unless he teaches how it is lawful to lie, and consequently unless he teaches how we ought to lie; and so, according to the school of lying, it would be praiseworthy to lie by falsely feigning foreign doctors [163] and sayings of holy doctors, to give colour to this [teaching]{g:sentencia}.[^9] But who would believe such a school? And I say "with lies of allegation" because the saying of Augustine on that [verse] of Psalm 5:7, "Thou wilt destroy all that speak a lie", was alleged against me in this form: "Many lies," he says, "seem [to be told] for someone's safety or advantage, not from malice but from kindness: such was that of the midwives in Exodus, who reported what was false to Pharaoh, so that the infants of the sons of Israel should not be killed; but even these are praised not in the thing but in the guile."[^10] And here those who allege this form of words note how there is a pious guile by which it is lawful for us to deceive our neighbour by lying. But I have looked into many books of Augustine in which he says consistently that those midwives sinned in that lie, although the sin was profitable to the Hebrews; hence, since no good is unrewarded, God, for the piety which they had, caused them to be rewarded in such a way.[^11] Hence the Master of the Histories, on Exodus, chapter 3, which begins thus, "On the promise of the midwives", speaks thus: "Because they feared God, he built them houses, that is, he enriched them, since they were poor, or made them fruitful, since they were barren." Augustine indeed says that the midwives sinned venially, but Gregory seems to hold that [they sinned] mortally; for he says that the reward of their kindness could not be repaid in eternal life, but because of the fault of lying was turned aside into an earthly recompense.[^12]

### 08-c ¶1 · 164.1–166.15  (8 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 164] Unde in textu originalis beati Augustini inveni sic scriptum: ista non re, **sed** indole laudantur. Nec dubium quin sic habent libri correcti, tum quia magnus ille doctor Petrus Comestor intitulat capitulum suum De indole obstetricum, et sic loquitur sanctus Thomas secunda secunde q. CXª; tum secundo quia tunc textus Augustini diceret non in re, **sed** in dolo laudantur, nunc **autem** dicitur non re, **sed** indole laudantur; tum tercio quia dato hoc textu sic perverso adhuc militat in oppositum pervertentis, cum textus dicit quod non laudantur re. Certum est tamen quod in re fecerunt bonum opus de genere, quod fuisset virtuosum, si fuisset non infectum mendacio. Unde econtra est sentencia Augustini quod obstetrices laudantur in re vel opere, non **autem** in dolo quo regem mendaciter fefellerunt. Unde inveni in margine originalis beati Augustini sic scriptum: indolem vocat profectum; sicut enim est corporalis indoles corporaliter crescencium, sic est spiritualis indoles spiritualiter proficiencium. Vult ergo Augustinus dicere quod ista verba mendacia obstetricum non laudantur in re mendacii, **sed** in profectu vel fructu qui exinde provenit crescencie Hebreorum. Est ergo sentencia Augustini quod Deus non posset sinere peccatum fieri, nisi exinde bonum proveniat; unde propter bonum exinde occasionaliter proveniens vocatur peccatum Ade a beato Gregorio necessarium atque felix. Et dicitur a beato Augustino expediens; unde XIV°, De Civitate Dei, cap. XIII°, sic scribit: Audeo dicere superbis esse utile cadere in aliquod apertum manifestumque peccatum, unde sibi displiceant qui iam sibi placendo ceciderant. Salubrius enim [p. 165] Petrus sibi displicuit quando flevit quam sibi placuit quando presumpsit. **Sed** per se notum est theologis quod non exinde peccandum est, quod bonum ex gracia Dei exinde proveniat. Unde credo quod peccatum publicum doctorum quo manifeste approbant mendacium ut virtutem, et mendax allegacio originalium, ut mendacio pallient dictum suum, vertetur ex gracia Dei illis in commodum, dum tamen in isto se corrigant et recognoscentes defectum suum humiliter resipiscant. Quis rogo aliter caperet eos in testimonium vel allegacioni eorum crederet, dum dicerent quod privatum commodum meritorium sit mentiri; revera nescio que opinio redderet hominem plus infamem. Quantum ad secundam evidenciam quod propter salvanda privilegia ecclesie sicut in casu qui nuper contigit meritorium sit mentiri, manifestum est quod dogma illud inficiendo privilegia redderet procuratorem ecclesie nimis suspectum; cum enim omne privilegium vel libertas sit fundatum in veritate que est sponsus ecclesie, manifestum est quod professione veritatis et non mendacii veritati oppositi defenderetur, dicente eadem veritate Joh. VIII°, 31, 32: Si vos manseritis in sermone meo, vere discipuli mei eritis, et vos cognoscetis veritatem, et veritas liberabit vos. Ex quibus patet cum fide scripture quod, de quanto orator ecclesie declinat a sermone Dei qui est veritas in verba mendacii que sunt sermones diaboli, ut sic se ipsum inhabilitat ad privilegia et libertates ecclesie procurandum; nam nulla talia dantur nisi per sponsum ecclesie, veritatem que odit omne mendacium. Nam mentitor ut sic offendit in naturam divinam a qua [p. 166] omne bonum profluit sponse Christi, et ut sic offendit in totam ecclesiam, quia contra hoc mandatum domini: Non loqueris contra proximum tuum falsum testimonium, Exod. XX°, 16, iniuriatur ut sic sancte matri ecclesie: ideo absit ecclesiam expetere huiusmodi oratorem. Nam secundum Augustinum super Psalmum Vᵘᵐ: Si hoc dicitur quod est, verum dicitur, et ut sic ad eum qui est acceditur; si **autem** hoc dicitur quod non est, mendacium fit et a veritate receditur. Ideo dicit propheta: Perdes omnes qui loquuntur mendacium. Unde ne quis arbitretur perfectum et spiritualem hominem pro ista temporali vita in cuius morte non occiditur anima, sive sua sive alterius, debere mentiri, non inmerito in alio loco scriptum est: Os quod mentitur occidit animam.

ENGLISH: Hence in the text of the original of blessed Augustine I found it written thus: "these are praised not for the thing but for the promise".[^1] Nor is there doubt that the corrected books have it so: first, because that great doctor Peter Comestor entitles his chapter "On the promise of the midwives", and so speaks Saint Thomas, Secunda secundae, question 110;[^2] secondly, because then the text of Augustine would say "they are praised not in the thing but in the guile", whereas in fact it says "they are praised not for the thing but for the promise"; thirdly, because, granted this text so perverted, it still tells against the one who perverts it, since the text says that they are not praised for the thing.[^3] Yet it is certain that in the thing they did a work good in its kind, which would have been virtuous had it not been infected with lying. Hence, on the contrary, the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of Augustine is that the midwives are praised in the thing or work, but not in the guile by which they deceived the king with a lie. Hence I found written in the margin of the original of blessed Augustine thus: "He calls 'promise' progress; for just as there is a bodily promise of those who grow bodily, so there is a spiritual promise of those who advance spiritually." Augustine, therefore, means to say that those lying words of the midwives are not praised in the thing of lying, but in the progress or fruit which came of it for the increase of the Hebrews.[^4] It is, therefore, the teaching of Augustine that God could not permit sin to be done unless good came of it; hence, because of the good that came of it [by occasion]{g:occasionaliter}, the sin of Adam is called by blessed Gregory necessary and happy. And it is called expedient by blessed Augustine; hence in De civitate Dei XIV, chapter 13, he writes thus: "I dare to say that it is useful for the proud to fall into some open and manifest sin, whereby they may be displeased with themselves, who had already fallen by pleasing themselves. For more wholesomely [165] was Peter displeased with himself when he wept than pleased with himself when he presumed."[^5] But it is self-evident to theologians that one is not to sin on the ground that good comes of it by the grace of God. Hence I believe that the public sin of the doctors, by which they openly approve lying as a virtue, and the lying allegation of the originals, to cloak their saying with a lie, will by the grace of God be turned to their benefit, provided that they correct themselves in this and, acknowledging their defect, humbly come to their senses. Who, I ask, would otherwise take them as witnesses, or believe their allegation, while they said that to lie is a meritorious private advantage? Truly I do not know what [opinion]{g:opinio} would make a man more infamous.[^6] As to the second [evidence]{g:evidencia}, that for the saving of the privileges of the church, as in the case which lately befell, it is meritorious to lie, it is manifest that that dogma, by infecting the privileges, would render the [proctor]{g:procurator} of the [church]{g:ecclesia} all too suspect; for since every privilege or liberty is founded in the truth, which is the [bridegroom]{g:sponsus} of the church, it is manifest that it would be defended by the profession of truth, and not of the lie opposed to the truth, as the same truth says, John 8:31–32: "If you continue in my word, you shall be my disciples indeed. And you shall know the truth: and the truth shall make you free."[^7] From which it is clear, together with the faith of scripture, that in so far as an advocate of the church turns aside from the word of God, which is truth, to words of lying, which are the speeches of the devil, to that extent he makes himself unqualified for procuring the privileges and liberties of the church; for none such are given except through the bridegroom of the church, the truth, which hates every lie.[^8] Therefore he says, John 8:34: "Whosoever committeth sin is the servant of sin." Nor is there doubt that everyone who makes a lie sins as such; therefore lying is the worst means for procuring the goods of the church. For the liar as such offends against the divine nature, from which [166] every good flows forth to the bride of Christ; and as such he offends against the whole church, because, against this commandment of the Lord, "Thou shalt not bear false witness against thy neighbour", Exodus 20:16, he as such wrongs holy mother church: therefore far be it that the church should seek out an advocate of this kind. For according to Augustine on Psalm 5: "If what is is said, the truth is said, and so one draws near to him who is; but if what is not is said, a lie is made, and one withdraws from the truth. Therefore the prophet says: Thou wilt destroy all that speak a lie. Hence, lest anyone should think that the perfect and spiritual man ought to lie for this temporal life, in whose death the soul is not slain, whether his own or another's, not undeservedly is it written in another place: The mouth that belieth, killeth the soul."[^9]

### 08-c ¶2 · 166.16–168.3  (2 sed/autem)

Non enim licet negare quin Christus privilegiavit suum collegium, cum dedit eis leges singulares, [p. 177] ut consilia evangelica ad maximam utilitatem sui atque ecclesie, sicut patet ex indole que inde provenerat. Ergo ex diffinicione privilegii in hoc privilegiavit suam ecclesiam. Confirmatur secundo: Ille status est perfectissimus, ut patet in VI° decretalium, cap. Exiit qui seminat, per Nicolaum III., et omne ponere viatorem in statu tam alto est proporcionaliter privilegiare ipsum, ergo conclusio. Si enim dare bona fortune que sunt abiectissima sit privilegiare datarium, multo magis dare sibi bona gracie. Item, quicunque vians exoneratus fuerit sarcina temporalium, ad propinquius, paracius atque laudabilius sequendum hynnulum cervorum salientem in montibus, ut sic est privilegiatus uberius, **sed** sic fuit de apostolis renunciantibus quoad civilitatem omnibus bonis suis, ergo conclusio. Non enim negabit fidelis quin possessio talis temporalium sit pondus anime, ut docet beatus Gregorius XXXI° Moralium (sicut declaravi XII° cap. huius). Unde apostoli vere reputarunt eis magnum privilegium, quod exonerati fuerunt a ministerio cotidiano in mensis fidelium (ut patet Actorum VI°), ergo multo magis exonerare eos a statu seculari plus abiecto. In hoc enim consistit excellencia status cleri super dominos seculares (ut hic supponitur), non **autem** in caractere ordinis, cum stat cum illo servitus abiectissima (ut patet de Scarioth), **sed** in hoc quod deputati sunt ut milites plus strenui ad plus meritorie peragendum bella domini et conquirendum regnum celorum. Nam Matthei V°, 3 dicitur: Beati pauperes spiritu, quoniam ipsorum est regnum celorum.

ENGLISH: Hence, to pacify the disciples and instruct them what privilege and [priority]{g:prioritas} they should choose, he says that whoever will be prior or greater among them in heaven must be a humbler and more diligent servant here on earth (as I have shown elsewhere), in the way opposite to primacy or the honour of the world.[^1] Therefore Chrysostom says: "He has set heavenly primacy as the fruit of humility, [173] so that whoever desires heavenly primacy may follow heavenly humility here; but those who desire primacy on earth will find confusion in heaven. Therefore," he says, "let there now be no contest about primacy among the servants of Christ, nor let each of them hasten after how he may appear greater than another, but after how he may seem lower than all."[^2] And this [doctrine]{g:doctrina} would suffice the whole [church]{g:ecclesia} for the choice of privilege or primacy; nor is it possible that the church be truly pacified before this rule has been taught in practice. From these things it is drawn out that there is no true privilege of the church except in so far as it is founded on, taught by or drawn out of scripture.[^3] It is clear thus: every law useful to holy mother church is taught explicitly or implicitly in scripture; every privilege is of this kind, from what has been said; therefore the [conclusion]{g:conclusio}. It is confirmed from what has often been said: all truth is in sacred scripture; every privilege is a truth, because it is a law; therefore the conclusion. Hence Augustine, De doctrina christiana II, at the end: "Whatever a man has learned outside sacred scripture, if it is harmful it is condemned there; if it is useful it is found there"; and he says the same in the fourth letter to Volusianus (as I have expounded at length elsewhere).[^4] It follows, then: if whatever useful thing anyone has learned outside sacred scripture is found there, [and] every privilege granted in charters or bulls is something useful (as is clear from the definition), therefore every privilege of the church is in sacred scripture; therefore, since scripture is of greater authority and acceptance than bulls or charters (as I have said elsewhere), these are [for the most part]{g:ut plurimum} superfluous among the [clergy]{g:clerus}.[^5] For if they were taken away, the one privileged would lean on the ministry of the church, which is of itself the cause of the privilege, and would not so trust in [174] charters, leaving his office aside. For such security confounds the church, since with sophistical darts, upon an equivocation which is hypocrisy and a lie, the devil and his members close in upon the church.[^6] Who, I ask, is not deceived today in adhering to a part of the church of the malignant and cultivating it as a member of the church? Who, moreover, does not aspire to the pomp of the world, or to primacy or privilege, although it is a most wicked corruption, insomuch that we often fall into that prophetic woe, Isaiah 5:20: "Woe to you that call good evil, and evil good"?[^7] Does not our diligence show that we value charters and human privileges more than sacred scripture and the privileges which Christ instituted for his own? And undoubtedly, as such, we fall by abuse from whatever true privileges [we have], because according to blessed Gregory (and it is placed in the Decretum, C. 11 q. 3 c. Privilegium) "he ought to lose a privilege who abuses his power".[^8] Since, therefore, everything reasonable or useful to the church of Christ is first granted by him and inscribed in his charter or book, it is clear that we ought first to attend to the scrutiny of that book and to the condition under which he privileged. For it does not follow: a given potentate of the world granted this as a privilege to a given church, therefore it is reasonable or true. Therefore beyond the grant one must scrutinize the rule by which the ground of the privilege ought to be judged.[^9] Suppose, I say, that Peter, a potentate of the world, grants a privilege to Westminster in this form: that whoever, however much a malefactor, has entered its precincts shall have immunity of every kind; provided, however, that in the first conclusion which is put to him after his entry by the [superior]{g:prepositi} of the place [175] he speaks the truth.[^10] Now the case seems possible and honest, since he could use the privilege given him for this for a lie and for any crime whatever, as is said; therefore much more for the humble confession of the truth. I suppose, moreover, that Peter, a malefactor entering Westminster to have immunity, is first asked by the superior of Westminster whether he will have immunity in the said church; and I suppose that Peter, conscious of the desert he has contracted by his crime, says, despairing of pardon or truly reckoning himself worthy of temporal death, that he will not have immunity in the said church. Then, this case having been carried through at will, it is clear that from the case and the other circumstances posited and to be posited each part of the contradiction follows, as the sophists know in the matter of insolubles.[^11] And in truth anyone who enters the threshold of the church in order to be harboured in crime falls more culpably into an insoluble matter, of which kind is sin, than it follows that Peter falls.[^12] Hence I do not know how to solve it otherwise, nor do I believe that anyone else knows, except that the said so-called privilege is not a privilege, because it cannot be founded in scripture; and so, generally, every private law not foundable in scripture is not a privilege, because it does not turn to the profit of the church, since (as Augustine says) whatever is useful is found there. And if [it implies a contradiction]{g:claudit contradictionem} that this should be a privilege, much more every case in which a privilege is posited to be given to a place, that whoever has entered with whatever crime should enjoy immunity in a given church; for that is an impossibility further removed from appearance than the preceding.[^13] Since many enter sanctuary with that sin by reason of which they are bound to lose the liberty, indeed, since the principle of Gregory is true, that everyone, and especially one [foreknown]{g:prescitus}, [176] "is bound to lose a privilege who abuses his power", and every criminal as such abuses his power, since by reason of the mortal sin infecting him he abuses the goods of nature: it follows that everyone entering the precincts of the church criminally is bound, as such, not to enjoy the privileges of the church for that time.[^14] And this is known to those who know that whatever a criminal does, by reason of the sin infecting him he sins mortally. Let a man, therefore, found the reasonableness of a privilege on scripture, and otherwise it is worth nothing. Now those privileges to which we ought to attend more shine out more in the mirror of scripture, and the less necessary ones more [indistinctly]{g:confuse}; because God, who could not fail his [bride]{g:sponsa} in privileges and in writings that witness to them, knew that the chaff would turn aside more to earthly than to heavenly things; therefore, so that the degree of scripture's making known might be a rule for us, he expressed the spiritual privileges, adding an indistinct teaching about the bodily ones which would be adduced.[^15] From these things, thirdly, it is gathered that the highest privilege for the state of [the way]{g:via} would be for the church to be discharged of the civil possession of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, running freely after Christ in evangelical poverty.[^16] It is clear from the two preceding conclusions, for so the believer is disposed more like Christ and more ready for conquering the [kingdom]{g:regnum} of heaven. Hence also this privilege is noted in the church more expressly, more highly and more often for the dearer disciples of Christ (as is clear, Matthew 10, Luke 14 and 1 Timothy 6); for upon this the goods of grace followed more copiously.[^17] It is confirmed, first, thus: Christ placed his vicars in the greatest liberty and privilege in this, that he enjoined that rule singularly on his apostles; therefore the conclusion. For it is not lawful to deny that Christ privileged his college when he gave them singular laws, [177] such as the evangelical counsels, for the greatest profit of himself and of the church, as is clear from the [promise]{g:indoles} which came of it. Therefore, by the definition of privilege, he privileged his church in this.[^18] It is confirmed, secondly: that state is the most perfect, as is clear in the Sext, chapter Exiit qui seminat, by Nicholas III; and to place a wayfarer in so high a state is proportionally to privilege him; therefore the conclusion. For if to give the goods of fortune, which are the most abject, is to privilege the recipient, much more to give him the goods of grace.[^19] Again, whatever [wayfarer]{g:viator} has been discharged of the burden of temporal goods, to follow more nearly, more readily and more laudably the young hart leaping upon the mountains, is as such more richly privileged; now so it was with the apostles, who renounced, as to civil possession, all their goods; therefore the conclusion.[^20] For a believer will not deny that such possession of temporal goods is a weight on the soul, as blessed Gregory teaches, Moralia XXXI (as I declared in chapter 12 of this [work]). Hence the apostles truly reckoned it a great privilege to themselves that they were discharged of the daily ministry at the tables of the faithful (as is clear, Acts 6); therefore much more [is it a privilege] to discharge them from the secular state, which is more abject.[^21] For in this consists the excellence of the state of the clergy over the secular lords (as is here supposed): not in the character of order, since the most abject servitude stands with that (as is clear of Iscariot), but in this, that they are deputed as more vigorous [knights]{g:milites} to wage more meritoriously the wars of the Lord and to conquer the kingdom of heaven. For in Matthew 5:3 it is said: "Blessed are the poor in spirit: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven."[^22]

### 09-b ¶1 · 186.32–187.15  (1 sed/autem)

Vª, Non mediocriter, ubi dicitur quod graviter peccant qui eligunt minus bonum sibi maiori bono postposito. It is confirmed, secondly, from what is said in book 5, chapter [ ], where it is taught that one [on the road]{g:viantes} must always proceed by growing better. And thirdly it is confirmed from this, that the counsels of Christ are perpetual and not to be dispensed for his bride.[^2]

### 09-b ¶2 · 187.16–188.13  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 187] Hic videtur mihi quod unum concluditur ex assumpto necessario, cum omnis christianus debet sequi Christum in moribus quantum sufficit, unde videtur mihi quod beatus Silvester potest dici ex pietate suscepisse elemosinas Cesaris corporales ad exonerandum eum, ad excitandum eius devocionem et procurandum tenerius pro fidelibus quam faceret populus laycalis; quod, cum stat cum paupertate evangelica, patet quod clerus non debet exinde inniti proprietati vel fastui, **sed** ad sui tedium secundum partes plus abiectas ministrare pauperibus de collatis elemosinis. Unde tales elemosine non possunt donari in perpetuam elemosinam quomodocunque clerus vixerit, **sed** sub condicione saltem tacita quod clerus sit vere sequax apostolorum in vita et opere. Et ita credo quod oportet ut reputet sibi omne tediosum ministrare pauperibus et fore gaudiosum, dando operam contemplacioni [p. 188] et predicacioni, quod fideles layci accipiant illud onus (ut alias exemplificavi de Augustino et aliis sanctis). Unde quia fragilitas humana prona est ad relinquendum primam caritatem de elemosinis, superbiendo, se ipsam nimis laute pascendo et pastoris officium relinquendo, ideo apostoli et sancciores ecclesie talia reliquerunt. ENGLISH: Here it seems to me that one [thing] is [inferred]{g:concludere} from the [assumption]{g:assumptum} necessarily, since every Christian ought to follow Christ in manners as far as suffices.[^3] Hence it seems to me that blessed Silvester can be said to have received the bodily [alms]{g:elemosina} of Caesar out of piety: to unburden him, to stir up his devotion, and to provide more tenderly for the faithful than the lay people would; and since this stands with evangelical poverty, it is clear that the [clergy]{g:clerus} ought not on that account to rely on property or pomp, but, to its own weariness, to minister to the poor from the alms conferred, in the more abject parts [of the service].[^4] Hence such alms cannot be given as perpetual alms however the clergy may have lived, but under the condition, at least tacit, that the clergy be truly a follower of the apostles in life and work. And so I believe that it must count all ministering to the poor wearisome to itself, and count it joyful, while it gives its labour to contemplation [188] and preaching, that faithful laymen take up that burden (as I have shown elsewhere by the example of Augustine and other saints).[^5] Hence, because human frailty is prone to leave its first [charity]{g:caritas} over alms, by growing proud, by feeding itself too sumptuously and by leaving the office of shepherd, therefore the apostles and the holier [members] of the church left such things. Hence it is a wonder if the conferring of a temporal thing is a privilege to the clergy; indeed, had the apostles asked it of Christ, it would have heard the answer given above: "You know not what you ask."[^6] Does not experience teach that such an [endowment]{g:dotacio} subjects those endowed to worldly care, [service]{g:servicium} and exaction, and consequently draws them away from the love of Christ and from the priestly ministry?

### 09-b ¶3 · 188.14–190.12  (4 sed/autem)

Nam habens in potestate tam [p. 190] faciliter proficiendi sibi et aliis, glorificando ecclesiam plus quam Cesar, non haberet excusacionem de negligenti desidia, **sed** scitur demonstracione certissima quod omne spirituale privilegium superat quodcunque corporale privilegium in infinitum, et sic non est privilegium clerum stipari seculari milicia, vesci in auro, uti fibula vel habere secundum mundanam gloriam splendencia ornamenta. Omnia enim ista gentes exquirunt (ut patet I° Machabeorum IX° et XI°), Christus **autem** et eius discipuli cum habere possent facillime hec omnia, si non repugnarent sue perfeccioni, quia repugnant, ideo omnia ista penitus respuerunt.

Hence the decree, C. 11 q. 1 [c. 29], recites from the letter of blessed Peter to Clement, written in this form: "It behoves you indeed to live irreproachably and to strive with the utmost zeal to cast off all the occupations of this life, that you be not a surety, nor become an advocate in lawsuits, nor be found at all entangled in any other occupation by occasion of worldly business. For Christ does not wish to ordain you today a judge or an examiner of secular business, lest, choked by the present cares of men, you be unable to be free for the word of God. But these works, which we say do not at all befit you, let laymen who are free for them perform for one another; and let no one occupy you away from those studies through which salvation is given to men."[^7] How then is it lawful for us to depart from this apostolic rule, we to whom it has been left [189] by God to convert the church of the gentiles to the perfect [law of Christ]{g:lex Christi}? For there is no room for authority or reason for dispensing; hence it is likely that the root of all the disturbance in the world is the clergy's turning aside from the law of Christ to civil life and traditions. But Christ has always left, in one part of his church or another, some faithful who desert the world; and in them perhaps, who have cast primacies aside, stand the faith and the continuance of holy mother church.[^8] And it is clear that the so-called endowment of the clergy in [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} is often not a privilege; and consequently, in taking these things from the clergy, it is not necessary that privileges be taken from it, but rather that, the hindrance being removed, the apostolic church be restored to the true primitive privileges; which I believe the [temporal lords]{g:temporales domini} are bound in the case to do, under pain of most grievous damnation.[^9] Do we believe it a great privilege for a [bishop]{g:episcopus} or an abbot that they have gallows and pit, in which the condemned are regularly put to death, and so of the prison and of other worldly privileges or insignia? Indeed, it would not be a privilege for a [prelate]{g:prelatus} to have the power that whatever he touched should at once be turned into gold; for then the air that carries [breath], which must be breathed in, would perish, and food and covering fitted to the body would perish.[^10] Hence, in detestation of this love of money, it is read that the blessed Evangelist turned branches of the wood into gold and stones of the shore into gems. That saint could at once have been, in the world's eyes, the richest and, according to worldly glory, the most illustrious, the most lordly and the most famous, had it not been repugnant to apostolic perfection and to the privilege granted by Christ; for otherwise he would have sinned gravely by omitting it.[^11] For one having it in his power so [190] easily to profit himself and others, glorifying the church more than Caesar, would have no excuse for negligent sloth; but it is known by most certain demonstration that every spiritual privilege surpasses any bodily privilege infinitely; and so it is not a privilege for the clergy to be thronged about by secular [knighthood]{g:milicia}, to feed in gold, to wear a buckle, or to have shining ornaments according to worldly glory.[^12] For all these things the heathens seek (as is clear, 1 Maccabees 10 and 11); but Christ and his disciples, although they could most easily have had all these things if they were not repugnant to their perfection, because they are repugnant, therefore utterly rejected all these things.[^13]
