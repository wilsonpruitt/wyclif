# *De potestate pape*, chapter 6, part 9: the duty of reproving superiors

## ¶1 · 133.26–135.11

> [p. 133] Ad tercium patet quod status denigratur vel serenatur solummodo concomitanter, et hoc quoad Deum, quomodo omne mortale denigrat, et super isto currunt multe leges hominum propter quas pusillanimes timent sibi nedum corripere sed peccata publice predicare, ut V decretalium De Hereticis cap. Cum ex iniuncto in fine prohibetur quod inferior publice corripiat patrem suum; et [p. 134] multe sunt leges huiusmodi, que omnes intelliguntur quod nemo debet illegitime fratrem suum corripere; non enim subiacet ordinacioni humane correpcionem fraternam quam Christus precipit Matth. XVIII cap. prohibere. Nec debet quis attemptare corripere suum prepositum, nisi propter defectum notorium, quem dicere debet ecclesie, quando prepositus notorie excidit a racione patris et pastoris. Quando scilicet nec gignit nec fovet subditum verbo Dei, tunc enim est tam expers racionis patris quam expers est quilibet a quocumque patris opere alienus; unde peccant qui mendaciter tales patres nominant vel pastores. Sed ut procedam minus intricabiliter et remocius a verbis scandali, securius servo me in verbis generalibus et condicionalibus, que ad auditorium et personam rei debent faciliter applicari efficaciter ad errantem, ut nullus papa nisi forte sibi conscius imponeret sibi raciones declaratas superius convenire autonomatice pseudopape. Unde non evidencius posset quis scandalizare se ipsum, quam sibi applicare sine pluri data evidencia verbum huiusmodi generale. Sic enim exierunt incipiendo a senioribus adulteram accusantes Joh. VIII. Sic et sacerdotes et Pharisei cognoverunt quod Christus de ipsis diceret in suis parabolis, Matth. XXI. Quod si probabiliter cognovero, quod predicans applicat ad me verbum huiusmodi generale, examinabo primo veritatem sentencie, secundo utrum in hoc sum conscius michi ipsi. Et tercio festinanter instar me ipsum corrigere et reddendo Deo gracias verba generalia nitar equaliter tollerare, quia nimis ostendunt suam stulticiam, qui ex talibus verbis communibus [p. 135] convincunt illegitimam correpcionem vel scandalum, verumtamen sciens peccatum quantumcumque alti prepositi esse notorium et periculosum ecclesie, debet ex fervore spiritus peccatorem quem credit aliunde non corrigi publice arguere cum Johanne, ut patet Mar. VI de Herode; oportet tamen in talibus quod homo a se ipso correpcionem incipiat et pura intencione procedat et utrobique sensui legis Domini innitatur, pro cuius exposicione nullus fidelium offendetur, ut patet de Paulo.

To the third it is clear that a state is blackened or made bright only concomitantly, and this as regards God, in the way that every mortal [sin] blackens. And on this point run many laws of men, on account of which the faint-hearted fear for themselves not only to reprove but to preach sins publicly; as in book 5 of the Decretals, *On Heretics*, chapter *Cum ex iniuncto*, at the end, it is forbidden that an inferior should publicly reprove his father.[^1] And [134] there are many laws of this kind, which are all understood [to mean] that no one ought to reprove his brother unlawfully; for it is not subject to human [ordinance]{g:ordinacio} to forbid the fraternal correction which Christ commands in Matthew 18. Nor ought anyone to attempt to reprove his [superior]{g:prepositi} except for a notorious fault, which he ought to tell to the [church]{g:ecclesia}, when the superior notoriously falls away from the character of father and shepherd: namely, when he neither begets nor nourishes his subject with the word of God; for then he is as devoid of the character of a father as anyone is who is a stranger to every work of a father. Hence those sin who lyingly name such men fathers or shepherds.[^2] But that I may proceed less intricately and further from words of scandal, I keep myself, more safely, to general and conditional words, which should be applied easily to the audience and the person concerned, and effectively to the one who errs, so that no [pope]{g:papa}, unless perhaps one conscious [of it] in himself, would take upon himself that the reasons declared above fit the pseudopope [by antonomasia]{g:autonomatice}.[^3] Hence no one could scandalize himself more evidently than by applying to himself, without more evidence given, a general word of this kind. For so those who accused the adulteress went out, beginning at the eldest (John 8). So too the priests and Pharisees knew that Christ was speaking of them in his parables (Matthew 21).[^4] And if I shall know with probability that the preacher applies to me a general word of this kind, I will examine first the truth of the [teaching]{g:sentencia}, second whether in this I am conscious [of it] in myself; and third I will press on speedily to correct myself, and, giving thanks to God, I will strive to bear general words with an even mind;[^5] for those show their folly too much who from such common words [135] make out unlawful reproof or scandal. Nevertheless one who knows that the sin of a superior, however high, is notorious and dangerous to the church ought, out of fervour of spirit, publicly to rebuke the sinner whom he believes will not otherwise be corrected, as John did, as is clear in Mark 6 concerning Herod. Yet in such cases a man must begin the correction from himself, and proceed with pure intention, and on both sides rely on the sense of the law of the Lord, at the exposition of which none of the faithful will be offended, as is clear of Paul.[^6]

## Notes

[^1]: **T/S/R.** "The third" is the third argument (131.30 ff.). *Solummodo* is Loserth's reading; the manuscript has *sodo* (133.27). *Cum ex iniuncto* is Innocent III's decretal in *Decretales Gregorii IX* 5.7.12, under *De haereticis*, whose close forbids *patrem suum etiam delinquentem reprehendere manifeste* (LEDGER P06i-1); Loserth's reference reads "Decr. Greg. IX, Lib. V, tit. XXXII, cap. 2" (133.32; LEDGER P06i-2, editor), which is the homonymous X 5.32.2 (*De novi operis nunciatione*, also *Quum ex iniuncto*), not the decretal meant. AB in marg.: *Ad tercium* (133.26); *Corripere potest subditus prepositum* (133.30).

[^2]: **S/T/C.** Matt. 18:15–17 (DR "tell the church", 18:17). B omits *patres* (134.13). *Racio patris*: what makes a man a father, here "the character of father".

[^3]: **T/C.** *Ad* (*ad auditorium*) is supplied by Loserth; the manuscripts omit it (134.16). B omits *et* (134.16) and reads *et rei* (134.17). The construal of *debent faciliter applicari efficaciter ad errantem* is doubtful: taken as two applications, easy to the hearers and effective to the one who errs; alternatively "should be applied easily and effectively to the one who errs". *Raciones declaratas superius*: presumably the marks of the false christ set out at 125.32 ff.

[^4]: **S.** John 8:9 (DR "went out one by one, beginning at the eldest"); Matt. 21:45 (DR "the chief priests and Pharisees … knew that he spoke of them"; Wyclif has *sacerdotes*, "priests").

[^5]: **C.** *Instar* is read as the deponent future of *instare*, "I will press on", matching *nitar* beside it; the plate shows *instar*. *Equaliter*, "equally", is rendered "with an even mind".

[^6]: **T/S.** *Pro cuius* is Loserth's correction; the manuscripts read *procius* (135.10). Mark 6:18 (DR "For John said to Herod: It is not lawful for thee to have thy brother's wife"). "As is clear of Paul": no reference is given; perhaps Gal. 2:11–14, Paul withstanding Peter to the face (our suggestion).
