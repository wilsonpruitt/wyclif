# *Dialogus*, chapter 12: belief, opinion and faith

## Falsehood and Truth · 23.2–25.3

> [p. 23] MENDACIUM. Video quod implicas multa inconveniencia in deliramentis stolidis que effaris; quia si in tali materia solum debet fidelis credere scripture sacre non crederet bullis papalibus vel diffinicioni curie romane, et sic omnis fides adhibenda sociis fidelibus conversantibus deperiret et multo magis non crederetur indulgenciis, literis episcopalibus vel literis fraternitatum de suffragio spiritali. Sed quis vivens vitam communem hominum non tunc de sua beatitudine desperaret. Ymmo contractus humani qui fiunt communiter juramentis et testificacionibus deperirent, et quod plus est hereditates secundum leges humanas, cum nemo crederet quod juvenis habuit talem patrem. Sed hoc nimis perturbaret rempublicam et legem politicam. VERITAS. Video quod a patre mendacii didicisti distincciones et equivocaciones subterfugere, et inconveniencia saltem tibi apparencia concludere videaris. Est autem credere equivocum ad cum hesitacione date sentencie adherere, vel sine hesitacione, opiniative tamen, adherere date sentencie, vel 3° adherere sentencia tanquam fidei que dicatur a veritate prima cum tamen aliunde non humanitus demonstretur. Et sic supponimus in toto isto colloquio credere fide sumi, non autem loquimur tam laxe in proposito sicut boicius, quod quecunque opinio quam quis habuerit sit sibi fides; sed loquimur de fide catholica, quoniam necesse est opinionem transcendere, cum facit certitudinem infallibilem, pro qua fidelis debet mortis periculo exponere [p. 24] vitam suam. Et sic opinative potest homo credere bullis papalibus; et specialiter si super illas rei experiencia addat opinionem; quia ille per se non faciunt fidem et multis hominibus parvam aut nullam credulitatem, cum tam papa quam sua curia falli poterunt et fallere propter lucrum et ignoranciam veritatis. Et sic dictum est communiter quod ad quesita sive proposita est quadruplex responcio adaptanda. Quedam enim sunt simpliciter supra opiniones hominum concedenda ut veritates fidei, veritates sensibiles, et veritates doctrinales ex certis principiis demonstrate. In secundo vero gradu sunt alique neganda simpliciter ut falsitates opposite veritatibus supradictis. 3° vero sunt alique dubitanda ut verba hominibus proposita que propter evidencias contrarias nec sciunt simpliciter esse vera nec sciunt simpliciter esse falsa. In 4° vero gradu sunt alique supponenda ut opinabilia citra vera primi modi, de quibus homo non est certus fide vel demonstracione vel eorum oppositis, licet non habeat evidenciam ad contrariam opinandum; ut habita experiencia de conversacione sacerdotis qui vivit catholice, supponi potest ab experiente quod sit membrum ecclesie, et tamen non debet credi ab aliquo tamquam fides, quia nescitur si sit predestinatus aut prescitus ad tartarum propter lapsum. Ideo debemus catholice relinquere famosum puerile principium quod omnis proposicio proposita sit concedenda, neganda, vel dubitanda. Cum proposicio mihi si salvabor nec concedo nec nego nec dubito, sed spero. Et de aliis hominibus nullum istorum habeo sed suppono secundum evidenciam quam habeo citra fidem. Et patet quod expertus de curie romane malicia potest loqui cum prudencia contra illam, [p. 25] licet non loquatur contra Christum et suam ecclesiam; quia non est fides quod illa curia sit militans ecclesia, licet quandoque acciderit quod fuit ecclesia hic in via.

FALSEHOOD. I see that you involve many [absurdities]{g:inconveniencia}[^1] in the stupid ravings that you utter; for if in such a matter the faithful man ought to believe holy scripture alone, he would not believe papal bulls or the definition of [the Roman curia]{g:curia-romana}, and so all the faith that should be placed in faithful companions living among us would perish, and much more would [indulgences]{g:indulgencie} not be believed, nor [bishops]{g:episcopus}' letters, nor [letters of fraternity]{g:litere-fraternitatum} concerning spiritual suffrage.[^2] But who, living the common life of men, would not then despair of his blessedness? Indeed, human contracts, which are commonly made by oaths and testimonies, would perish, and, what is more, inheritances according to human laws, since no one would believe that a young man had such a father. Now this would too greatly disturb the commonwealth and the political law.

TRUTH. I see that you have learned from the father of lies[^3] to evade distinctions and equivocations, and you seem to [infer]{g:concludere} absurdities that are at least apparent to you. And "to believe" is equivocal as regards: adhering with hesitation to a given [teaching]{g:sentencia}; or adhering to a given teaching without hesitation, yet as [opinion]{g:opinio}; or, thirdly, adhering to a teaching as to faith, which is said to come from the first truth, although it is not, for all that, demonstrated by human means from any other source.[^4] And so we suppose throughout this whole colloquy that "to believe" is taken for faith; but we do not speak so loosely in this matter as Boethius, [holding] that whatever opinion anyone holds is faith to him; but we speak of the catholic faith, since it must transcend opinion, because it produces infallible certainty, for which the faithful man ought to expose [24] his life to the peril of death.[^5] And so a man can believe papal bulls as opinion; and especially if experience of the matter adds opinion over and above them; for they do not of themselves produce faith, and in many men they produce little or no [credence]{g:credulitas}, since both the [pope]{g:papa} and his curia could be deceived, and deceive, for the sake of gain and through ignorance of the truth. And so it is commonly said that a fourfold response is to be fitted to questions or [propositions]{g:proponere}. For some are to be granted [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}, above the opinions of men, as the truths of faith, [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} truths, and doctrinal truths demonstrated from certain principles. And in the second degree some are to be denied without qualification, as the falsehoods opposed to the truths mentioned above. And thirdly some are to be doubted, as words proposed to men which, because of contrary [evidence]{g:evidencia}, they know neither to be true without qualification nor to be false without qualification. And in the fourth degree some are to be supposed, as matters of opinion falling short of the truths of the first mode, of which a man is not certain by faith or by demonstration, nor of their opposites, although he has no evidence for holding the contrary opinion; as, when one has experience of the [manner of life]{g:conversacio} of a [priest]{g:sacerdos} who lives catholicly, it can be supposed by the one who has the experience that he is a member of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, and yet this ought not to be believed by anyone as faith, because it is not known whether he is [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} or [foreknown]{g:prescitus} to hell on account of a fall. Therefore we ought, as catholics, to abandon the famous childish principle that every proposition proposed is to be granted, denied, or doubted.[^6] When it is put to me whether I shall be saved, I neither grant nor deny nor doubt, but hope.[^7] And concerning other men I have none of these, but I suppose according to the evidence that I have, [short of faith]{g:citra-fidem}. And it is clear that one who has experience of the malice of the Roman curia can speak with prudence against it, [25] although he does not [thereby] speak against Christ and his church; for it is not of faith that that curia is [the church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans}, although it has sometimes happened that it was the church here on [the way]{g:via}.

## Pollard's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 23.2: Mend. objects:
- 23.4: To accept scripture proof only would destroy belief in papal bulls and decisions.
- 23.7: And in indulgences, etc.
- 23.10: As questions of oaths or paternity could not be settled contracts and wills would be void.
- 23.15: Ver. answers:
- 23.17: This fails to distinguish credence in matters of opinion from faith in revelation.
- 24.6: Propositions may be (i) granted as matters of faith transcending opinion,
- 24.10: (ii) similarly denied,
- 24.12: (iii) doubted,
- 24.15: (iv) taken as true, but not certainly so.
- 24.23: There are other moods beside assent, denial, and doubt. As, e.g. of one's own salvation, hope.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Inconveniencia*: what does not fit, in argument the absurd consequence, in affairs the disorder that follows (GLOSSARY, CONTEXTUAL). Here, and at 23.16, the logical sense, so "absurdities".
[^2]: **R.** *Literae fraternitatum*: letters by which a religious order, chiefly the friars, admitted a lay benefactor to a share in the order's prayers and good works ("spiritual suffrage") (LEDGER D12-1).
[^3]: **S.** John 8:44, of the devil: "he is a liar, and the father thereof."
[^4]: **C/T.** *Credere equivocum ad*: *ad* governs the three infinitive phrases that follow, the senses of *credere*; C reads *aliud* for *ad*. In the second sense B (corrected), B¹, C read *tantum* for *tamen* ("only as opinion"). In the third the print has *adherere sentencia*, which does not construe with *tanquam fidei*; B, B¹, C read *sibi* ("adhere to it as to faith"), with the same sense.
[^5]: **C/T.** *Credere fide sumi*: "'to believe' is taken for faith"; B, B¹, C read *fidem*. B, B¹, C add *loquitur* after *boicius*, and B, B¹, C read *quam* for *quoniam*: "the catholic faith, which must transcend opinion". The Boethius reference is unidentified; Pollard gives none (LEDGER D12-2).
[^6]: **R.** The "childish principle" is the rule of the scholastic disputation *de obligationibus*, in which the respondent must answer each proposition put to him by granting, denying, or doubting it (LEDGER D12-3).
[^7]: **T.** The print reads *Cum proposicio mihi si salvabor*, which lacks a construction; we translate *proposito* (B, B¹, C), an ablative absolute: "when it is put to me whether I shall be saved". B¹ reads *an* for *si*; *spero* in B is corrected by another hand from *suppono*.
