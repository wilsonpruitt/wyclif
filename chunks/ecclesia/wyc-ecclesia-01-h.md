# *De ecclesia*, chapter 1, part 8: the heads of the church; the saints are members, not heads; hoping with misgiving

## ¶1 · 22.6–25.22

> [p. 22] Hic dicitur quod idem corpus ecclesie habet multa capita, ut quelibet particularis ecclesia habet ad minimum tria capita, scilicet divinitatem, Christi humanitatem et capitaneum illius ecclesie. Ecclesia autem universalis que est totum corpus Christi misticum habet duo capita, scilicet caput extrinsecum que est divinitas et caput intrinsecum que est Christi humanitas, et modo ineffabili divinitas et humanitas Christi sunt duo capita tocius ecclesie et cum hoc eciam unum caput: Sicut filius et spiritus sanctus sunt unus paraclitus, intelligendo principative vel essencialiter, et tamen sunt duo paracliti, intelligendo personaliter (cum Joh. XIV°, 16 dicitur: Ego rogabo patrem et alium paraclitum dabit vobis): sic iste due nature sunt duo capita ecclesie, et tamen sunt personaliter unum caput. Particulares autem ecclesie, sicut fuit pusillus grex cui presidebat sanctus Petrus, non erant nisi iuvencule tocius matris ecclesie, sicut exponit Gregorius XIX° Moralium cap. VIII, declarans etatem ecclesie quomodo in principio fuit soror parvula, carens uberibus predicacionis. Unde sic infert: Universe quippe ecclesie que unam catholicam faciunt adolescentule vocantur. Unde beatum Petrum et alias particulares ecclesias vocat membra illius ecclesie quam leenam nominat, Christum vero leonem (ut [p. 23] declarat XXVIII° Moralium cap. XI), dicens quod, sicut membra corporis non sunt ipsum corpus, sic particulares ecclesie non sunt illa sancta ecclesia, sed (ut docet apostolus i° Cor. XII, 12) membra eius. Istis positis dico quod nec dominus Ardmacanus nec aliquis doctor catholicus debet asserere quod aliquis christianus sit caput universalis sed particularis ecclesie, cum non sit color: Hoc caput est illius ecclesie, ergo hoc est caput illius ecclesie; nec est color: Si hoc est caput partis illius ecclesie, ergo hoc est caput illius ecclesie. Sic enim omnis vir foret caput Dei, sicut omnis prelatus inferior foret caput sue matricis ecclesie. Sed apostolus contradicit istis arguciis, I° Cor. XI, 3, dicens: Volo autem vos scire quod omnis viri caput est Christus, caput autem mulieris vir, caput vero Christi Deus. In quo dicto patet ordo capitum et deteccio illarum fallaciarum. Quia aliter eque diceret econtra quod caput Christi et Dei foret tam vir quam mulier. Ecclesia itaque catholica, secundum beatum Gregorium super illo Matthei XXIII°, 34: Ecce mitto ad vos sapientes et scribas, est vinea domini plantata a principio mundi usque ad finem fructificans, quotquot sanctos gignit tot palmites producit. Et sicut nemo est naturaliter caput matris sue sed pars nisi Christus: sic nullus aliorum sanctorum est caput matris ecclesie sed membrum. Sic enim dicit beatus Gregorius (ubi supra) quod beatus Job, beatus Petrus et ceteri precones matris ecclesie sunt os eius, et breviter, quilibet [p. 24] aliorum sanctorum proporcionaliter ad analogiam virtutis sue et proprietatis membri corporalis est pars sancte matris ecclesie. Unde beatus Gregorius IV° Moralium cap. XXXII narrat quomodo a principio mundi Deus ordinavit sponse sue patriarchas secundum virtutum principia tamquam membra radicalia ecclesie inserenda, ut patet de innocencia Abel, de mundicia Enoch, de castimonia coniugali Isaac, de tolerancia Jacob, de spe Noe, de obediencia Abrahe, de paciencia Job, de castitate Joseph, de mansuetudine Moysi, de fiducia Josue, de humilitate David, de zelo Helye, et sic discurrendo per omnes species virtutum; que secundum etatem seculi habuerunt in excessu notabili aliquos patriarchas eorum principia in quorum labores sponsus ordinavit posteros intrare (ut dicitur Johannis IV°). Nec solum isti sed omnes eorum imitacionis filii sunt eadem membra sancte matris ecclesie quod, si alicui desit virtutis officium, deerit sibi indubie quod sit sancte matris ecclesie membrum. Nullus autem eorum unquam presumpsit dicere quod sit caput et dominus matris sue, quia indubie, si aliquis homo sit caput tocius generis christiani (quod est sancta mater ecclesia), tunc ipse est Christus, quia solus Christus est huius generacionis principium; alii autem in ipso sunt palmites. Necesse ergo est hodie seminare istam fidem ecclesie, cum multi ex ignorancia occasione male accepta, dormiendo in nocte ignorancie uruntur a sancta matre ecclesia que est pulchra ut luna. Uruntur dico secundum inferiorem hominem pravitate heretica, quia omnes qui dicunt: Ecce hic, et ecce illic est Christus caput ecclesie, solum eius vicarium demonstrantes. [p. 25] Oportet autem volentes proficere vel iudicare in ista materia primo cognoscere quiditatem sancte matris ecclesie ad modum loquendi scripture sacre et sanctorum qui sensum eius elucidant. Oportet secundo cognoscere materiam predestinacionis divine, quanto sit nobis incognita. Et tunc tercio possunt discernere, si sit sanum vel stolidum quod asseramus absque formidine nos esse membra sancte matris ecclesie, hoc est, de numero predestinatorum. Debemus autem hoc sperare et credere sed cum formidine de gracia perseverancie: ideo talis fides habet formidinem annexam quam non habet fides qua credimus nos esse in gracia secundum presentem iusticiam, cum casus futuri sit nobis incercior quam actus caritatis vel habitus pro presenti. Unde gentilitati inserte loco Judeorum in ecclesia dicit apostolus Rom. XI°, 20. Noli altum sapere, sed time. Timor ergo de casu a finali perseverancia stat cum spe, et cum caritas secundum presentem iusticiam foras mittit timorem de peccato habito pro presenti, videtur tamen quod omnis homo debet habere pro suo tempore specialem revelacionem, quia aliter non foret in firma spe excuciente omnem timorem.

To this the answer is that the same body of the [church]{g:ecclesia} has many heads, so that any
particular church has at least three heads, namely the [divinity]{g:divinitas}, the humanity of Christ,
and the captain of that church.[^1] But the universal church, which is the whole [mystical body of
Christ]{g:corpus-christi-misticum}, has two heads, namely the extrinsic head, which is the divinity,
and the intrinsic head, which is the humanity of Christ; and in an ineffable way the divinity and the
humanity of Christ are two heads of the whole church and, with this, also one head: just as the Son and
the Holy Spirit are one Paraclete, understanding [it] as principle or essentially, and yet are two
Paracletes, understanding [it] personally (since in John 14:16 it is said: "I will ask the Father: and
he shall give you another Paraclete"), so these two natures are two heads of the church, and yet are
personally one head.[^2] And particular churches, such as was the "little flock" over which St Peter
presided, were nothing but young girls of the whole mother church, as Gregory expounds in *Moralia*
XIX, chapter 8, declaring the age of the church, how in the beginning she was a "little sister",
lacking the breasts of preaching.[^3] Hence he infers thus: "For all the churches which make up the one
catholic [church] are called young maidens."[^4] Hence he calls the blessed Peter and the other
particular churches members of that church which he names the lioness, Christ being the lion (as [23]
he declares in *Moralia* XXVIII, chapter 11), saying that, as the members of the body are not the body
itself, so particular churches are not that holy church but (as the apostle teaches, 1 Corinthians
12:12) its members.[^5]

These things laid down, I say that neither the lord of Armagh nor any catholic doctor ought to assert
that any Christian is head of the universal [church], but [only] of a particular church; since there is
no colour [in]: "This head is of that church, therefore this is the head of that church"; nor is there
colour [in]: "If this is head of a part of that church, therefore this is head of that church."[^6] For
so every man would be head of God, as every lower [prelate]{g:prelatus} would be head of his mother
church.[^7] But the apostle contradicts these [quibbles]{g:argucie}, 1 Corinthians 11:3, saying: "But I
would have you know that the head of every man is Christ: and the head of the woman is the man: and the
head of Christ is God." In which saying the order of heads is plain, and the exposure of those
[fallacies]{g:fallacia}. For otherwise he would equally say, conversely, that the head of Christ and of
God would be both man and woman.[^8] The catholic church, then, according to the blessed Gregory on that
[word] of Matthew 23:34, "Behold I send to you wise men and scribes", is the vineyard of the Lord,
planted from the beginning of the world and bearing fruit to the end: as many saints as it begets, so
many branches it puts forth.[^9] And as no one is by nature head of his mother but a part, except
Christ, so none of the other saints is head of mother church but a member. For so the blessed Gregory
says (in the place cited) that blessed Job, blessed Peter and the other heralds of mother church are
her mouth; and, briefly, each [24] of the other saints, proportionally to the analogy of his virtue and
of the property of a bodily member, is a part of holy mother church.[^10]

Hence the blessed Gregory, in *Moralia* IV, chapter 32, relates how from the beginning of the world God
ordained for his [bride]{g:sponsa} patriarchs according to the beginnings of the virtues, as root
members to be grafted into the church: as is clear of the innocence of Abel, the cleanness of Enoch, the
conjugal chastity of Isaac, the endurance of Jacob, the hope of Noah, the obedience of Abraham, the
patience of Job, the chastity of Joseph, the meekness of Moses, the confidence of Joshua, the humility
of David, the zeal of Elijah, and so running through all the species of virtues;[^11] which [virtues],
according to the age of the world, had in notable excess certain patriarchs as their beginnings, into
whose labours the bridegroom ordained that those who came after should enter (as is said in John
4).[^12] Nor only these, but all the sons of their imitation are the same members of holy mother church;
so that, if the office of virtue is lacking to anyone, it will without doubt be lacking to him that he
is a member of holy mother church.[^13] But none of them ever presumed to say that he was head and
[lord]{g:dominus} of his mother; for without doubt, if any man is head of the whole Christian race
(which is holy mother church), then he is Christ, since Christ alone is the beginning of this
generation; and the others are branches in him.[^14] It is therefore necessary today to sow this faith
of the church, since many, out of ignorance, on an occasion wrongly taken, sleeping in the night of
ignorance, are burned by holy mother church, which is "fair as the moon". They are burned, I say,
according to the lower man, by [heretical depravity]{g:heretica-pravitas}: for all who say "See here,
and see there is Christ, the head of the church", pointing only to his vicar.[^15]

[25] And those who wish to make progress or to judge in this matter must first know the
[quiddity]{g:quiditas} of holy mother church, after the manner of speaking of holy scripture and of the
saints who elucidate its sense. They must, second, know the matter of divine predestination, how far it
is unknown to us. And then, third, they can discern whether it is sound or stupid that we should assert
without [misgiving]{g:formido} that we are members of holy mother church, that is, of the number of the
[predestinate]{g:predestinatus}.[^16] And we ought to hope and believe this, but with misgiving about the
grace of perseverance: therefore such faith has misgiving annexed, which the faith by which we believe
ourselves to be in grace according to present justice does not have, since a future fall is more
uncertain to us than the act or habit of [charity]{g:caritas} for the present.[^17] Hence the apostle
says to the gentiles, grafted into the church in place of the Jews, Romans 11:20: "Be not highminded,
but fear." Fear, therefore, of a fall from final perseverance stands with hope; and although charity
according to present justice "casteth out fear" of sin had for the present, yet it seems that every
man must have, for his time, a special revelation, since otherwise he would not be in a firm hope that
shakes off all fear.[^18]

## Notes

[^1]: **T/G.** *Hic dicitur*: the answer to Armagh's objection (A's margin: *Responsio*). A reads
    *deitatem* (22.8) and *deitas* (22.12–13), "the Godhead"; GLOSSARY keeps *deitas* and *divinitas*
    apart, and we translate the print. *Capitaneus*: "captain" (glossary proposal).

[^2]: **S/T.** John 14:16 DR. *Principative vel essencialiter*: as one principle, in the one
    essence, against "personally", as two persons. B *principative et* (22.16); B lacks *eciam* (22.13).
    B's margin calls the divinity the extrinsic and Christ's humanity the intrinsic head.

[^3]: **S.** Luke 12:32, DR "little flock"; Song 8:8, DR "Our sister is little, and hath no
    breasts". Gregory, *Moralia* XIX: Wyclif's cap. 8, Loserth's cap. 12 (the Maurist number;
    PL 76:108A–B; Opp. I, 613; LEDGER E01h-1). *Iuvencule*, "young girls", is kept apart from Gregory's *adolescentule*.

[^4]: **S.** Gregory's sentence glosses Song 1:2, DR "young maidens have loved thee"; wording
    unchecked (LEDGER E01h-2, `[verify]`).

[^5]: **S/C.** Lion and lioness: Wyclif's *Moralia* XXVIII.11; the passage is XVIII.35.56 (PL 76:68C–69A), as
    Loserth gives; XXVIII.10–11 has no lion (LEDGER E01h-3). *Beatum Petrum et alias particulares ecclesias*: Peter is counted as a particular
    church. 1 Cor. 12:12 is a reference, not a quotation.

[^6]: **C/T.** *Non est color*: "there is no colour", no plausible ground (GLOSSARY *color*). *Caput universalis sed particularis*: we supply "[only]": no one should claim
    headship of the universal church, only of a particular one. Latin has no article, so the two
    inferences pass from "a head belonging to that church" and from "head of a part" to "the head of
    that church"; these are the *fallacie* named next. A reads at 23.7–8 *Nec si hoc est caput illius
    partis ecclesie, ergo*; B has the clause in the margin.

[^7]: **C.** *Matrix ecclesia*: a cathedral or metropolitan church, kept apart from *mater ecclesia*,
    "mother church", of which the whole sentence makes a pun. The inference to "head of God" runs by
    1 Cor. 11:3 read backwards, as the next sentences say.

[^8]: **S.** 1 Cor. 11:3 DR, which agrees with Wyclif's Latin. *Deteccio*: "exposure". B's margin:
    *Econtra*.

[^9]: **S.** Gregory, *Hom. in Ev.* I.19 (Loserth: Opp. I, 1510; LEDGER E01h-4), the homily on the
    vineyard (Matt. 20:1; PL 76:1154B), paraphrased; Wyclif ties it to Matt. 23:34. There Vg has *prophetas, et sapientes, et scribas* (DR
    "prophets and wise men and scribes"); Wyclif lacks *prophetas*, and we translate his Latin.
    *Palmites*: "branches", as in John 15:5.

[^10]: **S/R.** *Ubi supra*: not the homily, which has no "mouth"; Peter as the lioness's mouth is
    *Moralia* XVIII.35.56 (PL 76:68D, *hujus leaenae ori*); Job as "mouth" is not found (LEDGER E01h-4). A's margin: the members of holy
    church, the bride of Christ, are the saints alone. B *sunt* (23.29).

[^11]: **S/T.** Gregory, *Moralia* IV.32.63 (PL 75:671C–672A; Loserth: Opp. I, 132; LEDGER E01h-5), on
    the just from the world's beginning (Abel, Enoch, Noah, Abraham, Isaac, Jacob); the virtues are
    named after the Preface 6.13 (PL 75:524B–C), with David's humility and Elijah's zeal from X.6.9 (PL
    75:925A–B); Joseph's chastity is Wyclif's. Loserth notes that the
    MSS have *cap. X* and that he has corrected it. AA₁ lack *de spe Noe … de paciencia Job*
    (24.9–10); the print follows B. *Radicalia … inserenda*: the figure of grafting from Rom. 11, used
    again at 25.15.

[^12]: **C/S.** *Que … eorum principia*: we take *que* as the species of virtues, with *eorum*
    loosely for *earum*; B *quo secundum* (24.13). John 4:38, DR "you have entered into their labours".

[^13]: **T.** B *in dubio*, "in doubt" (24.18–19, 24.21), for *indubie*. *Imitacionis filii*: sons
    by imitation, not by descent.

[^14]: **T/S.** B *prescripsit* (24.20) for *presumpsit*. *Principium … palmites*: John 15:5, "I am the
    vine: you the branches", taken up from the vineyard above.

[^15]: **S/C.** Song 6:9, DR "fair as the moon"; the burning moon is Ps. 120:6, DR "nor the moon by
    night": those who take scandal from the church are, as it were, moonstruck. *Secundum inferiorem
    hominem*: in the lower, fleshly man. The *quia* clause has no main verb, and we leave it
    incomplete. Luke 17:23 (Loserth), DR "See here, and see there"; Wyclif adds *est Christus* as in
    Matt. 24:23, and we translate his Latin.

[^16]: **T/R.** A and B margins: *Triplex epilogus*. B's reader asks whether Christ, head of the
    universal church, should also be called its member. A₁ *qui secundum sensum* (25.4); B lacks
    *membra* (25.8). *Formido*: the misgiving that qualifies assent (GLOSSARY).

[^17]: **C/R.** *Casus futuri*: a future fall from grace. *Secundum presentem iusticiam*: justice as
    one now has it. B's margin restates the point: the wayfarer believes with misgiving that he is a
    member of the church, but believes without it that he is now in grace.

[^18]: **S/C/R.** Rom. 11:20 DR. 1 John 4:18, Vg *perfecta caritas foras mittit timorem* (DR "perfect
    charity casteth out fear"); Wyclif lacks *perfecta*. *Videtur tamen*: the chapter closes on a
    difficulty, not a conclusion. B *fiat cum spe* (25.18). Loserth notes that Hus, *De ecclesia*, capp.
    1–3, draws on this chapter (LEDGER E01h-6).
