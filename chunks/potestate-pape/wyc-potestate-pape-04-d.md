# *De potestate pape*, chapter 4, part 4: many bishops over one people

## ¶1 · 72.16–74.11

> [p. 72] Sed triplex ponitur causa quare tempore legis gracie sacerdocium non sic vadit iure hereditario. Prima quia sacerdotes nostri et specialiter summi pontifices debent caste vivere instar Christi et per consequens non relinquere sue carnali soboli ius ad quicquam. Secunda causa est quia Christus noluit eius vicarios habere seculare dominium vel honorem sed omnino specie hereditatis celestis, que solum datur patrissantibus in moribus spiritualibus filiis adoptivis. Tercia causa est ut nos tempore legis gracie spiritualius procedentes, discamus eligere meliores et amare duplicem virginitatem, et quod spiritualis prepositura vel dignitas sacerdotis non sit carnalis sed spiritualis ex filiacione in moribus; cum hereditas illa sit cuicumque promerenti indifferens. Necesse est igitur quod summus sacerdos noster sit in se totus collectus circa edificacionem ecclesie et non [p. 73] divisus ad placendum uxori sue; ut loquitur Apostolus I. Cor. VII. Et hec racio quare oportuit legem illam cessare et novam introduci, ut dicitur Heb. VII. Sed illud calliditate dyaboli est frustratum, cum episcopi nostri gravius fornicantur cum seculo. Sed quarto obicitur per hoc quod iuxta dictam sentenciam contingit duos episcopos super eodem populo legittime ordinari; quod concedo; cum Christus animarum necessario est episcopus cuiuslibet populi christiani et habet multos episcopos vicarios super eodem populo adiutores. Ipse tamen excellenter et differenter est episcopus ecclesie triumphantis et tocius ecclesie militantis. Similiter, eedem persone, quas Petrus, Paulus et ceteri apostoli ordinarunt episcopos, non excluserunt eos a superintendencia populi sed iuverunt, ut beatus Clemens quem beatus Petrus ordinavit prudenter ut oportet dicere episcopum Romanorum fuit episcopatui Petri nedum conpossibilis, sed adiutor, nam in quadam epistola beati Clementis directa Jacobo Jerusalimitano sic scribitur: Beatus Petrus morte sibi appropinquante in collegio fratrum positus ipsum Clementem per manum accipiens dixit, hunc Clementem episcopum vobis ordino, qui ab inicio mecum fuit. Nec dubium quin tanti capitis ordinacio quam oportuit ex Spiritu Sancto procedere non instituisset Clementem episcopum, nisi de facto foret episcopus; ymo videtur quod sicut actus episcopales non repugnant tam simul quam paulative super eodem populo, sic nec [p. 74] pontificum dignitates, sicut videtur de Christo et Petro, cum simul cum eo fuit summus pontifex. Sic enim beatus Johannes fuit episcopus continue ecclesie Asyane et tamen habuit septem episcopos adiutores. Actus ergo spirituales non repugnant et per consequens potestates vel habitus respectu eiusdem populi. Sed tota repugnancia videtur civilitas et dominium, quod haberent in populo. Hec enim fuit racio quare Abraham et Loth scilicet propter proprietates possessionum non potuerunt habitare simul, ut Gen. XIII.

But a threefold cause is assigned why, in the time of [the law of grace]{g:lex-gracie}, the priesthood does not pass in this way by hereditary right. The first, because our [priests]{g:sacerdos}, and especially the [highest pontiffs]{g:summi-pontifices}, ought to live chastely after the pattern of Christ, and consequently not to leave to their carnal offspring a right to anything. The second cause is that Christ did not will his vicars to have secular [lordship]{g:dominium} or honour, but [to have] wholly the likeness of the heavenly inheritance, which is given only to spiritual adoptive sons who take after their father in conduct.[^1] The third cause is that we, proceeding more spiritually in the time of the law of grace, may learn to choose the better men and to love the twofold virginity, and [learn] that the spiritual [office of superior]{g:prepositura} or dignity of a priest is not carnal but spiritual, from sonship in conduct; since that inheritance lies open to whoever merits it, without distinction. It is necessary, therefore, that our high priest be wholly gathered in himself about the edification of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, and not [73] divided in order to please his wife, as the Apostle says, 1 Cor. 7. And this is the reason why that law had to cease and a new one be brought in, as is said in Heb. 7.[^2] But that has been frustrated by the cunning of the devil, since our bishops commit fornication more grievously with the world. But it is objected fourthly, [by appeal to]{g:obicitur-per} this: that according to the said [teaching]{g:sentencia} it comes about that two bishops are lawfully ordained over the same people. This I grant; since Christ is necessarily bishop of souls of every Christian people,[^3] and has many vicar bishops as helpers over the same people. Yet he is bishop, excellently and in a different way, of [the church triumphant]{g:ecclesia-triumphans} and of the whole church militant. Likewise, the same persons whom Peter, Paul and the other apostles ordained bishops did not exclude them from the oversight of the people, but helped them;[^4] as blessed Clement, whom blessed Peter ordained (prudently, as one must say) bishop of the Romans, was not only [compossible]{g:compossibilis} with Peter's episcopate but a helper to it. For in a certain letter of blessed Clement addressed to James of Jerusalem it is written thus: "Blessed Peter, when death was drawing near to him, placed in the company of the brethren, took Clement himself by the hand and said, This Clement I ordain bishop for you, who was with me from the beginning."[^5] Nor is there doubt that the ordination of so great a [head]{g:caput}, which had to proceed from the Holy Spirit, would not have instituted Clement bishop unless he were in fact a bishop; indeed, it seems that just as episcopal acts are not incompatible over the same people, whether at once or by turns, so neither are [74] the dignities of pontiffs, as appears of Christ and Peter, since he was highest pontiff together with him.[^6] For thus blessed John was continuously bishop of the church of Asia, and yet had seven bishops as helpers. Spiritual acts therefore are not incompatible, and consequently neither are [powers]{g:potestas} or habits with respect to the same people. But the whole incompatibility seems to be the [civil standing]{g:civilitas} and lordship which they would have among the people. For this was the reason why Abraham and Lot, namely because of the property of their possessions, could not dwell together, as Gen. 13.[^7]

## ¶2 · 74.12–76.11

> [p. 74] Tercio confirmatur de rectoribus et suis episcopis, qui sunt simul episcopi eiusdem parrochie, et Deus scit quis quoad Deum est suo adiutore superior, quia certum est quod ille qui magis spiritualiter prodest parrochie est episcopus superior quoad Deum; et certum est quod profectus suus est extensior. Nec valet dicere quod sacerdos talis non est episcopus, quia iuxta decretum Jeronimi positum 95 distinccione Olim; Olim, inquit, erat idem presbiter, qui et episcopus, et antequam dyaboli instinctu studia in religione fierent et diceretur in populis, ego sum Pauli, ego Apollo, ego sum Cephe, communi presbiterorum consilio ecclesie gubernabantur. Postquam autem unusquisque eos quos baptizaverat suos esse putabat, non Christi, decretum est ut unus de presbiteris superponeretur et scismatum semina tollerentur. Nec est fingendum quod propter adinventas preposituras secundum dignitates reputacionis humane fit novus ordo supra sacerdotum, sed manent septem ordines antiqui humanitus alterati. Non enim princeps secularis per viam dotacionis et maioritatis reputacionis mundane [p. 75] instituit novum ecclesiasticum sacramentum. Multi igitur sunt episcopi super eodem populo se iuvantes, quia aliter non liceret habere episcopum suffraganeum, cum ille sit episcopus non Nullatensis, sed illius loci quem incolit et illius populi, cui superintendit. Et idem est iudicium de Petro et Paulo qui simul erant Romani episcopi. Paulus enim erat episcopus sicut Scarioth et omnes apostoli, sed cuius populi si non gentium quas tantum edificando superintendit predicacionibus, miraculorum faccionibus et scriptura, et hec consumavit excellenter et finaliter cum Romanis. Ideo non beatus Petrus sed ipse fuit precipue episcopus Romanorum. Sic igitur non repugnat sed consonat multos angelos deputari ad unius custodiam, sed fides scripture testatur Deum, Paulum et ceteros apostolos habere in eodem officio spirituali plurimos adiutores; sic est de spirituali episcopatus officio senciendum; sed oportet illum excellere qui spiritualiter magis servit. Nec est credendum institucionibus civilibus vel ficticiis de repugnancia iurisdicionum, nisi de quanto fundata fuerint in scriptura, quomodo preeminencia Petri super ceteros apostolos stat in universali iurisdiccione super totam militantem ecclesiam, aut quod certa potestas iurisdicionalis debet esse in alios apostolos, per illam potestatem ad locum et populum limitata. Nam ceteri apostoli edificarunt ecclesiam in regionibus variis, non petita licencia a Petro, Paulus vero universalius quam Petrus convertit gentes, eciam antequam vidit Petrum, ut patet Actuum IX et infra, Galat. [p. 76] primo et infra; Joh. in Asya minori diu post Petri martirium. Nec oportuit vel decuit illum, qui tam crebras revelaciones Spiritus Sancti habuit; quomodo agendum fuerit, querere instruccionem a suo conservo et consocio Petro vel eius vicario qui instruccione eiusdem magistri indiguit. Et idem est iudicium de sancto Thoma, quando Christus in foro venalium precepit sibi, quod transiret in Yndiam, et sic de ceteris apostolis, ut patet ex fideli eorum cronica quam legit et sollempnizat ecclesia.

Thirdly, it is confirmed from rectors and their bishops, who are at once bishops of the same parish; and God knows which of them, as regards God, is superior to his helper, for it is certain that he who profits the parish more spiritually is the superior bishop as regards God; and it is certain that his profit reaches further. Nor does it avail to say that such a priest is not a bishop, for according to Jerome's decree placed in the 95th distinction, *Olim*: "Formerly," he says, "the [presbyter]{g:presbiter} was the same as the bishop; and before, by the prompting of the devil, factions arose in religion and it was said among the peoples, I am of Paul, I of Apollo, I am of Cephas, the churches were governed by the common counsel of the presbyters. But after each one thought that those whom he had baptized were his own and not Christ's, it was decreed that one of the presbyters should be set over [the rest], and the seeds of schisms be removed."[^8] Nor is it to be feigned that, because of offices of superior devised according to the dignities of human reputation, a new order is made above that of priests; rather the seven ancient orders remain, altered in human fashion. For a secular prince does not, by way of [endowment]{g:dotacio} and the [greatness]{g:majoritas} of worldly reputation, [75] institute a new ecclesiastical sacrament. There are, therefore, many bishops over the same people helping one another, since otherwise it would not be lawful to have a suffragan bishop; for he is a bishop not ["of Nowhere"]{g:episcopus-nullatensis}, but of that place which he inhabits and of that people over which he has oversight.[^9] And the same judgement holds of Peter and Paul, who were at the same time bishops of Rome. For Paul was a bishop, as Iscariot was and all the apostles; but of what people, if not of the gentiles, over whom he had oversight, building them up so greatly by preachings, the working of miracles and scripture? And these he brought to completion, excellently and finally, with the Romans. Therefore not blessed Peter but he was chiefly bishop of the Romans.[^10] Thus, then, it is not incompatible but consonant that many angels be deputed to the guard of one [person]; and the faith of scripture testifies that God, Paul and the other apostles have very many helpers in the same spiritual office; so one must think of the spiritual office of the episcopate; but he must excel who serves more spiritually.[^11] Nor is credence to be given to civil or feigned institutions concerning the incompatibility of [jurisdictions]{g:iurisdiccio}, except in so far as they are founded in scripture: [such as] how the preeminence of Peter over the other apostles consists in a universal jurisdiction over the whole church militant, or that a definite jurisdictional power must extend to the other apostles, [limited]{g:limitare} to place and people by that power.[^12] For the other apostles built up the church in various regions without asking [licence]{g:licencia} from Peter; and Paul converted the gentiles more universally than Peter, even before he saw Peter, as is clear from Acts 9 and following, Gal. [76] 1 and following; [and] John in Asia Minor long after Peter's martyrdom. Nor was it needful or fitting for him who had such frequent revelations of the Holy Spirit as to how he should act to seek instruction from his fellow servant and companion Peter, or from his vicar, who himself needed the instruction of the same master. And the same judgement holds of Saint Thomas, when Christ in the market of the sellers commanded him to go over into India; and so of the other apostles, as is clear from their faithful chronicle, which the church reads and keeps solemnly.[^13]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 72.16: Reasons against its present use.
- 73.7: There may be more than one bishop in a diocese if all work together usefully.
- 74.12: No essential distinction between priests and bishops.

- 75.21: We need not concern ourselves about clash of jurisdictions.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *sed omnino specie hereditatis celestis*: the ablative *specie* hangs loosely on *habere*; taken as "to have … the likeness (form) of the heavenly inheritance". It could also be read adverbially, "but altogether in the manner of the heavenly inheritance". *Patrissantibus* ("who take after their father") is the medieval verb *patrissare*.

[^2]: **S.** 1 Cor. 7:33 (DR "he that is with a wife is solicitous … how he may please his wife. And he is divided"); Heb. 7:12 ("the priesthood being translated, it is necessary that a translation also be made of the law"). Neither is quoted; Wyclif gives the sense.

[^3]: **S.** 1 Pet. 2:25, "the shepherd and bishop of your souls". The point of *animarum … episcopus* is that Christ's oversight never excludes his vicars'.

[^4]: **C.** The subject is *eedem persone* (those ordained), and *eos* the apostles who ordained them: the new bishops did not shut the apostles out of their oversight but helped them. A reading with *eos* = the new bishops (the apostles did not exclude them) would need *persone* as object, which the nominative does not allow.

[^5]: **S.** The pseudo-Clementine *Epistola Clementis ad Iacobum* (Clement I, *Epist. decret.*), cited by Loserth from Migne, *PG* 1, 464 (LEDGER P04d-1); to be checked.

[^6]: **C.** *cum simul cum eo fuit summus pontifex*: Peter is the subject, Christ the *eo*: Peter was highest pontiff at the same time as Christ. *Paulative*, "little by little", is set against *simul*: taken as successive tenure.

[^7]: **S.** Gen. 13:6 (DR "for their substance was great, and they could not dwell together"). John's seven bishops are the angels of the seven churches of Asia, Apoc. 1–3.

[^8]: **S.** Jerome, in *Decretum* D.95 c.5 *Olim* (Loserth: "Decreti prima pars, dist. XCV, cap. V"; the modern form is ours) (LEDGER P04d-2). The quoted strife is 1 Cor. 1:12. **T.** *superponeretur* is Loserth's correction; A and B read *supponeretur* ("be set under").

[^9]: **R.** For the titular bishop "of Nowhere" (*Nullatensis*), consecrated without a see, Loserth compares *De ecclesia* p. 583 and *Sermones* II, 65 (LEDGER P04d-3).

[^10]: **T.** Loserth notes at 75.13 that the manuscripts lack *non* ("Codd.: non deest"), so a *non* on that line is his supplement; the likeliest is *non beatus Petrus*, translated as printed. **S.** Judas's "bishopric", Acts 1:20.

[^11]: **C.** The second *sed* (*sed fides scripture testatur*) does not oppose what precedes; it is rendered "and". The period runs as a comparison ("as … the faith of scripture testifies …, so one must think …"), which a lost *sicut* would make explicit.

[^12]: **C.** The *quomodo* and *quod* clauses give examples of the feigned institutions, not of what scripture founds. *Limitata* agrees with *potestas*: the power that reaches the other apostles is bounded to place and people by Peter's power. *Ficticiis* is the adjective with *institucionibus* ("feigned"), not the noun *ficticia*.

[^13]: **S.** Thomas sold in the market as a builder for India is the legend of the *Acts of Thomas*; Loserth notes that the Sarum Breviary lesson says instead *Apparuit ei Dominus in visu noctis* (LEDGER P04d-4).
