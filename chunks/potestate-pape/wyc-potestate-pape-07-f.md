# *De potestate pape*, chapter 7, part 6: the sixth argument: Peter's second fishing and the 153 fishes

## ¶1 · 152.10–154.15

> [p. 152] Iste sanctus papa novit condiciones et pericula adiacencia perverso preposito et specialiter Romano pontifici, cum peccatum commissionis vel omissionis in ipso foret scandalum toti gregi. Per hoc patet responsio ad sextam racionem, in qua ex secunda piscacione Petri notatur in ipso preeminencia sepedicta. Ex quo notaret papa ex preeminencia dignitatis periculum, sciens quod vocatus ad tale officium ex corpore vel negligencia gravius est dampnandus, ymo acceptans illud officium plus propter piscacionem fastus aut questus, quam propter piscacionem animarum trahendarum ad litus soliditatis perpetue in retibus regis Christi, ut sic peccat mortaliter, ut patet XXIII cap. quinti libri, et per consequens quamdiu duraverit talis declinans intencio in occupacione vocati beneficii ecclesiastici vel dignitatis crimen amplius continue invalescit, dicente Lyncolniensi in quadam epistola: Dispensacione beneficiorum quisquis abutitur, qui ad alium finem quam ad fidei et caritatis augmentacionem eadem utitur. Quomodo igitur excusatur distribuens bona ecclesie propter carnem vel favorem [p. 153] mundi lucrificacione Christi postposita. Recole quomodo Petro dabatur titulus beatitudinis, quia caro et sanguis non revelavit sibi, et per consequens ex equo opposito acquirit titulum dampnacionis qui propter carnem vel sanguinem sic accipit vel distribuit bona Dei. Consule, inquit, conscienciam tuam, utrum plura ecclesiastica queris beneficia, ut caritas augeatur in ovibus aut tu augearis ex ovium operibus, ut tu pascas oves verbo, exemplo et oracione aut tu pascaris ex ovium lacte, vide utrum Deo dicere possis cum Augustino in De Verbis Domini Sermone XLIX: Domine, tu scis quia dixi scis quia non tacui scis quo animo dixi, scis quia flevi, cum dicerem et non audirer; ipsa puto integra racio est nostra. In ista materia convincere potest pius theologus, attendens ad conversacionem pontificum ydolorum, quod peccatum Symonis est tam publice induratum, ut nisi pastor summus trahat clerum ad vitam pauperem per subtraccionem elemosinarum secularium tam grex quam pastores vadent in precipicium. Stupor enim huius veneni mortiferi ex diutina consuetudine ad tantum invaluit, quod verba quantumlibet acuta et ex fide desiderabilia non movent clerum possessionibus soporatum. Ideo non restat nisi denudare stertentem litargicum, subtrahendo velamentum sompniferum; olim enim verificatum est illud Apostoli Hebre. V, 4: Nemo sibi sumit honorem, sed qui vocatur a Deo, ymo hodie debet esse, si non essemus peccatis cecati occultatis periculis, quod nemo sumeret sibi dignitatem huiusmodi per procuratoria media sumptuosa, subdola et laboriosa, quin pocius necessitatus apostolo, propter vite sanctitatem [p. 154] coactus susciperet onus tam arduum cum intencione faciendi opus evangeliste, subducta affeccione prosperitatis terrene et per consequens cum timore. Inde distinccione XL sub auctoritate Jeronimi dicit canon: Non est facile stare in loco Petri et Petri tenere cathedram, quia non sanctorum filii sunt qui tenent loca sanctorum sed qui exercent opera eorum. Attendamus igitur ad opera pastorum primitive ecclesie et non ad temporalia preter ordinacionem primevam ex pia ignorancia conglobata, et videbimus ex decreto (ut sepe repecii) quod nimis multi mendaciter sunt prelati. Ideo dicit Spiritus Apoc. III, 9 quomodo illi de synagoga sathane dicunt se esse Iudeos et non sunt sed menciuntur.

This holy pope knew the conditions and dangers that attend a perverse [superior]{g:prepositi}, and especially the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex}, since a sin of commission or omission in him would be a scandal to the whole flock. By this the reply to the sixth reason is clear, in which the often-mentioned [preeminence]{g:preeminencia} is noted in Peter from his second fishing.[^1] From this the [pope]{g:papa} should note the danger that comes from the preeminence of dignity, knowing that one called to such an office is to be damned more heavily for [a fault of] the body or for negligence;[^2] indeed one who accepts that office more for a fishing of pride or of gain than for the fishing of souls, to be drawn to the shore of everlasting firmness in the nets of Christ the king, sins mortally as such, as is clear in ch. 23 of the fifth book;[^3] and consequently, as long as such a swerving intention lasts in the occupation of the so-called [ecclesiastical benefice]{g:beneficium-ecclesiasticum} or dignity, the crime grows continually stronger, as Lincoln says in a certain letter: "Whoever uses benefices for any other end than the increase of faith and [charity]{g:caritas} abuses their dispensation."[^4] How then is he excused who distributes the goods of the [church]{g:ecclesia} for the sake of flesh or of the world's [153] favour, the winning [of souls] for Christ set aside? Recall how Peter was given the [title]{g:titulus} of blessedness because "flesh and blood hath not revealed it" to him; and consequently, by the equal opposite, he acquires the title of [damnation]{g:dampnacio} who for the sake of flesh or blood so receives or distributes the goods of God.[^5] "Consult," he says, "your conscience, whether you seek several ecclesiastical benefices so that charity may be increased in the sheep, or so that you may be increased from the works of the sheep; whether to feed the sheep by word, example and prayer, or to be fed from the milk of the sheep. See whether you can say to God with Augustine in *On the Words of the Lord*, Sermon 49: Lord, thou knowest that I spoke, thou knowest that I did not keep silent, thou knowest with what mind I spoke, thou knowest that I wept when I spoke and was not heard; that, I think, is the whole of our account."[^6] In this matter a pious theologian, attending to the [manner of life]{g:conversacio} of the idol-pontiffs, can prove that the sin of Simon is so publicly hardened that, unless the chief shepherd draws the [clergy]{g:clerus} to a poor life by withdrawing secular [alms]{g:elemosina}, both flock and shepherds will go over the precipice.[^7] For the stupor of this deadly poison has grown so strong from long [custom]{g:consuetudo} that words however sharp, and desirable [as faith teaches]{g:ex-fide}, do not move a clergy lulled to sleep by possessions. Therefore nothing remains but to uncover the snoring lethargic by withdrawing the sleep-bringing covering; for once that [saying] of the Apostle in Heb. 5:4 was verified, "No man taketh the honour to himself, but he that is called by God"; indeed today it ought to be so, if we were not blinded by sins to hidden dangers, that no one would take to himself a dignity of this kind by costly, crafty and laborious proctorial means, but rather, under necessity like the apostle, [154] constrained on account of holiness of life, would take up so heavy a burden with the intention of doing the work of an evangelist, the affection for earthly prosperity withdrawn, and consequently with fear.[^8] Hence in distinction 40, under the authority of Jerome, the canon says: "It is not easy to stand in Peter's place and to hold Peter's chair, for they are not the sons of the saints who hold the places of the saints, but they who do their works."[^9] Let us attend, then, to the works of the shepherds of the primitive church, and not to the [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} heaped up out of pious ignorance beyond the first [ordinance]{g:ordinacio}, and we shall see from the decree (as I have often repeated) that far too many are [prelates]{g:prelatus} falsely. Therefore the Spirit says in Apoc. 3:9 how those "of the synagogue of Satan, who say they are Jews and are not, but do lie".

## ¶2 · 154.16–156.25

> [p. 154] Redeundo igitur ad exposicionem scripture videamus secundum mellifluum sub cortice verborum centum qninquaginta trium magnorum piscium. Nam, ut inquit Augustinus, De Verbis Domini Sermone XIV: Quando possumus de divinis operibus, que leguntur, intellectum alicuius mistice significacionis exculpere quasi de abstrusis favorum cellis mella producimus. Vel Christi discipulos imitantes spicas manibus confricamus, ut ad grana vitalia in eis latencia contingamus. Exponit autem Augustinus LXXXIII questionum questione LVII illum numerum seriose notans, primo quatuor gradus status ecclesie; primo enim a quaternario resultat denarius sub quo ecclesia ymo tota universitas continetur. Dicit enim Apostolus prima Cor. XI, 3 quod mulieris caput est vir sed viri caput est Christus, caput autem Christi est Deus, in quo gradu quadruplici patet quod [p. 155] Deo correspondet unitas, Christo binarius propter duplicitatem naturarum, cum humanitas in potencia fuit sibi semper adaptata, trinarius vero correspondet homini, cum sit memoria, racio et voluntas, quaternarius autem correspondet carni vel corpori quod dicitur ex quatuor principiis scilicet punctali, lineari, superficiali et profundali vel quatuor electis, colligendo autem unum duo tria quatuor constituunt decem. Sed natura humana egrediens denarium ex prevaricacione decalogi (ut dicitur cap. XXIII, V huius) indiguit reformante, ideo mediator Dei et hominum, sponsus et formator ecclesie, corpus suum quod est quaternarius duxit in dictum denarium et passione sua redemit ecclesiam. Et hec racio quare ieiunavit quadraginta diebus. Sed quia quadragenarius resultans ex ductu quaternarii in denarium habet septem partes aliquotas, ut XX, X; VIII, V, IV, II, I, que integrant quinquaginta, iam post resureccionem quadraginta diebus exspectavit ascensionem et post decem dies reformatam ecclesiam confirmavit, mittendo consolantem Spiritum dono septemplici. Sed quarto dictus cesar semper augustus augmentando semper progrediens dictam ecclesiam, consumavit, quando dictum quinquagenarium triplicavit, quod constituit centum quinquaginta et in eternitate extra illum numerum beatificavit; quod fit, cum fides, spes et caritas tocius ecclesie quoad miliciam consumatur. Pisces igitur captos a spiritu predestinante ministerio hortancium ad bona gracie Petrus trahit in terram vivencium, quando usque ad [p. 156] finem oves diligens corroborat verbo et opere finalem docens perseveranciam in virtute. Qui igitur voluerit esse Christi vel Petri vicarius; oportet ut ad instar eorum ex animo faciat similiter cum vita imitatoria et non nudum nomen vel eleccio in facie ecclesie facit papam. Predicet igitur cum apostolo, ubi nominatus est Christus, donec in vasis capacibus formetur Christus et tunc ex quaternario evangeliorum format ecclesiam. Secundo lapsis conversis suis det animam complens quadragesimam penitencie pro ovibus reformandis, tercio implorando eis Spiritum Sanctum imponat eis manus operatrices, oves morbidas confirmando, recolens illud Luce XXII cap. 32 dictum Petro: Et tu aliquando conversus confirma fratres tuos. Unde Act. VIII, 17 legitur quod Petrus confirmando imposuit eis manus. Et quarto reducat oves ad stabilimentum cubi supra petram firmam quomodolibet proiecti, stantis sine vituperio anguli. Quod si quatuor virtutibus cardinalibus tamquam quatuor equis superficialibus lateralibus substrata sit equa operacio et supposita virtutum continuacio secundum similitudinem captam ex operibus presidentis, tunc enim vendicare potest ex debito locum Petri.

Returning, then, to the exposition of scripture, let us look, according to the honey-flowing [sense], beneath the rind of the words about the hundred and fifty-three great fishes.[^10] For, as Augustine says in *On the Words of the Lord*, Sermon 14: "When we can carve out of the divine works which are read the understanding of some mystical signification, we bring forth honey as from the hidden cells of the combs. Or, imitating Christ's disciples, we rub the ears of corn in our hands so as to reach the life-giving grains hidden in them."[^11] And Augustine, in the *Eighty-three Questions*, question 57, expounds that number, marking in order, first, the four grades of the [state]{g:status} of the church; for first from the four results the ten, under which the church, indeed the whole universe, is contained.[^12] For the Apostle says in 1 Cor. 11:3 that "the head of the woman is the man", but "the head of the man is Christ", "and the head of Christ is God"; in which fourfold grade it is clear that [155] unity corresponds to God; the two to Christ, because of the doubleness of natures, since the humanity was always fitted to him in [potency]{g:potencia};[^13] the three corresponds to man, since he is memory, reason and will; and the four corresponds to the flesh or body, which is said [to be] of four principles, namely of point, line, surface and depth, or of four elements; and, adding up, one, two, three, four make ten.[^14] Now human nature, going out of the ten through [transgression]{g:prevaricator} of the decalogue (as is said in ch. 23 of [book] 5 of this [work]), needed one to reform it; therefore the mediator of God and men, the bridegroom and former of the church, multiplied his body, which is the four, into the said ten, and by his passion redeemed the church. And this is the reason why he fasted forty days.[^15] Now since the forty, which results from multiplying the four into the ten, has seven [aliquot parts]{g:partes-aliquote}, namely 20, 10, 8, 5, 4, 2, 1, which together make fifty, after the resurrection he waited forty days for the ascension, and after ten days confirmed the reformed church, sending the comforting Spirit with the sevenfold gift.[^16] Now fourthly, the said Caesar ever august, ever going forward in augmenting the said church, consummated [it] when he tripled the said fifty, which makes a hundred and fifty, and in eternity, beyond that number, beatified [it]; which comes about when the faith, hope and charity of the whole church is consummated as regards its [soldiery]{g:milicia}.[^17] The fishes, then, caught by the predestinating Spirit through the ministry of those who exhort to the goods of grace, Peter draws into the land of the living, when, loving the sheep, he strengthens them to the [156] end by word and work, teaching final perseverance in [virtue]{g:virtus}. Whoever, therefore, wishes to be [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius-christi} or [of Peter]{g:vicarius-petri} must, after their pattern, do likewise from the heart, with a life that imitates [them]; and it is not the bare name or an election in the face of the church that makes a pope. Let him therefore preach with the Apostle where Christ has been named, until Christ be formed in capable vessels; and then from the four of the gospels he forms the church.[^18] Secondly, to those of his converts who have fallen let him give his soul, completing the forty [days] of penance for the sheep to be reformed; thirdly, imploring the Holy Spirit for them, let him lay working hands on them, confirming the sickly sheep, remembering that [word] in Luke 22:32 said to Peter: "and thou, being once converted, confirm thy brethren". Hence in Acts 8:17 it is read that Peter, confirming, "laid hands upon them".[^19] And fourthly let him bring the sheep back to the stability of the cube upon the firm rock, which, however it is thrown, stands without reproach of corner.[^20] And if, beneath the four cardinal virtues as four equal lateral surfaces, an equal working be laid, and a [continuity]{g:continuacio} of the virtues set under it, after the likeness drawn from the works of the one presiding, [it is well;] for then he can claim Peter's place as his due.[^21]

## Notes

[^1]: **C/S/R.** "This holy pope" is Gregory the Great, whose *Pastoral Care* has just been quoted. The second fishing: John 21:1–11 (the first, Luke 5:4–11). A notes in the margin at 152.14 *Ad 6*, "to the sixth".
[^2]: **T.** *ex corpore* is doubtful; *ex torpore*, "from sluggishness", would pair with *negligencia* (a conjecture, not adopted). Translated as printed.
[^3]: **S/C.** "The fifth book" is the fifth book of Wyclif's *Summa*, *De civili dominio* III; Loserth: *De civili dominio* III, 485 ff. (LEDGER P07f-1). *ut sic*: "as such", in so far as he accepts it for that end.
[^4]: **S/C.** Robert Grosseteste, letter to Hugh of Pateshull, ed. Luard, p. 99 (Loserth; LEDGER P07f-2); the manuscripts read *Linconiense*. *vocati beneficii*: "so-called", as the glossary fixes *vocatus*; it could also be "the benefice [to which he was] called", taking up *vocatus ad tale officium* above.
[^5]: **S/C.** Matt. 16:17. *ex equo opposito*: the argument from contraries. *dampnacio* is CONTEXTUAL: here the title to damnation, not a judicial condemnation.
[^6]: **S/C.** The speaker of "he says" is not named; we take it to be Lincoln still (to be checked; LEDGER P07f-3). Augustine, *De verbis Domini*, sermon 49 in Wyclif's numbering (LEDGER P07f-4). Loserth's italic runs from *conscienciam tuam* to *racio est nostra*. *integra racio*: "account", the reckoning a steward renders.
[^7]: **C/R.** "Idol-pontiffs" (*pontificum ydolorum*): cf. Zach. 11:17 (*o pastor et idolum*). The "chief shepherd" (*pastor summus*) is the pope, who could withdraw the alms; Christ (1 Pet. 5:4) is the other possible referent. The "sin of Simon" is simony (Acts 8:18–20). A and B note in the margin at 153.17 *Prelatus verus* (B *bonus*) *non est qui vita bona caret*, and at 153.29 *Dignitates ecclesiasticas quomodo usurpant*.
[^8]: **S/C.** Heb. 5:4 in Wyclif's form (*Nemo sibi sumit*; Vulgate *Nec quisquam sumit sibi … tamquam Aaron*, DR "Neither doth any man take … as Aaron was"). *necessitatus apostolo*: the dative is hard; we take it "under necessity like the apostle" (1 Cor. 9:16). "Proctorial" (*procuratoria*): as in "proctorial power". "The work of an evangelist": 2 Tim. 4:5.
[^9]: **S.** Decretum D.40 c.2 (Loserth: "Cap. II"; the modern form is ours), under Jerome's name (LEDGER P07f-5). "The decree" below is this canon.
[^10]: **C/R.** *secundum mellifluum*: "according to the honey-flowing [sense]", the honey of the Augustine figure that follows; it may instead mean "according to the mellifluous [doctor]", or *secundum* may stand for *sensum* ("let us look at the honey-flowing sense"). The 153 fishes: John 21:11. A and B note in the margin at 154.25 *Pisces 153 quid signant*.
[^11]: **S.** Loserth: Augustine, Opp. tom. V, App., p. 131/32, i.e. among the sermons printed as spurious (LEDGER P07f-6). The disciples and the ears of corn: Luke 6:1.
[^12]: **S/C.** Augustine, *De diversis quaestionibus LXXXIII*, q. 57, "De centum quinquaginta tribus piscibus" (Loserth: tom. VI, p. 25; LEDGER P07f-7). *seriose*: "in order". *quaternarius*, *denarius*: the numbers four and ten.
[^13]: **S/C/R.** 1 Cor. 11:3 with the clauses in another order than the Vulgate's (DR "the head of every man is Christ: and the head of the woman is the man: and the head of Christ is God"). *in potencia*: "in potency" in Aristotle's sense, the humanity always apt to be united to him. B notes *vel anime* ("or of souls") in the margin at *naturarum*.
[^14]: **T.** *punctali* is Loserth's emendation (the manuscripts read *peccali*), from Augustine's progression from point to length to breadth. *electis*, so printed (read on the plate), does not construe; we read *elementis*, "elements" (our emendation).
[^15]: **S/C.** "Ch. 23 of [book] 5 of this [work]": *De civili dominio* III (Loserth: III, 485; LEDGER P07f-8). The mediator: 1 Tim. 2:5; the forty days' fast: Matt. 4:2. *duxit in* is the arithmetic of multiplication. *prevaricacio* takes the "transgression" of the *prevaricator / prevaricancia* row.
[^16]: **T/S.** The aliquot parts of forty (20, 10, 8, 5, 4, 2, 1) sum to fifty. B omits *quadraginta … ascensionem* (155.20–21). Forty days to the ascension: Acts 1:3; the Spirit at Pentecost: Acts 2:1; the sevenfold gift: Isa. 11:2.
[^17]: **C/G.** *cesar semper augustus*: the imperial style "ever august", glossed by *augmentando*, the play on *augustus* and *augere*. The three "beyond that number" make up 153. *milicia*: the church's soldiery, i.e. the church militant (the Pauline-figure sense of the row).
[^18]: **S/T/R.** "Where Christ has been named": Rom. 15:20 says the opposite, "not where Christ was named" (*non ubi nominatus est Christus*); a *non* may have been lost here. Loserth's margin reference "Cf. Rom. XV, 20" is not in the established Latin. "Until Christ be formed": Gal. 4:19. A and B note in the margin at 156.2 *Vicarius Christi verus qualis est*.
[^19]: **C/S.** *det animam*: "give his soul", the good shepherd's *animam suam dat* (John 10:11). The forty of penance: Lent, as the forty of 155. Luke 22:32. Acts 8:17 in the Vulgate is plural, of Peter and John (*imponebant manus super illos*, DR "they laid their hands upon them"); Wyclif's *imposuit eis manus* makes Peter the subject.
[^20]: **C.** The cube stands on whichever face it falls. *stantis sine vituperio anguli* is hard: we take *anguli* with *sine vituperio* ("without reproach of corner", whatever corner it falls on); it may instead depend on *cubi* ("the cube of the corner"), Christ the cornerstone. The rock: Matt. 16:18.
[^21]: **C.** *equis* is *aequis*, "equal" (the four side faces of the cube), not "horses". The *si*-clause has no main clause before *tunc enim*; "[it is well]" is supplied. "The one presiding" (*presidentis*) is Christ, or Peter.
