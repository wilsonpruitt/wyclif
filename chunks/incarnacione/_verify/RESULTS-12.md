## Anselm's "c. 7" (the coordinator's question)

The passage Wyclif quotes (*Sicut … in Deo una natura est plures persone … non alius Deus alius homo … naturam aliam assumpsit, non aliam personam*) is **Schmitt c. 11** (II, 28.11–29.9; the quoted words at 28.23–29.1). It is **Migne's c. VI** (PL 158, 278B–279A, headed *CAPUT VI [al. IV]*, *Quomodo in Christo non sunt duae personae*). Schmitt c. 11 and Migne VI both open with the objection *Dicunt enim quidam: Quomodo dicimus in Christo non esse duas personas … homo assumptus persona est* (Schmitt II, 28.14–22; PL 158, 278B). That objection is the "Nestorian" argument which Wyclif places *in principio ca° 7* (146.16). His "beginning of c. 7" is therefore exact, on a division that starts this chapter where Migne and Schmitt start theirs.

Wyclif's "7" is neither Schmitt's 11 nor Migne's VI or its alternative "al. IV". It sits **one above Migne's number**, and does so consistently:
- Wyclif "6" (the *quamlibet parvum inconveniens* maxim, I05d-1) = Migne V = Schmitt 10.
- Wyclif "7" = Migne VI = Schmitt 11. This holds here (three times) and in *De compositione hominis* c. 1 (*7° ca°*, the same doctrine; see I02d-1).

Harris's Index II maps *De Inc. Verbi, 7* to *vi* (latinworks03 ll. 21880–21890). Wyclif's "1" for the universals passage (I09a-4) is Schmitt 1 = Migne II, so the offset does not run from the start of the work. The medieval chapter division Wyclif used remains unidentified. The notes should give "Wyclif's c. 7 = Schmitt c. 11 = PL 158 c. VI".

## For the coordinator to decide

1. **Anselm chapter style.** Wyclif's c. 7 = Schmitt 11 = Migne VI throughout (and his 6 = Migne V). Earlier notes (08-b n15, 02-d n5, 05-d) give Schmitt first. The replacements above follow that, and add Migne for the offset. Decide whether the edition's convention note should state the offset once: "Wyclif's numbers for *De inc. Verbi* run one above Migne's from c. 6 on; the division he used is unidentified".
2. **I09a-1 wording.** Wyclif's *et non tamen tres dii* (the print) agrees with Anselm and the Creed on *tamen*. B's omission of *et non … Deus* (144.4–6) is against both.
3. **150.4 O *donum*.** Augustine's sentence on God as Lord from time follows directly on the Spirit as *donum* (*De Trin.* V.16.17). O's *donum* for *dominium* may be a reminiscence, but the sense needs *dominium*. No change is proposed.
4. **I08e-6/7** stay untraced until the Latin Avicenna (*De diluviis*, ed. Alonso Alonso 1949; *Metaph.* IX.5 for the *dator formarum*) is in hand.
5. **I09a-3.** "Gregory" in Wyclif's list (144.17) may be Nazianzen, whom the Latins knew through texts like Rusticus, rather than Gregory the Great. Wyclif's Augustine and Jerome for *aliud/alius* are not checked.
