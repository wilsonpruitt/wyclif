# *De potestate pape*, chapter 3, part 2: Peter's primacy: the rock, the keys, and the gospel's figures

## ¶1 · 47.27–49.31

> [p. 47] Quantum ad privilegia dignitatis ecclesie Math. XVI; 18— 19 post confessionem Petri de filiacione Christi naturali, respondit Christus, quasi pro arris mercedis fidei: Et ego dico tibi quia tu es Petrus, et super hanc petram edificabo ecclesiam meam et porte inferi non prevalebunt adversus eam, et tibi dabo claves regni celorum, et quodcunque ligaveris super terram erit ligatum et in [p. 48] celis, et quodcunque solveris super terram, erit solutum et in celis. Sed post Math. XVIII et Joh. XX dat apostolis communiter potestatem. Sed nusquam legitur in scriptura quod alteri quam Petro dedit dominus claves celi; per hoc igitur quod Petrus ex approbacione Christi respondit pro omnibus in precipua materia fidei, que est fundamentum spirituali preposito, et per hoc quod Christus mutato nomine a Symone in Petrum quod nomen contraxit a se ipso, qui est Petra et fundamentum ecclesie, ut patet I. Corinth. X et I. Corinth. Ill, videtur concedere Petro quandam prerogativam super ceteros apostolos ad regendam suam ecclesiam, ac si diceret secundum exposicionem Augustini De Verbis Domini sermone XIII: Ego sum petra, (quia Christus) a qua tu es Petrus, id est christianus in fide firmus, inmediatus mei vicarius, super quam petram, quam confessus es, per te ut proximum eius capitaneum fundabo ecclesiam meam. Ideo, dicit Augustinus, a petra petrus non a petro petra, sicut a Christo Christianus, non a Christiano Christus. Ideo super me edificabo ecclesiam meam, non super te, nam volentes edificare super homines dicebant: Ego quidem sum Pauli, ego quidem Apollo, ego vero Cephe, sed apostolus dicit se esse lapidis, qui est Christus. Cum igitur Christus nominat Petrum primo nomine suo; videtur concedere primitatem, ideo dicit Augustinus quod apostolatus tenuit principatum in signum cuius possessionis et preeminencie subdit Christus: Dabo tibi post mortem meam claves ecclesie, quam fortificabo [p. 49] super ecclesiam malignancium, dando tibi potestatem ligandi atque solvendi; ut non superflue occupes claves ecclesie, quas dedi tibi racione confessionis meritorie humanitatis et deitatis qua a Spiritu Sancto edoctus dixisti, tu es Christus et per consequens verus homo filius Dei vivi; quod non fuisset pertinenter additum, nisi intelligeretur de filio naturali. Et ista confessio est maxime pertinens christiano preposito, ut patet Joh. IV; eo quod maxime obviat Antichristo, cum ipse negabit divinitatem et infatuabit; quantum in ipso erit, Christi pauperiem et voluntariam passionem. Ubi enim nos christiani docemur a Christo et ecclesia expetere constanciam, prospera mundi despicere et nulla eius adversa formidare, Antichristus econtra cum multis suis docet homines ad mundi prospera aspirare et penalitates contempnere, unde Petrus, quia dixit Christo; absit hoc a te Domine ut vadas Jerusalem crucifigi, vocatus est ab eo Sathanas paulo post hoc privilegium singulare, ut patet Math. XVI; 23, ad denotandum quod ipse est potissime Christo adversans tamquam alter Antichristus, qui in paupertate et penalitate abnuit sequi Christum. Unde isto scuto fidei armatus est Petrus postmodum, cum pro Christo reliquit uxorem et omnia, sequendo ipsum Math. XIX, 27, pro Christo promisit se mori percuciendo servum pontificis Joh. XVIII, 10; pro Christo exposuit se in aquam Math. XIV et Joh. ultimo et demum in capite mundi Roma pro Christo pertulit mortem acerbam.

As to the privileges of the dignity of the [church]{g:ecclesia}: in Matthew 16:18–19, after Peter's confession of Christ's natural sonship, Christ answered, as if in earnest of the wages of faith: "And I say to thee: That thou art Peter; and upon this rock I will build my church, and the gates of hell shall not prevail against it. And I will give to thee the keys of the kingdom of heaven. And whatsoever thou shalt bind upon earth, it shall be bound also in [48] heaven: and whatsoever thou shalt loose upon earth, it shall be loosed also in heaven."[^1] But afterwards, in Matthew 18 and John 20, he gives [power]{g:potestas} to the apostles in common. Now nowhere is it read in scripture that the Lord gave the keys of heaven to another than Peter; by this, therefore, that Peter with Christ's approval answered for all in the chief matter of faith, which is the foundation for a spiritual [superior]{g:prepositi}, and by this, that Christ changed his name from Simon to Peter, a name he drew from himself, who is the Rock and foundation of the church, as is clear from 1 Corinthians 10 and 1 Corinthians 3, he seems to grant Peter a certain prerogative over the other apostles for ruling his church, as if he said, according to the exposition of Augustine, *De verbis Domini*, sermon 13: "I am the rock (since [I am] Christ), from which you are Peter, that is, a Christian firm in faith, my immediate [vicar]{g:vicarius-christi}, upon which rock, which you have confessed, I will found my church through you as its nearest [captain]{g:capitaneus}."[^2] Therefore, says Augustine, "from *petra* comes *Petrus*, not *petra* from *Petrus*, as Christian from Christ, not Christ from Christian. Therefore upon me will I build my church, not upon thee; for those who wished to build upon men said: I indeed am of Paul, I indeed of Apollo, and I of Cephas; but the apostle says that he is of the stone, which is Christ."[^3] Since therefore Christ names Peter first by his own name, he seems to grant him firstness; therefore Augustine says that "he held the principate of the apostolate", in sign of which possession and preeminence Christ adds: "I will give thee", after my death, "the keys of the church", which I will fortify [49] over the church of the malignant, giving you the power of binding and loosing; so that you may not hold idly the keys of the church which I have given you by reason of the meritorious confession of humanity and [Godhead]{g:deitas}, by which, taught by the Holy Spirit, you said, "Thou art Christ", and consequently true man, "the Son of the living God"; which would not have been fittingly added unless it were understood of the natural son.[^4] And this confession pertains most of all to a Christian superior, as is clear from John 4, because it most opposes Antichrist, since he will deny the divinity and will make foolish, as far as in him lies, Christ's poverty and voluntary passion.[^5] For where we Christians are taught by Christ and the church to seek constancy, to despise the prosperity of the world and to fear none of its adversities, Antichrist on the contrary, with many of his own, teaches men to aspire to the world's prosperity and to scorn [hardships]{g:penalitas}; hence Peter, because he said to Christ, "Lord, be it far from thee" that thou go to Jerusalem to be crucified, was called Satan by him shortly after this singular privilege, as is clear from Matthew 16:23, to denote that he is most of all adverse to Christ, as another Antichrist, who refuses to follow Christ in poverty and hardship.[^6] Hence Peter was afterwards armed with this shield of faith, when for Christ he left his "wife and all things", following him (Matthew 19:27); for Christ he promised to die, "striking the servant of the high priest" (John 18:10); for Christ he exposed himself in the water (Matthew 14 and the last chapter of John); and finally in Rome, the head of the world, he suffered for Christ a bitter death.[^7]

## ¶2 · 49.32–50.27

> [p. 49] Quid igitur datur intelligi, quod solus Petrus videbatur a domino Math. XIV venire ad ipsum supra mare, nisi quod presens seculum per mare [p. 50] signatum ipse a Deo adiutus, singulariter presidens navicule significanti ecclesiam Christi, subiceret servituti; et istum sensum ponit Augustinus Sermone IV. Quid autem per secundam in mari missionem, de qua Johannis ultimo, aliis discipulis navigio ad Christum venientibus, ipse solus tunica succinctus audacter misit se in mare, daretur nobis intelligi vere quod omnes alii Christi discipuli ducatu Petri tunica virtutum et zona potencie ligandi ornati fuerant in via tendendi ad terminum regulandi? Hoc eciam figuratur per hoc, quod soli tempore passionis Salvator dixerit Marc. XIV, 37: Simon dormis? Non potuisti una hora vigilare mecum? per hoc innuens quod ipse solus pre ceteris et pro ceteris haberet capitaliter in cura pastoralis officii vigilare. Et hoc idem figuratur per hoc, quod ipse solus in secunda piscacione traxit ad litus rete plenum centum quinquaginta tribus magnis piscibus, ut dicitur Joh. ult; per hoc enim figuratur quod ipse solus inter apostolos trahit capitaliter sponsam Christi ad terram vivencium in retibus legis Dei. Magnum itaque privilegium vicarii, qui potest trahere animas ad beatitudinem, cum ista secunda piscacio significat capcionem hominum ad ecclesiam triumphantem, sicut prima piscacio signat capcionem hominum ad ecclesiam militantem.

What, then, is given to be understood by this, that Peter alone was seen by the Lord (Matthew 14) coming to him upon the sea, except that, helped by God and presiding singly over the little ship that signifies the church of Christ, he should bring under servitude the present world, [50] signified by the sea? And this sense Augustine sets out in Sermon 4.[^8] And what is given us to understand by the second casting into the sea, of which the last chapter of John speaks, when, "the other disciples" coming to Christ "in the ship", he alone, girt with his coat, boldly cast himself into the sea, unless truly that all the other disciples of Christ, under Peter's leadership, were adorned with the coat of virtues and the girdle of the [power]{g:potencia} of binding, on the way that tends to the goal of ruling?[^9] This too is figured by this, that in the time of the passion the Saviour said to him alone, Mark 14:37: "Simon, sleepest thou? Couldst thou not watch one hour with me?", thereby intimating that he alone, before the rest and for the rest, should keep watch [as head]{g:capitaliter} in the care of the pastoral office.[^10] And the same is figured by this, that he alone, in the second fishing, drew to land the net "full of a hundred and fifty-three great fishes", as is said in the last chapter of John; for by this it is figured that he alone among the apostles draws, as head, the [bride]{g:sponsa} of Christ to the land of the living in the nets of [God's law]{g:lex-dei}. Great, then, is the privilege of the vicar, who can draw souls to blessedness, since this second fishing signifies the catching of men for the church triumphant, as the first fishing signifies the catching of men for the [church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans}.[^11]

## ¶3 · 50.28–51.10

> [p. 50] Idem eciam figuratur per hoc quod Christus doctrinam correpcionis, quam voluit tradi cunctis fidelibus, per Petrum voluit in alios derivari. Nam Math. XVIII, 15 legitur: Aspiciens Ihesus in discipulos suos dixit Simoni Petro: Si peccaverit in te frater tuus, vade et corripe eum inter te et [p. 51] ipsum solum, ut exposui VIII capitulo IV huius et XXIV cap. V libri. Idem figuratum est Joh. ultimo. ubi in conventu duodecim discipulorum universitatem fidelium Christi signancium post triplicem examinacionem amoris ipse singulariter recepit a pastore summo curam et pastum simpliciter tam ovium quam agnorum; ut sicut ter Christum negaverat; sic ostendat eius amorem in pascendo magnates, vulgus et clericos verbo; opere et exemplo.

The same is also figured by this, that Christ willed the [doctrine]{g:doctrina} of correction, which he willed to be handed down to all the faithful, to be passed on to others through Peter. For in Matthew 18:15 it is read: "Jesus, looking upon his disciples, said to Simon Peter: If thy brother shall offend against thee, go, and rebuke him between thee and [51] him alone", as I set out in chapter 8 of the fourth [book] of this [work] and in chapter 24 of the fifth book.[^12] The same is figured in the last chapter of John, where, in the gathering of the twelve disciples, signifying [the whole body of the faithful]{g:universitas-fidelium} of Christ, after the threefold examination of love he singly received from the chief shepherd the care and feeding, [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}, of both sheep and lambs; so that, as he had thrice denied Christ, so he might show his love for him in feeding [magnates]{g:magnates}, the common people and [clerics]{g:clericus} by word, work and example.[^13]

## Notes

[^1]: **R/S.** A in marg.: *Petra Christus, non Petrus* (47.27). Matt. 16:18–19 (DR). *Arris*, "earnest": a pledge paid in advance.

[^2]: **S.** Matt. 18:18; John 20:22–23; 1 Cor. 10:4 (DR "the rock was Christ"); 1 Cor. 3:11. Augustine, *Sermo* 76 (on Matt. 14), Maurist *Opera* V, 415, which Wyclif cites as *De verbis Domini* sermon 13; cf. Hus, *Opera*, fol. 210a (Loserth, 48.15; LEDGER P03b-1, editor; to be checked). The words put in Christ's mouth are Wyclif's expansion; Loserth's italics begin at *a petra*. B reads *peter* for *per te* (48.19).

[^3]: **S.** The *petra*/*Petrus* passage is from the same sermon of Augustine (*Sermo* 76.1; to be checked, LEDGER P03b-2). 1 Cor. 1:12 (DR "I indeed am of Paul; and I am of Apollo; and I of Cephas"); Augustine's wording is translated. B omits *Petrum* (48.28).

[^4]: **S/C.** "He held the principate of the apostolate": Augustine, *Sermo* 76, *Opera* V, 416 (Loserth, 48.30; LEDGER P03b-3, editor; to be checked). *Primitatem*, "firstness", a non-classical abstract beside *primatus*, "primacy". The italics mark only *Dabo tibi* and *claves ecclesie*; the rest is Wyclif's expansion of Matt. 16:19. "The church of the malignant": Ps. 25:5 (DR "the assembly of the malignant"). Matt. 16:16 (DR "Thou art Christ, the Son of the living God").

[^5]: **S.** "John 4" as printed; the sense fits 1 John 4:2–3 (DR "every spirit that dissolveth Jesus is not of God. And this is Antichrist").

[^6]: **S.** Matt. 16:22 (DR "Lord, be it far from thee, this shall not be unto thee"); the words about going to Jerusalem to be crucified are Wyclif's (cf. 16:21). Matt. 16:23 (Loserth). B omits *aspirare* (49.17).

[^7]: **S.** Eph. 6:16 (DR "the shield of faith"). Matt. 19:27 has "all things" only (DR "we have left all things"); "wife" comes from 19:29. The promise to die, Matt. 26:35; John 18:10 (DR "struck the servant of the high priest"); Matt. 14:29; John 21:7. Peter's death at Rome: no source is given.

[^8]: **S/C.** Matt. 14:29. Augustine, "Sermon 4": Loserth refers to the same volume, p. 320 (*ubi supra*) (LEDGER P03b-4, editor; to be checked). *Videbatur a domino … venire*: "was seen by the Lord coming"; it may be "seemed … to come".

[^9]: **T/S/C.** *Ad Christum*: the manuscripts read *ad dominum* (50.11); Loserth's print is translated. John 21:7–8 (DR "girt his coat about him … cast himself into the sea"; "the other disciples came in the ship"). *Potencia* is here "power", the power of binding, used as *potestas* is.

[^10]: **T/S.** The manuscripts give *Marci XXVI* (50.13); Loserth prints Mark 14. Mark 14:37 (DR "Simon, sleepest thou? Couldst thou not watch one hour?"); *mecum*, "with me", comes from Matt. 26:40.

[^11]: **S.** John 21:11 (DR "full of great fishes, one hundred and fifty-three"); Wyclif's order is translated. The first fishing is Luke 5:1–11. "The land of the living": Ps. 26:13.

[^12]: **S.** Matt. 18:15 (DR "If thy brother shall offend against thee, go, and rebuke him between thee and him alone", without *autem*). The opening words, *Aspiciens Ihesus in discipulos suos dixit Simoni Petro*, are not in the Vulgate; they resemble a lectionary incipit for this gospel (LEDGER P03b-5, to be checked). For the cross-reference Loserth gives *De civili dominio* II, 62 and III, 519 (LEDGER P03b-6, editor): the books are counted within Wyclif's *Summa*. B reads 34 for XXIV (51.2).

[^13]: **R/S/C.** A in marg., another hand: *Nota quod sensus figuraliter equivalet literali* (51.2). John 21:15–17 (DR "Feed my lambs", "Feed my sheep"); John 21:2 names seven disciples, not twelve. *Simpliciter*: the care of the whole flock, without qualification.
