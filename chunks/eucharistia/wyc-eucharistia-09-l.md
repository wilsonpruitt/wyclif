# *De eucharistia*, chapter 9, part 12: the kinds of adoration, idolatry, the worship of the host, and the goods of the poor

## ¶1 · 316.23–317.3

> [p. 316] Confirmatur ex hoc quod omnis homo ad ymaginem Dei factus expressius representat Deum et est natura superior quam hostia consecrata. Unde quia fuit origo femine non originatus ab alio sue nature, precipit Apostolus quod oret in ecclesia velato capite, ut patet I. Cor. XIᵒ, 5. Et naturales cum mandatis Domini docent omnem hominem diligi et magnates [p. 317] precipue honorari; honoracio vero vel dileccio hostie non habet tantam auctoritatem, cum homo sit expressius sacramentum.

This is confirmed from this, that every man, made to the image of God, represents God more expressly and is by [nature]{g:natura} higher than the [consecrated host]{g:hostia-consecrata}. Hence, because [the man] was the origin of the woman, not originated from another of his nature, the Apostle commands that she pray in the [church]{g:ecclesia} "with veiled head", as is clear in 1 Cor. 11:5.[^1] And the natural [philosophers], with the commandments of the Lord, teach that every man is to be loved and [magnates]{g:magnates} [317] especially honoured; but the honouring or [love]{g:dileccio} of the host has not so much [authority]{g:auctoritas}, since man is more expressly a [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}.[^2]

## ¶2 · 317.4–317.7

> [p. 317] Hic oportet primo cognoscere quod aliquid adoratur ut signum alterius quod adoratur in ipso et aliquid in natura preeminenter adoratur ut Deus; et isto modo nemo rite adorat hostiam et specialiter si sit quantitas.

Here one must first know that something is adored as the [sign]{g:signum} of another, which is adored in it, and something is adored preeminently in [its own] nature, as God; and in this [latter] way no one rightly adores the [host]{g:hostia}, and especially if it is a [quantity]{g:quantitas}.[^3]

## ¶3 · 317.9–317.29

> [p. 317] Secundo notandum quod multiplex est racio adorandi naturam racionalem, vel pro excellencia quam ex se habet qualiter solus Deus est adorandus, vel pro excellencia quam a Deo participat, sive fuerit angelus sive homo secundum excellenciam secularem sive excellenciam sacerdotalem. Unde secundum istam racionem mediam Jacob adoravit Esau fratrem suum Genes. XXXIIIᵒ. Sed tercio modo adoramus ymagines nude ut signa, Deum autem debemus quantum sufficimus adorare. Sed in adoracione creature consistit nimia religionis perturbacio ut ydolatria et personarum accepcio; quandocunque enim per se adoratur ymago creata ut Deus vel inordinate aut moroso amore committitur ydolatria. Et isto modo dicit Apostolus Ephes. Vᵒ, 5 quod avarus est ydolorum servitus. Et in isto peccato gentes quas vocamus infideles laborant, adorantes bestias vel simulacra muta tanquam ymagines Dei quem adorant et credunt finaliter. Et in ista ydolatria nos vocati christiani peccamus sepe plus barbaris adorando sanctorum ymagines, reliquias et sacramentum altaris.

Second, it is to be noted that the ground of adoring rational nature is manifold: either for the excellence it has of itself, in which way God alone is to be adored; or for the excellence it participates from God, whether it be an angel or a man, according to secular excellence or priestly excellence. Hence according to this middle ground Jacob adored Esau his brother, Gen. 33.[^4] But in the third way we adore images barely as signs; but God we ought to adore as far as we are able. But in the adoration of a [creature]{g:creatura} there lies an [exceeding]{g:nimis} disturbance of religion, such as [idolatry]{g:ydolatria} and [respect of persons]{g:accepcio-personarum}; for whenever a created image is adored [of itself]{g:per-se} as God, or inordinately, or with lingering love, idolatry is committed.[^5] And in this way the Apostle says, Eph. 5:5, that "the covetous man is the [servitude of idols]{g:servitus-ydolorum}."[^6] And in this sin the gentiles whom we call [unbelievers]{g:infidelis} labour, adoring beasts or dumb idols as images of the God whom they adore and believe in as their end.[^7] And in this idolatry we who are called Christians often sin more than the barbarians, by adoring the images of saints, relics and the [sacrament of the altar]{g:sacramentum-altaris}.

## ¶4 · 317.30–318.11

> [p. 317] Unde penes infideles fiunt plura vocata miracula, ut decipiant infideles. Religio autem christiana primeva non habuit tales ymagines nec sanctos canonisatos [p. 318] sed vixerunt perfeccius nobis in fide filii Dei, cum omnia talia signa non adiuvant nisi de quanto iuvant et excitant ad Christum sponsum ecclesie diligendum. Et hinc creditur Deum ordinasse corpus matris Domini non adorari in loco habitabilis nostre, ut nec corpus Moysi in lege veteri nec coli corpora Apostolorum tam sumptuose et splendide, sicut coluntur corpora sanctorum in Anglia. Et hinc creditur Apostolum tam crebro vocare sacramentum altaris panem. Et omnia ista sicut prohibicio ymaginum erant propter ydolatriam precavendam.

Hence among unbelievers many [so-called]{g:vocatus} miracles happen, so as to deceive the unbelievers. But the primitive Christian religion did not have such images nor [canonized]{g:canonizare} saints, [318] but they lived more perfectly than we in the faith of the Son of God, since all such signs do not help except insofar as they help and stir [us] to love Christ, the [bridegroom]{g:sponsus} of the church.[^8] And hence it is believed that God ordained that the body of the mother of the Lord should not be adored in any place of our habitable [world], as neither the body of Moses in the old law, nor that the bodies of the Apostles should be worshipped so sumptuously and splendidly as the bodies of the saints are worshipped in England.[^9] And hence it is believed that the Apostle so often calls the sacrament of the altar [bread]{g:panis}. And all these, like the prohibition of images, were for guarding against idolatry.

## ¶5 · 318.12–318.29

> [p. 318] Conceditur ergo quod reliquie, ymagines et sacramenta sunt cum prudencia adoranda. Ille ergo qui sciret suspendere consideracionem signi sensibilis et appendere totam intencionem et affeccionem in creatore ymaginis, modo quo clericus suspendit scripturas concipiendo sentenciam; ille inquam adoraret latria Deum in illo signo. Sed nimis multi sunt layci et bestiales sensibilibus nimium intendentes et multi, ut loquitur Apostolus Iᵃ Cor. XIIᵒ, 2, cum adorando hostiam tanquam gentes ad simulacra muta prout ducebantur captivi euntes ad ydolatrandum sicut brutum ad iugum servilis operis. Qui autem adorat humanitatem Christi ut talem in hostia, adorat in ipsa Christum yperdulia, et nemo rite adorat ipsam sub racione propria, et sic vere concluditur quod homo sit multiplicius honorandus quam hostia et adorandus tanquam ymago Dei, vas virtutum et sic Christus, verius quam hostia consecrata.

It is granted, therefore, that relics, images and sacraments are to be adored with prudence. He therefore who could [suspend]{g:suspendere} the consideration of the [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} sign and hang his whole intention and affection on the creator of the image, in the way a cleric suspends the letters in conceiving the [sense]{g:sentencia-verborum}, he, I say, would adore God with [latria]{g:latria} in that sign.[^10] But exceedingly many are lay and beastly men, attending too much to perceptible things; and many, as the Apostle speaks, 1 Cor. 12:2, with adoring the host "as the heathens to dumb idols, according as they were led" captive, going to [idolatrize]{g:ydolatrare} like a brute to the [yoke]{g:iugum} of servile work.[^11] But he who adores the [humanity]{g:humanitas} of Christ as such in the host adores in it Christ with [hyperdulia]{g:yperdulia}, and no one rightly adores the host under its own aspect; and so it is [truly]{g:vere} [inferred]{g:concludere} that a man is to be honoured in more ways than the host, and to be adored as the image of God, a vessel of virtues and so Christ, more truly than the consecrated host.[^12]

## ¶6 · 318.30–320.14

> [p. 318] Et sic creditur quod error circa cultum hostie dimittit caritatem in homine. Tres enim sunt cause profectus et fructus huius sacramenti scilicet ut pax et caritas in hominibus augeatur, ut memoria gratitudinis [p. 319] Christi recencius habeatur et ut vigilancius in modo conversandi misse particeps Domini exequatur; et omnium horum trium opposita contingunt populo et specialiter sacerdotibus misse qui ex officio pro istis tribus magis sollicite laborarent. Nemo enim plus perturbat pacem Christi, extinguit caritatem, minus fructuose memoratur vitam Christi, ut sequatur ipsum in moribus quam faciunt sacerdotes, et specialiter prelati qui ad hoc ex nomine plus tenentur. Debemus enim instar Christi minus intendere mundo cum suis ritibus, plus aufugere secularia desideria cum suis contencionibus, cum ipsa plus extinguunt pacem et karitatem ecclesie. Nemo minus studet, ut vivat conformiter vite Christi, ymmo nullus Antichristus magis ab ea degenerat, cum non studemus circa vitam parcam penalem et exproprietariam, quam indubie Christus vixit in terris, sed circa vitam largam, lautam, proprietariam, pompaticam et maxime secularem. Cum ergo cultus hostie maxime consistit in complexu istorum trium et in aufugio horum: ita non in preciosis vel sumptuosis ornamentis nec in ritibus inclinacionum, genuflexionum vel decapuciacionum sed in mente pura et sanctitate vite conversacioni Christi similis. Et hinc videtur quod sacerdotes quibus singulariter commissum est hoc ministerium debent in paupere vita sequi Christum et sine offensione ecclesie vel cura de propriis habere omnia in communi, ut dicit Apostolus Iᵃ Cor. Xᵒ, 17: Unus panis et unum corpus multi sumus. Unde solebam pro tempore illius perturbacionis et invasionis regni nostri asserere quod, cum bona regni in manibus religiosorum sint bona pauperum, quorum bonorum ipsi religiosi sunt procuratores [p. 320] non domini, expediret communitati regni in parliamentis constanter instare quod de bonis illis pauperum magis vacantibus regnum intrinsecus ab hostibus defendatur, servatis bonis vivis ad usus pauperum et proviso religiosis sic alleviatis de necessariis vite et ministerio eorum; quod fieret si pensata gravitate, quantitate, qualitate ipsorum limitata sit eis porcio bonorum de quibus debent racionabiliter contentari. Tota ista sentencia patet ex dictis alibi; et repetitur hic triplex sentencia, nam Wilhelmus Conquestor cepit nedum temporalia sed possessiones et vitam licite ab Anglicana ecclesia in Deum et ipsum leviter delinquente: ergo multo magis licet regi et regno micius recipere elemosinas a sua ecclesia gravius delinquente.

And so it is believed that error about the [worship]{g:cultus} of the host lets [charity]{g:caritas} go in a man. For there are three causes of the profit and fruit of this sacrament: namely, that peace and charity among men be increased; that the memory of the [kindness]{g:gratitudo} [319] of Christ be had [more freshly]{g:recencius}; and that the partaker of the Lord's mass may carry [it] out more watchfully in his manner of [living]{g:conversari}.[^13] And the opposites of all these three befall the people, and especially the [priests]{g:sacerdos} of the mass, who by office should labour the more solicitously for these three. For no one disturbs the peace of Christ more, extinguishes charity, remembers the life of Christ less fruitfully so as to follow him in conduct, than priests do, and especially [prelates]{g:prelatus}, who by their name are more bound to this. For we ought, after the pattern of Christ, to attend less to the world with its [rites]{g:ritus}, to flee more the secular desires with their contentions, since these most extinguish the peace and charity of the church. No one studies less to live in conformity with the life of Christ; indeed no Antichrist degenerates more from it, since we do not study for the sparing, [hard]{g:penalis} and [propertyless]{g:exproprietarii} life which without doubt Christ lived on earth, but for a life large, luxurious, [proprietary]{g:proprietarius}, boastful and supremely secular. Since, therefore, the worship of the host consists chiefly in the embrace of those three and in the flight from these, so [it consists] not in precious or sumptuous ornaments, nor in rites of bowings, genuflexions or doffings of hoods, but in a pure mind and holiness of life like the [manner of life]{g:conversacio} of Christ.[^14] And hence it seems that priests, to whom [alone]{g:singulariter} this ministry is committed, ought to follow Christ in a poor life and, without offence to the church or care for their own things, have all things in common, as the Apostle says, 1 Cor. 10:17: "We, being many, are one bread, one body."[^15] Hence at the time of that disturbance and invasion of our [kingdom]{g:regnum} I used to assert that, since the goods of the kingdom in the hands of the [religious]{g:religiosi} are the goods of the poor, of which goods the religious themselves are [proctors]{g:procurator}, [320] not [lords]{g:dominus}, it would be expedient for the community of the kingdom to press constantly in parliaments that from those goods of the poor which lie more idle the kingdom be defended within from its enemies, the living goods being kept for the uses of the poor, and provision being made for the religious, so relieved, of the necessaries of life and of their ministry; which would be done if, their gravity, quantity and quality being weighed, a portion of goods were [assigned]{g:limitare} to them with which they ought reasonably to be content.[^16] This whole [teaching]{g:sentencia} is clear from what is said elsewhere; and a threefold teaching is repeated here. For William the Conqueror took, lawfully, not only [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} but possessions and life from the English church, which offended lightly against God and him: therefore much more is it lawful for the king and kingdom more mildly to take back [alms]{g:elemosina} from their own church, which offends more gravely.[^17]

## ¶7 · 320.15–320.22

> [p. 320] Secundo confirmatur idem arguendo ad hominem per hoc quod domini temporales Anglie capiunt ab ecclesia Francie nimis rigide temporalia (ut probabiliter creditur) minus quam propria ecclesia delinquente, ergo possunt licencius ex maiori causa ab ecclesiis suis hec facere. Nec dicent satrape nostri utrumque istorum fuisse illicitum, quia virtute primi tenent sua predia et ad secundum consulunt et conducunt.

Second, the same is confirmed by arguing *ad hominem*, from this: that the [temporal lords]{g:temporales-domini} of England take temporal goods from the church of France exceedingly rigidly (as is [with probability]{g:probabiliter} believed), though that church offends less than their own church; therefore they can more freely, from a greater cause, do this to their own churches.[^18] Nor will our [satraps]{g:satrapa} say that either of these was unlawful, for by virtue of the first they hold their estates, and to the second they give counsel and lead the way.[^19]

- 317.9: Different kinds of adoration: 1. of God; 2. of man as sharing in God's excellence.

- 317.18: Danger of idolatry in this.
- 317.30: There were neither images nor Saints in the primitive Church.
- 318.12: We may adore relics and images, but only in fixing our mind on the Creator.

## Notes

[^1]: **S/C.** 1 Cor. 11:5 (DR): "every woman praying or prophesying with her head not covered disgraceth her head"; "with veiled head" is Wyclif's wording. The masculine *originatus* makes the man the subject of *fuit origo*; the woman is the subject of *oret*.
[^2]: **C.** *Sacramentum* in the wider sense, a sign of God: man, as God's image, is a more express sign than the host. The *naturales* are the natural philosophers.
[^3]: **C.** *Isto modo* is taken as the second way, adoration of a thing in its own nature as God: the host may be adored only as a sign. "If it is a quantity" answers the adversaries, for whom the host is an accident.
[^4]: **S.** Gen. 33:3 (DR): Jacob "bowed down with his face to the ground seven times, until his brother came near." *Racio adorandi* is the ground of adoring.
[^5]: **C.** *Morosus amor* "lingering love": the love that dwells on its object, as in the schoolmen's *delectatio morosa*; not "morose".
[^6]: **S.** Eph. 5:5 (DR): "covetous person (which is a serving of idols)." Wyclif's *avarus est ydolorum servitus* makes the covetous man himself the servitude; his wording is translated.
[^7]: **C.** *Credunt finaliter* is taken as "believe in as their end"; alternatively "believe in at the last". The dumb idols are those of 1 Cor. 12:2.
[^8]: **C.** *Vixerunt* is plural: the members of the primitive religion. "In the faith of the Son of God" echoes Gal. 2:20.
[^9]: **S.** For Moses, Deut. 34:6 (DR): "no man hath known of his sepulchre until this present day." The bodies of the saints in England are Wyclif's instance; no shrine is named.
[^10]: **G/C.** *Suspendere* "suspend" and *appendere* "hang on" keep Wyclif's pair. *Suspendit scripturas* is taken as the reader setting aside the written letters to conceive their sense (*sentencia (verborum)*).
[^11]: **S/T.** 1 Cor. 12:2 (DR): "when you were heathens, you went to dumb idols, according as you were led." Wyclif adds *captivi*. The sentence has no main verb for the second *multi*: *cum adorando hostiam* is translated as it stands, "with adoring the host"; a verb (or *sunt*) may be lost before or after *cum* (Latin doubt).
[^12]: **T.** ABCD read *quam bestia* at 318.27 and ABCE *virgo Dei* for *ymago Dei* at 318.28; the print is translated. *Sub racione propria*: the host taken as what it is in itself.
[^13]: **T/C.** *Misse particeps Domini exequatur*, printed so, is taken as "the partaker of the Lord's mass may carry [it] out", the object supplied from *memoria*; ABC read *Dominum*, "that the partaker of the mass may follow the Lord", which construes more easily. *Gratitudo* is the kindness received from Christ.
[^14]: **C.** *Istorum trium* "those three" are the three fruits; *horum* "these" the vices just named. The *cum … ita* period is loose in the Latin and is kept so. *Decapuciaciones*, the doffing of hoods, is a coinage of the rite.
[^15]: **C/S.** *Singulariter* is CONTEXTUAL; here "alone". 1 Cor. 10:17 in DR's wording; Wyclif's Latin agrees with the Vulgate.
[^16]: **C/T.** *Magis vacantibus* "lying more idle", of goods not in use; *bonis vivis* is printed so; ABCD read *unus* for *vivis* (Loserth's apparatus, 320.4), which does not construe here. The disturbance and invasion are named by Wyclif without a date. *Ipsorum* refers to the religious.
[^17]: **C.** *Nedum … sed* "not only … but"; *recipere* "take back", the alms having come from the realm. "A threefold teaching" announces the arguments of which this is the first; "Second" follows at 320.15.
[^18]: **C.** The ablative *minus quam propria ecclesia delinquente* is taken of the French church: it offends less than the English lords' own church. *Probabiliter* "with probability".
[^19]: **C.** *Primi / secundi* are William's seizure and the taking of the French church's goods. *Conducunt* is taken as "lead the way"; it may also be "hire" (cf. *conducentes*, "those who hire them").
