# *De ecclesia*, chapter 15, part 1: may the king take away the clergy's temporal goods? the duty to warn, and the precedents

## ¶1 · 328.27–330.12

> [p. 328] Secundum dubium est, si rex Anglie a clero suo legio potest legitime auferre temporalia donata ecclesie [p. 329] pro cleri sui abusu elemosine vel contemptu. Et est dubium istud necessariissimum ad tractandum, quia multis et magnis de regno nostro videtur quod pars affirmativa conclusionis sit satis catholica, aliis autem pinguioribus regis elemosinariis videtur quod conclusio sit omnino heretica. Et indubitanter assero quod, si conclusio ista sit heretica, supposito facto regali regum Anglie, vel totum regnum vel maior pars eius est exhinc infectum heretica pravitate: et cum sacerdotes et specialiter episcopi et scolastici regni nostri tenentur predicere regi et regno suo periculum, patet quod ipsi specialiter et regnum communiter tenentur in isto puncto cognoscere veritatem. Non enim sunt rex et regnum adeo indurati quin, docto quod dogma illud foret hereticum, ipsum non amplius practizarent, sed ad vocem multitudinis cleri sui concito revocarent. Sic enim ad predicacionem Jone Ninivite salubriter penitebant (ut patet Jone III° et Matthei XII°), sic eciam Nabugodonozor conversus est ab infidelitate et salvatus finaliter virtute Dei et prudencia Danielis (ut patet in canone XXIII, q. IV. Nabuchodonosor ex historia prophete et testimonio Augustini); et revera videtur maior disposicio in regno nostro et eius principibus quam fuit in predicto populo infideli. Quomodo ergo foret fidelis vel caritative diligens regem suum qui videret ipsum ex ignorancia super oram baratri paratum ad precipicium sempiternum et assistente revocandi noticia in sepe conversionis ex negligenti desidia permitteret tam gravem populum in corpore et anima perpetuo interire, specialiter si ad speculandum et prenarrandum regis et regni pericula sit pinguiter premiatus? Nonne dicit Deus prophete [p. 330] Ezechielis XXXIII°, 7, 8: Et tu fili hominis speculatorem dedi te domui Israel, audiens ergo ex ore meo sermonem annunciabis eis ex me. Si me dicente ad impium: Impie, morte morieris, non fueris locutus, ut custodiat se impius a via sua, ipse impius in iniquitate sua morietur, sanguinem autem eius de manu tua requiram. Et multo magis in tempore legis gracie, quando debemus ex lege Christi habere ampliorem noticiam et amorem et insuper habemus a rege temporalem retribucionem amplius quam habuit Ezechiel vel quam propheta alius veteris testamenti. Dampnabilis ergo foret nostra segnicies.

The second doubt is whether the king of England can lawfully take away from his liege [clergy]{g:clerus} the [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} given to the [church]{g:ecclesia}, [329] for his clergy's abuse of the [alms]{g:elemosina} or for contempt.[^1] And this doubt is most necessary to treat, because to many great men of our [kingdom]{g:regnum} it seems that the affirmative side of the [conclusion]{g:conclusio} is catholic enough, but to others, the king's fatter [almsmen]{g:elemosinarius}, it seems that the conclusion is altogether heretical. And I assert without doubt that, if this conclusion is heretical, given the royal deed of the kings of England, either the whole kingdom or the greater part of it is thereby infected with [heretical depravity]{g:heretica pravitas}; and since the [priests]{g:sacerdos}, and especially the [bishops]{g:episcopus} and the schoolmen of our kingdom, are bound to foretell its danger to the king and his kingdom, it is clear that they especially, and the kingdom generally, are bound to know the truth on this point. For the king and kingdom are not so hardened that, once it had been taught that that doctrine was heretical, they would not cease to [put it into practice]{g:practizare}, but would quickly revoke it at the voice of the multitude of their clergy. For so at the preaching of Jonah the Ninevites did penance to their salvation (as is clear in Jonah 3 and Matthew 12); so too Nebuchadnezzar was converted from [unbelief]{g:infidelitas} and finally saved by the power of God and the prudence of Daniel (as is clear in the canon C. 23 q. 4 *Nabuchodonosor*, from the history of the prophet and the testimony of Augustine);[^2] and truly there seems to be a greater disposition in our kingdom and its princes than there was in that unbelieving people. How then would he be faithful, or love his king in [charity]{g:caritas}, who saw him through ignorance on the brink of the abyss, ready for the everlasting plunge, and, though knowledge of how to call him back was at hand in the hedge of conversion,[^3] through negligent sloth allowed so weighty a people to perish forever in body and soul, especially if he is richly rewarded for watching over and foretelling the dangers of king and kingdom? Does not God say to the prophet, [330] Ezekiel 33:7–8: "So thou, O son of man, I have made thee a [watchman]{g:speculator} to the house of Israel: therefore thou shalt hear the word from my mouth, and shalt tell it them from me. When I say to the wicked: O wicked man, thou shalt surely die: if thou dost not speak to warn the wicked man from his way: that wicked man shall die in his iniquity, but I will require his blood at thy hand." And much more in the time of the [law of grace]{g:lex gracie}, when we ought, from the [law of Christ]{g:lex Christi}, to have fuller knowledge and love, and moreover have from the king a temporal reward beyond what Ezekiel or any other prophet of the old testament had. Damnable, therefore, would be our sloth.

## ¶2 · 330.13–330.30

> [p. 330] Nec valet ista triplex infamis instancia, prima qua dicitur quod rex et regnum forent exhinc exasperati ad auferendum temporalia que habemus. Ista enim accusans obieccio indicat quod plus diligimus nostra temporalia quam datorem, cum irreligiose plus timemus amissionem nostrorum temporalium quam amissionem perpetuam corporis et anime regis nostri. Secunda instancia dicit quod ista materia ventilata est et decisa in Romana curia coram papa. Revera non credo sed, supposito quod sic, illi quibus illud est cognitum sunt accusabiles tamquam proditores qui sic celant istud periculum. Nam infinitum plus est periculi quam si quotlibet corporales hostes ex scitu celantis invaderent et destruerent funditus regnum nostrum, et insuper cum tales dampnantes istam materiam sunt mortui vel divisi, stultum foret expectare publicacionem decisionis eorum in tanto periculo. Tercia instancia dicit quod tam rex quam regnum

Nor does this threefold infamous [objection]{g:instancia} hold. The first is the one in which it is said that the king and kingdom would thereby be provoked to take away the temporal goods we have. For this objection accuses [us] and shows that we love our temporal goods more than the giver,[^4] since we irreligiously fear the loss of our temporal goods more than the perpetual loss of the body and soul of our king. The second objection says that this matter has been aired and decided in the [Roman curia]{g:curia romana} before the [pope]{g:papa}. Truly, I do not believe it; but, supposing that it were so, those to whom it is known are open to accusation as traitors, who thus conceal this danger. For there is infinitely more danger in it than if any number of bodily enemies, with the knowledge of the one who conceals it, invaded and utterly destroyed our kingdom; and moreover, since such as condemn this matter are dead or divided,[^5] it would be foolish to await the publication of their decision in so great a danger. > [p. 330] processit prospere, quando clerus noster a tractatu talis materie supersedit. Ista ficticia destructa [p. 331] est superius XII. cap.; et in proposito inficit se ipsam. Quomodo, inquam, regno nostro inesset prosperitas, quando a rege et capitalibus eius tam prelatis quam dominis defenderetur tanta iniquitas. Error enim cui non resistitur approbatur; sed altera istarum est nedum error sed heresis pro qua dignum esset ut rex et totum suum sacerdocium destruatur. Ideo non dubium rex et domini seculares sed specialiter prelati regni facerent in isto diligenter inquirere veritatem et excitarent defenderentque theologos hoc tractantes, cum eis incumbit errores tales discutere et de periculo regem et regni incolas premunire. Et revera ex utraque parte contradiccionis sequitur grande periculum, eo quod, sive sit hereticum sive non sit hereticum, supposito facto magna pars regni nostri ex adhesione, ex defensione vel omissione inficitur heretica pravitate.

How, I say, would there be prosperity in our kingdom when so great an iniquity was defended by the king and his chief men, prelates as well as lords? For an error that is not resisted is approved; now one or the other of these is not only an error but a heresy, for which it would be fitting that the king and all his priesthood be destroyed.[^7] Therefore without doubt the king and the secular lords, but especially the [prelates]{g:prelatus} of the kingdom, should cause the truth to be diligently inquired into in this, and should rouse and defend the theologians who treat it, since it falls to them to discuss such errors and to forewarn the king and the inhabitants of the kingdom of the danger. And truly great danger follows from either side of the contradiction, in that, whether it is heretical or not heretical, given the deed, a great part of our kingdom is infected with heretical depravity through adherence, through defence, or through omission.

## ¶4 · 331.17–332.19

> [p. 331] Intendo ergo isto modo procedere: primo narrare factum regum Anglie in hac parte, secundo recitare opiniones doctorum in materia, et tercio obicere contra illas. Quantum ad primum, patet legenti cronicas regni nostri quomodo Willelmus conquestor multas elemosinas regum Saxonum de abbatiis, de episcopatibus et aliis dotacionibus sub nomine perpetue elemosine pro perpetuo variavit, nunc subtrahendo, nunc transferendo et nunc loco cleri prioris extraneos inducendo. Nec valet fingere sompnia quod vel a papa habuit licenciam plus ad ista quam ad conquestum regni de iure sibi debiti. Et sic de sompnio quo fingitur ipsum post restituere omnes ablatarios clericos, sicut nec restituit heredes Saxonum rebellantes; et revera hereticare ipsum finaliter, cum totum ius regni fuit ab ipso ad omnes reges medios successione hereditaria usque hodie derivatum, foret minis infectiva presumpcio. Infectiva dico, quia hereticaret reges clerum et populum interim succedentes; [p. 332] sed dimissis destruccionibus Templariorum et ablacionibus privatis que interim contigerunt, recolamus de ablacionibus talibus que diebus nostris per illustrem regem Edwardum tercium erant facte. Nam temporalia domini Willelmi Bathman Norwycensis capta sunt in manus regis et tenta duodecim annis continuis pro contemptu. Et idem contigit de domino Johanne Gransoni Exoniensi, de fratre Thoma de Lyle, episcopo Heliensi, et sic de multis ablacionibus quas diebus nostris cognovimus. Ymmo Ricardus II. rex noster sicut avus suus practizat hoc annuatim in religiosis possessionatis de Francia; nec super isto consulunt curiam Romanam, quia ut constanter defendunt ista potestas directe pertinet ad coronam. Nec recolo quod temporalia mobilia ablata restituunt, cum contemptus vel forisfactura elemosinariorum regis requirit ut gravius puniantur, nisi quod rex misericorditer eos respicit, sicut in possessionem elemosinarum regni sui eos misericorditer introduxit.

I intend, therefore, to proceed in this way: first, to narrate the deed of the kings of England in this regard; second, to recite the [opinions]{g:opinio} of doctors on the matter; and third, to object against them. As to the first, it is clear to anyone who reads the chronicles of our kingdom how William the Conqueror permanently altered many alms of the Saxon kings (abbeys, bishoprics and other [endowments]{g:dotacio} under the name of [perpetual alms]{g:elemosina perpetua}), now withdrawing them, now transferring them, and now bringing in foreigners in place of the former clergy. Nor is it any use to feign dreams that he had licence from the pope for these things any more than for the conquest of the kingdom owed him by right.[^8] And so with the dream in which it is feigned that he afterwards restored all the clerics whose goods had been taken away, any more than he restored the heirs of the Saxons who rebelled; and truly, to [pronounce him heretical]{g:hereticare} at the last, since the whole right of the kingdom has been derived from him through all the intervening kings by hereditary succession down to today, would be an all too infectious presumption.[^9] Infectious, I say, because it would pronounce heretical the kings, clergy and people who have succeeded in the meantime; [332] but, leaving aside the destructions of the Templars and the private takings-away that occurred in the meantime, let us recall such takings-away as were made in our own days by the illustrious king Edward III. For the temporal goods of the lord William Bateman of Norwich were taken into the king's hands and held for twelve continuous years for contempt.[^10] And the same happened with the lord John Grandisson of Exeter, with brother Thomas de Lisle, bishop of Ely, and so with many takings-away which we have known in our days.[^11] Indeed, Richard II our king, like his grandfather, puts this into practice every year upon the [possessioner]{g:possessionati} [religious]{g:religiosi} of France; nor do they consult the Roman curia on this, because, as they steadily maintain, this power belongs directly to the crown.[^12] Nor do I recall that they restore movable temporal goods once taken away, since the contempt or forfeiture of the king's almsmen requires that they be punished more heavily, except in so far as the king regards them mercifully, just as he mercifully brought them into possession of the alms of his kingdom.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Pro cleri sui abusu elemosine vel contemptu*: *abusu* and *contemptu* are taken as parallel ablatives after *pro*, the contempt being the clergy's (cf. *pro contemptu*, 332.6). A in the margin: *Materia de ablacione temporalium cleri*.

[^2]: **S.** Jonah 3:5–10; Matt. 12:41. Decretum C. 23 q. 4 c. 22 *Nabuchodonosor* (Loserth; form ours, LEDGER E15a-1). A in the margin: *Nabuchodonosor salvatus est*.

[^3]: **C.** *In sepe conversionis* (so the print, on the plate, and A A₁): taken as *saepes*, "hedge, fence", the barrier at the abyss's edge; *in spe conversionis*, "in hope of conversion", would give easier sense but has no witness.

[^4]: **C.** *Ista enim accusans obieccio*: the objection "accuses" those who make it, by what it reveals. A in the margin: *Triplex instancia*.

[^5]: **R.** *Mortui vel divisi*: the popes and cardinals of the Schism that opened in 1378 (LEDGER E15a-2).

[^6]: **T.** The paragraph division at 330.30/31 falls inside one sentence, *Tercia instancia dicit quod tam rex quam regnum / processit prospere*; the singular *processit* takes king and kingdom together. "Chapter 12": Loserth refers to p. 265 above (LEDGER E15a-8).

[^7]: **S/C.** The maxim *Error cui non resistitur approbatur* is a canon of the Decretum, D. 83 c. 3 (Innocent: *Error, cui non resistitur, approbatur*) (LEDGER E15a-3). *Sed* brings the minor premiss. *Altera istarum*, "one or the other of these": the affirmative or the negative answer to the doubt, as 331.13–16 explains.

[^8]: **C.** *Vel a papa*: *vel* is "even", "any more": no papal licence for the seizures is to be feigned, any more than for a conquest William claimed by right. *Ablatarios clericos*: the clerics deprived.

[^9]: **T.** 331.33: the print reads *minis* (so on the plate), which construes only as "infectious with threats"; read as *nimis*, "all too" (our emendation). Print *minis*; translated *nimis*. *Finaliter*: at the end of his life, as one who died a heretic (cf. 335.7–9).

[^10]: **R.** William Bateman, bishop of Norwich: Loserth (citing Walsingham, *Hist. Angl.* I, 262) notes, after Matthew, that he was bishop for not quite eleven years (1344–55), often employed abroad in the king's favour, and that Wyclif may mean a single estate lost in the king's courts, not all his temporal goods (LEDGER E15a-4).

[^11]: **R.** John Grandisson, bishop of Exeter (A A₁ *Oxoniensis*; B and the print *Exoniensi*): Walsingham I, 309; Rolls of Parliament II, 245 (Loserth, LEDGER E15a-5). Thomas de Lisle, bishop of Ely (A *Hyle*): Walsingham I, 285 (Loserth, LEDGER E15a-6). The modern forms of the names are ours.

[^12]: **R.** The seizure of the alien priories' revenues in the French war; Loserth cites Rolls of Parliament III, 22b. The identification with the alien priories is ours (LEDGER E15a-7). *Practizare in*: "put into practice upon".
