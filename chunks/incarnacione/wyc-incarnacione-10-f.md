# *De benedicta incarnacione*, chapter 10, part 6: the humanity's acts and blessedness, and the properties of a man in it

## ¶1 · 179.20–181.12

> [p. 179] Sed ne dicatur tales denominaciones humanitatis non posse probabiliter sustineri, cum non sit persona; arguitur primo sic ad illam probandam. Nam illa humanitas est mole magna et accidentaliter qualis cum aliis denominacionibus corporeis accidencium; ergo per idem habet denominaciones spirituales accidentales secundum animam. Probatur consequencia eo quod dato antecedente⁶ illa humanitas crevit a quantitate infantili⁶ usque ad quantitatem virilem, et hoc⁷ secundum omnes condiciones augmentacioni proprie competentes: ergo augmentabatur, et per idem ambulabat super terram et mare, predicavit, manducavit, et sic de ceteris actibus⁸ animalibus, que Christo conveniebant. Nam, cum componitur ex omnibus partibus,⁹ ex quibus et Christus; fuit ex se mobilis, sicut et Christus: et per consequens [p. 180] sufficeret exspirando aerem a suo pulmone secundum suam ymaginativam et¹ cetera² vocis organa formare voces simillime sicut Christus. Non enim haberet organa sensuum² et operum suppositalium superflua; sic quod, habendo oculos et aures et instrumenta vocalia, preclusa esset sibi via per positivam³ assumpcionem ne sufficiat plus sentire quam surdus et mutus; sic quod sufficiencia senciendi et personaliter operandi foret per negacionem,⁴ qua⁵ imperficitur relaxata. Patet ergo, cum⁶ humanitas Christi habet de propriis tam formas quam organa ad humanitus operandum, nec ligatur ex benedicta unione, qua⁷ tantum perficitur; quod sufficit, ut loquitur scriptura, humanitus operari. Et confirmatur ex hoc quod Christus non predicat,⁸ vel ambulat,⁸ nec ‘interpellat pro nobis’ ‘factus obediens,’ nisi secundum illam humanitatem. Sed quandocunque duobus nate⁹ sunt inesse denominaciones et non uni nisi¹⁰ per reliquum, sequitur quod illi reliquo denominacio inest primo iuxta illud principium Aristotelis primo Posteriorum; ‘propter quod unumquodque et illud magis.’ Ergo prius taliter¹¹ predicavit et ceteros actus humanos fecit illa humanitas quam Christus, cum solum secundum illam sic fecerat: sic enim iuxta sentenciam 5ᵗᵒ Physicorum: si homo sit sanus secundum thoracem vel moveatur secundum partem, tunc illa pars primo movetur et totum per accidens. Cum ergo denominaciones humane insunt Verbo secundum illam humanitatem, que est primum et completum subiectum eorum; videtur quod prius insunt sibi quam Verbo, cum non possunt inesse Verbo nisi per illam, et sibi possunt inesse cum hoc quod non insunt Verbo; ut signato motu progressivo Christi certum est quod iste non potuit fuisse sine illa humanitate, cum motus ex 5ᵗᵒ Physicorum individuatur¹² a spacio, a [p. 181] tempore, et subiecto. Si ergo Christus secundum aliam humanitatem moveretur pro eodem loco et tempore secundum quamlibet eius partem quantitativam, alius foret motus. Cum igitur illi humanitati dimisse potest inesse dictus motus sine hoc quod insit Verbo, ut dicitur, et Verbo non possit inesse nisi sibi infuerit¹; patet² quod naturaliter prius sibi inest.³ Si enim denominacio creata post naturaliter insit Verbo hoc foret² secundum deitatem vel secundum humanitatem vel mixtim; non secundum deitatem quia nulla forma accidentalis inest sibi nisi secundum naturam qua est mobilis; si secundum humanitatem sive parcialiter⁴ sive totaliter; tunc illi humanitati naturaliter prius inest.

But lest it be said that such [namings]{g:denominacio} of the [humanity]{g:humanitas} cannot be upheld [with probability]{g:probabiliter}, since it is not a [person]{g:persona}, it is argued first thus to prove it. For that humanity is of great bulk and accidentally qualified, with the other bodily namings of [accidents]{g:accidens}; therefore by the same token it has spiritual accidental namings as to the soul. The [inference]{g:consequencia} is proved by this, that, the [antecedent]{g:antecedens} being granted, that humanity grew from the quantity of infancy up to the quantity of manhood, and this according to all the conditions properly belonging to [augmentation]{g:augmentacio}: therefore it was augmented, and by the same token it walked upon land and sea, preached, ate, and so of the other animal acts that belonged to Christ. For since it is composed of all the parts of which Christ also [is], it was [movable]{g:mobilis} of itself, as Christ also [was]: and consequently [180] it would suffice, breathing out air from its lung according to its imagination, and [with] the other organs of the voice, to form utterances most like Christ's.[^1] For it would not have the organs of the senses and of the works of a [supposit]{g:suppositum} superfluous, so that, having eyes and ears and vocal instruments, the way would be closed to it by a positive [assumption]{g:assumpcio}, so that it could sense no more than one deaf and dumb; so that the sufficiency for sensing and for acting personally would come through a [negation]{g:negacio}, upon whose relaxing it is made imperfect.[^2] It is clear, then, since Christ's humanity has of its own both forms and organs for acting [humanly]{g:humanitus}, and is not bound by the blessed [union]{g:unio}, by which it is only perfected, that it suffices, as scripture speaks, to act humanly. And this is confirmed by this, that Christ does not preach or walk, nor "maketh intercession for us", "becoming obedient", except according to that humanity.[^3] Now whenever namings are of a nature to be in two, and in the one only through the other, it [follows]{g:sequitur} that the naming is first in that other, according to that principle of Aristotle in the first [book] of the *Posterior* [*Analytics*]: "that on account of which each thing [is such] is itself more [so]". Therefore that humanity preached and did the other human acts in this way before Christ [did], since he had done so only according to it: for so, according to the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of *Physics* V, if a man is healthy as to his chest, or is moved as to a part, then that part is moved first and the whole [by accident]{g:per-accidens}.[^4] Since, then, human namings are in the [Word]{g:verbum} according to that humanity, which is their first and complete [subject]{g:subiectum}, it seems that they are in it before [they are] in the Word, since they cannot be in the Word except through it, and they can be in it without being in the Word; as, designating Christ's progressive motion, it is certain that it could not have been without that humanity, since motion, from *Physics* V, is individuated by space, [181] time and subject. If, then, Christ were moved according to another humanity for the same place and time, as to every quantitative part of it, it would be another motion.[^5] Since, then, the said motion can be in that humanity, once [laid aside]{g:dimittere}, without being in the Word, as is said, and cannot be in the Word unless it has been in it, it is clear that it is naturally first in it. For if a created naming were afterwards naturally in the Word, this would be either [as to the Godhead]{g:secundum-deitatem} or [as to the humanity]{g:secundum-humanitatem} or mixedly; not as to the Godhead, since no accidental form is in him except according to the [nature]{g:natura} by which he is movable; if as to the humanity, whether partially or totally, then it is naturally first in that humanity.[^6]

## ¶2 · 181.13–182.8

> [p. 181] Et indubie⁵ idem videtur iudicium de spirituali denominacione secundum animam. Nam si anime sanctorum sunt beate in celo, ymo et corpora; quanto magis completa Christi humanitas, cum habeat in se primo subiective omnem creatam beatitudinem qua Christus humanitus est beatus. Augustinus itaque, qui ponit quod anima prius nedum intelligit ymo sentit quam totum compositum, diceret quod anima Christi in celo haberet visionem et fruicionem beatificam ultra quamcunque aliam animam. Nec est fingendum quod assumpcio ligat spiritum Iesu ne beatifice delectetur. Et per idem habendo quatuor⁶ dotes corporis foret perfecte et complete beata eadem beatitudine, qua est Christus; et idem est iudicium de omnibus accidentibus creatis que humanitus⁷ insunt Christo. Quomodo, queso, aliter adorarem eam latria, si non tantum benefecit michi ut crux Christi? aut quomodo dimissa recoleret de operibus Christi sui, vel maneret inter passionem et mortem dimissa a Deo corpore fatigata⁸ si nichil antea⁹ fuit passa. Dimissa ergo in uno loco et Christo assumente eam in alio, foret plus quam Christi germanus, ut patet de convenienciis substancialium [p. 182] et accidentalium: et non est conveniencia amplior nisi fuerit personalis; in nullo itaque casu Verbum assumeret personam, que esset natura Verbi passa et quomodolibet temporaliter¹ formata; ut patet de comitancia absolutorum conmultiplicatorum cum taliter multiplicatis; et sic facta revelacione illi homini quod adnichilabitur, patet quod dolor redundaret in Christum, nulli autem alteri homini posset sic affici.

And undoubtedly the same judgement seems [to hold] of a spiritual naming as to the soul. For if the souls of the [saints]{g:sancti} are blessed in heaven, indeed the bodies too, how much more the complete humanity of Christ, since it has in itself first, as subject, all the created blessedness by which Christ humanly is blessed. So Augustine, who holds that the soul [not only]{g:nedum} understands but even senses before the whole composite, would say that Christ's soul in heaven would have the beatific vision and [fruition]{g:fruicio} beyond any other soul.[^7] Nor is it to be feigned that the assumption binds the spirit of Jesus so that it may not delight beatifically. And by the same token, having the four [dowries]{g:dotes} of the body, it would be perfectly and completely blessed with the same blessedness with which Christ is; and the same is the judgement of all the created accidents that are in Christ humanly.[^8] How, I ask, should I otherwise adore it with [latria]{g:latria}, if it has not done me as much good as the cross of Christ? Or how, laid aside, would it recall the works of its Christ, or remain between the passion and death, laid aside by God, wearied in body, if it had suffered nothing before?[^9] If, then, it were laid aside in one place and Christ assumed it in another, it would be more than Christ's brother, as is clear from the [agreements]{g:conveniencia} of substantial [182] and accidental [things]: and there is no fuller agreement unless it is personal; so in no case would the Word assume a person which was a nature of the Word that had suffered and been formed in any way in time; as is clear from the concomitance of absolutes multiplied together with the things so multiplied; and so, if it were revealed to that man that he will be [annihilated]{g:adnichilare}, it is clear that the grief would redound to Christ; but he could be so affected toward no other man.[^10]

## ¶3 · 182.9–182.31

> [p. 182] Quo dato, videtur multipliciter quod humanitas Christi sit homo, et sic persona Verbi. Nam nichil est risibile nisi homo; humanitas Christi est risibilis, cum sufficiat predicare: ergo est homo. Maior patet ex hoc quod risibilitas est propria passio hominis convertibilis cum specie. Et idem est iudicium de passione mortalitatis capta a causa materiali, de productivitate sui similis in specie, que competit sibi passionaliter a causa efficiente, et de beatificabilitate² secundum corpus et animam que competit sibi a³ causa finali.³ Cum ergo predicaciones huiusmodi solum possunt formaliter inesse suppositis, videtur, cum humanitati Christi insunt, quod ipsa sit suppositum Verbi. Secundo confirmatur idem ex hoc quod omnis forma⁴ accidentalis non est aliud⁵ nisi subiectum accidentaliter se habere; ut mocio est mobile⁶ moveri, locucio est ipsum loqui, et ita de ceteris, ut hic supponitur. Cum ergo eodem accidente informatur Christus et ista humanitas, sequitur quod idem est Christus et illa humanitas: vel⁷ si Christus non sit illa humanitas sed movetur secundum illam, alia est mocio Christi et alius motus eius: et ita numerarentur omnes forme complete accidentales in Christo secundum numerum subiectorum: quod⁸ est impossibile, cum nullum tale accidens foret pars alterius, sed quelibet pars unius pars alterius et e contra.

This granted, it seems in many ways that Christ's humanity is a man, and so the person of the Word. For nothing is [risible]{g:risibilis} but a man; Christ's humanity is risible, since it is able to preach: therefore it is a man. The major is clear from this, that risibility is the proper [attribute]{g:passio} of man, [convertible]{g:convertibilis} with the species.[^11] And the same is the judgement of the attribute of mortality, taken from the material cause; of the productivity of its like in species, which belongs to it [as an attribute]{g:passionaliter} from the efficient cause; and of [beatifiability]{g:beatificabilitas} as to body and soul, which belongs to it from the final cause. Since, then, predications of this sort can be formally only in supposits, it seems, since they are in Christ's humanity, that it is a supposit of the Word.[^12] Second, the same is confirmed by this, that every accidental form is nothing but the subject's being accidentally disposed; as motion is the [movable]{g:mobile}'s being moved, speech is the speaking itself, and so of the rest, as is supposed here.[^13] Since, then, Christ and this humanity are informed by the same accident, it follows that Christ and that humanity are the same: or if Christ is not that humanity but is moved according to it, the motion (*mocio*) of Christ is one, and his movement (*motus*) another; and so all complete accidental forms in Christ would be numbered according to the number of subjects: which is impossible, since no such accident would be a part of the other, but every part of the one [is] a part of the other and conversely.[^14]

## Notes

[^1]: **C/T.** "And [with] the other organs": *cetera vocis organa* stands in the accusative beside *aerem* without a verb of its own; "[with]" is supplied. C omits *humanitas* (179.26), O reads *illa humanitas crevit infacili* (179.26–27); A B C read *sic* for *hoc* (179.28), *accidentibus* for *actibus* (179.31), omit *partibus* (179.32) and *et* (180.2); A B C rearrange or shorten *cetera … sensuum* (180.2–3).

[^2]: **C/T.** The opponents' account turned on itself: if the assumption is "positive" and the person is constituted by a negation of dependence, the assumed humanity would lose by the assumption the powers it had as a person; *qua … relaxata* is construed as an ablative absolute, "upon whose relaxing". A B C read *mediacionem* "mediation" for *negacionem* (180.8); O *quia* for *qua*; A B *propositum*, C *perpositam*, for *positivam* (180.6).

[^3]: **S/T.** Rom. 8:34 ("who also maketh intercession for us"; cf. Heb. 7:25); Phil. 2:8 ("becoming obedient"). A B C read *quod* for *cum* (180.9) and *predicavit vel meruit* "preached or merited" for *predicat, vel ambulat* (180.13); O *quam* for *qua* (180.11).

[^4]: **S/T.** Aristotle, *Posterior Analytics* I.2 (72a29–30), the axiom *propter quod unumquodque tale, et illud magis*, the cause of an attribute's inherence inhering more than the attribute (LEDGER I10f-1, verified); Wyclif has no *tale*. *Physics* V.1 (224a21–26): the body is healed because the eye or the chest is; Aristotle calls this change in respect of a part, which he distinguishes from change by accident (LEDGER I10f-2, verified). *Sentencia*: the teaching of the book. O reads *causaliter* "causally" for *taliter* (180.19); B *innate*, C omits *sunt* (180.15); A B C read *unica per* without *nisi* (180.16).

[^5]: **S/T.** *Physics* V.4 (227b21–29), a motion one without qualification by the oneness of that in which, the time, and the thing moved (LEDGER I10f-3, verified). O reads *indîatur* (180.31).

[^6]: **C/T.** *Post* "afterwards" (181.7) is doubtful; A B C, which omit the passage from *Si enim* to *hoc foret*, have *prius* in what they keep (*patet quod si naturaliter prius insit verbo hoc foret*, 181.6–8). O omits *infuerit* (181.6), reads *in esse* for *inest* (181.7) and *particulariter* for *parcialiter* (181.11).

[^7]: **S/T.** Augustine, that the soul understands and senses before the composite: place not found; compare *De Genesi ad litteram* III.5.7 (PL 34, 282), *sentire non est corporis, sed animae per corpus* (LEDGER I10f-4, untraced). O omits *Et indubie* (181.13).

[^8]: **G/T.** The four dowries of the glorified body. B omits *quatuor* (181.23); O reads *humana* for *humanitus* (181.26).

[^9]: **C/T.** Latria, the worship due to God alone: Wyclif argues from the adoration of Christ's humanity, which would be idle were it no subject of merit. O reads *fangata* for *fatigata* (181.30) and *anna* for *antea*, which Harris asks whether to take as *anima*.

[^10]: **C/T.** The case of the humanity laid aside and reassumed: it would be nearer to Christ than any brother, and since no agreement is fuller than the personal, the Word could not assume as a separate person a nature that had been his. "Absolutes": absolute accidents, multiplied with their subjects. A B C read *temporalis* for *temporaliter* (182.4).

[^11]: **C/G.** Risibility is the stock example of a *proprium*, the proper attribute convertible with the species; "since it is able to preach": the power of speech goes with reason, and so with risibility.

[^12]: **T.** A B C read *et beatificante* for *beatificabilitate* (182.16) and omit *a causa finali* (182.17).

[^13]: **T.** "Is nothing but": C and O omit *aliud* (182.21), against the print's *non est aliud nisi* "is not anything other than"; the English follows C O. C omits *forma* (182.21) and *mobile* (182.22).

[^14]: **C/T.** *Mocio* and *motus* are set apart here: one motion of Christ, another of the humanity by which he is moved. O reads *ut* for *vel* (182.26) and *et* for *quod* (182.30).
