# sed/autem extract, wave 23: chunks 08-h 08-i 08-j

### 08-h ¶3 · 260.19–262.3  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 260] Prima istarum quinque conclusionum videtur ex hoc quod omnis rei temporalis duracio potest esse tempus **sed** omnis talis rei duracio est quidquid potest esse, ergo omnis rei talis duracio est tempus. Assumptum patet per hoc quod tempus et quando sunt accidencia diversorum generum, sic quod omne tempus per se primo est tempus, sicut omnis homo est per se homo, et sic de quando; posito ergo de quacunque re permanente quod per se foret successive durans, tunc eius duracio foret tempus, quod sic opinans dicit esse contingencium; sive ergo fuerit forma sive materia sive accidens per se positum, ipsum posset per se subiectare tempus; ymmo tempus cum sit quantitas poterit per se esse; quod est magis quam posse informare quamcunque substanciam permanentem; ideo non negabit ista secta possibilitatem assumpti. Nec [p. 261] valet allegari Aristoteles vel Averrois quod tempus solum sequitur motum primi mobilis in quo est solummodo subiective, quia illi negant accidens per se esse; ideo miror qua fronte ipsi allegarent istos pro sua sentencia, cum tantum discordant in principiis. Isti enim dicunt quod Deus non potest facere quod incipiat esse tempus, cum non potest (ut inquiunt) creare mundum nec potest movere aliquam partem mundi nisi mediante motu primi mobilis; quorum quodlibet est summe hereticum, ideo est baculus nimis arundineus accipere sentencias istas hereticas ad stabiliendum subiectum temporis. Ideo iuxta sentenciam Augustini, XII Confessionum, totus mundus est subiectum temporis, cum quiescente toto celo et mota rota figuli foret idem tempus quod modo; cum ergo tempus non potest migrare de subiecto in subiectum, manifestum est quod tempus individuatur et subiectatur in mundo; nam quiescente quacunque parte mundi non subiectaret tempus et per idem nec modo. Et istam sentenciam consideraret hec secta, antequam dixisset tot sentencias hereticas in materia de sacramento altaris. Ponit enim quod inpossibile est panem remanere post consecracionem (ut dictum est quinto capitulo) et fundatur super isto principio summe heretico: Nichil potest esse ubi prius non fuit, nisi vel moveatur illuc localiter vel aliud convertatur in ipsum; quod indubie destrueret totam fidem, quia tunc Deus non posset creare celum vel aliquid nec dominus Jesus Christus posset crescere corporaliter (ut deductum est ibidem) nec Spiritus Sanctus posset elevare hominem ad conversandum in celo, nisi moveat illuc localiter corpus suum. Que tria destruerent [p. 262] totam fidem. Oportet ergo fidelem rimari aliud fundamentum ad stabiliendum subiectum temporis a quo ipsum dependeat.

ENGLISH: The first of these five [conclusions]{g:conclusio} appears from this: the duration of every temporal thing can be time; now the duration of every such thing is whatever it can be; therefore the duration of every such thing is time. [The assumption]{g:assumptum}[^5] is clear from the fact that time and [the when]{g:quando} are [accidents]{g:accidens} of different genera, so that every time is [of itself]{g:per-se},[^6] first, time, as every man is of himself man; and so of the when. So, supposing of any permanent thing whatever that of itself it were lasting successively, then its duration would be time, which the one who holds this [opinion]{g:opinio} says is among contingent things.[^7] So whether it be form or matter or an accident posited of itself, it could of itself [be the subject of]{g:subiectare} time; indeed time, since it is a [quantity]{g:quantitas}, will be able to be of itself; which is more than to be able to [inform]{g:informare} any permanent [substance]{g:substancia} whatever; therefore this sect will not deny the possibility of the assumption. Nor [261] is it any use to [allege]{g:allegare} Aristotle or Averroes to the effect that time follows only the motion of [the first movable]{g:primum-mobile}, in which alone it is [subjectively]{g:subiective},[^8] for they deny that an accident is of itself; so I wonder with what face they would allege these men for their [teaching]{g:sentencia},[^9] since they disagree so much in principles. For these men say[^10] that God cannot make time begin to be, since (as they say) he cannot create the world, nor can he move any part of the world except by means of the motion of the first movable; each of which is supremely heretical; so it is an [exceedingly]{g:nimis} reedy staff[^11] to take these heretical teachings to establish the [subject]{g:subiectum} of time. Therefore, according to the teaching of Augustine, *Confessions* XII,[^12] the whole world is the subject of time, since, if the whole heaven were at rest and a potter's wheel were moved, there would be the same time as now. Since, then, time cannot migrate from subject to subject, it is manifest that time is individuated and has its subject in the world; for when any part of the world whatever is at rest, it would not be the subject of time, and by the same token not now either. And this sect should have considered this teaching before it had uttered so many heretical teachings in the matter of [the sacrament of the altar]{g:sacramentum-altaris}. For it posits that it is impossible for the [bread]{g:panis} to remain after the [consecration]{g:consecracio} (as was said in the fifth chapter), and it is founded on this supremely heretical principle: "Nothing can be where it previously was not, unless either it is moved there [locally]{g:localiter} or something else is converted into it";[^13] which would without doubt destroy the whole faith, because then God could not create heaven or anything, nor could the Lord Jesus Christ grow [bodily]{g:corporaliter} (as was deduced in the same place), nor could the Holy Spirit raise a man up to [live his life]{g:conversari} in heaven, unless he moves his body there locally.[^14] Which three things would destroy [262] the whole faith. The faithful man must therefore search out another foundation, on which it may depend, for establishing the subject of time.

### 08-h ¶6 · 262.14–262.17  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 262] Et tercia conclusio, scilicet quod quidlibet foret in quolibet, videtur ex hoc, quod quidlibet foret in tempore cuiuslibet, **sed** nichil est in tempore subiecti, nisi fuerit in ipso; ergo quidlibet foret in quolibet.

ENGLISH: And the third conclusion, namely that anything would be in anything, appears from this: anything would be in the time of anything; now nothing is in the time of a subject unless it is in it; therefore anything would be in anything.

### 08-i ¶2 · 265.20–266.4  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 265] Nec valet in ista materia allegare diffiniciones Aristotelis in hac parte, quia iuxta hanc sectam oportet dicere quod Aristoteles cum secta peripatetica et Augustinus cum multis sanctis diffiniendo accidencia blasfemarunt, ut ipsi asserunt quod nullum accidens habet esse aliud preter formaliter substancie inherere; terna dimensio corporis quod est longitudo, latitudo et profunditas, vel ipsa substancie magnitudo aut corporeitas non potest per se esse. Ista **autem** secta dicit quod hoc est hereticum, cum ista quantitas sit per se necessario sacramentum, et sic verecundaretur hec secta ex testimonio Aristotelis vel sanctorum doctorum quidquam describere vel aliquam per se [p. 266] differenciam assignare, ymmo nescit si de communi cursu nature Deus sic ordinavit, cum noviter ponit quidditatem et miraculum sacramenti altaris quod nec scriptura nec sancti doctores cognoverant.

ENGLISH: Nor is it any use in this matter to [allege]{g:allegare} Aristotle's [definitions]{g:diffinicio} on this point, because according to this sect one has to say that Aristotle with the Peripatetic sect, and Augustine with many of [the saints]{g:sancti}, blasphemed in defining accidents, since they assert that no accident has any [being]{g:esse} other than to inhere formally in a [substance]{g:substancia};[^9] the threefold dimension of a body, which is length, breadth and depth, or the very magnitude or corporeity of the substance, cannot be of itself. But this sect says that this is heretical, since this [quantity]{g:quantitas} is of itself necessarily the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum};[^10] and so this sect would be ashamed[^11] to [define]{g:describere} anything from the testimony of Aristotle or of the holy doctors, or to assign any [difference]{g:differencia} of itself; [266] indeed it does not know whether God has so ordained in the common course of nature, since it newly posits a quiddity and a miracle of [the sacrament of the altar]{g:sacramentum-altaris} which neither scripture nor the holy doctors had known.

### 08-i ¶3 · 266.5–266.14  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 266] **Sed** dimissis istis deliramentis sectivagis notandum quod eternitas, evitas et tempus sic differunt: Eternitas est permanencia Dei in se, quam Boetius describit quod est interminabilis vite possessio tota simul, et illa est prima mensura possibilis; unde denominat non solum naturam divinam, **sed** omnes relaciones in Deo, omnes ydeas et omnes eternas veritates in Deo ad intra, cum secundum racionem et non essencialiter distinguuntur a Deo; et ideo sunt eterna, quia sunt natura que est sine principio vel fine suo.

ENGLISH: But, these [sect-roving]{g:sectivagus} [ravings]{g:deliramenta}[^12] being set aside, it is to be noted that eternity, [aeveity]{g:evitas} and time differ thus. Eternity is the permanence of God in himself, which Boethius defines thus: that it is "the whole and simultaneous possession of interminable life";[^13] and that is the first possible measure. Hence it [names]{g:denominare} not only the divine [nature]{g:natura}, but all relations in God, all [ideas]{g:ydea} and all eternal truths in God within (*ad intra*), since they are distinguished from God by reason and not [essentially]{g:essencialiter}; and therefore they are eternal, because they are the nature which is without beginning or end of its own.[^14]

### 08-i ¶5 · 267.4–267.22  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 267] Tercia mensura est tempus quod est mensura certissima, quia numerus instancium mensurancium secundum prius et posterius motum mundi; quod est idem quod mundi duracio successiva; ipsum **autem** est ad omnem punctum mundi. Instans vero est mensura partis indivisibilis motus mundi; et sic tempus consequitur motum mundi in communi (ut sonant verba philosophi), Deus **autem** ex immensitate eternitatis est ante omne instans temporis et post omne instans et presens cuilibet instanti preterito vel futuro; tempus **autem** a mundi inicio usque ad finem mensurat ipsum per se primo quoad successionem et quandamlitatem que est mensura alterius generis quamlibet eius partem. Quando enim est illud quod derelinquitur ex adiacencia temporis (ut hic supponitur), et sic post diem iudicii erit idem evum mundi sine tempore; quod evum licet sit modo, tamen consideracione eius sopita attendimus ad tempus nobis magis sensibile.

ENGLISH: The third measure is time, which is the most certain measure, because [it is] the number of the instants measuring the motion of the world according to before and after; which is the same as the successive duration of the world; and it is at every point of the world. And the instant is the measure of an indivisible part of the motion of the world; and so time follows the motion of the world in common (as the Philosopher's words run).[^17] But God, from the immensity of eternity, is before every instant of time and after every instant, and present to every instant past or future; and time, from the beginning of the world to the end, measures it of itself, first, as to succession, and [measures] each part of it as to whenness, which is a measure of another genus.[^18] For the when is that which is left behind from the adjacency of time[^19] (as is supposed here); and so after the day of judgement there will be the same aeviternity of the world without time; which aeviternity, although it is now, yet, our consideration of it being lulled asleep, we attend to time, which is more [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} to us.

### 08-j ¶1 · 267.23–268.9  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 267] Ulterius notandum quod non esset fuisse vel fore nisi racione temporis et per consequens subducto omni tempore Deus non fuit vel erit **sed** est absolute necessario. Et sic in eternitate non est formaliter hec differencia, et per consequens cum Deus et sua eternitas, angelus et sua evitas fuerunt et erunt, oportet quod capiant illas denominaciones eis accidentales a tempore et sic habent quando; quod non presupponit in eis naturalem periodum sicut creature naturaliter [p. 268] corruptibiles, et ita Deus equivoce fuit et erit in comparacione ad naturas corruptibiles, sicut alia respectiva accidencia equivoce sibi insunt. Idem ergo tempus in numero mensurat eternitatem Dei, evitatem angeli et sic de aliis supra tempus quoad denominacionem respectivam qua tempore mensurantur. Et ita videtur quod non propterea tempus aliud **sed** alia quandalitas sine mutacione essencie est ponenda. Ista **autem** hic sufficiunt.

ENGLISH: It is further to be noted that there would be no having-been or going-to-be except by reason of time;[^1] and consequently, all time being taken away, God was not, nor will be, but is, absolutely, necessarily. And so in eternity this [difference]{g:differencia} is not formally; and consequently, since God and his eternity, an angel and its [aeveity]{g:evitas}, were and will be, they must take those [namings]{g:denominacio}, accidental to them, from time, and so they have a when;[^2] which does not presuppose in them a natural period, as [it does in] [creatures]{g:creatura} naturally [268] corruptible; and so God was and will be [equivocally]{g:equivoce} in comparison with corruptible natures, as other [relational]{g:respectivus} [accidents]{g:accidens} are in him equivocally. The same time in number, then, measures the eternity of God, the aeveity of an angel, and so of the other things above time, as to the [relational naming]{g:denominacio-respectiva} by which they are measured by time. And so it seems that not, on this account, another time, but another [whenness]{g:quandalitas},[^3] without change of essence, is to be posited. And these things suffice here.

### 08-j ¶2 · 268.10–271.24  (16 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 268] **Sed** redeundo post digressionem ad difficultatem tactam de multiplicacione videtur quod secunda sentencia sit tenenda; cum enim inpossibile sit idem corpus multiplicari situaliter vel dimensionaliter per diversa loca et tam fides de eukaristia quam eciam communis philosophia docet esse multiplicacionem substancie, patet veritas secunde sentencie. Vocatur **autem** multiplicacio, quando eadem res creata est simul in tempore per multa loca secundum se totam, ut anima, universalia et instancia; tempus **autem** non sic multiplicatur, cum omni loco pro quocunque instanti partes sue defuerint ut partes preterite et future; potest tamen extense loquendo concedi multiplicari, quia secundum multas partes suas est per diversa loca et sic commune lumen, communis species, vel similitudo rei multiplicatur, quia per unum magnum situm est et per quamlibet eius partem secundum aliquod eius suppositum, sicut universale et ubicunque habuerit aliquod singulare. Si **autem** quodlibet tale singulare habuerit esse per quamlibet partem dati situs, tunc et illud singulare multiplicatur per situm eundem, sicut ponitur de speciebus visibilis. [p. 269] Deus **autem** non potest multiplicari, quia necessario est secundum se totum ubique; nec potest aliquo loco vel tempore diffinire vel secundum motum sui alicubi esse; ideo ex parte sui non contingit multiplicacio vel secundum tempus diffinicio. Nam secundum sanctos non est in eo formaliter multitudo, alia **autem** licet sint ubique scilicet mundus, tempus et universalia quorum individua sunt, ubique tamen dispariter a Deo; nam mundus non secundum se totum est ubique, quia non quelibet eius pars quantitativa ubique distenditur, nec quelibet pars subiectiva generis corporis vel universalis huius est ubique, nec tempus habet omnes partes suas simul pro eodem instanti, **sed** quadam dependencia ex motu aliter est in celo et aliter in terra. Deus **autem** secundum immensitatem suam primo et per se replet omnem locum et secundum eternitatem suam primo et per se est per omne tempus; non potest contingere variacio ex parte sui in essendo per locum vel tempus **sed** oportet quod sit variacio in substancia creata; cuius variacio vocatur accidens, ut Deus per se efficienter replet omnem locum et corpus, vel eius principium per se formaliter replet locum, sic quod punctale materialis essencie cum non possit maiorari vel minui est per se punctale, cuius punctalitas est punctus; et sic de quantitate corporis que consequitur ad suum primum subiectum, sicut risibilitas ad hominem et secundum racionem qua Deus immobilis ponit substanciam materialem secundum racionem immobilem, cuius situacio vel locacio est situs vel locus quem Aristoteles vocat inmobile primum. Deus enim non posset movere illam racionem secundum primum motum qui est motus localis. Et hec est prima racio immobilitatis sensibilis qua ascendimus ad cognoscendum inmobilitatem [p. 270] ultimam Dei nostri; et cum videmus rem moveri de loco ad locum ex ordinacione Dei, vocamus illam vicissitudinem motus tempus vel duracionem, et individuamus locum et tempus a genere substancie vel motus cum aliis circumstanciis individuantibus quas Deus ad hoc ordinat. Et sic videmus dominum nostrum in omni vicissitudine creature. Et sicut Deus est primo magnus et immensus per se efficaciter et finaliter replens locum, ita est primo per se durans et eternus, per se finaliter mensurans omnium temporalium vices, et sic inmensitas et eternitas Dei non sunt accidencia vel divisibiles (ut hic supponitur); et sicut cum Deus non potest esse alius Deus, ita per eundem situm punctalem non potest esse simul alius punctus vel materialis essencia, cum tota capacitas sua repletur, quia non est aliud nisi illam materialem essenciam situari vel saltem aliam tantam aut tantam speciem quantitatis. Cum **autem** forma substancialis aut accidentalis non sit nisi talem essenciam informari, patet quod non exinde multiplicatur situs replecio. Loquor **autem** de situ vel loco (ut supra), ut dicit materialem essenciam situari. Et patet logicis quod aliud est Petrum esse in loco sibi adequato et aliud est ipsum esse per locum sibi adequatum. Cum enim sint mille loca inter ipsum et celum sibi equalia, quia ut sunt laciora sunt magis tenua, et omnia ipsum undique circumstancia, patet quod est in totidem locis sibi equalibus et per consequens adeo sibi adequatis, et tamen per nullum illorum locorum est, cum per nullam partem talis loci sit aliqua pars Petri cum a quolibet tali distat, ideo longe aliud est Petrum esse in multis locis sibi adequatis et aliud est ipsum [p. 271] esse per multa loca sibi adequata. Est enim per locum, quando ad aliquam partem illius loci signati est aliqua pars sui, sicut omnis materialis substancia per locum diffunditur, qualiter repugnat idem corpus in numero per distancia loca simul distendi; potest tamen distendi in uno loco et habere aliud esse spirituale in alio tamquam in signo aut virtute, sicut dictum est de rege. Et sic patet de corpore Christi, quod est dimensionaliter in celo et virtualiter in hostia ut in signo. **Sed** in isto est magna dissensio de modo essendi corporis Christi in hostia: Quidam enim dicunt quod corpus Christi est tantum secundum essenciam suam ibi presens; quod tantum diffinitur loco hostie quod posset esse illo modo ibidem et alibi nusquam esse, **sed** alii admittunt hoc, quia foret Deo longe facilius quam servare accidens sine subiecto, ideo super illos iacet difficultas, quomodo multiplicatur cum corpore Christi quantitas et alia accidencia committantur. Super nos **autem** non iacet difficultas ista: Esto quod corpus Christi sit in sacramento panis, aut virtus et non corpus Christi quod in celo extenditur, sic quod corpus Christi non est non corpus, **sed** est non corpus Christi ibi; et est illud quod ibi extenditur **sed** non ibi extenditur.

ENGLISH: But, returning after the digression to the difficulty touched on concerning [multiplication]{g:multiplicacio-corporis}, it seems that the second [teaching]{g:sentencia}[^4] is to be held. For since it is impossible for the same body to be multiplied [situally]{g:situaliter} or [dimensionally]{g:dimensionaliter} through diverse places, and both the faith about [the Eucharist]{g:eukaristia} and also common philosophy teach that there is a multiplication of [substance]{g:substancia},[^5] the truth of the second teaching is clear. And it is called multiplication when the same created thing is at once in time through many places as a whole, as the soul, [universals]{g:universale} and instants; but time is not so multiplied, since for any instant whatever its parts are lacking to every place, as the past and future parts. Yet, [speaking in an extended sense]{g:extense-loquendo}, it can be granted to be multiplied, because according to its many parts it is through diverse places; and so a common light, a common species,[^6] or likeness of a thing is multiplied, because it is through one great site and through every part of it according to some [supposit]{g:suppositum} of it, as a universal is also wherever it has some singular. And if each such singular has being through every part of a given site, then that singular too is multiplied through the same site, as is posited of the species of a visible thing. [269] But God cannot be multiplied, because he is necessarily everywhere as a whole; nor can he be defined[^7] by any place or time, or be somewhere according to his own motion; so on his side there occurs no multiplication, nor definition according to time. For according to [the saints]{g:sancti} there is not formally [multitude]{g:multitudo} in him; and other things, although they are everywhere (namely the world, time, and the universals whose individuals are [everywhere]), are yet everywhere otherwise than God. For the world is not everywhere as a whole, since not every [quantitative part]{g:pars-quantitativa} of it is spread out everywhere; nor is every [subjective part]{g:partes-subiective} of the genus body, or of such a universal, everywhere; nor does time have all its parts at once for the same instant, but by a certain dependence on motion it is one way in heaven and another way on earth. But God, according to his immensity, first and [of himself]{g:per-se}[^8] fills every place, and according to his eternity is first and of himself through all time. No variation can occur on his side in being through place or time, but the variation must be in the created substance, whose variation is called an accident: as God of himself efficiently fills every place and body, or its principle of itself formally fills the place, so that the [punctual]{g:punctale} of a material essence, since it cannot be made greater or less, is of itself punctual, and its [punctuality]{g:punctalitas} is the point; and so of the [quantity]{g:quantitas} of a body, which follows on its first [subject]{g:subiectum} as [risibility]{g:risibilitas} on man; and according to the aspect by which God, [immovable]{g:immobilis}, posits material substance according to an immovable aspect, whose situation or location is the site or place which Aristotle calls the first immovable.[^9] For God could not move that aspect with the first motion, which is local motion. And this is the first ground of perceptible immovability, by which we ascend to knowing the ultimate immovability [270] of our God. And when we see a thing moved from place to place by God's [ordinance]{g:ordinacio}, we call that [alternation]{g:vicissitudo} of motion time or duration, and we individuate place and time from the genus of substance or of motion, with the other individuating circumstances which God ordains for this. "And so we see our Lord in every alternation of the creature."[^10] And as God is first great and immense, of himself efficaciously and finally filling place, so he is first of himself lasting and eternal, of himself finally measuring the turns of all temporal things; and so the immensity and eternity of God are not accidents, nor divisible (as is supposed here). And as God cannot be another God, so through the same punctual site there cannot be at once another point or material essence, since its whole capacity is filled; for [its being filled] is nothing other than for that material essence to be sited, or at least another as great, or so great a species of quantity.[^11] And since a [substantial form]{g:forma-substancialis} or accidental form is nothing but such an essence's being [informed]{g:informare}, it is clear that the filling of the site is not multiplied thereby. And I speak[^12] of site or place (as above) as it [signifies]{g:dicere} a material essence's being sited. And it is clear to logicians that it is one thing for Peter to be in a place adequate to him and another for him to be through a place adequate to him. For since there are a thousand places between him and the heaven equal to him (since, as they are wider, they are thinner), and all surrounding him on every side, it is clear that he is in as many places equal to him, and consequently so far adequate to him; and yet he is through none of those places, since through no part of such a place is there any part of Peter, since he is distant from every such [place]. So it is a far different thing for Peter to be in many places adequate to him and for him [271] to be through many places adequate to him. For a thing is through a place when at some part of that designated place there is some part of it, as every material substance is spread out through place; in which way it [is incompatible]{g:repugnare} for the same body in number to be extended at once through distant places. Yet it can be extended in one place and have another, spiritual, being in another, as in a sign or in power, as was said of the king.[^13] And so it is clear of [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} that it is [dimensionally]{g:dimensionaliter} in heaven and [virtually]{g:virtualiter} in the [host]{g:hostia} as in a sign. But on this there is great disagreement about the mode of being of the body of Christ in the host. For some say that the body of Christ is present there only according to its essence, which is so far defined by the place of the host that it could be there in that mode and be nowhere else; but others admit this, because it would be far easier for God than to preserve an [accident without a subject]{g:accidens-sine-subiecto};[^14] so on them lies the difficulty how quantity is multiplied with the body of Christ, and the other accidents accompany it.[^15] But this difficulty does not lie on us. Grant that the body of Christ is in the sacrament of bread, or [its] power, and not the body of Christ which is extended in heaven; so that the body of Christ is not not-a-body, but is not the body of Christ there; and it is that which is extended there, but it is not extended there.[^16]

### 08-j ¶3 · 271.25–272.11  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 271] Et iste est tercius punctus in quo vario a modernis, verumptamen (ut sepe protestatus sum) volo parate obedire docto quod quantum ad veritatem illius materie fuero sive superfluus, sive parcus. Miratur **autem** mundus quomodo post dotacionem ecclesie vel variaretur sacramentum vel innotesceret nostris plus a Christo extraneis vel racio vel fieret revelacio plus quam Paulo et sanctis prioribus. Sic enim tanquam domini leges Christi possent extraneare totam fidem [p. 272] catholicam. Nec dubium quin veritas catholica fuit per auctores scripture sufficienter edocta, licet officium ecclesie peregrinantis cui Deus dedit scienciam sit detegere fidem in scriptura implicita, quando pullulant errores circa eius sensum. Et nunquam est per illos aliquod novum fidei catholice fabricandum; et hinc Deus semper ordinat aliquos qui intendant loyce et sensui scripture, cum tamen debet credi cuicunque pontifici in istis, de quanto validius adduxerit testimonium scripturarum; et notum est eos post dotacionem ecclesie in isto sicut in moribus defecisse.

ENGLISH: And this is the third point in which I differ from [the moderns]{g:moderni};[^17] nevertheless (as I have often [solemnly declared]{g:protestari}) I am willing readily to obey a learned man [who shows] that, as regards the truth of this matter, I have been either excessive or sparing.[^18] And the world wonders how, after the [endowment]{g:dotacio} of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, either the sacrament should be varied, or either a reason should become known or a revelation be made to our men, more estranged from Christ, more than to Paul and the earlier saints. For so, as lords, they could [estrange]{g:extraneare} the laws of Christ, the whole catholic [272] faith.[^19] Nor is there doubt that the catholic truth was sufficiently taught by the authors of scripture, although it is the office of the church [on pilgrimage]{g:peregrinari}, to which God has given knowledge, to [expose]{g:detegere} the faith implicit in scripture[^20] when errors sprout about its [meaning]{g:sensus}. And never is anything new of the catholic faith to be fabricated by them; and hence God always ordains some who attend to the logic and the meaning of scripture, since yet any [pontiff]{g:pontifex} whatever is to be believed in these things in the measure that he has adduced the testimony of the scriptures more strongly; and it is known that they, after the endowment of the church, have failed in this as in morals.
