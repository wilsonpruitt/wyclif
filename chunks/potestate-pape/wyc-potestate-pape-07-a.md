# *De potestate pape*, chapter 7, part 1: Peter's preeminence lies in service, not lordship

## ¶1 · 135.13–135.21

> [p. 135] Sed ulterius transcurrendum est per argumenta capta ex fide evangelii, ut sciatur quam sentenciam reportant in testimonium veritatis. Ex primo itaque argumento capto a diffinicione Christi Luce XXII elicitur, quod preeminencia Petri in alios non stat in honore, in dominatu vel gloria seculari sed in omnino oppositis. Patet primo ex hoc quod stat in fidei sinceritate et humilitate et in servitute, ut patet ex dictis.

But further we must run through the arguments drawn from the faith of the gospel, so that it may be known what [teaching]{g:sentencia} they bring back in testimony of the truth. From the first argument, then, taken from Christ's [definition]{g:diffinicio} in Luke 22, it is drawn out that the preeminence of Peter over others does not consist in honour, in [lordly rule]{g:dominatus} or secular glory, but in the wholly opposite things. This is clear, first, from this: that it consists in sincerity of faith and in humility and in servitude, as is clear from what has been said.[^1]

## ¶2 · 135.22–135.24

> [p. 135] Similiter primatus Petri stat in preeminencia sequendi Christum in moribus; Christus autem omnia predicta abnuit et condiciones oppositas acceptavit.

Likewise, the [primacy]{g:primatus} of Peter consists in preeminence in following Christ in conduct; now Christ refused all the aforesaid things and accepted the opposite conditions.[^2]

## ¶3 · 135.25–136.14

> [p. 135] Similiter, Luce XXII; 26 sic loquitur: Qui maior est vestrum, fiat ut minor. Constat autem ex doctrina prima Petri II quod maiores in ecclesia debent esse quasi modo geniti infantes, qui magis alienati sunt ab honore, a dominatu vel gloria seculari; debet autem primas post Christum esse minor, ad serviendum flexibilior, ut dicitur [p. 136] Matth. XX. Sic enim Christus fuit omnium hominum servus necessarior ac mitior, ut docet Ambrosius epistola, et Augustinus De Verbis Domini sermone VIII: hominem coaptatum unam fecit Deus cum servo personam. Voluit inquam regnum suum conquirere per humilem pacienciam iniurie, et cetera arma virtutum et non per gentilem dominatum, quem apostolis suis prohibuit, sed reverti voluit ad iudicium in celesti Dominio glorioso. Unde infidelior, horribilior et stulcior non posset fieri argucia quam ex vicaria primatus Petri et Christi eius magistri, arguere vel fundare in eorum vicariis triplicitatem predictam mundanam, cum omnino contrarium inferatur.

Likewise, Luke 22:26 speaks thus: "He that is the greater of you, let him become as the younger."[^3] Now it is evident from the teaching of 1 Peter 2 that the greater in the [church]{g:ecclesia} ought to be as newborn babes, who are more estranged from honour, from lordly rule or secular glory; and the primate after Christ ought to be the lesser, more pliant to serve, as is said in [136] Matthew 20.[^4] For so Christ was the servant of all men, more needful and milder [than any], as Ambrose teaches in an epistle; and Augustine, *On the Words of the Lord*, sermon 8: having fitted the man [to himself], God made one person with the servant.[^5] He willed, I say, to conquer his [kingdom]{g:regnum} by humble patience under injury and the other arms of the virtues, and not by gentile lordly rule, which he forbade to his apostles; but he willed to return to judgement in glorious heavenly [lordship]{g:dominium}.[^6] Hence no more faithless, more horrible or more foolish [quibble]{g:argucia} could be made than, from the vicarship of the primacy of Peter and of Christ his master, to argue or ground in their vicars the aforesaid worldly threefold [honour, lordly rule and glory], since altogether the contrary is inferred.

## ¶4 · 136.16–137.31

> [p. 136] Pro declaracione vero istius materie adduxi alia multa testimonia de Christo et unum de beato Bernhardo oportet repetere. Nam libro secundo ad Eugenium ita scribit: Quid dimisit tibi sanctus apostolus, quod habeo, hoc tibi do. Quid illud? Unum scio: non est aurum neque argentum, cum ipse dicat: argentum et aurum non est michi. Si habere contingat, utere non pro libitu sed pro tempore. Sic eris utens illis quasi non utens. Ipsa quidem ad animi bonum non spectant, cuiusmodi sunt divicie, nec sunt per se bona nec mala, usus autem horum bonus, abusio mala, sollicitudo peior, questus turpior. Esto ut alia racione hec tibi vendices sed non apostolico iure. Nec enim ille tibi dare potuit quod non habuit; quod habuit hoc dedit, sollicitudinem ut dixi super ecclesias. Numquid dominacionem? [p. 137] Audi ipsum: non dominantes, ait, in clero, sed forma facti gregis. Et ne dictum sola humilitate putes non eciam veritate, vox Domini est in evangelio: Reges gencium dominantur eorum etc. Et infert: vos autem non sic. Planum est, apostolis interdicitur dominatus. Et igitur tu tibi usurpare audes aut dominans apostolatum aut apostolicus dominatum. Planum est, ab utroque prohiberis. Si utrumque simul habere voles, perdes utrumque. Alioquin non te exceptum de illorum numero putes, de quibus Deus sic conqueritur: Ipsi regnaverunt et non ex me, principes extiterunt et non cognovi eos. Iam si regnare sine Deo iuvat, habes gloriam sed non apud Deum, at si interdictum tenemus audiamus edictum, id est qui maior est vestrum, fiat sicut minor et qui precessor est sicut qui ministrat, forma apostolica hec est, dominacio interdicitur, indicitur ministracio, que et commendatur exemplo ipsius legislatoris, qui secutus adiungit, ego autem in medio vestrum sum tamquam qui ministrat. Ecce quam breviter iste sanctus et quam plane dilucidat sentenciam sepedictam tamquam sequentem formaliter ex scriptura, et revera dampnare, hereticare vel impugnare sentenciam tam sancti, tam devoti, tam profundi atque subtilis theologi sicut fuit beatus Bernhardus ab ecclesia canonizatus et in celo cum angelis gloriosus propter istam sentenciam quam verbo docuit et exemplo, videretur nimis ceca presumpcio et specialiter in materia fidei quam dictavit tam lex Christi quam lex naturalis.

And for the declaration of this matter I have adduced many other testimonies about Christ, and one from blessed Bernard I must repeat. For in the second book *To Eugenius* he writes thus: "What did the holy apostle leave you? 'What I have, this I give you.' What is that? One thing I know: it is not gold or silver, since he himself says: 'Silver and gold I have none.' If it happens that you have it, use it not at your pleasure but as the time requires. So you will be using them as though not using them. These things, indeed, of which kind are riches, do not bear on the good of the soul; they are neither good nor bad in themselves, but the use of them is good, the abuse bad, anxiety over them worse, gain from them more shameful. Grant that you claim these for yourself on some other ground, but not by apostolic [right]{g:ius}. For he could not give you what he did not have; what he had, that he gave: anxiety, as I have said, over the churches. Was it the [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio}?[^7] [137] Hear him: 'Neither as [lording it]{g:dominari} over the clergy,' he says, 'but being made a pattern of the flock.' And lest you think this said only from humility and not also in truth, the voice of the Lord is in the gospel: 'The kings of the Gentiles lord it over them,' etc. And he adds: 'But you not so.' It is plain: lordly rule is forbidden to the apostles. And so you dare to usurp for yourself either the apostolate as one who lords it, or lordly rule as an apostolic man. Plainly you are forbidden either. If you wish to have both at once, you will lose both.[^8] Otherwise do not think yourself excepted from the number of those of whom God thus complains: 'They have reigned, but not by me: they have been princes, and I knew them not.' If it now delights you to reign without God, you have glory, but not with God. But if we hold to the prohibition, let us hear the edict, that is: 'He that is the greater of you, let him become as the younger, and he that is the leader, as he that serveth.' This is the apostolic form: the exercise of lordship is forbidden, ministry is enjoined; and it is also commended by the example of the lawgiver himself, who, following on, adds: 'But I am in the midst of you, as he that serveth.'"[^9] Behold how briefly this saint, and how plainly, sets out the oft-mentioned teaching as following formally from scripture. And in truth to condemn, to [pronounce heretical]{g:hereticare}, or to impugn the teaching of so holy, so devout, so profound and subtle a theologian as was blessed Bernard, [canonized]{g:canonizare} by the church and glorious in heaven with the angels, on account of this teaching which he taught by word and example, would seem too blind a presumption, and especially in a matter of faith which both [the law of Christ]{g:lex-christi} and the natural law dictated.[^10]

## ¶5 · 137.32–138.22

> [p. 137] Constat quidem ex dictis sanctorum et testatur canon XXIV q. III cap. Heresis, quod qui aliter [p. 138] intelligit scripturam sacram, quam Spiritus Sanctus flagitat, adiuncta pertinacia, est expresse hereticus. Iste autem sanctus intellexit et expressit tam opere quam sermone quod non licet sacerdoti Christi, specialiter Romano pontifici, seculariter vel civiliter dominari, in tantum quod papatus et vera secularis dominacio qualis est licita in laycis formaliter contradicunt. Et virtute huius precepti vixerunt apostoli, sancti monachi et mendicantes exproprietarie, usque hodie dicentes tam vita quam verbo quod Dominus suis sacerdotibus hoc precipit; aliquos tamen voluit habere usum temporalium largius et alios striccius ex titulo elemosine secularium dominorum subducto seculari dominio. Unde credo beatum Bernhardum habuisse noticiam de toxico dominandi in Romano pontifice; et ideo dicit libro III ad Eugenium: Nullum tibi venenum, nullum gladium plus formido, quam libidinem dominandi; non enim sequitur ex primo testimonio scripture Luce XX, quo docetur Petrum super alios preeminere sed eius oppositum.

It is evident, indeed, from the sayings of the saints, and the canon attests it, 24, question 3, chapter *Heresis*, that whoever "understands [138] holy scripture otherwise than the Holy Spirit demands", with pertinacity added, is expressly a heretic.[^11] Now this saint understood and expressed, by deed as much as by word, that it is not lawful for a priest of Christ, especially the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex}, to exercise lordship secularly or civilly, so much so that the papacy and true secular exercise of lordship, such as is lawful in lay people, formally contradict each other. And by virtue of this precept the apostles lived, and holy monks and mendicants, [without property]{g:exproprietarie}, saying down to this day by life as well as by word that the Lord commands this to his priests; yet he willed some to have the use of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} more largely and others more strictly, by [title]{g:titulus} of the [alms]{g:elemosina} of [secular lords]{g:seculares-domini}, secular lordship being withdrawn.[^12] Hence I believe that blessed Bernard had knowledge of the poison of lording it in the Roman pontiff; and therefore he says in book 3 *To Eugenius*: "No poison, no sword do I fear more for you than the lust of lordship."[^13] For from the first testimony of scripture, Luke 20, by which it is taught that Peter is preeminent over others, [lordship] does not [follow]{g:sequitur}, but its opposite.[^14]

## ¶6 · 138.23–138.31

> [p. 138] Et ideo libro II declarans pape officium: Si hec facis, inquit, honorificas ministerium tuum, non mediocris est iste principatus. Hoc enim est extirpare malas bestias a terminis tuis, quo greges tui securi in pascua educantur. Domabis lupos sed ovibus non dominaberis. Pascendas utique non premendas suscepisti. Timebat enim iste sanctus ex dominacione illius sedis subversionem vocate ecclesie.

And therefore in book 2, declaring the pope's office, he says: "If you do these things, you honour your ministry; not mean is this principate. For this is to root out evil beasts from your borders, so that your flocks may be led out safe to pasture. You will tame the wolves, but you will not lord it over the sheep. You took them up to be fed, not to be oppressed." For this saint feared, from the exercise of lordship of that see, the subversion of the [so-called]{g:vocatus} church.[^15]

## Notes

[^1]: **S/C.** Luke 22:24–27, Christ's ruling on which of the disciples is the greater. *Argumenta capta ex fide evangelii*: a participle governs *ex*, so "drawn from the faith of the gospel", not the formula "as faith teaches".

[^2]: **C/R.** *Autem* brings the second premiss; the conclusion (Peter's primacy lies in the opposite of honour, lordly rule and glory) is left to the reader. A in marg.: *2* (135.22), *3* (135.26). *Acceptavit* was read on the plate.

[^3]: **S.** Luke 22:26 (DR "he that is the greater among you, let him become as the younger"); Wyclif has *vestrum* for *in vobis* and *ut* for *sicut*, translated. *Minor* is DR's "younger"; in Wyclif's own sentence that follows it is "the lesser".

[^4]: **T/S.** B omits *infantes qui magis* (135.29). 1 Pet. 2:2 (DR "As newborn babes"). Matt. 20:26–27 (DR "whosoever is the greater among you, let him be your minister"). *Primas*: the holder of the primacy.

[^5]: **S/C.** Ambrose, "in an epistle": not identified (LEDGER P07a-1, to-verify). Augustine, *De verbis Domini* sermon 8: Loserth, *Opera* V, 373 (136.3; LEDGER P07a-2, editor); the wording is to be checked (LEDGER P07a-3). *Hominem coaptatum unam fecit Deus cum servo personam* is construed with *Deus* as subject: the man fitted to the Word, and one person with the "form of a servant" (Phil. 2:7); alternatively "God made the man he fitted [to himself] one person with the servant". *Necessarior ac mitior* are comparatives without a stated term.

[^6]: **T/S.** *Virtutum*: the manuscripts read *veritatum* (136.7); Loserth's correction is translated. Gentile lordly rule: Luke 22:25.

[^7]: **S/T/R.** Bernard, *De consideratione* II.6.10 (Loserth: ch. 6, *Opera* (Migne) I, 748; 136.19; LEDGER P07a-4, editor). Acts 3:6 (DR "Silver and gold I have none; but what I have, I give thee"); 1 Cor. 7:31 (DR "as if they used it not"). *Utere* is Loserth's correction; the manuscripts read *vere* (136.23); at 136.24 they read *malicia temporis*, apparently for *pro tempore*. A in marg.: *Nota. Nota beatum Bernardum contra dominacionem cleri sed non contra temporalium possessionem et appropriacionem evangelicam*; B in marg.: *Quid dimisit Petrus suis successoribus* (136.18).

[^8]: **S/T.** 1 Pet. 5:3 (DR, without "from the heart"); Luke 22:25–26 (DR). *Et igitur tu tibi usurpare audes* is translated as printed; Bernard's text as usually cited has *I ergo tu, et tibi usurpare aude* ("Go then, and dare …", ironic; from memory, LEDGER P07a-5, to-verify). The manuscripts read *aut dominus … aut apostolatus dominium* (137.7–8); Loserth prints Bernard's *aut dominans apostolatum aut apostolicus dominatum*.

[^9]: **S/T.** Hos. 8:4 (DR "They have reigned, but not by me: they have been princes, and I knew not"; Bernard adds *eos*, "them"). Luke 22:26 (DR "he that is the leader, as he that serveth"; Bernard has *vestrum*); Luke 22:27 (DR; *tamquam* for *sicut*). *Que et commendatur* is wanting in the manuscripts and supplied by Loserth (137.18); they read *adiungi* for *adiungit* (137.19).

[^10]: **C.** *Sentenciam sepedictam*: the teaching that Peter's preeminence lies in service, not in lordship (135.15 ff.). *Dictavit* is singular with the two laws as subject.

[^11]: **S.** Decretum C.24 q.3 c.27 *Heresis* (Jerome), from memory; Loserth gives no reference (LEDGER P07a-6, to-verify). The canon, as remembered, reads *quam sensus Spiritus Sancti flagitat*; Wyclif's wording is translated (to be checked with P07a-6). *Scripturam* and *quam* were read on the plate.

[^12]: **C.** *Autem* brings the second premiss: whoever understands scripture otherwise than the Spirit demands, pertinaciously, is a heretic; now Bernard understood it so; the conclusion, that those who condemn him on this point fall under the canon, is left implicit. *Ex titulo elemosine … subducto seculari dominio*: the clergy hold temporal goods as alms of the lords, lordship being withdrawn from the clergy.

[^13]: **S.** Bernard, *De consideratione* III.1.2 (Loserth: ch. 1, p. 769; 138.18; LEDGER P07a-7, editor). *Formido* is the verb, "I fear", not the noun "misgiving".

[^14]: **S/C.** "Luke 20" as printed; the first testimony was Luke 22 (135.15), and *XX* may be a slip for *XXII*. The subject of *non sequitur* is unexpressed: "lordship" is supplied from the context; alternatively "it does not follow … that Peter is preeminent, but its opposite", which sits ill with *quo docetur*.

[^15]: **S/T.** Bernard, *De consideratione* II.6.13 (Loserth: ch. 6, p. 749; 138.24; LEDGER P07a-8, editor). Loserth notes that Bernard reads *ministerium tuum et ministerium te* (you honour your ministry, and your ministry you); the print is translated. *Vocata ecclesia*: the church so called, not the true church.
