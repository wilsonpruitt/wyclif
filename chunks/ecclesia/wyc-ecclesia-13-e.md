# *De ecclesia*, chapter 13, part 5: endowment breeds war; the false security of charters

## ¶1 · 290.20–292.1

> [p. 290] Immo ut probabiliter creditur tota dissensio inter regna et gentes diversarum sectarum exoritur propter omissionem culpabilem consiliorum ac privilegiorum que Christus instituit clero suo. Nam si omnes redditus quibus in christianismo dotatur ecclesia, forent inter seculares dominos distributi et clerus ostenderet in se concorditer ac efficaciter contemptum seculi ac tercio predicaret laicis vigilanter de fallaci affeccione temporalium pro momento quo peregrinamur a domino, et quomodo instantaneum seculare desiderium inducit dampnacionem perpetuam, brevis autem mundi contemptus preparat efficaciter ad beatitudinem consequendam, [p. 291] tunc cessarent bella in christianismo, vel simpliciter vel in parte, et disponeretur ecclesia Christi ad conquirendum armis virtutum populum infidelem. Sic enim fuit tempore apostolorum, quando exproprietarie pure secundum Christi consilia vixit clerus, sed commixtus sinistris tradicionibus humanis cum dexteris Christi consiliis, vinum in quo inebriaretur ecclesia est limphatum et genus nervorum caritatis ecclesie debilitatum et sine custode zizannium discordie undique seminatum. Cum enim corpus ecclesie sit limitatum a Deo, quibus cibis et qualiter secundum partes dispares pasceretur, Deus sicut tribuit commensurale alimentum spirituale per prelatos distribuendum fidelibus, sic dat escam corporalem parciendam per activos tam predicatoribus in agro quam contemplatoribus in lecto. Ideo usurpantibus illis violentis officium et consumentibus inordinate eorum pabulum necesse est totum corpus ecclesie esse inordinatum ex defectu observacionis ordinis suarum parcium. Si enim fit bolismus in homine et breviter quocunque membro secundum appetitum caninum contra regulas virtutis regitive trahente alterius nutrimentum vel occupante alterius membri officium, fit in toto corpore discrasia. Si enim viator haberet vermem in stomacho qui consumeret nutrimentum membrorum, quomodo servaretur sanitas et valitudo eorum? Sic si sacerdotes Baal cum uxoribus et filiis consumunt mendaciter per meatus nocturnos et subterraneos bona plebis, patet littera Danielis XIV°: sic autem est in corpore ecclesie cuius virtus regitiva est patris sapiencia; et leges eius, ut mandata atque consilia, sunt privilegia singulariter membris ecclesie officium operandi et nutrimentum limitancia, ut membra superiora debent pasci precipue spiritu animali et regere ac movere membra supposita; [p. 292] quod si dislocantur et innaturaliter pascuntur, necesse est ut in aliis membris fiat discordia.

Indeed, as is probably believed, the whole dissension between [kingdoms]{g:regnum} and peoples of different [sects]{g:secta} arises from the culpable omission of the counsels and [privileges]{g:privilegium} which Christ instituted for his [clergy]{g:clerus}. For if all the [revenues]{g:reditus} with which the [church]{g:ecclesia} is endowed in Christendom were distributed among the secular lords, and the clergy showed in themselves, with one accord and to good effect, contempt of the world, and thirdly preached watchfully to the laity of the deceitful affection for [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} for the moment in which we are [pilgrims]{g:peregrinari} away from the Lord,[^1] and of how an instantaneous worldly desire brings on perpetual damnation, while a brief contempt of the world prepares effectively for attaining blessedness, [291] then wars would cease in Christendom, either [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} or in part, and the church of Christ would be disposed to conquer the [unbelieving]{g:infidelis} people with the arms of the virtues. For so it was in the time of the apostles, when the clergy lived purely [without property]{g:exproprietarie} according to the counsels of Christ; but, [the clergy] being mingled with left-hand human traditions together with the right-hand counsels of Christ,[^2] the wine with which the church was to be inebriated has been watered, and the kind of the sinews of the church's [charity]{g:caritas} weakened, and, there being no keeper, the [cockle]{g:zizania} of discord sown everywhere.[^3] For since the body of the church has been [assigned]{g:limitare} by God what foods it should be fed with, and how, according to its unlike parts, God, as he grants commensurate spiritual nourishment to be distributed to the faithful through the [prelates]{g:prelatus}, so gives bodily food to be shared out through the active, to the preachers in the field as well as to the contemplatives in bed.[^4] Therefore, when those violent ones usurp [their] office and inordinately consume their fodder, the whole body of the church must be disordered, from failure to observe the order of its parts.[^5] For if bulimia arises in a man, and, in short, if any [member]{g:membrum}, by a canine appetite against the rules of the governing power, draws away the nourishment of another or occupies the office of another member, a dyscrasia arises in the whole body.[^6] For if a [wayfarer]{g:viator} had a worm in his stomach which consumed the nourishment of the members, how would their health and strength be preserved?[^7] So it is if the [priests]{g:sacerdos} of Baal with their wives and children consume the goods of the people deceitfully, through nightly and underground passages, as the letter of Daniel 14 makes clear; and so it is in the body of the church, whose governing power is the wisdom of the Father.[^8] And its laws, as the commandments and counsels, are privileges assigning singly to the members of the church the office of working and their nourishment, as the higher members ought to be fed chiefly by the animal spirit, and to rule and move the members placed beneath; [292] and if these are dislocated and fed unnaturally, discord must arise in the other members.[^9]

## ¶2 · 292.2–292.17

> [p. 292] Unde credo quod nunquam in corpore ecclesie cessabit simpliciter bellum, dolus atque contencio, antequam cleri dislocacio, secularis officii usurpacio, et per consequens officii speculatoris omissio sit per virtutem regitivam ecclesie restituta; in tanta namque armonia constituta est pars mundi superior quod secundum Commentatorem una stella addita celo vel moveret cum fatigacione et pena, vel simpliciter non moveret; et multo magis in regno celorum quod est ecclesia contrariacio superiorum suo motori facit quod tota ecclesia fatigabiliter sit turbata. Tota autem contrariacio stat in hoc quod bona temporalia plus bonis perpetuis sunt amata. Ideo ve illis qui stulte faciunt in affeccione pastorum temporalia spiritualibus anteponi.

Hence I believe that war, guile and contention will never cease without qualification in the body of the church before the dislocation of the clergy, the usurpation of secular office, and in consequence the omission of the office of [watchman]{g:speculator}, has been set right by the governing power of the church. For the upper part of the world is constituted in such harmony that, according to the Commentator, one star added to the heaven would either move with fatigue and pain or would not move, without qualification;[^10] and much more, in the kingdom of heaven, which is the church, the contrariety of superiors to their mover makes the whole church troubled to the point of fatigue. And the whole contrariety consists in this, that temporal goods are loved more than perpetual goods. Therefore woe to those who foolishly cause temporal things to be put before spiritual in the affection of pastors.

## ¶3 · 292.18–292.28

> [p. 292] Si autem prelati sint magis avari, honoris mundani plus cupidi, plus viventes secundum fastum seculi, per bella excitata de bonis pauperum ad vindicandum proprias iniurias amplius secularibus insistentes, et (quod periculosissimum est) sub pallio sanctitatis quicquid perpetraverint fuco adulterandi verbum Dei sophistice defendentes, quomodo cum isto circulo quinarii stabit regnum? Scio quod in ista rota currus diaboli seducerentur (si fieri potest) eciam electi, et non sic preparatur ad hic augendum populum domini sed satellitum Antichristi.

But if prelates are more avaricious, more covetous of worldly honour, living more according to the pomp of the world, pressing harder upon secular things through wars stirred up out of the goods of the poor to avenge their own injuries, and (what is most dangerous) defending sophistically, under the cloak of holiness, whatever they have perpetrated, with the paint of adulterating the word of God, how will the kingdom stand with this circle of five?[^11] I know that on this wheel of the devil's chariot even the elect would be led astray (if it can be), and so no preparation is made here for increasing the people of the Lord, but [the people] of the henchmen of Antichrist.[^12]

## ¶4 · 292.29–294.10

> [p. 292] Et quantum ad secundam partem assumpti, patet quod infideliter vendicatur a clericis in talibus dotacionibus a mundo securitas; nam carta evangelii quam Christus concessit non potest deficere. Unde Matthei VI°, 25, precipit clero suo ne sit sollicitus [p. 293] quid manducabit vel bibet, aut quomodo induatur, sicut solliciti sunt viventes gentiliter, quia, ut arguit per locum a maiori: Si Deus providet pastum volucribus celi et tegumentum liliis agri, quanto magis domesticis fidei? Si enim tempore legis veteris quando minus suadebatur elemosina, Psalmista dicat, Psalmo XXXVI°, 26: Junior fui, etenim senui et non vidi iustum derelictum nec semen eius querens panem, quanto plus hodie? Tollat ergo clerus offensam domini non declinando a lege sua, sicut communiter facimus affectando inordinate voluptatem et curiositatem, et non est compossibile quod ad deterioriacionem desint nobis necessaria vite nostre, quia Matthei VI°, 33, dicit veritas: Querite ergo primum regnum Dei et iusticiam eius, et hec omnia adicientur vobis. Privilegium autem Christi concessum Luce X°, suis discipulis est securius quam carta vel concessio mundana, cum dominus mundi qui mentiri non poterit sic precipit: In quamcunque domum intraveritis ibi manete edentes et bibentes que apud illos sunt. Sed debemus cavere sollicite ne amittamus hoc privilegium per abusum, quod fit quando contra regulam et vitam apostoli sumus hospitibus onerosi. Quomodo ergo stat cum fide et religione christiana quod clerus confederatus et obligatus mundo per cartas plus confidit in dotacione cesarea quam divina, cum Luce XXI°, 34, precipiat ut caveamus a crapula et curis seculi. Et patet ex fide scripture quomodo observantes ordinem illum preposterum sunt insecurissimi quoad homines et quoad Deum. Oportet enim primo quod originetur in Deo securitas que non potest fundari nisi in observancia legis sue. Et [p. 294] hec racio quare Deus ad abstergendum cecitatem mundanam ab oculis mentis nostre ordinat nunc translacionem regnorum in qua extinguitur cleri mundana securitas, nunc permittit predones, fures, incendium vel alium eventum fortuitum evenire. Unde tollantur temporalia in quibus seculares clerici prepostere confidebant, et nunc tercio permittit dissensiones oriri inter partes, tollens seculi potentes de medio per quod coniectura providencie humane eciam in superioribus ecclesie sepius est cassata.

And as to the second part of the [assumption]{g:assumptum}, it is clear that security from the world is claimed faithlessly by clerics in such [endowments]{g:dotacio}; for the charter of the gospel which Christ granted cannot fail.[^13] Hence in Matthew 6:25 he commands his clergy not to be solicitous [293] what they shall eat or drink, or how they shall be clothed, as those who live like the heathen are solicitous; because, as he argues by [the topic from the greater]{g:locus-a-maiori}: if God provides food for the birds of the air and covering for the lilies of the field, how much more for those who are of the household of the faith?[^14] For if in the time of the old law, when [alms]{g:elemosina} were less urged, the Psalmist says in Psalm 36:26, "I have been young and now am old; and I have not seen the just forsaken, nor his seed seeking bread," how much more today?[^15] Let the clergy therefore remove the offence of the Lord by not turning aside from his law, as we commonly do by inordinately [desiring]{g:affectare} pleasure and curiosity; and it is not [compossible]{g:compossibilis} that the necessaries of our life should fail us to [our] impairment, for in Matthew 6:33 the Truth says: "Seek ye therefore first the kingdom of God, and his justice, and all these things shall be added unto you." And the privilege of Christ granted to his disciples in Luke 10 is more secure than a worldly charter or [grant]{g:concessio}, since the Lord of the world, who cannot lie, commands thus: "Into whatsoever house you enter, there remain, eating and drinking such things as they have."[^16] But we must take careful heed not to lose this privilege through abuse, which happens when, against the rule and life of the apostle, we are burdensome to our hosts. How then does it stand with the faith and the christian religion that the clergy, confederated with and bound to the world by charters, trusts more in caesarean endowment than in divine, since in Luke 21:34 he commands that we beware of surfeiting and the cares of the world?[^17] And it is clear [from the faith of scripture]{g:ex-fide} how those who observe that preposterous order are most insecure as regards men and as regards God. For security must first originate in God, and it cannot be founded except in the observance of his law. And [294] this is the reason why God, to wipe worldly blindness from the eyes of our mind, now ordains the transfer of kingdoms, in which the worldly security of the clergy is extinguished, now permits robbers, thieves, fire or some other chance event to come about, so that the temporal goods in which secular clerics preposterously trusted may be taken away; and now, thirdly, he permits dissensions to arise between parties, removing the mighty of the world from the midst, whereby the conjecture of human providence, even in the superiors of the church, is quite often brought to nothing.[^18]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 290.20: Quarrels and wars would end if the Church kept Christ's privilege of poverty.
- 291.11: The Church is disordered when the clergy encroaches upon the work and reward assigned by God to the laity.
- 292.2: All the evils in the Church spring from worldliness.
- 292.18: The security that comes of endowments is a false one.
- 293.1: The true security depends on the promise of Christ who commanded us not to be careful for these things.

## Notes

[^1]: **S/C.** 2 Cor. 5:6, *peregrinamur a Domino* (DR "we are absent from the Lord"); the glossary's "pilgrims" keeps Wyclif's Augustinian sense. *Ac tercio* counts a third condition after the distribution of the revenues and the clergy's showing contempt of the world.
[^2]: **C.** *Commixtus* (nom. sg.) has no verb of its own and is taken with *clerus*; it may instead be meant absolutely ("once [the clergy] was mingled …"). *Sinistris … dexteris* plays on left and right hand: human traditions against Christ's counsels.
[^3]: **T/S.** *Genus nervorum* ("the kind of the sinews") is printed without variant and translated as it stands; a word may be lost or corrupt here (e.g. *omne genus*), see the report. *Limphatum*: wine mixed with water (cf. Isa. 1:22, "thy wine is mingled with water"). The cockle sown without a keeper recalls Matt. 13:25.
[^4]: **S/C.** *In agro … in lecto*: the field and the bed of Luke 17:34–36 (Matt. 24:40), read of preachers and contemplatives. *Activos*: those of the active life, who share out bodily food.
[^5]: **T/C.** Loserth's foot (291.17): "*violentis*; recte: *violenter*" ("those usurping the office violently"). His print keeps *violentis*, which construes; translated as printed. *Illis* are the clergy who take over the office and fodder of the laity (side-note 291.11).
[^6]: **C.** Medical terms: *bolismus* (bulimia, ravenous hunger), *appetitus caninus*, *virtus regitiva* (the governing power of the body), *discrasia* (dyscrasia, ill mixture of the humours). B *discrusia*.
[^7]: **T.** *Consumeret* (291.25) is broken across the line in the print (*con-|sumeret*) and is restored from the plate.
[^8]: **S/C.** Dan. 14:1–21 (Bel and the dragon), where the priests with their wives and children came in by hidden doors and consumed the offerings (14:20); the Vulgate's idol is Bel, Wyclif writes *Baal*. The period is loose (*Sic si … patet … sic autem est*): taken as "so it is if …, as Daniel shows; and so it is in the church".
[^9]: **C.** *Spiritus animalis*: the "animal spirit" of the physicians, seated in the head, which governs sense and motion; the higher members are the clergy.
[^10]: **S/C.** The Commentator is Averroes; the passage (presumably on *De caelo* II) is to be checked (LEDGER E13e-1). *Moveret* may be transitive, "would move [the heaven]".
[^11]: **S/R/T.** *Adulterandi verbum Dei*: 2 Cor. 4:2 ("nor adulterating the word of God"). A in marg. (292.18): *Nota quinque*, the five vices just counted, which make the *circulus quinarii*. B (292.21–22) adds *et per quod*.
[^12]: **S/T.** Matt. 24:24 ("insomuch as to deceive (if possible) even the elect"). *Ad hic augendum* (print): B *hoc augendum* ("for increasing this people"), A₁ *sic agendum*; the print construes and is kept.
[^13]: **T.** B *terciam partem* (292.29). The "assumption" is the premiss Wyclif is proving in this chapter; its second part concerns the security of endowments (side-note 292.18).
[^14]: **S.** Wyclif's paraphrase is nearer Matt. 6:31 (*quid manducabimus, aut quid bibemus, aut quo operiemur*) than 6:25; Loserth refers the argument to 6:26–28 (birds of the air, lilies of the field). *Domesticis fidei* is Gal. 6:10 (DR). *Clero suo*: Wyclif reads the disciples as Christ's clergy.
[^15]: **S.** Ps. 36:25 in the Clementine (the print gives verse 26); Wyclif's words agree with the Vulgate.
[^16]: **S/C.** Luke 10:7 (*manete, edentes et bibentes quae apud illos sunt*), opened with the words of Luke 9:4 (*in quamcumque domum intraveritis, ibi manete*), which Loserth cites. *Autem* here moves to the next privilege. The rule of the apostle: 2 Thess. 3:8, "lest we should be chargeable to any of you".
[^17]: **S/C.** Luke 21:34 paraphrased (DR "lest perhaps your hearts be overcharged with surfeiting … and the cares of this life"). *Dotacione cesarea*: endowment from Caesar, against God's provision (cf. GLOSSARY *ecclesia cesarea*).
[^18]: **C/T.** *Unde tollantur*: the subjunctive is taken as the purpose of the losses God permits ("so that … may be taken away"); it could be a wish ("hence let … be taken away"). A A₁ *servantes ordinem* for *observantes* (293.29).
