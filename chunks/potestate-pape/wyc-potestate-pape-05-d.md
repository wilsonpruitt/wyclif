# *De potestate pape*, chapter 5, part 4: lawful use, the more perfect state, and the likeness of Peter's vicar

## ¶1 · 89.21–93.30

> [p. 89] Notandum tamen quod temporalia non occasione ab eis data sed male accepta venenant ecclesiam. Si enim totus clerus diceret effectualiter quod omnia temporalia que habemus ut clerici forent pure elemosine secularium et bona communia pauperum, secundo effectualiter et indifferenter ministraremus de illis magis egentibus et tercio consumeremus precise de illis quantum est necessarium ad nostrum clericale officium, possemus proprietate usus et ministerii occupare licite omnia temporalia que habemus. Et sic occuparunt omnes sancti episcopi ex mundi diviciis commendandi. Quod si blasfemamus quod nude materia [p. 90] successionis Christi et apostolorum qui dominati sunt seculariter omnibus mundi diviciis habemus hec omnia, et secundo consumimus illa differenter seculariter et proprietarie, et tercio dimittimus vel remittimus exinde debitum officium clericale, manifestum est quod irreligiose, iniuste et proditorie consumimus bona Dei; et hoc totum inportaret in clerico monstruosa civilitas. Sed dubitatur supposito usu legitimo, quis modus vivendi foret perfeccior; nec dubium quin modus vivendi parcior et exproprietarius, quia est securior et vite Christi ac innocencie utrique conformior. Vix enim reperies mundi divitem quin vel nimis afficiatur et occupetur circa eius divicias vel inique ipsas distribuat vel tercio voluptuose atque superflue ipsas consumat, in tantum quod de divite evadente ista pericula scribitur Eccli. XXXI, 9: Quis est hic et laudabimus eum? fecit enim mirabilia in vita sua. Et hec fuit racio quare sapiencia Dei patris ob amorem nostri sic vixit exproprietarie et docuit suos cariores sic vivere. Ex quo invincibiliter colligitur quod status cuiuscumque cleri vel episcopi foret subducta appropriacione dotantis perfeccior, licet aliqui sancti in hoc evadant periculum, quia indubie predicta triplex occupacio temporalium foret ad onus cuiuscumque clerici qui intenderet perficere officium clericale; nam multitudo familie et mundani honoris nimis distraherent. Distribucio vero corporalis elemosine pertinet ad ministerium laycale, clerico intento ad distribuendum spirituales elemosinas pociores. Et sic ex dotacione sparguntur in clero macule, que ultra varietatem, quam Christus instituit, non pulcrificant matrem [p. 91] nostram. Et si dicatur quod alicui clerico foret melius sic dotari, revera hoc est valde remotum ad inferendum quod status dotati cleri sit statu exproprietarie viventis perfeccior. Sic enim expediret alicui clerico quod foret laycus, ymo ut dicit Christus Math. XVIII: Expedit scandalizanti ut suspendatur mola asinaria circa collum eius et demergatur in profundum maris. Hoc est secundum beatum Gregorium in De Cura Pastorali II expediret prelato prescito quod ob errorem laycalis officii sit dampnatus. Non igitur sequitur: Utilius foret Petro esse in statu tali; igitur status ille est alio statu perfeccior. Nam iuxta istam vesaniam quilibet gradus turpis status foret statu honesto perfeccior, quia status perfeccior, quem statuatus deturpat, facit per accidens quod deturpatori peius eveniat, sicut inferioritas status potest cum gracia Dei esse causa per accidens unde homo amplius mereatur: ymo necesse est ut status dyaboli ac membrorum suorum prosit per accidens ecclesie, sicut status peccandi Petri et aliorum sanctorum secundum Augustinum fuit multum expediens. Numquid propterea servabimus talem statum? Notato igitur illo clerico, quem decorat imperatoris dotacio, patet quod ipse posset eque perficere opus predicandi, orandi et contemplandi sine tanta copia terrenorum; cum omne quod per se superaddit sonat in mundi gloriam et retardat ab officio clericali, quomodo igitur non foret status signancius ex dotacione imperfeccio? Et idem est argumentum de quocumque clerico signato; ex quo colligitur quod si status tocius cleri dotati foret alteratus [p. 92] in statum exproprietarium similis statui apostolorum vel rectorum in communi vivencium pure de oblacionibus, decimis et privatis elemosinis, uniformitas faceret ad decorem ecclesie, quia pulchrius pulchriori additum, facit totum aggregatum pulchrius quam totum minus pulcrum additum minus pulchro. Unde igitur auctoritas membris ecclesie sic strangulare contra exemplum vel autoritatem Domini clerum suum, cum in veteri testamento non licuit; arare in bove et asino nec indui vestimento ex lana et lino, ut patet Numeri XII et Deutronomii XXII. Si igitur levite et sacerdotes legis veteris et Christi discipuli vixerunt conformiter, quare non sic viveret clerus sequens? quam uniformitatem Christi vicarius debet incipere, ut coraula; Christus enim fuit homo pauperrimus sic quod nemo potuit esse pauperior; ut hic supponitur, quomodo igitur non eius sequax in terris simillimus, cum paupertas illa sonat perfeccionem virtutis viancium. Sicut igitur oportuit Petrum in fide, in humilitate, in caritate evidencius sequi Christum ex dictis in fine tercii capituli, sic oportet Petri vicarium sequi ipsum. Sed que fides est regulam perfeccionis, quam Christus instituit, tam cito propter mundum deserere? Vel que humilitas est sine auctoritate Christi supra alios mortales, eciam supra elemosinantem seculariter se extollere? Nam libro IV dicit Bernhardus ad papam Eugenium, ut recitavi superius: In hiis, inquit, successisti Constantino non Petro. Et beatus Jeronimus, ut recitatum est XCII distinccione Olim, et XCV distinccione Absit, dicit quod tale sacramentum [p. 93] superioritas non est a Deo et tunc indubie est a dyabolo. Si igitur papa viveret recte exproprietarie cum ceteris paribus sicut Petrus, quomodo non omnes eius subditi conversarentur conformiter? Et istam doctrinam dat Petrus I Pet. IV precipiens quod seniores et prepositi non sint dominantes in clero sed forma gregis. Et illud verbum notaret Petri vicarius propter reverenciam dignitatis. Quantum ad caritatem non video quin repugnat perfectissime caritati viantis quod in Petri vicario sit tam dominativa et copiosa possessio, quia cum caritas non querit proprietatem huiusmodi, ut dicit apostolus I. Corint. XIII, patet quod si Petri vicarius ex fervore caritatis omnia illa propter amorem Christi relinqueret, sicut fecerunt apostoli, quod cum paribus foret in eo caritas intensior et foret Petro similior, qui Actuum III, 6 confitetur: Argentum, inquit, et aurum non est michi. Et tunc liberalis dominus donaret sibi meliorem possessionem, scilicet virtutem faciendi miracula, sic quod curatus dicere possit cum Petro: Quod autem habeo, hoc tibi do, scilicet sanitatem; qui enim habet virtutem sanandi, habet eius fructum, licet non formaliter. Cum igitur iste gradus caritatis foret vicario Petri possibilis et ab illo deficit in magnum dampnum ecclesie, videtur quod propter dissimilitudinem non sit Petri vicarius et per consequens neque heres.

It is to be noted, however, that [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} poison the [church]{g:ecclesia} not through an occasion given by them but through one wrongly taken.[^1] For if the whole [clergy]{g:clerus} said in effect that all the temporal goods we have as clerics were purely [alms]{g:elemosina} of seculars and the common goods of the poor; [if] secondly we ministered from them effectually and impartially to those in greater need; and [if] thirdly we consumed of them precisely as much as is necessary to our clerical office, we could lawfully occupy all the temporal goods we have with property of use and of ministry. And so all the holy bishops who are to be commended occupied [what they had] of the world's riches.[^2] But if we blaspheme [by saying] that we have all these things barely on the ground of [90] succession to Christ and the apostles, who [exercised lordship]{g:dominari} in a secular way over all the riches of the world, and secondly consume them with partiality, in a secular and proprietary way, and thirdly thereby abandon or [remit]{g:remittere} the clerical office that is due, it is manifest that we consume God's goods irreligiously, unjustly and treacherously; and monstrous [civil standing]{g:civilitas} in a cleric would bring all this with it.[^3] But it is doubted, lawful use being supposed, which manner of living would be more perfect; and there is no doubt [that it is] the sparer and [propertyless]{g:exproprietarii} manner of living, since it is safer and more conformed to both, the life of Christ and innocence.[^4] For you will scarcely find a rich man of the world who is not either too much attached to and occupied about his riches, or distributes them unjustly, or thirdly consumes them voluptuously and superfluously; so much so that of the rich man who escapes these dangers it is written, Ecclesiasticus 31:9: "Who is he, and we will praise him? for he hath done wonderful things in his life." And this was the reason why the wisdom of God the Father, for love of us, lived thus [without property]{g:exproprietarie} and taught his dearer [disciples] to live thus. From which it is invincibly gathered that the state of any clergy or bishop would be more perfect with the endower's [appropriation]{g:appropriacio} taken away, although some saints in this [state] escape the danger; for without doubt the aforesaid threefold occupation with temporal goods would be a burden to any cleric who intended to accomplish the clerical office; for the multitude of the [household]{g:familia} and of worldly honour would distract [him] too much.[^5] And the distribution of bodily alms belongs to the lay ministry, the cleric being intent on distributing the better spiritual alms. And so from [endowment]{g:dotacio} spots are scattered in the clergy, which, beyond the variety which Christ instituted, do not beautify our [91] mother.[^6] And if it is said that it would be better for some cleric to be so endowed, in truth this is very far from inferring that the state of an endowed clergy is more perfect than the state of [one] living without property. For in that way it would be expedient for some cleric that he were a layman; indeed, as Christ says, Matthew 18: "It is better for him that scandalizeth, that a millstone should be hanged about his neck, and that he should be drowned in the depth of the sea." That is, according to blessed Gregory in *Pastoral Care* II, it would be expedient for a [foreknown]{g:prescitus} [prelate]{g:prelatus} that he be damned for the error of a lay office.[^7] Therefore it does not [follow]{g:sequitur}: it would be more useful for Peter to be in such a state; therefore that state is more perfect than another state.[^8] For according to this madness any grade of a base state would be more perfect than an honourable state, since the more perfect state, which the one placed in it disgraces, brings it about accidentally that it turns out worse for the disgracer; just as lowliness of state can, with the grace of God, be an accidental cause whereby a man merits more; indeed it is necessary that the state of the devil and of his members should profit the church accidentally, as the state of sinning of Peter and of other saints was, according to Augustine, very expedient.[^9] Shall we then on that account keep such a state? Taking note, then, of that cleric whom the emperor's endowment adorns, it is clear that he could equally accomplish the work of preaching, praying and contemplating without so great a [plenty]{g:copia} of earthly things; since everything that it adds of itself sounds toward the world's glory and holds [him] back from the clerical office, how then would [his] state not be, more signally, an imperfection from endowment?[^10] And the argument is the same of any designated cleric; from which it is gathered that, if the state of the whole endowed clergy were altered [92] into a propertyless state like the state of the apostles or of rectors living in common purely on offerings, [tithes]{g:decime} and private alms, the uniformity would make for the beauty of the church, since the more beautiful added to the more beautiful makes the whole aggregate more beautiful than the less beautiful whole added to the less beautiful.[^11] Whence then [have] the members of the church authority so to deck his clergy in motley, against the Lord's example or authority, since in the Old Testament it was not lawful "to plough with an ox and an ass" nor to wear "a garment … of woollen and linen", as is clear from Numbers 12 and Deuteronomy 22?[^12] If then the Levites and [priests]{g:sacerdos} of the old law, and Christ's disciples, lived in conformity, why should not the clergy that follows live so? And the [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius-christi} ought to begin this uniformity, like a choir-leader;[^13] for Christ was the poorest of men, so that no one could be poorer, as is supposed here; how then [should] his follower on earth not [be] most like him, since that poverty sounds the perfection of the virtue of [those on the road]{g:viantes}? As therefore it behoved Peter to follow Christ more evidently in faith, in humility, in charity, from what was said at the end of the third chapter, so it behoves the [vicar of Peter]{g:vicarius-petri} to follow him. But what faith is it to desert so quickly, for the world's sake, the rule of perfection which Christ instituted? Or what humility is it, without Christ's authority, to exalt oneself above other mortals, even in a secular way above the [almsgiver]{g:elemosinans}?[^14] For in book IV Bernard says to Pope Eugenius, as I recited above: "In these," he says, "you have succeeded Constantine, not Peter." And blessed Jerome, as is recited in distinction 92, *Olim*, and distinction 95, *Absit*, says that such a sacrament, [93] superiority, is not from God, and then without doubt it is from the devil.[^15] If then the [pope]{g:papa} lived rightly without property, other things being equal, like Peter, how would not all his subjects live in conformity? And Peter gives this [doctrine]{g:doctrina}, 1 Peter 4, commanding that the elders and [superiors]{g:prepositi} be not "lording it over the clergy, but … a pattern of the flock".[^16] And the vicar of Peter should note that word, out of reverence for the dignity. As to [charity]{g:caritas}, I do not see but that it is repugnant to the most perfect charity of one on the road that there should be in the vicar of Peter a possession so lordly and so abundant; for since charity does not seek property of this kind, as the apostle says, 1 Corinthians 13, it is clear that if the vicar of Peter, out of the fervour of charity, left all those things for the love of Christ, as the apostles did, then, other things being equal, charity would be more intense in him and he would be more like Peter, who confesses, Acts 3:6: "Silver," he says, "and gold I have none." And then the liberal Lord would give him a better possession, namely the power of working miracles, so that the [curate]{g:curatus} could say with Peter: "But what I have, I give thee", namely health; for he who has the power of healing has its fruit, though not formally.[^17] Since, then, this degree of charity would be possible to the vicar of Peter, and he falls short of it, to the great harm of the church, it seems that because of the unlikeness he is not the vicar of Peter, and consequently not his heir either.

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 89.21: Endowments would be quite other if we recognized that they are the common property of the poor.
- 90.1: We claim wealth as Christ's heritage but use it as worldly property.
- 90.13: Danger of riches.
- 91.11: It may be best for a given man to be in a state that is not the best.
- 91.20: Even the devils profit the church accidentally.
- 92.1: All priests should live alike and only on alms.
- 92.16: The pope should set the example.
- 93.10: That the pope should have so much wealth is against charity,
- 93.18: If like Peter, he had not gold or silver, God might give him the power of healing.

## Notes

[^1]: **C/R.** *occasione … data sed male accepta*: the distinction of occasion given and occasion taken, as of scandal given and taken. A in the margin (89.21): *Divicie quomodo venenant ecclesiam*, "How riches poison the church".

[^2]: **R/C.** A in the margin (89.23): *Nota quomodo clerus potest cum Deo habere omnia bona que habet et quomodo ea sancti habuerunt*, "Note how the clergy can with God have all the goods it has, and how the saints had them". *proprietate usus et ministerii*: property of use and ministry, against civil property. *commendandi*: "to be commended".

[^3]: **C.** *nude materia successionis*: "barely on the ground of succession"; the relative *qui dominati sunt …* belongs to the claim Wyclif calls blasphemy. *differenter* answers *indifferenter*, "impartially", above. *remittimus*: "remit", let slacken.

[^4]: **R/C.** A in the margin (90.8): *Nota quod perfeccior modus est vivere clero exproprietarie, id est, nihil horum sibi appropriando vel ecclesie*, "Note that the more perfect way is for the clergy to live without property, that is, appropriating none of these things to itself or to the church". *utrique*: both the life of Christ and the state of innocence.

[^5]: **C.** *subducta appropriacione dotantis*: the endower's appropriating [of goods to the clergy] taken away. *intenderet*: of purpose, "intended".

[^6]: **S/G.** *varietatem*: the "variety" of Psalm 44:10 (88.13 above), which Christ instituted, against the spots added by endowment. *matrem nostram*: the church as our mother (GLOSSARY *mater nostra*).

[^7]: **S/T.** Matthew 18:6: Wyclif's *Expedit scandalizanti* compresses the Vulgate (*qui autem scandalizaverit … expedit ei*); DR's wording otherwise. Gregory, *Regula pastoralis* I.2 (Loserth: "I pars, cap. II. Loosely quoted"; Wyclif's *II* stands as printed; the modern form is ours) (PL 77, 16A; LEDGER P05d-1): better for such a man to fall in a lay state than in sacred office. *prelato prescito*: B reads *clerico prescito*; translated as printed. *prescitus*: one whom God foreknows will be damned (GLOSSARY).

[^8]: **C.** *Petro*: Peter as the logicians' sample name, any man.

[^9]: **T/S.** *membrorum suorum* (91.20): so the print; the manuscripts read *membrorum ecclesie*, "of the members of the church"; translated as printed. Augustine on the profit of Peter's fall: no reference in Loserth; *De civitate Dei* XIV.13: *superbis esse utile cadere in aliquod apertum manifestumque peccatum … Salubrius enim Petrus sibi displicuit quando flevit, quam sibi placuit quando praesumpsit* (LEDGER P05d-2).

[^10]: **C.** *quod per se superaddit*: what the endowment adds of itself (*per se*, against *per accidens* above). *signancius*: "more signally", more markedly.

[^11]: **C.** *rectorum … in communi vivencium*: rectors living in common on the parish's dues; *uniformitas*: one manner of life for the whole clergy. Loserth notes *pulcrus* in the manuscripts (92.5).

[^12]: **T/S.** *strangulare* (p. 92): so the print ("to strangle"); we translate the manuscripts' *stragulare* (AB), "to deck in motley" (as with a striped coverlet), which suits the ox and ass, and the wool and linen, of the next clause: the clergy decked out with lordship. Deuteronomy 22:10–11, DR; Wyclif's *Numeri XII* is not the place; the mixed garment is also forbidden at Leviticus 19:19.

[^13]: **C.** *coraula*: the leader of a chorus (*choraula*, the piper who leads the dance).

[^14]: **S/T.** *ex dictis in fine tercii capituli*: ch. 3 (Loserth). *elemosinantem*: one manuscript has *eliantem* (Loserth). *elemosinans*: the giver of the endowment (GLOSSARY "almsgiver").

[^15]: **S/C.** Bernard, *De consideratione* IV.3.6 (PL 182, 776A), quoted at 87.5 (Loserth "Cap. III. V. p. 87"; LEDGER P05d-3). Jerome: Loserth identifies *Olim* as Decretum D.95 c.5 (Jerome, on Titus, *Olim idem presbyter qui episcopus*) and finds the words not in the distinction *Absit*; Wyclif's "XCII" stands as printed (LEDGER P05d-4, editor; the modern form is ours). *tale sacramentum superioritas*: so the print, construed with *superioritas* in apposition; the phrase may be corrupt.

[^16]: **S.** 1 Peter 5:3 (Loserth; Wyclif "IV"), DR "Neither as lording it over the clergy but being made a pattern of the flock from the heart". Loserth's italics include *seniores et prepositi*, which Wyclif makes the subject; the quotation marks keep the scriptural words.

[^17]: **S/T/R.** 1 Corinthians 13:5 (*non quaerit quae sua sunt*), paraphrased as *non querit proprietatem huiusmodi*. *quin* (93.10): Loserth's emendation; the manuscripts read *quando*; B lacks *-modi* (93.13–14). Acts 3:6, DR. Loserth at 93.10: cf. Hus, Opp. 212b (LEDGER P05d-5, editor). *curatus*: the curate, a priest with the cure of souls; here the pope as Peter's vicar.
