# *De eucharistia*, chapter 8, part 9: the fifth conclusion; eternity, aeveity and time

## ¶1 · 263.14–265.19

> [p. 263] Et ex eodem patet quinta conclusio, scilicet quod periret omnis certitudo de mora temporis, quia quantumcunque breve tempus, eciam instans, potest servari quantumlibet diu, ideo diuturnitas et brevitas forent accidencia separabilia a tempore sicut a quolibet permanente, quia aliter non posset tempus per se esse vel prorogari, cum tamen tempus inter omnia accidencia minime dependeat a subiecto. Et patet confusio respondendi, cum diuturnitas temporis vel sit ipsum tempus vel accidens quod poterit per se esse; de quo non est nobis certitudo; et sic (ut sophiste arguunt) capiendo lineas infinitas girativas quarum quelibet infinitum diu durat, sicut est infinitum magna quoad molem, tunc videtur quod superficies, numerus vel respectus qui est per illa tempora (quantumcunque brevis fuerit) est per infinita tempora non communicancia, quorum quodlibet est dupliciter infinitum; et ita foret de omni corpore racione suarum parcium; et patet incertitudo atque confusio. Unde sophiste notant unam lineam girativam [p. 264] circumgirantem versus orientem in infinitum A columpnam cuius cingulum sit pedale, et ponatur quod A et tota superficies sua annichiletur servato B per se et quod una superficies rotunda appropinquet ab oriente quousque tangat B in C instanti, et tunc patet quod B fuit finitum in utroque extremo et quod fuit dare ultimam eius partem proporcionalem que fuit superficialis quod est dare maximum gradum curvedinis in circumgiracione; que omnia negat hec secta esse possibilia, ideo valde inconvenienter est sibi ipsi contraria; nam dato puncto extremali linee vel est dare pedale illius linee terminatum ad illum punctum vel linea non est tanta, sicut patet ablato illo puncto sine pluri vel rectificata illa linea secundum illud extremum vel fluente puncto uniformiter super illam incipiendo ab illo extremo vel fluente reliquo extremo in infinitum continuum et directum manente isto extremo fixo vel veniente ab oriente superficie rotunda, cuius dyameter sit pedalis. Et patet, sicut unus punctus illius superficiei tangit A datum punctum extremalem qui exempli gracia sit punctus ultimus illius diametri, sic quilibet alius punctus eiusdem circuli incipit vel incipiet tangere aliquem punctum illius linee girative; et ponatur quod Deus ex omnipotencia sua aptet sic motum dati circuli quod quilibet eius punctus eque primo tangat punctum sibi oppositum de linea girativa et secundum principia illius secte negare aliquem talem casum est condicio blasphemi, Dei omnipotenciam negantis. Et conformiter dicitur de tempore et instanti quod repugnaret Dei omnipotencie quod ipse non potest servare instans [p. 265] vel tempus per se quantumcunque sibi libuerit et per consequens Deus potest infinita tempora infinitum magna in instanti producere et quam cito sibi placuerit annichilare, sicut potest instans vel infinitum modicum tempus infinitum diu per se servare; et cum nullus nostrum sit consiliarius Domini noscens si sic facit, patet quod isti secte est ambiguum de quantitate duracionis cuiuslibet creature, vel Deus potest facere (ut inquiunt) quod et instans duret per infinitum multa tempora non communicancia quorum quodlibet sit infinitum magnum et potest facere quod omne sempiternum non duret nisi per instans vel horam ad maximum, quia Deus potest servare horam per se in perpetuum, secundum se totam; quia si oporteret aliquam partem eius umquam labendo deficere, tunc necesse foret quod in tempore finito tota hora deficeret, nec Deus posset servare tempus vel motum ultra suam naturalem periodum nec citra ipsum corrumpere.

And from the same the fifth [conclusion]{g:conclusio} is clear, namely that all certainty about the [abiding]{g:mora} of time would perish, because however short a time, even an instant, can be preserved however long; so [length]{g:diuturnitas} and shortness would be [accidents]{g:accidens} separable from time, as from any permanent thing whatever, since otherwise time could not be [of itself]{g:per-se}[^1] or be prolonged; whereas yet time, among all accidents, depends least on a [subject]{g:subiectum}. And the confusion in answering is clear, since the length of time is either time itself or an accident which will be able to be of itself; of which we have no certainty. And so (as the [sophists]{g:sophista} argue), taking infinite spiral lines,[^2] each of which lasts infinitely long as it is infinitely great as to bulk, then it seems that a surface, number or [relation]{g:respectus} that is through those times (however brief it may be) is through infinitely many [non-communicating]{g:non-communicans} times, each of which is doubly infinite; and so it would be with every body by reason of its parts; and the uncertainty and confusion are clear. Hence the sophists mark out one [spiral line]{g:linea-girativa} [264] winding round towards the east to infinity about A, a column whose girth is a foot; and let it be supposed that A and its whole surface are annihilated, B being preserved of itself,[^3] and that a round surface approaches from the east until it touches B at instant C; and then it is clear that B was finite at both ends, and that [one could posit]{g:est-dare} its last [proportional part]{g:partes-proporcionales}, which was at the surface, which is to posit a greatest degree of curvature in the winding;[^4] all of which this sect denies to be possible, so it is, very [absurdly]{g:inconveniens},[^5] contrary to itself. For, given an end-point of the line, either one may posit a foot-length of that line terminated at that point, or the line is not so great; as is clear if that point is taken away and no more, or if that line is straightened out from that end, or if a point flows uniformly over it beginning from that end, or if the other end flows on to infinity, continuous and straight, while this end stays fixed, or if a round surface whose [diameter]{g:dyameter} is a foot comes from the east. And it is clear that, as one point of that surface touches A, the given end-point,[^6] which for example let be the last point of that diameter, so every other point of the same circle begins or will begin to touch some point of that spiral line; and let it be supposed that God by his omnipotence so fits the motion of the given circle that every point of it touches equally first the point of the spiral line opposite to it; and according to the principles of that sect, to deny any such case is the condition of a blasphemer denying God's omnipotence. And correspondingly it is said of time and the instant that it would [be incompatible]{g:repugnare} with God's omnipotence that he could not preserve an instant [265] or a time of itself as long as he pleased; and consequently God can produce infinitely many infinitely great times in an instant and annihilate them as soon as he pleases, as he can preserve of itself an instant, or an infinitely small time, for infinitely long. And since none of us is the Lord's counsellor,[^7] knowing whether he does so, it is clear that for this sect the quantity of the duration of every [creature]{g:creatura} is uncertain; or[^8] God can make (as they say) even an instant last through infinitely many non-communicating times, each of which is infinitely great, and can make every sempiternal thing last only through an instant, or an hour at most, because God can preserve an hour of itself in perpetuity, as a whole; for if any part of it had at any time to fail by slipping away, then it would be necessary that in a finite time the whole hour should fail, and God could not preserve a time or a motion beyond its natural period, nor destroy it short of it.

## ¶2 · 265.20–266.4

> [p. 265] Nec valet in ista materia allegare diffiniciones Aristotelis in hac parte, quia iuxta hanc sectam oportet dicere quod Aristoteles cum secta peripatetica et Augustinus cum multis sanctis diffiniendo accidencia blasfemarunt, ut ipsi asserunt quod nullum accidens habet esse aliud preter formaliter substancie inherere; terna dimensio corporis quod est longitudo, latitudo et profunditas, vel ipsa substancie magnitudo aut corporeitas non potest per se esse. Ista autem secta dicit quod hoc est hereticum, cum ista quantitas sit per se necessario sacramentum, et sic verecundaretur hec secta ex testimonio Aristotelis vel sanctorum doctorum quidquam describere vel aliquam per se [p. 266] differenciam assignare, ymmo nescit si de communi cursu nature Deus sic ordinavit, cum noviter ponit quidditatem et miraculum sacramenti altaris quod nec scriptura nec sancti doctores cognoverant.

Nor is it any use in this matter to [allege]{g:allegare} Aristotle's [definitions]{g:diffinicio} on this point, because according to this sect one has to say that Aristotle with the Peripatetic sect, and Augustine with many of [the saints]{g:sancti}, blasphemed in defining accidents, since they assert that no accident has any [being]{g:esse} other than to inhere formally in a [substance]{g:substancia};[^9] the threefold dimension of a body, which is length, breadth and depth, or the very magnitude or corporeity of the substance, cannot be of itself. But this sect says that this is heretical, since this [quantity]{g:quantitas} is of itself necessarily the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum};[^10] and so this sect would be ashamed[^11] to [define]{g:describere} anything from the testimony of Aristotle or of the holy doctors, or to assign any [difference]{g:differencia} of itself; [266] indeed it does not know whether God has so ordained in the common course of nature, since it newly posits a quiddity and a miracle of [the sacrament of the altar]{g:sacramentum-altaris} which neither scripture nor the holy doctors had known.

## ¶3 · 266.5–266.14

> [p. 266] Sed dimissis istis deliramentis sectivagis notandum quod eternitas, evitas et tempus sic differunt: Eternitas est permanencia Dei in se, quam Boetius describit quod est interminabilis vite possessio tota simul, et illa est prima mensura possibilis; unde denominat non solum naturam divinam, sed omnes relaciones in Deo, omnes ydeas et omnes eternas veritates in Deo ad intra, cum secundum racionem et non essencialiter distinguuntur a Deo; et ideo sunt eterna, quia sunt natura que est sine principio vel fine suo.

But, these [sect-roving]{g:sectivagus} [ravings]{g:deliramenta}[^12] being set aside, it is to be noted that eternity, [aeveity]{g:evitas} and time differ thus. Eternity is the permanence of God in himself, which Boethius defines thus: that it is "the whole and simultaneous possession of interminable life";[^13] and that is the first possible measure. Hence it [names]{g:denominare} not only the divine [nature]{g:natura}, but all relations in God, all [ideas]{g:ydea} and all eternal truths in God within (*ad intra*), since they are distinguished from God by reason and not [essentially]{g:essencialiter}; and therefore they are eternal, because they are the nature which is without beginning or end of its own.[^14]

## ¶4 · 266.15–267.3

> [p. 266] Secunda est mensura angelorum que vocatur evum vel evitas; et videtur michi quod quelibet tam spiritualis quam materialis essencia habet evitatem perpetuam, que est eius permanencia ante tempus. Angelus tamen quia habet visionem in verbo et in genere proprio que est permanens supra tempus, ideo habet secundum quandam excellenciam nomen evi. Unde quia angelus est primum perpetuum emanans ab eternitate divina, ideo eius permanencia vocatur eviternitas, et quot sunt angeli tot et plura sunt eva, cum alia sit evitas mensurans esse angeli et alia mensurans accionem eius intrinsecam secundum numerum speciei accionum que insunt angelis permanenter, sicut aliud est evum mensurans permanenciam hominis et cuiuscunque materialis essencie et aliud est tempus vel quando suum fundamentum in successiva [p. 267] transmutacione; et patet quod multa sunt eva que omnia reducuntur ad unum in specie non numero propter defectum unius subiecti in numero.

The second is the measure of angels, which is called [aeviternity]{g:evum} or aeveity; and it seems to me that every essence, spiritual as well as material, has a perpetual aeveity, which is its permanence before time. Yet an angel, because it has a vision in the Word and in its proper genus which is permanent above time, therefore has by a certain excellence the name of aeviternity. Hence, because an angel is the first perpetual thing emanating from the divine eternity, its permanence is therefore called [eviternity]{g:eviternitas};[^15] and as many as there are angels, so many and more are the aeviternities, since one aeveity measures the being of an angel and another measures its intrinsic action, according to the number of the species of actions which are in angels permanently; as one aeviternity measures the permanence of a man and of any material essence, and another thing is the time or the when [that has] its foundation in successive [267] transmutation.[^16] And it is clear that there are many aeviternities, all of which are reduced to one in species, not in number, for want of one subject in number.

## ¶5 · 267.4–267.22

> [p. 267] Tercia mensura est tempus quod est mensura certissima, quia numerus instancium mensurancium secundum prius et posterius motum mundi; quod est idem quod mundi duracio successiva; ipsum autem est ad omnem punctum mundi. Instans vero est mensura partis indivisibilis motus mundi; et sic tempus consequitur motum mundi in communi (ut sonant verba philosophi), Deus autem ex immensitate eternitatis est ante omne instans temporis et post omne instans et presens cuilibet instanti preterito vel futuro; tempus autem a mundi inicio usque ad finem mensurat ipsum per se primo quoad successionem et quandamlitatem que est mensura alterius generis quamlibet eius partem. Quando enim est illud quod derelinquitur ex adiacencia temporis (ut hic supponitur), et sic post diem iudicii erit idem evum mundi sine tempore; quod evum licet sit modo, tamen consideracione eius sopita attendimus ad tempus nobis magis sensibile.

The third measure is time, which is the most certain measure, because [it is] the number of the instants measuring the motion of the world according to before and after; which is the same as the successive duration of the world; and it is at every point of the world. And the instant is the measure of an indivisible part of the motion of the world; and so time follows the motion of the world in common (as the Philosopher's words run).[^17] But God, from the immensity of eternity, is before every instant of time and after every instant, and present to every instant past or future; and time, from the beginning of the world to the end, measures it of itself, first, as to succession, and [measures] each part of it as to whenness, which is a measure of another genus.[^18] For the when is that which is left behind from the adjacency of time[^19] (as is supposed here); and so after the day of judgement there will be the same aeviternity of the world without time; which aeviternity, although it is now, yet, our consideration of it being lulled asleep, we attend to time, which is more [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} to us.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Per se* (CONTEXTUAL) is "of itself": time's being without a subject, the point at issue, not the spatial "apart".

[^2]: **C.** *Linea girativa*: a line wound round a column in turns that diminish proportionally without end, so that it is finite in length and yet turns infinitely often; the sophists' case, which Wyclif uses against the sect. "Spiral line" renders the term untagged.

[^3]: **T/C.** 264.3: B is not introduced. It is presumably the spiral line, left standing when the column A is annihilated; a letter may have been lost after *lineam girativam* (263.33–264.1). The print is translated.

[^4]: **C.** *Que fuit superficialis*: read as "which was at the surface" of the column (the turn lying on it). *Quod est dare* is taken as "which is to posit": positing a last proportional part is positing a greatest degree of curvature. Both construals are doubtful.

[^5]: **C.** *Inconvenienter* (from *inconveniens*, CONTEXTUAL): "absurdly", of an argument that contradicts itself, not of troubles in affairs.

[^6]: **C.** 264.20: *A* names the end-point here, though A was the column above; AC read *dictum* for *datum* ("the said end-point"). The print is translated.

[^7]: **S.** Rom. 11:34, "who hath been his counsellor?", alluded to, not quoted.

[^8]: **C.** *Vel* gives the alternative statement of the same uncertainty: either the duration of every creature is uncertain, or (on the sect's own principle) God can stretch an instant or shrink the sempiternal at will. 265.9: DE *quod eciam*; A has *quod* and a gap.

[^9]: **S.** The definition of accident as what inheres in a substance: Aristotle, *Categories* 2 (1a24–25), and the scholastic *accidentis esse est inesse* (LEDGER K08i-1). Wyclif names no work of Augustine. 265.25: ABCD *esse aliquod*.

[^10]: **C.** *Ista quantitas … sacramentum*: on the sect's account the quantity of the host, standing of itself, is the sacrament that bears the other accidents. Kept as Wyclif states it.

[^11]: **T.** 265.30: ABC *vendicaretur* for *verecundaretur*; E *per se et*. The print is translated.

[^12]: **T/C.** 266.5: *sectivagis*, "sect-roving" (on the model of *noctivagus*); ABD *sectingis*, E *secctanigis*. Translated as printed; the coinage is proposed for the glossary.

[^13]: **S.** Boethius, *De consolatione* V pr. 6, which Loserth cites (LEDGER K08i-2). Boethius reads *interminabilis vitae tota simul et perfecta possessio*; Wyclif's *possessio tota simul* lacks *et perfecta* and puts *possessio* first (PL 63, 858A; LEDGER K08i-3). 266.7–8: E *diffinit* for *describit*.

[^14]: **C.** *Sunt natura*: the relations, ideas and eternal truths in God are the divine nature itself, distinguished from it only by reason; *suo* attaches beginning and end to that nature.

[^15]: **G/C.** *Evum*, *evitas* and *eviternitas* are three words: "aeviternity", "aeveity" and "eviternity", kept apart as the Latin keeps them. 266.23–24: A has *eternitas* struck out in the text, *eviternitas* in the margin by another hand.

[^16]: **T.** 267.1: AB *transsubstanciacione* for *transmutacione*; C corrected it. 266.25: C *mensurans essenciam*.

[^17]: **S.** Aristotle, *Physics* IV.11 (219b1–2): time as the number of motion according to before and after (LEDGER K08i-4).

[^18]: **T/C.** 267.15–16: the print has *quandamlitatem*; ABC read *quandalitatem*, the form Loserth prints at 268.6, and it is translated, "whenness" (being in a when). The phrase is read as two objects of *mensurat*: the world (*ipsum*) as to succession, and each part of it as to whenness. ABC *prius et per consequens* (267.6) for *prius et posterius*.

[^19]: **S.** The definition of *quando* in the *Liber sex principiorum* (most likely the chapter on *quando*, c. 4: *quando est quod ex adiacentia temporis relinquitur*; the text was not read here; LEDGER K08i-5). 267.18: ABC *ex iacencia*.
