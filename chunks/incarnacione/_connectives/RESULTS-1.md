## Doubtful, left as they are

- **01-b ¶7, p. 10**: *Sed quis queso fidelium trepidaret concedere …? … Non ergo timebat*, rendered "Now who of the faithful, I ask". A rhetorical question, which could read as (b), "But who …", set against the faithful who denied the adverbs. The *ergo* that follows (*Non ergo timebat*) makes it the premiss of the inference, so (a) "Now" follows the card's look-ahead rule. Kept.
- **01-b ¶4, p. 9**: *Sed oportet in istis diversas naturas subintelligere*, rendered "But". Either (b), qualifying the conclusion just drawn ("no contradiction, but one must understand two natures"), or (a), the next step. No *ergo* follows (*Unde* does). "But" is defensible.
- **01-b ¶1, p. 8**: *Sic autem quidam reddi sibi racionem flagitant* (Augustine, to Volusian), rendered "And some demand thus". The excerpt opens after *Et sequitur*, so (c) continuative fits. It could also be (b), the objectors set against the doctrine just stated. "And" is defensible.
- **01-c ¶4, p. 12**: *Ego autem finaliter concordo*, rendered "But I, finally". Either (b), Wyclif's own position against the *doctores moderniores*, or (c), the first item of the *Concordo … Concordo 2°* list. "But" is the plainer reading, given the contrast with the preceding ¶.
