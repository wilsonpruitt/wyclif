# *De eucharistia*, chapter 5, part 2: the Lombard's heresy of the sign, and a sign more efficacious than a sign

## ¶1 · 119.27–120.28

> [p. 119] Sed super istos recentes sunt antiquiores et graviores persone que videntur inpugnare sensum scripture de locucione figurativa in verbis Domini, per que sacramentum istud conficitur. Scribit enim Magister Sentenciarum IVᵒ, distinccione Xᵃ in principio: Sunt [p. 120] item alii precedencium insaniam transcendentes, qui Dei virtutem iuxta modum naturalium rerum mecientes audacius et periculosius veritati contradicunt, asserentes in altari non esse corpus Christi vel sanguinem nec substanciam panis et vini in substanciam carnis et sanguinis converti, sed ita Christum dixisse: Hoc est corpus meum, sicut Apostolus dixit: Petra autem erat Christus. Dicunt enim corpus Christi ibi esse tantum in sacramento, id est in signo, et tantum in signo manducari a nobis. Qui errandi occasionem sumunt a verbis Veritatis Joh. VIᵒ. Et illud recitant ut heresim posteriores doctores, scribentes super Lumbardi sentencias et specialiter Thomas et Bonaventura. Cum ergo nos dogmatisamus eandem sentenciam, videtur quod incidimus in caribdim, et quia modicam evidenciam facit assercio istorum doctorum, attendendum est ad eorum evidenciam pro qua asserunt quod faciunt tam racio quam scriptura; decet enim Christum dominum nostrum ex maiori miraculo, pietate et gracia procedere in novo testamento quoad sacramentum finale in quo sit memoriale suorum mirabilium consumatum. Sed in veteri testamento fuit corpus Christi in manna et ceteris figuris ut signo; oportet ergo quod in isto sacramento sit humanitas Christi efficacius quam in signo, quia aliter laycus vel avis faceret in voce corpus Christi esse eque efficaciter in signo, ut est in hostia consecrata.

But above these recent [doctors] there are older and weightier persons who seem to attack the [meaning]{g:sensus} of scripture concerning figurative speech in the words of the Lord, by which this [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} is [consecrated]{g:conficere}.[^1] For [the Master]{g:magister} of the Sentences writes in the fourth [book], distinction 10, at the beginning: "There are [120] also others, going beyond the madness of those before them, who, measuring the power of God after the manner of natural things, contradict the truth more boldly and more dangerously, asserting that [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} or the blood is not on the altar, nor is the [substance]{g:substancia} of [bread]{g:panis} and [wine]{g:vinum} converted into the substance of flesh and blood, but that Christ said 'This is my body' in the way the Apostle said 'and the rock was Christ'. For they say that the body of Christ is there only in the sacrament, that is, in the [sign]{g:signum}, and is eaten by us only in the sign. They take the occasion of erring from the words of the Truth in John 6."[^2] And the later doctors, writing on the Lombard's Sentences, and especially Thomas and Bonaventure, [recite]{g:recitare} that as a heresy.[^3] Since, therefore, we [teach as dogma]{g:dogmatisare} the same [teaching]{g:sentencia}, it seems that we fall into Charybdis; and since the assertion of these doctors makes little [evidence]{g:evidencia}, attention must be paid to their evidence, in favour of which, they assert, both reason and scripture tell:[^4] for it befits Christ our Lord to proceed in the new testament with greater miracle, [piety]{g:pietas} and grace as to the final sacrament, in which the [memorial]{g:memoriale} of his marvels is to be consummated. Now in the old testament the body of Christ was in the manna and the other [figures]{g:figura} as in a sign; therefore [the humanity]{g:humanitas} of Christ must be in this sacrament more efficaciously than in a sign, because otherwise a layman or a bird would, by its voice, make the body of Christ be in a sign equally efficaciously, as it is in the [consecrated host]{g:hostia}.[^5]

## ¶2 · 120.29–121.2

> [p. 120] Quantum ad auctoritatem scripture, videtur quod Paulus qui accepit suam sentenciam a Domino et tres alii evangeliste narrant quoad hoc sacramentum [p. 121] verba sacramentalia conversiva, cum aliter forent inpertinencia sive falsa.

As to the [authority]{g:auctoritas} of scripture, it seems that Paul, who received his teaching from the Lord, and the three other evangelists [relate]{g:narrare}, as to this sacrament, [121] [sacramental words that convert]{g:verba-sacramentalia-conversiva}, since otherwise they would be impertinent or false.[^6]

## ¶3 · 121.3–121.8

> [p. 121] Sed patet ex dictis proximo capitulo quod ista evidencia non obviat nostre sentencie; omnis enim katholicus concordat cum heretico in quibusdam. Primo itaque dicendum est de veritate et diversitate nostre sentencie ab ista sentencia reprobata. Et secundo respondendum est obiectibus.

But it is clear from what was said in the last chapter that this evidence does not [meet]{g:obviare} our teaching; for every catholic agrees with a heretic in some things. First, then, we must speak of the truth of our teaching and of its difference from this condemned teaching; and secondly the objections must be answered.[^7]

## ¶4 · 121.9–121.24

> [p. 121] Quantum ad primum, videtur quod Christus sit in hostia ut in signo, quia aliter non foret hostia sacramentum, non tamen est sic in signis quin sit ibi realiter et vere secundum totam humanitatem; et sic conceditur quod Christus non est ibi solummodo ut in signo, est tamen ibi efficacius quam in signo; nec repugnat sed consonat quod idem sit veritas et figura vel signum, cum secundum Augustinum Enchiridion capitulo LII mors Christi, resurreccio et ascensio erant figure mortis nostre, resurreccionis et ascensionis spiritualis, et tamen erant veritas rei geste. Quamdiu itaque sumus viantes, oportet nos uti signis et vivere in velamento fidei et figuris, et sic cum in signis sint gradus multiplices, patet quod nos tempore legis gracie habemus signa efficaciora quam habebant patres veteris testamenti.

As to the first, it seems that Christ is in the host as in a sign, because otherwise the host would not be a sacrament; yet he is not so in signs that he is not there [really]{g:realiter} and [truly]{g:vere} according to his whole humanity; and so it is granted that Christ is not there only as in a sign, yet he is there more efficaciously than in a sign. Nor is it [incompatible]{g:repugnare}, but consonant, that the same thing be truth and figure or sign, since according to Augustine, *Enchiridion* chapter 52, the death, resurrection and ascension of Christ were figures of our death, resurrection and spiritual ascension, and yet were the truth of a thing done.[^8] So long, then, as we are [those on the road]{g:viantes}, we must use signs and live in the veil of faith and in figures; and so, since there are manifold degrees in signs, it is clear that we, in the time of [the law of grace]{g:lex-gracie}, have signs more efficacious than the fathers of the old testament had.[^9]

## ¶5 · 121.25–122.8

> [p. 121] Et patet quod nec error predictus nec racio facta contra illum errorem contrariatur nostre sentencie, nam signum nostrum habet concomitanciam corporis Christi signati, cum sit signum presens ultra signa veteris testamenti et verba signorum habent coefficienciam ut corpus Christi sit taliter ibi presens, et utrumque istorum deficit in lege veteri. Ideo signanter Christus docet istam preeminenciam Joh. VIº, 49, 52, [p. 122] dum dicit: Patres vestri manducaverunt manna et mortui sunt; qui manducat hunc panem non morietur in eternum. Unde Augustinus libro Sermonum, Sermone VIIIº, notat quomodo patres legis veteris eandem escam spiritualem manducaverunt, sicut dicit Apostolus I. Cor. Xº, 3, quia patres illi fideles per manna Christum intellexerunt quem nobiscum spiritualiter comederunt.

And it is clear that neither the aforesaid error nor the argument made against that error is contrary to our teaching; for our sign has the [concomitance]{g:concomitancia} of the body of Christ signified, since it is a present sign beyond the signs of the old testament, and the words of the signs have a [co-efficiency]{g:coefficiencia}, that the body of Christ be present there in such a way; and each of these is lacking in the old law.[^10] Therefore Christ pointedly teaches this [preeminence]{g:preeminencia} in John 6:49, 52, [122] when he says: "Your fathers did eat manna and are dead. He that eateth this bread shall not die for ever."[^11] Hence Augustine, in the book of Sermons, sermon 8, notes how the fathers of the old law ate the same spiritual food, as the Apostle says, 1 Cor. 10:3, because those faithful fathers understood through the manna Christ, whom they ate [spiritually]{g:spiritualiter} with us.[^12]

## ¶6 · 122.9–123.28

> [p. 122] Ulterius quantum ad verba sacramentalia, videtur probabile domino Armachano libro nono capitulo 5ᵗᵒ De Questionibus Armenorum, quod Christus confecit hoc sacramentum pura voluntate, antequam protulit illa verba, quia ut narrant Mathaeus et Marcus Jesus accepit panem, benedixit et fregit deditque discipulis suis et ait illis: Accipite et manducate, hoc est corpus meum; ubi notata serie verborum, cum uniformiter demonstravit panem quem consecratum precepit eis comedere erantque ante ista verba benediccio, fraccio et manducandi precepcio, videtur quod erat ante ista verba hoc est corpus meum panis consecracio, videtur secundo sibi probabile iuxta fidem scripture quod verba consecratoria intelligunt de pane et vino, cum omnes predicti quatuor apostoli notant quod Jesus accepit panem materialem in manus suas, quem fregit, manducari precepit et esse corpus suum asseruit; et idem iudicium est de vino. Quid enim planius quam textus Marci XIVº, 23, 24, ubi dicitur: Accepto calice gracias agens dedit eis et biberunt ex eo omnes et ait illis: Hic est sanguis meus, ubi patet quod vocat vinum contentum in [p. 123] calice sanguinem suum; et idem patet Math. XXVIº, 26. Ex quibus videtur consonancius quam dicit magister quod talis locucio sit tropica et quodammodo sed non essencialiter conversiva vini in sanguinem, cum sensus tropicus sit verus sed demonstrando panem et vinum foret falsum quoad predicacionem ydempticam quod hoc est corpus Christi vel sanguis. Si autem demonstratur pronomine panis aut vinum in principio et in fine fit conversio, tunc proposicio in sua successione foret falsa vel conversioni inpertinens, nisi sompnietur quod Deus instituit proposicionem talem ad habendum efficaciam taliter convertendi; quod non fundabitur. Si demonstretur pronomine corpus Christi, tunc nichil novi constituitur, ut sit Christi carnis effectivum. Si autem pronomen connotat quod hoc sub istis accidentibus non subiectatis est corpus Christi, petitur infundabiliter contra scripture ordinem sompniatum; et sensum istius scripture vellem illos attendere qui fantasiant ex isto textu quod est dare conversionem sine essencia differente; videtur ergo utrobique in verbis sacramentalibus esse locucionem tropicam efficaciorem quam in ista: Petra autem erat Christus, cum illa verba faciunt occasionaliter quod humanitas Christi sit realiter sed sacramentaliter ad omnem punctum hostie consecrate, et sic est illa hostia signum efficacius quam panis benedictus a layco et secundum formam aliam a presbitero benedictus.

Further, as to the sacramental words, it seems probable to [the lord of Armagh]{g:dominus-ardmacanus}, in book nine, chapter 5, *Of the Questions of the Armenians*, that Christ consecrated this sacrament by pure will before he uttered those words;[^13] because, as Matthew and Mark relate, "Jesus took bread and blessed and broke and gave to his disciples" and said to them: "Take ye and eat: this is my body";[^14] where, once the order of the words is noted, since he uniformly pointed to the bread which, consecrated, he commanded them to eat, and the blessing, the breaking and the command to eat came before these words, it seems that the [consecration]{g:consecracio} of the bread was before these words "this is my body".[^15] It seems to him, secondly, probable according to the faith of scripture that the consecrating words understand [their subject] of the bread and wine, since all the four aforesaid apostles note that Jesus took material bread into his hands, which he broke, commanded to be eaten, and asserted to be his body; and the same judgement holds of the wine.[^16] For what is plainer than the text of Mark 14:23, 24, where it is said: "And having taken [the chalice]{g:calix}, giving thanks, he gave it to them. And they all drank of it. And he said to them: This is my blood"; where it is clear that he calls the wine contained in the [123] chalice his blood; and the same is clear in Matt. 26:26.[^17] From which it seems more consonant than what the master says that such speech is [tropical]{g:locucio-tropica}, and in a certain way, but not [essentially]{g:essencialiter}, converting the wine into blood; since the tropical sense is true, but, when the bread and wine are pointed to, it would be false as to [identical predication]{g:predicacio-ydemptica} that "this is the body of Christ" or "the blood".[^18] And if by the pronoun the bread or the wine is pointed to at the beginning, and at the end the conversion takes place, then the [proposition]{g:proposicio} in its succession would be false, or impertinent to the conversion, unless it is dreamed that God instituted such a proposition to have the efficacy of so converting; which will not be grounded. If the body of Christ is pointed to by the pronoun, then nothing new is constituted, so as to be productive of the flesh of Christ. And if the pronoun connotes that "this, under these unsubjected [accidents]{g:accidens}, is the body of Christ", a dream against the order of scripture [is begged]{g:petitur} [without foundation]{g:infundabiliter};[^19] and I would wish those who imagine from this text that [one may posit]{g:est-dare} a conversion without a differing essence to attend to the meaning of this scripture.[^20] It seems, therefore, that in both places in the sacramental words there is tropical speech more efficacious than in this: "and the rock was Christ"; since those words make [by occasion]{g:occasionaliter} that the humanity of Christ is really but [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} at every point of the consecrated host; and so that host is a sign more efficacious than bread blessed by a layman, and [than bread] blessed by a [presbyter]{g:presbiter} according to another form.[^21]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 120.1: Peter Lombard, St. Thomas and Bonaventura are against us;
- 121.3: but their evidence does not overthrow our opinion.
- 121.9: A thing may be real and yet also a figure.
- 121.25: This figure outdoes all those of the O. T. since Christ is present with it.
- 122.9: Christ had consecrated before saying: 'This is my body'.
- 123.8: Force of the pronoun (hoc).
- 123.21: The sacramental words are the occasion of Christ's sacramental presence, and they are of more efficacy than ordinary blessing.

## Notes

[^1]: **C/T.** *Istos recentes*: the recent doctors answered in part 1 (Scotus and the *doctores signorum*); the older persons are the Lombard and those who recite him. *Sensus scripture* "the meaning of scripture" (GLOSSARY *sensus*, of words). D reads *supra* for *super*, and *scienciores* (in the margin *ssuores*) (119.27).

[^2]: **S/T.** Peter Lombard, *Sent.* IV d. 10 c. 1 (Loserth: "dist. X, on the heresy of others who say the body of Christ is not on the altar except in a sign"); the quotation is to be checked (LEDGER K05b-1). "And the rock was Christ": 1 Cor. 10:4. All the manuscripts read *mencientes* "lying" (120.2–3) for the print's *mecientes* "measuring", which is translated. ABC read *ibi corpus esse corpus Christi*, D *ibi esse corpus* (120.8–9); C omits *et … signo* (120.10); E has *verbis Domini* for *verbis Veritatis* (120.11).

[^3]: **S.** Thomas and Bonaventure, each on *Sent.* IV d. 10, naming this opinion a heresy: to be checked (LEDGER K05b-2).

[^4]: **C/T.** *Sentencia* (CONTEXTUAL): "teaching", the position Wyclif holds, which the Lombard's text seems to condemn; *incidimus in caribdim* is the proverb of Scylla and Charybdis. *Modicam evidenciam facit assercio*: the doctors' bare assertion weighs little, so their grounds are weighed; *pro qua … faciunt*, "tell in favour of" it. ABC read *docet* for *decet* (120.19); E *Deum*.

[^5]: **T/C.** The argument from reason: a mere sign could be made by anyone's voice. AB read *vel a veris* (120.26), and Loserth's apparatus says "rectius legendum: vel anus", "or an old woman"; the print's *avis*, a bird that speaks, construes and is translated. ABC read *fit* for *sit* (120.22); AB *fuit Christus* (120.23).

[^6]: **S/C.** Paul's receiving from the Lord: 1 Cor. 11:23 ("For I have received of the Lord"). Paul is counted with "the three other evangelists", the "four apostles" of ¶6. *Conversiva*: words that effect a conversion; *inpertinencia*, not bearing on the conversion. CE repeat *verba* (121.1); AB *foret*; D *accipit* for *accepit* (120.30).

[^7]: **C/T.** "The last chapter": chapter 4. *Non obviat* (GLOSSARY *obviare* "meet"): the objection does not touch Wyclif's teaching. The two heads announced here are taken up in ¶4 and in ¶5–¶6. AB omit *dicendum est* (121.6).

[^8]: **S/T.** Augustine, *Enchiridion* 52 (Loserth: Opp. VI p. 215); to be checked (LEDGER K05b-3). *Spiritualis* (genitive singular) stands after the last of the three nouns; C reads *spiritualiter*, "spiritually" (121.19). ABCD read *figura* for *figure* (121.18). D omits *ut* and *non … signo* (121.14–15).

[^9]: **G/C.** *Viantes* "those on the road": the wayfarers of this life, against the homeland, where signs cease. *Gradus*: degrees of efficacy among signs, untagged (the GLOSSARY row is of belief). D reads *figure* for *figuris* (121.22).

[^10]: **C/T.** *Concomitanciam corporis Christi signati*: the thing signified accompanies the sign; *coefficienciam*, the words work together to make the body present (cf. *occasionaliter*, ¶6). "Each of these", the presence and the co-working words. ABCE read *predictis* for *predictus* (121.25); CD *signo* (121.28); D *coefficaciam* (121.29–30).

[^11]: **S.** John 6:59; Wyclif cites 6:49, 52 (Loserth's verses). His *non morietur in eternum* stands where the Vulgate has *vivet in aeternum*, "shall live for ever"; his Latin is translated.

[^12]: **S/T.** Loserth: "more correctly the sermon *De utilitate agendae paenitentiae*" (Opp. V), that is, Augustine, *Sermo* 352; to be checked (LEDGER K05b-4). 1 Cor. 10:3: "And did all eat the same spiritual food". C reads *commanducaverunt*, "ate together" (122.8).

[^13]: **S/T.** Richard FitzRalph (*dominus Armachanus*), *Summa de quaestionibus Armenorum* IX.5 (Loserth cites the Paris print of 1511); to be checked (LEDGER K05b-5). D reads *Ardmachano*, E *Ardmacano* (122.10); ABD *Arimenorum* (122.11).

[^14]: **S.** Matt. 26:26 (Loserth's reference); Mark 14:22 has *Sumite*. Wyclif's *manducate* stands for the Vulgate's *comedite*, both "eat"; the Douay wording is kept.

[^15]: **C/T.** *Demonstravit*: pointed to, the logicians' "demonstrating" of the pronoun *hoc*; FitzRalph's ground is the order of the narrative, which puts the blessing, breaking and command before the words. C has *benediccio … verba* in the margin in another hand (122.18–20); C omits *et* (122.19); E omits *quem* (122.17); B reads *Domini* at *ante* (122.18).

[^16]: **C/T.** *Verba … intelligunt* is active: the words "understand", that is, are meant of, the bread and wine; the four apostles are Matthew, Mark, Luke and Paul. D reads *tantum* for *cum* (122.23).

[^17]: **S/T.** Mark 14:23–24, which goes on "of the new testament"; Wyclif stops at *sanguis meus*. For the chalice in Matthew, 26:27–28; Wyclif's reference is 26:26. C has *textus* in the margin in another hand (122.27); E *eis* (122.29).

[^18]: **C/T.** *Magister*, lower case in the print: taken as the Master of the Sentences of ¶1, who teaches the conversion of substance; it could be FitzRalph, whose argument precedes. A has *Marcus* in the text and *magister* in the margin in another hand; D reads *quantum dicit* (123.2). *Quoad predicacionem ydempticam*: read identically, "this is the body" said of the bread is false; read tropically, true. D reads *esset falsum* (123.6).

[^19]: **C/T.** The three cases of what *hoc* demonstrates: the bread or wine (the proposition false or idle), the body (nothing new made, so the words do not produce Christ's flesh), the body under unsubjected accidents (a dream begged). D reads *corporis* and B *convocat* at 123.15; C has *petitur* in the margin in another hand (123.17).

[^20]: **C/T.** *Sine essencia differente*: a conversion with no essence that differs, as Wyclif reads the opponents' conversion, in which nothing of the bread is the term; the construal is doubtful. D reads *esse differente*, ABCE *deferente* (123.20). C has *illius* in the margin in another hand, not in the text (123.18); B *texto* (123.19).

[^21]: **C/T.** *Utrobique*: in both places, the words over the bread and over the chalice. ABD read *non occasionaliter*, and C wrote *non* and struck it out (123.23–24); ABD omit *autem* (123.23). B reads *humanitus* (123.24); E *in illa hostia* (123.26). The bread blessed by a presbyter "according to another form" is blessed bread given outside the consecration.
