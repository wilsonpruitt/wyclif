# sed/autem extract, wave 8: chunks 04-b 04-c 04-d

### 04-b ¶1 · 87.29–88.2  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 87] Tres, inquam, inter multas erant figure huius sacramenti in veteri testamento; prima erat agnus pascalis qui nedum figuravit agnum Dei qui tollit peccata mundi Joh. Iº, 29 **sed** ipsummet sacramentum; et [p. 88] hec racio quare debuit cessare esus agni paschalis post institucionem huius sacramenti signati pocioris.

ENGLISH: Three, I say, among many, were the [figures]{g:figura} of this [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} in the old testament. The first was the paschal lamb, which [not only]{g:nedum} [figured]{g:figurare} "the lamb of God who taketh away the sins" of the world (John 1:29)[^1] but the sacrament itself; and [88] this is the reason why the [eating]{g:esus} of the paschal lamb had to cease after the institution of this sacrament, the greater [thing signified]{g:signatum}.[^2]

### 04-b ¶2 · 88.3–88.8  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 88] Secunda figura huius sacramenti fuit fluxus aque de lapide (de quo Exodi XIIº); hoc enim significat secundum Apostolum bibicionem spiritualem sanguinis petre Christi: Omnes, inquit, spiritualem potum eundem biberunt, bibebant **autem** de spirituali consequente eos petra; petra **autem** erat Christus.

ENGLISH: The second figure of this sacrament was the flowing of water from the stone (of which Exodus 12[^3]); for this signifies, according to the Apostle, the spiritual drinking of the blood of the rock, Christ: "All," he says, "drank the same spiritual drink; and they drank of the spiritual rock that followed them: and the rock was Christ" (1 Cor. 10:4).

### 04-b ¶3 · 88.9–88.26  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 88] Tercia figura erat manna (de quo Exod. XVIº); unde Christus Joh. VIº, 59 complectens excellenciam huius sacramenti et rei sue super hoc manna: Non, inquit, sicut manducaverunt patres vestri manna et mortui sunt. Qui manducat hunc panem vivet in eternum. Nam secundum exposicionem beati Augustini Omelia XXVI licet commune sit nobis et illis mori, tamen illi manducantes manna corporaliter et remurmurantes contra Dominum mortui sunt corporaliter et in mente dampnabiliter, **sed** qui manducat spiritualiter panem Christum vivet spiritualiter in eternum, cum iuxta dicta ille rite manducat corpus Christi spiritualiter qui assimilatur sibi ut membrum glutino caritatis perpetue. Quamvis **autem** multi comedentes manna fideliter erant incorporati ecclesie, tamen illo sacramento non meruerunt presencialiter **sed** valde distanter apercionem ianue. Et patet in parte responsio ad dubium motum in capite huius capituli.

ENGLISH: The third figure was the manna (of which Exod. 16); hence Christ, in John 6:59, embracing the excellence of this sacrament and of [its reality]{g:res-sacramenti} over this manna, says: "Not as your fathers did eat manna and are dead. He that eateth this [bread]{g:panis} shall live for ever."[^4] For according to the exposition of blessed Augustine, Homily 26, although it is common to us and to them to die, yet they, eating the manna [bodily]{g:corporaliter} and murmuring against the Lord, died bodily, and in mind damnably; but he who eats [spiritually]{g:spiritualiter} the bread, Christ, will live spiritually for ever, since, according to what has been said, he rightly eats the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} spiritually who is assimilated to him as a [member]{g:membrum} by the glue of perpetual [charity]{g:caritas}. And although many who ate the manna faithfully were incorporated into the [church]{g:ecclesia}, yet by that sacrament they did not merit the opening of the door presently, but very distantly.[^5] And in part the reply to the doubt raised at the head of this chapter is clear.[^6]

### 04-b ¶4 · 88.27–89.3  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 88] **Sed** pro declaracione ulteriori sunt tria plus declaranda: primo de veritate et virtute verborum huius sacramenti, secundo quid et qualiter de hoc sacramento [p. 89] in compendio est credendum, et tercio quomodo ad dignitatem recipientis huius sacramenti debet homo disponi.

ENGLISH: But for further clarification three things more are to be [made clear]{g:declarare}: first, concerning the truth and [power]{g:virtus} of the words of this sacrament; second, what and how one is to believe about this sacrament [89] in summary; and third, how a man ought to be disposed to the worthiness of a recipient of this sacrament.

### 04-b ¶5 · 89.4–89.20  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 89] Quoad primum dicitur quod verba ipsa successiva et interpolata non habent ex se efficaciam convertendi, **sed** sermo Dei assistens in cuius persona dicuntur facit opera; et creditur quod placet verbo Dei operari per verba vocalia, ut vel sic discamus quod ipsum facit opera et inproprie atque equivoce eius instrumenta. Summus enim artifex operatur per valde abiecta et debilia instrumenta. Si queratur quando talis proposicio sacramentalis incipit esse vera, dicitur quod non quando desinit esse per remocionem de presenti, cum tunc non est, et quod non est, non est verum, **sed** pro ultimo instanti esse sui, cum sit dare composicionem continui ex non quantis, et cum omnia que fuerunt vel erunt sunt, interpollacio non impedit proposicionem esse vel secundum unam partem vel aliam, **sed** pro tempore interpolacionis desinit taliter esse; que omnia hic suppono ex alibi declaratis.

ENGLISH: As to the first, [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that the words themselves, successive and [interrupted]{g:interpolatus}, have not of themselves the efficacy of [converting]{g:conversio}, but the [Speech]{g:sermo} of God, assisting, in whose person they are said, does the works; and it is believed that it pleases the [Word]{g:verbum} of God to work through spoken words, so that at least in this way we may learn that he does the works, and his instruments improperly and [equivocally]{g:equivoce}.[^7] For the highest craftsman works through very lowly and weak instruments. If it is asked when such a sacramental [proposition]{g:proposicio} begins to be true, the answer is: not when it ceases to be by removal from the present, since then it is not, and what is not is not true; but for the last instant of its [being]{g:esse}, since [one may posit]{g:est-dare} the composition of a continuum out of things not [quantified]{g:quantus}; and since all things that were or will be are, the interruption does not prevent the proposition from being, either as to one part or another, but for the time of the interruption it ceases to be in that way: all of which I here suppose from what has been made clear elsewhere.[^8]

### 04-b ¶6 · 89.21–90.3  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 89] Et videtur michi modo quod panis demonstratur pronomine, et sic est proposicio vera ad suum sensum, sicut videtur Augustinum concedere, hoc est Christus, demonstrata quacumque creatura, ut patet super Johanne Omelia XIII. **Sed** in demonstrando hoc sacramentum est quedam singularis efficacia et significacio a Christo imposita, ideo theologus debet notare quomodo ista est singulariter concedenda. In casu **autem** quo intelligatur proposicio non figurative **sed** effective, tunc per pronomen demonstratur sub habitudine accidencium illud in celo quod est realiter [p. 90] corpus Christi. Si **autem** intelligatur proposicio figurative, sicut videtur historiam evangelii intelligere, tunc intelligi et demonstrari debet pronomine panis ille.

ENGLISH: And it seems to me now that the bread is demonstrated by the pronoun, and so the proposition is true in its [meaning]{g:sensus}, as Augustine seems to grant "this is Christ", any [creature]{g:creatura} whatever being demonstrated, as is clear on John, Homily 13.[^9] But in demonstrating this sacrament there is a certain singular efficacy and signification imposed by Christ; therefore the theologian ought to note how this proposition is to be granted [in a singular way]{g:singulariter}.[^10] And in the case where the proposition is understood not [figuratively]{g:figurative} but [by way of effect]{g:effective}, then by the pronoun there is demonstrated, under the [respect]{g:habitudo} of the [accidents]{g:accidens}, that in heaven which is [really]{g:realiter} [90] the body of Christ.[^11] But if the proposition is understood figuratively, as the gospel history seems to understand it, then that bread ought to be understood and demonstrated by the pronoun.

### 04-b ¶7 · 90.4–90.12  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 90] **Sed** quantum ad formam verborum declarat dominus Armachanus IXº De Questionibus Armenorum per multa capitula quomodo nulla forma verborum quoad linguas et nomina est generaliter requisita, cum autores varie scripserant quoad linguas, ut Mattheus in Hebraico, Marcus in Ytalico, Lucas in Siro et Paulus in Greco. Et cum usque hodie secundum istas linguas et plures iuxta doctrinam fidei scripture in diversis contratis conficiunt, patet conclusio.

ENGLISH: But as to the form of the words, [the lord of Armagh]{g:dominus-ardmacanus} makes clear in [book] 9 of *On the Questions of the Armenians*, through many chapters, how no form of words, as to languages and names, is generally required, since the authors wrote variously as to languages: as Matthew in Hebrew, Mark in Italic, Luke in Syriac and Paul in Greek.[^12] And since to this day they [consecrate]{g:conficere} according to these languages and more, following the [doctrine]{g:doctrina} of the faith of scripture, in diverse [districts]{g:contrata}, the [conclusion]{g:conclusio} is clear.

### 04-c ¶1 · 90.23–93.20  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 90] Ulterius quoad formam verborum in lingua latina in qua Romana ecclesia habet fidem scripture, videtur quod non sit aliqua forma verborum ad confeccionem eukaristie generaliter requisita; nam usus ecclesie nostre variat ab omnibus illis quatuor, sicut et omnes illi variant ab invicem plus vel minus. Nam Mattheus XXVIº, 28 habet ista verba consecracionis vini: Hic est enim sanguis meus novi testamenti qui pro multis effundetur in remissionem peccatorum; Lucas vero XXIIº, 20 ponit sic: Hic est calix novum testamentum in meo sanguine qui pro vobis fundetur. Marcus vero XIVº, 24 [p. 91] varians a Mattheo dimittit enim et in remissionem peccatorum. Paulus vero tradens nobis sentenciam quam accepit a Domino I. Cor. XI° sic loquitur: Hic calix novum testamentum est in meo sanguine; hoc facite quocienscunque biberitis in meam commemoracionem. **Sed** ab omnibus istis variat usus nostre ecclesie, ut patet tercio decretalium Cum Marthe, titulo De Celebracione Missarum. Cum ergo secundum has quinque formas contingit vere conficere, patet conclusio. Per hanc itaque variacionem quintimembrem docemur preponderanter ad sensum attendere et quod talis variacio verborum non est de substancia sacramenti; nam contingit (sicut de facto contigit) per verba istarum quinque formarum conficere, quia aliter ante istam ordinacionem quintam ex defectu scripture deficerent ecclesie sollempnes, ymmo forent ydolatre decipientes se ipsos et populum ex falsitate scripture, et sic ex narracione evangelistarum tocius et omnium titubaret auctoritas, et per consequens superflue laboraret Augustinus per totum librum De Concordia Evangelistarum. Nec valet dicere quod quintus ordo est necessarius post publicacionem usus ecclesie Romane et non ante, quia sic liceret pape contra evangelium dispensare, eius errores corrigere et per consequens novum evangelium de toto condere; quod suppono hic a iuribus fidelium esse horribile, cum decreta Romanorum pontificum in fide sepius titubaverunt, ut patet distinccione XVI, capitulo VI. Sinodus, prima distinccione, capitulo I, De Penitencia [p. 92] quasi per totum et specialiter in ista materia. Quando enim quis peccando mortaliter deficit in moribus deficit et in fide, ideo inprovida et nimis levis videtur diffinicio eorum qui dicunt quod dimittendo verbum vel quamcunque aliam formam qua utitur ecclesia Romana sacerdos non conficit, quia diverse lingue habent diversum modum sonandi eciam latinum, ut patet in sonacione huius littere ‘C’; ymmo oportet diversimode organisantes secundum disposicionem figure celestis diversimode sonare; ideo idem foret dicere quod oportet omnem sacerdotem vel populum istam formam sonorum in confeccione huius sacramenti attendere et dicere quod oportet omnem salvandum esse istius complexionis et plage, quod foret infidelis accepcio personarum; ideo (ut dicit doctor) satis est quod fideles concordent in sentencia ante linguam, ut quod panis figurative est corpus Christi et vinum eius sanguis; et sic salvantur dicti quatuor evangeliste a mendacio, quando asserunt Dominum sic dixisse, hoc est, dixisse istam sentenciam sive sensum. Concordare tamen debet sacerdos cum patria et usu ecclesie in istis et ritibus similibus stante substancia sentencie ut in neutris. Unde usus ecclesie quem habemus licet sit colatus ex scriptura sacra, non est melior quam quilibet quatuor usuum evangelistarum; et Deus scit utrum sapuit peccatum illa varietas introducta. Teneamus ergo sentenciam requisitam; illud **autem** reputamus requisitam sentenciam in qua omnis isti quatuor evangeliste concordant et illam in qua variant esse necessariam ad [p. 93] mores, **sed** preter essenciam sacramenti; et utinam nimis observantes Judayce ritus et consuetudines hominum quos ultra veritatem Dei colunt attenderent ad istam sentenciam, nam phariseice colant culicem et degluciunt camelum, exigentes quod populus sequatur ritus et precepta et mores eorum; **sed** dicunt stultum esse attendere ad consilia Christi, ad vitam vel conversacionem Christi ac Petri, cum oportet propter distanciam et variacionem temporis nunc observare contrarium, et sic observantes paleam dimittimus granum purum; ideo oportet quod nos vertiginosi non excuciamus religionem quam Christus instituit, quia Eccli. XIX°, i dicitur: Qui spernit modica paulatim decidet; debemus ergo postponendo ritus novellos observare mores Christi et opera, quia secundum Gregorium I° Pastoralium III° idcirco Jesus in carne venerat ut non solum nos per passionem in carne redimeret, verum eciam per conversacionem doceret; ideo ve nobis, si latemus in tenebris vel retrocedendo vel declinando ipsum non sequimur.

ENGLISH: Further, as to the form of the words in the Latin language, in which the Roman [church]{g:ecclesia} has the faith of scripture, it seems that there is no form of words generally required for the [consecration]{g:confeccio} of the eucharist; for the use of our church varies from all those four, just as all those also vary from one another, more or less. For Matthew 26:28 has these words of the consecration of the [wine]{g:vinum}: "For this is my blood of the new testament, which shall be shed for many unto remission of sins"; and Luke 22:20 puts it thus: "This is the [chalice]{g:calix}, the new testament in my blood, which shall be shed for you."[^1] And Mark 14:24, [91] differing from Matthew, leaves out "for" and "unto remission of sins".[^2] And Paul, handing down to us the [sense]{g:sentencia-verborum} which he received from the Lord, speaks thus in 1 Cor. 11: "This chalice is the new testament in my blood. This do ye, as often as you shall drink, for the commemoration of me."[^3] But the use of our church varies from all these, as is clear in the third [book] of the [decretals]{g:decretalis}, *Cum Marthe*, under the title *De celebratione missarum*.[^4] Since, therefore, it is possible to [consecrate]{g:conficere} [truly]{g:vere} according to these five forms, the [conclusion]{g:conclusio} is clear. By this [fivefold]{g:quintimembris} variation, then, we are taught to attend preponderantly to the [meaning]{g:sensus}, and that such variation of words is not of the [substance]{g:substancia} of the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}; for it is possible (as in fact it has happened) to consecrate by the words of these five forms, because otherwise, before this fifth [ordinance]{g:ordinacio}, the solemn churches would have failed through a defect of scripture; indeed, they would be idolaters, deceiving themselves and the people through the falsity of scripture; and so, from the narration of the evangelists, the [authority]{g:auctoritas} of the whole and of all would totter,[^5] and consequently Augustine would have laboured superfluously through the whole book *On the Agreement of the Evangelists*. Nor is it valid to say that the fifth order is necessary after the publication of the use of the Roman church and not before, because then it would be lawful for the pope to dispense against the gospel, to correct its errors, and consequently to found a new gospel altogether; which I here suppose, from the laws of the faithful, to be horrible, since the [decrees]{g:decretum} of the [Roman pontiffs]{g:romanus-pontifex} have quite often tottered in faith, as is clear in distinction 16, chapter 6, *Sinodus*, the first distinction, chapter 1, *De penitencia*, [92] almost throughout, and especially in this matter.[^6] For when anyone, by sinning mortally, fails in morals, he fails also in faith; therefore the [definition]{g:diffinicio} of those seems improvident and [exceedingly]{g:nimis} light who say that, by leaving out a word or any other form which the Roman church uses, a [priest]{g:sacerdos} does not consecrate; because diverse tongues have a diverse manner of sounding even Latin, as is clear in the sounding of this letter 'C'; indeed, those who [form]{g:organisare} [sounds] with their organs diversely, according to the disposition of the figure of the heavens, must sound diversely.[^7] Therefore it would be the same to say that every priest or people must attend to this form of sounds in the consecration of this sacrament, and to say that everyone who is to be saved must be of this [complexion]{g:complexio} and [region]{g:plaga}; which would be a faithless [respect of persons]{g:accepcio-personarum}. Therefore (as the doctor says) it is enough that the faithful agree in the sense before the language, as that the [bread]{g:panis} is [figuratively]{g:figurative} the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} and the wine his blood;[^8] and thus the said four evangelists are saved from falsehood when they assert that the Lord spoke thus, that is, that he spoke this sense, or meaning. Yet the priest ought to agree with his [homeland]{g:patria} and the use of the church in these and similar [rites]{g:ritus}, the substance of the sense standing, as in [neutral things]{g:neutra}. Hence the use of the church which we have, although it is strained out of holy scripture, is not better than any one of the four uses of the evangelists; and God knows whether that variety, when introduced, savoured of sin.[^9] Let us therefore hold the required sense; and we reckon that to be the required sense in which all these four evangelists agree, and that in which they vary to be necessary for [93] morals, but outside the [essence]{g:essencia} of the sacrament. And would that those who exceedingly observe, Jewishly, the rites and [customs]{g:consuetudo} of men, which they cultivate beyond the truth of God, attended to this sense; for pharisaically they strain the gnat and swallow the camel,[^10] demanding that the people follow their rites and precepts and morals; but they say that it is foolish to attend to the counsels of Christ, to the life or [manner of life]{g:conversacio} of Christ and of Peter, since, because of the distance and variation of time, the contrary must now be observed; and so, observing the chaff, we let go the pure grain. Therefore it is needful that we giddy ones do not shake off the religion which Christ instituted, for in Ecclus. 19:1 it is said: "He that contemneth small things, shall fall by little and little." We ought therefore, [setting aside]{g:postponere} the newer rites, to observe the morals and works of Christ, because according to Gregory, *Pastoral Rule* 1.3, "for this reason Jesus had come in the flesh, that he might not only redeem us by his passion in the flesh, but also teach us by his manner of life";[^11] therefore woe to us if we lurk in darkness, or, going back or turning aside, do not follow him.

### 04-c ¶2 · 93.21–93.24  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 93] **Sed** contra istud instatur tripliciter; videtur primo, cum Christus instituit et precepit capi hoc sacramentum in cena, quod nos debemus ipsum sequi et apostolos eius celebrando post prandium.

ENGLISH: But against this [it is objected]{g:instatur} in three ways. It seems, first, since Christ instituted and commanded that this sacrament be taken at supper, that we ought to follow him and his apostles by celebrating after dinner.

### 04-c ¶3 · 93.25–93.29  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 93] Hic dicitur quod conclusio est vera; **sed** pro materia notandum est quod, sicut Christus cum apostolis sumpsit hoc sacramentum ieiunus a crimine, sic oportet nos in hoc sacramento eorum discipulos sequi ipsos.

ENGLISH: Here [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that the conclusion is true; but for the matter it must be noted that, as Christ with the apostles [received]{g:sumpcio} this sacrament fasting from crime, so we, their disciples, must follow them in this sacrament.[^12]

### 04-c ¶4 · 93.30–94.15  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 93] **Sed** quia in nobis ex cibario causatur communiter ventris ingluvies, ideo pia religione ieiuni corpore [p. 94] sacramentum istud accipiamus; et ne paulativa declinacio inducat periculum, statuimus nobis pro regula quod omnino ieiuni in corpore celebramus et nimis prepostere plus ponderamus illud quam imitacionem Christi in ieiunio a peccato, et sic de ritu quoad locum et tempus ac alias observancias circa hoc sacramentum racionabiliter ordinatas. Illas **autem** laudo **sed** preponderancias earum et nimietatem detestor; unde cecitas pharisaica est qua putamus Christum pocius sequendum in ritibus quam virtute, ut licet Christus sic prandebat cum duodecim quorum unus fuit traditor et sic de infinitis circumstanciis: non tamen oportet nos illas attendere, cum non per se dicant virtutes secundum imitacionem Christi quoad genus virtutum omnimode conservare.

ENGLISH: But because in us gluttony of the belly is commonly caused by food, therefore by a pious religion let us receive [94] this sacrament fasting in body; and lest a gradual declining bring on danger, we have laid down for ourselves as a rule that we celebrate wholly fasting in body, and exceedingly preposterously we weigh that more than the imitation of Christ in fasting from sin; and so of the rite as to place and time, and other observances about this sacrament reasonably ordained. And these I praise, but their [preponderances]{g:preponderancia} and excess I detest; hence it is pharisaic blindness by which we think that Christ is to be followed rather in rites than in [virtue]{g:virtus}: as, although Christ thus dined with the twelve, of whom one was a traitor, and so of infinite circumstances, yet we need not attend to these, since they do not [of themselves]{g:per-se} [signify]{g:dicere} virtues to be kept in every way, according to the imitation of Christ, as to the kind of virtues.[^13]

### 04-c ¶5 · 94.16–94.28  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 94] Tres **autem** assignant homines causas quare Christus secundum hunc ordinem sacramentum istud instituit; prima quia oportuit esum agni paschalis, cum fuit figura huius sacramenti, precedere; secunda ut Christus doceat nos ordinaciones futuras imitacione sua in moribus minus attendere; et tercia ut legislator et auctor doceat ex ista varietate in ipso esse preeminenciam potestatis; ideo cum debemus sequi Christum, non precedere vel ex equo viare cum ipso, debemus gaudere de privilegio quod habet ut Deus et ut primogenitus ex vi gracie unionis et utrobique sequi ipsum vel proporcionaliter vel similiter secundum moralem congruenciam nostri status.

ENGLISH: And men assign three causes why Christ instituted this sacrament in this order: the first, because the [eating]{g:esus} of the paschal lamb, since it was a [figure]{g:figura} of this sacrament, had to precede; the second, that Christ might teach us, by imitation of him in morals, to attend less to the ordinances to come; and the third, that as lawgiver and author he might teach by this variety that there is in him a [preeminence]{g:preeminencia} of [power]{g:potestas}. Therefore, since we ought to follow Christ, not to go before him or [be a wayfarer]{g:viator} level with him, we ought to rejoice in the [privilege]{g:privilegium} which he has as God and as firstborn by force of the [grace of union]{g:gracia-unionis}, and on both sides to follow him, either proportionally or similarly, according to the moral [fittingness]{g:congruencia} of our [state]{g:status}.[^14]

### 04-d ¶1 · 94.29–95.7  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 94] In isto **autem** exemplatur infirmo vel in alia necessitate posito quod licet sibi prandenti accipere corpus Christi; cum devotus audiens missam post gentaculum melius sepe accipit quam sacerdos suus [p. 95] qui celebrat: Melius eciam celebraret in casu sacerdos eadem die artificiali post nonam et prandium quam institutis humanitus quantumcunque ordinata apparencia celebrando, sicut melius in casu comederet tam clericus quam laycus corpus Christi, predicando vel opus meritorium faciendo quam conficiendo, quia copiosius haberet Christum in anima.

ENGLISH: And in this an example is given for the sick man, or one placed in some other necessity: that it is lawful for him, having dined, to receive the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi};[^1] since a devout man hearing mass after breakfast often receives better than his [priest]{g:sacerdos} [95] who celebrates. Also, in a given case, a priest would celebrate better on the same [artificial day]{g:dies-artificialis} after nones and dinner than by celebrating with institutions made [humanly]{g:humanitus}, however ordered their appearance;[^2] just as in a given case both a cleric and a layman would eat the body of Christ better by preaching or doing a meritorious work than by [consecrating]{g:conficere}, because he would have Christ more abundantly in the soul.

### 04-d ¶3 · 95.15–97.28  (7 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 95] Hic dicitur quod benedicere accipitur in proposito pro gracias agere, modo quo omnis creatura **sed** precipue racionalis benedicit Deum. Unde in confirmacionem istius sentencie, ubi Mattheus et Marcus dicunt benedixit Lucas et Paulus dicunt: Gracias egit vel gracias agens fregit, et in isto nos sacerdotes Christum sequimur modo nostro; debemus enim in omnibus operibus nostris sequi Christum; vel quadam propinqua similitudine, ut quoad opera humanitatis sue vel quadam analoga proporcione ut quoad opera divinitatis et primogeniture sue, et diversi debent sequi eum diversimode secundum quod Christus debet esse regula status sui, ut posito duce exercitus qui errare non poterit et quem severe iniunctum est quodlibet membrum exercitus sequi modo suo, patet quod aliqui sequerentur uno incessu et aliqui alio, [p. 96] secundum differenciam qua post ipsum sunt positi. Aliqui eciam sequerentur ipsum propinquius et alii distancius secundum quod in calore et virtute secundum locum motiva et organis variantur; sic dispari incessu secuntur fideles Christum secundum disparitatem statuum et gracie quibus capitaneus illos vocat. Omnes tamen salvandos oportet sequi hunc ducem (ut docet Crisostomus In Imperfecto Omelia VII°), quia ipse est via sine qua nemo potest celestem civitatem attingere. Unde ait supra: Triplex ponitur causa, quare quatuor evangeliste qui sunt organa Spiritus Sancti tantum in istis verbis sacramentalibus variabant; potuit enim facillime facere omnes illos quatuor similime concordare; **sed** ista variacio fuit magis conveniens, primo ut doceret per hoc predictam veritatem katholicam quomodo totus christianus exercitus debet dispariter sequi Christum; sicut istorum quatuor evangelistarum discipuli quadam varietate decenti sub variis formis conficiunt sacramentum, et omnes sequuntur Christum; secundo ut iste subtilis et realis philosophus ostendat se habere curam parvam de signis **sed** principaliter attendere ad sentenciam designatam, et tercio ut lex sua in varietate verborum que sunt nobis exercicia sit gravidata multiformi sentencia. Non enim est aliqua istorum evangelistarum quantumcunque parva varietas, quin trahit secum notandam subtilitatem sentencie, ut in sacramento sanguinis singulariter fit mencio de testamento, quamvis tam consecracio panis quam calicis ad novum testamentum pertineat; cuius misterium credo esse quod [p. 97] liquor solet effundi in lege veteri in testimonium contracti federis, in signum quod sanguis eius effundi debeat qui fedus illud diruperit, ut patet; Christus **autem** authonomatice effudit sanguinem suum preciosum in testimonium federis inter sponsum et ecclesiam et pene dissipancium legem suam, et sic effusione sanguinis Christi testamentum suum confirmatum est. Cui sentencie videtur Zachariam alludere, dum IX° capitulo sic alloquitur Jesum nostrum: Tu quoque in sanguine testamenti tui eduxisti vinctos tuos de lacu in quo non est aqua. Istud **autem** misterium non sic pertinenter exprimeretur faciendo cum verbis sacramenti panis memoriam testamenti. **Sed** notandum ulterius quod evangelium Luc. XXII°, 20 habet sic: Hic est calix novum testamentum in meo sanguine. Evangelium **autem** Apostoli I. Cor. XI°, 25 habet simpliciter: Hic calix novum testamentum est in meo sanguine, ubi patet primo quod accipitur methonomatice contentum pro continente. Et patet secundo quod loquitur figurative et non ydemptice, quando dicit quod calix est novum testamentum, ut vel sic manuducamur ad intelligendum sensum verborum quibus eukaristia est confecta; unde Math. XXVI°, 28 sic loquitur: Hic est sanguis meus novi testamenti. Et eandem seriem habet Marc. XIV°, 24, ubi utrobique demonstratur vinum in calice; et sic verborum istorum quatuor evangelistarum diversitas plena est misterio.

Hence, in confirmation of this [teaching]{g:sentencia},[^5] where Matthew and Mark say "he blessed", Luke and Paul say: "he gave thanks", or "giving thanks, broke"; and in this we priests follow Christ in our manner. For we ought in all our works to follow Christ: either by a certain near likeness, as with regard to the works of his [humanity]{g:humanitas}, or by a certain analogous proportion, as with regard to the works of his [divinity]{g:divinitas} and his [primogeniture]{g:primogenitura};[^6] and different men ought to follow him differently, according as Christ ought to be the rule of each one's [state]{g:status}. As, supposing a leader of an army who cannot err, and whom every [member]{g:membrum} of the army has been strictly enjoined to follow in his own manner, it is clear that some would follow with one gait and some with another, [96] according to the difference by which they are placed after him. Some also would follow him more nearly and others more distantly, according as they vary in heat, and in the power of moving with respect to place, and in their organs; so with unequal gait the faithful follow Christ according to the disparity of states and of grace in which the [captain]{g:capitaneus} calls them. Yet all who are to be saved must follow this leader (as Chrysostom teaches in the *Imperfect Work*, Homily 7), for he is the [way]{g:via} without which no one can reach the heavenly city.[^7] Hence he says above: A threefold cause is laid down why the four evangelists, who are the organs of the Holy Spirit, varied so much in these sacramental words; for he could very easily have made all those four agree most closely; but this variation was more suitable:[^8] first, that he might teach by it the aforesaid catholic truth, how the whole Christian army ought to follow Christ unequally, just as the disciples of these four evangelists consecrate the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} under various forms with a certain becoming variety, and all follow Christ; second, that this subtle and real philosopher might show that he has little care for [signs]{g:signum} but principally attends to the sense designated; and third, that his law, in the variety of words which are exercises for us, might be made pregnant with multiform sense.[^9] For there is no variety of these evangelists, however small, that does not draw with it a subtlety of sense worth noting: as mention of the testament is made in the sacrament of the blood [alone]{g:singulariter}, although the consecration both of the [bread]{g:panis} and of the [chalice]{g:calix} pertains to the new testament.[^10] The [mystery]{g:misterium} of this I believe to be that [97] in the old law a liquid used to be poured out in witness of a covenant contracted, in sign that the blood of him who should break that covenant ought to be shed, as is clear; and Christ, [by antonomasia]{g:autonomatice}, shed his precious blood in witness of the covenant between the [bridegroom]{g:sponsus} and the [church]{g:ecclesia}, and of the penalty of those who dissipate his law; and so by the shedding of the blood of Christ his testament was confirmed.[^11] To this teaching Zechariah seems to allude, when in chapter 9 he thus addresses our Jesus: "Thou also by the blood of thy testament hast brought forth thy prisoners out of the pit, wherein is no water."[^12] And this mystery would not be so pertinently expressed by making mention of the testament with the words of the sacrament of the bread. But it is to be noted further that the gospel, Luke 22:20, has thus: "This is the chalice, the new testament in my blood." And the gospel of the Apostle, 1 Cor. 11:25, has [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}: "This chalice is the new testament in my blood";[^13] where it is clear, first, that the contained is taken [metonymically]{g:methonomatice} for the container.[^14] And it is clear, second, that he speaks [figuratively]{g:figurative} and not [identically]{g:ydemptice} when he says that the chalice is the new testament, so that at least in this way we may be led by the hand to understand the [meaning]{g:sensus} of the words by which the eucharist is consecrated; hence Matt. 26:28 speaks thus: "This is my blood of the new testament." And Mark 14:24 has the same sequence, where in both places the [wine]{g:vinum} in the chalice is demonstrated; and so the diversity of the words of these four evangelists is full of mystery.

ENGLISH: And it is clear, third, that it is no obstacle but is consonant, as the [decretal]{g:decretalis} *Cum Marthe* says, that in this sacrament [98] there are at once truth and [figure]{g:figura}; for the sacrament [figures]{g:figurare} Christ and the union with him and with the church; and it [truly]{g:vere} makes and figures the sacramental presence of Christ beyond other figures.[^15] Hence, just as heresy is vainly [proved conclusively]{g:convincere} in this, that Christ is a creature, because Arius said it, so it is barely and foolishly argued that the teaching is heretical which says "this speech, *This is my body*, is [tropical speech]{g:locucio-tropica}" and "that host is a figure", from this, that heretics say it.[^16] For they do not attend to the manner and intention of the saying, with the other things added on top, but to the truth which every heretic confesses, which they defame; and yet it is known that in morals the manner of the work is most to be attended to.
