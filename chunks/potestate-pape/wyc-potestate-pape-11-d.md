# *De potestate pape*, chapter 11, part 4: sacramental power equal in all priests; episcopal rites and vestments

## ¶1 · 275.1–276.23

> [p. 275] Quantum ad secundum scribit dominus Ardmacanus in De Questionibus Armenorum libro XI in fine cap. V de questione Armenorum: Ex isto capite sequi michi videtur, quod conclusio ad potestatem ligandi et solvendi sive remittendi peccata sive retinendi, sive baptisandi aut consecrandi aut benedicendi, et breviter quoad quantumcumque potestatem actus sacramentalis expressam a Christo in evangelio, quiscumque sacerdos in ordinacione sua totam accipit aut nichil ipsius, quoniam singulis in simul et indivise sunt date a Christo, et Matth. XIV, 6 ac Mar. X, 9 mandat ipse, quod Deus coniunxit, homo non separet. Unde videtur racionabiliter posse concludi, quod hec potestates sacramentales in omnibus sacerdotibus sunt equales. Hec doctor. Sed notandum quod crescente multitudine presbiterorum m sciencia et potencia variata, potest racionabiliter limitari sacerdotibus habilioribus singulariter officium, attestans in eis preeminenciam dignitatis, salva tamen potestate sacerdotibus inferioribus tempore necessitatis vel indisposicionis superioris presbiteri sacramenta ista et sacramentalia ministrandi; nam in lege veteri, ubi magis attenderant ad tales ritus sensibiles, propter indisposicionem summi pontificis non solum licuit sacerdoti inferiori, sed debuit supplere ministerium summi pontificis: aliter enim quando summus sacerdos foret infirmus vel sompno nocturno vel alia causa pollutus, sequeretur inconveniens legis Dei; aliquando enim polluti sunt ad vesperum, ut dicitur Levitici XV, 5 et Deuteronomio XXIII, et aliquando usque ad septimum diem, ut dicitur Ezech. XLV. Cum igitur debuerunt cottidie offerre super altare incensi [p. 276] vespere et mane, ut patet Exod. XXX, patet, quod inferior sacerdos potestatem habuit defectus summi pontificis exsupplere. Quare igitur non in lege nova, ubi ritus talis minus attenditur? Hoc enim officium pertinebat singulariter cum paribus ad summum pontificem, ut patet Exod. XXX et XXXIX et Levitici VIII, oportet tamen tam ex parte superiorum quam inferiorum quam eciam tocius populi cavere pericula. Ex parte superiorum, ne exinde superbiant, putantes hoc eis contingere pure ex institucione divina, secundo ne ad illum statum sumantur inhabiles, et tercio omnino ne ex cupiditate eorum tale ministerium sit venale. Sacerdotem autem inferiorem oportet cavere ab istis et omnino, ne ex pre sumpcione temeraria det scandalum fratri suo. Et omnem fidelem oportet attendere ne talibus nimis ponderante precio innitatur. Aliqui enim plus appreciantur hos ritus bene sed humanitus adinventos quam evangelium vel mandata decalogi. Periculosum est, inquam, iudaice hos ritus preternecessarios nimis attendere; Deus enim supplet defectus eorum ex aliis operibus virtuosis.

As to the second, [the lord of Armagh]{g:ardmacanus} writes in *On the Questions of the Armenians*, book 11, at the end of chapter 5, on the question of the Armenians: "From this chapter it seems to me to follow, as a conclusion, that as regards the [power]{g:potestas} of binding and loosing, or of remitting sins or retaining them, or of baptizing or consecrating or blessing, and in short as regards any power whatever of a sacramental act expressed by Christ in the gospel, every priest in his [ordination]{g:ordinacio} receives the whole of it or nothing of it, since they were given by Christ to each together and undividedly; and in Matt. 19:6 and Mark 10:9 he commands: What God hath joined together, let no man put asunder. Hence it seems that it can reasonably [be inferred]{g:concludere} that these sacramental powers are equal in all priests." So the doctor.[^1] But it is to be noted that, as the multitude of presbyters grew, varied in knowledge and [power]{g:potencia}, an office may reasonably [be assigned]{g:limitare} singularly to the more [qualified]{g:habilis} priests, attesting in them a [preeminence]{g:preeminencia} of dignity; saving, however, to the lower priests the power of ministering these sacraments and [sacramentals]{g:sacramentalia} in time of need or of the [unfitness]{g:indisposicio} of the superior presbyter.[^2] For in the old law, where they attended more to such [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} rites, because of the unfitness of the [high priest]{g:summi-pontifices} it was not only lawful for a lower priest, but he was bound, to supply the ministry of the high priest: for otherwise, when the [high priest]{g:summus-sacerdos} was sick, or polluted by sleep at night or by another cause, an [ill consequence]{g:inconveniens} for God's law would follow;[^3] for sometimes they are polluted "until the evening", as is said in Leviticus 15:5 and Deuteronomy 23, and sometimes until the seventh day, as is said in Ezek. 45.[^4] Since therefore they had to offer daily upon the altar of incense [276] evening and morning, as is clear from Exod. 30, it is clear that the lower priest had power to make up the defects of the high priest. Why then not in the new law, where such a rite is less attended to? For this office belonged singularly, with its like, to the high priest, as is clear from Exod. 30 and 39 and Lev. 8;[^5] yet dangers must be guarded against, on the part of the superiors as well as of the inferiors, and also of the whole people. On the part of the superiors: lest they grow proud from it, thinking that this befalls them purely by divine institution; second, lest the [unqualified]{g:inhabilis} be taken up into that [state]{g:status}; and third, above all, lest through their covetousness such a ministry be for sale. And the lower priest must beware of these things, and above all lest from rash presumption he give scandal to his brother. And every believer must take heed lest he rest on such things, setting too heavy a price on them.[^6] For some prize these [rites]{g:ritus}, well but humanly devised, more than the gospel or the commandments of the decalogue. It is dangerous, I say, to attend too much, in Jewish fashion, to these rites, which are beyond necessity; for God supplies their defects from other virtuous works.[^7]

## ¶2 · 276.25–277.10

> [p. 276] Secundo principaliter sic arguitur: Ex lege veteri eliciunt sancti et leges ecclesie quod propter distincciones ordinum sunt ministeria episcopo deputata. Unde Dyonisius in De Ecclesiastica Yerarchia ponit tres ordines ecclesie, scilicet ordinem yerarchicum, quem episcopalem intelligit, ordinem sacerdotalem vel presbiteralem et ordinem ministerialem, et distinguens proprias operaciones uniuscuiusque istorum ordinum, [p. 277] narrat quomodo ad supremum ordinem, scilicet episcopalem, spectat sacerdotum consecracio, divini ungenti perfeccio et sacri altaris benediccio. Unde censeretur hodie insanus qui diceret quod quilibet sacerdos habet potestatem ordines conferendi, baptizatos confirmandi, ecclesias et ornamenta sacerdotalia benedicendi et sic de benediccionibus ornamentis et aliis ritibus, que per sapientes et sanctos antiquos episcopali dignitati sunt propria.

Second, principally, it is argued thus: from the old law the saints and the laws of the church draw out that, because of the distinctions of orders, ministries are [deputed]{g:deputacio} to the bishop. Hence Dionysius, in *On the Ecclesiastical Hierarchy*, sets down three orders of the church, namely the hierarchic order, which he understands as the episcopal, the priestly or presbyteral order, and the ministerial order; and, distinguishing the proper operations of each of these orders, [277] he relates how to the highest order, namely the episcopal, belong the consecration of priests, the perfecting of the divine ointment and the blessing of the holy altar.[^8] Hence he would today be thought mad who said that any priest whatever has the power of conferring orders, of confirming the baptized, of blessing churches and priestly ornaments, and so of the blessings, ornaments and other rites which, by the wise and holy ancients, are proper to the episcopal dignity.

## ¶3 · 277.11–279.15

> [p. 277] Quantum ad istud, patet ex dictis quomodo licite possunt fieri et licite pretermitti. Ideo requiritur magna prudencia ad acceptandum ista in suo ordine. Quidam enim putant sine istorum sacramentorum et sacerdotum ordine non posse recte viare ecclesiam, unde tantum ponderant has cerimonias, quod offendentem plus puniunt quam qui infideliter in caritate deficiat. Conceditur igitur cum beato Dyonisio quod sunt tales tres ordines sacerdotum, cum iuxta decretum Crysostomi positum distinccione XL Multi sunt sacerdotes et pauci sunt sacerdotes. Sed non oportet, quod sacerdos ex gracia Dei factus superior accipiat exinde novum ecclesiasticum sacramentum, dum ego omnino omitterem habere episcopalem consecracionem, antequam accipere presumerem hoc episcopale ministerium; est enim preternecessarium et predicacione, oracione ac multis aliis cultibus Dei inferius, nec non ad multos cultus Dei non tam necesse sicut creditur requisitum. Conceditur ergo, quod benediccio, consecracio et alia ministracio episcopalis est laudabilis rite facta. Sed grave est quod ecclesia [p. 278] oneretur tam multiplici cerimonia, gravius quod tantam vim ponat in illa, et gravissimum quod nemo potest salvari a Deo, ut dicunt, nisi credat et accipiat secundum formam iam currentem huiusmodi sacramenta. De istis autem in tractatu De Sacramentis est sermo lacior. Et sic conceditur quod vestes sacerdotales et ornamenta episcopalia possunt introduci pie et religiose, licet mendaciter et impie communius sint accepte, ut tractantes ritus humanos et statuta hominum, notant quinque vestimenta sacerdotalia, quibus communiter induitur celebrando, et variantur in parte ab ornamentis sacerdotum veteris testamenti, ut declarat Hugo libro secundo De Sacramentis parte IV. Singularis, inquit, habitus sacerdotibus Christi in ministerio traditur, ut per id quod foris apparet quales intus esse debeant ostendatur, sacrarum autem vestium ritus partim a veteri testamento assumptus est et partim a patribus novi testamenti adiectus. Legitur enim Exod. XXVIII, XXIX, XXX et XXXIX quomodo Moyses indueret Aaron fratrem suum, primo tunica bissina, que grece poderis id est talaris dicitur, que a collo usque ad talos extenditur, que in novo sacerdocio non solum de bisso sed de lino contexitur; ipsa autem modificata membris corporis et aptata nichil superfluum aut dissolutum in vita sacerdotis debere esse ostendit. Sed in novo testamento laxatur post dotacionem ecclesie, cum multa temporalia preter necessaria officio sacerdotis sibi monstruoso adiaceant. Hec autem vestis ob spem candoris nomen Albe sortitur, que signat spiritualem mundiciam ministrorum, iuxta [p. 279] illud Apok XIX, 8: Datum est sponse ut operiat se bissino splendenti candido. Etsequitur exposicio: Bissinum enim iustificaciones sunt sanctorum. Caro quidem hominis mundiciam quam ex natura non habet, studio bono aquirit per graciam, sicut bissus vel linum candorem suum, quem natura non habuit, per studium et industriam artificis aquisivit, dum multis tunsionibus ac locionibus illustracione solis siccantis quasi quadam violencia attritum vertitur in alborem. Oportet quidem crebro castigare corpus et in servitutem redigere, sicut dicit Apostolus prima Cor. IX se fecisse; et sic positus in aqua tribulacionis et tunsus tortoribus ac interim sole iusticie consolatus a tribulacionibus siccatus et calore veritatis accensus.

As to this, it is clear from what has been said how they can lawfully be done and lawfully be left undone. Therefore great prudence is required to accept these things in their order. For some think that without the order of these sacraments and priests the church cannot rightly [be a wayfarer]{g:viator}; hence they weigh these ceremonies so heavily that they punish one who offends [against them] more than one who faithlessly fails in [charity]{g:caritas}.[^9] It is granted, therefore, with blessed Dionysius that there are such three orders of priests, since, according to the decree of Chrysostom placed in Distinction 40, "Many are priests and few are priests".[^10] But it is not necessary that a priest made superior by the grace of God should receive thereby a new ecclesiastical sacrament; for my part I would altogether forgo having episcopal consecration sooner than presume to receive this episcopal ministry.[^11] For it is beyond necessity, and lower than preaching, prayer and many other acts of the worship of God, and moreover is not so necessarily required for many acts of worship of God as is believed. It is granted, therefore, that blessing, consecration and the other episcopal ministration is praiseworthy when duly done. But it is grievous that the church [278] should be burdened with so manifold a ceremony, more grievous that it should set such force in it, and most grievous that no one can be saved by God, as they say, unless he believes and receives such sacraments according to the form now current. And of these there is fuller discussion in the treatise *On the Sacraments*.[^12] And so it is granted that priestly vestments and episcopal ornaments can be introduced [piously]{g:pietas} and religiously, although more commonly they have been received lyingly and impiously; as those who treat of human rites and the statutes of men note five priestly vestments, with which [the priest] is commonly clothed in celebrating, and these vary in part from the ornaments of the priests of the old testament, as Hugh declares in the second book of *On the Sacraments*, part 4: "A singular habit," he says, "is handed down to the priests of Christ in the ministry, so that by what appears without it may be shown what they ought to be within; and the rite of the sacred vestments was in part taken from the old testament and in part added by the fathers of the new testament.[^13] For one reads in Exod. 28, 29, 30 and 39 how Moses clothed Aaron his brother, first with a tunic of fine linen, which in Greek is called *poderis*, that is, ankle-length, which reaches from the neck to the ankles, and which in the new priesthood is woven not only of fine linen but of linen; and it, measured and fitted to the members of the body, shows that there ought to be nothing superfluous or loose in the life of the priest." But in the new testament it is loosened after the [endowment]{g:dotacio} of the church, since many [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} beyond what the priest's office needs lie about him, made monstrous.[^14] "And this vestment, for the hope of its whiteness, takes the name Alb, which signifies the spiritual cleanness of the ministers, according to [279] that of Apoc. 19:8: It is granted to the bride that she should clothe herself with fine linen, glittering and white."[^15] And the exposition follows: "For the fine linen are the justifications of saints. The flesh of man indeed acquires by grace, through good effort, the cleanness which it does not have by nature, as fine linen or linen acquired its whiteness, which it did not have by nature, through the effort and industry of the craftsman, when, worn down by many beatings and washings, by the shining of the drying sun, as by a kind of violence, it is turned to whiteness." It is needful indeed often to chastise the body and bring it into subjection, as the Apostle says, 1 Cor. 9, that he did; and so, placed in the water of tribulation and beaten by tormentors, and meanwhile consoled by the sun of justice, dried from tribulations and kindled by the heat of truth.[^16]

## Notes

[^1]: **S/T.** FitzRalph, *Summa in quaestionibus Armenorum* 11.5 (end); no reference at the page foot; to be checked (LEDGER P11d-1). *Quod conclusio ad potestatem*: taken as "as a conclusion, as regards the power"; the print's *conclusio* does not construe cleanly and a word (e.g. *est*) or *quoad* may lie behind it. The gospel reference is Matt. 19:6 (print XIV); Wyclif omits *ergo* (DR "What therefore God hath joined together"). Codd. in marg. at 275.1: *Potestatem plenam accipit sacerdos quilibet in consecracione*.
[^2]: **C.** *Limitari … officium*: "assigned" (GLOSSARY *limitare*), not "restricted"; Loserth's side-note says "limited". *Attestans* agrees loosely with *officium*: the office attests the preeminence.
[^3]: **C/T.** *Inconveniens* here leans to the "troubles" sense: the law would be left unfulfilled, not an absurdity in argument. At 275.30 the manuscripts read *antea enim* for the print's *aliter enim*; the print is followed.
[^4]: **S.** Lev. 15:5 (DR "shall be unclean until the evening"); Deut. 23:10–11 (B reads *Deut. XXVIII*). The seven days are Ezek. 44:26 (DR "they shall reckon unto him seven days"), not ch. 45 as printed. Incense evening and morning: Exod. 30:7–8.
[^5]: **C.** *Cum paribus*: "with its like", i.e. with offices of the same kind; it might also mean "with his peers" (the other priests). Taken of the offices, since the sentence is about what was the high priest's alone.
[^6]: **C.** *Nimis ponderante precio*: ablative absolute, "the price weighing too much", i.e. valuing them too highly.
[^7]: **C/G.** *Preternecessarios*: "beyond necessity" (also 277.24; proposed for GLOSSARY). *Defectus eorum* is taken as the lack of these rites: where they are missing, God makes up from other virtuous works.
[^8]: **S/R.** Pseudo-Dionysius, *De ecclesiastica hierarchia* 5 (the hierarch's operations; the consecration of the myron, ch. 4); from memory, to be checked (LEDGER P11d-2). A in marg. at 277.31: *Dionysius concedit tres ordines ecclesie secundum officia distinctos*.
[^9]: **T/G.** *Viare*: GLOSSARY, the verb of *viator*. *Qui* at 277.18 is lacking in both manuscripts and is Loserth's supply; followed.
[^10]: **S.** Gratian, *Decretum* D.40 c.12 (*Multi sacerdotes*, from pseudo-Chrysostom, *Opus imperfectum in Matthaeum*); from memory, to be checked (LEDGER P11d-3).
[^11]: **C.** *Dum ego omnino omitterem … antequam accipere presumerem*: *antequam* in the sense "sooner than, rather than"; Wyclif speaks of himself.
[^12]: **S.** *In tractatu De Sacramentis*: not identified; probably Wyclif's own (projected or lost) treatment, not Hugh's work cited in the next sentence. To be checked (LEDGER P11d-4).
[^13]: **S/T.** Hugh of St Victor, *De sacramentis* 2.4 (on sacred vestments); Loserth: "Opp. (ed. Mog. 1617) III, 449" (LEDGER P11d-5). At 278.19–20 the manuscripts read *a fratribus* for *a patribus*; the print (Hugh) is followed. Vestments: Exod. 28–29, 39 (and 30). Codd. in marg. at 278.8: *Vestimenta episcopalia possunt introduci pie et religiose*.
[^14]: **C.** Wyclif's own aside inside Hugh's text (not italic in the print). *Sibi monstruoso adiaceant*: the dative *monstruoso* agrees with *sibi*: the goods cling to a priest who is made monstrous by them.
[^15]: **T/S.** *Ob spem candoris*: printed *spem*, "hope"; Hugh's sense is surely *speciem*, "appearance" (a common abbreviation); translated as printed and flagged. Apoc. 19:8, Vulg. *Et datum est illi ut cooperiat se byssino splendenti et candido* (DR "And it is granted to her that she should clothe herself with fine linen, glittering and white"); Wyclif reads *sponse*, "to the bride". At 279.1–2 the manuscripts read *bissinum splendentis candidum* and lack *ut*; the print is followed.
[^16]: **S/T.** 1 Cor. 9:27 (DR "I chastise my body and bring it into subjection"). B reads *Sed* for *Oportet* at 279.10. The last sentence has no main verb at the chunk's end (the participles await one, presumably "he acquires whiteness"); translated as it stands. Codd. in marg. at 279.3: *Alba prima vestis*.
