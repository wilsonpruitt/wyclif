# *De ecclesia*, chapter 5, part 2: one body of three churches; a digression on lines; the church seen from afar

## ¶1 · 96.20–97.3

> [p. 96] Secundo principaliter arguitur contra dicta per hoc quod nichil unum et specialiter non unum corpus componitur ex spiritibus in purgatorio, ex viantibus in mundo et ex triumphantibus in celo, cum continuacio et unio sint de essencia corporis, eo quod aliter periret sciencia linearum. Hic negatur assumptum, nec est continuacio vel unio, cum sit accidens de essencia cuiuscunque substancie; nam in quotquot partes quantitativas corpus martiris divisum fuerit, manet durante forma idem corpus in numero, et Christus fuit humanitus in limbo et mortuus in sepulchro. Absit ergo quod credamus animas fidelium defunctorum tunc non esse partes [p. 97] ecclesie, quando instar Abel a quo cepit ecclesia sunt secure de sua beatitudine corpori Christi insolubiliter incorporate.

Secondly and principally, [it is argued, by appeal to]{g:arguitur-per} this, against what has been
said: that nothing one, and specially not one body, is composed of spirits in purgatory, of [those on
the road]{g:viantes} in the world and of the triumphant in heaven, since continuity and union are of
the essence of a body; for otherwise the science of lines would perish.[^1] Here the
[assumption]{g:assumptum} is denied; nor is continuity or union, since it is an accident, of the
essence of any substance whatever; for into however many quantitative parts the body of a martyr may
have been divided, it remains, while the form endures, the same body in number; and Christ was in his
humanity in limbo, and dead in the sepulchre. Far be it, therefore, that we should believe the souls
of the faithful departed not to be parts [97] of the [church]{g:ecclesia} then, when, after the
pattern of Abel, from whom the church began, they are secure of their blessedness, indissolubly
incorporated in the body of Christ.[^2]

## ¶10 · 97.14–98.22

> [p. 97] Et quantum ad combinaciones parcium corporum tam eiusdem speciei quam disparium, dictum est diffuse alibi; quem ille solus inter homines per se perfecte novit numerum talium cuius sapiencia non habet numerum mensurantem. Et quantum ad lineas, dicitur quod licet resolvantur in attomos manent eedem, unde priorem et cerciorem noticiam habet de eis arismetricus geometro. Alia autem est racio continuacionis, unde prius considerat arismetricus de quantitate continui quam geometer, uterque tamen sicut ignorat quantitatem numeri attomorum compositi, sic ignorat eius precisam quantitatem; quadam tamen estimacione capta a sensu excedit geometer arismetricum, cum suffragio sensus cognoscit quomodo dyameter quadrati superat costam suam. Intellectus tamen ultra imaginativam percipit utrumque errorem: nam sicut in quadratis de costis nobis finitis quoad numerum punctorum contingit accipere dyametros [p. 98] eorum que paria puncta precise continuant, sic indubie quantumcunque magna fuerit figura quadrata contingit accipere dyametrum, que paria puncta contineat cum suo latere, ut capiendo dyametrum que de qualibet costa transversatili precise contineat unum punctum. Tunc precise quot sunt puncta in costa, precise tot sunt latera transversatilia, et precise tot puncta continet data dyameter. Et tamen contingit accipere aliam diametrum in cuius composicionem racione obliquacionis plura vel pauciora puncta ingrediantur. Nec scietur diffinite proporcio aut quantitas diametri respectu lateris, nisi scita quantitate numeri punctorum ex quibus componitur. Ideo querendum est de qua diametro arguens velit loqui, cum inter eadem puncta contingit accipere infinitas lineas, quarum nulla divisibiliter excedit a rectitudine racione variacionis punctorum dyametrum quantitative componencium. Et sic communiter est dyameter super qua cadit imaginacio maior costa, ut communiter dicitur in medietate proporcionis duple, sed certitudo deficit in tali imaginacione, cum error sensus in capiendo lineas corrigitur ex subtilitate intellectus (ut alias exposui).

And as to the combinations of the parts of bodies, both of the same species and of disparate
[species], it has been said at length elsewhere; the number of such things he alone among men knows
perfectly of himself whose "wisdom" has "no number" measuring [it].[^4] And as to lines, the answer is
that although they are resolved into atoms they remain the same; hence the arithmetician has a prior
and more certain knowledge of them than the geometer. But the account of continuity is another; hence
the arithmetician considers the quantity of the continuum before the geometer; yet each, just as he
is ignorant of the quantity of the number of atoms in the composite, so is ignorant of its precise
quantity; yet by a certain estimation taken from sense the geometer exceeds the arithmetician, since
with the aid of sense he knows how the diameter of a square exceeds its side.[^5] Yet the
understanding, beyond the imagination, perceives the error of both: for just as in squares whose
sides are finite to us as to the number of points it is possible to take diameters [98] of them that
continue precisely equal [numbers of] points, so without doubt, however great the square figure may
be, it is possible to take a diameter that contains as many points as its side, as by taking a
diameter that contains precisely one point of each transverse side. Then precisely as many points as
there are in the side, precisely so many are the transverse sides, and precisely so many points does
the given diameter contain. And yet it is possible to take another diameter into whose composition,
by reason of its obliquity, more or fewer points enter. Nor will the proportion or quantity of the
diameter with respect to the side be known definitely, unless the quantity of the number of points of
which it is composed be known. Therefore it must be asked of which diameter the arguer wishes to
speak, since between the same points it is possible to take infinitely many lines, none of which
departs divisibly from straightness by reason of the variation of the points that make up the
diameter in quantity.[^6] And so commonly the diameter on which the imagination falls is greater than
the side, as is commonly said, by half of the double proportion; but certainty fails in such
imagination, since the error of sense in taking lines is corrected by the subtlety of the
understanding (as I have expounded elsewhere).[^7]

## ¶11 · 98.23–99.31

> [p. 98] Sed redeundo ad materiam de ecclesia dicitur primo quod sicut sensus errat circa magnitudinem et rectitudinem linee, quia ignorat numerum punctorum ex quibus componitur, sic viantes errant circa magnitudinem dignitatis vel meriti viantis, quia ignorant numerum et mensuram in quibus verbum eternaliter disposuit ponere sponsam suam. Multi enim obliquant a rectitudine iusticie, et sensus infixi in legibus humanis hoc non Percipiunt, sed intellectus considerans veritatem ac sapienciam scripture probabiliter percipit quod declinant a mandatis domini, quia solum in speculo scripture sacre discitur, tenetur et docetur sapiencia mandatorum. [p. 99] Dicitur secundo quod propter elongacionem a vera ecclesia et ab irradiacione luminis capitalis frequenter putatur ecclesiam Christi esse continuam quoad locum et tempus, dum tamen membra Christi et sponse per vacuitates predestinacionis et operis rarenter sunt positi. Multi enim putant totum populum et totum genus sibi succedencium esse christianos verissimos et capitales precipuos in area sancte matris ecclesie, cum tamen sunt vacuitates et membra diaboli. Sicut enim iuxta naturales et perspectivos videns silvam vel campum segetum a longinquo iudicat totum esse continuum consitum arboribus vel frumento, sic nos inperfecta fide videntes ecclesiam a longinquo, non obstante quod habeat in se arbusta sterilia atque zizannia, iudicamus eam plenam fructuosis arboribus et frumento. Et ad istum sensum exponit Lincolniensis propheciam Michee VII°: Ve, inquit, tanta christianismi dilatacio, tanto opere Christi et suorum mediante lege scripture elaborata, est hodie velut in brevis anguli angustia coartata. Plurimam namque mundi partem occupavit infidelitas et a Christi ecclesia separavit; de parte vere dicta christiana separavit a Christo scisma, de parte vero residua que respectu duarum prenominatarum admodum est parva et pauca (ut puto) non modicam porcionem separavit a Christo pravitas heretica, quasi autem totalitatem residui concorporarunt diabolo et separarunt a Christo septem criminalia peccata, ut iam voce lugubri plangat Christus dicens: Ve mihi quia factus sum sicut qui colligit in autumpno racemos vindemie, non est botrus ad comedendum. Uvas preciosas desideravit anima mea;

But, returning to the matter of the church, the answer is, first, that just as sense errs about the
magnitude and straightness of a line, because it is ignorant of the number of points of which it is
composed, so those on the road err about the magnitude of the dignity or merit of one on the road,
because they are ignorant of the number and measure in which the Word eternally disposed to set his
[bride]{g:sponsa}. For many slant away from the straightness of justice, and senses fixed in human
laws do not perceive this; but the understanding that considers the truth and wisdom of scripture
perceives with probability that they decline from the commandments of the Lord, since only in the
mirror of sacred scripture is the wisdom of the commandments learned, held and taught. [99] The answer
is, secondly, that on account of distance from the true church and from the irradiation of the light
of the head, the church of Christ is often thought to be continuous as to place and time, whereas the
members of Christ and of the bride are set sparsely, through vacancies of predestination and of
work.[^8] For many think that the whole people, and the whole line of those succeeding them, are most
true Christians and foremost chiefs on the threshing-floor of holy mother church, when in fact they
are vacancies and members of the devil. For just as, according to the natural philosophers and the
writers on perspective, one seeing a wood or a field of crops from afar judges the whole to be
continuously planted with trees or corn, so we, seeing the church from afar with imperfect faith,
notwithstanding that it has in it barren shrubs and [cockle]{g:zizania}, judge it full of fruitful
trees and corn.[^9] And to this sense Lincoln expounds the prophecy of Micah 7: "Woe," he says, "so
great an expansion of Christianity, wrought with so great labour of Christ and his own by means of the
law of scripture, is today as it were confined in the narrowness of a small corner. For
[unbelief]{g:infidelitas} has occupied the greatest part of the world and separated it from Christ's
church; of the part truly called Christian, schism has separated [a part] from Christ; of the
remaining part, which in respect of the two aforenamed is very small and few, [heretical
depravity]{g:heretica-pravitas} (as I think) has separated no small portion from Christ; and almost
the whole of the remainder the seven criminal sins have [concorporated]{g:concorporacio} with the
devil and separated from Christ; so that now Christ laments with mournful voice, saying: 'Woe is me,
for I am become as one that gleaneth in autumn the grapes of the vintage: there is no cluster to eat.
My soul desired precious grapes;

## ¶12 · 100.1–100.12

> [p. 100] sanctus de terra et sanctus in hominibus non est Nam sponsus ecclesie plus invenit de ficu fatua et vite sterili, plus de zizannia quam de lignis fructiferis et frumento. Nam vitis que solebat facere uvas, ex quibus tamquam uberibus solebat mater ecclesia inebriare suos palmites (ut superius exponitur a Bernardo) iuxta vaticinium prophete Isaie V° 2 facit labruscas, id est, fatuitates honoris mundani atque discordias. Ideo non restat ut quid tales terram occupant in agro dominico, nisi ut reserventur colligendi in finali iudicio et (ut dicitur Matthei XIII° 30) ad laudem ecclesie comburendi.

[100] there is no holy man of the earth, and no holy man among men.'"[^10] For the bridegroom of the
church finds more of the foolish fig tree and the barren vine, more of cockle, than of fruit-bearing
trees and corn. For the vine which used to make grapes, from which, as from breasts, mother church
used to inebriate her branches (as is expounded above by Bernard), according to the oracle of the
prophet Isaiah 5:2, makes "wild grapes", that is, the follies of worldly honour, and discords.[^11]
Therefore no reason remains why such should occupy the ground in the Lord's field, except that they
be reserved to be gathered at the final judgement and (as is said in Matthew 13:30) to be burned to
the praise of the church.[^12]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 96.20: Objection: The Church dormant, militant and triumphant is not one body, since these parts are separate.
- 96.26: Answer: Separation is no bar to unity.
- 97.1: The souls of the blessed are incorporate in Christ's body.
- 97.19: A mathematical digression,
- 98.23: Men judge each other wrongly because their minds are fixed on human law.
- 98.31: The study of Scripture would make them wiser.
- 99.1: Some think that Christ's Church is united as to time and space, whereas the true members of Christ are scattered.
- 100.1: The visible Church contains more tares than wheat.

## Notes

[^1]: **T/R.** A margin *Secunda obieccio* (96.20), *Purgatorium* (96.22); B *unio sunt* (96.24). The
    *no* after *linearum* (about 96.25) is a fragment of the English side-note at 96.26 ("Separation is
    no bar to unity"), not Wyclif's, and is not translated. *Sciencia linearum*: geometry. The spirits
    in purgatory are the church sleeping.

[^2]: **T/C/R.** A margin *Responsio* (96.26).
    *Cum sit accidens*: continuity and union are accidents, so not of the essence of a substance.
    *Humanitus*: in his human nature; soul in limbo and body in the tomb, Christ remained one. Abel as
    the beginning of the church (*ecclesia ab Abel*) is a commonplace: Gregory, *Hom. in Ev.* 19.1, *ab Abel justo usque ad ultimum electum* (PL 76:1154B; cited by Wyclif at 02-b); Augustine, *Enarr. in Ps.* 142.3, *initium habet ab ipso Abel* (PL 37:1846). Wyclif names no source here (LEDGER E05b-1).

[^3]: **S/T.** The Dedication sequence *Rex Salomon fecit templum* (Loserth: Daniel, *Thesaurus
    hymnologicus* V, p. 106) (LEDGER E05b-2); its ascription (Adam of St Victor?) is from memory and to
    be checked (same row). *Cuius* (97.6) is Loserth's: the MSS *Quorum*, as the Angers missal in
    Daniel; *Cuius* as Wyclif quotes the stanza in his *Sermones mixti* (sermon *Propter fratres meos et
    proximos*, Ps. 121) and in the second part of the gospel sermons (*Egrediente Jesu de templo*, Mark
    13) (Loserth). *Ymma*: *ima*; *summâ* (97.10) carries a circumflex on the plate. The scaffold
    divides the stanza into ¶2–9 by its printed lines.

[^4]: **S/T/C.** *Quem* (97.15): so the MSS (Loserth *sic*); it construes only as drawn to *numerum*
    ("the number of such things, which …"). Ps. 146:5, DR "of his wisdom there is no number", alluded
    to; "he alone among men" is Christ. *Dictum est … alibi*: in another work, not identified here.

[^5]: **C/R.** The digression answers the objection's "science of lines" (96.25): lines are resolved
    into indivisible points (*attomi*), so the arithmetician, who counts, knows them before the
    geometer, who measures. A margin (96.29): *Arismetricus et geometer hec videant*. *Dyameter*: the
    diagonal; *costa*, the side. *Suffragio sensus*: with the aid of sense (not the *suffragia* of
    GLOSSARY §A).

[^6]: **C/T.** B *signa quadrata* (98.2); A *quantitatem* (98.17). *Paria puncta*: an equal number of
    points in diagonal and side. *Costa transversatilis*: a transverse row of points parallel to a side;
    a diagonal taking one point from each row has as many points as the side. *De costis nobis finitis*:
    with sides finite "to us", i.e. countable. *Divisibiliter*: by any divisible amount: the many
    point-lines between the same ends differ from straightness by less than any part.

[^7]: **C.** *In medietate proporcionis duple*: in the medieval doctrine of ratios, the diagonal stands
    to the side in half the double ratio (in modern terms √2 : 1). *Ut alias exposui*: in another work,
    not identified here.

[^8]: **S/T/C.** A margin *Nota* (98.23). Wis. 11:21, DR "thou hast ordered all things in measure, and
    number, and weight", alluded to (*numerum et mensuram … disposuit*). *Verbum*: the Word. A margin
    (99.1): *Nota quare iudicium nostrum errat circa ecclesiam*. B lacks *a vera — ecclesiam* (99.1–3).
    *Luminis capitalis*: the light of the head, Christ. *Positi* (99.5): masculine by sense, though
    *membra* is neuter. *Vacuitates*: empty places, gaps.

[^9]: **C.** *Capitales precipuos*: the foremost chief men; *capitalis* echoes *caput*. *Area*: the
    threshing-floor, where grain and chaff lie together (cf. 89.3). *Naturales et perspectivi*: the
    natural philosophers and the writers on optics (*perspectiva*).

[^10]: **S/T.** "Lincoln": Robert Grosseteste, bishop of Lincoln; his *Sermo* before Innocent IV at Lyon, 1250 (Brown, *Fasciculus rerum expetendarum* II, 1690, p. 251), quoted closely, *uvas preciosas* included
    checked (LEDGER E05b-3). Where his words end is our judgement. A margin *Lincolniensis* (99.16),
    *Ecclesia sancta catholica quantum sit artata* (99.17). *Mediante*: Loserth's expansion of the MSS
    *medi°* (99.18). *Vere*: Loserth; the MSS *vero* (99.22), "but of the part called Christian"; we
    translate the print. *Quia factus*: the MSS *qui factus* (Loserth's foot). Micah 7:1–2 (Loserth):
    Wyclif agrees with the Vulgate as far as *ad comedendum* (DR used); then *uvas preciosas* for the
    Vulgate's *praecoquas ficus* (DR "the first ripe figs"), and *sanctus de terra et sanctus in
    hominibus non est* for *Periit sanctus de terra, et rectus in hominibus non est* (DR "The holy man is
    perished out of the earth, and there is none upright among men"). B *Uvas preter quas desideravit*
    (99.31); B *in hominibus non erit* (100.1). *Septem criminalia peccata*: the seven deadly sins.

[^11]: **S/T.** Isa. 5:2: Wyclif *facit labruscas*, the Vulgate *fecit*; DR "it brought forth wild
    grapes". B *tanquam universalibus* for *uberibus* (100.5). *A Bernardo*: the place "above" is most likely
    Bernard, *Super Cant.* 12 (*meliora sunt ubera tua vino*), quoted in 03-g; the vine is not in it
    (LEDGER E05b-4). *Ficus
    fatua*: the barren fig tree (cf. Luke 13:6–9).

[^12]: **S/T/C.** *Iudicio* (100.10): the print (plate); the OCR had *iudic*. *Ut quid … terram occupant* echoes Luke 13:7, DR "Why cumbereth it the
    ground?"; we construe *non restat ut quid … nisi ut*: "no 'why' remains … except that". Matt. 13:30,
    DR "Gather up first the cockle, and bind it into bundles to burn", paraphrased; *ad laudem
    ecclesie* is Wyclif's.
