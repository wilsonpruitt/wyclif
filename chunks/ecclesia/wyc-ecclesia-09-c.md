# *De ecclesia*, chapter 9, part 3: the oldest privilege is poverty; ignorance does not excuse the endowers; the fifth objection

## ¶1 · 190.17–194.12

> [p. 190] Hic dicitur quod prima pars est falsa, sed secunda est mihi vera in casu; nam cum omne privilegium sit ad commodum privilegiati, patet quod multis dominis temporalibus et aliis assunt terrena privilegia ad exercicium virtutis. Hoc autem contingit communius laicis quam clericis, quia officium dispensandi temporalia et dominanter cohercendi subiectos est eis pertinencius, et clerici declinantes ad mundum communiter peccant gravius. Conceditur tamen quod ministracio temporalium, ne clerus dispergatur ad inhonestis mediis mendicandum, foret in casu clero in privilegium, sicut contigit III Regum XVII° quod Helias privilegialiter fuit pastus per corvum. Et breviter ministracio temporalium non est clero in privilegium, nisi de quanto promovet ad sequendum Christum in moribus et impetrandum privilegium [p. 191] amoris sui; quod contigit, cum Christus accepit elemosinas a laicis et sanctis feminis. Et secundum istam condicionem dotata fuit ecclesia non ad tardandum vel ad retrahendum eam a perfeccione primeva. Unde excommunicatissime peccant sive clerici sive laici qui hoc privilegium antiquum ecclesie scienter auferunt vel minuunt, et hinc XXV, q. II, sic loquitur Leo papa: Privilegia ecclesiarum et monasteriorum sanctorum patrum auctoritate instituta nulla possunt improbitate convelli, nulla novitate mutari. In quo opere auxiliante Christo fideliter exequendo necesse est sedis Romane pontifices perseverantem exhibere famulatum. Dispensacio enim nobis credita est et ad nostrum reatum tendit, si paternarum regule sanccionum nobis consencientibus vel negligentibus violentur. Talia sunt multa decreta: quod non licet antiquioribus privilegiis novis subsequentibus derogare. Cum ergo antiquissima et validissima privilegia, instituta a Christo, steterunt in paupertate altissima, sequitur quod non licet cuiquam privilegium illud subtrahere. Cum ergo privilegium illud stat in habendo cuncta exproprietarie in communi, sic quod sibi repugnat dominacio civilis et conversacio secularis, videtur quod hoc non licuit sine gravi periculo introducere in clerum. Unde XXVª q. I, canon sic loquitur: Que ad perpetuam generaliter sunt ordinata utilitatem, nulla commutacione varientur, nec ad privatum commodum trahantur que ad bonum sunt commune prefixa. Ex istis colligitur, cum nec dominus secularis debuit nec potuit dare clero in privilegium dominacionem [p. 192] secularem, stante illo ordine, nec licuit clero illam accipere, patet quod error, si ex ignorancia in illud processerit, debet utrimque celeriter emendari. Patet prima pars ex hoc quod non licet deteriorare proximum, auferendo ab eo privilegium a Deo datum, ad perficiendum in suis limitibus precipuis tantum statum, sed necessario foret deterioracio, quia deieccio status cleri ad laycos, habere dominacionem huiusmodi. Ideo non licuit seculari hoc facere. Unde tercio decretalium, Nec clerici vel monachi, capitulo Relatum, precipit papa quod monachi incorrigibiles vivendo seculariter alios corrumpentes eiciantur aliis religiose viventibus introductis, ubi tamquam per se manifestum supponitur quod secularis conversacio sit clericali inferior; nec dubium quin repugnat statui et perfeccioni cleri quod sic per affeccionem et occupacionem temporalium degradetur; ideo propter repugnanciam non potest fieri talis dotacio stante ordine. Non enim est privilegium peiorare. Et ex eodem patet quod non licet clero a perfeccione primitiva deficere, sicut non licet laico sponsam Christi sic inficere. Si enim non licet laico auferre suas elemosinas que sunt bona abiectissima, multo minus liceret auferre elemosinas a Deo institutas que sunt bona et privilegia Dei potissima. Quomodo ergo liceret seculari ordinacionem Dei inficiendo corrigere cui non licet iuxta falsigrafos suam culpam quam commisit ex ignorancia emendare? Numquid tam affectata ignorancia excusat dominum secularem: Si enim secularis cui minus repugnat mundialitas ex tali inclinacione ad seculum, non obstante quod putat in hoc prestare Deo obsequium, reddit eum [p. 193] dampnabilem, quanto magis necessitando personam cleri cui plus repugnat conversacio secularis? Revera si talis ignorancia omnino excusat, per idem excusaret quemlibet viciosum; nam, ut patet primo Ethicorum, sicut omnis homo appetit naturaliter esse felix, sic appetit media que credit ducere ad hunc finem, ut voluptuosus ponit felicitatem in cibariis delicatis, luxuriosus in actibus venereis, avarus in mundi diviciis, superbus in mundanis honoribus, et sic de aliis in quibus ignorans amplius delectatur. Sed quis diceret ignoranciam talem simpliciter excusare? Sic ergo posito quod Cesar ex ignorancia crediderit se prestare Deo obsequium in dando clero Christi mundi gloriam, sicut est accusabilis ex eleccione fatua et amore prepostero affectando talia sibi ipsi, sic est plus accusabilis retardando proditorie et cum mundano toxico precipuos milites exercitus christiani. Aliter enim foret elemosina privilegiare clerum eciam religiosum, ut sit voluptuosissime, delectabilissime et splendidissime cibatus, uxoratus et in omni mundi gloria pre omnibus secularibus predotatus. Sed quid privilegio tali insanius? Unde sola fabrica, cum super fallaciam equivocacionis privilegii decipit totum mundum, quia cum dominus temporalis potuit meritorie concedere hec privilegia, sicut clerus ea accipere, et potuit utrimque esse dampnabilis fastus et ignorancia: ideo neutram partem assero, nec quod succedentes abutuntur privilegio, sed veritatem condicionalem ut fidem christianam defendendo relinquens prepositis veritatem de inesse ex operibus iudicandam. Quinto arguitur per hoc quod iuxta sepe dicta paupertas evangelica et omnino spiritualia insignia que Christus concessit sue ecclesie stant cum cesarea [p. 194] dotacione, et ex hinc aliquod bonum ecclesie eveniet (ut patet in proximo argumento): ergo melius est quod ecclesia sic dotetur, et per consequens, Cesar dotavit ecclesiam supra Christum. Maior videtur ex hoc quod beatus Silvester et alii sancti episcopi et domini seculares non approbassent dotacionem huiusmodi, si fuisset ad deterioracionem ecclesie, sed curia Romana instetisset olim pro privilegiis antiquis ecclesie et propter facilitatem fuissent olim restituta, cum tam dotantes quam dotati statim annuerent expropriacioni, dato quod esset meritoria.

To this the answer is that the first part is false, but the second is, to my mind, true in a case; for since every [privilege]{g:privilegium} is for the benefit of the one privileged, it is clear that many [temporal lords]{g:temporales-domini} and others have earthly privileges for the exercise of virtue.[^1] And this happens more commonly to laymen than to clerics, because the office of dispensing [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} and of [coercing]{g:cohercere} subjects as a lord does belongs more fittingly to them, and clerics who decline to the world commonly sin more gravely. Yet it is granted that the ministering of temporal goods, lest the [clergy]{g:clerus} be scattered to [beg]{g:mendicare} by dishonest means, would in a case be to the clergy as a privilege, as happened, 1 Kings 17, when Elijah was fed by privilege through the raven.[^2] And in brief, the ministering of temporal goods is not to the clergy as a privilege except in so far as it promotes [it] to follow Christ in manners and to obtain the privilege [191] of his love; which happened when Christ accepted [alms]{g:elemosina} from laymen and holy women. And under this condition the [church]{g:ecclesia} was endowed: not so as to slow it or draw it back from its first perfection. Hence they sin most excommunicably, whether clerics or laymen, who knowingly take away or diminish this ancient privilege of the church; and hence, C. 25 q. 2 [c. 2], [Pope]{g:papa} Leo speaks thus: "The privileges of churches and monasteries, instituted by the authority of the holy fathers, can be torn up by no wickedness, changed by no novelty. In which work, to be faithfully carried out with Christ's help, the pontiffs of the Roman see must show persevering service. For the dispensation has been entrusted to us, and it tends to our guilt if the rules of the fathers' sanctions are violated with our consent or through our neglect."[^3] Many decrees are such: that it is not lawful to derogate from older privileges by newer ones following after. Since, then, the most ancient and most valid privileges, instituted by Christ, stood in the highest poverty, it follows that it is not lawful for anyone to withdraw that privilege. Since, then, that privilege stands in having all things in common, [without property]{g:exproprietarie}, so that civil [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio} and secular [manner of life]{g:conversacio} are repugnant to it, it seems that this could not lawfully be introduced into the clergy without grave peril. Hence, C. 25 q. 1 [c. 3], the canon speaks thus: "Things that have been ordained generally for perpetual utility are to be varied by no [exchange]{g:commutacio}, nor are things fixed for the common good to be drawn to private advantage."[^4] From these it is gathered: since a secular lord neither ought nor was able to give the clergy, as a privilege, secular [192] exercise of lordship while that order stands, nor was it lawful for the clergy to accept it, it is clear that the error, if it proceeded into this from ignorance, ought to be quickly amended on both sides.[^5] The first part is clear from this, that it is not lawful to make one's neighbour worse by taking from him a privilege given by God for perfecting so great a state within its chief [limits]{g:limites}; now there would necessarily be a making worse, because to have an exercise of lordship of this kind is a casting down of the state of the clergy to the laity. Therefore it was not lawful for a [secular]{g:secularis} to do this.[^6] Hence in the third [book] of the Decretals, Ne clerici vel monachi, chapter Relatum, the pope commands that incorrigible monks, living secularly and corrupting others, be expelled, others who live religiously being brought in; where it is supposed as self-evident that the secular manner of life is inferior to the clerical. Nor is there doubt that it is repugnant to the state and perfection of the clergy that it be thus degraded through affection for and occupation with temporal goods; therefore, because of the repugnance, such an [endowment]{g:dotacio} cannot be made while the order stands. For it is not a privilege to make worse.[^7] And from the same it is clear that it is not lawful for the clergy to fall away from the primitive perfection, as it is not lawful for a layman so to infect the [bride]{g:sponsa} of Christ. For if it is not lawful for a layman to take away his alms, which are the most abject goods, much less would it be lawful to take away the alms instituted by God, which are the chief goods and privileges of God. How then would it be lawful for a secular to correct the [ordinance]{g:ordinacio} of God by infecting it, when, according to the [false writers]{g:falsigraphus}, it is not lawful for him to amend his own fault which he committed in ignorance?[^8] Does so affected an ignorance excuse the secular lord? For if, in a secular, to whom [mundanity]{g:mundialitas} is less repugnant, [the ignorance that comes] from such an inclination to the world, notwithstanding that he thinks he does a service to God in this, renders him [193] damnable, how much more, when he constrains the person of the cleric, to whom the secular manner of life is more repugnant?[^9] Truly, if such ignorance excuses altogether, by the same token it would excuse any vicious man; for, as is clear in the first [book] of the Ethics, as every man naturally desires to be happy, so he desires the means which he believes lead to that end: as the voluptuary places happiness in delicate foods, the lecher in venereal acts, the avaricious in the riches of the world, the proud in worldly honours, and so of the others in which the ignorant man takes the more delight. But who would say that such ignorance excuses [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}?[^10] So then, supposing that Caesar out of ignorance believed that he was doing a service to God in giving the clergy of Christ the glory of the world: as he is accusable for a foolish choice and a preposterous love, in [desiring]{g:affectare} such things for himself, so he is more accusable for treacherously holding back, and with worldly poison, the chief [soldiers]{g:milites} of the Christian army.[^11] For otherwise it would be alms to privilege the clergy, even the religious, so that it be fed most voluptuously, delightfully and splendidly, [married]{g:uxoratus}, and endowed beforehand with all the glory of the world above all seculars. But what is more insane than such a privilege? Hence [it is] mere contrivance, since it deceives the whole world on the [fallacy]{g:fallacia} of equivocation in "privilege". For since a temporal lord could meritoriously grant these privileges, as the clergy [could] accept them, and pride and ignorance could be damnable on both sides, therefore I assert neither part, nor that the successors abuse the privilege; but [I assert] the conditional truth, as in defending the Christian faith, leaving to the [superiors]{g:prepositi} the truth [as a matter of fact]{g:de-inesse}, to be judged from works.[^12] Fifthly, [it is argued by appeal to]{g:arguitur-per} this: that, according to what has often been said, evangelical poverty and the wholly spiritual insignia which Christ granted to his church stand with the caesarean [194] endowment, and from this some good will come to the church (as is clear in the last argument); therefore it is better that the church be so endowed, and consequently Caesar endowed the church above Christ.[^13] The major seems [clear] from this, that blessed Silvester and other holy [bishops]{g:episcopus} and secular lords would not have approved an endowment of this kind if it had been to the worsening of the church; but [then] the [Roman curia]{g:curia-romana} would long ago have pressed for the ancient privileges of the church, and because of the ease [of it] they would long ago have been restored, since both the endowers and the endowed would at once have assented to the [renunciation of property]{g:expropriacio}, granted that it were meritorious.[^14]

## ¶2 · 194.13–194.25

> [p. 194] Hic dicitur negando primam consequenciam, nisi forte assumptum sit impossibile. Nam videtur multis quod laureola martirii qua milites Christi plus strenui decertarunt in floribus ecclesie, convertendo tam expedite tantum populum, non stetissent simul subiecto et tempore cum dotacione cesarea, nec tam abiecta paupertas, calcato mundo, submisso clero Christi consilio, voluntarie paciendo, nec tanta aspiracio ad bona patrie contempta confidencia in bonis fortune vel bonis nature, et per consequens non fuisset tanta status securitas sequendo Christum per compendium arte vite que ducit ad celum. Nam omnia ista quatuor cum suis correlativis videntur esse dotacioni terrene contraria.

To this the answer is by denying the first [inference]{g:consequencia}, unless perhaps the [assumption]{g:assumptum} is impossible.[^15] For it seems to many that the [aureole]{g:laureola} of martyrdom, with which the more vigorous soldiers of Christ strove in the flowering of the church, converting so great a people so readily, would not have stood together in subject and in time with caesarean endowment; nor so abject a poverty, the world trodden down, the clergy submitted to Christ's counsel, suffering voluntarily; nor so great an aspiration to the goods of the [homeland]{g:patria}, confidence in goods of fortune or goods of nature being despised; and consequently there would not have been so great a security of state in following Christ by the short way of the strait life that leads to heaven. For all these four, with their correlatives, seem to be contrary to earthly endowment.[^16]

## Notes

[^1]: **T/C.** A margins *Ablacio temporalium quomodo est limitanda* (190.18) and *Responsio* (190.21). "The first part": that there is no bodily privilege; "the second": that seculars are bound to take the goods away (190.14–16). *Assunt*: belong to, are present to.

[^2]: **S/C.** 1 Kings 17:6 (Vulgate 3 Kings): DR "the ravens brought him bread and flesh"; Wyclif has the singular *per corvum*. *Ad inhonestis mediis mendicandum*: to beg by dishonest means. *Dominanter*: as a lord, by lordly power. Side-note 190.27.

[^3]: **S/T.** Decretum C. 25 q. 2 c. 2, Leo to Marcian, *Ep.* 52 (Friedberg I, 1011–12; the modern form is ours; LEDGER E09c-1). A reads *sanctorum paternum* (191.9). *Excommunicatissime*: "most excommunicably", Wyclif's superlative: in the way that most incurs excommunication. Side-note 191.5.

[^4]: **S/T.** A reads *cunta*, A₁ corrected to *cuncta* (191.22). Decretum C. 25 q. 1 c. 3, Leo to Anatolius, *Ep.* 51 (Friedberg I, 1007–08; the modern form is ours; LEDGER E09c-2). A margin *Non licuit clero recipere dominacionem nec licuit dominis* (191.30). Side-note 191.17.

[^5]: **C.** The period's main verb is *patet*; the *cum* clause holds both premisses (the lord could not give, the clergy could not accept). *Stante illo ordine*: while the order Christ set for his clergy stands. A margin *Conclusio* (192.2). Side-note 192.1.

[^6]: **T/C.** B reads *in suis militibus* (192.6) for *limitibus*; the print is translated. *Deieccio status cleri ad laycos*: the degrading of the clergy to the lay state. *Sed*, bringing the minor premiss, is rendered "now".

[^7]: **S.** Decretals of Gregory IX, X 3.50.7 *Relatum*, under the title *Ne clerici vel monachi* (Alexander III to the bishop of London; Friedberg II, 659–60; the modern form is ours; Loserth: "a few words altered"; LEDGER E09c-3); Wyclif's "inferior" state is his inference. Side-note 192.20.

[^8]: **C.** *Suas elemosinas*: alms he has once given. *Inficiendo corrigere*: to "correct" by infecting. *Falsigrafos*: "the false writers", Wyclif's opponents, unnamed; on their view not even ignorance allows the secular to undo the endowment.

[^9]: **C/S.** The *si enim* sentence is an anacoluthon: *secularis* hangs in the nominative and the subject of *reddit* is understood (the ignorance, or the inclination); we supply it in brackets. "Thinks he does a service to God": cf. John 16:2. *Mundialitas*: rendered "mundanity", to keep it apart from *secularitas* (GLOSSARY "worldliness"). *Necessitando*: constraining the cleric by the endowment. Side-note 193.1.

[^10]: **S.** Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics* I (Loserth: "Cap. II, III"; LEDGER E09c-4). *Ignorans*: the man ignorant of the true end.

[^11]: **T/G.** Loserth corrects the codices' *mundana toxica* to *mundano toxico* (193.16–17); his correction is translated. *Milites*: soldiers, the Pauline figure (GLOSSARY *milites Christi*), not the knightly estate. *Retardando*: holding back from their warfare.

[^12]: **C/T.** *Sola fabrica*: the sentence has no main verb; we construe "[it is] mere contrivance", the case for endowment as a privilege resting on the equivocation in "privilege". The reading is doubtful. A margin *Protestacio: hoc verbum extinctum est* (193.22): Wyclif's protestation follows (*neutram partem assero*). *Uxoratus*: provided with a wife. *Veritatem condicionalem*: the conditional truth (if endowment harms the church, it is unlawful), as against the truth *de inesse*, whether particular endowers and endowed in fact sinned.

[^13]: **C/T.** A margin *Quinta obieccio* (193.31). *Proximo argumento*: we take it as the last (fourth) argument and its answer, which granted that the ministering of temporal goods can be a privilege; *proximo* could also mean the next. *Supra Christum*: more than Christ did. Side-notes 193.31, 194.2.

[^14]: **C.** *Maior*: the major premiss. *Sed curia Romana instetisset*: the counterfactual: had endowment harmed the church, the curia would have pressed for the old privileges. *Expropriacioni*: the giving up of property. *Dato quod esset meritoria*: granting that it would have been meritorious.

[^15]: **T/C.** A margin *Responsio* (194.12). "The first inference": from "poverty stands with endowment and some good comes of it" to "it is better that the church be so endowed". *Nisi forte assumptum sit impossibile*: Wyclif denies the inference, while hinting that the assumption itself (that the two stand together) is impossible. Side-note 194.13.

[^16]: **T/S/C.** A margin *Nota quattuor* (194.14). A₁ reads *calcata* (194.18). Loserth expands the codices' *correl'* (194.24); his expansion is translated. *Laureola*: the aureole, the martyr's special crown (GLOSSARY). *Subiecto et tempore*: in the same subject and at the same time. *Arte vite que ducit ad celum*: cf. Matthew 7:14, "strait is the way that leadeth to life"; Wyclif speaks of the strait life, and of heaven. A margin *Conclusio* (194.33) falls outside this chunk.
