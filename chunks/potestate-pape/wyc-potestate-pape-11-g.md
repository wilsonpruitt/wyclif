# *De potestate pape*, chapter 11, part 7: the priest's six vestments, and signs become false

## ¶1 · 286.30–288.6

> [p. 286] Notandum autem quod sacerdotibus inferioris ordinis non datur tunica iacinctinam nec superhumerale nec racionale nec lamina aurea, sed soli summo pontifici, sicut in nostris sacerdotibus [p. 287] est ordo vestium. Sex enim ornamenta habent simplices sacerdotes in missa; scilicet amictam, albam, zonam, manipulam, stolam et casulam. Per amictam notatur custodia sensuum sacerdotis, quia illud ornamentum tegit caput et collum, in quo sunt omnes sensus; sic clericus debet velare omnes sensus et specialiter tempore misse, ne adulterinis sensacionibus temptet mentem, nec collum, in quo est organum vocis, ministret vocem ab interioribus ad vicium lingue. Sed iuxta Petri monita: Si quis loquitur, quasi sermones Dei prima Petr. IV, 11. Verba enim sacerdotis debent esse evangelium. Alba camisia, que est stricta linea in veteri testamento signat omnia membra corporis sacerdotis divino servicio mancipanda. Sed tempore ecclesie Judeorum, qui spiritu servitutis erant astricti, erat hec tunica striccior, quam est modo, quando dilatamur libertate qua Christus nos liberavit. Sed quia surrepit ommissionis negligencia, cum inordinatis operibus quantumcumque divino cultui mancipemur, ideo quarto ponitur in sinistra manu mapula vel manipula, denotans quod crebro debemus ab oculis mentis sordes abstergere, et contra facta sinistre perpetrata ad opera penalia gratis obligacione perpetua nos parare. Unde quinto assumit stolam sive orarium, que collo sacerdotis imposita, signat, quod iuxta consilium Christi Matth. XI 29 sustulit super se iugum Domini longenimitatis sufferendum. Quod autem stola cancellata per anteriora descendit, signat quod sacerdos sustulit crucem suam sequendo Christum, per [p. 288] arma iusticie a dextris et a sinistris, ut loquitur Christus Luce IV et Apostolus II Cor. VI et debet stola huius perseverancie que sola coronabitur usque ad pedes affeccionum se extendere. Connexio vero cum zona signat concathenacionem perpetue castitatis cum ceteris virtutibus sacerdotis.

And it is to be noted that to [priests]{g:sacerdos} of the lower order are given neither the violet tunic nor the superhumeral nor the rational nor the golden plate, but to the high priest alone,[^1] as among our priests [287] there is an order of vestments. For simple priests have six ornaments at mass, namely the amice, the alb, the girdle, the maniple, the stole and the chasuble. By the amice is signified the custody of the priest's senses, because that ornament covers the head and neck, in which are all the senses; so the cleric ought to veil all his senses, and especially at the time of mass, lest he tempt his mind with adulterous sensations, and lest the neck, in which is the organ of the voice, minister the voice from within to the vice of the tongue. But according to Peter's [admonitions]{g:monita}: "If any man speak, let him speak, as the words of God", 1 Peter 4:11. For the words of a priest ought to be gospel. The alb, a shirt, which in the old testament is the close linen [tunic], signifies that all the [members]{g:membrum} of the priest's body are to be given over to divine [service]{g:servicium}.[^2] But in the time of the [church]{g:ecclesia} of the Jews, who were bound by the spirit of bondage, this tunic was narrower than it is now, when we are enlarged by the freedom wherewith Christ has made us free.[^3] But since negligence of omission creeps in, with disordered works, however much we are given over to the [worship of God]{g:cultus-dei}, therefore, fourth, the mappula or maniple is put on the left hand, denoting that we must often wipe filth from the eyes of the mind, and, against deeds done amiss, prepare ourselves freely, by perpetual obligation, for works of hardship. Hence, fifth, he takes the stole or orarium, which, placed on the priest's neck, signifies that according to Christ's counsel, Matthew 11:29, he has taken upon himself the [yoke]{g:iugum} of the Lord, to be borne with long-suffering.[^4] And that the stole descends crossed over the front signifies that the priest has taken up his cross, following Christ, through [288] "the armour of justice on the right hand and on the left", as Christ speaks in Luke 4 and the Apostle in 2 Corinthians 6;[^5] and the stole of this perseverance, which alone will be crowned, must extend down to the feet of the affections. And its joining with the girdle signifies the chaining together of perpetual chastity with the other virtues of the priest.

## ¶2 · 288.8–289.6

> [p. 288] Sed sexto induit casulam vel planetam ad denotandum quod eminenciori via incedit presbiter christianus, dum habet caritatem ad singulos. Unde ad denotandum perfeccionem rotunditatis et evacuacionem sordium angulorum declinat hec casula a quadratura superhumeralis in veteri testamento ad rotunditatem et simplicitatem. Non enim est lex Christi tam aspera in angulis ut lex vetus, cum nec apostoli nec patres eorum ipsam portare poterant, ut dicit Petrus Act. XV, 10 sed quibusdam circulis et catenulis fuerant a lateribus colligata. Nos autem sub tali concathenacione portamus suavem et levem casulam a dextris et sinistris. Caritas enim operatur magis, si est; et licet careat multitudine figurarum superhumeralis veteris testamenti propter unitatem simplicem capitis nostri Christi, debet tamen sacerdos esse planeta in populo, ut videant opera eius bona. Sic enim docet Christus Matth. V, quod luceat lux vestra coram hominibus. Et est aliud ornamentum in novo testamento, competens diacono vel levite, quod vocatur dalmatica, cuius nomen est sumptum a regione, ubi primo creditur institutum. Forma vero creditur capta a collobiis, quibus usi fuerunt apostoli. Quidam enim apostolorum successores adiectis [p. 289] manicis usi fuerunt huiusmodi indumentis; et eundem sensum misticum, quem habet casula in presbiteris, habet dalmatica in levitis; ipsi enim paracius et asperius debent caritative proficere tamquam alter Laurencius quam presbiteri etatis et corporis gravioris.

But, sixth, he puts on the chasuble or planeta, to denote that the Christian [presbyter]{g:presbiter} walks a more eminent way, while he has [charity]{g:caritas} toward each one. Hence, to denote the perfection of roundness and the emptying out of the filth of corners, this chasuble departs from the squareness of the superhumeral in the old testament to roundness and simplicity. For the law of Christ is not so rough in its corners as the old law, since neither the apostles nor their fathers could bear that, as Peter says in Acts 15:10; but they[^6] had been bound together at the sides with certain rings and little chains. But we, under such a chaining together, carry a sweet and light chasuble on the right and on the left. For charity works more, if it is;[^7] and although it lacks the multitude of figures of the superhumeral of the old testament, because of the simple unity of our head, Christ, yet the priest ought to be a planet among the people, that they may see his good works.[^8] For so Christ teaches in Matthew 5, that "your light shine before men". And there is another ornament in the new testament, proper to a deacon or levite, which is called the dalmatic, whose name is taken from the region where it is believed first to have been instituted. And its form is believed to have been taken from the colobia which the apostles used. For certain successors of the apostles, adding [289] sleeves, used garments of this kind; and the dalmatic has in levites the same mystical sense that the chasuble has in presbyters; for they ought to advance in charity more readily and more roughly, like another Lawrence, than presbyters of graver age and body.[^9]

## ¶3 · 289.7–290.19

> [p. 289] Quotlibet sunt talia signa sanctitatis presbiteri et peroracionis virtutum in anima; et utinam hodie non sint falsa. Interdicitur enim sacerdotibus tam rubor vestium quam viredo cum aliis novitatibus mundanis, ad denotandum quod crudelitas lasciva et secularitas sunt in clericis suspendenda. Latitudo vero corone signat per abrasionem temporalium denudacionem intellectus et affectus ad bona que sursum sunt. Abrasio vero pilorum in circulo sensuum specialiter in religiosis signat subtraccionem temporalium, per que sensus secularium suffocantur; et corona iusticie expectatur in eis, cum ad imitacionem Apostoli castigant corpus suum et in servitutem redigunt stricte contenti presentibus. Et sic videtur michi quod omne genus viancium a Christo per peccatum adulterans est saccus plenus mendacio, sic quod ad omnem punctum nostri est mendacium cum peccato, sic quod deserentibus Christus et necessariora bona mundo abscondita potest verificari dictum Christi Matth. XII, 39 Generacio prava et adultera signum querit. Cum enim sensibilibus et mundanis afficimur, de illis loquimur, illa prosequimur et in illis affectu delectato postpositis spiritualibus quietamur. Unde signum huius est quod nos cleri, dimisso signato, [p. 290] tamquam layci legentes literas quarum sensum nescimus, nimis appreciamur cerimonias et sensibilia humanitus instituta ut plus ponderamus quod alba sacerdotis anglici habeat in sinistris humeris biffurcatam varietatem, superinfictam secundum formam humanitus adinventam, quam appreciamur primum mandatum decalogi. Illa enim signat divisionem Anglorum a fide post Britones usque ad tempus Gregorii, quo per eius discipulos sunt conversi. Et revera iste ritus debet generaliter in omnibus christianis trahi in communem: alie naciones usque ad Romanum pontificem debent instar ribaldi vestimenta christianitatis gerere cum labello, ut si iste papa haberet tot commissuras in vestibus, quociens aliquis papa a fide ceciderit ipse forte gereret tunicam pictaciis consutam et non tunicam inconsutilem Ihesu nostri. Et sic patet falsitas in signis episcopalibus introductis.

Such signs of the presbyter's holiness and of the peroration of virtues in the soul are as many as you please;[^10] and would that today they were not false. For redness of garments, and greenness, are forbidden to priests along with other worldly novelties, to denote that wanton cruelty[^11] and [worldliness]{g:secularitas} are to be [suspended]{g:suspendere} in clerics. And the breadth of the crown signifies, by the shaving away of temporal things, the laying bare of intellect and affection toward the goods that are above. And the shaving of the hair in the circle of the senses, especially in [the religious]{g:religiosi}, signifies the withdrawal of temporal things, by which the senses of seculars are stifled; and the crown of justice is awaited in them, since in imitation of the Apostle they chastise their body and bring it into subjection, strictly content with present things.[^12] And so it seems to me that every kind of wayfarer that commits adultery away from Christ through sin is a sack full of lies,[^13] so that at every point of ours there is a lie along with sin; so that of those who desert Christ and the more necessary goods hidden from the world[^14] Christ's saying in Matthew 12:39 can be verified: "A wicked and adulterous generation seeketh a sign." For when we are affected by [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} and worldly things, we speak of them, pursue them, and rest in them with delighted affection, spiritual things set aside. Hence a sign of this is that we of the [clergy]{g:clerus}, having let go the thing signified, [290] like laymen reading letters whose sense we do not know, prize too highly ceremonies and perceptible things instituted by men, so that we weigh it more that the alb of an English priest should have on its left shoulders a forked patch of another colour, sewn on above after a form devised by men,[^15] than we prize the first commandment of the decalogue. For that signifies the division of the English from the faith after the Britons, until the time of Gregory, when they were converted by his disciples. And truly this [rite]{g:ritus} ought to be drawn generally into common [use] among all Christians:[^16] the other nations, up to the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex}, ought like a ribald to wear the vestments of Christianity with a patch, so that if this [pope]{g:papa} had as many seams in his vestments as there are times that some pope has fallen from the faith, he would perhaps wear a tunic sewn of patches, and not the seamless tunic of our Jesus. And so the falsity in the episcopal signs that have been introduced is clear.

## ¶4 · 290.20–290.27

> [p. 290] Unde baculus signat pastoris officium ad minandum et conprehendendum oves morbidas, ut sanentur, sed postquam corona baculi versa est in aurum preciosum et frigidum formasset in figuram artificiosam adinvencionis humane, dedignatur pastor cesareus refrigescente caritate curam apostolicam ovibus Christi impendere, sed in subtilitate secularis officii sigillatur.

Hence the staff signifies the shepherd's office of driving and catching the diseased sheep, that they may be healed; but after the crown of the staff was turned into precious gold and, cold, had been formed into an artful figure of human invention,[^17] the caesarean shepherd, his charity growing cold, disdains to spend apostolic care on the sheep of Christ, but is sealed up in the subtlety of secular office.[^18]

## Notes

[^1]: **G.** *Summus pontifex*, here the high priest of the old law ("high priest", as GLOSSARY *summi pontifices* for the Jewish priesthood); it is the phrase Wyclif uses of the pope ("highest pontiff"), and the parallel is his point. Loserth refers to Hugh of St Victor, *De sacramentis* II, p. 4, c. 9 ff., for the six vestments that follow (LEDGER P11g-1).
[^2]: **R.** The girdle, third in the list, receives no exposition: after the alb the text passes to the maniple as "fourth". A's margin at 287.11: *Estimo quod scriptor omisit misticacionem de zona*, "I think the scribe left out the mystical reading of the girdle."
[^3]: **S.** Rom. 8:15 ("the spirit of bondage", DR) and Gal. 4:31 ("by the freedom wherewith Christ has made us free", DR).
[^4]: **C.** *Longenimitatis* (B *longanimitas*) is taken as a genitive of manner with *sufferendum*; it could also go with *Domini*, "the yoke of the Lord of long-suffering". Matt. 11:29: "Take up my yoke upon you" (DR).
[^5]: **S.** 2 Cor. 6:7 (DR). The taking up of the cross is Luke 9:23, not Luke 4; the print's "IV" may be a misreading of IX. At 288.3 the manuscripts read *debent*, which Loserth corrects to *debet*.
[^6]: **C.** *Fuerant … colligata* has no expressed subject: the parts of the old vestments, which Exodus fastens with rings and little chains (Exod. 28:13–14, 22–28).
[^7]: **S.** Gregory, *Homiliae in Evangelia* 30.2: *Operatur enim magna, si est* ("for it works great things, if it is"), said of the love of God (to be checked, LEDGER P11g-2). The print's *magis* is translated; *magna* may be the reading.
[^8]: **C.** *Planeta* is both the chasuble (288.8) and a planet, a light in the sky. Loserth refers here to John 1:5 (LEDGER P11g-3); the words Wyclif gives are Matt. 5:16, "So let your light shine before men, that they may see your good works" (DR).
[^9]: **C.** *Asperius* answers the "rough" corners of the old law above: the deacon's charity is to be more strenuous. Lawrence, the Roman deacon and martyr.
[^10]: **T.** *Quotlibet* is Loserth's correction (codd. *Quolibet*). For *peroracionis* he suggests *preparacionis*; *perornacionis*, "adornment", would also suit. The print is translated.
[^11]: **C.** Perhaps *crudelitas, lascivia*, "cruelty, wantonness", one for each colour; the print's *crudelitas lasciva* is translated.
[^12]: **S.** Col. 3:1–2 ("the things that are above"), 2 Tim. 4:8 ("a crown of justice"), 1 Cor. 9:27 ("I chastise my body and bring it into subjection", DR). The margin of both manuscripts at 289.22: *Saccus plenus mendacio*.
[^13]: **C.** *Viancium*, from *viare*, are wayfarers (*viatores*), those still on the way. *Adulterans … a Christo* is spiritual adultery, answering "adulterous generation" in the text that follows.
[^14]: **C.** The print's *Christus* is nominative and does not construe with *deserentibus*; it is translated as *Christum*. Matt. 12:39 in Wyclif's form (*prava*); the Vulgate has *mala*, DR "An evil and adulterous generation seeketh a sign". The manuscripts' margin at 289.28: *Signorum cultus est generacio prava*, "the worship of signs is a wicked generation".
[^15]: **C.** *Varietas* is a piece of a different colour, the apparel set on the alb; *superinfictam* is not attested and is taken as "fixed on above" (*superinfixam*?).
[^16]: **T.** *Communem* is Loserth's correction of the manuscripts' *comunam*. The proposal is ironic: if each nation wore its own lapse on its vestments, the pope would wear nothing but patches.
[^17]: **C.** *Formasset* has no subject or object that construes; it is translated as if passive (*formatum esset*). The gold is "cold": charity grows cold beside it, as in Matt. 24:12, "the charity of many shall grow cold" (DR).
[^18]: **C.** *Pastor cesareus*, "caesarean shepherd", belongs with *papa cesareus*. *Sigillatur*, "is sealed up", is obscure: shut up in secular business as under a seal.
