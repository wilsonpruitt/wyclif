# *Dialogus*, chapter 31: who should disendow whom: the apish likeness

## Falsehood · 69.20–70.13

> [p. 69] MENDACIUM. Sed adhuc arguitur, si clerus sic debet deserere sua dominia, tunc ipso renuente gratis ipsa deserere debet cogi. Sed hoc non potest contingere per brachium seculare cum clerus sit potencior et ista temptacio nimis ecclesiam perturbaret. Nec hoc potest contingere per brachium spirituale, cum papa, potentissimus secularis dominus, ad oppositum est juratus, et sui subditi in isto gratis sibi resisterent. Et confirmatur ex hoc quod papa ex universalitate [p. 70] sui dominii confert regna, sic enim fertur regi anglie dedisse hiberniam et degradato uno rege hispanie alteri regi dedisse hispaniam. Quis ergo deponeret reges inhabiles et reges habiles induceret nisi papa, cum sit summus Christi vicarius et pertinet summo terreno officio hoc temptare? Nec est faciliter fingenda racio quare licet mundanis principibus auferre mundana dominia sic a clero habitualiter delinquente, quin per loco a majori licet spirituali brachio auferre secularia dominia a temporali brachio habitualiter delinquente. Et tamen hoc contigit facilius et patet major autoritas. Videtur quod docere debet pocius e converso quod clerici auferant totum seculare dominium a brachio seculari et reservent ex integro ipsum sibi.

FALSEHOOD. But it is still argued: if [the clergy]{g:clerus} ought thus to relinquish its [lordships]{g:dominium}, then, if it refuses to relinquish them freely, it ought to be compelled. But this cannot come about through the [secular arm]{g:brachium-seculare}, since the clergy is the more powerful, and this attempt would disturb the [church]{g:ecclesia} too much. Nor can it come about through the spiritual arm, since the [pope]{g:papa}, the most powerful secular [lord]{g:dominus}, is sworn to the opposite, and his subjects would freely resist him in this.[^1] And it is confirmed from this, that the pope, from the universality [70] of his lordship, confers [kingdoms]{g:regnum}: for thus he is said to have given Ireland to the king of England, and, one king of Spain having been degraded, to have given Spain to another king.[^2] Who, then, would depose [unqualified]{g:inhabilis} kings and bring in qualified kings except the pope, since he is the highest vicar of Christ and it pertains to the highest earthly office to attempt this? Nor can a reason easily be devised why it is lawful for worldly princes to take worldly lordships away from the clergy thus habitually offending, without its being lawful, by the topic from the greater, for the spiritual arm to take secular lordships away from the temporal arm habitually offending. And yet this happened more easily, and the greater authority is clear.[^3] It seems that one ought rather to teach the converse: that the [clerics]{g:clericus} should take away all secular lordship from the secular arm and reserve it wholly to themselves.

## Truth · 70.14–73.25

> [p. 70] VERITAS. Ista argucia non teneret nisi per locum a simiali similitudine et autoritate diaboli, ac si diabolus vellet destruere totam autoritatem ordinis Christiani, extollendo papam super omnes principes Christianos. Concedo ergo, cum cederet ad radicalem pacificacionem ecclesie, quod omnes tres ejus partes debent ad istam ordinacionem domini cum prudencia laborare: clerus monendo per evidencias fidei scripture ne cadat in heresim ex consensu; seculare brachium mortificacionem de cetero precavendo, et cum mortificacio in manus suas venerit, quod sepe contingit regi anglie, repplicacionem peccati cleri contra dominum subtrahendo. Multe autem sunt particulares prudencie ex quibus ista prevaricacio tolli poterit paulative, ut dicendo fratribus et cunctis sacerdotibus regni anglie quod sub pena legalitatis sue dicant regi et regno si ista dotacio sit consona legi divine. Vel aliter quod sub pena equivalente dicant regi et regno, ex efficaci autoritate scripture vel racione, quid in natura sua sit ipsa hostia consecrata. Tales autem sunt multi casus necessarii ad noticiam fidelis regni in quibus pater mendacii antichristum et omnes suos clericos usque hodie excecavit. Quesito autem a clero nostro publice sub eadem pena si status paupertatis honeste quem Christus docuit in verbo et opere sit perfeccior et consonancior militanti ecclesie quam [p. 71] status cesarius hodie introductus, et necessitato clero ex fide precellenciam ordinacionis Christi preferre, et specialiter in diebus novissimis, mundo declinante ad avariciam et cessante clero a suo ministerio propter temporalium copiam, necesse haberet clerus sibi ipsi concludere et confiteri catholicam veritatem. Et sic cum moderata prudencia purgari posset regnum a multis erroribus sine cleri strepitu vel partis diaboli isti facinori adherentis. Sed quomodocunque sit de tali prudencia, satis est fidelibus dicere quod sic secundum legem domini debet esse. Et mirabile foret quod sic ex causa terrena regnum anglie auderet invadere multa regna et nesciret in sua patria in causa dei adquirere... specialiter cum Christus luce. 14° docet brachium seculare ut sibi ministret, compellendo clerum intrare in statum quem ipse instituit. Unde idem est diabolum docere antichristum suum discipulum per similitudinem istam arguere ac si sic argueret: licet seculari brachio, habenti a deo potestatem vel gladium, rebelles legi domini corporaliter cohercere; ergo per idem licet clericis pugnare corporaliter et seculares dominos potestative corripere. Sed ista insania movit papas, episcopos et sacerdotes plurimos insanire ac si sic argueret: Christus dedit seculari brachio istum gladium, ergo per idem dare debuit istum gladium prudenciori et digniori brachio sacerdotum. Et sic si Christus concessit secularibus dominis temporale dominium per idem concedere debuit illud dominium sacerdoti. Nec fundatur istud in fide scripture, nisi ut loquitur ironice Mᵗ. 4° ex temptacione diaboli qua in temptacione 3ª temptavit Christum sacerdotem summum, promittendo sibi presumptive et false quod omnia regni mundi sibi daret si cadens ipsum adoraverit; ergo papa capitalis suus vicarius debet sic generaliter dominari et regna terrena suis servitoribus juxta suum imperium impartiri. Sed fidelis servus Christi negaret talem arguciam, de ordinacione domini contentatus. Christus enim ordinavit seculare brachium per potestatem coactivam et cohersitivam esse deitatis vicarium, dando ei gladium corporalem, et ordinavit sacerdocium [p. 72] esse humanitatis Christi vicarium paciendo et ipsum in humilitate et tribulacionibus imitando, ut docet augustinus in loco multiplici. Sed diabolus presumit quod Christus male in hoc ordinavit, cum esse debuit e converso, et sic locus a simiali similitudine qua sic arguitur: si seculare brachium accipere debuit a clero suum dominium quod dominus sibi dedit, ergo per idem sacerdocium accipere debet a seculari brachio seculare dominium quod diabolus usurpavit. Sic, inquam, arguens est nimis patens sophista diaboli; et sic potencia cleri cesarii, ex cautela diaboli super seculare brachium invalescens, in penam peccati secularis brachii ita crevit. Ideo debet cum Christi prudencia temperari et sic cleri temporalis potencia debet cum dei sapiencia mitigari. Et sicut dicitur constantinum anglicum istam vesaniam diabolicam presumpsisse, ita posset deus faciliter regem anglie et suum concilium ad destruccionem ejus prudentem cum spirituali juvamine ordinasse, et dare eis concilium sancti spiritus ut per medium apcius hoc attemptent. Sed satis est nobis detegere diabolicas argucias in oppositum fabricatas. Et sic si papa sit juratus ad oppositum est juratus cum diabolo contra Christum, sicut accepcio medietatis imperii fuit ex cautela diaboli usurpata. Et si pape subditi, antichristi discipuli, in isto sibi resisterent, ubi in causa Christi ad oppositum promoverent, sunt cum Christi prudencia paulative ad veritatis tramitem inclinandi. Et deus ordinavit in isto tempore ad hoc media que diebus preteritis sunt mundo abscondita. Et quantum ad confirmacionem de facto papali dicunt quidam quod est maledicta usurpacio antichristi. Ideo sicut regnum Anglie detinuit prudenter nonagentas marcas in quibus illi inde annuatim se dicitur obligasse, sic cum prudencia poterit aliunde paci... amplius in nomine domini attemptare. Et sic quantum ad regnum hispanie... in isto presumpserit, non est aliud nisi quod diabolus ad tempus parva... ihm Christum. Christus ergo deponit reges inhabiles per institutum suum absconditum, ideo necesse habent reges culpam suam de defendendo [p. 73] legem dei justissimam suo domino confiteri, et quod papa vel antichristus sic seculariter attemptat preter Christi licenciam contra reges, ipsi debent prudenter tirannidem istam diaboli declinare et ordinacionem domini suscitare. Christus enim dedit humiliter tributum cesari et multipliciter ampliavit suum dominium, et non sibi vel suis apostolis aliquod tale dominium adquisivit. Et sic videtur papam non esse Christi vicarium sed vicarium antichristi, cum dimisso celesti officio terrenum indebite sic attemptatur. Et sic prelati cesarii obliviscendo hortacionis humilis et sancte predicacionis Christi et suorum apostolorum usurpant potestative tirannidem antichristi, et in talibus prelatis specialiter ordinatis conversus est retrorsum et in suis filiis qui ipsos secuntur, dimittendo doctrinam Christi et sequendo vestigia antichristi. Et patet ex dictis diversitatis racio in sequenti similitudine simiali; cum spirituale brachium sit vicarius humanitatis Christi ad pacienciam et humilitatem dispositus, et seculare brachium sit vicarius divinitatis Christi ad prudentem et mitem gladium vindicte rebellis populi inclinatus. Et sic nec hoc contigit facilius nec ad hoc patet Christi autoritas, cum docuit oppositum tam in opere quam in verbis. Nec dubito quin oportet antichristi clericos in brevi tempore a domino castigari. Tales sunt multe antichristi argucie, quos facile est fidelem solvere, sancto spiritu per dona sua septiformia edocente.

TRUTH. This [quibble]{g:argucia} would not hold except by the [topic from apish likeness]{g:locus-a-simili} and on the devil's authority,[^4] as if the devil wished to destroy the whole authority of the Christian order by exalting the pope above all Christian princes. I grant, therefore, since it would make for the radical pacification of the church, that all three of its parts ought to labour with prudence toward this [ordinance]{g:ordinacio} of the Lord: the clergy, by warning through the [evidences]{g:evidencia} of the faith of scripture, lest it fall into heresy by consent; the secular arm, by guarding henceforth against [amortization]{g:mortificacio}, and, when an amortization comes into its hands, which often happens to the king of England, by withholding the [renewal]{g:repplicacio} of the clergy's sin against the Lord.[^5] And there are many particular prudent measures by which this transgression could be removed gradually: as by telling the [friars]{g:frater} and all the [priests]{g:sacerdos} of the kingdom of England that, on pain of their [legal standing]{g:legalitas},[^6] they tell the king and the kingdom whether this [endowment]{g:dotacio} is consonant with divine law. Or otherwise, that on an equivalent penalty they tell the king and the kingdom, from the effective authority of scripture or from reason, what the consecrated host itself is in its nature. And such are many cases necessary to the knowledge of a faithful kingdom, in which the father of falsehood has blinded Antichrist and all his clerics to this day.[^7] And if our clergy were asked publicly, under the same penalty, whether the state of honest poverty which Christ taught in word and deed is more perfect and more consonant with the [church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans} than [71] the caesarean state brought in today, and the clergy were bound, [as faith teaches]{g:ex-fide}, to prefer the pre-eminence of Christ's ordinance, especially in the last days, with the world declining into avarice and the clergy ceasing from its ministry because of the abundance of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, the clergy would of necessity have to [infer]{g:concludere} for itself and confess the catholic truth. And so, with moderate prudence, the kingdom could be purged of many errors without uproar from the clergy or from the devil's party that adheres to this crime. But however it may be with such prudence, it is enough for the faithful to say that thus, according to the law of the Lord, it ought to be. And it would be a wonder that the kingdom of England should thus dare, in an earthly cause, to invade many kingdoms, and should not know how to gain in its own [homeland]{g:patria}, in God's cause . . .[^8] especially since Christ, in Luke 14, teaches the secular arm to serve him by compelling the clergy to enter the state which he himself instituted.[^9] Hence it is the same thing as the devil teaching Antichrist, his disciple, to argue by this likeness as if he argued thus: "It is lawful for the secular arm, which has power or the sword from God, to coerce bodily those who rebel against the law of the Lord; therefore by the same token it is lawful for clerics to fight bodily and to chastise secular lords by force of power."[^10] But this madness moved popes, [bishops]{g:episcopus} and very many priests to rave, as if one argued thus: "Christ gave this sword to the secular arm; therefore by the same token he ought to have given this sword to the more prudent and worthier arm of the priests. And so, if Christ granted [temporal lords]{g:temporales-domini}hip to secular lords, by the same token he ought to have granted that lordship to the priest." Nor is this founded in the faith of scripture, except, as Matthew 4 speaks ironically, on the devil's temptation, by which in the third temptation he tempted Christ the high priest, promising him presumptuously and falsely that he would give him all [the things] of the kingdom of the world if, falling down, he would adore him; "therefore the pope, his chief vicar, ought thus to [lord it]{g:dominari} universally and to impart earthly kingdoms to his servants at his command."[^11] But a faithful servant of Christ would deny such a [quibble]{g:argucie}, content with the Lord's ordinance. For Christ ordained the secular arm, through coactive and coercive power, to be the [vicar of the Godhead]{g:vicarius-deitatis}, giving it the bodily sword; and he ordained the priesthood [72] to be the [vicar of Christ's humanity]{g:vicarius-humanitatis-christi}, by suffering and by imitating him in humility and tribulations, as Augustine teaches in many a place.[^12] But the devil presumes that Christ ordained badly in this, since it ought to have been the converse; and so [comes] the topic from apish likeness, by which it is argued thus: "If the secular arm had to take from the clergy its own lordship, which the Lord gave it, then by the same token the priesthood ought to take from the secular arm the secular lordship which the devil usurped."[^13] One who argues so, I say, is an all too evident sophist of the devil; and so the power of the [caesarean clergy]{g:clerus-cesareus}, growing strong over the secular arm by the devil's [wile]{g:cautela}, has grown thus as a punishment for the sin of the secular arm. Therefore it ought to be tempered with Christ's prudence, and so the temporal power of the clergy ought to be mitigated with God's wisdom. And as the English Constantine is said to have presumed [to begin] this devilish madness, so God could easily have ordained the king of England, prudent with spiritual help, and his council, for its destruction, and give them the counsel of the Holy Spirit, that they may attempt this by a more fitting means.[^14] But it is enough for us to [expose]{g:detegere} the devilish quibbles fabricated to the contrary. And so, if the pope is sworn to the contrary, he is sworn with the devil against Christ, just as the acceptance of half the empire was usurped by the devil's wile.[^15] And if the pope's subjects, disciples of Antichrist, should resist him in this, where in Christ's cause they would promote the contrary, they are to be inclined gradually, with Christ's prudence, to the path of truth. And God has ordained at this time means to this end which in days past were hidden from the world. And as for the confirmation from the pope's deed, some say that it is an accursed usurpation of Antichrist. Therefore, as the kingdom of England has prudently withheld the nine hundred marks in which it is said to have bound itself to him yearly on that account, so with prudence it will be able elsewhere . . . to attempt more in the name of the Lord.[^16] And so, as for the kingdom of Spain . . . has presumed in this, it is nothing else than that the devil for a time small . . . Jesus Christ. Christ, therefore, deposes unqualified kings by his hidden institution; so kings must confess to their Lord their fault concerning the defending of [73] [God's law]{g:lex-dei}, most just;[^17] and, as for what the pope or Antichrist thus attempts in secular fashion against kings beyond Christ's licence, they ought prudently to turn aside from this tyranny of the devil and to raise up the Lord's ordinance. For Christ humbly gave tribute to Caesar and in many ways enlarged his lordship, and acquired no such lordship for himself or his apostles.[^18] And so it seems that the pope is not the vicar of Christ but the vicar of Antichrist, since, having abandoned the heavenly office, he thus unduly attempts the earthly one. And so caesarean [prelates]{g:prelatus}, forgetting the humble exhortation and holy preaching of Christ and his apostles, usurp by force of power the tyranny of Antichrist; and in such prelates, specially ordained, [it] is turned backward, and in their sons who follow them, leaving the [doctrine]{g:doctrina} of Christ and following the footsteps of Antichrist.[^19] And from what has been said the reason for the difference in the apish likeness that follows is clear: since the spiritual arm is the vicar of Christ's humanity, disposed to patience and humility, and the secular arm is the vicar of Christ's [divinity]{g:divinitas}, inclined to a prudent and mild sword of vengeance on a rebellious people. And so neither did this happen more easily, nor is Christ's authority for it clear, since he taught the opposite both in deed and in words. Nor do I doubt that the clerics of Antichrist must in a short time be chastised by the Lord. Such are many quibbles of Antichrist, which it is easy for the faithful man to resolve, the Holy Spirit teaching [him] through his sevenfold gifts.

## Pollard's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 69.20: *Mend.* objects:
- 69.22: The secular arm is not strong enough to force the clergy to give up their dominions, and the Pope is sworn to maintain them.
- 70.1: The Pope, too, has the power of conferring kingdoms (e.g. Ireland on Henry II. and Spain on Henry of Trastamara), which no one could better exercise.
- 70.8: If the secular arm may disendow the clergy, *a fortiori* the clergy may disendow the secular lords, and this they should do.
- 70.14: *Ver.* Answers.
- 70.17: A beginning might be made by the clergy preaching the true doctrine, and by the secular arm granting no new endowments, and not renewing old ones when they lapse.
- 70.26: Again, friars and priests might be bidden on their allegiance to declare whether endowment is agreeable to the divine law, or what is the substance in the Host.
- 71.1: If the clergy were forced to speak out, they could not deny Christ's law.
- 71.15: By Antichrist's argument, because the secular arm may use bodily force on rebels against the divine law, the clergy may use bodily force on the secular lords!
- 71.21: Or, because Christ gave the sword and temporal dominion to secular lords, they must be allowed to the clergy!
- 71.27: Or, because Satan lyingly offered Christ the kingdoms of the earth, the Pope has a right to rule them!
- 71.34: But Christ made the secular arm the Vicar of his Godhead,
- 72.1: the clergy of His manhood. In this the devil pretends He was wrong, and argues that if the secular arm must receive the dominion which Christ gave it from the clergy, so the clergy must receive from the secular arm the dominion which the devil usurped!
- 72.14: The wicked folly begun by the English Constantine, may in God's ordinance be overthrown by the King of England.
- 72.29: The precedent of detaining the Pope's tribute must be extended.
- 72.34: To depose kings belongs to Christ only.
- 73.4: Christ Himself gave tribute to Cæsar.
- 73.7: The Pope seems, therefore, to be not Christ's Vicar, but Antichrist's.
- 73.21: Christ must surely soon punish the clergy of Antichrist.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Gratis sibi resisterent*: "would freely resist him", taking *sibi* as the pope, should he attempt disendowment; *gratis* as at 69.21 (*renuente gratis ipsa deserere*), of free will. Truth takes up the clause at 72.22–25.

[^2]: **R.** Pollard's side-note (70.1) identifies Ireland granted to Henry II and Spain to Henry of Trastámara (LEDGER D31-1).

[^3]: **C/T.** *Per loco a majori* is printed so (for *per locum*): the topical argument from the greater, i.e. *a fortiori* (Pollard's side-note). *Contigit* may be the perfect ("happened") or medieval spelling of *contingit* ("happens"); Truth throws the sentence back at 73.20 in the same words.

[^4]: **C.** *Locus a simiali similitudine*: a pun on the *locus a simili*, the topic from likeness; *simialis* is "ape-like", a likeness that only apes the true one. It returns at 72.4 and 73.16.

[^5]: **C/G.** *Mortificacio*: amortization, a grant of land into mortmain (compare *manus mortua*). *Repplicacio* here is not the disputation "rejoinder" (GLOSSARY) but a re-grant: Pollard's side-note (70.17) reads "not renewing old ones when they lapse". Only two of the *tres partes* are named; the third estate is left unstated.

[^6]: **C.** *Sub pena legalitatis sue*: literally "on pain of their lawfulness", their standing as lawful men under the crown; Pollard's side-note has "on their allegiance".

[^7]: **R.** *Pater mendacii*: "the father of falsehood" keeps the name of Truth's opponent, *Mendacium*, audible (cf. John 8:44).

[^8]: **T/G.** The dots are Pollard's: a gap in the text, which survives only in the one manuscript (editor's footnote at 69.19, "Hoc capitulum in ceteris codicibus deest"). Similar gaps at 72.31, 72.33, 72.34. *Patria* is here the earthly homeland, England.

[^9]: **S.** Luke 14:23, "compel them to come in" (*compelle intrare*); Pollard's margin gives Luke xiv. 21.

[^10]: **C.** *Idem est diabolum docere … arguere ac si sic argueret*: taken as "it is the same thing as the devil teaching Antichrist to argue", i.e. the likeness is diabolical in origin. *Potestative*: "by force of power", as again at 73.11.

[^11]: **S/C.** Matt. 4:8–9. Wyclif's *omnia regni mundi* departs from the Vulgate's *omnia regna mundi* ("all the kingdoms of the world"); translated as printed. The closing inference is the opponents' ("therefore the pope …"). *Suus vicarius*: most naturally Christ's vicar; it can also be read as the devil's, which sharpens the irony. *Dominari* is pejorative here.

[^12]: **S.** The king as vicar of the Godhead and the priest as vicar of Christ's humanity, "as Augustine teaches in many a place": as in ch. 1 (n7), the "Augustine" is the *Quaestiones Veteris et Novi Testamenti* of Ambrosiaster, which circulated as Augustine's: *Dei enim imaginem habet rex, sicut et episcopus Christi* (q. 35, PL 35:2234); the king *habens imperium Dei quasi vicarius ejus* (q. 106, PL 35:2319; also q. 91, *Rex enim adoratur in terris quasi vicarius Dei*, PL 35:2284). Gratian took q. 106 into the *Decretum* under Augustine's name (C.33 q.5 c.13). Ambrosiaster pairs the king with God and the bishop with Christ; "vicar of Christ's humanity" is Wyclif's sharpening of it (LEDGER D31-2).

[^13]: **C.** *Accipere a clero*: "take from the clergy", i.e. take back the secular arm's own lordship; Pollard's side-note (72.1) renders "receive".

[^14]: **C/R.** *Constantinum anglicum*: Constantine the Great, held in medieval legend to be British-born (LEDGER D31-3). *Prudentem* agrees with *regem*, not *concilium*. *Concilium* is printed twice: first "council" (the king's), then "counsel" (of the Holy Spirit), the medieval spelling serving both.

[^15]: **R.** *Accepcio medietatis imperii*: the Donation of Constantine (LEDGER D31-4).

[^16]: **T/R.** *Nonagentas* is read as *nongentas*, "nine hundred"; *nonaginta* would be "ninety". King John's concession of 1213 promised 1,000 marks yearly (700 for England, 300 for Ireland), so Wyclif's figure is loose. Payment had lapsed since about 1333; when Urban V demanded the arrears in 1366, Parliament refused and repudiated the tribute (LEDGER D31-5). *Ihm* (72.34) = *Ihesum*.

[^17]: **C.** *Culpam suam de defendendo legem dei*: their fault concerning the defence of God's law, i.e. their failure to defend it.

[^18]: **S/C.** Matt. 17:26 (the stater) and 22:21. *Suum dominium* is taken as Caesar's lordship, which Christ's tribute enlarged.

[^19]: **S/C.** *Conversus est retrorsum* has no expressed subject; it echoes Ps. 113:3 (Vulg.), *Jordanis conversus est retrorsum*. *Specialiter ordinatis* may play on ordination.
