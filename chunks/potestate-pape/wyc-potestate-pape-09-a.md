# *De potestate pape*, chapter 9, part 1: human election does not make a pope

## ¶1 · 194.8–195.6

> [p. 194] Cum autem necesse est papam continue in ecclesia esse militante, nec est papa vians nobiscum nisi limites Petri servaverit, necessarium videtur de limitibus istis et potestate pape cognoscere. Arguunt autem multi per raciones naturales et alii testimoniis scriptis quod continue oportet aliquem esse papam; quod est concedendum, nam necesse est Christum, qui solus est caput universalis ecclesie esse usque ad finem seculi cum ecclesia militante, ut patet Matth. ultimo; et preter hec necesse est esse confuse aliquem predestinatum viantem, quem Deus propter excessum edificacionis ecclesie sue plus approbat; quem oportet ex eleccione Domini esse papam; et illam eleccionem non potest frustrare humana eleccio. Et sic intelligo decretum VII questione prima, ubi beatus Gregorius ita scribit: In apibus princeps unus est, grues unam sequuntur ordine limitato. Imperator unus, iudex unus provincie. Roma autem condita duos fratres reges simul habere non potuit, sed fratricidio dedicatur. In Rebecce utero Esau et Yacob bella gesserunt. Sic [p. 195] singuli ecclesiarum episcopi, singuli archipresbiteri, singuli archidiaconi et omnis ordo ecclesiasticus suis rectoribus innititur. Oportet igitur unam militantem ecclesiam habere conversantem unum prepositum, cum secundum philosophos omnis multitudo ad unitatem reducitur.

And since it is necessary that there be a [pope]{g:papa} continually in the [church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans}, and no pope is [one on the way]{g:viator} with us unless he has kept the [limits]{g:limitare} of Peter, it seems necessary to learn about those limits and about the [power]{g:potestas} of the pope. And many argue by natural reasons, and others by written testimonies, that it is necessary that someone continually be pope; which is to be granted, for it is necessary that Christ, who alone is [head]{g:caput} of the universal [church]{g:ecclesia}, be with the church militant until the end of the world, as is clear from the last chapter of Matthew;[^1] and besides this it is necessary that there be, [indistinctly]{g:confuse}, some [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} [person] on the way whom God approves more for the surpassing edification of his church, who must be pope by the Lord's election; and human election cannot frustrate that election.[^2] And so I understand the decree, [Cause] 7, question 1, where blessed Gregory writes thus: "Among bees there is one ruler; cranes follow one in a fixed order. There is one emperor, one judge of a province. And Rome, when founded, could not have two brothers as kings together, but is dedicated by fratricide. In Rebecca's womb Esau and Jacob waged war. So [195] each bishop of the churches, each archpriest, each archdeacon, and every ecclesiastical order rests on its rulers."[^3] Therefore the one church militant must have one [superior]{g:prepositi} living among it, since according to the philosophers every multitude is reduced to unity.[^4]

## ¶2 · 195.7–197.2

> [p. 195] Data igitur tota ista sentencia patet ex dictis quod alienum est ex istis inferre quod talis prepositus sit Romanus pontifex vel humanis ritibus institutus. Sed sicut in brutis dux ostenditur a natura, ut patet ex decreto predicto, sic dux christiani exercitus debet ostendi revelacione divina, quia nichil falsius; quam quod humana eleccio facit papam nam solus Deus iustificat hominem. Omnis qui facit papam vel membrum sancte matris ecclesie, facit iustum, igitur solus Dominus facit papam. Maior patet per Augustinum in De Verbis Domini sermone XLV cum locis similibus, ubi reputat oppositum nimis hereticum. Et minor patet ex hoc quod papa (in quantum huiusmodi) est pater sanctissimus atque iustissimus, cum notum sit quod cardinales eligendo membrum non eligunt papam, nec proficit sed officit talem in ecclesia presidere. Ideo Act. primo legitur, quomodo dominus non apostoli Matthiam in apostolum elegerunt. Et certum est quod cardinales ex ficticia introducti non habent efficaciorem auctoritatem quam habuerunt dicti apostoli. Unde videtur multis quod ritus hodiernus eligendi papam inductus sit ecclesie scandalozus, primo quia est infundabilis ex scriptura. Christus enim cuius potestati humane non possunt equiparari apostoli, elegit sibi apostolos et unum [p. 196] capitaneum inter illos. Petrus autem et omnes alii apostoli non presumpserunt nec audebant eligere unum apostolum, ut patet Act. primo cap. de Matthia, igitur multo magis cardinales infundabiliter introducti non habent potestatem ad eligendum caput universalis ecclesie quod excederet omnes apostolos preter Petrum. Dico autem illos infundabiliter introductos, quia in utroque testamento, si non fallor, fundari non potest eorum institucio sive nomen. Unde quidam dicit quod cardinalis dicitur a cardine, quia in illis porte inferi sunt rotate; sunt, inquit, altera filia sanguisuge, de qua Proverb. XXX, 15: Sanguisuge due sunt filie, dicentes: Affer, affer: non enim gerunt vicem apostolorum, cum omnes apostoli erant episcopi. Multi autem eorum non sunt presbiteri. Isti autem videntur inferiores illo de quo Marci IX, 3: Magister, vidimus quendam in nomine tuo eiicere demonia, qui non sequitur nos, quia ut patet ex responsione Christi, ipse fuit non Christo contrarius, ideo fuit ex evangelio minus istis cardinalibus improbatus. Non enim videntur venire in nomine Christi eos mittentis, cum tamquam alter Geysy beneficia ecclesiastica congregant et cumulant et pro eorum vendicione nummos per se vel suos acceptant et sic bona pauperum voluptuose et superbe devorant. Sed nec faciunt miracula nec predicant neque orant, sic quod notando quantitatem ypocrisis et operis, nec Geysy nec Symon dignus est dici eorum minister in servicio Antichristi, quod si est ita: ve illis, ve eorum autoribus et ve omnibus, qui facinori eorum consenciunt. Nec supplent tales vicem dyaconorum, quos constituerunt apostoli, [p. 197] ut patet Act. VI, quia nec faciunt eorum officia nec vivunt eorum vita.

Granting, therefore, this whole [teaching]{g:sentencia}, it is clear from what has been said that it is foreign [to it] to infer from these things that such a superior is the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex}, or one instituted by human [rites]{g:ritus}.[^5] But as among the brute beasts the leader is shown by nature, as is clear from the aforesaid decree, so the leader of the Christian army must be shown by divine revelation; for nothing is more false than that human election makes a pope, for God alone justifies a man. Everyone who makes a pope or a [member]{g:membrum} of holy mother church makes [him] just; therefore the Lord alone makes a pope.[^6] The major is clear through Augustine in *On the Words of the Lord*, sermon 45, with similar passages, where he reckons the opposite exceedingly heretical.[^7] And the minor is clear from this, that the pope (as such) is a most holy and most just father, since it is known that cardinals in electing a member do not elect a pope, nor does it profit, but harms, that such a one preside in the church.[^8] Therefore in Acts 1 we read how the Lord, not the apostles, chose Matthias as an apostle.[^9] And it is certain that cardinals brought in by a [fiction]{g:ficticia} do not have more effective [authority]{g:auctoritas} than the said apostles had. Hence it seems to many that the present-day rite of electing the pope has been brought in as a scandal to the church, first because it is without foundation in scripture. For Christ, to whose power the apostles cannot humanly be equalled, chose for himself apostles and one [196] [captain]{g:capitaneus} among them.[^10] Now Peter and all the other apostles did not presume, nor did they dare, to elect one apostle, as is clear from Acts 1, the chapter on Matthias; therefore much more the cardinals, brought in without foundation, do not have power to elect the head of the universal church, who would surpass all the apostles except Peter.[^11] And I call them brought in without foundation because in neither testament, if I am not mistaken, can their institution or name be grounded. Hence someone says that "cardinal" is said from *cardo*, a hinge, because the gates of hell are turned on them; they are, he says, the other daughter of the horseleech, of which Proverbs 30:15: "The horseleech hath two daughters that say: Bring, bring";[^12] for they do not act in the place of the apostles, since all the apostles were [bishops]{g:episcopus}. But many of them are not [presbyters]{g:presbiter}. And these seem inferior to the one of whom Mark 9:37 [speaks]: "Master, we saw one casting out devils in thy name, who followeth not us"; for, as is clear from Christ's answer, he was not contrary to Christ, and therefore he was less disapproved by the gospel than these cardinals.[^13] For they do not seem to come in the name of Christ who sends them, since, like another Gehazi, they gather and heap up [ecclesiastical benefices]{g:beneficium-ecclesiasticum}, and for their sale accept money, themselves or through their own men, and so devour the goods of the poor voluptuously and proudly.[^14] But they neither do miracles nor preach nor pray, so that, noting the extent of the hypocrisy and of the work, neither Gehazi nor Simon is worthy to be called their minister in the [service]{g:servicium} of Antichrist; and if it is so, woe to them, woe to their authors, and woe to all who consent to their [misdeed]{g:facinus}. Nor do such supply the place of the deacons whom the apostles appointed, [197] as is clear from Acts 6, for they neither do their offices nor live their life.

## ¶3 · 197.3–197.20

> [p. 197] Nec valet indagare ex ritibus veteris testamenti, quia nec subest causa neque licencia; ymo quando doctores residebant Jerusalem, ut dicitur fuisse tempore Christi, tunc maxime dissipaverant legem Dei. Ideo nisi dominus dissipaverit consilium Achitophel, possent talia monstra crescere in ecclesia, in specie, in numero et in spoliacione sine fine. Unde igitur talibus monstris innominatis in scriptura potestas ad constituendum caput ecclesie, cum non sit opus hominis facere aliquod membrum ecclesie. Principem autem super eos quantumcumque indignum possunt eligere, eum papam instar sui nominare extranee, sed non possunt constituere caput ecclesie; quod si Deus elegerit et ipsi instinctu Dei in talem consenserint, possunt illum populo intimare. Sed unde talibus ultra apostolos a Deo auctoritas ad eligendum caput ecclesie?

Nor is it of any avail to search [this] out from the rites of the old testament, for there underlies it neither cause nor [licence]{g:licencia}; indeed, when the doctors resided at Jerusalem, as is said to have been the case in the time of Christ, then most of all had they dissipated the law of God.[^15] Therefore, unless the Lord should dissipate the counsel of Achitophel, such monsters could grow in the church, in kind, in number and in spoliation, without end.[^16] From where, then, do such monsters, unnamed in scripture, [have] power to constitute the head of the church, since it is not the work of man to make any member of the church? But a prince over them, however unworthy, they can elect, and name him pope after their own likeness, outlandishly; but they cannot constitute the head of the church; and if God has chosen [him], and they at God's prompting have consented to such a one, they can make him known to the people.[^17] But from where do such men have, beyond the apostles, authority from God to elect the head of the church?

## ¶4 · 197.21–197.27

> [p. 197] Secundo principaliter arguitur ad idem per hoc, quod non licet ecclesie recedere ab observanciis apostolicis quas Christus docuit, sed ante dotacionem ecclesie sacerdotes Christi sine dominio erant socii et communi consilio secundum legem Dei rexerunt ecclesiam, ut patet ex dictis; igitur non licet a limitibus illis ad ritus gentiles recedere.

Secondly, as a principal [argument], [it is argued]{g:arguitur-per} to the same [conclusion] by appeal to this: that it is not lawful for the church to depart from the apostolic observances that Christ taught; now before the [endowment]{g:dotacio} of the church the [priests]{g:sacerdos} of Christ were companions without [lordship]{g:dominium}, and ruled the church by common counsel according to [God's law]{g:lex-dei}, as is clear from what has been said; therefore it is not lawful to depart from those limits to the rites of the gentiles.[^18]

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** Matt. 28:20, "behold I am with you all days, even to the consummation of the world". **R.** A in the margin (194.12): *Caput ecclesie necesse est esse Christum*, with a note on Gregory's text for the pope, archbishops, archpriests and archdeacons.
[^2]: **C.** *Confuse*: in the logicians' sense; it is necessary that some predestinate wayfarer be pope, though not which one is known. *Propter excessum edificacionis ecclesie sue*: for the surpassing [measure] in which he builds up God's church. *Vians* (194.8) and *viantem* are the participle of *viare*: the true pope is a wayfarer of the church militant.
[^3]: **S.** Decretum C. 7 q. 1 c. 41 (*In apibus*), form ours; the canon is drawn from Jerome, Ep. 125 to Rusticus, though Wyclif names Gregory: Jerome, Ep. 125.15 (PL 22, 1080); in Friedberg's text the canon is inscribed *Idem ad Rusticum Monachum* after a canon of Gregory (c. 40), and only the Roman edition reads *Item Hieronymus*, which explains Wyclif's ascription (LEDGER P09a-1). **T.** 194.26: Loserth notes that the Decretum reads *literato*, "in lettered order" (the cranes' formation); the print's *limitato* is translated.
[^4]: **C.** *Habere conversantem unum prepositum*: one superior present and living among it, i.e. on earth. Loserth gives no source for the philosophers' maxim.
[^5]: **C.** *Alienum est ex istis inferre*: the inference does not follow from what is granted; Gregory's text shows that there must be one superior, not that he is the Roman pontiff.
[^6]: **C.** The syllogism: God alone justifies a man (major); whoever makes a pope or a member of the church makes him just (minor); so the Lord alone makes a pope. **R.** A and B in the margin (195.17): *Facere membrum ecclesie*.
[^7]: **S.** Augustine, *De verbis Domini*, sermon 45 in the medieval numbering, now *Sermo* 129; Loserth gives no reference here, but identifies the same citation at 187.1 as *Sermo* 129. At 129.6 (PL 38, 723) Augustine treats the claim to justify as the mark of Antichrist: *Quid autem expavescimus in Antichristo, nisi quia nomen suum honoraturus est …? Quid aliud facit qui dicit: Ego justifico?* (LEDGER P09a-2).
[^8]: **C.** *Cardinales eligendo membrum non eligunt papam*: *membrum* has no genitive. Read as "a member [of the church]", the clause says that even electing a member does not make him pope; read as "a member [of the devil]", which *talem ... presidere* ("that such a one preside") suits, a word may be lost. Translated as printed. *Nec proficit sed officit* plays on the two verbs.
[^9]: **C.** *Dominus non apostoli ... elegerunt*: the verb is plural, agreeing with the nearer *apostoli*; the sense is that the Lord chose. **S.** Acts 1:24–26: the apostles pray, "Thou, Lord ... shew whether of these two thou hast chosen", and cast lots.
[^10]: **C.** *Cuius potestati humane*: *humane* is taken as the adverb ("humanly"); it could be the adjective with *potestati*, "to whose human power the apostles cannot be equalled".
[^11]: **C.** *Quod excederet*: the neuter relative taken with *caput* (the head, who would surpass all the apostles but Peter); it could refer to the act of electing, a power beyond the apostles'.
[^12]: **S.** Prov. 30:15. The author of the etymology (*quidam*) is not named and has not been traced. Wyclif develops the same figure in *De blasphemia* cap. 5 (ed. Dziewicki, p. 64): *filie sanguisuge sunt cardinales apostate. In illis enim, ut cardine, vertuntur valve duplicitatis late vie que ducit ad baratrum* (LEDGER P09a-3, untraced). **T.** 196.11: the manuscripts read *dicunt*; Loserth prints *dicit*, translated. **R.** A and B in the margin (196.13): *Cardinales quid faciunt*.
[^13]: **T.** The print gives Mark "IX, 3"; the verse is 9:37 (Vulgate numbering), given here. **S.** Christ's answer follows in Mark 9:38–39.
[^14]: **S.** Gehazi, Elisha's servant, who took Naaman's gifts (4 Kings 5:20–27); Simon, who offered the apostles money (Acts 8:18–24). **T.** 196.25: the manuscripts read *commutant*, "exchange"; Loserth prints *cumulant*, translated.
[^15]: **C.** *Doctores*: the teachers of the law at Jerusalem. *Dissipaverant legem Dei* echoes Ps. 118:126, "they have dissipated thy law".
[^16]: **S.** 2 Sam. 17:14 (2 Kings in the Vulgate): "by the will of the Lord the profitable counsel of Achitophel was defeated" (*dissipatum est*).
[^17]: **C.** *Eum papam instar sui nominare extranee*: *instar sui* "after their own likeness" (a pope like themselves); *extranee*, "outlandishly", in a manner foreign [to scripture]. *Intimare*: to announce.
[^18]: **C.** The second principal argument against the present form of election: its major is proved at 197.29, its minor at 199.21. *Socii*: fellows, of equal standing.
