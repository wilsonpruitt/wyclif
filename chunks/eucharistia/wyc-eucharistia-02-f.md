# *De eucharistia*, chapter 2, part 6: the decree on Berengar preferred to the decretal on transubstantiation

## ¶1 · 48.23–50.27

> [p. 48] Hic videtur michi quod ecclesia primitiva illud non posuit, sed ecclesia novella, ut quidam infideliter et infundabiliter sompniantes baptizarunt terminum et fantasiarunt multa false ad onus ecclesie. Primo ergo videtur quod non pocius credendum est dicte decretali vel suis autoribus quam decreto ecclesie in persona Beringarii, quia (ut supra dictum est) in isto supereminebant sanctitate et sciencia papa cum sinodo magis credibili, qui locuti sunt conformius ad scripturas, ad sanctorum doctorum sentencias et plus consone racioni et preter hec expressius et [p. 49] facilius detexerunt credendum. Cum enim transsubstanciari sit terminus magistralis, licet fideli trahere eius significacionem ad quemcunque sensum katholicum particularem et sic videtur ambigue et incomplete innuere fidem ecclesie de Eukaristia. Aliud autem decretum explicat quomodo papa et sinodus decreverunt in Beringario quomodo panis et vinum que in altari ponuntur remanent post consecracionem non solum sacramentum sed eciam verum corpus Christi et sanguis. Unde illud credendum ut fidem misit papa iocunde diversis ecclesiis. Cum ergo signum materiale decretalium vel decretorum sit inpertinens fidei quam sentit ecclesia et tanta vel maior relucebat in ecclesia priori auctoritas, videtur quod tollitur evidencia capta ex decretali ecclesie de transsubstanciacione que tolleret naturam panis et vini post consecracionem hostie remanere. Quod si dicatur doctores glosantes ut glosam communem et Archidiaconum cum multis theologis intelligere per panem solummodo formam panis, patet quod coloracius diceret expositor prioris decreti quod isti expositores intelligunt per formam panis et vini realiter essenciam vel naturam panis et vini que est realiter ipsum corpus; et sic est locucio decreti expressior glosa sua. Confirmatur primo per hoc quod inevitabiliter sequitur quantumcunque rudi loico: Sub forma panis et vini est corpus Christi sacramentaliter, ergo ille panis et illud vinum sunt substancia illi forme et per consequens illa existente forma accidentali panis et vinum remanent maioris permanencie illa forma. Secundo per hoc quod CXIVᶜⁱᵐ episcopi non congregarentur ad discernendum quod panis et vinum que in altari ponuntur manerent post consecracionem, si intelligerent [p. 50] eius contradiccionem quod non panis et vinum remanent post consecracionem illud sacramentum altaris sed accidencia que quondam fuerunt panis et vinum; non enim illuderent populo, dicentes insolite quod accidencia ponuntur intendentes contradictorium dicti sui. Sic enim facile foret glosare quoscunque testes fidei per contradictorium, fidem ecclesie subvertendo; videat ergo primo glosator quomodo nullus diceret panem et vinum poni in altari ante consecracionem, hoc est, illa accidencia non illam substanciam; videat secundo quod eque falsum est illa accidencia esse corpus Christi et sanguinem sicut illas substancias, et eque vere figurant ille substancie corpus Christi ut accidencia; videat tercio quomodo scriptura sacra a qua exemplatur ista locucio non intelligit per panem et vinum illa accidencia sed ipsas essencias. Quare ergo non sic intelligeret decretum cum sanctis doctoribus? Tercio confirmatur ut sepe quod verba decreti magis concordant cum scriptura et sanctis doctoribus, cum racione et sensu; ideo tam maturi et sancti episcopi vel intelligerent famose per verba fidei vel exprimerent sensum suum. Sic enim explanat decretum multociens quomodo debet intelligi quod panis est corpus Christi, hoc est, signum sacramentale eius; qui modus loquendi fuit famosus in scriptura et tunc acceptus a populo, sed post declinavit ad predicacionem ydempticam.

Here it seems to me that the primitive [church]{g:ecclesia} did not posit this, but [the newer church]{g:novella-ecclesia} did, as certain men, dreaming [faithlessly]{g:infideliter} and [without foundation]{g:infundabiliter}, baptized the term and [fancied]{g:fantasiare} many things falsely, to the burden of the church.[^1] First, then, it seems that the said [decretal]{g:decretalis} or its authors are not to be believed rather than the [decree]{g:decretum} of the church in the person of Berengar, because (as was said above) in this the [pope]{g:papa}, with a synod more worthy of belief, excelled in holiness and knowledge; and they spoke more conformably to the scriptures and to the [teachings]{g:sentencia} of the holy doctors, and more consonantly with reason, and besides this they uncovered what is to be believed more expressly and [49] more easily.[^2] For since "to be transubstantiated" is a [masters' term]{g:termini-magistrales}, the faithful man may draw its [signification]{g:significacio} to any particular catholic [meaning]{g:sensus}; and so it seems to intimate ambiguously and incompletely the faith of the church about [the Eucharist]{g:eukaristia}.[^3] But the other decree explains how the pope and synod decreed in [the case of] Berengar how the [bread]{g:panis} and [wine]{g:vinum} which are placed on the altar remain after the [consecration]{g:consecracio} not only a [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} but also the true [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} and blood. Hence the pope gladly sent this to various churches as the faith to be believed.[^4] Since, then, the material [sign]{g:signum} of decretals or decrees is not pertinent to the faith which the church [holds]{g:sentire}, and as great or greater [authority]{g:auctoritas} shone forth in the earlier church, it seems that the [evidence]{g:evidencia} drawn from the church's decretal on [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio}, which would take away the nature of bread and wine remaining after the consecration of the [host]{g:hostia}, [is disposed of]{g:tollere}.[^5] And if it is said that the doctors who [gloss]{g:glosare}, such as the common [gloss]{g:glosa} and the Archdeacon, with many theologians, understand by "bread" only the form of bread, it is clear that an expositor of the earlier decree would say [more colourably]{g:coloracior} that these expositors understand by "the form of bread and wine" [really]{g:realiter} the [essence]{g:essencia} or [nature]{g:natura} of bread and wine, which is really the body itself; and so the speech of the decree is more express than its gloss.[^6] This is confirmed, first, by this: that it [follows]{g:sequitur} inescapably, for a logician however crude: "Under the form of bread and wine is the body of Christ [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter}; therefore that bread and that wine are [substance]{g:substancia} to that form"; and consequently, that accidental form existing, the bread and wine remain, of greater permanence than that form.[^7] Second, by this: that a hundred and fourteen [bishops]{g:episcopus} would not be gathered to determine that the bread and wine which are placed on the altar remain after the consecration, if they understood [50] the contradiction of it: that it is not bread and wine that remain after the consecration as that [sacrament of the altar]{g:sacramentum-altaris}, but [accidents]{g:accidens} which were once bread and wine; for they would not [delude]{g:illudere} the people, saying in an unwonted way that accidents are placed [there], meaning the contradictory of what they said.[^8] For in this way it would be easy to gloss any witnesses of the faith by the contradictory, subverting the faith of the church. Let the glossator, then, see first how no one would say that bread and wine are placed on the altar before the consecration, that is, those accidents, not that substance; let him see secondly that it is as false that those accidents are the body and blood of Christ as that those substances are, and that those substances [figure]{g:figurare} the body of Christ as [truly]{g:vere} as the accidents; let him see thirdly how holy scripture, from which this way of speaking is drawn, does not understand by bread and wine those accidents but the essences themselves. Why then would he not understand the decree so, with the holy doctors?[^9] Thirdly, this is confirmed, as often, by this: that the words of the decree agree more with scripture and the holy doctors, with reason and sense; therefore bishops so mature and holy would either understand by the words of faith in the well-known way, or would express their own meaning. For so the decree explains many times how it must be understood that "the bread is the body of Christ", that is, its sacramental sign; which manner of speaking was well known in scripture and then accepted by the people, but afterwards declined into [identical predication]{g:predicacio-ydemptica}.[^10]

## ¶2 · 50.28–51.17

> [p. 50] Unde hodie loquendum foret conformiter sensui exposito et scandalo in fide subiecto, ymmo Christus non omisit sic loqui propter scandalum, ut dicitur Joh. VIᵒ; sed subtiliter exponit se ipsum, ut docet decretum Augustini super Psalmo IVᵒ, quod ponitur [p. 51] in De Consecracione, distinccione IIᵃ, Prima quidem: Spiritualiter intelligite que locutus sum: Non hoc corpus quod videtis manducaturi estis nec bibituri sanguinem quem fusuri sunt qui me crucifigent. Sacramentum aliquod vobis commendavi, spiritualiter intellectum vivificabit vos. Unde ad intelligendum quod non illo modo quo ipsi putant erogat corpus suum dicit ipsum ascensurum in celum integrum. Cum videritis, inquit, filium hominis ascendentem, ubi erat prius, spiritus est qui vivificat. Antiquo ergo decreto patrum pocius credendum est, cum quinquagesima distinccione, Domino sancto, sub auctoritate Isidori sic habetur: Quocienscunque in gestis conciliorum discors sentencia invenitur, illius concilii magis teneatur sentencia cuius antiquior et pocior extat auctoritas. Cum ergo sentencia decreti sit propinquior fonti scripture, videtur quod illa in materia fidei pocius est tenenda.

Hence today one ought to speak in conformity with the meaning set out, the scandal in the faith being brought under; indeed, Christ did not leave off speaking so because of scandal, as is said in John 6;[^11] but he subtly expounds himself, as the decree of Augustine on Psalm 4 teaches, which is placed [51] in *De consecratione*, distinction 2, *Prima quidem*: "Understand [spiritually]{g:spiritualiter} what I have spoken: you are not to eat this body which you see, nor to drink the blood which they will shed who will crucify me. I have commended to you a certain sacrament; spiritually understood, it will give you life." Hence, so that [they might] understand that he does not dispense his body in that manner in which they think, he says that he "will ascend into heaven whole": "When you shall see", he says, "the Son of man ascending where he was before, it is the spirit that quickeneth."[^12] The ancient decree of the fathers is therefore rather to be believed, since in the fiftieth distinction, *Domino sancto*, it is had thus under the authority of Isidore: "Whenever a discordant teaching is found in the acts of councils, let the teaching of that council be held rather whose authority stands more ancient and more powerful." Since, then, the teaching of the decree is nearer to the source of scripture, it seems that it is rather to be held in matter of faith.[^13]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 49.1: We may assign any catholic meaning to the term 'transsubstantiation'.
- 50.28: Christ's language agrees best with the decree concerning Berengarius.

## Notes

[^1]: **C/T.** *Illud*: transubstantiation, which the rejoinder of 47.28–48.7 says the newer church posits and the primitive church did not. *Baptizarunt terminum*: "baptized the term", gave the thing its name (cf. *baptiste signorum*, "baptizers of the signs"). C reads *fantasiaverunt* and *falsa*.
[^2]: **S/C.** *Dicte decretali*: *Una vero* in Lateran IV's *Firmiter*, quoted at 48.1–4 (LEDGER K02e-5). The decree "in the person of Berengar" is *Ego Berengarius*, *De cons.* D. 2 c. 42 (the form is ours) (LEDGER K02f-1). *Magis credibili* agrees with *sinodo*, "a synod more worthy of belief"; *qui locuti sunt* has the pope and synod for subject. *Sentencias*: "teachings", the positions the doctors teach (CONTEXTUAL *sentencia*). C corrected to *hoc expressius*.
[^3]: **G/T.** *Terminus magistralis*: a masters' term, one made in the schools (side-note 49.1). The subject of *videtur innuere* is taken as the term, or the decretal that uses it. DE read *katholicum pertinentem*, "any pertinent catholic meaning"; D *racio magistralis*; E lacks *sic*.
[^4]: **S/T.** Wyclif paraphrases *Ego Berengarius* with *remanent*; the decree itself reads *panem et uinum, que in altari ponuntur, post consecrationem non solum sacramentum, sed etiam uerum corpus et sanguinem Domini nostri Iesu Christi esse* (Friedberg, *De cons.* D. 2 c. 42) (LEDGER K02f-2). The pope who sent the oath to the churches is Nicholas II (Rome, 1059): the canon's § 1 has *misit Nykolaus Papa per urbes Italiae, Germaniae, Galliae*, and Friedberg gives *Ex Concilio Romano hab. a. 1059* (LEDGER K02f-3). D lacks *iocunde*.
[^5]: **C/T.** *Signum materiale*: the written name, whether a text is styled decretal or decree, which Wyclif holds is not pertinent to the faith. *Sentit*: "holds", of the church's opinion. *Tollitur evidencia*: the proof from *Una vero* is disposed of; the second *tolleret* is "take away" in the plain sense. B lacks *hostie*.
[^6]: **S/C.** *Glosam communem*: the ordinary gloss on Gratian, which on *Ego Berengarius* reads "bread" as the species; Loserth quotes it at 26.12, *ad vocem Panem, id est, specie panis*, and at 34.22, *omnia referas ad species ipsas*; the gloss itself is not checked (LEDGER K02f-4). *Archidiaconum*: Guido de Baysio, the Archdeacon, presumably in his *Rosarium* on the same canon; the place is not checked (LEDGER K02f-5). *Que est realiter ipsum corpus*: *ipsum corpus* may be the bread's own body (its substance, against its form) or the body of Christ; the English keeps the Latin's ambiguity.
[^7]: **C/T.** The inference is set out as the logician's and quoted. *Substancia illi forme*: "substance to that form", what underlies it; C reads *subiecta illi forme*, "subject to that form". *Maioris permanencie illa forma*: ablative of comparison. D reads *Confunditur* for *Confirmatur*; C has *et vini — ille panis* in the margin in another hand; B *illud sacramentum*.
[^8]: **S/C.** The hundred and fourteen bishops are the synod of *Ego Berengarius*; Gratian's text reads *coram CXIII. episcopis*, 113 (Friedberg, *De cons.* D. 2 c. 42 § 1); Wyclif's 114 recurs at 31.6 and 32.24 (LEDGER K02f-6). *Dicentes insolite*: taken thus: had they meant accidents, they would have said "bread" in an unwonted sense, meaning the contradictory of their own words. C reads *manent*, E *remanerent* (49.33); D *contradictorium*; C lacks *quod*; C has *accidencia* in another hand.
[^9]: **C/T.** *Eque vere figurant* still hangs on *videat secundo quod*: the accidents figure the body no more truly than the substances. *Exemplatur*: "is drawn", the decree's way of speaking modelled on scripture's. C has *illas substancias — accidencia* in another hand and reads *signant* for *figurant*; D lacks *ille substancie*; B reads *Videant*; ABC *sic non*.
[^10]: **C/S.** *Famose*: "in the well-known way", the received sense of the words (cf. *famosus* just after); *sensum suum*, "their own meaning". *Explanat decretum multociens*: taken of Gratian's *Decretum*, whose *De cons.* D. 2 more than once glosses the bread as the sacrament, the sign, of the body (e.g. c. 32, *sacramentum, id est sacrum signum*, and c. 48 § 2, *celestis panis … suo modo uocatur corpus Christi, cum reuera sit sacramentum corporis Christi*) (LEDGER K02f-7). D lacks *enim* and reads *intelligere*.
[^11]: **C/S/T.** *Scandalo in fide subiecto*: taken as an ablative absolute, the scandal brought under (subdued); it could be a dative beside *sensui*, "in conformity with … the scandal lying in the faith". John 6:61–67 (the hard saying, the disciples going back) is referred to, not quoted. ABC read *emisit*, "uttered", for *omisit*, and *Joh. VI sicut dicitur*.
[^12]: **S/T.** *De cons.* D. 2 c. 44, *Prima quidem* (Loserth: *Decret.* l. c. cap. XLIIII, and at 51.8 *Decret.* l. c.; the form is ours) (LEDGER K02f-8). The words are Augustine's, *Enarr. in Ps.* 98.9 (PL 37, 1265, verbatim; Gratian's inscription is *Psal. LIV*, which his manuscripts ABDF read *Ps. IV*, Friedberg) (LEDGER K02f-9); the print's *super Psalmo IVᵒ* is translated (so on the plate); Loserth's foot-note at 50.32 reads *Recte: Psalmo LIV*, Gratian's usual inscription of c. 44; but Friedberg reports ABDF of Gratian with *Ps. IV*, so Wyclif's *IVᵒ* is likely his Decretum's reading. The words are on Psalm 98. *Ascensurum in celum integrum*: Augustine, *In Ioh. tr.* 27.3 (PL 35, 1616: *se ascensurum in coelum, utique integrum*), carried in the same canon (LEDGER K02f-10). John 6:63–64: the Vulgate reads *si ergo videritis Filium hominis ascendentem ubi erat prius? Spiritus est qui vivificat* (DR "If then you shall see the Son of man ascend up where he was before? It is the spirit that quickeneth"); Wyclif's *Cum videritis* is translated. AB lack *quod ponitur*, C reads *quod exponitur*; E *primo quidem*, *efusuri*, *commendavi vobis*.
[^13]: **S/T.** Gratian D. 50 c. 28, *Domino sancto* (Loserth: *Decr.* Prima Pars, dist. L, cap. XXVIII; the form is ours), under Isidore's name (LEDGER K02f-11). The manuscripts read *consiliorum*, "counsels"; B *consiliorum alias conciliorum*, A in the margin *alias conciliorum*; Loserth's *conciliorum* is translated. AB read *Domino facto*. *Sentencia*: the ruling a council teaches, kept as "teaching".
