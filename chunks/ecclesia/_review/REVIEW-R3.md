# Four-class review R3: *De ecclesia* chunks 08-f, 09-a, 09-c, 09-e

Reviewer: independent, fresh session, 2026-10-03. Method: each chunk read once, sentence by sentence, Latin against English, checked against CARD.md (four classes, connective table), CONVENTIONS §§6–9 and §14, GLOSSARY.md (grepped by term; every tag key in the four chunks checked against its row by hand), the italics rule (M5-RESUME brief 45) and the standing emendation rule (M5-RESUME, Rulings 2026-10-02). Sources were not re-verified. Line numbers are taken from the plates where I opened them (pp. 179, 181, 182, 185, 186, 201). Elsewhere they are placed from the side-notes and the apparatus and marked "c.".

Classes: 1 terms · 2 connectives · 3 medieval sense · 4 construal, or a note that contradicts the text. **Q** marks a quotation-bounds row under the italics rule. It is a class-4 matter, but I count it separately as R2 did. Italics were checked on the plates for pp. 179, 181, 182, 185, 186 and 201 only. The other pages of these chunks (178, 180, 183–184, 190–194, 198–200, 202) were not checked for italics, so the Leo canons at 191 and the Gregory canon at 200 are unchecked.

**Cross-chunk finding (markup).** Every tag key in the four chunks resolves to a real GLOSSARY row; none is orphaned. Two things lint does not catch. (a) Spaced or capitalised keys (`{g:citra Christum}`, `{g:lex Christi}`, …) still occur in 08-f, 09-a and 09-c, against CONVENTIONS §11; 09-e alone is hyphenated throughout. `lint.py` now normalises such keys, but it also passes any key whose *first word* is a first word of some row, so it would not catch a wrong spaced key. (b) Missing first-occurrence tags. Several are for rows ruled 2026-10-02 from these very chapter-9 pages (*exempcio*, *expropriacio*, *mundialitas*, *falsigraphus*, *sagena*, *iugum*, *ociatores*), and *privilegium*, "the key term of *Eccl.* 8–9", is untagged in 08-f, 09-a and 09-c. ## State of each chunk

- **08-f**: Sound. The arguments are construed correctly and the hard spots (*ciresorum*, *globum temporalium*, the opponents' *cum* clause) are noted honestly. One relative, *cuius maioritas*, is left attaching to God in the English. The rest is glossary conformity and one quotation bound.
- **09-a**: Sound English. The work is in the quotation bounds on p. 185, where the Augustine runs past its italics and the Matthew opens too late, and in a few word choices that predate rulings (*vulgari*, *perquisita*, the *inconveniens* C note). The supply in the Pelagius canon needs its alternative on the print.
- **09-c**: The cleanest of the four. Nothing is mis-construed. Most rows are missing tags for rows ruled from these pages, plus *dominacio* once slipping to "lordship", *potissima*, and *fabrica* colliding with *commentum*.
- **09-e**: Good English and well noted. The connectives are its weak point: one minor-premiss *sed* given as "but", one chain *autem* as "and", and one *autem* dropped. On p. 201 the Gratian quotation runs past its italics.
