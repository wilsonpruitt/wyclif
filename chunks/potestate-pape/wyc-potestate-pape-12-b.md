# *De potestate pape*, chapter 12, part 2: the abomination of desolation in the holy place

## ¶1 · 320.23–321.10

> [p. 320] Item, magnificantes necessitatem huius sacramenti cesarei oportet docere ipsam ex testimonio scripture vel per racionem ostendere necessarium influxum papalis preeminencie, quia aliter fingi posset novella religio servatis eisdem nominibus vel mutatis omnino contraria ordini christiano. Constat autem, quod primum deest in toto corpore scripture. Et quantum ad secundum, patet quod oporteret illum influxum vel adiutorium esse spirituale, quia sine corporali preter adiutorium quod ecclesia habet de seculari brachio posset stare. Si autem sit spirituale vel est actus anime vel habitus. Cum autem actus anime sint nobis notissimi, perceptibilis foret tam talis actus quam eius absencia in morte, in divisione vel [p. 321] omissione pape et in eius operacione apostolica; quando magis copiose et attente influeret? Sed nedum abest a nobis talis experimentalis noticia, sed propinquiores huic fonti sunt magis a Dei instinctibus alieni, ymo cum papa sit crebro infidelis vel expers donorum Dei quam eius subditi magis sancti, videtur quod non oportet regulariter hunc solem eclipsabilem sic in quoscumque lucidiores eciam plus tenebrosum influere.

Again, those who magnify the necessity of this caesarean sacrament must teach it from the testimony of scripture, or show by reason the necessary [inflow]{g:influxus} of papal [preeminence]{g:preeminencia}, because otherwise a new religion could be feigned, with the same names kept or changed, wholly contrary to the Christian order.[^1] Now it is evident that the first is lacking in the whole body of scripture. And as to the second, it is clear that that inflow or help would have to be spiritual, because without bodily [help], beyond the help that the [church]{g:ecclesia} has from [the secular arm]{g:brachium-seculare}, it could stand. And if it is spiritual, it is either an act of the soul or a habit. Now since the acts of the soul are most known to us, both such an act and its absence would be perceptible at the death, the division or the [321] omission of a [pope]{g:papa}, and in his apostolic working: when would it flow in more copiously and attentively? But not only is such experiential knowledge absent from us; those nearer to this fountain are even more estranged from the promptings of God; indeed, since the pope is often more [an unbeliever]{g:infidelis}, or more devoid of the gifts of God, than his more holy subjects, it seems that this eclipsable sun, even when more darkened, need not [as a rule]{g:regulariter} so flow into any who are brighter than it.[^2]

## ¶2 · 321.11–321.25

> [p. 321] Nec valet dicere quod papa dat habitum, quia sic extolleretur super omne quod dicitur Deus, dans quantumcumque distanter eciam personis Deo indignis spiritualia dona Dei, quia quid blasphemius vel infidelius quam quod oportet Deum cum omni tali concurrere complendo quicquid promiserit. Unde si habitus vel donum spirituale Dei quiescat, sic quod ex influxu pape in animam subiecti quemque inclinaret ad opus, quod non experitur ecclesia, sed eius contrarium, tunc enim deesset Petro et aliis apostolis influencia illa cesarea, et cesare excedente Christum et eius vicarium correspondenter ad dotacionem vel ritus cesareos, cresceret vel decresceret potestas taliter influendi.

Nor is it valid to say that the pope gives the habit, because so he would be "lifted up above all that is called God", giving the spiritual gifts of God at however great a distance, even to persons unworthy of God; for what is more blasphemous or more unbelieving than that God must concur with every such [giving], fulfilling whatever he has promised?[^3] Hence if a habit or spiritual gift of God should rest [in the soul], so that from the pope's inflow into the subject's soul it inclined each one to work (which the church does not experience, but its contrary), [the claim fails]; for then that caesarean [influence]{g:influencia} would have been lacking to Peter and the other apostles, and, Caesar exceeding Christ and his [vicar]{g:vicarius-christi}, the [power]{g:potestas} of so flowing in would grow or decrease in correspondence with the [endowment]{g:dotacio} or the caesarean [rites]{g:ritus}.[^4]

## ¶3 · 321.26–322.17

> [p. 321] Ex mille evidenciis patet quod nichil est infidelius isto porismate, quod post dotacionem ecclesia non potest rite procedere sine tali papa cesareo. Nichil enim plus accusaret dotacionem vel caput huiusmodi de condicionibus Antichristi, quod foret abhominacio desolacionis, stans in loco sancto, ut expositum est in fine noni capituli et concordat Crysostomus Omelia ultima Operis Imperfecti. Pro cuius sensus declaracione repeto, [p. 322] quod Christus propheta summus prediceret pericula maiora ecclesie, quibus non est maius vel blasfemius quam cum vita yma, ut declarant Gregorius et Bernhardus, vendicare pompatice statum Christi. Cum igitur hoc sepe contingit de papa, ut patet ex cronicis allegatis, videtur quod Christus debuit periculum illud predicere. Sed ubi expressius, quam Matth. XXIV, 15 dicens: Cum videritis abhominacionem desolacionis, que dicta est a Daniele propheta stante in loco sancto, qui legit, intelligat. Daret autem fidelis operam ad intelligendum hunc textum propter tria, primo quia est textus viri desideriorum, ut patet Daniel IX, secundo quia est textus evangelicus a summa sapiencia repetitus, et tercio quia Christus ex magna causa precipit quod hoc legens ipsum intelligat.

From a thousand [evidences]{g:evidencia} it is clear that nothing is more unbelieving than this [porism]{g:porisma}: that after the endowment the church cannot go forward rightly without such a [caesarean pope]{g:papa-cesareus}. For nothing would more accuse the endowment, or a [head]{g:caput} of this kind, of the conditions of Antichrist, namely that it would be "the abomination of desolation, standing in the holy place", as has been expounded at the end of the ninth chapter; and Chrysostom agrees, in the last homily of the *Opus imperfectum*.[^5] To make its sense plain I repeat [322] that Christ, the highest prophet, would foretell the greater dangers of the church, than which none is greater or more blasphemous than, with the lowest life, to claim [boastfully]{g:pompare} the [state]{g:status} of Christ, as Gregory and Bernard declare.[^6] Since, then, this often happens with the pope, as is clear from the chronicles [alleged]{g:allegare}, it seems that Christ ought to have foretold that danger. But where more expressly than in Matthew 24:15, saying: "When you shall see the abomination of desolation, which was spoken of by Daniel the prophet, standing in the holy place: he that readeth let him understand"?[^7] And the faithful man should take pains to understand this text for three reasons: first, because it is the text of "the man of desires", as is clear in Daniel 9; second, because it is a gospel text repeated by the highest wisdom; and third, because Christ for a great cause commands that whoever reads this should understand it.[^8]

## ¶4 · 322.18–323.21

> [p. 322] Expositores autem verificant illud de Antichristo concorditer. Sed alii verificant hoc de Adriano imperatore vel alio, qui posuit statuam imaginis sue in templo Domini. Et alii fingunt sensus extraneos. Michi autem videtur quod nichil plus concordat cum litera vel ebdomalibus Danielis quam exponendo hoc de pseudopapa, qui est potissimus Antichristus. In hoc enim est abhominacio desolacionis, ut supra exposui. Nam communitas abhominaretur sentire unum fingentem personam Christi in interludio estivo et super hec extra formam ludi facientem turpidinem vel loquentem, quanto magis, quando quis pretendit vivaciter tamquam spectaculum toti ecclesie, quod gerit personam Christi, et tamen committendo peccata turpissima vivit Christo summe contrarie. Hec, inquam, nedum est abhominacio ut in ludo sed desolacio, deficiente [p. 323] capitali suffragio hoste Christi subdole introducto. Communis autem modus loquendi concordat, quod, papa stante in loco Petri vel papatu, per tantum aut tantum temporis ille locus debet esse sanctissimus, cum papa pretendit se stare in loco sancti sanctorum, in cuius signum consentit quod vocetur pater sanctissimus. Studeat igitur calumpniator huius sentencie de qua persona ecclesie pertinencius verificaretur dictum Christi ad literam, et cum de multis pseudopapis sit verum, quod sint abhominacio desolacionis stans in loco sancto, quid obest scripturam sacram gravidari ista sentencia, aut quid derogat profecie Christi, quod ipse predixit nobis illud periculum? Non enim videtur quod Christus locutus est de loco materiali, ut templo Jerusalem vel alio, quia nulla vel modica sanctitas nisi equivoce est in illo, sed loquitur de loco spirituali, qui est status sanctus, in quo succedit unus post alium et abhominacionem in tali loco necesse est fundari in clero.

And the expositors with one accord take it as true of Antichrist. But others take it as true of the emperor Hadrian, or of another, who set up a statue of his own image in the temple of the Lord. And others feign foreign senses.[^9] But it seems to me that nothing agrees more with the letter, or with the weeks of Daniel, than expounding this of the [pseudo-pope]{g:pseudopapa}, who is the chief Antichrist. For in this lies the abomination of desolation, as I expounded above. For the community would feel abomination to perceive someone playing the person of Christ in a summer interlude and, over and above this, outside the form of the play, doing or speaking filth; how much more when someone claims in living earnest, as a spectacle to the whole church, that he bears the person of Christ, and yet, committing the foulest sins, lives in the highest degree contrary to Christ.[^10] This, I say, is not only an abomination, as in a play, but a desolation, since the [323] [aid]{g:suffragium} of the head fails when the enemy of Christ has been craftily brought in. And the common manner of speaking agrees that, while the pope stands in Peter's place or papacy, for so much or so much time that place ought to be most holy, since the pope claims that he stands in the place of the holy of holies, in token of which he consents to be called "most holy father".[^11] Let the challenger of this [teaching]{g:sentencia}, then, study of which person of the church Christ's saying would more pertinently be verified to the letter; and since it is true of many pseudo-popes that they are "the abomination of desolation standing in the holy place", what hinders holy scripture from being pregnant with this teaching, or what does it take away from Christ's prophecy that he foretold that danger to us? For it does not seem that Christ spoke of a material place, such as the temple of Jerusalem or another, because there is no holiness in it, or little, except [equivocally]{g:equivoce}; but he speaks of a spiritual place, which is a holy state, in which one succeeds after another; and the abomination in such a place must be founded in the [clergy]{g:clerus}.

## ¶5 · 323.22–324.6

> [p. 323] Sed contra illud arguitur per hoc, quod Apostolus II. Tessal., II, 4 de Antichristo loquens prophetat quod in templo Dei sedeat, non igitur stat in loco sancto, et per consequens Christus ibi non loquitur de Antichristo. Primo tollo instanciam detegens fallaciam, nam et stabit et sedebit. Stabit ad erigendum et magnificandum suam potenciam tamquam paratus ad temporalia rapiendum, nam sicut Stephanus vidit Ihesum stantem a dextris virtutis Dei Act. VII, paratum ad membra sua certancia relevandum, et sic utrobique loquitur scriptura de stacione spirituali, quia (ut creditur) Stephanus non vidit stacionem corporalem ad tantam distanciam oculo corporali, [p. 324] ut notat Augustinus in De Cognicione Vere Vite prope finem. Nec phas est imponere in hoc militi Christi mendacium. Et ista maledicta stacio Antichristi implicat ipsum sedere, quia a ministerio Christi ad quod excellenter sed false se obligat ociantem. Et ista est mala sessio spiritualis.

But against this [it is argued by appeal to]{g:arguitur-per} the fact that the Apostle in 2 Thessalonians 2:4, speaking of Antichrist, prophesies that "he sitteth in the temple of God"; therefore he does not stand in the holy place, and consequently Christ is not speaking there of Antichrist.[^12] First I [dispose of]{g:tollere} the [objection]{g:instancia}, [exposing]{g:detegere} the [fallacy]{g:fallacia}: for he will both stand and sit. He will stand to raise up and magnify his [power]{g:potencia}, as one ready to seize [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}; for as Stephen saw Jesus "standing on the right hand" of the power "of God" (Acts 7), ready to relieve his [members]{g:membrum} in their struggle, so in both places scripture speaks of a spiritual standing; for (as is believed) Stephen did not see a bodily standing at so great a distance with the bodily eye, [324] as Augustine notes in *De cognitione verae vitae*, near the end.[^13] Nor is it right in this to impute a lie to the [soldier]{g:milites} of Christ. And this accursed standing of Antichrist implies that he sits, because it shows him idling away from the ministry of Christ, to which he binds himself excellently but falsely.[^14] And this is the evil spiritual sitting.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Sacramenti cesarei*: Wyclif's irony, the caesarean papacy spoken of as if it were a sacrament the church needs. *Ipsam* (the necessity) is the object of *docere*.
[^2]: **R.** Both manuscripts note in the margin *Fons papa*, "the pope a fountain" (321.3), and *Sol*, "sun" (321.7), against the two figures. **C.** *Magis* governs both *infidelis* and *expers*, against *subditi magis sancti*.
[^3]: **S.** 2 Thess. 2:4, "Who … is lifted up above all that is called God"; Wyclif's *super* for the Vulgate's *supra*. **C.** *Quicquid promiserit*: whatever the pope has promised, which God would be bound to make good.
[^4]: **C.** The period breaks off: the *si* clause has no main clause, and *tunc enim* gives the reason why the supposition fails; we supply "[the claim fails]". *Quiescat*, "should rest": the gift abiding in the soul as from the pope's inflow.
[^5]: **S.** Matt. 24:15. The cross-reference is to the end of ch. 9 of this work (to be checked, LEDGER P12b-1); pseudo-Chrysostom, *Opus imperfectum in Matthaeum*, last homily, to be checked (LEDGER P12b-2). **T.** B reads *et expositum est* for *ut expositum est* (321.32); the print is translated.
[^6]: **S.** Gregory and Bernard on claiming Christ's state with the lowest life: no reference at the page foot; to be checked (LEDGER P12b-3).
[^7]: **S.** Matt. 24:15. Wyclif's Latin lacks the Vulgate's *ergo* ("therefore"), and reads *stante* where the Vulgate has *stantem*: as printed, the participle could go with Daniel; we translate it with the abomination, as the Vulgate does.
[^8]: **S.** Dan. 9:23, "thou art a man of desires"; the abomination of desolation, Dan. 9:27.
[^9]: **C.** *Verificant illud de*: "take it as true of". The *autem* of the first sentence is continuative; *alii* sets some interpreters against the common reading. **S.** Hadrian's statue in the temple: the reading of Matt. 24:15 is not identified at the page foot; to be checked (LEDGER P12b-4).
[^10]: **C.** *Interludio estivo*: a summer interlude, a play acted in the summer season. *Vivaciter*, "in living earnest", as against the play.
[^11]: **T.** At 323.3 the manuscript reads *stat* where Loserth prints *stante* (the apparatus does not name the codex); the print is translated. **C.** *Capitali suffragio* (323.1): the aid that should come from the head of the church; *capitalis* here is the adjective of *caput*. *Sentencia* (323.7, 323.12) is the teaching just set out, not a judicial sentence.
[^12]: **R.** Both manuscripts note in the margin at 323.22: *Antichristus quomodo ostenditur*, "how Antichrist is shown".
[^13]: **S.** Acts 7:55, "Jesus standing on the right hand of God"; *virtutis Dei* ("the power of God") is Luke 22:69, not in the Vulgate of Acts. Pseudo-Augustine, *De cognitione verae vitae*: Loserth gives *Opp.* VI, App., 182–183 (LEDGER P12b-5). **C.** *Potenciam*: "power", the might Antichrist displays, not Aristotle's potency.
[^14]: **C.** *Ociantem* agrees with *ipsum*: the standing implies that Antichrist "sits", since he is idle from the ministry he professes.
