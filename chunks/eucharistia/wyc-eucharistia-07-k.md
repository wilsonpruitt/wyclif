# *De eucharistia*, chapter 7, part 11: the third way, and what Wyclif will not contend over

## ¶1 · 230.32–231.9

> [p. 230] Tercia via laxius loquitur, ut quod panis sacratus nedum est, fit vel erit corpus Christi ad sensum [p. 231] expositum, sed corpus Christi movetur corporaliter, conficitur, sanctificatur, et sic de aliis predicacionibus quas panis sacratus suscipit. Nec repugnat corpori Domini ut sit signum; unde decretum Jeronimi positum in De Consecracione, distinccione IIᵃ, distinguit de duplici corpore Christi. Et sic intelligitur caro Christi et sanguis, vel spiritualis illa de qua ait: Caro mea vere est cibus, vel caro illa que crucifixa est; et sic de sanguine.

The third way speaks more loosely: so that the hallowed bread [not only]{g:nedum} is, becomes or will be [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} in the [meaning]{g:sensus} [231] expounded, but the body of Christ is moved [bodily]{g:corporaliter}, [is consecrated]{g:conficere}, is sanctified, and so with the other [predications]{g:predicacio} that the [hallowed]{g:sacrare} [bread]{g:panis} receives.[^1] Nor is it [incompatible with]{g:repugnare} [the body of the Lord]{g:corpus-domini} that it be a [sign]{g:signum}; hence the [decree]{g:decretum} of Jerome placed in *De consecracione*, distinction 2, distinguishes concerning the twofold body of Christ.[^2] And thus the flesh and blood of Christ is understood: either that spiritual [flesh] of which he says, "My flesh is meat indeed", or that flesh which was crucified; and so of the blood.[^3]

## ¶2 · 231.10–231.24

> [p. 231] Nec contendo circa istam variacionem loyce cum hoc quod sane intelligatur quod panis non sit essencialiter sed tropice corpus Christi, sed quod natura panis remanet post consecracionem subiectans talia accidencia, licet consideracio eius ex presencia nobilioris corporis sit sopita. Nec contendo quod ad sensus equivocos concedatur quod panis est corpus Christi et ille panis non est corpus Christi, cum Christus et Baptista propter equivocacionem non fuerunt contrarii. Aliquando autem pro instruccione populi expedit nunc uno modo loqui nunc alio. Unde quia in tercia via iacet periculum, sicut patet ex paulativa decepcione ecclesie, ideo expedit cavere in illa declinando ad viam primam nec alteram duarum viarum sequencium exprimere nisi sensu exposito.

Nor do I contend over this variation of logic, provided that it is soundly understood that the bread is not [essentially]{g:essencialiter} but [tropically]{g:tropice} the body of Christ, but that the [nature]{g:natura} of bread remains after [consecration]{g:consecracio}, [being the subject of]{g:subiectare} such [accidents]{g:accidens}, although the consideration of it is lulled to sleep by the presence of the nobler body. Nor do I dispute its being granted, in equivocal meanings, that "the bread is the body of Christ" and "that bread is not the body of Christ", since Christ and the Baptist were not contrary, because of [equivocation]{g:equivocacio}.[^4] And sometimes, for the instruction of the people, it is expedient to speak now in one way, now in another. Hence, because danger lies in the third way, as is clear from the gradual deceiving of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, it is therefore expedient to beware in it, by turning to the first way, and not to express either of the two ways that follow except with the meaning expounded.

## ¶3 · 231.25–232.3

> [p. 231] Quoad terciam viam oportet notare quid competit pani ut sacramentum et quid sibi convenit ut panis; et primum potest concedi competere corpori Christi ad sensum equivocum expositum et secundum non; ut non conceditur quod corpus Christi fuit factum, per cilbam, pistum per tedam, visum, fractum vel [p. 232] taliter transmutatum per hominem vel bestiam. Et in isto confinio propter latenciam sensus iacet periculum.

As to the third way, one must note what belongs to the bread as a [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} and what befits it as bread; and the first can be granted to belong to the body of Christ in the equivocal meaning expounded, and the second not: as it is not granted that the body of Christ was made by the [board]{g:cilba}, baked by the brand, seen, broken, or [232] so transmuted by man or beast.[^5] And on this border, because of the hiddenness of the meaning, danger lies.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Nedum … sed*: not only … but. The third way (side-note 230.32) grants of the body what is said of the bread; *ad sensum expositum*, "in the meaning expounded", the tropical one. ABDE read *et erit* for *vel erit* (230.33).
[^2]: **S.** The decree "of Jerome" on the twofold body: Loserth, "rather Augustine's", *De cons.* D. 2 (cap. LXII as the scan reads), *Dupliciter corpus Christi intelligitur*; modern form ours. The canon number and the attribution are to be checked (LEDGER K07k-1). DE read *ut est* (231.4).
[^3]: **S.** John 6:56 (DR); Wyclif omits *enim*.
[^4]: **C.** *Cum hoc quod*: provided that. *Nec contendo quod … concedatur*: "I do not dispute its being granted". Christ and the Baptist: Matt. 11:14 and John 1:21, as in the previous part (230.15–17). B omits *et — Christi* (231.17); E *propter instruccionem* (231.19); C has *ideo — sequencium* in the margin in another hand (231.22–24).
[^5]: **T/C.** *Cilbam*: Loserth, "more correctly *cillibam*" (Greek *killibas*, a board or stand), the baker's board; *tedam*, the firebrand of the oven. B reads *per istum per sedam* (231.30). *Quid competit … quid convenit*: what belongs to the bread as sign and what as bread. D *autem viam*, E *negare* for *notare* (231.25); D *ut concedetur*, E *non fuit* (231.29). ABC omit *Et*, D *sensus latencia* (232.1–2).
