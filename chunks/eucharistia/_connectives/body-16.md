### 06-f ¶2 · 179.4–179.31  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 179] Videtur **autem** quod pregravatus eciam mortali potest in casu dolere de crimine et, assistente sancto ac devoto proposito faciendi in memoriam Christi hanc celebritatem sacramenti, ut animus contritus devocius et attencius serviat Deo suo. Potest enim hoc sacramentum occasionaliter delere tam veniale peccatum quam mortale et sic penas tollere ac gracias et virtutes inducere, non quod ipsum habeat graciam in se formaliter vel efficienciam intrinsecam virtutum, **sed** est occasio et obiectum anime ut memorando gesta Christi per eius efficaciorem influenciam acquirat copiosius actus et habitus virtuales, sic quod oportet quod omnis virtus huius sacramenti primo procedat a Deo, secundo ab homine et tercio occasione obiectiva a sacramento, sicut sanitas corporalis fit primo a Deo communiter influente, secundo a virtute regitiva anime et tercio obiective et quodammodo effective a corporali medicamine: sic est quodammodo in sacramenti suscepcione. Nam sicut non prodest corporalis medicina, si vis anime sanativa defuerit, sic est de sacramenti suscepcione. Unde sicut medicina non prodest lapidi, tumido fluxui vel membro sicco in calore et virtute assimilativa claudicanti, sic in parte ymaginandum est de medicina sacramentali quoad infidelem ut lapis; quoad lubricum et superbum ut corpus plectoratum et repletum; et quoad mundialem avaricia in devocione siccatum et calore ignis celestis per frigus terrenorum mortificans destitutum.

ENGLISH: And it seems that one weighed down even by mortal [sin] can in a given case grieve for the crime and, with a holy and devout purpose assisting of performing this celebration of the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} in memory of Christ, so that the contrite mind may serve its God more devoutly and attentively.[^2] For this sacrament can [by occasion]{g:occasionaliter} blot out venial sin as well as mortal, and so take away penalties and bring in graces and virtues: not that it has grace in itself formally, or an intrinsic efficiency of virtues, but it is the occasion and object for the soul, so that by remembering the deeds of Christ it may, through his more efficacious influence, acquire more copiously virtuous acts and habits; so that all the power of this sacrament must proceed first from God, secondly from man, and thirdly from the sacrament by objective occasion,[^3] just as bodily health is brought about first by God [flowing in]{g:influere} commonly, secondly by the ruling power of the soul, and thirdly objectively and in a certain way effectively by bodily medicine: so it is in a certain way in the [receiving]{g:suscepcio} of the sacrament. For just as bodily medicine does not profit if the healing power of the soul is lacking, so it is with the receiving of the sacrament. Hence just as medicine does not profit a stone, a swollen flux, or a limb dry in its heat and halting in its assimilative power, so in part one must picture the sacramental medicine: as to the [unbeliever]{g:infidelis}, as a stone; as to the lewd and proud, as a body plethoric and replete; and as to the [worldly man]{g:mundialis}, as [a limb] dried up in devotion by avarice and destitute of the heat of the heavenly fire through the deadening cold of earthly things.[^4]

### 06-f ¶3 · 179.32–180.8  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 179] Et utinam nimis false colentes hoc sacramentum ut signum, reliquias sanctorum et peregrinaciones pro [p. 180] indulgenciis clare attenderent istam sentenciam; tunc enim summe ponderarent insensibile signatum et quodcunque signum sensibile colendo respicerent proporcionaliter ut elevat animum ad Deum et invisibilia devocius attendendum. Conclusiones **autem** fideles sequentes ex isto principio fidei publicet cordatus theologus qui pro veritate fidei et correccione erroris ecclesie vult pati obprobria et occidi.

ENGLISH: And would that those who [exceedingly]{g:nimis} falsely worship this sacrament as a [sign]{g:signum}, the relics of the saints, and pilgrimages for [180] [indulgences]{g:indulgencie}, would clearly attend to this [teaching]{g:sentencia};[^5] for then they would weigh most highly the [imperceptible]{g:insensibilis} [thing signified]{g:signatum}, and in worshipping any [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} sign whatever they would regard it in proportion as it raises the mind to God and to attending more devoutly to invisible things. And let a [stout-hearted]{g:cordatus} theologian publish the faithful conclusions that follow from this principle of faith, one who for the truth of the faith and the correction of the error of the [church]{g:ecclesia} is willing to suffer reproaches and to be killed.[^6]

### 06-f ¶4 · 180.9–180.27  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 180] Ad quantum **autem** Deus iuvat efficaciter cultorem propter signa talia inanimata, non est nostrum discutere, **sed** credo quod proporcionaliter ut placet Deo dignitatem hominis vel eius meritum et sic donum proprium coronare. Et istam sentenciam pertractarent doctores antequam tantum difficultarent ecclesiam, quomodo hoc sacramentum in comparacione ad reliqua tollit peccata et dat graciam tam vivis quam mortuis; hec enim facit ut ipse persone sunt digne divino suffragio vel ut sic discamus quod non sit aliud nomen sub divinitate preter Christum in quo oportet nos salvos fieri, et quod ipse pater apud quem non est personarum accepcio omnem gentem que confidit in ipso et addit opera meritoria vult salvare. Fragilitas itaque indisponit hominem **sed** non per se **sed** inordinata affeccio, sicut nihil per se disponit hominem ad beatitudinem nisi Deus efficaciter et virtus formaliter; **sed** alia per accidens iuvant de quanto promovencia sunt ad ista.

ENGLISH: But how far God efficaciously helps the [worshipper]{g:cultor} on account of such inanimate signs is not ours to discuss; but I believe [that he does so] in proportion as it pleases God to crown the dignity of the man, or his merit, and so his own gift. And the doctors should treat this teaching thoroughly before they troubled the church so much [over] how this sacrament, in comparison with the rest, takes away sins and gives grace to the living as well as to the dead; for it does these things according as the persons themselves are worthy of divine [aid]{g:suffragium}, or so that we may thus learn that there is no other name under the [divinity]{g:divinitas} besides Christ in which we must be saved,[^7] and that the Father himself, with whom there is no [respect of persons]{g:accepcio-personarum}, wills to save every nation that trusts in him and adds meritorious works.[^8] So frailty makes a man [unfit]{g:indispositus}, but not of itself; rather inordinate affection does, just as nothing of itself disposes a man to blessedness except God efficaciously and virtue formally; but other things help [by accident]{g:per-accidens} insofar as they are promotive of these.

### 06-f ¶5 · 180.28–181.3  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 180] **Sed** tercio obicitur per hoc quod iuxta dicta omnes pertinaciter dogmatisantes inpossibile in hoc sacramento sunt expresse heretici, omnes heretici sunt [p. 181] fugiendi et finaliter occidendi: ergo tota ista secta in scola eukaristie est taliter castiganda, quod si fieret, foret nimis magna perturbacio in ecclesia.

ENGLISH: But thirdly [it is objected, by appeal to]{g:obicitur-per} this: that according to what has been said, all who [pertinaciously]{g:pertinaciter} [teach as dogma]{g:dogmatisare} the impossible in this sacrament are expressly heretics; all heretics are [181] to be shunned and finally to be killed: therefore this whole [sect]{g:secta} in the school of the eucharist is to be chastised in such a way; and if this were done, there would be an exceedingly great disturbance in the church.[^9]

### 06-f ¶6 · 181.4–182.8  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 181] Hic videtur sentenciam istam moderatam esse katholicam et notandam; nam fides Christi docet katholicum plus attendere ad nutritiva ac edificatoria ad salutem anime quam corporis. Aliter enim foret infideli deterior, **sed** coquus parans venenum domino vel aliter prodens ipsum aut regnum debet post noticiam tractari ut proditor: ergo multo magis proditores anime severius sunt tractandi. Prelati enim et clerici sunt nedum coqui regi et regno **sed** mediatores inter Deum et ipsos, oblaciones populi fideliter offerentes; in quo si defuerint, ubi maior prodicio? Dicitur **autem** nostros mundo divites se ipsos et plebem prodere, cum ordinant tales curatos inter Deum et populum mediare qui maxime elongantur a Christi conversacione, maxime cecantur in evangelica veritate et maxime dissimilantur apostolis in conversacione. Quomodo, inquam, non tales et omnes eis consentanei pararent subditis cibum toxicum, procurarent proditorie vindictam apud Deum et tardarent volentes stricto itinere sequi Christum? Nam predicare nesciunt, consecrare nolunt et orare fastidiunt. Quod si tales quidquam istorum faciunt ad detrimentum sui et populi, sese fallunt; quid, inquam, valet predicacio prelati qui in facto Dei oderit disciplinam? Quid valet eius celebracio, qui in conversacione est tante Christo contrarius, ut puta in superflua familia ecclesie pauperum maxime onerosus, in delicata mensa non pauperum **sed** discolorum maxime sumptuosus et in occupacione mundana constanter sollicitus? Talis, inquam, memoratur Christum sicut [p. 182] Scarioth, qui habuit ipsum in vigilanti memoria ad prodendum. Et in isto laborant divites et pauperes, sicut fratres qui vel tacent vel excitant dominos ad bellandum, obliti ad pacem consulere et salubres veritates katholicas promovere. Et presagium huius divisionis in moribus est multiplicitas opinionum in materia de sacramento altaris, scilicet inter episcopos et plebeos.

ENGLISH: Here it seems that this teaching, moderated, is catholic and to be noted;[^10] for the faith of Christ teaches the catholic to attend more to what nourishes and builds up to the health of the soul than to that of the body. For otherwise he would be worse than an unbeliever;[^11] now a cook who prepares poison for his lord, or otherwise betrays him or the [kingdom]{g:regnum}, ought, once it is known, to be treated as a traitor: therefore much more are traitors of the soul to be treated more severely. For prelates and clerics are [not only]{g:nedum} cooks to the king and the kingdom but mediators between God and them, faithfully offering the oblations of the people; and if they fail in this, where is a greater treason? And it is said that our men rich to the world betray themselves and the common people when they ordain such [curates]{g:curatus} to mediate between God and the people as are farthest removed from the [manner of life]{g:conversacio} of Christ, most blinded in the gospel truth, and most unlike the apostles in manner of life. How, I say, would not such men, and all who consent with them, prepare poisoned food for their subjects, treacherously procure vengeance before God, and hold back those who wish to follow Christ by the strait way? For they do not know how to preach, they will not consecrate, and they disdain to pray. And if such men do any of these things, to the detriment of themselves and of the people, they deceive themselves; what, I say, is the preaching of a prelate worth who in the deed of God hates discipline?[^12] What is his celebrating worth, who in manner of life is so contrary to Christ: for instance, most burdensome to the church of the poor in a superfluous [household]{g:familia}, most lavish at a dainty table, not for the poor but for the [froward]{g:discoli}, and constantly busy in worldly occupation? Such a man, I say, remembers Christ as [182] Scarioth did, who had him in watchful memory in order to betray him. And in this the rich and the poor labour, as do the friars, who either keep silent or stir up the lords to war, forgetting to counsel peace and to promote wholesome catholic truths.[^13] And the presage of this division in morals is the multiplicity of [opinions]{g:opinio} in the matter of the sacrament of the altar, namely between bishops and common folk.

### 06-f ¶7 · 182.9–182.27  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 182] Unde episcopi nostri et sue ecclesie in ista materia multipliciter variantur cuius variacionis causa est vacillacio magistrorum; secundum enim quod pedagogus prelatum docuerit ita credet; et est verisimile quod episcopi apponent diligenciam ad istum articulum cognoscendum. Aliter enim adorarent quod nesciunt, et ipsimet cece caderent in profundum fovee ydolatrie et ecclesiam suam traherent post ipsos precipitem, cum regulariter consequitur grex pastorem; et iterum non tot forent scripta de isto ut fidei articulo, nisi ecclesia et specialiter episcopi obligati ad instruendum ecclesiam suam in fide supereminenter debeant istud scire. Turpe quidem foret quod nostri episcopi scandalizarentur in isto quod bene cognoscunt ymagines auri et argenti **sed** ignorant generaliter quidditatem sacramenti altaris, cum pro libro sic palpant figuras pecunie et per hoc sacramentum quod infinitum dotacionem temporalem sui episcopatus exsuperat emunt a sapiencia odiente ignoranciam regnum celi.

ENGLISH: Hence our [bishops]{g:episcopus} and their churches vary in many ways in this matter, and the cause of this variation is the wavering of the masters; for as the tutor has taught the prelate, so he will believe; and it is likely that the bishops will apply diligence to knowing this article. For otherwise they would adore what they do not know,[^14] and they themselves would fall blind into the depth of the pit of idolatry and drag their church headlong after them, since [as a rule]{g:regulariter} the flock follows the shepherd; and again, there would not be so many writings about this as an [article of faith]{g:articulus-fidei}, unless the church, and especially the bishops, who are bound to instruct their church in the faith, ought pre-eminently to know it. It would indeed be shameful that our bishops should be scandalized in this: that they know well images of gold and silver but are generally [ignorant of]{g:ignorare} the quiddity of the sacrament of the altar, since in place of a book they thus finger the figures of money, and through this sacrament, which infinitely exceeds the temporal [endowment]{g:dotacio} of their bishopric, they buy the kingdom of heaven from wisdom, which hates ignorance.[^15]

### 06-g ¶1 · 182.28–183.3  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 182] Unde nota nimis magne infidelitatis foret in illis quod figuram pecunie et eius quidditatem cognoscerent **sed** formam et quidditatem huius hostie penitus [p. 183] ignorarent. Quid enim plus scandalizaret prelatum, quam quod diligenter curet de karactere sigilli vel carte et non curet de quidditate hostie consecrate.

ENGLISH: Hence it would be a mark of [exceedingly]{g:nimis} great [unbelief]{g:infidelitas} in them that they should know the figure of money and its quiddity but be wholly [183] [ignorant of]{g:ignorare} the form and quiddity of this [host]{g:hostia}. For what should scandalize a [prelate]{g:prelatus} more than that he should care diligently about the stamp of a seal or a charter and not care about the quiddity of the consecrated host?

### 06-g ¶2 · 183.4–184.15  (6 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 183] Ex istis colligitur primo quod illi qui ex ista ignorancia infideli sine causa accusant prelatos ecclesie sunt culpandi. Secundo quod illi prelati religionum qui in suis capitulis accusant precipue fratres suos, quia predicant in patria quod sacramentum altaris nec est corpus Christi nec sanguis, **sed** efficax eius signum; tales enim prelati tanquam infideles heretici inhabilitant se ad quascunque dotaciones regis et alia suffragia regni nostri. Et tercio creditur quod episcopi acute prosecuntur errores dogmatisantes in ista materia, cum instanter persecuti sunt evangelisantes quod domini temporales debent exonerare eos a temporalibus auferendis in casu quo eorum ponderacio ceca undique sit nociva. Ille enim qui preponderat signum fallax et fetidum prosperitatis mundane signo vero et venerabili corporis domini Jesu Christi nedum est indignus possidere predia **sed** ut sit incola terre nostre: ideo timendo istam persecucionem severam protestatus sum publice quod volo humiliter corrigi per quoscunque et specialiter per episcopos qui docuerint in ista materia veritatem. **Sed** volo querere signum, unde doctrine quam asserunt fideliter possum confidere. Possunt **autem** omnes usus ecclesiarum regis nostri ad istam triplicitatem reduci: primo dicitur secundum illos episcopos qui innituntur legi cesaree quod sacramentum altaris est ponderositas: utinam non libret suos usurarios ad infernum. Fundatur **autem** istud super dicto glosse ordinarie cuiusdam [p. 184] cesarei in De Consecracione, distinccione IIᵃ, capitulo Sacramentum, ubi movet dubium si hoc sacramentum potest cadere, cum sit accidens et non pondus: Dicit, inquit, quod potest cadere et dicit quod ponderositas remanet cum accidentibus aliis et dicit quod illa non est aliquid ponderosum, cum sacramentum quod per se movetur non sit ponderosum, relinquitur quod sit ponderositas in abstracto. Et sic maior pars Anglicane ecclesie ad ponderositatem hostie declinabit, cum quasi per totam Cantuariensem provinciam servabitur iste usus. **Sed** constat quod auctor huius sentencie ignorat vocem propriam, cum hoc sacramentum potest ad omnem eius punctum ponderositatem deperdere; oportet ergo dare aliquid ponderosum quod fiat continue remissius ponderosum.

ENGLISH: From these things it is gathered, first, that those who from this unbelieving ignorance accuse the prelates of the [church]{g:ecclesia} without cause are to be blamed. Secondly, that those prelates of the religious [orders] who in their chapters accuse their brothers chiefly because they preach in the [homeland]{g:patria} that the [sacrament of the altar]{g:sacramentum-altaris} is neither the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} nor the blood, but its efficacious [sign]{g:signum}:[^1] for such prelates, as unbelieving heretics, [disqualify]{g:inhabilitare} themselves for any [endowments]{g:dotacio} of the king and the other [aids]{g:suffragium} of our [kingdom]{g:regnum}. And thirdly, it is believed that the bishops sharply prosecute the errors of those who [teach as dogma]{g:dogmatisare} in this matter, since they have urgently persecuted those [preaching the gospel]{g:evangelizans} that the [temporal lords]{g:temporales-domini} ought to unburden them of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, which are to be taken away in the case where their blind [weighing]{g:ponderacio} [of them] is harmful on every side.[^2] For he who weighs the false and fetid sign of worldly prosperity above the true and venerable sign of the body of the Lord Jesus Christ is unworthy [not only]{g:nedum} to possess estates but even to be a dweller in our land: therefore, fearing this severe persecution, I have publicly [declared solemnly]{g:protestari} that I wish to be humbly corrected by anyone whatever, and especially by the bishops who shall teach the truth in this matter.[^3] But I wish to seek a sign by which I can faithfully trust the [doctrine]{g:doctrina} that they assert. And all the [uses]{g:usus} of the churches of our king can be reduced to this threefold: first it is said, according to those bishops who lean on the [caesarean law]{g:lex-cesarea}, that the sacrament of the altar is [heaviness]{g:ponderositas}: would that it may not weigh their usurers down to hell.[^4] And this is founded on a saying of the ordinary gloss of a certain [184] caesarean on *De consecratione*, distinction 2, chapter *Sacramentum*, where it raises the doubt whether this sacrament can fall, since it is an [accident]{g:accidens} and not a weight. It says, he says, that it can fall, and it says that heaviness remains with the other accidents, and it says that that is not anything heavy;[^5] since the sacrament, which is moved [of itself]{g:per-se}, is not heavy, it remains that it is heaviness in the abstract. And so the greater part of the English church will incline to the heaviness of the host, since this use will be kept through almost the whole province of Canterbury. But it is evident that the author of this [teaching]{g:sentencia} is ignorant of the proper word,[^6] since this sacrament can lose heaviness at every point of it; one must therefore posit something heavy which becomes continually more remissly heavy.

### 06-g ¶3 · 184.16–185.11  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 184] Secundus usus sparsus in multis ecclesiis est quod hostia altaris sit quantitas; **sed** iste usus est non minus a katholicis abrogandus, cum stat sacramentum istud per totum rarefieri vel condensari et per consequens ipsum sacramentum sic moveretur, quod non potest competere quantitati, quia sic remaneret antiqua quantitas acquirens ad omnem punctum quantitatem novam, et per consequens immediate postquam maiorari inceperit, fieret infinitum, quia infinitas quantitates secundum se totas acquireret, quarum quelibet foret maior quam sacramentum in principio assignatum; ideo difficultatur iste usus quomodo [p. 185] hoc sacramentum potest tales quantitates acquirere tanquam partes, cum nec sint partes eius quantitative nec qualitative; nec quantitas talis potest esse accidens quantitati nec sacramentum istud sic subito deperdi; unde quamvis diocesis Linconiensis cui dicitur iste usus competere sit ampla et rara, tamen oportet ipsam inniti magis stabili fundamento, nam superficies vel linea non potest sic qualificari in hostia, **sed** sanctitas sacramentalis videtur esse in qualitate que est de genere activorum; constancior enim videtur esse duricies quantitate.

ENGLISH: The second use, spread in many churches, is that the host of the altar is [quantity]{g:quantitas}; but this use is no less to be abrogated by catholics, since it can be that this sacrament is rarefied or condensed throughout, and consequently the sacrament itself would be so moved, which cannot belong to quantity: because thus the old quantity would remain, acquiring at every point a new quantity, and consequently [immediately]{g:immediate} after it had begun to be increased it would become infinite, because it would acquire infinitely many quantities, each in its whole self, any one of which would be greater than the sacrament assigned at the beginning.[^7] Therefore this use is put in difficulty [over] how [185] this sacrament can acquire such quantities as parts, since they are neither its [quantitative parts]{g:pars-quantitativa} nor its [qualitative]{g:pars-qualitativa} ones; nor can such a quantity be an accident to quantity; nor can this sacrament be so suddenly lost. Hence, although the diocese of Lincoln, to which this use is said to belong, is ample and rare, yet it must lean on a more stable foundation;[^8] for a surface or a line cannot be so qualified in the host, but sacramental holiness seems to be in a [quality]{g:qualitas} which is of the genus of the active [qualities]; for hardness seems to be more constant than quantity.

### 06-g ¶5 · 185.18–186.19  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 185] **Sed** patet error in isto usu ex hoc quod nec variatur hoc sacramentum in specie ut color hostie, nec qualitas talis potest exspectare intencionem et remissionem in colore ut hostia, quia nec potest sic alterari nec componi ex suis partibus intensivis, sic quod una medietas intensiva sit sacramentum et alia non sacramentum. Sic enim posset rarefieri et condensari, et per consequens tanquam prius natura posset sibi subicere quantitatem. Potest enim successive fieri maius aut minus et per consequens acquirere vel deperdere magnitudinem. Et tunc nimis errarunt philosophi ponentes quantitatem secundum genus precedere qualitatem. Si ergo hoc sacramentum sit sic quantum, tunc est coloratum et per consequens non est color, **sed** distinguuntur ut accidens et subiectum. Si, inquam, [p. 186] hoc sacramentum sit quantum accidentatum et quale intensum, tunc quantitas sibi accidentalis quam acquirit est alterius racionis et est dignius quantitate. Et istud videtur decretalis sentire libro III, capitulo Cum Marthe, que ponit colorem vini sine subiecto posse profundere se per aquam maiorem, quod non potest competere quantitati; quantitas ergo que potest per se occupare locum maiorem quam nunc occupat et sic acquirere ac perdere quantitatem potest rarefieri et condensari. Et iuxta hoc quot sunt ecclesie tot possunt esse errores mendaces de quidditate hostie, ut unus dicit quod est odor, alius quod est duricies, tercius quod est sapor et sic in infinitum secundum quod afficiunt qualitati quam faciunt. Et omnes isti senciunt minus erronee quam illi qui ponunt quod sacramentum est quantitas vel fasciculus omnis generis accidentis. Omnes tamen isti in hoc conveniunt quod hoc sacramentum est infinitum abieccioris nature quam aliqua vilis substancia corporea assignanda.

ENGLISH: But the error in this use is clear from this: that neither is this sacrament varied in species, as the colour of the host is, nor can such a quality await intension and remission in colour, as the host can; because it can neither be so altered nor be composed of its intensive parts, so that one intensive half would be the sacrament and the other not the sacrament.[^10] For thus it could be rarefied and condensed, and consequently, as prior by nature, could subject quantity to itself. For it can successively become greater or less, and consequently acquire or lose magnitude. And then the philosophers erred exceedingly in positing that quantity precedes quality in genus. If therefore this sacrament is thus [quantified]{g:quantus}, then it is coloured, and consequently it is not colour, but they are distinguished as accident and [subject]{g:subiectum}. If, I say, [186] this sacrament is a quantified thing [subject to accidents]{g:accidentatus} and an intensified qualified thing, then the quantity accidental to it which it acquires is [of another kind]{g:alterius-racionis} and is worthier than quantity. And this the [decretal]{g:decretalis} seems to [hold]{g:sentire}, book 3, chapter *Cum Marthe*, which posits that the colour of wine [without a subject]{g:accidens-sine-subiecto} can pour itself out through a greater [quantity of] water, which cannot belong to quantity;[^11] therefore the quantity which can of itself occupy a greater place than it now occupies, and so acquire and lose quantity, can be rarefied and condensed. And according to this, as many churches as there are, so many lying errors can there be about the quiddity of the host: as one says that it is odour, another that it is hardness, a third that it is savour, and so to infinity, according as they are attached to the quality which they make [the sacrament].[^12] And all these hold less erroneously than those who posit that the sacrament is quantity, or a [bundle of accident]{g:fasciculus-accidentis} of every genus. Yet all these agree in this: that this sacrament is of an infinitely more abject nature than any vile corporeal [substance]{g:substancia} that could be assigned.

### 06-g ¶6 · 186.20–186.28  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 186] Et quantum ad mores attinet, modicum honoramus hoc sacramentum, cum honor ille stat in imitacione Christi in moribus, in vita pauperi et penali et in mundi abieccione pro ritu iusticie; quod licet sit infinitum melius hostia parum attendimus **sed** mendaciter fingimus indumentum abiectissimum corpori Christi competere et accumulamus mendacium, dicentes Christum signum istius nature ad cultum suum statuere.

ENGLISH: And as far as morals are concerned, we honour this sacrament little, since that honour consists in the imitation of Christ in morals, in a poor and [hard]{g:penalis} life, and in the casting-off of the world for the [rite]{g:ritus} of justice; which, though it is infinitely better than the host, we attend to little, but we lyingly feign that a most abject garment belongs to the body of Christ, and we heap up the lie, saying that Christ appointed a sign of this nature for his [worship]{g:cultus}.[^13]

### 06-h ¶1 · 187.1–188.23  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 187] Revera glossantes decreta et decretales opinantur honorabilius quod panis et vinum sunt hoc sacramentum, quod non dampnant nec reprobant **sed** confirmant. Et sic fecerunt sancti doctores concorditer. Unde Rabanus libro De Naturis rerum, libro Vᵒ, capitulo XI declarat quare Deus voluit se ipsum coli sacramentaliter in pane et vino et in fine sic loquitur: Aliud est sacramentum, aliud virtus sacramenti; sacramentum enim ore percipitur, virtute sacramenti homo interior saciatur; igitur quia panis corpus confirmat, ideo ille corpus Christi congruenter nuncupatur; vinum **autem** quia sanguinem operatur in carne, ideo ad sanguinem Christi refertur; hec **autem** dum sunt visibilia sanctificata per Spiritum Sanctum, in sacramentum divini corporis transeunt. Ecce plane sentencia quam predixi. Unde ante sic loquitur: Maluit enim Dominus corporis et sanguinis sacramenta fidelium ore percipi et in partem eorum redigi, ut per visibile opus invisibilis ostenderetur effectus. Ecce quod iste magnus doctor dictat cum experiencia quod panis et vinum que sunt sacramentum corporis et sanguinis Domini rediguntur in partem hominis quoad corpus, ut figuretur pastus interioris hominis per rem huius sacramenti, ut sepe declarat in libro suo De corpore et sanguine Domini. Et idem allegatum est ex testimonio Augustini in De Consecracione, distinccione IIᵃ, Corpus, et Johannis Damasceni et aliorum, quamvis ista sentencia sit per novos recentesque qui venerunt nimis sophistice palliata. [p. 188] Antiqua itaque sanctorum est sentencia quod forma, species vel figura panis et vini sunt hoc sacramentum; et per hec tria nomina intelligunt essenciam vel quidditatem panis et vini. Sic enim loquitur Apostolus Hebr. I, 3, quando dicit Christum eternaliter fuisse in forma Dei, cum sit splendor patris et figura substancie eius. Non enim intelligunt sancti quod hoc sacramentum quod vocant figuram panis sit qualitas de IVᵗᵃ specie qualitatis, quia illa est debilissime entitatis. Nec dubium alicui loyco quin sequitur: Christus sumitur in forma panis et vini, ergo ille panis et vinum sunt, ut patet per argumentum Christi quo (ut dicitur Luc. XX) convicerat Saduceos. Et illi qui dicunt quod false vocatur forma panis et vini, quia est forma per se stans, que olim fuit accidens panis et vini, que iam non sunt secundum aliquam partem sui, nituntur sophisticare sensus populi, ne credat illud quod plane sentit **sed** cogatur credere false fictum incredibile, ut hoc sit via ad populum amplius seducendum. Unde solebam replicare contra hos glossatores quod ista est sentencia unius falsi et heretici ydiote, quia est sentencia eorum, qui olim fuerunt huiusmodi, quia ante baptisacionem suam; et utinam huiusmodi non sint modo.

ENGLISH: [In truth]{g:revera} those who gloss the [decrees]{g:decretum} and [decretals]{g:decretalis} are of the more honourable opinion that the [bread and wine]{g:panis} are this [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}, which they do not condemn or reprove but confirm.[^1] And so the holy doctors did with one accord. Hence Rabanus, in the book *On the Natures of Things*, book 5, chapter 11, makes clear why God willed himself to be worshipped [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} in bread and wine, and at the end speaks thus: "The sacrament is one thing, the power of the sacrament another; for the sacrament is received by the mouth, by the power of the sacrament the inner man is filled; therefore, because bread strengthens the body, so it is fittingly named the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}; and wine, because it works blood in the flesh, is therefore referred to the blood of Christ; and these, while they are visible things sanctified by the Holy Spirit, pass into the sacrament of the divine body."[^2] Behold plainly the [teaching]{g:sentencia} that I spoke of before.[^3] Hence before [this] he speaks thus: "For the Lord preferred that the sacraments of his body and blood be received by the mouth of the faithful and be brought into a part of them, so that by a visible work an invisible effect might be shown." Behold, this great doctor lays down, with experience, that the bread and wine which are the sacrament of the [body of the Lord]{g:corpus-domini} and his blood are brought into a part of man as to the body, so that the feeding of the inner man by the [reality of this sacrament]{g:res-sacramenti} may be [figured]{g:figurare}, as he often makes clear in his book *On the Body and Blood of the Lord*.[^4] And the same has been [alleged]{g:allegare} from the testimony of Augustine in *De consecratione*, distinction 2, *Corpus*, and of John Damascene and others,[^5] although this teaching has been [cloaked]{g:palliare} [exceedingly]{g:nimis} [sophistically]{g:sophistice} by "new and fresh ones that are come".[^6] [188] So the ancient teaching of [the saints]{g:sancti} is that the form, [species]{g:species} or [figure]{g:figura} of bread and wine are this sacrament; and by these three names they understand the essence or quiddity of bread and wine. For so the Apostle speaks, Heb. 1:3, when he says that Christ was eternally in the form of God, since he is the brightness of the Father and "the figure of his substance".[^7] For the saints do not understand that this sacrament, which they call the figure of bread, is a [quality]{g:qualitas} of the fourth species of quality, since that is of the feeblest entity.[^8] Nor is it doubtful to any logician that [it follows]{g:sequitur}: "Christ is received in the form of bread and wine, therefore that bread and wine are", as is clear by the argument of Christ by which (as is said in Luke 20) he had [proved conclusively]{g:convincere} against the Sadducees.[^9] And those who say that it is falsely called the form of bread and wine, because it is a form standing [of itself]{g:per-se}, which once was an [accident]{g:accidens} of the bread and wine, which now are not in any part of themselves, strive [to counterfeit]{g:sophisticare} the senses of the people, lest it believe what it plainly [perceives]{g:sentire}, and so that it be forced to believe an incredible thing falsely feigned, that this may be a way to seducing the people further. Hence I used [to rejoin]{g:replicatur} against these glossators that this is the teaching of a false and heretical [unlettered man]{g:ydiota}, because it is the teaching of those who once were such, because [they were so] before their baptism; and would that they are not such now.[^10]

### 06-h ¶2 · 188.24–189.28  (6 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 188] Ex istis probabiliter creditur quod episcopi tenentur sub pena amissionis omnium temporalium suorum (quia sub pena amissionis regni celorum) cognoscere quid sit hoc sacramentum, quia aliter sunt inepti fideliter informare populum; et omnes qui auctorisant et defendunt tales cifras integre incurrunt perdicionis periculum. Unde utinam gens Roberti quereret diligenter ab ipso cum suis cardinalibus quid de quidditate hostie sit credendum. Hoc enim secundum beatum Petrum est precipuum prelatorum officium parate et [p. 189] fideliter reddere racionem fidei et illam mundo postposito defendere et docere. Videtur, inquam, quod omne sensibile accidens in sacra hostia iuxta descripcionem sacramenti sit sacramentum vel saltem accidens adiuvans sacramenti; **sed** fides tractantis sacramentum et sanctificacio Dei spiritualiter iuvant et faciunt sacramentum. Cum **autem** omne per accidens sit reducibile ad aliquod per se, patet quod est dare substancialem essenciam vel naturam accidentibus illis substratam; **sed** ad racionem illam debemus parum attendere **sed** in racionem sui signati totam cogitacionem nostram suspendere. Et hec est racio quare sancti dicunt panem converti in corpus Christi vel sacramentum, ymmo panem esse corpus Christi, sicut dicimus peccatorem converti a membro dyaboli in filium Dei; que conversio est magis mirabilis quam conversio sacramenti. **Sed** nullibi in istis miraculis oportet quod Deus destruat substancialem essenciam, **sed** mutat ipsam in melius servando naturam. Et hoc servaremus nos qui signa tantum attendimus; Christus enim ordinavit nos comedere et bibere hostiam in hiis rebus, ut figurarent pastum anime tanquam finem. Et utinam tales cultores accidencium non sint a substancia corporis ecclesie exsufflandi; ponunt enim religioni Christi accidere totam regulam quam Christus tradidit et sic in accidentibus involvuntur; plus enim appreciantur terrena que adiacent quam virtutas.

ENGLISH: From these things it is believed [with probability]{g:probabiliter} that the [bishops]{g:episcopus} are bound, under pain of the loss of all their [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} (because under pain of the loss of the [kingdom]{g:regnum} of heaven), to know what this sacrament is, because otherwise they are [inept]{g:ineptus} to [inform]{g:informare} the people faithfully; and all who authorize and defend such [ciphers]{g:cifra} wholly incur the danger of perdition.[^11] Hence would that the people of Robert would diligently ask of him, with his cardinals, what is to be believed about the quiddity of the [host]{g:hostia}.[^12] For this, according to blessed Peter, is the chief office of [prelates]{g:prelatus}: readily and [189] faithfully to render an account of the faith, and, the world [set aside]{g:postponere}, to defend and teach it.[^13] It seems, I say, that every [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} accident in the sacred host, according to the [definition]{g:descripcio} of the sacrament, is the sacrament, or at least an accident assisting the sacrament;[^14] but the faith of the one handling the sacrament and the [sanctification]{g:sanctificacio} of God [spiritually]{g:spiritualiter} help and make the sacrament. Now since everything [by accident]{g:per-accidens} is reducible to something of itself, it is clear that [one may posit]{g:est-dare} a substantial essence or nature underlying those accidents; but to that [aspect]{g:racio} we ought to attend little, and rather hang our whole thought on the aspect of what it signifies. And this is the reason why the saints say that the bread is converted into the body of Christ, or the sacrament, indeed that the bread is the body of Christ, just as we say that a sinner is converted from a [member]{g:membrum} of the devil into a son of God; which [conversion]{g:conversio} is more wonderful than the conversion of the sacrament. But nowhere in these miracles must God destroy the substantial essence, but he changes it for the better, preserving the nature. And this we should preserve, we who attend only to the signs;[^15] for Christ ordained us to eat and drink the host in these things, so that they might figure the feeding of the soul as the end. And would that such [worshippers of accidents]{g:cultores-accidencium} be not [blown out]{g:exsufflare} from the substance of the body of the [church]{g:ecclesia};[^16] for they posit that the whole rule which Christ handed down befalls the religion of Christ [as an accident], and so they are wrapped up in accidents; for they prize the earthly things that lie beside more than virtues.[^17]
