# *De ecclesia*, chapter 1, part 2: the church is the predestinate; no vicar of Christ is its head

## ¶1 · 3.14–6.15

> [p. 3] Hec est sancta ecclesia catholica quam confitentur christiani immediate post fidem in spiritum sanctum propter tria: primo quia secundum Augustinum est summa creatura. Ideo immediate ponitur post trinitatem increatam. Secundo quia amore spiritus sancti Christo in matrimonio perpetuo copulatur. Et tercio quia posita trinitate oportet ipsam habere templum vel domum quam inhabitet. Oportet dico ex necessitate supposicionis atque de congruo. Ista autem ecclesia secundum partem peregrinantem non habet aliquem prescitum partem sui, sicut non [p. 4] habet secundum partem triumphantem, dum est huiusmodi, aliquem miserum vel quomodolibet maculatum, ut ostendit Augustinus, III° de doctrina christiana XXXII°, ubi, postquam ostenderat quod corpus Christi et caput eius Christus sunt una persona, increpat Tyconium in secunda sui regula qua vocat ecclesiam corpus domini bipartitum. Non inquit ita debuit appellare; non enim revera corpus domini est quod cum illo non manebit in eternum. Ideo vocat fideles ad tempus sed prescitos ad dampnacionem ecclesiam similatam ex ypocritis. Potest autem ista sentencia demonstrari ex scriptura Johannis VI°, 54, ubi dicitur Judeis et per idem cuilibet viatori: Nisi manducaveritis carnem filii hominis et biberitis eius sanguinem, non habebitis vitam in vobis. Sed nemo potest esse membrum corporis Christi, nisi fuerit membrum vivum: ergo nemo potest esse de illo corpore, nisi manducaverit corpus Christi. Quomodo queso potest continuari cum nervis capitis, nisi fide formata in caput crediderit? Quod est ipsum manducare, ut exponit Augustinus omelia XXVª. Habet autem iste cibus contrarie ad cibos corporales virtutem assimilandi sibi manducantem secundum quatuor digestiones, de quibus Rom. VIII°, 30, scilicet predestinacionem, vocacionem, iustificacionem et glorificacionem. Quamvis enim sic manducans moritur temporaliter, non tamen eternaliter, cum Christus, resurreccio et vita in finali iudicio vivificat omnia membra sua, quia leo rugiens suscitat suam ecclesiam. Caritas autem que nunquam excidit est vinculum [p. 5] continuans membra illius corporis; foret ergo contra racionem, vel quod sponsus in persona propria, vel quod alius ex permissione sua laniaret sponsam suam divisione eterna. Ymmo claudit contradictorium, si bene inspicitur; cum Jeremie XXXI°, 3, sibi dicitur: In caritate perpetua dilexi te. Ista igitur et non lapides est mater nostra per orbem terrarum confitens caput suum. Quomodo queso tam potens, tam sciolus et tam benivolus, sicut est increata trinitas, eternaliter eligeret sibi sponsam quam finaliter repudiaret? Patet ergo ex fide et signacione quid nominis ecclesie catholice quod ipsa est omnes predestinati presentes, preteriti et futuri. Ex istis sequuntur alique conclusiones. Prima, quod nullus vicarius Christi debet presumere asserere se esse caput ecclesie sancte catholice, ymmo nisi habuerit specialem revelacionem, non assereret se esse aliquod membrum eius. Patet ex hoc quod capitalem illius ecclesie oportet esse super omnes angelos beatos, cum ipsi sint membra illius ecclesie, quod soli Christo potest competere inter omnes predestinatos, ut patet Hebr. 1°, 4. Nam Rom. 1°, 4, dicitur Christum esse predestinatum; et sic ecclesia est solum numerus predestinatorum. Sicut igitur homo sine speciali revelacione non assereret sine formidine se esse predestinatum, sic nec assereret se esse membrum, et per consequens, non caput illius ecclesie. Unde Augustinus super psalmum CXXXIᵘᵐ postquam ostenderat quod nullum preterquam domum suam exaudit Deus ad vitam eternam: Ille inquit ad domum Dei [p. 6] pertinet qui est in caritate compaginatus lapidibus vivis; qui autem caritatem non habuerit, finalis perseverancie ruinam facit, sed domus stat. Et post docet quod nemo debet ex pretense potestatis vicaria pompare vel minari quod ab eo dependet status ecclesie, sicut fecerunt Judei ex filiacione et merito Abrahe, quia filiis imitacione virtutum et non filiis carnis degenerantibus promittitur hereditas. Et sic est indubie quoad Christi vicarios. Itaque inquit Augustinus in hiis est domus Dei quos predestinavit et prescivit perseveraturos, de quibus dictum est: Ubi steterunt sedes eius. Et hii qui non perseverant, non sunt ipsi ecclesia, non ipsi pertinent ad illud modo tabernaculum et tunc domum, sed solum in hiis qui perseveraverint usque in finem.

This is the holy catholic [church]{g:ecclesia} which Christians confess immediately after faith in the
Holy Spirit, for three reasons.[^1] First, because according to Augustine she is the highest
creature;[^2] therefore she is placed immediately after the uncreated Trinity. Second, because by the
love of the Holy Spirit she is joined to Christ in perpetual marriage. And third, because, the Trinity
being posited, it must have a temple or house which it may inhabit: "must", I say, by [necessity of
supposition]{g:necessitas-supposicionis} and [by congruity]{g:de-congruo}.[^3] And this church, in
its [pilgrim part]{g:pars-peregrinans}, has no one [foreknown]{g:prescitus} as a part of itself,[^4]
just as, in its triumphant part, [4] it has no one wretched or in any way stained, while he is
such,[^5] as Augustine shows in *On Christian Teaching* III.32. There, after showing that "the body of
Christ and its head, Christ, are one person", he rebukes Tyconius over his second rule, in which he
calls the church the Lord's bipartite body. "He ought not", he says, "to have called it so; for what
will not remain with him for ever is not truly the Lord's body." Therefore he calls those who are
faithful for a time but foreknown to damnation a simulated church, made of hypocrites.[^6] And this
[teaching]{g:sentencia} can be demonstrated from the scripture of John 6:54, where it is said to the
Jews, and by the same token to every [wayfarer]{g:viator}:[^7] "Except you eat the flesh of the Son of
man and drink his blood, you shall not have life in you." Now no one can be a member of the body of
Christ unless he is a living member; therefore no one can be of that body unless he has eaten the body
of Christ. How, I ask, can he be joined to the sinews of the head unless he has believed in the head
with [formed faith]{g:fides-formata}? And that is what eating is, as Augustine expounds in Homily
25.[^8] Now this food, contrary to bodily foods, has the power of assimilating the eater to itself[^9]
according to four digestions, of which Romans 8:30 speaks: namely, predestination, calling,
justification and glorification.[^10] For although one who eats in this way dies in time, yet he does
not die eternally, since "Christ, the resurrection and the life", at the final judgement gives life to
all his members, because the roaring lion raises up his church.[^11] Now [charity]{g:caritas}, which
"never falleth away", is the bond [5] joining the members of that body; it would therefore be against
reason that either the [bridegroom]{g:sponsus} in his own person, or another by his permission, should
tear his [bride]{g:sponsa} apart by an eternal division. Indeed, if it is well considered, [it implies
a contradiction]{g:claudit-contradictorium},[^12] since it is said to her in Jeremiah 31:3: "I have
loved thee with an everlasting love."[^13] This, then, and not stones, is [our mother]{g:mater-nostra},
confessing her head throughout the world.[^14] How, I ask, would one so powerful, so
[knowing]{g:sciolus} and so benevolent as the uncreated Trinity is,[^15] choose eternally for itself a
bride whom it would finally repudiate? It is clear, therefore, [as faith teaches]{g:ex-fide} and from
the signification [of the term], [what the name]{g:quid-nominis} "catholic church" [means]: that she
is all the [predestinate]{g:predestinatus}, present, past and future.[^16]

From these some conclusions [follow]{g:sequitur}. The first: that no vicar of Christ ought to presume
to assert that he is head of the holy catholic church; indeed, unless he has had a special
revelation, he should not assert that he is any member of it.[^17] This is clear from the fact that
the head of that church must be above all the blessed angels, since they are members of that church;
and this can belong to Christ alone among all the predestinate, as is clear from Hebrews 1:4.[^18] For
in Romans 1:4 Christ is said to be "predestinated"; and so the church is only the number of the
predestinate. Therefore, just as a man without a special revelation would not assert without
misgiving that he is predestinate,[^19] so neither would he assert that he is a member, and
consequently not that he is head, of that church. Hence Augustine on Psalm 131, after he had shown
that God hears no one unto eternal life except his own house:[^20] "He belongs", he says, "to the
house of God [6] who is fitted together in charity with the living stones; but he who has not had the
charity of final perseverance falls in ruin, but the house stands."[^21] And afterwards he teaches that
no one ought, on the ground of the vicarship of a pretended power, to make a show or to threaten that
the state of the church depends on him, as the Jews did on the ground of their sonship to Abraham and
his merit; because the inheritance is promised to sons by the imitation of virtues, and not to
degenerate sons of the flesh. And so it is, without doubt, as regards the vicars of Christ.[^22] "So
then", says Augustine, "the house of God is in those whom he predestined and foreknew would persevere,
of whom it is said, 'Where his seats have stood.'[^23] And those who do not persevere, they are not
the church, they do not belong to that which is now a tabernacle and will then be a house; but [it is]
only in those who will have persevered to the end."[^24]

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** In the Apostles' Creed the article on the church immediately follows *credo in Spiritum
    Sanctum*. Wyclif gives three reasons for the order. B's margin (apparatus to 3.14): *Nota quare
    immediate post fidem in spiritum sanctum ponitur fides de sancta matre catholica*.

[^2]: **S.** *Summa creatura*: not identified by Loserth, and not yet traced (LEDGER V-06).

[^3]: **G.** *Ex necessitate supposicionis*: conditional necessity. *Given* that the Trinity is
    posited as dwelling somewhere, it must have a house. *De congruo*: by fittingness, not by strict
    necessity. The double qualification keeps the church from becoming something God is absolutely
    compelled to have.

[^4]: **G.** ⭐ *Prescitus*, "foreknown": Wyclif's term for those whom God foreknows will be damned.
    It is the counterpart of *predestinatus*, and it is not the same word as *reprobus* (though
    Loserth's side-note at 3.23 says "reprobate"). The claim is that no one who will finally be damned
    is a *part* of this church, whatever his office or present standing. See GLOSSARY §A.

[^5]: **C/R.** *Dum est huiusmodi*: the subject of *est* is ambiguous. It could be the triumphant
    part ("while it is such") or the wretched person ("while he is such"). We follow the early reader
    of MS B, whose gloss Loserth prints (apparatus to 3.24). He takes it of the person: the
    predestinate still wayfarers, "involved in miseries and stains", will later be parts of the church
    triumphant, but are not counted such *while* they are so involved, "and therefore it is said
    pointedly, *while he is such*".

[^6]: **S.** Augustine, *De doctrina christiana* III.32.45, on Tyconius's second rule, *de Domini
    corpore bipartito*; the first rule (III.31.44) is the one on Christ and his body as one person.
    Augustine proposes *de Domini corpore vero atque permixto, aut, vero atque simulato*, "the
    Lord's true and *simulated* body", the source of Wyclif's *ecclesiam similatam*. Augustine has
    *quod cum illo non erit in aeternum*; Wyclif's *manebit* stands for his *erit* (PL 34:82; LEDGER
    V-08). Loserth: *Opp.* III.1, p. 58.

[^7]: **G.** *Per idem*, "by the same token": the saying to the Jews applies by parallel argument to
    every Christian (Thakkar, p. 365). Loserth glosses *viator* "a traveller on earth" (cf. *De civ.
    dom.* I, p. 4).

[^8]: **S.** Augustine, *Tractates on John* 25–26: *crede, et manducasti* (25.12) `[verify section]`
    (LEDGER V-09). Loserth corrects the MSS' XXVI to XXV and refers to *Opp.* III.2, p. 489.

[^9]: **S.** The reversal of ordinary nourishment echoes Augustine, *Confessions* VII.10.16: *nec tu
    me in te mutabis sicut cibum carnis tuae, sed tu mutaberis in me* `[verify; our identification,
    not Loserth's]` (LEDGER V-10).

[^10]: **G/R.** *Digestiones*: the metaphor of eating continues. The chain of Rom. 8:30 is the four
    stages by which the food assimilates the eater. Wyclif gives the nouns; DR has the verbs ("whom he
    predestinated … called … justified … glorified"). B's margin: *Christus (sic) digestio*.

[^11]: **S.** John 11:25 (DR "I am the resurrection and the life") with 6:40 (Loserth). The lion is
    the bestiary lion, whose cubs are born dead and are raised on the third day by their father's
    roar (LEDGER V-11). The *leo rugiens* of 1 Pet. 5:8 is the devil; Wyclif gives the phrase to
    Christ.

[^12]: **G.** *Claudit contradictorium* is stronger than *contra racionem* just before, and the step
    up (*ymmo*) is the argument: given Jer. 31:3, it is not only unreasonable for Christ to lose a
    member of his bride but impossible.

[^13]: **S/G.** DR's wording; the Latin is *in caritate perpetua*, so the bond just named ("charity")
    is the very word of the prophecy.

[^14]: **S/R.** *Non lapides*: the church is not the stone buildings. *Per orbem terrarum confitens*
    echoes the Te Deum, *Te per orbem terrarum sancta confitetur Ecclesia* (LEDGER V-12).

[^15]: **G.** *Sciolus* without contempt (GLOSSARY §D). Power, knowledge and goodness are the three
    attributes appropriated to Father, Son and Spirit.

[^16]: **G/R.** *Ipsa est omnes predestinati*: singular verb, plural predicate. The church *is* the
    predestinate, not a body in which they hold membership, and the English keeps the identity. *Ex
    fide et signacione* pairs the two grounds: what faith teaches, and what the term signifies. The
    definition as Hus restated it heads the articles condemned at Constance `[verify]` (LEDGER V-04).

[^17]: **C.** *Nullus vicarius Christi*: the quantifier governs the whole: no vicar, the pope
    included (A's margin: *Nullus papa debet se presumere esse caput huius ecclesie*). *Assereret*
    (imperfect subjunctive) is potential: "he should not assert", "would not assert".

[^18]: **C/S.** *Capitalem*: literally "the capital [one]", the one who is head. Heb. 1:4: Christ
    "made so much better than the angels". Rom. 1:4: "Who was predestinated the Son of God in power".
    *Numerus* is the number, the full count, of the predestinate.

[^19]: **G.** *Sine formidine*: *formido* is the schools' "fear of the opposite", the doubt that
    attends opinion as against knowledge. Hence "without misgiving", not "without fear".

[^20]: **T/S.** *Ostenderat*: Loserth's correction (B *ostenderet*, AA₁ *ostenderit*). Augustine,
    *Enarrationes in Psalmos* 131; Loserth: *Opp.* IV.2, p. 1477 (LEDGER E01b-1).

[^21]: **C.** *Qui autem caritatem non habuerit, finalis perseverancie ruinam facit*. We take
    *finalis perseverancie* with *caritatem*: "the charity of final perseverance". It could instead
    hang on *ruinam*: "he falls in ruin from final perseverance". Either way the person falls and
    the house stands. *Finalis perseverancie* is Wyclif's addition: Augustine has only *ruinam facit;
    et illo ruente domus stat* (PL 37:1722). *Compaginatus lapidibus vivis*: 1 Pet. 2:5.

[^22]: **T/C.** *Vicaria*: Loserth's correction of the MSS' *vicarie* (apparatus to 6.4); *ex
    pretense potestatis vicaria*, "on the ground of the vicarship of a pretended power". *Pompare*,
    "to make a show". The last sentence is Wyclif's application of Augustine to the vicars of Christ.
    *Filiis imitacione virtutum*: cf. John 8:39, Rom. 9:8.

[^23]: **S/T.** Ps. 131:7: Vg *adorabimus in loco ubi steterunt pedes ejus* (DR "where his feet
    stood"). The print has *sedes*, "seats"; Loserth records no variant. We translate the print; *sedes*
    may be a slip for *pedes*, which Augustine reads here (*Enarr. in Ps.* 131.13, PL 37:1722; LEDGER
    E01b-2). Loserth: Augustine, *loc. cit.*, p. 1480.

[^24]: **G.** *Prescivit perseveraturos*: here the verb is neutral ("foreknew would persevere"), of
    the predestinate, not the technical *prescitus* of 3.24. *Modo tabernaculum et tunc domum*: the
    tabernacle of the wayfaring church now, the house of the homeland then (Ps. 131:7 *tabernaculum*).
