## Outside the four classes (process; not counted)

- **Latin intact.** The `>` blocks agree with `text/incarnacione/bt/pp223–227` line for line.
- **Front matter `italics: none` is wrong.** Harris italicizes *si Deus voluerit* (227.5; plate n0266), and note 23 rightly says so. The italic-run list for this chunk lost it; the field is the coordinator's, not the translator's.
- **Note 11** follows O (*alter quam dyabolus*) against A B C (*aliter quam dyabolus*), both against Harris's omission of the words as a gloss. That is within the card (all four manuscripts have words Harris dropped), and the note gives both readings. The coordinator may want to weigh A B C's *aliter*, which echoes the sentence's own close (*aliter tamen Verbum quam duo alii*); A B C and O are one witness each under the Families rule, so the choice is a judgment, not an error.
- **Note 6** offers "the reason for this diversity" as an alternative "taking *cuius* with *diversitas*": that parse does not yield that English (it would need *racio* nominative), though the sense it reaches (the change of supposition is the reason the two answers differ) is arguably the better one. Not flagged; the note gives the alternative.
- **Density:** 25 notes on 1,109 Latin words, about one per 44 words: above the card's one per 70–100. No note is wrong for that reason, but notes 7 and 21 (print kept, no argument turning on the variant) are the candidates if the coordinator thins.
