About this edition

The Wyclif Society printed John Wyclif’s Latin works in 33 volumes between 1883 and 1922. Most have never been translated into English in full. This edition sets the Society’s Latin beside a new English translation, one work at a time, beginning with the Dialogus.

The Latin

The text of each work is the Society’s edition, which is in the public domain. The editors’ marginal summaries are shown beside the text as they printed them, in their words, not ours.

We check the printed text word by word against several independent witnesses: the OCR of every free scan of the printed book, and the Fordham transcription where there is one. Where the witnesses disagree, or agree on a form that is not a Latin word, the word is read on the page image of the printed book. Each chapter records how its Latin was established, at the end of its notes. The page numbers in the text link to the printed pages on the Internet Archive.

The first work: the Dialogus

Wyclif wrote the Dialogus sive Speculum Ecclesie Militantis (“Dialogue, or The Mirror of the Church Militant”) in 1379. Truth and Falsehood argue over whether the clergy should hold lands and revenues; Truth’s answer is Wyclif’s. The Latin is Alfred W. Pollard’s edition (1886), from the only English manuscript collated with three Vienna manuscripts. It was checked word by word against six independent witnesses: the Fordham transcription and the OCR of five scans of the printed book.

The English

The translation is new. It keeps Wyclif’s technical vocabulary fixed, one Latin term to one English rendering, because his arguments depend on the reader recognising a term when it comes back; the glossary lists them. It keeps every connective in the argument: “for”, “therefore”, “now” (for the second premise of an argument), and “but”. Words the English needs and the Latin does not have are in square brackets.

The notes identify Wyclif’s sources, record where the manuscripts differ from Pollard’s text, and explain how a difficult sentence has been construed. Sources were checked against the texts themselves, not quoted from memory.

How to cite

Cite by the editor’s page and line, as “Dial. 27.5”. Every page has an address: /dialogus/p/27 opens the Dialogus at Pollard’s page 27.
