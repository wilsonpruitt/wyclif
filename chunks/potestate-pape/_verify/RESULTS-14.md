## For the coordinator
1. **P12l-1, translation:** Chrysostom's sentence is *si oportet altum sapere*, "if one must be proud, let it be in poverty". Wyclif drops *altum*, so his *debet sapere in penuria* reads as "ought to be wise in poverty". The English should follow Wyclif's Latin, but the note now shows the difference. Check that the translation doesn't import "proud".
2. **P12g-2:** the attribution changes from Chrysostom to Ps.-Chrysostom (*Opus imperfectum*). The text's "Chrysostom" stays, since Wyclif names him.
3. **P12k-2 and P12k-3:** both stay untraced. If you want a "cf." note for k-2, *De civ. dom.* I cc. 37–40 is the candidate.
4. **P12g-1:** the gloss's "Io." is perhaps Johannes Teutonicus. Wyclif ascribes the gloss to Bernard (of Parma, the ordinary glossator). I have left both names out of the note.
