# *De potestate pape*, chapter 12, part 20: the seventh to twelfth abuses: prayer, burdens, appointments, indulgences, captains, earthly cares

## ¶1 · 389.28–391.10

> [p. 389] Septima quod papa negligat disponere se in mentis mundicia, ut pro subiectis debite interpellet. Hoc enim est inseparabiliter papale officium, cum talis debet tamquam intercessor pacificans inter Deum et populum mediare. Inficitur autem oratoris officium per superbiam, ut patet in sex abusionibus predictis et per avariciam [p. 390] que est alia radix malicie. Sicut enim fistula per quam transiret liquor inficitur per metalli rubiginem inherentem, sic inficitur rectoris fistula ad orandum, quando temporalibus nimis afficitur, cum ad hoc consequitur servitus ydolorum; et sicut liquor non descendit per fistulam ad subiectum, nisi proporcionaliter ad scaturiginem sit depressa, sic sacerdos orando non mediat inter Deum et populum nisi deponendo elacionem de se ipso humiliter senciat, subiciendo se debite quoad Deum. Ideo caveret papa, quomodo de necessitate salutis tenetur orare pro populo, et precipue emundare spirituale organum ab istis duobus criminibus; aliter enim nedum omittendo peccaret, sed mediando inficeret gregem suum, dicente Gregorio primo Pastoralis X: Cuncti, inquit, liquido novimus, quia cum is qui displicet ad intercedendum mittitur, irati animus ad deteriora provocatur. Qui igitur adhuc desideriis terrenis astringitur, caveat ne districti iram iudicis gravius accendens, dum loco delectatur glorie fiat subditis autor ruine. Octava abusio est quod papa oneret se infundabiliter onere alieno. Hic autem exprimit Gregorius primo Pastoralis sub hiis verbis: Hinc precipites colligant cum quanta culpa ex proprio appetitu ceteris preferri non metuunt, si sancti viri plebium ducatum suscipere Deo eciam iubente timuerunt. Moyses suadente Domino trepidat, infirmus quisque, ut honoris onus suscipiat, anhelat; et qui ad casum valde urgetur ex propriis, humerum libenter opprimendus submittit ponderibus alienis; que egit ferre non valet, eciam auget, quod [p. 391] portet. Ex istis videtur quod prelacia ecclesiastica sine peccato saltem veniali non poterit acceptari. Omnes enim quotquot venerunt sunt fures et latrones, qui accipiunt illud propter honorem vel commodum temporale, quod plus prelaciam quesitam sollicitudo et actus eorum diiudicant. Si igitur sine tali obligacione superaddita possumus plus vel tantum prodesse populo, ad quid querimus vincula temporalium et professione superaddita colligari?

The seventh: that the [pope]{g:papa} neglects to dispose himself in purity of mind so as to intercede duly for his subjects. For this is inseparably the papal office, since such a one ought, as a peacemaking intercessor, to mediate between God and the people. And the office of the [suppliant]{g:orator} is infected by pride, as is clear in the six abuses aforesaid, and by avarice, [390] which is the other root of malice.[^1] For as a pipe through which liquor would pass is infected by the rust of the metal clinging to it, so the ruler's pipe for praying is infected when he is too much affected by temporal things, since upon this follows the servitude of idols; and as the liquor does not descend through the pipe to what lies beneath unless it is lowered in proportion to the spring, so the priest in praying does not mediate between God and the people unless, putting down elation, he thinks humbly of himself, subjecting himself duly with respect to God. Therefore let the pope take heed how he is bound, of necessity of salvation, to pray for the people, and above all to cleanse the spiritual organ from these two crimes; for otherwise he would not only sin by omitting, but by mediating would infect his flock, as Gregory says, *Pastoral* I.10: "We all know clearly," he says, "that when one who displeases is sent to intercede, the mind of the angered one is provoked to worse. Let him therefore who is still bound by earthly desires beware lest, kindling the more heavily the wrath of the strict judge, while he delights in the place of glory he become to his subjects the author of ruin."[^2] The eighth abuse is that the pope burdens himself [without foundation]{g:infundabiliter} with another's burden. And this Gregory expresses, *Pastoral* I, in these words: "Hence let the headlong gather with how great a fault they do not fear to be preferred to others from their own appetite, if holy men feared to undertake the leadership of peoples even at God's bidding. Moses trembles when the Lord persuades him; every weak man pants to undertake the burden of honour; and he who is sorely pressed toward a fall by his own [weights] willingly puts his shoulder, to be crushed, under the weights of others; what he has done he cannot bear, and he even increases what [391] he must carry."[^3] From this it seems that ecclesiastical prelacy cannot be accepted without at least venial sin. For "all, as many as have come, are thieves and robbers" who accept it for honour or temporal advantage, which their care and their acts, more than the prelacy sought, make plain.[^4] If therefore, without such an obligation added on, we can profit the people more or as much, to what end do we seek the chains of temporal things, and to be bound by a profession added on?

## ¶2 · 391.11–391.27

> [p. 391] Et sub ista octava stulticia sunt mille alie germinantes, ut nona abusio est, quod papa presumit per totum christianismum statuere duces spirituales et medicos, pro quorum ducatu oportet ipsum summo iudici respondere. Sic, inquam, non fecerunt Petrus, Gregorius et ceteri sancti ecclesie primitive, sed permiserunt aliorum apostolorum instituciones et expertorum elecciones procedere. Nam hoc debet populus plus curare, dicente Gregorio primo Pastoralis IX capitulo: Cum curam populi electus presul suscipit, quasi ad egrum medicus accedit. Unde creditur quod provisiones, reservaciones et privilegiaciones tam amplas induxit non amor salutis subditi vel excellens specularis prudencia sed mater cupiditas; cum ubi lucrum tollitur cessant constituciones huiusmodi, eciam licet magis prodessent ecclesie.

And under this eighth folly a thousand others are sprouting, as the ninth abuse is that the pope presumes throughout all Christendom to appoint spiritual leaders and physicians, for whose leadership he must answer to the highest judge. Not so, I say, did Peter, Gregory and the other saints of the primitive [church]{g:ecclesia} do; but they allowed the institutions of the other apostles and the elections of experienced men to proceed. For the people ought to care more for this, as Gregory says, *Pastoral* I, chapter 9: "When the bishop chosen undertakes the care of the people, he comes as a physician to the sick." Hence it is believed that not love of the subject's salvation, nor the excellent prudence of a watchman, but mother cupidity brought in [provisions]{g:provisio}, reservations and [privilegings]{g:privilegiacio} so ample; since where gain is taken away constitutions of this kind cease, even though they would profit the church more.[^5]

## ¶3 · 391.28–392.7

> [p. 391] Decima abusio est, quod papa presumit ultra Christum vel sanctos apostolos concedere sic cece indulgencias et privilegia, cum ignorat communiter si Deus hec approbat, vel ad utriusque partis deterius reprehendit. Nam vel est indulgencia talis sophistica vel personalis et empta vel dignis quibus proficeret nimis parca. Si igitur [p. 392] servus timeret sibi expendere corporalem thesaurum domini dominorum, et specialiter presumendo blasfeme potestatem et modum proprium regi regum, cum enim talis non humiliter intrat per ostium, sed ascendit superbe dirumpens tectum ecclesie caritatem, et sophisticat thesaurum Domini, restat ut in finali iudicio condempnetur.

The tenth abuse is that the pope presumes, beyond Christ or the holy apostles, to grant [indulgences]{g:indulgencie} and [privileges]{g:privilegium} so blindly, since he commonly does not know whether God approves them, or reproves them, to the worse of both parties. For such an indulgence is either sophistical, or personal and bought, or too sparing to the worthy whom it would profit. If therefore [392] a servant should fear to spend for himself the bodily treasure of the lord of lords, and especially by blasphemously presuming the [power]{g:potestas} and manner proper to the king of kings (for such a one does not enter humbly by the door, but climbs up proudly, breaking through the roof of the church, [charity]{g:caritas}, and [counterfeits]{g:sophisticare} the Lord's treasure), it remains that he be condemned in the final judgment.[^6]

## ¶4 · 392.9–392.21

> [p. 392] Undecima abusio est quod papa in preficiendo duces christiani exercitus contra dyabolum, in preiudicium Christi acceptat personas vel respicit suum commodum temporale. Nichil enim convinceret evidencius quod venit proditor Antichristus, cum subdole seducit christianum exercitum per capitaneos subdolos secte sue, ymo cum Christus in paciendo accipit denominaciones membrorum suorum, ut patet Matth. XXV, manifestum est quod falsius, nequius et multiplicius quam Scarioth crudelioribus hostibus legis veteris tradunt Christum, ut tangit Crysostomus Omelia XXVII Operis Imperfecti.

The eleventh abuse is that the pope, in setting leaders over the Christian army against the devil, accepts persons to the prejudice of Christ, or looks to his own temporal advantage. For nothing would prove more evidently that Antichrist the traitor has come than when he craftily seduces the Christian army through crafty [captains]{g:capitaneus} of his sect; indeed, since Christ in suffering takes the [names]{g:denominacio} of his [members]{g:membrum}, as is clear in Matthew 25, it is manifest that they hand Christ over more falsely, more wickedly and more manifoldly than Iscariot did, to enemies crueller than those of the old law, as Chrysostom touches on in Homily 27 of the *Opus imperfectum*.[^7]

## ¶5 · 392.22–393.30

> [p. 392] Duodecima abusio est quod papa tam in persona sua quam in suis satrapis innititur tam studio quam praxi contrariis legi Christi, cum lex Dei et ars curandi sua postponitur et ars cesarea vel antichristiana inducitur. Et istum abusum Gregorius sepe conquiritur, unde mandat libro II cap. primo: Quod rector sit pre cunctis contemplacione suspensus internorum, curam in exteriorum occupacioue non minuens. Cum enim predicare verbum Dei et curare animas secundum artem Christi sit precipuum pape officium, patet quod ad eum spectat dimissis curis terrenis verbo illi intendere. Et idem credit iste sanctus in monitu legis Dei. [p. 393] Unde libro II cap. XVIII: Dum, inquit, per animarum presulem terreni exercetur officium iudicis, a gregis custodia vacat cura pastoris et subiecti veritatis lumen apprehendere nequeunt, quia dum pastoris sensus terrena iudicia occupant, vento temptacionis impulsus, ecclesie oculos pulvis cecat. Quod cum Christus a ventris voracitate compesceret, adiunxit dicens: Attendite igitur vobis, ne graventur corda vestra in crapula et ebrietate, illico sic adiunxit: Aut in curis huius vite. Ubi pavorem adiciens, ne forte, inquit, superveniat dies illa repentina, cuius qualitatem denuncians: Tamquam laqueus, inquit, veniat super omnes, qui habitant super faciem omnis terre. Hinc iterum dicit: Nemo potest duobus dominis servire; hinc Paulus religiosorum mentes a mundi consorcio conveniendo suspendit dicens: Nemo militans Deo implicat se negociis secularibus, ut ei placet cui se probavit. Hinc ecclesie rectoribus et vacandi studia precipit et consulendi remedia ostendit dicens: Secularia ergo iudicia, si habueritis contemptibiles qui sunt in ecclesia illos constituite ad iudicandum, ut illi videlicet dispensacionibus terrenis inserviant, quos dona spiritualia non exornant, ut quia penetrare intima nequeunt saltem foris necessaria operentur. Hinc Moyses a Getro alienigena iudicatur quod terrenis negociis populorum stulto labore deserviat, cui consilium mox prebetur, ut pro se alios ad iurgia dirimenda constituat, et ipse liberius ad erudiendos populos spiritualia archana cognoscat.

The twelfth abuse is that the pope, both in his own person and in his [satraps]{g:satrapa}, relies both in study and in practice on things contrary to the [law of Christ]{g:lex-christi}, since [God's law]{g:lex-dei} and its art of curing is put after, and the caesarean or antichristian art is brought in. And this abuse Gregory often complains of; hence he enjoins, book II, chapter 1: "That the ruler be, before all, suspended in contemplation; not lessening his care of inward things in the busyness of outward things."[^8] For since preaching the word of God and curing souls according to Christ's art is the pope's chief office, it is clear that it belongs to him, setting aside earthly cares, to attend to that word. And this saint believes the same [to be] in the admonition of God's law. [393] Hence book II, chapter 18: "While," he says, "the office of an earthly judge is exercised by the bishop of souls, the shepherd's care is idle from guarding the flock, and the subjects cannot apprehend the light of truth; because while earthly judgments occupy the shepherd's mind, the dust, driven by the wind of temptation, blinds the eyes of the church. When Christ was restraining [men] from the voracity of the belly, he added, saying: Take heed therefore to yourselves, lest your hearts be overcharged with surfeiting and drunkenness; at once he added thus: or with the cares of this life. Where, adding fear: lest perhaps, he says, that day come upon you suddenly; declaring its quality: As a snare, he says, it shall come upon all that dwell upon the face of the whole earth. Hence again he says: No man can serve two masters. Hence Paul, addressing the minds of [the religious]{g:religiosi}, suspends them from the fellowship of the world, saying: No man, being a soldier to God, entangleth himself with secular businesses: that he may please him to whom he hath engaged himself. Hence he both enjoins on the rulers of the church the pursuits of leisure and shows the remedies of counsel, saying: If therefore you have judgments of things pertaining to this world, set them to judge who are the most despised in the church; that is, that those may serve earthly dispensations who are not adorned with spiritual gifts, so that, because they cannot penetrate inward things, they may at least work the necessary things without. Hence Moses is judged by Jethro, a foreigner, in that he serves the earthly business of the peoples with foolish labour; and counsel is soon given him to appoint others in his place for settling quarrels, so that he himself may more freely come to know spiritual secrets for instructing the peoples."[^9]

## Notes

[^1]: **C/S.** *Oratoris officium*: the office of one who prays to God for the people, so "suppliant" (GLOSSARY *orator*), not the bedesman paid to pray. *Alia radix malicie*: avarice as the root of evils (1 Tim. 6:10), beside pride.
[^2]: **S/T.** Gregory, *Regula pastoralis* I.10 (PL 77, 23C; LEDGER P12t-1). *Servitus ydolorum*: Eph. 5:5 (DR "covetous person (which is a serving of idols)"). B reads *gloriose* for *glorie* (390.21).
[^3]: **T/S.** Gregory, *Reg. past.* I.7 (PL 77, 20D–21A; Loserth's foot gives chapter V; LEDGER P12t-2); Moses' fear: Exod. 4:10–13. *Que egit ferre non valet* is the print; the manuscripts read *quem eger ferre* (390.33). The print is Gregory's text (*quae egit ferre non valet, et auget quod portet*, PL 77, 21A), "what he has done he cannot bear"; we translate the print. The manuscripts' margin numbers the abuse *8*.
[^4]: **S/C.** John 10:8 (DR "All others, as many as have come, are thieves and robbers"). *Quod plus prelaciam quesitam sollicitudo et actus eorum diiudicant*: we take *quod* as the motive (honour or advantage) and *plus prelaciam quesitam* as "more than the prelacy sought"; doubtful. The alternative: "which their care and acts judge [to weigh] more [than] the prelacy sought". The manuscripts have no stop at 391.2.
[^5]: **C/S.** Gregory, *Reg. past.* I.9 (PL 77, 22D, which reads *causam populi* for Wyclif's *curam populi*; LEDGER P12t-3). *Specularis prudencia*: the prudence of a watchman (*speculator*, the bishop's name); "of a mirror" is the alternative. The manuscripts' margin numbers the abuse *9*.
[^6]: **C/S.** The period has no clear main clause for *si*: we take *restat ut … condempnetur* as its close and the *cum enim* clause as a parenthesis; a lost comparison (such as *quanto magis*) is possible. The *corporalis thesaurus* is set against the spiritual treasury from which indulgences are drawn. *Intrat per ostium … ascendit*: John 10:1 (DR "climbeth up another way"). A has *iudicio* only in the margin (392.7).
[^7]: **S/C.** Matt. 25:40 (DR "as long as you did it to one of these my least brethren, you did it to me"). Pseudo-Chrysostom, *Opus imperfectum in Matthaeum*, hom. 27 as Wyclif numbers it; Migne's hom. 27 (on Matt. 11) has nothing to the point, and the nearest passage found is hom. 35 (PG 56, 823ff., on Matt. 20:19), where the priests of the present *tradunt* the word of truth *gentibus*, Christians in name and gentiles in manners; the source is untraced (LEDGER P12t-4). *Crudelioribus hostibus legis veteris*: we take it as "to enemies crueller than [those] of the old law"; "to the crueller enemies of the old law" is the alternative. The subject of *tradunt* is the captains of Antichrist's sect. The manuscripts' margin numbers the abuse *11*.
[^8]: **S/C/T.** Gregory, *Reg. past.* II.1 (PL 77, 27A; LEDGER P12t-5). The print sets a comma after *internorum*; we join *internorum* to *curam*, as the balance with *exteriorum* asks; with the print's stop, "suspended in the contemplation of inward things" is the alternative. B lacks *non* (392.30), which would reverse the sense. The manuscripts' margin numbers the abuse *12*.
[^9]: **S/C.** Gregory, *Reg. past.* II.7 (PL 77, 39A–D; Loserth: *est cap. VII sec. partis*; the text's *XVIII* is the older running count, which Migne notes as *Al. XVIII*; LEDGER P12t-6); the quotation runs on into the next part. Within it: Luke 21:34–35 (DR), with *habitant* for the Vulgate *sedent* ("sit"); Luke 16:13 (DR, without *servus*; cf. Matt. 6:24); 2 Tim. 2:4 (Loserth: "I Tim."), with *placet* for *placeat*; 1 Cor. 6:4 (DR); Exod. 18:17–18 (DR "foolish labour"). *Conveniendo*: "addressing", calling to account. *Vacandi studia*: freedom for contemplation, set against *consulendi remedia*.
