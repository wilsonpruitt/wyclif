## (a) *potestas* vs *potencia*

In this chunk Wyclif does not distinguish the two words. He equates them. He doubles them as synonyms (*potestas vel potencia*, 4.7; *potestatem sive potenciam*, 4.11). He calls the uncreated *potencia* just divided *potestas* (3.3) and says that "every such *potestas* … is the divine nature, which is that *potencia*" (3.9). He pluralises Anselm's *potestas servandi rectitudinem* as *tales potencie* (4.5). FitzRalph's line (2.4) contrasts scope (power in general against the power of rational nature), not two concepts. "Potency" is right where Wyclif is working Aristotle's *dynamis*: active and passive potency (2.8, 2.12, 3.21), "terminating the potency" (2.24), the division of potency (2.25–26), Aristotle's rational potency (3.27, 3.32), and the acquired habit (4.13). It reads wrong in two places. One is Anselm's free choice (4.5), where it imports potentiality. The other is God's power (2.26–3.9, 3.31), where it is defensible only because Wyclif carries the Aristotelian division into the Trinity (*cuius actum terminat Verbum*); there it needs a C note that active potency in God is not unrealised potentiality. **Ruling recommended:** *potestas* "power", fixed (it has no plain row: add one). *Potencia* becomes **CONTEXTUAL**: "potency" in the Aristotelian act/potency analysis, including its extension to the uncreated potency in ch. 1; "power" for ability or might outside that analysis (Anselm's *potencie*, God's power in ordinary theological use, and so consistent with the existing *potencia absoluta* "absolute power" and *potencia regulata racione* "power regulated by reason" rows). Doublets stay visible as "power or potency". A **C** note goes at the first occurrence in each chunk and wherever the sense leans the other way. Note that *virtus* also has a "power (faculty)" row: where *virtus* and *potencia* meet, a C note should say which Latin lies behind "power".

## (b) Card changes before the volume runs

1. **Replace the *potestas/potencia* sentence** ("Keep … distinct wherever Wyclif distinguishes them (ch. 1 sets them side by side)") with the ruling in (a). As written it led the translator to a blanket split, a headnote claim (row 16) and a note (row 13) that the Latin contradicts.
2. **Say what to do with OCR scars in the Latin block.** Either "leave the token, translate the print's word, report it" or "repair it and log it in Transcription notes". The current wording ("stay out of the chunk" / "the coordinator fixes the Latin" / "never alter the Latin") was read as licence to edit silently.
3. **Italic runs inside indirect speech:** say whether a scriptural phrase that Loserth italicises inside a *quod/quomodo* clause gets quotation marks or a note. Three sites here were left unmarked.
4. **Headnote:** the card's list for 01-a ("work, date, editor, sigla, the compendium") does not match CONVENTIONS §12 (occasion, text, move, where answered, in that order). The headnote here has no "where it is answered" movement and runs its sections out of §12's order. Align the card with §12.
5. **Connective table:** add *vero* in enumerations (*secundo/quarto vero modo*, "and"), *verumtamen* ("nevertheless"), *unde* ("hence"), and a line that distributive *nunc … aliquando* is never "now" (row 11). The translator handled the first three correctly, so this is a safeguard, not a fix.
6. **Numerals:** a one-line warning that Loserth's old-style ordinals (3°, 1°) can print or OCR as *30*, *10*. The same misprint must be read the same way twice (row 7).
7. **Glossary proposals for this volume:** *potestas* "power" (fixed); *potencia* CONTEXTUAL (a); *potentatus* "sovereignty" / "the one who holds sovereignty" by referent; *potestas dominativa* "lordly power"; *describere / descripcio* "describe / description" (§D, not "define"); *relacio racionis* "relation of reason"; *debitum* (conjugal) "the [marriage] debt" (§D).
