# Wyclif site — design plan (v0, 2026-09-29)

**Subject.** Wyclif's *Dialogus* (1379): a disputation between Truth and Falsehood over whether the church
should own property. Readers: students and scholars of Wyclif, Lollardy and late-medieval ecclesiology;
curious church readers. Job: read the Latin and the English side by side, with Pollard's side-notes,
the notes, and the glossary to hand, and cite by page.line.

**The one bold thing: the disputation made visible.** Every English speech carries a colour band for its
speaker, Truth in *russet* (the undyed russet gowns of Wyclif's poor priests) and Falsehood in *gilt*
(the endowed church's gold). The home page opens with the whole work drawn as a strip: one column per
chapter, height = length, colour = who is speaking. It shows at a glance what Pollard says in prose:
Falsehood's voice thins out, and after ch. 3 Truth is Wyclif. The strip is grouped by Pollard's four
movements (1–10 scripture, 11–23 authority, 24–33 practical objections, 34–36 conclusion, then the Epilogue).

**Colour.** paper #E7EAE2 (cool linen, greenish grey, not cream) · ink #33291F (iron-gall brown) ·
russet #7C3F24 · gilt #7A5F12 (text) / #B08A2E (bands) · rule #B9BDB2 · margin grey #5A5E55.
Dark: paper #1C1B17 · ink #DCD7CB · russet #CE8E68 · gilt #D4B55E · rule #45433B.

**Type.** One family: Libre Caslon Text (body, both languages) + Libre Caslon Display (the title only).
Caslon is the old-style of Victorian English printing, the world of the Wyclif Society edition this text
comes from. Speaker names are set as Pollard sets them, in small capitals. No sans, no mono.
No all-caps labels, no eyebrows, no middle-dot meta strings, no arrows on links.

**Layout.** Chapter page: a three-track grid, Pollard's side-notes in a narrow left rail (as in his
margins), Latin (narrower, slightly smaller) then English (the reading column). Speech bands run down the
left edge of the English. Page marks sit in the rail as plate links. Notes follow the chapter in one
column. Below 900px: English first, the Latin folded under each passage.

```
| rail 10rem       | Latin 1fr        | English 1.3fr      |
| 26.28 Mend.      | [p.26] MENDACIUM | ▌FALSEHOOD. Again… |
|  objects:        | Iterum manifeste |▌                   |
| p. 27 (plate)    |                  | ▌TRUTH. It has been|
```
**Principles.** The text leads; chrome is minimal. Colour means speaker and nothing else. Every
structural device encodes something real (page numbers are Pollard's pages; bands are speakers).
