### 12-a ¶2 · 317.4–317.16  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 317] **Sed** ambos istos simul convinco tripliciter, primo in hoc quod perfecte stetit ecclesia cum seculari dominio trecentis annis post ascensionem Christi et amplius, quando non fuit talis ceca et venenosa dotacio. Si igitur tunc crevit utrumque istorum membrorum ecclesie, et post decrevit, eciam quousque utrumque istorum capitum reliquum destruxerit ad non gradum, quomodo non est evidens, quod utramque istarum parcium foret viare plus prospere quoad Deum et homines reducta ad ordinem ecclesie quem Christus instituit, quam sit irreligiose commixta in ritibus ex auctoritate cesarea.

ENGLISH: But I refute both of these together in three ways: first, in that the [church]{g:ecclesia} stood perfectly, together with secular lordship, for three hundred years and more after Christ's ascension, when there was no such blind and poisonous [endowment]{g:dotacio}. If, then, each of these [members]{g:membrum} of the church grew at that time, and afterwards declined, even until each of these heads should destroy the other to the point of nothing, how is it not evident that each of these parts would [fare on its way]{g:viator} more prosperously, as regards God and men, brought back to the order of the church which Christ instituted, than it does now, irreligiously mingled in [rites]{g:ritus} by caesarean [authority]{g:auctoritas}.[^2]

### 12-a ¶5 · 318.1–318.26  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 318] Et quantum ad legem civilem, patet quod imperator sicut et quilibet civilis dominus tenetur defendere suum dominium seculare vel mutare in melius, **sed** in solucione ordinis cleri Christo deterioratur utraque pars saltem in spiritualibus quoad Deum, igitur repugnat principiis legis humane talis prepositi spiritualis mundana dotacio. Item, prius tempore et natura est secularis dominacio quam talis cleri dotacio, ut docet Crysostomus In Imperfecto Omelia XXXIV, **sed** omne sic prius potest esse tali posteriori subducto, igitur possibile est ecclesiam viare prospere sine tali capitaneo. Mirum itaque foret, quod ita fuit de facto ex ordinacione divina, et cum modo non potest sic esse propter ordinacionem cesaream, sic quod ordinacionem Christi licet extinguere, **sed** ordinacio cesaris non potest deficere stante prosperitate ecclesie. Inconsequenter igitur loquuntur qui dicunt quod ecclesia potest prospere procedere sine Romano imperio adiuvante, ut patuit in primitiva ecclesia, et patet hodie de multis christianis, exemtis ab illo imperio infirmato, **sed** non potest stare sine suo episcopo vel ab eo collato privilegio. Numquid fumus est maioris constancie quam ignis?

ENGLISH: And as to the civil law, it is clear that the emperor, like any civil [lord]{g:dominus}, is bound to defend his secular lordship or to change it for the better; now in the loosening of the clergy's order to Christ each part is made worse, at least in spiritual things as regards God; therefore such worldly endowment of a spiritual [superior]{g:prepositi} [is incompatible]{g:repugnare} with the principles of human law.[^5] Again, the secular [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio} is prior in time and nature to such endowment of the clergy, as Chrysostom teaches in the *Opus imperfectum*, homily 34;[^6] now everything so prior can exist with such a posterior thing withdrawn; therefore it is possible for the church to fare prosperously on its way without such a captain. It would therefore be a wonder that it was so in fact by divine [ordinance]{g:ordinacio}, while now it cannot be so because of the caesarean ordinance, so that it is lawful to extinguish Christ's ordinance, but Caesar's ordinance cannot fail while the church's prosperity stands. Those, therefore, speak inconsistently who say that the church can go forward prosperously without the help of the Roman empire, as was clear in the primitive church and is clear today of many Christians exempt from that weakened empire, but cannot stand without its bishop or the privilege conferred by it. Is smoke, then, of greater constancy than fire?[^7]

### 12-a ¶6 · 318.27–318.30  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 318] Nec valet ficticia qua dicitur, quod ordinacio pape processit immediate a Christo, ut patet de Petro, ordinacio **autem** imperatoris processit ab homine.

ENGLISH: Nor is the [fiction]{g:ficticia} valid by which it is said that the [pope]{g:papa}'s ordinance proceeded immediately from Christ, as is clear of Peter, but the emperor's ordinance proceeded from man.[^8]

### 12-a ¶7 · 318.31–319.34  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 318] In ista **autem** materia necesse foret utramque partem cognoscere conclusiones ad confutandum cautelas dyaboli. Necesse est secundo distinguere inter papam, vel episcopum cesareum et ministrum [p. 319] simplicem christianum; nam iuxta dicta talis cesareus innititur privilegiis humanis tamquam principiis per se constituentibus servum talem. Minister **autem** pure christianus irridet hec privilegia ut superflua vel nociva. Et patet tercio defectus argucie, quando sic arguitur: Ordinacio pape immediate processit a Christo, ut patet de Petro, igitur ordinacio pape cesarei sic processit ex ordinacione Christi. Per papam Petrum et suos socios adiutores regebatur ecclesia, ergo per idem ex ordinacione Constantini oportet quod per gradus prelatorum cesareos regatur ecclesia; Christus indubie docens Matth. XX et Luce XXII maioritatem, quam sui debent attendere, negat istam arguciam. Si igitur Christus per se constituit capitaneum tocius militantis ecclesie, nec Petrus vel alius apostolus audebat talem capitaneum constituere vel se ipsum huiusmodi vendicare, **sed** constituerunt per loca episcopos, quanta irreligiosa presumpcio in cesare statuere, quod generaliter suus episcopus foret caput tocius ecclesie aut quod maioritatem civilem sub pallio privilegii contra decretum Christi in ecclesiam suam induceret? Ordinacio **autem** regis longe ante hec tempora processit a Deo, ut patet II De Civitate Dei XII, sic quod, licet Christus tempore legis veteris approbavit dominium seculare divinitus, et tempore legis nove approbavit multipliciter imperatorem, regem et dominum secularem humanitus, tamen nedum layci **sed** professores scripture verecunde proclamant, quando deficiunt argumenta, quod per idem vel forcius deficeret in ecclesia quodcumque dominium seculare.

ENGLISH: And in this matter it would be necessary for each party to know the conclusions, in order to confute the devil's [wiles]{g:cautela}. It is necessary, secondly, to distinguish between the pope, or caesarean [bishop]{g:episcopus}, and the [319] simple Christian minister; for, according to what has been said, such a caesarean leans on human privileges as on principles that of themselves constitute such a servant. But the purely Christian minister laughs at these privileges as superfluous or harmful. And, thirdly, the defect of the quibble is clear when it is argued thus: "The pope's ordinance proceeded immediately from Christ, as is clear of Peter; therefore the ordinance of the caesarean pope so proceeded from Christ's ordinance. The church was ruled by Pope Peter and his companions and helpers; therefore, by the same token, by Constantine's ordinance the church must be ruled through the caesarean grades of [prelates]{g:prelatus}." Christ, who undoubtedly teaches in Matthew 20 and Luke 22 the [greatness]{g:majoritas} his own ought to attend to, denies this quibble.[^9] If, then, Christ himself set up the captain of the whole [church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans}, and neither Peter nor any other apostle dared to set up such a captain or to claim himself to be such, but they set up bishops place by place, how great an irreligious presumption [was it] in Caesar to decree that his bishop should be, in general, head of the whole church, or to bring civil greatness into his church under the cloak of privilege against Christ's decree? But the ordinance of a king proceeded from God long before these times, as is clear from *De civitate Dei* II.12,[^10] so that, although Christ in the time of the old law approved secular lordship divinely, and in the time of the new law approved in many ways emperor, king and secular lord humanly, yet not only laymen but professors of scripture shamefacedly proclaim, when arguments fail them, that by the same token or more strongly any secular lordship whatever would fail in the church.[^11]

### 12-a ¶8 · 320.1–320.21  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 320] **Sed** ista deduccio non habet fundamentum, nisi ut ironice loquar, quod autor scripture crebro ponit secularem primatum et sacerdotalem primatum cesareum interdicit. Et ideo dicit Crysostomus ut supra, quod in acceptando primatum ecclesiasticum neque est racio neque utilitas, quia tempore suo saltem in gente sua non fuit adiuncta primatui ecclesiastico, ut modo, pinguedo commodi temporalis. Si igitur signetur quicumque papa, cuius ordinacio immediate processit a Christo, videtur michi quod hoc docto totus populus debet eum venerabiliter amplecti. **Sed** nimis aliena est ab ista doctrina humana eleccio vel institucio. Ideo oportet quod illud doceatur ex vite et operum sanctitate; non ex aspiracione ad seculi commodum vel honorem. Nec ex fulminacione ficte censure ad ostendendum dominacionem nec ex tonsione gregis subiecti per fraudulentam temporalium subtraccionem, **sed** per condiciones apostolorum istis oppositas; et talis papa non foret alicui fidelium onerosus **sed** procurans undique pro lucro anime et profectu.

ENGLISH: But this deduction has no foundation, unless, to speak ironically, that the author of scripture often sets down a secular [primacy]{g:primatus} and forbids a caesarean priestly primacy. And so Chrysostom says, as above, that in accepting ecclesiastical primacy there is neither reason nor profit, because in his time, at least in his nation, the fatness of temporal advantage was not joined to ecclesiastical primacy, as it is now.[^12] If, then, any pope whatever be pointed out whose ordinance proceeded immediately from Christ, it seems to me that, this being shown, the whole people ought to embrace him with reverence. Now human election or institution is far too foreign to this [doctrine]{g:doctrina}.[^13] Therefore it must be shown from the holiness of life and works, not from aspiring to the world's advantage or honour; nor from the thundering of a feigned [censure]{g:censura} to display the exercise of lordship, nor from the shearing of the subject flock by a fraudulent withdrawal of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, but by the conditions of the apostles that are opposed to these; and such a pope would be burdensome to none of the faithful, but would make provision everywhere for the gain and progress of the soul.

### 12-b ¶1 · 320.23–321.10  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 320] Item, magnificantes necessitatem huius sacramenti cesarei oportet docere ipsam ex testimonio scripture vel per racionem ostendere necessarium influxum papalis preeminencie, quia aliter fingi posset novella religio servatis eisdem nominibus vel mutatis omnino contraria ordini christiano. Constat **autem**, quod primum deest in toto corpore scripture. Et quantum ad secundum, patet quod oporteret illum influxum vel adiutorium esse spirituale, quia sine corporali preter adiutorium quod ecclesia habet de seculari brachio posset stare. Si **autem** sit spirituale vel est actus anime vel habitus. Cum **autem** actus anime sint nobis notissimi, perceptibilis foret tam talis actus quam eius absencia in morte, in divisione vel [p. 321] omissione pape et in eius operacione apostolica; quando magis copiose et attente influeret? **Sed** nedum abest a nobis talis experimentalis noticia, **sed** propinquiores huic fonti sunt magis a Dei instinctibus alieni, ymo cum papa sit crebro infidelis vel expers donorum Dei quam eius subditi magis sancti, videtur quod non oportet regulariter hunc solem eclipsabilem sic in quoscumque lucidiores eciam plus tenebrosum influere.

ENGLISH: Again, those who magnify the necessity of this caesarean sacrament must teach it from the testimony of scripture, or show by reason the necessary [inflow]{g:influxus} of papal [preeminence]{g:preeminencia}, because otherwise a new religion could be feigned, with the same names kept or changed, wholly contrary to the Christian order.[^1] Now it is evident that the first is lacking in the whole body of scripture. And as to the second, it is clear that that inflow or help would have to be spiritual, because without bodily [help], beyond the help that the [church]{g:ecclesia} has from [the secular arm]{g:brachium-seculare}, it could stand. And if it is spiritual, it is either an act of the soul or a habit. Now since the acts of the soul are most known to us, both such an act and its absence would be perceptible at the death, the division or the [321] omission of a [pope]{g:papa}, and in his apostolic working: when would it flow in more copiously and attentively? But not only is such experiential knowledge absent from us; those nearer to this fountain are even more estranged from the promptings of God; indeed, since the pope is often more [an unbeliever]{g:infidelis}, or more devoid of the gifts of God, than his more holy subjects, it seems that this eclipsable sun, even when more darkened, need not [as a rule]{g:regulariter} so flow into any who are brighter than it.[^2]

### 12-b ¶2 · 321.11–321.25  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 321] Nec valet dicere quod papa dat habitum, quia sic extolleretur super omne quod dicitur Deus, dans quantumcumque distanter eciam personis Deo indignis spiritualia dona Dei, quia quid blasphemius vel infidelius quam quod oportet Deum cum omni tali concurrere complendo quicquid promiserit. Unde si habitus vel donum spirituale Dei quiescat, sic quod ex influxu pape in animam subiecti quemque inclinaret ad opus, quod non experitur ecclesia, **sed** eius contrarium, tunc enim deesset Petro et aliis apostolis influencia illa cesarea, et cesare excedente Christum et eius vicarium correspondenter ad dotacionem vel ritus cesareos, cresceret vel decresceret potestas taliter influendi.

ENGLISH: Nor is it valid to say that the pope gives the habit, because so he would be "lifted up above all that is called God", giving the spiritual gifts of God at however great a distance, even to persons unworthy of God; for what is more blasphemous or more unbelieving than that God must concur with every such [giving], fulfilling whatever he has promised?[^3] Hence if a habit or spiritual gift of God should rest [in the soul], so that from the pope's inflow into the subject's soul it inclined each one to work (which the church does not experience, but its contrary), [the claim fails]; for then that caesarean [influence]{g:influencia} would have been lacking to Peter and the other apostles, and, Caesar exceeding Christ and his [vicar]{g:vicarius-christi}, the [power]{g:potestas} of so flowing in would grow or decrease in correspondence with the [endowment]{g:dotacio} or the caesarean [rites]{g:ritus}.[^4]

### 12-b ¶3 · 321.26–322.17  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 321] Ex mille evidenciis patet quod nichil est infidelius isto porismate, quod post dotacionem ecclesia non potest rite procedere sine tali papa cesareo. Nichil enim plus accusaret dotacionem vel caput huiusmodi de condicionibus Antichristi, quod foret abhominacio desolacionis, stans in loco sancto, ut expositum est in fine noni capituli et concordat Crysostomus Omelia ultima Operis Imperfecti. Pro cuius sensus declaracione repeto, [p. 322] quod Christus propheta summus prediceret pericula maiora ecclesie, quibus non est maius vel blasfemius quam cum vita yma, ut declarant Gregorius et Bernhardus, vendicare pompatice statum Christi. Cum igitur hoc sepe contingit de papa, ut patet ex cronicis allegatis, videtur quod Christus debuit periculum illud predicere. **Sed** ubi expressius, quam Matth. XXIV, 15 dicens: Cum videritis abhominacionem desolacionis, que dicta est a Daniele propheta stante in loco sancto, qui legit, intelligat. Daret **autem** fidelis operam ad intelligendum hunc textum propter tria, primo quia est textus viri desideriorum, ut patet Daniel IX, secundo quia est textus evangelicus a summa sapiencia repetitus, et tercio quia Christus ex magna causa precipit quod hoc legens ipsum intelligat.

ENGLISH: From a thousand [evidences]{g:evidencia} it is clear that nothing is more unbelieving than this [porism]{g:porisma}: that after the endowment the church cannot go forward rightly without such a [caesarean pope]{g:papa-cesareus}. For nothing would more accuse the endowment, or a [head]{g:caput} of this kind, of the conditions of Antichrist, namely that it would be "the abomination of desolation, standing in the holy place", as has been expounded at the end of the ninth chapter; and Chrysostom agrees, in the last homily of the *Opus imperfectum*.[^5] To make its sense plain I repeat [322] that Christ, the highest prophet, would foretell the greater dangers of the church, than which none is greater or more blasphemous than, with the lowest life, to claim [boastfully]{g:pompare} the [state]{g:status} of Christ, as Gregory and Bernard declare.[^6] Since, then, this often happens with the pope, as is clear from the chronicles [alleged]{g:allegare}, it seems that Christ ought to have foretold that danger. But where more expressly than in Matthew 24:15, saying: "When you shall see the abomination of desolation, which was spoken of by Daniel the prophet, standing in the holy place: he that readeth let him understand"?[^7] And the faithful man should take pains to understand this text for three reasons: first, because it is the text of "the man of desires", as is clear in Daniel 9; second, because it is a gospel text repeated by the highest wisdom; and third, because Christ for a great cause commands that whoever reads this should understand it.[^8]

### 12-b ¶4 · 322.18–323.21  (6 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 322] Expositores **autem** verificant illud de Antichristo concorditer. **Sed** alii verificant hoc de Adriano imperatore vel alio, qui posuit statuam imaginis sue in templo Domini. Et alii fingunt sensus extraneos. Michi **autem** videtur quod nichil plus concordat cum litera vel ebdomalibus Danielis quam exponendo hoc de pseudopapa, qui est potissimus Antichristus. In hoc enim est abhominacio desolacionis, ut supra exposui. Nam communitas abhominaretur sentire unum fingentem personam Christi in interludio estivo et super hec extra formam ludi facientem turpidinem vel loquentem, quanto magis, quando quis pretendit vivaciter tamquam spectaculum toti ecclesie, quod gerit personam Christi, et tamen committendo peccata turpissima vivit Christo summe contrarie. Hec, inquam, nedum est abhominacio ut in ludo **sed** desolacio, deficiente [p. 323] capitali suffragio hoste Christi subdole introducto. Communis **autem** modus loquendi concordat, quod, papa stante in loco Petri vel papatu, per tantum aut tantum temporis ille locus debet esse sanctissimus, cum papa pretendit se stare in loco sancti sanctorum, in cuius signum consentit quod vocetur pater sanctissimus. Studeat igitur calumpniator huius sentencie de qua persona ecclesie pertinencius verificaretur dictum Christi ad literam, et cum de multis pseudopapis sit verum, quod sint abhominacio desolacionis stans in loco sancto, quid obest scripturam sacram gravidari ista sentencia, aut quid derogat profecie Christi, quod ipse predixit nobis illud periculum? Non enim videtur quod Christus locutus est de loco materiali, ut templo Jerusalem vel alio, quia nulla vel modica sanctitas nisi equivoce est in illo, **sed** loquitur de loco spirituali, qui est status sanctus, in quo succedit unus post alium et abhominacionem in tali loco necesse est fundari in clero.

ENGLISH: And the expositors with one accord take it as true of Antichrist. But others take it as true of the emperor Hadrian, or of another, who set up a statue of his own image in the temple of the Lord. And others feign foreign senses.[^9] But it seems to me that nothing agrees more with the letter, or with the weeks of Daniel, than expounding this of the [pseudo-pope]{g:pseudopapa}, who is the chief Antichrist. For in this lies the abomination of desolation, as I expounded above. For the community would feel abomination to perceive someone playing the person of Christ in a summer interlude and, over and above this, outside the form of the play, doing or speaking filth; how much more when someone claims in living earnest, as a spectacle to the whole church, that he bears the person of Christ, and yet, committing the foulest sins, lives in the highest degree contrary to Christ.[^10] This, I say, is not only an abomination, as in a play, but a desolation, since the [323] [aid]{g:suffragium} of the head fails when the enemy of Christ has been craftily brought in. And the common manner of speaking agrees that, while the pope stands in Peter's place or papacy, for so much or so much time that place ought to be most holy, since the pope claims that he stands in the place of the holy of holies, in token of which he consents to be called "most holy father".[^11] Let the challenger of this [teaching]{g:sentencia}, then, study of which person of the church Christ's saying would more pertinently be verified to the letter; and since it is true of many pseudo-popes that they are "the abomination of desolation standing in the holy place", what hinders holy scripture from being pregnant with this teaching, or what does it take away from Christ's prophecy that he foretold that danger to us? For it does not seem that Christ spoke of a material place, such as the temple of Jerusalem or another, because there is no holiness in it, or little, except [equivocally]{g:equivoce}; but he speaks of a spiritual place, which is a holy state, in which one succeeds after another; and the abomination in such a place must be founded in the [clergy]{g:clerus}.

### 12-b ¶5 · 323.22–324.6  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 323] **Sed** contra illud arguitur per hoc, quod Apostolus II. Tessal., II, 4 de Antichristo loquens prophetat quod in templo Dei sedeat, non igitur stat in loco sancto, et per consequens Christus ibi non loquitur de Antichristo. Primo tollo instanciam detegens fallaciam, nam et stabit et sedebit. Stabit ad erigendum et magnificandum suam potenciam tamquam paratus ad temporalia rapiendum, nam sicut Stephanus vidit Ihesum stantem a dextris virtutis Dei Act. VII, paratum ad membra sua certancia relevandum, et sic utrobique loquitur scriptura de stacione spirituali, quia (ut creditur) Stephanus non vidit stacionem corporalem ad tantam distanciam oculo corporali, [p. 324] ut notat Augustinus in De Cognicione Vere Vite prope finem. Nec phas est imponere in hoc militi Christi mendacium. Et ista maledicta stacio Antichristi implicat ipsum sedere, quia a ministerio Christi ad quod excellenter **sed** false se obligat ociantem. Et ista est mala sessio spiritualis.

ENGLISH: But against this [it is argued by appeal to]{g:arguitur-per} the fact that the Apostle in 2 Thessalonians 2:4, speaking of Antichrist, prophesies that "he sitteth in the temple of God"; therefore he does not stand in the holy place, and consequently Christ is not speaking there of Antichrist.[^12] First I [dispose of]{g:tollere} the [objection]{g:instancia}, [exposing]{g:detegere} the [fallacy]{g:fallacia}: for he will both stand and sit. He will stand to raise up and magnify his [power]{g:potencia}, as one ready to seize [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}; for as Stephen saw Jesus "standing on the right hand" of the power "of God" (Acts 7), ready to relieve his [members]{g:membrum} in their struggle, so in both places scripture speaks of a spiritual standing; for (as is believed) Stephen did not see a bodily standing at so great a distance with the bodily eye, [324] as Augustine notes in *De cognitione verae vitae*, near the end.[^13] Nor is it right in this to impute a lie to the [soldier]{g:milites} of Christ. And this accursed standing of Antichrist implies that he sits, because it shows him idling away from the ministry of Christ, to which he binds himself excellently but falsely.[^14] And this is the evil spiritual sitting.

### 12-c ¶1 · 324.8–326.3  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 324] Secundo notandum quod Augustinus II De Civitate Dei quasi per totum exponens scripturam de Antichristo, specialiter Apokalipsim Iohannis et propheciam Danielis inter cetera capitulo II exponit textum predictum apostoli de Antichristo. Textus **autem** Apostoli, quem Augustinus dicit nullomodo pretereundum, in nostris codicibus ita iacet: Non terreamini, quasi instet dies Domini, quoniam, inquit, nisi venerit dissensio primum, et revelatus fuerit homo peccati filius perdicionis, qui adversatur et extollitur super omne quod dicitur Deus, aut quod colitur, ita ut in templo Dei sedeat, ostendens se tamquam ipse sit Deus, supple nisi talis Antichristus prius venerit, non veniet dies iudicii; nulli dubium est, inquit Augustinus, eum de Antichristo isto dixisse, unde Apostolus videns abhominacionem desolacionis, pro dolore loquitur interrupte, sicut Christus videns peccatum Jerusalem flevit dicens, quia si cognovisses et tu; supple peccatum tuum et penam exinde tibi infligendam; tu fleres, ut ego te defleo. Unde Apostolus consequenter declarat Antichristi versucias in hec verba: Non retinetis, quod cum apud vos essem, hec dicebam vobis, et nunc quid detineat [p. 325] scitis, ut reveletur in suo tempore; nam misterium iam operatur iniquitatis, tantum ut qui tenet nunc teneat, donec de medio fiat. Et tunc revelabitur ille iniquus, quem dominus Ihesus interficiet spiritu oris sui et destruet illustracione adventus sui eum, cuius est adventus secundum operacionem sathane in omni virtute et signis et prodigiis mendacibus et in omni seduccione iniquitatis hiis, qui pereunt, eo quod caritatem veritatis non receperunt, ut salvi fierent; ideo mittet illis Deus operacionem erroris, ut credant mendacio, ut iudicentur omnes, qui non crediderunt veritati, **sed** consenserunt iniquitati. Istum **autem** textum exposuit Augustinus secundum partem ex certa sciencia et secundum aliam partem tamquam sensum probabilem; certus est enim quod Apostolus hic loquens de Antichristo vocat eum hominem peccati et filium perdicionis, qui extollitur super omne, quod dicitur Deus. Est iterum Augustinus sicut et pius theologus de hoc certus, quod cuicumque viatori iste condiciones conveniunt hic est Antichristus. Et est tercio iste sanctus certus quod Christus non veniet ad diem finalis iudicii, quem Apostolus vocat diem Domini, iudicaturus vivos et mortuos nisi prius venerit ad seducendum in anima mortuos eius adversarius Antichristus. Primo quod iste sit sensus textus Apostoli, quod Antichristus, quem vocat refugam, sedebit in templo Dei, hoc est in ecclesia, non, inquit, templum alicuius ydoli vel demonis templum Dei vocat apostolus intelligens per Antichristum non solum ipsum principem, **sed** multitudinem impiorum hominum qui sunt quodammodo corpus eius. Unde in greco dicitur, tamquam in templum [p. 326] Dei sedeat, tamquam ipse sit templum Dei, quod est ecclesia. Sicut dicimus Petrum sedere in amicum id est velut amicus.

ENGLISH: Secondly, it is to be noted that Augustine, in *De civitate Dei* II, expounding scripture about Antichrist almost throughout, especially the Apocalypse of John and the prophecy of Daniel, among other things expounds in chapter 2 the aforesaid text of the Apostle about Antichrist.[^1] And the Apostle's text, which Augustine says is by no means to be passed over, runs thus in our books: "Be not terrified, as if the day of the Lord were at hand," for, he says, "unless there come a dissension first, and the man of sin be revealed, the son of perdition, who opposeth and is lifted up above all that is called God or that is worshipped, so that he sitteth in the temple of God, shewing himself as if he were God";[^2] supply: unless such an Antichrist come first, the day of judgment will not come. "No one doubts," says Augustine, "that he said this of Antichrist"; hence the Apostle, seeing the abomination of desolation, speaks brokenly for grief, as Christ, seeing the sin of Jerusalem, wept, saying: "If thou also hadst known" (supply: your sin and the punishment to be inflicted on you for it), you would weep, as I weep over you.[^3] Hence the Apostle next declares Antichrist's cunning in these words: "Remember you not that, when I was with you, I told you these things? And now [325] you know what withholdeth, that he may be revealed in his time. For the mystery of iniquity already worketh: only that he who now holdeth do hold, until he be taken out of the way. And then that wicked one shall be revealed: whom the Lord Jesus shall kill with the spirit of his mouth and shall destroy with the brightness of his coming: him whose coming is according to the working of Satan, in all power and signs and lying wonders: and in all seduction of iniquity to them that perish: because they receive not the [charity]{g:caritas} of the truth, that they might be saved. Therefore God shall send them the operation of error, to believe lying: that all may be judged who have not believed the truth but have consented to iniquity."[^4] And Augustine expounded this text in part from certain knowledge and in another part as a probable sense; for he is certain that the Apostle, speaking here of Antichrist, calls him "the man of sin" and "the son of perdition", "who is lifted up above all that is called God". Again, Augustine, like any pious theologian, is certain of this: that whatever [wayfarer]{g:viator} these conditions fit, he is Antichrist. And, thirdly, this saint is certain that Christ will not come to the day of final judgment, which the Apostle calls the day of the Lord, to judge the living and the dead, unless his adversary Antichrist has first come to lead astray those dead in soul. First, that this is the sense of the Apostle's text: that Antichrist, whom he calls "the deserter", will sit in the temple of God, that is, in the [church]{g:ecclesia}; "for the apostle does not," he says, "call the temple of some idol or demon the temple of God", understanding by Antichrist not only the prince himself but the multitude of impious men who are in a way his body.[^5] Hence in the Greek it is said, "as if he sits into the temple [326] of God", as if he himself were the temple of God, which is the church; just as we say that Peter "sits into a friend", that is, as a friend.[^6]

### 12-c ¶2 · 326.4–326.27  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 326] Secundo vero exponit sequencia: **Sed** nunc quid detineat scitis, ne adhuc veniat ille magnus Antichristus, quia oportet ecclesiam parum profundari in fide, nec ad coronam eius Antichristus reveletur in suo tempore. Unde quia sensus Apostoli est multum absconditus, non confunditur iste sanctus dicere: Ego prorsus quid dixerit me fateor ignorare. Et recitando tres opiniones dicit prima, quod Neronem vocavit Apostolus tacite Antichristum, secunda quam Augustinus approbat, dicit hunc esse sensum Apostoli, qui tenet imperium, teneat donec tollatur de medio, quia post dotacionem ecclesie revelabitur Antichristus. ENGLISH: And secondly he expounds what follows: "But now you know what withholdeth", that is, that that great Antichrist may not yet come, because the church must be somewhat deepened in faith, nor Antichrist be revealed, to its crown, in his time.[^7] "For already" Antichrist "worketh the mystery of iniquity" silently in his [members]{g:membrum}, as the evangelist says, so that the faithful Christian, having the judgments of his army, he who holds the faith of the church, should now hold that faith in the beginning, until the revelation of his malice comes out of the midst.[^8] For victory at the beginning of a war confounds the adversaries and gives heart to those who attack; for in the midst of that contest one chief evil one will come, although after and before there will be evil men, even up to the day of judgment, as is clear in Daniel 9. Hence, because the Apostle's sense is much hidden, this saint is not ashamed to say: "I confess that I am [wholly ignorant]{g:ignorare} of what he said." And, reciting three [opinions]{g:opinio}, he says: the first, that the Apostle tacitly called Nero Antichrist; the second, which Augustine approves, says that this is the Apostle's sense: "he who holds the empire, let him hold until he be taken out of the midst", because after the [endowment]{g:dotacio} of the church Antichrist will be revealed.[^9] And the third [teaching]{g:sentencia} is the one stated above.

### 12-c ¶3 · 326.28–327.15  (1 sed/autem)

### 12-c ¶7 · 328.14–328.20  (1 sed/autem)

### 12-d ¶1 · 328.21–329.17  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 328] **Sed** absit fidelem credere, si papa sit Antichristus, tunc omnis papa est Antichristus, ut magnus Antichristus fuit Cayfas, et tamen non omnis episcopus; magnus Antichristus fuit Nero et tamen non omnis secularis dominus; magnus Antichristus fuit Sergius et tamen non omnis monachus. Et ita in mediis opinabilibus stat duos meritorie credere expresse contraria et credulitatem fidelis propter evidencias variari, ut de sanctis canonizatis credo quosdam absque formidine quia sunt beati in celo, ut apostolos, martyres, confessores primitive ecclesie; qui non [p. 329] sunt victi cum mundo, alios **autem** opinor vel reputo beatos, ut posteriores de quorum vita habeo ad hoc evidenciam, quia narracio sanccionis vocati miraculi vel canonizacio Romane curie est nimis tenuis ad fundandum fidem ut mortuus sit in cathalogo beatorum. Potest enim in talibus vocatis miraculis esse multiplex seduccio, et potest curia Romana esse veritatis ignara ex seduccione testium qui deponunt, ymo potest esse cecata propter pecuniam vel favorem. Ideo absit fidelem credere quod sit articulus fidei credendus quicquid papa in canonizacione, in absolucione vel bullari attestacione decreverit. ENGLISH: But God forbid that a believer should believe that, if a [pope]{g:papa} is Antichrist, then every pope is Antichrist: as Caiaphas was a great Antichrist, and yet not every [bishop]{g:episcopus}; Nero was a great Antichrist, and yet not every [secular lord]{g:seculares-domini}; Sergius was a great Antichrist, and yet not every monk.[^1] And so in middle matters open to opinion it can stand that two persons meritoriously believe expressly contrary things, and that a believer's [credence]{g:credulitas} varies on account of the [evidence]{g:evidencia}:[^2] as, of the [canonized]{g:canonizare} saints, I believe some without [misgiving]{g:formido} to be blessed in heaven, such as the apostles, martyrs and confessors of the primitive [church]{g:ecclesia}, who were not [329] overcome with the world; but others I opine or reckon blessed, such as later ones of whose life I have evidence to that end; for the narrative of the sanctioning of a [so-called]{g:vocatus} miracle, or the canonization of the [Roman curia]{g:curia-romana}, is too slender to ground faith that a dead man is in the catalogue of the blessed. For in such so-called miracles there can be manifold deception, and the Roman curia can be ignorant of the truth through the deception of the witnesses who depose; indeed, it can be blinded by money or favour. Therefore God forbid that a believer should believe that whatever the pope has decreed in a canonization, in an absolution, or in an attestation by bull is an [article of faith]{g:articulus-fidei} to be believed. Since the pope attests his own frequent deception in such matters, and God is not the author or abettor of a lie, therefore God forbid that the church be servilely weighed down with such a faith.[^3]

For many are led astray by evidence taken from the curia, and many, for gain, carnal affection or blind favour, leave off knowing and cultivating the faith of Christ. And others make topical arguments for assenting that a [pseudo-pope]{g:pseudopapa} is the foremost Antichrist:[^4] first by this, that whatever man is deeper in hell becomes, for the time of the [way]{g:via}, a deeper Antichrist; now some pope, in all probability, since the [endowment]{g:dotacio} of the church, has deserved to be a deeper Antichrist.[^5]

### 12-e ¶1 · 330.13–333.5  (7 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 330] Similiter, Augustinus cap. IX De Gradibus Abusionum: Sciat, inquit, rex sicut in regno hominum primus constitutus est, sic et in penis, si iusticiam non fecerit, primatum habiturus est. Omnes namque quoscumque peccatores sub se habuerit in presenti, supra se modo implacabili in pena futura habebit. Et idem est evidencius de papa, ut docet Crysostomus Omelia LI Operis Imperfecti et patet distinccione XL: Si papa. Quomodo sacerdos, inquit, super omnem populum glorificatur, si bene fecerit, non solum propter suam iusticiam **sed** propter omnium quia ipse causa est iusticie universorum, ita et peccans super omnes punitur non solum propter sua peccata, **sed** propter omnium, quia ipse causa fit omnibus ad peccandum. Ideo ubi non valet Meander dyaboli, ipse supplet temptacionem per hominem, a quo non prodest benedicere, qua arte fugatur dyabolus. Et utrobique servabitur idem genus; [p. 332] quod fit cece et male preficiendo ineptum prepositum, suspendendo boni prelati officium, et omnino negligendo se movere, prohibens exercicium Christi ad expugnandum dyabolum. Et ista tam contraria oportet inniti duobus contrariis capitibus cum suis legibus. Et evidencia facti docet descripciones membrorum. [p. 333] Constat quidem quod clerus ecclesie claudicat in duas partes divisa, scilicet in partem Christi et partem cesaream. Ideo necesse est laycos supportatos per ipsos in opinione et vita tam dispares titubare.

And these, being so contrary, must lean on two contrary heads with their laws. And the evidence of the fact teaches the definitions of the [members]{g:membrum}. ### 12-f ¶1 · 333.6–338.8  (15 sed/autem)

Judas enim non dormit, **sed** facit velociter facta sua, et consonabit quod illud maledictum commercium foret undique mendaciis pertractatum. Sic igitur secundum regulam, qua Augustinus concordat evangelia, credi debet tam Petri evangelio quam Matthei; nec caret misterio quod Yudas pontifex legis nove sic mercatus fuit abscondite cum pontificibus veteris testamenti, quia sic continuatur generacio eorum adultera; [p. 335] et docetur quomodo pseudopontifices in lege gracie exsuperant malicia pontifices veteris testamenti. Illi enim emerunt agrum cum dicta pecunia secundum religionem fictam ad sepeliendum extraneos, quia precium sanguinis licet effusi licite exhorrebant. **Sed** dicitur nostros pontifices mittere in corbonam precium seminis, quod est preciosius, et effusum vilius ac culpabilius, in quo videntur superare cupidine veteres sacerdotes. Nec dubium quin symoniace vendendo spiritualia, et sic Christum, nostri pontifices peccarent gravius precium inbursantes, et sic (ni fallor) dominacio secularis in nostris episcopis non fundabitur in lege nova vel veteri, nisi forte in Scarioth, Anania et Zaphira, de quibus Act. V; **sed** omnes hii tres exinde divino iudicio sunt occisi. Quod **autem** vindicta nunc non capitur de nostris, ut in primitiva ecclesia de minus peccantibus, nostre culpe et signo maioris periculi possumus imputare; Deus enim quandoque ex amore predestinati peccantis hunc arguit et castigat, quemque **autem** ex amore populi, quem vult a toxico talis macule preservari, nunc **autem** signum est quod ex peccati participio deseruit ut desperatos tam populum quam peccantes. Et istud videtur michi esse tempus periculosum, quale non fuit a mundi inicio a Christo et apostolo prophetatum. Vix enim invenies unum presbiterum vel potentem laycum, qui non sit irretitus opere vel consensu episcoporum symonica heresi plus vel minus. Et patet quam horrenda est desercio securitatis cleri de sorte Domini et preeleccio securitatis mundane, in qua mundus blasfeme Deo preponitur. Nec valet [p. 336] locus a simili captus a laycis, quia et hoc foret in seculari dampnabile, et mundana securitas Dei adiecta in ipso est licita et non sic in clerico legis Christi. Clericis enim legis veteris, quibus imponebatur sarcina intollerabilis, suffecit pars Domini de decimis, oblacionibus et privatis elemosinis, et tercia pars stricta suffecit Christo et suis apostolis. Nobis **autem** non sufficit decima pars Domini ampliata proditorie et iudaice vendicata. **Sed** terciam partem prediorum laycalium ex titulo possessionis Christi mendaciter vendicamus, et ex uno latere cum bonis pauperum quorum sumus nudi procuratores, non habentes a Domino ad hoc procuratorium, possessiones seculares incessanter perquirimus. Ex alio vero latere extendimus magnificantes tradiciones hominum que questum sapiunt, et ex hinc ad grave dampnum ecclesie est ipsa lex Dei nimium pretermissa. Dicimus enim quod nedum est licitum **sed** necessarium propter consilium cesaris postponere Christi consilia, **sed** necessario oportet quod fiat dispensacio cum illis tradicionibus onustatis quas Christus instituit, **sed** cum donacionibus hominum et ritibus sequentibus propter sacrilegium non licet Deo vel homini dispensare, in hoc indubie preponentes hominem vel verius mammonam Deo nostro. Consideraret itaque clerus, quomodo ad fornicandum spiritualiter cum bonis fortune est clerus proclivior, ad resipiscendum tardior, et omnino est dirupcio amoris Dei detestabilior. Ideo considerarent arguentes per locum de possibili ad periculum de inesse, quomodo quilibet claustralis in Anglia potest meritorie habere feminam in cubili et studio celatam, sic [p. 337] valet quod non abutatur femina, igitur debet; fornicacio (inquam) corporalis non est tam assidua, cum oportet quod carnalis fornicacio, quando natura deficit et invalescit avaricia, sit extincta et per consequens tamquam innaturalior et difficilior est a Domino plus odita. Ideo dicit Matth. XI, 24: Dico vobis, quod terre Sodomorum remissius erit in die iudicii quam tibi. Neque enim saltamus cum filio hominis, nec plangimus cum Johanne Baptista, **sed**, omittentes doctrinam Christi nobis facilem quam expressit in vita et moribus et verbis, de vita paupere et exproprietaria, sequimur sanctos novellos quorum canonizacio fuit empta, licet pro maiori parte vite sue vixerunt mendaciter; et addimus in commendacionem nostre sentencie quod non legimus de patronorum nostrorum resipiscencia, igitur respondebam. Constitueremus, inquam, nobis Jhesum patronum et regulam, postponendo alios nec obediendo nisi de quanto sequuntur ipsum. In cuius confirmacionem oraciones nostras, quas habemus de sanctis, singulariter ad ipsum dirigimus. Non simulemus igitur maniace stulticiam auditorii in nostris sermonibus, quia ipsum percipit sophisticaciones nostras subtilius quam sufficimus palliare. Materiam (inquam) fidei, que opinionem excedit, magnificare debemus, quantum sufficimus, Sic igitur tollendo istas notas perfidie de plus credendis factis et dictis unius peccantis prepositi quam verbo vel operi Jhesu nostri, debemus singulariter doctrine sue intendere, quia revera est infidelis et mundo suspecta excusacio in peccatis capere thema nostrum de Christo, et dimissa doctrina sua inniti uni erranti preposito licet sancto, et specialiter in hiis mundialibus, in [p. 338] quibus obliquant a Christo, addendo fallaciam; Ipsi sic sequuntur, igitur nos possumus licite ac meritorie sic viare. Et sequitur punctus fallacie, quando concluditur, igitur de facto nos servando naturam operis licet modo contrario nec iniuste facimus nec peccamus. Revera prevalente ista fallacia liceret cuilibet religioso qualibet seculari vita quomodolibet conversari.

ENGLISH: And these two parts can be told apart chiefly by this, that the [caesarean clergy]{g:clerus-cesareus} presses more earnestly for the defending of human traditions and [privileges]{g:privilegium} that savour of the pride and gain of the world.[^1] But the clergy of Christ labours assiduously for the showing forth of the laws of Christ and his privileges, by which spiritual good is acquired; nor is it lawful for a believer to disbelieve that this part is right and the former erroneous, so much so that everyone who leans on the former part is an [unbeliever]{g:infidelis}, a heretic, because in his affection he weighs [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} above eternal goods; and since he obstinately defends this error as a covetous man, "which is a serving of idols", it is clear that every such man is a heretic, an unbeliever.[^2] And out of this faithlessness arises the heresy that it is lawful for clerics, and especially for [bishops]{g:episcopus}, to [exercise civil lordship]{g:dominari}; which is propped up by this, that the civil [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio} is lawful, though difficult; therefore he who had greater prudence for avoiding the danger, as bishops have, would the more lawfully take up this honourable life of exercising lordship. By this sham it is [inferred]{g:concludere} that it is most fitting for clerics to make war, to marry and to take up any dangerous [state]{g:status}, since [virtue]{g:virtus} is concerned with what is difficult.[^3] But first I [demolish]{g:tollere} this cunning. It is evident, indeed, that many things are lawful which, on account of their inferiority, are Antichrist's and foreign to clerics, so that they would sin gravely if, leaving noble work aside, they gave themselves to ignoble work; and so, on account of the perfection of their state, it would be most shameful [334] for them to be occupied in such things,[^4] as is clear in Christ, who knew not how to act so, since then he would have known how to degrade himself to the lay state and order, and consequently would have known how to do evil and to sin. The prudence of the clergy, therefore, consists in declining such imperfect work, as it would be good in lay people who give themselves purely to the school of Christ. We should note, then, that of the twelve bishops whom Christ had nurtured in his school, Iscariot alone, loving himself, had given himself to property after the apostolate; for in Acts 1:18 Peter says of Iscariot: "this man hath possessed a field of the reward of iniquity."[^5] Nor is it lawful to believe that Peter lied in this, nor does the gospel, Matthew 26, contradict this [teaching]{g:sentencia};[^6] for since Iscariot had earlier sold the Saviour to the [pontiffs]{g:pontifex} for thirty pieces of silver, meaning to make sure of the price, and believing his master's words that he was soon to die, he could have provided himself with a revenue near Jerusalem, so that, cheated of the gain he seemed to have from the theft of the common purse, he might provide for himself and his own by possessing it as a proprietor; nor [is it incompatible]{g:repugnare} that he took a possession so called in name, or [seisin]{g:sessiva} in the estate, and afterwards, bringing back the money to be given for the field, on hearing that his master was condemned, repenting fruitlessly, hanged himself.[^7] For Judas does not sleep but does his deeds quickly, and it will accord [with this] that that accursed [trade]{g:commercium} was handled throughout with lies.[^8] So then, according to the rule by which Augustine harmonizes the gospels, credence must be given to Peter's gospel as much as to Matthew's;[^9] nor is it without mystery that Judas, a pontiff of the new law, thus traded in secret with the pontiffs of the old testament, for so their adulterous generation is continued; [335] and it is taught how the false pontiffs under the [law of grace]{g:lex-gracie} surpass in malice the pontiffs of the old testament. For they bought a field with the said money, according to a feigned religion, "to be a burying place for strangers", because they shrank from the "price of blood", though the blood was lawfully shed.[^10] But it is said that our pontiffs "put into the corbona" the price of seed, which is more precious and is shed more vilely and more culpably; in which they seem to surpass the old priests in cupidity.[^11] Nor is there doubt that, selling spiritual things, and so Christ, simoniacally, our pontiffs would sin more gravely in pocketing the price; and so (unless I am mistaken) secular exercise of lordship in our bishops will not be founded in the new law or the old, unless perhaps in Iscariot, Ananias and Sapphira, of whom Acts 5 speaks; but all these three were slain for it by divine judgement. And that vengeance is not now taken on ours, as it was in the primitive [church]{g:ecclesia} on those who sinned less, we may impute to our fault and to a sign of greater danger. For God sometimes, out of love for a [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} sinner, rebukes and chastises him, and someone, again, out of love for the people, whom he wills to be preserved from the poison of such a stain; but now it is a sign that, by reason of [participation]{g:participium} in the sin, he has abandoned both the people and the sinners as desperate.[^12] And this seems to me to be the perilous time, such as has not been from the beginning of the world, foretold by Christ and the apostle.[^13] For you will scarcely find one [presbyter]{g:presbiter} or powerful layman who is not entangled, by the deed or the consent of the bishops, in the simoniacal heresy, more or less. For to the clerics of the old law, on whom an intolerable burden was laid, the Lord's portion of [tithes]{g:decime}, oblations and private [alms]{g:elemosina} sufficed, and a strait third part sufficed Christ and his apostles.[^15] But for us the Lord's tenth part does not suffice, enlarged treacherously and claimed in Jewish fashion. I would therefore answer:[^20] We should establish, I say, Jesus for ourselves as patron and rule, setting others after him and obeying them only in so far as they follow him.
