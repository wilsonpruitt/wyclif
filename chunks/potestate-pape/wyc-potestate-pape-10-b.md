# *De potestate pape*, chapter 10, part 2: the plea of papal poverty and Otto's demand

## ¶1 · 223.13–228.13

> [p. 223] Quantum ad primum obiectum de ministerii pape insufficiencia, patet quod falsum assumitur, ut patet de Christo, Petro et ceteris papis, sub quibus crevit ecclesia. A tempore autem, quo dimisso papali officio mundo intenderant, de tanto erant magis circa temporalia sitibundi, quia Ecclesiastes V, 9 dicit Salamon: Avarus non implebitur pecunia, et qui amat divicias fructus non capiet ex eis. Unde in predicta cronica que intitulatur Flores historiarum dicitur, quomodo anno domini 1226 magister Otho nunccius domini pape ostendit literas domini pape consilio regni nostri apud Westmonasterium, in quibus papa allegavit scandalum Romane ecclesie et obprobrium vetustissimum, notam scilicet cupiditatis que radix est omnium malorum, in hoc precipue, quod nullus potest aliquod negocium in Romana curia expedire, nisi effusa magna summa pecunie et donorum exhibicione. Sed quoniam scandali huius et infamie Romana paupertas est causa, deberent matris inopiam [p. 224] sublevare filii naturales, quia nisi a vobis et aliis viris honestis dona receperimus, deficerent nobis vite necessaria, quod esset omnino Romane incongruum dignitati. Ad illud itaque scandalum penitus eradicandum per consilium fratrum nostrorum sancte Romane ecclesie cardinalium, quandam providimus formam, cui si volueritis consentire, a scandalo matrem vestram poteritis liberare et in curia sine donorum obsequio exhibicionem iusticie obtinere. Forma autem provisa est hec: Petimus inprimis ab omnibus ecclesiis cathedralibus duas nobis prebendas exhiberi, unam de porcione episcopi et alteram de capitulo, et similiter de cenobiis, ubi diverse sunt porciones abbatis et conventus, a conventibus quantum pertinet ad unum monachum equali facta distribucione bonorum suorum, et tantumdem de abbate. Sed postquam maturatum est consilium, posuerunt responsum in ore magistri Johannis archidiaconi Bedfordensis, qui, cum dixisset nunccio quod peticio concernit ius regis, ius dominorum secularium et multorum absencium patronorum, clerus ille non potuit in eorum absencia finaliter respondere. Interimque venit dominus Johannes Marescallus et alii nunccii regis, districcius inhibentes quod nec clerus nec Otto nunccius attemptet aliquid quod tangit feodum regis nec molestet clerum Anglie, qui tenet baronias vel alias elemosinas de rege in capite. Sic scandalosa pape peticio est frustrata. Habet autem mandatum regis racionem vivacem. Cum enim ante dotacionem ecclesie fuit regnum nostrum iure possessionatum conformiter legi Christi, nimis monstruosum foret quod papalis exaccio extorqueret aliquid contrarium iuri regni. Rex autem nedum debet defendere secularem ecclesiam, sed [p. 225] ecclesiam cleri Anglie in sua libertate primeva, ut dicit beatus Ysidorus de summo bono libro III et corporatur XXIII questio V: Principes. Christus autem prohibuit vicariis suis dominacionem huiusmodi, et in signum confirmacionis ac subieccionis cleris seculari dominio dedit per Petrum tributum Cesari, ut patet Matth. XVII; potens igitur blasfemie tali resistere et omittens nimis est culpabilis ex consensu. Et dico quod fuit scandalosa peticio, quia non posset patencius accusare curiam, quam sibi contradictorius nominat sanctam sedem. Si enim tanta cupiditatis macula sic regnat in illa curia, indubie sequitur quod papa et cardinales sunt rei illius criminis vel consenciendo, auctorizando vel ipse in persona propria illam facinorosam cupidinem committendo; nam ex fide pro nullo bono possibili committerent tantum crimen, igitur multo magis non pro temporali iudicio tantum ab apostolicis alieno. Iterum, videtur quod forma excedit peticionem apostolicam, tum quia multi centenarii paparum possent de tanta elemosina sustentari, eciam complendo plenum papale officium, ut dicit Bernhardus libro IV ad Eugenium, et testatur Apostolus prima Thim. VI, 8—10, ubi dicens quod radix omnium malorum est cupiditas pro remedio sic subdit: Habentes igitur alimenta et quibus tegamur hiis contenti simus. Forma igitur apostolica ibi deest, cum Apostolus consulit pro relevamine multorum sanctorum Jerusalem quod quilibet ecclesie Chorinthiorum qui voluerit benediccionem dare sanctis Jerusalem reponat quod placuerit dare abscondite, ut patet II. Cor. IX. [p. 226] Hic autem papa et cardinales diffiniunt dominative quantum de redditu annuali in animarum periculum papa vult habere de Anglia, Ista, inquam, non est vox egentis sed exactoris bona pauperum rapina subdola subtrahentis. Cum enim papa ante dotacionem ecclesie sine tali elemosina melius exercuit papale officium et post dotacionem ad valorem multiplicis regni nostri sit magis cupidus et in operibus plus remissus, probabile est quod plus elemosine magis accenderet eius cupidinem, ut dixi superius ex Salamonis sentencia Ecclesiastes V. Sic enim contingit in aliis regnis nostri temporis, quos dotarunt domini, ut quiecius complerent episcopale officium et minus subditos onerarent, in quo, quia dereliquerunt Christi regulam, in toto contingit oppositum. Sunt enim in visitacionibus plus cupidi, minus intendentes spirituali profectui et plus subditis onerosi, in tantum quod, ut experti referunt, multitudo ministrorum episcopi excoriat pauperes, ut scriptor, sigillator, barbitonsor et ceteri secundum superfluas cerimonias introducti. Item, querendum est quo iure papa vendicat a regno Anglie tam dominanter subsidium; non iure divino, cum ipsum limitat vite necessaria, de quanto sustentat in evangelio laborantem, ut patet I. Cor. IX. Cum igitur papa non evangelizat Anglie, sed consumendo personas atque pecuniam per suam vocatam iusticiam multipliciter debilitat regnum nostrum, videtur quod non exinde meretur a nobis benediccionem recipere. Ideo oportet quod iure cesaris vendicet huiusmodi principatum, ut multipliciter declaratum est VIII cap. Sed cum regnum Anglie non subiacet Romano imperio, videtur quod non iure vel concessione [p. 227] cesaris debet ipse dominium in nobis vel nostris possessionibus vendicare. Non enim habet ex titulo cesaris amplius dominium, quam originaliter habuit qui donavit. Sed patet ex facto et cronicis quod rex noster devicit cesarem, licet ad morulam ex casu proditorio sibi per modum tyrannidis est subiectus. Notet igitur homo cartam cesaris de dotacione ecclesie XCVI distinccione Constantinus cum aliis cronicis, et videbit quod regnum nostrum, sicut non subiugatur imperio, sic nec Romano pontifici, nisi sicut quilibet christianus debet ex lege caritatis subdi alteri, et conversi fuerunt subditi sancto Petro. Sed longe hoc a forma sic vendicandi subsidium. Unde videtur michi quod consulendum est regno Anglie quod eo accepcius subdatur vero pape (sicut contigit de multis Romanis episcopis) quo liberius modo quo debet homo promerens illud facit, cavendo semper ne aliquis pontifex vendicando dominium blasfemet in eum, quia nichil magis enervat imperium. Sic enim translatum est Romanum imperium in Germanos et tantum aporiatur quod vix sibi subditur civiliter dux vel comes. Et generaliter sic decrescit dominium quod est blasfemo clericali dominio subiugatum. Satis est igitur quod regnum nostrum tantum sibi obediat, quantum docere potest ex scriptura, in qua est omnis veritas, quod populus obedire debuit sancto Petro, docto ex sanctitate vite et operis quod sit verus Petri vicarius, ymo posito quod sit blasfemus, adhuc videtur michi quod populus debet obedire sibi dupliciter: [p. 228] primo in quantum est creatura Dei, dicens naturaliter veritatem catholicam faciendam, et secundo resistendo, dum a lege Dei exorbitat, cum naturaliter illud petit. Sic autem obedivit Paulus Petro Gal. II et sic obedierunt reges et clerici pseudopapis ipsos deponendo. Obediendo autem homini secundum racionem falsam, ut puta secundum racionem qua false creditur habere potestatem vel excellenciam quam adeo non habeat, est utrumque peccatum dampnabile, cum ut sic colitur Antichristus et abhominacio desolacionis stans in templo, de qua legitur evangelium cum propheta.

As to the first objection, on the insufficiency of the [pope]{g:papa}'s ministry,[^1] it is clear that a falsehood is assumed, as is clear in the case of Christ, Peter and the other popes under whom the [church]{g:ecclesia} grew. And from the time when, having left off the papal office, they had given their minds to the world, they were by so much the more thirsty for [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}; for in Ecclesiastes 5:9 Solomon says: "A covetous man shall not be satisfied with money: and he that loveth riches shall reap no fruit from them." Hence in the aforesaid chronicle, which is entitled *Flores historiarum*, it is told how in the year of the Lord 1226 Master Otto, the lord pope's nuncio, showed the lord pope's letters to the council of our [kingdom]{g:regnum} at Westminster, in which the pope alleged the scandal of the Roman church and its most ancient reproach, namely the mark of greed, "which is the root of all evils", in this especially, that no one can dispatch any business in the [Roman curia]{g:curia-romana} except by pouring out a great sum of money and the offering of gifts. "But since the poverty of Rome is the cause of this scandal and ill fame, her natural sons ought to relieve their mother's [224] need; for unless we received gifts from you and from other honest men, the necessaries of life would fail us, which would be wholly unbefitting the dignity of Rome.[^2] Therefore, to root out that scandal entirely,[^3] by the counsel of our brethren the cardinals of the holy Roman church we have provided a certain form, to which if you will consent, you will be able to free your mother from scandal and to obtain the showing of justice in the curia without the service of gifts. And the form provided is this: we ask first that two prebends be given us from all cathedral churches, one from the bishop's portion and the other from the chapter's; and likewise from monasteries where the portions of the abbot and of the convent are distinct, from the convents as much as pertains to one monk, an equal distribution of their goods having been made, and as much again from the abbot." But after the counsel had ripened, they put the answer in the mouth of Master John, archdeacon of Bedford, who, having told the nuncio that the request concerned the [right]{g:ius} of the king, the right of the [secular lords]{g:seculares-domini} and of many absent [patrons]{g:patronus}, [said that] that clergy could not answer finally in their absence.[^4] And meanwhile there came the lord John Marshal and other messengers of the king, strictly forbidding that either the clergy or Otto the nuncio attempt anything that touches the king's fee, or trouble the [clergy]{g:clerus} of England who hold baronies or other [alms]{g:elemosina} of the king [in chief]{g:in-capite}.[^5] So the pope's scandalous request was frustrated. And the king's mandate has a lively reason. For since before the [endowment]{g:dotacio} of the church our kingdom was [possessioned]{g:possessionatus} by right, in conformity with the [law of Christ]{g:lex-christi}, it would be too monstrous that a papal exaction should extort anything contrary to the right of the kingdom. And the king is bound to defend not only the [secular church]{g:ecclesia-secularis} but [225] the church of the clergy of England in its first liberty, as blessed Isidore says in *On the Highest Good*, book 3, and it is incorporated in [Causa] 23, question 5, *Principes*.[^6] Now Christ forbade his vicars [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio} of this kind, and as a sign of the confirmation and subjection of the clergy to secular [lordship]{g:dominium} he gave tribute to Caesar through Peter, as is clear in Matthew 17; therefore one who is able to resist such blasphemy and omits to do so is gravely culpable by consent.[^7] And I say that it was a scandalous request, because he could not more openly accuse the curia, which, contradicting himself, he names "the holy see". For if so great a stain of greed reigns thus in that curia, [it follows]{g:sequitur} without doubt that the pope and the cardinals are guilty of that crime, either by consenting, by authorizing, or by himself committing that wicked greed in his own person;[^8] for, [as faith teaches]{g:ex-fide}, they ought not to commit so great a crime for any possible good; therefore much less for a temporal judgement so foreign to [apostolic men]{g:apostolicus}. Again, it seems that the form exceeds an apostolic request: first because many hundreds of popes could be sustained by so great an alms, even fulfilling the full papal office, as Bernard says in book 4 to Eugenius,[^9] and as the Apostle testifies in 1 Timothy 6:8–10, where, saying that "the desire of money is the root of all evils", he adds this as a remedy: "Having therefore food and wherewith to be covered, with these we are content."[^10] Therefore the apostolic form is lacking there, since the Apostle counsels, for the relief of many saints of Jerusalem, that each one of the church of the Corinthians who wishes to give a blessing to the saints of Jerusalem should lay up secretly what it pleases him to give, as is clear in 2 Corinthians 9.[^11] [226] But here the pope and the cardinals define in the manner of lords how much yearly [revenue]{g:reditus} the pope wills to have from England, to the peril of souls. This, I say, is not the voice of one in need but of an exactor who takes away the goods of the poor by sly robbery. For since before the endowment of the church the pope exercised the papal office better without such alms, and after an endowment worth many times our kingdom he is more covetous and slacker in works, it is probable that more alms would kindle his greed the more, as I said above from Solomon's [teaching]{g:sentencia}, Ecclesiastes 5. For so it happens in other kingdoms of our time [with the bishops] whom the lords endowed so that they might fulfil the episcopal office more quietly and burden their subjects less;[^12] in which, because they have abandoned Christ's rule, the opposite happens altogether. For in visitations they are more covetous, less intent on spiritual profit and more burdensome to their subjects, so much so that, as the experienced report, the bishop's multitude of servants flays the poor: the scribe, the sealer, the barber and the rest, brought in according to superfluous ceremonies. Again, it must be asked by what right the pope claims so lordly a subsidy from the kingdom of England. Not by divine right, since that [limits]{g:limitare} it to the necessaries of life, in so far as it sustains one who labours in the gospel, as is clear in 1 Corinthians 9.[^13] Since, therefore, the pope does not preach the gospel to England, but, consuming persons and money through his [so-called]{g:vocatus} justice, weakens our kingdom in many ways, it seems that he does not on that account deserve to receive a blessing from us. Therefore he must claim a principality of this kind by Caesar's right, as was shown in many ways in chapter 8. But since the kingdom of England is not subject to the Roman empire, it seems that he ought not to claim lordship over us or our possessions by the right or grant [227] of Caesar. For he does not have more lordship from Caesar's [title]{g:titulus} than the one who gave it originally had. Now it is clear from the fact and from the chronicles that our king overcame Caesar, although for a short while, by a treacherous mischance, he was subjected to him by way of tyranny.[^14] Let a man therefore note Caesar's charter on the endowment of the church, distinction 96, *Constantinus*, together with other chronicles, and he will see that our kingdom, as it is not subjugated to the empire, so neither to the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex}, except as any Christian ought by the law of [charity]{g:caritas} to be subject to another, and as converts were subject to St Peter.[^15] But this is far from the form of claiming a subsidy in this way. Hence it seems to me that the kingdom of England should be counselled to submit to a true pope (as happened with many [Roman bishops]{g:romanus-episcopus}) the more acceptably the more freely it does so, in the way in which a man who merits ought to,[^16] always taking care that no pontiff, by claiming lordship, blaspheme against him; for nothing more enervates an empire. For so the Roman empire was transferred to the Germans, and is so impoverished that scarcely a duke or count is civilly subject to it. And in general, lordship that is subjugated to blasphemous clerical lordship dwindles in this way. It is enough, therefore, that our kingdom obey him as far as one can show from scripture, in which is all truth, that the people ought to have obeyed St Peter, [he being] shown by holiness of life and work to be the true [vicar of Peter]{g:vicarius-petri};[^17] indeed, supposing that he is a blasphemer, it still seems to me that the people ought to obey him in two ways: [228] first in so far as he is God's creature, by nature declaring the catholic truth that is to be done; and secondly by resisting him while he swerves from God's law, since by nature he asks for that.[^18] And so Paul obeyed Peter (Galatians 2), and so kings and clerics obeyed [pseudo-popes]{g:pseudopapa} by deposing them. And to obey a man on a false account, as for instance on the account by which he is falsely believed to have a [power]{g:potestas} or excellence that he does not have at all, is on both sides a damnable sin,[^19] since in that way Antichrist is worshipped, and "the abomination of desolation standing in the temple", of which the gospel reads together with the prophet.[^20]

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** The "first objection" was put in the preceding part of the chapter: that the pope's ministry cannot be sustained without a subsidy from the kingdoms. Wyclif answers that the church grew under poor popes (Loserth's side-note at 223.13).
[^2]: **S/C.** *Flores historiarum*, ed. H. R. Luard, vol. 2, p. 184, for the year 1226 (Loserth; LEDGER P10b-1). Loserth's italics run from "Otto, the lord pope's nuncio" to "from the abbot", one quotation of the chronicle; we put quotation marks only where the letter speaks in its own person ("But since the poverty …"), since the opening is the chronicle's report. "The root of all evils" is 1 Tim. 6:10, quoted inside the letter. The shift from *filii* (third person) to *a vobis … receperimus* is the letter's.
[^3]: **T.** *Eradicandum* is not in the manuscripts; Loserth supplied it ("Codd.: eradicandum deest. Addidi", 224.5), and the *Flores historiarum* he follows reads *eradicandum* (LEDGER P10b-1). Translated as printed.
[^4]: **C.** The sentence breaks off: the relative *qui, cum dixisset …* gets no verb of its own, and *clerus ille non potuit … respondere* gives what John said. We supply "[said that]".
[^5]: **G.** *Feodum* "fee" (the fief); *in capite* "in chief", tenure of the king directly. The clergy's baronies are counted among their "alms" held of the king (the tenure in alms).
[^6]: **S.** Isidore, *Sententiae* (*De summo bono*) 3.51.4 (old chapter 53; LEDGER P10b-2); received in Gratian, *Decretum* C.23 q.5 c.20 *Principes saeculi* (Loserth gives "Decreti Secunda Pars, Causa XXIII, Quaest. V, cap. XX"; the modern form is ours; LEDGER P10b-3). *Corporatur*: "is incorporated" in the Decretum.
[^7]: **S/C.** Matt. 17:23–26, the stater found in the fish's mouth and paid "for me and thee" (DR 17:26). *Cleris* is dative plural with *subieccionis*: the clergy's subjection to secular lordship. *Autem* brings the premiss on which *igitur* closes: "Now".
[^8]: **T/C.** *Auctorizando* is Loserth's emendation; the manuscripts read *autendo* (225.15). The singular *ipse … committendo* after "the pope and the cardinals" is the manuscripts' reading (Loserth: "ipse; ita codd."): Wyclif turns to the pope alone. *Committerent*: "ought not to commit", the potential subjunctive.
[^9]: **S.** Bernard, *De consideratione* 4.3.6 (to Eugenius III; PL 182, 776): Peter went without gems, silk, gold, horse, soldiers and ministers, *absque his tamen credidit satis posse impleri salutare mandatum: Si amas me, pasce oves meas … In his successisti, non Petro, sed Constantino* (LEDGER P10b-4). Loserth gives no reference; the "hundreds of popes" are Wyclif's.
[^10]: **S.** 1 Tim. 6:10, 6:8. Wyclif's *igitur* stands where the Vulgate has *autem* (DR "But having food …"); otherwise he agrees with the Vulgate, and DR is kept.
[^11]: **S/G.** 2 Cor. 9:5–7 ("this blessing … so as a blessing"; "every one as he hath determined in his heart"); the laying up (*reponat*) is closer to 1 Cor. 16:2 ("laying up what it shall well please him"). *Benediccio* is Paul's word for the collection, a gift: "blessing" (DR), so again at 226.27.
[^12]: **C.** *Quos* has no expressed antecedent (226.11; the plate shows no word lost): Wyclif means the bishops whom the lords endowed, and we supply "[with the bishops]".
[^13]: **T/S.** At *iure divino* (226.25) the manuscripts read *iure dominio* (Loserth, foot; so the plate); the print is translated. A reads *sustentant* (226.26). The rule is 1 Cor. 9:14: "they who preach the gospel should live by the gospel."
[^14]: **S.** Richard I, taken by the duke of Austria (1192) and sold to the emperor Henry VI: Loserth's reference, *Flores historiarum* 2.107, 114 (Loserth prints "I", but Luard's first volume ends at 1066), and Matthew Paris 2.394 (LEDGER P10b-5).
[^15]: **S.** Gratian, *Decretum* D.96 c.14 *Constantinus*, the Donation of Constantine, quoted at 232.18 (Loserth there: "Cap. XIV"; LEDGER P10b-6).
[^16]: **T/C.** Loserth: "Codd.: Sed contigit. Hic locus corruptus esse videtur" (227.17). The print's *sicut contigit* is translated, and the *eo … quo* comparison as it stands: the submission is the more acceptable the more freely it is given; *promerens* "who merits", i.e. who does it meritoriously. *In eum* ("against him") most likely means the true pope, or God.
[^17]: **C.** *Docto* agrees with *sibi*: the pope, "shown" by holiness of life to be Peter's true vicar. The subject of *docere potest* is unexpressed; we take it as general ("one"); it may be the pope himself.
[^18]: **C/S.** *Cum naturaliter illud petit*: his nature as God's creature itself asks to be resisted when he swerves. *Illud* could instead be the catholic truth just named. Paul's obedience by resistance: Gal. 2:11, "I withstood him to the face" (Loserth 228.16).
[^19]: **C.** *Utrumque*: on the side of the one who obeys and of the one obeyed. *Racio* is the ground on which obedience is given.
[^20]: **S.** Matt. 24:15; Dan. 9:27 (Loserth 228.11). Wyclif's *stans in templo* is Daniel's *in templo* set into Matthew's phrase; the Vulgate of Matthew has *stantem in loco sancto* (DR "standing in the holy place").
