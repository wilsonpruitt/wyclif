# *De ecclesia*, chapter 20, part 1: the song of the valiant woman; her finding, her price and her husband's trust (Prov. 31:10–11)

## ¶1 · 471.14–472.25

> [p. 471] Fructus huius longe contencionis de nostra sancta matre ecclesia foret sequens noticia scripture sacre, et per consequens dileccio sponsi et sponse inducens laborantes in ista fide fideliter in domum domini ad regulandum, ita quod illi applicetur illud Apoc. ultimo: Spiritus et sponsa dicunt: Veni. Quamvis autem quilibet liber scripture requirit pro eius sensu noticiam istius articuli fidei de ecclesia sancta catholica, specialiter tamen liber Apocalypsis, canticum ecclesie Proverbiorum XXXI° et Cantica canticorum. Quando enim partes ecclesie turbantur erga plurima, vocantur adolescentule non habentes ubera, quarum Salomon dicit non esse numerum propter volubilem affeccionem animi circa multa; sed quando Martha dimissa vita activa induit figuram Marie, intendens incomplete tamen uni necessario contemplando, inducta [p. 472] in thalamum, secundum numerum octogenarium dicitur concubina; sed tercio plus accedens ad unitatem que est omnia in omnibus notatur sub nomine regine et sub numero sexagenario post miliciam triumphalem; sed quarto quando cessabit omnis exaccio, area tocius ecclesie plene purgata a palea per ventilabrum finalis iudicii, non derelicta in corpore tocius ecclesie macula sive ruga, tunc dicitur una columba adepto fine unitatis caritative, secundum quam tota ecclesia est Christus. Patet ista sentencia Canticorum VI° et sparsim alibi in processu. Intendo ergo illam partem mediam scilicet canticum de muliere forti succincte transcurrere et difficultates profundas tam expositorias quam scolasticas superioribus meis relinquere. Video tamen (licet obscure) quomodo omnis difficultas logica, philosophica vel methaphisica includitur in scriptura de sancta matre ecclesia, ymmo (ut habundancius loquar) si quid subtilitatis repertum est a nobis in aliqua istarum arcium, eius sentenciam innuit nobis spiritus sanctus in cantico sapientissimi Salomonis de sancta matre ecclesia, Proverbiorum XXXI°, 10, Mulierem fortem quis inveniet; quod potest dividi in tres partes, in quarum prima narratur ecclesie sancte coniugium, in secunda describitur eius officium et in tercia concluditur eius preconium.

The fruit of this long contention about [our holy mother]{g:mater-nostra} the [church]{g:ecclesia} would be the knowledge of holy scripture that follows, and consequently the love of the [bridegroom]{g:sponsus} and the [bride]{g:sponsa}, leading those who labour faithfully in this faith into the house of the Lord to rule [it], so that to it may be applied that [text] of the last [chapter] of the Apocalypse: "And the spirit and the bride say: Come."[^1] And although every book of scripture requires for its sense the knowledge of this [article of faith]{g:articulus-fidei}, the holy catholic church, yet especially [so] the book of the Apocalypse, the song of the church in Proverbs 31, and the Canticle of canticles.[^2] For when the parts of the church are troubled about very many things, they are called [young maidens]{g:adolescentula} having no breasts, of whom Solomon says there is no number, because of the fickle affection of the mind about many things; but when Martha, the active life left behind, puts on the figure of Mary, attending, though incompletely, to the one thing necessary by contemplating, brought [472] into the bridechamber, she is called a concubine, according to the number eighty;[^3] but thirdly, coming nearer to the unity which is all in all, she is marked under the name of queen and under the number sixty, after the triumphal [soldiery]{g:milicia}; but fourthly, when all exaction shall cease, the floor of the whole church being fully purged of the chaff by the fan of the final judgement, and no spot or wrinkle being left in the body of the whole church, then she is called one dove, the end of the unity of [charity]{g:caritas} being attained, according to which the whole church is Christ.[^4] This [teaching]{g:sentencia} is clear from Canticles 6, and here and there elsewhere in [the course of the book]{g:processus}. I intend, therefore, to run briefly through that middle part, namely the song of the [valiant woman]{g:mulier-fortis}, and to leave the deep difficulties, expository as well as scholastic, to my superiors. Yet I see (though darkly) how every difficulty, logical, philosophical or metaphysical, is included in scripture concerning holy mother church; indeed (to speak more abundantly), if any subtlety has been found by us in any of these arts, the Holy Spirit intimates its teaching to us in the song of the most wise Solomon about holy mother church, Proverbs 31:10, "Who shall find a valiant woman?" It can be divided into three parts: in the first of these the marriage of the holy church is narrated, in the second its office is described, and in the third its praise is concluded.[^5]

## ¶2 · 472.26–475.31

> [p. 472] Primo autem supponitur esse unum primum coniugium quod sit metrum omnium aliorum inter fortissimum virorum et mulierem sibi proporcionalem faciendum, nam secundum philosophos in omni genere est reperibile tale primum. In genere ergo coniugii supposita incarnacione verbi (quod est Deus fortis et potens in prelio) oportet invenire sibi coniugem bene fortem. Ideo primo de illa queritur: Mulierem fortem [p. 473] quis inveniet, ubi secundum sanctos doctores concorditer oportet per mulierem fortem intelligere sanctam matrem ecclesiam, quia quamvis sexaginta sint regine et octoginta concubine adolescentularumque non est numerus (ut dicitur Canticorum VI°, 7), tamen ex omnibus illis constat una columba (ut dicitur ibidem). Quis ergo inveniet dignam coniugem tanto sponso? Et videtur quod nullus hominum nisi ipse, nam in constitucione primi carnalis coniugii primus coniux concurrebat materialiter ad causandum suam coniugem (ut patet Genesis III°): ergo multo magis in illo coniugio celesti oportet coniugem concurrere in causa triplici ad causandum suam coniugem ex natura angelica et humana. Primo ergo opere creacionis secundum raciones exemplares in verbo, secundo vero opere ministracionis secundum raciones seminales in genere humano, et tercio opere recreacionis in Christo facto circulariter ista duo inventa est mulier ista fortis. Nam secundum illam partem lapsa in devium erravit, sicut ovis que periit in vepribus peccatorum, et (ut dicitur Matthei XVIII°, 12) pastor et sponsus dimissis nonaginta novem ovibus in pascuis semper virentibus in celo perditam ovem invenit et humeris suis labore et penalitate debita reportavit. Nec moveat quod beatus Augustinus in tractatu huius cantici dicit quod hec mulier est civitas supra montem posita quam difficile est nescire. Videmus enim modo per fidem secundum quam tamquam lucem secundam illuminat mundum, faciens nos luscos tenebra ignorancie obfuscatos videre eam speculariter et obscure, sic quod ignoramus quoad noticiam distinctam partes ecclesie, sicut videns civitatem longinque, confuse et [p. 474] indistincte videt domos eius et alia ornamenta. Nec sufficeremus sic cernere, nisi dicta mulier a sponso prius in racionibus exemplaribus et visceribus misericordie sit inventa. Unde post conclusionem propositam supponendo quod ille invenit hanc mulierem qui secundum divinitatem est immensus, a cuius oculis nichil potest esse absconditum et secundum humanitatem ascendit super omnes celos, subditur inventorem illum quoad naturam et quoad tempus respectu patrum veterum esse procul, sicut et mulier illa procul erat localiter a Iudeis, partim in celo secundum angelos et partim in terra secundum gentes pro tempore gracie convertendas, sicut procul erat a Deo per culpam, cum non sit possibile quicquam esse a Deo distancius quam peccatum. Inventa itaque muliere forti copulanda in primo coniugio videndum est secundo de suis passionibus, utrum sit apta nubere tanto sponso; et respondet litera consequenter quod de ultimis finibus precium eius. Quod videtur sic posse intelligi: causa finalis quare ecclesia est valida, ut Christo matrimonialiter coniungatur, sunt predestinaciones suorum membrorum. Nam causa celestis particularis coniugii est opus meritorium viatoris, et causa operis meritorii est gracia infusa, et causa gracie infuse est predestinacio que quasi ultimus finis citra Deum dignificat ecclesiam ad illud coniugium, quia (ut dicitur Johannis I°, 16), de eius plenitudine nos omnes accepimus graciam pro gracia. Nam ex caritate eterna dedit nobis caritatem subiectivam secundum quam cum opere meritorio simus digni vel (ut habet litera Augustini) preciosiorem preciosis lapidibus que eiuscemodi est; cum quo verbo innuit eiusmodi [p. 475] mulierem in mente habere dileccionem usque ad finem; que Canticorum VIII°, 6 fortis ut mors dicitur. Ex quo sequitur quod mulier habens membra dileccione sic forcia sit sponsa racione consumate fortitudinis preciosa, quia usque ad finem diligit sponsum suum. Alii autem lapides scilicet caritates horales finaliter excidentes (ut fuit in Donato et aliis prescitis) non sunt tanti precii, ut subiecta sua sponso ecclesie coniungantur; vel tercio intelligitur non contrarie supradictis quod de ultimis finibus celorum et seculorum est precium, unde est dignitas huius sponse; vel quarto de divinitate ac humanitate in Christo qui sunt ultimi fines circuli intelligibilis est precium huius mulieris, ut patet ex fide, quia sicut verbum creat, ita dignificat sponsam suam. Et de ipso dicitur Psalmo XVIII°, 7: A summo celo egressio eius, et in ultima etate seculi consummata est gracia unionis virtute cuius originatur tota dignitas huius coniugii; Ia Cor. X°, 11 scribitur: Nos sumus in quos fines seculorum devenerunt. Inventa ergo coniuge et inventa eius dignitate ex precio vel valore, cum sponsus sit totum per se dignificans opus suum non restat quid tardaret illud coniugium. Ideo innuitur tercio affidacio celestis, dum subditur: Confidit in ea cor viri sui. Plane, inquit Augustinus, confidit et ut confidamus in eum edocuit. Prescius predestinacionis proprie confidit qui falli non poterit. Mulier autem cum filiis suis potest in ipsum confidere, eo quod mentiri non poterit nec sponse vel membris suis deficere, sed totus timor huius affidacionis stat in nostra desidia quod per nos non sit precium vel dos huius coniugii consummata.

Now first it is supposed that there is one first marriage, which is to be the measure of all the others, to be made between the strongest of men and a woman proportioned to him; for according to the philosophers such a first is to be found in every genus.[^6] In the genus of marriage, then, the incarnation of the Word being supposed (who is God "strong and mighty in battle"), a spouse right strong must be found for him. Therefore first it is asked of her: "Who shall find a valiant woman?" [473] Here, according to the holy doctors with one accord, we must understand by the valiant woman holy mother church; for although "there are threescore queens, and fourscore concubines, and young maidens without number" (as is said in Canticles 6:7), yet of all of them one dove is made up (as is said in the same place). Who, then, shall find a spouse worthy of so great a bridegroom? And it seems: no one of men but he himself; for in the constitution of the first carnal marriage the first spouse concurred materially in causing his spouse (as is clear from Genesis 3); much more, then, in that heavenly marriage must the spouse concur in a threefold cause in causing his spouse out of angelic and human nature.[^7] First, then, by the work of creation, according to the exemplary reasons in the Word; secondly, by the work of ministration, according to the seminal reasons in the human race; and thirdly, by the work of re-creation in Christ, made in a circle these two, this valiant woman was found. For according to that part she fell and strayed into the byway, like the sheep that perished among the brambles of sins; and (as is said in Matthew 18:12) the shepherd and bridegroom, leaving the ninety-nine sheep in the ever-green pastures in heaven, found the lost sheep and carried it back on his shoulders with due labour and [hardship]{g:penalitas}.[^8] Nor let it trouble [anyone] that blessed Augustine says in his treatise on this song that this woman is a city seated on a mountain, which it is hard not to know. For we see now through faith, according to which, as a second light, she illuminates the world, making us, purblind and darkened by the darkness of ignorance, see her as in a glass and darkly; so that we are ignorant, as to distinct knowledge, of the parts of the church, as one who sees a city from far off sees [indistinctly]{g:confuse} and [474] without distinction its houses and its other ornaments.[^9] Nor should we suffice to discern even so, unless the said woman had first been found by the bridegroom in the exemplary reasons and in the bowels of mercy. Hence, after the conclusion propounded, supposing that he found this woman who according to [divinity]{g:divinitas} is immense, from whose eyes nothing can be hidden, and according to humanity ascended above all the heavens, it is added that that finder was "far" off, as to nature and as to time, with respect to the old fathers; as that woman too was far off in place from the Jews, partly in heaven in the angels and partly on earth in the gentiles to be converted in the time of grace, as she was far from God by guilt, since nothing can possibly be more distant from God than sin.[^10] The valiant woman to be joined in the first marriage having, then, been found, we must see secondly about her [attributes]{g:passio}, whether she is fit to marry so great a bridegroom; and the letter answers next that "from the uttermost coasts is the price of her". This, it seems, can be understood thus: the final cause why the church is strong, so as to be joined to Christ in matrimony, is the predestinations of its [members]{g:membrum}. For the cause of the particular heavenly marriage is the meritorious work of the [wayfarer]{g:viator}, and the cause of the meritorious work is infused grace, and the cause of infused grace is predestination, which, as the last end, as it were, short of God, makes the church worthy of that marriage; since (as is said in John 1:16) "of his fulness we all have received grace for grace."[^11] For out of eternal charity he gave us subjective charity, according to which, with meritorious work, we are worthy, or (as Augustine's text has it) "more precious than precious stones is she who is of this kind"; by which word he intimates that a woman of this kind [475] has in mind love unto the end, which in Canticles 8:6 is called "strong as death".[^12] From which [it follows]{g:sequitur} that a woman having members so strong in love is a bride precious by reason of consummate strength, since she loves her bridegroom unto the end. But the other stones, namely charities for an hour that finally fall away (as it was in Donatus and other [foreknown]{g:prescitus} men), are not of such price that their subjects should be joined to the bridegroom of the church;[^13] or, thirdly, it is understood, not contrary to what was said above, that the price is from the uttermost ends of the heavens and of the ages, whence is the dignity of this bride; or, fourthly, the price of this woman is from the divinity and the humanity in Christ, which are the uttermost ends of the intelligible circle, as is clear, [as faith teaches]{g:ex-fide}, since as the Word creates, so it makes its bride worthy. And of him it is said in Psalm 18:7: "His going out is from the end of heaven"; and in the last age of the world the grace of union was consummated, by virtue of which the whole dignity of this marriage takes its origin; in 1 Corinthians 10:11 it is written: "We are [those] upon whom the ends of the world are come."[^14] The spouse, then, having been found, and her dignity found from her price or value, since the bridegroom is the whole, of himself making his work worthy, nothing remains that should delay that marriage. Therefore, thirdly, the heavenly affiance is intimated, when it is added: "The heart of her husband trusteth in her." "Plainly," says Augustine, "he trusts, and he has taught us to trust in him. Foreknowing his own predestination, he trusts who cannot be deceived." And the woman with her sons can trust in him, because he cannot lie, nor fail his bride or his members; but the whole fear of this affiance stands in our sloth, lest through us the price or dowry of this marriage should not be consummated.[^15]

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Fructus … foret sequens noticia*: *fructus* is the subject and *noticia* the complement. *Illi applicetur*: most naturally the love of bridegroom and bride. **T.** B reads *ad regnandum*, "to reign" (471.17–18). The codices read *Sponsus et sponsa dicunt*; Loserth corrected the text from Apocalypse 22:17 (471.19). **R.** Side-note 471.14.

[^2]: **C.** *Canticum ecclesie Proverbiorum XXXI*: the song of the valiant woman, Proverbs 31:10–31, read as a song of the church. *Istius articuli fidei de ecclesia sancta catholica*: the creed's article of the holy catholic church. **T.** A reads *quilibet librum* (471.20).

[^3]: **S.** *Turbantur erga plurima*: Luke 10:41 ("thou art troubled about many things"); *uni necessario*, Luke 10:42 ("one thing is necessary"). *Non habentes ubera*: Canticles 8:8 ("Our sister is little, and hath no breasts"). The numbers are those of Canticles 6:7. **C.** Four grades follow: the young maidens (the many-minded), the concubines (eighty: contemplatives, still incomplete), the queens (sixty), and the one dove. **R.** A in the margin at 472.1: *De adolescentulis, concubinis et reginis Salomonis*.

[^4]: **G.** *Milicia*: the soldiery of the church in the Pauline figure, here its warfare brought to triumph (GLOSSARY *milicia*). **S.** *Area … ventilabrum*: Matthew 3:12 ("Whose fan is in his hand, and he will thoroughly cleanse his floor"). *Macula sive ruga*: Ephesians 5:27 ("not having spot or wrinkle"). *Omnia in omnibus*: 1 Corinthians 15:28. *Una columba*: Canticles 6:8. **C.** *Exaccio*: the exaction of toil and tribute laid on the church in this life. *Unitatis caritative*: the unity of charity. **T.** B reads *si tercio* (472.2).

[^5]: **C.** *Illam partem mediam*: of the three books just named, the middle one, Proverbs 31. *Superioribus meis*: my betters. *Tam expositorias quam scolasticas*: of exegesis and of the schools. *Quod potest dividi*: *quod* refers to the song. **R.** A in the margin at 472.22: *Exposicio illius: Mulierem fortem quis inveniet*. Side-notes 472.11, 472.21.

[^6]: **C.** *Primum … metrum omnium aliorum*: the first in a genus as the measure of the rest; for the philosophers' principle cf. Aristotle, *Metaphysics* X.1, 1052b18–20, where to be one is to be the first measure of each kind (LEDGER E20a-1). *Faciendum* goes with *coniugium*. **T.** B reads *omne unum* (472.26). **R.** A in the margin at 472.26: *Supposicio*.

[^7]: **S.** *Deus fortis et potens in prelio*: Psalm 23:8 reads *Dominus* ("the Lord who is strong and mighty: the Lord mighty in battle"). Proverbs 31:10; Canticles 6:7–8. *Genesis III°*: the forming of Eve is Genesis 2:21–22. **C.** *Concurrebat materialiter*: Adam's rib as matter. *In causa triplici*: the three works that follow. **T.** B reads *quod est dominus* (472.31). **R.** A in the margin at 473.14: *Nota de coniugio* (superscript *matrimonio*) *Christi et sponsa ecclesie*. Side-notes 473.1, 473.11.

[^8]: **C.** *Raciones exemplares … seminales*: the exemplars of things in the Word and the seed-causes set in creatures, Augustine's terms (LEDGER E20a-2). *In Christo facto circulariter ista duo*: Christ, in whom God and man are joined, closes the circle (cf. 475.13–14, *ultimi fines circuli intelligibilis*); the construal is doubtful. *Secundum illam partem*: the human part, which fell. **S.** Matthew 18:12; *humeris suis*, Luke 15:5 ("lay it upon his shoulders").

[^9]: **S.** Augustine, *Sermo* 37 on Proverbs 31 (Loserth: Aug. Sermo XXXVII de Prov. cap. XXXI, Opp. V, p. 181) (LEDGER E20a-3). *Civitas supra montem posita*: Matthew 5:14 ("A city seated on a mountain cannot be hid"). *Speculariter et obscure*: 1 Corinthians 13:12 ("through a glass in a dark manner"). **C.** *Tamquam lucem secundam*: the church lights the world by faith as a second light. *Luscos*: one-eyed, purblind. **G.** *Confuse et indistincte*: *confuse* by GLOSSARY "indistinctly"; *indistincte* is rendered "without distinction".

[^10]: **C.** *In racionibus exemplaribus et visceribus misericordie*: in God's exemplars and in his mercy (cf. Luke 1:78, "the bowels of the mercy of our God"). *Procul*: Wyclif reads the verse's "far" of the finder (far from the old fathers in nature and time) and of the woman (far in place, and by guilt). **S.** *Ascendit super omnes celos*: Ephesians 4:10. **R.** A in the margin at 474.7: *Solus Christus potuit talem mulierem fortem invenire, que est sua sponsa*.

[^11]: **G.** *Passionibus*: her attributes (GLOSSARY *passio*), not her sufferings. **C.** *Valida*: strong, answering to *fortis*. *Citra Deum*: short of God. *Sunt predestinaciones*: the plural predicate is kept as "the predestinations". **S.** Proverbs 31:10; John 1:16 (Wyclif without *et*). **R.** A in the margin at 475.5: *Nota de passionibus ecclesie, an posset nubere Christo*. Side-note 474.19.

[^12]: **S.** Augustine's text of Proverbs 31:10 is the Old Latin, *pretiosior est lapidibus pretiosis quae eiuscemodi est*, not the Vulgate (Loserth: Aug. l.c. cap. III, at 474.31) (LEDGER E20a-4). Canticles 8:6 ("love is strong as death"). **C.** *Preciosiorem* stands in the accusative as the object of *habet*. *Caritatem subiectivam*: charity as it is in its subject, given out of the eternal charity that is God. **R.** Side-note 475.4.

[^13]: **C.** *Lapides*: the stones of Augustine's verse. *Caritates horales*: charities for an hour, failing at the end (cf. *momentanea caritas*, 468.29). *Donato*: Donatus, the schismatic of Augustine's day, named among the foreknown who fall away (LEDGER E20a-5). *Subiecta sua*: their subjects, those in whom such charity is. **T.** B reads *licet caritates* (475.6). For *sponso ecclesie* (475.8–9) AA₁ read *sponsa ecclesia* and B *sponso ecclesia*; translated as printed.

[^14]: **S.** Psalm 18:7. 1 Corinthians 10:11, where *Nos sumus* is not in the Apostle's text (Loserth). **C.** *Ultimi fines*: the verse's "uttermost coasts" read of heaven and the ages, and of the Godhead and manhood in Christ. *Gracia unionis*: the grace of the union of the two natures. **T.** B reads *Eciam de* (475.15) and *erigitur tota* (475.18).

[^15]: **S.** Proverbs 31:11. Augustine, the same sermon (LEDGER E20a-3); Loserth gives Augustine's wording as *Prescius confidit, falli non potest qui confidit* (LEDGER E20a-6). **C.** *Sit totum per se dignificans*: the bridegroom is himself the whole cause of the bride's worth. *Affidacio*: betrothal by plighted trust; "affiance" keeps both senses and the play on *confidit*. *Dos*: dowry. **T.** B reads *dictum coniugium* (475.22–23) and *prescitus* (475.26). **R.** Side-note 475.24.
