# *De eucharistia*, chapter 7, part 7: the angel seen in an assumed body, and the argument for identification

## ¶1 · 213.21–214.3

> [p. 213] Sed ulterius notandum quod Augustinus dicit Deum apparere oculis corporalibus in dicto casu et in ista carne; sed loquitur de apparicione remota et abstracta. Nam videndo corpus assumptum ab angelo ad dandum officium dicit scriptura sepe quod homo videt illum angelum, ubi oportet quod visus sumatur aggregative pro visione sensus et visione intellectus terminatis ad visibile aggregatum ex assumpto corpore et ipso angelo. Et sepe dicit scriptura quod homo taliter videt Deum; unde unus in casu videt angelum, et bestia vel alius homo videns eandem corporalem ymaginacionem non videt angelum, quia deficit sibi visio [p. 214] intellectus appropriata spiritui elaborata a tali noticia sensitivo. Sed secus est de visione hominis, cum ipse sit realiter ipsum corpus.

But further it is to be noted that Augustine says that God appears to bodily eyes in the said case and in this flesh;[^1] but he speaks of a remote and abstract appearing. For when the body assumed by an angel to discharge an office is seen, scripture often says that a man sees that angel, where "seeing" must be taken [aggregatively]{g:aggregative} for the vision of sense and the vision of the intellect, terminated at the visible thing aggregated from the assumed body and the angel himself. And scripture often says that a man sees God in this way; hence one man in the case sees the angel, and a beast or another man seeing the same bodily image does not see the angel, because he lacks the vision [214] of the intellect appropriated to a spirit, worked out from such sensitive knowledge.[^2] But it is otherwise with the seeing of a man, since he is [really]{g:realiter} the body itself.[^3]

## ¶2 · 214.4–215.4

> [p. 214] Sed quarto obicitur sic: Homo videt angelum, sicut oportet concedere ex fide scripture, et ita abscondita est natura angeli in illo corpore assumpto, sicut corpus Christi in hostia consecrata, ergo per idem homo videt in hostia sensibiliter corpus Christi. Nec valet dicere quod homo videt corpus oculo corporis et angelum solum oculo mentis, quia tunc falsa foret scriptura Genes. XVIIIᵒ, 2 ubi dicitur: Cum Abraham elevasset oculos, apparuerunt ei tres viri stantes iuxta eum; quos cum vidisset, cucurrit in occursum eorum; ubi notum est quod loquitur de visione oculi corporalis. Unde Num. XXIIᵒ, 24 dicitur quod asina Balaam vidit angelum in angustiis duarum maceriarum, ubi patet quod intelligitur solum de visione sensitiva, cum asinus non habuit intellectum. Et idem patet expresse Judic. XIIIᵒ, 3 de parentibus Sampsonis quod viderunt angelum oculo corporali. Juxta quem modum loquendi loquitur evangelium. Nam Math. XXVIIIᵒ, 3 dicitur de angelo: Erat autem aspectus eius sicut fulgur, et vestimenta eius sicut nix. Et Marc. XVIᵒ, 5 de eodem angelo dicitur quod mulieres introeuntes in monumentum viderunt revolutum lapidem et iuvenem sedentem in dextris coopertum stola candida. Hic et in quotlibet locis aliis patet quod loquitur de visione corporea. Cum ergo non sit phas fideli negare istam scripturam nec argumentum captum ab ea per locum a completa similitudine, videtur quod si homo potest videre angelum qui est omnino invisibilis in sua natura per visionem corporis contingentissime sibi coniuncti; multo magis [p. 215] homo videret in sacramento corpus Christi, cum sit eque presens in hostia, cui permanencius copulatur quam angelus corpori sibi magis alieno et habet in se racionem sensibilis.

But fourthly it is objected thus: a man sees an angel, as must be granted [from the faith of scripture]{g:ex-fide}, and the [nature]{g:natura} of the angel is hidden in that assumed body just as the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} is in the consecrated [host]{g:hostia}; therefore by the same token a man sees [perceptibly]{g:sensibiliter} in the host the body of Christ. Nor does it avail to say that a man sees the body with the eye of the body and the angel only with the eye of the mind, for then scripture would be false, Gen. 18:2, where it is said: "When Abraham had lifted up his eyes, there appeared to him three men standing near him: and when he saw them, he ran to meet them";[^4] where it is known that it speaks of the vision of the bodily eye. Hence in Num. 22:24 it is said that the ass of Balaam "saw the angel" "in a narrow place between two walls",[^5] where it is clear that this is understood only of sensitive vision, since the ass did not have intellect. And the same is expressly clear in Judg. 13:3 of the parents of Samson, that they saw the angel with the bodily eye. In keeping with this manner of speaking the gospel speaks. For in Matt. 28:3 it is said of the angel: "And his countenance was as lightning and his raiment as snow." And in Mark 16:5 it is said of the same angel that the women "entering into the sepulchre saw the stone rolled back and a young man sitting on the right side, clothed with a white robe."[^6] Here and in any number of other places it is clear that it speaks of bodily vision. Since therefore it is not lawful for a faithful man to deny this scripture, nor the argument taken from it by [the topic from complete likeness]{g:locus-a-completa-similitudine},[^7] it seems that if a man can see an angel, who is wholly invisible in his nature, through the vision of a body most contingently joined to him, much more [215] would a man see in the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} the body of Christ, since it is equally present in the host, to which it is more permanently coupled than the angel is to a body more alien to him, and it has in itself the character of a [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} thing.[^8]

## ¶3 · 215.5–215.18

> [p. 215] Hic dicitur quod sancti patres negantes visionem corporalem corporis Christi in hostia satis preceperunt hanc evidenciam et quotquot alias quas scimus facere. Conceditur ergo scriptura tanquam fides, sed ad sensum katholicum, scilicet quod homo videt oculo corporali corpus assumptum ab angelo et oculo mentis videt naturam angeli; et illa visio aggregata terminatur ad duplex visibile aggregatum, scilicet corpus sentitum et angelum. Unde ad docendum nos istam secundam partem visionis dicitur Genes. XVIIIᵒ, 3 sic singulariter: Domine si inveni graciam in oculis tuis, ne transeas servum tuum. Et multa sunt similia singulariter dicta pro visione mentali que principaliter terminabatur ad Deum.

To this the answer is that the holy fathers, denying [bodily vision]{g:visio-corporalis} of the body of Christ in the host, anticipated well enough this [evidence]{g:evidencia} and as many others as we know how to make.[^9] Scripture is therefore granted as faith, but in the catholic [meaning]{g:sensus}, namely that a man sees with the bodily eye the body assumed by the angel and with the eye of the mind sees the nature of the angel; and that aggregated vision terminates at a twofold aggregated visible, namely the body [perceived]{g:sentire} and the angel. Hence, to teach us this second part of the vision, it is said in Gen. 18:3, thus [in the singular]{g:singulariter}:[^10] "Lord, if I have found favour in thy sight, pass not away from thy servant." And there are many similar things said in the singular for the mental vision, which terminated [principally]{g:principaliter} at God.

## ¶4 · 215.19–215.24

> [p. 215] Et quantum ad illud Num. XXIIᵒ, patet quod loquitur de nuda visione sensitiva que terminabatur ad corpus quod secundum tropum sepe dictum vocatur angelus; non enim fuit asina elevata ad cognoscendum Deum sed docuit hominem quantum sibi pertinuit.

And as for that text of Num. 22, it is clear that it speaks of the bare sensitive vision, which terminated at the body that, by the [trope]{g:tropus} often spoken of, is called the angel; for the ass was not raised up to know God, but taught the man as far as it belonged to her.

## ¶5 · 215.25–216.7

> [p. 215] Triplex autem ponitur racio diversitatis quare conceditur quod homo sic videt angelum et non sic corpus Christi in hostia. Primo quia figura, motus et vox viva que apparent in corpore assumpto per angelum significant expressius presenciam racionalis nature quam significat hostia vel suum accidens corpus Christi, ideo solo intellectu et fide illud concipitur. Secundam causam videtur beatum Gregorium [p. 216] notare quod angelus cum sit racionale animal assumpsit corpus in unitatem persone, et sic videndo idem corpus videt homo suppositum quod est angelus, quia illud corpus foret persona angeli. Et tercio quia natura angeli est in corpore illo diffinitive, sed corpus Christi licet sit ibi vere et realiter, tamen est ibi non verius quam sacramentaliter ut in signo.

And a threefold reason of the difference is given why it is granted that a man sees an angel in this way and does not in this way see the body of Christ in the host. First, because the figure, motion and living voice that appear in the body assumed by the angel signify the presence of a rational nature more expressly than the host or its [accident]{g:accidens} signifies the body of Christ;[^11] therefore that [body] is conceived by intellect and faith alone. The second cause blessed Gregory seems [216] to note:[^12] that the angel, since he is a rational animal, assumed a body into unity of [person]{g:persona}, and so in seeing that same body a man sees the [supposit]{g:suppositum} which is the angel, because that body would be the person of the angel.[^13] And third, because the nature of the angel is in that body [definitively]{g:diffinitive}, but the body of Christ, although it is there [truly]{g:vere} and really, yet is there no more truly than [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter}, as in a [sign]{g:signum}.

## ¶6 · 216.8–216.11

> [p. 216] Sed istas difficultates hic non discucio; sed faciat adversarius in toto plenam similitudinem utrobique et concedam sibi quod ipse videt corpus Christi in hostia oculo corporali.

But I do not discuss these difficulties here; but let the adversary make out a full likeness [on both sides]{g:utrobique} in every respect, and I will grant him that he sees the body of Christ in the host with the bodily eye.

## ¶7 · 216.12–216.20

> [p. 216] Tercio principaliter arguitur pro ydemptificacione quod iuxta fidem scripture, iuxta sanctos doctores et leges ecclesie panis sensibilis fiet corpus Christi et per consequens fiet idem cum corpore Christi et sic sibi ydemptificabitur. Aliter enim non foret signanda conversio, consecracio vel mutacio talis panis nisi extenderit se ad ydemptificacionem cum corpore Christi; et ad hoc sonant multe auctoritates quas allegat Magister IVᵒ Sentenciarum, distinccione...

Third, as a main head, it is argued for [identification]{g:ydemptificacio} that, according to the faith of scripture, according to the holy doctors and the [laws of the church]{g:leges-ecclesie}, the perceptible [bread]{g:panis} will become the body of Christ, and consequently will become the same with the body of Christ, and so will be identified with it.[^14] For otherwise no [conversion]{g:conversio}, [consecration]{g:consecracio} or change of such bread would have to be signified, unless it extended to identification with the body of Christ; and to this many [authorities]{g:auctoritas} point which [the Master]{g:magister} [alleges]{g:allegare} in the fourth [book] of the *Sentences*, distinction . . .[^15]

## ¶8 · 216.21–217.3

> [p. 216] Hic dicitur primo quod multe rime reperte sunt ad colendum false hoc sacramentum, ut patet de transsubstanciacione, de conversione, de ydemptificacione et de inpanacione cum eis similibus, in quibus omnibus oportet fidelem cavere de heresi, quia sicut prima heresis discipulorum orta est circa hoc sacramentum, sic et heresis multiplicior exinde est orta. Ideo oportet attendere ad sensum scripture katholicum et ad prudencias sanctorum doctorum in materia ista loquencium; hoc autem servabitur pro theoreumate quod scriptura debet undique concedi ut fides katholica [p. 217] et specialiter verba Christi quibus sacramentum conficitur: Hoc est corpus meum; cuius sensum sepe exposui.

To this the answer is, first, that many [chinks]{g:rima} have been found for worshipping this sacrament falsely,[^16] as is clear of [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio}, of conversion, of identification and of [impanation]{g:inpanacio}, with the things like them, in all of which the faithful man must beware of heresy; for just as the first heresy of the disciples arose about this sacrament, so also a more manifold heresy has arisen from it.[^17] Therefore one must attend to the catholic meaning of scripture and to the wise sayings of the holy doctors who speak on this matter; and this will be kept as a theorem, that scripture must be granted everywhere as the catholic faith, [217] and especially the words of Christ by which the sacrament is [consecrated]{g:conficere}: "This is my body" (Matt. 26:26); whose meaning I have often expounded.

## ¶9 · 217.4–217.22

> [p. 217] Nec sequitur quod quilibet dicens talem sensum dicet scripturam sacram, cum oportet quod ad scripturam sacram Spiritus Sanctus sibi inprimat sensum sacrum. Unde salva veritate scripture sacre ex integro licet nobis christianis negare quod panis quem sacramus sit ydemptice corpus Christi, licet sit efficax eius signum. Licet enim nobis variare in loyca stante fide, sicut in ecclesia primitiva baptisabant in nomine Domini Jesu Christi et post secundum expressiorem formam evangelii baptisabant in nomine Trinitatis; ut dictum est licere prudenti pretori aperire varie et claudere portas castri. Sicut enim multi abierunt retrorsum credentes carnem Christi et sanguinem esse cibum corporalem ab eis corporaliter comedendum, ut patet Joh. VIᵒ, 67; sic usque hodie sunt multi qui putant panem illum sensibilem in altari esse ydemptice corpus Christi, non distinguentes inter illam figuram et suum figuratum nec attendentes ad illum sensum tropicum.

Nor does it follow that whoever says such a meaning will say holy scripture, since for holy scripture the Holy Spirit must impress on it a sacred meaning. Hence, the truth of holy scripture being kept entire, it is lawful for us Christians to deny that the bread which we [hallow]{g:sacrare} is [identically]{g:ydemptice} the body of Christ, although it is its efficacious sign. For it is lawful for us to vary in [logic]{g:loyca} while the faith stands, as in the primitive [church]{g:ecclesia} they baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ, and afterwards, according to the more express form of the gospel, they baptized in the name of the Trinity;[^18] as it has been said that it is lawful for a prudent praetor to open and close the gates of the castle in various ways.[^19] For just as "many . . . went back", believing the flesh of Christ and his blood to be bodily food to be eaten by them [bodily]{g:corporaliter}, as is clear from John 6:67,[^20] so to this day there are many who think that that perceptible bread on the altar is identically the body of Christ, not distinguishing between that [figure]{g:figura} and its thing figured, nor attending to that [tropical meaning]{g:sensus-tropicus}.

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 213.21: Augustine says that God appears to the bodily eye; but this is as when an angel takes a man's body; the body is visible to the eye, the angel only to the mind.
- 214.5: It is objected again that Scripture gives instances of angels being seen with the bodily eye.
- 215.5: The true sense of this is that the man saw the assumed body with the bodily, the angel with the mental eye.
- 215.25: Three differences between this case and that of the host: 1. A body is more significant of a rational nature than the host is of Christ's body;
- 216.1: 2. The angel assumed the body in unity of person.
- 216.4: 3. The nature of the angel is in the body definitely, not merely symbolically.
- 216.12: 3, argument: Bread is said by Scripture to become the body of Christ, therefore it is identified with the body.
- 216.21: False and unscriptural terms invented as to the sacrament.
- 217.4: We must take the words of Scripture in the sense that the Holy Spirit gave them.

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** Augustine on God's appearing to bodily eyes "in this flesh", the case discussed in the pages before; Loserth's references at 213.3 (*Opp.* V, 34, 313; VIII, 866) stand above this chunk. The work and place are to be checked (LEDGER K07g-1).
[^2]: **T/C.** *noticia sensitivo*: the adjective does not agree with *noticia* (feminine); translated as *sensitiva*, the print kept. *Visio intellectus appropriata spiritui* is the intellect's vision fitted to a spirit, which the beast lacks.
[^3]: **C.** *De visione hominis*: the seeing of a man (the man seen), set against the seeing of an angel. *Ipse sit realiter ipsum corpus*: the man seen is really his body, so that seeing the body is seeing him; the angel is not his assumed body.
[^4]: **S.** Wyclif names Abraham and reads *iuxta eum* where the Vulgate has *Cumque elevasset oculos … prope eum*, and stops before *de ostio tabernaculi*; translated from his Latin, the Douay wording kept where they agree.
[^5]: **S.** Num. 22:24 Vulgate: *stetit angelus in angustiis duarum maceriarum*; that the ass *saw* the angel is v. 23. Wyclif joins the two.
[^6]: **S.** *viderunt revolutum lapidem* is Mark 16:4, joined by Wyclif to v. 5; Douay wording for both. The angel of Matt. 28:3 is "the same angel" in Wyclif's reading.
[^7]: **G.** *Per locum a completa similitudine*: the topic from complete likeness, in the series of *locus a sufficienti similitudine* and *locus a simili*; no row of its own.
[^8]: **C.** *Racionem sensibilis*: the subject is the body of Christ, which, being a body, has the character of a perceptible thing, as the angel's nature has not.
[^9]: **C.** *Preceperunt* is *praeceperunt*, "took beforehand, anticipated", not "commanded": the fathers met this argument in advance.
[^10]: **C.** *Singulariter* here is grammatical number: Abraham addresses the three men as one Lord. Neither "alone" nor "in a singular way" of the CONTEXTUAL row; "in the singular" is the grammarians' sense.
[^11]: **G.** *Figura* here is the shape of the assumed body, beside its motion and voice, not *figura* the sign of ¶9.
[^12]: **S.** Gregory, *Homiliae in Evangelia* X (Loserth: *Opp.* I, 1468); the place in the homily is to be checked (LEDGER K07g-2).
[^13]: **C.** *Racionale animal* of the angel stands so in the print; the inference turns on the angel's taking the body *in unitatem persone*, so that the body seen "would be" the angel's person. *Foret* is subjunctive: Wyclif reports the reason without asserting it.
[^14]: **C.** *Fiet idem cum corpore Christi … sibi ydemptificabitur*: the objection's chain is "become the body", "become the same with the body", "be identified with it". Wyclif's answer (07-h ¶1) breaks the second link.
[^15]: **T/S.** The print breaks off at *distinccione…*; A has a gap in the manuscript here and E omits *distinccione*. Loserth compares Peter Lombard, *Sent.* IV d. 21 ("Cf. … dist. XXI", read on the plate n287); whether Wyclif meant d. 21 or the conversion of the bread in d. 11 (cf. 07-i ¶1) is to be checked (LEDGER K07g-3).
[^16]: **C.** *Rime*: chinks, crevices (*rima*); Wyclif's figure for loopholes through which a false worship slips in, taken up at 221.11 (*per aliam rimam vacuam*) and in *rimantur* (221.31). D reads *experte sunt*.
[^17]: **S.** The first heresy of the disciples about this sacrament: John 6:61–67, the disciples who went back (see ¶9).
[^18]: **S.** Baptism "in the name of the Lord Jesus": Acts 8:16 (cf. 19:5); "in the name of the Trinity": Matt. 28:19. ABDE omit *Christi*.
[^19]: **C.** *Ut dictum est*: the praetor's opening and closing the gates is referred to an earlier place not named here. E reads *predicatori*, "preacher", for *pretori*.
[^20]: **S.** John 6:67 Vulgate: *multi discipulorum eius abierunt retro*; Wyclif has *retrorsum*. The "bodily food" belief is his gloss on John 6:53–61.
