# *De eucharistia*, chapter 3, part 7: what the accident without a subject does to natural knowledge and to faith

## ¶1 · 77.29–78.8

> [p. 77] Ex quo videtur cum neutrum istorum potest esse sine accidente, cum tunc foret purus spiritus accidentatus, quod totum accidens sit essenciale utrique supposito, et per consequens cum omne agens naturale in agendo repatitur, videtur quod nec A nec B potest [p. 78] agere vel pati accione corporea et specialiter procreare, ymmo si capta una duricie uniformi et coextensa una mediante cum alia, et sic infinicies usque ad superficiei duriciem, qua lineantur adextra undique, videtur quod nulla armatura, ymmo infinita acucies non posset huiusmodi penetrare quod si tota gravitas esset ablata tam A quam B cum armatura predicta, quam gloriosum suppositum foret et agile.

From which it seems, since neither of these can be without an [accident]{g:accidens} (since then the spirit [subject to accidents]{g:accidentatus} would be pure spirit),[^1] that the whole accident is essential to each [supposit]{g:suppositum}; and consequently, since every natural agent is acted upon in return in acting,[^2] it seems that neither A nor B can [78] act or be acted upon by a bodily action, and especially [cannot] procreate. Indeed, if one uniform hardness is taken, and one coextended with another by means of [it], and so infinitely many times up to the hardness of the surface, by which they are lined [outward]{g:adextra} on every side, it seems that no [armament]{g:armatura}, indeed not infinite sharpness, could pierce such [a supposit]; so that, if all heaviness were taken away from both A and B, with the said armament, how glorious and agile a supposit it would be![^3]

## ¶2 · 78.9–78.26

> [p. 78] Secundo sequitur quod omnis sensus philosophorum vel naturales eorum non sufficiunt convincere de aliqua substancia aliquam quatuor questionum, sed in istis necessitati sunt ad fidem recurrere quoad diffinicionem, distinccionem et perfeccionem specificam. In omnibus istis sunt compares idiotis. Nam iuxta dicta nullus sensus vel intellectus convincit esse quamcunque materialem substanciam, cum stante omni noticia sensitiva vel experiencia apparet compossibile et consonum quod tota universitas creata sit globus accidencium; ideo ponens talia necesse habet recurrere ad fidem scripture et supponere eius sensum; et deficiente naturali noticia questionis si est de quacunque materiali substancia, necesse est quod deficiat questio quid est et questio cuiusmodi est et questio propter quid est de quolibet assignando, quia omnes dicte tres questiones presupponunt questionem si est, que non potest naturaliter stabiliri.

Second, it follows that all the sense of the philosophers, or [of] the natural [philosophers] among them,[^4] do not suffice to [prove conclusively]{g:convincere}, of any [substance]{g:substancia}, any of the four questions; but in these they are compelled to have recourse to faith as to the [definition]{g:diffinicio}, distinction and specific perfection.[^5] In all these they are on a par with [unlearned men]{g:idiota}. For according to what has been said, no sense or intellect proves conclusively that any material substance exists, since, all sensitive knowledge or experience standing, it appears [compossible]{g:compossibilis} and consonant that [the whole created totality]{g:universitas-creata} be a mass of accidents;[^6] therefore one who posits such things must have recourse to the faith of scripture and suppose its sense. And if natural knowledge of the question whether it is fails for any material substance, then the question what it is, and the question of what sort it is, and the question why it is, must fail for anything to be assigned; since all the said three questions presuppose the question whether it is, which cannot be naturally established.

## ¶3 · 78.27–79.9

> [p. 78] Sed contra istam perfidiam arguitur quod nedum tollit omnem naturalem scienciam sed eciam omnem fidem; nam sic devians nedum tenetur sentire cum antiquis errantibus quod nullam affirmacionem cognoscunt [p. 79] sed negaciones ut quod nichil sciunt et eis similia sed tenentur habere conscienciam de asserendo vel iurando aliquid contingens praxim hominum, et periret quelibet policia vel religio christiana; nam nemo debet mentiri pro toto mundo, sed generaliter pars securior est tenenda; cum ergo nulla noticia quam habemus de materiali substancia fundatur infallibiliter in principio infallibili cognoscendo, videtur quod irregulariter debemus nullam talem asserere.

But against this faithlessness it is argued that it takes away [not only]{g:nedum} all natural knowledge but also all faith. For one who strays so is bound not only to [hold]{g:sentire} with the ancient erring [philosophers] that they know no affirmation [79] but [only] negations, as that they know nothing, and things like these; but they are bound to [be troubled in conscience over]{g:habere-conscienciam-super} asserting or swearing anything touching the practice of men;[^7] and every [polity]{g:policia} or Christian religion would perish. For no one ought to lie for the whole world; now in general the safer part is to be held;[^8] since therefore no knowledge that we have of material substance is founded infallibly on an infallible principle of knowing, it seems that, [irregularly]{g:irregulariter}, we ought to assert no such [substance].[^9]

## ¶4 · 79.10–79.23

> [p. 79] Item, de quocunque nostrum non convincitur ex fide scripture ipsum esse, nec sensus nec racio hoc convincit, ergo de quolibet tali est pie dubitandum si est et quid est, et periret noticia de examinando opera cleri, de certificando numerum claustralium et de commemorando numismata vel dona ab homine eis data; ponit enim esse quod spiritus esset nudis accidentibus copulatus; quo posito non foret talis homo, ut patet ex dictis; nec valet in isto allegare aliquod miraculum vel communem cursum nature, quia ex dubio sic loquenti ab origine mundi fuit irregulariter spiritus nudis accidentibus copulatus; et sic daretur occasio multiplicandi sectas contrarie opinantes sine conviccione notoria partis false.

Again, of any one of us it is not proved conclusively [from the faith of scripture]{g:ex-fide} that he exists, nor does sense or reason prove it conclusively; therefore of every such one it is [piously]{g:pie} to be doubted whether he is and what he is; and the knowledge would perish for examining the works of the clergy, for certifying the number of the [cloisterers]{g:claustrales}, and for recording the coins or gifts given them by a man. For he posits it as being so that a spirit were coupled to bare accidents; which posited, such a man would not be [a man], as is clear from what has been said.[^10] Nor does it avail in this to [allege]{g:allegare} some miracle or the common course of nature, since, [for all]{g:ex-sibi-dubio} one who speaks so [knows], from the origin of the world a spirit has been irregularly coupled to bare accidents; and so occasion would be given for multiplying [sects]{g:secta} holding contrary opinions, without a notorious conclusive proof of the false party.

## ¶5 · 79.24–80.10

> [p. 79] Item, periret fides scripture, cum posset negari alicui persone hominis revelacionem vel noticiam huiusmodi esse datam, licet nature ex spiritu et accidente que servat equivoce idem nomen, quod nos inponimus nostris substanciis; et illa natura non foret inperfeccior propter carenciam inperfecte materialis substancie, sed quantum ad omnem operacionem [p. 80] eque perfectam; et periret omnis differencia specifica et sic species alleganda et sic scala per quam fidelis ascenderet ad Deum, cum non sit Dei boni noticie humane instabili sic sine regulari fundamento deficere vel omnem stabilitatem sensacionis tollere, et specialiter in sacramento quod est Deo proprium, in quo minime foret sensus illusio circa materialem substanciam. Ideo videtur quod non est credendum istis maniacis in hiis que sine fundamento sompniant, cum publicant se non credere sibi ipsis.

Again, the faith of scripture would perish, since it could be denied that a revelation or knowledge of this kind was given to any [person]{g:persona} of a man, although [it was given] to a [nature]{g:natura} [made] of spirit and accident, which keeps [equivocally]{g:equivoce} the same name that we impose on our substances;[^11] and that nature would not be less perfect for lacking imperfect material substance, but, as to every operation, [80] equally perfect;[^12] and every specific [difference]{g:differencia} would perish, and so the species to be alleged, and so the ladder by which the faithful man would ascend to God; since it is not [the part] of a good God thus to fail human knowledge, unstable [as it is], without a regular foundation, or to take away all stability of [sensation]{g:sensacio}; and especially in the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} which is proper to God, in which there would least of all be [illusion]{g:illusio} of sense about material substance.[^13] Therefore it seems that these madmen are not to be believed in the things they dream without foundation, since they publish that they do not believe themselves.

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 78.9: If accidents can exist alone, our senses cannot tell what anything is, nor if it is.
- 78.27: Faith would perish as well as natural knowledge.
- 79.10: It is not by Scripture that we are convinced of our own existence.
- 79.24: All test of truth would be lost, and we could have no access to God.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Cum tunc foret purus spiritus accidentatus* (77.30–31) is construed with *spiritus accidentatus* as subject and *purus spiritus* as predicate: without its accidents the spirit that bears them would be bare spirit, and A or B would no longer be. E reads *fit* for *foret*.
[^2]: **S/C.** That every natural agent is acted upon in acting (*repatitur*) is Aristotle's principle, *De generatione et corruptione* I.7 (LEDGER K03g-1, to be checked). Since the accidents are essential to A and B, a bodily action that changed them would change what they are.
[^3]: **C.** A mock of the case: hardness laid on hardness to infinity would make A and B impenetrable, and without heaviness they would have the impassibility and agility of a glorified body. *Armatura* is rendered "armament" both times, covering the weapon (78.6) and the armour (78.7). *Coextensa una mediante cum alia* (78.2) is construed loosely; the construal is doubtful, and *quod si* (78.7) is taken as consecutive.
[^4]: **C.** *Naturales eorum* (78.10) is taken as "the natural philosophers among them", as *secundum naturales* at 84.24; it could also be "their natural [powers]", beside *sensus*. C first wrote *non possunt* and corrected it to *sufficiunt* in the margin.
[^5]: **S.** The four questions of Aristotle, *Posterior Analytics* II.1 (LEDGER K03g-2, to be checked). Wyclif names them below as *si est*, *quid est*, *cuiusmodi est* and *propter quid*; ABCE read *et quomodo* (78.24).
[^6]: **G.** *Globus accidencium*: of things, "mass", untagged; the row *globus* "band" is for persons.
[^7]: **C.** The ancient erring are taken as the sceptics who said that they knew nothing (LEDGER K03g-3, to be checked: the Academics, perhaps as Augustine reports them in *Contra academicos*). The Latin passes from the singular *devians ... tenetur* to the plural *tenentur*; the English keeps both.
[^8]: **S.** That no one ought to lie to save the whole world is Augustine's teaching on the lie (LEDGER K03g-4, to be checked: *De mendacio*; *Enchiridion* 22). The rule of the safer part (*pars securior*) answers to the canonists' rule that in doubt the safer way is to be chosen (LEDGER K03g-5, to be checked).
[^9]: **C.** *Irregulariter* (79.9) is the row "irregularly", against the rule of nature, as at 79.21; here it marks the conclusion as running against the ordinary course of assent. The sense is doubtful.
[^10]: **T/C.** *Ponit enim esse* (79.16) is the print: the adversary posits it as the case. C reads *potuit enim*, "for it could have been", and D *est*. *Non foret talis homo* is taken as "such a man would not be [a man]", by the conclusion at 77.24–28 that neither A nor B is an animate substance.
[^11]: **C/T.** *Licet nature* (79.26): on this view the revelation would be granted only to the compound of spirit and accident that bears the name "man" equivocally. D reads *sed*, "but to a nature".
[^12]: **T.** *Perfectam* (80.1) agrees with nothing in the sentence; ABCD read *perfecta*, with *natura*. The English is the same either way.
[^13]: **C.** *Noticie humane instabili ... deficere*: *deficere* with the dative, "to fail" human knowledge. The sacrament is taken as the eucharist, in which the senses perceive bread.
