# Four-class review R5: *De ecclesia* chunks 12-d, 12-e, 13-a, 14-g

Reviewer: independent, fresh session, 2026-10-03. Method: each chunk read once, sentence by sentence, Latin against English, checked against CARD.md (four classes, connective table), CONVENTIONS §§6–9 and §14, GLOSSARY.md (grepped by term), the italics rule (M5-RESUME brief 45) and the standing emendation rule (M5-RESUME, Rulings 2026-10-02). Sources were not re-verified. **Every page of all four chunks was read on the plate for italics** (pp. 263–272, 274–278, 322–326), so the line numbers below are the print's. **Every tag key was resolved by script to the GLOSSARY row it actually hits** (whole key, not the first word). Spaced/capitalised keys (`{g:temporales domini}` etc.) are a known volume-wide issue being fixed by script and are not listed.

Classes: 1 terms · 2 connectives · 3 medieval sense · 4 construal, or a note that contradicts the text. **Q** marks a quotation-bounds row under the italics rule (a class-4 matter, counted separately as in R2–R3).

**Tag keys.** All keys in the four chunks resolve to a row, and all but two resolve to the *right* row. The two wrong ones: 13-a `{g:elemosinare}` on "almsgiver" (that row is the verb "to give alms to" / *elemosinatus* "almsman"; the almsgiver row is `elemosinans`), and 14-g `{g:dotacio}` on the participle "endowed" (the row is the noun "endowment", and there is no *dotare* row). Both are given in the rows below, along with the missing first-occurrence tags.

## State of each chunk

- **12-d**: Sound and carefully noted. The hard spots (*sinistre extortum est ut*, *quem amorem*, the subjectless *polluunt*) are construed and flagged honestly. The weak points are the *quarto principaliter* formula read with the verb, and the duke's speech, which the print italicises through *fugitivi* but the English quotes for one sentence only.
- **12-e**: The cleanest of the four. Connectives and construal hold throughout, and the hard *ve speculatoribus* clause carries an honest C note. What remains is five rows with the right rendering but no tag, the missing CONTEXTUAL note on *sentencia*, one italic maxim left unquoted, and a garbled sentence in n. 10.
- **13-a**: Good English that follows the proof's structure. The work is in the terms: a tag on the wrong row (*elemosinare*), the compound *elemosina temporalis* row unused in the chapter it was ruled for, *bona de genere* off its ruling, and "cast aside" for *abiectam*.
- **14-g**: Sound construal with good notes. The quotation bounds are its weak point: four places on pp. 322–323 depart from the print's italics (three italic stretches unquoted, one roman stretch quoted), and the Augustine has no S note. There is one real English/note contradiction (*restrinxerit*), and *denominacio* is off its ruling.
