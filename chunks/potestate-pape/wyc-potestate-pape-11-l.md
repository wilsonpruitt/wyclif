# *De potestate pape*, chapter 11, part 12: lay baptism, the woman pope, and confession beyond necessity

## ¶1 · 307.29–308.14

> [p. 307] Contra ista arguitur per hoc, quod iuxta hanc viam, sicut laycus sufficit baptizare sic et confirmare, conficere ac sacramenta quecumque alia ministrare. Quis igitur pro absolucione de percuciendo aut occidendo clericum adiret Romanum pontificem? Ad illud dictum est quod non sequitur. [p. 308] cum racio diversitatis sit necessarioritas baptismi supra alia sacramenta. Verumtamen subtiliantes in Dei potencia absoluta dicunt quod sicut Deus potest communicare nedum layco sed femine ac alii irracionali potenciam conficiendi ac ministrandi quelibet sacramenta, sic et ipsum potest ministrare huiusmodi sacramenta. Nec credo quod aliquis impugnabit illum articulum, quia cum multa absolute sunt necessaria, que est inefficax populo predicare; et grosse procedo in ista materia limitate loquendo de posse legali et Dei potencia ordinata, ideo dimitto scrutinium talium conclusionum detegens sentencias catholicas, quas credo prodesse ecclesie.

Against these things [it is argued, by appeal to]{g:arguitur-per} this, that according to this way, just as a layman suffices to baptize, so also to confirm, to [consecrate]{g:conficere} and to minister any other sacraments whatever. Who then, for absolution from striking or killing a [cleric]{g:clericus}, would go to the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex}? To that the answer has been given that it does not [follow]{g:sequitur}, [8] since the reason for the difference is the greater necessity of baptism over the other sacraments.[^1] Nevertheless those who [refine]{g:subtiliare} upon God's [absolute power]{g:potencia-absoluta} say that, just as God can communicate, not only to a layman but to a woman and to another, irrational [creature], the [power]{g:potencia} of consecrating and ministering any sacraments whatever, so it too can minister such sacraments.[^2] Nor do I believe that anyone will impugn that article; because, since many things are absolutely necessary which it is ineffective to preach to the people, and I proceed coarsely in this matter, speaking within limits of legal possibility and of God's ordained power, I therefore leave aside the scrutiny of such conclusions, [exposing]{g:detegere} the catholic [teachings]{g:sentencia} which I believe profit the [church]{g:ecclesia}.[^3]

## ¶2 · 308.15–309.27

> [p. 308] Videtur igitur michi quod licet et expedit in casu layco baptizare, et cum non sit de necessitate salutis cuiuscumque predestinati recipere corporaliter alia sacramenta, videtur michi quod non debet presumere ministrare alia sacramenta, et sic reservacio ac restriccio potestatis potest tam bene quam male fieri, sicut reus potest tam stulte quam pie adire Romanum pontificem pro absolucionis beneficio obtinendo. Et debet supponi, quod utrobique bene, nisi sit patens evidencia contradicens et specialiter superbia ac avaricia prelati et omnino utrimque preponderancia sacramenti. Videtur enim quod plus obesset monstruositas peccati in prescito masculo ad recipiendum vel ministrandum legittime quelibet sacramenta quam foret quantumcumque corporalis monstruositas vel distinccio sexus in femina predestinata et virtutibus perornata, sive ad recipiendum sive ministrandum quelibet sacramenta, et tamen in penam peccati multi de [p. 309] generacione adultera sic phariseice seducuntur, quod preponderant signum visibile humanitus institutum signato invisibili quod Deus preponderat, ut in casu et facto Romane ecclesie, quod narrat Cestrensis cap. Ill libri V scilicet quod quedam femina sedit in papatu duobus annis et quinque mensibus post Leonem. Hec dicitur fuisse puella Agnes vocata nacione Moguntina, a suo amasio Athenis in habitu virili adducta, et Johannes Anglicus nominata que in diversis scienciis sic profecit, quod Romam veniens magnos magistros habens auditores trivium legit, demum in papam electa, per suum amasium impregnatur, et dum Lateranensem ecclesiam ab ecclesia Petri pergeret, angustiata pariendi doloribus, inter Collosseum et sanctum Clementem peperit, et postmodum ibi fuit mortua et sepulta. Et hinc dicitur papam hanc viam communiter obliquare, unde non ponitur in cathalogo paparum. In isto casu saltem possibili dicunt leges hominum ipsam non fecisse actus papales nec fuisse electam legittime propter indisposicionem corporis, licet fuerit iam beata. Sed grave est hoc fundare vel fugere, quin generaliter desit eleccio quando eligitur inhabilis quoad Deum; unde angustiatur institucio humana describere verum papam.

It seems to me, therefore, that it is lawful and expedient for a layman to baptize in a [given] case; and since it is not of necessity of salvation for any [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} [person] to receive the other sacraments bodily, it seems to me that he ought not to presume to minister the other sacraments.[^4] And so the reservation and restriction of [power]{g:potestas} can be done as well as badly, just as a guilty man can go to the Roman pontiff as foolishly as piously to obtain the benefit of absolution. And it ought to be supposed that [it is done] well on both sides, unless there is patent [evidence]{g:evidencia} to the contrary, and especially the pride and avarice of the [prelate]{g:prelatus}, and, on each side, the preponderance of the sacrament in every respect.[^5] For it seems that the monstrousness of sin in a [foreknown]{g:prescitus} male would be more of an obstacle to receiving or ministering lawfully any sacraments whatever than would be any bodily monstrousness however great, or the distinction of sex in a predestinate woman adorned with virtues, whether for receiving or for ministering any sacraments whatever.[^6] And yet, in punishment of sin, many of [9] an adulterous generation are so pharisaically led astray that they set the visible sign humanly instituted above the invisible thing signified, which God sets above it,[^7] as in the case and deed of the Roman church which [the Chester chronicler]{g:cestrensis} narrates in ch. 3 of book 5, namely that a certain woman sat in the papacy two years and five months after Leo.[^8] She is said to have been a girl called Agnes, "of Mainz by nation, brought to Athens by her lover in man's dress, and named John the Englishman, who so advanced in diverse sciences that, coming to Rome and having great masters for her hearers, she lectured on the trivium; at length, elected pope, she is made pregnant by her lover, and while she was going from the church of Peter to the Lateran church, gripped by the pains of childbirth, she gave birth between the Colosseum and Saint Clement's, and afterwards died there and was buried. And hence the pope is said commonly to turn aside from this road; and so she is not placed in the catalogue of popes."[^9] In this case, possible at least, the laws of men say that she did not perform papal acts and was not lawfully elected, because of the [unfitness]{g:indisposicio} of her body, even though she were already blessed. But it is hard to ground this, or to escape [the consequence] that election generally fails whenever one [unqualified]{g:inhabilis} as regards God is elected; hence human institution is hard put to define the true [pope]{g:papa}.[^10]

## ¶3 · 309.28–310.6

> [p. 309] Sed secundo obicitur per hoc, quod tam necessarium est sacramentum penitencie quam necessarium est sacramentum baptismi, sicut sacramentum ordinis est tam dignum vel dignius [p. 310] utrobique; igitur sicut licet layco propter necessitatem sacramenti ministrare baptismum, sic et in casu necessitatis licet sibi ministrare alia sacramenta. Antecedens videtur ex hoc, quod nullum istorum sacramentorum est necessarium ad salutem nisi necessitate ex supposicione.

But secondly [it is objected, by appeal to]{g:obicitur-per} this, that the sacrament of penance is as necessary as the sacrament of baptism, just as the sacrament of order is as worthy or worthier [10] on both counts; therefore, just as it is lawful for a layman, because of the necessity of the sacrament, to minister baptism, so also in a case of necessity it is lawful for him to minister the other sacraments. The [antecedent]{g:antecedens} appears from this, that none of these sacraments is necessary to salvation except by necessity [on supposition]{g:ex-supposicione}.

## ¶4 · 310.7–311.2

> [p. 310] Hic dicitur quod materia ista requirit fundabilem declaracionem sacramentorum cum suis passionibus. Ideo opportet leviter et breviter in istis transcurrere. Videtur enim quod baptismus habet maiorem auctoritatem scripture quam nostra penitencia humanitus circumstancionata. Unde videtur michi, quod sicut non est possibile peccatorem salvari nisi baptizetur baptismo flaminis, sic nec est possibile peccatorem salvari nisi mente peniteat; verumtamen videtur quod nulla scriptura obviat sed consonat, quod quantumcumque gravis peccator in fine contritus fuerit, et si nullum sacerdotem secum conversantem consuluerit aut peccata sua auriculariter ei ostenderit et dicendo verba humanitus inventa manum super caput penitentis imposuerit, non eo minus per absolucionem Christi quem opportet prius absolvere est vere de numero salvandorum, quia communiter in contricione precedenti peccatum deletur a Domino. Verumtamen meritorium atque legittimum est confiteri secundum formam iam currentem presbitero, sed preternecessarium quoad salvacionem, et licet posterius sufficiens et negligens confiteri proprio sacerdoti incurrit offensam Domini, tamen illa offensa per contricionem anime potest tolli, unde officium prelati foret ostendere libertatem Christi ecclesie et non [p. 311] propter fastum aut questum subditos infundabiliter in servitutem redigere.

Here [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that this matter requires a well-founded declaration of the sacraments with their [attributes]{g:passio}. Therefore one must run over these things lightly and briefly. For it seems that baptism has greater [authority]{g:auctoritas} of scripture than our penance with its humanly devised circumstances. Hence it seems to me that, just as it is not possible for a sinner to be saved unless he is baptized with the [baptism of the Spirit]{g:baptismus-flaminis}, so neither is it possible for a sinner to be saved unless he repents in mind;[^11] nevertheless it seems that no scripture opposes, but accords with this: that however grave a sinner, if he is contrite at the end, even if he has not consulted any [priest]{g:sacerdos} living with him, nor shown him his sins in the ear, nor has [the priest], saying words humanly invented, laid his hand upon the head of the penitent, he is none the less, through the absolution of Christ, who must absolve first, truly of the number of those to be saved; for commonly in the preceding contrition the sin is blotted out by the Lord.[^12] Nevertheless it is meritorious and lawful to confess to a [presbyter]{g:presbiter} according to the form now current, but [beyond necessity]{g:preternecessarius} as regards [salvation]{g:salvacio}; and although one who is afterwards able, and neglects to confess to his own priest, incurs the Lord's offence, yet that offence can be removed by contrition of soul;[^13] hence the office of the prelate would be to show the church the freedom of Christ, and not, [11] for pomp or gain, to reduce subjects [without foundation]{g:infundabiliter} into [servitude]{g:servitus}.

## ¶5 · 311.3–311.33

> [p. 311] Unde soleo dicere frequenter in ista materia, quod talia possunt bene fieri, sed si bene fiunt, trepido vel non dico. Et quando queritur si oportet sic fieri, detego libertates ecclesie. Unde credunt multi quod reservantes absoluciones ab excomunicacione, ab alio crimine vel alia censura imposita stulte sepe imponitur ad gravamen ecclesie, ut licet homo possit meritorie petere Romanam curiam, et talis ordinacio per accidens prosit multis, faciens eos timidos ad peccandum, verumtamen communius sapit peccatum in prelato et sollicitudinem preternecessariam in subdito. Sic quod melius foret multis domi satisfaciendo conteri, et quicumque dixerit quod cum hoc non potest salvari propter obedienciam faciendam ordinacioni prepositi, negando libertatem ecclesie transgreditur legem Dei. In votis quidem et obligacionibus stulte factis oportet penitere de stulticia et spiritum consilii flagitare. Unde videtur illis quod non est color concludere quod tale decretum reservandi est licitum, si subiectis timorem incutit ad peccandum; omne enim malum per accidens facit bonum, unde timor filialis debet incuti per sanctam predicacionem prelati, et non timor servilis iudicii per exaccionem prelati cesarei. Et sic videtur michi, quod in quocumque casu christianus non debet accipere dicta sacramenta a layco, cum hoc foret christianismum serviliter altrinsecus onerare. Dignitas autem sacramentorum pensanda consistit in dignitate rei, sacramenti et sua efficacia.

Hence I am accustomed to say often in this matter that such things can be done well; but whether they are done well, I tremble, or do not say. And when it is asked whether it must be done so, I expose the liberties of the church. Hence many believe that those reserving absolutions from excommunication, from another crime or from another [censure]{g:censura} imposed, often impose it foolishly, to the burden of the church;[^14] so that, although a man may meritoriously make for [the Roman curia]{g:curia-romana}, and such an [ordinance]{g:ordinacio} may by accident profit many, making them fearful of sinning, nevertheless it more commonly savours of sin in the prelate and of a care beyond necessity in the subject. So that it would be better for many to be contrite at home, making satisfaction; and whoever says that with this he cannot be saved, because of the obedience to be paid to the ordinance of a [superior]{g:prepositi}, by denying the liberty of the church transgresses [the law of God]{g:lex-dei}.[^15] In vows indeed and obligations foolishly made, one must repent of the folly and beg for the spirit of counsel. Hence it seems to them that there is no [colour]{g:color} for [inferring]{g:concludere} that such a decree of reservation is lawful if it strikes fear of sinning into the subjects; for every evil makes good by accident;[^16] hence filial fear ought to be struck by the holy preaching of the prelate, and not the servile fear of judgement by the exaction of a [caesarean prelate]{g:prelatus-cesarius}.[^17] And so it seems to me that in no case whatever ought a Christian to receive the said sacraments from a layman, since this would be to burden Christianity servilely from the other side. And the dignity of the sacraments, as it is to be weighed, consists in the dignity of the reality, of the sacrament, and its efficacy.[^18]

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Necessarioritas*, a non-classical comparative abstract: "greater necessity". The print's full stop after *sequitur* (307.33) interrupts a single sentence; we run it on.
[^2]: **C.** *Ipsum* we take as the irrational creature (*alii irracionali*): it too could then minister. It could also be read as God Himself ministering the sacraments, but *et ipsum* answers to the creature just named. **G.** *Subtiliare*: "refine", argue subtly (CONTEXTUAL row).
[^3]: **C.** The *quia cum* clause governs both members (*multa … predicare* and *grosse procedo …*), and *ideo dimitto* is its main verb. "God's ordained power" (*potencia ordinata*) is the scholastic pair of *potencia absoluta*. The marginal note in B at 308.13, *Baptizare si potest laicus*, marks the question.
[^4]: **C.** The subject of *non debet presumere* is the layman of the preceding clause.
[^5]: **C.** The final member (*et omnino utrimque preponderancia sacramenti*) is loosely attached; we take it as a further thing that may tell against the presumption of right dealing on either side: the sacrament overweighted. The construal is uncertain.
[^6]: **R.** A notes in the margin at 308.27 *Cave heresim*, "beware heresy": the A copyist's reader objects to the claim about a predestinate woman.
[^7]: **S.** "Adulterous generation" echoes Matt. 12:39 (DR "An evil and adulterous generation seeketh a sign"), not quoted.
[^8]: **S.** Ranulf Higden, *Polychronicon*; Loserth refers the story to vol. 6, pp. 330–352 (so printed; the Rolls Series edition, where the tale stands at V, 32, pp. 330–32; cf. 11-c n3) (LEDGER P11l-1). The A margin corrects the chapter: "it is not ch. 3 but 32", rightly (V, 32) (LEDGER P11l-2).
[^9]: **S.** The quotation bounds follow Loserth's italics, which begin at "of Mainz by nation" and run to "the catalogue of popes", the clause on the pope's detour included. **R.** Hus took this story from *De potestate pape* (Loserth: Hus, *Opera* I, 220a) (LEDGER P11l-3). A notes in the margin at 309.5 that Wyclif "here contradicts himself", having spoken otherwise of this woman earlier in the chapter; both manuscripts note at 309.8 *Mulier papa Agnes*.
[^10]: **C.** *Hoc fundare vel fugere, quin …*: "to ground this [legal position], or to avoid [admitting] that …". If bodily unfitness voids the election, so should unfitness before God, and then human law cannot say who the true pope is. **T.** For *fuerit* (309.23) B reads *fuit*; no change of sense.
[^11]: **R.** Both manuscripts note at 310.12 *Baptismus flaminis est necessarius ad salutem*. **T.** For *mente* (310.16) B reads *mentem*; we translate the print.
[^12]: **C.** *Et si* is concessive ("even if"); the three negated acts are consultation, auricular confession, and the priest's laying-on of hands with the formula. *Quem opportet prius absolvere*: Christ must absolve before any priest can.
[^13]: **C.** *Posterius sufficiens*: one who afterwards has the means or opportunity to confess. **R.** Both manuscripts note at 310.26 *Confessio auricularis est meritoria*.
[^14]: **C.** The Latin does not construe as printed: *reservantes* (plural participle) has no verb, and *imponitur* has no expressed subject. We take the sense to be that the reserving prelates impose the reservation foolishly. See also the next note.
[^15]: **T.** Loserth's foot at 311.18 reads *Recte: et negando* (plate): "and denying". The sense is the same either way, and we translate the print. Both manuscripts note at 311.11 *Romam adire pro peccato*, and at 311.19 *Votum stulte factum deletur per penitenciam*.
[^16]: **C.** The sentence turns the reserving prelates' argument: that fear of sinning is a good proves nothing, since any evil produces some good by accident (cf. 11-k).
[^17]: **T.** For *iudicii* (311.27) the manuscripts read *iudici*, "of the judge"; we translate the print, "servile fear of judgement".
[^18]: **C.** *Rei, sacramenti* may be the single phrase *res sacramenti*, "the reality of the sacrament", with the print's comma dividing it; we keep the comma and render three items.
