# *De potestate pape*, chapter 12, part 11: excommunication for withheld tithes, the schism, and alms against rents

## ¶1 · 352.32–354.32

> [p. 352] Et patet quod quicumque condit legem vel dat mandatum sub pena excommunicacionis ad nutriendum tales, ipse est pater eorum et precipuus Antichristus. Modicum itaque debet christianus [p. 353] curare de excommunicacione huiusmodi, cum Deus excommunicat hoc implentes, quia ut sic defraudant pauperes, dando improvide bona sua indignis; quod est indebitum et onerans procuratorem Domini ad satisfaciendum, cum talis solucio non sit apud summum iudicem allocanda. Nam secundum leges ecclesie: Judice fallaci pronunciante talem esse indignum sive hereticum non liceret sic ministrare sibi bona ecclesie, sed vel errat talis iudex vel curatus fuit eque indignus, antequam tulit ipse sentenciam, igitur cum notorietas facti plus docet indignitatem quam sentencia talis iudicis, videtur quod ante sentenciam talem debet credens operibus operari. Nam pars Roberti Gybbonensis excommunicat Urbanum nostrum cum suis complicibus et econtra; et consequenter potentes auferunt ab eis bona ecclesie. Nec dubium quin non sit propter nudam excommunicacionem plus vel minus, sed ut prevaricator excommunicatus a Deo in legem suam offenderit. Ideo multas pulcras veritates catholicas plane docet ista felix dissensio. Sicut enim Roberto volente excommunicare ecclesiam Anglicanam, nisi det suis cardinalibus et aliis indignis decimas et proventus nostre ecclesie, derideremus suam sentenciam, non minus servantes bona pauperum veris ministris ecclesie. Sic Urbano nostro excommunicante ecclesiam Anglicanam nisi det fructus ecclesie eque indignis, deridendo rebellaremus contra suam sentenciam pro lege Domini, ut patet in materia de excommunicacione. Et patet, quomodo antichristus cum suis fraudibus, que sunt leges Antichristiane, superat in fraude seculares. Ipsi enim, docto secundum leges eorum quod creditor sit indignus, faciunt quod cesset exaccio; isti autem statuunt quod fiat exaccio partis Domini, licet fuerit indignitas quoad Deum [p. 354] et homines, et sic contra Christum et legem suam palliant ex se aliam dignitatem. Manet (inquiunt) quantumcumque indignus quoad Deum ex auctoritate nostra dignus, quousque nos vel satrape nostri decreverimus quid sit iustum; et illud decretum fit communiter contra iusticiam, dum emens iniuriam voluerit plus conferre. Seculares eciam volunt quod secundum leges suas Dei iniuria destruatur. Isti autem sophisticant quod ad multiplicandum membra Antichristi quoad Christum iniuria foveatur, ut patet de pseudoprepositis, quos statuunt ad bona pauperum rapiendum. Seculares statuunt quod destruantur iniusti pacem reipublice perturbantes et libertatem ac regaliam regis sui proditorie machinantes. Isti autem infundabiliter statuunt antichristianas leges, quod multiplicentur segnes et sibi conformes prepositi, ex quibus oportet pacem Dei et libertatem regis regum in membris suis diminui. Vendicat enim dux exercitus Antichristi quod subditi non attendant ad dignitatem prelati, quem ipse instituit, cum eo ipso censetur habilis, quod est contra fidem evangelii et libertatem membrorum Christi, Matth. VII, 15, ubi docet Christus: Attendite a falsis prophetis, qui veniunt ad vos in vestimentis ovium, intrinsecus autem sunt lupi rapaces. Nam elemosinam dandam parte dignis prepositis ampliarunt ultra civile debitum, nedum tyrannice sed blasfeme per palliatas censuras, quas menciuntur a Christo procedere exigendam; et totum hoc seminavit in ecclesia civilitas cesarea, que est in clero ad deterius transformata.

And it is clear that whoever makes a law or gives a command under pain of excommunication for the nourishing of such [men] is himself their father and the chief Antichrist. So a Christian ought to care little [353] about excommunication of this kind, since God excommunicates those who fulfil this, because as such they defraud the poor, giving their goods improvidently to the unworthy; which is not owed, and burdens the Lord's [proctor]{g:procurator} with making [satisfaction]{g:satisfaccio}, since such a payment is not to [be allowed]{g:allocari} before the highest judge.[^1] For according to the laws of the [church]{g:ecclesia}: when a fallible judge pronounces such a one to be unworthy or a heretic, it would not be lawful thus to minister the goods of the church to him; but either such a judge errs, or the [curate]{g:curatus} was equally unworthy before the judge passed the [sentence]{g:sentencia}; therefore, since the notoriety of the fact teaches unworthiness more than the sentence of such a judge, it seems that before such a sentence the believer ought to act upon the works.[^2] For the party of Robert of Geneva excommunicates our Urban with his accomplices, and the other way about; and consequently the powerful take the goods of the church away from them.[^3] Nor is there doubt that this is not on account of the bare excommunication, more or less, but because as a [transgressor]{g:prevaricator} excommunicated by God he has offended against his law. Therefore this happy dissension plainly teaches many fair catholic truths. For just as, if Robert wished to excommunicate the English church unless it gave to his cardinals and other unworthy men the [tithes]{g:decime} and proceeds of our church, we would laugh at his sentence, keeping no less the goods of the poor for the true ministers of the church; so, if our Urban excommunicates the English church unless it gives the fruits of the church to men equally unworthy, we would, laughing, rebel against his sentence for the law of the Lord, as is clear in the matter of excommunication.[^4] And it is clear how antichrist with his frauds, which are antichristian laws, surpasses the seculars in fraud. For they, when it has been shown according to their laws that the creditor is unworthy, cause the exaction to cease; but these decree that the exaction of the Lord's portion be made, although there was unworthiness as regards God [354] and men; and so against Christ and his law they [cloak]{g:palliare} out of themselves another worthiness.[^5] "He remains," they say, "however unworthy as regards God, worthy by our [authority]{g:auctoritas}, until we or our [satraps]{g:satrapa} have decreed what is just"; and that decree is commonly made against justice, when the one buying the wrong is willing to contribute more. The seculars, too, will that according to their laws the wrong [done] to God be destroyed. But these [play the sophist]{g:sophisticari} [to bring it about] that, to multiply the [members]{g:membrum} of Antichrist, the wrong as regards Christ be fostered, as is clear of the [false superiors]{g:pseudoprepositi} whom they appoint to plunder the goods of the poor.[^6] The seculars decree that the unjust be destroyed who disturb the peace of the commonwealth and treacherously plot against the liberty and [regality]{g:regalia} of their king. But these, [without foundation]{g:infundabiliter}, decree antichristian laws that sluggish [superiors]{g:prepositi} conformed to themselves be multiplied, by whom the peace of God and the liberty of the King of kings in his members must be diminished. For the leader of the army of Antichrist claims that subjects are not to attend to the worthiness of the [prelate]{g:prelatus} whom he himself has instituted, since by that very fact he is deemed [qualified]{g:habilis}; which is against the faith of the gospel and the liberty of the members of Christ, Matthew 7:15, where Christ teaches: "Beware of false prophets, who come to you in the clothing of sheep, but inwardly they are ravening wolves."[^7] For the [alms]{g:elemosina} to be given in part to worthy superiors they have extended beyond the civil debt, not only tyrannically but blasphemously, through cloaked [censures]{g:censura}, which they falsely claim proceed from Christ for its exaction; and all this was sown in the church by the caesarean [civil standing]{g:civilitas}, which in the clergy has been transformed for the worse.[^8]

## ¶2 · 354.33–355.3

> [p. 354] Sed contra dicta arguitur: Videtur enim iuxta hanc viam quod nemo daret redditus infideli [p. 355] domino vel tyranno, quod est contra fidem scripture, que docet obedire eciam discolis, quod Ihesus noster in facto docuit.

But against what has been said it is argued: for it seems, on this way, that no one would give [revenues]{g:reditus} to an [unbelieving]{g:infidelis} [355] lord or tyrant; which is against the faith of scripture, which teaches [us] to obey even the [froward]{g:discoli}, as our Jesus taught in deed.[^9]

## ¶3 · 355.4–356.20

> [p. 355] Sed hic dicitur negando contrarium; quia omnia temporalia data clerico ut huiusmodi debent dari secundum racionem elemosine, que debet esse voluntaria et discreta; redditus autem dati seculari domino ut tali debent dari secundum racionem contractus et subieccionis civilis, que non respicit dignitatem prepositi quoad Deum; nec est tenens culpandus, licet coacte det temporalia tyranno, quem credit esse indignum, ymo propter pacem emendam debet gratis dare et servire domino infideli, ut docet Apostolus; ne nomen Domini blasfemetur; et si crediderit per subtraccionem servitutis vel redditus convertere dominum infidelem debet ea intencione subtrahere salva fide; talem autem fidem non fecit legibus Antichristi. Quamvis autem leges civiles erant diu ante incarnacionem Domini et sint secundum Augustinum mixte multis nequiciis, tamen sunt plurimum iuste, cum pati rapinas bonorum et ceteras iniurias sit meritorium; lex autem Antichristi sub specie sanctitatis et legis Domini est heretica et blasfema, cui omnes fideles debent resistere et execucioni sue secundum formam ipocrisis; quia si vocatus clerus velit rapere bona subditi per modum tyrannidis est meritorie tollerandus. Sed cum defert talem civilem tyrannidem et blasfeme palliat ac sub colore legis civilis exaccionem bonorum fortune per viam excommunicacionis vel alterius censure, que nec cogit corporaliter nec nocet anime impetiti in contemptum legis, debent servari temporalia, cum nec sit fides nec elemosina [p. 356] sic dare blasfemis bona pauperum, ideo quod ea racione debetur caret colore meriti. Sed dimittendo hanc formam et innitendo pugnis et spoliacioni civili, ut modo faciunt discipuli Antichristi, videtur michi quod via meritorior atque securior foret pati iniurias, confidenter expectando divinum iudicium, sed non propter timorem fulminis quod pretendunt. Sic igitur verificata est de nomine christianis et paganis vel clero et layco prophecia Ezechielis XXIII de duabus sororibus Oolla et Oolliba; nam Oolliba christiana plus fornicata est spiritualiter cum tradicionibus infidelium quam Oolla, quia plus insanivit sophisticando legem, et excanduit in temporalium voluptatem, ideo secundum diffinicionem Dei tradendi sunt in manus gencium puniendi; deseruerunt enim viam, quam Christus docuit, ad intrinsecus se regendum et exteros conquirendum; nam mamme pubertatis sunt fracte, dum leges infancium sunt dirupte.

But here [the answer is]{g:dicitur} [given] by denying the contrary;[^10] because all [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} given to a cleric as such ought to be given according to the nature of alms, which ought to be voluntary and discreet; but revenues given to a secular lord as such ought to be given according to the nature of a contract and of civil subjection, which does not regard the worthiness of the superior as regards God; nor is the tenant to be blamed, although under compulsion he gives temporal goods to a tyrant whom he believes to be unworthy; indeed, for the sake of buying peace he ought to give freely and serve an unbelieving lord, as the Apostle teaches, lest the name of the Lord be blasphemed;[^11] and if he believes that by withdrawing [servitude]{g:servitus} or revenue he can convert the unbelieving lord, he ought to withdraw them with that intention, saving faith; but such faith he did not pledge to the laws of Antichrist.[^12] And although the civil laws existed long before the incarnation of the Lord, and are, according to Augustine, mixed with many wickednesses, yet they are for the most part just, since to suffer the plundering of one's goods and other wrongs is meritorious;[^13] but the law of Antichrist, under the appearance of holiness and of the law of the Lord, is heretical and blasphemous, and all the faithful ought to resist it and its execution according to the form of hypocrisy; because if the [so-called]{g:vocatus} clergy wishes to seize the goods of a subject by way of tyranny, it is to be meritoriously tolerated. But when it sets aside such civil tyranny, and blasphemously cloaks, and under [colour]{g:color} of civil law [pursues], the exaction of goods of fortune by way of excommunication or another censure, which neither coerces bodily nor harms the soul of the one assailed in contempt of the law, the temporal goods ought to be kept, since it is neither faith nor alms [356] to give the goods of the poor thus to blasphemers, because what is owed on that ground lacks the colour of merit.[^14] But, leaving this form and relying on fists and civil despoiling, as the disciples of Antichrist now do, it seems to me that the more meritorious and safer way would be to suffer the wrongs, confidently awaiting the divine judgment, but not for fear of the thunderbolt which they hold out. Thus, then, the prophecy of Ezekiel 23 about the two sisters Oolla and Ooliba has been verified of Christians and pagans in name, or of clergy and laity;[^15] for the Christian Ooliba has committed spiritual fornication with the traditions of unbelievers more than Oolla, because she went more mad in [counterfeiting]{g:sophisticare} the law, and burned with desire for the pleasure of temporal goods; therefore according to the [definition]{g:diffinicio} of God they are to be delivered into the hands of the gentiles to be punished; for they have deserted the way which Christ taught for ruling themselves inwardly and winning those outside; for the teats of [their] virginity are bruised, while the laws of the infants are torn apart.[^16]

## ¶4 · 356.21–356.26

> [p. 356] Sed secundo obicitur per hoc quod de lege Dei evangelizantibus debentur stipendia, ut docet Apostolus prima Cor. IX et Luce X; oportet igitur esse aliquam legem que iniuriantes coherceat, cuiusmodi est lex ecclesie, quam oportet credere in hac parte.

But, secondly, [it is objected, by appeal to]{g:obicitur-per} this: that by God's law stipends are owed to [preachers of the gospel]{g:evangelizans}, as the Apostle teaches, 1 Corinthians 9 and Luke 10; there must therefore be some law that [coerces]{g:cohercere} those who do [them] wrong, of which kind is the law of the church, which must be believed in this respect.[^17]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 352.32: Excommunication for refusal to pay may be disregarded.
- 353.7: Such excommunication is wrongful or superfluous.
- 353.21: The schism teaches many catholic truths, especially as to the unimportance of excommunication.
- 353.32: Secular laws less unjust than those of Antichrist.
- 354.20: The pope claims that whoever he appoints is to be taken as qualified.
- 355.4: Tithes and offerings are alms to be given willingly and with judgment.
- 355.12: Payments to secular lords are on another footing.
- 355.23: Exactions enforced by excommunication should be resisted
- 356.3: We should submit to injustice patiently, awaiting God's judgment.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *onerans procuratorem Domini ad satisfaciendum*: the giver, as steward of goods that are the Lord's, is charged with making satisfaction for them; *allocanda* is the accounting sense: such a payment will not be allowed in his account before God.
[^2]: **C.** *Judice fallaci pronunciante*: ablative absolute; *fallax* taken as "fallible" (it may also be "deceitful"). The argument: by canon law payment stops once even a fallible judge pronounces the incumbent unworthy; the notorious fact shows unworthiness better than such a sentence, so payment may stop before it. *Sentenciam* is the judicial sentence, so "sentence". **S.** *credens operibus operari* recalls John 10:38, "believe the works".
[^3]: **R.** Robert of Geneva, elected by the dissident cardinals at Fondi in September 1378 as Clement VII, against Urban VI, whom England recognised; *Gybbonensis* is the print's form of *Gebennensis* (LEDGER P12k-1).
[^4]: **C.** *ut patet in materia de excommunicacione*: Wyclif's treatment of excommunication elsewhere; the work is not named (LEDGER P12k-2). *Proventus* "proceeds", kept apart from *redditus* "revenues".
[^5]: **C.** *Ipsi* are the seculars, *isti* the makers of antichristian law; *creditor* is the one to whom payment is owed, here the incumbent. *Antichristus* is lower-case in the print at 353.33. *Ex se*: a worthiness of their own making.
[^6]: **C.** *sophisticant quod*: the active verb with a *quod*-clause, "they argue sophistically to secure that"; the sense is that of *sophisticari* "to play the sophist", not the transitive "counterfeit" (which comes at 356.16). *Dei iniuria*: the wrong done to God.
[^7]: **S.** Matthew 7:15, agreeing with the Vulgate; Douay-Rheims. **T.** At 354.22 B reads *instituunt* for *instituit* and lacks *censetur*; the print is followed.
[^8]: **C.** *elemosinam dandam parte dignis prepositis*: *parte* does not construe easily; taken as "in part", i.e. the alms owed, within limits, to worthy superiors. A word may be lost, or *parte* may stand for *parce* "sparingly". *Exigendam* agrees with *elemosinam*. *Civilitas cesarea*: the civil standing the clergy took from Caesar's endowment.
[^9]: **S.** 1 Peter 2:18 (DR "not only to the good and gentle but also to the froward"). "In deed": probably the tribute paid for Christ and Peter, Matthew 17:26. **C.** *redditus* here are the tenant's rents to his lord; the glossary's "revenues" is kept.
[^10]: **C.** *negando contrarium*: Wyclif denies that the contrary of scripture's teaching follows from his position; the distinction between alms and civil dues follows.
[^11]: **S.** 1 Timothy 6:1 (DR "lest the name of the Lord and his doctrine be blasphemed"); Wyclif shortens the verse, and Loserth does not italicise it, so it is left unquoted.
[^12]: **C.** *fidem facere*: "to pledge faith", the tenant's fealty to his lord; *salva fide* keeps that fealty while the dues are withheld. No such fealty binds anyone to Antichrist's laws.
[^13]: **S.** Augustine on civil law as mixed with wickedness: to be checked (LEDGER P12k-3). The merit of suffering the plundering of one's goods: Hebrews 10:34 (DR "took with joy the being stripped of your own goods").
[^14]: **C.** *defert*: taken as "sets aside" (as against *dimittendo hanc formam* in the next sentence); *palliat* governs *exaccionem*. *Impetiti*: the one assailed. *In contemptum legis* may go with *nocet* or describe the one excommunicated "for contempt of the law". *Color*: "colour", a plausible ground (also *colore meriti*). **T.** At 355.24–25 the manuscripts read *blasfemia*; the print's *blasfeme* is followed.
[^15]: **S.** Ezekiel 23:4: Oolla is Samaria, Ooliba Jerusalem; the names are given in the Douay-Rheims form (print *Oolliba*). **T.** The manuscripts read *Cella et Colliba* (356.11, 13); the names are Loserth's correction. **C.** *de nomine christianis et paganis*: taken as "Christians and pagans in name".
[^16]: **S.** Ezekiel 23:11 (DR "she was mad with lust more than she"); 23:9 ("delivered her into the hands of her lovers"); 23:3 (DR "the teats of their virginity were bruised"). **C.** *diffinicio Dei*: God's determining sentence. *Leges infancium*: the law given to the church in its infancy, taken as the sense; uncertain.
[^17]: **S.** 1 Corinthians 9:14 (DR "they who preach the gospel should live by the gospel"); Luke 10:7 ("the labourer is worthy of his hire"). The print gives chapters only.
