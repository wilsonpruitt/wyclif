# *De potestate pape*, chapter 11, part 10: clerical dignity in poverty; Gregory refuses the name "universal"; an argument for the caesarean order

## ¶1 · 299.6–300.26

> [p. 299] Ista autem sentencia placeret quibusdam episcopis qui, licet debeant habere omnia cum fratribus in communi, tamen stante ista sentencia pro conservandis suis privilegiis non tribuerent eis temporalia plus vel minus. Et multo evidencius nec aliquis secularis. Dignitas itaque clericalis est dignitate seculari necessarior, sed oportet quod sit illa dignitas paupertatis et moris quam Christus instituit, quia secularis dignitas cleri utramque partem matris nostre intoxicat. Christus enim in cunabulis satis pauperibus adoratus fuit a regibus, ut patet Matth. II. Sed indutus vestem purpuream fuit flagellatus a militibus, ut patet Mar. XV. Unde premittit exoneracionem et excussionem festuce fedantis ecclesiam, et sic tamquam formam ultimam vie inducit apostolicam dignitatem, ut patet Matth. XIX. Et ibidem dicitur: Si vis perfectus esse, vade et vende omnia que habes, et da pauperibus, et veni, sequere me. Christus enim noluit suos apostolos occupari circa tam contemptibile officium, cuiusmodi est civilis distribucio elemosine corporalis; hoc enim est laycale officium, quod sacerdotes debent postponere. Tercium vero exemplum periculi est quod Apostolo ferenti benediccionem vel elemosinam sanctis Jerusalem opposite fuerunt insidie, ut innuitur Act. XX. Non obstante autem multiplici evidencia fideli, nonnulli, qui gustaverunt altissime paupertatis dulcedinem, habent ex religione omnia [p. 300] in communi, apostatant plus quam Ananyas et Saphira, palliantes dignitatem episcopalem et bona pauperum non cum fratribus, sed nimis seculariter et proprietarie consumentes; unde videtur ut supra, quod fratres sui qui multipliciter apostasie tam gravi consenciunt, carerent benediccione populi dicentis quod capiant de bonis suis, quibus habundant populo copiosius in se et suo episcopo. Nec dubium fideli quin fratres qui propter personale commodum vel honorem ordinis vel aliunde omittendo talem corripere culpe sue consenciunt, et sic indignificant se ad recipiendum elemosinam pauperum laycorum. Nam debet esse per se notum fidelibus quod propter peccatum personale est communitas punienda, ut propter peccatum primi hominis totum genus humanum patitur, ut propter errorem archiprepositi inferiores prepositi et tota communitas paciuntur. Ideo (ut dixi alias) ad destruendum errorem paparum totus christianismus et specialiter episcopi debent consurgere. Blasfemant enim in Deum, dicentes quod ipsi habent infinitum maiorem potestatem quam aliquis episcopus citra ipsos, quia infinitum ampliores indulgencias, dispensaciones et cetera privilegia possunt concedere quam aliquis episcopus citra ipsos.

And this [teaching]{g:sentencia}[^1] would please certain [bishops]{g:episcopus} who, although they ought to have all things in common with their brethren, yet, while this teaching stands, would not, for the preserving of their [privileges]{g:privilegium}, give them [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, more or less.[^2] And much more evidently, neither would any [secular]{g:secularis}. Clerical dignity, then, is more necessary than secular dignity; but it must be that dignity of poverty and of conduct which Christ instituted, because secular dignity in the [clergy]{g:clerus} poisons both parts of [our mother]{g:mater-nostra}. For Christ, in a cradle poor enough, was adored by kings, as is clear in Matt. 2. But clothed in a purple garment he was scourged by soldiers, as is clear in Mark 15.[^3] Hence he puts first the unburdening and the shaking off of the mote that fouls the [church]{g:ecclesia}, and so, as the last form of the way, brings in the apostolic dignity, as is clear in Matt. 19. And it is said in the same place: "If thou wilt be perfect, go and sell all that thou hast, and give to the poor, and come, follow me."[^4] For Christ did not want his apostles to be occupied with so contemptible an office as is the civil distribution of bodily [alms]{g:elemosina}; for this is a lay office, which priests ought to put second.[^5] And the third example of danger is that ambushes were laid against the Apostle as he was bringing a blessing or alms to the saints of Jerusalem, as is suggested in Acts 20.[^6] But notwithstanding the manifold faithful [evidence]{g:evidencia}, some who have tasted the sweetness of the highest poverty, [and] have all things [300] in common by their [religion]{g:religio-privata}, apostatize more than Ananias and Sapphira, [cloaking]{g:palliare} themselves in the episcopal dignity, and consuming the goods of the poor not with their brethren but too secularly and [proprietarily]{g:proprietarius};[^7] hence it seems, as above, that their brethren, who in many ways consent to so grave an apostasy, should go without the blessing of the people, who say that they should take from their own goods, in which, in themselves and in their bishop, they abound more copiously than the people.[^8] Nor is it doubtful to the faithful man that the brethren who, for personal advantage or the honour of the order or from some other cause, omit to [reprove]{g:corripere} such a one, consent to his fault, and so make themselves unworthy to receive the alms of poor lay people. For it ought to be self-evident to the faithful that for a personal sin the community is to be punished, as for the sin of the first man the whole human race suffers, as for the error of the arch-superior the lower [superiors]{g:prepositi} and the whole community suffer.[^9] Therefore (as I have said elsewhere) the whole of Christendom, and especially the bishops, ought to rise up together to destroy the error of the popes. For they blaspheme against God, saying that they have infinitely greater [power]{g:potestas} than any bishop below them, because they can grant infinitely ampler [indulgences]{g:indulgencie}, [dispensations]{g:dispensacio} and other privileges than any bishop below them.

## ¶2 · 300.27–302.22

> [p. 300] Et hoc indubie sonat in scandalum toti ecclesie propter multa: primo quia blasfemia facta in Christum caput, qua innuitur quod papa sit maioris potestatis date a cesare quam Christus habuit, facit subtraccionem capitalis suffragii; secundo quia papa habente solum finitam potestatem quilibet illorum prelatorum non haberet potestatem sub aliquo gradu signabili, et tercio quia propter subtraccionem minoris boni quam [p. 301] foret subsidium, quod papa subtrahit populus licite et laboriose prosequitur. Quare igitur non prosequitur contra papam correptibilem fratrem nostrum? Et ista videtur sentencia beati Gregorii posita XCIX distinccione: Ecce, ubi precepit Eulogio patriarche Alexandrino quod non vocet eum amplius papam universalem: Vobis, inquit, subtrahitur quod alteri plus quam racio exigit prebetur, ego vero non verbis quero prosperari sed moribus, nec honorem esse deputo in quo fratres nostros honorem suum perdere cognosco. Meus namque est honor universalis ecclesie, meus honor est fratrum meorum solidus vigor; tunc ego honoratus sum, cum singulis quibusque honor debitus non negatur. Si me enim universalem papam vestra sanctitas dicit, negat se esse hoc, quod me fatetur, universum, sed absit hoc, recedant verba, que unitatem inflant et caritatem vulnerant. Unde Pellagius papa: Nullus patriarcharum universalitatis vocabulo umquam utatur, quia si unus eorum universalis dicitur, patriarcharum nomen ceteris derogatur. Sed absit a fidelibus hoc sibi quempiam nomen velle arripere, unde honorem fratrum suorum ex quantidacumque parte minuere videatur; quapropter caritas vestra neminem umquam in suis epistolis universalem nominet, ne sibi debitum subtrahat, dum alteri honorem offert indebitum. Ideo ex generali concilio decretum est, quod prime sedis episcopus non appelletur princeps sacerdotum aut summus sacerdos aut aliquid huiusmodi, sed tantum prime sedis episcopus. Universalis autem nec eciam romanus pontifex appelletur. [p. 302] Licet enim possent hec nomina sane intelligi, mirum est tamen quomodo posteriores, peiores prioribus, tale nomen singulariter sibi vendicant sine fructu; etenim onus nimium, licet omnes tales episcopi comportent et regulent ecclesiam communi concilio sicut olim. Et patet, quod nuda est ista argucia, Gregorius vel alius sanctus Romanus episcopus fuit precellens pre aliis, igitur per idem et quilibet Romanus pontifex. Variatur enim stulta affeccio dignitatis. Stultum quidem est assumere nomen Christi, ut Bernhardus, Lyncolniensis et Jeronymus describunt sine possibili correspondencia dignitatis. Idem enim est hoc facere et symeam ad summum repere ut profundius et gravius preceps ruat. Sed contra ista arguitur per hoc, quod totus iste ordo cesareus videtur esse ad honorem et utilitatem ecclesie matris sancte, non igitur destruendum est tam utile et tantum faciens ad honorem ecclesie, potissime cum nescitur quibus prodesset eius destruccio, et nulla persona ecclesie est improvide punienda.

And this without doubt sounds to the scandal of the whole church, for many reasons: first, because the blasphemy done against Christ the [head]{g:caput}, by which it is implied that the [pope]{g:papa} is of greater power, given by Caesar, than Christ had, brings about the withdrawal of the head's aid;[^10] secondly, because, if the pope had only finite power, each of those [prelates]{g:prelatus} would have power at no [assignable]{g:signabile} degree;[^11] and thirdly, because for the withdrawal of a lesser good than [301] would be the subsidy which the pope withdraws, the people lawfully and laboriously go to law. Why then do they not proceed against the pope, our brother, who is open to reproof? And this seems to be the teaching of blessed Gregory set down in distinction 99, *Ecce*, where he commanded Eulogius, patriarch of Alexandria, not to call him universal pope any more: "To you," he says, "is withdrawn what is offered to another beyond what reason requires. I, however, seek to prosper not in words but in conduct; nor do I count that an honour in which I know my brethren lose their honour. For my honour is the honour of the universal church; my honour is the solid strength of my brethren; then am I honoured, when the honour due is not denied to each and every one. For if your holiness calls me universal pope, you deny yourself to be what you confess me to be, universal. But far be this; away with words that puff up vanity and wound [charity]{g:caritas}."[^12] Hence Pope Pelagius: "Let none of the patriarchs ever use the word of universality, for if one of them is called universal, the name of patriarch is taken from the rest. But far be it from the faithful that anyone should wish to snatch this name for himself, whereby he might seem to diminish the honour of his brethren in however small a part; wherefore let your charity never name anyone universal in your letters, lest you withdraw from yourself what is due, while you offer undue honour to another."[^13] Therefore it was decreed by a general council "that the bishop of the first see should not be called prince of priests or [high priest]{g:summus-sacerdos} or anything of the kind, but only bishop of the first see. And not even the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex} is to be called universal."[^14] [302] For although these names could be soundly understood, yet it is a wonder how later men, worse than the earlier, claim such a name singly for themselves without fruit; for [it is] too great a burden, even though all such bishops should carry it together and rule the church by common counsel, as of old.[^15] And it is clear that this [quibble]{g:argucie} is bare: "Gregory, or another holy [Roman bishop]{g:romanus-episcopus}, was pre-excellent above the others; therefore by the same token so is every Roman pontiff." For the foolish affection for dignity varies. It is indeed foolish to assume the name of Christ, as Bernard, Lincoln and Jerome describe, without a possible correspondence of dignity.[^16] For to do this is the same as for an ape to creep to the top, so that it may fall headlong the more deeply and heavily. But against these things [it is argued by appeal to]{g:arguitur-per} this: that this whole [caesarean order]{g:ordo-cesareus} seems to be for the honour and benefit of the church, our holy mother; therefore a thing so useful, and doing so much for the honour of the church, is not to be destroyed, especially since it is not known whom its destruction would profit, and no person of the church is to be punished improvidently.

## ¶3 · 302.23–302.33

> [p. 302] Assumptum videtur ex hoc quod, etsi omnes dotati forent dampnandi ex dotacione, adhuc dotacio foret ad honorem et utilitatem ecclesie, igitur multo magis multis personis hinc altrinsecus merentibus et salvatis. Assumptum patet ex hoc, quod totus prescitorum numerus sicut et omnis culpa proderit per accidens et honorabit numerum salvandorum, nam dampnati exercitabunt predestinatos, glorificabunt eorum victorias et cedent eis ad augmentum et racionem beatitudinis ut hic supponitur.

[The assumption]{g:assumptum} seems [clear] from this: that even if all the endowed were to be damned by reason of the [endowment]{g:dotacio}, the endowment would still be for the honour and benefit of the church; therefore much more so, when many persons on either side merit by it and are saved.[^17] The assumption is clear from this: that the whole number of [the foreknown]{g:prescitus}, as also every fault, will profit by accident and will honour the number of those to be saved; for the damned will exercise [the predestinate]{g:predestinatus}, will glorify their victories, and will turn to their increase and to the ground of their blessedness, as is here supposed.[^18]

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Sentencia* in its sense "teaching" (GLOSSARY, CONTEXTUAL): the opinion just answered, that endowment serves the church as miracles once did.
[^2]: **C.** These are bishops taken from the orders, who by their profession should hold all in common with their *fratres*, the brethren (friars) of their order. *Plus vel minus*: "at all".
[^3]: **S.** Matt. 2:11; Mark 15:15–17, where Christ is scourged and then clothed in purple by the soldiers. *Milites* here are the Roman soldiers, not the estate of knights.
[^4]: **S.** Matt. 19:21. Wyclif reads *vade et vende omnia que habes* for the Vulgate's *vade, vende quae habes*, and omits "and thou shalt have treasure in heaven"; his Latin is translated.
[^5]: **S.** Cf. Acts 6:2, "It is not reason that we should leave the word of God and serve tables."
[^6]: **S.** Acts 20:3, "the Jews laid wait for him"; the collection for the saints at Jerusalem is Rom. 15:25–26. *Tercium exemplum* follows the two examples of Matt. 2 and Mark 15.
[^7]: **C.** *Habent* stands without a connective after the relative clause; it is read as a second clause of the same relative ("[and] have"). *Palliantes dignitatem episcopalem* may also mean "using the episcopal dignity as a cloak" (for consuming the goods of the poor). **S.** Ananias and Sapphira: Acts 5:1–10.
[^8]: **C.** *Carerent benediccione populi*: the people's "blessing" is their alms (as *benediccio* for the collection in the previous sentence); the people's answer is reported indirectly.
[^9]: **G.** *Archiprepositus* "arch-superior" is formed on *prepositi* "superiors"; no row yet.
[^10]: **C.** *Capitalis suffragii*: the aid that comes from Christ as head (*caput*), not GLOSSARY's *capitalis* "capital" (of Rome).
[^11]: **C.** If the pope's power is finite and yet infinitely greater than any bishop's, the bishop's power falls below every degree that can be named, that is, it would be nothing. *Signabilis*: CONTEXTUAL, "assignable" (any one could name); cf. the logical noun "signifiable".
[^12]: **S.** Decretum D.99 c.5 (*Ecce*), from Gregory the Great's letter to Eulogius of Alexandria (*Reg.* 8.29); the canon is Loserth's (modern form ours; LEDGER P11j-1), the letter's place ours (PL 77, 933C, numbered there 8.30). **T.** The print reads *unitatem* "unity" (so on the plate); Gregory's text (PL 77, 933C) and Gratian's (PL 187, 474A) both read *vanitatem*, and the English follows them: "puff up vanity" (our emendation).
[^13]: **S.** Decretum D.99 c.4 (*Nullus patriarcharum*), under the name of Pope Pelagius (II); Loserth's reference, modern form ours (LEDGER P11j-4).
[^14]: **S.** Decretum D.99 c.3 (*Prime sedis*), a canon of an African council (Carthage) that Wyclif calls "general"; Loserth's reference, modern form ours (LEDGER P11j-5); Gratian's rubric *ex Concilio Africano*; it is Carthage III (397) c. 26 (PL 84, 192C). Loserth's italics give the canon as a quotation; its last sentence (*Universalis autem …*) is Gratian's dictum introducing c.4, not part of the canon.
[^15]: **C.** *Etenim onus nimium* has no verb: "[it is] too great a burden", the burden of the name's office, which even all bishops together would carry only with difficulty. At 302.4 Loserth's page foot reports a reading with *fructum* ("sive fructum"); the OCR is too rough to say which witness, and the print's *sine fructu* construes.
[^16]: **S.** Bernard, Grosseteste (*Lincolniensis*, "Lincoln") and Jerome on assuming Christ's name without his dignity: no passage given by Loserth. For Bernard cf. *De consideratione* 2.7.14–15 (*Simia in tecto, rex fatuus in solio … Monstruosa res gradus summus et animus infimus*), which the ape image here echoes and which Wyclif quotes in *De officio regis*; the Grosseteste and Jerome passages are untraced.
[^17]: **C.** *Hinc altrinsecus*: "on either side" of the endowment, the givers and the receivers. *Assumptum videtur* lacks a predicate; "seems [clear]" is supplied from the next sentence's *patet*.
[^18]: **C.** The second *Assumptum* is the premiss of the sub-argument just given (that even the damned endowed serve the church). *Ut hic supponitur* marks this as the objector's supposition; Wyclif answers in the next part. *Racionem beatitudinis*: the ground or measure of their blessedness.
