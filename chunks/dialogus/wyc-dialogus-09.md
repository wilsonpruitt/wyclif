# *Dialogus*, chapter 9: the new chief priests

## Truth · 17.2–19.12

> [p. 17] Adhuc pro declaracione ulteriori objectus heretici quem inculcas, notandum est quod sicut avari principes sacerdotum et pharisei legis veteris humanitatem Christi occiderant propter timorem amissionis bonorum temporalium, sic diciores principes sacerdotum pro tempore legis gracie divinitatem Christi, quia veritatem legis evangelice quantum suffecerant extinxerunt. Ideo ut multis videtur postquam majorem Christi gratitudinem perceperant tanquam plus ingrati magis peccaverant. Unde jo 11° sic scribitur: collegerunt pontifices et pharisei concilium adversus jhm et dicebant, quid facimus quia hic homo multa signa facit? Si dimittimus eum sic omnes credent in eum, et venient Romani et tollent locum nostrum et gentem. Sed principes sacerdotum et Pharisei tempore legis gracie habent scriptas bullas et cartas, que omnes sonant in suam avariciam et veritatem evangelicam nituntur extinguere, et promulgantes eam licet trepide persecuntur: et sic ut videtur multis nostri principes sacerdotum et pharisei multipliciter magis peccant, quia sunt sub majori ipocrisi plus ingrati, et non solum cum majori multitudine Christum persecuntur in membris, sed tanquam eis summum odibile contempnunt predicacionem evangelice veritatis. Cujus causa videtur quia sciunt ex fide catholica quod debent Christum sequi in moribus. Et evangelium docet cum vita eorum quod sunt vite et doctrine Christi nimis contrarii. Ideo odiunt quod istud populo declaretur... Et sic quantum ad gloriam quam fingis Christi sacerdotibus debere modo competere, patet quod ignominiam vocas gloriam, cum in statu plus periculoso hortaris quod clerus relinquat arma spiritualia, in quibus debet diabolum, carnem et seculum [p. 18] superare et preter hec imponis Christo manifestam blasfemiam quod ipse tanquam reditus istius ignarus, vel conversacionem ecclesie sue necgligens, istum reditum dereliquit. Et quantum ad nominacionem patrimonii crucifixi, patet quod multiplicas blasfemias contra clerum; quia magnum peccatum est prevaricari sic in mandatum domini, sed major blasfemia est imponere Christo quod ipse approbat et necessitat clericos sic peccare. Sic enim, tanquam alter adam, nititur clerus suam culpam in dominum retorquere: mulier inquit, quam dedisti michi sociam dedit michi de ligno et comedi ut patet gen 3° Clerus autem mentitur innuitive longe abjeccius cum dicat quod Christus in ligno fuerat crucifixus ut clerus suus postmodum taliter dominetur. Sed que major blasfemia quam Christo imponere talem culpam? Et per consequens est summa blasfemia quod clerus Christi virtute hujus passionis pauperis et humilis debet sic toto seculo dominari. Et quantum ad cartas et concessiones secularium dominorum patet quod clerus erubesceret inniti tam culpabili fundamento Nam imperator et domini alii in hoc per regem superbie sunt seducti. Ideo expergiscant sompno istius criminis soporati, et cognoscant hoc principium clare demonstrabile apud catholicos quod in nullo valet humana concessio, nisi prehabita licencia a domino capitali. Cum ergo non possunt docere quod ad hoc dominium habebant a Christo licenciam, patet quod tam lege humana quam divina est ista stulta donacio irritanda. Vel ergo oportet ipsos negare blasfeme Christi capitale dominium supra cesarem, vel concedere donacionem istius dominii esse illicitam et per consequens a catholicis respuendam, vel 3° ad hoc ostendere Christi licenciam specialem. Et de quanto clerus vel seculares [p. 19] domini in isto crimine diucius immorantur, de tanto in peccato maximo diabolice se indurant. Et quantum ad leges et vitas papales patet quod est confirmacio criminis sicut prius. Quid enim valent iste evidencie nisi probate fuerint racionibus vel scriptura. Sed, cum non sunt in Christo est et non, patet quod impossibile est istorum alterutro tradiciones istas papales vel leges hominum stabilire. Et vellem quod, clerus contra expropriacionem istam remurmurans, etiam papa ad istam racionem efficaciter responderet. Et certum est quod non superest fructuosa responsio, nisi reddicio rei tam diu injuste detente, et istud cum contricione, potest per dei graciam capitali domino complacere.

[TRUTH.] Further, for a further exposition of the heretical objection which you press,[^1] it is to
be noted that, as the avaricious chief [priests]{g:sacerdos} and Pharisees of the old law killed
Christ's humanity for fear of losing their [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, so the richer chief priests in the time of
[the law of grace]{g:lex-gracie} [have killed] Christ's [divinity]{g:divinitas}, because they have
extinguished the truth of [the gospel law]{g:lex-evangelica} as far as they were able. Therefore, as
it seems to many, since they have received a greater kindness from Christ, they have sinned the more,
as being more ungrateful.[^2] Hence it is written in John 11: "The chief priests and the Pharisees
gathered a council against Jesus and said: What do we, for this man doth many miracles? If we let
him alone so, all will believe in him; and the Romans will come, and take away our place and
nation."[^3] Now the chief priests and Pharisees in the time of the law of grace have bulls and
charters in writing, all of which make for their avarice and strive to extinguish the gospel truth;
and they persecute, albeit fearfully, those who proclaim it.[^4] And so, as it seems to many, our
chief priests and Pharisees sin more, in many ways, because under a greater hypocrisy they are more
ungrateful; and not only do they persecute Christ in his members with a greater multitude, but they
despise the preaching of the gospel truth as the thing most hateful to them. The cause of which
seems to be that they know [from the catholic faith]{g:ex-fide} that they ought to follow Christ in
their conduct; and the gospel, together with their life, teaches that they are exceedingly contrary
to the life and [doctrine]{g:doctrina} of Christ. Therefore they hate that this should be made plain to the
people. And so, as for the glory which you pretend ought now to belong to Christ's priests, it is
clear that you call shame glory, since you urge that, in a more perilous state, [the
clergy]{g:clerus} should forsake the spiritual arms with which it ought to [18] overcome the devil,
the flesh and the world; and besides this you lay to Christ's charge a manifest blasphemy: that he,
as though ignorant of this return, or neglectful of the manner of life of his [church]{g:ecclesia}, passed this
return over.[^5] And as for the naming of "the patrimony of the crucified", it is clear that you
multiply blasphemies against the clergy;[^6] for it is a great sin to transgress the Lord's
commandment in this way, but a greater blasphemy to lay to Christ's charge that he approves and
necessitates [clerics']{g:clericus} sinning so. For so, like a second Adam, the clergy strives to
turn its guilt back upon the Lord: "The woman," he says, "whom thou gavest me to be my companion,
gave me of the tree, and I did eat," as is clear in Genesis 3. But the clergy lies by implication
far more abjectly, since it says that Christ was crucified on the tree so that his clergy might
afterwards [lord it]{g:dominari} in this way. But what greater blasphemy than to lay such a fault on
Christ? And consequently it is the highest blasphemy [to say] that Christ's clergy, by virtue of this
poor and humble passion, ought thus to lord it over the whole world.[^7] And as for the charters and
[grants]{g:concessio} of the secular [lords]{g:dominus}, it is clear that the clergy should blush to rest on so culpable a
foundation. For the emperor and the other lords were in this led astray by the king of pride.[^8]
Therefore let them, lulled asleep in the slumber of this crime, awake, and let them recognise this
principle, clearly demonstrable among catholics: that a human grant is of no force in anything
unless leave has first been had from the [chief lord]{g:capitalis-dominus}. Since, therefore, they
cannot show that they had leave from Christ for this [lordship]{g:dominium}, it is clear that, by
human law as much as by divine, this foolish donation is to be made void.[^9] Therefore they must
either blasphemously deny Christ's chief lordship over Caesar, or grant that the donation of this
lordship is unlawful and consequently to be rejected by catholics, or, third, show Christ's special
leave for it. And by as much as the clergy or the secular [19] lords linger the longer in this
crime, by so much do they harden themselves devilishly in the greatest sin. And as for the papal
laws and lives, it is clear that [this] is a confirmation of the crime, as before. For what are
these [evidences]{g:evidencia} worth unless they have been proved by reasons or by scripture? Now, since in Christ
there is not "It is and It is not", it is clear that it is impossible by either of these to
establish these papal traditions or the laws of men.[^10] And I would wish that the clergy, murmuring
against this expropriation, and even the [pope]{g:papa}, would answer this reasoning effectively.[^11] And it
is certain that no fruitful answer remains except the restoration of a thing so long unjustly
withheld; and this, with contrition, can by God's grace please the chief lord.

## Pollard's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 17.4: As the rich Pharisees killed Christ's manhood for fear of losing their wealth,
- 17.7: so our richer priesthood would kill His godhead by trying to crush gospel truth.
- 17.15: Their bulls and charters aim at this.
- 17.21: So also they hate gospel truth to be preached.
- 17.25: Knowing that their lives contradict it.
- 17.28: As for the 'glory of the clergy' it is really their shame.
- 18.2: And the argument imputes ignorance to Christ.
- 18.5: Even worse is the blasphemy in speaking of the 'patrimony of Christ crucified.'
- 18.9: For this shifts upon Christ the blame of the clergy's sin.
- 18.17: As vain is the plea of concessions from temporal lords.
- 18.22: For no human concession holds good except by leave of the superior lord.
- 18.26: And no such leave can be shown from Christ.
- 18.28: They must either deny Christ's lordship, own the concessions invalid, or show His leave.
- 19.4: The example of popes is vain unless supported by reason or scripture.
- 19.6: As Christ does not contradict Himself such proof is impossible.
- 19.11: The only true answer for the clergy is contrite restitution.

## Notes

[^1]: **R/C.** The chapter continues Truth's reply begun at 15.19. The print gives no speaker's name
    here (plate n48), so "[TRUTH.]" is supplied. *Objectus heretici quem inculcas*: Falsehood's
    objection at 15.6–18 (chapter 8). Truth now takes its points in their order: the clergy's
    "former glory", "the patrimony of the crucified", the grants of the temporal lords, and the
    popes' lives and laws.

[^2]: **C.** The second clause has no verb of its own. *Occiderant* is understood with *divinitatem
    Christi*, and *quia … extinxerunt* says how they kill it. The pluperfects *perceperant* and
    *peccaverant* speak of the present clergy and are rendered as perfects. *Gratitudinem* is the
    kindness shown to them, not their thankfulness; it answers *ingrati*. *Postquam* is taken as
    causal ("since"); temporal "after" is possible.

[^3]: **S/T.** John 11:47–48 in DR, with Wyclif's changes: he omits *ergo* ("therefore") and adds
    *adversus jhm*, "against Jesus", which the Vulgate lacks. At 17.10 B, B¹, C read *peccaverunt*
    for *peccaverant*.

[^4]: **C.** *Promulgantes eam licet trepide persecuntur*: we take *licet trepide* ("albeit
    fearfully") with *persecuntur*, recalling the fear of the priests in John 11. It may instead go
    with *promulgantes*: "they persecute those who proclaim it, though timidly".

[^5]: **C.** *Reditus* is taken as the clergy's "return" to wealth: Falsehood's *clerus ad priorem
    gloriam revertatur* (15.9) and Truth's *redire* (15.21). The medieval sense "revenue" is
    possible: "as though ignorant of this revenue … he gave up this revenue". *Dereliquit*,
    literally "abandoned", is rendered "passed over".

[^6]: **R.** "The patrimony of the crucified": Falsehood's name for the church's estates at 15.10
    (LEDGER D08-1).

[^7]: **S/C/T.** Gen. 3:12 in DR; Wyclif adds *inquit*, "he says". Wyclif plays on the tree: Adam's
    *de ligno*, Christ crucified *in ligno*. *Innuitive*: "by implication". *Dominari* is
    pejorative here, "to lord it". *Virtute hujus passionis* throws back Falsehood's *virtute
    passionis sue* (15.12). At 18.9 B, B¹, C read *deum* for *dominum* ("upon God"); at 18.11 they
    omit *de ligno*; at 18.14–16 they omit *quam … blasfemia*, running the two sentences together:
    "But what greater blasphemy than that Christ's clergy … ought thus to lord it".

[^8]: **S/R.** *Rex superbie*: the devil, from Job 41:25, "he is king over all the children of
    pride". *Imperator*: Constantine, whose endowment of the church is *ista stulta donacio* below
    (18.27) and is laid at his door at p. 72 (LEDGER D04-1).

[^9]: **R/T.** *Dominus capitalis*: the feudal chief lord of whom land is held; Christ is chief lord
    over Caesar (18.28), so no grant to the clergy holds without his leave. Compare the English
    mortmain law, under which land passed to the church only by licence (Statute of Mortmain,
    1279) (LEDGER D09-1). At 18.23 B reads *contraria* for *humana*, with *humana* in the margin. At
    18.27 *irritanda*, "to be made void", has *evitanda* ("to be shunned") written above it by
    another hand in B; B¹ and C read *evitanda*, with *irritanda* in the margin in another hand.

[^10]: **S/C.** *Est et non*: 2 Cor. 1:19, "was not: It is and It is not. But, It is, was in him".
    Christ does not contradict himself, so neither reasons nor scripture (*istorum alterutro*) can
    establish the papal traditions. At 19.5 B¹ reads *nisi est est et non*.

[^11]: **C/T.** *Clerus … remurmurans, etiam papa … responderet*: the verb is singular. We take
    *clerus* and *papa* as joint subjects. Alternatively *clerus … remurmurans* stands absolute:
    "since the clergy murmurs against this expropriation, I would wish that even the pope would
    answer". *Expropriacionem*: the taking of their property from the clergy. B, B¹, C omit
    *contra*; B reads *exprobracionem* ("reproach") with *correpcionem* between the lines; B¹ has
    *exprobracionem* in the margin in another hand.
