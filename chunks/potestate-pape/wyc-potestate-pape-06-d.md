# *De potestate pape*, chapter 6, part 4: the power of order and the gentile name of authority

## ¶1 · 114.30–115.10

> [p. 114] Similiter, suscipiendo potestatem ordinis, quis suscipit adeo potestatem et auctoritatem quia potestatem pascendi ecclesiam, quod est optimum regimen et iurisdiccio summa, quam Christus umquam instituit, cum plus sit differencie [p. 115] ius Dei quam ius cesaris. Nec dependet potestas regiminis a loco vel populo, quia distinguitur ab actu potestatis, data indistincte a Deo ante locum vel populum, ideo cum non variatur correspondenter ad tale obiectum, manifestum est quod quiescit ante tale iurisdicionis obiectum. Sic enim habuerunt apostoli quiescentes in Jerusalem datam a Spiritu Sancto potestatem regiminis, et tamen nondum fuit ad eorum noticiam distinctus populus limitatus.

Likewise, in receiving the [power of order]{g:potestas-ordinis}, one receives power and authority to this degree, because [one receives] the power of feeding the [church]{g:ecclesia}, which is the best rule and the highest [jurisdiction]{g:iurisdiccio} that Christ ever instituted, since God's [right]{g:ius} is of more difference [115] than Caesar's right.[^1] Nor does the power of rule depend on place or people, because it is distinguished from the act of the power, being given by God indistinctly, before place or people; therefore, since it does not vary in correspondence with such an object, it is manifest that it is at rest before such an object of jurisdiction [is given it]. For so the apostles, resting in Jerusalem, had the power of rule given by the Holy Spirit, and yet no distinct people had yet been [assigned]{g:limitare} to their knowledge.[^2]

## ¶2 · 115.11–117.15

> [p. 115] Ad primum videtur michi, ut sepe tetigi, quod omnis clericus debet pure inniti auctoritati divine, dimittendo auctoritatem gentilem adinventam regibus gencium; qui infideliter blasfemaverant quod sunt Deus, ut patet de Nabugodonosor Danielis IV; et hinc sumpserunt sibi nomen auctoritatis et capitalis dominii, Christus autem docet suos apostolos gentilitatem istam aufugere, ut patet Luce XXII et Matth. XX. Unde quia aliquid licencie remansit in ista gentilitate, ideo remansit cum civilitate verbum auctoritatis, per quod ego intelligo superioritatem potestatis civilis. Sed postquam dotata est ecclesia, introducta sunt irreligiose nomen iurisdicionis et auctoritatis cum similibus, que civilitatem sapiunt. Et sic conceditur quod unus episcopus est reliquo maioris potestatis quam quidam vocant auctoritatem. Referendo autem auctoritatem ad Deum et negando auctoritatem sed promulgacionem inesse formaliter creature neganda foret in apostolis auctoritatis maioritas. Secundo vero intelligendo auctoritatem in quantum dicit Dei preconem habere potestatem ad promulgandum eius sentenciam, sic omnes apostoli vel evangeliste [p. 116] sunt paris auctoritatis, cum ipsa non potest maius aut minus suscipere. Ad quem sensum scriptura sacra est paris auctoritatis utrobique. Sed tercio michi infundabiliter loquuntur quidam de auctoritate, ut dicit maioritatem sanctitatis vel carismatum multitudinis, et quia illud est nobis incognitum, transferunt auctoritatem gentiliter ad ordinacionem humanam, ut quotlibet gradus ponunt in papa, archiepiscopo, rectore et simplici sacerdote, cum quotlibet intercisionibus, que quia auctoritate divina bene possunt fieri secundum divisiones graduum, discrecionis et potestatis quam ipse distribuit, ideo sollempnizantur instituciones humane, quamvis ordinacioni divine contrarie, et observantur amplius quam lex Dei. Sed loquatur sic de auctoritate, qui in scriptura sacra sciverit hoc fundare. Leges autem papales de maioritate et obediencia possunt intelligi, quando institucio humana de preposituris hominum correspondet divino iudicio, sic videtur quod proporcionaliter ut quis ponitur in maiori preeminencia quoad homines, sic ponatur realiter in maiori preeminencia virtutum et gracie quoad Deum; quod quia nunquam vel raro contingit, nec superest (ut credo) ad illud fundandum scripture auctoritas, ideo taceo quoad leges et facta, dicens quod illud bene potest fieri de Dei potencia absoluta, sciens quod sic non fuit inter Christi sacerdotes in ecclesia primitiva. Et patet in parte quomodo dicendum est ad primum obiectum. Ad secundum patet ex dictis, quomodo ex sanctitate ut signo remoto debet capi duplex auctoritas, et non ut de equata mensura, quia tunc diminueretur vel extingueretur ut sanctitas [p. 117] promulgantis, quod est falsum, cum infideles ut Balaam, Cayfas et ceteri dampnabiles prophetarunt. Sanctitas tamen est signum auctoritatis divine et firmitatis inaugmentabilis auctoritatis humane, et sic gradus prepositure et auctoritatis civilis nuncupate surrepserunt cum dotacione et temporalium cupiditate non solum in seculares clericos sed in fratres, cum ipsi constituunt tribunos, centuriones, decanos et alios sicut Judei, qui servarunt cerimonias veteris testamenti, ut notatum est Exod. XVIII. Sed servata vera humilitate octuplici, de qua superius, omnia ista superfluerent sicut superfluissent in sanctis apostolis, et sic defectus instruccionis virtutis est causa, quare ista sunt modo utilia.

To the first it seems to me, as I have often touched on, that every [cleric]{g:clericus} ought to rely purely on divine [authority]{g:auctoritas}, leaving aside the gentile authority devised for the kings of the gentiles, who [unbelievingly]{g:infidelis} blasphemed that they are God, as is clear of Nebuchadnezzar in Daniel 4;[^3] and hence they took to themselves the name of authority and of chief [lordship]{g:dominium}. But Christ teaches his apostles to flee that [paganism]{g:gentilitas}, as is clear in Luke 22 and Matthew 20.[^4] Hence, because something of [licence]{g:licencia} remained in that paganism, therefore the word "authority" remained with [civil standing]{g:civilitas}, by which I understand the superiority of civil power. But after the church was endowed, the names of jurisdiction and of authority, with others like them that savour of civil standing, were irreligiously brought in. And so it is granted that one [bishop]{g:episcopus} is of greater power than another, which some call authority. But, referring authority to God, and denying that authority, rather than promulgation, is formally in a creature, greatness of authority would have to be denied in the apostles.[^5] And secondly, understanding authority as it signifies that God's herald has power to promulgate his [teaching]{g:sentencia}, in this way all the apostles or evangelists [116] are of equal authority, since it cannot admit of greater or less. In this sense holy scripture is of equal authority on both sides.[^6] But thirdly some speak of authority, groundlessly in my view, as it signifies [greatness]{g:majoritas} of holiness or a multitude of charisms; and because that is unknown to us, they transfer authority in gentile fashion to human [ordinance]{g:ordinacio}, so that they posit any number of grades in pope, archbishop, rector and simple priest, with any number of subdivisions; and because these can well be made by divine authority according to the divisions of the grades of discernment and of power which he distributes, therefore human institutions are solemnized, although contrary to divine ordinance, and are observed more than [God's law]{g:lex-dei}.[^7] But let him speak so of authority who knows how to ground it in holy scripture. And the papal [laws]{g:ius} on greatness and obedience can be understood [to hold] when the human institution of men's [offices of superior]{g:prepositura} corresponds to the divine judgement; [then] it seems that, in proportion as someone is set in greater preeminence as regards men, so he should really be set in greater preeminence of virtues and of grace as regards God.[^8] And because this never or rarely happens, and there is not, as I believe, any authority of scripture left to ground it, therefore I am silent as to the laws and the deeds, saying that this can well be done by God's [absolute power]{g:potencia-absoluta}, knowing that it was not so among Christ's priests in the primitive church. And so it is clear in part how one must answer the first [objection]{g:objectum}. To the second it is clear from what has been said how a twofold authority must be taken from holiness as from a remote sign, and not as from an equal measure; because then [authority] would be diminished or extinguished as the holiness [117] of the one who promulgates [is], which is false, since unbelievers such as Balaam, Caiaphas and other damnable men prophesied.[^9] Yet holiness is a sign of divine authority and of the firmness, which cannot be increased, of human authority;[^10] and so the grades of the office of superior and of what is called civil authority crept in, with the endowment and the cupidity for [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, not only among secular clerics but among the friars, since they appoint tribunes, centurions, deans and others, like the Jews who kept the ceremonies of the old testament, as is noted in Exodus 18.[^11] But if the true eightfold humility, of which [I spoke] above, were kept, all these things would be superfluous, as they would have been superfluous in the holy apostles; and so the lack of instruction in virtue is the cause why these things are now useful.[^12]

## ¶3 · 117.16–118.5

> [p. 117] Ad tercium patet quod potestas ordinis est potestas regiminis, quam quilibet habet in quantum presbiter; quam solum cognoscunt qui noverunt universalia, et illa distinguitur a potestate ordinis sive regiminis excedente gradum remississimum potestatis. Et sic idem episcopus habet multas potestates ordinis sive regiminis, ut patet superius. Nec prelatus dat curato novam potestatem regiminis nisi sit fidelis preco Dei prius dantis; ymo videtur michi quod sacerdos prius fuit debitor eidem populo, cui per episcopum suum preponitur, cum apostolus Rom. I, 14 confitetur se esse Grecis Latinis et barbaris debitorem, verumtamen ex novo voto et munere recepto est amplius obligatus. Ritus autem gentilis ecclesie in hac parte videtur dare licenciam ad congregandum commodum temporale et inhabili quoad Deum, et talis iurisdicio non frenata per scripturam maxime induceret sectam Antichristi. Si igitur nostri habent potestates episcopales pares [p. 118] cum apostolis, ostendunt se dampnabiles, quod ociando tantum illis potestatibus abutuntur. Si autem potestates terrenas habuerint supra apostolos, hoc false et antichristiane habent a principe mundi huius.

To the third it is clear that the power of order is a [power]{g:potestas} of rule, which each has inasmuch as he is a [presbyter]{g:presbiter}; which only those recognize who have known [universals]{g:universale}; and that power is distinguished from the power of order or of rule that exceeds the most remiss degree of power.[^13] And so the same bishop has many powers of order or of rule, as is clear above. Nor does a [prelate]{g:prelatus} give a [curate]{g:curatus} a new power of rule unless he is a faithful herald of God, who gave [it] before; indeed, it seems to me that the priest was already a debtor to the same people over whom he is set by his bishop, since the apostle, Romans 1:14, confesses himself a debtor to Greeks, Latins and barbarians;[^14] nevertheless, by the new vow and the charge received, he is the more bound. But the [rite]{g:ritus} of the gentile church in this matter seems to give licence for gathering temporal advantage even to one [unqualified]{g:inhabilis} as regards God; and such jurisdiction, not bridled by scripture, would bring in the [sect]{g:secta} of Antichrist above all.[^15] If, therefore, our [bishops] have episcopal powers equal [118] with the apostles, they show themselves damnable, in that by idling they so far abuse those powers. But if they have had earthly powers above the apostles, they have this falsely and antichristianly from "the prince of this world".[^16]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 114.30: 3, In receiving orders a man receives authority and rule,
- 115.11: I reply: 1. The clergy should rely only on divine authority, having nothing to do with civil power.
- 115.28: All authority belongs to God; man's part in it is only promulgation,
- 116.6: We do not know what grace or power God has given to any man, and we try to make up for our ignorance by ordaining degrees in the clergy.
- 116.32: 2. Holiness is a sign but not a measure of authority.
- 117.1: Balaam and others prophesied though wicked.
- 117.16: 3. The power of orders is also a power to rule,
- 117.23: but a curate does not receive it by a bishop's appointment unless he is a faithful herald of God, from whom the gift comes.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** The third objection (A in the margin: "3"), answered at 117.16. *Adeo … quia*, "to this degree … because", is loose; the sense is that receiving orders confers the highest jurisdiction. *Cum plus sit differencie ius Dei quam ius cesaris* does not construe cleanly: the sense is that God's right stands further above Caesar's. A word may be lost at the page turn (114.33–115.1).

[^2]: **S.** Luke 24:49 and Acts 1:4, the apostles staying in Jerusalem until the Spirit came; *limitatus*, "assigned", as a people assigned to a bishop.

[^3]: **S.** Dan. 4 (Vulgate 4:27, "Is not this the great Babylon, which I have built … by the strength of my power"). The boast that the king is God is closer to Judith 3:13 and 6:2 (LEDGER P06d-1).

[^4]: **S.** Luke 22:25–26 ("The kings of the Gentiles lord it over them … But you not so"); Matt. 20:25–26.

[^5]: **C.** The first of three senses of *auctoritas* (the second and third follow at 115.31 and 116.3): if authority belongs to God alone and a creature has only promulgation, no apostle had more authority than another.

[^6]: **C.** *Utrobique*, "on both sides": in both testaments, or in every part of scripture alike.

[^7]: **T.** At 116.3 Loserth's apparatus reads "tercio; adde: videtur", proposing *videtur* after *tercio*; the print does not have it and construes without it. *Ordinacio* here is the arrangement of grades, so "ordinance".

[^8]: **T.** The manuscripts read *correspondent* and *sic videt* (apparatus 116.19–20); Loserth prints *correspondet* and *sic videtur*, which we translate.

[^9]: **S.** Num. 22–24 (Balaam); John 11:51 (Caiaphas "prophesied that Jesus should die for the nation").

[^10]: **C.** *Firmitatis inaugmentabilis auctoritatis humane*: holiness signals the firmness of human authority, a firmness that does not grow with holiness. We take *inaugmentabilis* with *firmitatis*; it could also agree with *auctoritatis*.

[^11]: **S.** Exod. 18:21, the "rulers of thousands, and of hundreds, … and of tens" (Vulgate *tribunos, et centuriones, et quinquagenarios, et decanos*).

[^12]: **S.** The eightfold humility is treated earlier in this treatise (place to be located; LEDGER P06d-2).

[^13]: **G.** *Gradus remississimus*, "the most remiss degree", the lowest degree in the scholastic intension and remission of qualities: the bare priestly power, which higher powers of order exceed.

[^14]: **S.** Rom. 1:14, Vulgate *Graecis ac barbaris, sapientibus et insipientibus debitor sum* (Douay "To the Greeks and to the barbarians, to the wise and to the unwise, I am a debtor"). Wyclif adds "Latins".

[^15]: **C.** *Gentilis* may agree with *ritus* ("the gentile rite of the church") or with *ecclesie* ("the rite of the gentile church"); we follow the word order.

[^16]: **S.** John 12:31, Douay–Rheims.
