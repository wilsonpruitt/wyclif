# *De ecclesia*, chapter 1, part 6: no christian is head; the pope as head of what he rules; three errors about the church

## ¶1 · 17.1–19.9

> [p. 17] Ex istis colligitur, cum solus Christus sit caput tocius ecclesie que non est pars ad aliam iuxta primam conclusionem, oportet concedere quod sicut nullus christianus debet presumere se esse caput universalis ecclesie, sic nec debet sine timore et sine speciali revelacione asserere quod sit caput cuiuscunque particularis ecclesie. Prima pars patet ex conclusione prima; si enim quis sit caput talis universalis ecclesie, tunc est melior angelis effectus, ymmo omni beato spiritu creato, quod soli Christo potest competere secundum apostolum Heb. 1°, 4. Oportet enim ipsum esse primogenitum ex multis fratribus, et per consequens, capitaneum ex dignitate iuris primogeniture. Unde si aliquis christianus foret cum eo caput universalis ecclesie, cum ipsa non potest esse monstrum, habens ex equo duo capita, ut dicit secunda conclusio, oporteret concedere quod est Christus, vel aliter quod Christus foret ipso inferior et humile membrum eius. Ideo apostoli confessi sunt concorditer se esse servos istius capitis et humiles ministros ecclesie sponse sue, nunquam autem presumpsit aliquis apostolorum asserere quod fuit caput vel sponsus dicte ecclesie, quia hoc foret antichristiane adulterari cum regina celi, zelotipando dominum Jesum Christum; quod foret nimis grave, cum Exodi XX°, 5, vere dicitur quod ipse sit fortis zelotes Et secunda pars patet ex hoc quod caput est nomen dignitatis atque officii dignitatis quoad predestinacionem eternam, qua Christus ordinavit prepositos (qui presunt secundum legem suam in ecclesia militante) habere mansiones superiores racione sui regiminis in ecclesia triumphante, quia si prepositi omnino deficiant ab officio ministrandi subditis sensum scripture [p. 18] et motum ad sequendum caput ecclesie, tunc non sunt caput in civitate Dei, sed in civitate diaboli capitis perimendi, quia Sap. VI°, 6, vere dicitur: Durum iudicium hiis qui presunt fiet. Cum ergo nos miseri ignoramus si sumus ad celestem Jerusalem predestinati, (quod tamen ex speciali revelacione habuerunt sancti) patet quod nimia presumpcio foret nobis pertinaciter asserere quod sumus capita cuiuscunque particularis ecclesie que foret pars sancte matris ecclesie, quia notum est ex concordi testimonio sanctorum quod nullus prescitus est vel membrum ipsius ecclesie. Quomodo ergo presumeret aliquis nostrum asserere quod sit caput, cum Eccl. IX°, 1, vere dicitur quod nemo talis scit, utrum sit dignus amore vel odio quoad predestinacionem vel reprobacionem. Insuper si respicimus ad sensum et motum quos in subditos influimus et ex alio latere ad speculum scripture iuxta quam totam vitam nostram regularemus, eligeremus pocius vocari servi et ministri ecclesie quam capita, cum notum sit, si non facimus officium capitatis, non sumus capita, quia secundum Augustinum in libello suo De Decem Cordis maritus perversus non est caput uxoris sue, multo magis prepositus ecclesie qui pure haberet a Deo dignitatem huiusmodi, si a Christo degenerat. Unde postquam Augustinus ostenderat quod femina vere christiana debet dolere de fornicacione viri non propter carnem sed propter caritatem et castitatem debitam viro Christo, dicit consequenter quod Christus loquitur in cordibus feminarum bonarum, ubi vir non audit dicens: Dole de viri tui iniuriis sed noli imitari, quin pocius ipse te imitetur in bono. Nam [p. 19] in eo quod male facit noli eum putare caput tuum sed me Deum tuum. Et probat hoc debere fieri. Si inquit in hoc quod male facit caput est, et consecuturum est corpus caput suum, eunt ambo in precipucium. Ut autem christianus non sequatur malum caput suum, teneat se ad caput ecclesie Christum, huic debens castitatem suam, huic deferens honorem suum, absens sit vir privatus, presens sit vir matri ecclesie coniugatus.

From these things it is gathered, since Christ alone is head of the whole [church]{g:ecclesia}, which
is not a part relative to another (according to the first conclusion), that it must be granted that,
just as no Christian ought to presume that he is head of the universal church, so neither ought he,
without fear and without special revelation, to assert that he is head of any particular church.[^1]
The first part is clear from the first conclusion; for if anyone is head of such a universal church,
then he is "made better than the angels", indeed than every blessed created spirit, which can belong to
Christ alone, according to the apostle, Hebrews 1:4. For he must be "the firstborn" out of many
brethren, and consequently the captain by the dignity of the [right]{g:ius} of primogeniture.[^2] Hence
if some Christian were, with him, head of the universal church, since she cannot be a monster having
two heads on an equal footing, as the second conclusion says, it would have to be granted that he is
Christ, or else that Christ would be inferior to him and a lowly member of his. Therefore the apostles
confessed with one accord that they were servants of that head and lowly ministers of the church, his
[bride]{g:sponsa}; but none of the apostles ever presumed to assert that he was head or
[bridegroom]{g:sponsus} of the said church, for this would be to commit adultery, antichristianly, with
the queen of heaven, provoking the Lord Jesus Christ to jealousy; which would be too grave, since in
Exodus 20:5 it is truly said that he is "mighty, jealous".[^3]

And the second part is clear from the fact that "head" is a name of dignity, and of the office of
dignity, with respect to eternal predestination, by which Christ ordained that
[superiors]{g:prepositi} (who are set over [others] according to his law in the [church
militant]{g:ecclesia-militans}) should have higher mansions, by reason of their rule, in the church
triumphant;[^4] for if superiors wholly fail in the office of ministering to their subjects the sense
of scripture [18] and the motive to follow the head of the church, then they are not head in the city of
God, but in the city of the devil, the head that is to be destroyed; for in Wisdom 6:6 it is truly said:
"A severe judgment shall be for them that bear rule."[^5] Since, therefore, we wretches [are ignorant
of]{g:ignorare} whether we are [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} to the heavenly Jerusalem (which the
saints nevertheless had by special revelation), it is clear that it would be too great a presumption
for us to assert pertinaciously that we are heads of any particular church which would be part of holy
mother church; for it is known from the concordant testimony of the saints that no
[foreknown]{g:prescitus} [person] is even a member of that church.[^6] How then would any of us presume
to assert that he is head, since in Ecclesiastes 9:1 it is truly said that no such man knows whether
he is "worthy of love, or hatred", as to predestination or reprobation?[^7] Moreover, if we look to the
sense and motion which we pour into our subjects, and on the other side to the mirror of scripture, by
which we should rule our whole life, we would choose rather to be called servants and ministers of the
church than heads; since it is known that if we do not do the office of headship we are not heads.[^8]
For according to Augustine, in his little book *On the Ten Strings*, a perverse husband is not the head
of his wife; much more [is this so of] a superior of the church, who would have such a dignity purely
from God, if he degenerates from Christ.[^9] Hence, after Augustine had shown that a truly Christian
woman ought to grieve over the fornication of her husband, not on account of the flesh but on account
of the [charity]{g:caritas} and chastity owed to the husband Christ, he says next that Christ speaks in
the hearts of good women, where the husband does not hear, saying: "Grieve over your husband's wrongs,
but do not imitate [them]; rather, let him imitate you in good. For [19] in that in which he does evil,
do not think him your head, but me, your God." And he proves that this ought to be done: "If," he says,
"he is head in that in which he does evil, and the body is to follow its head, both go over the
precipice."[^10] And so that a Christian should not follow his evil head, let him hold to the head of
the church, Christ, owing to him his chastity, bearing to him his honour; let the private husband be
absent, let the husband wedded to mother church be present.[^11]

## ¶2 · 19.10–19.30

> [p. 19] Patet ergo quod, sicut infinitum maiorem honorem debemus patri spirituali domino Jesu Christo et sponse sue ecclesie que est regina, assistens a dexteris eius (ut dicitur Psal. XLIV°, 10) quam alicui parenti nostro carnali, sic nemo est parens vel proles huius celestis coniugii, nisi faciat debitum quod in predestinacione divina est deputatum isti officio. Nam esse caput vel membrum sancte matris ecclesie est inseparabiliter nomen officii. Ex istis septimo sequitur quod dominus papa, si predestinatus est et exercet pastorale officium, est caput tante militantis ecclesie quantam regit, ut si sic regit capitaliter secundum legem Christi totam iam militantem ecclesiam, tunc est eius particularis capitaneus sub archicapite, domino Jesu Christo. Hoc autem debet supponi in domino Romano pontifice, nisi factum doceat evidenter oppositum, cum clerus iam peregrinans consensit in eum ut capitaneum. Verumptamen non est in potestate alicuius christiani constitucione, eleccione vel acceptacione statuere quod dominus papa sit caput vel membrum sancte matris ecclesie. Nam hoc consistit in predestinacione et gracia Dei nostri.

It is clear, therefore, that, just as we owe infinitely greater honour to our spiritual father, the
Lord Jesus Christ, and to his bride the church, who is queen, standing at his right hand (as is said in
Psalm 44:10), than to any carnal parent of ours, so no one is parent or offspring of this heavenly
marriage unless he renders the due which in divine predestination is assigned to that office.[^12] For
to be head or member of holy mother church is inseparably the name of an office. From these things, in
the seventh place, [it follows]{g:sequitur} that the lord [pope]{g:papa}, if he is predestinate and
exercises the pastoral office, is head of as much of the church militant as he rules; so that, if he
thus rules as head, according to the law of Christ, the whole church now militant, then he is its
particular captain under the arch-head, the Lord Jesus Christ.[^13] And this ought to be supposed in the
lord Roman pontiff unless the fact evidently shows the opposite, since the clergy now [on
pilgrimage]{g:peregrinari} consented to him as captain. Nevertheless it is not in the power of any
Christian, by constitution, election or acceptance, to establish that the lord pope is head or member
of holy mother church. For this consists in the predestination and grace of our God.[^14]

## ¶3 · 19.31–20.17

> [p. 19] In ista vero materia sunt quotlibet subterfugia, ut unus dicit quod nulla mater ecclesia potest esse; sed [p. 20] iste videtur michi infideli deterior, cum nedum negat fidem scripture et fidem ecclesie de credulitate qua oportet christianum credere ecclesiam sanctam catholicam, sed de facto non est filius illius celestis coniugii, sine cuius gignicione non possumus esse filii Dei, et Per consequens, nec heredes regni. Alii autem dicunt quod sancta mater ecclesia est eciam ecclesia, sed non est aliquid, et sic nec mulier nec corpus Christi mysticum nec regina Sed isti; (ut tetigi alias) debent negare magnam partem fidei scripture, decreta ecclesie Romane et simbolum fidei christiane. Tercii vero dicunt quod universalis ecclesia non est nec erit ante diem iudicii, sed multi fuerunt et multi sunt qui erunt tunc partes eius, sed nec habet nec habuit partes aliquas, eo quod nec est nec fuit, cum tunc foret universitas predestinatorum qui erunt in die iudicii. Quod est impossibile, cum multi eorum sint generandi.

But in this matter there are any number of subterfuges: as one says that there can be no mother
church; but [20] this man seems to me worse than an [unbeliever]{g:infidelis}, since he not only denies
the faith of scripture and the faith of the church concerning the [credence]{g:credulitas} by which a
Christian must believe the holy catholic church, but in fact he is not a son of that heavenly
marriage, without whose begetting we cannot be sons of God, and consequently not heirs of the
[kingdom]{g:regnum}.[^15] And others say that holy mother church is also a church, but is not anything,
and so is neither a woman nor the [mystical body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi-misticum} nor a queen. But
these (as I have touched on elsewhere) must deny a great part of the faith of scripture, the decrees of
the Roman church and the creed of the Christian faith.[^16] And a third [party] say that the universal
church is not, nor will be before the day of judgement; but many have been and many are who will then
be its parts; but it neither has nor has had any parts, because it neither is nor was, since [only]
then would it be the totality of the predestinate who will be at the day of judgement. Which is
impossible, since many of them are yet to be begotten.[^17]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 17.1: Sixth conclusion. No Christian should assert that he is head even of a local Church.
- 17.19: None of the apostles called himself head or spouse of the Church.
- 17.26: A Head must be predestinate,
- 18.6: which no one can know himself to be.
- 18.21: Augustine says that a wicked man is not the head of his wife.
- 19.18: Seventh conclusion. The Pope, if he is predestinate and does his pastoral office, is head of so much of the Church, as is under his government.
- 19.31: Some deny that there is a Church;
- 20.6: Others say that there is a Church, but that it is not a thing (i. e. is only a concept);
- 20.11: Others, that the Church will not exist until the day of judgment.

## Notes

[^1]: **C/T.** The sixth conclusion (so the margins of the MSS). *Que non est pars ad aliam*: the
    universal church, which is not part of any other church. *Sine timore*: "fear", not the
    *formido* of assent. B reads *primam questionem* for *conclusionem* here and at 17.7 and after.

[^2]: **S/T.** Heb. 1:4, DR "Being made so much better than the angels". Rom. 8:29, Vg *primogenitus
    in multis fratribus* (DR "amongst"); Wyclif has *ex multis*, and we translate his Latin.
    *Capitaneus*: "captain", kept apart from *caput* (glossary proposal). AA₁ *omnino beato* (17.9); AB
    lack *esse* (17.11).

[^3]: **S/T.** Exod. 20:5, DR "mighty, jealous"; B lacks *vere* and glosses it in the margin. The MSS
    have *antichristine* (17.23). The queen of heaven is the church (Ps. 44:10, ¶2); *zelotipando*,
    "provoking to jealousy", keeps the marriage figure. *Zelotes* stands at line-end without stop
    before *Et* (plate reading).

[^4]: **C/T.** *Nomen dignitatis atque officii dignitatis*: we keep the repeated *dignitatis* ("the
    office of dignity"); it may be a doubling for "of dignity and of office". *Prepositi*: "superiors"
    (GLOSSARY), here everyone set over others in the church, not only heads of houses. *Mansiones*: John
    14:2. A *sue regiminis* (17.30).

[^5]: **C/S.** *Diaboli capitis perimendi*: we take *capitis perimendi* in apposition to the devil,
    "the head to be destroyed"; B *capitatis perimendi* (18.2). Wisd. 6:6, Vg *iudicium durissimum*,
    DR "a most severe judgment"; Wyclif has *durum*, and we translate his Latin.

[^6]: **G/T.** *Prescitus*: those God foreknows will be damned (GLOSSARY). *Vel*: "even". AA₁ lack
    *ecclesie — membrum* (18.9–11), which would leave the sentence without its ground. B *quantum ex
    speciali* (18.6).

[^7]: **S.** Eccles. 9:1, Vg *nescit homo utrum amore an odio dignus sit*; Wyclif's *nemo talis scit*
    paraphrases, and the DR words are used only where the Latin agrees.

[^8]: **T/C.** *Capitatis*, "headship", is not classical; B *capitis* (18.19). *Sensum et motum*: the
    figure of the head, from which sense and motion flow into the body, picking up *sensum scripture
    et motum* at 17.32–18.1.

[^9]: **S.** Augustine, *Sermo* 9 (*De decem chordis*), capp. 3 and 11 (PL 38:77, 83–84; Loserth:
    Opp. V.1, 50, 56, his "9" is §11; LEDGER E01f-1). *Pure*: "purely", that is, only from God and not by election.

[^10]: **S/T.** Augustine, *Sermo* 9.11 (PL 38:83–84; LEDGER E01f-2), abridged and reworded: Augustine has
    *Dole; sed ipsum noli imitari … eunt ambo per praeceps*. *Precipucium* for *precipicium*; B *tum ambo* (19.4). Cf. Matt. 15:14, "both fall into the pit". B's margin: an evil
    prelate is not to be imitated or obeyed in what he does ill.

[^11]: **C/T.** Wyclif turns the counsel to the wife into a rule for any Christian (*christianus …
    suam*), so we write "him … his"; the closing pair keeps the marriage figure: the private husband
    absent, Christ, wedded to mother church, present. B *hinc debens* (19.7).

[^12]: **S/C.** Ps. 44:10, DR "The queen stood on thy right hand"; a reference, not a quotation.
    *Debitum*: "the due", which in the figure of marriage is also the marriage debt (1 Cor. 7:3).

[^13]: **C.** The seventh conclusion (AB margins). *Capitaliter*: "as head". Even ruling the whole
    church militant, the pope would be a "particular" captain, because the church militant is a part
    of the church.

[^14]: **C/T.** *Clerus iam peregrinans*: the clergy of the church on its pilgrimage, the electors.
    The last sentence limits the seventh
    conclusion: election makes a captain, not a head or member.

[^15]: **S/C.** *Infideli deterior*: 1 Tim. 5:8, DR "worse than an infidel". *De credulitate qua*: we
    take *de* as "concerning". A's margin: "against those who deny that the church exists". B *igitur
    oportet* (20.3).

[^16]: **T/C.** *Est eciam ecclesia, sed non est aliquid*: they grant the name "church" and deny that
    it is a thing (side-note 20.6); B *aliquis*, "anyone" (20.8). *Regina Sed isti;* (20.7) is the print's
    pointing. B lacks *autem* (20.6).

[^17]: **C/T.** *Quod est impossibile …* completes the third party's argument (the church cannot
    exist now, since many of its members are not yet born); Wyclif's answer opens the next part. B
    *nunc non est* (20.12).
