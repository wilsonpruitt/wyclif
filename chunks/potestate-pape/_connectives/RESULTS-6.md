None. Every occurrence is rendered, and each rendering fits its class. Each *sed* that brings a minor premiss closed by *igitur* / *ideo* already reads "now": 07-h p. 163 *sed status proprietarius … igitur* and *sed precipuum privilegium … igitur*; 08-b p. 172 *Sed Rome … igitur*; 07-f p. 155 *Sed natura humana … ideo*.

## Doubtful, left as they are

- **07-b ¶2, p. 141**: *Sacerdos autem debet habere claves*, rendered "And the priest". This could be (a), "Now", the step that applies the two gates to the priest's keys. Or it could be (c), extending the image. "And" is defensible.
- **07-f ¶2, p. 154**: *Exponit autem Augustinus … illum numerum*, rendered "And Augustine … expounds". This could be (a), "Now", the step that turns from the method quotation (Sermon 14) to the exposition itself. Or it could be (c). "And" is defensible.
- **07-g ¶1, p. 158**: *Dicitur autem quod nostri prelati … nec dicunt nec faciunt*, rendered "And it is said". This could be (a), the premiss that completes the comparison with the priests of the old law ("they say and do not"; ours neither say nor do). Or it could be (c), a remark added to it.
- **07-g ¶1, p. 158**: *pseudo autem nedum est interpositus … sed persequitur*. *autem* is rendered "but", which is correct (b). There is no "but" for the *sed* of *nedum … sed*. The English "far from being set there …, persecutes" carries the correlative, so it is not an omission. Adding a second "but" would be clumsy.
- **08-a ¶1, p. 165**: *Sed pro nomine pape, notandum*, rendered "But as for the name". This opens a preliminary on the word, so it could be (a), "Now", or a mild (b). It is not a syllogism, so "But" stands.
- **08-b ¶7, p. 173**: *locus autem (ut dictum est) est indifferens ad papatum*, rendered "and the place". This could be (a), the further premiss that defeats the inference from Clement. Or it could be (c), an added reason. "and" is defensible.
