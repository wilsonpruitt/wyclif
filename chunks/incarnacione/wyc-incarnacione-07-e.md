# *De benedicta incarnacione*, chapter 7, part 5: the three confirmations answered, and the true passion

## ¶1 · 121.8–122.24

> [p. 121] Quantum ad primam trium confirmacionum² patet quod similitudo non est sufficiens³; cum humanitas Christi non solum sit ut vestimentum Verbo, vel ignis⁴ ferro,⁴ sed vera Christi quiditas, idem personaliter cum subiecto. Ad 2ᵃᵐ negatur consequencia quod mocio, passio, et similia positiva, cum sint predicata personalia, sunt⁵ persone Verbi racione⁶ forme assumpte⁶ principaliter tribuenda. Non sic autem negaciones, que non sunt predicata personalia. Ideo dixi superius quod incepcio non est esse et prius non fuisse, sed causatur ex illis; cum stat illa esse eterna et incepcionem solum instantaneam, et faciens incepcionem non potest facere dictam negacionem. Ideo non oportet, si Deus temporaliter generatur, quod ante⁷ non fuit; cum satis est quod sit illud ex vi generacionis, quod non effectualiter fuit ante. Ad 3ᵃᵐ patet quod non oportet in toto esse correspondenciam figurati cuiuslibet ad figuram. Unde satis est quod Abraham ducens filium significet Deum Patrem, qui de facto duxit et obtulit super montem Calvarie filium suum unigenitum, sicut Ysaac unigenitus imolatus⁸ est super montem ubi templum constitutum est. Ipse quidem fuit forma risus domini nostri Iesu Christi: et satis est quod humanitas idem personaliter Ysaac nostro, sicut aries est idem genere cum Ysaac figurante,⁹ sic passa et occisa persona Verbi secundum deitatem et animam semper salva; et sic de aliis, que huic loco non pertinet¹⁰ applicare. Sed constat repugnare figure quod sit in toto conformitas; quia tunc evidenter [p. 122] sequeretur¹ Christi mobilitas; cum Ysaac portavit ligna sicut Christus crucem, ligatus est super² struem, sicut Christus affixus est ad crucem. Igitur cum Ysaac noster ex partibus³ quantitativis componitur, que tam⁴ varie movebantur: et, mota parte tocius, movetur et totum, cuius est pars; sequitur quod Christus compositus ex illis partibus movebatur. Nec credo⁵ philosophum dicere Deum esse hominem, nisi consequenter dicat eum componi ex partibus, et consequenter moveri ad motum progressivum parcium⁶; cum⁷ impossibile sit aliquid non moveri, quod tamen subiective habeat in se motum.⁷ Unde Ieronimus exponendo fidem catholicam: ‘passus est, inquit, Dei Filius non putative sed vere.’ Et idem docet Augustinus in Enchiridion 40: et Decretalis in Clementinis De Summa Trinitate et Fide Catholica: ‘confitemur, inquit, Filium Dei, una cum Patre eternaliter subsistentem, partes nostre nature simul unitas, ex quibus ipse verus Deus in se existens fieret⁸ verus homo, (humanum videlicet⁹ corpus et animam intellectivam seu racionalem, ipsum corpus vere et per se et essencialiter informantem,) assumpsisse ex tempore in virginali thalamo ad unitatem sue ypostasis¹⁰ seu persone: et quod in hac assumpta natura ipsum Dei Verbum pro omni operanda salute non solum affigi cruci et in ea mori voluit, sed eciam emisso iam spiritu perforari lancea sustinuit latus suum.’

As to the first of the three confirmations, it is clear that the likeness is not sufficient; since the [humanity]{g:humanitas} of Christ is not only as a garment to [the Word]{g:verbum}, or fire to iron, but the true [quiddity]{g:quiditas} of Christ, the same personally with the [subject]{g:subiectum}. To the second, [the inference]{g:consequencia} is denied, since motion, passion, and like positive [predicates], being personal predicates, are to be attributed to the [person]{g:persona} of the Word principally by reason of the [assumed]{g:assumere} form. But not so negations, which are not personal predicates.[^1] So I said above that beginning is not to be and not to have been before, but is caused from those; since it is consistent that those be eternal and the beginning only instantaneous, and he who makes the beginning cannot make the said negation. So it is not necessary, if God is begotten in time, that he was not before; since it is enough that he be, by force of the generation, that which he was not effectually before. To the third, it is clear that there need not be, in the whole, a correspondence of every [thing figured]{g:figuratum} to the figure. Hence it is enough that Abraham leading his son signifies God the Father, who in fact led and offered upon Mount Calvary his only begotten son, as Isaac, the only begotten, was sacrificed upon the mountain where the temple was set up (Gen 22:2; II Chron 3:1). He indeed was the form of the laughter of our Lord Jesus Christ:[^2] and it is enough that the humanity, the same personally with our Isaac (as the ram is the same in genus with the Isaac who figures him), suffered thus and was slain, the person of the Word, [as to the Godhead]{g:secundum-deitatem} and the soul, remaining ever safe;[^3] and so of the other things, which it does not pertain to this place to apply. But it is evident that it is [incompatible]{g:repugnare} with a figure that there be conformity in the whole; for then evidently [122] the [movability]{g:mobilitas} of Christ would follow; since Isaac carried the wood as Christ the cross, and was bound upon the pile as Christ was fixed to the cross (Gen 22:6, 9). Therefore, since our Isaac is composed of [quantitative parts]{g:pars-quantitativa}, which were moved so variously; and, when a part of a whole is moved, the whole of which it is the part is moved too; [it follows]{g:sequitur} that Christ, composed of those parts, was moved. Nor do I believe that a philosopher says God is man, unless he consequently says that he is composed of parts, and consequently is moved with the progressive motion of the parts; since it is impossible that anything not be moved which yet has motion subjectively in itself. Hence Jerome, expounding the catholic faith: "the Son of God," he says, "suffered, not in seeming but truly."[^4] And Augustine teaches the same in the *Enchiridion*, 40;[^5] and the Decretal in the Clementines, *On the Supreme Trinity and the Catholic Faith*: "we confess," it says, "that the Son of God, subsisting eternally together with the Father, assumed in time in the virginal chamber, to the unity of his [hypostasis]{g:ypostasis} or person, parts of our [nature]{g:natura} united together, from which he, existing in himself true God, would become true man (namely a human body and an intellective or rational soul, truly and [of itself]{g:per-se} and essentially informing the body itself); and that in this assumed nature the very Word of God, for the working of all salvation, not only willed to be fixed to the cross and to die on it, but also, the spirit already sent forth, suffered his side to be pierced with a lance."[^6]

## ¶2 · 122.25–122.26

> [p. 122] Ecce primo univocacio¹¹ Christi cum fratribus propter¹² convenienciam¹² in natura specifica.

Behold, first, the [univocation]{g:univocacio} of Christ with his brothers, because of [agreement]{g:conveniencia} in [the nature of the species]{g:natura-specifica}.[^7]

## ¶3 · 122.27–122.27

> [p. 122] Ecce 2°. vera passio Iesu Christi.

Behold, second, the true passion of Jesus Christ.

## ¶4 · 122.28–122.29

> [p. 122] Et 3°. cum passus sit et mortuus, sequitur quod fuit homo pro illo triduo.

And third, since he suffered and died, it follows that he was man for [the three days]{g:triduum}.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Negatur consequencia quod ...*: the *quod* clause is taken as the ground of the denial, not as the inference denied. The second confirmation (117.17–21) argued from the humanity's not having been to the Word's; Wyclif answers that positive predicates pass from the assumed form to the person, negations do not.
[^2]: **S.** Isaac's name is "laughter": Gen 21:6, "God hath made a laughter for me". The mountain of the sacrifice is the temple mount, Moria (II Chron 3:1).
[^3]: **C.** *Satis est quod humanitas ... sic passa et occisa*: the verb is understood ("suffered ... and was slain"), and *persona Verbi ... semper salva* is taken as an ablative absolute. Read as a nominative, it would make the person of the Word the subject of *passa et occisa*, which Wyclif also holds (117.28–118.4).
[^4]: **S.** The *Libellus fidei* of Pelagius, transmitted under Jerome's name as an exposition of the creed: *Passus est enim Dei Filius non putative, sed vere*, PL 45, 1717; also among Augustine's spurious sermons, PL 39, 2182 (*verified*, LEDGER I07e-1).
[^5]: **S.** Augustine, *Enchiridion* 40 (PL 40, 252), not on the passion itself but on the unity of person that grounds it: *ut idem ipse esset filius Dei qui filius hominis, et filius hominis qui filius Dei* (*verified*, LEDGER I07e-2).
[^6]: **S/T.** Clement V, *Fidei catholicae fundamento* (Council of Vienne), *Clem.* 1.1.1 (LEDGER I07e-3). *Ypostasis* (122.21) is the print's, with O, against A B C *ypostatice*; the decretal reads *ad unitatem suae hypostasis et personae* (Friedberg II, 1133), which supports the print. *Pro omni operanda salute*: Friedberg prints *pro omnium*; Wyclif's *pro omni* is kept as his. He also omits *in his omnibus, in quibus Deus Pater exsistit* and *passibile*, and has *seu persone* for *et personae* (*verified*, LEDGER I07e-3).
[^7]: **T.** *Univocacio*: O reads *unic͡o*, perhaps *unicio*. *Propter convenienciam*: A *propter convenienciam specificam*; O *propter pm convenienciam* (*propriam* or *primam*).
