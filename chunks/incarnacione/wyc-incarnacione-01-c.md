# *De benedicta incarnacione*, chapter 1, part 3: wisdom created and uncreated, and three agreements

## ¶1 · 11.21–12.12

> [p. 11] Et istum sensum⁷ declarat Ecclesiasticus 24 cap. loquens de eadem sapiencia: primo sic: ‘ego ex ore altissimi prodii primogenita ante omnem creaturam,’ et sic⁸ de multis que conveniunt divinitati. Sed post prosequitur quoad eius humanitatem: ‘tunc precepit et dixit mihi creator omnium, et qui creavit me requievit in tabernaculo⁹ meo:’ dixit, inquam, in plenitudine temporis quando misit me incarnandam; iuxta¹⁰ illud Psalmi 31 iuxta exposicionem Apostoli ad Hebre. 10. ‘holocaustum et propeccato non postulasti; tunc dixi, ecce venio’ scilicet incarnandus; et sic deitas creavit me secundum essenciam corpoream ante omnia secula: que quidem essencia transivit honorata¹¹ a patribus veteris testamenti nunc in gentibus et nunc in patribus, ut patet [p. 12] alibi. Sed in plenitudine temporis quando erectum est tabernaculum ex carne assumpta de beata virgine et forma humanitatis copulata ypostatice, tunc requievit tota Trinitas in assumpto corpore iuxta illud Colos. 2.¹ ‘In ipso, inquit Apostolus, habitat omnis plenitudo divinitatis corporaliter.’ Nam tota Trinitas est eadem divina essencia que est Christus existens illud corpus. Unde et illud tabernaculum figuratum est per quelibet tabernacula veteris testamenti. Et ad istum sensum² loquitur apostolorum capitaneus de corpore quod est ipse: ‘iustum, inquit, arbitror quamdiu sum³ in hoc tabernaculo suscitare vos in commonicione, certus quod velox est deposicio tabernaculi mei.’ 2 Petri 1° capitulo.

And Ecclesiasticus, chapter 24, [makes clear]{g:declarare} this sense, speaking of the same wisdom: first thus: "I came out of the mouth of the most High, the firstborn before all [creatures]{g:creatura}", and so with many things that befit the [divinity]{g:divinitas}. But afterwards it goes on with regard to its [humanity]{g:humanitas}: "Then the creator of all things commanded, and said to me: and he that made me, rested in my tabernacle":[^1] he said, I say, in the fullness of time, when he sent me to be incarnate; according to that [text] of Psalm 31, according to the exposition of the Apostle in Hebrews 10: "Burnt offering and sin offering thou didst not require: then said I, Behold I come", that is, to be incarnate.[^2] And so [the Godhead]{g:deitas} created me as to the bodily essence before all ages; which essence indeed passed on, honoured by the fathers of the old testament, now among the gentiles and now among the fathers, as is clear [12] elsewhere.[^3] But in the fullness of time, when the tabernacle was set up from the flesh [assumed]{g:assumere} from the blessed virgin and the form of the humanity joined [hypostatically]{g:ypostatice}, then the whole Trinity rested in the assumed body, according to that [text] of Colossians 2: "In him," says the Apostle, "dwelleth all the fulness of the Godhead corporeally."[^4] For the whole Trinity is the same divine [essence]{g:essencia} which is Christ, existing [as] that body. Hence that tabernacle too was prefigured by every tabernacle of the old testament. And to this sense the [captain]{g:capitaneus} of the apostles speaks of the body that is himself:[^5] "I think it meet," he says, "as long as I am in this tabernacle, to stir you up by putting you in remembrance, being assured that the laying away of this my tabernacle is at hand." 2 Peter, chapter 1.

## ¶2 · 12.13–12.19

> [p. 12] Sic ergo, intelligendo⁴ per sapienciam Verbum Dei quod est tam deitas⁵ quam raciones quotlibet exemplares et per consequens veritates, quae terminant⁶ creatam sapienciam⁶ obiective, ac ultimo ipsa sapiencia incarnata; possunt⁷ dicta in libris Sapienciae⁸ optime verificari ad litteram de eadem sapiencia incarnata:⁷ ut testantur Augustinus et Ieronimus duo⁹ non parvi pugiles scripturarum.

So, therefore, understanding by wisdom the [Word]{g:verbum} of God, who is both the Godhead and any number of [exemplary reasons]{g:raciones-exemplares}, and consequently truths, which terminate created wisdom as its object, and lastly the incarnate wisdom itself: the sayings in the books of Wisdom can very well [be true]{g:verificari}, to the letter, of the same incarnate wisdom,[^6] as Augustine and Jerome testify, two no small champions of the scriptures.[^7]

## ¶3 · 12.20–12.27

> [p. 12] Tercio autem, declinante scola ab antiqua logica¹⁰ de universalibus et a recta metaphisica¹¹ de formis substancialibus, negarunt doctores moderniores concorditer quod homo est anima, vel Christus humanitas, et¹² consequenter quod Christus est creatura, intelligentes autonomatice¹³ Christum solum secundum excellentissimum quod est Christus; et sic licet verum concipiant, multum tamen degenerant a logica scripturarum.

And third, as the school declined from the old logic of [universals]{g:universale} and from the right [metaphysics]{g:metaphisica} of substantial forms, [the more modern doctors]{g:moderniores-doctores} denied with one accord "man is the soul", or "Christ is the humanity", and consequently "Christ is a creature", understanding "Christ" [by antonomasia]{g:autonomatice} only according to the most excellent [thing] that Christ is; and so, although they conceive something true, yet they greatly degenerate from the logic of the scriptures.

## ¶4 · 12.28–13.2

> [p. 12] Ego autem finaliter¹⁴ concordo cum quocunque catholice loquente in illa materia, concedens cum fidelibus primitive ecclesie quod Christus est purissimus homo possibilis: verumtamen non pure¹⁵ vel tantum est homo, cum sit assumpta [p. 13] humanitas; et cum plene sit divinitas, patet quod est aliud sed non alius quam illa humanitas.

But I, finally, agree with whoever speaks in a catholic way on that matter, granting with the faithful of the primitive [church]{g:ecclesia} that Christ is the purest man possible: nevertheless he is not purely or only man, since he is the assumed [13] humanity; and since he is fully the divinity, it is clear that he is something other, but not someone other, than that humanity.[^8]

## ¶5 · 13.3–13.6

> [p. 13] Concordo 2° cum approbatis doctoribus ut Augustino Ieronimo et ceteris concedens quod Christus nedum est creator et essencia que nullo¹ modo creari poterit vel moveri, sed eciam² creatura.

Second, I agree with the [approved doctors]{g:approbati-doctores}, such as Augustine, Jerome and the rest, granting that Christ is not only [creator]{g:creator} and an essence that can in no way be created or moved, but also a creature.[^9]

## ¶6 · 13.7–13.12

> [p. 13] Concordo 3° cum novellis quod Christus secundum deitatem non est creatura, nec minor Patre, corporalis, mobilis, visibilis, subicibilis, et sic de quocunque quae scriptura ascribit sibi humanitus. Et³ ad istum sensum concedo⁴ affirmativas, modifico negativas.

Third, I agree with the [newer]{g:novelli} [doctors] that Christ [as to the Godhead]{g:secundum-deitatem} is not a creature, nor less than the Father, bodily, movable, visible, subjectable, and so with whatever scripture ascribes to him humanly. And in this sense I grant the affirmatives, I qualify the negatives.

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** Ecclus. 24:5 and 24:12, as the Vulgate. The Douay-Rheims "he that made me" renders *qui creavit me*: the verb is the one the argument turns on, "created".
[^2]: **S.** The words are Ps. 39:7–8, not "Psalm 31", and the print has no variant on the number; Heb. 10:5–7 expounds them of Christ's coming. Wyclif quotes the psalm's form (*holocaustum et pro peccato non postulasti*), not Hebrews' (*holocautomata pro peccato non tibi placuerunt*).
[^3]: **C.** *essenciam corpoream*: the bodily essence, the matter of Christ's body, created before all ages and handed down through his forebears, gentile and Jewish. *Honorata*: O reads *onerata* "burdened" (11.32). "As is clear elsewhere": the place is not named (untraced, LEDGER I01c-1); compare *De compositione hominis* 2 (ed. Beer, pp. 26–27), where every man's bodily essence is created at the beginning and every man *fuit in Adam*, as Levi was tithed *in lumbis Abrahe* (Heb. 7:9).
[^4]: **S.** Col. 2:9: Wyclif has *habitat* for the Vulgate's *inhabitat*. The Douay-Rheims "Godhead" here renders *divinitatis*.
[^5]: **C.** *Christus existens illud corpus*, *de corpore quod est ipse*: bare predication, as the argument needs (Christ *is* that body; Peter *is* his body); "as" and the reflexive are the English's only means of keeping it.
[^6]: **T.** The main clause, *possunt … incarnata* (12.16–18), stands only in O; A B C omit it, leaving the sentence without a verb. *Deitas* (12.14): A B C *divinitas*.
[^7]: **T.** *duo non parvi pugiles*: all four manuscripts read *non duo* (12.19); Harris prints *duo*, against them. The English follows the print.
[^8]: **C.** *est aliud sed non alius*: neuter against masculine, the scholastic form: Christ is another *thing* (nature) than the humanity, not another *one* (person).
[^9]: **T.** *Eciam* (13.6) is O and M; A B C read *est*.
