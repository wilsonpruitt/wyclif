# *De potestate pape*, chapter 9, part 8: the dignity of sees, worship in spirit, and the abomination of desolation

## ¶1 · 218.25–221.16

> [p. 218] Et patet ad quem sensum valent leges de eleccionibus et de locorum particularium dignitate, ut XXII distinccione: Sacrosancta dicitur, quod Romana ecclesia non ab apostolis, sed a Domino primatum obtinuit, et post illam primam sedem secunda sedes est apud Alexandriam, a beato Marco et evangelista Petri discipulo consecrata; tercia sedes est apud Anthiochiam, ubi Petrus antequam [p. 219] Romam venerat, sedit episcopus et Ignacium episcopum constituit, ubi primum christianorum nomen exortum est et post synodus decrevit Constantinopolitanam ecclesiam tenere secundum sedem post Romam, propter hoc quod sit quodammodo nova Roma; frivola forent ista, nisi sedes dicta factis compensent. Ideo dicit Gregorius ibidem: Cum culpa non exigit, omnes secundum racionem humilitatis equales sumus. Et confirmacio est quod iste tres sedes a nostris tamquam scismatice sunt abiecte, et ipsi econtra censent nostros scismaticos, et Asyani et alii fideles reputant utrosque deliros maniacos, constituentes in suis ecclesiis sedes alias, asserentes vere, cum non locus sed virtus gentem sanctificat, quecumque ecclesia est maior, que humilius sequitur Ihesum Christum. Et ista est vera sentencia, cum Joh. IV. 20 dicit Veritas mulieri Samaritane opposite Christo: patres nostri in monte hoc adoraverunt, et vos dicitis quia Jerusolimis est locus in quo adorare oportet; venit, inquit, hora et nunc est, quando veri adoratores adorabunt patrem in spiritu et veritate. Cum enim spiritus sit Deus, quemlibet locum replens, patet quod nec oportet in montem altum ascendere, ut Deus melius audiat preces nostras, ut Samaritani ex gestis patriarcharum crediderant, nec oportet in templum Salamonis cum Yudeis ascendere sed adorare in spiritu et montuose aspirando ad celestia secundum memorativam potenciam anime, scilicet intelligenciam, et cum anima sit, ubi afficitur, iuxta illud Apostoli ad Phil. III. 20 nostra [p. 220] conversacio in celis est, patet, quod sic orans mons altus efficitur per Deum sibi ipsi, et ideo Veritas docens nos patrem orare Matth. VI. 9, docet consideracionem nostram incipere ab altitudine adorati: Pater noster, qui es in celis etc. quantum ad templum Yudeorum Jerusalem, oportet adorare in veritate, sic quod vita non discordet mendaciter a peticione et animo adorantis, et tunc fit templum infinitum domino prestancius quam basilica Yudeorum, quia I. Cor. III. 16 dicit Apostolus: templum Domini est, quod estis vos. Quando enim est veritas in conversacione et veritas in peticione, que quidem veritas est adequacio ad intellectum divinum, tunc ex plena conformitate voluntati divine, orans sic mente et spiritu constituitur templum Dei, et tunc sicut dicit digne primam peticionem oracionis dominice Deo patri propter intellectus sublimitatem, sic potest digne dicere duas peticiones sequentes ad secundas personas alias, cum taliter difformem oportet trahi ad regnum, ubi erit perfecte voluntatis increate et create conformitas. Ideo oramus, quod sicut in celo est plena conformitas sic in terra sit proporcionaliter conformitas voluntatum. Et patet ex ista duplici racione orandi, quomodo verificantur decreta distinccione XL: Non est facile cum duobus capitulis sequentibus et capitulo: Multi sunt sacerdotes cum eis similibus. In quibus plane docetur quod locus quicumque sicut et sacramentum vel ritus sensibilis est adoracioni isti et preeminencie persone apud [p. 221] Deum impertinens, nisi forte per accidens excitaverit sanctitatem, unde signum est, quod adoramus samaritane sive yudaice tamquam irracionale animal, reptans depresso capite mentis nostre, quod tantum loca et cerimonias observamus. Christus autem ad hoc ascendit in celum, ut parvipendentes hec temporalia ascendamus per scalam virtutum ab istis sublunaribus in quibus infigimur ad spiritualia bona celestia finaliter affectanda. Unde Veritas correspondenter ad fidem mulieris, qua vere credidit Christum esse prophetam, ipsam alloquitur: Mulier, crede michi, quasi diceret propter imperfeccionem fidei es longe a sponsa, sed cum salus ex Yudeis est, oportet quod in fundamento scale orandi statuens tibi substanciam fidei credas michi.

And it is clear in what sense the laws about elections and about the dignity of particular places have force; as in distinction 22, *Sacrosancta*, it is said that "the Roman [church]{g:ecclesia} obtained the [primacy]{g:primatus} not from the apostles but from the Lord; and after that first see the second see is at Alexandria, consecrated by blessed Mark, evangelist and disciple of Peter; the third see is at Antioch, where Peter, before he came [219] to Rome, sat as bishop and appointed Ignatius bishop, where the name of Christians first arose"; and afterwards a synod decreed that the church of Constantinople should hold the second see after Rome, "because it is in a manner a new Rome".[^1] These things would be frivolous unless the said sees made up for them with deeds. Therefore Gregory says in the same place: "When fault does not require it, we are all equal by the measure of humility."[^2] And the confirmation is that these three sees have been cast off by ours as schismatic, and they in turn reckon ours schismatics; and the Asians and other faithful reckon both to be raving madmen, setting up other sees in their own churches, and asserting truly, since not place but virtue sanctifies a people, that whatever church more humbly follows Jesus Christ is the greater. And this is the true [teaching]{g:sentencia}, since in John 4:20 Truth speaks to the Samaritan woman, who had set against Christ: "Our fathers adored on this mountain: and you say that at Jerusalem is the place where men must adore"; "the hour cometh," he says, "and now is, when the true adorers shall adore the Father in spirit and in truth."[^3] For since God is a spirit, filling every place, it is clear that there is no need to go up a high mountain so that God may hear our prayers better, as the Samaritans had believed from the deeds of the patriarchs, nor to go up into Solomon's temple with the Jews; but to adore in spirit, and as on a mountain, aspiring to heavenly things according to the memorative [power]{g:potencia} of the soul, namely the understanding.[^4] And since the soul is where it has affection, according to that of the Apostle, Philippians 3:20, "our [220] conversation is in heaven", it is clear that one praying thus is made by God a high mountain to himself.[^5] And therefore Truth, teaching us to pray to the Father (Matthew 6:9), teaches our consideration to begin from the height of the one adored: "Our Father who art in heaven" etc. As for the temple of the Jews at Jerusalem, one must adore in truth, so that the life does not disagree, lying, with the petition and mind of the one adoring; and then there comes to be a temple infinitely more excellent for the Lord than the basilica of the Jews, for in 1 Corinthians 3:16 the Apostle says: "the temple of the Lord is, which you are."[^6] For when there is truth in [manner of life]{g:conversacio} and truth in petition (which truth is adequation to the divine intellect), then, from full conformity to the divine will, the one praying thus in mind and spirit is constituted the temple of God. And then, just as he worthily says the first petition of the Lord's prayer to God the Father on account of the sublimity of the intellect, so he can worthily say the two following petitions to the other persons, in the second place, since one so unconformed must be drawn to the kingdom, where there will be perfect conformity of the uncreated and the created will.[^7] Therefore we pray that, as in heaven there is full conformity, so on earth there may be, proportionally, conformity of wills.[^8] And it is clear from this twofold manner of praying how the decrees in distinction 40 are verified: *Non est facile*, with the two chapters following, and the chapter *Multi sunt sacerdotes*, with those like them.[^9] In these it is plainly taught that any place whatever, as also a sacrament or a [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} [rite]{g:ritus}, is [irrelevant]{g:impertinens} to this adoration and to a person's [preeminence]{g:preeminencia} with [221] God, unless perhaps it has stirred up holiness by accident. Hence it is a sign that we adore in the Samaritan or Jewish way, like an irrational animal creeping with the head of our mind bowed down, that we observe only places and ceremonies. But Christ ascended into heaven for this: that, setting little store by these temporal things, we may ascend by the ladder of virtues from these sublunary things, in which we are fixed, to spiritual and heavenly goods, which are finally [to be desired]{g:affectare}. Hence Truth, answering to the faith of the woman, by which she truly believed Christ to be a prophet, addresses her: "Woman, believe me";[^10] as if he said: through the imperfection of your faith you are far from being a [bride]{g:sponsa}; but since "salvation is of the Jews", you must believe me, setting for yourself the substance of faith in the foundation of the ladder of prayer.

## ¶2 · 221.17–222.18

> [p. 221] In istis igitur ymaginibus et fantasticis post dotacionem et affeccionis subversionem delusa est ecclesia in penam peccati et specialiter in constituendo sibi principem tamquam Christum. Et sic cum protestacione predicta intelligo illud Matth. XXIV, 15: Cum igitur videritis abhominacionem desolacionis, que dicta est a Daniele propheta, stantem in loco sancto, qui legit intelligat. Locus autem sanctus videtur michi esse status sanctus, quem papa vendicat, ideo dicitur stantem et Mar. XIII. 14 dicitur stantem, ubi non debet. Revera, quod auctoritate et imperio cesaris vel falsa eleccione cardinalium constituatur summus Christi vicarius, est abhominacio in abstracto, et si fideles anime viderent clare utrobique statum spiritualem ecclesie, quomodo solebat in ea presidere unus paterfamilias, qui graciose acceptaret invitando, pasceret refovendo atque defenderet [p. 222] adiuvando quoscumque suos domesticos. Modo autem invenirent in eadem domo ecclesie presidere unum blasfemum ypocritam, omnino condicionis opposite, non mirum si plurimi desolentur, ut si itinerans solebat hospitari cum uno patrefamilias magnifice largitatis et post inveniat monstruosam beluam, que in hospites famelice, subdole et proditorie tyrannizet, intrans domum: videndo talem sedere in cathedra pii patrisfamilias desolaretur non modicum in aspectu. Sic igitur ego intelligo abhominacionem desolacionis prophetatam et evangelizatam quam Christus precipit parti intellectuali ecclesie intelligere ex effectu. Nec mirum si posteriores docti ab experiencia noscant hunc sensum particularius quam sancti priores quibus non est ex effectu vel prophetice revelatus. Non enim generaliter noticia sensus scripture et sanctitas correspondent.

In these images, then, and [imaginary]{g:fantasticus} things, after the [endowment]{g:dotacio} and the overturning of affection, the church has been deluded in punishment of sin, and especially in setting up for itself a prince as if he were Christ. And so, with the [declaration]{g:protestacio} made above, I understand that of Matthew 24:15: "When therefore you shall see the abomination of desolation, which was spoken of by Daniel the prophet, standing in the holy place: he that readeth let him understand."[^11] Now the holy place seems to me to be the holy [state]{g:status} which the [pope]{g:papa} claims; therefore it says "standing", and in Mark 13:14 it says "standing where it ought not". In truth, that by the authority and command of Caesar, or by a false election of the cardinals, a supreme [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius-christi} should be constituted is an abomination in the abstract. And if faithful souls saw clearly, on both sides, the spiritual state of the church: how there used to preside in it one [householder]{g:paterfamilias}, who would graciously receive by inviting, feed by refreshing, and defend [222] by helping all his domestics whatever.[^12] But now they would find presiding in the same house of the church a blasphemous hypocrite, of altogether the opposite condition: no wonder if very many are made desolate. It is as if a traveller used to lodge with a householder of magnificent largesse, and afterwards, entering the house, found a monstrous beast which tyrannizes over guests hungrily, slyly and treacherously: seeing such a one sit in the chair of the kind householder, he would be made not a little desolate at the sight. So then I understand the abomination of desolation, prophesied and proclaimed in the gospel, which Christ commands the intellectual part of the church to understand from the effect. Nor is it a wonder if later men, taught by experience, know this sense more particularly than the earlier saints, to whom it was not revealed by the effect or prophetically. For knowledge of the sense of scripture and holiness do not in general correspond.

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** Gratian, *Decretum* D. 22 c. 2 (*Sacrosancta*), with c. 3 for the see of Constantinople, as Loserth gives them; the form is ours (LEDGER P09h-1). Loserth's italics end the quotation at *exortum est* and resume at *propter hoc … nova Roma*; we follow them.
[^2]: **S.** *Decretum* D. 22 c. 4, from Gregory, as Loserth gives it (LEDGER P09h-2). *Ibidem* is the same distinction.
[^3]: **T/C.** *Opposite Christo*: so Loserth, marked *(sic)*; the manuscripts read *oppositi*. We take *opposite* as a participle agreeing with *mulieri*, "the woman, who had set against Christ" the words of John 4:20, which are hers, not Christ's; Christ's answer, "the hour cometh …", is John 4:23. At 219.20 B omits *adorabunt*. **S.** Wyclif's *in quo* for the Vulgate's *ubi* (4:20) changes nothing in the English.
[^4]: **C.** *Montuose*, "as on a mountain": the mind's ascent replaces the Samaritans' mountain. *Memorativa potencia* is the soul's faculty (*potencia* as "power", not Aristotle's potency); Wyclif glosses it as *intelligencia*. *Cum spiritus sit Deus* echoes John 4:24 ("God is a spirit").
[^5]: **G.** Inside the Douay quotation of Philippians 3:20, DR's "conversation" (heavenly citizenship) stands for *conversacio*; elsewhere it is "manner of life". **C.** *Ubi afficitur*: the soul is where its affection is set.
[^6]: **S.** Wyclif's *templum Domini est, quod estis vos* is closest to 1 Corinthians 3:17 (Vulgate *templum enim Dei sanctum est, quod estis vos*; DR "the temple of God is holy, which you are"); his reference is 3:16 ("you are the temple of God"). We translate his Latin.
[^7]: **C.** *Ad secundas personas alias*: taken as the other two persons of the Trinity, addressed "in the second place" by the second and third petitions (thy kingdom come, thy will be done), after the first is said to the Father. *Difformem*: one not yet conformed to the divine will; the print is read on the plate.
[^8]: **T.** At 220.23–24 the apparatus reports *sic in terra sit proporcionaliter conformitas* as missing in a manuscript (the siglum is not legible in the scan); the print governs.
[^9]: **S.** *Decretum* D. 40 c. 2 (*Non est facile*), c. 3 (*Nos qui presumus*), c. 4 (*Non loca*), and c. 12 (*Multi sunt sacerdotes*), as Loserth gives them (LEDGER P09h-3).
[^10]: **S.** John 4:21; "salvation is of the Jews" is John 4:22. At 221.11 B reads *a fide* and *creditur*; the print governs.
[^11]: **G.** *Cum protestacione predicta*: the submission formula Wyclif made earlier, that he will retract what is shown contrary to scripture or reason. **S.** The words of Christ (Matthew 24:15) agree with the Vulgate; Daniel 9:27.
[^12]: **C.** The conditional *si fideles anime viderent …* has no main clause; the period breaks off, and *Modo autem invenirent* opens the contrast. We keep the break.
