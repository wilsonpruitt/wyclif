# *De eucharistia*, chapter 5, part 4: Augustine's bread of many grains, and Anselm's species

## ¶1 · 127.15–128.4

> [p. 127] Consolacio itaque contra istos doctores est quod iuxta propria principia non debet credi alicui eorum et specialiter in materia theologica, nisi de quanto se fundaverit (quod non poterit) in hac glossa. Et sic patet quod panis et vinum sunt hoc venerabile sacramentum; verumptamen protestor publice quod, si Robertus Gilbonensis vel quicunque de suis fautoribus doceat contrarium, paratus sum ut fidelis filius ecclesie ad credendum. Sed credo quod Robertus cum omnibus suis complicibus non faciet ita probabiles evidencias ad credendum quod hoc sacramentum non est essencialiter panis aut vinum, sed unum accidens ignotum. Necesse est tamen has hereses esse ad puniendum eos in penam peccati qui phariseice ponderant nimis signa. Exhinc, inquam, occupatur scola circa falsas et vanas ficticias, dimissa doctrina [p. 128] veritatis fidei necessaria ad salutem; et oportet omnino ex hoc consequi contemptum cultus Dei et animum attentum ad cultum dyaboli ad profundacionem infinitam in vanitate signorum seculi.

The consolation, then, against these doctors is that, by their own principles, none of them ought to be believed, especially in theological matter, except so far as he has grounded himself (which he will not be able to do) in this gloss.[^1] And so it is clear that [bread]{g:panis} and [wine]{g:vinum} are this venerable [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}; nevertheless I [declare solemnly]{g:protestari}, in public, that if Robert of Geneva or any of his supporters teaches the contrary, I am ready, as a faithful son of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, to believe [it].[^2] But I believe that Robert with all his accomplices will not make such probable [evidence]{g:evidencia} for believing that this sacrament is not [essentially]{g:essencialiter} bread or wine, but one unknown [accident]{g:accidens}. Yet these heresies must be, to punish, as a penalty of sin, those who in Pharisee fashion weigh signs [exceedingly]{g:nimis}.[^3] From this, I say, the school is occupied about false and vain [fictions]{g:ficticia}, the [doctrine]{g:doctrina} [128] of the truth of faith that is necessary to salvation being set aside; and from this there must wholly follow contempt of the [worship of God]{g:cultus-dei}, and a mind attentive to the worship of the devil, to an infinite sinking in the vanity of the signs of the world.

## ¶2 · 128.5–129.11

> [p. 128] Unde Augustinus libro Sermonum, Sermone LV, promittit narrare populo quid sit hoc sacramentum quod fideles accipiunt: Panis, inquit, iste quem videtis in altari sanctificatus per verbum Dei corpus Christi est. Quod habet calix sanctificatum per verbum Dei sanguis Christi est. Per ista voluit Dominus commendare corpus suum et sanguinem. Si bene accipitis, unus panis, unum corpus multi sumus, sicut exponit Apostolus. Et sic commendatur vobis in isto pane quomodo unitatem diligere debeatis. Numquid enim panis ille de uno grano factus est? ymmo multa erant tritici grana? Sed antequam ad panem venirent, separata erant, per aquam coniuncta sunt et post quandam contricionem. Nisi enim molatur triticum, per aquam conspergatur et per ignem pinsatur, ad formam minime venit que panis vocatur. Sic et vos ante ieiunii humiliacionem exorcismi sacramento quasi molebamini. Accessit baptismum; quasi aqua conspersi estis et igne karitatis constantes, ut ad formam panis veniretis. Nec dubium quin illum panem sic artificiatum quem sanctus doctor dicit esse essencialiter sacramentum mistice manducandum non vocat accidens ignotum sed realiter panem communem verbo Domini consecratum; nec umquam concepit iste vel aliquis ex hoc sanctus quod benediccione et [p. 129] virtute verborum Domini panis consumitur usque ad fundamenta in eo vel corrumpitur secundum se totum sed pocius melioratus remanet figurans nobis secundum proprietates predictas sensum misticum, scilicet allegoricum, anagogicum et moralem. Non enim pinsavit Deus unam pastam monstruosam accidencium per se existencium, ut tam varia in naturis figurent veritatem summe unam, nec epistolatus est aliquis secundum illam fidem in azimis veritatis, sed contra deitatem ac humanitatem Christi in fermento erroris nimis veteri.

Hence Augustine, in the book of Sermons, sermon 55, promises to [relate]{g:narrare} to the people what this sacrament is which the faithful receive: "This bread," he says, "which you see on the altar, sanctified by the word of God, is [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}. What the [chalice]{g:calix} holds, sanctified by the word of God, is the blood of Christ. Through these the Lord willed to commend his body and blood. If you receive well, 'we, being many, are one bread, one body', as the Apostle expounds. And so it is commended to you in this bread how you ought to love unity. For was that bread made of one grain? Rather, were there not many grains of wheat? But before they came to the bread they were separate; they were joined through water, and after a certain crushing. For unless the wheat is ground, sprinkled with water and baked by fire, it by no means comes to the form which is called bread. So you too, before the [humbling]{g:humiliacio} of fasting, were as it were ground by the sacrament of exorcism. Baptism came: you were as it were sprinkled with water, and standing firm by the fire of [charity]{g:caritas}, so that you might come to the form of bread."[^4] Nor is there doubt that that bread so crafted, which the holy doctor says is essentially the sacrament to be eaten [mystically]{g:mistice}, he does not call an unknown accident but [really]{g:realiter} [calls] common bread consecrated by the word of the Lord;[^5] nor did he, or any saint from this [teaching], ever conceive that by the blessing and [129] [power]{g:virtus} of the words of the Lord the bread is consumed down to the foundations in it, or is corrupted according to its whole self; but rather it remains, bettered, figuring to us, according to the aforesaid properties, the mystical sense, namely the allegorical, the anagogical and the moral.[^6] For God did not bake one monstrous paste of accidents existing [of themselves]{g:per-se}, so that things so varied in their [natures]{g:natura} should [figure]{g:figurare} the truth that is supremely one; nor did anyone write epistles according to that faith "in the unleavened bread of truth", but against [the Godhead]{g:deitas} and [the humanity]{g:humanitas} of Christ, in the exceedingly old leaven of error.[^7]

## ¶3 · 129.12–130.2

> [p. 129] Deus enim non posset destruere tantam substanciam partem mundi sine recompensacione facta aliunde, nisi iniuste dampnificet quamlibet partem mundi. Sed constat quod illa accidentalis existencia humanitatis Christi in hostia non equivalet bonificacioni quam creatura haberet ex substancia desinente, tum quia Christus tantum benefacit universitati sue, pausando limitate in celo et essendo virtualiter in creatura quam perficit, tum eciam quia blasphema ficcio falsitatis de Deo qui est veritas effugiens ficcionem, perfeccionem accidentalem abstrahit a creatis. Quomodo ergo consonat Deo bono quod sine demerito dampnificet eciam bonos ex subtraccione perfeccionis mundi, de quo antea congaudebat? Sic ergo potest fidelis colligere quod sacramentum eukaristie sit realiter panis et vinum, vel (ut alii intelligunt) sacramentum est unum aggregatum ex illa substancia et corpore Christi (ut innuit Augustinus De Consecracione, distinccione secunda, Hoc est); quod autem aggregatum [p. 130] ex benediccione Domini vel virtute, sic quod nec panis nec vinum in hoc sacramento destruitur.

For God could not destroy so great a [substance]{g:substancia}, a part of the world, without compensation made from elsewhere, unless he unjustly [damages]{g:dampnificare} every part of the world. Now it is evident that that accidental existence of the humanity of Christ in the [host]{g:hostia} does not equal the [bettering]{g:bonificacio} which the [creature]{g:creatura} would have from the substance that ceases: first, because Christ benefits [his universe]{g:universitas} only by resting, limited, in heaven, and by being [virtually]{g:virtualiter} in the creature which he perfects; and also because the blasphemous [fiction]{g:ficcio} of falsity about God, who is truth that shuns fiction, abstracts accidental perfection from created things.[^8] How then does it accord with a good God that, without demerit, he should damage even the good by withdrawing a perfection of the world, in which he formerly rejoiced? So, then, a faithful man can gather that the sacrament of the eucharist is really bread and wine; or (as others understand) the sacrament is one aggregate of that substance and the body of Christ (as Augustine intimates, *De consecratione*, distinction 2, *Hoc est*); and that aggregate [130] [is] from the blessing or power of the Lord, so that neither the bread nor the wine is destroyed in this sacrament.[^9]

## ¶4 · 130.3–130.21

> [p. 130] Tercio replicatur per provecciores doctores contra predictam sentenciam. Scribit enim Anshelmus in libro suo De Corpore Christi, quomodo sub forma panis et vini datur nobis tota humanitas Christi, sic quod panis fieret corpus Christi et vinum eius anima, cum sanguis generatus ex vino sit secundum philosophos sedes anime, ymmo cum corpus Christi sit inviolabile et incorruptibile; et videtur quandoque hostiam violare et a soricibus comedi et corrodi. Tollendo istam difficultatem sic loquitur secundum diffiniciones sanctorum patrum: Intelligendum est panem super altare positum per illa sollempnia verba in corpus mutari, nec remanere substanciam panis et vini, speciem tamen intelligendum est remanere, scilicet formam, colorem et saporem, et secundum illa fit attricio, soricis corrosio et multiplex localis mocio, ut in ventrem trahicio, non autem secundum corpus Christi. Es istis videtur hunc sanctum sentire nudum accidens per se remanere, ut dicunt moderni.

Thirdly, [it is rejoined]{g:replicatur} by [more advanced doctors]{g:provecciores-doctores} against the aforesaid [teaching]{g:sentencia}.[^10] For Anselm writes in his book *On the Body of Christ* how under the form of bread and wine the whole humanity of Christ is given to us, so that the bread would become the body of Christ and the wine his soul, since the blood generated from wine is, according to the philosophers, the seat of the soul; indeed, [he writes] since the body of Christ is inviolable and incorruptible, and the host is sometimes seen to be violated and to be eaten and gnawed by mice.[^11] [Disposing of]{g:tollere} this difficulty, he speaks thus, according to the [definitions]{g:diffinicio} of the holy fathers: "It is to be understood that the bread placed on the altar is changed into the body through those solemn words, and that the substance of bread and wine does not remain; yet the [species]{g:species} is to be understood to remain, namely the form, colour and savour; and according to these there takes place the wearing away, the gnawing of the mouse and manifold local [motion]{g:mocio}, as the drawing into the belly, but not according to the body of Christ."[^12] From these it seems that this saint [holds]{g:sentire} that a bare accident remains by itself, as [the moderns]{g:moderni} say.

## ¶5 · 130.22–131.13

> [p. 130] Sed pro isto notandum quod Anshelmus et alii sancti laboraverant non ad honorem illius signi sed ad veneracionem corporis Christi signati et sic Christi; et sic nitebantur quod quidditas substancie signantis sopiatur quoad consideracionem hominis, et tota devota intencio coiligatur in Christum, ad quod per se movent signa per se sensibilia et non sic materialis substancia. Sic enim in presencia maioris vel preponderacioris obiecti sopitur appreciacio inferioris, ut patet de elementis inmixtis que multi propter latenciam [p. 131] ponunt non esse; de frigore latente in ferro candente et omnino de litteris quarum essencias et figuram clericus non attendit, cum hoc sit officium idiote, avis vel bestie; sed quantum potest consideracione tali suspensa respicit ad signatum. Sic facit fidelis quoad signum sacramentale et suum signatum; et sic conceduntur ille materiales essencie post consecracionem a quibusdam minus bene intelligentibus simpliciter nusquam esse, decretiste vero dicunt quod panis et vinum remanent, sed nescitur ubi sunt. Sed tercii dicunt quod convertuntur in corpus Christi et sanguinem; quod potest tam bene quam male intelligi.

But on this it is to be noted that Anselm and the other [saints]{g:sancti} laboured not for the honour of that [sign]{g:signum} but for the veneration of the body of Christ signified, and so of Christ; and so they strove that the [quiddity]{g:quiditas} of the signifying substance should be lulled to sleep as to man's consideration, and the whole devout intention be gathered into Christ, to which the signs [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} of themselves move of themselves, and not so the material substance.[^13] For so, in the presence of a greater or more preponderant object, the appreciation of the lower is lulled to sleep: as is clear of the elements mingled [in a compound], which many, on account of their latency, [131] hold not to be [there]; of the cold latent in red-hot iron; and altogether of letters, whose essences and shape the cleric does not attend to, since this is the office of an [unlearned man]{g:idiota}, a bird or a beast; but, as far as he can, with such consideration suspended, he looks to [the thing signified]{g:signatum}.[^14] So does the faithful man as to the sacramental sign and its thing signified; and so it is granted by some who understand less well that those material essences are after the [consecration]{g:consecracio} [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} nowhere; but the [decretists]{g:decretiste} say that the bread and wine remain, but it is not known where they are. But a third [party] say that they are converted into the body and blood of Christ; which can be understood well as well as badly.[^15]

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Iuxta propria principia*: by the doctors' own rule that no one is believed except so far as he grounds what he says; *nisi de quanto se fundaverit … in hac glossa*, unless he gives this gloss (the accident under the word "bread", 05-c ¶5) a ground. C has *principia* in the margin in another hand (127.16).

[^2]: **C/T/G.** *Protestor* (GLOSSARY *protestacio; protestari*): the submission formula, an offer to believe the contrary if it is taught, not defiance. *Gilbonensis* is the print's form of Robert's title (ABC *Gillianensis*, E *Gilbanensis*, 127.21); for *Gebennensis*, "of Geneva"; the English gives the modern "Robert of Geneva" (Clement VII; LEDGER K05d-1). D omits *ut* (127.22); C has *ecclesie* in the margin in another hand (127.23).

[^3]: **S/T.** "These heresies must be" recalls 1 Cor. 11:19 ("For there must be also heresies"). D reads *in probabiles* (127.24); C corrected *ignotum* to *ignoratum* (127.27).

[^4]: **S/T.** Wyclif's "sermon 55" is Augustine, *Sermo* 227 (*In die Paschae* IV, *Ad infantes de sacramentis*), Loserth: Opp. V 973, cf. 1103, (PL 38, 1099–1100: *Panis ille quem videtis in altari, sanctificatus per verbum Dei, corpus est Christi*); Loserth compares Gratian, *De cons.* D. 2 c. 36 (the form is ours), a parallel from another Augustine text (*coepistis moli jejuniis et exorcismis*), not the words quoted (LEDGER K05d-2). The Apostle: 1 Cor. 10:17. Loserth's apparatus says *humiliacione* is right (128.21), with Augustine: "you were ground by the humbling of fasting and the sacrament of exorcism"; the print's *ante … humiliacionem* construes and is translated. A has *communicare* in the margin, BCD *communicare* for *commendare* (128.10–11); ABDE *Si unde*, C corrected *bene* (128.11); C has *accessistis* in the margin (128.22); *constantes* is translated as printed.

[^5]: **C.** *Realiter* stands before *panem*: he really calls it bread, not an accident. *Mistice manducandum*: to be eaten mystically, untagged (no GLOSSARY row for *mistice*).

[^6]: **C/T.** *Aliquis ex hoc sanctus*: "any saint" who holds from this teaching; it could be "any saint since". *Sensus misticus* keeps "sense" (GLOSSARY *sensus*, note). C has *verborum Domini* in the margin in another hand (129.1); D *fundamentum* (129.2); D omits *pocius*, ABDE *melioratur* (129.3); C has *anagogicum* in the margin in another hand (129.5).

[^7]: **C/S/T.** *In azimis veritatis … in fermento … veteri*: 1 Cor. 5:8 ("not with the old leaven … but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth"), where the Vulgate's verb is *epulemur*, "let us feast". The print's *epistolatus est*, "wrote epistles", is translated; *epulatus est*, "feasted", would answer the Pauline text (a doubt, no variant given). C reads *pinsuit* (129.5); B *istam* (129.9); D *humilitatem* for *humanitatem* (129.10).

[^8]: **C/T.** *Tantum benefacit* is taken as "benefits only" by heaven and by virtual presence, so that the accidental presence adds no good to the universe; *tantum* could be "so much". *Bonificacio* "bettering", untagged. C omits *limitate* (129.19).

[^9]: **S/T.** Gratian, *De cons.* D. 2 c. 48 *Hoc est* (Loserth; the form is ours), under Augustine's name (*in libro sententiarum Prosperi*) — the text is in fact Lanfranc's, *De corpore et sanguine Domini* 10 (PL 150, 421A; LEDGER K07h-7): *sacrificium ecclesiae duobus confici, duobus constare … sacramento, et re sacramenti* (PL 187, 1754) (LEDGER K05d-3). *Quod autem aggregatum*: no verb is expressed; "is" is supplied. E reads *quin sine* (129.23), *anteea* (129.25); B *Sicut* (129.25); B omits *vel ut … unum* (129.27–28); ABCE vary at *ex illa … aggregatum* (129.28–30; the apparatus is cut off).

[^10]: **C.** *Tercio replicatur*: the third rejoinder of the chapter, after Scotus and the Lombard; *provecciores doctores*, "more advanced doctors", untagged. *Sentencia* (CONTEXTUAL) "teaching".

[^11]: **S/T/C.** Loserth: Anselm, *Epistolae* IV, 107, *De corpore Christi*, pp. 452–453 (Gerberon); not found in PL 158–159 by phrase (LEDGER K05d-4). That the blood is the seat of the soul "according to the philosophers" is the Lombard's, *Sent.* IV d. 11 c. 6 (PL 192, 863): *vinum ad animam, quia vinum operatur sanguinem, in quo sedes animae a physicis esse dicitur* (LEDGER K05d-5). E reads *figurat*, D *figuret* for *fieret* (130.7), "so that the bread would figure the body"; D *fit* (130.8). The print's *hostiam violare* is construed as passive; E reads *violari et corrodi* (130.11).

[^12]: **T.** *Species* (GLOSSARY), glossed in the passage itself as "form, colour and savour". E reads *et in ventrem* (130.19); ABC *manere* (130.21).

[^13]: **C/T.** *Per se movent signa per se sensibilia*: *per se* twice, the signs perceptible of themselves move of themselves to devotion. C reads *laboraverunt* (130.23).

[^14]: **C/T.** *Elementis inmixtis*: the elements present in a mixed body, which many deny remain in it. *Hoc sit officium idiote*: to look at the shapes of letters, not their sense. ABC read *Similiter*, D *Sic* (130.29); ABC *sit hoc sic*, ABE *officium iante*, C corrected *idiote* (131.3).

[^15]: **C/T.** Three opinions on where the bread goes: nowhere; it remains but no one knows where (the decretists); it is converted, which Wyclif allows a good sense. D reads *et ad* (131.6); B omits *bene* (131.8); B *et decretiste* (131.9).
