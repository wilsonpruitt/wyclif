# sed/autem extract, wave 17: chunks 07-a 07-b 07-c

### 07-a ¶1 · 189.29–190.12  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 189] Ut **autem** a sacramento veritatis clarius fugetur falsitas, restat transcurrendum per alia dubia quibus scola theologie nimis inaniter accusatur, et primo de [p. 190] sentencia ydemptificacionis sacramenti in corpus Christi, qua creditur, quod nudus panis et vinum fient ydemptifice corpus Christi et sanguis. Et recitat illam Magister Sentenciarum IVᵒ, distinccione XIᵃ, capitulo primo; et habet ista sentencia pro se evidencias multas sophisticas et testimonia sanctorum sophistice intellecta. Arguitur ergo primo sic contra istam sentenciam: Panis et vinum non fient aliquid quod non erunt, **sed** non erunt ydemptice corpus Christi et sanguis, ergo non fient sic corpus Christi et sanguis. Si enim erunt idemptice hec partes Christi et omnia que erunt sunt, tunc sunt hec partes Christi.

ENGLISH: And so that falsehood may be put to flight more clearly from the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} of truth, it remains to run through other doubts by which the school of theology is accused of being [exceedingly]{g:nimis} empty;[^1] and first of the [190] [teaching]{g:sentencia}[^2] of the [identification]{g:ydemptificacio} of the sacrament into the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}, by which it is believed that bare [bread]{g:panis} and [wine]{g:vinum} will become [by identification]{g:ydemptifice} the body of Christ and the blood.[^3] And the [Master]{g:magister} of the Sentences [recites]{g:recitare} it in the fourth [book], distinction 11, chapter 1;[^4] and this teaching has for itself many pieces of sophistical [evidence]{g:evidencia} and testimonies of [the saints]{g:sancti} [sophistically]{g:sophistice} understood. It is argued, therefore, first thus against this teaching: bread and wine will not become anything that they will not be; now they will not be [identically]{g:ydemptice} the body of Christ and the blood; therefore they will not so become the body of Christ and the blood.[^5] For if these will be identically parts of Christ, and all things that will be, are, then these are parts of Christ.[^6]

### 07-a ¶2 · 190.13–190.26  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 190] Contra quod arguitur secundo per hoc quod vinum non per accidens **sed** per se est vinum. **Sed** nihil quod per se est aliquid potest esse non illud, ergo nullum vinum potest esse non vinum. Si enim corpus Christi in fine verborum conversionis erit non vinum, tunc oportet quod materia vel forma vini aut aliquid sibi equale deficiat, quia manente omni essenciali requisito ad esse vini non est tunc racio quare non remaneret vinum et per consequens non tunc erit non vinum conversum in aliud. Si tunc deficiet materia, forma vel aliquid sibi essenciale, tunc oportet quod vinum desinet esse et per consequens ipsum vinum non tunc erit aliud quam vinum, quia tunc non erit aliquid.

ENGLISH: Against which it is [argued]{g:arguitur-per} secondly by appeal to this, that wine is wine not [by accident]{g:per-accidens} but [of itself]{g:per-se}.[^7] Now nothing that is something of itself can be not that; therefore no wine can be not-wine. For if the body of Christ at the end of the words of [conversion]{g:conversio} will be not-wine,[^8] then the matter or the form of the wine, or something equal to it, must fail;[^9] because, while everything essential that is required for the [being]{g:esse} of wine remains, there is then no reason why it would not remain wine, and consequently it will not then be not-wine converted into another thing. If then the matter, the form or something essential to it fails, then the wine must cease to be, and consequently the wine itself will not then be anything other than wine, because then it will not be anything.

### 07-a ¶3 · 190.27–191.12  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 190] Item, vel erit ipsa ydemptificacio individualis per motum vel sine motu; si per motum, oportet signare aliquid quod deperdetur vel acquiretur vel utrumque; [p. 191] et signato illo quod acquiretur vel deperdetur, queritur (ut supra) utrum erit essenciale vel accidentale ydemptificando, si essenciale signatum non ydemptificatur **sed** destruetur. Si vero sit accidentale ydemptificando, sequitur quod non obstante illo motu manebit utrumque quidquid prefuit; non ergo ydemptificatur unum alteri, **sed** manebunt due res tantum essencialiter distincte sicut in principio. Si sine motu fiat ydemptificacio, tunc a neutro fiet ablacio alicuius essencialis vel accidentalis pertinens ydemptificacioni, et per consequens remanebunt tante distincta substancialiter et accidentaliter sicut in principio.

ENGLISH: Again, the individual identification itself will be either through motion or without motion. If through motion, one must assign something that will be lost or will be acquired, or both; [191] and, that which will be acquired or lost being assigned, it is asked (as above) whether it will be essential or accidental to the thing being identified.[^10] If essential, the thing assigned is not identified but will be destroyed. But if it is accidental to the thing being identified, [it follows]{g:sequitur} that, notwithstanding that motion, each will remain whatever it was before; therefore the one is not identified with the other, but two things will remain as essentially distinct as in the beginning. If the identification comes about without motion, then from neither will there be a [taking-away]{g:ablacio} of anything essential or accidental pertaining to the identification, and consequently they will remain as distinct, [substantially]{g:substancialiter} and accidentally, as in the beginning.[^11]

### 07-a ¶4 · 191.13–193.10  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 191] Item, posito quod Deus ydemptificet hominem cum asino vel quidlibet significandum cum alio, queratur post casum, utrum homo remanebit homo post ydemptificacionem vel asinus vel utrumque. Si utrumque, remanebunt tantum distincta in principio, si remanebit homo et asinus desinit esse vel econtra, videtur primo quod illud desinens non ydemptificatur **sed** corrumpitur, quod est contra concessum. Videtur secundo quod sequitur: Homo ydemptificatur asino, ergo asinus ydemptificatur homini et econtra; et ita ex casu non sequitur pocius quod remaneat homo quam asinus vel econtra. Non ergo concedendum est unum quin per idem et aliud. Et videtur idem tercio ex hoc quod illud ydemptificatum quod desinit perdit aliquid sibi essenciale ut animam asininam vel animam humanam, et per consequens illa non foret ydemptificacio **sed** destruccio. Et hic querunt logici de subiecto et terminis ydemptificacionis ut distingueretur ab aliis motibus et de eius celeritate ac aliis eius proprietatibus. [p. 192] Item, pono quod Deus ydemptificet quodlibet cuilibet, quod oportet concedere esse possibile, quia aliter staret Dei potencia infinita ad aliqua que non posset ydemptificare, consequens inpossibile, cum posset ydemptificare creaturas valde abiectas sibi ipsi, ut patet in materia de incarnacione tam quoad corpus Christi quam animam; quarum utrumque est verbum Dei et personaliter ipse Deus; quod videtur summe mirabile. Hoc ergo admisso videtur quod quilibet manet in sua specie ac individuacione ut mansit in principio, quia da quod non Petrus remanet ut in principio, contra quidlibet ydemptificatur Petro, ergo Petrus remanet summe multiplex. Et idem est argumentum de quolibet assignando. Si ergo tota universitas remanet in sua specie ac sua individuacione ut in principio, videtur quod utrobique tollitur ydemptificacio; nam ad omnem punctum mundi est summa diversificacio; que cum sit contraria ydemptificacioni, videtur quod tollit ydemptificacionem pro eodem tempore et subiecto. Si dicatur quod summa ydemptificacio est summa diversificacio, tunc (ut supra) debet correspondenter hoc posito variari responsio, quia repugnat quod quidquam remaneat compositum ex partibus vel accidentatum, ymmo notum est si quidlibet est, quidlibet solum Deus est. Item, stante hoc casu videtur quod quelibet pars mundi sit indivisibilis, quia ydemptificata est puncto et per consequens est punctus indivisibilis. Et videtur quod quelibet pars mundi sit in infinitum magna, quia cum quelibet pars mundi sit totus mundus, quelibet pars quantitativa mundi foret ita magna ut totus [p. 193] mundus et per consequens infinitum modica pars mundi foret ita magna ut aliquid. Ex quo cum principiis geometrie sequitur quod omnis pars mundi sit infinita, quia da quod caput tuum non sit nisi pedale, contra nasus tuus est ita magnus ut mundus, ergo caput tuum cuius nasus est pars quantitativa est maius quam pedale, cum omne totum huius est maius sua parte quantitativa capitis, ydemptificatur maxime linee, maxime superficiei et maximo tempori mundi; et tunc est nimis magna.

ENGLISH: Again, supposing that God identifies a man with an ass, or anything that can be signified with another, let it be asked after the case whether the man will remain a man after the identification, or the ass, or both.[^12] If both, they will remain as distinct as in the beginning; if the man will remain and the ass ceases to be, or the other way round, it seems, first, that the thing ceasing is not identified but corrupted, which is against what was granted. It seems, secondly, that this follows: "a man is identified with an ass, therefore the ass is identified with the man", and conversely; and so from the case it does not follow that the man remains rather than the ass, or the other way round. Therefore the one is not to be granted without, by the same token, the other too. And the same seems to follow, thirdly, from this, that the identified thing which ceases loses something essential to it, as the ass's soul or the human soul, and consequently that would be not identification but destruction. And here the logicians ask about the [subject]{g:subiectum} and the terms of identification, so that it might be distinguished from other motions, and about its speed and its other properties.[^13] [192] Again, I posit that God identifies anything with anything, which one must grant to be possible; because otherwise God's infinite [power]{g:potencia} would stand toward some things that it could not identify, a consequent that is impossible, since he could identify very abject [creatures]{g:creatura} with himself,[^14] as is clear in the matter of the incarnation, both as to the body of Christ and as to the soul; each of which is the [Word]{g:verbum} of God and personally God himself;[^15] which seems supremely marvellous. This, then, being admitted, it seems that each thing remains in its species and individuation as it remained in the beginning; for grant that Peter does not remain as in the beginning: against this, anything whatever is identified with Peter; therefore Peter remains supremely manifold.[^16] And the argument is the same of anything one may assign. If, then, [the whole universe]{g:universitas} remains in its species and its individuation as in the beginning, it seems that identification is taken away [on both sides]{g:utrobique}; for at every point of the world there is supreme diversification; and since this is contrary to identification, it seems that it takes away identification for the same time and subject. If it is said that supreme identification is supreme diversification, then (as above), this being posited, the response must vary correspondingly; because it [is incompatible]{g:repugnare} that anything should remain composed of parts or [subject to accidents]{g:accidentatus}; indeed, it is known that if anything whatever is, anything whatever is God alone.[^17] Again, while this case stands, it seems that any part of the world is indivisible, because it is identified with a point and consequently is an indivisible point. And it seems that any part of the world is infinitely great; because, since any part of the world is the whole world, any [quantitative part]{g:pars-quantitativa} of the world would be as great as the whole [193] world, and consequently an infinitely small part of the world would be as great as anything. From which, with the principles of geometry, it follows that every part of the world is infinite; for grant that your head is only a foot long: against this, your nose is as great as the world; therefore your head, of which the nose is a quantitative part, is greater than a foot long, since every whole of this [kind] is greater than its quantitative part. [Any part] of the head is identified with the greatest line, the greatest surface and the greatest time of the world; and then it is exceedingly great.[^18]

### 07-b ¶2 · 194.6–194.14  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 194] **Sed** adversarius dicit quod omnes termini sunt accidentales, cum quilibet potest esse quidlibet, et ita non sequitur quidquid erit Petrus illud nunc est, **sed** asinus erit Petrus, ergo asinus ipse nunc est; ut in simili non sequitur: quidquid tempore nativitatis tue fuisti illud nunc es, **sed** infidelis ydiota tunc fuisti, ergo infidelis ydiota nunc es; et quam accidentale est tibi esse infidelem, tam accidentale est tibi esse hominem.

ENGLISH: But the adversary says that all terms are accidental, since anyone can be anything; and so it does not follow: "whatever will be Peter, that now is; but an ass will be Peter; therefore the ass itself now is",[^3] as, in a like case, it does not follow: "whatever you were at the time of your birth, that you now are; but you were then an [unbelieving]{g:infidelis} [unlettered man]{g:ydiota}; therefore you are now an unbelieving unlettered man";[^4] and as accidental as it is to you to be an unbeliever, so accidental is it to you to be a man.

### 07-b ¶3 · 194.15–194.22  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 194] **Sed** contra istud obicitur primo per hoc quod omne quod Christus umquam habebit partem suam, illud nunc habet partem suam; **sed** omne quod est vel erit panis aut vinum consecrandum, Christus habebit partem suam, cum ydemptificatur parti sue, ergo omne quod est vel erit, panis aut vinum consecrandum, Christus iam habet partem suam; ista quidem forma valet in terminis accidentalibus.

ENGLISH: But against this [it is objected]{g:obicitur-per}, first, by appeal to this: that everything which Christ will ever have as his part, that he now has as his part; now everything that is or will be bread or wine to be consecrated, Christ will have as his part, since it is identified with his part; therefore everything that is or will be bread or wine to be consecrated, Christ already has as his part; and this form indeed holds in accidental terms.[^5]

### 07-b ¶6 · 195.9–195.25  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 195] Similiter iuxta hanc viam periret omnis motus vel accio naturalis, quia tam fundamentum quam materia motus deficeret; si enim scirem quod homo ambulat, scirem quod homini accidit ambulacio. **Sed** contra pro toto tempore ambulandi ex tibi dubio hoc fit non homo **sed** lapis vel aliud ydemptificatum membris Christi; non ergo scitur quod homo ambulat, cum tollitur omnis sciencia de rerum quidditate, distinccione vel transmutacione. Si enim materia motus foret continue ydemptificata cuilibet, quomodo specificaretur, distingueretur vel attenderetur motus? **Sed** totum hoc ignoras et dubitas, cum ydemptificacio sit habitudo reciproca que potest fieri insensibiliter ignorante homine subducto quocunque motu. Nichil itaque stulcius aut irracionabilius ista sentencia, cum ydemptificatur opinioni stultissime summe heretice et maxime detestande.

ENGLISH: Likewise, on this way every natural motion or action would perish, because both the foundation and the matter of motion would fail; for if I knew that a man walks, I would know that walking befalls a man. Now, on the contrary, for the whole time of walking, [for all you know]{g:ex-tibi-dubio}, this becomes not a man but a stone or something else identified with the [members]{g:membrum} of Christ;[^11] therefore it is not known that a man walks, since all knowledge of the [quiddity]{g:quiditas}, distinction or transmutation of things is taken away. For if the matter of motion were continually identified with anything whatever, how would motion be specified, distinguished or observed? But all this you [are ignorant of]{g:ignorare} and doubt, since identification is a reciprocal [respect]{g:habitudo}[^12] which can come about [imperceptibly]{g:insensibiliter}, without a man's knowing, with any motion whatever withdrawn. Nothing, therefore, is more foolish or more irrational than this teaching, since it is identified with the most foolish [opinion]{g:opinio}, supremely heretical and most to be detested.[^13]

### 07-b ¶8 · 195.30–196.3  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 195] **Sed** pro ista via arguitur primo per hoc quod quidquid producit individuum speciei, ydemptificat [p. 196] illud nedum illi speciei **sed** cuilibet eius individuo, cum sit triplex ydemptitas, scilicet generalis, specialis et numeralis, est ergo ydemptificacio possibilis.

ENGLISH: But for this way [it is argued]{g:arguitur-per}, first, by appeal to this: that whatever produces an [individual]{g:individuum} of a species identifies [196] it [not only]{g:nedum} with that species but with every individual of it, since identity is threefold, namely of genus, of species and numerical; identification is therefore possible.[^14]

### 07-b ¶9 · 196.4–196.22  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 196] **Sed** pro isto non est difficultas quin talis ydemptificacio sit necessaria, **sed** difficultas loquitur de ydemptificacione numerali duorum individuorum que prius essencialiter et numeraliter sunt distincta, ut panis consecrandus et corpus Christi in celo sunt duo individua loco et forma multiplici distincta et virtute verborum conversionis ponitur eandem rem que prius fuit panis foret ydemptice corpus Christi; **sed** corpus Christi negatur fore ille panis: et hoc videtur fore difficile, cum ponitur formam substancialem panis et materiam primam ac quamlibet partem panis pro instanti conversionis desinere esse secundum se totam. Et ista foret mirabilis ydemptificacio in qua unum extremum secundum se totum destruitur et per idem non foret transitus, conversio vel transsubstanciacio panis in corpus Christi, nec corpus Christi fieret ex illo, sicut patet admittenti significaciones signorum antiquas. Et non valet baptisare terminos sic extranee et infundabiliter ad fingendum mendacium.

ENGLISH: But as for this, there is no difficulty but that such identification is necessary, but the difficulty speaks of the numerical identification of two individuals which before are essentially and numerically distinct, as the bread to be consecrated and the body of Christ in heaven are two individuals, distinct in place and in manifold form, and it is posited that by virtue of the words of conversion the same thing which before was bread would be [identically]{g:ydemptice} the body of Christ; but the body of Christ is denied to be going to be that bread.[^15] And this seems to be difficult, since it is posited that the [substantial form]{g:forma-substancialis} of the bread and the [prime matter]{g:materia-prima} and every part of the bread cease to be in its entirety at the instant of conversion. And this would be a marvellous identification, in which one extreme is destroyed in its entirety; and by the same token there would be no passing, conversion or [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio} of the bread into the body of Christ, nor would the body of Christ come to be from it, as is clear to one who admits the ancient [significations]{g:significacio} of [signs]{g:signum}. And it is no use to baptize terms so strangely and [without foundation]{g:infundabiliter} in order to feign a lie.[^16]

### 07-b ¶12 · 197.10–197.22  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 197] Item, cum illud sacramentum sit visibile vel est albedo vel album, **sed** non est album; ergo est albedo per se visibilis, non quantitas ut magnitudo vel superficies, vel non est hoc solum, quia potest manere idem album quantitate variata, ut patet de maioracione vel minoracione sacramenti in calice, ymmo sacramentum sub forma panis, sicut potest variari in albedine, sic potest per exspiracionem variari in quantitate. Sicut ergo quantitas per se existens est quanta, sic albedo per se existens est alba, quia aliter sensus nimis deciperetur in sensacione, cum prius videt album quam albedinem in abstracto, quia hoc est virtutis abstractive discernere.

ENGLISH: Again, since that sacrament is visible, it is either whiteness or white; now it is not white; therefore it is whiteness, visible of itself, not quantity, as magnitude or surface, or it is not this alone, because the same white can remain with the quantity varied, as is clear from the increasing or lessening of the sacrament in the chalice; indeed the sacrament under the form of bread, as it can be varied in whiteness, so it can be varied in quantity by evaporation.[^20] As, then, quantity existing of itself is [quantified]{g:quantus}, so whiteness existing of itself is white; because otherwise sense would be [exceedingly]{g:nimis} deceived in [sensation]{g:sensacio}, since it sees the white before whiteness in the abstract, because to discern this belongs to the abstractive [power]{g:virtus}.[^21]

### 07-c ¶1 · 197.23–198.13  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 197] Item, nulla alia qualitas coexistens informatur albedine, quia hoc foret potissime qualitas prima, ex qua resultat albedo. **Sed** quis dicit quod qualitates elementares sunt sic colorate? Tunc enim quilibet sensus perciperet qualitates tangibiles et homo videret potissime gravitatem, cum illa vel sit forma substancialis (ut placet Averroi) vel forma propinquissime principians colorem; ymmo cum color secundum philosophos non sit nisi lux incorporata dyaphano, videtur quod ad variacionem lucis varietur et color. Cum ergo coexistencia qualitatum non sufficit ut una [p. 198] informet reliquam, necesse habet hec via dicere, quomodo causatur informacio qualitatum. Non enim est color concludere quod, si qualitas resultat ex elemento, ex materia et forma, quod propterea qualificat suum principium, quia sic omnis qualitas qualificaret Deum. Videtur ergo cum hoc sacramentum non sit album quod sit albedo et per idem qualiscunque, qualitas aut quantitas, et nullomodo aggregatum ex omnibus accidentibus in hostia consecrata, cum illud non potest informari albedine, tum quia continue variatur, tum eciam quia multa sunt ibi accidencia distincta in genere que non possunt albari; **sed** pocius albedo denominatur ab illis.

ENGLISH: Again, no other coexisting [quality]{g:qualitas} is [informed]{g:informare} by whiteness, because this would be above all the [first quality]{g:qualitas-prima}, from which whiteness results.[^1] But who says that the elemental qualities are thus coloured? For then every sense would perceive tangible qualities, and a man would see heaviness above all, since that is either a [substantial form]{g:forma-substancialis} (as Averroes holds) or the form that most proximately [is the principle of]{g:principiare} colour;[^2] indeed, since colour, according to the philosophers, is nothing but light incorporated in the diaphanous, it seems that colour too varies with the variation of light.[^3] Since, then, the coexistence of qualities does not suffice for one [198] to inform the other, this way must say how the [informing]{g:informacio} of qualities is caused. For it is no [colour]{g:color}[^4] to [infer]{g:concludere} that, if a quality results from an element, from matter and form, it therefore qualifies its principle; because so every quality would qualify God. It seems, then, since this [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} is not white, that it is whiteness, and by the same token any quality or [quantity]{g:quantitas} whatever, and in no way the aggregate of all the [accidents]{g:accidens} in the [consecrated host]{g:hostia-consecrata}; since that cannot be informed by whiteness, both because it continually varies and also because there are many accidents there distinct in genus which cannot be whitened; but rather whiteness is [named]{g:denominare} from them.

### 07-c ¶2 · 198.14–199.5  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 198] Sicut ergo glossa ordinaria decretorum (ut supra exposui) dicit quod hoc sacramentum non est ponderosum **sed** ipsa ponderositas vel sibi subiectum, sic debet dicere de albedine, duricie, sapore et eis similibus; et per consequens sunt in eadem hostia infinita sacramenta distincta in genere. Non ergo foret unitas in hostia vel denominacio accidentalis; quod manifeste repugnat sensui; videmus enim quod hostia est continua, frangitur et nutrit, odorat et sapit, sonat et descendit. Numquid credimus illud quod sic demonstratur esse nichil vel unum aggregatum ex sono, odore, sapore, descensu et alia accione? Talia enim continue variantur et accidunt sacramento, sicut conficere in fermento vel azimo. Numquid credimus quod accidens illud sit fermentatum vel azimum, extensum vel fractum et cum hoc multiplicatum et non huius, quia secundum alias partes quarum quelibet est sacramentum invisibile; alias multiplicavi argumenta que [p. 199] darent fidem fidelibus quod nulla qualitas poterit per se esse et multo minus alia genera accidentis. Non enim est figura, pulcritudo, febris vel potencia anime res talis quam debemus orare, pro se colere vel timere.

ENGLISH: As, then, the ordinary gloss on the [decrees]{g:decretum} (as I set out above) says that this sacrament is not [heavy]{g:ponderosum} but [heaviness]{g:ponderositas} itself, or what is subject to it,[^5] so it must say of whiteness, hardness, taste and the like; and consequently there are in the same [host]{g:hostia} infinite sacraments distinct in genus. There would therefore be no unity in the host, nor accidental [naming]{g:denominacio}; which is manifestly [incompatible]{g:repugnare} with sense; for we see that the host is continuous, is broken and nourishes, smells and tastes, sounds and descends. Do we believe that that which is thus pointed out is nothing, or one aggregate of sound, smell, taste, descent and other action? For such things continually vary and befall the sacrament, as does [consecrating]{g:conficere} in leavened or unleavened [bread].[^6] Do we believe that that accident is leavened or unleavened, extended or broken, and with this multiplied, and not such, because according to other parts, each of which is an invisible sacrament?[^7] Elsewhere I have multiplied arguments that [199] would give the faithful faith that no quality can be [of itself]{g:per-se}, and much less the other genera of accident. For figure, beauty, fever or a [power]{g:potencia} of the soul is not such a thing as we ought to pray to, worship for itself or fear.[^8]

### 07-c ¶4 · 199.19–199.33  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 199] Sacramentum **autem** altaris potest vocari, ut sepe dixi, corpus sic rite benedictum a presbitero, quelibet eciam eius pars; et quodlibet eius sensibile accidens potest vocari secundarie vel per accidens sacramentum, sicut et corpus aggregatum ex corpore consecrato et alio sibi commixto; ymmo quandoque corpus permanens vocatur sacramentum et quandoque corpus successivum. **Sed** inpossibile est sacrum accidens, potissime quantitatem et qualitatem, esse per se sacramentum, cum secundum Augustinum non poterit per se esse. Et patet quod nullum istorum sacramentorum est ydemptice Deus vel pars eius, **sed** signum efficax quod ad sensum equivocum et tropicum suscipit predicacionem partis Christi in modis loquendi sanctorum et Romane ecclesie.

ENGLISH: And the [sacrament of the altar]{g:sacramentum-altaris} can be called, as I have often said, the body thus duly blessed by the [presbyter]{g:presbiter}, and also any part of it;[^10] and any [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} accident of it can be called a sacrament secondarily or [by accident]{g:per-accidens}, as also the body aggregated from the consecrated body and another mixed with it; indeed, sometimes a [permanent]{g:permanens} body is called the sacrament, and sometimes a [successive]{g:successivum} body. But it is impossible that a sacred accident, above all quantity and quality, should be of itself a sacrament, since according to Augustine it cannot be of itself.[^11] And it is clear that none of these sacraments is [identically]{g:ydemptice} God or a part of him, but an efficacious [sign]{g:signum} which, in an equivocal and tropical [meaning]{g:sensus}, receives the [predication]{g:predicacio} of a part of Christ in the manners of speaking of [the saints]{g:sancti} and of the Roman [church]{g:ecclesia}.[^12]
