# *Dialogus*, chapter 2: the rules of the three parts

## Truth · 3.15–4.2

> [p. 3] VERITAS. Correspondenter ad vitas et officia istarum trium parcium ordinavi illis regulas vivendi, ne in suis officiis confundantur. Clerum autem, qui debet esse supremus et celo propinquissimus, ordinavi a causis mundialibus et mundi sollicitudinibus maxime sequestratum, et sic non dominaret seculariter sed deberet esse pauper, similis statui innocencie instar mei. Et sic debuit docere duas partes alias mundum deserere tam opere quam sermone. Et ista vita similis aquiline est ad similitudinem vite angelice contemplativa [p. 4] perfeccior quam activa. Et qui est indispositus hanc vitam vivere est indispositus clericari.

TRUTH. Corresponding to the lives and offices of these three parts, I ordained rules of living for them, lest they be confounded in their offices. And [the clergy]{g:clerus}, which ought to be highest and nearest to heaven, I ordained to be set apart in the highest degree from worldly causes and the cares of the world; and so it should not [exercise lordship]{g:dominari} in secular fashion, but ought to be poor, like the state of innocence, after my likeness. And so it was bound to teach the two other parts to forsake the world, by deed as well as by word. And this life, like the eagle's, is contemplative after the likeness of the angelic life, [4] more perfect than the active.[^1] And he who is [unfit]{g:indispositus} to live this life is unfit to be made a cleric.

## Truth · 4.3–4.7

> [p. 4] Secunda autem pars ecclesie debet dominari seculariter et esse in temporalibus opulenta, quia hoc est instrumentum necessarium suo officio quod isti parti ecclesie limitavi. Sic enim deitas cohercet homines dando illis rerum affluenciam, et immiscet quodammodo potentiam coactivam.

And the second part of the [church]{g:ecclesia} ought to exercise lordship in secular fashion and be wealthy in [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, because this is an instrument necessary to its office, which I [assigned]{g:limitare} to this part of the church. For thus the [Godhead]{g:deitas} [coerces]{g:cohercere} men by giving them [the lords] abundance of goods, and in some manner mingles in [coactive power]{g:coactivus}.[^2]

## Truth · 4.8–5.11

> [p. 4] Terciam autem partem et infimam volo in labore corporali vivere et circa lucrum temporalium se cum moderamine ocupare; et sic omnes has tres partes ecclesie volo, tam in spirituali quam in temporali quantum racio exigit, ad suum officium habundare. Et sicut in trinitate increata est summa concordia, sic volo in hiis tribus partibus ecclesie esse amoris concordiam, controversia expulsa secundum virtutis regulas, cum ecclesia sit corpus liberi arbitrii quod per Christi capitis influencias reguletur. Disponat ergo se corpus ecclesie, et specialiter suprema pars que secundum augustinum debet esse montes, ut influencie hujus capitis coaptetur, et tunc bene regulabitur secundum graciam jhu Christi. Sicut enim pars bassior in qua vigent terre nascencia recipit influenciam suam a solari lumine, sic pars inferior terrene ecclesie recipit influenciam suam derivatam per clerum a sole justicie. Et nichil sensibilius conturbat ecclesiam vel inducit in eam morbidam discrasiam quam quod una ejus pars accipiat ministeria alterius, et correspondenter officia et per consequens dimittat officia sibimet limitata. Nec est possibile Christum deficere officium et statum parti ecclesie limitare, nisi illi [p. 5] parti deficiat influxa gracia ex peccato proprio, que se ad recipiendum dictam graciam indisponit. Nec omnia membra istarum parcium eque perficiunt officium quod statui suo attinet, sed unum magis et aliud minus, secundum quod radius gracie cum ipso homine operatur. Alia autem membra diaboli que seminantur in ecclesia non sunt proprie partes ejus, licet habeant carecterem sensibilem et nomen quoad famas hominum pupplicatum, quia verum officium quod deus requirit de persona status ecclesie est signum evidencius quam signum humanitus introductum. Ideo dicit Christus jo 10 operibus credite.

And the third and lowest part I will to live by bodily labour and to busy itself with moderation about the gain of temporal goods; and so I will all these three parts of the church to abound, in the spiritual as well as in the temporal, as far as reason requires, for their office. And as in the uncreated Trinity there is the highest concord, so I will there to be in these three parts of the church a concord of love, controversy being driven out according to the rules of virtue, since the church is a body of [free choice]{g:liberum-arbitrium} which is to be regulated by the influences of Christ the head. Let the body of the church therefore dispose itself, and especially the highest part, which according to Augustine ought to be the mountains, so that it may be fitted to the influence of this head; and then it will be well regulated according to the grace of Jesus Christ.[^3] For as the lower part, in which the things born of the earth flourish, receives its influence from the light of the sun, so the lower part of the earthly church receives its influence, derived through the clergy, from the sun of justice. And nothing more [perceptibly]{g:sensibilis} troubles the church or brings into it a morbid distemper than that one part of it should take up the ministries of another, and correspondingly its offices, and in consequence abandon the offices assigned to itself. Nor is it possible for Christ to fail to assign office and state to a part of the church, unless through its own sin the inflowing grace fail that [5] part, which makes itself unfit to receive the said grace.[^4] Nor do all the members of these parts equally perform the office that belongs to their state, but one more and another less, according as the ray of grace works with the man himself. But other members, the devil's, which are sown in the church, are not properly parts of it, although they have a [perceptible character]{g:carecter} and a name published as far as men's reports go, because the true office which God requires of a person in a state of the church is a more evident sign than a sign introduced by men.[^5] Therefore Christ says in John 10[:38], "Believe the works."

## Pollard's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 3.15: Each order has its own rule of life.
- 3.17: The clergy by their life of poverty and contemplation must teach the other orders to forsake
  the world.
- 4.1: Else they are no true clergy.
- 4.3: The temporal lords must be rich and powerful.
- 4.8: The people must work for their living.
- 4.13: The three orders must live in the harmony of love.
- 4.15: For the Church is a body of free judgment which should so dispose itself as to be open to the
  influence of its head.
- 4.20: The goodness of the other orders depends on that of the clergy.
- 4.23: Nothing more disturbs the Church than for one order to do the work of another and leave its
  own.
- 5.2: Not all members of these orders have the same fitness for their work.
- 5.5: But members of the devil living in the Church are not members of the Church.
- 5.10: These may be known by their works.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Similis aquiline*: taken as "like [the life] of the eagle", *aquiline* agreeing with
    an understood *vite* (dative after *similis*). *Contemplativa perfeccior quam activa* is taken as
    "contemplative, more perfect than the active [life]". It could also be read "more perfectly
    contemplative than active", i.e. more contemplative than active. B inverts *est aquiline*.

[^2]: **C.** *Dando illis*: *illis* is taken as the members of the second part, the lords, to whom
    God gives wealth as the instrument of coercion (*instrumentum*, 4.5). It could also be *homines*:
    "coerces men by giving them abundance of goods". The Godhead here recalls the king as vicar of
    the Godhead (ch. 1, 2.14–16).

[^3]: **S/C.** *Influencias*, *influencie*: the inflowing of the head into its body, and of a
    heavenly body into the earth, as the sun in the next sentence shows. Augustine reads the
    *montes* of the psalms as the great ones of the church, its preachers and apostles, e.g. on
    Ps. 71:3 and 120:1 (Vulgate numbering) (LEDGER D02-1). The "sun of justice" (4.22) is Mal. 4:2.

[^4]: **T/C.** 4.24–27. For *ministeria* ("ministries") B, B¹, C read *instrumenta* ("instruments"),
    echoing *instrumentum* at 4.5. For *correspondenter* they read *consequenter*, and they omit
    *et officia*. In the next sentence *deficere* governs *limitare*: "to fail to assign". At that
    line B reads *unum*, with *Christum* by another hand; B's *limitare* stands against *militantes*
    (B¹ *militanti*); B, B¹, C omit *parti*. The print is translated. *Que* agrees with *parti*.

[^5]: **C.** *Carecterem sensibilem*: the perceptible mark of orders (B, B¹, C *caracterum*).
    *Nomen … pupplicatum*: "a name published", i.e. a public repute; *pupplicatum* agrees with
    *nomen*. *De persona status ecclesie*: "of a person in a state of the church", any of the three
    orders; it could also be "of the person [holding] the church's state".
