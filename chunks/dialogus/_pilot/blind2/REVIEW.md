# Blind review 2: chunks 02, 14, 30 (A vs B)

Method: each pair read sentence by sentence against the frozen Latin, with the checklist from
`CARD.md` (four error classes, connectives table) and `GLOSSARY.md`. Apparatus claims in the notes
were checked on the plates (`text/dialogus/raw/leaves/`, n35, n58–59, n94–n100). No other version
of these chunks was opened.

Classes: 1 terms · 2 connectives · 3 construal · 4 a note that misdescribes the Latin or English.
Severity: **error** (the English or a note says something the text does not, or drops a step) ·
**weak** (defensible, but loses a nuance the argument uses) · **style**.

Where both versions have the same fault, it is listed once with version "A, B" and counted for both.

## Chunk 02 (3.15–5.11)

The two Englishes are nearly identical, often word for word. They differ mainly in the notes.

| version | page.line | Latin | English | class | severity | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| B | 3.23–4.1 (n. 1) | *ista vita similis aquiline est … contemplativa perfeccior quam activa* | n. 1: "taken as *vita … contemplativa*, 'the contemplative life'…; it could also be read as a string of predicates" | 4 | error | The note says the attributive reading was chosen and gives the predicate reading as the alternative. **Counts.** A: 0 error · 1 weak · 0 style. B: 1 error · 0 weak · 3 style.

**Notes coverage.** B has more on sources and allusions. It has the eagle as a figure of
contemplation (Aug. *Tr. in Io.* 36.5), the precise *Enarr. in Ps.* 71.5 and *Tr. in Io.* 1.2 for
*montes*, and the cockle of Matt. 13:25 behind *seminantur*. A has more on construal. It explains the
sense of *influencia* (the head and the sun), says what *que* agrees with (4.27), and gives an
alternative for *de persona status ecclesie*. B's apparatus report at 4.26–27 is correct and A's is
garbled. Neither note mentions 4.19 *regulam* for *graciam* (B¹) or 4.14 *virtutum* (B, B¹, C).

**Verdict.** Equivalent as translations. A is slightly ahead on accuracy, because B's n. 1 misstates
its own construal. B is slightly ahead on source notes.

**Notes coverage.** A catches apparatus that B misses: *ad Christum* for *Christo* (27.20) and

should be checked. Both miss 27.26 *aut audacius* for *auditivus* and *misereri* for *miseri*, 28.12

apparatus coverage and cleaner prose. Call it roughly equivalent, with A marginally ahead on
reliability.

**Counts.** A: 0 error · 1 weak · 6 style. B: 0 error · 7 weak · 6 style.

**Notes coverage.** A is stronger on apparatus and construal, and every claim checked on the plates
is right: *propter* (63.19), *tales* (63.21), *et specialiter*, marginal *aufferre* and *non*
(64.11), *episcopi* for *ipsi* (66.1), *pauperiores* (B¹, 67.9) and *hic* (69.9). A also has *stat*,
*Videat … fundet se* with its alternative, the concessive alternative for *cum habuerunt*, and the
link between *completa similitudo* and the "apish" topic. B is stronger on sources and reception. It
has Gal. 2:6 ("who seem to be something", 66.18, which A misses), the structure of the 66.22 question,
*vivant* (B), *verificatur* (B¹), *igitur* (C), *viverent* (C), *Ad conditorem canonum* alongside
*Cum inter nonnullos*, a proposed referent for the confiscation (Wykeham, 1376; to verify), and the
point that Acts does not say Stephen left the office. But two of B's apparatus reports are wrong
(65.22, 66.15). Both miss 63.23 *dotacio* for *donacio* (B corr., C), which matters in an argument
about donation against endowment. Both also miss 64.10 *dum* for *cum sic* and 67.31 *sequentem*.
Neither remarks on *seriose* (66.20). A has "in order" and B has "in earnest", and the medieval sense
should be checked.

**Verdict.** A is better, clearly. Neither version has an outright mistranslation. But B has seven
weak points, four of them in the English (the *autem* connective, the *perpetua* echo, concessive
*licet*, the *curatum* agreement) and three in notes that misreport the apparatus or cross-reference
the wrong note. A's only weak point is the 66.22–26 question. B's source notes are richer, and its
long periods track the Latin more closely (A's sentence splitting costs it several style points).

## Overall

**A is the better version, moderately.** Neither version mistranslates the argument anywhere. The
English is close in quality: B tends to keep Wyclif's long periods and "since" for causal *cum*,
while A splits sentences and sometimes loosens a connective. The difference is reliability. Both of
B's errors are in its notes: one note contradicts B's own English (02 n. 1), and one reports a
reading the apparatus does not contain (14 n. 5). B also has three further apparatus or
cross-reference misreports in chunk 30, and more construal slips (*directe*, *licet*, *curatum*,
*perpetua* → "eternal"). A's apparatus claims check out on the plates, with one garbled exception
(02 n. 4). B's notes cover sources and reception more fully (Gal. 2:6, Matt. 13:25, Tr. in Io.,
*Ad conditorem*, the Wykeham suggestion, the liturgical *dicere de*). They would be worth folding
into A.
