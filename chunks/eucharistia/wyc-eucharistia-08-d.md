# *De eucharistia*, chapter 8, part 4: the answers on quantity, on Ambrose, and on the loaves

## ¶1 · 244.17–245.23

> [p. 244] Et idem est iudicium de quantitate et qualitate coextensis cum materiali essencia. Si enim quantitas, qualitas, forma et materia haberent per se dimensiones, sicut habet materialis substancia (ut fantasiatur error predictus de eukaristia), tunc forent quatuor quantitates preter quantitatem communem coextense per quinque loca coextensa, et quantitas cum suo loco esset prius quoad consequenciam quam alia loca vel alie quantitates quatuor sequentes, quia (ut fantasiatur) quantitas cum suo loco posset esse vacuum sine aliqua aliorum quatuor, non econtra, et (ut videtur michi) tunc foret locus mundi diminutus, cum deficerent quatuor communitatis multitudines individualium; aliud enim foret unam istarum quinque rerum per se situari et aliud aliam sic situari, [p. 245] cum Deus posset annichilare posterius (ut inquiunt) servato priori; vel enim oportet dare materialem substanciam sine tali quantitate limitata vel si hoc non potest contingere, tunc quantitas talis est longe prior materiali substancia. Deus enim (ut inquiunt) potest miraculose servare quantitatem sine materiali substancia, non econtra, et sic videtur quod ad omnem punctum mundi relictum foret infinitum multum de vacuo, cum Deus (ut inquiunt) posset ponere in situ istius mundi infinita corpora et alia que poterunt per se esse infinitum disparium specierum et cuilibet illorum oporteret dare locum proprium. Non enim est locus prius natura quam corporalis substancia sed econtra, ideo non est possibile eundem locum in numero suscipere vicissim quotlibet species materialis substancie, formarum substancialium et accidentalium, quia tunc foret locus vacuum; quod Deus non posset movere precedens omnia ista. Sed quid stulcius aut infidelius. Oportet ergo omne situatum per se possibile habere situm proprium qui est illam essenciam situari (ut nunc supponitur). Et patet quod secunda pars maioris assumpte sit falsa nec per minorem probata.

And the judgement is the same of [quantity]{g:quantitas} and [quality]{g:qualitas} coextended with material [essence]{g:essencia}. For if quantity, quality, form and matter had dimensions [by themselves]{g:per-se},[^1] as material [substance]{g:substancia} has (as the aforesaid error about [the Eucharist]{g:eukaristia} [fancies]{g:fantasiare}), then there would be four quantities besides the common quantity, coextended through five coextended places; and quantity with its place would be prior as to [inference]{g:consequencia} to the other places or the four other quantities that follow, for (as it fancies) quantity with its place could be a vacuum without any of the other four, not conversely; and (as it seems to me) the place of the world would then be diminished, since four multitudes of individual [sites] of the common [place] would be lacking;[^2] for it would be one thing for one of these five things to be situated by itself and another for another to be so situated, [245] since God could annihilate the posterior (as they say) while keeping the prior; for either one must assign material substance without such a limited quantity,[^3] or, if this cannot happen, then such quantity is far prior to material substance. For God (as they say) can miraculously keep quantity without material substance, not conversely; and so it seems that at every point of the world left there would be infinitely much of vacuum, since God (as they say) could put in the site of this world infinitely many bodies, and other things that could be by themselves, of infinitely many disparate species, and to each of them one would have to assign a proper place. For place is not prior by nature to bodily substance, but conversely; therefore it is not possible for the same place in number to receive in turn any number of species of material substance, of [substantial forms]{g:forma-substancialis} and accidental forms, for then place would be a vacuum; which God could not move, [it] preceding all these.[^4] But what is more foolish or more faithless? Therefore every situated thing possible by itself must have a proper site, which is that essence's being situated (as is now supposed). And it is clear that the second part of the [assumed]{g:assumere} major is false,[^5] and not proved by the minor.

## ¶2 · 245.24–246.5

> [p. 245] Et quantum ad miracula (ut secundo narratur de beato Ambrosio), dicitur quod tam quoad tempus quam quoad visum potest esse decepcio. Quoad tempus, quia facile contingit homines errare de ydemptitate instantis; quod patet ex hoc quod nemo scit notare per motum vel sensum primum instans vel medium diei vel temporis; quomodo ergo notarent diversi homines in locis distantibus quod pro eodem instanti corpus beati Ambrosii sit hic et ibi? Et in [p. 246] differencia temporis nobis insensibili posset corpus beati Ambrosii per angelos ab uno loco ad alium deferri et sic interpolari, ut patet de ductu cicionis sic quod apparet in utroque loco ministerium sancti esse continuum.

And as to the miracles (as is [related]{g:narrare} second, of blessed Ambrose), [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that there can be deception both as to time and as to sight. As to time, for it easily happens that men err about the identity of an instant; which is clear from this, that nobody knows how to mark, by motion or by sense, the first instant or the middle of a day or of a time; how then would different men in distant places mark that at the same instant the body of blessed Ambrose was here and there? And in [246] a difference of time [imperceptible]{g:insensibilis} to us the body of blessed Ambrose could be carried by angels from one place to another and so be [interrupted]{g:interpolatus}, as is clear of the drawing of a firebrand,[^6] so that the ministry of the saint appears to be continuous in both places.

## ¶3 · 246.6–246.14

> [p. 246] Sed da quod per idem tempus sine interpolacione sit sanctus per illa loca distancia, adhuc non oporteret concedere illud propositum, cum stat sensus illusio propter similitudinem de ydemptificacione corporis; possunt enim spiritui sancti Ambrosii aptari corpora similia et eandem personam secundum illa corpora diversa ministrare simul tempore per diversa loca, ideo illud miraculum supposita eius veritate est nimis nudum ad inferendum propositum.

But grant that through the same time, without [interruption]{g:interpolacio}, the saint is through those distant places: still one would not have to grant that point proposed, since an [illusion]{g:illusio} of sense can stand,[^7] on account of the likeness, about the [identification]{g:ydemptificacio} of the body; for like bodies can be fitted to the spirit of Saint Ambrose, and the same [person]{g:persona}, according to those different bodies, can minister at once in time through different places; therefore that miracle, its truth supposed, is [exceedingly]{g:nimis} bare for inferring the point proposed.

## ¶4 · 246.15–247.11

> [p. 246] Et quantum ad illud dictum, patet quod non iuvat propositum, cum idem corpus numero potest vicissim esse per diversa loca, sicut idem corpus potest esse per diversa instancia. Unde si applicaretur ad propositum, concederetur quod stat idem corpus esse simul per diversa instancia, sic intenditur quod sit simul per diversa loca, nam tam irracionale et tam inpossibile foret unum sicut reliquum; ideo sicut non potest esse quod idem corpus sit simul per diversa instancia, sic non potest esse quod ipsum sit simul per diversa loca, loquendo de esse dimensionali; faciat ergo sic opinans vel probet quod sint simul tempore diversa eius instancia, et tunc est pro opinione evidencia; sicut enim non possunt simul distendi tempora sic nec loca; loquimur enim de situ inmobili equali linea lineari superficiali vel profundali substancie locate in qua est locus subiective; iste (inquam) sensus de loco est pertinencior quam sensus de concavitate [p. 247] corporis continentis. Et patet quod patencius est nulla instancia posse esse simul tempore quam loca posse esse simul situaliter; immediata (inquam) esse possunt sed simul esse non possunt, quia si A locus sit simul cum B loco sibi equali, tunc ex equali continencia uterque locat reliquum, et sic uterque superfluit componens cum reliquo intensive, et conformiter si multa instancia sunt simul; non enim loquimur de simultate inmediacionis sed de simultate adequacionis, sic quod eadem res de genere loci vel temporis quodlibet sit simul cum alio contineat adequate.

And as to that saying,[^8] it is clear that it does not help the point proposed, since the same body in number can be in turn through different places, just as the same body can be through different instants. Hence, if it were applied to the point proposed, it would be granted that it can stand that the same body is at once through different instants, as it is [maintained]{g:intendere} that it is at once through different places; for the one would be as irrational and as impossible as the other. Therefore, just as it cannot be that the same body is at once through different instants, so it cannot be that it is at once through different places, speaking of dimensional [being]{g:esse}. Let him then who so opines make, or prove, that its different instants are at once in time, and then there is [evidence]{g:evidencia} for the [opinion]{g:opinio}; for just as times cannot be stretched apart at once, so neither can places. For we speak of the immovable site equal, line by line,[^9] linear, superficial or in depth, to the located substance in which place is [subjectively]{g:subiective}; this sense (I say) of place is [more fitting]{g:pertinencior} than the sense of the concavity [247] of the containing body. And it is clear that it is more evident that no instants can be at once in time than that places can be at once [situally]{g:situaliter}; they can (I say) be immediate,[^10] but they cannot be at once, for if place A is at once with place B equal to it, then by equal containing each locates the other, and so each is superfluous, composing with the other intensively; and in conformity if many instants are at once. For we do not speak of the simultaneity of immediacy but of the [simultaneity of adequacy]{g:simultas-adequacionis}, so that the same thing of the genus of place or of time, whichever is at once with another, contains it adequately.[^11]

## ¶5 · 247.13–247.28

> [p. 247] Quantum ad tercium dicitur iuxta sanctorum sentencias quod Deus in dictis miraculis fecit materias absconditas ministrari et fecit formas illas paucas et parvas habere virtutem ad assimilandum sibi insensibiliter illas materias, ita quod nulla materialis essencia fit simul et semel per diversa loca; et sic verum est quod ex tanto cibo fecit Christus tantum miraculum saciando tantum populum, tum quia synonymice secundum partem cibi et principative secundum virtutem sibi datam ad assimilandum sibi tantum de huiusmodi alimento quantum Deus voluerit ad finem huiusmodi ministrari; nec sequitur ex isto sensus illusio, Dei ioculacio, miraculi minoracio vel aliud inconveniens nominandum. Et patet in genere solucio illius triplicis textus scripture pro multiplicacione corporis allegate.

As to the third, the answer is, following the [teachings]{g:sentencia} of [the saints]{g:sancti},[^12] that God in the said miracles caused hidden matters to be supplied, and caused those few and small forms to have the [power]{g:virtus} to assimilate those matters to themselves [imperceptibly]{g:insensibiliter}, so that no material essence comes to be at once and at the same time through different places; and so it is true that from so much food Christ did so great a miracle in satisfying so great a people,[^13] both because [he did it] synecdochically according to a part of the food,[^14] and [principatively]{g:principative} according to the power given to it to assimilate to itself as much of such nourishment as God willed to be supplied for such an end. Nor does there follow from this an illusion of sense, a [jesting]{g:ioculacio} on God's part, a [lowering]{g:minoracio} of the miracle, or any other [absurdity]{g:inconveniens} to be named.[^15] And the [resolution]{g:solucio} in general is clear of that threefold text of scripture [alleged]{g:allegare} for the [multiplication of a body]{g:multiplicacio-corporis}.

## Notes

[^1]: **G.** *Per se* here in the spatial sense, "by themselves", apart from the substance; so *per se situari* below.

[^2]: **C.** *Cum deficerent quatuor communitatis multitudines individualium* is obscure: taken as the four multitudes of individual sites, drawn from the one common place, that the four further quantities would need. Flagged as a doubt.

[^3]: **T.** *Limitata* is the print (245.3); ABC read *linita*, DE *liniata* ("lined", having lines). The print is translated.

[^4]: **C.** *Quod Deus non posset movere precedens omnia ista*: taken as the absurd end of the case, a vacuum place prior to every body that God could not move; *precedens* agrees with *quod*. The sentence is uncertain.

[^5]: **C.** The major of the argument for multiplication (243.23–31) has two parts; its second part, that several bodies can be situated at once in one place, is the one denied. *Assumpte* is the participle "assumed", not the logical noun *assumptum*.

[^6]: **T.** *Cicionis* is the print, which Loserth queries ("Ita codd."); read as *ticionis*, from *titio*, the firebrand that seems a continuous ring of fire when whirled (our emendation). D reads *ex ductu* (246.3).

[^7]: **C.** *Stat* "can stand", is consistent: the illusion is possible, not asserted. So *concederetur quod stat* in ¶4.

[^8]: **C.** "That saying": the clause of the second confirmation, "just as the same body can be multiplied through different times, so also through different places" (244.4–5).

[^9]: **T.** *Equali linea lineari* may carry a doubled word (*linea* beside *lineari*); taken as "equal, line by line" and flagged.

[^10]: **C.** *Immediata*: adjoining with nothing between (cf. *immediate* "without intermediary"); set against *simul*, at once in the same place. Hence *simultas inmediacionis* against *simultas adequacionis* below.

[^11]: **C.** *Quodlibet sit simul cum alio contineat adequate* is taken as: whatever thing of the genus of place or time is "at once" with another in this sense contains it exactly. D reads *adequata* (247.11–12).

[^12]: **C/S.** *Sentencia* in the sense "teaching" (the CONTEXTUAL row). Which of the saints Wyclif has in view is not said; Augustine's account of the loaves (*In Ioh.* tract. 24) is the likeliest: *In Ioh.* tract. 24.1 (PL 35, 1593), *unde multiplicat de paucis granis segetes, inde in manibus suis multiplicavit quinque panes*, which Thomas (*ST* III q. 44 a. 4 ad 4) reads as *additionem extraneae materiae in panes conversae*, the matter supplied and assimilated of Wyclif's answer (LEDGER K08d-1).

[^13]: **S.** The feeding of the multitude from five loaves and two fishes (John 6:9; Matt. 14:20, "they did all eat, and were filled"). *Ex tanto cibo* "from so much food": the measure of the food, set against *tantum miraculum* and *tantum populum*.

[^14]: **T.** Print *synonymice* ("synonymously") does not yield a sense; E reads *synodochice*, "synecdochically" (247.20–21), which is translated: the food fed the people by a part standing for the whole. *Tum quia* has no second *tum*; a member may have been lost. Print *saciando*; E *faciando*, ABCD *faciendo* (247.20).

[^15]: **C.** *Inconveniens* here an absurdity in argument (the CONTEXTUAL row), the last of a list of consequences the answer avoids.
