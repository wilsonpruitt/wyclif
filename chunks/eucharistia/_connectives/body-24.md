### 09-a ¶2 · 272.23–273.4  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 272] Hic sunt multe instancie; prima loycorum que dicit quod non docetur efficaciter quod ista fuit sentencia dicti Innocencii cum tot prelatis. Secunda quod non docetur quod ipse vel illi fuerunt membra ecclesie, nam solum predestinatus est membrum ecclesie, **sed** ex tibi dubio nullus istorum fuit predestinatus, ergo [p. 273] ex tibi dubio nullus istorum fuit membrum ecclesie. Qualis ergo racio: Si homo debet credere ecclesie, ergo illis? De ista materia, quomodo fides data ecclesie iniciat fidem catholicam, patet alibi.

ENGLISH: Here there are many [objections]{g:instancia}:[^3] the first, the logicians', which says that it is not effectively shown that this was the [teaching]{g:sentencia}[^4] of the said Innocent with so many prelates. The second, that it is not shown that he or they were [members]{g:membrum} of the church; for only a [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} is a member of the church; now, [for all you know]{g:ex-tibi-dubio}, none of these was predestinate; therefore, [273] for all you know, none of these was a member of the church. What sort of reasoning is it, then: "If a man ought to believe the church, therefore [he ought to believe] them"? On this matter, how faith given to the church begins catholic faith, it is clear elsewhere.

### 09-a ¶6 · 276.9–276.20  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 276] Ulterius vero considera quo tendit exposicio huius sententie: Quodcunque ligaveris super terram erit ligatum et in celo, nedum quod intelligitur singulariter de Petro, de ipso, et quocunque Romano pontifice, **sed** quod sit excepcio de quocunque quod ipse in temporalibus vel spiritualibus decreverit faciendum. Quod si per inpossibile sit verum, quilibet Romanus pontifex extollitur super omne quod dicitur Deus, cum sit Deus in terris et inpeccabilis Antichristus. Et inde dictus papa modificat quomodo imperator dominatur laycis et non clericis qui a iurisdiccione Cesaris simpliciter sunt exempti.

ENGLISH: And further, consider where the exposition of this saying tends, "Whatsoever thou shalt bind upon earth, it shall be bound also in heaven": [not only]{g:nedum} that it is understood of Peter [alone]{g:singulariter}, of himself, and of any Roman pontiff whatever,[^25] but that the exception is about whatever he has decreed to be done in temporals or spirituals.[^26] And if, per impossibile, this were true, every Roman pontiff "is lifted up above all that is called God", since he would be God on earth and an Antichrist unable to sin.[^27] And thence the said pope qualifies how the emperor [exercises lordship]{g:dominari} over laymen and not over clerics, who are exempt [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} from the [jurisdiction]{g:iurisdiccio} of Caesar.

### 09-b ¶1 · 276.21–277.14  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 276] Tercio vero extorquet ex illo Gen. Iᵒ, 16: Fecit Deus duo luminaria, luminare maius, ut preesset diei et luminare minus ut preesset nocti, quomodo Spiritus Sanctus per illud intenderat ipsum et omnes Romanos pontifices super omnes cesares preeminere. Que omnia si sint vera, quomodo pendebit sensus scripture derivandus toti ecclesie a tali capite? Unde solebam sciscitari a satrapis sensum huius Iᵃ Cor. XI, 28: Probet **autem** se ipsum homo et sic de pane illo edat et de calice bibat. Manifestum (inquam) videtur quod loquitur [p. 277] de hoc sacramento corporis et sanguinis Domini, sicut ante: Panis quem frangimus, nonne participacio corporis Domini est? Et quocienscunque manducabitis panem hunc etc. Quicunque manducaverit panem vel biberit calicem etc. Omnia enim ista intelliguntur de pane in cena Domini manducato. Non **autem** audemus asserere illum esse sensum Spiritus Sancti: De pane illo edat, hoc est, de accidentibus illis edat, frangimus vel quod panis quem frangimus, hoc est, accidencia que frangimus hoc figurant, quia oporteret specificare illa accidencia quod nesciunt; nam securius est loqui vocando illud panem, ut facit evangelium Apostoli, quousque habuerimus revelacionem vel racionem de sensu extraneo.

ENGLISH: And thirdly he wrests from that [text] of Genesis 1:16, "God made two lights: a greater light to rule the day; and a lesser light to rule the night",[^1] [the claim] that the Holy Spirit by it had meant him and all [Roman pontiffs]{g:romanus-pontifex} to be preeminent over all caesars.[^2] If all these things are true, how will the [meaning]{g:sensus} of scripture, which is to be derived to the whole [church]{g:ecclesia}, hang on such a head? Hence I used to inquire of the [satraps]{g:satrapa} the meaning of this [text] of 1 Corinthians 11:28: "But let a man prove himself: and so let him eat of that bread and drink of the chalice." It seems manifest (I say) that he speaks [277] of this [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} of the body and blood of the Lord, as before: "The bread which we break, is it not the partaking of the [body of the Lord]{g:corpus-domini}?" And: "As often as you shall eat this bread" etc. "Whosoever shall eat the bread, or drink the chalice" etc.[^3] For all these are understood of the [bread]{g:panis} eaten at the supper of the Lord. But we dare not assert that this is the meaning of the Holy Spirit: "Let him eat of that bread", that is, "let him eat of those [accidents]{g:accidens}"; "we break"; or that "the bread which we break", that is, "the accidents which we break", [figure]{g:figurare} this;[^4] for it would be necessary to specify those accidents, which they do not know [how to do]; for it is safer to speak calling it bread, as the gospel of the Apostle does, until we have a revelation or a reason for the strange meaning.

### 09-b ¶2 · 277.15–277.29  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 277] Et confirmacio illius est quod beatus Augustinus sepe respondet in talibus quod ideo sic loquitur, quia sacra scriptura sic loquitur. Quare ergo debemus nos pocius quam doctores sancti modum loquendi scripture sacre deserere? Et specialiter propter tam extraneam sentenciam ex aliis lapsibus tam suspectam. Unde inter alia valde suspectum est quod decretalis nec racionem nec scripturam sacram allegat pro sensu quem exprimit. Ideo cum testificacio non sit maior quam quatuor magnorum doctorum, ut docet decretum, illi **autem** prohibuerunt credi dictis suis, nisi de quanto scriptura sacra vel racio ipsa docent, que ergo racio credendi vel capiti in hoc parte? Honorare tamen debemus et glossare dicta papalia quantum permittit religio christiana.

ENGLISH: And the confirmation of this is that blessed Augustine often answers in such cases that he speaks so because holy scripture speaks so.[^5] Why, then, ought we, rather than the holy doctors, to desert the manner of speaking of holy scripture? And especially for so strange a [teaching]{g:sentencia},[^6] so suspect from other lapses. Hence, among other things, it is highly suspect that the [decretal]{g:decretalis} [alleges]{g:allegare} neither reason nor holy scripture for the meaning it expresses. Therefore, since no testimony is greater than that of the four great doctors, as the [decree]{g:decretum} teaches, and they forbade their sayings to be believed except so far as holy scripture or reason itself teaches,[^7] what reason, then, is there for believing even the head in this part? Yet we ought to honour and [gloss]{g:glossa} the papal sayings as far as the Christian religion permits.[^8]

### 09-b ¶3 · 278.1–278.24  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 278] Nec contendo quod iste Innocencius irreligiose subiugavit sibi Angliam obligacione nimis civili in nongentis marcis solvendis curie annuatim, seminavit discordiam inter Angliam et Franciam, contra imperatorem et alios seculares dominos, contra abbatem Joachim et alios irreligiose processit, ut narrat Cestrensis libro septimo, capitulo XXIV et infra; temporeque suo duo ordines Fratrum inceperant, **sed** multa mala passa fuit ecclesia quod figuratum est portento multiplici. Nam crescente secta signa colencium ultra religionem quam Christus instituit crescere oportet irreligiositatem fictam in signis. Multa **autem** opera insignia dereliquit, ut tractatum De Miseria condicionis humane et magnam partem sentencie quinque librorum decretalium; **sed** non ceperunt auctoritatem ab eo, cum successit post eum Honorius tercius qui dicitur confirmasse ordines fratrum Predicatorum et Minorum, et post eum successit Gregorius IX qui quinque libros decretalium per fratrem Raymundum dicitur compilasse. Et utrobique dicuntur opera eius laudanda primo de quanto consonant legi Dei. Nec dicet aliquis quod dicta sua (in quantum sua) sunt magis autentica quam glossa Innocencii quarti quam ecclesia per se acceptat, in quantum consonat veritati.

ENGLISH: Nor do I contend that this Innocent irreligiously subjugated England to himself by an [exceedingly]{g:nimis} civil obligation, of nine hundred marks to be paid to the curia every year; sowed discord between England and France; proceeded irreligiously against the emperor and other [secular lords]{g:seculares-domini}, against Abbot Joachim and others, as [the Chester]{g:cestrensis} [chronicler] relates in book seven, chapter 24 and following;[^9] and in his time the two orders of Friars had begun, but the church suffered many evils, which was figured by a manifold portent. For as the sect of those who worship signs grows beyond the religion which Christ instituted, the [irreligion]{g:irreligiositas} feigned in signs must grow.[^10] But he left many notable works, such as the treatise *On the Misery of the Human Condition* and a great part of the teaching of the five books of the Decretals;[^11] but they did not take [their] [authority]{g:auctoritas} from him, since after him succeeded Honorius III, who is said to have confirmed the orders of the friars Preachers and Minors, and after him succeeded Gregory IX, who is said to have compiled the five books of the Decretals through Brother Raymond.[^12] And [on both sides]{g:utrobique} his works are said to be praiseworthy first so far as they accord with the law of God. Nor will anyone say that his sayings (insofar as [they are] his) are more [authentic]{g:autenticus} than the gloss of Innocent IV, which the church accepts [of itself]{g:per-se}, [insofar as]{g:in-quantum} it accords with the truth.[^13]

### 09-b ¶4 · 278.25–279.10  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 278] Quinta **autem** responsio in qua senex fideliter potest quiescere stat in isto quod, cum videtur esse scrupulus in determinacione ecclesie, quiescendum est humiliter in sentencia antiqua magis probabili, quousque ecclesia declaraverit veritatem. Ponit enim synodus sub Nicholao IIᵒ expresse quod panis et vinum que in altari [p. 279] ponuntur sunt post consecracionem non solum sacramentum **sed** corpus Christi et sanguis. In nulla **autem** decretali sequenti tam expresse ponitur quod post consecracionem non remanet panis aut vinum eukaristia, et interim volo parate acquiescere racioni, quia scio quod in nullius pape potestate est cum millesies mille satrapis determinare quidquam de fide katholica, nisi quod reperire poterunt in scriptura, quia aliter indubie foret Antichristus et plus quam Deus, potens totam fidem quam credit christianismus subvertere.

ENGLISH: And the fifth reply, in which an old man may faithfully rest, stands in this: that when there seems to be a scruple in the [determination of the church]{g:determinaciones-ecclesie}, one must rest humbly in the ancient teaching, the more probable, until the church has [made clear]{g:declarare} the truth.[^14] For the synod under Nicholas II lays down expressly that "the bread and the [wine]{g:vinum} which are placed on the altar [279] are after [consecration]{g:consecracio} not only the sacrament but the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} and [his] blood".[^15] But in no later decretal is it laid down so expressly that after consecration the [Eucharist]{g:eukaristia} does not remain bread or wine;[^16] and meanwhile I am willing readily to acquiesce in reason, because I know that it is in the [power]{g:potestas} of no [pope]{g:papa}, with a thousand times a thousand satraps, to determine anything concerning the catholic faith except what they can find in scripture; for otherwise he would without doubt be Antichrist and more than God, able to overturn the whole faith that Christendom believes.

### 09-b ¶6 · 279.18–279.30  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 279] Hic dictum est quod decretum Nicholai IIⁱ est probabilius, non solum quia ibi fuerunt plures episcopi, vel quia fuit antiquius, fonti fidei christiane propinquius, **sed** eo potissime quod fonti fidei scripture est similius et sanctis doctoribus conformius. Unde non propter loquelam illius synodi **sed** propter veritatem fidei scripture debet pocius credi priori sentencie; nec solum debet credi cuicunque parti scripture plus quam millies mille episcopis eciam Romanis vel Avinonicis sub racione qua tales, verum eciam plus beato Augustino et aliis sanctis doctoribus quoad materiam fidei quam papis et cardinalibus qui non sunt in fide scripture adeo approbati.

ENGLISH: Here it has been said that the decree of Nicholas II is more probable, not only because more [bishops]{g:episcopus} were there, or because it was more ancient, nearer the fountain of the Christian faith, but most of all because it is more like the fountain of the faith of scripture and more conformable to the holy doctors.[^18] Hence not on account of the speech of that synod but on account of the truth of the faith of scripture ought the earlier teaching rather to be believed; and not only ought any part of scripture to be believed more than a thousand times a thousand bishops, even Roman or Avignonese, considered as such, but also blessed Augustine and the other holy doctors [ought to be believed] more, as to the matter of faith, than popes and cardinals who are not so approved in the faith of scripture.[^19]

### 09-b ¶7 · 279.31–280.16  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 279] In hiis **autem** que concernunt leges quas fabricant sunt boni iudices, quia ut dicit Jeronimus, quod medicorum [p. 280] est medici tractant, fabrilia fabri. Et ista est sentencia legis ecclesie distinccione XXᵃ in principio. Si **autem** simea in tecto se simulat scire quod nescit, bonum est temptare, si opere, vita et auctoritate scripture confirmat quod fingit; lex namque nature quam philosophi detegunt immediate et testimonia Augustini et aliorum sanctorum recitata superius satis docent quod accidencia de quibus loquebantur philosophi non possunt esse sine subiecto. Qua ergo presumpcione seminarent isti contrariam infidelitatem quam nec sciunt colorare racionibus nec scriptura? Quo ergo colore debet fidelis hoc credere? Cum ergo secundum Augustinum credere non potest nisi volens, patet quod preceptum eciam Romani pontificis non est ad hoc efficax nisi supra preceptum adduxerit in testem vel naturalem evidenciam vel scripturam.

ENGLISH: But in those things which concern the laws they forge they are good judges, for, as Jerome says, "what is [280] the physicians', physicians handle; smiths' work, smiths".[^20] And this is the teaching of the [law of the church]{g:leges-ecclesie} in Distinction 20, at the beginning.[^21] But if the ape on the roof pretends to know what it does not know, it is good to test whether by work, life and the authority of scripture it confirms what it feigns;[^22] for the law of nature, which the philosophers [expose]{g:detegere} [without intermediary]{g:immediate},[^23] and the testimonies of Augustine and the other [saints]{g:sancti} recited above, teach sufficiently that the accidents of which the philosophers spoke cannot be without a [subject]{g:subiectum}. With what presumption, then, would these men sow the contrary [unbelief]{g:infidelitas}, which they know how to [give colour to]{g:colorare} neither by reasons nor by scripture? By what [colour]{g:color}, then, ought the faithful man to believe this?[^24] Since, then, according to Augustine, [a man] cannot believe unless willing,[^25] it is clear that the command even of the Roman pontiff is not effective for this unless, over and above the command, he has brought in as witness either natural [evidence]{g:evidencia} or scripture.

### 09-c ¶1 · 280.17–281.17  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 280] Quod si obicitur necessario aliquos esse qui autentice doceant sensum scripture et alias veritates katholicas quas scriptura non exprimit, ut patet de beato Augustino contra Donatistas et multis aliis articulis fidei adiectis: hic dicitur quod necesse est aliquos esse preeminentes in ecclesia qui sint simul pastores et doctores (ut concludit Apostolus) et illis oportet credere non tanquam auctoribus **sed** tanquam doctoribus. Quod si prelati errant in pastu et circa mundana [p. 281] solliciti christiane fidei sunt ignari: quomodo curremus in isto ad ipsos ut capita ydolorum? Quod si desunt tales, melius est quiescere in fide explicita quam habemus quam suscitare novam sentenciam; ymmo ignoto sensu mistico vel litterali secundario melius est pausare in sensu rudi, relinquendo Spiritui Sancto cum sanctificacione vite sensum alium quam infundabiliter sculpere novum sensum. Unde quia secure possumus vocare eucaristiam panem, ut notat fides scripture cum sanctis doctoribus, debemus ad imitacionem eorum illud eligere et ambiguum de corpore isto vel alio accidente ignoto respuere nisi forte declaretur revelacione. **Sed** Deus non tardaret revelare fidem istam carioribus eius discipulis; nec credo Augustinum vel alios sanctos innovare quidquam credendum ecclesie nisi elaboratum ex fide scripture.

ENGLISH: But if [it is objected]{g:obicitur} that there must necessarily be some who teach [authentically]{g:autenticus} the [meaning]{g:sensus} of scripture and other catholic truths which scripture does not express, as is clear of blessed Augustine against the Donatists and of many other [articles of faith]{g:articulus-fidei} that have been added: here the answer is that it is necessary that there be some preeminent in the [church]{g:ecclesia} who are at once pastors and doctors (as the Apostle [infers]{g:concludere}),[^1] and these one must believe not as authors but as doctors. But if prelates err in feeding [the flock] and, busy [281] about worldly things, are ignorant of the Christian faith, how shall we run in this to them as to heads of idols?[^2] And if such are lacking, it is better to rest in the [explicit faith]{g:fides-explicita} which we have than to raise up a new [teaching]{g:sentencia};[^3] indeed, when the mystical or the secondary literal sense is unknown, it is better to pause in the rough sense, leaving the other sense to the Holy Spirit, with the [sanctification]{g:sanctificacio} of life, than [without foundation]{g:infundabilis} to carve out a new sense.[^4] Hence, since we can safely call the [Eucharist]{g:eukaristia} [bread]{g:panis}, as the faith of scripture with the holy doctors notes, we ought in imitation of them to choose that, and to spit out the ambiguity concerning this body or another unknown [accident]{g:accidens}, unless perhaps it is [made clear]{g:declarare} by revelation.[^5] But God would not delay to reveal this faith to his dearer disciples; nor do I believe that Augustine or other saints introduced anything new to be believed by the church unless worked out [from the faith of]{g:ex-fide} scripture.

### 09-c ¶4 · 282.1–283.17  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 282] Hic dicitur quod nulli pure creature credendum est nisi ut testi, non ut domino veritatis, in tantum quod Christus venit in mundum humanitus, ut testimonium perhibeat veritati et cum omnis veritas sit in scriptura (ut dicit Augustinus), patet quod nulli creature credendum est nisi de quanto fundaverit se in scriptura; quanto magis nos testes abiecti debemus dolere de errore in falsitate et testificare quantum ex fide scripture sufficimus veritatem et docto errore nostro humiliter revocare. In testem **autem** veritatis non debet accipi nisi qui habuerit eius noticiam, in tantum quod in veritate facti humani non debet secundum poleticos capi in testem nisi qui de ipso noticiam habuerit, quia aliter testis foret necessitatus mentiri. Et in isto inpugnatur quedam lex Anglie, que necessitare videtur testem asserere quod vel distincte scit vel non scit quis commisit facinus perpetratum. Et hec est racio quare leges ecclesie quoad fidem scripture plus credunt quatuor sanctis doctoribus et specialiter Augustino quam aliis quos credunt nec ex dono Dei nec ex studio humano tantum de veritate fidei cognovisse. In ambiguis tamen credendum est illis evidencia topica, exspectando inspiracionem auctoris fidei a quo est omnis noticia nostra veritatis; de veritate **autem** et sufficiencia scripture non licet ambigere. Ideo cum nullibi docet, vel quod eukaristia sit aliquid de novem generibus accidentis vel quod potest ipsum permittere per se esse, **sed** periciores sancti dixerunt oppositum, videtur stultum recentes diffinire sic esse de facto et cum illo ut fide onerare [p. 283] ecclesiam. Stetit enim sponsa Christi in floribus (ut creditur) circiter mille annos, antequam fuerit onerata ista perfidia, quia usque ad indebitos cultores. Et patet quod doctores legum hominum sunt valde suspecti in ista materia, cum spissim ex ignorancia seminant hereticos sensus. In ambiguis **autem** extra fidem scripture solent periti silere vel ipsa abicere curiosis, concedentes quod expedit nobis nedum multa ignorare **sed** nec cogitare, cuiusmodi sunt impertinencia fidei. Itaque quantum ad glossatores, patet ex dictis quod non debet credi illis et specialiter in ista materia nisi de quanto se fundaverint in racione vel scriptura autentica. Unde propter perplexitatem illius materie datam a nostris novellis doctoribus glossatores decretorum titubant et discordant; ideo dictum eorum in ista materia non est argumentum nisi ad hominem.

ENGLISH: Here the answer is that no pure [creature]{g:creatura} is to be believed except as a witness, not as lord of truth, so much so that Christ came into the world [humanly]{g:humanitus} to give testimony to the truth;[^9] and since all truth is in scripture (as Augustine says),[^10] it is clear that no creature is to be believed except so far as it has founded itself on scripture. How much more ought we, outcast witnesses, to grieve over error in falsehood, and to testify the truth as far as we are able from the faith of scripture, and, our error being shown, humbly to revoke [it]. And none ought to be accepted as a witness of truth except one who has knowledge of it, so much so that in the truth of a human deed none ought, according to the political [writers], to be taken as witness except one who has knowledge of it, for otherwise the witness would be necessitated to lie.[^11] And in this a certain law of England is impugned, which seems to necessitate a witness to assert that he either distinctly knows or does not know who committed the [misdeed]{g:facinus} perpetrated.[^12] And this is the reason why the [laws of the church]{g:leges-ecclesie}, as to the faith of scripture, believe the four holy doctors, and especially Augustine, more than others whom they believe to have known so much of the truth of faith neither by the gift of God nor by human study. In doubtful things, however, they are to be believed with [topical evidence]{g:evidencia-topica},[^13] awaiting the inspiration of the author of faith, from whom is all our knowledge of truth; but of the truth and sufficiency of scripture it is not lawful to doubt. Therefore, since it nowhere teaches either that the Eucharist is something of the nine genera of accident or that [God] can permit it to be [of itself]{g:per-se}, but the more expert saints said the opposite, it seems foolish for recent [men] to define that it is so in fact, and with it to burden [283] the church as with faith.[^14] For the [bride]{g:sponsa} of Christ stood among flowers (as is believed) about a thousand years before she was burdened with this perfidy, [that is,] until the undue [worshippers]{g:cultor} [came].[^15] And it is clear that the doctors of men's laws are highly suspect in this matter, since they frequently, out of ignorance, sow heretical meanings. And in doubtful things outside the faith of scripture the expert are wont to be silent, or to cast them away to the curious, granting that it is expedient for us [not only]{g:nedum} to be [ignorant of]{g:ignorare} many things but not even to think of them; of which kind are things [irrelevant]{g:impertinens} to faith. So, as to the [glossators]{g:glossator}, it is clear from what has been said that they are not to be believed, especially in this matter, except so far as they have founded themselves on reason or on authentic scripture. Hence, because of the [perplexity]{g:perplexitas} given to that matter by our [newer]{g:novelli} doctors, the glossators of the decrees stagger and disagree; therefore their saying in this matter is no argument except [to the man]{g:argumentum-ad-hominem}.[^16]

### 09-c ¶5 · 283.18–284.4  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 283] Quantum ad doctores legis, dicit primus, et precipuus videtur esse Lanfrancus abbas Beccensis in libello suo contra Beringarium De Sacramento Altaris, ubi videtur inpugnare quod panis et vinum remanent post consecracionem **sed** in consecracione desinunt. Iste **autem** Lanfrancus, licet fuerit sanctus, aliunde tamen in sua inveccione inartificiose procedit, quia sine evidencia nunc contendit modo quo abbas solet alloqui suos novicios et nunc simulat falsa contra que arguit. Non enim invenit homo in toto scripto suo (si non fallor) nisi evidenciam triplicem: Prima si panis remanet, tunc sacramentum parvi panis indignius est quam manna. Secunda racio est quod prophecia veteris testamenti figurans corpus Christi de futuro foret sollempnius quam nostrum sacramentum quod habet [p. 284] nudam efficaciam ad signandum corpus Christi de preterito; quod cum non sit prophecia **sed** veritas de preterito, ydiota sciret ostendere. Et tercia racio stat super decretis ecclesie que allegat.

ENGLISH: As to the doctors of law, the first who speaks, and the chief, seems to be Lanfranc, abbot of Bec, in his little book against Berengar, *On the Sacrament of the Altar*,[^17] where he seems to impugn [the view] that the bread and [wine]{g:vinum} remain after [consecration]{g:consecracio}, [holding] rather that in consecration they cease. But this Lanfranc, though he was a saint, nevertheless proceeds in his invective unskilfully on another side, for now he contends without [evidence]{g:evidencia}, in the way an abbot is wont to address his novices, and now he feigns falsehoods against which he argues. For one finds in his whole writing (if I am not mistaken) only a threefold evidence. The first: if bread remains, then the sacrament of a little bread is less worthy than the manna. The second reason is that the prophecy of the Old Testament figuring the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} as future would be more eminent than our sacrament, which has [284] the bare efficacy of signifying the body of Christ as past;[^18] which, since it is not a prophecy but a truth about the past, an [unlettered man]{g:ydiota} would know how to [show]{g:ostendere}. And the third reason rests on the decrees of the church which he [alleges]{g:allegare}.

### 09-c ¶7 · 284.15–284.31  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 284] Quoad secundum patet quod false assumitur professio Beringarii, cum decretum de ipso recitat quod confessus fuit secundum informacionem ecclesie, quod panis est post consecracionem nedum sacramentum, ut in errore suo confessus est, **sed** verum corpus Christi, hoc est figurat efficaciter, ut sacramentum nove legis, verum corpus Christi sacramentaliter ibi presens. Nec plus dicit adversarius; unde creditur Lanfrancum arguere contra eum pro dictis suis hereticis ante suam correccionem. **Sed** quid hoc ad nos? vel quod metu et non fide Beringarius ista dixit in sinodo? et sic sacramentum eukaristie non solum dicit veritatem corporis Christi de preterito **sed** facit quod verum corpus Christi sit vere et realiter ibi presens; quod non fecerunt sacramenta legis veteris. Nec ad hoc pertinet desicio nature panis vel eius destruccio.

ENGLISH: As to the second, it is clear that the profession of Berengar is falsely taken up, since the decree about him [recites]{g:recitare} that he confessed, according to the instruction of the church, that "the bread is after consecration not only the sacrament", as he had confessed in his error, "but the true body of Christ", that is, it [figures]{g:figurare} effectively, as a sacrament of the new law, the true body of Christ [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} present there.[^21] Nor does the adversary say more; hence Lanfranc is believed to argue against him for his heretical sayings before his correction.[^22] But what is this to us? Or that Berengar said these things in the synod out of fear and not faith? And so the sacrament of the Eucharist not only [signifies]{g:dicere} the truth of the body of Christ as past, but makes the true body of Christ to be [truly]{g:vere} and [really]{g:realiter} present there; which the sacraments of the old law did not do.[^23] Nor does the [ceasing]{g:desicio} of the nature of the bread, or its destruction, pertain to this.
