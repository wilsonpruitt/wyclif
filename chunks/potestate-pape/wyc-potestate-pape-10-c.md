# *De potestate pape*, chapter 10, part 3: primacy from the Lord, the submission of kingdoms, the doctors

## ¶1 · 228.14–230.21

> [p. 228] Sed secundo arguitur quod papa habet potestatem talem a Domino, ut dicit decretum XXII distinccione Sacrosancta, ubi Anacletus papa sic loquitur: Sacrosancta Romana ecclesia non ab apostolis sed ab ipso Domino primatum obtinuit. Ex quo sequitur quod non ab imperatore vel homine sed immediate a Deo papa habet excellentissimam dignitatem. Et idem confirmatur secundo ex submissione regnorum, et tercio ex testimonio doctorum de potestate pape absolvendi a pena et a culpa scribencium. Quantum ad primam partem huius argucie debet supponi quod iste papa intellexit per sacrosanctam Romanem ecclesiam non basilicam vel mixtum putridum, sed sanctos Petrum et Paulum et ceteros qui inhabitaverant illum locum. Ideo in eodem decreto dicit quod Petrus et Paulus erant socii in urbe Romana, unde signanter dicitur quod primatum obtinuit. Debet supponi secundo quod loquitur de primatu hominum quoad Deum, ex primicia virtutum et edificacionis ecclesie et non [p. 229] de primatu diviciarum temporalium vel glorie humane. In hoc enim Scarioth qui possedit agrum de mercede iniquitatis, ut dicit Petrus Act. primo, Petrum excesserat, propter quod sancti et specialiter apostoli timuerunt impetrare sibi redditus de bonis pauperum. Et ex istis patet tercio quam nuda foret argucia: Romana ecclesia non ab apostolis sed ab ipso Domino primatum obtinuit, igitur omnis Romanus pontifex vel alius debet in gloria mundana precellere vel seculariter dominari, quin pocius ex decreto sequitur quod Romanus pontifex tenetur servire illi populo, non sibi dominando vel ipsum suspendendo, sed pro illo efficaciter exorando, ut allegat Paulum fecisse Rom. primo: Licet, inquit, pro omnibus apud Dominum omnium sanctorum effundatur oracio, hiis tamen verbis Paulus beatissimus Romanis proprio cyrographo pollicetur dicens: Testis enim michi est Deus, cui servio in spiritu meo, in evangelio filii eius, quod sine intermissione memoriam vestri facio semper in oracionibus meis. Ex isto decreto videtur quod papa Anacletus noluit asserere quod ipse debuit super omnes alios dominari civiliter vel primatum habere quoad Deum super omnes alias personas ecclesie militantis, quia sic querendo gloriam propriam, ut dicitur Joh. V, haberet signum evidentissimum Antichristi, ymo Bonifacius VIII. in sua extravagante Unam sanctam non audebat expresse illud asserere, quia tam fideles quam infideles pertinenter sibi obicerent: Tu de te ipso testimonium perhibes, testimonium tuum non est verum. Unde in istam perplexitatem ex dotacione et exaltacione cesaris involvuntur Romani pontifices, quod imperatore [p. 230] querente ab eis: si ipsi in potestate, dignitate vel primatu quoad Deum excedunt omnes mortales alios iam viventes, oportet ipsos illud concedere, quia aliter, ut inquiunt, nemo debet credere quod sunt pape, et cum a Deo non habent illam preeminenciam, ut patet ex dictis, nec beatus Petrus illud concederet, patet eis concedendum quod hoc habent a cesare. Nam beatus Paulus Petri socius confessus fuit fideliter prima Cor. XV, 9: Ego, inquit, sum minimus apostolorum, qui non sum dignus vocari apostolus. Quare igitur sic non profiteretur Romanus pontifex, si imperialis dignitas non obstaret? Et sic necessitari videtur ex dotacione tam imperator quam suus episcopus involvi blasfemiam. Et sic verum concluditur quod a Deo immediate et non a natura humana papa habet excellenciam dignitatis, sed oportet quod mereatur illam excellenciam dignitatis humiliter sine pompa. Et revera non est medium merendi ipsam accipere dignitatem talem cesaream.

But secondly it is argued that the [pope]{g:papa} has such [power]{g:potestas} from the Lord, as the decree says in distinction 22, *Sacrosancta*, where Pope Anacletus speaks thus: "The most holy Roman [church]{g:ecclesia} obtained the [primacy]{g:primatus} not from the apostles but from the Lord himself."[^1] From which [it follows]{g:sequitur} that the pope has the most excellent dignity not from the emperor or from man but immediately from God. And the same is confirmed, secondly, from the submission of [kingdoms]{g:regnum}, and thirdly from the testimony of doctors who write on the pope's power to absolve from punishment and from guilt. As to the first part of this [quibble]{g:argucia},[^2] it must be supposed that this pope understood by "the most holy Roman church" not a basilica or a rotting mixture, but Saints Peter and Paul and the others who had dwelt in that place. Therefore in the same decree he says that "Peter and Paul were companions in the city of Rome"; hence it is said pointedly that it "obtained the primacy". It must be supposed secondly that he speaks of men's primacy with respect to God, from being first in virtues and in the building up of the church,[^3] and not [229] of a primacy of temporal riches or of human glory. For in this Iscariot, who "possessed a field of the reward of iniquity", as Peter says in Acts 1, had surpassed Peter; for which reason the saints, and especially the apostles, feared to [obtain]{g:impetrare} [revenues]{g:reditus} for themselves from the goods of the poor. And from these things it is clear, thirdly, how bare the quibble would be: "The Roman church obtained the primacy not from the apostles but from the Lord himself; therefore every [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex}, or anyone else, ought to excel in worldly glory or to [lord it]{g:dominari} in secular fashion." Rather, from the decree it follows that the Roman pontiff is bound to serve that people, not by lording it over it or [suspending]{g:suspendere} it, but by praying effectively for it, as [the decree] alleges Paul to have done, Romans 1: "Although," it says, "prayer is poured out for all before the Lord of all the saints, yet with these words the most blessed Paul promises the Romans in his own handwriting, saying: 'For God is my witness, whom I serve in my spirit in the gospel of his Son, that without ceasing I make a commemoration of you, always in my prayers.'"[^4] From this decree it seems that Pope Anacletus did not wish to assert that he ought to exercise civil lordship over all others or to have primacy with respect to God over all the other persons of the [church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans}; for, seeking his own glory in this way, as is said in John 5, he would have the most evident sign of Antichrist.[^5] Indeed, Boniface VIII in his extravagant *Unam sanctam* did not dare to assert that expressly, because believers and unbelievers alike would pertinently object to him: "Thou givest testimony of thyself: thy testimony is not true."[^6] Hence the Roman pontiffs are entangled, through the endowment and exaltation [they had] from Caesar, in this [perplexity]{g:perplexitas}: that if the emperor [230] asks them "whether they themselves, in power, dignity or primacy with respect to God, exceed all other mortals now living", they must grant it, because otherwise, as they say, no one ought to believe that they are popes; and since they do not have that [preeminence]{g:preeminencia} from God, as is clear from what has been said, nor would blessed Peter grant it, it is clear that they must grant that they have it from Caesar.[^7] For blessed Paul, Peter's companion, faithfully confessed in 1 Corinthians 15:9: "I am," he says, "the least of the apostles, who am not worthy to be called an apostle." Why therefore would the Roman pontiff not profess so, if the imperial dignity did not stand in the way? And so it seems to be made necessary by the [endowment]{g:dotacio} that both the emperor and his bishop are entangled in blasphemy. And so it is truly [inferred]{g:concludere} that the pope has excellence of dignity immediately from God and not from human nature; but he must merit that excellence of dignity humbly, without pomp. And in truth to accept such a caesarean dignity is no means of meriting it.

## ¶2 · 230.22–231.27

> [p. 230] Quantum ad secundum partem argucie, patet quod non est color si regna vel persone submittunt se improvide Antichristo, quod propterea ampliatur in eo potestas vel iurisdiccio; ymo dyabolus negaret istam arguciam, cum filii sui subiecti per peccatum non augent in eo potenciam, eo quod tam ipse quam fidelis concederet quod non capit potestatem suam ab indignissimo servo suo, sed habet ipsam ab inicio a solo Deo. Non igitur ex submissione tali docetur quod papa habet a Deo vel homine preeminenciam aliqualem, sed pocius cum omnis populus vel involuntarie sic sibi subicitur vel non debet sic sibi subici, evidenter sequitur ex tali subieccione blasfema carencia dignitatis. Unde non video quomodo papa posset patencius accusare se ipsum [p. 231] quam respondendo, ut narratur in quodam gesto Anglie, quomodo vacante episcopatu Dunelmie ex quadam subdola subtilitate clerici rex dicitur misisse pape, ut illud beneficium conferat suo clerico: rex autem et regnum ex scitu pape perante decreverant quod non obstante quacumque donacione pape promotus pape non perciperet fructus beneficii regni nostri, nisi regi et regno visus habilis, distribuendo illos fructus provide infra regnum, ipsum regnum velit incolere. Habito itaque longo consilio de literis regis papa fertur taliter respondisse: Et si rex misisset asinum pro illo beneficio, habuisset. Quod responsum manifeste indicat papam, et si prius dignus fuit ex lege privata papali illud officium contulisse, ab illo privilegio excedisse, quia XI questio III ex decreto beati Gregorii sic habetur: Privilegium omnino debet amittere, qui sibi permissa abutitur potestate. Maior autem in isto non posset esse abusio, quam scienter constituere indignum in clericali officio. Ista autem fallacia imaginatur de potestate ecclesiastica quod secularis potest eam dare sicut bovem vel asinum, vel quod ex defectu defensionis partis contrarie preposito spirituali acquiritur, si instituitur in quibusdam tradicionibus humanis. Sed deliramenta sunt ista.

As to the second part of the quibble, it is clear that there is no [colour]{g:color} [for saying], if kingdoms or persons submit themselves improvidently to Antichrist, that his power or [jurisdiction]{g:iurisdiccio} is therefore enlarged in him;[^8] indeed, the devil would deny this quibble, since his sons, subjected [to him] through sin, do not increase [power]{g:potencia} in him, because both he and a believer would grant that he does not take his power from his most unworthy servant, but has it from the beginning from God alone.[^9] Not therefore from such submission is it shown that the pope has any preeminence from God or from man; rather, since every people is either subjected to him thus involuntarily or ought not to be subjected to him thus, from such blasphemous subjection there follows evidently a lack of dignity.[^10] Hence I do not see how the pope could accuse himself more openly [231] than by answering as is told in a certain English chronicle: how, when the bishopric of Durham was vacant, the king, through some sly subtlety of a cleric, is said to have sent to the pope that he should confer that benefice on his cleric. And the king and the kingdom, with the pope's knowledge, had decreed beforehand that, notwithstanding any gift of the pope, the pope's promoted man should not receive the fruits of a benefice of our kingdom unless, having seemed [qualified]{g:habilis} to the king and the kingdom, and distributing those fruits providently within the kingdom, he were willing to dwell in the kingdom itself.[^11] So, having held long counsel over the king's letters, the pope is reported to have answered thus: "Even if the king had sent an ass for that benefice, he would have had it." Which answer plainly shows that the pope, even if before he was worthy by private papal law to have conferred that office, has gone out from that [privilege]{g:privilegium}; for in [Causa] 11, question 3, from a decree of blessed Gregory, it is held thus: "He ought wholly to lose privilege who abuses the power permitted to him."[^12] Now there could be no greater abuse in this than knowingly to set an unworthy man in a clerical office. And this [fallacy]{g:fallacia} imagines, concerning ecclesiastical power, that [a secular]{g:secularis} can give it like an ox or an ass, or that it is acquired by a spiritual [superior]{g:prepositi} from the failure of the opposing party to make a defence, if he is instituted in certain human traditions.[^13] But these are [ravings]{g:deliramenta}.

## ¶3 · 231.28–231.31

> [p. 231] Quantum ad tercium partem, patet quod doctoribus istis non debet credi nisi ut testibus vel autoribus in quibus Christus loquitur velut probantibus suam sentenciam racione.

As to the third part, it is clear that these doctors ought not to be believed except as witnesses or authors in whom Christ speaks, as proving their [teaching]{g:sentencia} by reason.[^14]

## ¶4 · 232.1–232.3

> [p. 232] Quantum ad testimonium eorum, patet, cum tam potestas quam absolucio sit insensibilis, testimonium tante distancium non est conveniens.

As to their testimony, it is clear that, since both power and absolution are [imperceptible]{g:insensibilis}, the testimony of men so far removed is not fitting.[^15]

## ¶5 · 232.4–232.13

> [p. 232] Quantum ad secundum membrum, non est signum quod Deus eis revelavit istam sentenciam, cum non patet ex scriptis vel factis apostolorum, et quatuor maiores doctores hoc non recitent. Augustinus autem sepe recitat quomodo non debet credi sibi nisi de quanto se fundaverit ex scriptura. Nec dubito quin nec racio nec autoritas hoc convincit. Ideo ve illis, qui introduxerunt hunc errorem novellum infundabilem in ecclesiam sanctam Dei.

As to the second member, it is no sign that God revealed this teaching to them, since it is not clear from the writings or deeds of the apostles, and the four greater doctors do not relate it. And Augustine often relates how he is not to be believed except so far as he has grounded himself on scripture.[^16] Nor do I doubt that neither reason nor authority proves this. Therefore woe to those who brought this new error, [without foundation]{g:infundabilis}, into the holy church of God.

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** Gratian, *Decretum* D.22 c.2 *Sacrosancta* (Loserth 228.16 "Cap. II"; the modern form is ours; LEDGER P10c-1), a Pseudo-Isidorian decretal under the name of Anacletus (LEDGER P10c-2). The words "Peter and Paul were companions in the city of Rome" (228.28) are from the same canon.
[^2]: **C.** *Argucia* (here "quibble") names the whole argument just stated, with its three parts: the decree (228.24), the submission of kingdoms (230.22), the doctors (231.28).
[^3]: **C.** *Ex primicia virtutum*: *primicia*, from *primicie* "first-fruits", is being first in virtue; kept apart from *primatus* "primacy" and *primitas* "firstness". The *primatus quoad Deum* is primacy in God's sight, set against primacy in riches.
[^4]: **S/C.** Rom. 1:9–10, quoted within the canon (D.22 c.2), whose words Wyclif gives. *Pro omnibus apud Dominum omnium sanctorum*: we take *omnium sanctorum* with *Dominum*, "the Lord of all the saints"; it could also go with *omnibus*, "for all the saints". *Proprio cyrographo*: in his own hand, i.e. in his own letter.
[^5]: **S.** John 5:41–44 ("the glory which is from God alone, you do not seek"); cf. John 7:18. Reference only.
[^6]: **S.** Boniface VIII, *Unam sanctam* (1302), *Extrav. comm.* 1.8.1 (Loserth 229.29; the modern form is ours; LEDGER P10c-3). The objection is John 8:13, the Pharisees' words to Christ.
[^7]: **C.** Loserth italicises the emperor's question (*si ipsi … viventes*); we quote it. The *perplexitas* is the dilemma: the popes must claim a primacy over all, and since it is not from God it can only be from Caesar.
[^8]: **G.** *Color*: a plausible ground, as in "colour of title"; not "pretext".
[^9]: **C.** *Potencia* here is "power", as *potestas* in the same sentence; Wyclif uses the two words for the same thing.
[^10]: **C.** We take *blasfema* with *subieccione* (ablative): "from such blasphemous subjection". Taken with *carencia* it gives "a blasphemous lack of dignity follows".
[^11]: **S.** A vacancy at Durham: Loserth refers to Walsingham, *Ypodigma Neustriae* p. 283, and *Historia Anglicana* 2.382, with his own *Studien zur Kirchenpolitik Englands im 14. Jahrhundert*, *Sitzungsberichte* Vienna 136, 27 (LEDGER P10c-4). *Promotus pape*: the man the pope has provided to the benefice.
[^12]: **S.** Gratian, *Decretum* C.11 q.3 c.63 *Privilegium* (Loserth 231.18 "Cap. LXIII"; the modern form is ours; LEDGER P10c-5), under Gregory's name. *Excedisse*: he has gone out of, forfeited, the privilege.
[^13]: **T.** *Ista autem fallacia* is Loserth's correction; the manuscripts read *Istam autem fallaciam* (231.21). At *si instituitur* (231.25) the manuscripts read *si*; Loserth asks whether *sed* is meant ("but he is instituted [only] in human traditions"). The print *si* is translated.
[^14]: **C.** *Suam sentenciam*: "their teaching", the doctors'; *suam* could refer to Christ ("his teaching").
[^15]: **C.** *Tante distancium*: men far removed (in time from the apostles, or from the things they testify to, which cannot be perceived). The third part has two members: the doctors as witnesses (¶4) and as receivers of revelation (¶5).
[^16]: **S/R.** Augustine says this of his own writings repeatedly, e.g. *Epistula* 82 and *De Trinitate* 3 prooem. (LEDGER P10c-6). The manuscripts note in the margin (232.8): *Credere doctoribus quando debemus* ("when we ought to believe the doctors"). The "four greater doctors" are Ambrose, Augustine, Jerome and Gregory.
