# *De eucharistia*, chapter 1, part 5: berengar's confession, and the senses of blessing and consecrating

## ¶1 · 25.19–27.19

> [p. 25] Et per hoc potest glosari decretum sequens: Ego Beringarius, ubi iste diaconus anathematizavit heresim qua prius fuerat irretitus; et facit protestacionem sue fidei sub hiis verbis: Consencio autem sancte Romane ecclesie et apostolice sedi, et ore et corde profiteor de sacramentis dominice mense eandem fidem me tenere quam dominus et venerabilis papa Nicholaus et hec sancta sinodus auctoritate evangelica et apostolica tenendam tradidit michique firmavit, scilicet panem et vinum que in altari ponuntur post consecracionem non solum sacramentum sed eciam verum corpus et sanguinem domini nostri Jesu Christi [p. 26] esse sensualiter, non solum sacramentaliter sed in veritate manibus sacerdotum tractari et frangi et fidelium dentibus atteri, iurans per sanctam et omousion Trinitatem et per hec sacrosancta Christi evangelia. Eos qui contra hanc fidem venerint cum dogmatibus et consecutoribus suis eterno anathemate dignos esse pronuncio. Ista tam sollempnis confessio non potest intelligi quod panis et vinum sit formaliter vel essencialiter corpus Christi et sanguis, sed figurative vel tropice, modo quo loquitur Apostolus I. Cor. Xº, 4: Bibebant autem de spirituali consequente eos petra, petra autem erat Christus. Unde glosa ordinaria intelligit per panem et vinum speciem panis et vini, modo quo loquitur perante eadem distinccio sub auctoritate Gregorii: Species et similitudo earum rerum vocabula sunt que ante fuerant, scilicet panis et vini; unde in fine cuiusdam misse oratur et dicitur: Perficiant in nobis, quesumus, Domine tua sacramenta que continent, ut que nunc sub specie panis et vini gerimus rerum veritate capiamus. Quotlibet sunt dicta sanctorum atque ecclesie que docent quod illa sacramenta sensibilia non sunt corpus Christi et sanguis sed eorum figura; et forte in isto multi sunt christiani nomine in fidelitate paganis peiores. Nam minus malum foret quod homo illud quod primo videt mane per totum residuum diei honoret ut Deum quam quod regulariter illud accidens quod videt in missa inter manus sacerdotis in hostia consecrata sit [p. 27] realiter Deus suus; et per hec est medium ad intelligendum decreta similia, ut distinccione XCVª, Ecce ego dico sub auctoritate Jeronimi sic habetur: Quid maius est Christo aut quid poterit corpori et sanguini eius anteponi? Si presbiter Christum consecrat, cum in altari sacramenta benedicat, benedicere populo non debet qui Christum consecrare non metuit? Videtur autem sanctum intelligere figurative per Christum hostiam consecratam, ita quod eadem sit consecracio qua consecrat hostiam et qua consecrare dicitur corpus Christi; hostiam tamen facit esse sacram saltem occasionaliter, dum rite facit ministerium sacerdotis, et Deus appropriate perficit quod suum est supernaturaliter alterando; quod si equivocetur in consecracione, vocando eam ostensionem rei sacre, tunc sicut laycus potest benedicere Trinitati, sic eciam consecrare, sicut laycus facit quod corpus Christi sit sacrum in isto loco vel sub isto sacramento et tamen non propterea consecrat corpus Christi.

And by this the following [decree]{g:decretum} can be [glossed]{g:glosare}: *Ego Berengarius*,[^1] where this deacon anathematized the heresy in which he had before been entangled; and he makes a [declaration]{g:protestacio} of his faith in these words: "And I consent to the holy Roman [church]{g:ecclesia} and the apostolic see, and with mouth and heart I profess that I hold, concerning the [sacraments]{g:sacramentum} of the Lord's table, the same faith which the lord and venerable pope Nicholas and this holy synod have delivered to be held by evangelical and apostolic authority and have confirmed to me, namely that the [bread]{g:panis} and wine which are placed on the altar are after the consecration not only a sacrament but also the true body and blood of our Lord Jesus Christ, [26] [and that] [sensually]{g:sensualiter},[^2] not only [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} but in truth, they are handled and broken by the hands of the priests and ground by the teeth of the faithful; swearing by the holy and homoousion Trinity and by these most holy gospels of Christ. Those who come against this faith, with their dogmas and their followers, I pronounce worthy of eternal anathema." So solemn a confession cannot be understood [to mean] that the bread and wine is formally or essentially the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} and [his] blood,[^3] but [figuratively]{g:figurative} or [tropically]{g:tropice}, in the way the Apostle speaks, 1 Cor. 10:4: "And they drank of the spiritual rock that followed them: and the rock was Christ." Hence the ordinary [gloss]{g:glosa} understands by "bread and wine" the [species]{g:species} of bread and wine,[^4] in the way the same distinction speaks earlier under the authority of Gregory: "The species and the likeness are the names of those things which were before", namely of bread and wine;[^5] hence at the end of a certain mass it is prayed and said: "May thy sacraments, we beseech thee, O Lord, perfect in us what they contain, that what we now perform under the species of bread and wine we may receive in the truth of the things."[^6] There are any number of sayings of [the saints]{g:sancti} and of the church that teach that those [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} sacraments are not the body of Christ and [his] blood but their [figure]{g:figura}; and perhaps in this many are Christians in name, worse than pagans in faithfulness.[^7] For it would be a lesser evil that a man should honour as God, through the whole rest of the day, that which he first sees in the morning, than that, [as a rule]{g:regulariter}, that [accident]{g:accidens} which he sees at mass between the hands of the priest in the [consecrated host]{g:hostia-consecrata} should be [27] [really]{g:realiter} his God;[^8] and by these things there is a means of understanding similar decrees, as in distinction 95, *Ecce ego dico*, it is held thus under the authority of Jerome: "What is greater than Christ, or what can be put before his body and blood? If a [presbyter]{g:presbiter} consecrates Christ when he blesses the sacraments on the altar, ought he who does not fear to consecrate Christ not to bless the people?"[^9] And the saint seems to understand figuratively by "Christ" the consecrated host, so that the consecration by which he consecrates the host and that by which he is said to consecrate the body of Christ is the same; yet he makes the host to be [sacred]{g:sacer} at least [by occasion]{g:occasionaliter}, while he duly performs the ministry of the priest, and God appropriately completes what is his, by altering [it] supernaturally; and if one equivocates on "consecration", calling it the showing of a sacred thing, then just as a layman can bless the Trinity, so [he can] also consecrate, as a layman brings it about that the body of Christ is sacred in this place or under this sacrament and yet does not on that account consecrate the body of Christ.[^10]

## ¶2 · 27.20–27.28

> [p. 27] Est ergo vera intencio sancti doctoris quod predicare, conficere et orare sunt pociora quam benedicere populo, ecclesie vel alii ornamento; ideo sicut est periculosum tam episcopo quam layco inprudenter suspendere maiorem benediccionem in presbitero sic et minorem, per quam sine simonia populus possit melius suffragari et presbiter per laxacionem potencie promereri; ideo prelatus timeret sibi in suspensionibus et laxacionibus de iniuria undequaque.

So the true intention of the holy doctor is that preaching, [confecting]{g:conficere}[^11] and praying are weightier than blessing the people, a church, or another ornament; therefore, just as it is dangerous for a [bishop]{g:episcopus} as for a layman imprudently to [suspend]{g:suspendere} the greater blessing in a presbyter, so also the lesser, by which without [simony]{g:simonia}[^12] the people could better [aid by intercession]{g:suffragari} and the presbyter merit, by the relaxation of his [power]{g:potencia};[^13] therefore a [prelate]{g:prelatus} should fear for himself, in suspensions and relaxations, injury on every side.

## ¶3 · 27.29–28.6

> [p. 27] Sed arguitur quod sacerdos potest consecrare corpus Christi, quia potest benedicere deitati, potest [p. 28] ymmolare Filium Deo Patri et potest sanctificare Deum in corde suo (ut dicitur prima Petri IIIº, 15). Quodlibet autem istorum est plus quam consecrare corpus Christi; si ergo sacerdos habet potestatem in maius, habet potestatem et in minus, sicut dicit sanctus doctor linguarum peritissimus.

But it is argued that a [priest]{g:sacerdos} can consecrate the body of Christ, because he can bless the [Godhead]{g:deitas}, he can [28] immolate the Son to God the Father, and he can sanctify God in his heart (as is said in 1 Peter 3:15).[^14] Now each of these is more than to consecrate the body of Christ; if therefore a priest has [power]{g:potestas} for the greater, he has power also for the lesser, as the holy doctor most skilled in tongues says.[^15]

## ¶4 · 28.7–28.17

> [p. 28] Hic dicitur quod benedicere potest homo tripliciter: scilicet laudative, inprecative et effective. Laudative omnis creatura benedicit Dominum, ut patet dictum in cathalogo sanctorum. Inprecative unus orans pro alio misero, ut Christus Deus noster benedicat; effective... Talem autem equivocacionem non vidi in consecrare, cum secundum grammaticos sit dare Deo vel simul sacrare; nam in veteri testamento dederunt Deo bestias et oblaciones que dicebantur Domino postea consecrate; sed quomodo consecramus corpus Christi vel sanguinem, cum sacrare sit sacrum facere?

To this [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that a man can bless in three ways: namely [by way of praise]{g:laudative}, [by way of imprecation]{g:inprecative},[^16] and [by way of effect]{g:effective}. By way of praise every [creature]{g:creatura} blesses the Lord, as is clear from what is said in the catalogue of the saints.[^17] By way of imprecation, one praying for another, a wretched [man], as "May Christ our God bless"; by way of effect …[^18] But such an [equivocation]{g:equivocacio} I have not seen in "consecrate", since according to the grammarians it is to give to God, or to [hallow]{g:sacrare} together;[^19] for in the old testament they gave to God beasts and oblations, which were afterwards said to be consecrated to the Lord; but how do we consecrate the body of Christ or [his] blood, since to hallow is to make sacred?

## ¶5 · 28.18–28.21

> [p. 28] Quantum ad secundum, dicitur quod ymmolatur sumitur equivoce pro sacrificare corporaliter et pro meritoria accione, ut verbum Dei pro aliquo interpellet; et sic non sumitur sacrare activum.

As to the second, the answer is that "is immolated" [is taken]{g:sumitur-pro} [equivocally]{g:equivoce} for sacrificing [bodily]{g:corporaliter} and for a meritorious action, as that the Word of God should intercede for someone;[^20] and in this way "hallow" is not taken in the active [sense].

## ¶6 · 28.22–29.9

> [p. 28] Quantum ad tercium, dicitur quod sanctificare est iterum equivocum ad facere sanctum et ad sancte colendum, ut Deus sanctificat se in homine, quando facit se sancte coli per eum; quod homo potest facere sed non corpus Domini consecrare. Nec movet sentencia beati Jeronimi ut sic equivocetur in consecrare, cum ipse videtur loqui de consecracione in signo que terminatur ad sacramentum et non ad [p. 29] Dominum, nisi dicatur quod sacerdos facit Dominum quia in signo. Equivocaciones autem que excludunt contradiccionem oportet loquentes in ista materia diligenter attendere, quia sicut conceditur quod sacerdos conficit corpus Christi, sic concederetur quod ista hostia sit corpus Christi quam loycam propter periculum est bonum suspendere et equivocacionem detegere. Et patet quod hoc sacramentum est forma panis et vini et non Christus vel pars eius.

As to the third, the answer is that "sanctify" is again equivocal, to making holy and to worshipping in a holy way,[^21] as God sanctifies himself in a man when he makes himself to be worshipped in a holy way by him; which a man can do, but not consecrate the [body of the Lord]{g:corpus-domini}. Nor does the [teaching]{g:sentencia}[^22] of blessed Jerome move [us] to equivocate thus on "consecrate", since he seems to speak of consecration in the [sign]{g:signum}, which is terminated at the sacrament and not at [29] the Lord, unless it be said that the priest makes the Lord because [he makes him] in the sign.[^23] And those who speak in this matter must carefully attend to the equivocations that exclude contradiction, because just as it is granted that the priest confects the body of Christ, so it would be granted that this [host]{g:hostia} is the body of Christ; which [logic]{g:loyca}, because of the danger, it is good to suspend, and to [expose]{g:detegere} the equivocation. And it is clear that this sacrament is the form of bread and wine, and not Christ or a part of him.[^24]

## ¶7 · 29.10–29.24

> [p. 29] Et quamvis omnes christiani teneantur cognoscere istam sentenciam, specialiter tamen theologi et professores legum ecclesie; leges enim ipsorum quas oportet eos defendere sunt plene ista sentencia, ut specialiter patet IIIº Decretalium, De Celebracione Missarum, Cum Marthe. Leges, inquam, iste vel sunt abrogande tanquam heretice vel defendende et docende tanquam katholice, et eo diligencius quo supra alias leges sunt de pane celesti lucrando necessarie; ideo nota infidelitatis est dicere quod ille sunt tacende et alie leges licium declarande. Dicunt enim quod sacramentum tantum et non pars Christi est hostia consecrata sed Christus insensibiliter absconsus est sub illa.

And although all Christians are bound to know this teaching, yet especially theologians and professors of [the laws of the church]{g:leges-ecclesie}; for their laws, which they must defend, are fully this teaching, as is especially clear in the third [book] of the Decretals, *De celebratione missarum*, *Cum Marthe*.[^25] These laws, I say, are either to be abrogated as heretical or to be defended and taught as catholic, and the more diligently as, above other laws, they are necessary for gaining the heavenly bread; therefore it is a mark of [unbelief]{g:infidelitas} to say that those are to be passed over in silence and the other laws, of lawsuits, to be [made clear]{g:declarare}. For they say that the consecrated host is [a sacrament only]{g:sacramentum-tantum}, and not a part of Christ, but Christ is imperceptibly hidden under it.[^26]

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** Gratian, *De cons.* D. 2 c. 42 (*Ego Berengarius*), the oath of Berengar of Tours at the Roman synod under Nicholas II; Loserth's reference, the modern form ours (LEDGER K01e-1). Wyclif spells the name *Beringarius*; the decree keeps its title.

[^2]: **T/C.** *Sensualiter*, Berengar's word in the oath: rendered "sensually", kept apart from *sensibiliter* "perceptibly". The print has no *et* before it; C reads *et sensualiter* (26.1), which supplies the link given here in brackets. The oath's *sacramento* stands here as *sacramentaliter*; at 30.24 Wyclif's second citation has *sacramentum*.

[^3]: **C.** *Panis et vinum sit*: the verb is singular, bread and wine taken as one subject; kept as "is". Wyclif sets *formaliter vel essencialiter* against *figurative vel tropice*: the mode of the predication, not its terms, is in question.

[^4]: **S.** The ordinary gloss on *De cons.* D. 2 c. 42 (*ad vocem* "Panem"): bread "that is, the species of bread and wine", which is "a sacrament only"; Loserth's reference (LEDGER K01e-2).

[^5]: **C/S.** *Species et similitudo earum rerum vocabula sunt que ante fuerant*: the species and likeness keep the names of the things they were. The Gregory text, with the prayer it quotes, is *De cons.* D. 2 c. 34, inscribed *Gregorius in omelia paschali* (Loserth; Friedberg), the form ours (LEDGER K01e-3).

[^6]: **S.** The postcommunion of the Ember Saturday of September, as Loserth gives it: *Perficiant in nobis, Domine, quaesumus, tua sacramenta quod continent: ut, quae nunc specie gerimus, rerum veritate capiamus*; Wyclif's *sub specie panis et vini* expands the prayer's *specie* (LEDGER K01e-4).

[^7]: **T.** *In fidelitate*, two words in the print. Read as one (*infidelitate*) it would give "worse than pagans in unbelief"; the print is translated and the doubt left open.

[^8]: **C.** The comparison is elliptical: a lesser evil to honour as God the first thing one sees in the morning than that the accident seen at mass "should be really his God", that is, that he should hold it so. *Regulariter* may also be "as a rule [of the church]"; the plain sense is kept.

[^9]: **S/T.** Gratian, *Decretum* I D. 95 c. 6 (*Ecce ego dico*), under Jerome's name; Loserth's reference, the form ours (LEDGER K01e-5). ABCE read *XXXIII*, D *XCIII*, for the print's *XCV* (27.2). The apparatus at 27.7 reports *promeruit* in the manuscripts, apparently for *metuit*; the print is translated.

[^10]: **C.** Two senses of *consecrare* are set out: making a thing sacred (which only the priest's ministry does to the host, and only by occasion, God completing it) and, equivocally, showing a sacred thing (which a layman can do, as he can "bless" the Trinity). Wyclif refuses the second sense for the body of Christ, which is already holy.

[^11]: **G.** *Conficere* is rendered "confect" throughout this chunk, as the GLOSSARY row has it beside *consecrare*: at 27.20 *conficere* glosses Jerome's *consecrat*, and the argument of 28.7–29.9 sets the two verbs against each other (the priest "confects" the body of Christ, 29.5, but cannot "consecrate" it).

[^12]: **T.** *Simonia* is C's correction; AB read *sine summo*, DE *sine synonimo* (27.25). Loserth prints C's word.

[^13]: **C.** *Potencia* (CONTEXTUAL): the presbyter's power of blessing, "power", not Aristotle's potency. *Laxacio* is the relaxing of a suspension; untagged.

[^14]: **S.** 1 Pet. 3:15 (DR "But sanctify the Lord Christ in your hearts"): Wyclif gives the reference, not the words.

[^15]: **S.** Jerome, as at 27.2 (Loserth: "Vide supra pag. 27, l. 2"). The argument *a maiori* (whoever can do the greater can do the lesser) is put in his mouth from the *Ecce ego dico* canon.

[^16]: **C.** *Inprecative*: praying a blessing upon another, the old sense of "imprecation", not cursing. ABDE read *imprecacione* (28.8).

[^17]: **S.** *In cathalogo sanctorum* is not identified by Loserth. The Benedicite (Dan. 3:57, "All ye works of the Lord, bless the Lord") fits *omnis creatura benedicit Dominum*; a conjecture: no source is traced, and Wyclif elsewhere (*De civili dominio* II) uses *cathalogus sanctorum* for the roll of the saints (LEDGER K01e-6).

[^18]: **T.** After *effective* the print breaks off; Loserth: "hic aliquot verba excidisse apparet" (some words seem to have fallen out). The third member is left unexplained. C omits *talem* in the next clause; A corrects *tamen* to *talem*.

[^19]: **C.** The grammarians' etymology: *con-secrare* as "give to God" or *simul sacrare*, "hallow together". *Sacer* is rendered "sacred" and *sanctus* "holy" throughout, to keep *sacrare* and *sanctificare* (28.22) apart.

[^20]: **C.** *Ut verbum Dei pro aliquo interpellet* is read of Christ the Word interceding (cf. Rom. 8:34; Heb. 7:25), immolation as a meritorious action; it may also be the spoken word of God in the priest's prayer.

[^21]: **T.** ABCE read *sacrificare est* (28.22), D alone the print's *sanctificare*; Loserth's text answers the third argument (*sanctificare Deum in corde suo*, 28.1), which the four manuscripts would not.

[^22]: **C.** *Sentencia* (CONTEXTUAL): Jerome's position taught, "teaching", not a judicial sentence. Loserth refers *beati Jeronimi* to the letter *ad Hedibiam* (Opp. II, 824) (LEDGER K01e-7).

[^23]: **C.** *Terminatur ad sacramentum*: the consecration ends in the sign, the host, and not in the Lord; the priest "makes the Lord" only as he makes the sign of him.

[^24]: **C.** *Forma panis et vini*: the form of bread and wine, against the Thomist and Scotist accounts of ch. 2; the proposition is kept in Wyclif's order, *hoc sacramentum* the subject.

[^25]: **S.** *Liber Extra* (Decretals of Gregory IX) 3.41.6, *Cum Marthe*, under the title *De celebratione missarum*; Loserth's reference, the form ours (LEDGER K01e-8).

[^26]: **C.** *Dicunt enim*: the subject is read as the laws just named (*leges … ille*), which say it; it may be their defenders. *Sacramentum tantum*: "a sacrament only", the scholastic "sacrament alone" against the reality of the sacrament. D reads *invisibiliter absconditus*, E *est absconsus* (29.22).
