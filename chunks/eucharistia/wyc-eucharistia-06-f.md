# *De eucharistia*, chapter 6, part 6: the sacrament as occasion of grace, and the third objection, that heretics must be killed

## ¶1 · 178.31–179.3

> [p. 178] Secundo per se dantur gracie ad cavendum a malo et innitendum bono; et tercio datur bonum perseverancie [p. 179] ad malum ingratitudinis fugiendum et bonum virtutis finaliter amplectendum; et indigna accepcio facit omnino oppositum.

Secondly, [of itself]{g:per-se} graces are given for guarding against evil and leaning upon the good;[^1] and thirdly the good of perseverance is given [179] for fleeing the evil of [ingratitude]{g:ingratitudo} and finally embracing the good of virtue; and unworthy reception does altogether the opposite.

## ¶2 · 179.4–179.31

> [p. 179] Videtur autem quod pregravatus eciam mortali potest in casu dolere de crimine et, assistente sancto ac devoto proposito faciendi in memoriam Christi hanc celebritatem sacramenti, ut animus contritus devocius et attencius serviat Deo suo. Potest enim hoc sacramentum occasionaliter delere tam veniale peccatum quam mortale et sic penas tollere ac gracias et virtutes inducere, non quod ipsum habeat graciam in se formaliter vel efficienciam intrinsecam virtutum, sed est occasio et obiectum anime ut memorando gesta Christi per eius efficaciorem influenciam acquirat copiosius actus et habitus virtuales, sic quod oportet quod omnis virtus huius sacramenti primo procedat a Deo, secundo ab homine et tercio occasione obiectiva a sacramento, sicut sanitas corporalis fit primo a Deo communiter influente, secundo a virtute regitiva anime et tercio obiective et quodammodo effective a corporali medicamine: sic est quodammodo in sacramenti suscepcione. Nam sicut non prodest corporalis medicina, si vis anime sanativa defuerit, sic est de sacramenti suscepcione. Unde sicut medicina non prodest lapidi, tumido fluxui vel membro sicco in calore et virtute assimilativa claudicanti, sic in parte ymaginandum est de medicina sacramentali quoad infidelem ut lapis; quoad lubricum et superbum ut corpus plectoratum et repletum; et quoad mundialem avaricia in devocione siccatum et calore ignis celestis per frigus terrenorum mortificans destitutum.

And it seems that one weighed down even by mortal [sin] can in a given case grieve for the crime and, with a holy and devout purpose assisting of performing this celebration of the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} in memory of Christ, so that the contrite mind may serve its God more devoutly and attentively.[^2] For this sacrament can [by occasion]{g:occasionaliter} blot out venial sin as well as mortal, and so take away penalties and bring in graces and virtues: not that it has grace in itself formally, or an intrinsic efficiency of virtues, but it is the occasion and object for the soul, so that by remembering the deeds of Christ it may, through his more efficacious influence, acquire more copiously virtuous acts and habits; so that all the power of this sacrament must proceed first from God, secondly from man, and thirdly from the sacrament by objective occasion,[^3] just as bodily health is brought about first by God [flowing in]{g:influere} commonly, secondly by the ruling power of the soul, and thirdly objectively and in a certain way effectively by bodily medicine: so it is in a certain way in the [receiving]{g:suscepcio} of the sacrament. For just as bodily medicine does not profit if the healing power of the soul is lacking, so it is with the receiving of the sacrament. Hence just as medicine does not profit a stone, a swollen flux, or a limb dry in its heat and halting in its assimilative power, so in part one must picture the sacramental medicine: as to the [unbeliever]{g:infidelis}, as a stone; as to the lewd and proud, as a body plethoric and replete; and as to the [worldly man]{g:mundialis}, as [a limb] dried up in devotion by avarice and destitute of the heat of the heavenly fire through the deadening cold of earthly things.[^4]

## ¶3 · 179.32–180.8

> [p. 179] Et utinam nimis false colentes hoc sacramentum ut signum, reliquias sanctorum et peregrinaciones pro [p. 180] indulgenciis clare attenderent istam sentenciam; tunc enim summe ponderarent insensibile signatum et quodcunque signum sensibile colendo respicerent proporcionaliter ut elevat animum ad Deum et invisibilia devocius attendendum. Conclusiones autem fideles sequentes ex isto principio fidei publicet cordatus theologus qui pro veritate fidei et correccione erroris ecclesie vult pati obprobria et occidi.

And would that those who [exceedingly]{g:nimis} falsely worship this sacrament as a [sign]{g:signum}, the relics of the saints, and pilgrimages for [180] [indulgences]{g:indulgencie}, would clearly attend to this [teaching]{g:sentencia};[^5] for then they would weigh most highly the [imperceptible]{g:insensibilis} [thing signified]{g:signatum}, and in worshipping any [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} sign whatever they would regard it in proportion as it raises the mind to God and to attending more devoutly to invisible things. And let a [stout-hearted]{g:cordatus} theologian publish the faithful conclusions that follow from this principle of faith, one who for the truth of the faith and the correction of the error of the [church]{g:ecclesia} is willing to suffer reproaches and to be killed.[^6]

## ¶4 · 180.9–180.27

> [p. 180] Ad quantum autem Deus iuvat efficaciter cultorem propter signa talia inanimata, non est nostrum discutere, sed credo quod proporcionaliter ut placet Deo dignitatem hominis vel eius meritum et sic donum proprium coronare. Et istam sentenciam pertractarent doctores antequam tantum difficultarent ecclesiam, quomodo hoc sacramentum in comparacione ad reliqua tollit peccata et dat graciam tam vivis quam mortuis; hec enim facit ut ipse persone sunt digne divino suffragio vel ut sic discamus quod non sit aliud nomen sub divinitate preter Christum in quo oportet nos salvos fieri, et quod ipse pater apud quem non est personarum accepcio omnem gentem que confidit in ipso et addit opera meritoria vult salvare. Fragilitas itaque indisponit hominem sed non per se sed inordinata affeccio, sicut nihil per se disponit hominem ad beatitudinem nisi Deus efficaciter et virtus formaliter; sed alia per accidens iuvant de quanto promovencia sunt ad ista.

But how far God efficaciously helps the [worshipper]{g:cultor} on account of such inanimate signs is not ours to discuss; but I believe [that he does so] in proportion as it pleases God to crown the dignity of the man, or his merit, and so his own gift. And the doctors should treat this teaching thoroughly before they troubled the church so much [over] how this sacrament, in comparison with the rest, takes away sins and gives grace to the living as well as to the dead; for it does these things according as the persons themselves are worthy of divine [aid]{g:suffragium}, or so that we may thus learn that there is no other name under the [divinity]{g:divinitas} besides Christ in which we must be saved,[^7] and that the Father himself, with whom there is no [respect of persons]{g:accepcio-personarum}, wills to save every nation that trusts in him and adds meritorious works.[^8] So frailty makes a man [unfit]{g:indispositus}, but not of itself; rather inordinate affection does, just as nothing of itself disposes a man to blessedness except God efficaciously and virtue formally; but other things help [by accident]{g:per-accidens} insofar as they are promotive of these.

## ¶5 · 180.28–181.3

> [p. 180] Sed tercio obicitur per hoc quod iuxta dicta omnes pertinaciter dogmatisantes inpossibile in hoc sacramento sunt expresse heretici, omnes heretici sunt [p. 181] fugiendi et finaliter occidendi: ergo tota ista secta in scola eukaristie est taliter castiganda, quod si fieret, foret nimis magna perturbacio in ecclesia.

But thirdly [it is objected, by appeal to]{g:obicitur-per} this: that according to what has been said, all who [pertinaciously]{g:pertinaciter} [teach as dogma]{g:dogmatisare} the impossible in this sacrament are expressly heretics; all heretics are [181] to be shunned and finally to be killed: therefore this whole [sect]{g:secta} in the school of the eucharist is to be chastised in such a way; and if this were done, there would be an exceedingly great disturbance in the church.[^9]

## ¶6 · 181.4–182.8

> [p. 181] Hic videtur sentenciam istam moderatam esse katholicam et notandam; nam fides Christi docet katholicum plus attendere ad nutritiva ac edificatoria ad salutem anime quam corporis. Aliter enim foret infideli deterior, sed coquus parans venenum domino vel aliter prodens ipsum aut regnum debet post noticiam tractari ut proditor: ergo multo magis proditores anime severius sunt tractandi. Prelati enim et clerici sunt nedum coqui regi et regno sed mediatores inter Deum et ipsos, oblaciones populi fideliter offerentes; in quo si defuerint, ubi maior prodicio? Dicitur autem nostros mundo divites se ipsos et plebem prodere, cum ordinant tales curatos inter Deum et populum mediare qui maxime elongantur a Christi conversacione, maxime cecantur in evangelica veritate et maxime dissimilantur apostolis in conversacione. Quomodo, inquam, non tales et omnes eis consentanei pararent subditis cibum toxicum, procurarent proditorie vindictam apud Deum et tardarent volentes stricto itinere sequi Christum? Nam predicare nesciunt, consecrare nolunt et orare fastidiunt. Quod si tales quidquam istorum faciunt ad detrimentum sui et populi, sese fallunt; quid, inquam, valet predicacio prelati qui in facto Dei oderit disciplinam? Quid valet eius celebracio, qui in conversacione est tante Christo contrarius, ut puta in superflua familia ecclesie pauperum maxime onerosus, in delicata mensa non pauperum sed discolorum maxime sumptuosus et in occupacione mundana constanter sollicitus? Talis, inquam, memoratur Christum sicut [p. 182] Scarioth, qui habuit ipsum in vigilanti memoria ad prodendum. Et in isto laborant divites et pauperes, sicut fratres qui vel tacent vel excitant dominos ad bellandum, obliti ad pacem consulere et salubres veritates katholicas promovere. Et presagium huius divisionis in moribus est multiplicitas opinionum in materia de sacramento altaris, scilicet inter episcopos et plebeos.

Here it seems that this teaching, moderated, is catholic and to be noted;[^10] for the faith of Christ teaches the catholic to attend more to what nourishes and builds up to the health of the soul than to that of the body. For otherwise he would be worse than an unbeliever;[^11] now a cook who prepares poison for his lord, or otherwise betrays him or the [kingdom]{g:regnum}, ought, once it is known, to be treated as a traitor: therefore much more are traitors of the soul to be treated more severely. For prelates and clerics are [not only]{g:nedum} cooks to the king and the kingdom but mediators between God and them, faithfully offering the oblations of the people; and if they fail in this, where is a greater treason? And it is said that our men rich to the world betray themselves and the common people when they ordain such [curates]{g:curatus} to mediate between God and the people as are farthest removed from the [manner of life]{g:conversacio} of Christ, most blinded in the gospel truth, and most unlike the apostles in manner of life. How, I say, would not such men, and all who consent with them, prepare poisoned food for their subjects, treacherously procure vengeance before God, and hold back those who wish to follow Christ by the strait way? For they do not know how to preach, they will not consecrate, and they disdain to pray. And if such men do any of these things, to the detriment of themselves and of the people, they deceive themselves; what, I say, is the preaching of a prelate worth who in the deed of God hates discipline?[^12] What is his celebrating worth, who in manner of life is so contrary to Christ: for instance, most burdensome to the church of the poor in a superfluous [household]{g:familia}, most lavish at a dainty table, not for the poor but for the [froward]{g:discoli}, and constantly busy in worldly occupation? Such a man, I say, remembers Christ as [182] Scarioth did, who had him in watchful memory in order to betray him. And in this the rich and the poor labour, as do the friars, who either keep silent or stir up the lords to war, forgetting to counsel peace and to promote wholesome catholic truths.[^13] And the presage of this division in morals is the multiplicity of [opinions]{g:opinio} in the matter of the sacrament of the altar, namely between bishops and common folk.

## ¶7 · 182.9–182.27

> [p. 182] Unde episcopi nostri et sue ecclesie in ista materia multipliciter variantur cuius variacionis causa est vacillacio magistrorum; secundum enim quod pedagogus prelatum docuerit ita credet; et est verisimile quod episcopi apponent diligenciam ad istum articulum cognoscendum. Aliter enim adorarent quod nesciunt, et ipsimet cece caderent in profundum fovee ydolatrie et ecclesiam suam traherent post ipsos precipitem, cum regulariter consequitur grex pastorem; et iterum non tot forent scripta de isto ut fidei articulo, nisi ecclesia et specialiter episcopi obligati ad instruendum ecclesiam suam in fide supereminenter debeant istud scire. Turpe quidem foret quod nostri episcopi scandalizarentur in isto quod bene cognoscunt ymagines auri et argenti sed ignorant generaliter quidditatem sacramenti altaris, cum pro libro sic palpant figuras pecunie et per hoc sacramentum quod infinitum dotacionem temporalem sui episcopatus exsuperat emunt a sapiencia odiente ignoranciam regnum celi.

Hence our [bishops]{g:episcopus} and their churches vary in many ways in this matter, and the cause of this variation is the wavering of the masters; for as the tutor has taught the prelate, so he will believe; and it is likely that the bishops will apply diligence to knowing this article. For otherwise they would adore what they do not know,[^14] and they themselves would fall blind into the depth of the pit of idolatry and drag their church headlong after them, since [as a rule]{g:regulariter} the flock follows the shepherd; and again, there would not be so many writings about this as an [article of faith]{g:articulus-fidei}, unless the church, and especially the bishops, who are bound to instruct their church in the faith, ought pre-eminently to know it. It would indeed be shameful that our bishops should be scandalized in this: that they know well images of gold and silver but are generally [ignorant of]{g:ignorare} the quiddity of the sacrament of the altar, since in place of a book they thus finger the figures of money, and through this sacrament, which infinitely exceeds the temporal [endowment]{g:dotacio} of their bishopric, they buy the kingdom of heaven from wisdom, which hates ignorance.[^15]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 179.4: It may be the occasion of destroying even mortal sin, although it has no such power in itself.
- 179.32: Sign-worshippers; astray also as to relics and pilgrimages.
- 180.9: How far God makes outward signs profitable to the worshipper depends on His good pleasure.
- 180.28: We are accused of wishing to persecute and slay all our opponents.
- 181.4: It is true that false clergy are traitors of the worst kind.
- 182.9: Surely our bishops must strive to know the true nature of the sacrament.
- 182.21: They know perfectly the forms of money although that is of little importance.

## Notes

[^1]: **T.** At 178.31 ABDE read *Sed non per se* ("but not of itself") for the print's *Secundo per se*; translated the print, which continues the count (*secundo … tercio*) begun in the preceding chunk.

[^2]: **C/T.** The period does not close: *potest … dolere de crimine et, assistente … proposito …, ut animus … serviat* has no second verb after *et*, so the purpose clause hangs. A word (e.g. "approach" or "receive") may have been lost at 179.5–7; translated as printed. D reads *et devocius* at 179.7–8.

[^3]: **C.** *Virtus* here is "power" (of the sacrament, of the soul's ruling faculty), and "virtues" only of the moral habits the sacrament occasions (*gracias et virtutes*, *habitus virtuales*), by the split of the GLOSSARY row. The threefold order (God, man, the sacrament as object) is the one the medical figure then repeats.

[^4]: **C/T.** *Avaricia* is Loserth's reading; the manuscripts all have *avariciam* (179.29), which does not construe. *Mortificans* agrees with *frigus* (neuter accusative after *per*), "the deadening cold"; the supplied "[a limb]" takes up *membro sicco* of the figure. At 179.25 B lacks *tumido* (a gap in the manuscript); at 179.26 B reads *claudicandi*.

[^5]: **C.** *Sentencia* (CONTEXTUAL): the position taught, "teaching", here and at 180.10 and 181.4.

[^6]: **C.** *Cordatus*: "stout-hearted"; the classical sense "wise, judicious" is possible, but the clause that follows (willing to suffer reproach and death) leans to courage.

[^7]: **S.** Acts 4:12, given in indirect speech; the Vulgate has *sub caelo* ("under heaven"), Wyclif *sub divinitate*. Not quoted.

[^8]: **S/T.** Col. 3:25 (DR "there is no respect of persons with God"); *omnem gentem* recalls Acts 10:34–35 ("in every nation, he that feareth him … is acceptable to him"). All the manuscripts read *ipse puer*; *pater* is C's correction in the margin by another hand (180.20), which Loserth prints.

[^9]: **C.** The objection's minor premiss (*omnes heretici sunt fugiendi et finaliter occidendi*) stands without a connective. *Quod si fieret* is taken as a relative linking the consequence to *castiganda*.

[^10]: **C.** *Sentenciam istam moderatam*: the objection's teaching that heretics are to be punished, taken in a moderated form; the answer then argues from false clergy as traitors.

[^11]: **S.** 1 Tim. 5:8 (DR "is worse than an infidel"), in Wyclif's own construction.

[^12]: **S.** Ps. 49:17 (DR "Seeing thou hast hated discipline"), worked into the question.

[^13]: **T.** At 182.3 AB read *tunc fratres*, D *non fratres*, E *et fratres*; C has *ut fratres*, corrected to *sicut fratres*, which Loserth prints.

[^14]: **S.** John 4:22 (DR "You adore that which you know not"); the blind falling into the pit recalls Matt. 15:14 (DR "both fall into the pit").

[^15]: **C/T.** Translated as printed: the bishops "buy" the kingdom of heaven "through this sacrament", i.e. what they could buy with the knowledge they neglect; the period is compressed and may be ironic. Loserth notes at 182.24 that D, "as it seems, more rightly", reads *cum per hoc sic*; the plate foot is needed to see which words D's reading replaces. At 182.27 AB read *regni*, C corrects to *regnu* (sic).
