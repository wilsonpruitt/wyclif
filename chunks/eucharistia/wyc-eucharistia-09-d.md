# *De eucharistia*, chapter 9, part 4: the councils before Lanfranc, and the earlier doctors against the later

## ¶1 · 285.1–285.19

> [p. 285] Et quantum ad tercium credo quod ante Lanfrancum et Wimundum non erant aliqua decreta ecclesie contraria huic sentencie. Unde quarta synodus Ephesina, que statim post mortem beati Augustini tempore Celestini pape celebrata est, dampnat unam stulticiam hereticam que dicit quod non vera Christi caro vivificata sed quelibet caro unius iusti virtute verborum sacramenti sit ibi vel terminus conversionis, et vel hoc vel aliud ibi dictum ad inferendum proposicionem. Nec obest quod Gregorius in epistola ad patriarchas acceptat hec quatuor consilia ut quatuor evangelia, quia nullum eorum contradicit isti sentencie. Unde creditur religiosos qui nimis magnificant sua signa introduxisse illum errorem in ecclesiam in penam peccati prioris. In cuius signum Augustinus et alii patres in quibus viguit sapiencia vel asseruerunt expresse oppositum et negant hoc tanquam hereticum quia inpossibile, vel tacuerunt illud tanquam inauditam stulticiam in quam credebant nullos sane mentis prorumpere.

And as to the third, I believe that before Lanfranc and Guitmund[^1] there were no [decrees]{g:decretum} of the [church]{g:ecclesia} contrary to this [teaching]{g:sentencia}.[^2] Hence the fourth synod, of Ephesus, which was held immediately after the death of blessed Augustine in the time of Pope Celestine, condemns a heretical folly which says that not the true flesh of Christ, made living, but any flesh of some just man is there by the power of the words of the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}, or is the term of the [conversion]{g:conversio}; and either this or something else said there [serves] to infer the [proposition]{g:proposicio}.[^3] Nor does it stand in the way that Gregory, in his letter to the patriarchs, accepts these four councils as the four gospels,[^4] for none of them contradicts this teaching. Hence it is believed that the [religious]{g:religiosi} who [exceedingly]{g:nimis} magnify their signs brought that error into the church in punishment of an earlier sin. In sign of which Augustine and other fathers in whom wisdom flourished either asserted the opposite expressly and deny this as heretical because impossible, or kept silent about it as an unheard-of folly into which they believed no one of sound mind would break out.

## ¶2 · 285.21–287.12

> [p. 285] Unde quando arguitur contra Lanfrancum et sectam suam in isto, sepe prorumpunt in excusaciones istas inpossibiles in peccatis. Hoc (inquiunt) salubriter credi potest, investigari non potest, nam (ut sepe dixi) una pars nimis heretice contradicit alteri, quia absolute necessario omne credibile et specialiter fides katholica potest investigari salubriter, cum beatus Augustinus sub obtentu sue beatitudinis investigavit sollicite materiam de Trinitate que est infinitum superior sacramento [p. 286] eukaristie; ideo nullum velamen hereticum posset magis cecare ecclesiam; quia, inducta quacunque novitate heretica sine fundacione scripture si doctores incipiant idem examinare sicut sancti examinarunt alios articulos fidei, statim proponitur hoc principium Machometi: Hoc investigari non potest. Et meretricantur scripture ad colorandum hoc dictum, que docent quod nemo scrutaretur inprovide sine fide et humilitate priori aliquam katholicam veritatem. Sed pro distinccione doctorum in ista materia notandum quod Sathan pater mendacii ligatus est mille annis post ascensionem Domini (ut dicitur Apok. XX°, 2). Tempore autem huius ligacionis successerunt doctores veridici, recti loyci atque philosophi conformes fidei scripture, ut patet de quatuor magnis doctoribus et multis eis similibus; et illos sanctos oportet defendere in loyca et theologia tanquam pugiles veritatis. Sed tempore solucionis patris mendacii inter sanctos doctores surrepserunt nimis multi, sed plus vel minus discipuli Antichristi plus declinantes a loyca fidei scripture ex suggestione dyaboli, et licet omnibus istis doctoribus eciam Augustino qui fuit in loyca et theologia precipuus non debemus credere nisi de quanto se fundaverit in scriptura, ut patet distinccione IX Noli meis, et capitulo Negare non possum, et capitulo Ego solum, tamen testimonium priorum longe plus valet ad fundandum argumentum topicum quam sequencium. Quotquot ergo duodene allegate fuerint contra me de secta secunda non solum non [p. 287] valent, nisi de quanto se fundaverint in scriptura, sed non valent in testimonium fidei, nisi de quanto concordaverint in se ipsis. Duodena enim admittitur in causa politica, nisi prius concordaverit cum se ipsa. Oportet ergo concordare recentes doctores cum prioribus et exemplare fidei scripture et post est eorum testimonium admittendum. Nunc autem conturbant reciproce sentencias suas; ideo infidelis foret qui gustaret aquam sic turbidam, dum a prioribus potare poterit aquam claram, nam illis existentibus nobis contrariis, plures sunt nobiscum quam cum illis, quia totus exercitus ecclesie triumphantis.

Hence when it is argued against Lanfranc and his sect on this point, they often break out into these impossible "excuses in sins":[^5] "This (they say) can wholesomely be believed; it cannot be investigated." For (as I have often said) the one part contradicts the other exceedingly heretically, since by absolute necessity everything believable, and especially the catholic faith, can wholesomely be investigated; since blessed Augustine, as he hoped for his blessedness,[^6] carefully investigated the matter of the Trinity, which is infinitely higher than the sacrament [286] of the [Eucharist]{g:eukaristia}. So no heretical veil could blind the church more; for, once any heretical novelty has been brought in without foundation in scripture, if doctors begin to examine it just as the [saints]{g:sancti} examined other [articles of faith]{g:articulus-fidei}, at once this principle of Mahomet is put forward: "This cannot be investigated." And they prostitute[^7] the scriptures to [give colour to]{g:colorare} this saying, [scriptures] which teach that no one should search improvidently, without prior faith and humility, into any catholic truth. But for the distinction of doctors in this matter it is to be noted that Satan, the father of lies, "was bound a thousand years" after the ascension of the Lord (as is said in Apoc. 20:2).[^8] And in the time of this binding there followed truthful doctors, right logicians, and philosophers conformed to the faith of scripture, as is clear of the four great doctors and many like them; and those saints must be defended in [logic]{g:loyca} and theology as champions of the truth. But in the time of the loosing of the father of lies there crept in among the holy doctors exceedingly many, but more or less disciples of Antichrist,[^9] declining further from the logic of the faith of scripture at the suggestion of the devil; and although to all these doctors, even Augustine, who was foremost in logic and theology, we ought not to give credence except in so far as he has founded himself in scripture, as is clear in distinction 9, *Noli meis*, and the chapter *Negare non possum*, and the chapter *Ego solum*,[^10] yet the testimony of the earlier is worth far more for founding a [topical argument]{g:argumentum-topicum} than that of those who follow. However many juries of twelve,[^11] then, are [alleged]{g:allegare} against me from the second sect, not only are they [287] of no worth, except in so far as they have founded themselves in scripture, but they are of no worth as testimony to the faith, except in so far as they have agreed among themselves. For a jury of twelve is [not] admitted in a civil cause unless it has first agreed with itself.[^12] The recent doctors must therefore agree with the earlier and with the exemplar of the faith of scripture,[^13] and afterwards their testimony is to be admitted. Now in fact they throw one another's teachings into confusion; so he would be an [unbeliever]{g:infidelis} who tasted water so muddied while he could drink clear water from the earlier; for, though those are contrary to us, "there are more with us than with them",[^14] since the whole army of the [church triumphant]{g:ecclesia-triumphans} [is with us].

## ¶3 · 287.13–287.19

> [p. 287] Sed quarto obicitur quod multi sancti asserebant istam sentenciam. Qui ergo sumus nos qui presumimus inducere sentenciam istam novellam contra decretum? Ymmo contra scripturam de veritate verborum sacramentalium et contra devocionem populi in hac parte; unde deficiente evidencia clamant contra nos quod ex dictis nostris devocio populi est extincta.

But fourthly [it is objected]{g:obicitur} that many saints asserted this teaching. Who then are we, who presume to bring in this newer teaching against the decree? Indeed, against scripture on the truth of the sacramental words, and against the devotion of the people in this regard;[^15] hence, for lack of [evidence]{g:evidencia}, they cry out against us that from our sayings the devotion of the people is extinguished.

## ¶4 · 287.20–288.10

> [p. 287] Ad illud sepe dixi quod illud porizoma dyaboli excecat plurimos; nam obiecta sunt nobis scripta sacra et sancti doctores prestanciores et prudenciores cum dictis suis que floruerunt in primitiva ecclesia, et in alio genere sunt oblata nobis facta, dicta et scripta novorum doctorum recenciumque qui venerunt. Et vitam, facta scriptaque priorum contempnimus, sed posteriorum sentencias et facta amplexamur. Nec dubium, cum ista pars sit infirmior et ad peccatum proclivior, quin peccati cecitas sit in causa admittere istam quod multi sancti asserebant sentenciam nobis contrariam, sicut multi tam in speculativis quam practicis [p. 288] peccaverunt sed sancciores et antiquiores tenuerunt nostram sentenciam. Quare ergo non debemus sine presumpcione humiliter sequi priores, potissime cum aliis nec ex alia evidencia pro sua sentencia asserunt raciones? Nam omnes auctores scripture qui illud sacramentum meminerant, sicut et omnes sancti doctores ecclesie usque ad tempus dotacionis et diu post eciam (si non fallor) usque ad tempus Nicholai IIⁱ qui fuit anno domini MLIX vocaverunt hoc sacramentum panem et non accidens.

To that I have often said that this apron of the devil[^16] blinds very many; for there are set before us the sacred writings and the more eminent and more prudent holy doctors, with their sayings, which flourished in the primitive church; and in another kind there are offered us the deeds, sayings and writings of the new and recent doctors who have come. And we despise the life, deeds and writings of the earlier, but embrace the teachings and deeds of the later.[^17] Nor is there doubt, since this part is the weaker and the more prone to sin, that the blindness of sin is the cause of admitting this, that many saints asserted the teaching contrary to us; just as many have sinned in speculative as well as in practical [288] matters; but the holier and more ancient held our teaching. Why, then, ought we not, without presumption, humbly follow the earlier, most of all since they put forward reasons for their teaching neither with others nor from any other evidence?[^18] For all the authors of scripture who made mention of that sacrament, as also all the holy doctors of the church up to the time of the [endowment]{g:dotacio}, and long after too (if I am not mistaken), up to the time of Nicholas II, who was in the year of the Lord 1059,[^19] called this sacrament [bread]{g:panis} and not [accident]{g:accidens}.

## Notes

[^1]: **S/T.** *Wimundum*: Guitmund, on whom Loserth refers to Fabricius, *Bibl. lat. med. et infimae aetatis* III, 162 (LEDGER K09d-1). D reads *Veymunduiis*, E *Raymundum*.

[^2]: **C.** *Sentencia*: "teaching", the position taught (CONTEXTUAL row); here the teaching Wyclif defends, that the sacrament is bread.

[^3]: **S/C.** Loserth refers to *De cons.* D. 2 c. 80 *Necessario igitur* (modern form ours) and to Harduin, *Concil. coll.* I, 1194 (LEDGER K09d-2). Wyclif counts Ephesus the "fourth" synod; Gregory's list in D. 15 c. 2, cited in the next sentence, has it third, after Nicaea and Constantinople, and c. 80 is headed only *ex Symbolo Ephesino* (LEDGER K09d-3). The last clause has no verb (*ad inferendum proposicionem*): we supply "serves"; the proposition is the one just stated, that no decree before Lanfranc was against this teaching.

[^4]: **S.** *Decretum* D. 15 c. 2 *Sicut sancti evangelii*: "as the four books of the holy gospel, so I confess that I receive and venerate the four councils" (Loserth's foot; modern form ours) (LEDGER K09d-4).

[^5]: **S.** Ps. 140:4, "to make excuses in sins" (*ad excusandas excusationes in peccatis*); *inpossibiles* is Wyclif's.

[^6]: **C.** *Sub obtentu sue beatitudinis*: taken as the formula "as he hoped to obtain his blessedness"; or "on pain of [losing] his blessedness". Either way Augustine staked his blessedness on the inquiry.

[^7]: **T.** C has *intricantur* "they entangle" in the text and *meretricantur* in the margin; the print follows the margin.

[^8]: **S.** Apoc. 20:2, "bound him for a thousand years" (*ligavit eum per annos mille*); the words in quotation marks follow Loserth's italics but are Wyclif's, and the reckoning from the ascension is his. Loserth's foot at 286.17 refers to Wyclif's *Sermones* II, sermon 61 (LEDGER K09d-5).

[^9]: **C.** *Nimis multi, sed plus vel minus discipuli Antichristi*: the *sed* qualifies the "many": doctors still, but all more or less disciples of Antichrist. ABE omit *discipuli*; C adds it in the margin in another hand.

[^10]: **S/T.** *Decretum* D. 9 c. 3 (*Noli meis*), c. 4 (*Negare non possum*), c. 5 (Loserth's foot; modern form ours) (LEDGER K09d-6). The print reads *Ego solum*; Loserth notes that the right incipit is *Ego solis*. We keep the print.

[^11]: **G.** *Duodena*: a "dozen", the jury of twelve (Loserth's side-note: "a jury of modern writers"); the later doctors are set against Wyclif like a jury.

[^12]: **T.** The print *Duodena enim admittitur … nisi prius concordaverit* does not construe: a jury is admitted only when it agrees. We supply [not] (our emendation; a *non* may be lost at 287.4). DE *poletica*.

[^13]: **C.** *Exemplare* taken as the noun after *cum* ("with the exemplar"); as the infinitive, "and copy the faith of scripture".

[^14]: **S.** 2 Kgs 6:16 (Vulgate 4 Kgs), "for there are more with us than with them" (*plures enim nobiscum sunt quam cum illis*); Wyclif's order is *plures sunt nobiscum*.

[^15]: **C.** The objector speaks in the first person (*qui sumus nos qui presumimus*), putting the charge to Wyclif; its three heads (the decree, the truth of the sacramental words, the devotion of the people) are answered in that order at 289, 291.24 and 293.33. *Ymmo* may continue the objection or open Wyclif's retort ("rather, [it is they who go] against scripture"); *clamant* is Wyclif reporting the adversaries.

[^16]: **S.** *Porizoma*, the Vulgate's *perizoma*: the aprons of fig leaves of Gen. 3:7 ("made themselves aprons").

[^17]: **C.** The first person is the church at large under the blindness just named, not Wyclif's own party.

[^18]: **C.** *Potissime cum aliis nec ex alia evidencia … asserunt raciones*: the subject is taken as the earlier doctors and *aliis* as "with others" ("neither with others nor from other evidence"); the phrase is hard. If *aliis* stands for *alii*: "since the others put forward reasons for their teaching from no other evidence".

[^19]: **S.** Nicholas II, pope January 1058 to 27 July 1061 (Loserth's foot) (LEDGER K09d-7).
