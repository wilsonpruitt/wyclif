# *De eucharistia*, chapter 5, part 10: Ambrose, the canon of the mass, and tropical speech

## ¶1 · 151.24–152.30

> [p. 151] Secundo obiciunt dogmatisantes istius secte voce rigida et robusta sed ydiotica ut terreant ydiotas. Ista sentencia, inquiunt, est contra beatum Ambrosium et decretum ecclesie, Panis est in altari. Sed non timeant fideles hos torvos vultus vel voces rigidas, [p. 152] sed attendant sentenciam. Nam beatus Ambrosius dicit ibidem primo innuitive quod panis est corpus Christi. Ista vero secta dicit hoc esse inpossibile atque hereticum. Secundo dicit beatus Ambrosius in eodem decreto illud quod erat panis ante consecracionem iam corpus Christi est post consecracionem. Hoc, inquit ista glossa, est inpossibile et hereticum, sed quantitas per se significat corpus Christi. Tercio regulant glossatores istius secte quod generaliter per panem et vinum in dictis sanctorum istius materie intelligitur sua quantitas benedicta, et sic intelligitur quod ipsa erit corpus Christi, hoc est, ydemptificabitur corpori Christi ut ipsum sacramentaliter significat. Et sic glossant Apostolum et usum ecclesie in canone misse, quando orant ut hanc oblacionem Deus multipliciter benedicat, ut fiat ecclesie corpus et sanguis domini nostri Jesu Christi; hoc est, inquiunt, ut illa quantitas benedicatur et fiat corpus Christi et sanguis ad sensum expositum, cum panis et vinum non exspectant illam benediccionem, sed virtute benediccionis prioris totaliter destruuntur et quantitas per se remanens supplet vicem eorum in benediccione in sacramentali significacione et in virtuosa operacione. Non dubito quin glossa ista sit heretica, primis sanctis doctoribus et ecclesie Christi incognita, quousque glossatores magnificantes signa in quibus ponunt stare suam religionem ut in per se differencia subintrabant; propterea enim creditur dyabolum cecare per eos populum ut ydolatranda signa plus magnificent quam signata.

Secondly, those of this [sect]{g:secta} who [teach as dogma]{g:dogmatisare} object, with a stiff and sturdy but [unlettered]{g:ydiota} voice, so as to terrify the unlettered: "This [teaching]{g:sentencia}," they say, "is against blessed Ambrose and the [decree]{g:decretum} of the [church]{g:ecclesia} *Panis est in altari*."[^1] But let the faithful not fear these grim faces or stiff voices, [152] but attend to the [sense]{g:sentencia-verborum}.[^2] For blessed Ambrose says there, first, by intimation, that "[bread]{g:panis} is the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}". But this sect says this is impossible and heretical. Secondly, blessed Ambrose says in the same decree that "what was bread before the [consecration]{g:consecracio} is now the body of Christ after the consecration". "This," says this [gloss]{g:glosa}, "is impossible and heretical; but [quantity]{g:quantitas} [by itself]{g:per-se} signifies the body of Christ."[^3] Thirdly, the glossators of this sect lay down as a rule that generally, by "bread and [wine]{g:vinum}" in the sayings of the [saints]{g:sancti} on this matter, its blessed quantity is understood; and so it is understood that it will be the body of Christ, that is, it will be [identified]{g:ydemptificare} with the body of Christ, as it [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} signifies it.[^4] And so they gloss the Apostle and the usage of the church in the canon of the mass, when they pray that God may bless "this oblation" manifoldly, "that it may become for the church the body and blood of our Lord Jesus Christ":[^5] "that is," they say, "that that quantity may be blessed and become the body and blood of Christ in the sense set out", since the bread and wine do not wait for that blessing, but by the power of the earlier blessing are wholly destroyed, and the quantity remaining by itself [supplies the place]{g:vicem-supplere} of them in the blessing, in the sacramental signification and in the powerful operation. I do not doubt that this gloss is heretical, unknown to the first holy doctors and to the church of Christ, until the glossators crept in, magnifying the [signs]{g:signum} in which they place their religion to stand as in its [differentia]{g:differencia} of itself;[^6] for on this account the devil is believed to blind the people through them, so that they magnify the signs that are to be [idolized]{g:ydolatrare} more than the things signified.[^7]

## ¶2 · 153.1–153.14

> [p. 153] Ulterius descendendo ad evidenciam qua probaretur istam sentenciam esse hereticam et scripturis sacris contrariam quia falsam, cogitavi per quod medium teneret ista argucia, et non inveni nisi forte arguatur, ut glossatur a sensu contrario yronice, ex hoc quod hec proposicio sacramentalis: Hoc est corpus meum, est verissima sacramentaliter conversiva panis in corpus Christi et ultra sacramenta legis veteris plus miraculose faciens quod ille panis sacramentaliter figuret corpus Christi; et sic est verior et pertinencior quam foret ista: Hic est corpus meum. Et iudicet fidelis vel infidelis utrum ex hoc sequitur quod sentencia ista sit heretica; manifeste, inquam, patet contrarium.

Going down further to the [evidence]{g:evidencia} by which it would be proved that this teaching is heretical and contrary to the holy scriptures because false, I have considered by what [middle term]{g:medium} this [quibble]{g:argucia} would hold, and I have found none, unless perhaps it be argued, as it is glossed [ironically]{g:yronice} from the contrary sense, from this: that this sacramental [proposition]{g:proposicio}, "This is my body", is most true, sacramentally [converting]{g:conversivus} the bread into the body of Christ, and, beyond the sacraments of the old law, more miraculously making that bread sacramentally [figure]{g:figurare} the body of Christ; and so it is truer and [more fitting]{g:pertinencior} than this would be: "This [one] is my body".[^8] And let believer or [unbeliever]{g:infidelis} judge whether from this [it follows]{g:sequitur} that this teaching is heretical: manifestly, I say, the contrary is clear.

## ¶3 · 153.15–153.32

> [p. 153] Sed replicatur per hoc quod responsio ista implicat locucionem istam esse tropicam: Hoc est corpus meum, quod est hereticum, cum tunc foret falsa de virtute sermonis. Sed stulti sic arguentes obliti racionis argumenti vel consequencie, ideo oportet acute respondere illis iuxta suam stulticiam, negando arguciam tam in materia quam in forma. Locucio autem tropica est verissima, summe katholica et miraculosissime conversiva. Nec est color ex istis concludere quemlibet talem modum loquendi per locum a simili vel quod quidditas aut natura panis et vini corrumpitur pocius quam si peccator convertitur in iustum, ergo natura illa destruitur. Sic, inquam, natura panis melioratur per benediccionem, quia post nudum esse naturale habet superadditum esse sacramentale, ut efficaciter figuret et faciat verum corpus Christi ad quemlibet eius punctum, et sic vere accipit sed tropice predicacionem corporis.

But [it is rejoined]{g:replicatur}, on the ground that this answer implies that this speech is tropical, "This is my body", which is heretical, since then it would be false [in the proper sense of the words]{g:de-virtute-sermonis}. But the fools who argue so have forgotten the nature of an argument or [inference]{g:consequencia};[^9] so one must answer them sharply according to their folly,[^10] denying the quibble both in matter and in form. But [tropical speech]{g:locucio-tropica} is most true, supremely catholic, and most miraculously converting. Nor is there [colour]{g:color}[^11] to [infer]{g:concludere} from these [premisses] any such manner of speaking [by the topic from likeness]{g:a-simili}, or that the quiddity or [nature]{g:natura} of bread and wine is corrupted, any more than "if a sinner is converted into a just man, therefore that nature is destroyed".[^12] So, I say, the nature of the bread is bettered by the blessing, because after its bare natural [being]{g:esse} it has [sacramental being]{g:esse-sacramentale} added over it, so that it efficaciously figures and makes the true body of Christ at every point of it; and so it [truly]{g:vere}, but [tropically]{g:tropice}, receives the predication of the body.[^13]

## ¶4 · 154.1–154.8

> [p. 154] Sed tercio replicatur per hoc quod doctores aliter intelligunt scripturam sacram, sanctos doctores et canones ecclesie, ut puta quod tota substancia panis et vini secundum se totam corrumpitur et remanent solum accidencia, de quibus solum locuntur auctoritates predicte, quando faciunt mencionem de sacramento altaris, cum aliter foret talis locucio tropica inpossibilis et heretica.

But thirdly it is rejoined, on the ground that the doctors understand holy scripture, the holy doctors and the canons of the church otherwise: for instance, that the whole [substance]{g:substancia} of bread and wine is corrupted in its whole self and only the [accidents]{g:accidens} remain, of which alone the aforesaid authorities speak when they make mention of the [sacrament of the altar]{g:sacramentum-altaris}; since otherwise such tropical speech would be impossible and heretical.[^14]

## ¶5 · 154.9–154.27

> [p. 154] Hic firmiter ut fidem teneo quod tam scriptura sacra quam ecclesia sancta intellexit per panem et vinum ipsa corpora ex materia et forma substanciali composita et nec quantitatem per se nec qualitatem nec alium fasciculum accidentis. In cuius signum scriptura sacra et sancti doctores antequam subintroierunt baptiste signorum cecantes ecclesiam vocaverunt ipsa panem et amiserant illum sensum. Ambrosius eciam vocat illud remanens panem propterea usitatum, Augustinus eciam propterea exponit quomodo panis ille est tam panis quam sacramentum; panis, ut in De Consecracione, distinccione IIᵃ, Panis est, et capitulo Hoc est. Et patet quod dolendum est doctores sic intelligere. Unde cavendum est dogma eorum, dum dicunt Apostolum I. Cor. Xᵒ, 16 intelligere per panem quem frangimus istam maneriem accidentis, et sic de illa oblacione quam meminimus in canone misse, quod erit talis maneries accidentis, et sic de glosa sanctorum ac decreto ecclesie.

Here I firmly hold as faith that both holy scripture and holy church understood by "bread and wine" the bodies themselves, composed of matter and [substantial form]{g:forma-substancialis}, and neither quantity by itself nor [quality]{g:qualitas} nor any other [bundle of accident]{g:fasciculus-accidentis}. In token of this, holy scripture and the holy doctors, before the [baptizers of the signs]{g:baptiste-signorum} crept in, blinding the church, called them bread; and [afterwards] they lost that [sense]{g:sensus}.[^15] Ambrose also calls that which remains "bread", on that account "usual [bread]"; Augustine also on that account expounds how that bread is both bread and sacrament: bread, as in *De consecratione*, distinction 2, *Panis est*, and the chapter *Hoc est*.[^16] And it is clear that it is to be lamented that doctors understand so. Hence their dogma is to be guarded against, when they say that the Apostle, 1 Cor. 10:16, understands by "the bread which we break" that [kind]{g:maneries} of accident;[^17] and so of that oblation which we recall in the canon of the mass, that it will be such a kind of accident; and so of the gloss of the saints and the decree of the church.

## ¶6 · 154.28–155.7

> [p. 154] In cuius signum omnes dicti falsigraphi erubescunt specificare in istis speciem accidentis, ymmo vident quod pertinencius diceretur panem et vinum esse corpus Christi et sanguinem ad sensum tropicum [p. 155] quam fictum ydolum accidentis. Vident eciam ex textu Pauli et Luce, ubi in ista eadem materia dicunt calicem esse novum testamentum quod oportet omnes concedere illam proposicionem esse tropicam sine falsitate vel heresi. Nec sciunt istam ordinacionem Spiritus Sancti detegere quin per idem alie proposiciones sacramentales sunt tropice modis suis.

In token of this, all the said [false writers]{g:falsigraphus} blush to specify in these the species of the accident;[^18] indeed, they see that it would be more fittingly said that "bread and wine are the body and blood of Christ" in the tropical sense [155] than [that they are] a feigned idol of accident.[^19] They see also from the text of Paul and of Luke, where in this same matter they say that "the chalice is the new testament",[^20] that all must grant that proposition to be tropical without falsity or heresy. Nor do they know how to [expose]{g:detegere} this [ordinance]{g:ordinacio} of the Holy Spirit without [its following that], by the same token, the other sacramental propositions are tropical in their own ways.

## ¶7 · 155.8–155.15

> [p. 155] Sed finaliter arguitur sequi ex dictis quod omnes pertinaciter defendentes sentenciam contra quam arguitur forent heretici, et cum per multa tempora papa et quasi tota ecclesia et specialiter scola theologorum et capitula religiosorum constanter defendit illud ut articulum fidei (ut patet ex scriptis editis in ista materia), sequitur quod omnes illi forent heretici, quod foret temerarium sustinere.

But finally it is argued that it follows from what has been said that all who obstinately defend the teaching against which the argument is made would be heretics; and since for many times the [pope]{g:papa} and almost the whole church, and especially the school of theologians and the chapters of [the religious]{g:religiosi}, have constantly defended it as an [article of faith]{g:articulus-fidei} (as is clear from the writings published on this matter),[^21] it follows that all those would be heretics, which it would be rash to maintain.

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 151.24: Ambrose is really on our side.
- 152.8: Our opponents say that not the bread but the quality becomes Christ's body.
- 153.15: An expression is not untrue because it is tropical.
- 154.1: It is falsely alleged that 'bread' means the accidents of bread.
- 154.28: We are not informed what kind of accident it is.
- 155.8: It is said that we hereticate numerous popes and doctors.

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** *De cons.* D. 2 c. 55 *Panis est in altari*, Ambrose (Loserth's reference at 151.14, 27; the form of the reference is ours; LEDGER K05j-1). **C.** *Sentencia* "teaching": Wyclif's position that the bread remains.

[^2]: **C.** *Attendant sentenciam*: here the sense of the decree's words, the row *sentencia (verborum)* "sense", against the opponents' manner of voice.

[^3]: **C.** *Ista glossa*: the opponents' gloss on the decree, quoted as speaking. *Quantitas per se*: quantity existing apart from a subject, the spatial sense of *per se*. D reads *signat* (152.13–14).

[^4]: **C.** *Ut ipsum sacramentaliter significat*: "as" is the likelier sense of *ut* here; it can also be read "so that it sacramentally signifies it". E reads *corpus domini* twice (152.16, 18).

[^5]: **S.** The canon of the mass, *Quam oblationem*; Loserth prints the Sarum text (calling it the Breviary; it is the canon of the Sarum Missal, ed. Dickinson, col. 616), *Quam oblationem tu Deus omnipotens in omnibus quaesumus benedictam … facere digneris, ut nobis corpus et sanguis fiat dilectissimi Filii tui domini nostri Jesu Christi* (LEDGER K05j-2). Wyclif paraphrases (*multipliciter* for *in omnibus*, *ecclesie* for *nobis*); the quotation marks follow Loserth's italics. E reads *expectat* (152.20).

[^6]: **C.** *Ut in per se differencia*: the signs are to their religion what a differentia of itself (an essential differentia) is to a species, the mark that makes it what it is. The codices read *a primis sanctis doctoribus* (152.24), with the same sense. AB read *magis* for *magnificantes*, C corrects (152.26).

[^7]: **G.** *Signa / signata*, "signs / things signified". AB omit *enim* and *per eos*; C supplies both in the margin (152.28–29).

[^8]: **C.** *Hoc* is neuter, *hic* masculine: Wyclif turns the opponents' argument (150.18–21) against them, since on his view the neuter that points to the bread makes the proposition the more fitting. D reads *frequenter cogitavi* (153.3); C has *vel infidelis* in the margin in another hand (153.12); E reads *est*, B *in qua patet* (153.13).

[^9]: **T.** *Consequencie*, "inference", is the print; ABCE read *commune* (153.19), which does not construe here. C has *racionis* in the margin in another hand (153.18), A *argumenti* (153.19); D reads *non oportet* (153.19), B *iuxta sunt* (153.20).

[^10]: **S.** Prov. 26:5, "Answer a fool according to his folly", alluded to.

[^11]: **C.** *Color*: a plausible ground for an inference, the period sense, never "pretext".

[^12]: **C.** The sentence is elliptical: from the tropical form one may not infer by likeness that every such manner of speaking is tropical in the same way, nor that the bread's nature is destroyed, any more than a sinner's nature is destroyed when he is converted into a just man. D reads *Locucio enim* (153.24); CE *vel natura*, D *aut vini* (153.25); E *peius quam* (153.26).

[^13]: **G.** *Vere … sed tropice*: two mode adverbs, kept apart. E reads *fieret* for *figuret*; D *Christi esse* (153.30).

[^14]: **C.** *Doctores*: the doctors of the sect, set against the *sancti doctores* they interpret. *Cum aliter foret* gives their ground: without this reading the tropical speech would be impossible and heretical.

[^15]: **T.** "[Afterwards]": Loserth's apparatus proposes *Rectius: et post amiserant* (154.16), which the print *et amiserant* needs to construe; the subject is most likely the church after the *baptiste signorum* came in. D reads *subintroerunt*, *vocarunt* (154.14–16); AB omit *sacra* (154.14).

[^16]: **S.** *De cons.* D. 2 c. 55 *Panis est in altari usitatus ante verba sacramentorum* (Ambrose, the source of *usitatum*), and c. 48 *Hoc est quod dicimus* (under Augustine's name — the text is in fact Lanfranc's, *De corpore et sanguine Domini* 10 (PL 150, 421A; LEDGER K07h-7); Loserth's reference at 154.21; the form is ours; LEDGER K05j-3, K05j-4). DE read *Ambrosii* (154.16–17); E omits *eciam* (154.17), reads *quomodo panis*, omits *est* (154.18–19).

[^17]: **S.** 1 Cor. 10:16. E reads *dum intelligunt* (154.23).

[^18]: **G.** *Speciem accidentis*: the logical species (which kind of accident it is), not the sacramental species; the side-note so takes it.

[^19]: **C.** *Quam fictum ydolum accidentis*: the comparison is with saying that the bread and wine are an accident, "a feigned idol". E reads *quod evidencius et pertinencius* (154.30).

[^20]: **S.** Luke 22:20; 1 Cor. 11:25, "This chalice is the new testament in my blood", in indirect speech.

[^21]: **S.** The writings are not named, and Loserth gives no foot (LEDGER K05j-5). E reads *videtur sequi*, D *ex istis* (155.8); B *et quod* (155.10); C has *papa … religiosorum* in the lower margin in another hand (155.10–12).
