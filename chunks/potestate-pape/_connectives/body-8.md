### 08-c ¶2 · 174.12–176.4  (6 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 174] Item, non subiacet humane potestati **sed** solum divine constituere quemquam summum pontificem, igitur non debet supponi in Petro tanta presumpcio, ut eligat hominem de quo non fuit sibi ad hoc facta revelacio in summum pontificem et immediatum Christi vicarium super terram, specialiter cum super ista fatuitate, non relinquitur nobis scriptum autenticum, **sed** in eleccione Mathie reliquit hoc divine iudicio, ut patet Actuum primo; quanto plus in eleccione maioris. Quis, inquam, crederet quod Petrus diffiniret beatum Clementem sequi Christum similius quam Evangelistam, qui diu vixit post Petrum, vel beatum Evangelistam debere Clementi ut maiori? vel capiti necessario regulandi obedienciam et reverenciam, qualem pape iam exigunt a subiectis. vel quod Christus aut alius apostolus talem maioritatem instituit, vel policiam in sua ecclesia approbavit? Voluit enim omnes apostolos esse socios sibi invicem obedientes et servientes sine dominio vel magisterio alicuius capitanei preter Christum. Et ex istis infirmatur tercia ficticia quod per viam eleccionis et institucionis humane ponitur [p. 175] quis Romanus et summus pontifex. Et quamvis ista sentencia sit maxime propinqua facto, tamen nimis elongatur a religione fideli et stabili fundamento. Cum enim aliquem stat esse Romanum pontificem et non papam et econtra, ut patet in Christo et Petro, patet quod oportet istas duas elecciones esse racionis disparis et nature ordine separatas. Stat enim aliquem esse Romanum pontificem et pessimum Antichristum, sicut econtra stat aliquem esse summum pontificem, cum hoc quod sit loco illi indifferens, ut patet ex dictis. Unde dominus Ardmacanus libro VII, cap. XVIII De Questionibus Armenorum dicit quod sunt duo pontificatus naturaliter differentes, et oportet quod sint eciam apud Deum due elecciones. **Sed** videtur michi quod eleccio qua quis eligeretur in papam vel summum pontificem non subiacet potestati vel eleccioni humane. Nam idem est hominem eligere in papam vel summum pontificem et eligere ipsum in apostolicum maxime propinque per graciam sequentem Christi vestigia; **sed** hoc non subiacet ordinacioni vel eleccioni humane, igitur nec aliud sive idem; sic enim eligeret quis hominem in predestinatum vel quocumque aliter graciosum. Et confirmatur ex factis et verbis apostolorum Act. I in eleccione Mathie apostoli, quod est minus quam eligere summum pontificem, ubi ipsi non eligunt **sed** rogant Deum; ut ostendat ipse quem elegerit. Sicut igitur electores non eligunt summum pontificem in eligendo alterum Scarioth vel Antichristum; sic casualiter eligendo eum quem Deus elegerit non [p. 176] eligunt talem in summum pontificem, **sed** in fungentem tali officio, quod non dicit excellenciam gracie vel alia carismata, que sunt propria dona Dei.

ENGLISH: Again, to appoint anyone highest pontiff is subject not to human power but only to divine; therefore so great a presumption ought not to be supposed in Peter as that he should elect as highest pontiff and immediate [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius-christi} on earth a man about whom no revelation had been made to him for this, especially since on this folly no [authentic]{g:autenticus} writing is left to us; but in the election of Matthias he left this to the judgement of God, as is clear from Acts 1;[^4] how much more in the election of a greater one. Who, I say, would believe that Peter would define blessed Clement to follow Christ in closer likeness than the Evangelist, who lived long after Peter, or that the blessed Evangelist owed to Clement, as to a greater, or as to a head necessary for ruling, obedience and reverence such as popes now exact from their subjects?[^5] or that Christ or another apostle instituted such [greatness]{g:majoritas}, or approved such a polity in his [church]{g:ecclesia}? For he willed all the apostles to be companions, obeying and serving one another, without the [lordship]{g:dominium} or [rule]{g:magisterium} of any [captain]{g:capitaneus} besides Christ. And from these things the third fiction is weakened, that by way of human election and institution someone is set up [175] as Roman and highest pontiff. And although this [teaching]{g:sentencia} is the closest to the fact, yet it is far removed from faithful religion and a stable foundation.[^6] For since it is possible for someone to be [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex} and not pope, and conversely, as is clear in Christ and Peter, it is clear that these two elections must be of disparate character and separated in the order of nature. For it is possible for someone to be Roman pontiff and the worst Antichrist, just as, conversely, it is possible for someone to be highest pontiff while being indifferent to that place, as is clear from what has been said. Hence the [lord of Armagh]{g:dominus-ardmacanus}, in book 7, chapter 18 of the *Questions of the Armenians*, says that there are two pontificates naturally differing, and that there must also be two elections before God.[^7] But it seems to me that the election by which someone would be elected pope or highest pontiff is not subject to the power or election of men. For it is the same to elect a man pope or highest pontiff and to elect him an [apostolic man]{g:apostolicus} following most closely, through grace, the footsteps of Christ; now this is not subject to human [ordinance]{g:ordinacio} or election; therefore neither is the other, or rather the same thing; for so one would elect a man to be [predestinate]{g:predestinatus}, or gracious in any other way.[^8] And it is confirmed from the deeds and words of the apostles, Acts 1, in the election of the apostle Matthias, which is less than electing a highest pontiff, where they do not elect but ask God that he himself may show whom he has chosen. As, then, the electors do not elect a highest pontiff in electing another Iscariot or Antichrist, so, electing by chance the one whom God has chosen, they do not [176] elect such a one as highest pontiff, but as one discharging such an office, which does not signify excellence of grace or other charisms, which are the proper gifts of God.[^9]

### 09-g ¶1 · 214.3–215.17  (7 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 214] Et quantum ad implicatum de eleccione humana patet ex sepe dictis, quod confirmatur ex lege triplici; primo docetur LXXIX distinccione: Si quis pecunia vel gracia humana vel tumultu populari sine canonica eleccione fuerit intronizatus, non apostolicus **sed** apostaticus habeatur. Cum igitur sit maior error in eleccione activa, quando eligentes coacti sunt a dyabolo eligere personam quam Deus reprobat, quod patule patet ex opere et omissione papalis officii, multo magis vel a pari sequitur, quod electus talis non apostolicus **sed** apostaticus est habendus. Non enim licet humanitus reprobare, ut ex fide supponitur, nisi ut Deus ex lege sua docuerit reprobandum; nec valet vel deficit humana eleccio nisi de quanto eleccioni divine consentit vel discrepat. Ex quo patet, cum stat electores cogi humanitus ad eligendum aput Deum ydoneum, quod dicta lex debet intelligi, quando militaris vel popularis tumultus cogit ad eligendum inhabilem, quare igitur non, si dyabolus vel peccatum collegii electorum et observatis humanis ritibus cogit ad eligendum tam inhabilem, eque non apostolicus **sed** apostaticus habendus est? Nec est probacio legis humane tam valida, sicut sensibilis probacio docta ex vita prepositi: nam maior capta ex lege Domini et minor capta ad sensum ex vita prepositi, concludunt cercius, quam evidencie palliate ex humanis legibus. Secundo docetur eadem sentencia ex lege Jeronymi distinccione [p. 215] XL sic loquentis: Non est facile stare in loco Petri et Pauli et tenere cathedram, quia non sanctorum filii sunt, qui tenent loca sanctorum, **sed** qui exercent opera eorum. Ex quo patet quod nemo sit papa, nisi sit Christi et Petri filius, imitando eos in moribus, **sed** sicut non subiacet potestati eleccionis humane hoc facere, sic nec subiacet potestati humane papam constituere. Si **autem** vocetur papa quicumque quem occidentalis ecclesia acceptat ad capitaliter decidendum causas ecclesie, ad precipiendum fidelibus infundabiliter et blasfeme pro congregandis pecuniis et mundanis honoribus, abusio est termini infundabilis, iuxta quam oportet in casu concedere, quod maximus hereticus et antichristus sit papa, **sed** et rudissimus laycus vel femella; et periret fides vel religio capiti ecclesie adhibenda.

ENGLISH: And as to what is implied about human election, it is clear from what has often been said; and this is confirmed from a threefold law. First, it is taught in distinction 79: "If anyone has been enthroned by money or human favour or popular tumult without canonical election, let him be held not apostolic but apostatic."[^1] Since, therefore, there is a greater error in active election when the electors are compelled by the devil to elect a person whom God reproves (which is openly clear from the doing and the omission of the papal office), it follows much more, or equally, that such a man elected is to be held not apostolic but apostatic. For it is not lawful to reprove humanly, as is supposed [as faith teaches]{g:ex-fide}, except as God has taught by his law that one is to be reproved; nor does human election avail or fail except in so far as it agrees with or departs from divine election. From this it is clear, since it is possible for electors to be compelled humanly to elect one who is fit in God's sight, that the said law must be understood [of the case] when military or popular tumult compels the election of an [unqualified]{g:inhabilis} man. Why then, if the devil or the sin of the college of electors, even with the human rites observed, compels the election of a man so unqualified, is he not equally to be held not apostolic but apostatic?[^2] Nor is the proof from human law so strong as the [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} proof taught from the life of the [superior]{g:prepositi}: for a major taken from the law of the Lord and a minor taken by sense from the life of the superior [infer]{g:concludere} more certainly than [evidences]{g:evidencia} [cloaked]{g:palliare} from human laws. Secondly, the same [teaching]{g:sentencia} is taught from the law of Jerome in distinction [215] 40, speaking thus: "It is not easy to stand in the place of Peter and Paul and to hold the chair, for it is not the sons of saints who hold the places of saints, but those who do their works."[^3] From this it is clear that no one is [pope]{g:papa} unless he is a son of Christ and of Peter, imitating them in conduct; but just as it does not lie in the power of human election to bring this about, so neither does it lie in human [power]{g:potestas} to constitute a pope. But if whoever the western [church]{g:ecclesia} accepts to decide the church's cases [as head]{g:capitaliter}, to command the faithful [without foundation]{g:infundabiliter} and blasphemously for the gathering of money and worldly honours, is called pope, it is an abuse of the term, without foundation;[^4] by which one must grant, in the case, that the greatest heretic and antichrist is pope, and even the rudest layman or a little woman; and the faith or religion to be given to the [head]{g:caput} of the church would perish.

### 09-g ¶2 · 215.19–217.13  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 215] Unde notaremus cronicas predictas, quomodo inolevit papalis dignitas. Et narrat dominus Ardmacanus libro VII cap. XXIV de questionibus Armenorum, quomodo imperator Constantinus circa annum domini trecentesimum primum hoc censuit et precepit, quod suus episcopus ab omnibus papa vocaretur. Et Foca imperator circa annum domini sexcentesimum hoc idem ex cleri instancia confirmavit, ut legitur in suis annalibus, quomodo igitur saltaret in summum sacramentum ecclesie, quod imperator terrenus tam irreligiose instituit, ymo ut inquit: Si imperator Thartarie ecclesiam de Cambalek aut de Cathay conversus ad [p. 216] christianismum caput omnium aliarum ecclesiarum constitueret, cederet ceteris paribus capitalitas Romane ecclesie. Horribile itaque atque necessario defectibile fundamentum ecclesie a seculari principe ita inductum. Numquid credimus quod humana eleccio plus iuris per se tribuit quam Christi eleccio? Christi **autem** eleccio non est per se sufficiens ad constituendum quamcumque partem ecclesie, cum requiritur eterna predestinacio cum gracia consumante. Et hinc credo quod ille qui nichil facit ex indifferencia reliquit nobis exemplum ad consenciendum isti sentencie per hoc quod ipsemet elegit dyabolum. Joh. VI, 71 dicitur: Nonne duodecim vos elegi et unus ex vobis dyabolus est? Tercio docetur eadem sentencia ex decreto beati Jeronymi posito XXIV questio I: Quoniam vetus oriens, ubi dicens mistice multa commendabilia de Romana ecclesia subiungit condicionem, quam oportet de ipsa subintelligi: Fatiscat, inquit, invidia Romani culminis, recedat ambicio; cum successore piscatoris et discipulo Christi loquor: Et concludit in fine. Quicumque tecum non colligit, spergit, hoc est qui Christi non est, Antichristi est. In qua lege sunt tria notanda, primo quod Romana ecclesia est ubicumque habitaverit universitas fidelium viatorum, sic quod si iam non esset alius Romanus christianus, adhuc fideles conversi a quocumque apostolo, quia sunt discipuli Petri et Pauli, qui fuerunt Romani, possunt dici Romana ecclesia; tanta namque fuit Christi et omnium apostolorum concors [p. 217] amicicia, quod christiano sequente unum eorum in moribus, sequitur eorum quemlibet cuiuslibet eorum in gradu suo discipulus. Satis est, cum et omnino oportet quod christianus sit Christi discipulus, et sic nitetur explanacio beati Jeronymi in fine addita, quando dicitur: Quicumque non colligit cum Romano pontifice spargit, hoc est, inquit, qui Christi non est, Antichristi est, non tamen quod Romana ecclesia sepe sumitur pro qualicumque papa et suis cardinalibus, ubicumque habitaverint, quomodolibet conversando, **sed** ista significacio seculariter et infundabiliter est extorta, et ideo nimirum intricat ecclesiam.

ENGLISH: Hence we should note the chronicles mentioned before, how the papal dignity grew up. And [the lord of Armagh]{g:dominus-ardmacanus} relates in book 7, chapter 24 of the *Questions of the Armenians* how the emperor Constantine, about the year of the Lord 301, decreed and commanded this: that his bishop should be called pope by all.[^5] And the emperor Phocas, about the year of the Lord 600, confirmed this same thing at the [urging]{g:instancia} of the clergy, as is read in his annals.[^6] How then would what an earthly emperor so irreligiously instituted leap up into the highest sacrament of the church?[^7] Indeed, as he says: "If the emperor of Tartary, converted to [216] Christianity, were to constitute the church of Cambalek or of Cathay head of all the other churches, the capital standing of the Roman church would give way, other things being equal."[^8] Horrible, then, and of necessity bound to fail, is a foundation of the church brought in in this way by a secular prince. Do we believe that human election of itself confers more [right]{g:ius} than Christ's election? Now Christ's election is not of itself sufficient to constitute any part of the church whatever, since eternal predestination is required, with [consummating grace]{g:gracia-consumans}. And hence I believe that he who does nothing at random left us an example for assenting to this teaching, in that he himself chose a devil.[^9] In John 6:71 it is said: "Have not I chosen you twelve? And one of you is a devil." Thirdly, the same teaching is taught from the decree of blessed Jerome placed in Causa 24, question 1, *Quoniam vetus oriens*, where, saying mystically many commendable things of the Roman church, he adds a condition which must be [understood as implied]{g:subintelligere} of it: "Let envy of Roman eminence give way," he says, "let ambition withdraw; I speak with the successor of the fisherman and the disciple of Christ." And he concludes at the end: "Whoever gathereth not with thee, scattereth; that is, he who is not Christ's is Antichrist's."[^10] In this law three things are to be noted. First, that the Roman church is wherever [the whole body of the faithful]{g:universitas-fidelium} who are [wayfarers]{g:viator} dwells; so that if there were now no other Roman Christian, the faithful converted by any apostle whatever, since they are disciples of Peter and Paul, who were Romans, could still be called the Roman church. For so great was the concordant [217] friendship of Christ and of all the apostles that, when a Christian follows one of them in conduct, he follows every one of them, a disciple of each of them in his degree.[^11] It is enough, since it is altogether necessary too that a Christian be Christ's disciple; and on this will rest the explanation of blessed Jerome added at the end, where it is said: "Whoever gathereth not with the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex}, scattereth; that is," he says, "he who is not Christ's is Antichrist's." Yet not as "Roman church" is often [taken for]{g:sumitur-pro} any pope whatever and his cardinals, wherever they dwell and however they conduct themselves; but that signification has been extorted in a secular way and without foundation, and therefore no wonder it entangles the church.

### 09-g ¶3 · 217.14–218.6  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 217] Secundo notandum ex dicto beati Jeronymi quod quicumque diviserit contra Christum est Antichristus, cuiusmodi sunt superbi et specialiter sacerdotes cesarei legi Christi contrarii. Nam secundum beatum Gregorium nullus in ecclesia magis nocet, ut allegatum est ex secundo cap. primi De Cura Pastorali, et per consequens nullus est nequior Antichristus. Et isto modo loquitur decretum de Antichristo sub autoritate Gregorii, XVI questio VII: Pervenit ad nos, ubi dicitur, quod quidam episcopi consumunt bona pauperum tribus modis: primo in militibus et aliis laycis, qui ipsos fovent et protegunt in gloria seculari, secundo in monstruosa familia et tercio quod est gravius in consanguineis. Et ex diffinicione sancti talis episcopus inter maximos hereticos et antichristos non minimus habeatur, et forte si omnes episcopi hodie in Anglia cum religiosis et secularibus eis consentaneis essent signati in fronte signo bestie, pateret quod longe [p. 218] plures quam sunt catholici ex parte Christi forent infideles, quia heretici et realiter antichristi. Sic **autem** loquuntur tot sancti doctores de antichristis et hereticis cum lege ecclesie et scriptura. Alias **autem** significaciones somniant ut heretici et antichristi absconsius delitescant.

ENGLISH: Secondly, it is to be noted from the saying of blessed Jerome that whoever divides against Christ is Antichrist; of which kind are the proud, and especially [caesarean priests]{g:sacerdos-cesarius} contrary to the law of Christ. For according to blessed Gregory no one in the church does more harm, as was [alleged]{g:allegare} from the second chapter of the first [part] of the *Pastoral Care*; and consequently no one is a more wicked Antichrist.[^12] And in this way the decree speaks of Antichrist under the authority of Gregory, Causa 16, question 7, *Pervenit ad nos*, where it is said that "certain bishops consume the goods of the poor in three ways: first on [knights]{g:milites} and other laymen who cherish and protect them in worldly glory, second on a monstrous [household]{g:familia}, and third, which is graver, on kinsmen."[^13] And by the saint's [definition]{g:diffinicio} such a bishop is to be held not the least among the greatest heretics and antichrists. And perhaps, if all the bishops in England today, with [the religious]{g:religiosi} and [seculars]{g:secularis} who consent with them, were marked on the forehead with the mark of the beast, it would be clear that far [218] more than there are catholics on Christ's side would be [unbelievers]{g:infidelis}, because heretics and really antichrists.[^14] And so do so many holy doctors speak of antichrists and heretics, with the law of the church and scripture. But they dream up other significations, so that heretics and antichrists may lie hidden more secretly.[^15]

### 09-h ¶1 · 218.25–221.16  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 218] Et patet ad quem sensum valent leges de eleccionibus et de locorum particularium dignitate, ut XXII distinccione: Sacrosancta dicitur, quod Romana ecclesia non ab apostolis, **sed** a Domino primatum obtinuit, et post illam primam sedem secunda sedes est apud Alexandriam, a beato Marco et evangelista Petri discipulo consecrata; tercia sedes est apud Anthiochiam, ubi Petrus antequam [p. 219] Romam venerat, sedit episcopus et Ignacium episcopum constituit, ubi primum christianorum nomen exortum est et post synodus decrevit Constantinopolitanam ecclesiam tenere secundum sedem post Romam, propter hoc quod sit quodammodo nova Roma; frivola forent ista, nisi sedes dicta factis compensent. Ideo dicit Gregorius ibidem: Cum culpa non exigit, omnes secundum racionem humilitatis equales sumus. Et confirmacio est quod iste tres sedes a nostris tamquam scismatice sunt abiecte, et ipsi econtra censent nostros scismaticos, et Asyani et alii fideles reputant utrosque deliros maniacos, constituentes in suis ecclesiis sedes alias, asserentes vere, cum non locus **sed** virtus gentem sanctificat, quecumque ecclesia est maior, que humilius sequitur Ihesum Christum. Et ista est vera sentencia, cum Joh. IV. 20 dicit Veritas mulieri Samaritane opposite Christo: patres nostri in monte hoc adoraverunt, et vos dicitis quia Jerusolimis est locus in quo adorare oportet; venit, inquit, hora et nunc est, quando veri adoratores adorabunt patrem in spiritu et veritate. Cum enim spiritus sit Deus, quemlibet locum replens, patet quod nec oportet in montem altum ascendere, ut Deus melius audiat preces nostras, ut Samaritani ex gestis patriarcharum crediderant, nec oportet in templum Salamonis cum Yudeis ascendere **sed** adorare in spiritu et montuose aspirando ad celestia secundum memorativam potenciam anime, scilicet intelligenciam, et cum anima sit, ubi afficitur, iuxta illud Apostoli ad Phil. III. 20 nostra [p. 220] conversacio in celis est, patet, quod sic orans mons altus efficitur per Deum sibi ipsi, et ideo Veritas docens nos patrem orare Matth. VI. 9, docet consideracionem nostram incipere ab altitudine adorati: Pater noster, qui es in celis etc. quantum ad templum Yudeorum Jerusalem, oportet adorare in veritate, sic quod vita non discordet mendaciter a peticione et animo adorantis, et tunc fit templum infinitum domino prestancius quam basilica Yudeorum, quia I. Cor. III. 16 dicit Apostolus: templum Domini est, quod estis vos. Quando enim est veritas in conversacione et veritas in peticione, que quidem veritas est adequacio ad intellectum divinum, tunc ex plena conformitate voluntati divine, orans sic mente et spiritu constituitur templum Dei, et tunc sicut dicit digne primam peticionem oracionis dominice Deo patri propter intellectus sublimitatem, sic potest digne dicere duas peticiones sequentes ad secundas personas alias, cum taliter difformem oportet trahi ad regnum, ubi erit perfecte voluntatis increate et create conformitas. Ideo oramus, quod sicut in celo est plena conformitas sic in terra sit proporcionaliter conformitas voluntatum. Et patet ex ista duplici racione orandi, quomodo verificantur decreta distinccione XL: Non est facile cum duobus capitulis sequentibus et capitulo: Multi sunt sacerdotes cum eis similibus. In quibus plane docetur quod locus quicumque sicut et sacramentum vel ritus sensibilis est adoracioni isti et preeminencie persone apud [p. 221] Deum impertinens, nisi forte per accidens excitaverit sanctitatem, unde signum est, quod adoramus samaritane sive yudaice tamquam irracionale animal, reptans depresso capite mentis nostre, quod tantum loca et cerimonias observamus. Christus **autem** ad hoc ascendit in celum, ut parvipendentes hec temporalia ascendamus per scalam virtutum ab istis sublunaribus in quibus infigimur ad spiritualia bona celestia finaliter affectanda. Unde Veritas correspondenter ad fidem mulieris, qua vere credidit Christum esse prophetam, ipsam alloquitur: Mulier, crede michi, quasi diceret propter imperfeccionem fidei es longe a sponsa, **sed** cum salus ex Yudeis est, oportet quod in fundamento scale orandi statuens tibi substanciam fidei credas michi.

ENGLISH: And it is clear in what sense the laws about elections and about the dignity of particular places have force; as in distinction 22, *Sacrosancta*, it is said that "the Roman [church]{g:ecclesia} obtained the [primacy]{g:primatus} not from the apostles but from the Lord; and after that first see the second see is at Alexandria, consecrated by blessed Mark, evangelist and disciple of Peter; the third see is at Antioch, where Peter, before he came [219] to Rome, sat as bishop and appointed Ignatius bishop, where the name of Christians first arose"; and afterwards a synod decreed that the church of Constantinople should hold the second see after Rome, "because it is in a manner a new Rome".[^1] These things would be frivolous unless the said sees made up for them with deeds. Therefore Gregory says in the same place: "When fault does not require it, we are all equal by the measure of humility."[^2] And the confirmation is that these three sees have been cast off by ours as schismatic, and they in turn reckon ours schismatics; and the Asians and other faithful reckon both to be raving madmen, setting up other sees in their own churches, and asserting truly, since not place but virtue sanctifies a people, that whatever church more humbly follows Jesus Christ is the greater. And this is the true [teaching]{g:sentencia}, since in John 4:20 Truth speaks to the Samaritan woman, who had set against Christ: "Our fathers adored on this mountain: and you say that at Jerusalem is the place where men must adore"; "the hour cometh," he says, "and now is, when the true adorers shall adore the Father in spirit and in truth."[^3] For since God is a spirit, filling every place, it is clear that there is no need to go up a high mountain so that God may hear our prayers better, as the Samaritans had believed from the deeds of the patriarchs, nor to go up into Solomon's temple with the Jews; but to adore in spirit, and as on a mountain, aspiring to heavenly things according to the memorative [power]{g:potencia} of the soul, namely the understanding.[^4] And since the soul is where it has affection, according to that of the Apostle, Philippians 3:20, "our [220] conversation is in heaven", it is clear that one praying thus is made by God a high mountain to himself.[^5] And therefore Truth, teaching us to pray to the Father (Matthew 6:9), teaches our consideration to begin from the height of the one adored: "Our Father who art in heaven" etc. As for the temple of the Jews at Jerusalem, one must adore in truth, so that the life does not disagree, lying, with the petition and mind of the one adoring; and then there comes to be a temple infinitely more excellent for the Lord than the basilica of the Jews, for in 1 Corinthians 3:16 the Apostle says: "the temple of the Lord is, which you are."[^6] For when there is truth in [manner of life]{g:conversacio} and truth in petition (which truth is adequation to the divine intellect), then, from full conformity to the divine will, the one praying thus in mind and spirit is constituted the temple of God. Quantum ad primam partem huius argucie debet supponi quod iste papa intellexit per sacrosanctam Romanem ecclesiam non basilicam vel mixtum putridum, **sed** sanctos Petrum et Paulum et ceteros qui inhabitaverant illum locum. Ideo in eodem decreto dicit quod Petrus et Paulus erant socii in urbe Romana, unde signanter dicitur quod primatum obtinuit. Debet supponi secundo quod loquitur de primatu hominum quoad Deum, ex primicia virtutum et edificacionis ecclesie et non [p. 229] de primatu diviciarum temporalium vel glorie humane. In hoc enim Scarioth qui possedit agrum de mercede iniquitatis, ut dicit Petrus Act. primo, Petrum excesserat, propter quod sancti et specialiter apostoli timuerunt impetrare sibi redditus de bonis pauperum. Et ex istis patet tercio quam nuda foret argucia: Romana ecclesia non ab apostolis **sed** ab ipso Domino primatum obtinuit, igitur omnis Romanus pontifex vel alius debet in gloria mundana precellere vel seculariter dominari, quin pocius ex decreto sequitur quod Romanus pontifex tenetur servire illi populo, non sibi dominando vel ipsum suspendendo, **sed** pro illo efficaciter exorando, ut allegat Paulum fecisse Rom. primo: Licet, inquit, pro omnibus apud Dominum omnium sanctorum effundatur oracio, hiis tamen verbis Paulus beatissimus Romanis proprio cyrographo pollicetur dicens: Testis enim michi est Deus, cui servio in spiritu meo, in evangelio filii eius, quod sine intermissione memoriam vestri facio semper in oracionibus meis. Ex isto decreto videtur quod papa Anacletus noluit asserere quod ipse debuit super omnes alios dominari civiliter vel primatum habere quoad Deum super omnes alias personas ecclesie militantis, quia sic querendo gloriam propriam, ut dicitur Joh. V, haberet signum evidentissimum Antichristi, ymo Bonifacius VIII. in sua extravagante Unam sanctam non audebat expresse illud asserere, quia tam fideles quam infideles pertinenter sibi obicerent: Tu de te ipso testimonium perhibes, testimonium tuum non est verum. Unde in istam perplexitatem ex dotacione et exaltacione cesaris involvuntur Romani pontifices, quod imperatore [p. 230] querente ab eis: si ipsi in potestate, dignitate vel primatu quoad Deum excedunt omnes mortales alios iam viventes, oportet ipsos illud concedere, quia aliter, ut inquiunt, nemo debet credere quod sunt pape, et cum a Deo non habent illam preeminenciam, ut patet ex dictis, nec beatus Petrus illud concederet, patet eis concedendum quod hoc habent a cesare. Nam beatus Paulus Petri socius confessus fuit fideliter prima Cor. XV, 9: Ego, inquit, sum minimus apostolorum, qui non sum dignus vocari apostolus. Quare igitur sic non profiteretur Romanus pontifex, si imperialis dignitas non obstaret? Et sic necessitari videtur ex dotacione tam imperator quam suus episcopus involvi blasfemiam. Et sic verum concluditur quod a Deo immediate et non a natura humana papa habet excellenciam dignitatis, **sed** oportet quod mereatur illam excellenciam dignitatis humiliter sine pompa. Et revera non est medium merendi ipsam accipere dignitatem talem cesaream.

ENGLISH: But secondly it is argued that the [pope]{g:papa} has such [power]{g:potestas} from the Lord, as the decree says in distinction 22, *Sacrosancta*, where Pope Anacletus speaks thus: "The most holy Roman [church]{g:ecclesia} obtained the [primacy]{g:primatus} not from the apostles but from the Lord himself."[^1] From which [it follows]{g:sequitur} that the pope has the most excellent dignity not from the emperor or from man but immediately from God. And the same is confirmed, secondly, from the submission of [kingdoms]{g:regnum}, and thirdly from the testimony of doctors who write on the pope's power to absolve from punishment and from guilt. As to the first part of this [quibble]{g:argucia},[^2] it must be supposed that this pope understood by "the most holy Roman church" not a basilica or a rotting mixture, but Saints Peter and Paul and the others who had dwelt in that place. Therefore in the same decree he says that "Peter and Paul were companions in the city of Rome"; hence it is said pointedly that it "obtained the primacy". It must be supposed secondly that he speaks of men's primacy with respect to God, from being first in virtues and in the building up of the church,[^3] and not [229] of a primacy of temporal riches or of human glory. For in this Iscariot, who "possessed a field of the reward of iniquity", as Peter says in Acts 1, had surpassed Peter; for which reason the saints, and especially the apostles, feared to [obtain]{g:impetrare} [revenues]{g:reditus} for themselves from the goods of the poor. And from these things it is clear, thirdly, how bare the quibble would be: "The Roman church obtained the primacy not from the apostles but from the Lord himself; therefore every [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex}, or anyone else, ought to excel in worldly glory or to [lord it]{g:dominari} in secular fashion." Rather, from the decree it follows that the Roman pontiff is bound to serve that people, not by lording it over it or [suspending]{g:suspendere} it, but by praying effectively for it, as [the decree] alleges Paul to have done, Romans 1: "Although," it says, "prayer is poured out for all before the Lord of all the saints, yet with these words the most blessed Paul promises the Romans in his own handwriting, saying: 'For God is my witness, whom I serve in my spirit in the gospel of his Son, that without ceasing I make a commemoration of you, always in my prayers.'"[^4] From this decree it seems that Pope Anacletus did not wish to assert that he ought to exercise civil lordship over all others or to have primacy with respect to God over all the other persons of the [church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans}; for, seeking his own glory in this way, as is said in John 5, he would have the most evident sign of Antichrist.[^5] Indeed, Boniface VIII in his extravagant *Unam sanctam* did not dare to assert that expressly, because believers and unbelievers alike would pertinently object to him: "Thou givest testimony of thyself: thy testimony is not true."[^6] Hence the Roman pontiffs are entangled, through the endowment and exaltation [they had] from Caesar, in this [perplexity]{g:perplexitas}: that if the emperor [230] asks them "whether they themselves, in power, dignity or primacy with respect to God, exceed all other mortals now living", they must grant it, because otherwise, as they say, no one ought to believe that they are popes; and since they do not have that [preeminence]{g:preeminencia} from God, as is clear from what has been said, nor would blessed Peter grant it, it is clear that they must grant that they have it from Caesar.[^7] For blessed Paul, Peter's companion, faithfully confessed in 1 Corinthians 15:9: "I am," he says, "the least of the apostles, who am not worthy to be called an apostle." Why therefore would the Roman pontiff not profess so, if the imperial dignity did not stand in the way? And so it seems to be made necessary by the [endowment]{g:dotacio} that both the emperor and his bishop are entangled in blasphemy. And so it is truly [inferred]{g:concludere} that the pope has excellence of dignity immediately from God and not from human nature; but he must merit that excellence of dignity humbly, without pomp. And in truth to accept such a caesarean dignity is no means of meriting it.

### 10-c ¶2 · 230.22–231.27  (6 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 230] Quantum ad secundum partem argucie, patet quod non est color si regna vel persone submittunt se improvide Antichristo, quod propterea ampliatur in eo potestas vel iurisdiccio; ymo dyabolus negaret istam arguciam, cum filii sui subiecti per peccatum non augent in eo potenciam, eo quod tam ipse quam fidelis concederet quod non capit potestatem suam ab indignissimo servo suo, **sed** habet ipsam ab inicio a solo Deo. Unde non video quomodo papa posset patencius accusare se ipsum [p. 231] quam respondendo, ut narratur in quodam gesto Anglie, quomodo vacante episcopatu Dunelmie ex quadam subdola subtilitate clerici rex dicitur misisse pape, ut illud beneficium conferat suo clerico: rex **autem** et regnum ex scitu pape perante decreverant quod non obstante quacumque donacione pape promotus pape non perciperet fructus beneficii regni nostri, nisi regi et regno visus habilis, distribuendo illos fructus provide infra regnum, ipsum regnum velit incolere. Habito itaque longo consilio de literis regis papa fertur taliter respondisse: Et si rex misisset asinum pro illo beneficio, habuisset. Quod responsum manifeste indicat papam, et si prius dignus fuit ex lege privata papali illud officium contulisse, ab illo privilegio excedisse, quia XI questio III ex decreto beati Gregorii sic habetur: Privilegium omnino debet amittere, qui sibi permissa abutitur potestate. Maior **autem** in isto non posset esse abusio, quam scienter constituere indignum in clericali officio. Ista **autem** fallacia imaginatur de potestate ecclesiastica quod secularis potest eam dare sicut bovem vel asinum, vel quod ex defectu defensionis partis contrarie preposito spirituali acquiritur, si instituitur in quibusdam tradicionibus humanis. **Sed** deliramenta sunt ista.

ENGLISH: As to the second part of the quibble, it is clear that there is no [colour]{g:color} [for saying], if kingdoms or persons submit themselves improvidently to Antichrist, that his power or [jurisdiction]{g:iurisdiccio} is therefore enlarged in him;[^8] indeed, the devil would deny this quibble, since his sons, subjected [to him] through sin, do not increase [power]{g:potencia} in him, because both he and a believer would grant that he does not take his power from his most unworthy servant, but has it from the beginning from God alone.[^9] Not therefore from such submission is it shown that the pope has any preeminence from God or from man; rather, since every people is either subjected to him thus involuntarily or ought not to be subjected to him thus, from such blasphemous subjection there follows evidently a lack of dignity.[^10] Hence I do not see how the pope could accuse himself more openly [231] than by answering as is told in a certain English chronicle: how, when the bishopric of Durham was vacant, the king, through some sly subtlety of a cleric, is said to have sent to the pope that he should confer that benefice on his cleric. And the king and the kingdom, with the pope's knowledge, had decreed beforehand that, notwithstanding any gift of the pope, the pope's promoted man should not receive the fruits of a benefice of our kingdom unless, having seemed [qualified]{g:habilis} to the king and the kingdom, and distributing those fruits providently within the kingdom, he were willing to dwell in the kingdom itself.[^11] So, having held long counsel over the king's letters, the pope is reported to have answered thus: "Even if the king had sent an ass for that benefice, he would have had it." Which answer plainly shows that the pope, even if before he was worthy by private papal law to have conferred that office, has gone out from that [privilege]{g:privilegium}; for in [Causa] 11, question 3, from a decree of blessed Gregory, it is held thus: "He ought wholly to lose privilege who abuses the power permitted to him."[^12] Now there could be no greater abuse in this than knowingly to set an unworthy man in a clerical office. And this [fallacy]{g:fallacia} imagines, concerning ecclesiastical power, that [a secular]{g:secularis} can give it like an ox or an ass, or that it is acquired by a spiritual [superior]{g:prepositi} from the failure of the opposing party to make a defence, if he is instituted in certain human traditions.[^13] But these are [ravings]{g:deliramenta}.

### 10-d ¶1 · 232.14–235.34  (9 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 232] Patet **autem** ex predictis quomodo Romanus pontifex fuit consocius aliis pontificibus usque ad dotacionem ecclesie et exhinc ex auctoritate cesaris cepit capitaliter dominari, unde decretum XCVI distinccione quod non possumus pro verecundia negare sic loquitur: Constantinus imperator quarto die sui baptismi privilegium Romane ecclesie pontifici contulit, ut in toto orbe pontifices ita hunc caput habeant sicut iudices regem. Illa **autem** est alterius racionis quam potestas regiminis apostolica quam imperator non potest concedere, **sed** divisa per istam divisionem bipapalem variaretur ut populus sibi consenciens, vel diminueretur finaliter ad non quantum; quod superbia non sinit concedere, ideo dicit quod habet potestatem regiminis super totum mundum. ENGLISH: And it is clear from what has been said how the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex} was a fellow of the other pontiffs until the [endowment]{g:dotacio} of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, and from then on, by Caesar's authority, began to exercise lordship [as head]{g:capitaliter}; hence the decree in distinction 96, which we cannot for shame deny, speaks thus: "The emperor Constantine on the fourth day after his baptism conferred a [privilege]{g:privilegium} on the pontiff of the Roman church, that throughout the world the pontiffs should have him as [head]{g:caput}, as judges have the king. In which privilege, among other things, it reads thus: 'We grant him [power]{g:potestas} and vigour and imperial honour, decreeing that he hold the principate as well over the four sees, Alexandria, Antioch, Jerusalem and Constantinople; let him be loftier than, and prince over, all the [priests]{g:sacerdos} of the whole world.'"[^1] And afterwards he specifies the jurisdiction of his bishop, specifying the lands, India, Greece and the rest, according to the four parts of the world.[^2] For this reason some call this [power of jurisdiction]{g:potestas-iurisdiccionis}, the [indulgences]{g:indulgencie} and other privileges of the popes, caesarean. [233] And hence it is no wonder that Christians in India, in Greece and other countries distrust this privilege, knowing that "it is not man's to give spiritual power, but God's alone", as Lincoln says, Letter 23,[^3] and so they set aside his indulgences and privileges, knowing that one will have indulgence from God according to the worthiness of the person, and that it is not his to grant it except instrumentally, in so far as he is a faithful proclaimer of the Lord; and otherwise he makes sport of the people. This [teaching]{g:sentencia} I heard that our [pope]{g:papa} Urban VI spoke to the cardinals of Gregory, who transgressed the decalogue; and because, rebuking their [avarice or luxury], he [limited]{g:limitare} them to the first apostolic life, they conspired against him, electing for themselves Robert of Geneva, a man, as is said, dissolute, proud, warlike and ignorant of the [law of Christ]{g:lex-christi}.[^4] Therefore far from the English be his school, since in Luke 17:23 Christ says of such a division: "Go ye not away, nor follow"; do not go away from the first religion that you learned from the apostles, nor follow such a leader, since he would lead his kids into the precipice of the prince of darkness.[^5] Let us rather follow Urban, in so far as he runs ahead more rightly on the way of Christ, and no further;[^6] indeed, supposing that the said Robert should miraculously run so, we ought humbly to follow both, him together [with Urban], because, as is related from Gregory in distinction 22, *De Constantinopolitana*, sees or places do not draw [us] apart so that we should not imitate the better in good without distinction: "when," he says, "fault does not require [otherwise], we are all equal according to the reckoning of humility."[^7] Although therefore many [234] Roman pontiffs are eminently good, of whom the laws and the sayings of the doctors are understood, yet many, ignorant of the apostolic life, thirsted for worldly [lordship]{g:dominium}; and this, and consent to it and to other errors, we ought to flee like poison,[^8] and especially the error about indulgences and about the feigned power of jurisdiction or rule, which they say extends by the emperor's authority over the whole of our inhabited world uniquely as regards the Roman pontiff. And that [jurisdiction] is of another kind than the apostolic [power of rule]{g:potestas-regiminis}, which the emperor cannot grant; but, divided by this two-pope division, it would vary as the people consenting to him [varies], or would at last be diminished to no quantity at all;[^10] which pride does not allow [him] to grant, and so he says that he has power of rule over the whole world. Indeed, since that power is in the spirit as in its subject, [235] it seems that it remains perpetually, like a [character]{g:caracter}; and so we should have very many popes at once,[^11] and so the modern popes would make themselves far more damnable through the idleness of their power. For since, as they say, they have as much power as Peter and Paul together, indeed as Jesus had in his humanity, and no defect can be feigned on God's side but on theirs, why do they not bear fruit with power of this kind as before the endowment?
