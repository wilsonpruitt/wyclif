## For the coordinator

1. **101.11, 101.12 *participem* (twice).** A B C *participium* agrees with the source, *De visit. inf.* II.2: *quoddam participium … sibi participium*. The print agrees with O. Note n4 already records the A B C reading. Decide whether to adopt *participium*.
2. **101.15–16.** A B C *quod meum est meum quod ex me michi quadam cogitacione et cognacione* agrees with the source: *quia meum est meum, quia et ex me mihi quadam cogitatione et cognatione*. The source then goes on *et secreta quadam affectione conglutino, quodam proprio jure*, which bears on n5:
   - Its comma joins *secreta* to *affectione*, as the print does. This supports the print against the families' *cognacione secreta*.
   - Its *et* stands where Harris brackets his [*et*], so Harris's supply has a basis in the source.
   - *Conglutino* is a verb, so the print's *conglutinio* is corrupt.
   Note n5 may need a sentence on this.
3. **102.25 *quod … filius*.** A B C omit it. The source (Ambrose and the Lombard) has it, so print + O stand.
4. **I06e-3 and I06e-6** both come through one chapter of the Lombard, *Sent.* III d. 21 c. 4. **I06e-5** comes through d. 13 c. 3. Wyclif's two "*De Spiritu Sancto* III" citations are the Lombard's mislabel of *De incarnationis dominicae sacramento*. The first is the Lombard's; the second is Wyclif's extension of it.
5. **I06e-7.** The Bekker chapter is settled. The medieval "I c. 4" and Harris's index pairing (xxv?) are left open. Harris's xxv may need a plate check of Index II, p. 103.
