# *De eucharistia*, chapter 1, part 4: the decrees on the sight of the body and the spilled blood

## ¶1 · 22.21–24.9

> [p. 22] Nec docetur racio quare corpus Christi in mutando visum videtur eadem visione qua hostia, quin per idem in mutando alios sensus sentitur conformiter sicut hostia. Et revera tam fidelis quam infidelis horreret de concessione conclusionum quas oportet sic errantes concedere in ista materia; ideo beati qui non viderunt et crediderunt eorum opposita. Quid enim horribilius quam quod sacerdos post celebracionem portat corporaliter Deum in extremitatibus digitorum et quidquid tetigerit cum illis digitis facit [p. 23] corpus dominicum illud tangere? Non enim tollit locio quin sacramentum imbibitum remaneat secundum partes subtiles in corpore continente. Cum ergo remanent odor et sapor, patet naturalibus quod oportet subiectum corporeum remanere. Et patet ista sentencia secundum leges ecclesie; nam in De Consecracione, distinccione II, sub verbis Augustini sic scribitur: Nos autem sub specie panis et vini quam videmus res invisibiles, id est, carnem Christi et sanguinem honoramus. Ex quibus patenter patet quod illa species sensibilis quam sacramentum dicimus non est corpus Christi vel sanguis. Et idem patet eadem distinccione sub auctoritate Ambrosii Forte dicis: Similitudinem, inquit, non video carnis, non video sanguinis veritatem. Verumptamen, si viderem sanguinis Christi essenciam, viderem per idem omnem quantitatem vel qualitatem sensibilem, que informat corpus Christi in toto; quod est tercio expresse contra eundem Augustinum eadem distinccione Prima quidem et sepe inferius recitatum, quomodo sanctus doctor exponit dictum Christi Joh. VIº, 67, propter quod multi discipuli abierunt retrorsum; quod debet intelligi spiritualiter, non quod morsibus corporalibus comesturi erant corpus quod oportet esse sursum in eodem loco et figura inmobile, donec seculum finiatur, sed spiritualiter per idem non videret corporaliter corpus Christi. Unde sequitur huius sancti sentencia sub hiis verbis, De Consecracione, distinccione II: Non hoc corpus quod videtis manducaturi [p. 24] estis et bibituri illum sanguinem quem effusuri sunt illi qui me crucifigent, et post detegens equivocacionem sic loquitur: ipsum quidem et non ipsum, ipsum invisibiliter, non ipsum visibiliter; necesse est, inquit, celebrari illud visibiliter, necesse est tamen invisibiliter intelligi. Multa sunt similia dicta sanctorum et legum ecclesie que oportet fidelem credere et per consequens in eis propter carnales hereticos informari.

Nor is any reason taught why [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}, in acting on the sight, is seen with the same vision as the [host]{g:hostia}, without its being by the same token [perceived]{g:sentire} in like manner as the host in acting on the other senses.[^1] And in truth the faithful as much as the [unbeliever]{g:infidelis} would shudder at granting the [conclusions]{g:conclusio} which those who err thus must grant in this matter; therefore "blessed are they that have not seen and have believed" their opposites.[^2] For what is more horrible than that the [priest]{g:sacerdos} after the celebration carries God [bodily]{g:corporaliter} in the tips of his fingers, and whatever he touches with those fingers he makes [23] the Lord's body touch? For washing does not take away that the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}, once imbibed, remains according to its subtle parts in the containing body. Since, then, the smell and the savour remain, it is clear to natural philosophers that a bodily [subject]{g:subiectum} must remain. And this [teaching]{g:sentencia}[^3] is clear according to [the laws of the church]{g:leges-ecclesie}; for in *De consecratione*, distinction 2, under the words of Augustine it is written thus: "We, under the [species]{g:species} of [bread]{g:panis} and [wine]{g:vinum} which we see, honour invisible things, that is, the flesh of Christ and the blood."[^4] From which it is openly clear that that [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} species which we call the sacrament is not the body or blood of Christ. And the same is clear in the same distinction under the authority of Ambrose: "Perhaps you say," he says, "'I do not see the likeness of flesh, I do not see the truth of blood.'"[^5] Nevertheless, if I saw the essence of the blood of Christ, I would by the same token see every perceptible [quantity]{g:quantitas} or [quality]{g:qualitas} which informs the body of Christ in the whole; which is, thirdly, expressly against the same Augustine in the same distinction, [the canon] *Prima quidem*, and [against what is] often recited below: how the holy doctor expounds the saying of Christ in John 6:67, on account of which many disciples went back; that it ought to be understood [spiritually]{g:spiritualiter}, not that they were going to eat with bodily bites the body which must be above, in the same place and figure, immovable, until the world is ended; but spiritually; by the same token one would not see the body of Christ bodily.[^6] Hence there follows the teaching of this saint in these words, *De consecratione*, distinction 2: "Not this body which you see are you going to eat, [24] and drink that blood which they who will crucify me are going to shed"; and afterwards, laying bare the [equivocation]{g:equivocacio}, he speaks thus: "the same and not the same: the same [invisibly]{g:invisibiliter}, not the same visibly"; "it is necessary," he says, "that it be celebrated visibly, yet it is necessary that it be understood invisibly."[^7] There are many like sayings of [the saints]{g:sancti} and of the laws of the [church]{g:ecclesia} which the faithful man must believe, and consequently be instructed in them on account of carnal heretics.

## ¶2 · 24.10–24.18

> [p. 24] Sed contra ista arguitur per easdem leges ecclesie; nam in De Consecracione, distinccione II, dicit Pius papa: Si per negligenciam aliquid de sanguine stillaverit super terram, lingua lambetur; si super tabulam, tabula radetur, sicut terra vel aliud ipsum recipiens, et igne consumetur et cinis iuxta altare condatur. Et multe sunt leges talis sentencie que omnes sonant quod illud sensibile liquidum et fluxibile contentum in calice est realiter sanguis Christi.

But against this [it is argued by appeal to]{g:arguitur-per} the same laws of the church; for in *De consecratione*, distinction 2, [Pope]{g:papa} Pius says: "If through negligence anything of the blood drips upon the ground, it shall be licked up with the tongue; if upon a board, the board shall be scraped, as shall the ground or anything else that receives it, and it shall be consumed by fire and the ash buried beside the altar."[^8] And there are many laws of this sense, which all sound to the effect that that perceptible liquid and flowing thing contained in the chalice is [really]{g:realiter} the blood of Christ.

## ¶3 · 24.19–24.29

> [p. 24] Hic dicitur quod multa dicta decretorum oportet sane intelligere ad sensum quem debent facere; unde figurative vel mistice sepe in locucionibus istius materie figuratum intelligitur per figuram, ut sicut grammaticus dicit quod dominus est nomen, et loycus quod hoc signum homo est species, sic dicit theologus quod panis est corpus Christi et vinum eius sanguis, quod oportet figurative intelligi signo supposito pro signato. Si autem doctores signorum intelligant predicacionem secundum essenciam vel non figurativam sed formalem, est error in ianuis.

To this [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that many sayings of [the decrees]{g:decreta} must be understood soundly, in the sense they ought to make; hence [figuratively]{g:figurative} or mystically, in expressions on this matter, the [thing figured]{g:figuratum} is often understood by the figure; so that, as the grammarian says that "lord" is a noun, and the logician that this [sign]{g:signum} "man" is a species, so the theologian says that the bread is the body of Christ and the wine his blood, which must be understood figuratively, the sign standing for the [thing signified]{g:signatum}.[^9] But if [the doctors of signs]{g:doctores-signorum} understand a [predication by essence]{g:predicacio-secundum-essenciam}, or one not figurative but [formal]{g:formalis}, error is at the doors.[^10]

## ¶4 · 24.30–25.18

> [p. 24] Et in ista equivocacione laborant multi. Unde absit katholicum credere quod propter effusionem huiusmodi [p. 25] sacramenti in calicem exhinc sanguis Domini effundatur. Tunc enim non preciperet ecclesia sanguinem illum comburere et cineres combusti Domini in ecclesia in detestacionem tam horrendi facinoris tamquam reliquias remanere; sed loquitur de nudo sacramento quod figurative nominat rem signatam modo quo posset vocare quamlibet creaturam Deum suum, cum sit signum efficax Dei sui. Tales enim locuciones tropicas non debemus nos admittere generaliter, quia sic diceremus quod Christus cotidie effundit per multos cados plus de sanguine quam umquam habuit hic in terris, et totidem racione multitudinis sui sanguinis foret complexione intemperatus, cum acquireret cotidie humores grossos inglorificatos cum aliis inconvenientibus reducendis; et sic glosari debet cantus ecclesie, dum dicit Quod fit vinum sanguis Christi, Etsi sensus deficit.

And many labour under this equivocation. Hence God forbid that a catholic believe that through the pouring out of such [25] a sacrament into the chalice the blood of the Lord is thereby poured out.[^11] For then the church would not command that that blood be burned, and the ashes of the burned Lord remain in the church as relics, in detestation of so horrible a [misdeed]{g:facinus}; but it speaks of the bare sacrament, which figuratively names the thing signified, in the way one could call any [creature]{g:creatura} its God, since it is an effective sign of its God.[^12] For we ought not to admit such [tropical expressions]{g:locucio-tropica} generally, because so we should say that Christ daily pours out through many casks more blood than he ever had here on earth, and by reason of the [multitude]{g:multitudo} of his blood he would be just as often intemperate in [complexion]{g:complexio}, since he would daily acquire gross unglorified humours, with other [absurdities]{g:inconveniens}[^13] to be brought in; and so the chant of the church must be [glossed]{g:glosare} where it says: "That wine becomes the blood of Christ, Though sense fail."[^14]

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *In mutando visum*: the object's acting on (changing) the sense, the scholastic *immutatio* of the organ. *Quin per idem …*: any reason given for the body's being seen with the host would equally make it heard, smelt, tasted and touched with the host, which 21.30–22.20 has shown absurd.

[^2]: **S.** John 20:29 (Loserth); Wyclif adds *eorum opposita*, "their opposites", the opposites of the conclusions.

[^3]: **C.** *Sentencia*: CONTEXTUAL; "teaching" here and at 23.28. At 24.17 (*leges talis sentencie*) it is the meaning of a text, the ruled "sense".

[^4]: **S.** Gratian, *De cons.* D. 2 c. 41, under Augustine's name (Loserth: *Decreti Tertia Pars, De Consecratione, dist.* II *cap.* XLI; the modern form is ours) (LEDGER K01d-1).

[^5]: **S.** *De cons.* D. 2 c. 43, under Ambrose's name (Loserth: *dist.* II *cap.* XLIII; modern form ours) (LEDGER K01d-2). The placing of *inquit* after *Similitudinem* is the print's; the words given to the objector, "Perhaps you say", are the canon's.

[^6]: **C/S.** Three authorities are counted: Augustine c. 41, Ambrose c. 43, and *tercio* Augustine's canon *Prima quidem*, *De cons.* D. 2 c. 44 (Loserth; modern form ours) (LEDGER K01d-3). John 6:67 is cited, not quoted (DR "After this, many of his disciples went back"). The last clause (*sed spiritualiter per idem non videret …*) is compressed: "but [it is to be understood] spiritually; by the same token [the believer] would not see …", the subject of *videret* left unexpressed.

[^7]: **S.** *De cons.* D. 2 c. 45 (Loserth, line 29, "cap. XLV", plate), under Augustine's name; the words are from his *Enarr. in Ps.* 98; to be checked (LEDGER K01d-4).

[^8]: **S.** *De cons.* D. 2 c. 27, under the name of Pope Pius (Loserth: *dist.* II *cap.* XXVII; modern form ours) (LEDGER K01d-5).

[^9]: **C.** *Signo supposito pro signato*: the sign "supposits for", stands for, the thing signified, in the logicians' sense of *supponere*. The grammarian's and the logician's examples are propositions about words ("'lord' is a noun"; "'man' is a species"), set beside the theologian's *panis est corpus Christi*, whose subject stands for what the sign signifies.

[^10]: **C.** *Est error in ianuis*: "error is at the doors", close at hand; Wyclif names the error only by its predication.

[^11]: **C/T.** *Effusionem … in calicem*: printed so (AB *in calice*, "in the chalice"). The sense the argument wants is the spilling of the sacrament from the chalice, the case of Pius's canon; the print is translated as it stands.

[^12]: **C.** *Posset vocare quamlibet creaturam Deum suum*: the subject of *posset* is indefinite ("one could"); *suum* and *sui* refer to the creature, which is a sign of the God who made it.

[^13]: **C.** *Inconvenientibus*: CONTEXTUAL; "absurdities", the unfitting consequences in argument. ABE read *inquientibus* (25.15).

[^14]: **S.** Wyclif's form of the line. Loserth gives the hymn's reading as *Fitque sanguis Christi merum, etsi sensus deficit* (Daniel, *Thesaurus hymnologicus* I, 245), the *Pange lingua* attributed to Thomas; to be checked (LEDGER K01d-6).
