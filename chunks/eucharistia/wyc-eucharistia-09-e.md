# *De eucharistia*, chapter 9, part 5: the saints call it bread; the decree, the pope's dispensing, and the truth of the words

## ¶1 · 288.11–290.3

> [p. 288] Sed multi dixerunt quod inpossibile est istam sentenciam de accidentibus esse veram. Et ita (ut utar verbis reprehensoris) quero qui sunt illi presumentes seminare istam infundabilem et novellam sentenciam contra diffiniciones sanctorum patrum quas Spiritus Sanctus in eis posuit. Recurrendum est (inquam) ad unum primum quod sit metrum omnium aliorum, cum illi non sunt credibiles, quia illi, nec potest doceri quod illi ut sic sentenciantes a Spiritu Sancto fuerunt illustrati. Ideo absit a nobis presumpcio ut mendicemus quod credatur nobis tanquam nobis in ista antiqua sentencia propter peccatum quod Deus novit ad tempus sopita sed tamquam preconibus scripture et sanctorum ac antiquorum doctorum illud dicencium. Unde Lanfrancus in libello suo contra Beringarium (velit nolit) fatetur quod sancti doctores ut Ambrosius, Jeronimus et specialiter Augustinus vocant generaliter hoc sacramentum panem et nunquam accidens, quia (ut vere dicit) hoc sacramentum habet signa que naturaliter significant formam panis. [p. 289] Cum ergo scriptura sacra et sancti doctores cum lege ecclesie sic locuntur, nos debemus indubie sic loqui ad sensum eorum et glossare eos quod per accidens panis intelligunt ipsum panem, sicut ipsi econtra de nobis asserunt; et potest fundari modus loquendi in hoc quod mundi divicie que sunt in suis naturis substancie vocantur accidencia, quia accidunt vel adiacent ipsis divitibus, sicut pani accidit quod sit signum corporis; ideo illud signum dicitur sibi accidens. Nec dubium quin non sit accidens univoce ut accidens limitatur ad novem genera, vel ut locuntur philosophi, Augustinus et sancti alii. Unde solebam exponere adversarios quod tale accidens significat naturaliter tam subiectum quam se ipsum, et prius causaliter sed non quoad consequenciam se ipsum significat; ideo subiectum mediante tali significacione sui accidentis suscipit equivoce predicaciones amborum; et quia vigili significacio accidentis est insopibilis sed significacio distincta substancie est racione corporis Christi sopita, ideo dicitur quod accidens manet sine subiecto suo in actu; unde philosophi vocant universale tempus et sensibile encia in actu per animam, quando sunt actualiter apprehensa; et aliter dicuntur encia in potencia. Sed illud dictum licet fuerit quoad sentenciam adversam plus coloratum, nescio tamen si ipsum intenderant; unde quoad primum de decreto ecclesie quoad fidem miror multum quomodo non vident quod nullus episcopus (in quantum Avinonicus vel Romanus) habet potestatem ad hoc decidendum, cum nec (in quantum talis) habet supereminenter scienciam fidei scripture vel vivacitatem racionis; nec [p. 290] habet ut sic supereminenter graciam aut virtutem ad regendum se ipsum et populum in fide domini nostri Jesu Christi.

But many have said that it is impossible that this [teaching]{g:sentencia} about accidents is true. And so (to use the words of the critic)[^1] I ask who are those who presume to sow this teaching, [without foundation]{g:infundabilis} and newer, against the definitions of the holy fathers which the Holy Spirit put in them. One must have recourse (I say) to one first [thing] which is to be the measure of all the others, since those are not to be believed because they are those,[^2] nor can it be taught that they, pronouncing so as such, were enlightened by the Holy Spirit. So far from us be the presumption of [begging]{g:mendicare} that we be believed as ourselves in this ancient teaching, laid asleep for a time on account of a sin which God knows; but [that we be believed] as [heralds]{g:preco} of scripture and of the [saints]{g:sancti} and ancient doctors who say it. Hence Lanfranc, in his little book against Berengar, confesses willy-nilly that the holy doctors, such as Ambrose, Jerome and especially Augustine, generally call this [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} [bread]{g:panis} and never [accident]{g:accidens}, because (as he [truly]{g:vere} says) this sacrament has signs which naturally signify the form of bread.[^3] [289] Since, then, sacred scripture and the holy doctors with the law of the [church]{g:ecclesia} speak thus, we ought without doubt to speak so, in their sense, and to gloss them[^4] [as meaning] that by "accident of bread" they understand the bread itself, just as they on the other hand assert of us; and the manner of speaking can be founded on this, that the riches of the world, which in their natures are [substances]{g:substancia}, are called accidents, because they befall or lie beside the rich themselves, just as it befalls bread that it is a sign of the body; so that sign is called its accident. Nor is there doubt that it is not an accident [univocally]{g:univoce}, as "accident" is restricted to the nine genera, or as the philosophers, Augustine and other saints speak. Hence I used to expound the adversaries [as meaning] that such an accident naturally signifies both the subject and itself, and signifies itself first causally but not as to [inference]{g:consequencia};[^5] so the subject, by means of such signification of its accident, admits [equivocally]{g:equivoce} the predications of both; and since to one awake the signification of the accident cannot be laid asleep,[^6] but the distinct signification of the substance is laid asleep by reason of the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}, so it is said that the accident remains without its subject in act; hence the philosophers call the universal, time and the perceptible beings in act through the soul, when they are actually apprehended, and otherwise they are called beings in [potency]{g:potencia}.[^7] But although that saying, as to the adverse teaching, was [given more colour]{g:colorare}, yet I do not know whether they had meant it. Hence, as to the first, on the [decree]{g:decretum} of the church as to the faith,[^8] I wonder greatly how they do not see that no bishop ([insofar as]{g:in-quantum} he is of Avignon or of Rome) has [power]{g:potestas} to decide this, since he does not (insofar as he is such) have pre-eminently the knowledge of the faith of scripture or liveliness of reason; nor [290] does he have, as such, pre-eminently grace or [virtue]{g:virtus} to rule himself and the people in the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ.

## ¶2 · 290.4–291.2

> [p. 290] Nec dubium quin ad faciendum hoc opus alterum istorum requiritur vel utrumque. Et assumptum patet de multis episcopis talibus post dotacionem ecclesie qui erant ydiote heretici et precipui Antichristi; ymmo licet concedatur quod quicunque locus quem incolunt fuerit plenus virtute et gracia per se stante (sic continue ipsas inhamarant), adhuc foret totum hoc istis inpertinens, quia facere qualitates istas informare, sine quo nichil valent in opus Domini singulare. Videant ergo si inter viantes simillime secuntur Christum et de isto sine pompa congaudeant, considerantes quod secundum tradiciones proprias, XXᵃ distinccione, capitulo primo, quoad causas secundum legem suam decidendas supereminet potestas sua cesarea, sed quoad veritatem scripture et mores est Augustino et ceteris doctoribus plus credendum. Unde infidelis videtur sentencia Graciani XXV, questione prima, Ideo permittente Domino quod, sicut Christus fuit dominus legis sue et tamen illi obtemperare voluit et ipsam implere, sic prima sedes, sic papa tribuit ius et auctoritatem canonibus et quandoque ostendit se dominum canonum sive suorum sive aliorum, iubendo, diffiniendo seu agendo contrarium, licet (inquit) sibi contra generalia decreta specialia privilegia indulgere. Ymmo in omni canone subintelligitur condicio nisi [p. 291] auctoritas Romane ecclesie aliter fieri mandaverit aut permiserit.

Nor is there doubt that for doing this work one or the other of these is required, or both. And the [assumption]{g:assumptum} is clear from many such bishops after the [endowment]{g:dotacio} of the church, who were [unlettered]{g:ydiota} heretics and chief Antichrists; indeed, though it be granted that whatever place they inhabit were full of virtue and grace standing [of itself]{g:per-se} (so continually do they adulterate them),[^9] still all this would be irrelevant to them, since [what is needed is] to make these qualities [inform]{g:informare}, without which they avail nothing for the singular work of the Lord.[^10] Let them see, then, whether among [those on the road]{g:viantes} they follow Christ most closely, and of this let them rejoice together without [boasting]{g:pompa}, considering that according to their own traditions, distinction 20, chapter 1,[^11] as to causes to be decided according to their law their caesarean power is pre-eminent, but as to the truth of scripture and morals more credence is to be given to Augustine and the other doctors. Hence the teaching of Gratian in [Causa] 25, question 1, *Ideo permittente Domino*,[^12] seems [unbelieving]{g:infidelis}: that, "just as Christ was the lord of his law, and yet willed to obey it and to fulfil it, so the first see, so the pope, gives [right]{g:ius} and [authority]{g:auctoritas} to the canons, and sometimes shows himself lord of the canons, whether his own or others', by commanding, defining or acting to the contrary"; "it is lawful" (he says) "for him to grant special [privileges]{g:privilegium} against general decrees. Indeed in every canon the condition is [understood as implied]{g:subintelligere}: unless [291] the authority of the Roman church has commanded or permitted it to be done otherwise."

## ¶3 · 291.3–291.17

> [p. 291] Ista autem sentencia clare docet omnes decretales et decreta Romane ecclesie de quanto non fundantur in scriptura esse superflua et iniusta. Superflua quidem, quia dependent ex voluntate persone nimis vertibilis ad omnia genera peccatorum. Intencionem itaque suam que est origo legis debet legista consulere, quia non valet lex sua nisi de quanto fuerit ab ipso approbata; ymmo cum (velit papa nolit) permittit fieri contrarium legi sue, cassata est eius efficacia et superfluitas declarata. Est secundo iniusta, quia quandocunque pape placuerit revocanda, et non est revocanda nisi fuerit iniusta, ergo est continue parate iniusta; quia ergo nescimus voluntatem vel factum illius pape vel priorum, ideo quiescimus in fide scripture, cuius auctoritatem scimus immobilem.

And this teaching clearly teaches that all the [decretals]{g:decretalis} and decrees of the Roman church, insofar as they are not founded in scripture, are superfluous and unjust. Superfluous indeed, because they depend on the will of a person [exceedingly]{g:nimis} turnable to every kind of sin. The [legist]{g:legiste} therefore must consult his intention, which is the origin of the law, for his law is of no worth except insofar as it has been approved by him; indeed, since (whether the pope will or no) he permits the contrary of his law to be done, its efficacy is annulled and its superfluity declared. It is, secondly, unjust, because it is to be revoked whenever it pleases the pope, and it is not to be revoked unless it is unjust; therefore it is continually unjust in readiness.[^13] Since, then, we do not know the will or deed of that pope or of the earlier ones, therefore we rest in the faith of scripture, whose authority we know to be [immovable]{g:immobilis}.

## ¶4 · 291.18–291.23

> [p. 291] Nec alii pape quoad fidem credimus, nisi de quanto se fundaverit in scriptura in qua scimus nec Petrum nec alium apostolum tantum dominium usurpasse sed consuluisse Deum per sortem, quando defecit eis inspiracio singularis. Patet Act. primo de eleccione Mathie.

Nor do we believe any other pope as to the faith, except insofar as he has founded himself in scripture, in which we know that neither Peter nor any other apostle usurped so great a [lordship]{g:dominium}, but that they consulted God by lot when singular inspiration failed them. It is clear in Acts 1 of the election of Matthias.[^14]

## ¶5 · 291.24–292.14

> [p. 291] Quoad secundum de veritate verborum sacramenti patet quod ipsa sunt verissima, cum sunt constans et salubris sentencia domini veritatis; et cum panis aut vinum demonstratur ubique nec dicta transsubstanciacio nec ydemptificacio nec inpanacio potest esse, non restat nisi sensus tropicus scilicet quod hoc figurat sacramentaliter corpus Christi. Quod autem demonstratur panis aut vinum, sonat quadruplex [p. 292] evangelium (ut recitavi sepius); et idem dicunt sancti doctores concorditer, scilicet Ambrosius, Jeronimus et specialiter Augustinus et alii sequentes eos, ut usus et leges ecclesie cum postillantibus super ipsos, ut Rabanus De Sacramento Altaris et alii qui omnes dicunt quod panis potest esse corpus Christi, erit et est corpus Christi, quod orant ut fiat. Illud autem esset stultissimum, nisi virtute verborum sacramentalium hoc fieret, et per consequens nisi in verbis consecracionis demonstraretur panis, et consequenter necesse est predictum sensum verbis Christi addicere; unde non solum doctores novelli sed et communitas artis humane clamat nedum quod de pane sed ex verbi Dei omnipotencia ipse panis fiet corpus Christi.

As to the second, on the truth of the words of the sacrament, it is clear that they are most true, since they are the constant and wholesome teaching of the Lord of truth; and since bread or wine is demonstrated everywhere,[^15] and neither the said [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio} nor [identification]{g:ydemptificacio} nor [impanation]{g:inpanacio} can be, there remains only the [tropical meaning]{g:sensus-tropicus}, namely that this [figures]{g:figurare} [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} the body of Christ. And that bread or wine is demonstrated, the fourfold [292] gospel sounds (as I have [recited]{g:recitare} often); and the holy doctors say the same in accord, namely Ambrose, Jerome and especially Augustine, and others following them, as do the [use]{g:usus} and [laws of the church]{g:leges-ecclesie} with those who write postils upon them, such as Rabanus, *On the Sacrament of the Altar*,[^16] and others, who all say that bread can be the body of Christ, will be and is the body of Christ, which they pray may come about.[^17] Now that would be most foolish unless this came about by the power of the sacramental words, and consequently unless bread were demonstrated in the words of [consecration]{g:consecracio}; and consequently it is necessary to add the aforesaid meaning to the words of Christ. Hence not only the [newer doctors]{g:novelli} but also the community of human art cry out [not only]{g:nedum} that [the body is made] of bread, but that by the omnipotence of the word of God the bread itself will become the body of Christ.[^18]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 288.11: It is the saints who declare the doctrine of accidents to be impossible.
- 288.20: We do not speak on our own authority, but as heralds of Scripture and the fathers.
- 289.7: Substances are sometimes called accidents; as riches are accidental to their owner.
- 289.12: Again in naming the accident we often imply the subject.
- 290.4: To settle this question knowledge of Scripture and God's grace are required.
- 291.3: The pope's power of dispensing shews that many laws are unjust or superfluous.
- 291.24: The sacramental words are true, but they are used in a tropical sense.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Reprehensoris*: the critic of 287.13–15, whose question ("who then are we who presume…?") Wyclif turns back on the adversaries. *Sentencia*: "teaching" (CONTEXTUAL row), the position taught.

[^2]: **C.** *Cum illi non sunt credibiles, quia illi*: "because they are those [men]", on their own say-so; *illi* are those who call the teaching impossible. *Unum primum* has no noun: we supply "thing".

[^3]: **S.** Loserth refers to Lanfranc, *Opera*, pp. 159, 163, 191, 192, and quotes: *Quod vero panis dicitur: consueto sacrorum codicum more id fit, qui res quaslibet saepe vocant nominibus illarum rerum ex quibus fiunt* ("that it is called bread comes about by the accustomed manner of the sacred books, which often call things by the names of the things from which they are made") (*De corpore et sanguine Domini* c. 20, PL 150, 438A) (LEDGER K09e-1).

[^4]: **C.** *Glossare eos*: "them" taken as the adversaries, who gloss the saints' "bread" as accident ("just as they … assert of us"); Wyclif returns the gloss, reading their "accident of bread" as the bread. If *eos* are the saints, "gloss them" is only "expound them".

[^5]: **T/C.** Loserth: "this place seems to be corrupt" (289.13); E *ad infirmos* for *adversarios*, D *signat* for *significat*. Translated as printed. *Prius causaliter sed non quoad consequenciam*: prior in the order of causes, not in the order of inference.

[^6]: **T.** The print's *vigili* is taken as a dative, "to one awake"; the MSS read *vigil* ("the signification of the accident is wakeful and cannot be laid asleep"), to the same sense. ABCD *sed signo* (289.18–19).

[^7]: **C.** *Potencia*: CONTEXTUAL row, here Aristotle's "potency" set against act.

[^8]: **C.** "The first" of the three heads of 287.13–15, the decree; the second, the truth of the words, follows at 291.24; the third, the devotion of the people, at 293.33.

[^9]: **T/C.** The print *inhamarant* (ABCD *inhamerant*) is no word; Loserth suggests *incamarant* (Du Cange: *incamarare* = *adulterare*), which we translate. *Per se stante*: "standing of itself" (the CONTEXTUAL row's first sense), virtue and grace taken apart from any subject they inform.

[^10]: **C.** *Quia facere qualitates istas informare* has no governing verb: we supply "what is needed is". Virtue and grace in the place avail nothing unless they inform the bishop.

[^11]: **S/T.** *Decretum* D. 20 c. 1 (modern form ours). Loserth: some words have dropped out here; the sentence does not occur in c. 1; compare the ordinary gloss on D. 20 (LEDGER K09e-2). The thought is Gratian's dictum before c. 1, D. 20 d.a.c. 1 § 1: the expositors *in sacrarum quidem scripturarum expositionibus eis praeponuntur, in causis vero definiendis secundum post eos locum merentur* (LEDGER K09e-3).

[^12]: **S/G.** *Decretum* C. 25 q. 1 c. 16 (modern form ours), which Wyclif names by its incipit; the words he gives are Gratian's dictum after it, d.p.c. 16 § 1 (*Sacrosancta Romana ecclesia ius et auctoritatem sacris canonibus impertitur … seu iubendo, seu definiendo, seu aliter agendo, se decretorum dominos … esse ostendunt*), whence his *sentencia Graciani*; "very loosely quoted" (Loserth) (LEDGER K09e-4). *Ius*: "right", the canons' binding force, not "law".

[^13]: **C.** *Continue parate iniusta*: "unjust in readiness", always ready to be found unjust, since every such law stands to be revoked at the pope's pleasure.

[^14]: **S.** Acts 1:23–26, "and the lot fell upon Matthias" (1:26). ABCE *alio* for *alii* (291.18).

[^15]: **C.** *Demonstratur*: what the demonstrative pronoun of the words of institution points to; "demonstrated" throughout. AC read *ubique nec sonat quadruplex evangelium dicta* (291.27), displacing the next clause.

[^16]: **S.** Rabanus, *De sacramento altaris*: Wyclif's title; no work of Rabanus bears it. The matter is in his exposition of the canon of the mass, *De sacris ordinibus* c. 19, *De ordine missae* (PL 112, 1192), on *ut nobis corpus et sanguis fiat*: *Quis unquam crederet, quod panis in carnem potuisset converti … nisi ipse Salvator diceret* (LEDGER K09e-5).

[^17]: **C.** *Quod orant ut fiat*: "which [thing] they pray may come about", the bread's becoming the body. D *quod fiat*.

[^18]: **C.** The bracketed words supply the ellipsis of *nedum quod de pane*. *Communitas artis humane*: taken as the common way of speaking in the human crafts, which name a thing by what it is made of. C has *addicere* on an erasure (292.11).
