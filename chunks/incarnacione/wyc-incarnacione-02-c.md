# *De benedicta incarnacione*, chapter 2, part 3: the third Arian error, Christ not truly a man; the thief in paradise and the descent into hell

## ¶1 · 21.23–22.2

> [p. 21] Tercio antem stat opinio Arrii in his que ex predictis erroribus sequuntur¹³: ut puta¹⁴ quod Christus nedum¹⁵ est univoce homo cum aliis sed non est homo simpliciter, cum non potest esse homo si non sit anima intellectiva; et per consequens non est passus¹⁶ mortuus aut sepultus, et sic de quibuscunque predicatis humanitatem Christi concernentibus.¹⁷ Quamvis enim dici possit quod angelus, coniunctus nature corporee tanquam motrix, sit homo; verumtamen est alterius speciei quam totus vel completus homo, id est, [p. 22] natura integra ex corpore et anima composita: homo enim est racione spiritus eiusdem speciei cum angelis et non racione nature corporee.

And third, the [opinion]{g:opinio} of Arius stands in the things that follow from the aforesaid errors:[^1] as, for example, that Christ, let alone being [univocally]{g:univoce} a man with others, is not a man [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}, since he cannot be a man if he is not an intellective soul; and consequently he did not suffer, die, or was buried, and so of whatever predicates concern [the humanity]{g:humanitas} of Christ.[^2] For although it could be said that an angel, joined to a bodily [nature]{g:natura} as its mover, is a man, nevertheless it is of another species than the whole or complete man, that is, [22] the integral nature composed of body and soul: for [such a] man is, by reason of the spirit, of the same species with the angels, and not by reason of the bodily nature.[^3]

## ¶2 · 22.3–24.3

> [p. 22] Et per hoc patet quod quidam minus intelligentes Augustinum putant ipsum contradicere sibi ipsi, quando dicit quod anima non completus aut totus¹ homo: nam² verum dicit ad sensum³ expositum. Unde Epistola 40 ad Dardanum solvendo questionem de dicto Salvatoris⁴ Luc. 23 quo⁵ promisit latroni quod foret secum illo die in paradiso; 'antequam, inquit, sciatur utrum secundum deitatem⁶ vel⁷ secundum humanitatem intelligatur promissio Veritatis, oportet videre quomodo sit loquendum de homine. Non, inquit Augustinus, sicut quidam heretici dicunt, Verbum Dei⁸ et carnem sine anima humana⁹ suscepit, sic quod Verbum esset carni pro anima; vel Verbum Dei carnem et animam sine mente, ut Verbum Dei esset anime¹⁰ pro mente. Sed accipis, inquit ad Dardanum, hominem Christum; sicut¹¹ superius aisti quod Christum Deum non crederes nisi perfectum hominem credidisses. Profecto, inquit, cum dicis hominem, perfectam totam illic naturam humanam vis intelligere. Non est¹² autem homo perfectus si vel anima carni vel anime ipsi mens¹³ humana defuerit.'¹⁴ Et illam naturam complete ex anima et corpore compositam vocat super Joh. Ome. 47¹⁵ totum hominem: quam¹⁶ notum est ex dictis De Anima distingui tam a corpore quam anima¹⁷ licet omnia ista tria sint eadem persona hominis. Sic ergo¹⁸ intelligendus est Augustinus quandocunque videtur dicere quod corpus vel anima non est homo; id est, non est¹⁹ tota vel completa natura ex corpore et anima composita. Sed pro solucione Dardani questionis videtur Augustino catholicum quod promissio facta latroni intelligatur secundum [p. 23] humanitatem Christi:¹ sed cum corpus Christi¹ sepultum sit solum in orto, ut patet Ioh. 19,² relinquitur quod sue anime in parasceue erant simul; quia modicum felicitatis fuisset animam latronis fuisse cum solo corpore Christi mortuo in sepulcro. Et cum vera mors fuit in Christi corpore, cum aliter non fuisset vera resurreccio; querendum est—quo devenit anima³ Christi in illo triduo? Quod cum evangelium non reserat, supplet⁴ beatus Petrus Actuum 2° probans ex illo Psalmi 15 dicto de Christo ‘quoniam non⁵ derelinques animam meam in inferno, nec dabis sanctum tuum videre corrupcionem,’ quod anima Christi descendit in illo triduo ad limbum patrum, quem Veritas Luc. 16. vocat ‘sinum Abrahe,’ ubi patres veteris testamenti feliciter quieverunt.⁶ Quamvis enim dictum psalmiste sit negativum, impertinenter tamen menti prophetice diceretur, nisi intenderet Messiam secundum mentem visitare inferos et statim sine media incarceracione corpori reuniri.⁶ Et hinc fundatus est de Christo articulus fidei, quod ‘descendit ad inferos.’ Cum ergo paradisus generale nomen est ubicunque feliciter⁷ vivitur, quid obest animam latronis fuisse cum anima Christi in regione quiescencium congaudendo et non in regione inferiori dolencium,⁸ inter quas est ‘magnum chaos’ secundum veritatem evangelicam interceptum?⁹ Redeundo¹⁰ ergo ad propositum patet ex dictis quantum differt ista posicio ab heresi Arriana. Ideo est michi verisimile quod doctores moderni¹¹ cum non ydemptificarent que minus intelligunt,¹² locuntur michi temptative, yronice, vel iocose. Yronice quidem, quia Arrius negavit Verbum Dei esse idem substancialiter Deo Patri, sed creatum esse secundum supremam substanciam, que est Christus. Ego autem dico [p. 24] quod Christus absolute necessario¹ est eadem deitas que est Pater,² nec posse secundum supremam naturam, que est Christus, creari vel fieri.

And by this it is clear that certain [people], understanding Augustine too little, think that he contradicts himself when he says that the soul is not the complete or whole man: for he says what is true in the sense expounded.[^4] Hence in letter 40, to Dardanus, solving the question about the saying of the Saviour, Luke 23, by which he promised the thief that he would be with him that day in paradise: "Before," he says, "it is known whether the promise of the Truth is understood [as to the Godhead]{g:secundum-deitatem} or [as to the humanity]{g:secundum-humanitatem}, it is necessary to see how one is to speak of man. Not," says Augustine, "as certain heretics say, [that] [the Word]{g:verbum} of God took up flesh without a human soul, so that the Word was to the flesh in place of a soul; or [that] the Word of God [took up] flesh and soul without mind, so that the Word of God was to the soul in place of mind. But you accept," he says to Dardanus, "the man Christ; as you said above that you would not believe Christ God unless you had believed him perfect man. Truly," he says, "when you say man, you wish the whole perfect human nature to be understood there. And a man is not perfect if either soul is lacking to the flesh or a human mind to the soul itself."[^5] And that nature, composed completely of soul and body, he calls, on John, homily 47, "the whole man": which, as is known from what is said in *On the Soul*, is distinguished both from the body and from the soul, although all these three are the same [person]{g:persona} of the man.[^6] So, then, Augustine is to be understood whenever he seems to say that body or soul is not man: that is, it is not the whole or complete nature composed of body and soul. But for the solution of Dardanus's question it seems catholic to Augustine that the promise made to the thief be understood as to [23] the humanity of Christ; now since the body of Christ was buried only in the garden, as is clear from John 19, it remains that their souls were together on the Parasceve; for it would have been little felicity for the soul of the thief to have been with the dead body of Christ alone in the sepulchre.[^7] And since there was true death in the body of Christ, since otherwise there would not have been a true resurrection, it must be asked: where did the soul of Christ go in [the three days]{g:triduum}? Since the gospel does not disclose this, blessed Peter supplies it, Acts 2, proving from that [text] of Psalm 15, said of Christ, "Because thou wilt not leave my soul in hell; nor wilt thou give thy holy one to see corruption," that the soul of Christ descended in the three days to the limbo of the fathers, which the Truth, Luke 16, calls "Abraham's bosom", where the fathers of the old testament happily rested.[^8] For although the psalmist's saying is negative, yet it would be said [irrelevantly]{g:impertinens} to the prophetic mind unless he meant that the Messiah, as to the mind, visits those below and at once, without intervening incarceration, is reunited to the body.[^9] And hence the [article of faith]{g:articulus-fidei} was founded concerning Christ, that "he descended into hell". Since, therefore, paradise is a general name for wherever there is happy living, what prevents the soul of the thief from having been with the soul of Christ in the region of those at rest, rejoicing with him, and not in the lower region of those in pain, between which there is "a great chaos" fixed, according to the gospel truth?[^10] Returning, then, to the point, it is clear from what has been said how much this [position]{g:posicio} differs from the Arian heresy. Therefore it seems likely to me that [the modern doctors]{g:moderni-doctores}, since they would not identify the things they understand too little, speak to me tentatively, ironically, or jokingly.[^11] Ironically indeed, because Arius denied that the Word of God is substantially the same as God the Father, but [said] that he was created as to the supreme substance, which is Christ. But I say [24] that Christ is, absolutely of necessity, the same [Godhead]{g:deitas} that is the Father, and that he cannot be created or made as to the supreme nature, which is Christ.

## ¶3 · 24.4–24.8

> [p. 24] 2° autem dixit Arrius quod Christus non habet animam sed pure carnem; spiritu illo creato³ supplente vicem anime non ypostatice, sed ut motrice, corpori copulato. Ego autem dico quod Christus nedum habet animam, sed est anima, nec posset⁴ carni nisi mediante anima ypostatice copulari.

And second, Arius said that Christ has no soul but purely flesh, that [created]{g:creatus} spirit supplying the place of the soul, joined to the body not [hypostatically]{g:ypostatice} but as its mover.[^12] But I say that Christ not only has a soul, but is a soul, and could not be joined hypostatically to the flesh except by means of the soul.

## ¶4 · 24.9–24.13

> [p. 24] 3° ubi posuit⁵ Christum non esse hominem nisi equivoce, nec proprie passum⁶ esse, mortuum, et sic de aliis que fides evangelica de Christo predicat. Ego autem⁷ dico Christum esse⁸ verissime, completissime, et univoce hominem cum quolibet fratre suo; et vere de virtute sermonis conversatum esse in terris, ut canit processus evangelicus.

Third, where he held that Christ is not a man except [equivocally]{g:equivoce}, nor properly suffered, died, and so of the other things that the gospel faith predicates of Christ. But I say that Christ is most truly, most completely, and univocally a man with every brother of his; and that truly, [in the proper sense of the words]{g:de-virtute-sermonis}, he conversed on earth, as [the course]{g:processus} of the gospel sings.[^13]

## ¶5 · 24.14–24.21

> [p. 24] Et sic, ubi Arrius peccavit in deitate Christi negando eum verum Deum⁹ eundem cum Patre; ubi eciam peccavit in humanitate Christi negando ipsum esse verum hominem; ego utrumque¹⁰ pleno corde confiteor non confundendo naturas nec¹¹ distinguendo personas in Christo. Et patet quod non, quia dixit Christum esse creaturam, censetur hereticus¹²; sed, quia dixit Christum esse puram¹³ creaturam meliorem homine; et non hominem passum abiectum et ab hominibus reprobatum.

And so, where Arius sinned in the Godhead of Christ, denying him [to be] true God, the same with the Father, and where he also sinned in the humanity of Christ, denying him to be true man, I confess both with a full heart, not confounding the natures nor dividing the persons in Christ.[^14] And it is clear that it is not because he said "Christ is a [creature]{g:creatura}" that he is judged a heretic, but because he said "Christ is a pure creature better than man", and not a man who suffered, cast down and rejected by men.[^15]

## ¶6 · 24.22–24.31

> [p. 24] Et utinam nostri loquentes attenderent ad regulam Augustini ‘quid propter quid de Christo dicitur,’ allegando complete scripturam vel determinaciones ecclesie non truncate. Ut primo libro contra Maximinianum ca° 11. ‘Audi, inquit, Maximiniane, auctoritatem synodice¹⁴ locucionis: Si quis ex nichilo Filium dicit non ex Deo Patre, anathema sit.’ Quis ergo color, si dico—Christus est creatura—sed et Arrius dixit Christum esse creaturam, quod coincido in heresim Arrianam? Vere non plus quam sequitur, si uterque nostrum dicat Christum esse aliquid¹⁵ sive Deum.

And would that our speakers attended to Augustine's rule, "what is said of Christ on account of what", [alleging]{g:allegare} scripture or the determinations of the [church]{g:ecclesia} completely, not truncated.[^16] As in the first book against Maximinus, chapter 11: "Hear," he says, "Maximinus, the [authority]{g:auctoritas} of a synodal utterance: If anyone says the Son is from nothing, not from God the Father, let him be anathema."[^17] What [colour]{g:color} is there, then, if I say "Christ is a creature", and Arius too said "Christ is a creature", [for saying] that I fall into the Arian heresy?[^18] Truly, no more than it would follow if each of us said "Christ is something" or "God".

## Notes

[^1]: **T.** *antem* (21.23) is a misprint in Harris for *autem*; the English reads *autem* (continuative, "and").
[^2]: **C.** *nedum est univoce homo … sed non est homo simpliciter*: the print's *nedum* is taken as "let alone": Christ is not even a man without qualification, still less univocally a man with others. **T.** A B C read *nedum non est*, "is not only not univocally a man … but is not a man without qualification"; the sense is the same. *Si non sit anima intellectiva*: "if he is not an intellective soul", the form Wyclif answers below (24, *est anima*).
[^3]: **C.** *tanquam motrix*: as the moving power of the body, not as its form. *Homo enim est racione spiritus*: the "man" is the angel so joined, which is of the angels' species by its spirit, and so not of the species of the complete man.
[^4]: **C.** *anima non completus aut totus homo*: the print has no verb; "is" is understood. O reads *non* for *nam* (22), and A B C *ad sermonem expositum* "in the expounded speech".
[^5]: **S.** Augustine, letter to Dardanus, *Ep.* 187 (here "letter 40"), on Luke 23:43: §§3–4, PL 33, 833 (*verified*, LEDGER I02c-1), quoted freely. Augustine's *Verbum Dei et carnem, hoc est sine anima humana* (the heretics' Christ is the Word and flesh) accounts for the stray *et*. **C.** *Verbum Dei et carnem … suscepit*: the *et* has no partner and is left untranslated. **T.** *humana* (22) is omitted by A B C.
[^6]: **S.** Augustine, *Tract. in Ioh.* 47 (A B C 40), "the whole man": 47.9, PL 35, 1737, *constat ex carne et anima, totus autem homo in Christo*, and 1738, *id est totus homo, anima et caro* (*verified*, LEDGER I02c-2). *De Anima*: Wyclif's own treatise, the *De Anima* that the prologue names as the introduction to this one, not Aristotle's. The doctrine, that one person is body, soul and the integral nature from both, is stated in *De compositione hominis* 1 (ed. Beer): *eadem persona corpus spiritus et connexio utriusque vel natura integra resultans ex ambobus* (LEDGER I02c-3). **C.** *quam … distingui*: the whole man is distinguished from body and from soul, yet the three are one person; O *quanquam* for *quam*.
[^7]: **S.** John 19:41–42 (the garden). **C.** *sue anime*: the souls of Christ and of the thief. *Sed cum corpus* brings the premiss from which *relinquitur* draws the conclusion, hence "now". **T.** O omits *sed … Christi* (23), the eye passing from *Christi* to *Christi*.
[^8]: **S.** Acts 2:27 citing Ps. 15:10; the Latin is the psalm's (*nec dabis*), and the English is DR's psalm. Luke 16:22.
[^9]: **T.** The sentence (23) is Harris's composite. A B C read *dictum psalmiste sit negatum … diceretur menti prophetice nisi intenderet Messiam visitare inferos ac statim sine mera incarnacione corpori uniri*, without *secundum mentem*; O has *incineracione corporis veniri* and otherwise the print. The print's *media incarceracione* "intervening imprisonment" and *reuniri* "be reunited" are given by no manuscript in the foot; the English follows the print. **C.** *menti prophetice*: "to the prophetic mind", what the prophet meant.
[^10]: **S.** Luke 16:26. *Interceptum* in all the manuscripts; Harris queries *interseptum* "fenced between". The English keeps DR's "fixed".
[^11]: **T.** *doctores moderni* (23): B C read *doctores mei* "my doctors", and O omits the adjective. **C.** *cum non ydemptificarent que minus intelligunt*: they would not make identical (in predication) the things they understand too little.
[^12]: **T.** *creato* (24) is the reading of B C O; M A read *increato* "uncreated". The print fits Arius's created spirit supplying the soul. **C.** *ut motrice*: as a moving power, the same sense as *motrix* (21).
[^13]: **S.** *conversatum esse in terris* echoes Baruch 3:38, "he was seen upon earth, and conversed with men". **T.** *autem* (24) is omitted by A B O.
[^14]: **C.** *peccavit in deitate*: "sinned in [the matter of] the Godhead". *Non confundendo naturas nec distinguendo personas* recalls the Athanasian Creed's *neque confundentes personas neque substantiam separantes*, applied to the natures and the person of Christ, as the Creed itself does further on: *Unus omnino, non confusione substantiae, sed unitate personae* (PL 88, 585A and 586B; *verified*, LEDGER I02c-4). B reads *non* for *nec*.
[^15]: **C.** The propositions are given as quoted. *Abiectum et ab hominibus reprobatum*: cf. 1 Pet. 2:4 "rejected indeed by men". B reorders the clause (*esse Christum censetur creaturam hereticus*); O *pure*.
[^16]: **C.** *determinaciones ecclesie*: the church's rulings, not the logician's *determinacio* (a qualifying addition), and so untagged. Augustine's rule is the one cited from the Pascentius letters (21).
[^17]: **S.** Not Augustine's words: in the *Collatio cum Maximino* 15.13 (PL 42, 730) it is Maximinus the Arian who says to Augustine *audi auctoritatem synodicae lectionis*. The synod is Ariminum (359), not Nicaea: *Si quis ex nihilo Filium dicit, et non ex Deo Patre, anathema sit* (*verified*, LEDGER I02c-5). Wyclif turns the address round (*Audi … Maximiniane*); his first book is the *Collatio*, as in chapter 2, part 2, note 9. Augustine refuses Ariminum's authority in *Contra Maximinum* II.14.3 (PL 42, 772).
[^18]: **C.** *color*: a plausible ground (the period sense). The *quod* clause states what the "colour" would be for: that from the shared words Wyclif falls into Arius's heresy. C reads *aliud* for *aliquid* (24.31).
