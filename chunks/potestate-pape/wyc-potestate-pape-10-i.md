# *De potestate pape*, chapter 10, part 9: Jerome's Roman church is the race of the predestinate

## ¶1 · 250.22–253.27

> [p. 250] Multe sunt et leges et sanctorum sentencie de preeminencia Romane ecclesie. Sed ad intelligendum ista decreta sanctorum doctorum theologie oportet postponere glossas Johannis de Deo, Johannis Andree, Johannis Monachi et eis similium, qui logice, methaphisice, theologice sanctorum sentencie sunt ignari. Oportet (inquam) notare iuxta dicta quod beatus Jeronymus loquitur de genere predestinatorum fidelium, qui principium sumpserant a Romanis. Paulus enim fuit Romanus ex genere, ut patet Act. XVI et [p. 251] XXII, et Petrus ex occisione. Illam autem predestinatorum generacionem, cuius racio est insensibilis, oportet necessario semper stare fidelem ubicumque migraverit; nec oportet nunc aliquam eius partem Romam vel Avinonam incolere vel esse presbiterum, nisi forte necessitate ex supposicione, quod est nobis ambiguum; et de tali Romana ecclesia intelligit decretum Jeronymi, quia certum est ex VIII. capitulo quod pape plurimi Romani et Avinonici erant manifesti heretici et signantissimi Antichristi. Istam autem generacionem simpliciter et concrete intelligendo non est possibile peccare dampnabiliter, quia cum sit predestinata, necessario salvabitur, et quomodocumque peccaverit, habebit veniam. Sed de tali privilegio non est pompa, cum ignoramus, qui sunt de illa ecclesia. Beatus autem Jeronymus reputative locutus est et credibiliter ex fide quam sumpsit ex apostolicis operibus huius pape, sicut sancti reputative loquuntur, quando vident prelatos declinantes a Christi vestigiis, dicentes quod sunt dampnandi vel membra dyaboli. Utrum autem beatus Jeronymus habuit revelacionem de predestinacione huius pape et iusticia suorum operum, ignoratur. Subtilitas autem huius sensus est valde remota a noticia legistarum. Et patet quomodo intelligenda est condicionalis Jeronymi quod si illa sua confessio confirmatur illius pape iudicio, quicumque impugnare voluerit foret hereticus; reputative enim vel ex revelacione asseruit, quod ille papa non confirmaret aliud nisi verum. Sed dementis foret credere quod hoc debet trahi in consequenciam de quocumque Romano pontifice, quia certum est, quod multi eorum, quantum in eis fuit, ratificarunt multas hereses propter lucrum, [p. 252] ut Gregorius, qui excessit decalogum, vel saltem pseudocardinales, qui sunt hodie cum Roberto, dicuntur dampnasse tamquam valde hereticum quod domini temporales possunt auferre temporalia ab ecclesia delinquente; et tamen Urbanus noster succedens mercede conduxit temporales dominos ad practizandum illam catholicam veritatem; sicut prior ecclesia commendavit et privilegiavit imperatorem ex hoc, quod pseudopapas deposuit et tam temporalia quam insignia pape ab illis abstulit nec non papas quos ipse instituit, approbavit, ut patet XXIII distinccione: In nomine Domini, ubi textus narrat, quomodo papa conqueritur quod crebris tunsionibus symoniace heresis sedes illa sepius est percussa, ideo volendo providere de remedio in futurum decrevit, quod mortuo papa cardinales, religiosi, clerici et layci conveniant ad eligendum unum papam idoneum de illius ecclesie gremio vel alterius, ubi magis fuerit idoneus, salvo semper privilegio imperatoris Henrici, quod in eleccione pape habeat cum suis heredibus interesse. Electus autem tamquam verus papa habebit ante consecracionem suam instar beati Gregorii potestatem disponendi papaliter de bonis ecclesie, et quemcumque qui ordinem eligendi impedierit anathematizat tamquam pessimum antichristum. Glossa autem ordinaria dicit, quod hic tangitur latenter quod legitur in cronicis, quomodo Benedictus qui successit Stephano eiectus est de pontificatu et Johannes Sabinensis episcopus factus est papa pro pecunia; cui impositum est nomen [p. 253] Silvester. Sed iste deiectus est et restitutus Benedictus, qui iterum est eiectus; et datus est papatus Johanni archipresbitero ante portam Latinam, cui impositum est nomen Gregorius; et hic ab Henrico imperatore est deiectus et ultra montes translatus; et hoc totum uno anno factum est. Et propter hoc datum fuit hoc privilegium Henrico. Hec glossa decreti. Considerarent itaque clerici nostri imponentes heresim detegentibus has leges ecclesie, quomodo ipsi ex scandalo sunt econtra heretici. Sicut secta Roberti imponit nobis quod sumus heretici, nos econtra. Si enim iuxta illud decretum Jeronymi ille est hereticus, qui impugnat acrius, quod verus papa fecerit in se ipso, et sic secte iste dicunt altrinsecus se esse hereticos, ita quod ab origine mundi, ut credo, non fuit crebrior imposicio heretice pravitatis. Sicut enim sacerdotes imposuerunt Christo blasfemiam, que est peior pars heresis, sic ignari Antichristi discipuli imponunt heresim tamquam inimicis ecclesie eis qui plus zelant pro eius privilegiis que Christus concesserat conservandis, in tantum quod quidam doctor ignarus quia adeptus est dotacionem pontificis declamat acucius in locis plus publicis, quod talis est hereticus et hostis ecclesie. Nullus enim peior est apostata, quam qui post altiorem statum ecclesie ad mundum excidit.

There are many laws and [teachings]{g:sentencia} of the saints on the [preeminence]{g:preeminencia} of the Roman [church]{g:ecclesia}.[^1] But to understand these decrees of the holy doctors of theology one must set aside the glosses of Johannes de Deo, Johannes Andreae, Johannes Monachus and those like them, who are ignorant, in logic, metaphysics and theology, of the teachings of the saints.[^2] One must (I say) note, in keeping with what has been said, that blessed Jerome speaks of the race of the [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} faithful, who had taken their beginning from the Romans. For Paul was a Roman by race, as is clear from Acts 16 and [251] 22, and Peter by his slaying.[^3] Now that generation of the predestinate, whose character is [imperceptible]{g:insensibilis},[^4] must necessarily always stand faithful wherever it has migrated; nor need any part of it now dwell in Rome or Avignon, or be a [presbyter]{g:presbiter}, except perhaps by [necessity on supposition]{g:ex-supposicione}, which is doubtful to us; and it is of such a Roman church that the decree of Jerome understands [it], for it is certain from chapter 8 that very many Roman and Avignonese popes were manifest heretics and most notable Antichrists. And understanding this generation [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} and concretely, it is not possible for it to sin damnably, since, being predestinate, it will necessarily be saved, and however it has sinned, it will have [forgiveness]{g:venia}.[^5] But there is no boasting over such a privilege, since we do not know who are of that church. And blessed Jerome spoke by estimation, and credibly, from the faith that he took from the apostolic works of this pope, as the saints speak by estimation when they see [prelates]{g:prelatus} turning aside from the footsteps of Christ, saying that they are to be damned, or members of the devil.[^6] But whether blessed Jerome had a revelation of the predestination of this [pope]{g:papa} and of the justice of his works is unknown. And the subtlety of this sense is very remote from the knowledge of the [legists]{g:legiste}. And it is clear how Jerome's conditional is to be understood, that if that confession of his is confirmed by that pope's judgement, whoever wishes to attack it would be a heretic: for he asserted, by estimation or from revelation, that that pope would confirm nothing but the true. But it would be a madman's part to believe that this must be drawn into an [inference]{g:consequencia} for any [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex} whatever,[^7] for it is certain that many of them, as far as in them lay, ratified many heresies for gain, [252] as Gregory, who exceeded the decalogue, or at least the [pseudo-cardinals]{g:pseudocardinales} who are today with Robert, are said to have condemned as very heretical [the proposition] that [temporal lords]{g:temporales-domini} can take away [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} from a delinquent church;[^8] and yet our Urban, succeeding, hired temporal lords for pay to [put into practice]{g:practizare} that catholic truth; just as the former church commended and [privileged]{g:privilegium} the emperor for having deposed [pseudo-popes]{g:pseudopapa} and taken from them both the temporal goods and the insignia of the pope, and approved the popes whom he himself instituted, as is clear in distinction 23, *In nomine Domini*, where the text tells how the pope complains that "by frequent blows of simoniacal heresy that see has been struck rather often", and therefore, wishing to provide a remedy for the future, he decreed that "on the death of the pope, cardinals, [religious]{g:religiosi}, clerics and laymen should come together to elect one suitable pope from the bosom of that church or of another, where a more suitable one may be found, saving always the privilege of the emperor Henry, that he with his heirs should have a part in the election of the pope. And the one elected, as true pope, shall have before his consecration, after the example of blessed Gregory, [power]{g:potestas} to dispose papally of the goods of the church; and whoever has impeded the order of electing he anathematizes as the worst antichrist."[^9] And the ordinary gloss says that "here is touched covertly what is read in the chronicles: how Benedict, who succeeded Stephen, was ejected from the pontificate, and John, bishop of Sabina, was made pope for money, to whom was given the name [253] Silvester. But he was cast out and Benedict restored, who was ejected again; and the papacy was given to John, archpriest at the Latin Gate, to whom was given the name Gregory; and he was cast out by the emperor Henry and taken across the mountains; and all this was done in one year. And for this reason this privilege was given to Henry."[^10] This is the gloss on the decree. Let our clerics therefore consider, who impute heresy to those who uncover these laws of the church, how they themselves, by the scandal, are on the contrary heretics.[^11] Just as the sect of Robert imputes to us that we are heretics, we [impute it to them] in return. For if, according to that decree of Jerome, he is a heretic who attacks too sharply what the true pope has done in himself, then these sects too say of one another that they are heretics, so that from the origin of the world, as I believe, there has not been a more frequent imputation of [heretical depravity]{g:heretica-pravitas}.[^12] For just as the priests imputed blasphemy to Christ, which is the worse part of heresy, so the ignorant disciples of Antichrist impute heresy, as to enemies of the church, to those who are more zealous for preserving its privileges, which Christ had granted; so much so that a certain ignorant doctor, because he has obtained the pontiff's [endowment]{g:dotacio}, declaims more sharply in the more public places that such a man is a heretic and an enemy of the church.[^13] For no apostate is worse than one who, after a higher [state]{g:status} of the church, has fallen away to the world.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *sentencie*: "teachings", positions taught (GLOSSARY's CONTEXTUAL row), not judicial sentences; so throughout this chunk.
[^2]: **R.** Johannes de Deo, Johannes Andreae and Johannes Monachus (Jean Lemoine), canonists of the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries (LEDGER P10i-1, to be checked). **C.** *logice, methaphisice, theologice* are adverbs qualifying *ignari*: they lack the logic, metaphysics and theology needed to read the saints. *postponere*: "set aside", literally "put after".
[^3]: **S.** Acts 16:37 and 22:25–28, Paul a Roman citizen; Wyclif's *ex genere* makes it a matter of race. *Petrus ex occisione*: Peter became Roman by his martyrdom at Rome.
[^4]: **C.** *racio* here is the ground by which the generation is what it is (predestination), not the technical "aspect" of GLOSSARY's *racio* row; "imperceptible" because no one can see who is predestinate (251.15).
[^5]: **T.** B reads *simpliciter conecte* for *simpliciter et concrete* (251.12); the print governs.
[^6]: **G.** *reputative* (also 251.26, 251.30): "by estimation", speaking on a presumption drawn from the evidence, not asserting as known; opposed to *ex revelacione* here and to *assertive* at 257.22.
[^7]: **T.** *consequenciam* is Loserth's expansion of the manuscript's *conam* (251.33). **C.** *trahi in consequenciam*, the canonists' idiom "to be drawn into a precedent", i.e. made a general rule; kept as "inference" under the GLOSSARY row.
[^8]: **R.** Loserth identifies the Gregory as Gregory XI (d. 1378), whose bulls of 1377 condemned propositions of Wyclif's, among them that temporal lords may take temporal goods from a delinquent church (Walsingham, *Historia Anglicana* I, 354, the sixth conclusion: *domini temporales possunt legitime ac meritorie auferre bona fortunae ab Ecclesia delinquente*; LEDGER P10i-2). Both manuscripts note in the margin at 252.1: *Auferre bona temporalia dampnatum est*. "Exceeded the decalogue" recurs at 254.25.
[^9]: **S.** Decretum, Part I, D. 23 c. 1, *In nomine Domini*, the election decree of Nicholas II (1059) (Loserth; modern form ours) (LEDGER P10i-3). The quoted phrases follow Loserth's italics; the last clause is Wyclif's report in the present tense (*anathematizat*). Both manuscripts note in the margin at 252.12: *Simonia sedis Romane*, and at 252.27: *Deposicio paparum*. **T.** B reads *habeant* for *habeat* (252.21).
[^10]: **S.** The *Glossa ordinaria* to the Decretum, D. 23 c. 1 (Loserth cites a Venice edition, vol. I, p. 97) (LEDGER P10i-4). The events are those of 1044–1046 (Benedict IX, Silvester III, Gregory VI, the last deposed by Henry III at Sutri); Loserth's foot to 253.1 reads *Glossa ordinaria: Benedictus X*, i.e. the gloss calls the restored Benedict the tenth; he was Benedict IX (Sigebert of Gembloux, *Chronica*, a. 1045–46, PL 160, 208C–209A, has the three rival popes and Henry's deposition of them) (LEDGER P10i-5).
[^11]: **T.** *imponentes* is Loserth's; both manuscripts read *impotentes* (253.8–9). The print governs.
[^12]: **C.** The *Si enim …* clause has no clean apodosis: *et sic … dicunt* is taken as its consequent ("then these sects too say"). The object of *impugnat* is read as "what the true pope has done in himself", i.e. confirmed by his own judgement (Jerome's condition); the alternative, that *quod* gives the content of the heresy, does not change the argument.
[^13]: **S.** Matt. 26:65, the high priest's charge of blasphemy. **C.** *quidam doctor* is not named.
