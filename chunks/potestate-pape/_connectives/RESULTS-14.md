## Doubtful, left as they are

- **12-g ¶5, p. 341**: *cum autem non sic ordinant pro coco et cibo corporis*, rendered "and since". Either (c), the next clause of the premiss behind *patet quanta infidelitate*, or (b), the body's cook set against the mind's ("but since they do not ordain so"). "and" is defensible.
- **12-h ¶4, p. 345**: *Ista autem trimembris maxima videtur … principium contra Christum*, rendered "Now". Either (a), the step that brings the quoted maxim to bear against Christ, or (c), "And this three-membered maxim". "Now" is defensible.
- **12-i ¶1, p. 346**: *Est autem maior blasfemia constanter asserere*, rendered "And". Either (c), the next item, or (b), set against what the pope would do (*Sic autem faceret papa*). "And" stands.
- **12-i ¶3, p. 348**: *dissipans oves suas; hoc autem est signum potissimum Antichristi*, rendered "and this is". Could be (a), the premiss that makes such a pope Antichrist; but no *ergo* follows and the clause only extends the step, so (c) "and" stands.
- **12-j ¶1, p. 350**: *omnis elector spiritualis prepositi sed papa precipue*, rendered "but the pope above all". Intensive *sed precipue* fits no class cleanly (cf. RESULTS-01, 01-d); the English renders it and "but … above all" reads as escalation, not contrast. Left.
- **12-l ¶2, p. 358**: *Sed caveatur tercio*, rendered "But, third". Continues the list of three dangers opened by *Sed oportet … de triplici periculo*; "And, third" would also do, but *sed* is rendered and not mis-classed. Left.
