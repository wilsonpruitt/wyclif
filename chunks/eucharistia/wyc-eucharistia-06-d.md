# *De eucharistia*, chapter 6, part 4: the love that fits a man to eat the host; objections from the legends and the decretals

## ¶1 · 169.27–171.11

> [p. 169] Sic ergo tota disposicio hominis ad comedendum hostiam stat in sincera et grata dileccione Christi et Dei. Est, inquam, sincera dileccio, quando amor Dei supereminet et amor cuiuslibet creature in ordine ad ipsum supponitur. Qui enim plus diligit aliquid [p. 170] Domino est ingratus et per consequens non ipso dignus. Omnes autem istam dileccionem sed nimis mendaciter profitemur; quod discerni potest ex hoc quod nos sacerdotes misse a summo usque ad infimum statim post missam propter superbiam, carnem vel similia dimittimus Christi servicium, legem suam, monita sua, et conversacionem suam abicimus in contemptum. Quis, inquam, nostrum nedum post missam, sed in missa non plus nititur servire mundo vel homini quam auctori? Inclinaciones autem, occupaciones et operaciones istud diiudicant, cum nedum facillime possemus ad delectaciones corporis vel commodi offendere veritatem sed de facto sic facimus: ut, inquam, dimittam maiora facinora, quis nostrum non est tam fragilis quin propter affeccionem carnalem sive pecuniam diceret testimonium contra veritatem vel prorumpat in assercionem sentencie, antequam secundum Dei regulas examinet veritatem, vel tercio ubi ad honorem Dei posset prodesse ecclesie, taceat vecorditer veritatem? Et omnium istorum causa est preponderans dileccio terrenorum. Et talis indigne capit corpus Christi et sanguinem Jesu Christi; et nisi hoc esset sacerdotes Christi non occuparentur in seculari negocio, non aspirarent ad temporalia sed pauper vita Christi foret eis amena; nec dimittendo certum et melius prorumperent in verba et opera minus utilia. Et bene esset illi sacerdoti qui sciret ab ista macula triplici se servare. Oportet enim quod ob amorem Christi quem comedit moderet intellectum, quia aliter prandium istud quod ordinatum est propter hanc medicinam, foret tam sumenti quam toti ecclesie venenosum; quanto magis si demonium meridianum [p. 171] commixtum fuerit in istis ministerio sacerdotum? Utile quidem foret ecclesie non habere elemosinarios sic celebrantes dotatos nec ypocritas proditores veritatis super falso fundamento religionis et ficcione mendacii sic orantes, sed evangelisacione Christi plus in suis iustis operibus confidentes. Tunc enim digne manducarent corpus Christi et sanguinem eius biberent, que in celis sunt, subducto omni conversante nobiscum presbytero celebrante. Ymmo efficacius et verius forent corpus Christi et sanguis tam in corpore nostro quam anima hic nobiscum.

So, therefore, the whole disposition of a man for eating the [host]{g:hostia} stands in a sincere and grateful love of Christ and of God. It is, I say, sincere love when the love of God is pre-eminent and the love of any creature whatever is placed beneath it in order to him. For he who loves anything [170] more than the Lord is [displeasing]{g:ingratus} to the Lord,[^1] and consequently not worthy of him. And we all profess this love, but exceedingly falsely; which can be discerned from this, that we [priests]{g:sacerdos} of the mass, from the highest to the lowest, immediately after mass, for pride, the flesh or the like, lay aside the [service]{g:servicium} of Christ, and cast his law, his admonitions and his manner of life into contempt. Who of us, I say, not only after mass but in the mass, does not strive more to serve the world or man than the author? And our inclinations, occupations and operations judge this, since not only could we very easily offend against the truth for the delights of the body or for advantage, but in fact we do so: so that, I say, to pass over greater [misdeeds]{g:facinus}, who of us is not so frail that, for carnal affection or for money, he would bear witness against the truth, or burst out into the assertion of a [sentence]{g:sentencia} before he examines the truth according to God's rules,[^2] or, thirdly, where he could benefit the [church]{g:ecclesia} to the honour of God, would faint-heartedly keep silent about the truth? And the cause of all these is the preponderant love of earthly things. And such a man unworthily takes the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} and the blood of Jesus Christ; and were it not for this, the priests of Christ would not be occupied in secular business, would not aspire to [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, but the poor life of Christ would be pleasant to them; nor, laying aside what is certain and better, would they burst out into less useful words and works. And it would be well with that priest who knew how to keep himself from this threefold stain. For it is necessary that, for the love of Christ whom he eats, he should moderate his understanding, because otherwise this meal, which is ordained for the sake of this medicine, would be poisonous both to the one who takes it and to the whole church; how much more if the noonday demon [171] has been mingled in this ministry of priests?[^3] It would indeed be useful to the church not to have endowed [almsmen]{g:elemosinarius} celebrating thus, nor hypocrites, [traitors]{g:proditor} to the truth, praying thus on the false foundation of religion and the fiction of falsehood, but [men] trusting more, by the preaching of the gospel of Christ, in their own just works.[^4] For then they would worthily eat the body of Christ and drink his blood, which are in heaven, with every celebrating [presbyter]{g:presbiter} who [lives his life]{g:conversari} among us taken away. Indeed, the body and blood of Christ would be more efficaciously and more [truly]{g:vere} here with us, both in our body and in our soul.

## ¶2 · 171.12–171.19

> [p. 171] Sed obicitur primo per hoc quod nedum in legendis et serviciis huius festi accidencia asseruntur esse sine subiecto, sed decretalis libro III hoc videtur expresse innuere, ut patet De Celebracione Missarum, capitulo Cum Marthe et in Clementinis capitulo... de hoc festo dicuntur multa similia. Negando itaque hoc posse fieri contradiceretur decretis et glosse ecclesie sed blaspheme contradiceretur divine potencie.

But [it is objected, by appeal to]{g:obicitur-per} this, first: that not only in the legends and services of this feast are [accidents]{g:accidens} asserted to be [without a subject]{g:accidens-sine-subiecto},[^5] but the [decretal]{g:decretalis} in book III seems to intimate this expressly, as is clear in *De celebratione missarum*, the chapter *Cum Marthe*; and in the Clementines, in the chapter … many like things are said of this feast.[^6] And so, by denying that this can be done, one would contradict the [decrees]{g:decretum} and the gloss of the church, but, what is more, would blasphemously contradict the divine [power]{g:potencia}.[^7]

## ¶3 · 171.20–172.7

> [p. 171] Quantum ad legendas patet quod nedum sunt collecta apocrifa sed sepe mendacia. Unde Augustinus in De Sermone Domini in Monte libro primo declarans quomodo Petrus et Paulus excommunicaverunt homines pro amore, sicut patet Act. V°, 3 de Anania et Saphira, et prima Cor. V°, 5, quem Paulus tradidit Sathane, ut spiritus salvus fiat: ymmo in libris illis quibus adversarii magnam auctoritatem tribuunt. Advertant inquit, quod dicimus ubi scriptum est apostolum [p. 172] Thomam inprecatum cuidam a quo palma percussus esset atrocissime mortis supplicium, animam tamen eius commendat ut in futuro seculo ei parceretur: cuius a leone occisi a cetero corpore discerptam manum canis intulit mensis convivarum, in quibus convivabatur Apostolus. Cui scripture, inquit, licet nobis non credere; non enim est in katholico canone.

As to the legends, it is clear that they are not only [apocryphal]{g:apocrifa} collections but often falsehoods. Hence Augustine, in *De sermone Domini in monte*, book one, making clear how Peter and Paul excommunicated men out of love, as is clear in Acts 5:3 of Ananias and Sapphira, and in 1 Cor. 5:5, whom Paul delivered to Satan "that the spirit may be saved": indeed, in those books to which the adversaries attribute great authority.[^8] "Let them take note," he says, "of what we say, where it is written that the apostle [172] Thomas imprecated on someone by whom he had been struck with the palm the punishment of a most atrocious death, yet commends his soul, that he might be spared in the world to come: whose hand, torn from the rest of his body when he had been killed by a lion, a dog brought in to the tables of the banqueters, among whom the apostle was feasting." "This scripture," he says, "we are permitted not to believe; for it is not in the catholic canon."[^9]

## ¶4 · 172.8–172.13

> [p. 172] Ex isto testimonio Augustini patet quod omnia talia extra fidem scripture licet vera, non oportet fidelem credere explicite; multo magis non oportet aliquomodo credere legendas que dicunt in sacramento altaris accidens esse sine subiecto, cum sint contrarie sanctis doctoribus et racioni.

From this testimony of Augustine it is clear that all such things outside the faith of scripture, though true, the faithful man need not believe explicitly; much more is there no need in any way to believe legends which say "in the [sacrament of the altar]{g:sacramentum-altaris} an accident is without a subject", since they are contrary to the holy doctors and to reason.

## ¶5 · 172.14–173.29

> [p. 172] Quantum ad decretales, videtur primo ex dicto Augustini quod non oportet ipsas credere, cum non sint in katholico canone, sed (ut sepe glossavi) potest dici quod per accidencia panis et vini intelliguntur panis et vinum, ut ecclesia sepe per signa intelligit sua signata. Sicut ergo scriptura sacra, sancti doctores et leges ecclesie vocant illa que dicimus accidencia panem et vinum, sic nos ad imitacionem eorum vocamus ea panem et vinum; nec habent auctoritatem vel evidenciam ad impugnandum nominacionem nostri baptismatis pocius quam econtra. Nec probare possunt quod interim sit miraculosa variacio pocius quam fuit tempore istorum auctorum. Sed maiori auctoritate vel evidencia licuit eis vocare illum panem et vinum accidencia quantitatis cum cognomine. Nec dubium quin Apostolus vocavit illam rem panem prima Cor. X et XI°, sicut generaliter scriptura et beatus Ambrosius, ut patet de consecracione, distinccione IIᵃ, [p. 173] Panis est, et Augustinus eadem distinccione, Hoc est, et Sermone XXVI (ut sepe recitavi); et pari evidencia qua glossatores glossant eos quod intelligunt per panem et vinum non substanciam sed accidencia, nos econtra glossamus eos quod non intelligunt per accidencia aliquas res novem generum accidentis, quia nemo scit quam; sed intelligunt per accidencia substanciam panis et vini et non accidens, quia (ut testatur Augustinus in isto katholicus) nullum accidens poterit per se esse. Nec videtur quod ecclesia sit oneranda ut fide eorum testimonio, cum cotidie fiant ista miracula que Augustinus dicit non posse fieri, sicut tempore Augustini Spiritus Sanctus noluit onerari ecclesiam dictis suis vel alterius preter auctores scripture tanquam fide katholica (ut sepe testatur iste sanctus); quomodo ergo oneretur contra dicta sanctorum et contra racionem, ut credat accidens esse ibi sine subiecto? Nec in isto contradicitur divine potencie, sicut nec Apostolus contradixit IIᵃ Thym. II°, quando asseruit Deus verax est, se ipsum negare non potest. Quid ergo obest quod longitudo, latitudo et profunditas materialis substancie (sicut Augustinus testatur) non poterunt per se esse? Leges itaque quotlibet constituit Deus in natura quarum opposita implicant contradiccionem, in quam Deus non potest, cum tunc foret contrarius sibi ipsi, cuiusmodi est lex de inpossibilitate substancie sine accidente; et sic de inpossibilitate ydemptificacionis generum, specierum et differenciarum.

As to the decretals, it seems first, from Augustine's saying, that there is no need to believe them, since they are not in the catholic canon; but (as I have often glossed) it can be said that by "the accidents of bread and wine" are understood [bread]{g:panis} and [wine]{g:vinum}, as the church often understands by [signs]{g:signum} the [things they signify]{g:signatum}. As, therefore, holy scripture, the holy doctors and the [laws of the church]{g:leges-ecclesie} call those things which we call accidents bread and wine, so we, in imitation of them, call them bread and wine; nor have they authority or [evidence]{g:evidencia} for impugning the [naming]{g:nominacio} of our baptism rather than the reverse.[^10] Nor can they prove that in the meantime there is a miraculous variation rather than there was in the time of these authors. But with greater authority or evidence it was permitted to them to call that bread and wine "accidents of quantity", with a surname.[^11] Nor is there doubt that the Apostle called that thing bread, 1 Cor. 10 and 11, as scripture does generally, and blessed Ambrose, as is clear in *De consecratione*, distinction 2, [173] *Panis est*, and Augustine in the same distinction, *Hoc est*, and in Sermon 26 (as I have often recited);[^12] and with equal evidence as that with which the glossators gloss them, that they understand by bread and wine not the [substance]{g:substancia} but the accidents, we on the contrary gloss them, that they do not understand by accidents any things of the nine genera of accident, since no one knows which; but they understand by accidents the substance of bread and wine and not an accident, since (as Augustine, catholic in this, testifies) no accident can be [by itself]{g:per-se}.[^13] Nor does it seem that the church is to be burdened, as with faith, by their testimony, since these miracles happen daily which Augustine says cannot happen, just as in Augustine's time the Holy Spirit would not have the church burdened with his sayings or another's, apart from the authors of scripture, as with catholic faith (as this saint often testifies);[^14] how then should it be burdened against the sayings of the [saints]{g:sancti} and against reason, so as to believe "an accident is there without a subject"? Nor in this is the divine power contradicted, as neither did the Apostle contradict [it], 2 Tim. 2, when he asserted "God is true", "he cannot deny himself".[^15] What harm is it, then, that the length, breadth and depth of material substance (as Augustine testifies) cannot be by themselves? So God has established in nature any number of laws whose opposites imply a contradiction, of which God is not capable, since then he would be contrary to himself;[^16] of which kind is the law of the impossibility of substance without accident; and so of the impossibility of the [identification]{g:ydemptificacio} of genera, species and [differences]{g:differencia}.

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 169.27: Fitness to receive the host consists in love to Christ and God.
- 170.4: Yet we set worldly delights above them.
- 171.12: Objections from the language of legends, services and decretals.
- 171.20: Some legends are false, as Augustine says.
- 172.14: Decretals are not of full authority; and even if they were could be glossed in our sense.
- 173.10: No reason why the Church should be burdened with additions to the faith of Scripture.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Qui enim plus diligit aliquid Domino est ingratus*: *Domino* is taken twice, as the standard of comparison ("more than the Lord") and with *ingratus* ("displeasing to the Lord"); the print has it once.
[^2]: **C.** *Sentencia* is the CONTEXTUAL row teaching / sentence; here "sentence" in the sense of a view pronounced, which the man asserts before he has examined it, not a body of teaching.
[^3]: **S.** "The noonday demon": cf. Ps. 90:6 (Vulgate numbering), *daemonio meridiano*, DR "the noonday devil"; an allusion within Wyclif's own sentence, so not quoted.
[^4]: **C.** *Sed evangelisacione Christi plus in suis iustis operibus confidentes*: there is no *quam*, so the comparison of *plus* is left implicit (more than in masses so celebrated and prayers so said). The ablative *evangelisacione* could also be the object of trust ("trusting more in the preaching of Christ's gospel, in their own just works"); we take it as the means. The following ablative absolute *subducto omni … presbytero celebrante* is rendered "with every celebrating presbyter … taken away".
[^5]: **C.** "This feast": the feast whose legends and services the chapter has in view; Wyclif does not name it in this part.
[^6]: **S/T.** The decretal is *X* 3.41.6, *Cum Marthe* (Gregory IX's Decretals, book III, title 41, *De celebratione missarum*, c. 6; Loserth 171.16, the modern form ours; LEDGER K06d-1). For the Clementines the print leaves the chapter name blank; Loserth notes at 171.16 that "the beginning of this chapter appears to have fallen out". Which Clementine chapter on the feast is meant is to be checked (LEDGER K06d-2). 171.13 ABDE *asseritur*.
[^7]: **C.** *Potencia* is the CONTEXTUAL row potency / power: here God's power. *Sed blaspheme* is rendered "but, what is more", the *sed* raising the charge from contradicting the decrees to blasphemy.
[^8]: **S/C.** Augustine, *De sermone Domini in monte* I.20.65 (Loserth 171.21; LEDGER K06d-3). The Latin breaks off after the participle *declarans* without a main verb; the *ymmo* clause anticipates Augustine's appeal to the adversaries' own books. Who the adversaries are in Augustine is not said here (LEDGER K06d-4). 1 Cor. 5:5 is given in the Douay–Rheims; Wyclif has *fiat* for the Vulgate *sit*.
[^9]: **S.** Augustine's own words, quoted from the same place; the story is of the apostle Thomas from a book Augustine rejects as outside the canon (LEDGER K06d-3).
[^10]: **C.** "The naming of our baptism" (*nominacionem nostri baptismatis*): Wyclif's figure for the name his side gives the thing, bread and wine, as if at its baptism; the adversaries have no better ground to attack that name than his side has to attack theirs.
[^11]: **C.** The print's *Sed maiori auctoritate vel evidencia licuit eis* affirms; the argument seems to require a denial or a question ("Nor was it permitted them …" or "But was it permitted them …?"). We translate the print. *Cum cognomine*: "accidents" with the qualifying name "of quantity" added; 172.28 D reads *cum cognomine quantitatis*.
[^12]: **S/T.** *De cons.* D. 2 c. 55, *Panis est* (Ambrose), and D. 2 cc. 48 and 63, *Hoc est* (Augustine, from *In Ioh.* tract. 26 on John 6); Loserth 172.31 and 173.1, the modern form ours (LEDGER K06d-5, K06d-6). Wyclif's "Sermon 26" is that tractate; 173.2 ABCE read *Sermone LV*.
[^13]: **C/S.** *Per se* is the CONTEXTUAL row: here the spatial sense, existing apart, by itself, as again at 173.22 of the three dimensions. The Augustinian teaching that no accident can be by itself, and that the dimensions of a body cannot, is to be checked (LEDGER K06d-7).
[^14]: **S.** Augustine's refusal to have his own writings or any but the canonical authors held as faith: to be checked (LEDGER K06d-8).
[^15]: **S.** Rom. 3:4 and 2 Tim. 2:13 (Loserth 173.21), joined as one assertion; the Douay–Rheims wording of each.
[^16]: **C/T.** *In quam Deus non potest*: *posse in* "be capable of", the relative referring to *contradiccionem*. 173.27 ABCE read *contradictorius* for *contrarius*. At 173.28–29 ABCD omit *substancie … impossibilitate* (the eye skipping from one *inpossibilitate* to the next); the print follows E.
