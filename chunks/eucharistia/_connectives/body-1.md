# sed/autem extract, wave 1: chunks prol-b prol-c 01-a

### prol-b ¶1 · 4.26–6.2  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 4] Capitulum quintum replicat de locucione tropica secundum modernos doctores et solvit. Secundo replicat per doctores antiquiores et solvit, ita quod tangit primo Scotum, Henricum et dominum Armachanum, ubi docet tripliciter quod hoc decretum Ego Beringarius non est abrogatum. **Sed** post tanguntur [p. 5] alii doctores provecciores, primo Petrus Lombardus, ubi ostenditur quod ipse non repugnat huic sentencie, et tollit evidenciam Bonaventure et solvit, declarans quid concluditur ex dictis multis sanctorum et decreti quod Eukaristia est panis et vinum sacramentaliter consecrata. Secundo replicat per Anshelmum et declarat ex testimonio suo et racionibus quod accidens non potest esse sine subiecto, et ostendendo quid Augustinus sentit de quantitate declarat quomodo ista via magnificaret ulterius Dei potenciam ponendo hostiam infinitum magis gloriosam quam modo sompniat, et quomodo error istius materie induxit in multos alios errores. Quarto obicit per hoc quod multi sancti canonizati moriebantur in ista fide de Eukaristia. Et allegat sanctum Thomam probantem quod via dicens panem remanere post consecracionem est incompetens, inpossibilis et heretica, et primo tollit evidenciam ex testimonio triplici quod non probatur multos sanctos in ista fide de Eukaristia decessisse. Secundo dicitur probabiliter, quod multe sancti Thome sentencie sunt corrupte. Tercio declaratur tripliciter quod non est de substancia fidei credere quod post benediccionem non remanet substancia sacramenti et colorat concordancias decreti Nicholai IIⁱ et decretalis Innocencii IIIⁱⁱ in hac parte. Quarto infringitur trimembre argumentum per quod sanctus Thomas videtur probare incompetenciam huius sentencie. Quinto probat sextupliciter falsitatem porismatis, per quod nititur Thomas probare inpossibilitatem huius sentencie. Sexto obicitur tripliciter contra evidenciam hereticantem sentenciam [p. 6] istam et declaratur quam irreligiose procedit ista secta in condempnando sentenciam quam ignorat.

ENGLISH: The fifth chapter [rejoins]{g:replicatur} on [tropical speech]{g:locucio-tropica} according to [the modern doctors]{g:moderni-doctores}, and [resolves]{g:solvere} [the rejoinder]. Second, it rejoins by appeal to the older doctors and resolves [the rejoinder], so that it touches first on Scotus, Henry and [the lord of Armagh]{g:dominus-ardmacanus}, where it teaches in three ways that this [decree]{g:decretum} *Ego Berengarius* has not been abrogated.[^1] But afterwards [5] other, more advanced doctors are touched on:[^2] first Peter Lombard, where it is [shown]{g:ostendere} that he [is not incompatible]{g:repugnare} with this [teaching]{g:sentencia};[^3] and it [disposes of]{g:tollere} the [evidence]{g:evidencia} of Bonaventure and resolves [it], [making clear]{g:declarare} what [is inferred]{g:concludere} from many sayings of [the saints]{g:sancti} and of the decree: that the Eucharist is [bread]{g:panis} and [wine]{g:vinum}, [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} consecrated.[^4] Second, it rejoins by appeal to Anselm, and makes clear from his testimony and arguments that an [accident]{g:accidens} cannot be without a [subject]{g:subiectum}; and, showing what Augustine thinks about [quantity]{g:quantitas}, it makes clear how this way would magnify God's [power]{g:potencia} further by positing a [host]{g:hostia} infinitely more glorious than it now dreams of, and how the error of this matter has led into many other errors.[^5] Fourth, it objects on the ground that many [canonized]{g:canonizare} saints died in this faith concerning the Eucharist.[^6] And it [alleges]{g:allegare} Saint Thomas, who proves that the way which says that the bread remains after the consecration is [unfitting]{g:incompetens}, impossible and heretical; and first it disposes of the evidence by a threefold testimony that it is not proved that many saints departed in this faith concerning the Eucharist.[^7] Second, it is said [with probability]{g:probabiliter} that many of Saint Thomas's teachings are corrupted.[^8] Third, it is made clear in three ways that it is not of the [substance]{g:substancia} of the faith to believe that after the blessing the substance of the sacrament does not remain; and it [gives colour to]{g:colorare} the concordances of the decree of Nicholas II and the decretal of Innocent III on this point.[^9] Fourth, the three-membered argument by which Saint Thomas seems to prove the unfittingness of this teaching is broken. Fifth, it proves in six ways the falsity of the [porism]{g:porisma} by which Thomas strives to prove the impossibility of this teaching. Sixth, it is objected in three ways against the evidence that [pronounces]{g:hereticare} [6] this teaching heretical, and it is made clear how irreligiously this [sect]{g:secta} proceeds in condemning a teaching of which it [is ignorant]{g:ignorare}.

### prol-b ¶3 · 7.1–8.3  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 7] Capitulum septimum introducit materiam de ydemptificacione; contra quam arguit septempliciter, et arguit pro ydemptificacione, primo pro idemptificacione individuorum in specie et genere, et dissolvit. Secundo replicatur per hoc quod hoc sacramentum est uni una qualitas et alii alia; et dissolvit negans illud, declarans quod qualitates non poterunt per se esse ex parte qualitatum anime. Et solvit tria argumenta, declarans multipliciter quomodo accidens varie denominat. Et tercio ostendendo quomodo Deus videtur declarat novem genera visionum. Et solvit quatuor obiectus quibus suadetur Deum videri oculo corporali. Quarto solvit tercium obiectum factum pro ydemptificacione per hoc quod panis fit corpus Christi, ubi declarando sensum istius ostendit qualis est virtus sacramenti et quomodo non sufficit facere panem esse idem corpori Christi. Et ultimo solvit quartum obiectum de inpanacione, exponendo primo et colorando posicionem; secundo narrat originem huius vie et quid sibi contingit ac quam sinistre glosantur dicta sanctorum et leges ecclesie ex ista infecta radice. Et tercio probat tripliciter quod sentencia ista de inpanacione est heretica, primo quia scriptura sacra et sancti doctores in verbis asserunt, ut allegant beatum Augustinum dicentem quod panis est corpus Christi, secundo per hoc quod quidditas panis non potest esse ydemptice quidditas carnis, et tercio per hoc quod ecclesia non est difficultata in fide sine magna racione; **sed** non est racio, ymmo inconvenientis multiplicis implicacio ponendo inpanacionem. Et exponit decretum ecclesie quod videtur [p. 8] contrarium narrans tres logicas in ista materia et quomodo usus earum est secundum epikeyam pro loco et tempore adaptandus.

ENGLISH: The seventh chapter introduces the matter of [identification]{g:ydemptificacio}; against which it argues in seven ways, and it argues for identification: first, for the identification of [individuals]{g:individuum} in species and genus, and it resolves [this].[^15] Second, it is rejoined on the ground that "this sacrament is one [quality]{g:qualitas} to one man and another to another"; and it resolves [this] by denying it, making clear that qualities could not be [of themselves]{g:per-se} on the side of the qualities of the soul.[^16] And it resolves three arguments, making clear in many ways how an accident [names]{g:denominare} variously. And third, in showing how God is seen, it makes clear nine kinds of vision. And it resolves four [objections]{g:objectum} by which [the case is made]{g:suadere} that God is seen with the bodily eye. Fourth, it resolves the third objection made for identification, on the ground that "the bread becomes the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}"; where, in making clear the sense of this, it shows what the power of the sacrament is, and how it does not suffice to make the bread be the same as the body of Christ.[^17] And last it resolves the fourth objection, concerning [impanation]{g:inpanacio}, first expounding the [position]{g:posicio} and giving it colour; second, it relates the origin of this way, and what befell it, and how perversely the sayings of the saints and the laws of the church are [glossed]{g:glosare} from this infected root. And third, it proves in three ways that this teaching of impanation is heretical: first, because holy scripture and the holy doctors assert [it] in words, as they allege blessed Augustine saying "the bread is the body of Christ";[^18] second, on the ground that the quiddity of bread cannot be [identically]{g:ydemptice} the quiddity of flesh; and third, on the ground that the church is not burdened in faith without great reason; now there is no reason, but rather the implication of manifold [absurdity]{g:inconveniens} in positing impanation.[^19] And it expounds the decree of the church which seems [8] contrary, relating three logics in this matter, and how their use is to be adapted according to [epikeia]{g:epikeya} for place and time.[^20]

### 01-a ¶2 · 11.17–12.2  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 11] **Sed** respondemus eis iuxta fidem predictam quod infideliter falsum assumunt, cum tales bestie possunt comedere hostiam consecratam que est nudum sacramentum et non corpus Christi vel sanguis. Ymmo sicut leo comedens corpus hominis non comedit eius animam, licet sit ad omnem punctum illius corporis, sic intelligendum est de corpore Christi in sacramento altaris. Ipsum enim est totum sacramentaliter, spiritualiter [p. 12] vel virtualiter ad omnem punctum hostie consecrate, sicut anima est in corpore.

ENGLISH: But we reply to them, according to the faith stated before, that they faithlessly assume a falsehood, since such beasts can eat the consecrated host, which is the bare sacrament and not the body or blood of Christ.[^3] Indeed, just as a lion eating the body of a man does not eat his soul, although it is at every point of that body, so it is to be understood of the body of Christ in the sacrament of the altar. For it is whole, [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter}, [spiritually]{g:spiritualiter} [12] or [virtually]{g:virtualiter}, at every point of the consecrated host, as the soul is in the body.[^4]

### 01-a ¶4 · 12.7–12.17  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 12] **Sed** nos respondemus iuxta fidem priorem quod infideliter falsum assumunt, cum frangimus sacramentum vel hostiam consecratam, non **autem** corpus Christi, cum distinguuntur, sicut non frangimus radium solis, licet frangamus vitrum vel lapidem cristallinum. Et hec videtur sentencia cantus ecclesie quo canitur: Fracto demum sacramento Non vacilles **sed** memento Tantum esse sub fragmento Quantum toto tegitur.

ENGLISH: But we reply, according to the former faith, that they faithlessly assume a falsehood, since we break the sacrament or consecrated host, but not the body of Christ, since they are distinguished; just as we do not break a ray of the sun, although we break the glass or the crystal stone. And this seems to be the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of the church's chant, in which it is sung: "When the sacrament is broken at last, do not waver, but remember that there is as much beneath a fragment as is covered in the whole."[^5]

### 01-a ¶7 · 13.1–13.17  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 13] **Sed** hic respondemus per distinccionem quod est dare duplicem visionem, duplicem esum et duplicem sumpcionem, scilicet corporalem et spiritualem; et sic concedimus quod non videmus in sacramento illo corpus Christi oculo corporali **sed** oculo mentali, scilicet fide per speculum in enigmate. Et sicut ymago est integra ad omnem punctum speculi, sic quod videri potest vel secundum partem vel totum ab aliquo oculo corporali alicubi posito: sic intelligendum est in parte de corpore Christi in hostia consecrata ut speculo. Et eodem modo dicitur quod non tangimus vel capimus corpus Christi tactu corporeo, sicut nec corporaliter ipsum comedimus. Et iste est sensus cantus ecclesie quo dicitur: Quod non capis, quod non vides Animosa firmat fides Preter rerum ordinem.

ENGLISH: But here we reply by a distinction: that [one may posit]{g:est-dare} a twofold vision, a twofold [eating]{g:esus} and a twofold [reception]{g:sumpcio}, namely bodily and spiritual; and so we grant that we do not see the body of Christ in that sacrament with the bodily eye but with the eye of the mind, namely by faith, "through a glass in a dark manner".[^8] And just as an image is whole at every point of a mirror, so that it can be seen either in part or whole by some bodily eye placed somewhere, so it is to be understood in part of the body of Christ in the consecrated host, as in a mirror.[^9] And in the same way the answer is that we do not touch or take the body of Christ by bodily touch, just as we do not eat it [bodily]{g:corporaliter} either. And this is the sense of the church's chant, in which it is said: "What you do not take, what you do not see, a spirited faith confirms, beyond the order of things."[^10]

### 01-a ¶8 · 13.18–13.24  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 13] Nec conterimus corpus Christi dentibus **sed** spiritualiter integrum ipsum accipimus. Et sic intelligimus istum cantum quo dicimus: Nulla rei fit scissura, Signi tantum fit fractura Qua nec status nec statura Signati minuitur.

ENGLISH: Nor do we grind the body of Christ with the teeth, but spiritually we receive it whole. And so we understand this chant, in which we say: "No rending of the thing is made; a breaking of the [sign]{g:signum} alone is made, by which neither the state nor the stature of the [thing signified]{g:signatum} is lessened."[^11]

### 01-a ¶9 · 13.25–14.15  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 13] **Sed** hic obiciunt quidam de nostris quod ista non sunt dicenda laycis qui nec ipsa concipiunt nec observant, cum ex istis fidem priorem amitterent. **Sed** nichil obiecto isto insanius; nam tam layci quam clerici nimis multi sunt tantum infideles in ista materia quod credunt paganis deterius quod hostia [p. 14] illa consecrata sit Deus illorum; et tunc indubie concludunt predicta argumenta paganica. Ille ergo qui non concipit ista male conciperet fidem de Trinitate vel incarnacione. Nec est fides predicta layca fides qua placeretur domino veritatis, **sed** infidelitas pessima quia ydolatria qua creatura valde abiecta non assumpta coleretur infideliter tamquam Deus; et sic secundum istam stulticiam nunquam doceretur vel argueretur aliquis error in fide, **sed** ne deterius contingat christianus valde disciplinabilis in infidelitate sordesceret, ac si diceretur quod spina in pede, telum in carne vel quecunque venenosa causa morbi in corpore non debet extrahi, ne forte contingat deterius. Episcoporum itaque est destruere has hereses, quia aliter ipsi forent heretici ex consensu.

ENGLISH: But here certain of our own object that these things are not to be said to lay people, who neither conceive them nor observe them, since from them they would lose their former faith. But nothing is more insane than this objection; for laymen as well as clerics, [exceedingly]{g:nimis} many, are so unbelieving in this matter that they believe, worse than the pagans, "[14] that consecrated host is their God"; and then the pagan arguments mentioned before undoubtedly [conclude]{g:concludere}.[^12] He, therefore, who does not conceive these things would ill conceive the faith concerning the Trinity or the incarnation. Nor is the aforesaid lay faith a faith by which the Lord of truth would be pleased, but the worst [unbelief]{g:infidelitas}, because [it is] idolatry, by which a very abject [creature]{g:creatura}, not [assumed]{g:assumere}, would be worshipped unbelievingly as God;[^13] and so, according to this folly, no error in the faith would ever be taught or reproved, but, lest something worse should happen, a very [teachable]{g:disciplinabilis} Christian would grow foul in unbelief; as if it were said that a thorn in the foot, a dart in the flesh, or any poisonous cause of disease in the body ought not to be drawn out, lest perhaps something worse should happen.[^14] It belongs to the [bishops]{g:episcopus}, therefore, to destroy these heresies, because otherwise they themselves would be heretics by consent.

### 01-a ¶11 · 14.19–15.7  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 14] **Sed** ista ficticia currit sub eadem perfidia. Nam Apostolus dicit Rom. III°, 8: Absit ut faciamus mala ut eveniant bona, ergo multo magis fidelis diceret: Absit ut committamus ydolatriam, ut ex falsa et infideli devocione populus amplius seducatur, quia revera paulo minus evidenter crederetur a populo, quod calix in quo est sanguis Christi et lignum per quod colitur crucifixus sunt realiter Deus noster. Sicut enim viso calice in tantum cultum prorumpimus sicut visa hostia consecrata, non propter ipsum calicem consecratum a pontifice **sed** propter sacramentum eximium in vase absconditum: sic visa [p. 15] hostia debemus credere quod ipsa non sit corpus Christi, **sed** ipsum corpus Christi est sacramentaliter in ipsa absconditum. Et iste sensus ecclesie est quem canit: Sub diversis speciebus, Signis tantum et non rebus, Latent res eximie.

ENGLISH: But this [fiction]{g:ficticia} runs under the same perfidy. For the Apostle says, Rom. 3:8: "God forbid that we should do evil that good may come"; therefore much more would a faithful man say: "God forbid that we commit idolatry, so that the people may be the further seduced by a false and unbelieving devotion";[^15] for in truth it would be believed by the people only a little less evidently that "the [chalice]{g:calix} in which is the blood of Christ, and the wood through which the crucified is worshipped, are really our God". For just as, at the sight of the chalice, we break out into as great worship as at the sight of the consecrated host, not on account of the chalice itself, consecrated by a [pontiff]{g:pontifex}, but on account of the excellent sacrament hidden in the vessel: so at the sight of the [15] host we ought to believe that it is not the body of Christ, but that the body of Christ itself is sacramentally hidden in it.[^16] And this is the sense of the church which it sings: "Under diverse species, signs only and not things, excellent things lie hidden."

### 01-a ¶13 · 15.18–15.26  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 15] Tercio obiciunt per hoc quod sic vilesceret sacerdotalis potestas cum non habet potestatem consecrandi vel faciendi corpus Christi vel sanguinem, ymmo videtur quod sicut laycus videndo quamlibet creaturam videt in fide deitatem que est plus quam corpus Christi, sic videndo quamlibet creaturam fideliter sufficienter accipit corpus Christi. **Sed** quis tunc audiret missam, missantes care conduceret vel acciperet more ecclesie sacramentum?

ENGLISH: Third, they object on the ground that the priestly [power]{g:potestas} would thus grow cheap, since it does not have the power of consecrating or making the body or blood of Christ; indeed, it seems that, just as a layman in seeing any creature whatever sees in faith the [Godhead]{g:deitas}, which is more than the body of Christ, so in seeing any creature whatever faithfully he sufficiently receives the body of Christ. But who then would hear mass, hire [mass-priests]{g:missantes} dearly, or receive the sacrament in the church's manner?[^18]
