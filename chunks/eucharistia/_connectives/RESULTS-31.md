## Doubtful, left as they are

- **02-f ¶1, p. 49**: *Aliud autem decretum explicat quomodo papa et sinodus decreverunt*, rendered "But the other decree". Either (b), the explicit decree of Berengar set against the decretal that "intimates ambiguously and incompletely", or (a), the next premiss of the step closed by *Cum ergo … videtur quod tollitur evidencia* ("Now the other decree"). The contrast with the preceding sentence is explicit, so "But" stays.
- **02-g ¶1, p. 51**: *licet autem fateantur accidens esse sine subiecto, tamen dicunt*, rendered "And although they admit". Either (c), the argument continued to close the adversaries' escape, or (b), an anticipated reply set against the syllogism just drawn ("But although they admit"). The concession is carried by "although" and the *tamen* by "yet", so "And" is defensible.
