# *De ecclesia*, chapter 3, part 5: the second and third objections answered: the church's digestion, and unformed faith

## ¶1 · 60.11–63.21

> [p. 60] Et sic vere dicit apostolus quod Christus est salvator maxime fidelium in tercio et quarto gradu, et intelligunt quidam dictum apostoli I ad Thimoteum II°, 4, directe ad litteram, quod Deus vult omnes homines salvos fieri. Ad secundum obiectum ex dicto beati Johannis est eadem sentencia. Christus enim est quoad sufficienciam propiciacio, hoc est, medicus vel medicina propiciativa tocius humani generis, quod habet ultra diabolum post ruinam angelorum libertatem arbitrii, ut salvetur, cum totum genus humanum sit homo quidam qui descendendo ab Jerusalem in Jericho incidit in latrones; qui relictus secundum totum semivivus ex gracia Samaritani habuit medicinam dispositam et ministros mercede et instruccione duplicis testamenti provide dispensantes, secundum quam totus ille homo sanari poterit. Sed preter illum hominem naturalem oportet secundum Adam generare spiritualiter unam generacionem priori permixtam que est sancta mater ecclesia. Et in illo corpore sunt aliqui vere membra ut predestinati, quarte digestioni assimilati, sive sint ad tempus discrasiati ut lapsi, sive [p. 61] solide post primam sanacionem in sanitate perpetua stabiliti. Illi autem qui sunt intra dictam ecclesiam non ut partes, sunt in gradu duplici, ut quidam in sua infidelitati perpetuo permanentes qui nunquam receperunt caritatem vel graciam. Et hii sunt tamquam aer aut ventus in homine, in quem nunquam egit suus calor naturalis. Hii autem sive commixti sint localiter sive solum in genere humano, prosunt ad refrigerium sancte matris ecclesie. Hii autem sunt tamquam superfluitates cibi sponsi ecclesie qui spiritualiter digerit predestinatos et demum assimilat. Et iste est cibus de quo loquitur Christus Johannis IV°, 32: Ego cibum habeo manducare quem vos nescitis. Unde superflua illius cibi sunt infecta duplici, ut aliqui sunt in stomacho sancte matris ecclesie recepti per graciam secundum presentem iusticiam, sed ex eorum ypocrisi vel heresi non sunt propter abhominacionem caritatis operantis digesti, sed in hac vita sensibiliter evomendi; de quibus loquitur spiritus sanctus Apocalypsis III°, 16: Neque calidus es neque frigidus, ideo incipiam te evomere de ore meo. Tales autem sunt sive clerici sive laici pretendentes se operari ad edificacionem sancte matris ecclesie quidquid agunt, sed tamen quia habent intenciones frigidas temporalium propter quas principaliter agunt, ideo sunt ypocrite Deo abhominabiles ut aqua tepida que provocat ad vomitum. Et hii sunt Deo maxime abhominabiles racione falsitatis in duplicitate latentis que opponitur veritati. Unde sicut iuxta Aristotelem primo Metyororum VI° cap. [p. 62] aqua sic calida cicius congelatur propter exalaciones frigidas quas pori caloris recipiunt: sic pretendentes sanctitatem huiusmodi sunt periculosissimi hostes ecclesie, per quos ut proditorie aperientes portas ecclesie subintrant frigidi fide et virtutibus; qui plus quam inter fideles extinguunt caritatem vocate ecclesie et congelant. Debet, inquam, ecclesia fervere pura caritate ex simplici participacione Christi qui est ignis per se calidus caritate, et matrem omnium malorum cupiditatem postponere, et per consequens, non adinvenciones lucrativas plus quam legem Dei diligere. Sunt autem intra ecclesiam alii presciti magis absconditi, evadentes evomicionem huiusmodi, ut hii tamquam superfluitas sicca digestionis prime, hii tamquam superfluitas humida digestionis secunde, et hii ut apostemata vel vermes digestionis tercie. Et patet quod non sequitur: Si quicunque viantes sunt in ecclesia, tunc sunt de ecclesia, sed econtra. Nam scimus zizania crescere inter frumenta, corvum pasci in eadem area cum columba et paleam inorreari pro tempore inter grana, et tamen est incommunicans distinccio inter ista, sicut exemplatum est in corpore humano. Ita ymaginari debemus de sancta matre ecclesia, et sic intelligendus est textus I. Joh. II°, 18, cum dicitur: Nunc Antichristi multi facti sunt; ex nobis prodierunt, sed non erant ex nobis, nam si fuissent ex nobis, permansissent utique nobiscum. Sicut enim superfluitas procedit ex cibo et membris solidis, dum tamen non sit ex eis, sic purgamenta ecclesie procedunt ex ea, non tamen erant ex ea ut partes, cum nulla pars eius potest ab ea excidere. Unde ad discernendum distinccionem puri et superfluorum huiusmodi dicit beatus Johannes epistola secunda canonica: Omnis, inquit, qui recedit et non permanet in doctrina Christi, Deum [p. 63] non habet. Doctrina autem Christi sunt humilitas, caritas et paupertas. Quicunque ergo oderit ista, non imitando Christum ut aquila, scito quod non sit de sua ecclesia. Et sic tres gentes excluduntur a membris ecclesie qui omnino peccant in fide scilicet omnino infideles ut pagani; heretici ut sacerdotes et pharisei cum eis similibus qui nunquam gustaverunt dulcedinem spei beatitudinis ex imitacione sapiencie incarnate, sed resident facietenus in ecclesia gracia affeccionis primatuum temporalium et carnalium voluptatum; tercii vero qui gustaverunt paululum digestionis divine, sed propter defectum caritatis perfecte aut finalis perseverancie excidunt tamquam inhabilia, ut formaliter induant caput Christum; et hii peccant in trinitatem et tres virtutes catholicas (ut dixi superius). Omnes autem tales, dicit Augustinus, non esse de membris ecclesie, sicut in corpore hominis naturali nemo dicit proporcionalia esse membra. Sanctus autem Thomas videtur loqui generaliter, equivocando de membro, scilicet de membro temporaliter vivo et de membro omnino mortuo putativo.

And so the apostle says truly that Christ is the saviour especially of the faithful in the third and
fourth degree; and some understand the saying of the apostle, 1 Timothy 2:4, directly according to the
letter, that God "will have all men to be saved".[^1] To the second [objection]{g:objectum}, from the
saying of blessed John, the [teaching]{g:sentencia} is the same. For Christ is, as to sufficiency, the
propitiation, that is, the physician or propitiatory medicine, of the whole human race, which has,
beyond the devil, after the fall of the angels, freedom of choice, so that it may be saved; since the
whole human race is "a certain man" who, going down "from Jerusalem to Jericho, fell among robbers";
who, left half dead in his whole [being], had by the grace of the Samaritan medicine prepared, and
ministers providently dispensing it with the wages and instruction of the twofold testament, according
to which that whole man could be healed.[^2] But besides that natural man, the second Adam must
spiritually beget one generation mingled with the former, which is holy mother [church]{g:ecclesia}. And
in that body some are truly members, as the [predestinate]{g:predestinatus}, assimilated in the fourth
digestion, whether they are for a time distempered, as the lapsed, or [61] firmly established in
perpetual health after the first healing.[^3] And those who are within the said church not as parts are
in a twofold degree: as some remaining perpetually in their [unbelief]{g:infidelitas}, who never
received [charity]{g:caritas} or grace. And these are like air or wind in a man, on which his natural
heat never acted. And these, whether they are mingled [with the church] in place or only in the human
race, serve for the cooling of holy mother church. And these [others] are like the superfluities of the
food of the church's [bridegroom]{g:sponsus}, who spiritually digests the predestinate and at last
assimilates [them]. And this is the food of which Christ speaks, John 4:32: "I have meat to eat which
you know not."[^4] Hence the superfluities of that food are tainted in two ways: as some are received
into the stomach of holy mother church through grace, according to present justice, but because of
their hypocrisy or heresy are not digested, on account of the abhorrence of working charity, but are to
be [perceptibly]{g:sensibilis} vomited up in this life; of whom the Holy Spirit speaks, Apocalypse 3:16:
"Thou art neither hot nor cold; therefore I will begin to vomit thee out of my mouth."[^5] And such are,
whether clerics or laymen, those who make a show of working for the edification of holy mother church
in whatever they do, but yet, because they have cold intentions of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, for
which they principally act, are therefore hypocrites abominable to God, like lukewarm water that
provokes to vomiting. And these are most abominable to God by reason of the falsehood lurking in
duplicity, which is opposed to truth. Hence, just as, according to Aristotle, *Meteorologica* 1,
chapter 6, [62] water thus heated freezes more quickly because of the cold exhalations which the pores
of heat receive, so those who make a show of sanctity of this kind are the most dangerous enemies of the
church; through whom, as through men treacherously opening the gates of the church, those cold in faith
and virtues creep in; who, more than [happens] among the faithful, extinguish and freeze the charity of
the so-called church.[^6] The church, I say, ought to glow with pure charity from simple participation in
Christ, who is a fire hot of itself with charity, and to put after [it] cupidity, the mother of all
evils, and consequently not to love lucrative inventions more than [God's law]{g:lex dei}. And there are
within the church other [foreknown]{g:prescitus}, more hidden, escaping such vomiting: some like the dry
superfluity of the first digestion, some like the moist superfluity of the second digestion, and some
like [abscesses]{g:apostema} or worms of the third digestion.[^7] And it is clear that this does not
[follow]{g:sequitur}: "If any [persons] [on the road]{g:viantes} are in the church, then they are of the
church"; but the converse [does]. For we know that [cockle]{g:zizania} grows among the wheat, that the
raven feeds on the same threshing-floor as the dove, and that chaff is laid up in the barn for a time
among the grain; and yet there is an incommunicable distinction between these, as has been exemplified
in the human body. So we must imagine of holy mother church; and so is to be understood the text of 1
John 2:18, where it is said: "Even now there are become many Antichrists. They went out from us, but they
were not of us. For if they had been of us, they would no doubt have remained with us."[^8] For just as
superfluity proceeds from the food and from the solid members, while yet it is not of them, so the
purgings of the church proceed from her, yet they were not of her as parts, since no part of her can
fall away from her. Hence, to discern the distinction between the pure and such superfluities, blessed
John says in the second canonical epistle: "Whosoever," he says, "revolteth and continueth not in the
[doctrine]{g:doctrina} of Christ hath [63] not God." Now the doctrine of Christ is humility, charity and
poverty. Whoever, therefore, hates these, not imitating Christ as an eagle [does], know that he is not of
his church.[^9] And so three peoples are excluded from the members of the church, who sin wholly in
faith: namely, the wholly unbelieving, as pagans; heretics, as priests and Pharisees with those like
them, who never tasted the sweetness of the hope of blessedness from the imitation of incarnate wisdom,
but sit in the church in outward face for the sake of an affection for temporal primacies and carnal
pleasures; and, third, those who have tasted a little of the divine digestion, but, for want of perfect
charity or of final perseverance, fall away as [unqualified]{g:inhabilis} to put on formally Christ the
head; and these sin against the Trinity and the three catholic virtues (as I said above).[^10] And all
such, Augustine says, are not of the members of the church, just as in the natural body of a man no one
says that the proportionals are members. But St Thomas seems to speak generally, equivocating on
"member": namely, of a member alive for a time and of a member wholly dead, a putative one.[^11]

## ¶2 · 63.22–64.10

> [p. 63] Ad tercium negatur ad sensum scripture quod omnes homines sunt fideles. Conceditur tamen cum Damasceno quod omnis homo habet fidem saltem informem cum naturaliter prima impressione entis cognoscit saltem confuse primo omnium Deum esse et omnem articulum fidei. Sed ista fides informis non sufficit denominare hominem fidelem vel partem ecclesie, sed oportet excludere omnem infidelitatem finalem. Et de tali fideli loquitur Christus Johannis X° 28, promittens, ut omnis qui credit in filium non pereat, supple morte perpetua; ille autem credit in [p. 64] filium qui credendo ipsum inheret sibi finaliter per amorem. Unde quia de tali finali inherencia est nobis dubium, ideo docet Augustinus super Johannem, Omelia XIIª, quomodo membrum ecclesie est Deo et cui ipse voluerit revelare satis cognitum. Novit, inquit, dominus qui sunt eius, novit qui permaneant ad coronam, qui permaneant ad flammam, novit in area sua triticum, novit paleam, novit segetem, novit zizania. Ceteris autem est illud incognitum qui sunt columbe et qui corvi.

To the third, it is denied, in the sense of scripture, that all men are faithful. Yet it is granted with
Damascene that every man has faith, at least [unformed]{g:fides informis}, since naturally, by the first
impression of [being]{g:ens}, he knows, at least [indistinctly]{g:confuse}, first of all, that God is,
and every [article of faith]{g:articulus fidei}.[^12] But this unformed faith does not suffice to name a
man faithful, or a part of the church; but all final [unbelief]{g:infidelitas} must be excluded. And
of such a faithful man Christ speaks, John 10:28, promising that everyone who believes in the Son may not
perish (supply: by everlasting death); now he believes in [64] the Son who, by believing him, adheres to
him finally through love.[^13] Hence, since about such final adherence we are in doubt, Augustine on
John, homily 12, teaches how a member of the church is well enough known to God, and to whom he wishes to
reveal [it]. "The Lord knoweth," he says, "who are his; he knows who will remain for the crown, who will
remain for the flame; he knows in his threshing-floor the wheat, he knows the chaff; he knows the crop,
he knows the [cockle]{g:zizania}." But to the rest it is unknown who are doves and who ravens.[^14]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 60.11: Christ is specially the saviour of the third and fourth class.
- 60.16: Answer to the second objection: Christ is a Sufficient propitiation for all men, who have naturally free will and may avail themselves of it;
- 60.26: but they need a second birth in Christ,
- 61.2: which those have not who are in the Church but not of it.
- 61.14: These the Church cannot digest,
- 62.4: since they want the warmth of charity.
- 63.4: Three classes who are not members of the Church 1. Infidels 2. Priests who make profession for the sake of wealth. 3. Those who have tasted of divine grace but lack final perseverance.
- 63.22: All men are not believers in the Scripture sense.

## Notes

[^1]: **T/S.** *the* (60.16, before *Ad secundum*), a word of Loserth's English side-note ("Answer to
    the second objection"), has been removed from the Latin. *Sal- vator* is one word broken
    at the line. *In tercio et quarto gradu*: the third and fourth sorts of 60.5–10 (03-d). 1 Tim. 2:4,
    DR "will have all men to be saved"; Wyclif *quod Deus vult …*, Vulgate *qui omnes homines vult
    salvos fieri*. *Directe ad litteram*: of God's effective will for all, against "as to sufficiency".
    B margin: *Deus vult omnes homines salvos fieri* (60.14).

[^2]: **S/C.** Luke 10:30, DR in part. The two pence of Luke 10:35 are read as the twofold testament.
    *Ultra diabolum*: unlike the devil, who after the angels' fall has no freedom to be saved; B margin:
    *Humanum genus ultra diabolum habet libertatem arbitrii* (60.18). *Libertatem arbitrii*: freedom of
    choice, cf. *liberum arbitrium* (GLOSSARY §C). *Secundum totum*: as a whole.

[^3]: **C/R.** *Secundum Adam*: Christ, the second Adam (1 Cor. 15:45–47), subject of *generare*. The
    four digestions of medieval physiology: the fourth is assimilation into the members; B's margin sends
    the reader back to chapter 1 (*Quadruplex digestio prius habetur cap. primo huius tractatus*, 60.32).
    *Discrasiati*: ill-tempered in their humours; B *distrahati* (60.32).

[^4]: **C/T.** A margin: *Dupliciter est homo in ecclesia. Ib.: Dupliciter dicitur non pars in ecclesia.
    Nota quomodo presciti sunt in ecclesia* (61.3); *Alia subdivisio* (61.12). *In sua infidelitati*,
    as printed, for *infidelitate*. The third *Hii autem* (61.11) is taken to open the second degree,
    the superfluities of food, as A's *Alia subdivisio* suggests; it could continue the first. B
    *dirigit* for *digerit* (61.11). John 4:32, DR.

[^5]: **S/C.** Rev. 3:16 in Wyclif's wording: Vulgate 3:15–16 *neque frigidus es neque calidus … sed
    quia tepidus es … incipiam te evomere ex ore meo* (DR "I will begin to vomit thee out of my mouth").
    *Infecta duplici*, as printed, is construed "tainted in two ways": those vomited up here, and the
    hidden foreknown of 62.9. *Propter abhominacionem caritatis operantis*: because charity at work
    abhors them.

[^6]: **S/C.** Aristotle, *Meteorologica* 1.12, 348b (Bekker p. 348, Loserth's reference; Wyclif's
    "chapter 6"): water previously heated freezes faster (LEDGER E03e-1). A margin: *Aristoteles*; B:
    *Quare aqua tepida cicius congelatur quam frigida secundum Aristotelem* (61.29); A: *Contra
    avariciam cleri et aliorum* (61.22). *Vocate ecclesie*: the so-called church. *Plus quam inter
    fideles*: obscure; perhaps "more than [the cold do] among [open] believers".

[^7]: **S/R.** *Matrem omnium malorum cupiditatem*: 1 Tim. 6:10, where the Vulgate has *radix* (DR "the
    desire of money is the root of all evils"); Wyclif "mother". *Postponere*: put after. AB margin at
    62.15: *Avari, lubrici, cupidi. Correlarium*, naming the three hidden sorts.

[^8]: **T/R/S.** *Ita* (62.23) is Loserth's: the MSS lack it; he compares Hus, *Opp.* I, 199b, where the
    passage is reused (cf. Hus, *De eccl.*). *Viantes*: those on the road (GLOSSARY §A); being *in* the
    church does not entail being *of* it, though the converse holds. *Incommunicans distinccio*: a
    distinction in which they share nothing. 1 John 2:18–19, DR, Wyclif citing 2:18; *zizania* from
    Matt. 13:25–30. A margin: *Nota* (62.18, 62.24).

[^9]: **S/C.** 2 John 9, DR. *Ut aquila*: as printed; the eagle that follows (cf. Matt. 24:28, Deut.
    32:11) is not explained. *Scito*: imperative, "know". *Nulla pars eius potest ab ea excidere*: the
    predestinate do not finally fall away.

[^10]: **T/C.** B lacks *spei* (63.7); B *sapiencie in caritate* (63.8). B margin: *Ecce qui non sunt
    membra ecclesie* (63.5). *Sacerdotes et pharisei*: the priests and Pharisees of the gospel, with
    those like them now. *Primatuum temporalium*: precedence in temporal things. *Inhabilia*, neuter
    plural, "as unqualified things". *Ut dixi superius*: 58.6–9 (03-c).

[^11]: **S/C.** The Augustine is not identified (LEDGER E03e-2); AB margin: *Augustinus* (63.16).
    *Proporcionalia*, as printed: perhaps the humours, proportioned to the body but not members; a
    doubt. *Membro … putativo*: a reputed member.

[^12]: **S/T.** John Damascene, *De fide orthodoxa* (Loserth: book 4, chapter 11; 63.24) (LEDGER
    E03e-3). The MSS read *vel informem* (63.25); Loserth prints *saltem informem*. *Prima impressione
    entis*: being as the first concept impressed on the mind (the Avicennian formula); *ens* here is the
    concept, not "a being". AB margin: *Damascenus* (63.24); A: *Nota fidem predestinatorum et
    prescitorum* (63.22).

[^13]: **S/C.** Wyclif cites John 10:28 (*non peribunt in eternum*); his words follow John 3:16, DR
    "that whosoever believeth in him may not perish", with *filium* for *eum*. *Credit in filium … per
    amorem*: Augustine's distinction of *credere in*, believing by loving: *Credendo amare, credendo diligere* (*In Ioh. tr.* 29.6,
    PL 35:1631; LEDGER E03e-4). B margin:
    *Quis credit in filium* (64.1).

[^14]: **S.** Augustine, *In Iohannis evangelium tractatus* 12.12 (PL 35), read this session, word for
    word (LEDGER E03e-5; Loserth: tract. 12, Opp. III/2, 389); *Novit dominus qui sunt eius*: 2 Tim. 2:19,
    DR "the Lord knoweth who are his". The last sentence is Wyclif's. AB margin: *Augustinus*; A: *De
    salvandis et dampnandis prescitis et predestinatis* (64.11).
