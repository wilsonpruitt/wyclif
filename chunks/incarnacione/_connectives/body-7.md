### 07-d ¶1 · 117.28–118.8  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 117] Hic dicitur quod non esset concedendum Verbum pati propter passionem sue humanitatis, nisi Verbum esset personaliter illa humanitas. **Sed** quia Verbum ex integro est illa humanitas tam secundum corpus quam secundum animam, et acciones ac passiones sunt primo suppositorum; patet quod illa humanitas non pateretur vel ageret,⁹ nisi [p. 118] quia Verbum, quod est eadem ypostasis vel persona, sic agit vel patitur. Firmiter itaque est tenendum quod Verbum Dei et per consequens Deus ipse pendebat dolens et passus in cruce, et sic de ceteris humanis actibus quos evangelium de Iesu prosequitur. Et sic, licet humanitas Christi sit essencia, substancia, vel natura separata non conservative **sed** essencialiter a deitate, que Christus est; non tamen sic separatur quin sit persona Christi.

ENGLISH: Here [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that it would not be granted that [the Word]{g:verbum} suffers because of the suffering of his [humanity]{g:humanitas}, unless the Word were personally that humanity. Now since the Word is wholly that humanity, as much as to the body as as to the soul, and actions and [being-acted-on]{g:passio} belong first to [supposits]{g:suppositum};[^1] it is clear that that humanity would not suffer or act, unless [118] because the Word, who is the same [hypostasis]{g:ypostasis} or [person]{g:persona}, so acts or suffers. So it must be firmly held that the Word of God, and in consequence God himself, hung grieving and suffering on the cross; and so of the other human acts which the gospel recounts of Jesus. And so, although the humanity of Christ is an [essence]{g:essencia}, substance, or [nature]{g:natura} separate from [the Godhead]{g:deitas}, which Christ is, not as to conservation but essentially, yet it is not so separated that it is not the person of Christ.[^2]

### 07-d ¶2 · 118.9–118.30  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 118] **Sed** pro nomine ‘habitus’ est notandum quod ‘habitus’ quadrupliciter sumitur, ut exponit Augustinus 83 Questionum Questione 73ᵃ. exponendo illud Apostoli ad Philipp. 2°. ‘habitu inventus est ut homo.’ Primo pro sapiencia, que est veritas, quam addiscimus, que manens¹ non mota movet animam, quam informat. 2°. pro alimentis habitis mutantibus corpus, quod nutriunt, et digestione mutatis. Omne quidem substanciale nobis adiacens, quod mutat et mutatur adiacendo subiecto, dicitur ‘habitus’ huiusmodi²: ‘habitus’² quidem, quia denominat subiectum habere quod sibi accidentaliter adiacet. 3ᵐ. genus est quando habituata formantur a subiectis habituatis³ †perfeccius quam quando membris aptata induuntur.†³ 4ᵐ. genus habitus, ut anulus in digito. Omnia **autem** hec quatuor, cum⁴ denominant substanciam habituari vel habere, dicuntur in predicacione secundum essenciam esse⁵ ‘habitus;’ ut ‘motus’ dicitur materialiter esse res, que est motu dicto formaliter acquisita, ut patet 3°. Physicorum 9°· 4°. Sumitur **autem** sufficiencia illorum quatuor penes hoc, quod primus habitus mutat substanciam cui advenit et⁶ non mutatur; secundus⁷ mutat et mutatur⁷; tercius mutatur et non mutat; quartus nec mutat necessario nec mutatur.

ENGLISH: But concerning the name "[habit]{g:habitus}" it is to be noted that "habit" is taken in four ways, as Augustine expounds in the *Eighty-Three Questions*, question 73, expounding that word of the Apostle to the Philippians 2, "in habit he was found as a man" (Phil 2:7).[^3] First, for wisdom, which is the truth that we learn, which, remaining unmoved, moves the soul that it informs. Second, for the foods we have, which change the body they nourish and are changed by digestion. Indeed every substantial thing lying beside us which changes and is changed by lying beside its [subject]{g:subiectum} is called a "habit" of this kind: a "habit", that is, because it [names]{g:denominare} the subject as having what lies beside it accidentally. The third genus is when the things worn are formed by the subjects that wear them, †more perfectly than when, fitted to the limbs, they are put on.†[^4] The fourth genus of habit, as a ring on the finger. And all these four, since they name a substance as being habited or as having, are said in [predication by essence]{g:predicacio-secundum-essenciam} to be "habits"; as "motion" is said, taken materially, to be the thing which is acquired by motion taken formally, as is clear from *Physics* III, 9, 4.[^5] And the sufficiency of those four is taken according to this: that the first habit changes the substance to which it comes and is not changed; the second changes and is changed; the third is changed and does not change; the fourth neither necessarily changes nor is changed.

### 07-d ¶3 · 118.31–119.10  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 118] **Sed** preter hec quatuor, que⁸ in se sunt⁸ substancie, sumitur habitus apud philosophos formaliter ad duos⁹ sensus [p. 119] equivocos. Primo pro qualitate preternaturali¹ de prima specie qualitatis; sive² sit² corporis habitus, ut sanitas; sive habitus anime, ut virtus intellectualis aut moralis³; habitus **autem** spiritualis ex habitu primo modo dicto³ materialiter generatur, sicut⁴ habitus corporalis efficitur ex secundo. **Sed** secundo modo sumitur habitus pro forma respectiva de 10°. genere, quod est habere, possessio, vel habicio, et talis habitus causatur ab habitu tercio et quarto modis materialiter intellectis: ‘divicie’ enim denominant homines divites,⁵ possessionatos, vel habentes formaliter.

ENGLISH: But besides these four, which in themselves are substances, "habit" is taken among the philosophers formally in two [119] equivocal senses. First, for a preternatural quality of the first species of quality;[^6] whether it is a habit of the body, as health, or a habit of the soul, as an intellectual or moral [virtue]{g:virtus}; and the spiritual habit is generated materially from habit said in the first way, as the bodily habit is produced from the second. But in the second way "habit" is taken for a [relational]{g:respectivus} form of the tenth genus, which is having, possession, or [holding]{g:habicio};[^7] and such a habit is caused by habit in the third and fourth ways, understood materially: for "riches" name men rich, [possessioned]{g:possessionatus}, or having, formally.

### 07-d ¶4 · 119.11–119.27  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 119] Et patet quod ille sex maneries habitus sunt satis equivoce. Dicitur ergo quod humanitas assumpta a Verbo est habitus tercii modi, cum accidit enti in actu, non mutans vel faciens ipsam aliam personam quam prefuit. Ideo dicunt sancti quod humanitas est quasi vestis detegens deitatem;⁶ et religiosi, qui Christum induunt, habent habitus⁷ corporis; hoc notantes, quod⁸ accidit Deo humanitas, **sed** non⁹ inseparabiliter. Ideo dicit Augustinus in Dialogo ad Felicianum quod humanitas est accidens Verbo, non quod sit res inherens ut accidencia novem generum, cum sit precipua creata substancia; nec quod sit coëva Verbo vel sicut passio naturaliter consequens¹⁰ ad subiectum; **sed** contingenter ex tempore nobis ineffabiliter¹¹ inest Verbo non mutando naturam, cui advenit, **sed** formata¹² mirabiliter, quia Verbo Dei ydemptificata vel ypostatice copulata: cum secundum Augustinum primo De Trinitate, ‘talis fuit unio¹³ incarnacionis, que Deum faceret¹⁴ hominem et hominem Deum.’

ENGLISH: And it is clear that those six [kinds]{g:maneries} of habit are quite [equivocally]{g:equivoce} so called. So the answer is that the humanity [assumed]{g:assumere} by the Word is a habit of the third mode, since it befalls [a being]{g:ens} in act, not changing it or making it another person than it was before.[^8] So [the saints]{g:sancti} say that the humanity is as it were a garment exposing the Godhead; and [the religious]{g:religiosi}, who put on Christ, have habits of the body;[^9] noting this: the humanity befalls God, but inseparably.[^10] So Augustine says in the *Dialogue to Felicianus* that the humanity is an accident to the Word: not that it is a thing inhering like the accidents of the nine genera, since it is the foremost created substance; nor that it is coeval with the Word, or like an attribute naturally following on its subject;[^11] but contingently, from time, in a way ineffable to us, it is in the Word, not by changing the nature to which it comes, but formed wonderfully, because identified with the Word of God or [hypostatically]{g:ypostatice} joined to him: since, according to Augustine, *On the Trinity* book 1, "such was the [union]{g:unio} of the incarnation, that it would make God man and man God."[^12]

### 07-d ¶5 · 119.28–120.18  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 119] Et patet quod dicta humanitas est forma in tribus conveniens cum formis per se in genere accidentis. Primo in hoc quod presupponit naturam, cui contingenter advenit, longe priorem ipsa naturaliter, quam accidens presupponit creatam substanciam, quam informat. 2°. in hoc quod non facit [p. 120] naturam, cui advenit, esse¹ quid vel aliud² quam absolute necessario semper erat.³ Et 3°. quod non potest esse sine supposito Verbi Dei, cui inseparabiliter **sed** contingenter adheret. Et constat quod proprietates proporcionales analogice conveniunt generi accidentis: assimilatur **autem** passionibus, que dicuntur per se egredi de propriis principiis⁴ subiectorum; cum non potest abesse, postquam infuit, cum inseparabiliter consequitur⁵ ad Verbum, sicut passio ad subiectum; **sed**, sicut accidentia separabilia, deesse poterit a subiecto. Convenit 2°. cum accidentibus absolutis per se in genere, cum sit per se substancia motiva et mobilis denominans subiectum proprie et per se moveri formaliter. 3°. convenit⁶ cum accidentibus respectivis. Nam, sicut relaciones, habitaciones, et respectus consimiles non exinde movent naturam, cui inherenter adveniunt; sic indubie ‘forma servi,’ que est humanitas, non potest movere naturam divinam, cum deitas solum denominacione respectiva denominatur ex eius adiacencia summe mirabili.⁷

ENGLISH: And it is clear that the said humanity is a form [agreeing]{g:convenire} in three things with forms that are [of themselves]{g:per-se} in the genus of accident. First, in this, that it presupposes the nature to which it contingently comes, by nature far prior to it, [more] than an accident presupposes the created substance which it informs.[^13] Second, in this, that it does not make [120] the nature to which it comes be something, or other than it absolutely necessarily always was. And third, that it cannot be without the supposit of the Word of God, to which it adheres inseparably but contingently. And it is evident that proportional properties agree analogically with the genus of accident: and it is likened to the attributes, which are said to issue of themselves from the proper principles of their subjects; since it cannot be absent after it has been present, since it follows inseparably on the Word, as an attribute on its subject; but, like separable accidents, it could be lacking from the subject.[^14] It agrees, second, with absolute accidents that are of themselves in the genus, since it is of itself a substance motive and [movable]{g:mobilis}, naming the subject properly and of itself as moved formally. Third, it agrees with relational accidents. For, as relations, [habitations]{g:habitacio}, and like respects do not thereby move the nature to which they come by inhering; so without doubt "the form of a servant" (Phil 2:7), which is the humanity, cannot move the divine nature, since the Godhead is named only by a [relational naming]{g:denominacio-respectiva} from its most wonderful lying-beside.

### 07-d ¶6 · 120.19–121.7  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 120] Convenit **autem** cum ceteris formis substancialibus⁸ in his tribus. Primo quod facit suppositum, cui advenit, esse aliud quam prefuit; quia, postquam Verbum fuit pure deitas, nunc est homo. 2°. in hoc quod est idem ypostatice cum subiecto, cui advenit. Sicut generaliter omnis forma substancialis est idem essencialiter vel personaliter⁹ cum subiecto forme et composito ex eisdem; sic, inquam, ista humanitas; cum sit eiusdem racionis cum aliis et idem personaliter Verbo Dei, licet Verbum commune sit natura divina, que non potest esse illa humanitas; **sed** cum sit individuum humane nature homo Iesus Christus, suscipit saltem in predicacione et secundum essenciam predicata temporalia, que historia evangelica de Christo contexuit, ut post declarabitur. Et patet mira subtilitas in verbis Apostoli quando dicit ‘habitu inventus ut homo’: non enim video alium terminum, quo [p. 121] posset apcius quoad auditorii intelleccionem, copiosius quoad philosophicam consideracionem, aut expressius quoad fidei explanacionem, humanitatem Christi exprimere. Ideo respectu¹ sentencie Apostoli, qui fuit prudentissimus predicator, et Augustini sui discipuli, qui fuit scripture sacre subtilissimus explanator, oportet nos ignaros colla mentis submittere.

ENGLISH: And it agrees with the other [substantial forms]{g:forma-substancialis} in these three things. First, that it makes the supposit to which it comes be something other than it was before; since, after the Word was purely Godhead, now he is man. Second, in this, that it is the same hypostatically with the subject to which it comes. As in general every substantial form is the same essentially or personally with the subject of the form and with the composite of the two; so, I say, is this humanity; since it is [of the same kind]{g:eiusdem-racionis} as the others and the same personally as the Word of God, although the Word [taken as] common is the divine nature, which cannot be that humanity;[^15] but since the man Jesus Christ is an [individual]{g:individuum} of human nature, he [takes up]{g:suscipere}, at least in predication and by essence, the temporal predicates which the gospel history has woven together about Christ, as will be [made clear]{g:declarare} later. And a wonderful subtlety is clear in the words of the Apostle when he says "in habit found as a man" (Phil 2:7): for I do not see another term by which [121] he could express the humanity of Christ more aptly as to the understanding of his hearers, more copiously as to philosophical consideration, or more expressly as to the explanation of the faith. So, with respect to the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of the Apostle, who was the most prudent preacher, and of Augustine his disciple, who was the subtlest explainer of holy scripture, we ignorant ones must bow the necks of the mind.

### 07-e ¶1 · 121.8–122.24  (6 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 121] Quantum ad primam trium confirmacionum² patet quod similitudo non est sufficiens³; cum humanitas Christi non solum sit ut vestimentum Verbo, vel ignis⁴ ferro,⁴ **sed** vera Christi quiditas, idem personaliter cum subiecto. Ad 2ᵃᵐ negatur consequencia quod mocio, passio, et similia positiva, cum sint predicata personalia, sunt⁵ persone Verbi racione⁶ forme assumpte⁶ principaliter tribuenda. Non sic **autem** negaciones, que non sunt predicata personalia. Ideo dixi superius quod incepcio non est esse et prius non fuisse, **sed** causatur ex illis; cum stat illa esse eterna et incepcionem solum instantaneam, et faciens incepcionem non potest facere dictam negacionem. Ideo non oportet, si Deus temporaliter generatur, quod ante⁷ non fuit; cum satis est quod sit illud ex vi generacionis, quod non effectualiter fuit ante. Ad 3ᵃᵐ patet quod non oportet in toto esse correspondenciam figurati cuiuslibet ad figuram. Unde satis est quod Abraham ducens filium significet Deum Patrem, qui de facto duxit et obtulit super montem Calvarie filium suum unigenitum, sicut Ysaac unigenitus imolatus⁸ est super montem ubi templum constitutum est. Ipse quidem fuit forma risus domini nostri Iesu Christi: et satis est quod humanitas idem personaliter Ysaac nostro, sicut aries est idem genere cum Ysaac figurante,⁹ sic passa et occisa persona Verbi secundum deitatem et animam semper salva; et sic de aliis, que huic loco non pertinet¹⁰ applicare. **Sed** constat repugnare figure quod sit in toto conformitas; quia tunc evidenter [p. 122] sequeretur¹ Christi mobilitas; cum Ysaac portavit ligna sicut Christus crucem, ligatus est super² struem, sicut Christus affixus est ad crucem. Igitur cum Ysaac noster ex partibus³ quantitativis componitur, que tam⁴ varie movebantur: et, mota parte tocius, movetur et totum, cuius est pars; sequitur quod Christus compositus ex illis partibus movebatur. Nec credo⁵ philosophum dicere Deum esse hominem, nisi consequenter dicat eum componi ex partibus, et consequenter moveri ad motum progressivum parcium⁶; cum⁷ impossibile sit aliquid non moveri, quod tamen subiective habeat in se motum.⁷ Unde Ieronimus exponendo fidem catholicam: ‘passus est, inquit, Dei Filius non putative **sed** vere.’ Et idem docet Augustinus in Enchiridion 40: et Decretalis in Clementinis De Summa Trinitate et Fide Catholica: ‘confitemur, inquit, Filium Dei, una cum Patre eternaliter subsistentem, partes nostre nature simul unitas, ex quibus ipse verus Deus in se existens fieret⁸ verus homo, (humanum videlicet⁹ corpus et animam intellectivam seu racionalem, ipsum corpus vere et per se et essencialiter informantem,) assumpsisse ex tempore in virginali thalamo ad unitatem sue ypostasis¹⁰ seu persone: et quod in hac assumpta natura ipsum Dei Verbum pro omni operanda salute non solum affigi cruci et in ea mori voluit, **sed** eciam emisso iam spiritu perforari lancea sustinuit latus suum.’

ENGLISH: As to the first of the three confirmations, it is clear that the likeness is not sufficient; since the [humanity]{g:humanitas} of Christ is not only as a garment to [the Word]{g:verbum}, or fire to iron, but the true [quiddity]{g:quiditas} of Christ, the same personally with the [subject]{g:subiectum}. To the second, [the inference]{g:consequencia} is denied, since motion, passion, and like positive [predicates], being personal predicates, are to be attributed to the [person]{g:persona} of the Word principally by reason of the [assumed]{g:assumere} form. But not so negations, which are not personal predicates.[^1] So I said above that beginning is not to be and not to have been before, but is caused from those; since it is consistent that those be eternal and the beginning only instantaneous, and he who makes the beginning cannot make the said negation. So it is not necessary, if God is begotten in time, that he was not before; since it is enough that he be, by force of the generation, that which he was not effectually before. To the third, it is clear that there need not be, in the whole, a correspondence of every [thing figured]{g:figuratum} to the figure. Hence it is enough that Abraham leading his son signifies God the Father, who in fact led and offered upon Mount Calvary his only begotten son, as Isaac, the only begotten, was sacrificed upon the mountain where the temple was set up (Gen 22:2; II Chron 3:1). He indeed was the form of the laughter of our Lord Jesus Christ:[^2] and it is enough that the humanity, the same personally with our Isaac (as the ram is the same in genus with the Isaac who figures him), suffered thus and was slain, the person of the Word, [as to the Godhead]{g:secundum-deitatem} and the soul, remaining ever safe;[^3] and so of the other things, which it does not pertain to this place to apply. But it is evident that it is [incompatible]{g:repugnare} with a figure that there be conformity in the whole; for then evidently [122] the [movability]{g:mobilitas} of Christ would follow; since Isaac carried the wood as Christ the cross, and was bound upon the pile as Christ was fixed to the cross (Gen 22:6, 9). Therefore, since our Isaac is composed of [quantitative parts]{g:pars-quantitativa}, which were moved so variously; and, when a part of a whole is moved, the whole of which it is the part is moved too; [it follows]{g:sequitur} that Christ, composed of those parts, was moved. Nor do I believe that a philosopher says God is man, unless he consequently says that he is composed of parts, and consequently is moved with the progressive motion of the parts; since it is impossible that anything not be moved which yet has motion subjectively in itself. Hence Jerome, expounding the catholic faith: "the Son of God," he says, "suffered, not in seeming but truly."[^4] And Augustine teaches the same in the *Enchiridion*, 40;[^5] and the Decretal in the Clementines, *On the Supreme Trinity and the Catholic Faith*: "we confess," it says, "that the Son of God, subsisting eternally together with the Father, assumed in time in the virginal chamber, to the unity of his [hypostasis]{g:ypostasis} or person, parts of our [nature]{g:natura} united together, from which he, existing in himself true God, would become true man (namely a human body and an intellective or rational soul, truly and [of itself]{g:per-se} and essentially informing the body itself); and that in this assumed nature the very Word of God, for the working of all salvation, not only willed to be fixed to the cross and to die on it, but also, the spirit already sent forth, suffered his side to be pierced with a lance."[^6]

### 08-a ¶2 · 123.2–123.12  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 123] Ex istis patet solucio argumentorum factorum communiter ad probandum quod Christus non sit univoce homo cum aliis. Primo per hoc, quod nichil reponitur in specie per aliquod sibi accidentale: **sed** humanitas Christi est sibi accidentalis: ergo non est in nova specie propter illam: **sed** ante incarnacionem non fuit eiusdem speciei nobiscum: ergo nec¹ post propter² adventiciam unionem. Confirmatur per locum a simili. Nam si homo de novo acquirat albedinem vel quamcumque formam aliam accidentaliter³ inherentem, non ex hoc⁴ est disparis speciei: ergo per idem nec Christus propter humanitatem tam adventiciam.

ENGLISH: From these things the solution is clear of the arguments commonly made to prove "Christ is not [univocally]{g:univoce} man with others." First, by this: nothing is placed in a species through something accidental to it: now the [humanity]{g:humanitas} of Christ is accidental to him: therefore he is not in a new species because of it: now before the incarnation he was not of the same species with us: therefore neither after, because of an adventitious [union]{g:unio}. This is confirmed by the topic from likeness. For if a man newly acquires whiteness, or any other form inhering accidentally, he is not by this of a different species: therefore, by the same token, neither is Christ, because of a humanity so adventitious.

### 08-a ¶3 · 123.13–123.21  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 123] Quantum ad illud patet ex dictis quod falsum assumitur. Nam Verbum, quod prius erat super omnem speciem, per assumpcionem humanitatis factum est in nostra specie et ‘sub lege.’ Et quantum ad similitudinem⁵ patet ex dictis racionis diversitas. Si enim per impossibile albedo vel alia forma inherens esset illud, quo subiectum esset aliquid vel aliud quam prius fuerat, (sicut⁶ humanitate fit Verbum aliquid aliud quam prius fuerat),⁶ procederet racio per locum a simili: **sed** antecedens est impossibile.

ENGLISH: As to that, it is clear from what has been said that something false is assumed.[^1] For [the Word]{g:verbum}, who before was above every species, by the [assumption]{g:assumpcio} of the humanity was made in our species and "under the law" (Gal 4:4). And as to the likeness, the difference of the reasoning is clear from what has been said. For if, per impossibile, whiteness or another inhering form were that by which the [subject]{g:subiectum} would be something, or other than it had been before (as by the humanity the Word becomes something other than he had been before), the argument would proceed by the topic from likeness: but the antecedent is impossible.

### 08-a ¶4 · 123.22–124.16  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 123] Et si multiplicentur argumenta sophistica ad probandum quod, quicquid est Verbum, eternaliter fuit Verbum; patet ex dictis de formis solucio. Nam aliquid, quia aliqua [p. 124] forma substancialis vel natura,¹ nunc est Verbum, quod non in primo instanti temporis erat Verbum. Non enim antequam ‘Verbum caro factum est’ ‘in plenitudine temporis,’ erat homo; et certum² est quod esse hominem est esse aliquid: ideo quando fiebat homo, fiebat aliquid,³ quod prius non fuerat. Verumptamen omne suppositum, ypostasis, vel persona, que est, vel que⁴ fuit, Verbum, eternaliter erat⁵ Verbum. **Sed** absit ex isto concludere—ergo quicquid.⁶ Aliud enim, sed⁷ non alius, est Verbum post incarnacionem quam prefuit.⁸ Et sic querenti—Quis est⁹ nunc Filius Dei, qui non semper erat ?—Idem—dicitur¹⁰: quod nullus¹¹ est vel esse potest Filius Dei naturalis, nisi¹² qui absolute necessario semper erat. **Sed** querenti—Quid est Filius Dei, quod ante incarnacionem ipse non fuerat ?—dicitur quod— Natura humana sive humanitas; et illa est quiditas suppositi vel persone—.

ENGLISH: And if sophistical arguments are multiplied to prove "whatever is the Word was eternally the Word", the solution is clear from what has been said about forms. For something, since it is some [124] [substantial form]{g:forma-substancialis} or [nature]{g:natura}, is now the Word which was not the Word in the first instant of time.[^2] For before "the Word was made flesh" (John 1:14) "in the fulness of time" (Gal 4:4), he was not man;[^3] and it is certain that to be a man is to be something: so when he was becoming man, he was becoming something which he had not been before. Nevertheless every [supposit]{g:suppositum}, [hypostasis]{g:ypostasis}, or [person]{g:persona} which is, or which was, the Word, was eternally the Word. But far be it to conclude from this, "therefore whatever".[^4] For the Word is something other, but not someone other, after the incarnation than he was before. And so to one asking, "Who is now the Son of God who was not always?", "The same", [the answer is]{g:dicitur}: [meaning] that no one is or can be the natural Son of God except one who absolutely necessarily always was.[^5] But to one asking, "What is the Son of God that he had not been before the incarnation?", the answer is: "Human nature, or humanity; and that is the [quiddity]{g:quiditas} of the supposit or person."

### 08-a ¶5 · 124.17–124.23  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 124] Et patet solucio ad tales argucias—Omnis¹³ persona, que est Verbum, eternaliter erat Verbum: **sed** quicquid iam est¹⁴ Verbum est persona, que est Verbum: ergo, quicquid iam est Verbum,¹⁵ eternaliter erat Verbum. Omnes quidem deducciones tales sophistice sanantur¹⁶ per noticiam fallacie accidentis. Nam persone Verbi accidentalis est quiditas in genere, sicut substancie create accidentalis est qualitas.

ENGLISH: And the solution is clear to such [quibbles]{g:argucie} as: "Every person which is the Word was eternally the Word: now whatever is now the Word is a person which is the Word: therefore whatever is now the Word was eternally the Word." Indeed all such deductions, [made] [sophistically]{g:sophistice}, are cured by knowledge of the [fallacy]{g:fallacia} of accident.[^6] For quiddity in a genus is accidental to the person of the Word, as quality is accidental to created substance.

### 08-a ¶7 · 124.30–125.23  (4 sed/autem)

### 08-c ¶1 · 130.8–132.9  (4 sed/autem)

Here it seems probable to me that Christ's personality is equivocal or analogical with the personality of any other brother of his; and yet they agree supremely univocally in species or [nature]{g:natura}. So it does not follow, if Christ is not univocally an individual or person with another man, that he is not therefore univocally species or nature with him: for it is known that [univocation]{g:univocacio} in the higher does not argue univocation in the lower; nor does [equivocation]{g:equivocacio} of the lower argue [131] equivocation of the more common, as is evident in an example. For all created things that are [of themselves]{g:per-se} in a genus divided[^3] by opposite [differences]{g:differencia} are univocal in the genus and not below it; as substance and [accident]{g:accidens} are univocal in a certain way in a transcendent analogue, man and ass in the genus of animal, and so of the others: an image[^4] and Hercules, too, are equivocal in the [sign]{g:signum} "man", and yet are univocal in the genus of body or substance: correspondingly Jesus and Peter are equivocal in the sign of personality and yet are univocal in the species of man, and in any created genus more common: since correspondingly, the more common something is, the more it makes univocal; and the more particular, so much the less: so much so that a person or individual cannot make another person univocal with itself; as the person of the [Word]{g:verbum} cannot [assume]{g:assumere} every person of a man, as it cannot any, since then the person would be supremely communicable. For real univocation, of which the discussion now is, is when some things communicate or agree according to the same account in something common; as each person of a man is the same common species that is man, under that common account — "a rational creature made to the image of God" —; but the person of a man is communicated equivocally to opposite natures, since one is the account or individuating condition which is this body, and another the account or individuating condition which is this spirit. And so those understand it who say that a person, by way of definition, is a rational nature of incommunicable essence or existence.[^5] Nor is it to be denied that under the same signs, although equivocally, both increate and created personality are said analogically; as each divine person is an individual substance of rational nature, and each person of a man is an individual substance of rational nature;[^6] but the natures are very equivocally both rational and essences or natures: [132] Peter too is very equivocally a substance or individual with respect to another, increate person. But on both sides there is a created account exemplified analogically in the higher, increate account of the same name. So the earlier doctors laboured to have in their [definitions]{g:descripcio} names signifying at once, though equivocally, both the eternal exemplars and the [things exemplified]{g:exemplata}; as truth, justice, [free choice]{g:liberum-arbitrium}; and person is said analogically, according to Anselm and Boethius, under those names.[^7]

ENGLISH: But first [it is objected, by appeal to]{g:obicitur-per} this, that Christ is an individual of the human species, and besides this is an individual of the divine nature: therefore he is a twofold individual, as there is a twofold nature.

### 08-c ¶3 · 132.13–132.18  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 132] Hic dicitur negando consequenciam sicut non sequitur, si Christus sit Filius Dei et filius hominis, quod sit duplex filius; idem enim est individuum nature humane et nature divine, **sed** per accidens individuum nature humane, quia secundum naturam assumptam, per se **autem** individuum, persona, vel suppositum nature divine.

ENGLISH: Here [the answer is]{g:dicitur}: by denying the [inference]{g:consequencia}, as it does not follow, if Christ is the Son of God and the Son of man, that he is a twofold son; for the same is the individual of the human nature and of the divine nature, but an individual of the human nature [by accident]{g:per-accidens}, since [it is] as to the assumed nature, but of itself the individual, person, or [supposit]{g:suppositum} of the divine nature.

### 08-c ¶4 · 132.19–132.23  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 132] 2°. obicitur per hoc quod universale et singulare dicuntur ad aliquid et per consequens sunt simul natura: **sed** Christus solum temporaliter est universale: ergo solum temporaliter est singulare; et per consequens aliqua individuatio solum temporaliter inest Christo.

ENGLISH: Second, it is objected, by appeal to this, that [universal]{g:universale} and singular are said relatively and consequently are simultaneous by nature:[^8] now Christ is universal only temporally: therefore he is singular only temporally; and consequently some individuation is in Christ only temporally.

### 08-c ¶5 · 132.24–133.5  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 132] Hic conceditur prima conclusio. Nam singulare et⁵ universale sunt res secunde intencionis que sese mutuo relative respiciunt ita, quod singulare, ut⁶ sic, sit individuum vel suppositum speciei. Ideo nulla persona divina est ad illum sensum⁷ quoad deitatem universalis vel singularis, ut patet in De Questionibus Veteris et Nove Legis. Questione 122; **sed** est⁸ individuum suppositum vel persona, Cum ista sint nomina significancia analogice extra genus, ut patet de nomine individui in⁹ descripcione Boecii De Persona. Nec est vis¹⁰ quare philosophi sic varie utuntur [p. 133] nominibus. Sicut ergo Christus solum ex tempore est singularis, sic quod singularitas a posteriori accidit sue¹ individuitati vel personalitati, sicut accidit sibi quod sit persona speciei humane: ideo individuitas vel personalitas in Christo non est singularitas que supponitur speciei.

ENGLISH: Here the first [conclusion]{g:conclusio} is granted. For singular and universal are [things of second intention]{g:res-secunde-intencionis} which regard one another relatively, in such a way that the singular, as such, is an individual or supposit of a species. So no divine person is in that sense, as to the [Godhead]{g:deitas}, universal or singular, as is clear in *De quaestionibus Veteris et Novae Legis*, question 122;[^9] but he is an individual, supposit or person, since these are names signifying analogically outside the genus, as is clear of the name "individual" in Boethius's definition, *De persona*. Nor is there any weight in why the philosophers use the [133] names so variously. As, therefore, Christ is singular only from time, so that singularity befalls his individuality or personality a posteriori, as it befalls him that he is a person of the human species: so individuality or personality in Christ is not the singularity that stands as supposit under the species.[^10]

### 08-c ¶7 · 133.13–133.28  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 133] Hic negatur causalis assumpta eo quod significat circumstanciam cause.⁴ Conceditur tamen de Christo vel aliis quod, quia est iste homo, ideo est persona hominis: **sed** non, quia persona hominis, ideo est persona; cum habet personalitatem nature superioris quam est eius⁵ humanitas, quod nulli alii homini potest competere; cum ipse solus⁶ sit homo per accidens et cum hoc summe univoce. Est **autem** in Christi duplex nativitas, una eterna secundum naturam divinam, que eternaliter absolute necessario personaliter nascitur et est natus; et illi accidit alia temporalis non interpellatim⁷ **sed** simul adveniens, secundum quam fuit natura alia et noviter persona hominis. Non enim sequitur —Christus solum temporaliter vel noviter est persona, individuum, vel suppositum humanum: ergo⁸ sic est suppositum—cum⁸ arguitur ab inferiori ad suum superius cum impedimento habente vim negacionis.

ENGLISH: Here the causal [proposition] assumed is denied,[^11] because it signifies the circumstance of a cause. It is granted, however, of Christ or of the others that, because he is this man, therefore he is a person of a man: but not that, because [he is] a person of a man, therefore he is a person; since he has the personality of a nature higher than his humanity is, which can belong to no other man; since he alone is man by accident and with this supremely univocally.[^12] And there is in Christ a twofold birth, one eternal as to the divine nature, which eternally, absolutely, necessarily, personally is born and has been born; and to it there befalls another, temporal, not at an interval but coming at once,[^13] according to which he was another nature and newly a person of a man. For it does not [follow]{g:sequitur} — "Christ only temporally or newly is a person, individual, or human supposit: therefore he is so a supposit" — since it is argued from the lower to its higher with an impediment having the force of negation.[^14]

### 08-c ¶8 · 133.29–134.3  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 133] 4°. obicitur per hoc quod Christus recenter est tam corpus quam anima, et utrumque illorum est suppositum et persona: ergo Christus recenter est suppositum vel persona: sic⁹ enim Christus⁹ est corpus et spiritus creatus: [p. 134] **sed** utrumque illorum est per se individuum et persona: ergo Christus est persona creata: et cum eciam sit persona increata, sequitur quod duplex personalitas inest Christo.

ENGLISH: Fourth, it is objected, by appeal to this, that Christ recently is both body and soul, and each of them is a supposit and a person: therefore Christ recently is a supposit or person: for thus Christ is body and created spirit: [134] now each of those is of itself an individual and person: therefore Christ is a created person: and since he is also an increate person, it follows that a twofold personality is in Christ.

### 08-d ¶1 · 134.4–135.15  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 134] Hic dicitur quod antecedens primi argumenti est verum et consequens impossibile. Nec est verum quod aliqua creatura preter Christum est primo per se suppositum vel persona; quia in omni persona creata proprietates accidentales individuant ipsam; et sic idem est sibi personalitas et singularitas que ad ipsam consequitur, sicut passio ad subiectum: Christus **autem** solum personatur per se primo ex¹ divinitate,¹ et non ex proprietatibus accidentalibus ut alia singularia. Ideo non mirum, si sua personalitas recipit singularitates personarum,² ut sibi accidencia, et non ut personalitates. Due ergo nature create eciam in sancto sabato sunt simul utraque eadem persona Verbi. Si ergo persona esset per se superius ad istam naturam, sicut aliquid est ad hominem, tunc sequeretur—si³ Christus est hoc singulare creatum, tunc⁴ est ista persona creata—; sicut sequitur, quod Christus⁵ sit aliquid creatum,⁴ ex hoc quod est natura creata; et tunc foret duplex persona creata, sicut est duplex natura creata, quarum utraque est persona. Modo⁶ **autem** ille ambe nature, et utraque illarum, sunt eciam pro triduo separate idem individuum, suppositum, et persona: licet enim sint due essencie singulares, tamen sunt unita individua substancia nature humane. Et sophista satis colorate concederet⁷ quod illa persona, demonstrando humanitatem Christi, est persona et natura eterna. Ideo in talibus oportet ultra pronomina significancia pure essencias explicare res de quibus loquitur per terminos specificantes formas.* Quod⁸ si arguitur eo ipso quo ponitur aliqua⁹ per se [p. 135] et completa causa causabilis, sequitur illud causabile: **sed** natura singularis in Christo cum communi creata substancia sufficit per se et complete causare creatum suppositum: ergo illis positis et unitis, sicut est in Christo, sequitur quod oportet ponere creatam personam ex illis principiis resultantem: nam eiusdem speciei specialissime est creata natura atque substancia in Christo, cuius est in quolibet fratre suo. Sicut ergo Christus est univoce cum Petro racionalis nature individua substancia, sic cum eo univoce est persona: cui enim univocatur diffinicio eciam competit univoce diffinitum. Confirmatur ex hoc quod tractantes pollitice¹ cum Christo acceptarent univoce cum aliis personam suam in testimonium et iudicium et quodlibet aliud opus sensibile vel quodlibet personale. Nec potest fingi quod deitas impedit ista principia ad causandum personam.

ENGLISH: Here [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that the [antecedent]{g:antecedens} of the first argument is true and the [consequent]{g:consequens} impossible.[^1] Nor is it true that any creature besides Christ is first and [of itself]{g:per-se} a [supposit]{g:suppositum} or [person]{g:persona}; since in every created person accidental properties individuate it; and so personality and the singularity which follows on it are the same for it, as an [attribute]{g:passio} [follows] on its [subject]{g:subiectum}: but Christ is made a person, first and of itself, only from the [divinity]{g:divinitas}, and not from accidental properties like other singulars. So no wonder if his personality receives the singularities of persons as accidents to it, and not as personalities.[^2] Therefore the two created natures, even on the holy Sabbath, are, each of them together, the same person of the [Word]{g:verbum}.[^3] If, therefore, person were of itself superior to this [nature]{g:natura}, as something is to man, then it would follow — "if Christ is this created singular, then he is this created person" —; as it follows that Christ is something created from the fact that he is a created nature; and then there would be a twofold created person, as there is a twofold created nature, each of which is a person. But as it is, both those natures, and each of them, are, even when separated for [the three days]{g:triduum}, the same [individual]{g:individuum}, supposit, and person: for although they are two singular [essences]{g:essencia}, yet they are a united individual substance of human nature. And a [sophist]{g:sophista} would grant, [with colour enough]{g:colorate}, that that person — pointing to Christ's [humanity]{g:humanitas} — is a person and an eternal nature. So in such matters one must, beyond pronouns signifying pure essences, make explicit the things one speaks of by terms specifying the forms. But if it is argued:[^4] by the very fact that some cause, of itself and complete, of a causable thing is posited, [135] that causable thing follows: now the singular nature in Christ, with the common created substance, suffices of itself and completely to cause a created supposit: therefore, those being posited and united, as is the case in Christ, it follows that one must posit a created person resulting from those principles: for the created nature and substance in Christ is of the same [most special species]{g:species-specialissima} as that in each of his brothers. As, therefore, Christ is univocally with Peter an individual substance of rational nature, so he is univocally a person with him: for to whatever the [definition]{g:diffinicio} belongs univocally, the thing defined also belongs [univocally]{g:univoce}. This is confirmed from this, that those dealing civilly with Christ would accept his person, univocally with others, in testimony and judgment and in any other [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} work or any personal one.[^5] Nor can it be feigned that the [Godhead]{g:deitas} impedes these principles from causing a person.

### 08-d ¶2 · 135.16–136.7  (8 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 135] Hic dicitur quod minor est falsa cum nulla creatura causat naturaliter nisi de quanto a posteriori subicitur voluntati divine prius causanti. **Sed** voluntas divina non potest ex istis causare creatam personam: nam assumpcio impedit, vel verius iuvat ut natura creata sit suppositum increatum. Cum enim omne recipiens denudatur a natura rei recepte, oporteret Verbum vel esse duas personas, ut dixit Nestorius; vel recipiendo personalitatem creatam personalitatem increatam dimittere; quorum utrumque cum sit summe impossibile, sequitur quod natura rei exaltantis naturam assumptam, ut quasi insorbendo, non permittat² eam habere personalitatem creatam. Et hoc intendunt loquentes de suppositacione aliena. Et dicitur quod ista natura est persona gloriosissima, **sed** illi non competit diffinicio persone create. Pro quo notandum quod substancia est commune in Greco ad ypostasim, usiosim, et usion: quando **autem** diffinitur persona per nomen substancie intelligitur ypostasis, quam alii differenter explicant per nomen subsistencie. Et sic Christus non est racionalis nature individua subsistencia vel suppositum, [p. 136] nisi suppositum nature increate. Ideo natura assumpta non est formaliter persona **sed** ydemptice vel ypostatice. Non enim est formaliter ypostasis vel subsistencia, **sed** substancia vel usion. Ideo in Christo sunt formaliter tres substancie, **sed** unica subsistencia, **sed** ille due nature create faciunt unam naturam completam, que est humanitas, non **autem** personam, cum deest complementum ex parte cause finalis.

ENGLISH: Here the answer is that the minor is false, since no creature causes naturally except insofar as it is subjected a posteriori to the divine will, which causes first. Now the divine will cannot cause a created person out of these: for the [assumption]{g:assumpcio} impedes, or more truly helps [to bring it about] that the created nature be an increate supposit. For since every recipient is stripped of the nature of the thing received,[^6] it would be necessary for the Word either to be two persons, as Nestorius said, or, by receiving created personality, to [lay aside]{g:dimittere} the increate personality; and since each of these is supremely impossible, it follows that the nature of the exalting thing, as it were absorbing the assumed nature, does not permit it to have a created personality. And this is what they [maintain]{g:intendere} who speak of "alien [suppositation]{g:suppositacio}". And it is said that this nature is a most glorious person, but the definition of a created person does not belong to it. For which it is to be noted that "substance" is common in Greek to *ypostasis*, *usiosis*, and *usion*: and when person is defined by the name "substance", [hypostasis]{g:ypostasis} is understood, which others explain differently by the name "subsistence". And so Christ is not an individual subsistence of rational nature, or a supposit, [136] except the supposit of the increate nature. So the assumed nature is not formally a person but identically or [hypostatically]{g:ypostatice}. For it is not formally hypostasis or subsistence, but substance or *usion*. So in Christ there are formally three substances, but a single subsistence; but those two created natures make one complete nature, which is the humanity, not, however, a person, since the completion on the part of the final cause is lacking.[^7]

### 08-d ¶3 · 136.8–136.27  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 136] Ulterius conceditur quod conversantes cum Christo putando eum esse univoce personam cum aliis hominibus ex ignorrancia sue divinitatis [acceptarent],¹ univoce personam suam in testimonium.¹ Ideo non potest iudicare² vel conversari civiliter, ut patet alibi, **sed** omne iudicium suum foret infinitum maioris auctoritatis quam iudicium persone create. Et ita videtur de omni actu personali quem fecit humanitus: auctoritas enim consequitur personam in dignitate et non naturam accionis pure. Et patet quod descripcio persone signata convertitur cum ista racionalis nature incommunicabilis existencia. Incommunicabilis dico quoad supposita, non naturas; et ita subsistencia est contractior quam substancia, ut sumitur ad hec tria. Et ita Trinitas commune principium et commune principatum non est persona vel subsistencia, **sed** persone vel subsistencie. Nec est color in illa equivocacione, quod divicie sunt substancia racionalis nature, **sed** substancia restricta ad subsistenciam est consequens ad omne suppositum substanciale. Tales*³ sunt multe argucie quibus decipiuntur incauti, facte pro stabilienda opinione Nestorii.

ENGLISH: Further, it is granted that those [living their life]{g:conversari} with Christ, thinking him to be univocally a person with other men out of ignorance of his divinity, [would accept] his person univocally in testimony.[^8] So he cannot judge or live his life civilly, as is clear elsewhere, but all his judgment would be of infinitely greater [authority]{g:auctoritas} than the judgment of a created person. And so it seems of every personal act that he did [humanly]{g:humanitus}: for authority follows the person in dignity, and not the nature of the action purely. And it is clear that the [definition]{g:descripcio} of person here signified is [convertible]{g:converti} with this: "incommunicable existence of rational nature".[^9] Incommunicable, I say, as to supposits, not natures; and so subsistence is narrower than substance, as [substance] is taken with respect to these three. And so the Trinity, common principle and common principality,[^10] is not a person or subsistence, but persons or subsistences. Nor is there [colour]{g:color} in that equivocation, that riches are "substance" of a rational nature,[^11] but substance restricted to subsistence is consequent on every substantial supposit. Of this kind are many [quibbles]{g:argucie} by which the unwary are deceived, made for establishing the [opinion]{g:opinio} of Nestorius.

### 08-d ¶4 · 136.28–137.8  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 136] **Sed** 4°. principaliter arguitur contra ydemptitatem specificam Christi cum aliis ex parte materie. Nam semen decisum⁴ a diversis sexubus et commixtum est alterius speciei quam simplex semen sexus feminei: sed⁵ Christus conceptus est pure ex semine sexus feminei,⁵ omnes **autem** alii ex commixtis seminibus maris et feminei: ergo materia ex qua [p. 137] factus¹ est Christus est alterius speciei quam materie proxime aliorum: et cum agens semper dat formam secundum condignitatem materie, ut patet philosophis, sequitur quod Christus sit alterius speciei quam alii secundum communem cursum nature univoce² procreati. Confirmatur per Commentatorem 8vo. Physicorum 9. 46. ponentem contra Avicennam quod si homo generaretur ex terra, ipse esset homo equivoce propter extraneacionem materie.

ENGLISH: But fourth, [as a main head]{g:principaliter}, it is argued against Christ's [identity of species]{g:ydemptitas-specifica} with the others on the part of matter. For seed cut off from diverse sexes and mingled is of another species than the simple seed of the female sex: now Christ was conceived purely from the seed of the female sex,[^12] but all the others from the mingled seeds of male and female: therefore the matter from which [137] Christ was made is of another species than the [proximate]{g:materia-proxima} matters of the others: and since an agent always gives a form according to the condignity of the matter, as is clear to the philosophers, it follows that Christ is of another species than the others, procreated univocally according to the common course of nature. This is confirmed by the Commentator, *Physics* 8, comment 46,[^13] who holds against Avicenna that if a man were generated from earth, he would be a man equivocally on account of the [estrangement]{g:extraneare} of the matter.

### 08-d ¶5 · 137.9–137.21  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 137] Hic dicitur quod illud argumentum deficit in pluribus. Primo in assumpto quod semen muliebre, quod differt a menstruo, non distat a virili semine nisi secundum maius et minus; sic quod semen virile de communi cursu nature sit in substancia spissius, complexione³ calidius, et informacione virtuosius. **Sed** quale magisterium Deo de beata virgine complexione³ mundissima descindere⁴ semen vel sanguinem pro mensura delectationis sue humilime ex gaudio salutacionis⁵ angelice, que excedat⁶ in bonitate⁷ complexionis⁷ in proporcione multitudinis et in limitacione virtutis omnem aliam materiam, ex qua fuit persona hominis procreata? quod quia factum est, unde non daretur illi capaci⁸ organo spiritus consimilis speciei?

ENGLISH: Here the answer is that that argument fails in several respects. First in the [assumption]{g:assumptum}, since female seed, which differs from the menstrual blood, does not differ from male seed except according to more and less;[^14] so that male seed, in the common course of nature, is thicker in substance, hotter in [complexion]{g:complexio}, and more powerful in informing. But what feat of mastery [was it] for God to cut from the blessed virgin, of most pure complexion, seed or blood according to the measure of her most humble delight from the joy of the angelic salutation, such as would exceed in goodness of complexion, in proportion of quantity and in limitation of power, every other matter from which a person of a man was procreated?[^15] And since this was done, why would a spirit of like species not be given to that head, the organ?[^16]
