# *De benedicta incarnacione*, chapter 3, part 3: against a second incarnation: the pierced side and the bride

## ¶1 · 36.15–37.4

> [p. 36] Sed consequens videtur¹⁴ falsum tripliciter.¹⁵ Primo ex hoc quod tunc resurreccio Christi foret eius nativitas vel generacio. Et sic Christus haberet triplicem nativitatem et generacionem multiplicem: nativitatem unam eternam et duas alias temporales.¹⁶ Nam generacio Christi prima¹⁷ in utero ideo est generacio quia est produccio a non esse hominem ad esse hominem. Cum ergo sic sit pro instanti¹⁸ resurreccionis, sequitur quod illa sit generacio, et per idem nativitas ex¹⁹ sepulcro, sicut foret posito quod semen virginis esset per se positum²⁰ in sepulcro, et²¹ successive vel subito lineatum,²² ac creata anima copulata.²² Nec est locus nativitati corporali pertinens, nisi forte racione conservacionis, sicut nec est pertinens nativitati ex spiritu, de qua Ioh. 3°., sed utrobique habita generacione renati consequitur proporcionaliter²³ [p. 37] nativitas. Oportet ergo ad resurreccionem persone quod eadem persona, que cecidit sopore mortis sive¹ intercisione humanitatis, resurgat; cum spiritus racionalis persone² sit humanitas et sic homo.

But the [consequent]{g:consequens} seems false in three ways. First from this, that then the resurrection of Christ would be his nativity or generation. And so Christ would have a threefold nativity and a manifold generation: one nativity eternal and two others temporal. For the first generation of Christ, in the womb, is a generation for this reason, that it is a production from not being a man to being a man. Since therefore it is so at the instant of the resurrection, [it follows]{g:sequitur} that that is a generation, and by the same token a nativity out of the sepulchre, as it would be if it were posited that the seed of the virgin had been placed by itself in the sepulchre, and successively or suddenly given its lineaments, and a [created]{g:creatus} soul joined [to it].[^1] Nor is place relevant to bodily nativity, except perhaps by reason of preservation, just as it is not relevant to the nativity from the spirit, of which John 3 [speaks]; but on both sides, once the generation is had, the nativity of the one reborn follows proportionally.[^2] [37] It is necessary therefore, for the resurrection of a [person]{g:persona}, that the same person who fell in the sleep of death, or by the interruption of [the humanity]{g:humanitas}, rise again; since the rational spirit of the person is the humanity and so man.[^3]

## ¶2 · 37.5–37.19

> [p. 37] Secundo obicitur contra dictum consequens per hoc, quod mundus per mortem Christi esset quantumlibet imperfectus: quod est impossible, cum illa fuit mors iustissima preciosissima et utilissima perficiens³ totum mundum: et consequencia probatur. Christus, in quantum homo, est pars mundi, ut patet eciam modernis loquentibus, qui concedunt Christum, in quantum homo est, esse creaturam: et mundus est universitas creaturarum. Cum ergo Christum esse hominem plus perficit mundum quam totum usque⁴ celum vel quecunque alia pars mundi; videtur quod, hoc ablato, plus proporcionaliter imperficitur ille mundus: quod est impossibile; cum tanta perfeccio, quanta ad humanitatem Christi attinet, servatur in anima et corpore incorrupto. Ideo cum totum⁵ sit sue partes, patet quod mansit idem homo in triduo.

Second, [it is objected]{g:obicitur} against the said consequent by this, that the world through the death of Christ would be imperfect to any degree you please:[^4] which is impossible, since that was a most just, most precious and most useful death, perfecting the whole world: and the [inference]{g:consequencia} is proved. Christ, insofar as man, is a part of the world, as is clear even to modern speakers, who grant "Christ, insofar as he is man, is a [creature]{g:creatura}": and the world is [the totality of creatures]{g:universitas-creaturarum}. Since therefore "Christ is man" perfects the world more than even the whole heaven[^5] or any other part of the world whatever, it seems that, this being taken away, that world is made proportionally more imperfect: which is impossible, since as much perfection as pertains to the humanity of Christ is preserved in the soul and in the incorrupt body. Therefore, since the whole is its parts, it is clear that he remained the same man in [the three days]{g:triduum}.

## ¶3 · 37.20–39.9

> [p. 37] Tercio confirmatur illud ex⁶ testimonio scripture. Nam Ioh. 19 scribitur: ‘ad Iesum autem cum venissent,⁷ ut⁸ viderunt eum iam mortuum, non fregerunt eius⁹ crura; sed unus militum lancea¹⁰ latus eius¹¹ apperuit,¹² et exivit sanguis¹² et aqua: et qui vidit, inquit Iohannes, testimonium perhibuit;¹³ et verum est testimonium eius.’ Rogo, quomodo venissent ad Iesum mortuum,¹⁴ nisi ille homo mortuus esset Iesus? aut quomodo miles lancea latus eius Iesu aperiret,¹⁵ si non tunc fuerat latus Jesu? Numquid credimus quod evangelista, tam signanter asserens¹⁶ se ista vidisse ultra omnia alia¹⁶ gesta Christi, finxit mendacium? Et si dicatur quod littera occidit, sensus autem spiritualis et rectus vivificat, [p. 38] cuiusmodi est talis ?—cum venissent ad illud corpus mortuum, quod non¹ potuit esse Iesus, sed¹ prius, non tunc fuit corpus Iesu; tunc unus militum, non latus eius, nec univoce latus alicuius aperuit, sed partem unius corporis mortui, quod non tunc fuit Christus vel pars eius²; et exivit, non sanguis redempcionis et aqua baptismatis de latere Christi, quia tunc non fuit Christus. Et sic spiritus, aqua,³ et sanguis non sunt nec fuerunt testes humanitatis Christi, quia non simul fuerunt, sicut videtur Iohannes dicere I Ioh 4°. Nec est verum ad verba illud quod dicit Apostolus ad Ephes. 5°. quod ‘Christus sic dilexit ecclesiam quod tradidit semetipsum pro illa, ut sanctificaret⁴ mundans eam lavacro aque in verbo vite;’ cum ecclesia per totum triduum fuit cum sponso vel capite Christo.⁵ Nec est illud Apostoli ad Ephes 5°. verum ad verba, quando illud Gen. 2.⁶ dictum⁷ ad sensum litteralem de primo Adam et de uxore⁷ sua, exponit ad sensum misticum de Christo et ecclesia: ‘hoc, inquit textus, nunc os ex ossibus meis et caro de carne mea: propter hoc relinquet⁸ homo patrem et matrem suam et adherebit uxori sue, et erunt duo in carne una.’ ‘Sacramentum,⁸ inquit Apostolus, hoc magnum est: ego autem dico in Christo et in ecclesia.’ Illa autem exposicio Apostoli est impossibilis, scilicet quod,⁹ sicut ex costa Adam resoluti¹⁰ in soporem¹¹ edificata est sponsa eius secundum membra ossea solida et tenera, sic ex apertura¹² lateris secundi Adami edificata est ecclesia sponsa Christi secundum varietatem¹³ membrorum ecclesie. Nec reliquit¹⁴ Christus Patrem semetipsum¹⁵ exinaniendo¹⁶ et exeundo a¹⁷ Patre, ut dicitur Ioh. 16°.; et ‘matrem synagogam in qua natus’ est Isa. 50;¹⁸ et adherebit uxori sue scilicet ecclesie, ut dicitur Osee 2°. Nec erant duo coniugata in eadem natura communi tam secundum carnem¹⁹ quam [p. 39] spiritum ac¹ affeccione tam sensitiva quam intellectiva concordes; ut exponit Augustinus et alii sequentes Apostolum. Sed quelibet illarum particularum est impossibilis de vi vocis cum Christus non passus est mortem in cruce; quia pro² instanti mortis Christus non est Christus, nec fuit illud sacramentum in Christo et ecclesia: cum pro toto triduo non fuit Christus, sed sponsa exspectavit usque ad instans resurreccionis sponsalia. Deliramenta sunt ista et verbis puerorum similia.³

Third, this is confirmed from the testimony of scripture. For in John 19 it is written: "But after they were come to Jesus, when they saw that he was already dead, they did not break his legs. But one of the soldiers with a spear opened his side: and there came out blood and water. And he that saw it," says John, "hath given testimony: and his testimony is true."[^6] I ask, how would they have come to Jesus dead, unless that dead man was Jesus? Or how would the soldier with a spear have opened his side, Jesus' side, if it was not then the side of Jesus? Do we believe that the evangelist, so markedly asserting that he saw these things beyond all the other deeds of Christ, made up a lie? And if it is said that the letter kills, but the spiritual and right sense quickens,[^7] [38] of what sort is such [a sense]? — "When they were come to that dead body, which could not be Jesus, but before, not then, was the body of Jesus,[^8] then one of the soldiers opened, not his side, nor [univocally]{g:univoce} the side of anyone, but a part of one dead body, which was not then Christ or a part of him; and there came out, not the blood of redemption and the water of baptism from the side of Christ, since then it was not Christ." And so the spirit, the water and the blood are not and were not witnesses of the humanity of Christ, since they were not together, as John seems to say, 1 John 4.[^9] Nor is that which the Apostle says in Ephesians 5 true to the words, that "Christ so loved the [church]{g:ecclesia} that he delivered himself up for it: that he might sanctify, cleansing it by the laver of water in the word of life";[^10] since the church through the whole three days was with [her] bridegroom or head, Christ. Nor is that [word] of the Apostle in Ephesians 5 true to the words, when he expounds that [word] of Genesis 2, said in the literal sense of the first Adam and his wife, in the mystical sense of Christ and the church: "This now," says the text, "is bone of my bones, and flesh of my flesh: for this cause shall a man leave his father and mother: and shall cleave to his wife. And they shall be two in one flesh." "This," says the Apostle, "is a great [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}:[^11] but I speak in Christ and in the church." And that exposition of the Apostle is impossible,[^12] namely that, as from the rib of Adam, dissolved in sleep, his bride was built according to the bony members, solid and tender, so from the opening of the side of the second Adam the church, the bride of Christ, was built according to the variety of the members of the church. Nor did Christ leave the Father, [emptying]{g:exinanire} himself and going out from the Father, as is said in John 16; and "his mother the synagogue, in which he was born," Isaiah 50;[^13] and [nor] will he cleave to his wife, namely the church, as is said in Hosea 2. Nor were the two joined in wedlock in the same common [nature]{g:natura}, as to the flesh as well as [39] the spirit, and concordant in affection both sensitive and intellective, as Augustine and others following the Apostle expound.[^14] But each of those clauses is impossible [by the force of the words]{g:de-vi-vocis}, since Christ did not suffer death on the cross; for at the instant of death Christ is not Christ; nor was that sacrament in Christ and the church, since for the whole three days there was no Christ, but the bride waited for the espousals until the instant of the resurrection.[^15] These are [ravings]{g:deliramenta}, and like the words of children.

## ¶4 · 39.10–39.21

> [p. 39] Unde omnes moderniores doctores, quorum scripta memini me legisse, dicunt concorditer quod in triduo corpus illud fuit corpus Christi sicut et anima fuit tunc anima Christi: quia manentibus extremis unionis⁴ Verbi ad naturam assumptam, manet eadem unio. Unde dicunt, quod est synecdochica⁵ locucio, sepelierunt Iesum, id est, corpus Iesu. Sed⁶ ego non video, quomodo foret tunc corpus Iesu,⁶ nisi Iesus tunc foret Iesus corporeus, habens omnes partes illius corporis partes suas, iuxta illam⁷ Veritatis vocem Mt 26°. ‘mittens hec unguentum⁸ hoc in corpus meum ad sepeliendum me fecit.’ Pro certo ego credo quod ipse Christus sicut et⁹ quelibet pars corporis sui sepultus est.

Hence all [the more modern doctors]{g:moderniores-doctores} whose writings I remember having read say concordantly that in the three days that body was the body of Christ, just as the soul also was then the soul of Christ: because, while the extremes of the [union]{g:unio} of [the Word]{g:verbum} to the [assumed]{g:assumere} nature remain, the same union remains. Hence they say that "they buried Jesus," that is, the body of Jesus, is a [synecdochic locution]{g:synecdochica-locucio}.[^16] But I do not see how it would then be the body of Jesus, unless Jesus then were a bodily Jesus, having all the parts of that body as his parts, according to that voice of the Truth, Matthew 26: "She in pouring this ointment on my body hath done it for my burial."[^17] For certain I believe that Christ himself, as well as every part of his body, was buried.

## ¶5 · 39.22–40.15

> [p. 39] Ideo hoc¹⁰ concedo cum istis doctoribus in prima parte, cum sit de fide, et concedo de summa virtute sermonis Christum iacuisse in sepulcro; cum illud corpus mortuum fuit persona¹¹ Verbi: non quod¹² quelibet pars quantitativa illius corporis generaretur¹³ pro instanti mortis, sic quod Christus tunc generaretur assumendo novam naturam: sed manens continue idem¹⁴ corpus prius vivum et¹⁵ post mortuum subiectum est perpetuo¹⁶ eidem ypostatice unioni. Nam per totum triduum Christus mansit homo tam secundum corpus quam secundum¹⁷ animam; et vere passus est mortem pro [p. 40] instanti separacionis; cum caro mortua, que tunc¹ fuit Christus, passa est privacionem vite: et vere de latere Christi exierunt aqua et sanguis Christi pro mora exitus; quia Christus pro toto triduo est idem corpus mortuum, cuius sanguine et spiritibus effluentibus, ut anima naturaliter separetur, reservata est miraculose subtilior pars sanguinis cum ichore² ad sponsalia celebranda inter sponsum viventem gloriose in triduo post triumphum et corpus suum misticum, quod est ecclesia. Nam Isae 11°.³ scribitur: ‘radix Iesse⁴ in signum populorum, ipsum gentes deprecabuntur; erit sepulcrum eius gloriosum.’ Et sic intelliguntur alie scripture de Christo ad litteram tam novi quam veteris testamenti. Oportet enim interpretari⁵ scripturas de Christi humanitate secundum sensum, quem in eis sancti doctores sunt sedule scrutati.

Therefore I grant this with these doctors in the first part, since it is of faith; and I grant in the fullest [proper sense of the words]{g:de-virtute-sermonis} that Christ lay in the sepulchre, since that dead body was the person of the Word:[^18] not that every [quantitative part]{g:pars-quantitativa} of that body was generated at the instant of death, so that Christ was then generated by assuming a new nature, but, remaining continuously the same body, before living and afterwards dead, it is subject perpetually to the same [hypostatic]{g:ypostatice} union. For through the whole three days Christ remained man, as to the body as well as as to the soul; and he truly suffered death at the [40] instant of separation, since the dead flesh, which then was Christ, suffered privation of life; and truly from the side of Christ there came out water and blood of Christ, for the duration of its issuing;[^19] for Christ for the whole three days is the same dead body, from which, as its blood and spirits flowed out so that the soul might naturally be separated, the subtler part of the blood was miraculously reserved, with the ichor,[^20] for celebrating the espousals between the bridegroom, living gloriously in the three days after the triumph, and his mystical body, which is the church. For in Isaiah 11 it is written: "The root of Jesse for an ensign of the people, him the Gentiles shall beseech, and his sepulchre shall be glorious."[^21] And so the other scriptures of both the new and the old testament are understood literally of Christ. For the scriptures must be fulfilled concerning Christ's humanity according to the sense which the holy doctors have diligently searched out in them.[^22]

## Notes

[^1]: **T.** 36.24 *lineatum*: A reads *creatum* (*lineatum* in the margin), B *creatum*, C *lineatum*; A and C double *anima*. *Lineatum*: given its lineaments, formed in outline.
[^2]: **C.** 36.27–28 *utrobique habita generacione renati*: *renati* is taken with *nativitas* ("the nativity of the one reborn"); it may hang on *generacione* ("once the generation of the one reborn is had"). *Utrobique*: bodily and spiritual birth (John 3:6).
[^3]: **T.** 37.3 *spiritus racionalis persone*: O reads *species racionalis per se*. *Sive* above: A B C *sine* ("without interruption of the humanity").
[^4]: **C.** 37.6 *quantumlibet imperfectus*: "imperfect to any degree you please": losing Christ's manhood would cost the world more than losing the whole heaven (below).
[^5]: **T.** 37.13 *totum usque celum*: *usque* is taken as intensive ("even"); A B C omit it.
[^6]: **S.** John 19:33–35, without the Vulgate's *continuo* ("immediately"), and with *inquit Iohannes* added. The foot at 37.21 reads "et A B C O, ut Vulg.": the manuscripts have *et* for the print's *ut* ("when").
[^7]: **S.** 2 Cor. 3:6: "the letter killeth: but the spirit quickeneth."
[^8]: **T.** 38 *quod non potuit esse Iesus, sed*: om. A B C.
[^9]: **S.** 1 John 5:8 (Wyclif "4"): "there are three that give testimony on earth: the spirit and the water and the blood. And these three are one." On the moderns' view the three were never together.
[^10]: **S.** Eph. 5:25–26: the Vulgate has *sicut et Christus dilexit ecclesiam et seipsum tradidit pro ea, ut illam sanctificaret* (DR "as Christ also loved the church and delivered himself up for it: That he might sanctify it"). A B C add *eam* after *sanctificaret*.
[^11]: **G.** *Sacramentum*: "sacrament" in the Augustinian sense, a mystery (Eph. 5:32). Gen. 2:23–24 is quoted in the form of Eph. 5:31 (*propter hoc*).
[^12]: **C.** *est impossibilis*: impossible on the moderns' position; this paragraph sets out its consequences, as again at 39.3 (*quelibet illarum particularum*).
[^13]: **S.** John 16:28: "I came forth from the Father." "His mother the synagogue, in which he was born" is not the text of Isaiah (50:1 speaks of "the bill of the divorce of your mother"); A B C cite *Isa. 7*. The phrase is Augustine's, *In Ioh. tract.* 9.10, PL 35, 1462, *Relinquendo synagogam Judaeorum, de qua secundum carnem natus est* (*verified*, LEDGER I03c-1); the Isaiah reference perhaps points to the mother of Isa. 50:1. Hosea 2:19: "I will espouse thee to me for ever."
[^14]: **S.** Augustine on Gen. 2:24 and Eph. 5:31–32 (Christ leaving the Father and the synagogue his mother), *In Ioh. tract.* 9.10, PL 35, 1462: *Reliquit Patrem, quia … semetipsum exinanivit*; *Relinquendo synagogam Judaeorum … et inhaerendo Ecclesiae*; *Dormit Adam ut fiat Eva: moritur Christus ut fiat Ecclesia* (*verified*, LEDGER I03c-2).
[^15]: **C.** 39.3–5 *cum Christus non passus est mortem in cruce ...*: these clauses state what follows on the moderns' position, not Wyclif's assertions.
[^16]: **T.** 39.14 *synecdochica*: Harris's correction (*pro synecdochica, i.e. per synecdochen*) of A *synochica*, B *sinodochia*, C *syndochica*, O *synodogica*: the figure that names the whole (Jesus) for the part (his body).
[^17]: **S.** Matt. 26:12, without the Vulgate's *enim* (DR "For she"); A B C omit *hec*.
[^18]: **T.** 39.25 *persona Verbi*: O adds *Christi*.
[^19]: **C.** 40.3 *pro mora exitus*: "for the duration of its issuing": the water and blood were Christ's while they flowed from him.
[^20]: **T.** 40.7 *ichore*: A B C *iecore* ("liver"), O *ycore*. *Ichor*: the watery humour of the blood.
[^21]: **S.** Isa. 11:10, without the Vulgate's *in die illa* ("in that day") and *qui stat* ("who standeth"), which A B C add; A B C cite *Isa. 7*.
[^22]: **T.** 40.13 *impleri*, with A B C O, against the print's *interpretari*, which is Harris's alone, against every manuscript.
