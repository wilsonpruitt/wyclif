# *De potestate pape*, chapter 6, part 1: the seventh teaching: no apostle had Christ's human power, nor every pope an apostle's

## ¶1 · 102.26–103.7

> [p. 102] Septima facta sentencia premittenda est hec: Sicut nullus apostolorum habuit tantam potestatem quantam habuit Christus humanitus, sic nec omnes [p. 103] pape habent tantam potestatem quantam habuit beatus Petrus vel alius Christi apostolus. Prima pars patet ex primogenitura Christi, que non potest communicari alicui eius vicario, cum tunc foret Deus et homo; cum igitur Christus habuit illam potestatem primogeniture humanitus, sequitur probandum.

The seventh [teaching]{g:sentencia} to be set out first is this: just as none of the apostles had as much [power]{g:potestas} as Christ had in his humanity, so neither do all [103] [popes]{g:papa} have as much power as blessed Peter or another apostle of Christ had.[^1] The first part is clear from the primogeniture of Christ, which cannot be communicated to any vicar of his, since then he would be God and man; since therefore Christ had that power of primogeniture in his humanity, what was to be proved [follows]{g:sequitur}.[^2]

## ¶2 · 103.8–104.8

> [p. 103] Item, Christus habuit potestatem ponendi animam suam et iterum sumendi eam, ut patet Johan. X, et sic non potuit aliquis eius vicarius, ut declarat Augustinus Omelia XLVII, ergo conclusio. Si enim Petrus haberet potestatem iterum sumendi animam suam prius positam, oporteret Petrum esse naturam terciam a corpore et anima cui inesset potestas resumendi animam sic positam, quod soli Deo potest competere, quia oportet dare subiectum cui competeret sic assumere, corpus vero exanime, non habet huiusmodi potestatem nec anima potest se ipsam assumere. Item, Christus habet per se potestatem ad redimendum genus humanum et ad principiandum graciam cuilibet alteri de suo genere, sed sic non habet aliquis eius vicarius, ergo conclusio. Antecedens patet per illud Johan. I, 14: Vidimus gloriam eius quasi unigeniti a patre plenum gracie et veritatis; de cuius plenitudine nos omnes accipimus, graciam pro Gracia. Sic igitur nullus alius potest mutari in Deum et hominem, ut sit per se fons gracie, a quo oportet omnem graciam datam ecclesie derivari, quia sic nec potest alius homo vel angelus Jhesu nostro in potencia, in plenitudine gracie vel preeminencia coequari. [p. 104] Nec hoc verti debet fideli in dubium, qui credit, quod nullus alius potest idemptificari personaliter Jhesu nostro, et quod natura divina ac humana idemptificantur in persona filii, licet ille nature infinite distinguantur, quodammodo necesse est divinitatem Christi in humanitatem eius ex vi unionis supereminenter incomunicabiliter aliis personis influere.

Again, Christ had power to lay down his soul and to take it up again, as is clear from John 10, and so no vicar of his could, as Augustine declares in Homily 47; therefore the [conclusion]{g:conclusio}.[^3] For if Peter had the power of taking up again his soul once laid down, Peter would have to be a third nature, apart from body and soul, in which the power of resuming the soul so laid down would inhere, which can belong to God alone; for a subject must be given to which it would belong so to take [it] up, and the lifeless body has no such power, nor can the soul take itself up.[^4] Again, Christ has of himself the power to redeem the human race and to originate grace for any other of his kind; now no vicar of his has [it] so; therefore the conclusion.[^5] The [antecedent]{g:antecedens} is clear from John 1:14: "We saw his glory as of the only begotten of the Father, full of grace and truth; of whose fulness we all receive, grace for grace."[^6] So therefore no other can be changed into God and man, so as to be of himself the fount of grace, from which all grace given to the [church]{g:ecclesia} must be derived; for so neither can another man or angel be made equal to our Jesus in [power]{g:potencia}, in plenitude of grace or in preeminence.[^7] [104] Nor ought this to be brought into doubt by the faithful man who believes that no other can be made identical in person with our Jesus, and that the divine and human nature are made identical in the person of the Son, although those natures are infinitely distinguished; in a certain way it is necessary that the divinity of Christ [flow]{g:influere} into his humanity, by force of the union, supereminently, incommunicably to other persons.[^8]

## ¶3 · 104.9–106.10

> [p. 104] Nec sunt obiectus, quos ignari obiciunt digni memoria. Arguunt enim quidam: cum gracia et potestas date Christo humanitus sint tantum finite, derogaretur infinite divine potencie nisi posset eandem vel tantam ponere in quocumque. Et confirmacio est quod Christus sicut est eiusdem speciei nobiscum sic et quelibet gracia vel potestas quam habet humanitus. Stat igitur Deum ex omnipotencia sua augere potestatem vel graciam in Petro, quousque parificata fuerit qualitati in Christo. Tercio confirmatur per hoc quod omnem qualitatem, potest per se ponere et per consequens in quocumque subiecto voluerit et cum anima Petri sit tante capacitatis, ut anima Christi, Deus potest facere qualitates tales positas in aliis spiritibus informare. Sed et istud arguunt ex hoc quod verbum Dei potest quamcumque naturam humanam seorsum vel simul assumere; quo facto inessent sibi tanta gracia et potestas sicut Christus habet humanitus, igitur non posita illa unione Deus potest faciliter facere qualitates huiusmodi informare hominem non assumptum. Sed benedictus dominus qui nos liberavit de istis arguciis. Dictum est autem in materia de [p. 105] incarnacione, quod Christus non potuit aliam humanitatem assumere, verumtamen potuit facillime si voluisset, dictum est eciam quod ipse tam secundum corpus quam animam est eiusdem speciei cum quolibet alio fratre suo. Et dictum est tercio quod ex vi unionis ipostatice gracia, potestas et pulchritudo vel speciositas in Christo est alterius speciei quam aliqua qualitas consimilis nominis in alio homine potest esse. Et ideo incarnacio Christi perfecit totam universitatem creatam tam secundum individua quam universalia, cum multa talia aliter non fuissent. Sicut igitur individuum unius speciei licet infinitum creverit, non potest in toto parificari individuo alterius speciei, sic est de gracia Christi, de potestate, speciositate sue humanitatis. Qualitates autem multe, sicut patet de corporali pulchritudine, principiantur a relacione, ideo idem esset querere istas qualitates in alio homine et querere alium hominem qui simul sit Deus et homo. Ideo errant, qui nude arguunt idemptitatem specificam qualitatum ex idemptitate specifica subiectorum. Oportet enim principia rerum tendere ad maiorem unitatem quam sit in suis effectibus, cum summa unitas sit in primo principio et summa diversitas in sua universitate creata. Nec est michi sermo de qualitate que potest per se esse, quia certum est quod est dare unam naturam qualitatis, que non potest esse nisi informet substanciam, cum non sit nisi substanciam accidentaliter esse qualem. Et qui sompniant aliam ex vanitate subtilitatis circa sacramentum eukaristie, ultra hoc quod sciunt fundare in scriptura sacra, in antiquis sanctis doctoribus vel racionis efficacia, debilitant scolam Christi. Paratus sum tamen eis annuere si illud probaverint et non ante. Et credo quod scola possit aliunde occupari [p. 106] circa fidem scripture ut olim usque ad tubam novissimam, antequam rima ad fundandum illud in scriptura sacra in qua est omnis veritas sit reperta; et tamen, si foret utile, autor scripture non omississet illud inserere nec quatuor sancti doctores illud detegere neglexissent; unde oportet ecclesiam cavere ab huiusmodi novitate. Et colligi potest solucio ad tres predictas argucias; quomodo vel peccant in materia vel in forma. Et patet prima pars predicte sentencie.

Nor are the [objections]{g:objectum} which the ignorant raise worthy of memory. For some argue: since the grace and power given to Christ in his humanity are only finite, it would derogate from the infinite divine power unless it could place the same, or as great, in anyone whatever.[^9] And the confirmation is that, just as Christ is of the same species with us, so too is any grace or power that he has in his humanity. It stands, therefore, that God by his omnipotence [can] increase power or grace in Peter until it has been made equal to the quality in Christ. Thirdly, it is confirmed by the fact that he can place any quality by itself, and in consequence in whatever subject he will; and since the soul of Peter is of as great capacity as the soul of Christ, God can make such qualities, placed in other spirits, inform [them].[^10] And they also argue this from the fact that the Word of God can assume any human nature whatever, separately or together; and when that is done there would be in it as much grace and power as Christ has in his humanity; therefore, without that union being posited, God can easily make qualities of this kind inform a man not assumed. But blessed be the Lord, who has freed us from these [quibbles]{g:argucia}. Now it has been said in the matter of the [105] incarnation that Christ could not assume another humanity, though he could most easily have done so had he willed; it has also been said that he, as much according to body as to soul, is of the same species with any other brother of his.[^11] And it has been said, thirdly, that by force of the hypostatic union the grace, power, and beauty or comeliness in Christ is of another species than any quality of like name can be in another man. And therefore the incarnation of Christ perfected the whole created universe, as much in individuals as in [universals]{g:universale}, since many such would not otherwise have been.[^12] As, therefore, an individual of one species, although it should grow infinitely, cannot be made wholly equal to an individual of another species, so it is with the grace of Christ, with the power [and] comeliness of his humanity. Now many qualities, as is clear of bodily beauty, originate from relation; therefore it would be the same to seek these qualities in another man and to seek another man who is at once God and man. Therefore they err who barely argue the specific identity of qualities from the specific identity of their subjects. For the principles of things must tend to greater unity than there is in their effects, since the highest unity is in the first principle and the highest diversity in its created universe. Nor am I speaking of a quality that can exist by itself; for it is certain that there is a nature of quality that cannot be unless it informs a substance, since it is nothing but a substance's being accidentally of such a kind.[^13] And those who dream of another [kind], out of the vanity of subtlety concerning the sacrament of the eucharist, beyond what they know how to ground in holy scripture, in the ancient holy doctors or in the force of reason, weaken the school of Christ. Yet I am ready to assent to them if they prove it, and not before. And I believe that the school could be otherwise occupied [106] about the faith of scripture, as of old, until the last trumpet, before a chink for grounding that in holy scripture, in which is all truth, would be found; and yet, if it were useful, the author of scripture would not have omitted to insert it, nor would the four holy doctors have neglected to [expose]{g:detegere} it; hence the church must beware of novelty of this kind.[^14] And the solution to the three aforesaid quibbles can be gathered: how they sin either in matter or in form. And the first part of the aforesaid teaching is clear.[^15]

## ¶4 · 106.11–106.13

> [p. 106] Et secunda pars patet ex potestate faciendi miracula et ex potestate autorizandi scripturam sacram et ex potestate edificandi ecclesiam.

And the second part is clear from the power of working miracles, from the power of [authorizing]{g:autorizare} holy scripture, and from the power of building up the church.[^16]

## ¶5 · 106.14–106.28

> [p. 106] Quantum ad miracula, patet quod cessant hodie in nostris episcopis, cum satis est post fidem in evangelio divulgatam ipsam impressam sanctis exhortacionibus confirmare. Cum igitur Deus non dat quicquam superflue, sequitur quod Deus non dat nostris prepositis, ut olim dedit discipulis, potestatem faciendi miracula; et per consequens nunc non habent; hec autem fuit racio; quare Apostolus non miraculose sed per viam naturalis medicine sanavit Thimoteum, bonum regimen iniungendo. Utere, inquit; modico vino propter stomachum tuum et frequentes tuas infirmitates prima Thim. V. Sed communicando cum infidelibus convertendis per miracula exercuit illam potestatem; ut patet Actuum ultimo copiose.

As to miracles, it is clear that they cease today in our [bishops]{g:episcopus}, since it is enough, after the faith has been spread abroad in the gospel, to confirm it, once impressed, with holy exhortations. Since therefore God gives nothing superfluously, it follows that God does not give to our [superiors]{g:prepositi}, as he once gave to the disciples, the power of working miracles; and in consequence they do not now have [it]; and this was the reason why the Apostle healed Timothy not miraculously but by way of natural medicine, enjoining a good regimen: "Use," he says, "a little wine for thy stomach's sake and thy frequent infirmities", 1 Timothy 5.[^17] But in communicating with [unbelievers]{g:infidelis} who were to be converted he exercised that power through miracles, as is clear abundantly in the last [chapter] of Acts.[^18]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 102.26: No apostle had all Christ's human powers, nor has every pope all the
- 103.1: powers of an apostle.
- 103.8: Christ could lay down his life and take it again, which only God can do,
- 103.20: Christ had power to redeem the human race.
- 104.1: This power was due to the two natures in Christ.
- 104.9: Some argue that God can give toa man the grace and power given to Christ's humanity.
- 105.1: In treating of the Incarnation we have said that Christ was of the same species with his brothers but by the hypostatic union his power and beauty were other than man's,
- 105.27: A quality cannot exist without a subject and those who fancy it may do so in the Host injure the Church.
- 106.11: The popes have not all the powers of the apostles.
- 106.14: Our prelates cannot work miracles, which are not needed now.

## Notes

[^1]: **C/R.** *Septima facta sentencia premittenda*: *facta* does not construe easily ("the seventh teaching, [now] made, to be set out first"); translated without it. *nec omnes pape*: "neither do all popes", kept as Wyclif orders it; Loserth's side-note reads it "nor has every pope". B in the margin (103.1): *Potestatem Christus habuit humanitus quantam nullus habuit apostolus*, "Christ had power in his humanity such as no apostle had". *humanitus*: as man, in his human nature.

[^2]: **C.** *primogenitura*: Christ as firstborn (Colossians 1:15, 18); the power of the firstborn belongs to him as man but cannot pass to a vicar without making the vicar God and man.

[^3]: **S.** John 10:18, DR "I have power to lay it down: and I have power to take it up again"; DR's "it" is the life (*anima*); "soul" is kept because the next sentence argues from body and soul. Augustine, *In Iohannis evangelium tractatus* 47; no reference in Loserth (LEDGER P06a-1, to be checked).

[^4]: **T.** *naturam terciam* (103.14): Loserth notes "sic in codd." and suggests *separatam*, "separate"; translated as printed. *corpus vero*: continuative, "and".

[^5]: **C.** *principiare graciam*: to be the principle (source) of grace for another. *sed*: the minor premiss, closed by *ergo*.

[^6]: **S/T.** John 1:14 and 1:16, joined. Wyclif departs from the Vulgate (*vidimus gloriam eius gloriam quasi unigeniti … et de plenitudine eius nos omnes accepimus et gratiam pro gratia*; DR "and we saw his glory, the glory as it were of the only begotten of the Father … And of his fulness we all have received: and grace for grace"); his Latin is translated. Loserth: *accipimus* is the manuscripts' reading (103.27), and the print has it too (plate).

[^7]: **C.** *potencia*: "power" (GLOSSARY *potencia*, CONTEXTUAL), the same as *potestas* in this argument, not Aristotle's potency.

[^8]: **C/T.** *idemptificari personaliter*: "made identical in person". The period lacks a link between *licet … distinguantur* and *quodammodo necesse est*; translated as it stands, with a semicolon. B reads *et personaliter* (104.2). *influere*: "flow in" (GLOSSARY *influxus / influere*).

[^9]: **T/R.** *obiectus*: the objections (GLOSSARY *objectum*). *infinite divine potencie* (104.12): the manuscripts read *infiniti*; translated as printed. A in the margin numbers the three arguments *1*, *2*, *3* (104.9, 14, 19).

[^10]: **C.** *Stat … augere*: "it stands", it is consistent, that God increase. *per se ponere*: to place a quality by itself, without a subject; the third argument rests on the separable accident, which Wyclif rejects below.

[^11]: **S/C.** Loserth: cf. *De benedicta incarnacione* cap. XI, p. 189 (his query) (LEDGER P06a-2, editor). *non potuit … verumtamen potuit facillime si voluisset*: Christ could not as things stand (he did not will it), yet could have done so with ease had he willed; the conditional, not the absolute, impossibility.

[^12]: **C.** *universitatem creatam*: the created universe; *individua … universalia*: the incarnation adds new individuals and new species (Christ's grace and power are of another species).

[^13]: **C/R.** *substanciam accidentaliter esse qualem*: a quality is nothing but the substance's being qualified. The quality "that can exist by itself" is the accident without subject of the eucharistic doctrine Wyclif rejects (the next sentence; Loserth's side-note at 105.27).

[^14]: **S/C.** *tubam novissimam*: 1 Corinthians 15:52, DR "at the last trumpet". *rima*: "a chink", a crack through which to ground it. *quatuor sancti doctores*: the four doctors of the Latin church (Ambrose, Augustine, Jerome, Gregory).

[^15]: **C.** *tres predictas argucias*: the three confirmations; the first argument and the fourth (*Sed et istud arguunt*) are counted with them.

[^16]: **T/R.** *auctonzandi*: the print's *autorizandi*, misread; GLOSSARY *autorizare*. AB in the margin (106.11): *Secunda pars*.

[^17]: **S/T/G.** 1 Timothy 5:23, DR "use a little wine for thy stomach's sake and thy frequent infirmities". A reads *utere inquam*; B in the margin *inquit* (106.24). *prepositis*: "superiors" (GLOSSARY *prepositi*), here the bishops.

[^18]: **S.** Acts 28:8–9: Paul heals the father of Publius and the other sick of Malta.
