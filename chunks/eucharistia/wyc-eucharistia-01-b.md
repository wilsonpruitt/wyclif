# *De eucharistia*, chapter 1, part 2: the priest does not make the body of Christ; spiritual eating

## ¶1 · 15.27–16.29

> [p. 15] Hic dicitur quod primum non sequitur, sed destructo errore blasphemie servaretur et comprehenderetur sacerdotalis potestas in suis limitibus. Nichil enim horribilius quam quod quilibet sacerdos celebrans [p. 16] facit vel consecrat cotidie corpus Christi; nam Deus noster non est Deus recens nec corpus suum cum sit summe sacrum atque perpetuum est sic sacramentale vel noviter faciendum, sed nos sacerdotes facimus et benedicimus hostiam consecratam que non est corpus dominicum sed efficax eius signum. Ista autem benediccio est maior quam benediccio calicis vel alterius ornamenti strictissime episcopo reservata, cuius causam Deus novit. Satis ergo est ad honorem et potenciam sacerdotis quod ipse benedicit, consecrat et conficit sacramentum; quod est hostia consecrata et non corpus Christi sed eius signum vel tegumentum; unde quia sacerdos non habet potestatem faciendi hoc sacramentum nisi ministeriale Deo principaliter faciente, ideo dicitur conficere non corpus Christi sed nudum sacramentum, et sic propter sanctitatem vite et non propter illud adeo est laudandus. Et sic missa sacerdotis sancti est devocius audienda et missa peccatoris notorii est fugienda. Nec emimus suffragia sacerdotum pecunia sed sine convencione civili retribuimus eis vite necessaria, sicut Apostolus docet esse faciendum cum predicatoribus I Cor. IX. Nota tamen quod sicut laudative non effective benedicimus tam Deo quam domino, sic et benedicimus corpori Christi et sanguini, non faciendo ipsum esse beatum vel sanctum, sed laudando et promulgando sanctitatem et beatitudinem quam Deus in corpore suo instituit, et sic ymmolamus Christum et ipsum offerimus Deo patri.

To this [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that the first does not [follow]{g:sequitur};[^1] but, once the error of blasphemy were destroyed, the priestly power would be kept and contained within its [limits]{g:limites}. For nothing is more horrible than that any [priest]{g:sacerdos} who celebrates [16] makes or consecrates [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} daily; for our God is not a new God,[^2] nor is his body, since it is supremely holy and everlasting, thus sacramental or to be made anew; but we priests make and bless the consecrated [host]{g:hostia}, which is not [the Lord's body]{g:corpus-dominicum} but its effective [sign]{g:signum}. And this blessing is greater than the blessing of a chalice or of another ornament, which is most strictly reserved to the [bishop]{g:episcopus}, the cause of which God knows. It is enough, therefore, for the honour and [power]{g:potencia} of the priest that he blesses, consecrates and [confects]{g:conficere} the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum};[^3] which is the consecrated host and not the body of Christ but its sign or [covering]{g:tegumentum}; hence, because the priest does not have power of making this sacrament except [ministerially]{g:ministerialiter}, God making it principally,[^4] therefore he is said to consecrate not the body of Christ but the bare sacrament; and so it is for the holiness of his life, and not for that, that he is so greatly to be praised. And so the mass of a holy priest is to be heard more devoutly, and the mass of a notorious sinner is to be shunned. Nor do we buy the [suffrages]{g:suffragia} of priests with money, but without a civil contract we pay back to them the necessaries of life, as the Apostle teaches is to be done with preachers, 1 Cor. 9.[^5] Note, however, that just as we bless God as well as a lord [by way of praise]{g:laudative}, not [by way of effect]{g:effective},[^6] so too we bless the body of Christ and the blood, not by making it to be blessed or holy, but by praising and proclaiming the holiness and blessedness which God instituted in his body; and so we immolate Christ and offer him to God the Father.

## ¶2 · 16.30–17.22

> [p. 16] Nota eciam ulterius ad accepcionem spiritualem corporis Christi quod non consistit in corporali accepcione, [p. 17] masticacione vel taccione hostie consecrate, sed in pastione anime ex fructuosa fide secundum quam nutritur spiritus noster in Domino. Et propter ignoranciam istius manducacionis corporis Christi et pocionis sui sanguinis multi discipuli abierunt retrorsum, ut patet Joh. VI°, 61: Durus, inquiunt, est iste sermo et quis potest eum audire? Nichil enim horribilius quam necessario manducare carnaliter carnem et bibere carnaliter sanguinem hominis tam tenere predilecti; ideo dicit Christus apostolis qui ultra indignos discipulos qui retrorsum abierant digni erant informari in sensu vivifico, quod sensus carnalis istius non proderit, sed spiritus est, hoc est spiritualis sensus, qui vivificat, ubi occasione male accepta sensus alius scandalizat. Oportet ergo fidelem credere quod illud quo anima pascitur obiective est spiritualis cibus anime; et sic oportet carnem Christi et sanguinem tam varie pro peccatoribus tribulatis accendere in nostris spiritibus amorem qui est cibus anime ut grate faciamus quodammodo recompensam. Et sic intelligit Veritas de manducacione et potacione spiritualibus quas oportet esse in quolibet salvandorum.

Note also, further, as to the spiritual reception of the body of Christ, that it does not consist in bodily reception, [17] chewing or touching of the consecrated host, but in the feeding of the soul out of fruitful faith, according to which our spirit is nourished in the Lord. And through ignorance of this [eating]{g:manducacio} of the body of Christ and drinking of his blood many disciples went back, as is clear in John 6:61: "This saying," they say, "is hard, and who can hear it?"[^7] For nothing is more horrible than of necessity to eat carnally the flesh and to drink carnally the blood of a man so tenderly loved; therefore Christ says to the apostles, who, beyond the unworthy disciples who had gone back, were worthy to be instructed in the life-giving sense, that the carnal sense of this will not profit, but "it is the spirit", that is, the spiritual sense, "that quickeneth", where, the occasion being ill taken, the other sense gives scandal. Therefore the faithful man must believe that that by which the soul is fed, as its object, is the spiritual food of the soul; and so the flesh of Christ and the blood must, for sinners so variously in tribulation,[^8] kindle in our spirits love, which is the food of the soul, so that we may gratefully make some sort of recompense. And so the Truth understands [this] of the spiritual eating and drinking which must be in every one of those who are to be saved.

## ¶3 · 17.23–17.30

> [p. 17] Unde Augustinus in Omelia: Hoc, inquit, est manducare illam escam et illum bibere potum, in Christo manere et illum manentem in se habere. Ac per hoc qui non manet in Christo et in quo non manet Christus, procul dubio non manducat spiritualiter eius carnem, licet carnaliter et visibiliter premat sacramenta corporis et sanguinis Christi, sed magis tante rei sacramentum ad iudicium sibi manducat et bibit.

Hence Augustine in a homily: "This," he says, "is to eat that meat and to drink that drink: to abide in Christ and to have him abiding in oneself. And by this, he who does not abide in Christ, and in whom Christ does not abide, without doubt does not eat his flesh [spiritually]{g:spiritualiter}, although he carnally and visibly presses the sacraments of the body and blood of Christ; but rather he eats and drinks the sacrament of so great a thing to judgment for himself."[^9]

## ¶4 · 18.1–18.13

> [p. 18] Et sic ex verbis Christi et sui discipuli Augustini tria notabilia sunt notanda, primo quod caro Christi et sanguis solum spiritualiter solent comedi; nam Deus ordinavit eternaliter quod sanctus suus non videret corrupcionem in aliquo membro suo. Patet secundo quod nec bestia nec prescitus manducat sic corpus Christi, licet manducet corporaliter sacramentum. Et patet tercio mira subtilitas verborum doctoris qua dicit non quod indignus visibiliter premit dentibus corpus Christi sed quod visibiliter premit dentibus sacramentum corporis Christi et sanguinis. Illud enim sacramentum valde distinguitur a corpore Christi quod est res huius sacramenti.

And so from the words of Christ and of his disciple Augustine three notable points are to be noted: first, that the flesh and blood of Christ are wont to be eaten only spiritually; for God ordained eternally that "his holy one should not see corruption" in any [member]{g:membrum} of his.[^10] It is clear secondly that neither a beast nor one of [the foreknown]{g:prescitus}[^11] eats the body of Christ in this way, although he eats the sacrament [bodily]{g:corporaliter}. And there is clear thirdly the wonderful subtlety of the doctor's words, by which he says not that the unworthy man visibly presses the body of Christ with his teeth, but that he visibly presses with his teeth the sacrament of the body of Christ and blood.[^12] For that sacrament is greatly distinguished from the body of Christ, which is the [reality of this sacrament]{g:res-sacramenti}.

## ¶5 · 18.14–18.20

> [p. 18] Unde notanda est differencia quam ponit Augustinus inter esum carnalem et spiritualem. Nam in esu carnali comestum cedit in nutrimentum edentis, cum inbibitur eius membris. In comestione vero spirituali est econtra, cum comedens corpus Christi spiritualiter exhinc incorporatur membris ecclesie et sic Christo; comestus autem spiritualiter superat comedentem.

Hence the [difference]{g:differencia} is to be noted which Augustine sets between carnal and spiritual [eating]{g:esus}. For in carnal eating what is eaten passes into the nourishment of the eater, since it is imbibed by his members. But in spiritual eating it is the reverse, since he who eats the body of Christ spiritually is thereby incorporated into the members of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, and so into Christ; and the one eaten spiritually overcomes the one who eats.[^13]

## ¶6 · 18.21–18.29

> [p. 18] Nec valent argucie et ficticie sompniate contra istam sentenciam. Arguunt enim quidam quod, si homo sentit hominem, dum sentit eius indumenta ex motu vite latentis intrinsecus, quod per idem sentit corpus Christi, dum sentit sacramentum ex eius mocione. Sed ista argucia procedens per locum a simili argueret quod homo videt Deum et animam et sic de qualibet quinque sensacionum, quando sentit subiectum ex eorum mocione vitali intrinseca.

Nor do the [quibbles]{g:argucie} and dreamed-up [fictions]{g:ficticia} against this [teaching]{g:sentencia}[^14] avail. For some argue that, if a man [perceives]{g:sentire} a man while he perceives his clothes through the motion of the life hidden within, then by the same token he perceives the body of Christ while he perceives the sacrament through its motion. But this quibble, proceeding by the topic [from likeness]{g:a-simili}, would argue that a man sees God and the soul, and so of each of the five [sensations]{g:sensacio}, when he perceives a [subject]{g:subiectum} through their vital inner motion.

## ¶7 · 18.30–19.8

> [p. 18] Conceditur ergo quod contingit sentire subiectum per accidens modo quo dicitur, sed tunc oportet sensum communem vel aliquem sensum intrinsecum [p. 19] comprehendere subiectum ex mocione per se sensibilis adiacentis. Sed modo nullus sensus hominis sed pure intellectus per fidem percipit corpus Christi; quod patet ex hoc quod commixta hostia consecrata cum aliis homo non plus discernit eam ab aliis subducta noticia fidei quam facit bestia. Sicut ergo bestia non sufficit apprehendere hanc rem sacramenti sub tali habitudine, ita nec sensus hominis.

It is granted, therefore, that a subject can be perceived [by accident]{g:per-accidens} in the way described; but then [the common sense]{g:sensus-communis} or some inner sense must [19] apprehend the subject from the motion of the adjacent thing that is [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} [of itself]{g:per-se}.[^15] Now, as it is, no sense of man, but the understanding purely, through faith, perceives the body of Christ; which is clear from this, that when a consecrated host is mixed with others, a man, with the knowledge of faith withdrawn, no more distinguishes it from the others than a beast does. Just as, therefore, a beast does not suffice to apprehend this reality of the sacrament under such a [respect]{g:habitudo}, so neither does the sense of man.

## ¶8 · 19.9–19.18

> [p. 19] Secundo sompniatur quod multi erant sancti qui tenuerunt quod sencierunt visu, auditu, olfactu, gustu et tactu corpus Christi in hostia consecrata. Illis ergo est credendum. Sicut, inquiunt, videmus in sacramento Eukaristie regem regum, sic audimus eum in fractura hostie, olfacimus eum in redolencia forme panis vel vini, gustamus eum quando inglutimus venerabile sacramentum et tangimus eum quando palpando hostiam trahimus vel tractamus digitis familiariter sacramentum.

Secondly, it is dreamed that there were many [saints]{g:sancti} who held that they perceived the body of Christ in the consecrated host by sight, hearing, smell, taste and touch. Therefore, [they argue,] these are to be believed. "Just as," they say, "we see the King of kings in the sacrament of the Eucharist, so we hear him in the [breaking]{g:fractio} of the host, we smell him in the fragrance of the form of [bread]{g:panis} or [wine]{g:vinum}, we taste him when we swallow the venerable sacrament, and we touch him when, handling the host, we draw or handle the sacrament familiarly with our fingers."[^16]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 16.1: The priest does not make the body of Christ.
- 16.10: He consecrates the Sacrament which is the sign or garment of Christ's body.
- 17.1: The true reception of Christ's body is spiritual through faith.
- 17.23: Augustine's distinction between the body and the Sacrament of the body.
- 18.21: The body of Christ not sensibly present.
- 19.9: Authority of Saints.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Hic dicitur* opens the answer to the objection that closes the preceding part (01-a); *primum*, "the first", is the first of that objection's points. The neuter does not say whether an inference or a consequent is meant.

[^2]: **S.** Ps. 80:10, "there shall be no new god in thee" (Vulgate *non erit in te deus recens*); an allusion, not a quotation.

[^3]: **G/T.** The Latin has two verbs, *consecrat et conficit*; where *conficere* stands beside *consecrare* it is "confect", the old technical English for making the sacrament, so that the pair stays two (GLOSSARY *conficere*); elsewhere it is "consecrate". *Satis ergo* is Loserth's correction (16.9: AB *datum ergo*, C *data*; *correxi*). *Potenciam*: "power", the CONTEXTUAL row's sense outside Aristotle's analysis of potency; *potestas* in the same sentence group is also "power".

[^4]: **C.** *Nisi ministeriale*: a neuter adjective (B *misteriale*) where an adverb is wanted; read as "ministerially" (as if *ministerialiter*), agreeing in sense with *sacramentum*, "[the sacrament as something] ministerial". *Deo principaliter faciente*: God as the principal agent, the priest as minister.

[^5]: **S.** Wyclif gives the chapter only; the place is 1 Cor. 9:13–14, "So also the Lord ordained that they who preach the gospel should live by the gospel" (9:14).

[^6]: **T/C.** *Deo* is Loserth's correction; all the manuscripts read *Deum* (16.24). *Domino* is printed lower-case: "a lord", i.e. we bless God as we bless an earthly lord, by praising, not by conferring blessing; "the Lord" (Christ) is the alternative.

[^7]: **S.** John 6:61, with *iste* for the Vulgate's *hic*; *inquiunt* is Wyclif's. The next sentence works in John 6:64, "It is the spirit that quickeneth: the flesh profiteth nothing", paraphrasing its second clause (Loserth: John 6:64).

[^8]: **T.** Printed *tribulatis*, agreeing with *peccatoribus*. BDE read *tribulatos* (and A before correction), agreeing with *carnem … et sanguinem*: "the flesh and blood of Christ, so variously afflicted for sinners". The print construes and is translated.

[^9]: **S.** Augustine, *In Ioh. tract.* 26 (Loserth: *In Joh. Ev. Tract.* XXVI, *Opp.* III, 2, 501); *Tract.* 26.18 (PL 35, 1614), in the expanded medieval form (*spiritualiter … licet carnaliter et visibiliter premat …*), which the Maurists bracket as additions (LEDGER K01b-1). The quotation has no *dentibus*; Wyclif's comment at 18.8–11 quotes the doctor as saying *premit dentibus*.

[^10]: **S.** Ps. 15:10 (Loserth), Vulgate *nec dabis sanctum tuum videre corruptionem*, "nor wilt thou give thy holy one to see corruption"; Wyclif recasts it as a clause of God's ordinance.

[^11]: **G.** *Prescitus*: those God foreknows will be damned, Wyclif's technical term; not "the reprobate".

[^12]: **S/T.** Wyclif's "doctor" says *premit dentibus*, which the quotation at 17.27 lacks; the fuller form, with *dentibus*, is the medieval text of *Tract.* 26.18 as in ps.-Bede and Alcuin on John (PL 92, 719A; PL 100, 836A, *dentibus* bracketed), so the omission at 17.27 is in Wyclif's copy or the print (LEDGER K01b-2). ABC omit *corpus Christi … dentibus* (18.10–11), an eye-skip that loses the contrast.

[^13]: **S.** Wyclif names Augustine without a work. The thought that the one eaten changes the eater into himself is in Augustine, *Conf.* VII.10.16 (*nec tu me in te mutabis … sed tu mutaberis in me*; PL 32, 742), the likeliest source (LEDGER K01b-3).

[^14]: **C.** *Sentencia*: CONTEXTUAL; here "teaching", the position just set out.

[^15]: **C.** *Sensum communem*: the common sense, the inner faculty that unites the five senses; not the *sensibile commune* (the common sensible). *Per se sensibilis*: the object perceptible of itself, through which a subject is perceived by accident. At 19.3 *pure intellectus* is C's corrected reading (ABC *principalis*, D *purus*). *Habitudine* (19.8): the "respect" sense of the CONTEXTUAL row.

[^16]: **C.** *Illis ergo est credendum* is the dreamers' inference from their premiss, which 19.19 calls the *assumptum*; *inquiunt* marks what follows as their words.
