# *De ecclesia*, chapter 23, part 2: the treasury of merits: the common opinion, its foundation, and Thomas and Bonaventure

## ¶1 · 550.24–554.4

> [p. 550] Istis premissis restat videre expressius quid sonat verbum prelati: Remitto tibi tantum tempus indulgencie pro peccatis, et dicitur communiter quod virtute talis indulgencie per tantum tempus minus pacieris in purgatorio, et hoc intelligi potest dupliciter, vel quod indulgencia habeat efficaciam tantam, quantam tantum temporis pro penitencia huius vie quoad statum purgatorii, vel secundo quod de tanto [p. 551] brevius pacieris secundum penam purgatorii, et ista glossa est sentencie decretistarum pertinencior et dictis eorum conformior (ut patet in De Penitenciis et Remissionibus; cap. Quod autem) in verbo, sed queres quid sonat? Sumitur autem fundamentum huius sentencie ex ista ymaginacione fantastica: Ponitur enim primo quod sunt supererogata merita sanctorum et specialiter Christi in celis infinita numero et valore, ultra quam requiritur ad eorum beatitudinem, et omnia ista vocantur spiritualis thesaurus ecclesie in capite eius reconditus. Ponitur secundo quod dominus papa, cum sit vicarius Christi in terris, habet plenam potestatem distribuendi thesaurum illum fidelibus, sed quod libuerit plus vel minus, nec licet diffidere quin potestas imperciendi ista spiritualia suffragia remaneat continue inexhausta, quia aliter deficeret Christus dando potestatem clavium suo vicario hic in terris. Tercio dicitur quod quando papa vel suus episcopus dispensat subditis de isto thesauro, commutando meritum beati loco meriti quo peccator mereretur remissionem peccati, tunc concedit indulgenciam plus vel minus. Et ex isto fundamento dicuntur hii vendere peccata sua et isti emere eciam pro pecunia et seductus est populus de cambio impossibili et infundabili propter fastum et questum adquisibilem sacerdoti. Et isti sentencie videtur sanctum Thomam annuere, super Quarto Sentenciarum, distinccione vicesima, questione [p. 552] tercia: Ab omnibus, inquit, conceditur indulgencias aliquid valere, quia impium esset dicere quod ecclesia aliquid vane faceret. Sed quidam dicunt quod non valent ad absolvendum a reatu pene quam quis in purgatorio secundum iudicium Dei meretur, sed valent ad solvendum ab obligacione qua sacerdos obligavit penitentem ad penam aliquam vel ad quam eciam ordinatur ex canonum statutis. Sed hec opinio non videtur vera; primo quia est expresse contra privilegium Petro datum, ut quod in terra remitteret et in celo remitteretur, unde remissio que fit quoad forum ecclesie, valet eciam quantum ad forum Dei. Item, ecclesia huiusmodi indulgenciam faciens magis dampnificaret quam adiuvaret, quia remitteret ad graviores penas, scilicet purgatorii, absolvendo a penitenciis iniunctis. Et manifeste patet deduccio, eo quod iuxta opinionem indulgencia non facit ad remissionem in purgatorio, sed subducta indulgencia in pleno penitencie faceret ad remissionem in purgatorio, quod foret infinitum prestancius quam aliqua penitencie vie remissio. Ideo iuxta hanc viam omnis indulgencia foret impeditiva fructus penitencie et nociva, ideo prudenter dicunt canoniste quod propter indulgencias non est dimittenda iniuncta penitencia [p. 553] pro peccato, nec haberent colorem indulgencie de multis millibus annorum quoad nudam mitigacionem penitencie hic pro via, cum certi sumus quod non subibitur eciam a Matussale tanta penitencia hic in via. Ideo, inquit doctor, aliter est dicendum quod valent indulgencie et quantum ad forum ecclesie et quantum ad iudicium Dei et quantum ad remissionem pene residue post contricionem, confessionem, absolucionem, sive sit iniuncta sive non. Racio autem quare valere possunt est unitas corporis mistici in qua multi in operibus penitencie supererogaverunt ad mensuram debitorum suorum et eciam multas tribulaciones iniuste sustinuerunt pacienter, per quas multitudo penarum poterat expiari, si eis deberetur. Quorum meritorum tanta est copia quod omnem penam debitam nunc viventibus excedunt et precipue propter meritum Christi; quod et si in sacramentis operatur, non tamen efficacia eius in sacramentis includitur, sed sua infinitate sacramentorum efficaciam excedit. Ideo cum unus pro alio satisfacere potest, sancti autem in quibus superhabundancia operum satisfaccionis invenitur, non determinate pro isto qui non remissionem indiget, huiusmodi opera fecerunt (quia alias absque omni indulgencia remissionem consequeretur) vel communiter fierent pro tota ecclesia, sicut apostolus dicit se implere ea que desunt passioni Christi in corpore suo pro ecclesia, ad quam scribit Corinth. I°. Et sic predicta merita quamvis sint communia tocius ecclesie, tamen papa distribuit ea dispariter, sicut ea que sunt communia alicuius multitudinis distribuuntur singulis de multitudine secundum arbitrium [p. 554] eius qui multitudini preest. Unde si quis consequeretur remissionem pene ac si alius pro eo satisfecisset ita quod satisfaccio alterius sibi per eum qui potest distribuatur; sic de indulgenciis est censendum.

These things premised, it remains to see more expressly what the prelate's formula means: "I remit to you so much time of indulgence for sins"; and it is commonly said that by virtue of such an indulgence you will suffer so much time less in purgatory. And this can be understood in two ways: either that the indulgence has as much efficacy as so much time of penance in this [way]{g:via} has as regards the state of purgatory; or secondly that by so much [551] you will suffer more briefly in the punishment of purgatory. And this gloss is more pertinent to the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of the decretists and more conformable to their sayings, (as is clear in *De penitenciis et remissionibus*, chapter *Quod autem*) at the words "But you will ask what it means".[^1] And the foundation of this teaching is taken from this fantastic imagining. For it is supposed, first, that there are supererogated merits of the saints, and especially of Christ, in heaven, infinite in number and value, beyond what is required for their blessedness; and all these are called the spiritual treasury of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, laid up in its [head]{g:caput}.[^2] It is supposed, secondly, that the lord [pope]{g:papa}, since he is [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius Christi} on earth, has full power of distributing that treasury to the faithful, but as much as he pleases, more or less; and it is not lawful to doubt that the power of imparting these spiritual [suffrages]{g:suffragia} remains continually unexhausted, since otherwise Christ would have failed in giving the power of the keys to his vicar here on earth.[^3] Thirdly, it is said that when the pope or his bishop dispenses to subjects from this treasury, [exchanging]{g:commutacio} the merit of a blessed one in place of the merit by which the sinner would merit remission of sin, then he grants indulgence, more or less. And on this foundation these are said to sell their sins and those to buy them, even for money; and the people has been seduced by an impossible and groundless barter, for the sake of the pomp and the gain to be acquired by the priest.[^4] And Saint Thomas seems to assent to this teaching, on the fourth book of the *Sentences*, distinction 20, question [552] 3: "It is granted by all," he says, "that indulgences avail something, since it would be impious to say that the church does anything in vain. But some say that they do not avail to absolve from the liability to the punishment which one merits in purgatory according to the judgment of God, but avail to loose from the obligation by which the priest has bound the penitent to some punishment, or to which he is also appointed by the statutes of the canons. But this [opinion]{g:opinio} does not seem true: first, because it is expressly against the [privilege]{g:privilegium} given to Peter, that what he remitted on earth should be remitted in heaven; hence the remission which is made as to the court of the church avails also as to the court of God. Again, the church making an indulgence of this kind would harm more than help, since it would remit them to heavier punishments, namely those of purgatory, by absolving from enjoined penances."[^5] And the deduction is manifestly clear, since according to the opinion an indulgence does nothing toward remission in purgatory; but with the indulgence withdrawn, one would work in the fullness of penance toward remission in purgatory, which would be infinitely more excellent than any remission of the penance of the way. Therefore on this view every indulgence would hinder the fruit of penance and be harmful; therefore the canonists prudently say that an enjoined penance [553] for sin is not to be dropped on account of indulgences; nor would indulgences of many thousands of years have [colour]{g:color} as to the bare mitigation of penance here on the way, since we are certain that so great a penance would not be undergone even by Methuselah here on the way.[^6] "Therefore," says the doctor, "it must be said otherwise: that indulgences avail both as to the court of the church and as to the judgment of God, and as to the remission of the punishment remaining after contrition, confession and absolution, whether it was enjoined or not. Now the reason why they can avail is the unity of the [mystical body]{g:corpus Christi misticum}, in which many in works of penance have paid out beyond the measure of their debts, and have also patiently borne many tribulations unjustly, by which a multitude of punishments could be expiated, if they were owed to them. Of which merits so great is the plenty that they exceed all punishment owed to those now living, and especially on account of the merit of Christ; which, though it works in the sacraments, yet its efficacy is not shut up in the sacraments, but by its infinity it exceeds the efficacy of the sacraments.[^7] Therefore, since one can make satisfaction for another, and the saints in whom a superabundance of works of satisfaction is found did such works not determinately for this man who needs remission (for otherwise he would obtain remission without any indulgence), or they were done in common for the whole church, as the apostle says that he fills up those things that are wanting to the passion of Christ in his body for the church, to which he writes, Corinthians 1: so the aforesaid merits, though they are common to the whole church," yet the pope distributes them unequally, as those "that are common to some multitude are distributed to the individuals of the multitude according to the judgment [554] of him who presides over the multitude. Hence, if anyone should obtain remission of punishment as though another had made satisfaction for him, so that the satisfaction of another is distributed to him by one who is able"; so it is to be judged of indulgences.[^8]

## ¶2 · 554.6–554.22

> [p. 554] Item, doctor Bonaventura super eadem distinccione, questione secunda, primo reprobat opinionem de indulgenciis dicentem quod tantum valent ad forum ecclesie militantis sic quod non ad forum ecclesie triumphantis. Si, inquit, prelatus relaxat et Deus non relaxat, tunc est pocius decepcio quam relaxacio, pocius crudelitas quam pietas. Et patet quod non foret dispensacio sed rebellio. Ideo ad excludendum ab ecclesia Christi fallaciam dicit quod indulgencia sic potest fieri: Obtinemus, inquit, bonum ex triplici merito, scilicet ex merito proprio, ex merito capitis et ex merito ecclesiastice unitatis. Et correspondenter ad istud triplex meritum bonum obtinendi est triplex meritum a malo liberandi, scilicet meritum proprium, ut puta, meritum contricionis, quo meremur liberari a pena eterna et ascribi ad gaudia sempiterna, sic quod hec duo sunt simul, sicut in angelis gracia et gloria.

Again, the doctor Bonaventure, on the same distinction, question 2, first rejects the opinion on indulgences which says that they avail only for the court of the [church militant]{g:ecclesia militans}, and so not for the court of the [church triumphant]{g:ecclesia triumphans}. "If," he says, "the prelate relaxes and God does not relax, then it is deception rather than relaxation, cruelty rather than [piety]{g:pietas}." And it is clear that it would not be dispensation but rebellion. Therefore, to exclude [fallacy]{g:fallacia} from the church of Christ, he says that an indulgence can be made thus: "We obtain good," he says, "from a threefold merit, namely from our own merit, from the merit of the head, and from the merit of the church's unity. And corresponding to this threefold merit of obtaining good there is a threefold merit of being freed from evil: namely our own merit, for example the merit of contrition, by which we merit to be freed from eternal punishment and to be enrolled for everlasting joys, so that these two are together, as grace and glory in the angels."[^9]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 550.24: So much time of indulgence may mean a) the equivalent of so much temporal penance; b) so much shortening of time in Purgatory.
- 551.1: The latter meaning is favoured by the decretists.
- 551.6: The opinion rests on an invention which supposes a) a store oft supererogatory merits;
- 551.13: b) that the store is inexhaustible and is at the disposal of the Pope;
- 551.20: c) that when the Pope makes over a share in such merit, he gives an indulgence,
- 552.1: St. Thomas says that indulgences are not simply remissions of temporal penance but avail in purgatory.
- 554.6: Bonaventura's opinion.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Quid sonat verbum prelati*: what the prelate's formula means. *Tantum tempus indulgencie*: so much time, as forty days or a year (part 1). The two readings: an indulgence worth so much penance in this life, or so much time off purgatory (side-note 550.24). *Huius vie*: of this way, the present life (row *via*). *Sed queres quid sonat?*: taken as the words of the gloss at which the matter is treated; it may instead be Wyclif's own question, opening the next sentence. **S.** X 5.38.4 *Quod autem* (Loserth, 551.4; the form is ours; LEDGER E23b-1). **T.** *In verbo* is Loserth's expansion, queried, of the codices' *in v.* (551.4). The print has a closing bracket after *Quod autem* with no opening one; it is not carried into the English. B *Brevius paciens* (551.1). **R.** Side-notes 550.24, 551.1. A and B in the margin at 550.24: *Nota quid sonat* (B *hoc*): *Remitto tibi tantum tempus indulgencie pro peccatis*; B *Remitto tibi tempus* (550.25).

[^2]: **C.** *Ymaginacione fantastica*: a fantastic imagining, Wyclif's verdict on the foundation that follows. *Supererogata merita*: merits paid out beyond what was owed. *In capite eius reconditus*: laid up in its head, Christ. **T.** A *numero et valere* (551.9). **R.** Side-note 551.6; A in the margin at 551.6: *Fundamentum indulgenciarum secundum communem opinionem*; B in the margin at 551.11: *Spiritualis thesaurus ecclesie*.

[^3]: **C.** *Sed quod libuerit plus vel minus*: so printed, "but as much as he pleases, more or less"; *sed* may stand for *secundum*, "according as he pleases". *Potestatem clavium*: the power of the keys (Matthew 16:19). **T.** A *in terra* (551.14); A *remaneant* (551.17). **R.** Side-note 551.13.

[^4]: **G.** *Commutando*: "exchanging" (row *commutacio*); *cambio*: "barter", untagged, kept apart from *commutacio*. **C.** *Hii vendere … et isti emere*: the sellers, the clergy, and the buyers, the people; *peccata sua* is ambiguous between the buyers' sins and the sellers'. *Fastum et questum adquisibilem sacerdoti*: the pomp and the gain to be won by the priest. **R.** Side-note 551.20.

[^5]: **S.** Thomas Aquinas, *In IV Sent.* d.20 (Loserth: *Opp.* IX, 374; LEDGER E23b-2); the place is q.1 a.3 qc.1, = *Supplementum* q.25 a.1 (from memory; LEDGER E23b-3). The privilege given to Peter is Matthew 16:19. **T.** *Indulgencias* is Loserth's correction of the codices' *indulgencia* (552.1). B lacks *a reatu … solvendum* (552.4) and *ab* (552.6). **C.** *Reatu pene*: liability to punishment. *Forum ecclesie … forum Dei*: the court of the church and the court of God. The quotation is closed at *iniunctis*; what follows, to *in via*, is taken as Wyclif's comment, until *inquit doctor* resumes the quotation. **R.** Side-note 552.1. A in the margin at 552.4: *Opinio falsa*; at 552.8: *Arguitur contra communem opinionem indulgenciarum*. B in the margin at 552.2 sums up Thomas and Bonaventure and objects: *revera non video quomodo sanctorum communio tantum dependet ex pape voto, cum magnam habuit illa communio efficaciam ante adinvencionem istius distribucionis*.

[^6]: **C.** *Patet deduccio*: Wyclif presses Thomas's second argument: if an indulgence only loosed enjoined penance, the penance it lifted would have done more toward remission in purgatory, infinitely more than any remission of penance in this life. *In pleno penitencie*: in the fullness of penance; the subject of *faceret* is unexpressed. *Iuxta hanc viam*: on this view (not the *via* of the wayfarer). *Colorem*: colour, a plausible ground (row *color*). *De multis millibus annorum*: indulgences of thousands of years, which no penance in this life could match, not even in Methuselah's 969 years (Genesis 5:27). **T.** B *quod deduccio* (552.16).

[^7]: **C.** *Ideo, inquit doctor*: the quotation of Thomas resumes. *Sive sit iniuncta sive non*: whether the punishment was enjoined by the priest or not. *Supererogaverunt ad mensuram debitorum suorum*: paid out beyond the measure of their debts. *Copia*: "plenty", kept apart from *habundancia*. **T.** Loserth notes that Thomas reads *multi operibus*, without *in* (553.10–11).

[^8]: **T.** *Non determinate pro isto qui non remissionem indiget*: the second *non* does not construe ("this man who does not need remission"); it is left untranslated (our emendation), as a doubling of *non determinate*; the print has both, without apparatus. *Huiusmodi*: the codices read *huius* here and often below (Loserth, 553.23). A *nihil communiter* (553.25); A A₁ *singularia* (553.31). **C.** *Vel communiter fierent*: so printed; taken as the alternative to works done for one man, "or rather they were done in common". *Tamen papa distribuit ea dispariter, sicut ea*: roman in the print, Wyclif's words inside Thomas's quotation; the English closes and reopens the quotation there. *Unde si quis consequeretur …*: the comparison is left incomplete as printed: as one obtains remission when another satisfies for him, so when another's satisfaction is distributed to him. The quotation is closed at *distribuatur*. **S.** The apostle's words are Colossians 1:24 (Loserth, 553.26), "fill up those things that are wanting of the sufferings of Christ, in my flesh, for his body, which is the church" (Douay–Rheims), given in indirect speech; the print cites *Corinth. I°*, A₁ *II. Cor. I.* (553.27).

[^9]: **S.** Bonaventure, *In IV Sent.* d.20 p.2 a.1 q.2 (Loserth: *Opp.* IV, 556, Venice 1734; LEDGER E23b-4); the codices lack the number of the distinction (Loserth, 554.6). **C.** *Tantum … sic quod non*: only for the court of the church militant, and not for that of the church triumphant. *Et patet quod non foret dispensacio sed rebellio*: taken as Wyclif's comment, outside the quotation. *Ascribi ad gaudia sempiterna*: to be enrolled for everlasting joys. *Sic quod hec duo sunt simul*: freedom from eternal punishment and enrolment for glory come together, as grace and glory in the angels. The quotation continues in the next part. **R.** Side-note 554.6. B in the margin at 554.6: *Nota quomodo percipias teriam* (sic) *indulgenciarum secundum fautores earum*; A at 554.15: *Bonum obtinemus ex merito proprio, ex merito capitis, ex merito ecclesie unitatis*; A at 554.17: *Bonaventura de indulgenciis*.
