## Doubtful, left as they are

- **08-e, p. 248**: *et tunc est maius nunc **autem** minus*, rendered "and is then greater and now less". This is a correlative pair: *tunc … nunc autem* means "at one time … at another". The second "and" carries the *autem* as continuative. "then greater, but now less" (b) is defensible, since the two times are set against each other. It is a real toss-up, so it is left.
- **08-e, p. 249**: ***Sed** est hoc verbum Christi gravidatum sensu morali multiplici*, rendered "But this word of Christ is pregnant". This turns from the literal sense (¶2 closes "the literal sense … is clear") to the moral sense. No *ergo* closes it, so "but" for the turn stands. "Now" for a new step could be argued. Left.
- **08-g, p. 259**: *distincta in specie, **sed** idem angelus in numero … causaret simul diversa tempora*, rendered "but the same angel in number". This raises the absurdity a step: not only many angels, but even one angel would cause diverse times. "But" is right for *sed*. It is noted only because the sense is climactic rather than strictly adversative. Left.
