# *De ecclesia*, chapter 2, part 5: first objection: subjects must consult scripture; Jerome on the sword that separates heretics

## ¶1 · 38.25–41.6

> [p. 38] Sed contra ista instatur tripliciter: Videtur enim primo quod subditi debent consulere scripturam sacram in quocunque quod papa mandaverit. Quantum ad istud, concedi debet conclusio, cum nemo debet parere sibi plus quam alii, nisi in quantum est superior Christi vicarius, et per consequens, nisi in quantum obeditur principalius Jesu Christo; sed in omni obediencia [p. 39] Jesu Christo debet christianus consulere legem suam; ideo in omni obediencia christiani in quantum huiusmodi debet obedienciarius consulere legem Dei; et ideo ordinarunt iura ecclesie quod lex scripture tam generaliter et tam precipue a sacerdotibus et specialiter maioribus debet sciri, ne turbentur aut musitent quid et qualiter ad mandatum superioris debeant respondere. In ista scola instructa est legio Thebayca et omnes martyres, immo in illa scola instructi sunt omnes qui monitis non sapientibus caritatem Dei et proximi contradicunt iuxta illud Christi Matth. X°, 37: Qui amat patrem vel matrem plus quam me, non est me dignus; et istam leccionem docent apostoli Act.V°, 29, dicentes quod obedire magis oportet Deo quam hominibus. Ideo dicit Christus Matth. X°, 35: Non veni pacem mittere in terram sed gladium. Veni ergo separare hominem adversus patrem suum, et filiam adversus matrem suam, et nurum adversus socrum suam, et inimici hominis domestici eius. Amiciciam enim carnalem propter Deum venit Christus dissolvere, quia ille qui videtur esse familiaris homini quoad mundum est hostis suus ut plurimum quoad Deum (ut dicitur Michee VII°). Beatus Jeronymus super illo capitulo videtur exponere textum evangelii ad hunc sensum quod Christus venit ad separandum hereticos ab ecclesia quam illudunt. [p. 40] Simulant enim ex palliata ypocrisi quod sint filii Dei per graciam adopcionis et filie ecclesie matris sue pro cuius iuribus tantum zelant. Dicamus inquit scriptum de hereticis; non enim nostram salutem sed sua querunt lucra, et deceptum gregem pedibus suis conterunt. Pater heretici est Deus qui creavit eum, et mater ecclesia; socrus ad literam est mater alterius coniugum. Ast uxor nati dicitur esse nurus, cum ergo Christus secundum humanitatem sit sponsus anime, imo tocius ecclesie; et cum hoc conceptus de spiritu sancto tamquam matre sua iuxta illud quod allegat Jeronymus.... Tulit me mater mea in uno capillorum meorum (caro enim est capillus anime), patet quod spiritus sanctus, cum sit mater Christi sponsi ecclesie, est socrus anime que vendicat esse sponsa Christi, quia mater mariti. Et ex eadem consideracione anima pretendens se esse uxorem filii spiritus sancti dicitur nurus eius. Heretici ergo qui pretendunt se esse nuros spiritus sancti quem vendicant socrum suam, separantur ab eo per Christum detecta eorum fallacia, et ita cum universalis ecclesia sit domus domini Jesu Christi, et per consequens, omnes heretici qui sunt facietenus in ecclesia sunt pretense eius domestici, patet quod omnes illi sunt illi homini patrifamilias inimici. Huius inquit Jeronymus inimici sunt qui putantur in domo Christi esse, hoc [p. 41] est, in ecclesia et palliantes a capite non recedere contra caput sentiunt. Heretici inquit cum ante crediderint scripturis que a spiritu sancto scripte sunt et edite, transferunt se ad novas doctrinas et fermentum phariseorum et mandata hominum, dumque sermonem Dei contempnunt, socrui sue faciunt iniuriam.

But against these things [it is objected]{g:instatur} in three ways. For it seems, first, that subjects
ought to consult holy scripture in whatever the [pope]{g:papa} has commanded.[^1] As to this, the
conclusion must be granted, since no one ought to obey him more than another, except in so far as he is
a superior vicar of Christ, and consequently except in so far as Jesus Christ is obeyed more principally
[through him]; now in every obedience [39] to Jesus Christ a Christian ought to consult his law;
therefore in every obedience of a Christian, as such, the one who obeys ought to consult the law of God.
And therefore the [laws]{g:ius} of the church have ordained that the law of scripture must be known so
generally and so especially by [priests]{g:sacerdos}, and specially by the greater ones, lest they be
troubled or hesitate over what and how they ought to answer to a superior's command.[^2] In this school
the Theban legion and all the martyrs were taught;[^3] indeed, in that school all are taught who oppose
[admonitions]{g:monita} that do not savour of the [charity]{g:caritas} of God and of neighbour, according
to that word of Christ, Matt. 10:37: "He that loveth father or mother more than me, is not worthy of
me"; and this lesson the apostles teach, Acts 5:29, saying that "we ought to obey God rather than men".
Therefore Christ says, Matt. 10:35: "I came not to send peace upon earth, but the sword. I came
therefore to set a man at variance against his father, and the daughter against her mother, and the
daughter in law against her mother in law; and a man's enemies, they of his own household."[^4] For
Christ came to dissolve carnal friendship for God's sake, since he who seems to be a man's intimate as
regards the world is [for the most part]{g:ut plurimum} his enemy as regards God (as is said in Micah
7).[^5] Blessed Jerome, on that chapter, seems to expound the text of the gospel in this sense: that
Christ came to separate the heretics from the [church]{g:ecclesia}, which they mock. [40] For out of
cloaked hypocrisy they feign that they are sons of God by the grace of adoption, and daughters of the
church their mother, for whose rights they are so zealous. "Let us say", he says, "what is written of
the heretics: for they seek not our salvation but their own gains, and trample the deceived flock under
their feet."[^6] The father of the heretic is God, who created him, and his mother is the church. A
mother-in-law, in the literal sense, is the mother of one of the spouses; "but the wife of a son is
called a daughter-in-law".[^7] Since, then, Christ according to his humanity is the
[bridegroom]{g:sponsus} of the soul, indeed of the whole church; and since, with this, he was conceived
of the Holy Spirit as of his mother, according to the saying that Jerome cites ... "My mother took me
by one of my hairs" (for the flesh is the hair of the soul),[^8] it is clear that the Holy Spirit, since
he is the mother of Christ the bridegroom of the church, is the mother-in-law of the soul that claims to
be the [bride]{g:sponsa} of Christ, because [he is] the husband's mother. And from the same
consideration the soul that pretends to be the wife of the son of the Holy Spirit is called his
daughter-in-law. Therefore the heretics, who pretend to be daughters-in-law of the Holy Spirit, whom
they claim as their mother-in-law, are separated from him by Christ, their [fallacy]{g:fallacia} being
exposed. And so, since the universal church is the house of the lord Jesus Christ, and consequently all
heretics who are outwardly in the church are pretended members of his household, it is clear that all
of them are enemies to that man, the householder.[^9] "Of him", says Jerome, "they are enemies who are
thought to be in the house of Christ, that [41] is, in the church, and, cloaking [it so as to seem] not
to withdraw from the head, think against the head." "Heretics", he says, "although they first believed
the scriptures, which were written and published by the Holy Spirit, go over to new
[doctrines]{g:doctrina}, and to the leaven of the Pharisees, and to the commandments of men; and while
they despise the word of God, they do injury to their mother-in-law."[^10]

## ¶2 · 41.7–41.31

> [p. 41] Christus ergo ex vita et doctrina venit confutare hereticos, ut discernantur a fidelibus et postmodum separentur. Et ita subtiliter et catholice potest intelligi secundum hunc sanctum per filium, filiam et nurum hereticus qui pretense et false se nominat filium Dei patris, filiam matris sue, sapiencie Dei patris et nurum spiritus sancti et sic cognatissimum trinitati. Sed vita et doctrina Christi sunt optimum speculum, ubi possunt hec discerni, cum manifestum sit quod omnis homo et solum talis qui est in vita et doctrina Christo contrarius est hereticus, et omnis christianus et solum talis qui est in vita et doctrina Christo conformis est ut sic ab heresi elongatus. Christus ergo venit non ad faciendum primam separacionem que est proprie per peccatum, sed ad faciendum sequentem, ut per eum discernantur heretici et ab ecclesia separentur. Et hec racio quare oportet omnem catholicum cognoscere scripturam sacram. Et certus sum quod, licet prelati nostri ipsam et professores suos dissipant et conculcant, lapides clamabunt, hoc est, seculares domini in sensu scripture a domino illustrati defendent fidem catholicam, quia pharisei volentes obturare ora discipulorum scripturam istam confitencium audierunt divinum responsum: Dico vobis, quia si hii tacuerint lapides clamabunt, Luce XIX°, 40. Magnam enim vim habet veritas scripturarum.

Christ, therefore, came by his life and teaching to confute the heretics, so that they may be discerned
from the faithful and afterwards separated. And so, subtly and in a catholic sense, according to this
saint, by "son", "daughter" and "daughter-in-law" can be understood the heretic, who pretendedly and
falsely names himself son of God the Father, daughter of his mother, the wisdom of God the Father, and
daughter-in-law of the Holy Spirit, and so most nearly kin to the Trinity.[^11] But the life and
teaching of Christ are the best mirror, in which these things can be discerned, since it is manifest
that every man, and only such a one, who in life and teaching is contrary to Christ is a heretic, and
every Christian, and only such a one, who in life and teaching is conformed to Christ is, as such,
removed far from heresy.[^12] Christ, therefore, came not to make the first separation, which is
properly through sin, but to make the one that follows, so that through him the heretics may be
discerned and separated from the church. And this is the reason why every catholic must know holy
scripture. And I am certain that, although our [prelates]{g:prelatus} scatter and trample it and those
who profess it, "the stones will cry out", that is, secular lords enlightened by the Lord in the sense
of scripture will defend the catholic faith;[^13] for the Pharisees, wishing to stop the mouths of the
disciples who were confessing this scripture, heard the divine answer: "I say to you that if these
shall hold their peace, the stones will cry out", Luke 19:40. For the truth of the scriptures has great
force.

## ¶3 · 42.1–42.9

> [p. 42] Secundo principaliter obicitur per hoc quod iuxta supradictam sentenciam nullus laicus teneretur credere de sacerdote suo, curato sive episcopo quod sit membrum ecclesie vel vere habens nomen talis officii, et periret omnis honor impendendus prelatis et fides populi laicalis; immo nullus viator teneretur credere Romane ecclesie in canonizando sanctos de privatis provinciis et titubaret cultus ecclesie in solemnizando festa et reliquias sanctorum.

[42] Second, as a principal [objection, it is objected, by appeal to]{g:obicitur per} this: that
according to the [teaching]{g:sentencia} given above no layman would be bound to believe of his priest,
[curate]{g:curatus} or [bishop]{g:episcopus} that he is a member of the church, or truly has the name
of such an office; and all honour to be paid to prelates, and the faith of the lay people, would perish;
indeed no [wayfarer]{g:viator} would be bound to believe the Roman church in [canonizing]{g:canonizare}
saints from particular provinces, and the worship of the church in solemnizing the feasts and relics of
the saints would totter.[^14]

## Notes

[^1]: **C/R.** *Instatur tripliciter*: three objections follow, the first here, the second at 42.1, the
    third at 47.7. A and B's margins: *Obieccio prima*; A: *Contra dicta*. *Ista*: the conclusion of
    38.12–24, that Urban is to be obeyed only so far as he commands Christ's counsels. *EnimL* (38.25)
    is translated as *enim*.

[^2]: **T/C.** *Concedi debet conclusio*: Wyclif grants the objection's claim (AB margin:
    *Responsio*), so the "objection" is turned into his thesis. *Principalius*: the pope is obeyed only
    as Christ is obeyed in him; "through him" is supplied. *Obedienciarius*: the one who renders
    obedience. *Ordinarunt*: Loserth's correction for the MSS' *in ordinarunt* (39.4). *Iura
    ecclesie*: the canons ("laws"), not rights; at 40.2 (*pro cuius iuribus*) the sense is "rights".
    *Musitent*: "mutter", here "hesitate". A's margin: *In obediencia ad superiorem consulenda est
    scriptura sacra*.

[^3]: **R.** The Theban legion under St Maurice, martyred for refusing an imperial command against
    their faith; Loserth refers to Bordier, *Dissertation critique sur le martyre de la légion
    thébéenne* (Amsterdam, 1705) (LEDGER E02e-1). B reads *concorditer contradicunt* (39.11).

[^4]: **S.** Matt. 10:37 (DR); Wyclif quotes only the first clause and has *vel* for the Vulgate's
    *aut*. Acts 5:29 (DR), given indirectly. Matt. 10:35 is Wyclif's reference; the quotation begins at
    10:34, abridged (*Non veni pacem mittere in terram sed gladium*; Vulg. *Nolite arbitrari quia pacem
    venerim … non veni pacem mittere, sed gladium*), and *ergo* stands for the Vulgate's *enim* in
    10:35. DR wording, adjusted to his Latin; 10:36 has no verb in the Latin. B's margin: *Math. 10, Act.
    5*.

[^5]: **S/C.** Mic. 7:6, DR "and a man's enemies are they of his own household". *Ut plurimum*: "for
    the most part" (GLOSSARY §D). *Familiaris … hostis*: Wyclif glosses *domestici* of the household's
    worldly intimacy. A's margin: *Dissolucio carnalis amicicie*. *M* (39.24) before *Beatus* is not
    construed.

[^6]: **S/T.** Jerome, *Commentarii in Michaeam* II, on Mic. 7:5–7 PL 25:1221C–1222B (Loserth's "1281" at
    39.20 is a slip for 1221; his "pag. 1180" at 40.6 is wrong) (LEDGER E02e-2). *Dicamus inquit scriptum
    de hereticis*: Jerome's own words (*et dicamus scriptum de haereticis*, 1221C), "let us say what is
    written of heretics". B *quod sint dei* (40.1); B *filie matris sue
    pro quibus viribus* (40.2); B *contrivit* (40.5–6).

[^7]: **S/C.** *Socrus … mater alterius coniugum. Ast uxor nati dicitur esse nurus*: a school
    definition, the second half an elegiac pentameter (*ast uxor nati dicitur esse nurus*), quoted to
    fix the kinship terms of Matt. 10:35 (LEDGER E02e-3). We end the sentence at *nurus*, against the
    print's comma: the *cum ergo* clause begins a new period whose main verb is *patet* (40.15).

[^8]: **S/T.** *Tulit me mater mea in uno capillorum meorum*: from the Gospel according to the Hebrews,
    which Jerome cites on Mic. 7:6 (*Modo tulit me mater mea, sanctus spiritus, in uno capillorum
    meorum*, PL 25:1221D–1222A, from the Gospel "which we recently translated"); Wyclif drops *Modo*
    (LEDGER E02e-4). Loserth marks a lacuna in the MSS after *Jeronymus* (40.12),
    printed as dots. B's margin (41.21, misplaced) repeats the quotation. *Caro enim est capillus
    anime* is Wyclif's gloss.

[^9]: **R/C.** B's lower margins (39–40) set out the scheme: *Deus pater per creacionem … Ecclesia
    nurus … Spiritus sanctus socrus*, against *Hereticus pretensus filius per adopcionem … filia secundum
    pretensionem … nurus secundum simulacionem*. *Fallacia*: here deceit; we keep the glossary's
    "fallacy". *Illi homini patrifamilias*: the *homo* of Matt. 10:36, Christ the householder. B *et
    igitur* (40.21). *Facietenus*: "outwardly", in face only.

[^10]: **T/S.** *Huius*: A₁'s correction, which Loserth adopts (cf. Jerome, *l.c.*); AB *huiusmodi
    inquit* (40.25). *Hoc est, in ecclesia*: Loserth notes the MSS' false *hoc est ecclesia* (41.1). B
    *transferuntur* (41.4). *Fermentum phariseorum*: Matt. 16:6; *mandata hominum*: Matt. 15:9.

[^11]: **C.** *Filiam matris sue, sapiencie Dei patris*: we take *sapiencie* in apposition to *matris*,
    so that the mother here is the wisdom of God the Father; B's margin reads it so (*Quomodo hereticus
    est filius matris sue sapiencie Dei patris*, 41.12). The heretic claims kinship with each person of
    the Trinity. B *possunt intelligi* (41.9).

[^12]: **C.** *Omnis … et solum talis*: both quantifiers are kept: every such man and only such is a
    heretic. *Ut sic*: "as such", in so far as he is conformed. AA₁ *ubi poterunt discerni* (41.14); B
    *Sed et vita* (41.13). AB margin: *Conclusio*; B: *Nota: quid est hereticus* (41.18).

[^13]: **C.** *Ipsam*: scripture; *professores suos*: those who profess it. *Seculares domini*: the
    lay lords as defenders of the faith, untagged (not the glossary's *temporales domini*). B *catholicam
    et pharisei* (41.27); A *abturare* (41.28).

[^14]: **C/R.** *Obicitur per hoc quod*: "by appeal to this, that" (GLOSSARY §C). *De privatis
    provinciis*: saints of particular provinces, as against the universal church. AB margin: *Secunda
    obieccio*. B lacks *iuxta* (42.1); B *tenetur* (42.2). The answer begins at 42.10 (next chunk).
