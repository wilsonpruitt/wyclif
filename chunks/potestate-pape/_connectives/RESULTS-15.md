## Doubtful, left as they are

- **12-n ¶3, p. 367**: *Alii autem intelligunt papam …*, rendered "But others". The *aliqui … alii* pair could be read (c), "And others", the second sense listed; (b) is defensible since the second sense is set against the first.
- **12-n ¶5, p. 368**: *Sed post lapsum … Sed ut credo …*, both "But". Each qualifies the preceding step (the composite-sense ruling, then the after-the-fall claim) rather than closing a syllogism; no *ergo* follows. (b) defensible; (c)/(a) arguable.
- **12-o ¶1, p. 369**: *Sed contra repugnat stante lege …, igitur repugnat legibus predictis*, rendered "But against this". An *igitur* follows, but *sed contra* is the formula for an opposing argument, not a minor premiss; "But" kept.
- **12-o ¶1, p. 369**: *Signa autem ut cruces … non vocamus privilegia*, rendered "And signs". Could be (b), signs set against Christ's law that suffices; (c) defensible as the next item of the third point.
- **12-o ¶2, p. 371**: *Pro declaracione autem conclusionis predicte capio*, rendered "And for the clarifying". (c) as a new proof extending the same point; "Now" would also fit.
- **12-m ¶4, p. 363**: *Minor autem patet*, rendered "And the minor is clear". (c), following *Maior … supponitur*; fine.
- **12-q ¶3, p. 380**: *De ornamentis autem corporalibus non legi …*, rendered "And as to bodily ornaments". Could be (b), bodily ornaments set against the moral "clothing" of justice just quoted; (c) defensible.
