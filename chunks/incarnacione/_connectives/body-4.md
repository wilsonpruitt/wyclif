### 03-d ¶1 · 40.16–42.2  (7 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 40] Et si⁶ obiciatur,⁷ quod ista posicio, vocando ‘hominem’ corpus mortuum et animam separatam, sequitur sentenciam plebeiorum,⁸ et per consequens est erronea, ut multa alia dicta vulgaria; dicitur quod non est color in consequencia; cum dicta vulgaria a prima veritate communitati⁹ hominum inspirata non possunt in toto esse falsa. Unde Salvator Ioh. 12°. approbat turbe¹⁰ sentenciam, que dixit: ‘Nos audivimus ex lege quod Christus manet in eternum. Adhuc, inquit, modicum lumen in vobis est; ambulate dum lucem habetis, ut¹¹ non tenebre vos comprehendant; dum lucem habetis,¹¹ credite in lucem, ut filii lucis¹² sitis.’ Quasi diceret¹² iuxta exposicionem beati Augustini Ome. 52. ‘Verum est principium quod de Christo concipitis, scilicet quod ipse eternaliter manet humanitus cognoscendo. **Sed** oportet ultra procedere, quod non obest eundem hominem¹³ esse mortuum corporaliter et cum hoc mente vivere. Ambulate, inquit Augustinus, attendite et totum intelligite tam moriturum¹⁴ Christum quam [p. 41] victurum in eternum. Cum aliquid, inquit, verum habetis, non sitis comprehensi tenebris ignorancie¹ contempnendo mortuum et occisum² hinc trahentem ad se omnia, et sic offendatis in lapidem offensionis et petram scandali.’ Unde Ome. 78³ super istud⁴ Ioh. 14. ‘non turbetur cor vestrum:⁵ declarando Christum esse duplicem formam, scilicet humanitatem et deitatem, subdit de eius humanitate. ‘Non, inquit, homo Christus, non dixit⁶ ‘‘Filius Dei,’’ quod eciam sola caro eius in sepulcro meruit appellari?’ Probat. ‘Cum,⁷ inquit, credimus in Dei Filium qui sepultus est, profecto et Filium Dei dicimus carnem que sola sepulta est.’ Et post declarando quomodo ad verba est⁸ verum, ‘Pater⁸ maior me est,’ per hoc quod est duarum formarum utraque, ita subdit: ‘Quid ergo, heretice?⁹ cum Christus sit Deus et homo, loquitur ut homo; et tu calumpniaris Deo?¹⁰ Ille in se commendat naturam humanam; et tu in illo audes deformare divinam? Infidelis, ingrate,¹¹ nonne tu minuis eum qui te fecit, quia ille dicitur factus¹² sic¹² propter te? Equalis enim Patri est Filius per quem factus est homo; ut minor esset Patre, factus est homo; quod¹³ nisi fieret, quid esset homo?¹³ agnoscamus, inquit, geminam substanciam Christi: divinam¹⁴ scilicet, qua equalis est Patri; et humanam, qua minor est Patre. Utrumque **autem** simul; non duo, **sed** unus est Christus, ne sit quaternitas non Trinitas Deus.¹⁵ Sicut enim unus est homo, anima racionalis et caro, Christum, inquit, in omnibus istis et¹⁶ singulis confitemur. Probat hoc. Quis est ergo, per quem factus est mundus? Christus Iesus; **sed** in forma Dei. Quis est sub Poncio Pilato crucifixus? Iesus Christus; **sed** in forma servi. Item de singulis ex quibus¹⁷ homo constat. Quis non¹⁸ derelictus est in inferno? Christus [p. 42] Iesus; **sed** in anima sola. Quis resurrecturus in triduo iacuit in sepulcro? Christus Iesus; **sed** in carne sola.’

ENGLISH: And if it is objected that this [position]{g:posicio}, by calling a dead body and a separated soul "man", follows the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of the common folk, and consequently is erroneous, like many other popular sayings, [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that there is no [colour]{g:color} in the [inference]{g:consequencia};[^1] since popular sayings inspired by the first truth in the community of men cannot be wholly false. Hence the Saviour in John 12 approves the teaching of the multitude, which said: "We have heard out of the law that Christ abideth for ever. Yet a little while, he says, the light is among you. Walk whilst you have the light, that the darkness overtake you not; whilst you have the light, believe in the light, that you may be the children of light."[^2] As if he said, according to the exposition of blessed Augustine, Homily 52:[^3] "The principle that you conceive of Christ is true, namely that he abides eternally, knowing [humanly]{g:humanitus}.[^4] But you must go further: that nothing prevents the same man being dead bodily and with this living in mind. Walk, says Augustine, attend, and understand the whole: Christ as much about to die as [41] about to live for ever. Since you have something true, he says, be not overtaken by the darkness of ignorance, despising the one dead and slain who from there draws all things to himself, and so stumble against the stone of stumbling and the rock of scandal."[^5] Hence in Homily 78, on that [verse] of John 14, "Let not your heart be troubled",[^6] [making clear]{g:declarare} that Christ is a twofold form, namely [the humanity]{g:humanitas} and [the Godhead]{g:deitas}, he adds about his humanity: "Is not, he says, the man Christ [the] 'Son of God', which even his flesh alone in the sepulchre deserved to be called?"[^7] He proves it: "When, he says, we believe in the Son of God who was buried, surely we call the flesh too, which alone was buried, the Son of God." And afterwards, making clear how it is true to the words "the Father is greater than I",[^8] through this, that he is each of two forms, he adds thus: "What then, heretic? Since Christ is God and man, he speaks as man; and do you slander God? He commends human [nature]{g:natura} in himself; and do you dare in him to disfigure the divine? [Unbeliever]{g:infidelis}, ungrateful one, do you not diminish him who made you, because he is said to have been made thus for your sake? For the Son, through whom man was made, is equal to the Father; that he might be less than the Father, he was made man; and if this were not done, what would man be? Let us acknowledge, he says, the twin substance of Christ: the divine, namely, by which he is equal to the Father; and the human, by which he is less than the Father. And both together; not two, but one is Christ, lest God be a quaternity, not a Trinity. For as man is one, rational soul and flesh, [so] we confess Christ, he says, in all these and in each.[^9] He proves this. Who then is he through whom the world was made? Christ Jesus; but in the form of God. Who was crucified under Pontius Pilate? Jesus Christ; but in the form of a servant. Again, of each of the things of which man consists. Who was not left in hell? Christ [42] Jesus; but in the soul alone. Who, to rise again, lay in the sepulchre in [the three days]{g:triduum}? Christ Jesus; but in the flesh alone."

### 03-d ¶2 · 42.3–42.15  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 42] Hec sentencia huius sancti que, sicut non potest dissolvi, sic non cognosci, nisi viso quod Christus est trium naturarum incommunicancium quelibet. Qui ergo voluerit illam materiam¹ verissimam de virtute sermonis infringere, incipiat communicacionem persone² Christi multis naturis, non multis personis, ut deitas, improbare. Parcant igitur emuli huic vie quod naturali instinctu a vulgo sit cognita; quod a scriptura sacra et sanctis doctoribus sit tam assidue declarata; et quod mentes fidelium substrata sentencia non fallaci **sed** veridica excitat pro cura mortuorum, ut sepultura cum aliis exequiis devocius peragenda. Eadem enim persona hominis, que heri viva conversata est cum proximis, deportatur hodie mortua ad ecclesiam, et deponitur in sepulcro.

ENGLISH: This is the teaching of this saint, which, just as it cannot be dissolved, so cannot be known, except once it is seen that Christ is each of three natures [that do not communicate]{g:incommunicantes}.[^10] Whoever therefore would break down that most true matter [in the proper sense of the words]{g:de-virtute-sermonis}, let him begin by disproving the communication of the [person]{g:persona} of Christ to many natures, not to many persons, as the Godhead [is communicated].[^11] Let the rivals therefore spare this way, because it is known to the common people by natural instinct; because it is so assiduously made clear by holy scripture and the holy doctors; and because, the teaching laid beneath it being not deceptive but truthful, it stirs the minds of the faithful to the care of the dead, so that burial with the other [exequies]{g:exequie} is to be performed more devoutly. For the same person of a man who yesterday, living, kept company with his neighbours, is carried today dead to the [church]{g:ecclesia}, and laid in the sepulchre.

### 03-d ¶4 · 42.29–43.16  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 42] Nec credo sophistam ad tantum desipere quod credat, si sola caro Christi fuit Christus in sepulcro et sola anima fuit Christus in inferno, quod¹¹ tunc sola caro vel sola anima fuit [p. 43] Christus. Ac si sic¹ argueretur solus Petrus est homo² in domo ista: ergo solus Petrus est homo² alicubi tenendo quidem diccionem exclusive, sicut facit Augustinus quando dicit³ quod nec sola caro nec sola anima fuit Christus, **sed** humanitas completa, que differt a parte qualitativa: patet decepcio sophistica introducta; et sicut dixi superius, quando Augustinus dicit quod pars qualitativa non est homo, exponit se ipsum quod non est completus homo, id est, natura composita⁴ ex corpore et anima: et sic intelligit 17⁵ de Trinitate ca. 7. exponens se ipsum quod intelligit ‘hominem’ ut veteres diffinierunt⁶ per ‘animal racionale mortale,’ vel iuxta descripcionem suam ‘substanciam racionalem constantem ex corpore et anima.’ Et⁷ per hoc distinguit inter triplicitatem naturarum in homine et trinitatem anime ac Trinitatem increatam. Affirmative itaque sue sunt concedende et negative modificande.

ENGLISH: Nor do I believe that a sophist is so far foolish as to believe, if the flesh of Christ alone was Christ in the sepulchre and the soul alone was Christ in hell, that then the flesh alone or the soul alone was [43] Christ. As if it were argued thus: "Peter alone is a man in this house: therefore Peter alone is a man anywhere", holding the word indeed exclusively, as Augustine does when he says that neither the flesh alone nor the soul alone was Christ, but the complete humanity, which differs from a [qualitative part]{g:pars-qualitativa}: the sophistical deception introduced is clear.[^14] And as I said above, when Augustine says that a qualitative part is not a man, he explains himself, that it is not a complete man, that is, a nature composed of body and soul: and so he understands [it in] *De Trinitate* 17, c. 7, explaining himself, that he understands "man" as the ancients defined [it], by "rational mortal animal", or according to his own [definition]{g:descripcio}, "a rational substance consisting of body and soul".[^15] And by this he distinguishes between the threeness of natures in man and the trinity of the soul and the uncreated Trinity. His affirmatives therefore are to be granted and his negatives qualified.

### 03-d ¶5 · 43.17–44.6  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 43] Ulterius si queratur, utrum verum sit in triduo quod hoc est corpus Christi et hec anima Christi pro nunc; dicitur⁸ quod sic: cum⁹ Christus vere sit tam corpus quam anima pro triduo, quo simul iacet mortuus et descendit vivus ad inferos, et sic persona viva est mortua secundum diversas naturas. Et movent me tria. Primo auctoritas scripture et exposicio beati Augustini superius recitata.¹⁰ Secundo quod aliter non esset concedendum quod Christus sit homo per illud triduum, quod nimis invaderet fidem meam. Et tercio quod doctores tam priores sapienciores quam moderniores capaciores¹¹ dicunt concorditer quod Christus nichil de humana natura dimisit quod¹² assumpsit, **sed** mansit per triduum unitus tam carni¹³ quam anime sicut ante. Cum igitur unione ypostatica, que non est per accidens unio ypostatica, fuerat antea copulatus;¹⁴ sequitur quod per illud triduum manet tam caro quam anima sicut prius. Unde [p. 44] dicunt loquentes nostri temporis concorditer quod sacerdos conficiens pro illo triduo conficeret dicendo verba sacramentalia corpus mortuum. **Sed** quomodo, queso, per verba falsissima¹ quibus diceret ‘hoc est corpus meum’ vere conficeret, nisi corpus confectum pro tunc fuerit corpus Christi?

ENGLISH: Further, if it is asked whether it is true in the three days that this is the body of Christ and this the soul of Christ for now, the answer is that it is: since Christ truly is both body and soul for the three days, in which he at once lies dead and descends living into hell, and so a living person is dead as to diverse natures. And three things move me. First, the [authority]{g:auctoritas} of scripture and the exposition of blessed Augustine recited above. Second, that otherwise it would not have to be granted that Christ is man through that three days' space, which would [exceedingly]{g:nimis} assail my [faith]{g:fides}. And third, that the doctors, the earlier and wiser as much as the more modern and more capable, say in concord that Christ [laid aside]{g:dimittere} nothing of the human nature that he [assumed]{g:assumere}, but remained through the three days united as much to the flesh as to the soul, as before.[^16] Since therefore he had been joined before by a hypostatic [union]{g:unio}, which is not a hypostatic union by accident, [it follows]{g:sequitur} that through that three days' space he remains both flesh and soul, as before.[^17] Hence [44] those who speak in our time say in concord that a priest [consecrating]{g:conficere} during those three days would, by saying the sacramental words, consecrate the dead body. But how, I ask, by most false words, in which he would say "This is my body", would he truly consecrate, unless the body consecrated were at that time the body of Christ?

### 03-e ¶1 · 44.27–45.13  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 44] **Sed** contra illud obicitur. Videtur primo quod corpus mortuum¹² sit corpus vivum; quia Christus, qui est corpus mortuum¹² in dicto triduo, est corpus vivum post et ante: et sic, cum corpus animatum et corpus inanimatum distinguuntur in specie, videtur quod illa non sunt idem corpus in numero, cum differencia specifica presupponit differenciam [p. 45] numeralem.¹ Hic dicitur quod corpus vivum et animatum est idem corpus in numero mortuum et inanimatum, licet non simul et semel; nec differt corpus in quantum² animatum specifice ab inanimato² corpore. Pro quo notandum quod dupliciter³ dicitur corpus animatum, scilicet, ab extrinseco participative, vel ab intrinseco qualitative⁴; ut corpus compositum ex corpore et anima est animatum anima, que est eius forma et quiditas, et illud non potest esse inanimatum: **sed** corpus, quod est altera pars qualitativa hominis, est animatum participacione anime; que, cum non sit forma eius intrinseca, potest abesse a corpore manente eodem in numero; sicut homines, qui participative dicuntur dii possunt manere iidem⁵ Deo absente ab eis per graciam.

ENGLISH: But against this [it is objected]{g:obicitur}. It seems, first, that a dead body is a living body; because Christ, who is a dead body in the said [three days]{g:triduum}, is a living body after and before: and so, since an animate body and an inanimate body are distinguished in species, it seems that they are not the same body in number, since a specific [difference]{g:differencia} presupposes a numerical [45] difference. Here [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that the living and animate body is the same body in number as the dead and inanimate, though not at one and the same time; nor does the body [insofar as]{g:in-quantum} animate differ in species from the inanimate body. For which it is to be noted that a body is called animate in two ways, namely from outside, [participatively]{g:participative}, or from within, qualitatively; as the body composed of body and soul is animated by the soul, which is its form and [quiddity]{g:quiditas}, and that cannot be inanimate: but the body which is the other [qualitative part]{g:pars-qualitativa} of man is animated by participation of the soul; and the soul, since it is not its intrinsic form, can be absent from the body while the body remains the same in number;[^1] as men, who are participatively called gods, can remain the same while God is absent from them by grace.[^2]

### 03-e ¶3 · 45.19–46.5  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 45] Hic dupliciter⁸ dicitur. Primo quod tam⁹ corpus quam anima⁹ eius¹⁰ est pars Christi, **sed** non in dicto triduo; quia non tunc qualitative componunt Christum, **sed** alias pro suo tempore. Unde posito quod essemus in dicto triduo, vere sciremus quod hec¹¹ anima que nunc est anima Christi, **sed** non nunc pars Christi, est pars Christi optima post et ante; et correspondenter de corpore. Ex quo patet quod hec non est synecdochica¹² locucio: ‘hoc corpus’ vel ‘hec anima est nunc Christus:’ **sed**, sicut homo est essencia substancia vel quiditas sua intransitive intelligendo, sic Christus est tunc hoc corpus et hec anima, que sunt sua ad¹³ sensum intransitivum, ut supra. Unde hec anima, licet non nunc animat corpus illud quod iacet mortuum, animat tamen Filium Dei; [p. 46] sicut caput¹ denominat hominem capitatum: et patet quod² animare est equivocum ad corpus subiectum vivificare et ad denominare personam cui copulatur ypostatice habentem³ animam, quam⁴ per se animat, cum ad invicem⁵ consequuntur⁶ vires inferiores sicut passio ad subiectum.

ENGLISH: Here the answer is twofold. First, that both his body and his soul is a part of Christ, but not in the said three days; because they do not then qualitatively compose Christ, but at other times, for their own time. Hence, supposing that we were in the said three days, we would truly know that this soul, which now is the soul of Christ but not now a part of Christ, is the best part of Christ after and before; and correspondingly of the body. From which it is clear that this is not a [synecdochic locution]{g:synecdochica-locucio}: "this body", or "this soul is now Christ":[^3] but, just as man is his [essence]{g:essencia}, substance or quiddity, understanding it [intransitively]{g:intransitive}, so Christ is then this body and this soul, which are his in the intransitive sense, as above.[^4] Hence this soul, although it does not now animate that body which lies dead, yet animates the Son of God; [46] as a head [names]{g:denominare} a man ["headed"]{g:capitatus}:[^5] and it is clear that "to animate" is equivocal between vivifying a body that is its [subject]{g:subiectum} and naming the [person]{g:persona} to whom it is joined [hypostatically]{g:ypostatice} as having a soul, which it animates through itself, since the lower powers follow upon one another as an [attribute]{g:passio} upon a subject.[^6]

### 03-e ¶4 · 46.6–46.16  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 46] Secunda responsio dicit quod tam corpus quam anima Christi est in triduo vere pars Christi, **sed** nec qualitativa nec quantitativa, cum non tunc⁷ componuntur ad idem,⁷ **sed** est pars discretiva. Et sic intelligit Damascenus, quod Christus descendit totus ad inferos, sicut totus iacuit in sepulcro, **sed** non totum; quia persona fuit homo in utroque loco, **sed** non secundum ambas naturas; et sic in triduo habuit omnes partes⁸ quas habuit⁸ post resurreccionem, sed⁹ tunc defecit unio vel¹⁰ composicio. **Sed** nullum absolutum¹¹: nec pono vim in illa variacione, quia utraque sentencia est satis¹² catholica ad sensus equivocos.¹³

ENGLISH: The second response says that both the body and the soul of Christ is truly in the three days a part of Christ, but neither qualitative nor quantitative, since they are not then composed into the same thing, but it is a [discrete part]{g:pars-discretiva}. And so Damascene understands that Christ descended whole into hell, as he lay whole in the sepulchre, but not the whole; because the person was man in each place, but not as to both [natures]{g:natura}; and so in the three days he had all the parts he had after the resurrection, but then the [union]{g:unio} or composition was lacking.[^7] But nothing absolute [was lacking]: nor do I lay weight on that variation, because each [teaching]{g:sentencia} is catholic enough in the equivocal senses.[^8]

### 03-e ¶5 · 46.17–46.25  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 46] **Sed** 3°. obicitur ex hoc, quod Christus in illo triduo nec est homo immortalis nec homo¹⁴ mortalis; et omnis homo est mortalis vel immortalis: ergo Christus pro illo triduo non est homo. Maior patet eo quod Christus tunc passus est mortem: ergo iuxta Apostolum ‘mors illi ultra¹⁵ non dominabitur.’ Et quod non sit homo immortalis videtur ex hoc quod iacet mortuus in sepulcro et nondum consecutus est dotem¹⁶ immortalitatis, **sed** primo in resurreccione sequente iuxta communem doctorum sentenciam.¹⁷

ENGLISH: But third, it is objected from this, that Christ in that three days is neither an immortal man nor a mortal man; and every man is mortal or immortal: therefore Christ for that three days is not a man. The major is clear from this, that Christ then suffered death: therefore, according to the Apostle, "death shall no more have dominion over him."[^9] And that he is not an immortal man appears from this, that he lies dead in the sepulchre and has not yet obtained the [dower of immortality]{g:dos-immortalitatis}, but first in the resurrection that follows, according to the common teaching of the doctors.[^10]

### 03-e ¶8 · 48.3–48.28  (7 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 48] Quod **autem** Magister Distinctione 22 aliter loquitur cum modernis est propter equivocacionem, cum non admittit aliquid pro quacunque mensura esse hominem, nisi tunc componatur ex corpore et anima. Nos **autem** sequentes² scripturam non ficte **sed** philosophice³ dicimus quod eadem persona Petri vel cuiuscunque sancti prioris, que conversata est hic corporaliter in terris, manet iam beata in celis; et ubicunque est spiritus ille creatus sive in corpore sive extra corpus est idem homo in numero, et solum, ubi est corpus vel anima, est persona humanitus. Ut patet Ep. 40 ad Dardanum. Et cum certum sit quod Christus pro illo triduo non componitur qualitative⁴ ex corpore et anima, eo quod unio illorum dissolvitur; patet quod tunc nec est sic homo mortalis nec homo immortalis, quia non ad illum⁵ sensum tunc est homo. Si **autem** vocatur⁶ vere corpus et anima separata utrumque homo, sicut vocat Hugo de sancto Victore sequendo Augustinum, scripturas, et philosophicas raciones; tunc respondendum est sicut superius ad obiectus. Nam anima est per se homo et corpus nuncupative; et sic utrumque non duo homines, **sed** unus tantum. Si **autem** Magister intelligit hominem collective pro⁷ duabus naturis ad⁸ invicem et Verbo ypostatice copulatis, non est color quod tunc sit homo nisi utraque natura sit homo: et sic esset homo immortalis sicut anima fuit a sua⁹ origine immortalis; et per consequens partim mortalis, quia mortuus, et partim immortalis, quia anima. Hoc **autem** concedendum est de quolibet alio homine.

ENGLISH: And that [the Master]{g:magister}, in Distinction 22, speaks otherwise, along with the moderns, is because of equivocation, since he does not admit that anything is a man for any measure [of time] unless it is then composed of body and soul.[^16] But we, following scripture not feignedly but philosophically, say that the same person of Peter or of any earlier saint, who lived here bodily on earth, remains now blessed in heaven; and wherever that [created]{g:creatus} spirit is, whether in the body or out of the body, it is the same man in number, and only where the body or the soul is, is the person there [humanly]{g:humanitus}.[^17] As is clear from Letter 40, to Dardanus.[^18] And since it is certain that Christ for that three days is not composed qualitatively of body and soul, in that their union is dissolved, it is clear that then he is neither a mortal man nor an immortal man in this way, because he is not then man in that sense. But if the body and the separated soul are truly each called man, as Hugh of Saint Victor calls them, following Augustine, the scriptures, and philosophical reasons, then the reply must be as above to the objections.[^19] For the soul is man through itself and the body [nuncupatively]{g:nuncupative}; and so the two are not two men, but one only. But if the Master understands "man" collectively, for the two natures joined to one another and to the Word hypostatically, there is no colour [for saying] that he is then man unless each nature is man: and so he would be an immortal man, as the soul was immortal from its origin; and consequently partly mortal, because dead, and partly immortal, because soul. And this is to be granted of any other man.

### 04-a ¶3 · 49.4–49.23  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 49] Scribit enim Bonaventura super Distinccionem 12.¹ 3ⁱⁱ Sentenciarum, quod triplex² est predicacio, scilicet actualis, aptitudinalis,³ et mixtim. Actualis, quando subiectum est ens actu et forma predicati sibi inest; ut cum dicitur homine existente ‘homo est animal.’ Predicacio secundum puram aptitudinem est, quando nec subiectum nec forma predicati est in actu, **sed** necessaria⁴ est ordinacio unius ad alterum fundata super principia nature; sicut nulla rosa existente ‘rosa est flos.’ Quando subiectum **autem** est in actu, et forma predicati non inest sibi actualiter **sed** secundum necessariam ordinacionem fundatam super principia nature, tunc est predicacio mixtim, id est, partim actualis et partim aptitudinalis. Et sic concedit quod Christus fuit homo in triduo, quia subiectum, quod est Verbum Dei, tunc fuit in actu, et forma humanitatis servata fuit in aptitudine coniunccionis anime ad corpus. Nec vidi planiorem sentenciam alicuius doctoris in illa materia; quia indubie, quicunque concedit quod corpus et anima fuerunt partes Christi in triduo, habet concedere consequenter quod Christus habet correspondenter esse hominis ex eisdem.

ENGLISH: For Bonaventure writes on Distinction 22 of the third [book] of the *Sentences* that [predication]{g:predicacio} is threefold, namely actual, habitudinal, and mixed.[^2] Actual, when the [subject]{g:subiectum} is [a being]{g:ens} in act and the form of the predicate is in it; as when it is said, a man existing, "man is an animal". Predication according to pure aptitude is when neither the subject nor the form of the predicate is in act, but there is a necessary [ordering]{g:ordinacio} of the one to the other founded on the principles of nature; as, no rose existing, "a rose is a flower".[^3] And when the subject is in act, and the form of the predicate is not in it actually but according to a necessary ordering founded on the principles of nature, then the predication is mixed, that is, partly actual and partly aptitudinal. And so he grants that Christ was man in the three days, because the subject, which is [the Word]{g:verbum} of God, was then in act, and the form of [the humanity]{g:humanitas} was preserved in the aptitude of the conjunction of the soul to the body. Nor have I seen a plainer teaching of any doctor in that matter; because undoubtedly whoever grants that the body and soul were parts of Christ in the three days has to grant consequently that Christ correspondingly has the [being]{g:esse} of a man from the same.[^4]

### 04-a ¶4 · 49.24–50.21  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 49] **Sed** Doctor Subtilis dilatat⁵ ibidem⁶ amplius istam materiam. Dicit enim quod in triduo fuit ita; quod ‘Christus est Christus, et quod Verbum habet tam corpus quam animam [p. 50] sibi unitam, et tamen non tunc fuit ita quod Christus est homo; quia humanitas dicit ultra naturas imperfectas et ultra unionem respectivam unitatem¹ absolutam, sicut est in numeris. Et illa unitas non componit cum aliis; quia tunc esset² processus in infinitum. Et sic in triduo corruptum est aliquid absolutum, quia humanitas; sic quod nulla pars eius remanserat, cum sit³ forma totalitatis³ non habens partes quantitativas vel qualitativas. Racio **autem** quare non sequitur—Christus est Christus: ergo Christus est homo—est ista. Christus secundum Damascenum ca°. 49 est nomen ypostaticum⁴ duarum naturarum⁵ completarum singulatim,⁶ scilicet humanitatis et deitatis. Cum ergo oportet racionem subiecti talis esse in se veram, antequam aliquid de eo enuncietur; quia aliter esset hec vera—maius Deo⁷ est— quod est falsum,⁸ cum subiectum includit repugnanciam: patet quod hec—Christus est homo—⁹ formata pro triduo⁹ foret falsa; quia humanitas non tunc infuit Verbo Dei. Ideo, sicut,¹⁰ nullo homine existente, homo albus¹¹ est homo albus,¹² nec ex hoc sequitur quod homo sit homo albus¹²: sic pro triduo Christus est Christus; sicut Deus-homo est¹³ Deus-homo, et tamen Deus non est tunc homo.’

ENGLISH: But [the Subtle Doctor]{g:doctor-subtilis} widens this matter more amply in the same place.[^5] For he says that in the three days it was so that "Christ is Christ" and that "the Word has both body and soul [50] united to him", "and yet it was not then so that Christ is man; because the humanity [signifies]{g:dicere}, beyond the imperfect natures and beyond the [relational]{g:respectivus} [union]{g:unio}, an absolute unity, as is the case in numbers. And that unity does not compose with other things; because then there would be a process to infinity. And so in the three days something absolute was corrupted, namely the humanity; so that no part of it had remained, since it is a [form of totality]{g:forma-totalitatis}, not having quantitative or qualitative parts.[^6] And the reason why it does not follow, 'Christ is Christ: therefore Christ is man', is this. Christ, according to Damascene, c. 49, is a hypostatic name signifying two complete natures, namely the humanity and [the Godhead]{g:deitas}.[^7] Since therefore the notion of such a subject must be true in itself before anything is enunciated of it (because otherwise this would be true, 'something greater than God is', which is false, since the subject includes an [incompatibility]{g:repugnancia}),[^8] it is clear that this, 'Christ is man', formed for the three days, would be false; because the humanity was not then in the Word of God. Therefore, just as, no man existing, a white man is a white man, and yet [it does not follow]{g:sequitur} from this that a man is a white man: so for the three days Christ is Christ; as [God-man]{g:deushomo} is God-man, and yet God is not then man."

### 04-a ¶5 · 50.22–51.5  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 50] Ulterius quo ad denominacionem Verbi ab illis partibus ita scribit: ‘Verbum, inquit, quamvis in triduo habuit¹⁴ corpus et animam sibi unitam a quibus potuit denominari: non placuit tamen doctoribus quod denominetur ab eis, et forte non sine causa: cum partes non denominant, nisi ut subsistunt naturaliter in suo toto. **Sed** tunc defuit humanitas: ideo non denominavit eum carneum vel carnalem.¹⁵ Verumptamen si essent¹⁵ nomina imposita, posset denominari ab illis; sed¹⁶ oporteret quod essent denominativa, que non saperent vicium.¹⁷ Unde sicut substancia¹⁷ non sufficit denominari¹⁸ alba ab albedine,¹⁸ nisi mediante subiecto: sic [p. 51] nec Christus denominari carnalis, nisi mediante humanitate: ut, si Christus assumpsisset solum quantitatem, adhuc¹ non esset quantus propter defectum subiecti materialis.’ Hoc Latinum huius doctoris est michi² et forte aliis difficilius quam³ sentencia Augustini.³

ENGLISH: Further, as to the [naming]{g:denominacio} of the Word from those parts, he writes thus: "The Word, he says, although in the three days he had body and soul united to him, from which he could [be named]{g:denominare}, yet it did not please the doctors that he be named from them, and perhaps not without cause: since parts do not name except as they subsist naturally in their whole. But then the humanity was lacking: therefore it did not name him fleshy or carnal.[^9] Nevertheless, if names were imposed, he could be named from them; but they would have to be denominatives that did not savour of vice. Hence, just as a substance does not suffice to be named white from whiteness except by means of a subject, so [51] neither [does] Christ [suffice] to be named carnal except by means of the humanity: so that, if Christ had [assumed]{g:assumere} quantity alone, he would still not be of a quantity, for lack of a material subject." This Latin of this doctor is to me, and perhaps to others, more difficult than the teaching of Augustine.[^10]

### 04-a ¶6 · 51.6–51.15  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 51] **Sed** pro eius intellectu notandum quod ipse admisit predicacionem secundum habitudinem⁴ sicut Doctor alius.⁵ Et illud antiquitus est famosum,⁶ ut: ‘Quandocunque predicatur idem de se vel per se superius⁷ de suo per se⁸ inferiori⁹ est predicacio vera,⁹ licet neutrum extremum sit in actu. Ut verum est, dicit Doctor, quod—Cesar est homo—eciam nullo homine existente.’ **Sed** quando predicatur formaliter res alterius generis, tunc limitat ad predicacionem actualem; ut, ‘si homo est qualis,¹⁰ quantus, pater, agens, et cetera; tunc est actu.’

ENGLISH: But for understanding him it is to be noted that he admitted predication according to [respect]{g:habitudo}, as the other Doctor [did].[^11] And that is famous of old, thus: "Whenever the same is predicated of itself, or what is per se higher of its per se lower, the predication is true, although neither extreme is in act. As it is true, says the Doctor, that 'Caesar is a man', even no man existing." But when a thing of another genus is predicated formally, then he [limits]{g:limitare} [it] to actual predication; as, "if a man is of a quality, of a quantity, a father, acting, and so on, then he is in act."

### 04-a ¶7 · 51.16–51.26  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 51] Et cum illo concordat Anselmus in De Concordia 5° ca°. ita dicens: ‘Quippe non idem est, rem esse preteritam, et, rem preteritam esse preteritam; aut,¹¹ rem esse presentem, et, rem presentem esse presentem; aut,¹¹ rem esse futuram, et, rem futuram esse futuram: sicut non est idem, rem esse albam, et, rem albam esse albam: lignum enim non¹² de necessitate semper est album, quia¹³ aliquando antequam fieret album, potuit non fieri album; et¹⁴ postquam fieret album,¹⁴ potest fieri non album: **sed** lignum album de necessitate semper est album, quia non potest¹⁵ fieri, ut album simul sit non album.’

ENGLISH: And with that Anselm agrees in *De concordia*, c. 5, saying thus: "For indeed it is not the same, 'the thing is past' and 'the past thing is past'; or 'the thing is present' and 'the present thing is present'; or 'the thing is future' and 'the future thing is future': just as it is not the same, 'the thing is white' and 'the white thing is white'. For wood is not of necessity always white, because at some time, before it became white, it could have not become white; and after it became white, it can become not white: but white wood is of necessity always white, because it cannot come about that white at the same time is not white."[^12]

### 04-a ¶8 · 51.27–52.12  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 51] Volunt ergo isti doctores quod necessaria sit predicacio aptitudinalis secundum esse possibile in causis secundis, quandocunque predicatur idem de se vel per se superius de suo per se inferiori. Nec ex hoc concluditur existencia talis rei. Nec contendo multum circa hanc logicam, cum nescio¹⁶ [p. 52] eam efficaciter impugnare. **Sed** considero quod si moderni dignarentur¹ attendere ad scripturas patrum priorum, non tantum mirarentur de hoc quod dico: Omne preteritum vel futurum esse pro suo tempore: quia ex illo dicto sequitur nedum omne preteritum vel futurum **sed** eciam omne possibile esse² pro semper. Et sic videtur quod infiniti homines, sicut quelibet signanda individua, sunt ubique; nam mille homines sunt mille homines, et sic infinitum,³ nullo homine existente. Nec videtur racio quare semper, quin⁴ per idem ubique et sic quilibet⁵ infinitum bonus foret infinitum bonus, et sic de quacunque denominacione possibili.

ENGLISH: These doctors therefore hold that aptitudinal predication according to possible being in second causes is necessary, whenever the same is predicated of itself, or what is per se higher of its per se lower. Nor is the existence of such a thing [inferred]{g:concludere} from this. Nor do I contend much about this logic, since I do not know how [52] to attack it effectively. But I consider that if the moderns deigned to attend to the writings of the earlier fathers, they would not wonder so much at what I say, "Every past or future thing is for its own time": because from that saying it follows [not only]{g:nedum} that every past or future thing but also that every possible thing is for always.[^13] And so it seems that infinite men, like any [individuals]{g:individuum} one might designate, are everywhere; for a thousand men are a thousand men, and so to infinity, no man existing. Nor does there seem to be a reason why [this holds] always without, by the same token, [holding] everywhere; and so anyone infinitely good would be infinitely good, and so of any possible naming.[^14]

### 04-a ¶9 · 52.13–52.27  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 52] **Sed** dimittendo istud argumentum, ad propositum quod sequitur: Christum fuisse⁶ hominem in triduo. Nam Christus est per se in specie humana, ut patet posterius: ergo est per se homo: et per consequens, si est Christus, tunc est homo. Confirmatur. Sicut conceditur quod Christus est Christus, sic concedi debet quod iste homo est iste homo, demonstrando Iesum.⁷ **Sed** impossibile est istum hominem esse istum hominem, nisi iste homo sit⁸ homo. Ergo concesso quod Christus est Christus pro triduo, concedendum est ipsum esse hominem pro eodem. Unde dicit Doctor quod,†⁹ ‘si hec esset per se Christus est homo et sufficiens quod causet inherenciam predicati ad subiectum, sine hoc quod subiectum dicat compositum unum hoc Christus est homo fuisset semper vera. **Sed** magis, inquit, credo quod requiritur hoc et plus.’⁹†

ENGLISH: But leaving that argument, [I come] to the matter in hand, which follows: "Christ was man in the three days". For Christ is per se in the human species, as is clear later: therefore he is per se man: and consequently, if he is Christ, then he is man. This is confirmed. Just as it is granted that Christ is Christ, so it ought to be granted that this man is this man, pointing to Jesus. Now it is impossible that this man be this man unless this man is man. Therefore, granted that Christ is Christ for the three days, it must be granted that he is man for the same [time]. Hence the Doctor says that "if this were per se, 'Christ is man', and sufficient that it cause the inherence of the predicate in the subject, without the subject's signifying one concept, this, 'Christ is man', would always have been true. But rather, he says, I believe that this is required, and more."[^15]

### 04-a ¶10 · 52.28–53.14  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 52] Item sicut conceditur quod pro triduo ille homo est ille homo, ita concedi debet ut convertibile per se, quod ille [p. 53] homo componitur ex hoc corpore et hac anima. Ex quo plane sequitur quod ille homo sit homo, sicut ille homo est Deus-homo. Nam Christus et Deus-homo convertuntur¹ simpliciter et ex equo:¹ et per consequens, sicut conceditur quod Christus est Christus, sic concedi debet quod Deus-homo est Deus-homo. Sicut ergo conceditur quod rosa est flos propter possibilitatem² licet nulla existat; sic evidencius videtur concedendum quod hec persona est homo, posito quod essemus in triduo cum sit nedum remota potencia, **sed** disposicio propinqua et necessaria, ut resurgat. Unde propter essenciam materialem perpetuam videtur Ieronimus³ in Ep. ad Paulam et Eustochium concedere quod Christus a principio seculi erat Christus: **sed** Doctor ille concedit ad sensum alium.

ENGLISH: Again, just as it is granted that for the three days that man is that man, so it ought to be granted, as [convertible]{g:convertibilis} per se, that that [53] man is composed of this body and this soul. From which it plainly follows that that man is man, just as that man is God-man. For "Christ" and "God-man" are convertible [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} and on equal terms: and consequently, just as it is granted that Christ is Christ, so it ought to be granted that God-man is God-man.[^16] Just as therefore it is granted that a rose is a flower on account of possibility, though none exists, so it seems more evidently to be granted that this [person]{g:persona} is man, supposing that we were in the three days, since there is not only a remote [potency]{g:potencia} but a proximate and necessary disposition that he rise again.[^17] Hence on account of the perpetual material [essence]{g:essencia} Jerome seems, in the Letter to Paula and Eustochium, to grant that Christ was Christ from the beginning of the world: but that Doctor grants it in another sense.[^18]

### 04-a ¶11 · 53.15–53.27  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 53] Item cum Christus secundum illum doctorem habet omnes partes⁴ quas habet post resurreccionem, non deficit ad hoc quod tunc⁵ sit homo nisi unio respectiva: **sed** nullum tale respectivum est de essencia substancie ut patet alibi De Continuacione: ergo corpus et anima unita Verbo pro triduo sufficiunt⁶ denominare ipsum esse hominem. Minor patet ex hoc: quod addita unione illa ad alia principia existencia⁷ pro illo triduo,⁷ foret consequens formaliter, quod Christus sit homo: ergo tunc non deficit⁸ nisi⁹ dicta unio; nec valet dicere quod humanitas deficit;⁸ quia humanitas est quod ipse est homo: ergo non est previe requisita¹⁰ ad hoc quod ipse sit homo, quia tunc idem esset previe requisitum ad se ipsum.

ENGLISH: Again, since Christ, according to that doctor, has all the parts which he has after the resurrection, nothing is lacking for his being man then except the relational union: but no such relational thing is of the essence of substance, as is clear elsewhere, in *De continuacione*:[^19] therefore the body and soul united to the Word for the three days suffice to name him as being man. The minor is clear from this: that, that union being added to the other principles existing for that three days, it would be [consequent]{g:consequens} formally that Christ is man: therefore nothing is then lacking but the said union; nor does it avail to say that the humanity is lacking; because the humanity is that he is man: therefore it is not [required beforehand]{g:previe-requisitum} for his being man, because then the same thing would be required beforehand for itself.[^20]

### 04-b ¶1 · 53.28–54.16  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 53] Item vel est dicta unio causa humanitatis vel e contra; cum distinguuntur ut absolutum et respectivum, et non sunt impertinencia. Si humanitas sit causa dicte unionis tanquam¹¹ passionis que consequitur ad subiectum: tunc [p. 54] unio non requiritur ad humanitatem, et e contra pro¹ dicto triduo sunt omnes cause posite, que requiruntur ad humanitatem Christi;¹ et per consequens tunc est homo. Si e contra unio sit causa dicte humanitatis, hoc² videtur tripliciter esse falsum. Primo per³ hoc quod continuacio per idem foret causa corporis; quod improbatum est alibi. 2°. per hoc quod unio illa, cum dependeat ab extremis, non est essenciale principium hominis, eo quod posset manere idem homo variato corpore vel ablata eius parte quantitativa. Non ergo est illa unio causa beatitudinis, sciencie et aliorum⁴ accidencium⁴ hominis que insunt racione anime;. et multo minus est causa⁵ humanitatis, que est prior illis accidentibus. 3°. per hoc quod illa unio nedum dependet ab illis extremis absolute, **sed** ut copulantur qualitative ad invicem; et per consequens presupponit perfectum hominem: et sic non est previe requisita **sed** accessoria.

ENGLISH: Again, either the said [union]{g:unio} is the cause of [the humanity]{g:humanitas} or the reverse, since they are distinguished as absolute and [relational]{g:respectivus}, and are not [irrelevant]{g:impertinens} [to each other].[^1] If the humanity is the cause of the said union, as of an [attribute]{g:passio} that follows upon its [subject]{g:subiectum}, then the [54] union is not required for the humanity; and, on the other side, for [the said three days]{g:triduum} all the causes are posited that are required for the humanity of Christ;[^2] and in consequence he is then a man. If, conversely, the union is the cause of the said humanity, this seems false in three ways. First, by this: that [continuity]{g:continuacio} would by the same token be the cause of the body, which has been disproved elsewhere. Second, by this: that that union, since it depends on its extremes, is not an essential principle of man, because the same man could remain with his body varied or a [quantitative part]{g:pars-quantitativa} of it taken away. So that union is not the cause of blessedness, of knowledge, and of the other accidents of a man that are in him by reason of the soul; and much less is it the cause of the humanity, which is prior to those accidents. Third, by this: that that union [not only]{g:nedum} depends on those extremes absolutely, but as they are joined qualitatively to each other; and in consequence it presupposes a complete man: and so it is not [required beforehand]{g:previe-requisitum} but accessory.[^3]

### 04-b ¶7 · 56.13–57.6  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 56] Item si pro dicto triduo corpus et anima sunt partes Christi, tunc denominant⁷ ipsum habere partes illas tanquam partes suas: quia constat quod non sequitur —Hec⁸⁹ res est mea et est pars; ergo est pars mea—quia quelibet pars creature esset pars Trinitatis increate; et, ut sophiste¹⁰ arguunt, homo¹¹ monstruose haberet multa capita asinina. Ideo concedit doctor¹² quod Christus potest vere denominari pro illo triduo habens partes illas ut partes suas, **sed** non carneus vel carnalis: ut si solum¹³ assumpsisset¹⁴ quantitatem, adhuc¹⁵ non¹⁶ esset quantus propter defectum subiecti materialis: sic licet habeat carneam partem suam, adhuc¹⁷ non est carneus, eo quod deest humanitas. **Sed** miror de ista similitudine propter tria. Primo cum impossibile sit quantitatem per se esse. 2°. cum hoc posito per impossibile Verbum esset quantum per ypostaticam unionem. Non enim est unio vel assumpcio personalis sine communicacione ydiomatum nature specifice assumpte¹⁸ Verbo vel persone assumenti: assumptum enim ypostatice oportet terminari suppositacione¹⁹ aliena et non esse suppositum proprie speciei, [p. 57] ut eciam¹ dicit doctor. 3°. videtur similitudo² impertinens; cum natura illa non dependet ab humanitate, cum manet naturaliter sine illa, et posset fuisse assumpta sine assumpcione humanitatis. Si enim Verbum posset pure assumere quantitatem, multo evidencius carnem mortuam per se de natura possibilem.

ENGLISH: Again, if for the said three days the body and soul are parts of Christ, then they name him as having those parts as his parts: for it is evident that it does not follow, "This thing is mine and is a part; therefore it is a part of mine", since [then] any part of a [creature]{g:creatura} would be a part of the uncreated Trinity; and, as [the sophists]{g:sophista} argue, a man would monstrously have many asses' heads.[^14] Therefore the Doctor grants that Christ can truly be named for those three days "having those parts as his parts", but not "[fleshy]{g:carneus}" or "[carnal]{g:carnalis}": as, if he had [assumed]{g:assumere} quantity alone, he would still not be [quantified]{g:quantus}, for lack of a material subject; so, although he has his fleshy part, he is still not fleshy, because the humanity is lacking.[^15] But I wonder at that comparison, for three reasons. First, since it is impossible for quantity to be by itself. Second, since, this being posited *per impossibile*, the Word would be quantified by the hypostatic union. For there is no personal union or assumption without the [communication of idioms]{g:communicacio-ydiomatum} of the assumed [nature of the species]{g:natura-specifica} to the Word, or to the [person]{g:persona} assuming: for what is assumed [hypostatically]{g:ypostatice} must be terminated by an alien [suppositation]{g:suppositacio}, and not be a [supposit]{g:suppositum} of its own species, [57] as the Doctor also says.[^16] Third, the comparison seems irrelevant, since that [nature]{g:natura} does not depend on the humanity, since it remains naturally without it, and could have been assumed without the assumption of the humanity. For if the Word could purely assume quantity, much more evidently [could he assume] dead flesh, which is possible by itself by nature.[^17]

### 04-c ¶1 · 57.7–58.12  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 57] Item pari³ evidencia, qua Christus habet corpus mortuum tanquam partem suam pro illo triduo, habet quamlibet partem illius corporis partem suam, ut latus, caput,⁴ et pedes,⁴ sicut scriptura sacra patenter asserit. **Sed** concesso consequente, cum non possunt poni nisi partes quantitive Christi, cum sint partes quantitative corporis, sequitur quod sunt partes quantitative Christi. Et cum omnis pars quantitativa rei componit ut sic quantitative⁵ omne totum cuius est pars, sequitur quod dicte partes quantitative componunt Christum quantitative,⁶ et per consequens sic componunt,⁷ ut sit maius⁸ sua parte quantitativa. Sequitur quod Christus sit⁹ pro triduo mole magnus, ut puta septipedalis, in tumulo, cum ibi habeat partes extra partes: et cum nichil sit quantum nisi substancia materialis, sequitur quod Christus fuit pro dicto triduo substancia corporea; et per hoc est verisimile¹⁰ Doctorem debere concedere, cum dicit quod Christus potest denominari pro illo triduo homo mortuus a tota natura, cui discrete copulatur. Et abreviator suus Cowtonus¹¹ dicit quod fuit pro illo triduo homo mortuus¹² sed¹³ non homo. **Sed** videtur michi quod si quis est homo mortuus,¹² tunc est animal et corpus mortuum¹⁴ et sic corpus, quamvis enim mortuum¹⁴ per se circumlocutum cum verbo equivaleat verbo ampliativo. Ita¹⁵ quod non sequitur—ista bestia nunc est mortua; ergo illa nunc est—Tamen si ista bestia nunc sit¹⁶ bestia mortua, ista nunc est bestia, que moritur [p. 58] aut¹ moriebatur. Si ergo res sit mortua secundum partem quantitativam ut² manum aridam³ vivente residuo, quanto magis erit mortua per totum, si quelibet pars quantitativa sui sit mortua? Patet ergo quod si Christus unquam⁴ fuit mortuus,⁵ hoc fuit in triduo, et indubie tunc fuit persona mortua: et cum oportet dare⁶ naturam unitam, secundum quam passus est mortem, que natura subiectet mortem; patet quod tunc fuit corpus sibi unitum sicut subtiliter⁷ dicit Doctor. Non enim fuisset mortuus, si pro primo instanti non-actuacionis corporis post vitam Christi tam corpus quam anima adnichilata⁸ fuerunt; quia tunc omnis mors esset adnichilacio.⁸

ENGLISH: Again, by the equal [evidence]{g:evidencia} by which Christ has the dead body as his part for those [three days]{g:triduum}, he has any part of that body as his part, as the side, the head, and the feet, as holy scripture plainly asserts.[^1] Now, the consequent granted, since they cannot be posited as anything but quantitative parts of Christ, since they are [quantitative parts]{g:pars-quantitativa} of the body, [it follows]{g:sequitur} that they are quantitative parts of Christ. And since every quantitative part of a thing, as such, composes quantitatively every whole of which it is a part, it follows that the said quantitative parts compose Christ quantitatively, and in consequence so compose [him] that he is greater than his quantitative part. It follows that Christ is for the three days great in bulk, say seven feet long, in the tomb, since there he has parts outside parts: and since nothing is [quantified]{g:quantus} but a material substance, it follows that Christ was for the said three days a corporeal substance; and by this it is likely that the Doctor must grant [it], since he says that Christ can be named for those three days "a dead man" from the whole [nature]{g:natura} to which he is discretely joined. And his abbreviator Cowton says that he was for those three days a dead man but not a man.[^2] But it seems to me that if anyone is a dead man, then he is an animal and a dead body, and so a body; for although "dead", circumlocuted by itself with a verb, is equivalent to an [ampliative]{g:ampliativus} verb, so that it does not follow, "this beast is now dead; therefore it now is", yet if this beast now is a dead beast, this now is a beast which is dying [58] or was dying.[^3] If, therefore, a thing is dead as to a quantitative part or a withered hand, the rest living, how much more will it be dead throughout, if every quantitative part of it is dead?[^4] It is clear, therefore, that if Christ was ever dead, this was in the three days, and without doubt he was then a dead [person]{g:persona}: and since one must posit a united nature as to which he suffered death, a nature that is the subject of death, it is clear that the body was then united to him, as the Doctor subtly says. For he would not have been dead if, for the first instant of the body's non-actuation after Christ's life, both body and soul had been annihilated; for then every death would be annihilation.[^5]

### 04-c ¶2 · 58.13–59.20  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 58] Item omnis anima, in quantum huiusmodi,⁹ animat, ut patet 2° De Anima. ### 04-d ¶3 · 63.5–64.15  (2 sed/autem)
