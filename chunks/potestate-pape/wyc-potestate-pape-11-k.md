# *De potestate pape*, chapter 11, part 11: sin profits the church by accident; simple priests supply when prelates fail

## ¶1 · 303.1–304.3

> [p. 303] Hic videtur michi quod prima conclusio concedi debeat, cum totus numerus prescitorum, ymo omne peccatum, prosit per accidens sancte ecclesie. Nam Rom. VIII, 28 dicitur: Scimus quoniam diligentibus Deum omnia cooperantur in bonum. Unde solet esse distinccio quomodo aliqua prosunt per se directe et occasione data ad beatitudinem sanctorum, sicut bona virtutis et gracie, aliqua autem prosunt per accidens indirecte vel occasione ex gracia Dei accepta, cuiusmodi sunt peccata; et sic loquitur beatus Gregorius vocans felicem culpam, ymo Augustinus dicit quod expedit multos incidisse in multa peccata enormia, ut exemplificat de Petro. Et patet quod non est color quod si res prosit ecclesie tunc non est destruenda; oportet enim peccatum prodesse ecclesie, sed talia per accidens prosunt, sic quod illis ablatis non minus aliunde insurgeret profectus ecclesie, quia aliter quilibet foveret peccatum, ymo ageret, ut dampnetur, quia tunc Deus preter istos dampnatos produceret tantam ecclesiam quantam eternaliter diffinivit, cum nullum peccatum potest mutare divinum propositum vel tollere gloriam spirituum beatorum; et isti dampnati cederent beatis ad gaudium, quo aliter caruissent, ut deductum est in materia de necessitate futurorum. Ymo videtur quod omne peccatum tantum prodest ad salutem ecclesie, sicut totum meritum Christi, quia omne peccatum facit unite totum profectum ecclesie, cum aliter passio Christi infinitum plus prodesset ecclesie quam peccatum vel meritum membri Christi, et cum [p. 304] meritum Christi tantum finite prodest ecclesie, videtur quod omne aliud meritum proderit sub non gradu.

Here it seems to me that the first [conclusion]{g:conclusio} ought to be granted, since the whole number of [the foreknown]{g:prescitus}, indeed every sin, profits holy [church]{g:ecclesia} by accident. For in Rom. 8:28 it is said: "We know that to them that love God all things work together unto good."[^1] Hence there is a customary distinction, how some things profit of themselves, directly and by occasion given, toward the blessedness of the saints, as the goods of virtue and of grace, while others profit by accident, indirectly or by occasion taken from the grace of God, of which kind are sins; and so blessed Gregory speaks when he calls it a "happy fault"; indeed Augustine says that "it was expedient for many to have fallen into many enormous sins, as he exemplifies in Peter."[^2] And it is clear that there is no [colour]{g:color} [for arguing] that if a thing profits the church, then it is not to be destroyed;[^3] for sin must profit the church, but such things profit by accident, so that, were they taken away, the advancement of the church would arise no less from elsewhere; for otherwise anyone would foster sin, indeed would act so as to be damned; for then God, besides those damned, would produce as great a church as He has eternally defined, since no sin can change the divine purpose or take away the glory of the blessed spirits; and those damned would yield to the blessed a joy which they would otherwise have lacked, as has been deduced in the matter of the necessity of future things.[^4] Indeed it seems that every sin profits as much toward the salvation of the church as the whole merit of Christ, since every sin makes, as one, the whole advancement of the church; since otherwise the passion of Christ would profit the church infinitely more than a sin or the merit of a [member]{g:membrum} of Christ; and since [4] the merit of Christ profits the church only finitely, it seems that every other merit will profit under no degree.[^5]

## ¶2 · 304.4–304.18

> [p. 304] Et ex istis videtur quod dicentes omnem hominem sumi de corpore pro tempore quo magis prodesset ecclesie, periculose sompniant, quia vel posset universalis ecclesia esse perfeccior quam ista erit de facto vel non. Si sic, concedendum foret consequenter quod mors cuiuscumque presciti posset plus prodesse ecclesie et per consequens non; si alias vel aliter fuisset mortuus; minus prodesset ecclesie, quia sic omne factum bonum vel malum actum esset ad maturiorem profectum ecclesie. Si non; tunc consequenter concedendum foret quod omnia que evenient absolute necessario evenirent, cum contingencia, si ponatur, eque respicit omne opus Dei vel aliquod quo ad ipsum.

And from these it seems that those who say that every man is taken from the body at the time at which he would most profit the church are dreaming dangerously; for either the universal church could be more perfect than it will in fact be, or not. If so, it would consequently have to be granted that the death of any foreknown man could profit the church more, and consequently not [at the time of most profit]; if he had died at another time or in another way, it would profit the church less, because so every deed, good or evil, done would be for the riper advancement of the church.[^6] If not, then it would consequently have to be granted that "all things that will come about would come about absolutely of necessity", since contingency, if it is posited, bears equally on every work of God, or on any one, as regards Him.

## ¶3 · 304.19–305.6

> [p. 304] Dimissis autem istis stulticiis conceditur quod omne peccatum facit ad utilitatem et honorem ecclesie, non tamen est propterea faciendum, quia cum caritas incipit a se ipsa; nemo faceret aliquid nisi quod sibi proderit et sancte ecclesie. Sed quid prodest homini, quod sic peccat? Non enim sequitur: Hoc peccatum per accidens infert bonum homini, quia penam et alia; igitur sibi prodest; quia illo subducto et quocumque tam malo sequitur quod melius sibi esset. Ideo oportet concedere quod omne peccatum presciti obest sibi; et non prodest; licet prosit ecclesie. Nec oportet, si homo debet velle in casu damnacionem suam ad profectum ecclesie, quod propterea debet velle peccare, ut patet alibi. Unde videtur quod omnia tantum et ad tantum [p. 305] prosunt ecclesie ut aliud, licet dispariter. Deus enim utitur qualibet creatura ut organo ad profectum ecclesie, eciam ad proficiendum omnibus et singulis membris ecclesie, in quocumque profectu signabili, sicut multitudo trahencium trahit navem.

And, these follies set aside, it is granted that every sin makes for the usefulness and honour of the church; yet it is not on that account to be done, because, since [charity]{g:caritas} begins from itself, no one should do anything except what will profit himself and holy church. But what does it profit a man that he sins so? For it does not [follow]{g:sequitur}: "This sin by accident brings good to the man, because punishment and other things; therefore it profits him"; because, that [sin] withdrawn, and whatever is as evil, it follows that it would be better for him.[^7] Therefore one must grant that every sin of a foreknown man harms him and does not profit him, although it profits the church. Nor, if a man ought in a [given] case to will his own damnation for the advancement of the church, must he on that account will to sin, as is clear elsewhere. Hence it seems that all things profit the church as much and toward as much [5] as any other, although unequally. For God uses every creature as an instrument for the advancement of the church, even for profiting all and each of the members of the church, in every [signifiable]{g:signabile} advancement, as a multitude of men hauling hauls a ship.

## ¶4 · 305.7–305.18

> [p. 305] Tres autem gradus sunt notabiles habentes modos disparis racionis. Christus enim prodest fontaliter, predestinati directe parcialiter et peccata indirecte occasionaliter. Et patet quod multi heretici prosunt multum ecclesie et tamen propter sua opera sunt culpandi, ut proponens in casu intoxicare hominem infert sibi per accidens sanitatem. Ideo docet Magister quod in merito est simplicitas oculi principaliter attendenda. Et tota laus est architectonice autori omnium attribuenda. Ipse enim necessitat peccatum ad proficiendum ecclesie.

And there are three notable degrees, having modes of unlike kind. For Christ profits as the fount, [the predestinate]{g:predestinatus} directly and in part, and sins indirectly, [by occasion]{g:occasionaliter}. And it is clear that many heretics profit the church much and yet are to be blamed for their works, as one who purposes in some case to poison a man brings him health by accident. Therefore the Master teaches that in merit the singleness of the eye is chiefly to be looked to.[^8] And all praise is to be attributed to the author of all, as master builder. For He necessitates sin to profit the church.

## ¶5 · 305.19–306.18

> [p. 305] Ulterius dicitur quod non est color, si nescitur quibus prodesset dotacionis vel peccati destruccio, igitur non debet intendi, cum sufficit pro natura intencio honoris Dei. Et prudens reputacio utilitatis Christi quod de facili contingeret de ablacione temporalium, non intencione puniendi vel deteriorandi ecclesiam vel aliquod eius membrum, sed intencione exonerandi vel prodessendi mundo oppressis et perfeccionem status, quem Christus instituit renovandi. Hoc enim debet facere in lege veteri eciam extrahendo iumentum de puteo et exemplificat Augustinus, ut recitatur V questio V: Non omnis, quomodo ex caritate freneticus privandus est gladio et litargicus excitandus a sompno, et si expectaremus in istis revelacionem [p. 306] vel demonstracionem gignentem scienciam, raro vel nunquam faceremus bonum opus extrinsecum, ut recitat decretum ex declaracione Augustini XXIII questio V: De occidendis. Et sic nec medicus corporalis nec medicus spiritualis umquam apponeret medicinam. In talibus igitur innitendum est arti scripture, et tunc Dominus nedum habebit nos excusatos, sed subducta invidia premiatos, eciam licet dampnum multis eveniat, sicut sepe contigit ex predicacione Christi. Nec sequitur ex isto, quod quilibet debet spoliare proximum occupantem superflua, quia aliis ut sacerdotibus limitatur sermo sancte exhortacionis, aliis ut secularibus dominis limitatur sermo severe monicionis, et aliis ut stulte dotantibus limitatur in aliorum defectu operacio prudentis ablacionis; nam omnis homo debet modo suo prodesse ecclesie.

Further, [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that there is no colour [in this]: "If it is not known whom the destruction of the [endowment]{g:dotacio} or of sin would profit, therefore it ought not to be intended"; since for [our] nature the intention of God's honour suffices.[^9] And [there suffices] a prudent estimation of the advantage of Christ which would easily come about from the [taking-away]{g:ablacio} of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, not with the intention of punishing or worsening the church or any member of it, but with the intention of relieving or profiting those oppressed by the world, and of renewing the perfection of the state which Christ instituted. For one ought to do this, as even in the old law by drawing a beast out of a pit;[^10] and Augustine gives the example, as is recited in [C.] 5 q. 5 [c.] *Non omnis*, how out of charity "a frenzied man is to be deprived of his sword and a lethargic man roused from sleep";[^11] and if in these matters we waited for a revelation [6] or a demonstration begetting knowledge, rarely or never would we do any good outward work, as the decree recites from Augustine's declaration, [C.] 23 q. 5 [c.] *De occidendis*.[^12] And so neither the bodily physician nor the spiritual physician would ever apply medicine. In such matters, therefore, one must rely on the art of scripture, and then the Lord will hold us not only excused but, ill will set aside, rewarded, even though harm befall many, as often happened from the preaching of Christ. Nor does it follow from this that anyone ought to despoil a neighbour who occupies superfluous goods; for to some, as to priests, is [assigned]{g:limitare} the word of holy exhortation; to others, as to [secular lords]{g:seculares-domini}, is assigned the word of stern admonition; and to others, as to those who foolishly endow, is assigned, where the others fail, the work of prudent taking-away; for every man ought in his own way to profit the church.

## ¶6 · 306.19–306.25

> [p. 306] Ulterius notandum finaliter quod deficiente quocumque papa cesareo vel quocumque alio eius prelato usque ad simplicem sacerdotem ipse sufficeret ministrare qualiacumque sacramenta in scriptura sacra fundabilia, vel eciam sacramentalia que post dotacionem ecclesie licite sunt adiecta.

Further, it is to be noted finally that, failing any [caesarean pope]{g:papa-cesareus} whatever, or any other [prelate]{g:prelatus} of his whatever down to the simple [priest]{g:sacerdos}, he [the simple priest] would suffice to minister any sacraments whatever that can be founded in holy scripture, or even the [sacramentals]{g:sacramentalia} that were lawfully added after the endowment of the church.[^13]

## ¶7 · 306.26–306.33

> [p. 306] Probatur primo ex hoc quod sacramenta et ritus, quos Deus singulariter summo sacerdoti instituit, potuit et debuit inferior sacerdos in defectu summi sacerdotis perficere, ut patet ex dictis. Sed longe minoris auctoritatis sunt tradiciones humane post dotacionem in istis adiecte, igitur multo magis ipsas sufficit supplere inferior sacerdos.

It is proved first from this, that the sacraments and [rites]{g:ritus} which God instituted singularly for the high priest, a lower priest could and ought to perform in default of the high priest, as is clear from what has been said.[^14] Now the human traditions added in these matters after the endowment are of far lesser [authority]{g:auctoritas}; therefore much more does a lower priest suffice to supply them.

## ¶8 · 307.1–307.10

> [p. 307] Item, ut patet ex dictis et testimonio domini Ardmacani, omnes potestates ordinis sunt equales. nec minuitur eciam potestatis iurisdicionis execucio, nisi de quanto racionabiliter est restricta. Sed deficiente quocumque superiori pontifice et imminente necessitate ad aliquod necessarium sacerdotale ministerium, non superesset, qui racionabiliter ita restringeret, ergo de communi lege Dei liceret inferiori presbitero in casu posito perficere ministerium assignatum.

Again, as is clear from what has been said and from the testimony of [the lord of Armagh]{g:ardmacanus}, all [powers of order]{g:potestas-ordinis} are equal;[^15] nor is even the execution of the [power of jurisdiction]{g:potestas-iurisdiccionis} diminished, except so far as it is reasonably restricted. Now, failing any superior pontiff whatever, and with necessity pressing for some needed priestly ministry, there would remain no one who could reasonably so restrict it; therefore by the common [law of God]{g:lex-dei} it would be lawful for a lower [presbyter]{g:presbiter} in the case posited to perform the ministry assigned.

## ¶9 · 307.11–307.28

> [p. 307] Confirmatur tercio per hoc quod ex fide scripture Christus assisteret sue ecclesie usque ad finem seculi, sed irracionalis et increpandus foret; si exigeret a clero suo officium et assistente potestate presbiteri ad faciendum hoc impediret ipsum presbiterum; ad supplendum igitur ex plenitudine potestatis Christi simplex sacerdos in tali casu haberet potenciam ac sufficienciam ad ministerium tam necessarium dispensandum. Sic enim propter necessitatem baptismi sufficit laycus in defectu cleri baptizare. Sic eciam sufficit quicumque sacerdos simplex eque plene cum papa absolvere in mortis articulo, quare igitur non eque sufficit deficiente superiori prelato ineque necessario sacramento? Leges enim vel constituciones humane non obviant legi Dei nec sunt credende nisi de quanto consonant racioni.

It is confirmed thirdly by this, that [from the faith of scripture]{g:ex-fide} Christ would stand by His church until the end of the world;[^16] now He would be unreasonable and to be rebuked if He required the office from His [clergy]{g:clerus} and, while the [power]{g:potestas} of the presbyter stood by to do this, hindered that presbyter himself; therefore, to supply [the want], out of the [plenitude of power]{g:plenitudo-potestatis} of Christ, a simple priest in such a case would have the [power]{g:potencia} and sufficiency to dispense so necessary a ministry.[^17] For so, because of the necessity of baptism, a layman suffices to baptize in default of the clergy. So also any simple priest whatever suffices, as fully as the [pope]{g:papa}, to absolve at the point of death; why then does he not equally suffice, the superior prelate failing, for a sacrament not equally necessary?[^18] For human laws or constitutions do not oppose the law of God, nor are they to be believed except so far as they accord with reason.

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** Rom. 8:28. Wyclif omits the Vulgate's *autem* ("And we know"); the rest is the Douay wording. Both manuscripts note in the margin here (303.4) *Prodesse dupliciter capitur*, "to profit is taken in two ways", and at 303.16 *Peccatum prodest ecclesie multum*.
[^2]: **S.** "Happy fault" (*felix culpa*) is the Easter *Exsultet*, which Wyclif ascribes to Gregory; Augustine's saying on Peter's fall is *De civitate Dei* 14.13 ("I dare say that it is useful for the proud to fall into some open and manifest sin … Peter was more wholesomely displeased with himself when he wept than pleased with himself when he presumed"), here paraphrased; Wyclif quotes it in full in *De ecclesia* p. 164. Loserth compares Wyclif's own *De ecclesia* p. 164, *Sermones* II 152 and *Opus evangelicum* III 198 (LEDGER P11k-2). The quotation bounds follow Loserth's italics, which run on through *ut exemplificat de Petro*.
[^3]: **C.** *Color*: a plausible ground for an argument, as in "colour of title". The argument rejected is the defence of the endowment: if it profits the church, it may not be taken away.
[^4]: **C.** The second *quia tunc* ("for then God …") gives the reason why sins profit only by accident: without them God would still bring His church to its full predestined number. It could also be read as the absurd consequence of the preceding *quia aliter*. "The necessity of future things" is the scholastic topic of future contingents; Wyclif treats it at length in *De dominio divino* I.14–19, and a tract *De necessitate futurorum* is listed among his lost works; the place of this deduction has not been found (LEDGER P11k-3). **T.** For *deductum* (303.26) the manuscripts read *reductum*; we translate the print.
[^5]: **C.** *Sub non gradu*, "under no degree": every other merit profits the church not by a lesser degree than Christ's, since Christ's own profits it only finitely. The phrase is compressed and the construal is ours.
[^6]: **C.** The semicolons are the print's. We take *et per consequens non* as "and so [the man is] not [taken at the time of greatest profit]", and *si alias … ecclesie* as the opponents' claim restated, refuted by *quia sic …*: on their view every act would serve the church's "riper" (*maturiorem*) advancement. The italics at 304.16 ("all things that will come about …") mark a stock formula of the debate on necessity.
[^7]: **C.** *Quia penam et alia*, "because [it brings] punishment and other things": the sin brings the man a good (just punishment) by accident. *Quocumque tam malo*: "and any other thing as evil" withdrawn with it.
[^8]: **S.** The Master of the *Sentences*, Peter Lombard, on intention in merit (*Sent.* II d. 38–40, chiefly d. 40 c. 1: acts are good "which accompany a good cause and intention"); the image of the single eye is Wyclif's, not Lombard's; "singleness of the eye" is Matt. 6:22 (DR "If thy eye be single").
[^9]: **C.** *Pro natura*: "for [human] nature", as much as a creature can do; the opposed view would require knowing the outcome. The colon marks the rejected argument as a quotation; Loserth does not italicise it. **R.** Loserth's side-note reads the passage as about disendowment.
[^10]: **S.** No quotation: cf. Deut. 22:4 (the fallen ass or ox) and Luke 14:5 (DR "fall into a pit and will not immediately draw him out").
[^11]: **S.** *Decretum* C. 5 q. 5 c. 2 *Non omnis* (form ours), from Augustine's letter to Vincentius (Ep. 93.2.4). Loserth gives no reference here. The canon reads "he who binds the frenzied and he who rouses the lethargic, troublesome to both, loves both"; the sword is Wyclif's paraphrase.
[^12]: **S.** *Decretum* C. 23 q. 5 c. 8 *De occidendis* (Loserth "Cap. VIII", 306.4; modern form ours), from Augustine's letter to Publicola (Ep. 47.5): what we do for a good and lawful end is not imputed to us if harm follows against our will.
[^13]: **R.** Both manuscripts note in the margin (306.19–23): *Sacerdos simplex deficiente papa cesario vel alio prelato*; B alone adds *potest sufficienter ministrare omnia sacramenta*, "can sufficiently minister all the sacraments".
[^14]: **S.** The "high priest" (*summus sacerdos*) is the Aaronic priest of the old law, treated earlier in this chapter: at 275.1–276.23 Wyclif argues that the lower priest supplied the high priest's ministry when he was ill or unclean (cf. 265.18 ff.).
[^15]: **S.** FitzRalph, *Summa in quaestionibus Armenorum*, XI.6 (Paris 1512; the passage Wyclif cited at 275.1 as 'end of ch. 5'), on the equality of the powers of order: every priest at ordination receives the whole sacramental power or none, and in the old law the power of order was equal in the high priest and the others; Loserth gives no place.
[^16]: **S.** Matt. 28:20, not quoted: DR "behold I am with you all days, even to the consummation of the world".
[^17]: **C.** *Potencia* here is ordinary power (CONTEXTUAL row), set beside *potestas* in the same sentence; Wyclif does not distinguish them. The *si*-clause is compressed: Christ would be unreasonable to demand the office and hinder the priest whose power is there to perform it.
[^18]: **T.** For *sufficit* (307.21–22) B reads *sufficiat*, without change of sense. Both manuscripts note here *Ministrare sacramenta deficiente papa quis potest*. *Ineque necessario*: "not equally necessary", that is, less necessary than absolution at death.
