# *De ecclesia*, chapter 1, part 3: Christ the head; the church is one, in three parts

## ¶1 · 6.16–10.15

> [p. 6] Unde sicut Deus iuravit David, quod de fructu ventris sui (scilicet Christum, qui fuit natus de ventre virginis, et per consequens, de illo homine manu forti) poneret super sedem suam: ita iuravit filiis suis, sub condicione tamen, si custodierint testamentum et testimonia Christi. Ne inquit Augustinus aliquis minaretur promissis Dei et in potestate sua ponat quod promisit Deus, ut impleretur, ideo iuravit, annectens condicionem, ne iam glorieris de promissis et dimittas te non custodire; tunc eris filius David, si custodieris: si autem non, non eris filius David. Filiis David promisit Deus; noli dicere filius sum David, si degeneras; sola vidua sedebit in ecclesia benedicta. Tota ecclesia una vidua est, cuius membra sunt pauperes saturati, quia deserti sunt in hoc seculo, quod non desponsant hereditarie sed relinquunt, non sperant in diviciis honoribus vel amicis, sed si habent contempnunt sperando pure in domino. [p. 7] Quid igitur valet dicere quod solum isti qui sunt Rome sunt ecclesia vel caput ecclesie? Tales enim uruntur luna per noctem, ut exponit Augustinus psalmo CXX°. Quisquis inquit putaverit ecclesiam in una parte esse et non eam cognoverit diffusam toto orbe terrarum et crediderit eis qui dicunt: Ecce hic est Christus et ecce illic, sicut loquitur evangelium Mathei XXIV°, cum ille totum orbem terrarum emerit quoad valorem precii et ille in proximo scandalizatur et uritur a luna. Ad quid igitur oportet contendere circa caput ecclesie? Ipsa inquit Augustinus caro Christi est caput ecclesie, id est, humanitas secundum quam Christus predestinatus est, ut dicit fides scripture Ephes. I°, IV° et V° capitulis et expressius Col. 1°, 18, dicit quod Christus est caput tocius ecclesie. Et istam conclusionem declarat bene dicta decretalis extravagans Bonifacii VIII que sic incipit: Unam sanctam. Igitur inquit ecclesie unius unum corpus, unum caput, non duo capita quasi monstrum. Secunda conclusio sequens ex quiditate matris ecclesie est quod tantum est una, sic quod non multe ecclesie catholice. Demonstratur sic: Eo ipso quod est ecclesia universalis sive catholica, ipsa continet in se omnes predestinatos. Non est possibile quod sit nisi una talis, igitur non est possibile quod sit nisi una universalis ecclesia. Nam secundum philosophos universale est quoddam totum atque perfectum cui [p. 8] nichil deest. Unde sicut iuxta Aristotelem primo de celo omnia primo super tria ponimus: sic non dicimus ecclesiam catholicam nisi que in se continet ista tria: partem in celo triumphantem, partem in purgatorio dormientem et partem in terris militantem. Et in figuram illius dicunt doctores hostiam vel sacramentum eucaristie dividi in tres partes: Primam immersam sacramento liquido dicunt signare ecclesiam triumphantem, que insorpta et inebriata est intuicione divine essencie, ut loquitur caput ecclesie Cantic. V° 1, exhilarans cives et commensales suos. Inebriamini inquit carissimi. Due autem alie partes in manu domini et merito ecclesie expurgande signantur per illas duas partes quas sacerdos tenet in manibus: Maior supposita signat ecclesiam militantem et minor innixa supposite signat ecclesiam in purgatorio expectantem. Ipsa enim innititur suffragiis militantis ecclesie. Et pro istis duabus partibus geminamus preces agno qui est caput ecclesie, ut misereatur nobis. Sed pro tercia parte ad cuius locum et requiem aspiramus petimus, ut idem agnus triplicis nature donet finaliter nobis pacem. Et hinc (ut ostendi alias) dictus sponsus secundum humanitatem suam visitavit tria loca ecclesie, scilicet umbilicum nostre habilitabilis, triginta tribus annis in Judea et Jerusalem conversando, limbum in quo patres purgati sunt, morsellum sue ecclesie in anima extrahendo; sed tercio finaliter ascendens in celum cepit captivitatem quam post triumphum coronavit ad dei dexteram collocando. [p. 9] Ista ergo est triplex pars unius universalis ecclesie sive catholice, licet quotlibet sint particulares ecclesie, quia tot quot sunt homines predestinati. Et hinc credo quod in simbolo ecclesie confitentur se credere unam sanctam catholicam et apostolicam ecclesiam. Apostoli enim sunt modo in celo in eadem personalitate qua vixerunt in terris, et sunt modo partes plene purgate eiusdem matris ecclesie, auctoritate quorum sui vicarii iam regunt adolescentulam modo querentem sponsum ecclesie; et sic loquitur decretum XXIV, q. I. Manet inquit Leo papa Petri privilegium ubicunque ex ipsius equitate fertur iudicium. Ipse enim residet in celis videns et procurans quod deus liget et solvat. Unde supradicta decretalis sic loquitur: Unam sanctam ecclesiam catholicam et ipsam apostolicam urgente fide credere cogimur et tenere. Unde unitatem illius ecclesie probat ex scriptura multiplici, primo per illud Canticorum VI°, 8: Una est columba mea, perfecta mea: una est matri sue, electa genitrici sue. Ista columba secundum concordem sanctorum sentenciam est mater ecclesia, cuius mater, quia creatrix est trinitas vel divina essencia, cuius genitrix et fotrix est patris sapiencia et ipsa mater virgo et sponsa humanitatis Christi sui capitis. Ex quibus parentibus spiritualiter, et per consequens, gignicione virginea filii ecclesie procreantur, iuxta quod loquitur apostolus 2ª Cor. XI°, 2, Despondi vos uni viro virginem castam exhibere Christo. Cum ergo in tota scriptura non ponitur vel una sillaba sine sensu, non restat quid pertinencius signaret hoc adiectum una [p. 10] in libro sapientissimi Salomonis nisi unitatem ecclesie catholice excludentem pluralitatem. Unde spiritus sanctus in patre Salomonis David sic orat in Christo psalmo XXI°, 21, Erue a framea Deus animam meam et de manu canis unicam meam. Quem textum secundo per decretalem allegatam beatus Augustinus sic exponit tractatu secundo eiusdem psalmi: Framea inquit gladius est, et per gladium mortem intelligi voluit. Unicam meam dixit ecclesiam. Canes sunt qui ceco more latrant nec intelligunt contra quos, eciam cum nichil eis fit, latrant contra viantes, licet velint eis proficere. Et utinam nostri possessionati non sint tales qui conspirant contra catholicos, tangentes eorum potestatem, fastum et ablacionem temporalium solummodo ad medelam.

Hence, just as God swore to David that of the fruit of his womb (namely Christ, who was born of the
womb of the virgin, and consequently of that man "strong of hand") he would set [one] upon his
seat,[^1] so he swore to his sons, yet under a condition: if they keep the testament and the
testimonies of Christ. "Lest anyone", says Augustine, "should threaten on the strength of God's
promises, and put in his own power what God promised, that it should be fulfilled, therefore he
swore, attaching a condition: lest you now glory in the promises and let yourself off keeping [them].
Then you will be a son of David, if you keep [them]; but if not, you will not be a son of David."[^2]
To the sons of David God made the promise; do not say "I am a son of David" if you are degenerate.
Only the widow will sit, blessed, in the [church]{g:ecclesia}.[^3] The whole church is one widow,
whose members are the poor who are filled, because they are forsaken in this world, which they do not
wed as an inheritance but leave; they do not hope in riches, honours or friends, but if they have
them they despise them, hoping purely in the Lord. [7] What then is it worth to say that only those
who are at Rome are the church, or the head of the church? For such are "burned by the moon by
night", as Augustine expounds on Psalm 120.[^4] "Whoever", he says, "thinks that the church is in one
part, and does not know her as spread through the whole world, and believes those who say 'Lo here
is Christ, and lo there', as the gospel of Matthew 24 speaks, although he bought the whole world as
regards the value of the price, he is scandalized in his neighbour and is burned by the moon."[^5] To
what end, then, must there be contention about the head of the church? "The very flesh of Christ",
says Augustine, "is the head of the church", that is, the humanity according to which Christ was
predestinated, as the faith of scripture says in Ephesians chapters 1, 4 and 5; and more expressly
Colossians 1:18 says that Christ is head of the whole church.[^6] And this conclusion is well set out
by the decretal extravagant of Boniface VIII which begins thus: *Unam sanctam*. "Therefore", it says,
"of the one church [there is] one body, one head, not two heads like a monster."[^7]

The second conclusion, following from the [quiddity]{g:quiditas} of mother church, is that she is
only one, so that there are not many catholic churches. It is demonstrated thus. By the very fact that
she is the universal or catholic church, she contains in herself all the
[predestinate]{g:predestinatus}. It is not possible that there be more than one such; therefore it is
not possible that there be more than one universal church.[^8] For according to the philosophers the
[universal]{g:universale} is a certain whole and perfect thing, from which [8] nothing is lacking.
Hence, just as according to Aristotle in *On the Heavens* I we first apply "all" to three, so we do
not call [a church] catholic unless it contains in itself these three: the part triumphant in heaven,
the part sleeping in purgatory, and the part militant on earth.[^9] And in figure of this the doctors
say that the host, or sacrament of the eucharist, is divided into three parts. The first,
immersed in the liquid sacrament, they say signifies the church triumphant, which is absorbed and
inebriated with the vision of the divine essence, as the head of the church speaks in the Canticle
5:1, gladdening his fellow citizens and table-companions: "Be inebriated", he says, "my dearly
beloved."[^10] And the other two parts, which are to be purged in the hand of the Lord and by the
merit of the church, are signified by those two parts which the priest holds in his hands: the larger,
placed beneath, signifies the church militant, and the smaller, resting on the one beneath, signifies
the church in purgatory, waiting. For it rests on the [suffrages]{g:suffragia} of the church militant.
And for these two parts we double our prayers to the Lamb, who is the head of the church, that he may
have mercy on us. But for the third part, to whose place and rest we aspire, we ask that the same Lamb
of threefold nature may finally grant us peace.[^11] And hence (as I have shown elsewhere) the said
[bridegroom]{g:sponsus}, according to his humanity, visited three places of the church: namely, the
navel of our habitable [world], living thirty-three years in Judea and Jerusalem; limbo, in which the
fathers were purged, drawing out a morsel of his church in his soul; but third, finally ascending
into heaven, he took captivity captive, which after the triumph he crowned, placing it at the right
hand of God.[^12] [9] This, then, is the threefold part of the one universal or catholic church,
although there are any number of particular churches, for there are as many as there are predestinate
men.[^13] And hence I believe that in the creed of the church they confess that they believe "one holy
catholic and apostolic church".[^14] For the apostles are now in heaven in the same personality in
which they lived on earth, and are now fully purged parts of the same mother church; by their
authority their vicars now rule the young maiden who is now seeking the bridegroom of the church.[^15]
And so the decree speaks in [Causa] 24, q. 1: "Peter's privilege remains", says Pope Leo, "wherever
judgement is given in accordance with his equity."[^16] For he sits in heaven, seeing and procuring
that God binds and looses. Hence the aforesaid decretal speaks thus: "We are compelled, faith urging
us, to believe and to hold one holy catholic church, and that apostolic." Hence it proves the unity of
that church from manifold scripture: first by that [verse] of the Canticle 6:8: "One is my dove, my
perfect one is but one; she is the only one of her mother, the chosen of her that bore her."[^17] This
dove, according to the concordant [teaching]{g:sentencia} of the saints, is mother church: whose
mother, because creatress, is the Trinity or the divine essence; whose genetrix and nurse is the
Father's wisdom; and she herself is mother, virgin and [bride]{g:sponsa} of the humanity of Christ, her
head.[^18] From these parents, spiritually, and consequently by a virginal begetting, the sons of the
church are procreated, according to what the apostle says in 2 Corinthians 11:2: "I have espoused you
to one husband, that I may present you as a chaste virgin to Christ." Since, therefore, in the whole
of scripture not one syllable is set down without sense, nothing remains that this adjective "one"
[10] in the book of the most wise Solomon could signify more pertinently than the unity of the
catholic church, excluding plurality. Hence the Holy Spirit, in David the father of Solomon, prays
thus in Christ in Psalm 21:21: "Deliver, O God, my soul from the sword: my only one from the hand of
the dog." This text, which the decretal cites second, the blessed Augustine expounds thus in the second
treatise on the same psalm: "The sword", he says, "is a blade, and by the blade he meant death to be
understood. 'My only one' he called the church. Dogs are those who bark in blind fashion and do not
understand against whom; even when nothing is done to them, they bark against those on the road,
although these wish to do them good."[^19] And would that our [possessioners]{g:possessionati} were not
such as conspire against catholics who touch on their power, their pride and the removal of their
[temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, only for [their] healing.[^20]

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** Ps. 131:11–12 (Loserth), reported indirectly. DR "of the fruit of thy womb I will set
    upon thy throne. If thy children will keep my covenant, and these my testimonies…". Wyclif's
    *testimonia Christi* for Vg *testimonia mea*. *Sedem* is kept as "seat" to match *sedes* at 6.9.
    *Manu forti*: the etymology of "David" as "strong of hand" (Jerome, *Liber interpretationis
    hebraicorum nominum*, PL 23:847A, *David … manu fortis*; LEDGER E01c-1).

[^2]: **S/C.** Augustine, *Enarr. in Ps.* 131 (Loserth, *loc. cit.*; LEDGER E01b-2). *Minaretur
    promissis Dei*: "threaten on the strength of God's promises" (ablative of ground, as *ex … vicaria
    … minari*, 6.4). *Dimittas te non custodire*: "let yourself off keeping [them]". Where the
    quotation ends is not marked; we close it after the second *filius David*.

[^3]: **C/S.** *Sola vidua sedebit in ecclesia benedicta*: *benedicta* may agree with *vidua* (our
    reading, after Ps. 131:15 *viduam ejus benedicens benedicam*, DR "Blessing I will bless her
    widow") or with *ecclesia* ("in the blessed church"). *Pauperes saturati*: the same verse, "I will
    satisfy her poor with bread". *Tota ecclesia una vidua est* is Augustine's phrase on that verse
    (*Enarr. in Ps.* 131.23, PL 37:1726; LEDGER E01c-2; A's margin *Tota ecclesia est vidua*).

[^4]: **S.** Ps. 120:6, DR "nor the moon by night", which Augustine reads of those deceived about the
    church. A reads *CX*. Loserth: Aug. *Opp.* IV, p. 1381 (LEDGER E01c-3). The question mark: the
    print's *ecclesie.'* is read as a question.

[^5]: **S/C.** Matt. 24:23, DR "Lo here is Christ, or there"; Wyclif has *et ecce illic*. *Cum ille
    … emerit*: *ille* is Christ, the buyer of the whole world (*cum* concessive: the man is scandalized
    although Christ bought the world); the second *ille* is the man, and *et ille* opens the main
    clause. *In proximo*: "in his neighbour", taking offence at Christians outside his one part.

[^6]: **S.** *Ipsa caro Christi est caput ecclesie*: Augustine, *Enarr. in Ps.* 120.12, PL 37:1615,
    the passage of n. 4: *quia ipsa caro Christi caput Ecclesiae est* (LEDGER E01c-4). B's margin cites Eph. 1, 4, 5 and Col. 1; Col. 1:18 DR "And he is the head of the
    body, the church". *Fides scripture*: the faith of scripture (cf. GLOSSARY *ex fide*).

[^7]: **S/R.** Boniface VIII, *Unam sanctam* (1302): *Igitur ecclesiae unius et unicae unum corpus,
    unum caput, non duo capita, quasi monstrum* (Friedberg II, 1245–46; Wyclif omits *et unicae*). Loserth: *Extrav. comm.* 1.8.1 (our modern
    form of his "Lib. I. Tit. VIII … cap. I"; LEDGER E01c-5). A later hand in A₁'s margin objects:
    *Scias quia istam decretalem falsificat, nichil enim ibi de predestinatis*, "know that he falsifies
    this decretal, for there is nothing there about the predestinate; but the decretal proves that to
    be subject to the Roman pontiff is of necessity for salvation".

[^8]: **C.** The minor premiss carries no connective in the Latin: "such" (*talis*) is a church that
    contains all the predestinate, and there can be only one such whole.

[^9]: **S/G.** Aristotle, *De caelo* I.1, 268a: "all" is first said of three (of two we say "both").
    Loserth quotes the Greek (*to gar tria panta einai*) (LEDGER E01c-6). *Dormientem*: the church
    sleeping, the souls in purgatory (GLOSSARY §A, *ecclesia dormiens*). *Totum atque perfectum cui
    nichil deest*: Aristotle, *Physics* III.6, 207a8–10.

[^10]: **T/S.** *Insorpta*: Loserth's print; read as *absorpta*, "absorbed". *Inebriata*: Loserth's
    correction of the MSS' *debriata*. Cant. 5:1, DR "be inebriated, my dearly beloved". The triple
    fraction is the *Triforme est corpus Christi* tradition (Amalarius; Gratian, *De cons.* D.2 c.22),
    though Wyclif's assignment of the parts differs: Amalarius, *De eccl. off.* III.35 (PL 105:1154D–1155A), and Gratian, *De cons.* D.2 c.22 (PL 187:1740A), read them as the risen body, the body still walking on earth and the body lying in the tombs, where Wyclif makes the two parts held in the hands the church militant and the church in purgatory (LEDGER E01c-7).

[^11]: **C/R.** The Agnus Dei: *miserere nobis* twice, then *dona nobis pacem*; the two petitions
    for mercy go to the two parts still to be purged, the prayer for peace to the third. *Agnus
    triplicis nature*: the threefold nature is not explained; B's margin: *Christus dicitur agnus
    tripliciter*.

[^12]: **T/S.** *Habilitabilis*: as printed (B breaks off at *umbili-*); read as *habitabilis*,
    "the habitable [world]", whose navel is Jerusalem (Ezek. 38:12, Vg *umbilici terrae*).
    *Conversando*: "living" (cf. GLOSSARY *conversacio*). *Cepit captivitatem*: Eph. 4:8 (Ps. 67:19),
    DR "he led captivity captive". *Ut ostendi alias*: not identified.

[^13]: **C.** *Tot quot sunt homines predestinati*: each predestinate person is a particular church;
    the universal church is one whole of three parts.

[^14]: **T/S.** The Nicene–Constantinopolitan Creed. *In simbolo … confitentur*: Loserth's text; the
    MSS have *quo in simbolo*, and AA₁ *confitetur*.

[^15]: **C/S.** *Adolescentulam*: the church militant as the maiden of the Song (Cant. 1:2
    *adolescentulae*), seeking the bridegroom (Cant. 3:1–2). *Sui vicarii* are the apostles' vicars;
    their rule depends on *auctoritate quorum*, the apostles' authority.

[^16]: **S.** Gratian, *Decretum* C.24 q.1 c.5 (Loserth; LEDGER E01c-8), from Leo I, Sermon 4.3
    (PL 54:151A–B). *Ex ipsius equitate*: Peter's equity, not the judge's. The next sentence is Wyclif's:
    Peter "procures" that God binds and looses; the binding is God's.

[^17]: **S.** *Unam sanctam*, opening words (Friedberg II, 1245–46; LEDGER E01c-5). Cant. 6:8, DR; Wyclif's *matri
    sue* for Vg *matris suae*.

[^18]: **C.** Three relations: the Trinity is the church's *mater* as creatress; the Father's wisdom
    (the Son) her *genitrix et fotrix*; and the church herself is mother, virgin and bride of Christ's
    humanity. *Fotrix*: "nurse", one who cherishes.

[^19]: **S/C.** Augustine, *Enarr. in Ps.* 21, *enarratio* II (Loserth: *Opp.* IV, p. 98; LEDGER
    E01c-9). *Secundo per decretalem allegatam*: *Unam sanctam* cites Ps. 21:21 after Cant. 6:8.
    *Licet velint eis proficere*: we take the subject as the travellers (*viantes*); it could be the
    dogs ("although they mean to do them good").

[^20]: **C.** *Tangentes* agrees with *catholicos*: the catholics who touch on the possessioners'
    power, pride and the taking away of their temporal goods, *solummodo ad medelam*, only as a
    remedy. It could agree with the possessioners ("touching their power"), but the sentence then
    loses its point.
