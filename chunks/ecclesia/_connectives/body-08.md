# sed/autem extract 08: chunks 19-h–22-d

### 19-h ¶1 · 469.1–471.12  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 469] et iudiciis prime iusticie. Si enim secundum leges hominum iudex iudicans seculare dominium vel ecclesiasticum officium pro procreato extra matrimonium quem vocat filium populi et illegitimum, quantumcunque predestinatus et habilis fuerit quoad Deum, multo magis iudex iudicans pro prescito quem Deus decrevit inhabilem iniuste iudicat, quia contrarie iusticie legis Dei. Nec valet ficta excusacio qua dicitur quod iudex facit suam diligenciam iudicando secundum suam noticiam, et nisi forent talia iudicia, maius malum contingeret. Nam quoad primum constat quod in talibus ambiguis, utrum iniusta fuerint, debet homo dubium illud dimittere et partem securam eligere. Hoc enim docuerunt Christus et sui apostoli tam scripto quam opere. Et quantum ad secundam partem, patet quod non est peccandum vel venialiter propter aliquod bonum signabile acquirendum vel malum culpe aut pene persone vel perpetuo precavendum. Et ex istis patet veritas conclusionis beati Augustini Epistola XXXVIIª ad Bonifacium quod leges hominum quas sanctus vocat iura civilia communiter sunt iniuste. Nam supposito quod Petrus sit predestinatus, carnaliter illegitimus, vendicans hereditatem vel ecclesiasticum officium, et supposito quod Paulus prescitus sit legitimus, vendicans illud idem beneficium, Petrus tamen habilis et Paulus inhabilis quoad Deum, et sint duo iudices quorum primus iudicet pro Paulo secundum leges hominum allegatas et testificaciones legalis testimonii plane depositas et iudicet secundus iudex pro Petro ex revelacione qua noscit Deum iudicare Petrum habilem ad datum beneficium et Paulum inhabilem, [p. 470] et patet quod secundus iudex iudicat iustum iudicium, et per consequens primus iudicando sibi contrarie iudicat secundum faciem et iniustum iudicium; et talis perplexitas est communiter in humano iudicio. Et patet possibilitas casus ex sentencia Augustini super Johannis, Sermone XI°, super illud Johannis III°, 1: Erat homo ex phariseis, ubi notat scripturam Exodi III°, quare Deus signanter dicit: Ego sum Deus Abraham, Deus Isaac, cum Noe, Job, Moyses et alii multi erant patres veteres nominandi, et respondet quod in istis tribus patriarchis qui erant principia generis Hebreorum, Israelitarum et Judeorum figuratum est genus populi christiani. Nam illi tres habuerunt prolem de liberis et ancillis secundum divisionem quadruplicem; et sic est in populo christiano. Nam sicut Abraham genuit ex Sara libera Isaac bonum, sic ex Agar ancilla figurans malos (ut notat Apostolus Galatis IV°, 30) peperit Ysmahelem. Iterum Rebeca uxor Isaac nedum peperit Jacob bonum **sed** Esau malum tam nomine quam figura, et Jacob de liberis et ancillis peperit duodecim patriarchas. Sic ergo habemus bonos procreatos ex bonis, ut Ysaac de Sara, malos de malis, ut Ysmael de Agar ancilla, malos de bonis, ut Esau de Rebeca, bonos de malis, ut sunt filii Jacob quibus datur hereditas de ancillis. Correspondenter (ut dixi superius) inter christianos quicunque servaverint semen fidei patris nostri Jesu, de quantumcunque malis processerint quoad carnem, de quantumcunque malis sacramenta et legem Dei receperint, si perseveranciam finalem servaverint, sunt finaliter premiandi. [p. 471] Ex quibus patet quod natale vicium sanari potest per viam morum tam quoad celeste beneficium quam terrenum, nec dubium quin dimisso affectu primatus vel data prepositura amovibili iuxta testimonium digni operis vel indigni foret melius in ecclesia quam est modo. Leges ergo secundum quas iudicatur contra magis habilem quoad Deum pro minus habili vel indigno sunt omnino iniuste vel, si non sic iudicatur secundum leges illas nec ille sufficiant habilitatem hominis ad talia edocere, stultum foret legibus illis in iudiciis cecis intendere et evidenciam operis quam Deus dedit relinquere.

ENGLISH: and the judgements of first justice.[^1] For if, according to the laws of men, a judge who adjudges a secular [lordship]{g:dominium} or an ecclesiastical office in favour of one begotten outside marriage, whom they call a son of the people and illegitimate, [judges unjustly,] however [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} and qualified he may have been with respect to God, much more does a judge who adjudges in favour of a [foreknown]{g:prescitus} [man], whom God has decreed [unqualified]{g:inhabilis}, judge unjustly, since [he judges] contrary to the justice of [God's law]{g:lex-dei}.[^2] Nor does the feigned excuse avail by which it is said that the judge does his diligence by judging according to his knowledge, and that, were there not such judgements, a greater evil would happen. For as to the first [part], it is evident that in such ambiguous cases, whether they were unjust, a man ought to leave that doubtful [course] and choose the safe part. For this Christ and his apostles taught, in writing as well as in deed. And as to the second part, it is clear that one is not to sin even venially for the sake of acquiring any [signifiable]{g:signabile} good, or of guarding against an evil of guilt or of punishment, for a person or perpetually.[^3] And from these things the truth is clear of the conclusion of blessed Augustine in Letter 37 to Boniface, that the laws of men, which the saint calls civil [laws]{g:ius}, are commonly unjust.[^4] For supposing that Peter is predestinate, illegitimate according to the flesh, claiming an inheritance or an ecclesiastical office, and supposing that Paul, foreknown, is legitimate, claiming that same benefice, Peter nevertheless qualified and Paul unqualified with respect to God; and let there be two judges, of whom the first judges for Paul according to the laws of men [alleged]{g:allegare} and the testimonies of legal witness plainly deposed, and the second judge judges for Peter from a revelation by which he knows that God judges Peter qualified for the given benefice and Paul unqualified: [470] then it is clear that the second judge judges a just judgement, and consequently the first, by judging contrary to him, judges according to the appearance, and an unjust judgement; and such perplexity is common in human judgement.[^5] And the possibility of the case is clear from the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of Augustine on John, Sermon 11, on that [text] of John 3:1, "There was a man of the Pharisees", where he notes the scripture of Exodus 3, why God expressly says: "I am the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac", although Noe, Job, Moses and many others were old fathers who might have been named; and he answers that in these three patriarchs, who were the beginnings of the race of the Hebrews, the Israelites and the Jews, the race of the christian people is figured.[^6] For those three had offspring by free women and by bondwomen, according to a fourfold division; and so it is in the christian people. For as Abraham begot of Sara, a free woman, Isaac, who was good, so of Agar, a bondwoman, figuring the wicked (as the Apostle notes, Galatians 4:30), he brought forth Ismael. Again Rebecca, Isaac's wife, bore not only Jacob, who was good, but Esau, who was wicked in name as well as in figure; and Jacob, of free women and bondwomen, brought forth the twelve patriarchs. So, then, we have the good begotten of the good, as Isaac of Sara; the wicked of the wicked, as Ismael of Agar the bondwoman; the wicked of the good, as Esau of Rebecca; the good of the wicked, as are the sons of Jacob to whom the inheritance is given, [born] of the bondwomen.[^7] Correspondingly (as I said above), among christians, whoever keep the seed of the faith of our father Jesus, from however wicked [parents] they have proceeded as to the flesh, from however wicked [men] they have received the sacraments and God's law, if they keep final perseverance, are finally to be rewarded.[^8] [471] From which it is clear that the defect of birth can be healed by way of conduct, as to the heavenly benefice as well as the earthly; nor is there doubt that, the affection for primacy being given up, or the [office of superior]{g:prepositura} being given as removable according to the testimony of worthy or unworthy work, it would be better in the [church]{g:ecclesia} than it is now.[^9] The laws, then, according to which judgement is given against one more qualified with respect to God in favour of one less qualified or unworthy, are altogether unjust; or, if judgement is not so given according to those laws, and they do not suffice to teach a man's qualification for such things, it would be foolish to attend to those laws in blind judgements and to abandon the [evidence]{g:evidencia} of work which God has given.[^10]

### 20-a ¶1 · 471.14–472.25  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 471] Fructus huius longe contencionis de nostra sancta matre ecclesia foret sequens noticia scripture sacre, et per consequens dileccio sponsi et sponse inducens laborantes in ista fide fideliter in domum domini ad regulandum, ita quod illi applicetur illud Apoc. ultimo: Spiritus et sponsa dicunt: Veni. Quamvis **autem** quilibet liber scripture requirit pro eius sensu noticiam istius articuli fidei de ecclesia sancta catholica, specialiter tamen liber Apocalypsis, canticum ecclesie Proverbiorum XXXI° et Cantica canticorum. Quando enim partes ecclesie turbantur erga plurima, vocantur adolescentule non habentes ubera, quarum Salomon dicit non esse numerum propter volubilem affeccionem animi circa multa; **sed** quando Martha dimissa vita activa induit figuram Marie, intendens incomplete tamen uni necessario contemplando, inducta [p. 472] in thalamum, secundum numerum octogenarium dicitur concubina; **sed** tercio plus accedens ad unitatem que est omnia in omnibus notatur sub nomine regine et sub numero sexagenario post miliciam triumphalem; **sed** quarto quando cessabit omnis exaccio, area tocius ecclesie plene purgata a palea per ventilabrum finalis iudicii, non derelicta in corpore tocius ecclesie macula sive ruga, tunc dicitur una columba adepto fine unitatis caritative, secundum quam tota ecclesia est Christus. Patet ista sentencia Canticorum VI° et sparsim alibi in processu. Intendo ergo illam partem mediam scilicet canticum de muliere forti succincte transcurrere et difficultates profundas tam expositorias quam scolasticas superioribus meis relinquere. Video tamen (licet obscure) quomodo omnis difficultas logica, philosophica vel methaphisica includitur in scriptura de sancta matre ecclesia, ymmo (ut habundancius loquar) si quid subtilitatis repertum est a nobis in aliqua istarum arcium, eius sentenciam innuit nobis spiritus sanctus in cantico sapientissimi Salomonis de sancta matre ecclesia, Proverbiorum XXXI°, 10, Mulierem fortem quis inveniet; quod potest dividi in tres partes, in quarum prima narratur ecclesie sancte coniugium, in secunda describitur eius officium et in tercia concluditur eius preconium.

ENGLISH: The fruit of this long contention about [our holy mother]{g:mater-nostra} the [church]{g:ecclesia} would be the knowledge of holy scripture that follows, and consequently the love of the [bridegroom]{g:sponsus} and the [bride]{g:sponsa}, leading those who labour faithfully in this faith into the house of the Lord to rule [it], so that to it may be applied that [text] of the last [chapter] of the Apocalypse: "And the spirit and the bride say: Come."[^1] And although every book of scripture requires for its sense the knowledge of this [article of faith]{g:articulus-fidei}, the holy catholic church, yet especially [so] the book of the Apocalypse, the song of the church in Proverbs 31, and the Canticle of canticles.[^2] For when the parts of the church are troubled about very many things, they are called [young maidens]{g:adolescentula} having no breasts, of whom Solomon says there is no number, because of the fickle affection of the mind about many things; but when Martha, the active life left behind, puts on the figure of Mary, attending, though incompletely, to the one thing necessary by contemplating, brought [472] into the bridechamber, she is called a concubine, according to the number eighty;[^3] but thirdly, coming nearer to the unity which is all in all, she is marked under the name of queen and under the number sixty, after the triumphal [soldiery]{g:milicia}; but fourthly, when all exaction shall cease, the floor of the whole church being fully purged of the chaff by the fan of the final judgement, and no spot or wrinkle being left in the body of the whole church, then she is called one dove, the end of the unity of [charity]{g:caritas} being attained, according to which the whole church is Christ.[^4] This [teaching]{g:sentencia} is clear from Canticles 6, and here and there elsewhere in [the course of the book]{g:processus}. I intend, therefore, to run briefly through that middle part, namely the song of the [valiant woman]{g:mulier-fortis}, and to leave the deep difficulties, expository as well as scholastic, to my superiors. Yet I see (though darkly) how every difficulty, logical, philosophical or metaphysical, is included in scripture concerning holy mother church; indeed (to speak more abundantly), if any subtlety has been found by us in any of these arts, the Holy Spirit intimates its teaching to us in the song of the most wise Solomon about holy mother church, Proverbs 31:10, "Who shall find a valiant woman?" It can be divided into three parts: in the first of these the marriage of the holy church is narrated, in the second its office is described, and in the third its praise is concluded.[^5]

### 20-a ¶2 · 472.26–475.31  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 472] Primo **autem** supponitur esse unum primum coniugium quod sit metrum omnium aliorum inter fortissimum virorum et mulierem sibi proporcionalem faciendum, nam secundum philosophos in omni genere est reperibile tale primum. In genere ergo coniugii supposita incarnacione verbi (quod est Deus fortis et potens in prelio) oportet invenire sibi coniugem bene fortem. Ideo primo de illa queritur: Mulierem fortem [p. 473] quis inveniet, ubi secundum sanctos doctores concorditer oportet per mulierem fortem intelligere sanctam matrem ecclesiam, quia quamvis sexaginta sint regine et octoginta concubine adolescentularumque non est numerus (ut dicitur Canticorum VI°, 7), tamen ex omnibus illis constat una columba (ut dicitur ibidem). Quis ergo inveniet dignam coniugem tanto sponso? Et videtur quod nullus hominum nisi ipse, nam in constitucione primi carnalis coniugii primus coniux concurrebat materialiter ad causandum suam coniugem (ut patet Genesis III°): ergo multo magis in illo coniugio celesti oportet coniugem concurrere in causa triplici ad causandum suam coniugem ex natura angelica et humana. Primo ergo opere creacionis secundum raciones exemplares in verbo, secundo vero opere ministracionis secundum raciones seminales in genere humano, et tercio opere recreacionis in Christo facto circulariter ista duo inventa est mulier ista fortis. Nam secundum illam partem lapsa in devium erravit, sicut ovis que periit in vepribus peccatorum, et (ut dicitur Matthei XVIII°, 12) pastor et sponsus dimissis nonaginta novem ovibus in pascuis semper virentibus in celo perditam ovem invenit et humeris suis labore et penalitate debita reportavit. Nec moveat quod beatus Augustinus in tractatu huius cantici dicit quod hec mulier est civitas supra montem posita quam difficile est nescire. Videmus enim modo per fidem secundum quam tamquam lucem secundam illuminat mundum, faciens nos luscos tenebra ignorancie obfuscatos videre eam speculariter et obscure, sic quod ignoramus quoad noticiam distinctam partes ecclesie, sicut videns civitatem longinque, confuse et [p. 474] indistincte videt domos eius et alia ornamenta. Nec sufficeremus sic cernere, nisi dicta mulier a sponso prius in racionibus exemplaribus et visceribus misericordie sit inventa. Unde post conclusionem propositam supponendo quod ille invenit hanc mulierem qui secundum divinitatem est immensus, a cuius oculis nichil potest esse absconditum et secundum humanitatem ascendit super omnes celos, subditur inventorem illum quoad naturam et quoad tempus respectu patrum veterum esse procul, sicut et mulier illa procul erat localiter a Iudeis, partim in celo secundum angelos et partim in terra secundum gentes pro tempore gracie convertendas, sicut procul erat a Deo per culpam, cum non sit possibile quicquam esse a Deo distancius quam peccatum. Inventa itaque muliere forti copulanda in primo coniugio videndum est secundo de suis passionibus, utrum sit apta nubere tanto sponso; et respondet litera consequenter quod de ultimis finibus precium eius. Quod videtur sic posse intelligi: causa finalis quare ecclesia est valida, ut Christo matrimonialiter coniungatur, sunt predestinaciones suorum membrorum. Nam causa celestis particularis coniugii est opus meritorium viatoris, et causa operis meritorii est gracia infusa, et causa gracie infuse est predestinacio que quasi ultimus finis citra Deum dignificat ecclesiam ad illud coniugium, quia (ut dicitur Johannis I°, 16), de eius plenitudine nos omnes accepimus graciam pro gracia. Nam ex caritate eterna dedit nobis caritatem subiectivam secundum quam cum opere meritorio simus digni vel (ut habet litera Augustini) preciosiorem preciosis lapidibus que eiuscemodi est; cum quo verbo innuit eiusmodi [p. 475] mulierem in mente habere dileccionem usque ad finem; que Canticorum VIII°, 6 fortis ut mors dicitur. Ex quo sequitur quod mulier habens membra dileccione sic forcia sit sponsa racione consumate fortitudinis preciosa, quia usque ad finem diligit sponsum suum. Alii **autem** lapides scilicet caritates horales finaliter excidentes (ut fuit in Donato et aliis prescitis) non sunt tanti precii, ut subiecta sua sponso ecclesie coniungantur; vel tercio intelligitur non contrarie supradictis quod de ultimis finibus celorum et seculorum est precium, unde est dignitas huius sponse; vel quarto de divinitate ac humanitate in Christo qui sunt ultimi fines circuli intelligibilis est precium huius mulieris, ut patet ex fide, quia sicut verbum creat, ita dignificat sponsam suam. Et de ipso dicitur Psalmo XVIII°, 7: A summo celo egressio eius, et in ultima etate seculi consummata est gracia unionis virtute cuius originatur tota dignitas huius coniugii; Ia Cor. X°, 11 scribitur: Nos sumus in quos fines seculorum devenerunt. Inventa ergo coniuge et inventa eius dignitate ex precio vel valore, cum sponsus sit totum per se dignificans opus suum non restat quid tardaret illud coniugium. Ideo innuitur tercio affidacio celestis, dum subditur: Confidit in ea cor viri sui. Plane, inquit Augustinus, confidit et ut confidamus in eum edocuit. Prescius predestinacionis proprie confidit qui falli non poterit. Mulier **autem** cum filiis suis potest in ipsum confidere, eo quod mentiri non poterit nec sponse vel membris suis deficere, **sed** totus timor huius affidacionis stat in nostra desidia quod per nos non sit precium vel dos huius coniugii consummata.

ENGLISH: Now first it is supposed that there is one first marriage, which is to be the measure of all the others, to be made between the strongest of men and a woman proportioned to him; for according to the philosophers such a first is to be found in every genus.[^6] In the genus of marriage, then, the incarnation of the Word being supposed (who is God "strong and mighty in battle"), a spouse right strong must be found for him. Therefore first it is asked of her: "Who shall find a valiant woman?" [473] Here, according to the holy doctors with one accord, we must understand by the valiant woman holy mother church; for although "there are threescore queens, and fourscore concubines, and young maidens without number" (as is said in Canticles 6:7), yet of all of them one dove is made up (as is said in the same place). Who, then, shall find a spouse worthy of so great a bridegroom? And it seems: no one of men but he himself; for in the constitution of the first carnal marriage the first spouse concurred materially in causing his spouse (as is clear from Genesis 3); much more, then, in that heavenly marriage must the spouse concur in a threefold cause in causing his spouse out of angelic and human nature.[^7] First, then, by the work of creation, according to the exemplary reasons in the Word; secondly, by the work of ministration, according to the seminal reasons in the human race; and thirdly, by the work of re-creation in Christ, made in a circle these two, this valiant woman was found. For according to that part she fell and strayed into the byway, like the sheep that perished among the brambles of sins; and (as is said in Matthew 18:12) the shepherd and bridegroom, leaving the ninety-nine sheep in the ever-green pastures in heaven, found the lost sheep and carried it back on his shoulders with due labour and [hardship]{g:penalitas}.[^8] Nor let it trouble [anyone] that blessed Augustine says in his treatise on this song that this woman is a city seated on a mountain, which it is hard not to know. For we see now through faith, according to which, as a second light, she illuminates the world, making us, purblind and darkened by the darkness of ignorance, see her as in a glass and darkly; so that we are ignorant, as to distinct knowledge, of the parts of the church, as one who sees a city from far off sees [indistinctly]{g:confuse} and [474] without distinction its houses and its other ornaments.[^9] Nor should we suffice to discern even so, unless the said woman had first been found by the bridegroom in the exemplary reasons and in the bowels of mercy. Hence, after the conclusion propounded, supposing that he found this woman who according to [divinity]{g:divinitas} is immense, from whose eyes nothing can be hidden, and according to humanity ascended above all the heavens, it is added that that finder was "far" off, as to nature and as to time, with respect to the old fathers; as that woman too was far off in place from the Jews, partly in heaven in the angels and partly on earth in the gentiles to be converted in the time of grace, as she was far from God by guilt, since nothing can possibly be more distant from God than sin.[^10] The valiant woman to be joined in the first marriage having, then, been found, we must see secondly about her [attributes]{g:passio}, whether she is fit to marry so great a bridegroom; and the letter answers next that "from the uttermost coasts is the price of her". This, it seems, can be understood thus: the final cause why the church is strong, so as to be joined to Christ in matrimony, is the predestinations of its [members]{g:membrum}. For the cause of the particular heavenly marriage is the meritorious work of the [wayfarer]{g:viator}, and the cause of the meritorious work is infused grace, and the cause of infused grace is predestination, which, as the last end, as it were, short of God, makes the church worthy of that marriage; since (as is said in John 1:16) "of his fulness we all have received grace for grace."[^11] For out of eternal charity he gave us subjective charity, according to which, with meritorious work, we are worthy, or (as Augustine's text has it) "more precious than precious stones is she who is of this kind"; by which word he intimates that a woman of this kind [475] has in mind love unto the end, which in Canticles 8:6 is called "strong as death".[^12] From which [it follows]{g:sequitur} that a woman having members so strong in love is a bride precious by reason of consummate strength, since she loves her bridegroom unto the end. But the other stones, namely charities for an hour that finally fall away (as it was in Donatus and other [foreknown]{g:prescitus} men), are not of such price that their subjects should be joined to the bridegroom of the church;[^13] or, thirdly, it is understood, not contrary to what was said above, that the price is from the uttermost ends of the heavens and of the ages, whence is the dignity of this bride; or, fourthly, the price of this woman is from the divinity and the humanity in Christ, which are the uttermost ends of the intelligible circle, as is clear, [as faith teaches]{g:ex-fide}, since as the Word creates, so it makes its bride worthy. And of him it is said in Psalm 18:7: "His going out is from the end of heaven"; and in the last age of the world the grace of union was consummated, by virtue of which the whole dignity of this marriage takes its origin; in 1 Corinthians 10:11 it is written: "We are [those] upon whom the ends of the world are come."[^14] The spouse, then, having been found, and her dignity found from her price or value, since the bridegroom is the whole, of himself making his work worthy, nothing remains that should delay that marriage. Therefore, thirdly, the heavenly affiance is intimated, when it is added: "The heart of her husband trusteth in her." "Plainly," says Augustine, "he trusts, and he has taught us to trust in him. Foreknowing his own predestination, he trusts who cannot be deceived." And the woman with her sons can trust in him, because he cannot lie, nor fail his bride or his members; but the whole fear of this affiance stands in our sloth, lest through us the price or dowry of this marriage should not be consummated.[^15]

### 20-b ¶3 · 476.15–477.25  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 476] a viro reddet ei bonum et non malum omnibus diebus vite sue. Operatur, inquit Augustinus, viro suo bona eterna continue, quia (ut mihi videtur) omnes predestinati semper prosunt ecclesie sine dampnacionis sue demerito vel ecclesie detrimento. In hoc enim differt peccatum predestinati a peccato presciti quod primum est necessario remissibile et secundum dampnabile. Primum per accidens prodest ecclesie et peccanti (ut patet de peccato Petri) et secundum licet per accidens faciat ad bonum ecclesie, tamen continue sibi nocet, quia sicut quicquid predestinatus hic fecerit tendit ad sui salvacionem, sic quicquid prescitus hic fecerit tendit ad sui dampnacionem. Quod **autem** quidam dicunt peccatum predestinati dampnabile, equivocatur in nomine dampnabile sumpto large vel proprie. Large dicitur dampnabile culpandum quod perpetuo dampnaretur, si non foret Christi gracia [p. 477] preservatum, et sic omnis peccator foret dampnabilis. **Sed** proprie solum illud dicitur dampnabile ad quod est Dei ordinacio, ut dampnetur, et sic nunquam hec mulier vel aliquod eius membrum fecit malum dampnabile sponso suo. Et (ut notat Augustinus) hec mulier fecit non sibi principaliter, **sed** sponso bonum quodcunque fecerit tamquam viro et capiti modo quo loquitur Apostolus IIª, Cor. V°, 15: Qui vivit iam non sibi vivat, **sed** ei qui mortuus est pro omnibus et resurrexit, sic quod ecclesia et quodlibet eius membrum quicquid operatum fuerit hoc facit, ut ei placeat cui se probavit. Omnes enim alii querunt que sua sunt, non que domini Jesu Christi. Et hec est racio (ut dixi superius), quare Christus non usus est proprietate civili in se nec in suis apostolis. Nec moveat quod nemo debet bonum reddere huic viro propter hoc quod omnis homo est servus sibi inutilis, cum fecerit omnia que rector iste precepit, ut dicitur Luce XVII°, 10; et Psalmo XV°, 2 dicitur: Dixi domino: Deus meus es tu, quoniam bonorum meorum non eges. Verum est quod Christus in persona propria non eget modo humanitus neque divinitus, **sed** nos egemus ut faciamus omnia nostra opera primo sibi, hoc est ad honorem suum et utilitatem sui corporis in quo eget.

For in James 2:12 it is written: "Judgment without mercy to him that hath not done mercy." "Let one who has," he says, "a full purse, storehouse or barn, spinning wholesomely, transfer from the distaff to the spindle." And much more is the spiritual work of mercy to be worked earnestly; and it is known from the text how the church gives more largely to the needier [484] of that which he needs, but exercises the power of defence in [the case of] the poor.[^13]
From these it follows tenthly that the husband of such a matron "shall not fear for [his] house in the cold of snow: for all [his] domestics are clothed with double garments." "How," says Augustine, "should the Lord fear for his domestics, who are [members]{g:membrum} of such a matron, since in Romans 8:30 it is written: 'Whom he predestinated, them he also called. And whom he called, them he also justified. And whom he justified, them he also glorified'?" Therefore neither Christ nor the church will fear for its members from the snowy persecution, in which "the charity of many shall grow cold" (as is said in Matthew 24:12), since all of the house of the church are clothed with double garments.[^14] This garment is most precious, since it is really the bridegroom himself. Hence in Galatians 3:27 it is said: "As many of you as have been baptized in Christ have put on Christ." Those, therefore, who are both predestined and work virtuously besides, worthily praising Christ divinely and humanly, are clothed with double garments. But [foreknown]{g:prescitus} heretics who are baptized, having a temporary baptismal grace, and afterwards barely confess in Christ one simple nature (for example, only the divinity or only the humanity, as the Photinians and Manichees), are clothed, as servants of the church, with a single garment, lest they pretend that they would will to persevere finally if they had had the garment.[^15] But the wicked, according to Augustine, are clothed only with the form of the sacrament, and the good with the work and final imitation.[^16]

Virtus enim que est sapiencia patris illapsa ecclesie fecit eam ebullire sapiencia tam secundum actus quam habitus virtutis que sunt eius vestigia, et in lingua eius ordinate ponitur lex laudandi in suo ordine domini et quodlibet opus suum. ENGLISH: Hence, for the further declaration of this [sect]{g:secta} of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, it is added eleventhly: "She hath made for herself clothing of tapestry: fine linen, and purple, is her covering."[^1] "It was not fitting," says Augustine, "for the matron of so great a husband to go about naked or in rags, but adorned on every side with precious things." For since the [members]{g:membrum} whom Christ clothes, from the [clergy]{g:clerus} and [485] the laity, are woven together variously, as a garment of varied quality, and this matron has woven this varied garment in herself, it is clear that "she hath made for herself clothing of tapestry"; and since of each [kind of] member some are white with virginity like fine linen, and others flourishing with martyrdom like purple, and Christ is clothed with both of these lights as with a garment, it is clear that she, putting on Christ, has fine linen and purple as garments, just as she also has [them] from the white soul of Christ and his reddened bodily nature.[^2] From these it follows twelfthly that "her husband is honourable in the gates, when he sitteth among the senators of the land"; for it is necessary, on the side of the good as much as of the wicked, that this course have an end, and consequently it is necessary (as is said in Isaiah 3:14) that the husband come to judgment with the ancients of his people. Hence also in Matthew 25:31 it is said: "When the Son of man shall come in his majesty, and all his angels with him, then shall he sit upon the seat of his majesty: and all nations shall be gathered together before him." And the place of judgment can be called "gates" because of its openness and the entry into the twofold city. Then the nobility of Christ will be manifest: first in his twofold form, as regards himself; secondly in his whole church without spot or wrinkle, as regards his [household]{g:familia}; and thirdly in the expulsion of the damnable, dishonest servant, since his nobility does not suffer the perpetual [joint dwelling]{g:cohabitacio} of such an evildoer, nor so great an injury unpunished.[^3] But let us recognize that the said father of the household has placed the increase from the body of his spouse in the free power of every man. Therefore it is inferred thirteenthly: "She made fine linen, and sold it, and delivered a girdle to the Chanaanite." By the fine linen I understand the evangelical [doctrine]{g:doctrina}, with which, as with a most pure and most fine linen garment, the mind is clothed.[^4] And she sold [it] by a spiritual sale, [486] since in Luke 19:23 it is said: [the husband] of this spouse gave his money at usury; and so this sale is quite gratuitous, of which it is said in Isaiah 55:1: "Come ye, buy wine and milk without money, and without any price."[^5] And in spiritual usury the price is given freely and the giver seeks the fruit, and so upon the increase, so that he may possess [it] jointly, to the profit of the receiver and to his own honour; indeed (as Augustine relates), since the one who preaches the gospel has a temporal wage for his preaching of the gospel, it seems that there is an account of mutual giving and receiving. For in 1 Corinthians 9:11 the apostle says: "If we have sown unto you spiritual things, it is no great matter if we reap your carnal things."[^6] But it is not a civil contract, since the one who preaches the gospel principally seeks the salvation of the people and not his own things; therefore wages are owed to those who preach the gospel, not as an [exchange]{g:commutacio} for things civilly possessed. Those who receive this fine linen must therefore give themselves, since it is better that for spiritual grain we sell ourselves as perpetual servants to our Joseph than that we perish under the devil for spiritual hunger; for when you give yourself to the flesh, the world or the devil, you give as great a price for transitory pleasure and sin as you would give for blessedness and for God. Therefore, when the weariness that goes with these things is weighed, it is clear that in the damned there is a foolish trade.[^7] And she delivered the girdle when she preached the gospel precepts to the gentiles; for the Chanaanites were, in the letter, [unbelievers]{g:infidelis} and aliens from Israel; and after the bridegroom said to his apostles, "Go, teach all nations," Luke, last [chapter], the apostle says in Ephesians 2:19: "Now you are no more strangers and foreigners: but you are fellow citizens with the saints and the domestics of God"; which the woman of Canaan, coming out [487] of her coasts, prefigured, Matthew 15.[^8] For by the preaching of the faith to the gentiles, those who went out from the error of heathendom were girded to fulfil the Lord's precepts.
And since this redounds to the profit of the church, therefore it is inferred fourteenthly: "Strength and beauty are her clothing, and she shall laugh in the latter day." For by the grafting in of the gentiles the church has put on the Lord Jesus Christ more abundantly.[^9] Strength, according to Augustine, signifies the constancy of bloody suffering, and beauty the flower of virginal chastity, according to what was said above: "Fine linen and purple is her covering." For these two virtues grew strong beyond Judea in the church of the converted gentiles, and hence, no wonder, this matron will have jubilee on the day of judgment.[^10] Nor let us wonder why scripture so often commands us to put on our Isaac; for it is known that the garments of the soul are the [virtues]{g:virtus}, and Christ is "the power of God and the wisdom of God", and the extrinsic exemplary form, the efficient [cause] and the end, without the informing of whose inflowing no creature is virtuous. And habits, which are qualities and intrinsic forms, are nothing but [customs]{g:consuetudo} and readinesses for acting. Hence Christ, having flowed into his member as into something accidental to him, is said both to clothe it and, weaving it with virtues, to adorn it with wisdom.[^11] Hence it follows fifteenthly: "She hath opened her mouth to wisdom, and the law of clemency is on her tongue." For the Power that is the wisdom of the Father, having flowed into the church, made her bubble over with wisdom, according to acts as much as to habits of virtue, which are its traces; and on her tongue is set in order the law of praising, each in its order, the Lord and each work of his.[^12] "May he grant us," says Augustine, "who are set in her, praising her, cleaving to her, awaiting his glory with her and in her as in a mother, that we too may open our mouth in fear, attentively and cautiously and solicitously." For in 1 Corinthians 2:3 the Apostle writes: "In fear and in much trembling [488] I was with you." And it follows: "Our mouth is open to you, O ye Corinthians."[^13] "She has set order," he says, "on her tongue, praising the Creator and each creature" in its order; or, more beautifully, in this order, by which a man loves each thing not as something else but truly in the order of goodness in which God has set it. For then he truly loves it as itself; hence [our mother]{g:mater-nostra}, in Canticles 2:4, testifies expressly of the [bridegroom]{g:sponsus}: "He set in order," she says, "[charity]{g:caritas} in me."[^14] But since for that is required the philosophical knowledge by which the orders of the goodnesses of things are known according to wisdom, therefore sixteenthly and lastly he adds thus: "She hath looked well on the paths of her house, and hath not eaten her bread idle." For one who watchfully considers the way by which holy mother church must ascend into God, contemplating him in order in his effects, will not be at leisure in culpable idleness, since he must studiously "watch at the doors" of uncreated wisdom. And in these sixteen stands the office of our mother.[^15]
As to the third part of the song, in which the commendation of this matron is proclaimed, first it is added thus: "Her children rose up, and called her blessed: her husband, and he praised her." The infallible testimony of the praise of this lady is taken by ascending from expert witnesses, who rose from the miseries of this [way]{g:via}, up to the testimony of her husband. For he is the uncreated truth, John 14; for many rose from purgatory and from limbo who communicated to us knowledge of the blessedness and glory of the [church triumphant]{g:ecclesia-triumphans}; and the Truth testifies, in the fact of his transfiguration and in the words of his preaching, to the praise of the [bride]{g:sponsa}, Matthew 25:34: "Come, ye blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom prepared for you"; and the word of praise follows: "For I was hungry, and you gave me to eat."[^16] [489] "And now," says Augustine, "we live in poverty, and when we die we sleep in poverty; but we shall rise and be truly enriched."[^17] Hence, since a part of blessedness is the possession of true goods, therefore secondly it is added in commendation of this mother: "Many daughters have gathered together riches: thou hast surpassed them all." For all [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} pass away and deceive whoever love them preponderantly. Therefore in Matthew 6:19 it is said: "Lay not up to yourselves treasures on earth: where the rust and moth consume, and where thieves break through and steal; but lay up to yourselves treasures in heaven"; and it follows: "For where thy treasure is, there is thy heart also."[^18] And the daughters, according to Augustine, are called the [foreknown]{g:prescitus} heretics, as those who are finally to be divided from the church. Among these daughters the church shines "as the lily among thorns": "Such thorns," he says, "have a flower without fruit"; for, being in the church for a time, they receive deceitfully the faith and the sacraments of the church, because consummated charity is lacking to them.[^19] Hence, since such men very often abound in worldly glory and the fame of the world on account of its riches, therefore thirdly it is added: "Favour is deceitful, and beauty is vain: the woman that feareth the Lord, she shall be praised." For the favour of human praise, of worldly glory or of carnal concupiscence turns men aside from God to the threefold enemy. Therefore it must be that this is deceitful favour, since it draws [men] away from the bridegroom, who is "full of grace and truth".[^20] Vain, too, is any [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} beauty whatever, since there is no beauty except on account of the proportional number, [imperceptible]{g:insensibilis} and uncreated, in which beauty is first, of itself and properly (as is clear in the chapter *Multe*). Therefore such perceptible, and above all temporal, beauty is expressly called vanity.[^21] Hence the right and essential order of beginning the matter of true praise is the fear of the Lord; for from the fact [490] that the bridegroom is feared chastely and filially, the aforesaid woman works for her bridegroom all the good narrated, in the said order; and it is certain that such good working cannot finally fail of praise.[^22]

### 21-a ¶1 · 491.2–492.10  (6 sed/autem)

Rex (inquit) non potest auferre simpliciter aliqua temporalia a clero suo legio delinquente, et quia sensus quem habet de signo isto simpliciter est multis incognitus, ideo specificat eius sensum. ### 21-c ¶2 · 500.3–500.25  (6 sed/autem)

ENGLISH: And he asks first whether a character is imprinted in all orders and, proving that it is by a fourfold argument, he recites two opinions, which he rejects. For since a character is a seal that is express, distinctive and perpetual and configuring to Christ, and this is so in all orders, it is not to be doubted that a character is imprinted in all.[^9]

He, therefore, who prays specially and absolutely for the beatification of his patron, now dead, granted that it has already been revealed to him that the patron has already been consigned to eternal fires, and this for greed of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, ought to be deprived of them as a useless [bedesman]{g:orator}, since he ought to rejoice with God and the blessed in heaven over the just judgment of God and the material joy of the blessed, even if it were one of his fleshly parents or the most special of his friends; because, as we do not ask God to recall time past, so neither do we ask him to recall such a judgment.[^11] And as to the saying of Augustine, it is clear that it is understood that the [church]{g:ecclesia} rightly prays for all who are capable, insofar as they are such. Hence this text of his: "It prays for all men only, its enemies set in this body; and yet it is not heard for all. For it is heard only for those who, although they oppose the church, are yet so predestined that they are made sons of the church. But if any will have an impenitent heart until death and will not be converted from enemies into sons, the church does not pray for the spirits of such dead, because he is now [531] reckoned in the devil's part who, while he was in the body, was not translated to Christ. There is therefore the same reason why prayer is not made then for men to be punished eternally as there is why prayer is made neither now nor then for the evil spirits."[^12] But thirdly it is principally [argued, by appeal to]{g:arguitur per} this: that a man can meritoriously pray generally for the departed, mingling in the intention of praying specially for a patron or another friend, granted that he is damned; in which case he prays lawfully for one damned; therefore this is lawful.[^13] It is confirmed in three ways. First, by this, that conditional prayer is prayer of a kind which must be granted to be lawful for one damned, since it is from grace; and consequently it necessitates praying for such. Secondly, it is confirmed by this, that blessed Gregory prayed specially for Trajan, whom he knew to be damned, because, John 3:18: "He that doth not believe is already judged." Now the emperor Trajan was an [unbeliever]{g:infidelis}, dying in mortal sin; therefore, since blessed Gregory was heard by the Lord for him, it seems that it is lawful to pray for such; therefore a saint can merit that such be raised again, can merit that they be saved.[^14] Thirdly, it is confirmed by this, that it is lawful to pray for anyone to whom prayer would be useful or would take away evil; now so it is with every merit of the [church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans} as regards the damned; therefore it is lawful to pray for them, at least by taking away the greater evil that would otherwise befall them. For it has often been said that the whole world acquires its second perfection from the merit of Christ, since otherwise the devils would be tormented more painfully, because otherwise they would have a greater fellowship punished with them, would demerit more by seducing further, and it would lack the second perfection, by which they are now, with the consolation of the saints, mercifully [532] punished.[^15] Therefore the good which the devil does to the predestinate by accident is done in virtue of the merit of Christ; which seems to profit them to that extent. And by the same token it seems that a [wayfarer]{g:viator}, insofar as he victoriously resists the devil, strengthening the brothers of the church, mitigates something of his evil, and consequently brings it about that his [damnation]{g:dampnacio} is more tolerable, and to that extent effectually prays for him; for his members harm him most, because as to them that fear God all things work together unto good, so to the foreknown all things turn out for evil, and most of all those which seem to them more useful.[^16] Here I say that the minor is false; for in the case put a man intends to pray for the given dead man, but he does not pray for him, because of his incapacity; but the intention, like the general prayer, is meritorious in the case.
