## Doubtful, left as they are

- **06-c, p. 95**: *non puto aliam creaturam, **sed** neque Deum cognoscere*, rendered "any other creature, nor even God". An escalating *non … sed neque* ("but not even"); "nor even" carries both the *sed* and the *neque*, and "but not even God" reads worse after "I do not think that any other creature". Kept.
- **07-b, p. 113**: ***Sed** ut manifestet quid ex hoc sequitur, subiungit*, rendered "Now". No syllogism: it is the second step of a three-step exposition of John 14:24 (… *Sed* … *Et 3°*). "Now" as "next step" is defensible; "And" would match the following *Et 3°* but is not a card rendering for *sed*. Kept.
- **07-c, p. 116**: *omne **autem** quod amplius est in disputacione theologica 'a malo est': ideo questio …*, rendered "and whatever is more". An *ideo* follows, so (a) "now" is possible, and the contrast with the permitted intents would allow (b) "but". But the clause echoes Matt 5:37 (*quod autem his abundantius est, a malo est*), whose Douay reads "and that which is over and above these, is of evil"; "and" follows the Douay. Kept.
- **07-b, p. 113**: *Licet **autem** sensus catholicus posset esse …; tamen prior sensus*, rendered "And although". Could be (b), the alternative sense set against the preferred one, but the contrast belongs to *tamen* ("yet"); (c) "and" stands.
- **Outside the sed/autem remit (not changed here), 06-c, p. 95**: *et⁶ presupponit substanciam tanquam subiectum*, rendered "but presupposes substance". The Latin is *et*, not *sed*; "but" adds a contrast the Latin does not have ("and presupposes"). Flagged for the coordinator.
