# *De eucharistia*, chapter 4, part 2: the three figures of the sacrament, and the power and form of its words

## ¶1 · 87.29–88.2

> [p. 87] Tres, inquam, inter multas erant figure huius sacramenti in veteri testamento; prima erat agnus pascalis qui nedum figuravit agnum Dei qui tollit peccata mundi Joh. Iº, 29 sed ipsummet sacramentum; et [p. 88] hec racio quare debuit cessare esus agni paschalis post institucionem huius sacramenti signati pocioris.

Three, I say, among many, were the [figures]{g:figura} of this [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} in the old testament. The first was the paschal lamb, which [not only]{g:nedum} [figured]{g:figurare} "the lamb of God who taketh away the sins" of the world (John 1:29)[^1] but the sacrament itself; and [88] this is the reason why the [eating]{g:esus} of the paschal lamb had to cease after the institution of this sacrament, the greater [thing signified]{g:signatum}.[^2]

## ¶2 · 88.3–88.8

> [p. 88] Secunda figura huius sacramenti fuit fluxus aque de lapide (de quo Exodi XIIº); hoc enim significat secundum Apostolum bibicionem spiritualem sanguinis petre Christi: Omnes, inquit, spiritualem potum eundem biberunt, bibebant autem de spirituali consequente eos petra; petra autem erat Christus.

The second figure of this sacrament was the flowing of water from the stone (of which Exodus 12[^3]); for this signifies, according to the Apostle, the spiritual drinking of the blood of the rock, Christ: "All," he says, "drank the same spiritual drink; and they drank of the spiritual rock that followed them: and the rock was Christ" (1 Cor. 10:4).

## ¶3 · 88.9–88.26

> [p. 88] Tercia figura erat manna (de quo Exod. XVIº); unde Christus Joh. VIº, 59 complectens excellenciam huius sacramenti et rei sue super hoc manna: Non, inquit, sicut manducaverunt patres vestri manna et mortui sunt. Qui manducat hunc panem vivet in eternum. Nam secundum exposicionem beati Augustini Omelia XXVI licet commune sit nobis et illis mori, tamen illi manducantes manna corporaliter et remurmurantes contra Dominum mortui sunt corporaliter et in mente dampnabiliter, sed qui manducat spiritualiter panem Christum vivet spiritualiter in eternum, cum iuxta dicta ille rite manducat corpus Christi spiritualiter qui assimilatur sibi ut membrum glutino caritatis perpetue. Quamvis autem multi comedentes manna fideliter erant incorporati ecclesie, tamen illo sacramento non meruerunt presencialiter sed valde distanter apercionem ianue. Et patet in parte responsio ad dubium motum in capite huius capituli.

The third figure was the manna (of which Exod. 16); hence Christ, in John 6:59, embracing the excellence of this sacrament and of [its reality]{g:res-sacramenti} over this manna, says: "Not as your fathers did eat manna and are dead. He that eateth this [bread]{g:panis} shall live for ever."[^4] For according to the exposition of blessed Augustine, Homily 26, although it is common to us and to them to die, yet they, eating the manna [bodily]{g:corporaliter} and murmuring against the Lord, died bodily, and in mind damnably; but he who eats [spiritually]{g:spiritualiter} the bread, Christ, will live spiritually for ever, since, according to what has been said, he rightly eats the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} spiritually who is assimilated to him as a [member]{g:membrum} by the glue of perpetual [charity]{g:caritas}. And although many who ate the manna faithfully were incorporated into the [church]{g:ecclesia}, yet by that sacrament they did not merit the opening of the door presently, but very distantly.[^5] And in part the reply to the doubt raised at the head of this chapter is clear.[^6]

## ¶4 · 88.27–89.3

> [p. 88] Sed pro declaracione ulteriori sunt tria plus declaranda: primo de veritate et virtute verborum huius sacramenti, secundo quid et qualiter de hoc sacramento [p. 89] in compendio est credendum, et tercio quomodo ad dignitatem recipientis huius sacramenti debet homo disponi.

But for further clarification three things more are to be [made clear]{g:declarare}: first, concerning the truth and [power]{g:virtus} of the words of this sacrament; second, what and how one is to believe about this sacrament [89] in summary; and third, how a man ought to be disposed to the worthiness of a recipient of this sacrament.

## ¶5 · 89.4–89.20

> [p. 89] Quoad primum dicitur quod verba ipsa successiva et interpolata non habent ex se efficaciam convertendi, sed sermo Dei assistens in cuius persona dicuntur facit opera; et creditur quod placet verbo Dei operari per verba vocalia, ut vel sic discamus quod ipsum facit opera et inproprie atque equivoce eius instrumenta. Summus enim artifex operatur per valde abiecta et debilia instrumenta. Si queratur quando talis proposicio sacramentalis incipit esse vera, dicitur quod non quando desinit esse per remocionem de presenti, cum tunc non est, et quod non est, non est verum, sed pro ultimo instanti esse sui, cum sit dare composicionem continui ex non quantis, et cum omnia que fuerunt vel erunt sunt, interpollacio non impedit proposicionem esse vel secundum unam partem vel aliam, sed pro tempore interpolacionis desinit taliter esse; que omnia hic suppono ex alibi declaratis.

As to the first, [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that the words themselves, successive and [interrupted]{g:interpolatus}, have not of themselves the efficacy of [converting]{g:conversio}, but the [Speech]{g:sermo} of God, assisting, in whose person they are said, does the works; and it is believed that it pleases the [Word]{g:verbum} of God to work through spoken words, so that at least in this way we may learn that he does the works, and his instruments improperly and [equivocally]{g:equivoce}.[^7] For the highest craftsman works through very lowly and weak instruments. If it is asked when such a sacramental [proposition]{g:proposicio} begins to be true, the answer is: not when it ceases to be by removal from the present, since then it is not, and what is not is not true; but for the last instant of its [being]{g:esse}, since [one may posit]{g:est-dare} the composition of a continuum out of things not [quantified]{g:quantus}; and since all things that were or will be are, the interruption does not prevent the proposition from being, either as to one part or another, but for the time of the interruption it ceases to be in that way: all of which I here suppose from what has been made clear elsewhere.[^8]

## ¶6 · 89.21–90.3

> [p. 89] Et videtur michi modo quod panis demonstratur pronomine, et sic est proposicio vera ad suum sensum, sicut videtur Augustinum concedere, hoc est Christus, demonstrata quacumque creatura, ut patet super Johanne Omelia XIII. Sed in demonstrando hoc sacramentum est quedam singularis efficacia et significacio a Christo imposita, ideo theologus debet notare quomodo ista est singulariter concedenda. In casu autem quo intelligatur proposicio non figurative sed effective, tunc per pronomen demonstratur sub habitudine accidencium illud in celo quod est realiter [p. 90] corpus Christi. Si autem intelligatur proposicio figurative, sicut videtur historiam evangelii intelligere, tunc intelligi et demonstrari debet pronomine panis ille.

And it seems to me now that the bread is demonstrated by the pronoun, and so the proposition is true in its [meaning]{g:sensus}, as Augustine seems to grant "this is Christ", any [creature]{g:creatura} whatever being demonstrated, as is clear on John, Homily 13.[^9] But in demonstrating this sacrament there is a certain singular efficacy and signification imposed by Christ; therefore the theologian ought to note how this proposition is to be granted [in a singular way]{g:singulariter}.[^10] And in the case where the proposition is understood not [figuratively]{g:figurative} but [by way of effect]{g:effective}, then by the pronoun there is demonstrated, under the [respect]{g:habitudo} of the [accidents]{g:accidens}, that in heaven which is [really]{g:realiter} [90] the body of Christ.[^11] But if the proposition is understood figuratively, as the gospel history seems to understand it, then that bread ought to be understood and demonstrated by the pronoun.

## ¶7 · 90.4–90.12

> [p. 90] Sed quantum ad formam verborum declarat dominus Armachanus IXº De Questionibus Armenorum per multa capitula quomodo nulla forma verborum quoad linguas et nomina est generaliter requisita, cum autores varie scripserant quoad linguas, ut Mattheus in Hebraico, Marcus in Ytalico, Lucas in Siro et Paulus in Greco. Et cum usque hodie secundum istas linguas et plures iuxta doctrinam fidei scripture in diversis contratis conficiunt, patet conclusio.

But as to the form of the words, [the lord of Armagh]{g:dominus-ardmacanus} makes clear in [book] 9 of *On the Questions of the Armenians*, through many chapters, how no form of words, as to languages and names, is generally required, since the authors wrote variously as to languages: as Matthew in Hebrew, Mark in Italic, Luke in Syriac and Paul in Greek.[^12] And since to this day they [consecrate]{g:conficere} according to these languages and more, following the [doctrine]{g:doctrina} of the faith of scripture, in diverse [districts]{g:contrata}, the [conclusion]{g:conclusio} is clear.

## ¶8 · 90.13–90.22

> [p. 90] Unde licet sacerdos in Anglico et aliis linguis barbaris posset conficere, tamen peccaret inde ex presumpcione postponendo latinum vel linguam quam famosius quoad locum, gentem et tempus approbat sua ecclesia, linguam quoad talem consecracionem contra loci consuetudinem commiscendo; circa limites vero huius difficultatis de gravedine peccati facti in ipsam est infructuosa contencio, cum homo debet nisi urgente magna causa et racionabili se consuetudini laudabili loci quem incolit conformare.

Hence, although a [priest]{g:sacerdos} could consecrate in English and other barbarous languages, yet he would sin thereby out of presumption, [setting aside]{g:postponere} Latin, or the language which his church approves [as best known]{g:famosius} as to place, people and time, mixing the language for such a consecration against the [custom]{g:consuetudo} of the place; but about the [limits]{g:limites} of this difficulty, concerning the gravity of the sin committed against it, contention is fruitless,[^13] since a man ought, unless a great and reasonable cause urges, to conform himself to the laudable custom of the place he inhabits.

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 87.29: The old law had three types of this sacrament:
- 87.31: I. the paschal lamb,
- 88.3: 2. the water from the rock,
- 88.9: 3. the manna.
- 89.4: The words spoken are but instruments; it is God's word that effects the change.
- 89.21: 'This' refers to the bread,
- 90.4: The evangelists used different languages.
- 90.13: In consecrating it is right to follow the established use.

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** John 1:29 (Douay–Rheims). Wyclif's *peccata* is plural, and he drops the second *ecce*; the Vulgate has *ecce qui tollit peccatum mundi* (DR "him who taketh away the sin of the world"). Quoted to the editor's italics.

[^2]: **C.** *Signati pocioris* stands in apposition to *huius sacramenti*: the sacrament is the greater thing signified, of which the lamb was a figure.

[^3]: **S.** Print *Exodi XIIº*; the water from the rock is Exod. 17:1–7 (cf. Num. 20:8–11). Translated as printed. In 1 Cor. 10:4 Wyclif's order *spiritualem potum eundem* does not change the Douay–Rheims English.

[^4]: **S/C.** John 6:59 (Douay–Rheims), from *Non sicut*: the verse's opening *Hic est panis qui de caelo descendit* is not quoted. Augustine, *In Iohannis evangelium tractatus* 26 (Loserth: *Opp.* III/2, 408; LEDGER K04b-1), is paraphrased, not quoted. *Rei sue* (ABC *rei sui*, 88.11) is the reality of the sacrament, set beside the sacrament itself as in the ruled distinction.

[^5]: **T.** *Presencialiter*: C first wrote *principaliter*, which is struck, and another hand gives *presencialiter* in the margin (88.24). The opposition is of time: the fathers merited the opening only from very far off.

[^6]: **C.** *In capite huius capituli*: at the opening of this chapter (chapter 4 begins at p. 83).

[^7]: **G/C.** *Sermo Dei* and *verbum Dei* are the divine titles, "Speech" and "the Word"; *ipsum facit opera* has the neuter of *verbum*, rendered "he". The spoken words (*verba vocalia*) are the instruments, of which "does the works" is said only improperly and equivocally.

[^8]: **C.** The words are spoken one after another (*successiva*) and with pauses (*interpolata*); the question is the instant at which the proposition is true. The answer rests on the thesis that a continuum is composed of what is not quantified (indivisibles), which Wyclif says he has made clear elsewhere, without naming the work (LEDGER K04b-2).

[^9]: **S/C.** Augustine, *In Iohannis evangelium tractatus* 13 (Loserth: *Opp.* III/2, 390; LEDGER K04b-3). *Hoc est Christus* is given as a proposition Augustine grants with any creature demonstrated. The pronoun under discussion is the neuter *hoc* of *Hoc est corpus meum*; the paragraph asks whether it demonstrates the bread (*panis*, masculine: *panis ille*, 90.3).

[^10]: **C.** *Singulariter* (CONTEXTUAL): "alone" will not construe (the proposition is not granted alone); "in a singular way" answers to the *singularis efficacia et significacio* just named.

[^11]: **C.** *Effective*, "by way of effect", stands against *figurative*: the proposition taken as effecting what it says. *Sub habitudine accidencium*: *habitudo* in its "respect" sense, the pronoun demonstrating, in respect of the accidents, the body in heaven; "under the condition of the accidents" is the alternative.

[^12]: **S/C.** *Dominus Armachanus*: Richard FitzRalph, *Summa de questionibus Armenorum*, book 9, "through many chapters", unspecified (LEDGER K04b-4). *Ytalico*: "Italic", that is, Latin. The languages are assigned to the authors as the Latin assigns them.

[^13]: **C.** *Facti in ipsam*: the feminine most likely takes up *consuetudinem*, the custom the sin offends; *linguam* (the approved language) is the alternative.
