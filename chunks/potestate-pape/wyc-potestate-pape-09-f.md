# *De potestate pape*, chapter 9, part 6: the wandering stars, and the primacy not to be sought

## ¶1 · 210.15–212.14

> [p. 210] Quantum ad tercium iugum, quod concernit peccatum dyaboli, dicitur primo quod sunt fluctus feri maris despumantes suas confusiones; quod fit, quando Antichristi sunt instrumenta impii et crudelis dyaboli, cui proprietates maris inseparabiliter sunt coniuncte; tales enim involvunt simplices in caribdim, quando spumant mendacia de sua fantastica potestate; hoc enim erit eis blasfema confusio in die iudicii. Sexto et ultimo dicitur quod tales pseudo sunt sydera volancia; tradunt enim naturales quod ex materia terrestri viscosa ad supremum intersticium aeris exalata fit materia secundum formam et disposicionem variam sublimita; et dicitur, postquam fuerit ignita et mota, nunc stella cadens, nunc draco ardens, et nunc stella cometa. Ita mortaliter ex statu laycali lutoso sunt aliqui ad supremum statum militantis ecclesie exaltati, quos cum malignus tetigerit, sunt succensi superbia, cupiditate et omni genere peccatorum, sed cum summe in Deum, qui est ignis, recalcitrant, decidunt secundum [p. 211] raciones Deo absconditas, nunc uno modo, nunc alio, in formam polipi vel abiecte materie, quibus procella tenebrarum perpetuo parata est post diem iudicii. Prepositi autem et specialiter summus pontifex timerent sibi de sensu hoc mistico et specialiter de superbia vel laude humana, de extollencia sui status; ipsa enim est spuma fantasticum Antichristi, cum ad nichil valet nisi ad frustrandum mercedem Domini dicentis Matth. VI, 2: Amen, dico vobis receperunt mercedem suam, et Joh. V, 44 quomodo potestis credere, qui gloriam ab invicem recipitis, et gloriam que a solo Deo est non queritis? Debemus enim, laudes humanas et occasiones earum aufugere, dicente Paulo papa, prima Tessa. II, 5, 6. 9 Neque, inquit, aliquando fuimus in sermone adulacionis sicut scitis, neque in occasione avaricie, Deus testis est, neque querentes ab hominibus gloriam, operantes continue, ne aliquem vestrum gravaremus, predicavimus in vobis evangelium Dei. Et antequam papa fuerit conversacione contrarius vite Pauli, dimitteret omnem eleccionem, omnem gloriacionem et omnem occupacionem, que hodie consequitur papas nostros. Unde beatus Crysostomus Omelia XXVIII Operis Imperfecti super Matth. XX, 27: Quicumque voluerit inter vos primus esse erit vester servus. Secularem, inquam, primatum desiderare et si racio non est, tamen causa est, quia etsi iustum non est, tamen utile est, primatum autem ecclesiasticum concupiscere neque racio est neque causa, quia neque iustum est neque utile. Quis enim sapiens ultro se subiceret servituti laboris, et quod [p. 212] maius est periculo tali, ut det racionem pro omni ecclesia apud iustum iudicem, nisi forte qui non credit iudicium Dei, nec timet uti abutens primatu suo ecclesiastico seculariter convertat eum in secularem. Ex istis videtur primo, quod non licet papatum vel aliquam dignitatem ecclesiasticam eleccione humana appetere, quia ut sic primatus stulte se supponit periculo, stulte quia non humana eleccio facit ius presidendi, sed promulgat in casu, quo in electo presint virtutum insignia, et insuper constituit se obligaciorem ad populum, nec accrescit exinde profectus, sed periculum, et ideo sancti apostoli non susceperunt primatum talem ab homine.

As to the third [yoke]{g:iugum}, which concerns the sin of the devil, it is said first that they are "raging waves of the sea, foaming out their own confusion"; which comes about when Antichrists are the instruments of the impious and cruel devil, to whom the properties of the sea are inseparably joined; for such men wrap the simple in Charybdis when they foam out lies about their [imaginary]{g:fantasticus} [power]{g:potestas}; for this will be a blasphemous confusion to them on the day of judgement. Sixthly and lastly, therefore, it is said that such false men are "flying stars";[^1] for the natural philosophers teach that from earthy, viscous matter exhaled to the highest interval of the air there comes to be matter sublimed according to varied form and disposition; and it is called, after it has been kindled and moved, at one time a falling star, at another a burning dragon, and at another a comet.[^2] So, to their death, some are exalted from the muddy lay state to the highest state of the [church]{g:ecclesia} militant; and when the evil one has touched them they are kindled with pride, covetousness and every kind of sin; but since they kick back to the utmost against God, who is fire, they fall down, for [211] reasons hidden with God, in one way at one time and in another at another, into the form of a polyp or of cast-off matter, "to whom the storm of darkness" is perpetually prepared after the day of judgement.[^3] And [superiors]{g:prepositi}, and especially the highest pontiff, should fear for themselves from this mystical sense, and especially from pride or human praise, from the exaltation of their state; for it is the imaginary foam of Antichrist,[^4] since it avails for nothing except to frustrate the reward, as the Lord says, Matthew 6:2: "Amen I say to you, they have received their reward", and John 5:44: "How can you believe, who receive glory one from another: and the glory which is from God alone, you do not seek?" For we ought to shun human praises and the occasions of them, as Paul the [pope]{g:papa} says, 1 Thessalonians 2:5, 6, 9: "Neither," he says, "were we at any time in the speech of flattery, as you know, nor in an occasion of covetousness, God is witness, nor seeking glory of men; working continually, lest we should be chargeable to any of you, we preached among you the gospel of God."[^5] And before a pope should be contrary in [manner of life]{g:conversacio} to the life of Paul, he should give up every election, every glorying and every occupation that today attends our popes. Hence blessed Chrysostom, Homily 28 of the *Unfinished Work* on Matthew 20:27: "Whosoever will be first among you shall be your servant. To desire secular [primacy]{g:primatus}," I say, "even if there is no reason, yet there is a cause, because even if it is not just, yet it is useful; but to covet ecclesiastical primacy there is neither reason nor cause, because it is neither just nor useful. For what wise man would of his own accord subject himself to a servitude of labour, and, what [212] is greater, to such a danger, that he must render account for the whole church before a just judge, unless perhaps one who does not believe the judgement of God, nor fears, so that, abusing his ecclesiastical primacy in a secular way, he turns it into a secular one?"[^6] From these things it appears, first, that it is not lawful to seek the papacy or any ecclesiastical dignity by human election, because as such [one seeking] primacy foolishly puts himself in danger: foolishly, because human election does not make the [right]{g:ius} of presiding, but promulgates it in the case where the marks of the virtues are present in the one elected; and moreover he makes himself more bound to the people, nor does profit accrue from it, but danger; and therefore the holy apostles did not accept such primacy from man.[^7]

## ¶2 · 212.15–213.13

> [p. 212] Videtur secundo quod, sive Robertus sive Urbanus, sive quicumque alius, appetit sic preesse ecclesie Anglie, signum probabile est quod sit fugibilis Antichristus; unde beatus Crysostomus ubi supra: Quicumque desideraverit primatum in terra, inveniet confusionem in celo, ut iam inter servos Christi non sit de primatu certamen, non festinet unusquisque, quomodo aliis maior appareat, sed quomodo omnibus inferior videatur, quoniam non qui maior fuerit in honore ille est iustior, sed qui inferior fuerit hic est maior. Ideo primatus in populo non est ab aliquo appetendus. Quomodo igitur non habebit ecclesia nostra talem suspectum, qui et nescit fidem scripture et se ipsum vel familiam suam privatam regere, et tamen presumit se digniorem sciencia et virtute, ut regat tantam ecclesiam? Utinam ecclesia vellet dicere talibus sentenciam Christi et beati Crysostomi Ad talem primatum pertinet respondere Deo pro animabus sibi subditis in die iudicii et servire illis evangelice laboriosius atque humilius, [p. 213] contempto omni seculari commodo vel honore. Quod si Robertus vel alius nobis facere voluerit, non contradicimus, Deus sibi retribuat, unde beatus Crysostomus ubi supra: Principes ecclesie fiunt, ut serviant minoribus suis et ministrent eis quecumque acceperunt a Christo, ut suas utilitates negligant et illorum procurent, ut si opus fuerit nec mori recusent pro salute eorum, sicut fecit Apostolus. Cum igitur hec sit religio paparum, patet quam culpabiles a Deo sunt principes seculi, qui consensu stulto ipsam dissolverint, specialiter promittendo thesaurum regni et legiorum suorum ad ipsos confluere.

It appears, secondly, that whether Robert or Urban or anyone else so seeks to preside over the church of England, it is a probable sign that he is an Antichrist to be fled;[^8] hence blessed Chrysostom in the place cited: "Whoever desires primacy on earth will find confusion in heaven, so that now among the servants of Christ there may be no contest about primacy; let not each one hasten how he may appear greater than others, but how he may seem lower than all; since it is not he who is greater in honour that is more just, but he who is lower is the greater." Therefore primacy among the people is not to be sought by anyone. How then shall our church not hold such a man suspect, who knows neither the faith of scripture nor how to rule himself or his own private [household]{g:familia}, and yet presumes himself worthier in knowledge and virtue, so as to rule so great a church? Would that the church would tell such men the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of Christ and of blessed Chrysostom: to such a primacy it belongs to answer to God for the souls subject to it on the day of judgement, and to serve them in the manner of the gospel more laboriously and more humbly, [213] scorning every secular advantage or honour.[^9] And if Robert or another will do this for us, we do not gainsay it; may God reward him. Hence blessed Chrysostom in the place cited: "The princes of the church are made so that they may serve their inferiors and minister to them whatever they have received from Christ, so that they may neglect their own interests and see to theirs, so that if need be they may not refuse even to die for their salvation, as the Apostle did." Since, then, this is the religion of popes, it is clear how culpable before God are the princes of the world who by foolish consent have dissolved it, especially by promising that the treasure of the realm and of their lieges should flow to them.[^10]

## ¶3 · 213.14–213.28

> [p. 213] Tercio patet ex dictis quomodo sancti susceperunt primatus huiusmodi, scientes quod humana eleccio vel est primatui suo impertinens vel nociva. Est enim in primatu tali servitus laboriosa et mundo abiecta ac infructuosa, sed meritoria quoad Deum, et illam voluerunt sancti doctores pro fructu premii promerendo. Est secundo in primatu extollencia mundana periculosa et insufficiencia vel negligencia perficiendi officium ruinosa, que paulative augebatur racione dotacionis ecclesie, et illam noluerunt sancti pastores ecclesie; et hinc post dotacionem inolevit religio prelatorum, quod verbo et mente dicerent in talibus eleccionibus nolens volo, quia nolens hoc secundum nefarium volo primum.

Thirdly, it is clear from what has been said how the saints accepted primacies of this kind, knowing that human election is either [irrelevant]{g:impertinens} to their primacy or harmful. For there is in such a primacy a laborious servitude, cast off by the world and unfruitful [in it], but meritorious with respect to God; and this the holy doctors willed, for the fruit of the reward to be merited. There is, secondly, in primacy a dangerous worldly exaltation and a ruinous insufficiency or negligence in performing the office, which increased little by little by reason of the [endowment]{g:dotacio} of the church; and this the holy pastors of the church did not will. And hence, after the endowment, the religion of [prelates]{g:prelatus} grew up, that in such elections they should say in word and mind, "Unwilling, I will": because, not willing this second, wicked thing, I will the first.[^11]

## ¶4 · 213.29–214.2

> [p. 213] Et patet in parte quomodo solvenda sunt dicta Crysostomi et obiectus communes facti in ista materia. Modo autem cautela dyaboli et incuria secularium ad perturbacionem magnam ecclesie, dicitur in facto contrarium, scilicet quod nolens ministerium evangelicum vel mercedem volo primatum et commodum temporale, et necesse [p. 214] est istam perversionem currere quousque hec duo separata fuerint sicut in ecclesia primitiva.

And it is clear in part how the sayings of Chrysostom and the common [objections]{g:objectum} made in this matter are to be resolved. But now, by the [wile]{g:cautela} of the devil and the carelessness of [seculars]{g:secularis}, to the great disturbance of the church, the contrary is said in deed, namely: "Not willing the gospel ministry or the reward, I will the primacy and temporal advantage"; and [214] this perversion must run on until these two have been separated, as in the primitive church.[^12]

## Notes

[^1]: **S/G.** Jude 13: Wyclif here writes *sydera volancia*, "flying stars", where the Vulgate (and his own quotation on p. 208) has *sidera errancia*, "wandering stars". *tales pseudo*: "such false men", a plural; not "the Pseudo" of ch. 6.
[^2]: **S/C.** The meteorology is Aristotle's (*Meteorologica* I.4: shooting stars and "torches" from an exhalation kindled in the upper air); comets from the same kindled exhalation at I.7; the identification is ours (LEDGER P09f-1). *nunc … nunc*: "at one time … at another". *draco ardens*: the "burning dragon" of the medieval meteorologists.
[^3]: **C/S.** *mortaliter*: "to their death" (or "in mortal sin"). "God, who is fire": Deut. 4:24, Heb. 12:29 ("a consuming fire"). *Deo absconditas*: "hidden with God", or "hidden from God"; the first is likelier. *polipi*: "polyp", the sense is doubtful (perhaps the jelly thought to fall with a shooting star). "To whom the storm of darkness": Jude 13 again.
[^4]: **C.** *spuma fantasticum*: the adjective does not agree with *spuma*; it is construed as "imaginary foam". *extollencia*: "exaltation", the raising of oneself.
[^5]: **T/S.** *Paulo papa*: Loserth notes "ita cod." (211.15): the manuscript calls Paul pope. 1 Thess. 2:5–6, 9 in Wyclif's form: *operantes continue*, "working continually", for the Vulgate's *nocte ac die operantes* (DR "working night and day"); he omits the end of 2:6 and the opening of 2:9. DR's wording is kept where his Latin agrees.
[^6]: **S/C/R.** Pseudo-Chrysostom, *Opus imperfectum in Matthaeum*: Wyclif's "Homily 28"; Loserth: "Recte Homilia XXXV ex cap. XX". The passage is hom. 35, PG 56, 830: *saecularem quidem primatum desiderare, etsi ratio non est, vel causa est … nec timet uti abutens primatu suo ecclesiastico saeculariter, convertat eum in saecularem* (LEDGER P09f-2). Matt. 20:27: Wyclif's *Quicumque* gives "whosoever" (DR "he that will be first"). *inquam* stands outside Loserth's italics, as Wyclif's "I say". *nec timet uti abutens*: *uti* is taken as *ut*, "so that". The manuscripts in the margin (211.25): *Appetere primatum secularem*.
[^7]: **C/T.** *ut sic primatus stulte se supponit periculo*: the subject is taken to be the one who seeks primacy, as with *constituit se* below; possibly "primacy so [sought] foolishly exposes itself to danger". The page foot records readings at 212.10 (*causu*; the manuscripts *prefuit*), apparently against *casu* and *presint*; the print is translated.
[^8]: **R.** Robert of Geneva (Clement VII) and Urban VI, the rival popes of the schism of 1378; Loserth's side-note speaks of "the rival popes" (LEDGER P09f-3). *fugibilis*: "to be fled".
[^9]: **S/C.** "In the place cited": the same homily of the *Opus imperfectum*, PG 56, 829–30 (*quicumque autem desiderat primatum in terra, inveniet confusionem in caelo … sed qui justior fuerit, ille est major*, where Wyclif has *inferior*), and so are the words at 213.4 (*principes autem Ecclesiae fiunt, ut serviant minoribus suis …*) (LEDGER P09f-4). Loserth's italics do not mark the clause "to such a primacy it belongs …", so it is given as Wyclif's statement of the teaching, not as a quotation.
[^10]: **C.** *religio paparum*: the rule binding on popes by their office, not a religious order (GLOSSARY *religio privata*). *a Deo*: "before God". *legiorum*: "lieges".
[^11]: **C.** *nolens volo*: the elect's formula of reluctance, "unwilling, I will"; Wyclif glosses it: not willing the second thing in primacy (worldly exaltation, the ruinous burden), the elect wills the first (the laborious servitude). *servitus* is "servitude", as in Chrysostom's *servituti laboris*.
[^12]: **C.** "The contrary": the formula turned round, refusing the service and willing the primacy. "These two": the gospel ministry and temporal advantage, joined since the endowment.
