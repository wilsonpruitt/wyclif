## Doubtful, left as they are

- **10-f ¶1, p. 240**: *Sed secundo notanda est cecitas*, rendered "Now, secondly". (a), the second of the points opened by *primo* (p. 237), is defensible; "But secondly" (b) would also stand, since the *Sed* turns back from the excursus on the saints' sin.
- **10-g ¶3, p. 243**: *Sed et nos sacerdotes … expectamus*, rendered "And we priests too". Intensive *sed et* ("and also, moreover") adds a further case of confident expectation; it is not a contrast, and "And … too" carries it. Note 10-j ¶1 renders the same idiom "But … also" (*Sed et consolacio quedam mundana*), where the worldly consolation is set against Christ's; both readings fit their places.
- **10-g ¶3, p. 245**: *Sed duplicem ordinem talium ministrorum considera*, rendered "But consider". Either (b), qualifying the necessity of an order of popes just stated, or (a), the next step (the distinction the argument turns on). "But" is defensible.
- **10-g ¶3, p. 245**: *Quamvis autem oporteat … ordinem cesareum*, rendered "Now although". This opens the first member of the twofold distinction just announced; that reads as continuative (c, "And although"), which the card says is never "now". But it can also be taken as the step that brings the caesarean order's premiss to bear on the conclusion *Unde nichil maius hereticum* (p. 246). A real toss-up, left.
- **10-h ¶3, p. 247**: *in Anglia autem necessitantur iurare Urbano*, rendered "and in England". Paris against England could be (b), "but in England"; the English carries the clash with the next clause, "although those two are directly contrary", so (c) is defensible.
- **10-h ¶4, p. 248**: *Sentencia autem catholica quam sepe repecii*, rendered "And the catholic teaching". It could be (b), the catholic teaching set against the legists' principles and the prelates' oaths, or (c), Wyclif supplying the teaching he has just asked the prelates to declare. "And" is defensible.
- **10-j ¶1, p. 255**: *Prescitus autem cum sit membrum dyaboli*, rendered "And the foreknown man". It could be (a): Christ's unity holds all good pastors, *now* the foreknown is a member of the devil, so Robert is not reckoned a member. But there is no *ergo*, and the sentence closes the Augustine step. (c) stands.
