# *De potestate pape*, chapter 5, part 2: charity would leave civil lordship to the laity

## ¶1 · 83.28–86.17

> [p. 83] Ex istis quarto colligitur, cum quilibet ex lege caritatis debet appetere maius bonum, sed maius bonum foret tam clero quam laicis, quod laici occuparent totam civilitatem secularis dominii, videtur quod clerici ex caritatis regulis hoc optarent. Ipsi enim forent exinde exonerandi et layci forent circa laycum officium in temporalibus meritorie occupati, quomodo igitur non dictaret [p. 84] caritas, que non querit que sua sunt, quod tota civilitas foret contenta in manibus laycorum. Nam nulli dubium quin secularia iudicia, contenciones, pugne et alia laboriosa manualia sunt laycis plus pertinencia, illud igitur magis bonum debet clerus appetere. Et quantum ad arbitrium cleri variantis in perfeccione ac utilitate bonorum seculi, standum est plus Christi et apostolorum arbitrio et non arbitrio nostrorum spiritualiter infirmorum. Et patet horrenda dampnacio istius sentencie tamquam heretice, quod seculares iuvent clericos exonerando eos a temporalibus, quibus nimis onerati sunt ex stultis dotacionibus dominorum. Et quantum ad obiectum quod per idem, cum layci conversi tempore apostolorum vixerunt exproprietarie, clerici debent ex habundanciori caritate auferre bona a laycis, constat quod pauci layci perfecte conversi per apostolos reliquerunt omnia temporalia sua et devenerunt clerici illiterati; et sic apostoli titulo elemosine auferebant illa relinquentes imperfectum, scilicet proprietatem civilem ut eis inconsonum. Et utinam nostri episcopi recolerent quomodo apostoli quorum officio funguntur taliter converterint laycos ad perfeccionem regule christiane, relinquendo laycis coaccionem civilem. Perfecti enim debent niti assimilare inferiores ad suam perfeccionem et non per media illicita inducendo in eorum imperfeccionem. Papa igitur qui debet esse proximus vicarius Christi ducendo christianum exercitum ad eius sanctitatem debet ista principaliter facere tam opere quam sermone. Item, secundum principia philosophorum melius est [p. 85] philosophari quam ditari, sed quam bonum est philosophari tam bonum est amare sapienciam incarnatam, igitur melius est ipsam diligere quam ditari. Sed de quanto christianus diligit legem suorum consiliorum sequendo ipsum in moribus, de tanto diligit ipsum, igitur hoc est melius quam ditari, cum stat hominem esse peccato miserrimum cum quantumlibet mundi multis diviciis. Ideo (ut sepe dixi) paupertas evangelica non stat in non habere temporalia, sed in moderate habere quoad modum habendi et multitudinem habiti propter amorem Christi, si quod quantumcumque temporalia iuverint ad faciendum opus viatoris officii, tantum precise Deus vult ut habeat. Unde cum in statu innocencie et statu patrie deficiet talis civilitas, videtur quod ipsa sapit imperfeccionem et est officio cleri dissonum; nec enim est necessarium ad humiliacionem nec evangelisacionem nec aliam sacerdotalem ministracionem sed ad vite ministracionem ad quam pauca ex promissione Dei sufficiunt. Ideo sapiens Proverb. XXX, 8 petit a Domino: Divicias et mendicitatem ne dederis michi, sed tantum viciui meo prebe necessaria. Consideremus igitur equalitatem iusticie, si aliquid habemus preter necessarium ad nostrum officium, et indubie senciemus quod superfluit nobis quodcumque civile dominium, cum multi clerici vivunt operosius sine tali; quare igitur non nos qui debemus scire quod Dei filius naturalis venit in mundum ob amorem nostrum in artissima paupertate, et hoc ad exemplandum nobis de sorte sua, ut ob eius amorem ac nostrum immensum commodum conversemur conformiter. Nec dubium quin sic multiplicaretur clerus ut olym, cum [p. 86] talis non foret familia secularibus onerosa nec in sustentacione subducto globo monstruose multitudinis sumptuosa. Si enim private elemosine, quando mundus fuit irreligiosior et clerus necessarior, suffecerunt ad sustentandum totum clerum Christi, quanto magis hodie superaddite oblaciones et decime. Subducamus itaque in clero avariciam, fastum et officio suo impertinens ac superfluum, et non dubium quin satis sufficerent et commodum spirituale ex hinc in secularibus et clericis circumcrescens infinitum excederet prosperitatem et gloriam mundanam ex opposito contingentem. Affecciones itaque personales caritati contrarie faciunt quod ordinacio Christi contempnitur et tot scripturarum ac sanctorum testimonia vel sunt sinistre glossata vel a clero infideliter parvipensa.

From these, fourth, it is gathered that, since everyone by the law of [charity]{g:caritas} ought to desire the greater good, and now the greater good, for clergy and laity alike, would be that the laity should occupy the whole [civil standing]{g:civilitas} of secular [lordship]{g:dominium}, it seems that the [clerics]{g:clericus} would wish this by the rules of charity.[^1] For they would thereby be relieved [of the burden], and the laity would be meritoriously occupied with the lay office in temporal things; how then would [84] charity, which "seeketh not her own", not dictate that the whole civil standing should be held in the hands of the laity?[^2] For no one doubts that secular judgements, disputes, fights and other laborious manual [tasks] belong more to the laity; therefore the [clergy]{g:clerus} ought to desire that greater good. And as for the judgement of the clergy, which wavers on the perfection and usefulness of the goods of the world, we must stand rather by the judgement of Christ and the apostles, and not by the judgement of our spiritually sick. And the condemnation of this [teaching]{g:sentencia} as heretical is plainly horrible: [the teaching] that seculars should help clerics by relieving them of the temporal goods with which they are overburdened through the foolish [endowments]{g:dotacio} of lords.[^3] And as for the objection that, by the same token, since lay converts in the apostles' time lived [without property]{g:exproprietarie}, clerics ought out of more abundant charity to take goods away from the laity, it is evident that the few laymen perfectly converted by the apostles left all their [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} and became unlettered clerics; and so the apostles took those [goods] by [title]{g:titulus} of [alms]{g:elemosina}, [the converts] leaving what is imperfect, namely civil property, as unbefitting them.[^4] And would that our [bishops]{g:episcopus} recalled how the apostles, whose office they discharge, converted laymen in such a way to the perfection of the Christian rule, leaving civil coercion to the laity. For the perfect ought to strive to make their inferiors like themselves in their perfection, and not, by unlawful means, by leading [them] into their imperfection.[^5] Therefore the [pope]{g:papa}, who ought to be the nearest [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius-christi}, leading the Christian army to his holiness, ought to do these things principally, in deed as well as in word.

Again, according to the principles of the philosophers it is better [85] to philosophize than to be [enriched]{g:ditare}; now it is as good to love incarnate wisdom as it is to philosophize; therefore it is better to love it than to be enriched.[^6] Now in so far as a Christian loves the law of his counsels, following him in conduct, in so far he loves him; therefore this is better than to be enriched, since it is possible for a man to be most wretched through sin with however many of the world's riches. Therefore (as I have often said) evangelical poverty does not consist in not having temporal goods, but in having them moderately, as to the manner of having and the quantity of what is had, for the love of Christ, [so] that however much temporal goods help toward doing the work of the [wayfarer]{g:viator}'s office, just so much precisely God wills that he should have.[^7] Hence, since in the state of innocence and the state of the [homeland]{g:patria} such civil standing will be lacking, it seems that it savours of imperfection and is out of keeping with the office of the clergy; for it is not necessary to humbling, nor to preaching the gospel, nor to any other priestly ministry, but to the ministry of life, for which, by God's promise, little suffices.[^8] Therefore the wise man asks of the Lord, Proverbs 30:8: "Give me neither riches nor beggary, but give me only the necessaries of life."[^9] Let us then consider the equality of justice, whether we have anything beyond what is necessary to our office, and without doubt we shall perceive that any civil lordship whatever is superfluous to us, since many clerics live more laboriously without such; why then do not we [do so], who ought to know that the natural Son of God came into the world for love of us in the narrowest poverty, and this to give us an example from his lot, so that for love of him and for our own immense advantage we should live in conformity [with him]?[^10] Nor is there doubt that the clergy would then be multiplied as of old, since [86] such a [household]{g:familia} would not be burdensome to seculars, nor costly to maintain, the mass of a monstrous multitude being taken away. For if private alms, when the world was more irreligious and the clergy more necessary, sufficed to maintain the whole clergy of Christ, how much more today the offerings and [tithes]{g:decime} added on top! So let us take away from the clergy avarice, pomp, and what is foreign to its office and superfluous, and there is no doubt that [these] would amply suffice, and the spiritual advantage growing up from this around seculars and clerics would infinitely exceed the prosperity and worldly glory that comes from the opposite [course].[^11] So personal affections contrary to charity bring it about that Christ's [ordinance]{g:ordinacio} is despised, and that so many testimonies of the scriptures and of the saints are either perversely glossed or faithlessly made light of by the clergy.[^12]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 83.28: who should desire all civil rule to be in the laity.
- 84.23: Would that our bishops like the apostles set an example of perfection and drew others to it.
- 85.8: Evangelical poverty does not consist in having no wealth, but in the spirit in which wealth is held,
- 85.25: For our work as priests we need no civil dominion.
- 86.3: Alms sufficed for the priests when Christians were fewer and there were no tithes.
- 86.14: Christ's ordinances are explained away or disregarded.

## Notes

[^1]: **C/G.** *civilitas*, "civil standing" (GLOSSARY): here the holding of civil lordship as a whole, possession with the rule that goes with it. The minor premiss (*sed maius bonum foret*) stands inside the *cum* clause; "now" marks it, and *videtur* draws the conclusion.

[^2]: **S.** 1 Corinthians 13:5 (Loserth's reference, 84.1), DR "seeketh not her own".

[^3]: **C.** *Et patet horrenda dampnacio istius sentencie tamquam heretice*: construed "the condemnation … is plainly horrible"; also possible, "the horrible condemnation … is evident". *dampnacio* is here a judicial condemnation of a teaching, not "damnation" (GLOSSARY *dampnacio*); the *quod* clause states the condemned teaching. Perhaps the papal condemnation of Wyclif's conclusions in 1377 [verify] (LEDGER P05b-2).

[^4]: **C.** *quod per idem …*: the objection is that, if lay converts gave up their property, clerics should all the more take goods from laymen. *relinquentes* has no expressed noun; construed of the converts, who left civil property, not of the apostles.

[^5]: **T/C.** *inducendo* (84.28): so the print; the manuscripts read *induendo* (Loserth), "putting on [themselves] their imperfection", i.e. the perfect taking on the civil property of their inferiors. Translated as printed; *eorum* is the inferiors'.

[^6]: **S/T.** The maxim is Aristotle, *Topics* III.2 (118a10–11), "to philosophize is better than to make money", as current in the schools (LEDGER P05b-1, to be checked). At 85.4 the manuscripts read *ditare*; the print's *ditari* is translated.

[^7]: **T/R.** *si quod* (85.9): so the print; read as *sic quod*, "so that" (our construal), since *si* gives no sense. B in the margin (85.8): *Paupertas evangelica in quo stat*, "In what evangelical poverty consists".

[^8]: **C.** *humiliacionem*: "humbling" (perhaps humility); *vite ministracionem*, the supply of what life needs. *necessarium* refers to civil lordship.

[^9]: **S.** Proverbs 30:8: Wyclif reverses the Vulgate's order (*mendicitatem et divitias*) and has *prebe* for *tribue*; DR's wording, in his order.

[^10]: **C.** *quare igitur non nos …*: the question has no verb; "[do so]" supplies the sense of the clause before (live without civil lordship).

[^11]: **C.** *quanto magis hodie superaddite oblaciones et decime*: elliptical; *sufficerent* is understood. Loserth's side-note (86.3) adds "there were no tithes"; the Latin says only that offerings and tithes are now "added on top".

[^12]: **C.** *ordinacio Christi*: "Christ's ordinance" (GLOSSARY), his appointment that the clergy live without civil lordship.
