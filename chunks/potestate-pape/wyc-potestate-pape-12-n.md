# *De potestate pape*, chapter 12, part 14: the papacy is service, and the name of pope is equivocal

## ¶1 · 365.1–367.3

> [p. 365] Hic possumus palpare si plus diligimus Christum sequendo eius vestigia, quam sacerdotes cesareos propter mundum. Et hec racio (ut credo) movit Augustinum epistola ad Cirillum, quando dixit, postquam allocutus est beatum Jeronymum iam mortuum: Testor Deum, quod a tempore illo pocius desideravi subesse quam preesse. Nam iuxta legem et diffinicionem Christi Matth. XX: Maior, superior vel prelatus ecclesie, debet subesse ministerio suis subditis, que servitus dum defuerit prelato cesareo non est papa, non enim habet cesar vel Deus potestatem sic mutandi officia prelatorum ecclesie, stante vero nomine eorum quod Christus instituit; Deus enim non potest in contradiccionem, cum tunc foret contrarius sibi ipsi. Ideo licet cesar ordinet, quod regulariter episcopus suus presit aliis episcopis, recipiendo a toto populo honores mundanos, et totus populus subsit generaliter suo decreto, non propterea sequitur quod sit papa; hoc enim potest competere layco ut cesari vel alii abiecto membro dyaboli. Racio igitur papatus consistit in humiliori officio ministrandi, sicut stetit in beato Petro et ceteris, quod solius Dei est creature concedere. Et patet, quod magis periculosa mutacio regis superbie stat in hoc quod excecat homines, mutando sacerdotes ad contrarium officii quod Christus eis instituit. Equivocato enim officio necesse est ut producant ecclesiam et fructus equivocatos. Et patet generaliter de omni curato sive presbitero quod a signo valde probabili indigne appetit pastoris officium, quando tam [p. 366] remote, tam sumptuose et periculose laborat ad primatum ecclesiasticum obtinendum, quia si pure vel principaliter intendit edificacionem ecclesie, plana est via, brevior, securior et Deo placencior, laborando indiffinite sicut fecerunt apostoli. Quod si primatum pure aut primo intendit, inhabilitat se ad omnem statum ecclesie, cum preponit regem superbie regi vero; et sic videtur quod ecclesia regulanda ut olim eligeret sibi presbiterum vel quod apostoli infidelis gentis constituant sibi presbiteros, quos percepto veneno dyaboli debent abicere. Et patet quam extraneum est a Romano pontifice symoniace principatus ecclesiis assignare; hoc enim foret minus dissonum in milite vel alio populari; ex quibus convincitur quod papa tam perplexe se onerans in tam alieno officio atque ambiguo, propter reputatum commodum temporale nimis gravat suum officium, cum fatue reddit se participem criminis hominum, quos sic prefert. Sic eciam dicitur ampliare potestatem eorum quam Deus minuit vel non causat, et restringere potestatem eorum, quam Deus amplificat. Quomodo igitur respondebit Deo stultus tam precipitanter et blasfeme sibi contrarius; non enim est fundabile quod habet potestatem in episcopis, ipsis sciencioribus et copiosius claves ecclesie habentibus potestatem ordinis sic restringere vel laxare. Nam de potestate propria hoc non potest, quos igitur funes habet circa potestatem alienam maiorem sua potestate, quam Deus dat appropiate ad prodessendum ecclesie? Non igitur valet fingere blasfemiam ad magnificandum suam potenciam, sed necesse habet stabilire ecclesiam in statu et religione primeva, quam Christus instituit, [p. 367] non excedendo limites in sacramentis et sacramentalibus evangelicis, quia tunc est indubie Antichristus.

Here we can feel out whether we love Christ more, by following his footsteps, than [we love] [caesarean priests]{g:sacerdos-cesarius} for the world's sake. And this reason (as I believe) moved Augustine in the letter to Cyril, when, after he had addressed blessed Jerome, now dead, he said: "I call God to witness that from that time I have desired rather to be under than to be over."[^1] For according to the law and definition of Christ, Matthew 20, the greater, the superior or the [prelate]{g:prelatus} of the [church]{g:ecclesia} must be under his subjects in ministry;[^2] and while this [servitude]{g:servitus} is wanting to a caesarean prelate he is not [pope]{g:papa}. For neither Caesar nor God has [power]{g:potestas} so to change the offices of the prelates of the church while yet their name, which Christ instituted, stands;[^3] for God cannot [do] what is a contradiction, since then he would be contrary to himself. So although Caesar ordain that his bishop [as a rule]{g:regulariter} preside over other bishops, receiving worldly honours from the whole people, and that the whole people be generally subject to his decree, it does not on that account [follow]{g:sequitur} that he is pope; for this can belong to a layman, as to Caesar or to another abject [member]{g:membrum} of the devil. The nature of the papacy therefore consists in the humbler office of ministering, as it stood in blessed Peter and the others; and this it is God's alone to grant to a creature. And it is clear that the more dangerous change wrought by the king of pride stands in this, that he blinds men, changing priests to the contrary of the office which Christ instituted for them. For when the office is made [equivocal]{g:equivoce}, they must produce an equivocal church and equivocal fruits. And it is clear generally of every [curate]{g:curatus} or [presbyter]{g:presbiter} that, by a very probable sign, he seeks the shepherd's office unworthily when he labours so [366] far off, at such cost and with such danger, to obtain ecclesiastical [primacy]{g:primatus}; for if he purely or principally intends the building up of the church, the way is plain, shorter, safer and more pleasing to God: labouring without fixed bounds, as the apostles did.[^4] But if he intends primacy purely or first, he disqualifies himself for every [state]{g:status} of the church, since he puts the king of pride before the true king; and so it seems that the church, to be ruled as of old, should choose a presbyter for itself, or that the apostles of an [unbelieving]{g:infidelis} people should appoint presbyters for them, whom, once the poison of the devil is perceived, they should cast off. And it is clear how foreign it is to the Roman pontiff to assign principalities to churches simoniacally;[^5] for this would be less discordant in a knight or another man of the people; from which it is proved that the pope, burdening himself so [perplexedly]{g:perplexus} in an office so alien and so doubtful, for the sake of a reputed temporal advantage, burdens his own office too heavily, since he foolishly makes himself a partaker in the crime of the men whom he so prefers. So too he is said to enlarge the power of those men which God diminishes or does not cause, and to restrict the power of those men which God amplifies. How then will the fool answer God, so headlong and so blasphemously contrary to himself? For there is no ground for holding that he has power over bishops, who are themselves more knowing and more abundantly have the keys of the church, so to restrict or [loose]{g:laxare} their [power of order]{g:potestas-ordinis}. For he cannot do this by his own power; what cords then has he about another's power, greater than his own power, which God gives fittingly for profiting the church? He may not therefore feign blasphemy to magnify his own [power]{g:potencia}, but he must establish the church in the primitive state and religion which Christ instituted, [367] not exceeding the [limits]{g:limites} in the gospel sacraments and [sacramentals]{g:sacramentalia}; for then he is undoubtedly Antichrist.

## ¶2 · 367.4–367.13

> [p. 367] Sed obicitur per hoc quod, si papa debet esse sanctissimus viatorum et aliter non est papa, sequitur quod omne peccatum mortale vel veniale debet degradare papam, quod est contra legem distinccione XL: Si papa, in qua dicitur quod solum debet deponi pro heresi. Et iterum videtur idem de regibus, episcopis et quibuscumque ministris ecclesie, ymo summus sacerdos in lege veteri non fuit regulariter sanctissimus, cum sepe fuit pollutus.

But [it is objected, by appeal to]{g:obicitur-per} this, that if the pope must be the holiest of [wayfarers]{g:viator} and otherwise is not pope, it follows that every sin, mortal or venial, must degrade the pope; which is against the law in distinction 40, *Si papa*, where it is said that he is to be deposed only for heresy.[^6] And again the same seems [to hold] of kings, [bishops]{g:episcopus} and any ministers of the church whatever; indeed the [high priest]{g:summus-sacerdos} in the old law was not as a rule the holiest, since he was often polluted.

## ¶3 · 367.14–368.10

> [p. 367] Hic dicitur quod maior pars huius difficultatis stat in signacione termini magistralis, ut aliqui intelligunt papam, ut dicit autonomastice super quoscumque alios mirabilem sanctitatem, sicut Christus se habuit ad suos apostolos. Et sic intelligunt homines de Romano pontifice in hiis que lucrum et honorem mundanam sapiunt, sed non in vita secundum forum consciencie quoad Deum. Et isto modo loquuntur tres sancti doctores predicti. Alii autem intelligunt papam, ut dicit episcopum supereminentem in aliqua sanctitate, ut Ambrosius, Augustinus et ceteri vocati sunt pape. Sed postquam declinavit clerus ad civilitatem, vocati sunt quicumque pape, qui ad decidendum principaliter causas ecclesie seculares sunt humanitus instituti. Et super ista equivocacione currunt raciones, nam Christus fuit papa, quem degradaret quodcumque peccatum, ideo stultum foret christianum ea intencione assumere [p. 368] nomen pape. Ideo caverunt de hoc Petrus, Gregorius et alii sancti, ignorantes si sunt membra dyaboli. In medio vero acceperant sibi sancti episcopi nomen pape, et in tercio propter blasfemum fastum et questum assumpserunt sibi confuse illud nomen prelati cesarei ultra auctoritatem novi vel veteris testamenti. Unde secus est de nomine pape et aliis nominibus primo officiorum, cum papa dicit principaliter preeminenciam sanctitatis. Et patet ex equivocacione solucio.

[The answer]{g:dicitur} here is that the greater part of this difficulty stands in the [signification]{g:signacio} of a [masters' term]{g:termini-magistrales}:[^7] for some understand "pope" as it signifies, [by antonomasia]{g:autonomatice}, a marvellous holiness above all others whatever, as Christ stood toward his apostles.[^8] And so men understand [it] of the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex} in those things that savour of worldly gain and honour, but not in life according to the court of conscience before God. And in this way the three holy doctors aforesaid speak.[^9] But others understand "pope" as it signifies a bishop supereminent in some holiness, as Ambrose, Augustine and the rest were called popes. But after the clergy declined to [civil standing]{g:civilitas}, any were called popes who are humanly instituted principally for deciding the secular causes of the church. And the arguments run upon this [equivocation]{g:equivocacio}; for Christ was pope, whom any sin whatever would degrade; so it would be foolish for a Christian to take [368] the name of pope with that intention. So Peter, Gregory and other saints were wary of this, not knowing whether they are members of the devil. But in the middle [sense] holy bishops had taken the name of pope to themselves, and in the third, out of blasphemous pride and gain, they assumed to themselves [indistinctly]{g:confuse} that name of a caesarean prelate, beyond the [authority]{g:auctoritas} of the new or the old testament. Hence it is otherwise with the name of pope than with other names that are primarily names of offices, since "pope" signifies principally a [preeminence]{g:preeminencia} of holiness.[^10] And the solution is clear from the equivocation.

## ¶4 · 368.11–368.14

> [p. 368] Secundo obicitur per hoc, quod iuxta hanc viam quilibet debet esse papa, ut debet esse sanctissimus viatorum. Debet enim esse papa et quocumque iam viante sanctior.

Secondly it is objected, by appeal to this, that on this way every man ought to be pope, as he ought to be the holiest of wayfarers. For he ought to be pope and holier than anyone now [on the road]{g:viantes}.

## ¶5 · 368.15–368.34

> [p. 368] Hic dicitur quod logica secundum sensum compositum et divisum in ista materia est cavenda. Non enim est aliqua sanctitas qua prepolleret peccator, quin debet esse sanctior, quia aliter non peccaret. Verumtamen nec in statu vie nec statu lapsus debet homo composite esse sanctissimus viatorum, quia subducto omni peccato foret gradus excessuum sanctitatum. Sed post lapsum non est alius sanctus vivens vitam communem quin debet esse sancior. Sed ut credo quilibet predestinatus debet finire in gradu sanctitatis non summo sed sibi debito. Unde luciferina presumpcio videtur quod christianus accipiat sibi nomen talis preeminencie sanctitatis. Ideo sancti concorditer illud respuerant. Quid igitur moveret Romanum episcopum ultra obligacionem apud Deum absconditam ex professione humana spontanea sibi assumere tale nomen; professio namque et officium mutantur ab officio et more apostolico ad legem et officium Antichristi.

The answer here is that in this matter one must beware of the logic of the [composite and divided sense]{g:sensus-compositus}. For there is no holiness in which a sinner may excel but that he ought to be holier, since otherwise he would not sin. Nevertheless, neither in the state of the way nor in the state of the fall ought a man, in the composite [sense], to be the holiest of wayfarers, since, with all sin taken away, there would [still] be a gradation of holinesses exceeding one another.[^11] But after the fall there is no other saint living the common life but ought to be holier. But, as I believe, every [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} [man] must end in a degree of holiness, not the highest, but the one due to him. Hence it seems a Luciferian presumption that a Christian take to himself the name of such a preeminence of holiness. So the saints with one accord rejected it. What then should move the [Roman bishop]{g:romanus-episcopus}, beyond an obligation hidden with God, to assume such a name to himself by a spontaneous human profession? For the profession and the office are changed from the apostolic office and custom to the law and office of Antichrist.

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** The letter to Cyril on Jerome's death is the pseudo-Augustinian *Epistola ad Cyrillum de magnificentiis Hieronymi*, to be checked (LEDGER P12n-1). Loserth (365.4) thinks rather of Augustine, *Ep.* 134 (to Apringius), *cui non tam praeesse quam prodesse desidero* (LEDGER P12n-2). **T.** The manuscripts read *Augustinus*; *Augustinum* is Loserth's.
[^2]: **S.** Matt. 20:26–27 (DR "whosoever is the greater among you, let him be your minister"), given in Wyclif's words, so not quoted. **R.** B in the margin (365.8): *Prelatus vere non est dum viciose deficit a ministerio*, "he is not truly a prelate while he fails viciously in ministry".
[^3]: **C.** *Stante vero nomine eorum*: we take *vero* as the particle ("while yet their name stands"), so that the contradiction is a changed office under an unchanged name; it could also be the adjective, "while their true name stands".
[^4]: **C.** *Indiffinite*: without bounds of place or charge, as the apostles laboured where they were sent; set against the distant primacy sought at Rome.
[^5]: **C.** *Principatus ecclesiis assignare*: to give churches their rulers, i.e. to provide prelates (Loserth's side-note, "The pope should not appoint prelates").
[^6]: **S.** *Decretum* D.40 c.6 *Si papa* (Loserth; the modern form is ours) (LEDGER P12n-3). Loserth italicises the clause *quod solum debet deponi pro heresi* as the canon's; we leave it unquoted, since Wyclif reports it.
[^7]: **T.** *Magistralis* is Loserth's text; A reads *ingracilis* (367.15). **R.** B in the margin (367.10): *Papa quid signat*, "what 'pope' signifies".
[^8]: **C.** *Dicit* here, and below (*ut dicit episcopum*, *papa dicit … preeminenciam*), is "signifies", the logicians' use (Loserth's side-note: "connotes").
[^9]: **C.** The three doctors are Jerome, Bernard and Lincoln, cited at 361.1–2.
[^10]: **C.** *Aliis nominibus primo officiorum*: other names that signify an office first, whereas "pope" first signifies holiness.
[^11]: **C.** *Foret gradus excessuum sanctitatum*, literally "there would be a degree of excesses of holinesses": even without sin, holiness would come in degrees, so no one is bound to the highest.
