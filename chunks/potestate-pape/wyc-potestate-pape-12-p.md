# *De potestate pape*, chapter 12, part 16: the king's justice: the twenty-two conditions of a king

## ¶1 · 373.29–377.23

> [p. 373] Ex istis colligitur talis racio ex lege primi mandati secunde tabule, omnis homo debet honorare sanctam matrem ecclesiam, sed honor eius precipuus foret auferre ab ea turpitudinis maculam, servando in ea statum tam dignum et nobilem et tuendo pristinam libertatem, igitur omnis homo et specialiter secularis dominus ac [p. 374] superior clericus hoc tenetur. Si igitur homo sit excommunicatus atque gravis sacrilegus qui spoliat ecclesiam bonis fortune et sacris ecclesie ornamentis, quanto magis qui extinguit in ecclesia vel aufert ab ea iocale preciosius et infinitum? quodammodo magis sacrum, cuiusmodi sunt status, privilegium et libertas, que Christus dedit ecclesie cleri sui. Et hec excommunicacio sacrilega perturbat nimirum totam ecclesiam militantem; nam tam auferentes quam consencientes sunt heretici a Deo maledicti, et eo gravius quo sic honorare matrem suam foret facilius. Nam via Domini est consolativa, plana et facilis, sed via dyaboli est litigativa, sumptuosa atque difficilis. Et istum scrupulum detegeret clerus mundi principibus, quia illorum est extirpare hos nidos hereticos. Melius enim foret et plus ad pacem nostram, quod non foret in Anglia domus vel prepositus sic possessionatus, quam quod sic duraret in ipsa hoc excomunicatum et hereticum sacrilegium antiquatum. Licet enim sit tam contrarium profectui populi et possessioni secularium dominorum induratum et tam diutine vix potest nisi per miraculum semoveri, et illi quos rex conduxit carius ad instruendum eum in hac fide proditorie plus resistunt. Et idem est iudicium de fratrum collegiis, que in magna parte fovent hoc facinus vel defendunt. Sicut enim Machomed habuit duos complices, scilicet Hebreum peritum in lege veteri, et Sergium monachum subdolum christianum, ita dicitur hodie prelatos cesareos habere ex uno latere peritos in lege cesarea et ex alio latere ypocritam exproprietarium, vocatum sacre pagine [p. 375] professorem; foret (inquam) copia cleri purgati, ut in vitis patrum priorum, clero Christi secundum eius privilegia, libertates Christi et regulas conservante. Nichil enim utile, iustum atque legitimum est alicui statui ecclesie vel legi, quem Christus instituit, contrarium, dampniferum vel nocivum, quia sic Christus foret in membris et legibus sibi ipsi contrarius. Dotacio igitur cleri non foret plus hodie quam tempore Christi contraria dominio seculari; et multo evidencius non foret contenciosa contraria sibi ipsi. Regum itaque est ad pacificacionem regni sui tales sectas et ritus novellos extinguere. Vel aliter quomodo stabit regnum? Domus enim supra domum cadet, tam in contencionibus quam pugnis spiritualibus aut corporalibus; tam inter religiosos quam laycos ac mixtim, cum forent sibi ipsis contrarii ex consensu peccati, quod omnino facit subiectum contrarium sibi ipsi. Nec dissonat sed consonat istis dictum Augustini in de Duodecim Gradibus Abusionum nono capitulo ubi dicit, quod regem non iniquum sed correctorem iniquorum esse oportet. Inde in semet ipso dignitatem sui nominis custodiret, nomen enim regis intellectualiter retinet, ut subiectis omnibus rectoris officium procuret. Ex isto plane sequitur: Si totus clerus regni habet aliquem regem suum, debet subesse eius regimini; ut patet super Jeronymum Omelia XI. Consequenter ponit iste sanctus viginti duas proprietates, que debent regibus convenire. Qualiter, inquit, alios corrigere poterit, qui proprios mores, ne iniqui sint, non corrigit, quoniam iusticia regis exaltatur solium [p. 376] et in veritate solidantur gubernacula populorum. Iusticia vero regis est neminem iniuste per potenciam opprimere, sine personarum accepcione inter virum et proximum suum iuste iudicare, advenis, pupillis et viduis defensorem esse, cohibere furta, adulteria punire, iniquos non exaltare, impudicos et histriones non nutrire, impios de terra perdere, patricidas et prave viventes vivere non sinere, ecclesias defendere, pauperes elemosinis alere, iustos super regni negocia constituere, senes sapientes et sobrios consiliarios habere, magorum, ariolorum, phitonissarum supersticionibus non contendere, iracundiam differre, patriam fortiter et iuste contra adversarios defendere, per omnia in Deo confidere, prosperitatibus animam non elevare, cuncta adversa pacienter tollerare, filios suos non sinere impie agere, certis horis oracionibus insistere, ante horas congruas non gustare cibum. Ve enim terre, cuius rex puer est et cuius principes mane comedunt. Hec regni prosperitatem in presenti faciunt et regem ad celestia regna meliora perducunt. Et post narrat iste sanctus duodecim condiciones, que ex opposito consequuntur: Qui, inquit, regnum non secundum legem hanc dispensat, multas nimirum adversitates in imperio tollerabit. Idcirco enim pax sepe ipsorum rumpitur, offendicula eciam de regno suscitantur, terrarum fructus diminuuntur, servicia populorum prepediuntur, multi et varii dolores prosperitatem regni inficiunt, carorum et liberorum mors tristiciam confert, hostium incursus et provincias undique vastant, bestie armentorum et pecorum greges dilacerant, tempestas aeris et hyemis turbata rerum fecunditatem et maris ministeria [p. 377] prohibent et aliquando fulminum ictus segetes, arborum flores et pampinos exurunt. Super omnia vero regis iniusticia non solum prosperitatem presentis imperii fuscat, sed eciam filios suos et nepotes, ne post se hereditatem teneant, obscurat. Propter piaculum enim regis Salomonis domus Israhel Dominus de manibus filiorum Israhel dispersit et propter iusticiam regis David lucernam de semine eius in Jerusalem semper reliquit. Ecce quantum iusticia regis seculo valet, intuentibus perspicue patet. Est enim pax populorum tutamentum patrie, immunitas plebis, munimentum gentis, cura languorum, gaudium hominum, temperies aeris, serenitas maris, terre fecunditas, solacium pauperum, hereditas filiorum et sibimet ipsi spes future beatitudinis. Attamen sciat rex: sicut in throno hominum primus constitutus est, sic et in penis, si iusticiam non fecerit, primatum habiturus est, omnes namque quoscumque peccatores in presenti sub se habuerit, supra se modo plagali in pena futura habebit. Et ista bona duodecim cum pena decima tercia moverent regem et populum altrinsecus se iuvare.

From these [points] an argument of this kind is gathered, from the law of the first commandment of the second table:[^1] every man ought to honour holy mother [church]{g:ecclesia}; now her chief honour would be to take from her the stain of foulness, by keeping in her a state so worthy and noble and by protecting her original liberty; therefore every man, and especially a [secular lord]{g:seculares-domini} and a [374] superior cleric, is bound to this. If then a man is excommunicate and gravely sacrilegious who despoils the church of the goods of fortune and of the sacred ornaments of the church, how much more he who extinguishes in the church, or takes from her, a jewel more precious and infinite, in a certain way more sacred,[^2] such as are the [state]{g:status}, [privilege]{g:privilegium} and liberty which Christ gave to the church of his clergy? And this sacrilegious excommunication[^3] without doubt disturbs the whole [church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans}; for both those who take away and those who consent are heretics cursed by God, and the more gravely in that so to honour their mother would be easier. For the way of the Lord is consoling, level and easy, but the way of the devil is litigious, costly and difficult. And [the clergy]{g:clerus} should [expose]{g:detegere} this scruple to the princes of the world, for it is theirs to root out these heretical nests. For it would be better, and more for our peace, that there were in England no house or [superior]{g:prepositi} so [possessioned]{g:possessionatus} than that this excommunicate and heretical sacrilege, grown old, should so endure in it. For although it is so contrary to the progress of the people and to the possession of the secular lords, it is hardened and so long-standing [that] it can scarcely be removed except by a miracle;[^4] and those whom the king has hired at greater cost to instruct him in this faith resist the more, treacherously. And the same judgement holds of the colleges of the [friars]{g:frater}, which in great part foster or defend this [misdeed]{g:facinus}. For as Muhammad had two accomplices, namely a Hebrew skilled in the old law and Sergius, a crafty Christian monk,[^5] so today the [caesarean prelates]{g:prelatus-cesarius} are said to have on one side men skilled in [caesarean law]{g:lex-cesarea}, and on the other side a [propertyless]{g:exproprietarii} hypocrite, called a professor of the sacred [375] page; there would be (I say) [plenty]{g:copia} of purged clergy, as in the lives of the earlier fathers, with the clergy of Christ keeping, according to his privileges, the liberties and rules of Christ.[^6] For nothing useful, just and lawful is contrary, harmful or hurtful to any state of the church, or to [any] law, which Christ instituted; for so Christ would be contrary to himself in his members and his laws. Therefore the [endowment]{g:dotacio} of the clergy would not be more contrary to secular [lordship]{g:dominium} today than in the time of Christ; and much more evidently it would not be contentious, contrary to itself. It belongs therefore to kings, for the pacifying of their [kingdom]{g:regnum}, to extinguish such [sects]{g:secta} and new [rites]{g:ritus}. Or otherwise how shall the kingdom stand? For "house upon house shall fall",[^7] both in contentions and in fights, spiritual or bodily; both among the [religious]{g:religiosi} and among lay people, and the two mixed, since they would be contrary to themselves by consent to sin, which altogether makes the subject contrary to itself. Nor does the saying of Augustine in *On the Twelve Degrees of Abuses*, the ninth chapter, disagree with these things, but agrees with them, where he says that "a king must be not unjust but a corrector of the unjust. Hence he would keep in himself the dignity of his name;[^8] for the name of king holds in its meaning that he should provide the office of ruler for all his subjects." From this it plainly follows: if the whole clergy of a kingdom has any king of its own, it ought to be under his rule, as is clear on Jerome, Homily 11.[^9] Next this saint sets down twenty-two properties which ought to belong to kings. "How", he says, "will he be able to correct others, who does not correct his own ways, lest they be unjust? For by the justice of the king the throne is exalted, [376] and in truth the helms of the peoples are made firm.[^10] And the justice of the king is: to oppress no one unjustly by [power]{g:potencia}; to judge justly between a man and his neighbour without respect of persons; to be the defender of strangers, orphans and widows; to restrain thefts; to punish adulteries; not to exalt the unjust; not to nourish the shameless and players; to destroy the impious from the land; not to suffer parricides and those who live wickedly to live; to defend the churches; to feed the poor with [alms]{g:elemosina}; to set just men over the business of the kingdom; to have old, wise and sober counsellors; not to strive after the superstitions of magicians, soothsayers and pythonesses;[^11] to defer anger; to defend the [homeland]{g:patria} bravely and justly against adversaries; to trust in God in all things; not to lift up his soul in prosperity;[^12] to bear all adversities patiently; not to suffer his sons to act impiously; to apply himself to prayers at fixed hours; not to taste food before the fitting hours. For woe to the land whose king is a child, and whose princes eat in the morning.[^13] These make the prosperity of the kingdom in the present and lead the king to the better heavenly kingdoms." And afterwards this saint recounts twelve conditions which follow from the opposite. "He who", he says, "does not dispense the kingdom according to this law will without doubt endure many adversities in his rule. For this reason the peace of their [peoples] is often broken, stumbling-blocks are raised up even concerning the kingdom, the fruits of the lands are diminished, the [services]{g:servicium} of the peoples are hindered, many and various griefs infect the prosperity of the kingdom, the death of dear ones and of children brings sadness, the incursions of enemies lay waste even the provinces on every side, beasts tear apart the herds of cattle and the flocks of sheep, the storm of the air and the troubled winter hinder the fruitfulness of things and the services of the sea,[^14] [377] and at times strokes of lightning burn up the crops, the blossoms of the trees and the vine-shoots. But above all the king's injustice not only darkens the prosperity of his present rule, but also overshadows his sons and grandsons, so that they do not hold the inheritance after him. For on account of the sin of King Solomon the Lord scattered the house of Israel out of the hands of the sons of Israel, and on account of the justice of King David he always left a lamp of his seed in Jerusalem.[^15] See how much the justice of a king avails the world: it is plainly clear to those who look. For [it] is the peace of peoples, the safeguard of the homeland, the immunity of the common people, the fortification of the nation, the cure of sicknesses, the joy of men, the temperateness of the air, the calm of the sea, the fruitfulness of the earth, the solace of the poor, the inheritance of the sons, and for himself the hope of future blessedness.[^16] Yet let the king know: as he has been set first on the throne of men, so also in punishments, if he has not done justice, he will have the [primacy]{g:primatus}; for all the sinners whatever he has had under him in the present, he will have above him, by way of blows, in the punishment to come."[^17] And these twelve goods, with the punishment as the thirteenth, should move king and people to help one another.

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 373.29: The fifth commandment teaches us to honour our mother the church and remove all. blots from her.
- 374.21: Those who should advise the king mislead him.
- 374.26: Prelates maintain false counsellors, lawyers and theologians.
- 375.11: It is the duty of kings to put down new sects and ceremonies.

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** The first commandment of the second table is "Honour thy father and thy mother" (Exod. 20:12), the fifth in the reckoning of Loserth's side-note; Wyclif applies it to the church as mother. The manuscripts' margin: *Auferre bona temporalia. argumentum bonum* (Loserth).
[^2]: **C.** The print sets a question mark after *infinitum* and then runs on *quodammodo magis sacrum*; we take the whole as one question and *quodammodo* with *magis sacrum*. The alternative is *infinitum quodammodo*, "in a certain way infinite". A in marg. at *ornamentis*: *Ornamenta sacra ecclesie*.
[^3]: **T.** *Excommunicacio sacrilega* is hard: in this argument it is the sacrilege, not an excommunication, that disturbs the church; compare *hoc excomunicatum et hereticum sacrilegium* a few lines below (374), "this excommunicate and heretical sacrilege". The print may have altered an ending (as *excommunicatum sacrilegium*); there is no variant at the foot, and we translate the print.
[^4]: **C.** *Licet enim sit tam contrarium …, induratum et tam diutine vix potest … semoveri*: the period lacks a main verb. We take *induratum [est]* and read *tam diutine* as consecutive ("so long-standing [that] …"); alternatively the whole is governed by *licet* and the concession is left unfinished.
[^5]: **S.** The legend that Muhammad was taught by a Jew and by the monk Sergius (Bahira); Loserth refers to *De ecclesia* pp. 290, 333, 511, where Wyclif uses it (LEDGER P12p-1). **R.** B in marg.: *Machomet habuit duos complices*.
[^6]: **C.** *Foret (inquam) copia cleri purgati* joins abruptly to what precedes; the sense seems to be "[were these removed,] there would be plenty of purged clergy". A word such as *aliter* may have been lost; we translate the print. *Secundum eius privilegia* we take with *conservante*, *eius* being Christ.
[^7]: **S.** Luke 11:17–18 (DR "house upon house shall fall"; "how shall his kingdom stand?"); Wyclif asks the question of "the kingdom", without *ejus*.
[^8]: **S/T.** Pseudo-Cyprian, *De duodecim abusivis saeculi*, ch. 9 (*rex iniquus*), ascribed in Wyclif's day to Augustine; Loserth gives Aug. *Opp.* VI, Appendix p. 214, and *nonus abusionis gradus* (LEDGER P12p-2). The manuscripts read the title *abusionis* (Loserth). *Inde … custodiret* is the print (read on the plate); the wording of the treatise here is to be checked (LEDGER P12p-3).
[^9]: **T/S.** *Super Jeronymum Omelia XI* is the print, and a homily "on Jerome" is unexpected; *super Ieremiam* (a homily on Jeremiah) would be the likelier source. There is no variant and no reference at the foot; to be checked (LEDGER P12p-4). The manuscripts' margin here: *Regi subesse debet clerus*; at the next sentence, *Regis condiciones 22*.
[^10]: **S.** Cf. Prov. 16:12 (DR "the throne is established by justice"); the words are the treatise's, not a quotation.
[^11]: **T.** *Supersticionibus non contendere* is the print; the sense wanted is "not to give heed to", and the treatise's received text may read *intendere* (to be checked, LEDGER P12p-5). We translate the print.
[^12]: **T.** Loserth: *rectius: animo non elevari* ("not to be lifted up in mind"); we translate the print, *animam non elevare*.
[^13]: **S.** Eccles. 10:16 (Loserth), DR "Woe to thee, O land, when thy king is a child, and when the princes eat in the morning"; the treatise's Latin differs slightly (*Ve enim terre*).
[^14]: **T.** Loserth on this sentence: for *mors … confert* "recte: mortes … conferunt" (deaths … bring); for *incursus* the manuscripts read *cursus*; for *fecunditatem et maris* they read *fecunditate et moris*, which he emends. We translate the print throughout.
[^15]: **S/C.** 3 Kings 11:11–13, 36 (Solomon's sin; the lamp left for David in Jerusalem). *De manibus filiorum Israhel* is the print; the sense wants "out of the hands of his sons" (Solomon's), and the second *Israhel* may repeat *domus Israhel*; we translate the print.
[^16]: **T.** The manuscripts lack *Est enim*, which Loserth supplies; hence "[it]", the justice of the king. For *munimentum* they read *immunimentum*.
[^17]: **C.** *Modo plagali*: "by way of blows", from *plaga*, a stroke: the sinners he ruled will be over him as his tormentors.
