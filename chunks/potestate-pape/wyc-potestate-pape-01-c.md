# *De potestate pape*, chapter 1, part 3: spiritual power redefined: of order and in common

## ¶1 · 9.11–10.24

> [p. 9] Quantum ad antiquitatem, patet quod sacerdocium gentile vel naturale precessit sacerdocium leviticum. Et correspondenter de potestate dicitur, ut tangitur XXI capitulo V libri. Unde illo modo creditur Adam et Cayn eius progenitum ante peccatum fuisse quodammodo naturaliter sacerdotes. et sic potestas spiritualis benedicendi et consecrandi, videtur dignitate, tempore et utilitate precedere potestatem civiliter coactivam, ymmo potestas sacerdotalis levitica videtur potestatem regiam Israheliticam antiquitate precedere; non autem potestatem regiam gentilem, cum tempore Abrahe legitur novem reges fuisse in valle congregatos. Dignitate autem et utilitate videtur sacerdotalis potestas levitica esse prior. In Deo autem videtur racio potestatis regie increate precedere potestatem sacerdotalem; sicut secunda persona origine precedit terciam. et opus sustentacionis; opus confirmacionis. que correspondent istis officiis, et per consequens sacerdos eternus primos parentes matrimonio copulavit, licet spirituale matrimonium Christi et ecclesie ipsum precesserit. [p. 10] Redeundo igitur ad descripciones potestatum predictas, videtur quod capiunt multas calumpnias; primo quia eidem persone insunt ambe potestates simul; que persona fuit layca in veteri testamento, ut patet de rege Salem Melchisedech Genes. XIV, de Moyse et aliis iudicibus qui non erant sacerdotes levitici, in tantum quod videtur multis ipsos fuisse populares vel laicos. Secundo quia in novo testamento videtur multis quod clerici habeant simul utramque potestatem, cum habent secularem dominacionem; ut fingitur. Et tercio quia reges temporales habent spiritualem ad destruendum vicia et plantandum ac augendum virtutes in se et in aliis, quotquot autem receperunt Christum per fidem, dedit eis potestatem racione filiacionis spiritualis, ad impendendum opera spiritualia misericordie que indubie est potestas spiritualis; quia potestas data divinitus immediate ad vitam spiritualem gignendam et alendam. Nec potest christianus sine hac potestate sanctificare sabbatum; quam potestatem auferre a quolibet Christiano foret contradicere legi Dei; ut patet Exod. XX. Omnis enim potestas qua homo sanctificiat se vel tempus est potestas spiritualis.

As to antiquity, it is clear that the gentile or natural priesthood preceded the Levitical priesthood. And correspondingly it is said of [power]{g:potestas}, as is touched on in chapter 21 of the fifth book.[^1] Hence in that way Adam and Cain his offspring are believed to have been in some way naturally priests before sin.[^2] And so the spiritual power of blessing and consecrating seems to precede in dignity, time and usefulness the civilly [coactive]{g:coactivus} power; indeed the Levitical priestly power seems to precede the Israelite royal power in antiquity; but not the gentile royal power, since in the time of Abraham nine kings are read to have been gathered in the valley (Genesis 14). But in dignity and usefulness the Levitical priestly power seems to be prior. But in God the notion of the uncreated royal power seems to precede the priestly power, as the second person precedes the third in origin, and the work of sustaining the work of confirming, which correspond to these offices;[^3] and consequently the eternal priest joined the first parents in matrimony, although the spiritual matrimony of Christ and the [church]{g:ecclesia} preceded it. [10] Returning, therefore, to the [definitions]{g:descripcio} of the powers given above, they seem to [be open to many challenges]{g:calumpnia}: first, because both powers are in the same person at once, a person who was lay in the old testament, as is clear of the king of Salem, Melchizedek, Genesis 14, and of Moses and the other judges, who were not Levitical priests, so much so that it seems to many that they were of the people, or lay. Secondly, because in the new testament it seems to many that clerics have both powers at once, since they have secular [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio}, as is feigned. And thirdly, because temporal kings have spiritual [power] to destroy vices and to plant and increase virtues in themselves and in others; and "as many as received" Christ by faith, "he gave them power",[^4] by reason of spiritual sonship, to perform the spiritual works of mercy, which is undoubtedly spiritual power, since it is a power given by God immediately for begetting and nourishing the spiritual life. Nor can a Christian sanctify the sabbath without this power; to take this power away from any Christian would be to contradict [God's law]{g:lex-dei}, as is clear in Exodus 20. For every power by which a man sanctifies[^5] himself or a time is spiritual power.

## ¶2 · 10.25–11.16

> [p. 10] Et patet impugnacio descripcionis supradicte. Ideo ut descripcio dictarum potestatum sit nocior et ecclesie consentaneor, potest dici quod potestas spiritualis est potestas spiritus per se immediate ordinata, ut creatura racionalis secundum bona gracie dirigatur, et sic individuatur tam a subiecto quam obiecto. Est autem omnis homo spiritus, cum sit duarum naturarum utraque, ut loquitur scriptura Luce IX; 55: Nescitis cuius [p. 11] spiritus estis, et I. Joh. IV, 3: Omnis spiritus, qui solvit Jhesum, ex Deo nou est, spiritus ibi est subtilis hereticus. Et sic sive analogizetur potestas ad Deum et creaturas racionales, sive restringatur ad potestates hominum et angelorum verum est, quod omnis potestas spiritualis est potestas spiritus. Et quamvis homo non det graciam, ministrat tamen sacramenta, ut subditus secundum bona gracie dirigatur. Potestas autem corporalis, quamvis sit gracia bonorum gracie; tamen immediate est; ut creatura Dei ordinetur secundum bona naturalia vel fortune. Et ita videtur omnis homo habere duplicem potestatem, cum omnis homo debet habere potestatem super actus membrorum, et preterea habet potestatem viandi in gracia.

And the impugning of the definition given above is clear. Therefore, so that the definition of the said powers may be better known and more consonant[^6] with the church, it can be said that spiritual power is a power of a spirit, ordered of itself immediately to this, that a rational creature be directed according to the goods of grace; and so it is individuated both by its subject and by its object.[^7] Now every man is a spirit, since he is of two natures, both of them,[^8] as scripture speaks in Luke 9:55: "You know not of what [11] spirit you are"; and 1 John 4:3: "Every spirit that dissolveth Jesus is not of God": spirit there is a subtle heretic.[^9] And so, whether power is analogized to God and to rational creatures, or restricted to the powers of men and angels, it is true that every spiritual power is a power of a spirit. And although man does not give grace, yet he ministers the sacraments, so that the subject may be directed according to the goods of grace. But bodily power, although it exists for the sake of the goods of grace, yet is immediately [ordered] so that God's creature may be ordered according to natural goods or those of fortune.[^10] And so every man seems to have a twofold power, since every man must have power over the acts of his members, and moreover he has the power of making his way in grace.[^11]

## ¶3 · 11.17–11.32

> [p. 11] Ideo potestas spiritualis capit subdivisionem multiplicem, cum aliqua sit ordinis, et aliqua sit communis. Potestas ordinis vocatur potestas spiritualis quam habet clericus ad ministrandum ecclesie sacramenta, ut spiritualiter prosit sibi et laicis, ut est potestas conficiendi, absolvendi et sacramenta ministrandi. Nam potestas conficiendi ordinatur per se et immediate, ut sacerdos conficiat, sicut habitus virtutis moralis ordinantur propter actus habitibus meliores; et cum sacerdos, ut digne conficiat, dirigitur secundum bona gracie; ideo patet in isto et sibi similibus descripcio supradicta. Potestas autem spiritualis communis, quam habet quilibet christianus, in exercendo opera spiritualia misericordie in se et in aliis; de quibus meminit iste versus:

Therefore spiritual power takes a manifold subdivision, since some is of order and some is common. [Power of order]{g:potestas-ordinis} is the name for the spiritual power which the [cleric]{g:clericus} has for ministering the sacraments of the church, so that he may profit himself and the laity spiritually, as is the power of consecrating, of absolving and of ministering the sacraments.[^12] For the power of consecrating is ordered of itself and immediately to this, that the [priest]{g:sacerdos} consecrate, as the habits of moral [virtue]{g:virtus} are ordered for the sake of acts better than the habits; and since the priest, in order to consecrate worthily, is directed according to the goods of grace, therefore the definition given above is clear in this and in things like it. And the common spiritual power, which every Christian has, in exercising the spiritual works of mercy in himself and in others; of which this verse makes mention:[^13]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 9.11: Antiquity: It is believed that Adam and Cain had a sort of priesthood.
- 9.26: In God however the kingly power has priority.
- 10.1: Objections to accepting Hugo's description. 1. Both powers were united in one person e. g. Melchizedek. 2. A similar union of powers in clerics, 3. and in kings, since it belongs to them to increase virtue.
- 10.25: Spiritual power is a power of the Spirit to direct a rational creature according to the gifts of grace.
- 11.10: Corporal power is the ordering of the creature according to gifts of nature or fortune.
- 11.17: Different kinds of spiritual power; the power given in orders and that which is common to all christians.

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** Loserth refers the place to *De civili dominio* III, p. 425 of the Society's edition (LEDGER P01c-1), where chapter 21 begins; the "fifth book" is that work counted within Wyclif's *Summa theologie*, as Wyclif numbers it himself on that page (*capitulo XI huius V*).

[^2]: **C.** *ante peccatum*: "before sin", most naturally the first sin; but Cain was born after the fall, so Wyclif may mean before Cain's own sin. *progenitum*: "offspring".

[^3]: **C.** The print stops after *terciam* and after *confirmacionis* (*terciam. et opus sustentacionis; opus confirmacionis. que …*). Construed across the stops, with *precedit* understood: the work of sustaining (answering to the king) precedes the work of confirming (answering to the priest), as the Son precedes the Spirit. The comparison may have lost a word.

[^4]: **S/C.** John 1:12 (Loserth 10.14), DR "as many as received him, he gave them power to be made the sons of God"; Wyclif writes *Christum per fidem* and turns the power to the works of mercy. The quoted words follow Loserth's italics. *habent spiritualem*: the noun is understood, supplied as [power]. Exodus 20:8, "keep holy the sabbath day".

[^5]: **T.** *sanctificiat* (p. 10): so the print, read on the plate; a misprint for *sanctificat*, our emendation.

[^6]: **T.** *consentaneor* (p. 10): so the print, read on the plate; a misprint for the comparative *consentanior*, our emendation.

[^7]: **C/R.** *individuatur tam a subiecto quam obiecto*: the power is marked off by its bearer (a spirit) and its object (the goods of grace). A in the margin (10.27): *Potestas spiritualis quid est?*; B: *Potestas spiritualis*. Hus repeats 10.27 ff. and all of p. 11 (Loserth: "Hussii Opp." 213a, 213ab) (LEDGER P01c-2, editor).

[^8]: **C.** *cum sit duarum naturarum utraque*: literally "since he is of two natures, both"; taken as man made of body and spirit together, so that he is rightly called a spirit. A word may be lost.

[^9]: **S.** Luke 9:55; 1 John 4:3. Loserth's italics stop at *Nescitis cuius*, where the page turns; the quotation is completed to the verse's end. Wyclif glosses the "spirit" of 1 John as the heretic.

[^10]: **C.** *gracia bonorum gracie*: *gracia* with the genitive taken as "for the sake of". *immediate est* lacks its participle, supplied as [ordered] after *ordinata* in the definition (10.27); a word may have dropped. AB in the margin (11.10): *Potestas corporalis*.

[^11]: **C.** *viandi*: from *viare*, "to be on the way", the verb of the *viator*, the wayfarer.

[^12]: **T.** *sacramenta* (11.21): the manuscripts read *sacramentalia*; Loserth prints *sacramenta*, translated. At 11.18 A reads *alia* for *aliqua*. *conficere*: to consecrate the eucharist. A in the margin (11.19): *Potestas ordinis*.

[^13]: **C.** The sentence has no main verb and runs on into the verse that opens p. 12 (the next part). B in the margin (11.29): *Potestas communis christianis*.
