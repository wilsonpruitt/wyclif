# sed/autem extract, wave 9: chunks 04-e 04-f 04-g

### 04-e ¶1 · 98.15–99.22  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 98] **Sed** tercio obicitur per hoc quod nedum laycus potest conficere, **sed** sacramentum posset confici sola mente, vel non est confeccio et per consequens eque in fermento ut azimis, et sic eque in aliis liquoribus ut in vino; ymmo cum omne vinum nostrum sit mixtum aqua, non oportuit inmiscere aquam **sed** simul benedicere aquam et vinum. Et videtur ulterius quod corpus Christi movetur de celo ad loca volita secundum arbitrium sacerdotis. Quantum ad istud, patet quod aliud est querere de posse quod se extendit ad potenciam Dei absolutam et aliud est secundum testimonium scripture querere de licere. Nullus (inquam) fidelis dubitat quin Deus posset dare layco potenciam conficiendi, sicut laycus cum possit esse sacerdos (ut dicunt loyci) possit conficere. Ymmo videtur iuxta testimonium Augustini, Crisostomi et aliorum sanctorum quod omnis predestinatus laycus est sacerdos, et multo magis devotus laycus conficiens, [p. 99] cum daret ecclesie sacrum ministerium, haberet racionem sacerdotis. Verumptamen sicut ecclesia racionabiliter variavit in verbis confeccionis ab omnibus hiis quatuor evangelistis servando sentenciam, quod patet ex fide scripture debere fieri, sic racionabiliter ordinavit quod soli sacerdotes propter religiositatem et dignitatem in moribus hoc sacramentum conficerent. Levis **autem** fundacio valet ad hoc quod Christus dedit singulariter illud officium suis apostolis, nec occurrit mihi efficax testimonium quod apostoli gerebant in hoc typum universalis ecclesie **sed** singulariter sacerdotum; nec oportet propter necessitatem ministerium accidencium huius hostie plus extendi, cum stat fidelem laycum laborando qui nunquam vidit hostiam accipere presbitero efficacius corpus Christi, cum secundum Augustinum super Johannem Omelia XXV accipere corpus Christi sit pie in ipsum credere: Ut quid, inquit, paras dentes et ventrem? crede et manducasti. Oportet enim quod sit spiritualis manducacio, ut dictum est, que consistit in pia consideracione et grata, quomodo Christus passus est pro humano genere.

ENGLISH: But, third, it is objected, by appeal to this: that not only can a layman [consecrate]{g:conficere}, but the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} could be consecrated by the mind alone, or else there is no [consecration]{g:confeccio}; and consequently [it could be made] equally in leaven as in unleavened [bread], and so equally in other liquids as in [wine]{g:vinum}; indeed, since all our wine is mixed with water, there was no need to mingle water in, but only to bless water and wine together.[^1] And it seems, further, that [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} is moved from heaven to the places willed, at the [priest]{g:sacerdos}'s discretion. As to this, it is clear that it is one thing to ask about what can be, which extends to God's [absolute power]{g:potencia-absoluta}, and another to ask, according to the testimony of scripture, about what is lawful.[^2] No believer, I say, doubts that God could give a layman the power of consecrating, just as a layman, since he can be a priest, can (as the logicians say) consecrate.[^3] Indeed, it seems, by the testimony of Augustine, Chrysostom and other [saints]{g:sancti}, that every [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} layman is a priest; and much more would a devout layman consecrating, [99] since he would be giving the [church]{g:ecclesia} a sacred ministry, have the account of a priest.[^4] Nevertheless, just as the church reasonably varied, in the words of consecration, from all these four evangelists while keeping the [sense]{g:sentencia} (which it is clear [from the faith of scripture]{g:ex-fide} ought to be done), so it reasonably ordained that priests alone, for their [religiousness]{g:religiositas} and dignity in conduct, should consecrate this sacrament.[^5] And a light foundation serves for this: that Christ gave that office to his apostles [alone]{g:singulariter}; nor does any effective testimony occur to me that the apostles bore in this the type of the universal church, but only of priests.[^6] Nor is there need, on account of necessity, for the ministry of the [accidents]{g:accidens} of this [host]{g:hostia} to be extended further, since it can stand that a faithful layman, labouring, who has never seen the host, receives the body of Christ more effectively than a [presbyter]{g:presbiter}; since according to Augustine on John, homily 25, to receive the body of Christ is to believe in him [piously]{g:pie}: "Why," he says, "do you make ready teeth and belly? Believe, and you have eaten."[^7] For it must be a spiritual [eating]{g:manducacio}, as has been said, which consists in a pious and grateful consideration of how Christ suffered for the human race.

### 04-e ¶2 · 99.23–100.10  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 99] **Sed** graviter ferunt plurimi de nimis levi efficacia verborum sacramenti, cum illis nihil conficitur vel mutatur, quod est contra decreta sanctorum et specialiter beati Ambrosii in libro suo De Officiis et ponitur in De Consecracione, distinccione II, Forte dicis, et in libro suo De Sacramentis, ut consequenter ponitur eadem distinccione, capitulo Panis est, ubi dicit quod de pane fit caro Christi; quod probat per [p. 100] hoc quod verbum Christi suffecit mutare aliquid a puro non esse ad existenciam naturalem, ut patet de creacione Genes. I°; ergo per locum a maiori illud verbum potest facere, ut quod erat panis ante consecracionem sit corpus Christi per consecracionem; et idem confirmat doctor per exempla scripture, ut patet eadem distinccione, capitulo sequente Revera, ubi probat conversionem illam esse possibilem per locum a maiori per illa que fiebant a prophetis in veteri testamento.

ENGLISH: But a great many take it hard that the efficacy of the words of the sacrament is made [exceedingly]{g:nimis} light, since by them nothing is consecrated or changed; which is against the [decrees]{g:decretum} of the saints, and especially of blessed Ambrose in his book *On Duties*, as is set down in *De consecratione*, distinction 2, *Forte dicis*, and in his book *On the Sacraments*, as is set down next in the same distinction, chapter *Panis est*, where he says that from the bread the flesh of Christ is made.[^8] This he proves by [100] this, that the word of Christ sufficed to change something from pure not-[being]{g:esse} to natural existence, as is clear of the creation, Genesis 1; therefore, by [the topic from the greater]{g:locus-a-maiori}, that word can bring it about that what was [bread]{g:panis} before the [consecration]{g:consecracio} is the body of Christ by the consecration.[^9] And the doctor confirms the same by examples of scripture, as is clear in the same distinction, the following chapter, *Revera*, where he proves that [conversion]{g:conversio} to be possible by the topic from the greater, through the things that were done by the prophets in the old testament.[^10]

### 04-e ¶3 · 100.11–101.7  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 100] **Sed** quantum ad omnia ista, patet quod iste sanctus non intendit dicere quod panis iste fiet ydemptice corpus Christi, **sed** quod fiet eius figura efficax modo dicto; et ad hoc vadunt tam argumenta quam sentencia. Et sic conversio illa non destruit naturam panis nec mutat naturam corporis, inducendo in materiam aliam quidditatem, **sed** facit presenciam corporis Christi et tollit principalitatem panis, ut in corpore Christi colligatur tota intencio adorantis; et istam conversionem probat sanctus per locum a maiori possibilem, et de ista mirabili et miraculosa conversione debet presbiter contentari. Et patet quod falsum assumitur, cum panis mutetur insensibiliter, ut figuret efficaciter corpus Christi; quod sancti intelligunt per hoc quod panis fit corpus Christi et quod post consecracionem panis est corpus Christi. Et qui ignorat istam predicacionem tropicam vel figurativam nedum male intelliget sanctos doctores et decreta ecclesie **sed** fidem scripture. Nam Christus dicit Joh. XV°, 5: Ego sum vitis vera, et Joh. XI°, 14, [p. 101] ac XIV°, 6: Ego sum pastor bonus. Ego sum via, etc., sicut eciam Luc. XXI°, 33 dicit quod celum et terra transibunt, **sed** verba sua non transibunt; ubi non dubium intelligit per verba que sunt signa inconstantissima signatam sentenciam; et isto tropo sepe utitur, sicut post eum utuntur sancti doctores et leges ecclesie.

ENGLISH: But as to all these things, it is clear that this saint does not mean to say that this bread will become [identically]{g:ydemptice} the body of Christ, but that it will become its effective [figure]{g:figura} in the way stated; and to this go both the arguments and the teaching.[^11] And so that conversion does not destroy the [nature]{g:natura} of the bread nor change the nature of the body by bringing another [quiddity]{g:quiditas} into the matter, but it makes the presence of the body of Christ and takes away the [principality]{g:principalitas} of the bread, so that the whole intention of the worshipper is gathered in the body of Christ; and this conversion the saint proves possible by the topic from the greater, and with this wonderful and miraculous conversion the presbyter ought to be content. And it is clear that a falsehood is assumed, since the bread is changed [imperceptibly]{g:insensibiliter}, so as to [figure]{g:figurare} the body of Christ effectively; which the saints understand by [saying] that the bread becomes the body of Christ and that after the consecration the bread is the body of Christ.[^12] And whoever [is ignorant of]{g:ignorare} this [tropical]{g:predicacio-tropica} or [figurative predication]{g:predicacio-figurativa} will misunderstand [not only]{g:nedum} the holy doctors and the decrees of the church but the faith of scripture. For Christ says, John 15:5, "I am the true vine," and John 10:14 [101] and 14:6, "I am the good shepherd," "I am the way," etc.;[^13] as also, Luke 21:33, he says that heaven and earth shall pass away but his words shall not pass away; where without doubt he understands by the words, which are most inconstant signs, the [thing signified]{g:signatum}, the sense; and he often uses this [trope]{g:tropus}, as after him the holy doctors and [the laws of the church]{g:leges-ecclesie} use it.[^14]

### 04-e ¶4 · 101.8–101.19  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 101] In isto **autem** tropo sunt gradus multiplices, quorum mirabilis et plus miraculosus est quod loco panis succedit corpus Christi sacramentaliter, ut probat sanctus Ambrosius Deo esse possibile et credibile, non quod panis destruitur, **sed** quod significat corpus Domini ibi presens in sacramento, cui oportet panis consideracione dimissa plene attendere. Unde hec est porta lapsus multorum qui ignorantes voces proprias baptizant terminos: aliqui ponentes transsubstanciacionem ad sensum expositum, alii ponentes ydemptificacionem, et alii ponentes inpanacionem, ut patet inferius.

ENGLISH: And in this trope there are many degrees, of which the wonderful and more miraculous one is that in the place of the bread the body of Christ succeeds [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} (as saint Ambrose proves to be possible to God and credible): not that the bread is destroyed, but that it signifies [the body of the Lord]{g:corpus-domini} present there in the sacrament, to which one must attend fully, the consideration of the bread being laid aside.[^15] Hence this is the gate of the fall of many who, ignorant of the proper words, baptize terms: some positing [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio} in the [meaning]{g:sensus} set out, others positing [identification]{g:ydemptificacio}, and others positing [impanation]{g:inpanacio}, as is clear below.[^16]

### 04-e ¶5 · 101.20–102.14  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 101] **Sed** redeundo conceditur quod Deus ex omnipotencia sua posset concedere fideli potenciam conficiendi eukaristiam pia mente, subducta voce. **Sed** de hoc non queritur, ymmo quid Deus ordinavit in lege scripture sue debere fieri; et patet quod ipse docendo verba confeccionis predicta docuit ut nos faciamus similiter. Et sic videtur domino Armachano probabile libro IX°, capitulo V°, De Questionibus Armenorum, quod Christus non illis verbis **sed** ante illorum prolacionem confecit in sacramento presenciam sui corporis et sanguinis. Sic enim ultimate instituit [p. 102] originaliter alia sacramenta, et ita levi evidencia **sed** probabili credimus quod sacerdos ex virtute Dei conficit illis verbis; et sic licet sacerdos posset habere virtutem conficiendi panem et non vinum, panem triticeum et non ordeaceum vel alterius speciei et econtra et sic de aliis variacionibus tam in pane quam in vino, ymmo verba quantumcunque varia possent esse pars huius sacramenti et sacramentum posset esse nude sacra hostia sine verbis, tamen formam quam observamus credimus a Domino institutam, sicut credimus profunditatem ordinacionis divine habere racionem, propter quam oportuit eum taliter ordinare, **sed** vias istas nobis investigabiles oportet transcurrere.

ENGLISH: But, to return, it is granted that God by his omnipotence could grant a believer the [power]{g:potencia} of consecrating the eucharist with a pious mind, the voice withdrawn.[^17] But that is not what is asked; rather, [what is asked is] what God has ordained in the law of his scripture ought to be done; and it is clear that he, in teaching the aforesaid words of consecration, taught that we should do likewise. And so it seems probable to [the lord of Armagh]{g:ardmacanus}, in book 9, chapter 5, of *On the Questions of the Armenians*, that Christ effected the presence of his body and blood in the sacrament not by those words but before uttering them.[^18] For in this way, in the last resort, he instituted [102] the other sacraments at their origin; and so on light but probable [evidence]{g:evidencia} we believe that the priest consecrates by those words by the [power]{g:virtus} of God.[^19] And so, although a priest could have the power of consecrating bread and not wine, wheaten bread and not barley or of another kind, and conversely, and so of other variations in the bread as in the wine (indeed words however varied could be part of this sacrament, and the sacrament could be barely the [sacred]{g:sacer} host without words), yet we believe the form we observe was instituted by the Lord, just as we believe that the depth of the divine [ordinance]{g:ordinacio} has a reason for which he had to ordain thus; but we must pass over these ways, unsearchable to us.[^20]

### 04-e ¶6 · 102.15–102.28  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 102] Ulterius quantum ad dissensionem inter nos et Grecos de confeccione in azimis vel fermento, dicitur quod confeccio in azimis quam nos observamus est conveniencior textui utriusque testamenti (ut dictum est alibi) et apcior ad figuram; dicit enim Apostolus primo Cor. V°, 8 quod epulari debemus non in fermento veteri malicie aut nequicie **sed** in azimis sinceritatis et veritatis. Utrum **autem** Greci vere conficiunt vel utrum foret confeccio in vino non lymphato, non aceto vel vino de aggresta aut liquido tali confecto, est dubium apud multos. **Sed** videtur mihi quod non tam necessaria est ista confeccio, quod oportet ipsum conficere in forma vel materia aliena, et sic vel Greci errant vel nos.

ENGLISH: Further, as to the dissension between us and the Greeks over consecration in unleavened bread or in leaven, [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that the consecration in unleavened bread which we observe is in better [agreement]{g:conveniencia} with the text of both testaments (as has been said elsewhere) and apter to the figure; for the Apostle says, 1 Corinthians 5:8, that we ought to feast not in the old leaven of malice or wickedness but in the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth.[^21] But whether the Greeks [truly]{g:vere} consecrate, or whether there would be consecration in wine not mixed with water, in vinegar, or in wine of verjuice or a liquid made in such a way, is doubtful among many.[^22] But it seems to me that this consecration is not so necessary that one must consecrate it in a foreign form or matter; and so either the Greeks err or we do.[^23]

### 04-f ¶1 · 102.29–104.12  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 102] Quoad ritum magis pertinenter conficimus et sic pertinenter inmiscemus aquam, cum sanguis et aqua figurati per hoc sacramentum fluxerunt de latere [p. 103] crucifixi Joh. XX°, 20. Et ideo (ut dicit Alexander papa) sic pertinenter quoad utrumque signatum recolitur memoria passionis. Nec oportet nos sollicitari de necessitate fidei ad distincte discernendum differenciam inter vinum et acetum, quia nullus nostrum in confinio conversionis sufficit illud facere; ymmo in liquore reputato et vere existente vinum secundum maiorem partem sui quecunque pars notabilis est acetum; nam aqua, terra etc. tam elementa quam mixta, licet sepe nobis insensibilia, sunt partes vini, et ideo mussitat decretalis libro III De Celebracione missarum capitulo Cum Marthe: Si aqua in vino confecto convertitur in partem Christi vel cedit in nihilum et recitando multas opiniones stolidas non diffinit. Prima sentencia est quod licet oportet aquam misceri vino in calice, tamen non debet fieri verbis sacramenti confeccio, antequam aqua assimiletur vino et sic aqua convertitur in aquam que est in Christo et vinum in sanguinem ut in hoc sacramento plene sit veritas et figura. Secunda est quod sit assimilacio aque in vinum et post totum transsubstanciatur in sanguinem, sic quod quelibet pars quantitativa consecrandi huiusmodi sit vinum, cum nulla pars quantitativa mixti sit elementum, licet phisici aquam a vino asserunt posse per artificium separari. **Sed** tercia est innaturalis sentencia cui videtur decretalis annuere: Potest, inquit, dici, quod aqua non transit in sanguinem **sed** remanet prioris vini accidentibus circumfusa ita quod vini saporem assumit; quod inde cognosci potest quod si post calicis consecracionem aliud vinum [p. 104] inmittatur in calicem, illud non transit in sanguinem, nec sanguini commiscetur, **sed** accidentibus prioris vini commixtum corpori quod sub eis latet undique circumfunditur non madidans circumfusum, ipsa tamen accidencia vinum appositum videntur afficere, quia si aqua fuerit apposita, vini saporem assumit. Contingit igitur accidencia mutare subiectum, sicut contingit subiectum accidencia permutare: cedit quippe natura miraculo et virtus supra consuetudinem operatur. Et finaliter videtur decretali illam sentenciam esse magis probabilem que asserit tam aquam quam vinum in Christi sanguinem transmutari.

ENGLISH: As to the rite, we [consecrate]{g:conficere} more [fittingly]{g:pertinenter}, and so fittingly mingle in water, since the blood and water figured by this [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} flowed from the side [103] of the crucified, John 20:20.[^1] And therefore (as [Pope]{g:papa} Alexander says) the memory of the passion is thus fittingly recalled as to both [things signified]{g:signatum}.[^2] Nor need we be anxious, as of necessity of faith, to discern distinctly the difference between [wine]{g:vinum} and vinegar, since none of us is able to do that at the border of the conversion; indeed, in a liquid reputed to be, and [truly]{g:vere} being, wine according to its greater part, some notable part or other is vinegar.[^3] For water, earth, etc., both elements and [mixed bodies]{g:mixtum}, though often [imperceptible]{g:insensibilis} to us, are parts of wine; and therefore the [decretal]{g:decretalis} mutters, in book 3, *On the celebration of masses*, chapter *Cum Marthe*, "whether the water in the consecrated wine is converted into a part of Christ or [gives way]{g:cedere} into nothing", and, [reciting]{g:recitare} many stolid [opinions]{g:opinio}, does not define.[^4] The first [teaching]{g:sentencia} is that, although water must be mixed with the wine in the chalice, yet the [consecration]{g:confeccio} ought not to be made with the words of the sacrament before the water is assimilated to the wine; and so the water is converted into the water that is in Christ and the wine into the blood, so that in this sacrament there may be fully both truth and [figure]{g:figura}.[^5] The second is that there is an assimilation of the water into the wine, and afterwards the whole is [transubstantiated]{g:transsubstanciacio} into the blood, so that every [quantitative part]{g:pars-quantitativa} of what is to be consecrated in this way is wine, since no quantitative part of a mixed body is an element; although natural philosophers assert that water can be separated from wine by art. But the third is an unnatural teaching, to which the decretal seems to nod: "It can be said," it says, "that the water does not pass into the blood but remains surrounded by the [accidents]{g:accidens} of the former wine, so that it takes on the taste of the wine; which can be known from this, that if after the [consecration]{g:consecracio} of the chalice other wine [104] is put into the chalice, that does not pass into the blood, nor is it mixed with the blood, but, mingled with the accidents of the former wine, it is poured all round the body that lies hidden under them, without wetting what it surrounds; yet the accidents themselves seem to affect the wine added, since if water has been added it takes on the taste of the wine. It happens, then, that accidents change the [subject]{g:subiectum}, just as it happens that the subject changes the accidents: for [nature]{g:natura} gives way to miracle, and [power]{g:virtus} works above [custom]{g:consuetudo}."[^6] And finally the decretal seems to hold that teaching the more probable which asserts that both the water and the wine are transmuted into the blood of Christ.[^7]

### 04-f ¶2 · 104.13–104.28  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 104] Quantum ad istam sentenciam patet philosophis et theologis quod ista est sentencia indigesta, nam quoad primam sentenciam patet philosophis quod sacerdos nescit quando aqua assimilatur vino, ergo sepe contingit quod aqua maneat seorsum per se quousque sacerdos insorbeat sacramentum, sicut sensualiter patet in hieme ubi aqua per se congelatur, et vinum ex calore intrinseco manet seorsum fluxibile, sicut primo, nec est color quod aqua solum vertatur in aquam lateris Christi et vinum in sanguinem, quia per idem quodlibet elementum in vino converteretur appropriate in elementum in Christo, cum tamen totus Christus sit in hoc sacramento ad quemlibet eius punctum, quia aliter non foret conversio aque in aquam **sed** oportet aquam infusam aque in Christi latere coequari vel ydemptificari aque alii.

ENGLISH: As to this teaching, it is clear to philosophers and theologians that it is an undigested teaching.[^8] For as to the first teaching, it is clear to philosophers that the [priest]{g:sacerdos} does not know when the water is assimilated to the wine; therefore it often happens that the water remains apart, by itself, until the priest swallows the sacrament, as is [sensually]{g:sensualiter} clear in winter, when the water freezes by itself and the wine, from its intrinsic heat, remains apart, fluid, as at first.[^9] Nor is there [colour]{g:color} [for saying] that the water is turned only into the water of Christ's side and the wine into the blood, since [by the same token]{g:per-idem} every element in the wine would be [converted]{g:conversio} correspondingly into an element in Christ; although the whole Christ is in this sacrament at every point of it; since otherwise there would not be a conversion of water into water, but the water poured in would have to be made equal to the water in Christ's side, or [identified]{g:ydemptificare} with that other water.[^10]

### 04-f ¶5 · 105.24–106.20  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 105] Ex quo videtur quod illa aqua sit sacramentum, quia forma eius est sacramentum, et sic coleret homo illam aquam ut sanguinem, ydolatrando (ut adversarii probant) quod panis non remanet ob hanc causam; ymmo videtur, cum accidens potest esse ad omnem punctum corporis cum hoc quod non informet, sine evidencia ponitur accidens taliter informare, ymmo inconsequenter dicitur quod illud sacramentum est ponderositas et non ponderosum, cum quantitas et qualitas alterius speciei possunt recipere predicaciones [p. 106] huiusmodi in concreto. Nec videtur ex evidenciis quod accidencia prioris vini circumfunduntur vino post consecracionem iniecto, quia eque vinum infusum utribus vel alii vasi recipit saporem ab eis quod non potest fingi tingi a sapore per se latente in utribus, **sed** corpora subtilia latencia consumat, odorem et saporem, ex quorum commixtione liquor infusus intingitur; et forte sic intelligunt hii accidencia que per se existunt in hostia esse corpora subtilia modo quo pictores vocant corpora quibus liniendo pingunt colores pictorum; nam (ut fatentur) sacramentum calicis potest misceri cum vino, et sic totum aggregatum foret sacramentum sine ydolatria, et tam quantitas quam qualitas rarefacta composita ex suis partibus intensivis, posito quod unum aliud sacramentum, tercium et quartum commisceantur in calice que constituant unum sacramentum, et sic nec quantitas nec qualitas foret sacramentum eucharistie, **sed** vel vinum vel ex vino et sanctitate aut alio aggregatum. Veritas itaque irridet tales vanas ficticias.

ENGLISH: From which it seems that that water is the sacrament, since its form is the sacrament; and so a man would worship that water as the blood, committing idolatry, which is the very ground on which the adversaries prove that the [bread]{g:panis} does not remain.[^15] Indeed it seems that, since an accident can be at every point of a body without [informing]{g:informare} it, it is posited without [evidence]{g:evidencia} that the accident informs in such a way. Indeed, it is said [inconsistently]{g:inconsequens} that that sacrament is heaviness and not heavy, since [quantity]{g:quantitas} and [quality]{g:qualitas} of another kind can receive predications [106] of this sort [in the concrete]{g:concretum}.[^16] Nor does it seem, from the evidences, that the accidents of the former wine are poured round the wine thrown in after the consecration; for equally wine poured into wineskins or another vessel receives taste from them, and it cannot be feigned to be tinged by a taste lying hidden by itself in the skins: rather, it takes up subtle bodies lying hidden, odour and taste, from whose mixture the liquid poured in is tinged.[^17] And perhaps in this way these men understand the accidents that exist of themselves in the [host]{g:hostia} to be subtle bodies, in the way in which painters call "bodies" the things with which, by smearing, they paint the painters' colours.[^18] For (as they admit) the sacrament of the chalice can be mixed with wine, and so the whole aggregate would be the sacrament without idolatry; and [so would] both the quantity and the quality, rarefied, composed of their intensive parts, supposing that one, another, a third and a fourth sacrament are mixed together in the chalice so as to make up one sacrament; and so neither quantity nor quality would be the sacrament of the eucharist, but either wine or an aggregate of wine and holiness or of something else.[^19] Truth therefore mocks such vain [fictions]{g:ficticia}.

### 04-g ¶1 · 106.21–107.4  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 106] **Sed** adhuc prodest curiam Avinonicam intromittere se circa tales materias quas ignorat et ignoranter testetur in aliquibus veritatem, ut exemplet nobis quomodo de talibus licitum est tractare, et ut videamus quam frivolum fundamentum est: si clerus Avinonicus sic diffinit in fide, tunc oportet omnes christianos sic credere. Nam predictum decretum ecclesie Romane in persona Beringarii et predicte decretales cleri Avinonici videntur expresse contradicere, ideo foret opus magistrale specialiter theologi aut decretiste si poterit harum ecclesiarum sentencias concordare, vel si non potest cui istarum sentenciarum credendum [p. 107] est dimissa reliqua tanquam heretica katholice declarare. Ego **autem** primum abnuens tanquam inutile non dubito de secundo quin decreti sentencia sit satis katholica ad sensum expositum.

ENGLISH: But it is still of profit that [the Avignon curia]{g:curia-avinonica} meddles in such matters, of which it is ignorant, and ignorantly testifies to the truth in some things: so that it may give us an example of how it is lawful to treat such things, and so that we may see how frivolous a foundation this is, "if the Avignon [clergy]{g:clerus} defines thus in faith, then all Christians must believe thus."[^1] For the aforesaid [decree]{g:decretum} of the Roman [church]{g:ecclesia} in the person of Berengar and the aforesaid [decretals]{g:decretalis} of the Avignon clergy seem expressly to contradict each other; so it would be a masterly work, especially for a theologian or a [decretist]{g:decretiste}, if he could bring the [teachings]{g:sentencia} of these churches into agreement; or, if he cannot, to [make clear]{g:declarare} in a catholic way which of these teachings is to be believed, [107] the other being laid aside as heretical.[^2] But I, refusing the first as useless, do not doubt as to the second that the teaching of the decree is catholic enough in the [meaning]{g:sensus} set out.[^3]

### 04-g ¶2 · 107.5–107.22  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 107] Ad quod preter evidencias predictas noto cronicam quam dicit Cistrensis libro VI°, capitulo XXXV°: Nicolaus, inquit, secundus Benedicto eiecto, sedit in papatu annis pene duobus, cuius diebus Gallicana ecclesia plurimum perturbatur per Beringarium Turonensem qui asserebat eukaristiam non verum corpus Christi esse **sed** figuram. Contra quem Nicholaus papa concilium celebravit in quo Beringarius errorem suum retractavit, sicut habetur in De Consecracione distinccione IIᵃ, capitulo Ego Beringarius. Lamfrancus eciam Beccensis abbas tonantem librum contra eundem edidit quem Scintillarium intitulavit; post hec Beringarius sancte vixit et obitum suum predixit. Ex istis videtur quod ecclesia quando plus habuit sollicitudinem de fide quam de seculari dominio, ponderavit errorem et fidelem revocacionem Beringarii que ex vite sue sanctitate sequente et dacione prophecie de suo obitu declaratur.

ENGLISH: For this, besides the [evidences]{g:evidencia} already given, I note the chronicle which the Chester monk tells, book 6, chapter 35: "Nicholas the second," he says, "after Benedict was cast out, sat in the [papacy]{g:papatus} almost two years; in whose days the Gallican church was greatly troubled by Berengar of Tours, who asserted that the eucharist was not the true [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} but a [figure]{g:figura}. Against him [Pope]{g:papa} Nicholas held a council, in which Berengar retracted his error, as is found in *De consecratione*, distinction 2, chapter *Ego Berengarius*. Lanfranc also, abbot of Bec, published a thundering book against him, which he entitled *Scintillarius*; after this Berengar lived a holy life and foretold his death."[^4] From these things it seems that the church, when it had more care for the faith than for secular [lordship]{g:dominium}, weighed the error and the faithful recantation of Berengar, which is made clear by the holiness of the life that followed and by the gift of prophecy about his death.[^5]

### 04-g ¶4 · 108.23–108.27  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 108] **Sed** redeundo querit ydiota si sacerdos potest conficere in calice ligneo, vitreo vel cupreo aut secundum ritum a nostris observanciis alienum, ut puta, dimittendo ornamenta sacerdotum et in maiori parte misse ac verbis sacramentalibus variando.

ENGLISH: But, to return, an [unlettered man]{g:ydiota} asks whether a [priest]{g:sacerdos} can [consecrate]{g:conficere} in a chalice of wood, glass or copper, or according to a rite foreign to our observances: for instance, by laying aside the vestments of priests and varying in the greater part of the mass and in the sacramental words.[^11]

### 04-g ¶5 · 109.1–110.21  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 109] **Sed** hic dicitur (ut supra) de duplici potencia; non est enim nostrum querere usque ad quos limites homo potest agere de Dei potencia absoluta; **sed** quid liceat homini et quid debeat facere de lege iam posita, sic quod expedit primo saltem confuse cognoscere quid homo potest facere in hoc ministerio. Expedit secundo magis distincte cognoscere quid de lege currente debet facere in hoc ministerio, et expedit tercio quod sine data occasione presumpcionis vel scandali nec excedat limites nec ocietur in illis que debet facere vel dicere in concernentibus fidem hostie consecrate; et hoc movit me dicere, primo quod sacramentum istud sensibile non est pars Christi, secundo quod nemo videt aliquam partem Christi in hoc sacramento oculo corporali, et tercio quod iuxta decretum Romane ecclesie hoc sacramentum est essencialiter panis et vinum sub quo debet credi Christum esse humanitus secundum quamlibet sui partem; et tam necessaria est noticia de quidditate huius sacramenti quod nemo potest cognoscere terciam questionem de illo, scilicet cuiusmodi est, nisi prius cognoverit eius quidditatem, quia, si scio qualis homo est, tunc scio hominem esse talem et per consequens scio quod ipse est homo et sic animal racionale, et per consequens scio quid est; et sic arguitur de quolibet cuius debeo cognoscere passionem. Sicut enim questio ‘quid est’, presupponit questionem ‘si est’, sic presupponitur ad terciam questionem ‘quia est’, que presupponitur ad questionem ‘propter quid est’, ut patet II° Posteriorum in principio; ideo cum [p. 110] unumquodque sic se habet ad esse sicut ad cognosci, patet quod oportet cognoscere questionem ‘quid est’ ante duas alias consequentes; noticia tamen quedam est confusa et communiter dicta; et quedam est distincta et propria; et priorem noticiam oportet eciam laicum prehabere de hostia antequam cognoverit duas alias questiones; ut si cognovero quod hoc est album et si ignorem eius immediatam speciem, adhuc confuse cognosco quod hoc est citra generalissimum, et aliter ignoro cuiusmodi hoc est, ut secundum tres opiniones recitatas in principio secundi capituli assignata ista proposicione hoc est sacramentum, cum cuiuslibet harum trium virtus intellectiva limitat singulariter intellectum iuxta suam opinionem esse hostiam, patet quod equivocant in pronomine, cum primus singulariter demonstrat quantitatem, secundus singulariter qualitatem **sed** nescit quam, et tercius singulariter substanciam panis; et patet quod nimis equivocant, cum quilibet horum negaret de demonstrato per reliquum, quid sit sacramentum.

ENGLISH: But here [the answer is]{g:dicitur} (as above) from the twofold [power]{g:potencia}: for it is not ours to ask up to what [limits]{g:limites} a man can act by God's [absolute power]{g:potencia-absoluta}, but what is lawful for a man and what he ought to do by the law already laid down; so that it is expedient, first, to know at least [indistinctly]{g:confuse} what a man can do in this ministry.[^12] It is expedient, second, to know more distinctly what he ought to do by the current law in this ministry; and it is expedient, third, that, without giving occasion of presumption or scandal, he neither exceed the limits nor be idle in the things he ought to do or say in what concerns the faith of the consecrated host.[^13] And this moved me to say, first, that this [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} is not a part of Christ; second, that no one sees any part of Christ in this sacrament with the bodily eye; and, third, that according to the decree of the Roman church this sacrament is [essentially]{g:essencialiter} bread and wine, under which Christ must be believed to be [humanly]{g:humanitus} as to every part of himself.[^14] And knowledge of the quiddity of this sacrament is so necessary that no one can know the third question about it, namely of what kind it is, unless he has first known its quiddity; since, if I know of what quality a man is, then I know that the man is such, and consequently I know that he is a man and so a rational animal, and consequently I know what he is; and so it is argued of anything whose [attribute]{g:passio} I am to know.[^15] For as the question "what it is" presupposes the question "whether it is", so it is presupposed to the third question, "that it is", which is presupposed to the question "why it is", as is clear in *Posterior Analytics* 2 at the beginning.[^16] Therefore, since [110] each thing stands to [being]{g:esse} as it stands to being known, it is clear that one must know the question "what it is" before the two others that follow.[^17] Yet one knowledge is indistinct and commonly so called, and another is distinct and proper; and the former knowledge even a layman must have of the host beforehand, before he has known the two other questions:[^18] as, if I have known that this is white and am ignorant of its [immediate]{g:immediate} species, I still know indistinctly that this is short of the most general [genus]; and otherwise I am ignorant of what kind this is.[^19] So, according to the three opinions recited at the beginning of the second chapter, when this [proposition]{g:proposicio} is assigned, "this is the sacrament", since the intellective [power]{g:virtus} of each of these three limits the understanding [alone]{g:singulariter} to what, by his opinion, is the host, it is clear that they equivocate in the pronoun: since the first demonstrates quantity alone; the second quality alone, but he does not know which; and the third the [substance]{g:substancia} of bread alone.[^20] And it is clear that they equivocate [exceedingly]{g:nimis}, since each of these would deny, of what is demonstrated by the other, that it is what the sacrament is.[^21]
