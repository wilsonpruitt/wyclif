# sed/autem extract 01: chunks 01-a–03-c

### 01-a ¶1 · 1.2–2.23  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 1] Quia nonnulli, eciam illi qui videntur esse aliquid discordant in materia de quiditate ecclesie, et fides est ecclesia que deberet in christianis omnibus esse una, decet christianos cognoscere matrem suam. Quomodo queso honoraret quis matrem illam primevam, sicut quilibet christianus tenetur sub pena dampnacionis ex primo mandato secunde tabule, nisi ipsam cognosceret? Aut quomodo ipsam perfecte cognosceret nisi cognoscendo eius quiditatem ac differencias sciretque a mulieribus alienis discernere? Unde inter omnes metaphisicas sub solis circulo non reputo quod sit alia christiano necessarior nec ad observanciam religionis christiane vel ad intelligenciam scripture sacre utilior. Christus enim ex fide est pater noster et dicta ecclesia sponsa sua est autonomatice mater nostra. Illos **autem** parentes debemus primo omnium honorare, quia recognoscendo eos in necessitatis articulo oportet eorum informacionem, castigacionem et suffragium invocare. Illis **autem** cultis ad plenum servatur plene christiana religio et de quanto deest eorum honoracio, deest preceptorum servacio, cum non sit possibile christianum delinquere, nisi de quanto subtrahit vel [p. 2] minuit honorem vel ministerium debitum istis parentibus. Et hec est racio, quare hoc precipitur in primo mandato secunde tabule tamquam metro et regula omnium aliorum. Quomodo ergo foret catholicus qui ignoraret partem simboli in qua fides matris sue inseritur? Nam credo ecclesiam catholicam dicit utrumque simbolum; nec credo dominis episcopis displicere quod tractetur fides christiana de quiditate ecclesie, tum quia ipsi maxime tenentur per se et per alios docere populum illam fidem, tum eciam quia contendunt de libertate et prosperitate, de edificacione et destruccione, de privilegiis et defensionibus ecclesie contra hereticos. **Sed** constat quod loqueretur ut pica qui tractaret de istis passionibus ignorando subiectum, cum secundo posteriorum in principio vere dicitur quod oportet cognoscere questionem, quid est de subiecto, antequam noscatur eius passio. Et error istius ordinis noscendi facit multos errare de fide ecclesie. Quomodo queso cognoscerem in quo staret eius prosperitas vel edificacio, nisi ipsam precognoscerem, cum sequatur: ego cognosco quod procedit prospere, ergo prius nosco illam esse? Adhibeamus ergo diligenciam, ut cognoscamus debite matrem nostram.

ENGLISH: Because some, even those who seem to be something,[^1] disagree on the question of the
[quiddity]{g:quiditas} of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, and [the object of] faith is the church, which
ought to be one in all Christians,[^2] it befits Christians to know their mother. How, I ask, would
anyone honour that primeval mother, as every Christian is bound to do under pain of damnation by the
first commandment of the second table,[^3] unless he knew her? Or how would he know her perfectly
except by knowing her quiddity and [differences]{g:differencia}, and knowing how to tell her apart
from [strange women]{g:mulier-aliena}?[^4] Hence among all the [metaphysics]{g:metaphisica} under the
circle of the sun I do not reckon that there is another more necessary to a Christian, nor more
useful for the observance of the Christian religion or for the understanding of holy scripture.[^5]
For, [as faith teaches]{g:ex-fide}, Christ is our father, and the said church, his
[bride]{g:sponsa}, is [by antonomasia]{g:autonomatice} [our mother]{g:mater-nostra}.[^6] Now those
parents we ought to honour first of all, because in acknowledging them we must,
[at the point of need]{g:in-necessitatis-articulo}, call upon their instruction, correction and
aid. And when they are honoured to the full, the Christian religion is fully
kept; and to the extent that honour to them is lacking, the keeping of the commandments is lacking,
since it is not possible for a Christian to transgress except to the extent that he withdraws or [2]
diminishes the honour or service owed to these parents. And this is the reason why this is commanded
in the first commandment of the second table, as the measure and rule of all the others. How, then,
would he be a catholic who was [ignorant of]{g:ignorare} the part of the creed in which the faith
concerning his mother is set down? For both creeds say "I believe the catholic church";[^7] nor do I
believe it displeases [the lord bishops]{g:dominus-episcopus} that the Christian faith concerning the
quiddity of the church should be treated, both because they themselves are bound above all, by
themselves and through others, to teach the people that faith, and also because they contend about
the liberty and prosperity, the building up and the destruction, the privileges and the defences of
the church against heretics.[^8] But it is evident that one who treated of these
[attributes]{g:passio} while ignorant of the [subject]{g:subiectum} would be speaking like a magpie,
since at the beginning of the second book of the *Posterior Analytics* it is truly said that one
must know the question "what is it?" about the subject before its attribute is known.[^9] And error
in this order of knowing makes many err about the faith of the church. How, I ask, could I know in
what her prosperity or building up consisted unless I knew her beforehand, since it follows: "I know
that she is prospering; therefore I first know that she exists"?[^10] Let us, then, apply diligence,
so that we may duly know our mother.

### 01-a ¶2 · 2.25–3.13  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 2] Quamvis **autem** ecclesia dicatur multipliciter in scriptura, suppono quod sumatur ad propositum pro famosiori, scilicet congregacione omnium predestinatorum. Illa **autem** est sponsa Christi, de qua est processus Cantici canticorum; de qua loquitur scriptura Isaie LXI°, 10: Tamquam sponsam decoravit me corona. Hec eciam est mulier fortis, de qua [p. 3] Proverb. XXXI°, 10, et corpus Christi misticum, de quo Iª Cor. 12°. Ista est Jerusalem, mater nostra, templum domini, regnum celorum et civitas regis magni, que tota inquit Augustinus Enchiridion XLVI°, accipienda est non solum ex parte qua peregrinatur in terra a solis ortu usque ad occasum, laudans nomen domini, et post captivitatem vetustatis cantans canticum novum, verum eciam ex illa que in celis semper ex quo condita est cohesit deo, nec ullum malum sui casus est experta. Hec enim in sanctis angelis beata persistit et sue parti peregrinanti, sicut oportet, opitulatur, quia utraque una erit consorcio eternitatis et nunc est una vinculo caritatis.

ENGLISH: And although "church" [is said in many ways]{g:dicitur-multipliciter} in scripture, I suppose that
for the present purpose it is taken for its [best-known]{g:famosior} [sense], namely the congregation
of all the [predestinate]{g:predestinatus}.[^11] And that church is the bride of Christ, of whom the
[course]{g:processus} of the Song of Songs treats; of whom scripture speaks in Isaiah 61:10: "As a
bride he has adorned me with a crown."[^12] She is also the [valiant woman]{g:mulier-fortis} of [3]
Proverbs 31:10, and the [mystical body of Christ]{g:corpus-Christi-misticum}, of which 1 Corinthians
12 speaks. She is Jerusalem our mother, the temple of the Lord, the [kingdom]{g:regnum} of heaven and
the city of the great king, "which as a whole", says Augustine in the *Enchiridion*, "is to be taken
not only in the part in which she [is a pilgrim]{g:peregrinari} on earth, praising the name of the
Lord from the rising of the sun to its going down, and, after the captivity of her old state, singing
a new song, but also in that part which in heaven has always, since it was founded, cleaved to God,
and has experienced no evil of its own fall. For this part persists blessed in the holy angels, and
helps its pilgrim part, as it should, because both will be one in the fellowship of eternity, and
are now one in the bond of [charity]{g:caritas}."[^13]

### 01-b ¶1 · 3.14–6.15  (9 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 3] Hec est sancta ecclesia catholica quam confitentur christiani immediate post fidem in spiritum sanctum propter tria: primo quia secundum Augustinum est summa creatura. Ideo immediate ponitur post trinitatem increatam. Secundo quia amore spiritus sancti Christo in matrimonio perpetuo copulatur. Et tercio quia posita trinitate oportet ipsam habere templum vel domum quam inhabitet. Oportet dico ex necessitate supposicionis atque de congruo. Ista **autem** ecclesia secundum partem peregrinantem non habet aliquem prescitum partem sui, sicut non [p. 4] habet secundum partem triumphantem, dum est huiusmodi, aliquem miserum vel quomodolibet maculatum, ut ostendit Augustinus, III° de doctrina christiana XXXII°, ubi, postquam ostenderat quod corpus Christi et caput eius Christus sunt una persona, increpat Tyconium in secunda sui regula qua vocat ecclesiam corpus domini bipartitum. Non inquit ita debuit appellare; non enim revera corpus domini est quod cum illo non manebit in eternum. Ideo vocat fideles ad tempus **sed** prescitos ad dampnacionem ecclesiam similatam ex ypocritis. Potest **autem** ista sentencia demonstrari ex scriptura Johannis VI°, 54, ubi dicitur Judeis et per idem cuilibet viatori: Nisi manducaveritis carnem filii hominis et biberitis eius sanguinem, non habebitis vitam in vobis. **Sed** nemo potest esse membrum corporis Christi, nisi fuerit membrum vivum: ergo nemo potest esse de illo corpore, nisi manducaverit corpus Christi. Quomodo queso potest continuari cum nervis capitis, nisi fide formata in caput crediderit? Quod est ipsum manducare, ut exponit Augustinus omelia XXVª. Habet **autem** iste cibus contrarie ad cibos corporales virtutem assimilandi sibi manducantem secundum quatuor digestiones, de quibus Rom. VIII°, 30, scilicet predestinacionem, vocacionem, iustificacionem et glorificacionem. Quamvis enim sic manducans moritur temporaliter, non tamen eternaliter, cum Christus, resurreccio et vita in finali iudicio vivificat omnia membra sua, quia leo rugiens suscitat suam ecclesiam. Caritas **autem** que nunquam excidit est vinculum [p. 5] continuans membra illius corporis; foret ergo contra racionem, vel quod sponsus in persona propria, vel quod alius ex permissione sua laniaret sponsam suam divisione eterna. Ymmo claudit contradictorium, si bene inspicitur; cum Jeremie XXXI°, 3, sibi dicitur: In caritate perpetua dilexi te. Ista igitur et non lapides est mater nostra per orbem terrarum confitens caput suum. Quomodo queso tam potens, tam sciolus et tam benivolus, sicut est increata trinitas, eternaliter eligeret sibi sponsam quam finaliter repudiaret? Patet ergo ex fide et signacione quid nominis ecclesie catholice quod ipsa est omnes predestinati presentes, preteriti et futuri. Ex istis sequuntur alique conclusiones. Prima, quod nullus vicarius Christi debet presumere asserere se esse caput ecclesie sancte catholice, ymmo nisi habuerit specialem revelacionem, non assereret se esse aliquod membrum eius. Patet ex hoc quod capitalem illius ecclesie oportet esse super omnes angelos beatos, cum ipsi sint membra illius ecclesie, quod soli Christo potest competere inter omnes predestinatos, ut patet Hebr. 1°, 4. Nam Rom. 1°, 4, dicitur Christum esse predestinatum; et sic ecclesia est solum numerus predestinatorum. Sicut igitur homo sine speciali revelacione non assereret sine formidine se esse predestinatum, sic nec assereret se esse membrum, et per consequens, non caput illius ecclesie. Unde Augustinus super psalmum CXXXIᵘᵐ postquam ostenderat quod nullum preterquam domum suam exaudit Deus ad vitam eternam: Ille inquit ad domum Dei [p. 6] pertinet qui est in caritate compaginatus lapidibus vivis; qui **autem** caritatem non habuerit, finalis perseverancie ruinam facit, **sed** domus stat. Et post docet quod nemo debet ex pretense potestatis vicaria pompare vel minari quod ab eo dependet status ecclesie, sicut fecerunt Judei ex filiacione et merito Abrahe, quia filiis imitacione virtutum et non filiis carnis degenerantibus promittitur hereditas. Et sic est indubie quoad Christi vicarios. Itaque inquit Augustinus in hiis est domus Dei quos predestinavit et prescivit perseveraturos, de quibus dictum est: Ubi steterunt sedes eius. Et hii qui non perseverant, non sunt ipsi ecclesia, non ipsi pertinent ad illud modo tabernaculum et tunc domum, **sed** solum in hiis qui perseveraverint usque in finem.

ENGLISH: This is the holy catholic [church]{g:ecclesia} which Christians confess immediately after faith in the
Holy Spirit, for three reasons.[^1] First, because according to Augustine she is the highest
creature;[^2] therefore she is placed immediately after the uncreated Trinity. Second, because by the
love of the Holy Spirit she is joined to Christ in perpetual marriage. And third, because, the Trinity
being posited, it must have a temple or house which it may inhabit: "must", I say, by [necessity of
supposition]{g:necessitas-supposicionis} and [by congruity]{g:de-congruo}.[^3] And this church, in
its [pilgrim part]{g:pars-peregrinans}, has no one [foreknown]{g:prescitus} as a part of itself,[^4]
just as, in its triumphant part, [4] it has no one wretched or in any way stained, while he is
such,[^5] as Augustine shows in *On Christian Teaching* III.32. There, after showing that "the body of
Christ and its head, Christ, are one person", he rebukes Tyconius over his second rule, in which he
calls the church the Lord's bipartite body. "He ought not", he says, "to have called it so; for what
will not remain with him for ever is not truly the Lord's body." Therefore he calls those who are
faithful for a time but foreknown to damnation a simulated church, made of hypocrites.[^6] And this
[teaching]{g:sentencia} can be demonstrated from the scripture of John 6:54, where it is said to the
Jews, and by the same token to every [wayfarer]{g:viator}:[^7] "Except you eat the flesh of the Son of
man and drink his blood, you shall not have life in you." Now no one can be a member of the body of
Christ unless he is a living member; therefore no one can be of that body unless he has eaten the body
of Christ. How, I ask, can he be joined to the sinews of the head unless he has believed in the head
with [formed faith]{g:fides-formata}? And that is what eating is, as Augustine expounds in Homily
25.[^8] Now this food, contrary to bodily foods, has the power of assimilating the eater to itself[^9]
according to four digestions, of which Romans 8:30 speaks: namely, predestination, calling,
justification and glorification.[^10] For although one who eats in this way dies in time, yet he does
not die eternally, since "Christ, the resurrection and the life", at the final judgement gives life to
all his members, because the roaring lion raises up his church.[^11] Now [charity]{g:caritas}, which
"never falleth away", is the bond [5] joining the members of that body; it would therefore be against
reason that either the [bridegroom]{g:sponsus} in his own person, or another by his permission, should
tear his [bride]{g:sponsa} apart by an eternal division. Indeed, if it is well considered, [it implies
a contradiction]{g:claudit-contradictorium},[^12] since it is said to her in Jeremiah 31:3: "I have
loved thee with an everlasting love."[^13] This, then, and not stones, is [our mother]{g:mater-nostra},
confessing her head throughout the world.[^14] How, I ask, would one so powerful, so
[knowing]{g:sciolus} and so benevolent as the uncreated Trinity is,[^15] choose eternally for itself a
bride whom it would finally repudiate? It is clear, therefore, [as faith teaches]{g:ex-fide} and from
the signification [of the term], [what the name]{g:quid-nominis} "catholic church" [means]: that she
is all the [predestinate]{g:predestinatus}, present, past and future.[^16]
From these some conclusions [follow]{g:sequitur}. The first: that no vicar of Christ ought to presume
to assert that he is head of the holy catholic church; indeed, unless he has had a special
revelation, he should not assert that he is any member of it.[^17] This is clear from the fact that
the head of that church must be above all the blessed angels, since they are members of that church;
and this can belong to Christ alone among all the predestinate, as is clear from Hebrews 1:4.[^18] For
in Romans 1:4 Christ is said to be "predestinated"; and so the church is only the number of the
predestinate. Therefore, just as a man without a special revelation would not assert without
misgiving that he is predestinate,[^19] so neither would he assert that he is a member, and
consequently not that he is head, of that church. Hence Augustine on Psalm 131, after he had shown
that God hears no one unto eternal life except his own house:[^20] "He belongs", he says, "to the
house of God [6] who is fitted together in charity with the living stones; but he who has not had the
charity of final perseverance falls in ruin, but the house stands."[^21] And afterwards he teaches that
no one ought, on the ground of the vicarship of a pretended power, to make a show or to threaten that
the state of the church depends on him, as the Jews did on the ground of their sonship to Abraham and
his merit; because the inheritance is promised to sons by the imitation of virtues, and not to
degenerate sons of the flesh. And so it is, without doubt, as regards the vicars of Christ.[^22] "So
then", says Augustine, "the house of God is in those whom he predestined and foreknew would persevere,
of whom it is said, 'Where his seats have stood.'[^23] And those who do not persevere, they are not
the church, they do not belong to that which is now a tabernacle and will then be a house; but [it is]
only in those who will have persevered to the end."[^24]

### 01-c ¶1 · 6.16–10.15  (6 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 6] Unde sicut Deus iuravit David, quod de fructu ventris sui (scilicet Christum, qui fuit natus de ventre virginis, et per consequens, de illo homine manu forti) poneret super sedem suam: ita iuravit filiis suis, sub condicione tamen, si custodierint testamentum et testimonia Christi. Ne inquit Augustinus aliquis minaretur promissis Dei et in potestate sua ponat quod promisit Deus, ut impleretur, ideo iuravit, annectens condicionem, ne iam glorieris de promissis et dimittas te non custodire; tunc eris filius David, si custodieris: si **autem** non, non eris filius David. Filiis David promisit Deus; noli dicere filius sum David, si degeneras; sola vidua sedebit in ecclesia benedicta. Tota ecclesia una vidua est, cuius membra sunt pauperes saturati, quia deserti sunt in hoc seculo, quod non desponsant hereditarie **sed** relinquunt, non sperant in diviciis honoribus vel amicis, **sed** si habent contempnunt sperando pure in domino. [p. 7] Quid igitur valet dicere quod solum isti qui sunt Rome sunt ecclesia vel caput ecclesie? Tales enim uruntur luna per noctem, ut exponit Augustinus psalmo CXX°. Quisquis inquit putaverit ecclesiam in una parte esse et non eam cognoverit diffusam toto orbe terrarum et crediderit eis qui dicunt: Ecce hic est Christus et ecce illic, sicut loquitur evangelium Mathei XXIV°, cum ille totum orbem terrarum emerit quoad valorem precii et ille in proximo scandalizatur et uritur a luna. Ad quid igitur oportet contendere circa caput ecclesie? Ipsa inquit Augustinus caro Christi est caput ecclesie, id est, humanitas secundum quam Christus predestinatus est, ut dicit fides scripture Ephes. I°, IV° et V° capitulis et expressius Col. 1°, 18, dicit quod Christus est caput tocius ecclesie. Et istam conclusionem declarat bene dicta decretalis extravagans Bonifacii VIII que sic incipit: Unam sanctam. Igitur inquit ecclesie unius unum corpus, unum caput, non duo capita quasi monstrum. Secunda conclusio sequens ex quiditate matris ecclesie est quod tantum est una, sic quod non multe ecclesie catholice. Demonstratur sic: Eo ipso quod est ecclesia universalis sive catholica, ipsa continet in se omnes predestinatos. Non est possibile quod sit nisi una talis, igitur non est possibile quod sit nisi una universalis ecclesia. Nam secundum philosophos universale est quoddam totum atque perfectum cui [p. 8] nichil deest. Unde sicut iuxta Aristotelem primo de celo omnia primo super tria ponimus: sic non dicimus ecclesiam catholicam nisi que in se continet ista tria: partem in celo triumphantem, partem in purgatorio dormientem et partem in terris militantem. Et in figuram illius dicunt doctores hostiam vel sacramentum eucaristie dividi in tres partes: Primam immersam sacramento liquido dicunt signare ecclesiam triumphantem, que insorpta et inebriata est intuicione divine essencie, ut loquitur caput ecclesie Cantic. V° 1, exhilarans cives et commensales suos. Inebriamini inquit carissimi. Due **autem** alie partes in manu domini et merito ecclesie expurgande signantur per illas duas partes quas sacerdos tenet in manibus: Maior supposita signat ecclesiam militantem et minor innixa supposite signat ecclesiam in purgatorio expectantem. Ipsa enim innititur suffragiis militantis ecclesie. Et pro istis duabus partibus geminamus preces agno qui est caput ecclesie, ut misereatur nobis. **Sed** pro tercia parte ad cuius locum et requiem aspiramus petimus, ut idem agnus triplicis nature donet finaliter nobis pacem. Et hinc (ut ostendi alias) dictus sponsus secundum humanitatem suam visitavit tria loca ecclesie, scilicet umbilicum nostre habilitabilis, triginta tribus annis in Judea et Jerusalem conversando, limbum in quo patres purgati sunt, morsellum sue ecclesie in anima extrahendo; **sed** tercio finaliter ascendens in celum cepit captivitatem quam post triumphum coronavit ad dei dexteram collocando. [p. 9] Ista ergo est triplex pars unius universalis ecclesie sive catholice, licet quotlibet sint particulares ecclesie, quia tot quot sunt homines predestinati. Et hinc credo quod in simbolo ecclesie confitentur se credere unam sanctam catholicam et apostolicam ecclesiam. Apostoli enim sunt modo in celo in eadem personalitate qua vixerunt in terris, et sunt modo partes plene purgate eiusdem matris ecclesie, auctoritate quorum sui vicarii iam regunt adolescentulam modo querentem sponsum ecclesie; et sic loquitur decretum XXIV, q. I. Manet inquit Leo papa Petri privilegium ubicunque ex ipsius equitate fertur iudicium. Ipse enim residet in celis videns et procurans quod deus liget et solvat. Unde supradicta decretalis sic loquitur: Unam sanctam ecclesiam catholicam et ipsam apostolicam urgente fide credere cogimur et tenere. Unde unitatem illius ecclesie probat ex scriptura multiplici, primo per illud Canticorum VI°, 8: Una est columba mea, perfecta mea: una est matri sue, electa genitrici sue. Ista columba secundum concordem sanctorum sentenciam est mater ecclesia, cuius mater, quia creatrix est trinitas vel divina essencia, cuius genitrix et fotrix est patris sapiencia et ipsa mater virgo et sponsa humanitatis Christi sui capitis. Ex quibus parentibus spiritualiter, et per consequens, gignicione virginea filii ecclesie procreantur, iuxta quod loquitur apostolus 2ª Cor. XI°, 2, Despondi vos uni viro virginem castam exhibere Christo. Cum ergo in tota scriptura non ponitur vel una sillaba sine sensu, non restat quid pertinencius signaret hoc adiectum una [p. 10] in libro sapientissimi Salomonis nisi unitatem ecclesie catholice excludentem pluralitatem. Unde spiritus sanctus in patre Salomonis David sic orat in Christo psalmo XXI°, 21, Erue a framea Deus animam meam et de manu canis unicam meam. Quem textum secundo per decretalem allegatam beatus Augustinus sic exponit tractatu secundo eiusdem psalmi: Framea inquit gladius est, et per gladium mortem intelligi voluit. Unicam meam dixit ecclesiam. Canes sunt qui ceco more latrant nec intelligunt contra quos, eciam cum nichil eis fit, latrant contra viantes, licet velint eis proficere. Et utinam nostri possessionati non sint tales qui conspirant contra catholicos, tangentes eorum potestatem, fastum et ablacionem temporalium solummodo ad medelam.

ENGLISH: Hence, just as God swore to David that of the fruit of his womb (namely Christ, who was born of the
womb of the virgin, and consequently of that man "strong of hand") he would set [one] upon his
seat,[^1] so he swore to his sons, yet under a condition: if they keep the testament and the
testimonies of Christ. "Lest anyone", says Augustine, "should threaten on the strength of God's
promises, and put in his own power what God promised, that it should be fulfilled, therefore he
swore, attaching a condition: lest you now glory in the promises and let yourself off keeping [them].
Then you will be a son of David, if you keep [them]; but if not, you will not be a son of David."[^2]
To the sons of David God made the promise; do not say "I am a son of David" if you are degenerate.
Only the widow will sit, blessed, in the [church]{g:ecclesia}.[^3] The whole church is one widow,
whose members are the poor who are filled, because they are forsaken in this world, which they do not
wed as an inheritance but leave; they do not hope in riches, honours or friends, but if they have
them they despise them, hoping purely in the Lord. [7] What then is it worth to say that only those
who are at Rome are the church, or the head of the church? For such are "burned by the moon by
night", as Augustine expounds on Psalm 120.[^4] "Whoever", he says, "thinks that the church is in one
part, and does not know her as spread through the whole world, and believes those who say 'Lo here
is Christ, and lo there', as the gospel of Matthew 24 speaks, although he bought the whole world as
regards the value of the price, he is scandalized in his neighbour and is burned by the moon."[^5] To
what end, then, must there be contention about the head of the church? "The very flesh of Christ",
says Augustine, "is the head of the church", that is, the humanity according to which Christ was
predestinated, as the faith of scripture says in Ephesians chapters 1, 4 and 5; and more expressly
Colossians 1:18 says that Christ is head of the whole church.[^6] And this conclusion is well set out
by the decretal extravagant of Boniface VIII which begins thus: *Unam sanctam*. "Therefore", it says,
"of the one church [there is] one body, one head, not two heads like a monster."[^7]
The second conclusion, following from the [quiddity]{g:quiditas} of mother church, is that she is
only one, so that there are not many catholic churches. It is demonstrated thus. By the very fact that
she is the universal or catholic church, she contains in herself all the
[predestinate]{g:predestinatus}. It is not possible that there be more than one such; therefore it is
not possible that there be more than one universal church.[^8] For according to the philosophers the
[universal]{g:universale} is a certain whole and perfect thing, from which [8] nothing is lacking.
Hence, just as according to Aristotle in *On the Heavens* I we first apply "all" to three, so we do
not call [a church] catholic unless it contains in itself these three: the part triumphant in heaven,
the part sleeping in purgatory, and the part militant on earth.[^9] And in figure of this the doctors
say that the host, or sacrament of the eucharist, is divided into three parts. The first,
immersed in the liquid sacrament, they say signifies the church triumphant, which is absorbed and
inebriated with the vision of the divine essence, as the head of the church speaks in the Canticle
5:1, gladdening his fellow citizens and table-companions: "Be inebriated", he says, "my dearly
beloved."[^10] And the other two parts, which are to be purged in the hand of the Lord and by the
merit of the church, are signified by those two parts which the priest holds in his hands: the larger,
placed beneath, signifies the church militant, and the smaller, resting on the one beneath, signifies
the church in purgatory, waiting. For it rests on the [suffrages]{g:suffragia} of the church militant.
And for these two parts we double our prayers to the Lamb, who is the head of the church, that he may
have mercy on us. But for the third part, to whose place and rest we aspire, we ask that the same Lamb
of threefold nature may finally grant us peace.[^11] And hence (as I have shown elsewhere) the said
[bridegroom]{g:sponsus}, according to his humanity, visited three places of the church: namely, the
navel of our habitable [world], living thirty-three years in Judea and Jerusalem; limbo, in which the
fathers were purged, drawing out a morsel of his church in his soul; but third, finally ascending
into heaven, he took captivity captive, which after the triumph he crowned, placing it at the right
hand of God.[^12] [9] This, then, is the threefold part of the one universal or catholic church,
although there are any number of particular churches, for there are as many as there are predestinate
men.[^13] And hence I believe that in the creed of the church they confess that they believe "one holy
catholic and apostolic church".[^14] For the apostles are now in heaven in the same personality in
which they lived on earth, and are now fully purged parts of the same mother church; by their
authority their vicars now rule the young maiden who is now seeking the bridegroom of the church.[^15]
And so the decree speaks in [Causa] 24, q. 1: "Peter's privilege remains", says Pope Leo, "wherever
judgement is given in accordance with his equity."[^16] For he sits in heaven, seeing and procuring
that God binds and looses. Hence the aforesaid decretal speaks thus: "We are compelled, faith urging
us, to believe and to hold one holy catholic church, and that apostolic." Hence it proves the unity of
that church from manifold scripture: first by that [verse] of the Canticle 6:8: "One is my dove, my
perfect one is but one; she is the only one of her mother, the chosen of her that bore her."[^17] This
dove, according to the concordant [teaching]{g:sentencia} of the saints, is mother church: whose
mother, because creatress, is the Trinity or the divine essence; whose genetrix and nurse is the
Father's wisdom; and she herself is mother, virgin and [bride]{g:sponsa} of the humanity of Christ, her
head.[^18] From these parents, spiritually, and consequently by a virginal begetting, the sons of the
church are procreated, according to what the apostle says in 2 Corinthians 11:2: "I have espoused you
to one husband, that I may present you as a chaste virgin to Christ." Since, therefore, in the whole
of scripture not one syllable is set down without sense, nothing remains that this adjective "one"
[10] in the book of the most wise Solomon could signify more pertinently than the unity of the
catholic church, excluding plurality. Hence the Holy Spirit, in David the father of Solomon, prays
thus in Christ in Psalm 21:21: "Deliver, O God, my soul from the sword: my only one from the hand of
the dog." This text, which the decretal cites second, the blessed Augustine expounds thus in the second
treatise on the same psalm: "The sword", he says, "is a blade, and by the blade he meant death to be
understood. 'My only one' he called the church. Dogs are those who bark in blind fashion and do not
understand against whom; even when nothing is done to them, they bark against those on the road,
although these wish to do them good."[^19] And would that our [possessioners]{g:possessionati} were not
such as conspire against catholics who touch on their power, their pride and the removal of their
[temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, only for [their] healing.[^20]

Unde si aliquis christianus foret cum eo caput universalis ecclesie, cum ipsa non potest esse monstrum, habens ex equo duo capita, ut dicit secunda conclusio, oporteret concedere quod est Christus, vel aliter quod Christus foret ipso inferior et humile membrum eius. Ideo apostoli confessi sunt concorditer se esse servos istius capitis et humiles ministros ecclesie sponse sue, nunquam **autem** presumpsit aliquis apostolorum asserere quod fuit caput vel sponsus dicte ecclesie, quia hoc foret antichristiane adulterari cum regina celi, zelotipando dominum Jesum Christum; quod foret nimis grave, cum Exodi XX°, 5, vere dicitur quod ipse sit fortis zelotes Et secunda pars patet ex hoc quod caput est nomen dignitatis atque officii dignitatis quoad predestinacionem eternam, qua Christus ordinavit prepositos (qui presunt secundum legem suam in ecclesia militante) habere mansiones superiores racione sui regiminis in ecclesia triumphante, quia si prepositi omnino deficiant ab officio ministrandi subditis sensum scripture [p. 18] et motum ad sequendum caput ecclesie, tunc non sunt caput in civitate Dei, **sed** in civitate diaboli capitis perimendi, quia Sap. VI°, 6, vere dicitur: Durum iudicium hiis qui presunt fiet. Cum ergo nos miseri ignoramus si sumus ad celestem Jerusalem predestinati, (quod tamen ex speciali revelacione habuerunt sancti) patet quod nimia presumpcio foret nobis pertinaciter asserere quod sumus capita cuiuscunque particularis ecclesie que foret pars sancte matris ecclesie, quia notum est ex concordi testimonio sanctorum quod nullus prescitus est vel membrum ipsius ecclesie. Quomodo ergo presumeret aliquis nostrum asserere quod sit caput, cum Eccl. IX°, 1, vere dicitur quod nemo talis scit, utrum sit dignus amore vel odio quoad predestinacionem vel reprobacionem. Insuper si respicimus ad sensum et motum quos in subditos influimus et ex alio latere ad speculum scripture iuxta quam totam vitam nostram regularemus, eligeremus pocius vocari servi et ministri ecclesie quam capita, cum notum sit, si non facimus officium capitatis, non sumus capita, quia secundum Augustinum in libello suo De Decem Cordis maritus perversus non est caput uxoris sue, multo magis prepositus ecclesie qui pure haberet a Deo dignitatem huiusmodi, si a Christo degenerat. Unde postquam Augustinus ostenderat quod femina vere christiana debet dolere de fornicacione viri non propter carnem **sed** propter caritatem et castitatem debitam viro Christo, dicit consequenter quod Christus loquitur in cordibus feminarum bonarum, ubi vir non audit dicens: Dole de viri tui iniuriis **sed** noli imitari, quin pocius ipse te imitetur in bono. Nam [p. 19] in eo quod male facit noli eum putare caput tuum **sed** me Deum tuum. Et probat hoc debere fieri. Si inquit in hoc quod male facit caput est, et consecuturum est corpus caput suum, eunt ambo in precipucium. Ut **autem** christianus non sequatur malum caput suum, teneat se ad caput ecclesie Christum, huic debens castitatem suam, huic deferens honorem suum, absens sit vir privatus, presens sit vir matri ecclesie coniugatus.

ENGLISH: From these things it is gathered, since Christ alone is head of the whole [church]{g:ecclesia}, which
is not a part relative to another (according to the first conclusion), that it must be granted that,
just as no Christian ought to presume that he is head of the universal church, so neither ought he,
without fear and without special revelation, to assert that he is head of any particular church.[^1]
The first part is clear from the first conclusion; for if anyone is head of such a universal church,
then he is "made better than the angels", indeed than every blessed created spirit, which can belong to
Christ alone, according to the apostle, Hebrews 1:4. For he must be "the firstborn" out of many
brethren, and consequently the captain by the dignity of the [right]{g:ius} of primogeniture.[^2] Hence
if some Christian were, with him, head of the universal church, since she cannot be a monster having
two heads on an equal footing, as the second conclusion says, it would have to be granted that he is
Christ, or else that Christ would be inferior to him and a lowly member of his. Therefore the apostles
confessed with one accord that they were servants of that head and lowly ministers of the church, his
[bride]{g:sponsa}; but none of the apostles ever presumed to assert that he was head or
[bridegroom]{g:sponsus} of the said church, for this would be to commit adultery, antichristianly, with
the queen of heaven, provoking the Lord Jesus Christ to jealousy; which would be too grave, since in
Exodus 20:5 it is truly said that he is "mighty, jealous".[^3]
And the second part is clear from the fact that "head" is a name of dignity, and of the office of
dignity, with respect to eternal predestination, by which Christ ordained that
[superiors]{g:prepositi} (who are set over [others] according to his law in the [church
militant]{g:ecclesia militans}) should have higher mansions, by reason of their rule, in the church
triumphant;[^4] for if superiors wholly fail in the office of ministering to their subjects the sense
of scripture [18] and the motive to follow the head of the church, then they are not head in the city of
God, but in the city of the devil, the head that is to be destroyed; for in Wisdom 6:6 it is truly said:
"A severe judgment shall be for them that bear rule."[^5] Since, therefore, we wretches [are ignorant
of]{g:ignorare} whether we are [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} to the heavenly Jerusalem (which the
saints nevertheless had by special revelation), it is clear that it would be too great a presumption
for us to assert pertinaciously that we are heads of any particular church which would be part of holy
mother church; for it is known from the concordant testimony of the saints that no
[foreknown]{g:prescitus} [person] is even a member of that church.[^6] How then would any of us presume
to assert that he is head, since in Ecclesiastes 9:1 it is truly said that no such man knows whether
he is "worthy of love, or hatred", as to predestination or reprobation?[^7] Moreover, if we look to the
sense and motion which we pour into our subjects, and on the other side to the mirror of scripture, by
which we should rule our whole life, we would choose rather to be called servants and ministers of the
church than heads; since it is known that if we do not do the office of headship we are not heads.[^8]
For according to Augustine, in his little book *On the Ten Strings*, a perverse husband is not the head
of his wife; much more [is this so of] a superior of the church, who would have such a dignity purely
from God, if he degenerates from Christ.[^9] Hence, after Augustine had shown that a truly Christian
woman ought to grieve over the fornication of her husband, not on account of the flesh but on account
of the [charity]{g:caritas} and chastity owed to the husband Christ, he says next that Christ speaks in
the hearts of good women, where the husband does not hear, saying: "Grieve over your husband's wrongs,
but do not imitate [them]; rather, let him imitate you in good. For [19] in that in which he does evil,
do not think him your head, but me, your God." And he proves that this ought to be done: "If," he says,
"he is head in that in which he does evil, and the body is to follow its head, both go over the
precipice."[^10] And so that a Christian should not follow his evil head, let him hold to the head of
the church, Christ, owing to him his chastity, bearing to him his honour; let the private husband be
absent, let the husband wedded to mother church be present.[^11]

### 01-f ¶2 · 19.10–19.30  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 19] Patet ergo quod, sicut infinitum maiorem honorem debemus patri spirituali domino Jesu Christo et sponse sue ecclesie que est regina, assistens a dexteris eius (ut dicitur Psal. XLIV°, 10) quam alicui parenti nostro carnali, sic nemo est parens vel proles huius celestis coniugii, nisi faciat debitum quod in predestinacione divina est deputatum isti officio. Nam esse caput vel membrum sancte matris ecclesie est inseparabiliter nomen officii. Ex istis septimo sequitur quod dominus papa, si predestinatus est et exercet pastorale officium, est caput tante militantis ecclesie quantam regit, ut si sic regit capitaliter secundum legem Christi totam iam militantem ecclesiam, tunc est eius particularis capitaneus sub archicapite, domino Jesu Christo. Hoc **autem** debet supponi in domino Romano pontifice, nisi factum doceat evidenter oppositum, cum clerus iam peregrinans consensit in eum ut capitaneum. Verumptamen non est in potestate alicuius christiani constitucione, eleccione vel acceptacione statuere quod dominus papa sit caput vel membrum sancte matris ecclesie. Nam hoc consistit in predestinacione et gracia Dei nostri.

ENGLISH: It is clear, therefore, that, just as we owe infinitely greater honour to our spiritual father, the
Lord Jesus Christ, and to his bride the church, who is queen, standing at his right hand (as is said in
Psalm 44:10), than to any carnal parent of ours, so no one is parent or offspring of this heavenly
marriage unless he renders the due which in divine predestination is assigned to that office.[^12] For
to be head or member of holy mother church is inseparably the name of an office. From these things, in
the seventh place, [it follows]{g:sequitur} that the lord [pope]{g:papa}, if he is predestinate and
exercises the pastoral office, is head of as much of the church militant as he rules; so that, if he
thus rules as head, according to the law of Christ, the whole church now militant, then he is its
particular captain under the arch-head, the Lord Jesus Christ.[^13] And this ought to be supposed in the
lord Roman pontiff unless the fact evidently shows the opposite, since the clergy now [on
pilgrimage]{g:peregrinari} consented to him as captain. Nevertheless it is not in the power of any
Christian, by constitution, election or acceptance, to establish that the lord pope is head or member
of holy mother church. For this consists in the predestination and grace of our God.[^14]

### 01-f ¶3 · 19.31–20.17  (7 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 19] In ista vero materia sunt quotlibet subterfugia, ut unus dicit quod nulla mater ecclesia potest esse; **sed** [p. 20] iste videtur michi infideli deterior, cum nedum negat fidem scripture et fidem ecclesie de credulitate qua oportet christianum credere ecclesiam sanctam catholicam, **sed** de facto non est filius illius celestis coniugii, sine cuius gignicione non possumus esse filii Dei, et Per consequens, nec heredes regni. Alii **autem** dicunt quod sancta mater ecclesia est eciam ecclesia, **sed** non est aliquid, et sic nec mulier nec corpus Christi mysticum nec regina **Sed** isti; (ut tetigi alias) debent negare magnam partem fidei scripture, decreta ecclesie Romane et simbolum fidei christiane. Tercii vero dicunt quod universalis ecclesia non est nec erit ante diem iudicii, **sed** multi fuerunt et multi sunt qui erunt tunc partes eius, **sed** nec habet nec habuit partes aliquas, eo quod nec est nec fuit, cum tunc foret universitas predestinatorum qui erunt in die iudicii. Quod est impossibile, cum multi eorum sint generandi.

ENGLISH: But in this matter there are any number of subterfuges: as one says that there can be no mother
church; but [20] this man seems to me worse than an [unbeliever]{g:infidelis}, since he not only denies
the faith of scripture and the faith of the church concerning the [credence]{g:credulitas} by which a
Christian must believe the holy catholic church, but in fact he is not a son of that heavenly
marriage, without whose begetting we cannot be sons of God, and consequently not heirs of the
[kingdom]{g:regnum}.[^15] And others say that holy mother church is also a church, but is not anything,
and so is neither a woman nor the [mystical body of Christ]{g:corpus Christi misticum} nor a queen. But
these (as I have touched on elsewhere) must deny a great part of the faith of scripture, the decrees of

church is not, nor will be before the day of judgement; but many have been and many are who will then
be its parts; yet it neither has nor has had any parts, because it neither is nor was, since [only]
then would it be the totality of the predestinate who will be at the day of judgement. Which is
impossible, since many of them are yet to be begotten.[^17]

### 01-g ¶1 · 20.18–22.5  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 20] **Sed** oportet sic opinantes negare maiorem partem scripture sacre et specialiter magnum sacramentum de Christo atque ecclesia de quo loquitur apostolus Ephesiis V°, 32. Immo oportet sic loquentem negare omnem particularem ecclesiam, cum particulare non dicitur nisi in comparacione ad totale et universale. Ideo negata universali et totali ecclesia neganda foret quelibet particularis ecclesia, vel aliter dicendum foret quod Deus cognoscit mille universales ecclesias: quod esset summe hereticum, quia nedum sentiret contra ecclesiam **sed** omnino destrueret ecclesiam et singulas leges eius. Sic ergo error in logica scripture quem diabolus introducit ad cecandum mentes fidelium, facit filios eius prorumpere in conclusiones multas blasphemas ex quibus mater ecclesia perturbatur. Nam sancti doctores dicunt concorditer quod omnes electi a principio mundi usque ad diem iudicii sunt una [p. 21] persona que est mater ecclesia, ut patet per beatum Gregorium XXIII° Moralium primo capitulo supra illo Job XIX°, 25, Scio quod redemptor meus vivit. Sciendum inquit quod redemptor noster unam personam se cum sua ecclesia quam assumpsit exhibuit. De ipso enim dicitur: Qui est caput Christus. Rursumque de eius ecclesia scriptum est: Et corpus Christi quod est ecclesia. Et sequitur: Quod Christum inquit, et ecclesiam unam personam credimus, hoc eciam unius persone actibus significari videamus. Et idem dicit XIX° Moralium cap. IX et IV° Moralium cap. IX. De illa siquidem ecclesia cuius Christus est caput (secundum testimonium huius sancti pape) suppono quod fiat nobis sermo. Et patet quod, si christianus foret caput illius ecclesie, tunc vel haberet multa capita quod negat decretalis, vel aliter christianus foret Christo superior. Non enim est color quod quilibet qui est caput partis ecclesie sit caput illius ecclesie, quia tunc nedum dominus papa **sed** quilibet uxoratus et omne caput bestie foret caput ecclesie, et sic (ut sophiste concedunt) ecclesia haberet monstruosissime caput et membra quotlibet asinina; quod foret nimis sophisticum. Videamus ergo racionem secundum quam Christus dicetur singulariter caput tocius sponse sue post diem iudicii, et patebit luce clarius quod nemo alius potest esse caput illius ecclesie. **Sed** obicitur per dominum Ardmacanum libro VII°, De Questionibus Armenorum cap. VI, ubi videtur tenere quod idem corpus habet multa capita subordinata, [p. 22] sicut idem populus habet multos dominos, unum subordinatum alteri (sicut patet in materia civili). Et sic videtur dicere quod eadem ecclesia habet multa capita ordinata secundum inferius atque superius.

ENGLISH: But those who hold this opinion must deny the greater part of holy scripture, and especially the great
sacrament of Christ and the [church]{g:ecclesia}, of which the apostle speaks in Ephesians 5:32.[^1]
Indeed, one who speaks so must deny every particular church, since "particular" is said only in
comparison to the total and universal. Therefore, if the universal and total church is denied, every
particular church would have to be denied; or else it would have to be said that God knows a thousand
universal churches: which would be supremely heretical, since it would not only think against the
church but would utterly destroy the church and each of its laws.[^2] So, therefore, the error in the
logic of scripture which the devil brings in to blind the minds of the faithful makes his sons break out
into many blasphemous conclusions, by which mother church is troubled.[^3] For the holy doctors say
with one accord that all the elect from the beginning of the world to the day of judgement are one [21]
person, which is mother church, as is clear through the blessed Gregory, *Moralia* XXIII, the first
chapter, on Job 19:25, "I know that my Redeemer liveth." "It is to be known," he says, "that our
Redeemer has shown himself one person with his church, which he assumed. For of him it is said: 'who is
the head, even Christ'. And again of his church it is written: 'and the body of Christ, which is the
church'." And he goes on: "What we believe," he says, "that Christ and the church are one person, let
us see signified also in the acts of one person."[^4] And the same [Gregory] says [this] in *Moralia*
XIX, chapter 9, and *Moralia* IV, chapter 9. It is of that church, indeed, whose head is Christ
(according to the testimony of this holy pope), that I suppose our discourse to be.[^5]
And it is clear that, if a Christian were head of that church, then either it would have many heads,
which the decretal denies, or else a Christian would be superior to Christ. For there is no colour [for
saying] that whoever is head of a part of the church is head of that church; for then not only the lord
[pope]{g:papa} but every married man, and every beast's head, would be head of the church; and so (as
the sophists grant) the church would most monstrously have a head and any number of asinine members;
which would be too sophistical.[^6] Let us see, therefore, the reason according to which Christ will be
called singularly the head of his whole [bride]{g:sponsa} after the day of judgement, and it will be
clearer than light that no one else can be head of that church.[^7] But it is objected, by appeal to the
lord of Armagh, in book 7 *On the Questions of the Armenians*, chapter 6, where he seems to hold that the
same body has many subordinate heads, [22] as the same people has many lords, one subordinate to
another (as is clear in civil matters). And so he seems to say that the same church has many heads,
ordered as lower and higher.[^8]

### 01-h ¶1 · 22.6–25.22  (16 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 22] Hic dicitur quod idem corpus ecclesie habet multa capita, ut quelibet particularis ecclesia habet ad minimum tria capita, scilicet divinitatem, Christi humanitatem et capitaneum illius ecclesie. Ecclesia **autem** universalis que est totum corpus Christi misticum habet duo capita, scilicet caput extrinsecum que est divinitas et caput intrinsecum que est Christi humanitas, et modo ineffabili divinitas et humanitas Christi sunt duo capita tocius ecclesie et cum hoc eciam unum caput: Sicut filius et spiritus sanctus sunt unus paraclitus, intelligendo principative vel essencialiter, et tamen sunt duo paracliti, intelligendo personaliter (cum Joh. XIV°, 16 dicitur: Ego rogabo patrem et alium paraclitum dabit vobis): sic iste due nature sunt duo capita ecclesie, et tamen sunt personaliter unum caput. Particulares **autem** ecclesie, sicut fuit pusillus grex cui presidebat sanctus Petrus, non erant nisi iuvencule tocius matris ecclesie, sicut exponit Gregorius XIX° Moralium cap. VIII, declarans etatem ecclesie quomodo in principio fuit soror parvula, carens uberibus predicacionis. Unde sic infert: Universe quippe ecclesie que unam catholicam faciunt adolescentule vocantur. Unde beatum Petrum et alias particulares ecclesias vocat membra illius ecclesie quam leenam nominat, Christum vero leonem (ut [p. 23] declarat XXVIII° Moralium cap. XI), dicens quod, sicut membra corporis non sunt ipsum corpus, sic particulares ecclesie non sunt illa sancta ecclesia, **sed** (ut docet apostolus i° Cor. XII, 12) membra eius. Istis positis dico quod nec dominus Ardmacanus nec aliquis doctor catholicus debet asserere quod aliquis christianus sit caput universalis **sed** particularis ecclesie, cum non sit color: Hoc caput est illius ecclesie, ergo hoc est caput illius ecclesie; nec est color: Si hoc est caput partis illius ecclesie, ergo hoc est caput illius ecclesie. Sic enim omnis vir foret caput Dei, sicut omnis prelatus inferior foret caput sue matricis ecclesie. **Sed** apostolus contradicit istis arguciis, I° Cor. XI, 3, dicens: Volo **autem** vos scire quod omnis viri caput est Christus, caput **autem** mulieris vir, caput vero Christi Deus. In quo dicto patet ordo capitum et deteccio illarum fallaciarum. Quia aliter eque diceret econtra quod caput Christi et Dei foret tam vir quam mulier. Ecclesia itaque catholica, secundum beatum Gregorium super illo Matthei XXIII°, 34: Ecce mitto ad vos sapientes et scribas, est vinea domini plantata a principio mundi usque ad finem fructificans, quotquot sanctos gignit tot palmites producit. Et sicut nemo est naturaliter caput matris sue **sed** pars nisi Christus: sic nullus aliorum sanctorum est caput matris ecclesie **sed** membrum. Sic enim dicit beatus Gregorius (ubi supra) quod beatus Job, beatus Petrus et ceteri precones matris ecclesie sunt os eius, et breviter, quilibet [p. 24] aliorum sanctorum proporcionaliter ad analogiam virtutis sue et proprietatis membri corporalis est pars sancte matris ecclesie. Unde beatus Gregorius IV° Moralium cap. XXXII narrat quomodo a principio mundi Deus ordinavit sponse sue patriarchas secundum virtutum principia tamquam membra radicalia ecclesie inserenda, ut patet de innocencia Abel, de mundicia Enoch, de castimonia coniugali Isaac, de tolerancia Jacob, de spe Noe, de obediencia Abrahe, de paciencia Job, de castitate Joseph, de mansuetudine Moysi, de fiducia Josue, de humilitate David, de zelo Helye, et sic discurrendo per omnes species virtutum; que secundum etatem seculi habuerunt in excessu notabili aliquos patriarchas eorum principia in quorum labores sponsus ordinavit posteros intrare (ut dicitur Johannis IV°). Nec solum isti **sed** omnes eorum imitacionis filii sunt eadem membra sancte matris ecclesie quod, si alicui desit virtutis officium, deerit sibi indubie quod sit sancte matris ecclesie membrum. Nullus **autem** eorum unquam presumpsit dicere quod sit caput et dominus matris sue, quia indubie, si aliquis homo sit caput tocius generis christiani (quod est sancta mater ecclesia), tunc ipse est Christus, quia solus Christus est huius generacionis principium; alii **autem** in ipso sunt palmites. Necesse ergo est hodie seminare istam fidem ecclesie, cum multi ex ignorancia occasione male accepta, dormiendo in nocte ignorancie uruntur a sancta matre ecclesia que est pulchra ut luna. Uruntur dico secundum inferiorem hominem pravitate heretica, quia omnes qui dicunt: Ecce hic, et ecce illic est Christus caput ecclesie, solum eius vicarium demonstrantes. [p. 25] Oportet **autem** volentes proficere vel iudicare in ista materia primo cognoscere quiditatem sancte matris ecclesie ad modum loquendi scripture sacre et sanctorum qui sensum eius elucidant. Oportet secundo cognoscere materiam predestinacionis divine, quanto sit nobis incognita. Et tunc tercio possunt discernere, si sit sanum vel stolidum quod asseramus absque formidine nos esse membra sancte matris ecclesie, hoc est, de numero predestinatorum. Debemus **autem** hoc sperare et credere **sed** cum formidine de gracia perseverancie: ideo talis fides habet formidinem annexam quam non habet fides qua credimus nos esse in gracia secundum presentem iusticiam, cum casus futuri sit nobis incercior quam actus caritatis vel habitus pro presenti. Unde gentilitati inserte loco Judeorum in ecclesia dicit apostolus Rom. XI°, 20. Noli altum sapere, **sed** time. Timor ergo de casu a finali perseverancia stat cum spe, et cum caritas secundum presentem iusticiam foras mittit timorem de peccato habito pro presenti, videtur tamen quod omnis homo debet habere pro suo tempore specialem revelacionem, quia aliter non foret in firma spe excuciente omnem timorem.

ENGLISH: To this the answer is that the same body of the [church]{g:ecclesia} has many heads, so that any
particular church has at least three heads, namely the [divinity]{g:divinitas}, the humanity of Christ,
and the captain of that church.[^1] But the universal church, which is the whole [mystical body of
Christ]{g:corpus Christi misticum}, has two heads, namely the extrinsic head, which is the divinity,
and the intrinsic head, which is the humanity of Christ; and in an ineffable way the divinity and the
humanity of Christ are two heads of the whole church and, with this, also one head: just as the Son and
the Holy Spirit are one Paraclete, understanding [it] as principle or essentially, and yet are two
Paracletes, understanding [it] personally (since in John 14:16 it is said: "I will ask the Father: and
he shall give you another Paraclete"), so these two natures are two heads of the church, and yet are
personally one head.[^2] And particular churches, such as was the "little flock" over which St Peter
presided, were nothing but young girls of the whole mother church, as Gregory expounds in *Moralia*
XIX, chapter 8, declaring the age of the church, how in the beginning she was a "little sister",
lacking the breasts of preaching.[^3] Hence he infers thus: "For all the churches which make up the one
catholic [church] are called young maidens."[^4] Hence he calls the blessed Peter and the other
particular churches members of that church which he names the lioness, Christ being the lion (as [23]
he declares in *Moralia* XXVIII, chapter 11), saying that, as the members of the body are not the body
itself, so particular churches are not that holy church but (as the apostle teaches, 1 Corinthians
12:12) its members.[^5]
These things laid down, I say that neither the lord of Armagh nor any catholic doctor ought to assert
that any Christian is head of the universal [church], but [only] of a particular church; since there is
no colour [in]: "This head is of that church, therefore this is the head of that church"; nor is there
colour [in]: "If this is head of a part of that church, therefore this is head of that church."[^6] For
so every man would be head of God, as every lower [prelate]{g:prelatus} would be head of his mother
church.[^7] But the apostle contradicts these [quibbles]{g:argucie}, 1 Corinthians 11:3, saying: "But I
would have you know that the head of every man is Christ: and the head of the woman is the man: and the
head of Christ is God." In which saying the order of heads is plain, and the exposure of those
[fallacies]{g:fallacia}. For otherwise he would equally say, conversely, that the head of Christ and of
God would be both man and woman.[^8] The catholic church, then, according to the blessed Gregory on that
[word] of Matthew 23:34, "Behold I send to you wise men and scribes", is the vineyard of the Lord,
planted from the beginning of the world and bearing fruit to the end: as many saints as it begets, so
many branches it puts forth.[^9] And as no one is by nature head of his mother but a part, except
Christ, so none of the other saints is head of mother church but a member. For so the blessed Gregory
says (in the place cited) that blessed Job, blessed Peter and the other heralds of mother church are
her mouth; and, briefly, each [24] of the other saints, proportionally to the analogy of his virtue and
of the property of a bodily member, is a part of holy mother church.[^10]
Hence the blessed Gregory, in *Moralia* IV, chapter 32, relates how from the beginning of the world God
ordained for his [bride]{g:sponsa} patriarchs according to the beginnings of the virtues, as root
members to be grafted into the church: as is clear of the innocence of Abel, the cleanness of Enoch, the
conjugal chastity of Isaac, the endurance of Jacob, the hope of Noah, the obedience of Abraham, the
patience of Job, the chastity of Joseph, the meekness of Moses, the confidence of Joshua, the humility
of David, the zeal of Elijah, and so running through all the species of virtues;[^11] which [virtues],
according to the age of the world, had in notable excess certain patriarchs as their beginnings, into
whose labours the bridegroom ordained that those who came after should enter (as is said in John
4).[^12] Nor only these, but all the sons of their imitation are the same members of holy mother church;
so that, if the office of virtue is lacking to anyone, it will without doubt be lacking to him that he
is a member of holy mother church.[^13] But none of them ever presumed to say that he was head and
[lord]{g:dominus} of his mother; for without doubt, if any man is head of the whole Christian race
(which is holy mother church), then he is Christ, since Christ alone is the beginning of this
generation; and the others are branches in him.[^14] It is therefore necessary today to sow this faith
of the church, since many, out of ignorance, on an occasion wrongly taken, sleeping in the night of
ignorance, are burned by holy mother church, which is "fair as the moon". They are burned, I say,
according to the lower man, by [heretical depravity]{g:heretica pravitas}: for all who say "See here,
and see there is Christ, the head of the church", pointing only to his vicar.[^15]
[25] And those who wish to make progress or to judge in this matter must first know the
[quiddity]{g:quiditas} of holy mother church, after the manner of speaking of holy scripture and of the
saints who elucidate its sense. They must, second, know the matter of divine predestination, how far it
is unknown to us. And then, third, they can discern whether it is sound or stupid that we should assert
without [misgiving]{g:formido} that we are members of holy mother church, that is, of the number of the
[predestinate]{g:predestinatus}.[^16] And we ought to hope and believe this, but with misgiving about the
grace of perseverance: therefore such faith has misgiving annexed, which the faith by which we believe
ourselves to be in grace according to present justice does not have, since a future fall is more
uncertain to us than the act or habit of [charity]{g:caritas} for the present.[^17] Hence the apostle
says to the gentiles, grafted into the church in place of the Jews, Romans 11:20: "Be not highminded,
but fear." Fear, therefore, of a fall from final perseverance stands with hope; and although charity
according to present justice "casteth out fear" of sin had for the present, yet it seems that every
man must have, for his time, a special revelation, since otherwise he would not be in a firm hope that
shakes off all fear.[^18]

### 02-a ¶3 · 26.20–28.7  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 26] **Sed** hic posset responderi tripliciter: primo quod Christus et tota successio suorum vicariorum sunt unum caput aggregative, **sed** nullus eorum per se. Vocatur enim caput universalis ecclesie persona capitalis vel principalis ecclesie quod notum est vicario Christi non posse competere, quia tunc foret super omnes angelos vel creaturas beatas in celo. ENGLISH: But here one could reply in three ways. First, that Christ and the whole succession of his vicars are
one head aggregatively, but none of them by himself. Yet I do not believe that any catholic of sound
head holds this, since then it would be denied that Christ is head of the universal church: [the head
would be] one aggregate of which Christ would be a part, and so the whole body of Christ would not have
Christ as head; which I reckon no less heretical than blasphemous.[^4] Similarly, since Christ is head
of the whole multitude of his vicars, and they head of the whole remainder, it seems that he is the
arch-head of the whole church, and none of them. For that is called the head of mother church which does
not share with the members of the body in another singular nature, since otherwise [27] the church would
have too great and monstrous a head;[^5] which would be repugnant to the perfection of the maker; and if
its head were such an aggregate, moreover, no pope would be head of that church.[^6]
If it is said, second, that every vicar of Christ is by participation Christ himself, [it is
objected]{g:instatur} in three ways. First by this, that such participation would not take away the
distinction of persons and of heads, and so mother church would have a thousand heads. Second by this,
that it would be rash presumption for a vicar of Christ to whom no revelation is made to assert that he
is Christ, since then that [word] of Luke 17:23, said of Antichrist, would be verified: "See here, and
see there." But Christ commands: "Go ye not after, nor follow them."[^7] Third by this, that on this
[evidence]{g:evidencia} every Christian who is a member of Christ would be head of the universal church.
And so the church would be too monstrous, for it would have every member as head, and consequently no
other member. For that is called the head of the universal church [which is] the capital or principal
person of the church; which, it is known, cannot belong to the vicar of Christ, since then he would be
above all the angels or blessed creatures in heaven.[^8]
Therefore, third, the answer is that we speak of the whole [church militant]{g:ecclesia militans}, of
which each pope is head. Against this sense it is objected in three ways. First, it seems that there
are as many churches as there are persons of the church, and by combination far more, and consequently
there would not be only one church in the sense of the decretal expounded in the second conclusion. For
this church would be these faithful persons, who are wholly other than the persons [on the
road]{g:viantes} in Peter's time: therefore also a wholly other church. And by the same token, since
this church is individuated by these persons, [28] it seems that, however corrupted or weighed down it
may be, it will be of the church; and consequently any [superior]{g:prepositi} of the church would be
widowed and wedded too many times, beyond [bigamy]{g:bigamia}, trigamy and any degree known to us; and
since the church is not a church by accident, it seems that, dead to this world, it remains a church,
but triumphant.[^9]

Et satis est sibi pro fide articuli de ecclesia catholica, ut credat fide formata quod sit una universitas fidelium predestinatorum, salvanda virtute meriti Christi, licet non expressius et particularius descenderit ad aliquem eius vicarium. Nec oportet credere vel implicite quod iste quicunque Romanus pontifex sit caput cuiusquam particularis ecclesie, nisi Deus ipsum predestinaverit, quia aliter oporteret fidem catholicam esse falsam et christianum fateri mendacium. Et ita, sicut non est de substancia fidei credere explicite quod iste Bonifacius, Clemens vel Urbanus sit predestinatus vel membrum sancte matris ecclesie, sic nec est de necessitate salutis omnium christianorum secum conversancium, ut explicite credant ipsum esse caput cuiusquam ecclesie, nisi facta et vita evangelica evidencius moveant ad credendum. **Sed** benedictus dominus matris nostre qui nostre peregrinanti iuvencule diebus istis providit caput catholicum, virum evangelicum Urbanum sextum qui rectificando instantem ecclesiam, ut vivat conformiter legi Christi, [p. 38] ordinatur ordinate a se ipso et suis domesticis. Ideo oportet ex operibus credere quod ipse sit caput nostre ecclesie, cum mediate vel immediate influit motum tendendi sursum ad corpus Christi, ubi congregabuntur et aquile; et in facto docet mediate vel immediate mundana spernere et sapide sentire celestia. Ista **autem** fides de nostro capite tam graciose et legitime nobis dato est credenda cum quadam formidine de corona sue finalis perseverancie. Ideo necesse est nobis fructuosa oracione pulsare Deum continue, ut ipse semper vacet nobis et eligat caput tale. Nec dubito quin ipse detestaretur hunc sensum decretalis predicte quod sit de absoluta necessitate salutis cuiuslibet christiani subesse sibi in mundanis honoribus vel servicio seculari, cum non querit nostra **sed** nos, volens instar apostoli aliquem fructum spiritualem in nobis sicut in ceteris gentibus impertiri. Nec dubium quin nos omnes tenemur subesse sibi de quanto tamquam verus Christi vicarius mandat magistri sui consilia et non ultra. Et sic licet omnes christiani tenentur esse subiecti invicem in timore, sibi tamen (in quantum talis) est necessarium ex supposicione subici, cum regit tam evangelice tantum gregem. Et ex istis potest colligi sensus decretalis catholicus.

ENGLISH: Third, it is confirmed from Christ's prophecy, Matt. 24:5, which must of necessity be fulfilled: "For
many", he says, "will come in my name saying, I am Christ. And they will seduce many." Since, then,
whoever has so held of himself [that he is] above the holy apostles says implicitly that he is Christ,
it seems that of any such man (as Augustine expounds) Christ's necessary prophecy is verified; and
consequently it is to be supposed that every [37] dogma of his is (as Christ says) a false dogma,
seductive of the [church]{g:ecclesia}.[^1] Hence, according to the sequence of Christ's prophecy, this
blasphemy, once presupposed, will be a seed-bed of battles, etc.[^2] From these things it seems to me
too rash to assert that any [pope]{g:papa} meant the said sense in his decretal; but whether Christ
immediately, or any disciple of his anywhere on earth sent by the Holy Spirit, has converted any person
or people in the power of Christ, it suffices him, with final perseverance, for salvation. And it is
enough for him, for faith in the article about the catholic church, that he believe with [formed
faith]{g:fides formata} that it is one whole body of the [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} faithful, to
be saved by virtue of the merit of Christ, although he has not descended more expressly and
particularly to any vicar of his.[^3] Nor is it necessary to believe, even implicitly, that this Roman
pontiff, whoever he is, is head of any particular church, unless God has predestined him, since
otherwise the catholic faith would have to be false, and a Christian to confess a lie. And so, just as
it is not of the substance of faith to believe explicitly that this Boniface, Clement or Urban is
predestinate or a member of holy mother church, so neither is it of necessity for the salvation of all
Christians living with him that they explicitly believe him to be head of any church, unless deeds and
evangelical life move them more evidently to believe.[^4]
But blessed be the lord of [our mother]{g:mater nostra}, who in these days has provided for our young
girl, [a pilgrim]{g:peregrinari}, a catholic head, an evangelical man, Urban VI,[^5] who, in setting
right the present church, so that it may live in conformity with the law of Christ, [38] is set in due
order, beginning with himself and his household.[^6] Therefore one must believe from his works that he is head of
our church, since mediately or immediately he infuses the motion of tending upward to the body of
Christ, where the eagles also will be gathered; and in deed he teaches, mediately or immediately, to
spurn worldly things and to savour heavenly things with relish.[^7] And this faith about our head, so
graciously and lawfully given to us, is to be believed with a certain [misgiving]{g:formido} about the
crown of his final perseverance. Therefore it is necessary for us with fruitful prayer to knock at God
continually, that he may always attend to us and choose such a head.[^8] Nor do I doubt that he [Urban]
would detest this sense of the aforesaid decretal, that it is of absolute necessity for the salvation
of every Christian to be subject to him in worldly honours or secular [service]{g:servicium}, since he
seeks not ours but us, wishing after the pattern of the apostle to impart some spiritual fruit in us,
as in the other gentiles.[^9] Nor is there doubt that we are all bound to be subject to him in so far
as, as a true vicar of Christ, he commands the counsels of his master, and no further. And so, although
all Christians are bound to be subject one to another in fear, yet to him (in so far as he is such) it
is necessary on supposition to be subject, since he rules so great a flock so evangelically. And from
these things the catholic sense of the decretal can be gathered.[^10]

### 02-e ¶1 · 38.25–41.6  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 38] **Sed** contra ista instatur tripliciter: Videtur enim primo quod subditi debent consulere scripturam sacram in quocunque quod papa mandaverit. Quantum ad istud, concedi debet conclusio, cum nemo debet parere sibi plus quam alii, nisi in quantum est superior Christi vicarius, et per consequens, nisi in quantum obeditur principalius Jesu Christo; **sed** in omni obediencia [p. 39] Jesu Christo debet christianus consulere legem suam; ideo in omni obediencia christiani in quantum huiusmodi debet obedienciarius consulere legem Dei; et ideo ordinarunt iura ecclesie quod lex scripture tam generaliter et tam precipue a sacerdotibus et specialiter maioribus debet sciri, ne turbentur aut musitent quid et qualiter ad mandatum superioris debeant respondere. In ista scola instructa est legio Thebayca et omnes martyres, immo in illa scola instructi sunt omnes qui monitis non sapientibus caritatem Dei et proximi contradicunt iuxta illud Christi Matth. X°, 37: Qui amat patrem vel matrem plus quam me, non est me dignus; et istam leccionem docent apostoli Act.V°, 29, dicentes quod obedire magis oportet Deo quam hominibus. Ideo dicit Christus Matth. X°, 35: Non veni pacem mittere in terram **sed** gladium. Veni ergo separare hominem adversus patrem suum, et filiam adversus matrem suam, et nurum adversus socrum suam, et inimici hominis domestici eius. Amiciciam enim carnalem propter Deum venit Christus dissolvere, quia ille qui videtur esse familiaris homini quoad mundum est hostis suus ut plurimum quoad Deum (ut dicitur Michee VII°). Beatus Jeronymus super illo capitulo videtur exponere textum evangelii ad hunc sensum quod Christus venit ad separandum hereticos ab ecclesia quam illudunt. [p. 40] Simulant enim ex palliata ypocrisi quod sint filii Dei per graciam adopcionis et filie ecclesie matris sue pro cuius iuribus tantum zelant. Dicamus inquit scriptum de hereticis; non enim nostram salutem **sed** sua querunt lucra, et deceptum gregem pedibus suis conterunt. Pater heretici est Deus qui creavit eum, et mater ecclesia; socrus ad literam est mater alterius coniugum. Ast uxor nati dicitur esse nurus, cum ergo Christus secundum humanitatem sit sponsus anime, imo tocius ecclesie; et cum hoc conceptus de spiritu sancto tamquam matre sua iuxta illud quod allegat Jeronymus.... Tulit me mater mea in uno capillorum meorum (caro enim est capillus anime), patet quod spiritus sanctus, cum sit mater Christi sponsi ecclesie, est socrus anime que vendicat esse sponsa Christi, quia mater mariti. Et ex eadem consideracione anima pretendens se esse uxorem filii spiritus sancti dicitur nurus eius. Heretici ergo qui pretendunt se esse nuros spiritus sancti quem vendicant socrum suam, separantur ab eo per Christum detecta eorum fallacia, et ita cum universalis ecclesia sit domus domini Jesu Christi, et per consequens, omnes heretici qui sunt facietenus in ecclesia sunt pretense eius domestici, patet quod omnes illi sunt illi homini patrifamilias inimici. Huius inquit Jeronymus inimici sunt qui putantur in domo Christi esse, hoc [p. 41] est, in ecclesia et palliantes a capite non recedere contra caput sentiunt. Heretici inquit cum ante crediderint scripturis que a spiritu sancto scripte sunt et edite, transferunt se ad novas doctrinas et fermentum phariseorum et mandata hominum, dumque sermonem Dei contempnunt, socrui sue faciunt iniuriam.

ENGLISH: But against these things [it is objected]{g:instatur} in three ways. For it seems, first, that subjects
ought to consult holy scripture in whatever the [pope]{g:papa} has commanded.[^1] As to this, the
conclusion must be granted, since no one ought to obey him more than another, except in so far as he is
a superior vicar of Christ, and consequently except in so far as Jesus Christ is obeyed more principally
[through him]; now in every obedience [39] to Jesus Christ a Christian ought to consult his law;
therefore in every obedience of a Christian, as such, the one who obeys ought to consult the law of God.
And therefore the [laws]{g:ius} of the church have ordained that the law of scripture must be known so
generally and so especially by [priests]{g:sacerdos}, and specially by the greater ones, lest they be
troubled or hesitate over what and how they ought to answer to a superior's command.[^2] In this school
the Theban legion and all the martyrs were taught;[^3] indeed, in that school all are taught who oppose
[admonitions]{g:monita} that do not savour of the [charity]{g:caritas} of God and of neighbour, according
to that word of Christ, Matt. 10:37: "He that loveth father or mother more than me, is not worthy of
me"; and this lesson the apostles teach, Acts 5:29, saying that "we ought to obey God rather than men".
Therefore Christ says, Matt. 10:35: "I came not to send peace upon earth, but the sword. I came
therefore to set a man at variance against his father, and the daughter against her mother, and the
daughter in law against her mother in law; and a man's enemies, they of his own household."[^4] For
Christ came to dissolve carnal friendship for God's sake, since he who seems to be a man's intimate as
regards the world is [for the most part]{g:ut plurimum} his enemy as regards God (as is said in Micah
7).[^5] Blessed Jerome, on that chapter, seems to expound the text of the gospel in this sense: that
Christ came to separate the heretics from the [church]{g:ecclesia}, which they mock. [40] For out of
cloaked hypocrisy they feign that they are sons of God by the grace of adoption, and daughters of the
church their mother, for whose rights they are so zealous. "Let us say", he says, "what is written of
the heretics: for they seek not our salvation but their own gains, and trample the deceived flock under
their feet."[^6] The father of the heretic is God, who created him, and his mother is the church. A
mother-in-law, in the literal sense, is the mother of one of the spouses; "but the wife of a son is
called a daughter-in-law".[^7] Since, then, Christ according to his humanity is the
[bridegroom]{g:sponsus} of the soul, indeed of the whole church; and since, with this, he was conceived
of the Holy Spirit as of his mother, according to the saying that Jerome cites ... "My mother took me
by one of my hairs" (for the flesh is the hair of the soul),[^8] it is clear that the Holy Spirit, since
he is the mother of Christ the bridegroom of the church, is the mother-in-law of the soul that claims to
be the [bride]{g:sponsa} of Christ, because [he is] the husband's mother. And from the same
consideration the soul that pretends to be the wife of the son of the Holy Spirit is called his
daughter-in-law. Therefore the heretics, who pretend to be daughters-in-law of the Holy Spirit, whom
they claim as their mother-in-law, are separated from him by Christ, their [fallacy]{g:fallacia} being
exposed. And so, since the universal church is the house of the lord Jesus Christ, and consequently all
heretics who are outwardly in the church are pretended members of his household, it is clear that all
of them are enemies to that man, the householder.[^9] "Of him", says Jerome, "they are enemies who are
thought to be in the house of Christ, that [41] is, in the church, and, cloaking [it so as to seem] not
to withdraw from the head, think against the head." "Heretics", he says, "although they first believed
the scriptures, which were written and published by the Holy Spirit, go over to new
[doctrines]{g:doctrina}, and to the leaven of the Pharisees, and to the commandments of men; and while
they despise the word of God, they do injury to their mother-in-law."[^10]

### 02-e ¶2 · 41.7–41.31  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 41] Christus ergo ex vita et doctrina venit confutare hereticos, ut discernantur a fidelibus et postmodum separentur. Et ita subtiliter et catholice potest intelligi secundum hunc sanctum per filium, filiam et nurum hereticus qui pretense et false se nominat filium Dei patris, filiam matris sue, sapiencie Dei patris et nurum spiritus sancti et sic cognatissimum trinitati. **Sed** vita et doctrina Christi sunt optimum speculum, ubi possunt hec discerni, cum manifestum sit quod omnis homo et solum talis qui est in vita et doctrina Christo contrarius est hereticus, et omnis christianus et solum talis qui est in vita et doctrina Christo conformis est ut sic ab heresi elongatus. Christus ergo venit non ad faciendum primam separacionem que est proprie per peccatum, **sed** ad faciendum sequentem, ut per eum discernantur heretici et ab ecclesia separentur. Et hec racio quare oportet omnem catholicum cognoscere scripturam sacram. Et certus sum quod, licet prelati nostri ipsam et professores suos dissipant et conculcant, lapides clamabunt, hoc est, seculares domini in sensu scripture a domino illustrati defendent fidem catholicam, quia pharisei volentes obturare ora discipulorum scripturam istam confitencium audierunt divinum responsum: Dico vobis, quia si hii tacuerint lapides clamabunt, Luce XIX°, 40. Magnam enim vim habet veritas scripturarum.

ENGLISH: Christ, therefore, came by his life and teaching to confute the heretics, so that they may be discerned
from the faithful and afterwards separated. And so, subtly and in a catholic sense, according to this
saint, by "son", "daughter" and "daughter-in-law" can be understood the heretic, who pretendedly and
falsely names himself son of God the Father, daughter of his mother, the wisdom of God the Father, and
daughter-in-law of the Holy Spirit, and so most nearly kin to the Trinity.[^11] But the life and
teaching of Christ are the best mirror, in which these things can be discerned, since it is manifest
that every man, and only such a one, who in life and teaching is contrary to Christ is a heretic, and
every Christian, and only such a one, who in life and teaching is conformed to Christ is, as such,
removed far from heresy.[^12] Christ, therefore, came not to make the first separation, which is
properly through sin, but to make the one that follows, so that through him the heretics may be
discerned and separated from the church. And this is the reason why every catholic must know holy
scripture. And I am certain that, although our [prelates]{g:prelatus} scatter and trample it and those
who profess it, "the stones will cry out", that is, secular lords enlightened by the Lord in the sense
of scripture will defend the catholic faith;[^13] for the Pharisees, wishing to stop the mouths of the
disciples who were confessing this scripture, heard the divine answer: "I say to you that if these
shall hold their peace, the stones will cry out", Luke 19:40. For the truth of the scriptures has great
force.

### 03-a ¶3 · 48.23–50.7  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 48] Isto supposito patet, primo quod sancti dum se intra semet ipsos sic colligunt, occulta tam de statu proprio quam de statu proximi comprehendunt. Patet ex hoc quod lux vera que non potest personas accipere, necessario secundum capacitatem irradiat talem mentem. Et hoc figuratum est Ezechiel VIII, 3, ubi sic legitur: [p. 49] Emissa similitudo manus apprehendit me in cincinno capitis mei et elevavit me spiritus inter terram et celum et adduxit me in Jerusalem in visione Dei iuxta ostium interius quod respiciebat ad aquilonem, ubi erat statutum ydolum zeli ad provocandum emulacionem. Manus missa est operativa volucio divina, cincinnus capitis est collecte cogitaciones mentis quibus habitis mens incomplete trahitur a terrenis, cum nec habet animum omnino celestem nec omnino in terrestribus soporatum, **sed** inter terram et celum in visione Dei in Jerusalem ducitur, quia zelo caritatis qualis debet esse mater ecclesia contemplatur. Et signanter animus talis adducitur ad ostium interius quod respicit ad aquilonem, quia interna contemplacione intra ecclesiam plus prava quam recta fieri deprehendunt, dum clerici in aquilonis pariete, id est, ad solis sinistrum oculos flectunt et elongata austerna caritate habundant frigore viciorum; cuius causa est globus temporalium quem ecclesia sola specie fidelis adorat; ex hinc enim zelus sponsi ecclesie ad feriendum ypocritas provocatur. Secundo patet ex quo signo perpenditur, si ecclesiastici flagrant terrenis, celestibus pretermissis: cum enim amor sit pondus animi, nec sit possibile hominem moveri libere quo affeccio mentis ipsum non tulerit, patet quod ex operibus debet mentis affeccio iudicari. Si ergo vides quod ecclesiasticus quantum ad perpetuacionem, quantum ad defensionem et quantum ad amissionem temporalium plus insudat, quam facit pro spiritualibus, quis dubitat quin eis magis afficitur? Unde quantum ad perpetuacionem temporalium sic [p. 50] loquitur iste sanctus: postposita quippe cura filiorum sepe eos videas se contra pericula immensi laboris accingere, maria transmeare, adire iudicia, pulsare principatus, palacia irrumpere, iurgantibus populorum cuneis interesse. Cum **autem** pro spiritualibus acquirendis non apponunt tantam diligenciam, quis dubitat quin ista temporalia magis amant?

ENGLISH: This supposed, it is clear, first, that the saints, while they so gather themselves within themselves,
grasp hidden things, as much of their own state as of their neighbour's. It is clear from this: that the
true light, which cannot respect persons, of necessity shines upon such a mind according to its
capacity.[^6] And this is figured in Ezekiel 8:3, where it reads thus: [49] "The likeness of a hand was
put forth and took me by a lock of my head: and the spirit lifted me up between the earth and the
heaven, and brought me in the vision of God into Jerusalem, near the inner gate, that looked toward the
north, where was set the idol of jealousy to provoke to jealousy." The hand put forth is the divine
operative will; the lock of the head is the gathered thoughts of the mind, which once had, the mind is
drawn incompletely from earthly things, since it has a soul neither wholly heavenly nor wholly asleep
in earthly things, but is led between earth and heaven in the vision of God into Jerusalem, because with
the zeal of [charity]{g:caritas} it contemplates what mother church ought to be.[^7] And significantly
such a soul is brought to the inner gate that looks toward the north, because by inward contemplation
they discover that within the church more crooked things than right are done, while clerics on the
north wall, that is, to the left of the sun, turn their eyes, and, [southern] charity removed far off,
abound in the cold of vices; the cause of which is the mass of temporal goods, which the church,
faithful in appearance only, worships; for hence the zeal of the church's [bridegroom]{g:sponsus} is
provoked to strike the hypocrites.[^8] Second, it is clear by what sign it is weighed whether churchmen
burn for earthly things, heavenly things being passed over: for since love is the weight of the soul,
and it is not possible for a man to be moved freely where the affection of his mind has not carried
him, it is clear that the affection of the mind must be judged from works.[^9] If, therefore, you see
that a churchman sweats more over the perpetuating, over the defence and over the loss of temporal goods
than he does for spiritual ones, who doubts that he is more attached to them? Hence, as to the
perpetuating of temporal goods, thus [50] speaks this saint: "For, the care of their sons put aside,
you may often see them gird themselves against the dangers of immense labour, cross the seas, go to the
courts, knock at the doors of princes, break into palaces, take part in the wrangling throngs of the
peoples." Since, though, they do not lay out so much diligence for acquiring spiritual things, who
doubts that they love these temporal ones more?[^10]

### 03-b ¶1 · 51.8–52.9  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 51] Tercio colligitur quod adulterantes scripturam sacram in ista sentencia sunt ypocrite, quos prophetavit apostolus Timotheo periculosis temporibus in ecclesiam successuros. Pro cuius declaracione suppono factum, quomodo moderni ecclesiastici fingunt secundum tradiciones humanas quod nedum licet eis, **sed** peccarent mortaliter, omittendo prosequi pro defensione matris ecclesie. Illud meminit beatus Gregorius (ubi supra). Istis, inquit, si forte dicatur: Cur ista agitis qui seculum reliquistis? respondent illico Deum se metuere et idcirco tanto studio in defendendis patrimoniis Christi insudare. Hodie **autem** superadditur quod bona pro quorum acquisicione, conservacione et repeticione tantum insistimus sunt bona ecclesie et patrimonium Christi: ideo licet pro bono universalis ecclesie conservacioni eorum insistere. Iste **autem** fucus ex hoc tollitur quod deserendo ipsa pro Christo tam relinquens quam ecclesia ipsa habet, ut dicit Augustinus super Joh. Omelia VIª. Ideo, ut dicit beatus Gregorius: Frustra laboravit nullo timore cogente, et ut dicitur Psalmo XIII°, 5: Illic trepidaverunt timore, ubi non [p. 52] erat timor. Ecce, inquit, divina voce precipitur: Si quis tulerit tibi tunicam et voluerit tecum in iudicio contendere, dimitte illi pallium. Et rursum: Si quis quod tuum est tulerit, ne repetas. Paulus quoque apostolus discipulos suos cupiens exteriora despicere, ut valeant interna servare, admonet eos dicens: Omnino delictum est in vobis quod iudicia habetis inter vos, quare non magis iniuriam accipitis quare non magis fraudem patimini?

ENGLISH: Third, it is gathered that those who adulterate holy scripture in [the matter of] this
[teaching]{g:sentencia} are hypocrites, whom the apostle prophesied to Timothy would come into the
[church]{g:ecclesia} in dangerous times.[^1] For the explanation of this I suppose as a fact how modern
churchmen pretend, according to human traditions, that not only is it lawful for them [to sue], but
they would sin mortally by omitting to sue for the defence of mother church. Blessed Gregory recalls
this (in the place cited): "If perhaps it is said to these men: Why do you do these things, who have
left the world? they answer at once that they fear God, and therefore sweat with such zeal in defending
the patrimonies of Christ."[^2] And today it is added besides that the goods for whose acquisition,
preservation and recovery we press so hard are the goods of the church and the patrimony of Christ;
therefore it is lawful, for the good of the universal church, to press for their preservation. But
this disguise is taken away by this: that by deserting them for Christ both the one who leaves them
and the church itself has them, as Augustine says on John, homily 6.[^3] Therefore, as blessed Gregory
says: "She hath laboured in vain, no fear constraining her"; and, as is said in Psalm 13:5: "There
have they trembled for fear, where there [52] was no fear." "Behold," he says, "it is commanded by the
divine voice: If any man take away thy coat and will contend with thee in judgment, let go thy cloak
unto him. And again: If any man take away what is thine, ask it not again. Paul the apostle also,
desiring his disciples to despise outward things so that they may be able to keep inward ones,
admonishes them, saying: There is plainly a fault among you, that you have law suits one with another.
Why do you not rather take wrong? Why do you not rather suffer yourselves to be defrauded?"[^4]

### 03-b ¶3 · 52.16–54.22  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 52] Nec valet secundo dicere quod oportet aliquem solicitari circa hec bona, ne ecclesia defraudetur, quia illis relictis pro Christo tu verius haberes hec bona, et sic ecclesia. Ideo vis exponere te periculo dimittendo perfeccionem divini consilii quam acceptando, Deus non posset de promissione temporalium deficere; nam cum istud officium incumbit laicis, patet quod indigencia ecclesie stat in paucitate procurancium pro spiritualibus bonis ecclesie, sicut fuit de contemplativis, quando crevit ecclesia. Cum ergo istud officium sit alcius, salubrius et ecclesie necessarius, manifestum est quod amor culpabilis temporalium est in causa huius cece eleccionis. Unde Gregorius ibidem capitulo X°: Plus Deo sese amare convincitur qui neglectis hiis que Dei sunt propria tuetur; perdere namque animas hominum ypocrite non timent, et [p. 53] amittere res cum mundo transeuntes quasi districto iudici posituri racionem timent; ac si placatum inveniant, cui desideratis rebus, id est, racionabilibus perditis insensibilia et non quesita conservent. Certum est quod non. Possidere, inquit, in hoc mundo aliquid volumus et ecce veritas clamat: Nisi quis renunciaverit omnibus que possidet, non potest meus esse discipulus. Perfectus ergo christianus quomodo debet res terrenas iurgando defendere quas precipitur non possidere? Itaque cum res proprias amittimus, si perfecte Deum sequimur, in huius vite itinere a magno onere levigamur. Non ergo debemus, periculo nos exponendo, perfecciorem statum relinquere et statum plus periculosum, plus a Christo elongatum et plus tardativum eligere. Nam ut dicit beatus Gregorius ibidem: Plus ipsis raptoribus debemus metuere quam inhiare ipsis rebus irracionabilibus [p. 54] defendendis, quia illa non rapta morientes amittimus, cum illis vero et nunc condicionis ordine, etsi corrigi studeant, post percepcionem finalis premii unum sumus. Quis **autem** nesciat, quia minus ea quibus utimur et plus debemus amare quod sumus? Si ergo pro sua utilitate raptoribus loquimur, iam non nobis que temporalia, **sed** eciam illis que sunt eterna vindicamus. Qua in re illud est solercius intuendum, ne per necessitatis metum cupiditas surrepat rerum et zelo succensa prohibicio, impetu immoderancie distensa, usque ad odiose turpitudinem contencionis erumpat. Dumque pro terrena re pax a corde cum proximo scinditur, liquido apparet quod plus res quam proximus amatur. Si enim caritatis viscera eciam circa raptorem proximum non habemus, nosmet ipsos peius ipso raptore persequimur graviusque nos quam alter poterat devastamus, quia dileccionis bonum sponte deserentes a nobis ipsis intus est quod amittimus, qui ab illo sola exteriora perdebamus. **Sed** hanc ypocrita formam caritatis ignorat, quia plus terrena quam celestia diligens contra eum qui temporalia diripit sese in intimis immani odio inflammat.

LATIN: [p. 54] Sciendum vero est quod sunt nonnulli quos caritatis gremio nutriens mater ecclesia tolerat, quousque ad spiritualis etatis incrementa perducat, qui nonnunquam et sanctitatis habitum tenent et perfeccionis meritum exequi non valent. Ad dona namque spiritualia minime assurgunt et idcirco hiis qui sibi coniuncti sunt ad tuenda ea que terrena sunt serviunt et nonnunquam in defensionis eiusdem iracundia excedunt. Et istos nomine religiosos declarat iste sanctus papa involvi duplici malicia, primo quia nedum infirmitate **sed** sciencia contra conscienciam suam peccant, secundo quia fingendo sanctitatem in defendendo [p. 55] bona ecclesie pro facinore querunt laudes. Ex istis conclusionibus huius sancti demonstrabilibus perpendere potest ecclesiasticus tam regularis quam secularis, si diligenter examinent conscienciam cum scriptura, palpando intencionem, volucionem et motus anime cum intencione supremi iudicis, quod non tam assidue contenderent pro vocatis bonis ecclesie. Patet secundo: Quomodo dominus papa non foveret **sed** extingueret lites huiusmodi secundum regulam evangelii: si enim committeretur regnis, provinciis et parrochiis examen vite clericorum, sic quod non ministrarentur eis stipendia subtracto sensibiliter eorum ministerio, viveretur superioribus prepositis tolerabilius et sacerdotibus cum populo melius quam est modo. Defectus **autem** patronorum et populi posset speculator superior facilius corrigere quam totum onus per se cum falsis testibus litem gignentibus supportare. Nec requiritur alia lex in distribuendo officia sacerdotum nisi cognicio a capite per testes probabiles, quod unus sit rector in ministerio sacerdotali, solicicior habiliorem preponere; de quo si non gauderet, depositus foret, ex ypocrisi ac cupiditate suspectus. Et patet tercio quod causa dissensionis et perturbacionis ecclesie sit malicia sacerdotum. Nam eorum virtuositas maxime pacificaret ecclesiam; ideo ex opposito eorum perversitas maxime perturbat ecclesiam, quia peccatum quod est per se causa perturbacionis ecclesie in ipsis est gravius. Dicunt enim contra fidem quod sunt viri sancte matris ecclesie, licet sint apostatantes partes diaboli, et per consequens, faciunt confusionem in fide, confusionem in sacramentis et subtraccionem spiritualis suffragii a Deo per ipsos instrumentaliter derivandi. [p. 56] Unde confidenter prenostico quod ante correccionem nostram secundum statum primevum non deficiet regnorum perturbacio. Cum igitur apostolus raptus fuit usque ad tercium celum, videns longitudinem cursus ecclesie quoad tempus, latitudinem quoad numerum prelatorum et profunditatem quoad sensum quo clerici sunt in sensu domini profundati, verti non debet in dubium quin prophetavit iuxta exposicionem beati Gregorii de ecclesia in instante, vocans eos ypocritas se ipsos amantes, verbum domini adulterantes et cauterizatam conscienciam habentes (ut patet I.Timotei IV°, 2). Que enim posset esse maior adulteracio verbi domini quam intelligere ipsum ad sensum diaboli iuxta quem defendatur esse hereticum et blasfemum, ymo quod licet ex auctoritate eius committere leve mendacium? Aut quid posset esse magis periculosum cauterium quam clericum habere conscienciam adustam cupidine, quod cessans ab operibus reprehensibilibus in facie ecclesie propter candentem amorem quem habet ad temporalia, publicet et defendat sine fructuosis operibus se esse membrum sancte matris ecclesie. Licet enim talis evacuet cauterio suo fluxum viciorum sensibilem, verumptamen diabolus auctor fluxus talis superflui tantum disseminat per meatus ypocritarum absconditos per totum residuum corporis fluxum peccati lubrici, et sic ex simulata consuetudine factus heresiarcha habet [p. 57] in capite, hoc est, in racione cauterium Antichristi. Nulli enim secundum beatum Gregorium, in Registro, secundo capitulo, periculosius vel amplius nocent in ecclesia sancti Dei. **Sed** redeundo ad difficultatem dimissam obicitur per sanctum Thomam de Christo, conclusione octava, articulo III°, ubi dicit quod Christus est caput omnium hominum, tam fidelium qui uniuntur sibi in actu per graciam, quam infidelium qui sunt solum in potencia eius membra, et post subdividit secundum predestinatos et prescitos qui recedentes ab hoc mundo totaliter desinunt esse membra Christi. Et probat hoc multiplici racione. Quantum ad hoc testimonium sancti Thome, videtur michi quod ipse equivocat vere dicendo quod Christus secundum deitatem est caput extrinsecum tocius humani generis; quod potest dici aggregative unum corpus naturale, cui eciam Christus benefacit sicut et toti mundo secundum humanitatem, cum virtute passionis Christi acquiritur quedam perfeccio secundaria toti mundo. Et sic secundum humanitatem benefacit toti humano generi, cum punit omnes dampnatos, vel ex infidelitate, ut eos qui non considerant in dominum Jesum Christum, vel ex desperacione qua dimissa debuerunt celestibus aspirare, vel tercio de iudicio temerario quo dimisso debuerunt domino Jesu Christo caritative finaliter adherere. Et sic intelligitur triplex causalis Christi Johannis XVI°, 8: Arguet mundum de peccato, de iusticia et [p. 58] de iudicio. De peccato infidelitatis que autonomatice dicitur peccatum, quia non crediderunt in patrem neque in me; de iusticia quam debuerunt habuisse, aspirando cum mediis congruis ad patriam naturalem, quia ad patrem vado; et de iudicio quo temere arbitrantur dogma mundi atque diaboli ardencius diligendum, cum princeps mundi iudicatus est per Christum docentem oppositum.
