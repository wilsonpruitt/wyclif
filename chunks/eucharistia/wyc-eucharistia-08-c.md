# *De eucharistia*, chapter 8, part 3: time and knowledge destroyed; the arguments for multiplication

## ¶1 · 240.31–241.22

> [p. 240] Item, suppono quod A punctus multiplicetur per omnem situm mundi annichilato toto residuo, patet quod adhuc superest materia movendi localiter, sicut [p. 241] Petrus constituendo coream per viam multiplicacionis, aliter esset persona multiplicata nimis cathenata, nisi ubicunque fuerit posset movere se localiter et facere quascunque acciones corporeas ad suam potenciam naturalem; et omnem operacionem huiusmodi quam potest corpus naturale multiplicatum facere potest quantitas plene qualificata multiplicata facere, ut patet de hostia consecrata; et per idem Deus potest a maxima multiplicata usque ad minimam facere cum omni multiplicato tantum quantum fit cum aliquo, quia aliter derogaretur divine potencie. Moveat ergo Deus totum equinoccialem circulum causando regulariter motum et tempus ut modo, sic tamen quod in tota equinocciali sit solum A punctus multiplicatus, et videtur primo quod per totum A non est motus, quia quiescit in utroque polo, et alibi non est situs, cum omnis situs multiplicatur ubilibet, ut suppono, et si non sit variacio motus, non est variacio temporis vel instantis, cum tempus vel instans quod est passio motus non variaretur vel individuaretur nisi a motu; et perirent dies, anni et omnia tempora, sicut recitatum est in tractatu De Tempore.

Again, I suppose that A, a point, is multiplied through every site of the world, the whole residue being annihilated: it is clear that there still remains matter for moving [locally]{g:localiter}, as [241] Peter [does] in forming a [ring-dance]{g:corea} by way of multiplication;[^1] otherwise the multiplied [person]{g:persona} would be [exceedingly]{g:nimis} chained, unless wherever it were it could move itself locally and perform any bodily actions whatever up to its natural [power]{g:potencia};[^2] and every operation of this kind that a multiplied natural body can perform, a [quantity]{g:quantitas} fully qualified and multiplied can perform, as is clear of the [consecrated host]{g:hostia}; and by the same token God can, from the greatest multiplied down to the least, do with every multiplied thing as much as is done with any one, for otherwise divine power would be derogated. Let God therefore move the whole equinoctial circle, causing motion and time [as a rule]{g:regulariter} as now, yet so that in the whole equinoctial there is only A, the point, multiplied: and it seems, first, that through the whole of A there is no motion, for it rests at either pole, and elsewhere there is no site, since every site is multiplied everywhere, as I suppose; and if there is no variation of motion, there is no variation of time or instant, since time or instant, which is an [attribute]{g:passio} of motion, would not vary or be individuated except by motion; and days, years and all times would perish, as has been [recited]{g:recitare} in the treatise *On Time*.[^3]

## ¶2 · 241.23–243.7

> [p. 241] Et conformiter fantasiatur ponendo quod Deus cum quolibet instanti ponat infinita instancia et cum quolibet tempore infinita tempora equalia non communicancia; et tunc videtur quod omne durans per tempus infinitum diu durat, quia per infinitum magnum tempus, eo quod per infinitum multa tempora equalia non communicancia, quod equivalet. Ymmo videtur quod Deus ex duobus coextensis potest constituere [p. 242] unum tempus, sicut ex duabus superficiebus, sitibus vel actibus eiusdem speciei parcialiter componibilibus potest constituere tercium compositum, et sic sicut nemo sciret utrum infinitum diu durabit, sic nesciret utrum velociter movebitur vel immediate post hoc erit dies iudicii propter multiplicacionem temporis et propter tempora et loca abscondita; et periret in toto mundo noticia sensibilium et per consequens omnis sciencia, cum omnis homo tamquam vertiginosus nesciret discernere de quocunque sensibili. Et omnium istorum errorum origo est mendacium fictum de sacramento altaris. Non enim finget cultor accidentis quod potencia Dei terminatur ad tantum mirabile quod potest facere et non ultra, vel quod sic est de facto quoad mundum et alia sensibilia et non modo infinitum magis mirabili nobis abscondito, et sic redirent opiniones antique, ut loquitur Aristoteles primo Ethicorum et specialiter quod omnis homo nichil scit sed solummodo opinatur; et cum vacuum non foret nisi qualitas carens materiali substancia, quilibet cultor accidencium ignorat si per totum mundum sit vacuum eciam infinitum, et sic rectificando in infinitum corticem interceptam inter duas lineas girativas in corulo columpnari deveniretur demum ad partem proporcionalem ultimam coruli et ad ultimum pedale linee girative; et oporteret dare ubi est extremum alterum, cum dato situato dandus est situs, sicut et dandus foret, ubi sit sor qui infigatur continue in extremo corticis moto. Sic enim dicitur quod servando ligulam secundum eius extrema in manibus distantibus fixis continue et rarefacta illa in infinitum atque correspondenter girata super corpus columpnare interceptum inter manus quod circumgiretur proporcionaliter [p. 243] ut ligula exigit se circumvolvi; quo facto foret ligula infinita et tamen secundum eius ambo extrema in manibus tuis tenta; mille sunt casus quibus perturbatur ecclesia, qui omnes dependent sive fundantur super fantasia maxime inpossibili atque heretica occasione materie de sacramento eukaristie introducta.

And in conformity it is [fancied]{g:fantasiare}, by positing that God with any instant whatever posits infinitely many instants, and with any time whatever infinitely many equal times [not communicating]{g:non-communicans};[^4] and then it seems that everything that lasts through a time lasts infinitely long, for [it lasts] through an infinitely great time, because through infinitely many equal times not communicating, which is equivalent. Indeed, it seems that God can constitute out of two coextended [times][^5] [242] one time, just as out of two surfaces, sites or acts of the same species, partially composable, he can constitute a third composite; and so, just as nobody would know whether he will last infinitely long, so he would not know whether he will be moved swiftly, or whether [without intermediary]{g:immediate} after this will be the day of judgement, on account of the multiplication of time and on account of hidden times and places; and in the whole world the knowledge of [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} things would perish, and consequently all science, since every man, like one dizzy, would not know how to discern about any perceptible thing whatever. And the origin of all these errors is the lie feigned about the [sacrament of the altar]{g:sacramentum-altaris}. For the [worshipper]{g:cultor} of the [accident]{g:accidens} will not [feign]{g:fingere} that the power of God is terminated at a marvel so great as it can do and not beyond, or that it is so in fact as to the world and other perceptible things and not now in an infinitely more marvellous [way] hidden from us;[^6] and so the ancient [opinions]{g:opinio} would return, as Aristotle speaks in the first [book] of the *Ethics*, and especially that every man knows nothing but only opines (LEDGER K08c-2); and since a vacuum would be nothing but a [quality]{g:qualitas} lacking material [substance]{g:substancia}, every one of the [worshippers of accidents]{g:cultores-accidencium} is ignorant whether through the whole world there is a vacuum, even an infinite one; and so, by straightening to infinity the bark intercepted between two winding lines on a [columnar hazel-rod]{g:corulus-columpnaris}, one would come at last to the last [proportional part]{g:partes-proporcionales} of the hazel-rod and to the last [foot-length]{g:pedalitas} of the winding line;[^7] and one would have to assign where the other end is, since, a situated thing being given, a site must be given, just as it would have to be given where Sor is, who is fixed continuously in the end of the moved bark.[^8] For so it is said: that, keeping a strap by its ends in distant hands held continuously fixed, and it being rarefied to infinity and correspondingly wound upon a columnar body intercepted between the hands, which is turned round proportionally [243] as the strap requires to be wound round, this done, the strap would be infinite and yet held by both its ends in your hands.[^9] A thousand are the cases by which the [church]{g:ecclesia} is disturbed, which all depend or are founded upon an [imagination]{g:fantasia} supremely impossible and heretical, introduced on occasion of the matter of the sacrament of [the Eucharist]{g:eukaristia}.

## ¶3 · 243.8–243.22

> [p. 243] Ideo (ut sepe dixi) doctor katholicus debet servare se in limitibus nature, asserendo quod solum Deus est potencie infinite, sic quod nec mundus nec creatura aliqua poterit esse infinitum magna, nec infinitum magnum poterit esse tale, et sic in assumptis argumentorum negetur iste saltus et sic in infinitum. Admitterem tamen omnes tales casus, docto quod subiacerent divine potencie, et sic negatur esse numerum infinitum, sicut negatur quantitas continua infinita, ut patet in materia de composicione continui ex non quantis et in materia de tempore. Illud enim cuius quantitas est nobis incognita vocamus ad modum loquendi scripture et philosophi equivoce infinitum, et sic negatur talis multiplicacio corporis et alii casus infundabiles in scriptura.

Therefore (as I have often said) the catholic doctor must keep himself within the [limits]{g:limites} of nature, asserting that God alone is of infinite power, so that neither the world nor any [creature]{g:creatura} could be infinitely great, nor could the infinitely great be such;[^10] and so, in the assumptions of the arguments, let this leap be denied, and so on to infinity. Yet I would admit all such cases, it having been shown that they would be subject to divine power; and so it is denied that there is an infinite number, just as an infinite continuous quantity is denied, as is clear in the matter of the composition of the continuum out of non-[quantified]{g:quantus} [parts] and in the matter of time. For that whose quantity is unknown to us we call [equivocally]{g:equivoce} infinite, after the manner of speaking of scripture and of the Philosopher; and so such [multiplication of a body]{g:multiplicacio-corporis} is denied, and the other cases [without foundation]{g:infundabilis} in scripture.

## ¶4 · 243.23–243.31

> [p. 243] Sed pro multiplicacione communiter sic arguitur: Non magis repugnat idem corpus in numero multiplicari simul per diversa loca quam repugnat diversa corpora situari simul per eundem locum eis adequatum, cum tanta sit utrobique distancia atque diversitas sed secundum est satis possibile, ut patet de corporibus glorificatis secundum dotem subtilitatis coextensis et de elementis immixtis: ergo et primum est per idem possibile.

But for multiplication it is commonly argued thus: "It is no more [incompatible]{g:repugnare} for the same body in number to be multiplied at once through different places than it is incompatible for different bodies to be situated at once through the same place adequate to them, since there is as great a distance and diversity [on both sides]{g:utrobique}; now the second is quite possible, as is clear of glorified bodies coextended by the [dower of subtlety]{g:dos-subtilitatis},[^11] and of elements mingled together: therefore the first too is by the same token possible."[^12]

## ¶5 · 243.32–244.6

> [p. 243] Confirmatur secundo ex miraculis quibus narratur experiri idem corpus sic simul dimensionaliter multiplicari, [p. 244] ut narratur de beato Ambrosio ipsum fuisse pro eodem tempore in locis distantibus et officium ecclesiasticum celebrasse; sicut enim idem corpus potest multiplicari per diversa tempora, sic et per diversa loca. Et tercio confirmatur ex evidenciis tactis in tercio argumento ex fide scripture.

This is confirmed, secondly, from miracles by which the same body is [related]{g:narrare} to be experienced as so multiplied at once [dimensionally]{g:dimensionaliter}, [244] as is related of blessed Ambrose, that he was at the same time in distant places and celebrated the church's office (LEDGER K08c-3);[^13] for just as the same body can be multiplied through different times, so also through different places. And thirdly it is confirmed from the [evidences]{g:evidencia} touched on in the third argument, [from the faith of scripture]{g:ex-fide}.[^14]

## ¶6 · 244.7–244.16

> [p. 244] Quantum ad istud, dicitur quod maior sit falsa, cum necesse sit corpora commixta occupare situs individuales correspondenter positos secundum sua indivisibilia, et ita dos subtilitatis facit ad penetracionem, non ad divisionem vel coextensionem, et sic post diem iudicii erit celum quoad locum extensius versus terram, correspondenter ut habebit plura punctalia de spera sublunari vel elementari, sic quod venter celi gravidabitur, servando figuram (ut hic supponitur).

As to this, [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that the major is false, since it is necessary that bodies mingled together occupy individual sites correspondingly placed according to their indivisibles; and so the dower of subtlety makes for penetration, not for division or coextension;[^15] and so after the day of judgement the heaven will be, as to place, more extended towards the earth, correspondingly as it will have more points of the sublunar or elementary sphere, so that the belly of the heaven will grow great, keeping its figure (as is here supposed).

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 240.31: It destroys time.
- 241.23: Every instant would last to infinity.
- 242.10: The origin of all these absurdities is the falsehood about the host.
- 243.8: Infinity in its true sense belongs to God alone.
- 243.23: Arguments for multiplication:
- 243.24: I. Several things can be in one place; why not one thing in several places?
- 243.32: 2. There are miracles that shew it to be possible.
- 244.4: 3. Scripture allows it.
- 244.7: Answers: Things that are mixed do not occupy the same place. It is a case of penetration, not of coextension.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Petrus* is the stock name of an example: a man multiplied in many places could still dance a ring with himself. ABCD read *multitudinis* for *multiplicacionis* (241.1).

[^2]: **C.** *Potencia* here "power" (a natural capacity, then God's power), not the "potency" of Aristotle's analysis. The *huiusmodi* of 241.5 is Loserth's: the manuscripts lack *-modi*.

[^3]: **S.** Loserth refers to Shirley's *Catalogue* p. 3 and the *Tractatus De Tempore* placed in *De ente* Book I, 6 (LEDGER K08c-1).

[^4]: **C.** *Non communicancia*: times that share no part or common term, each wholly distinct from the others though equal.

[^5]: **T.** *Coextensis* (241.30) is Loserth's; the manuscripts read *condescensis*. "Times" supplied from *unum tempus*.

[^6]: **C.** The construal of *tantum mirabile quod potest facere* is uncertain: taken as "as great a marvel as it can do", i.e. the opponent cannot set a limit to God's marvels and so cannot know the world is as it appears.

[^7]: **C.** A strip of bark between two spiral lines on a cylindrical hazel-rod (*corulus columpnaris*), unwound and straightened by proportional parts: the case asks where the strip ends. E reads *interceptat* (242.23), D and E *columpnar-* variously (242.24).

[^8]: **C/T.** *Sor* is taken as the logicians' short form of *Sortes* (Socrates), the stock subject of a case, fixed at the moving end of the bark; it may instead be a common noun lost in the transmission. Flagged.

[^9]: **C.** The *quod* clause after *dicitur* has no finite verb of its own (*circumgiretur* belongs to the relative); taken as the setting of a case, closed by *quo facto foret*.

[^10]: **C.** *Nec infinitum magnum poterit esse tale* is taken as "nor can anything infinitely great be such [a creature]"; the clause may be corrupt. ABC read *nature* for *negatur* at 243.16, which does not construe better; the print is kept.

[^11]: **G.** *Dos subtilitatis*: one of the dowers of the glorified body (cf. *dos immortalitatis*), by which it can be with another body in one place.

[^12]: **C.** *Elementis immixtis*: taken as elements "mingled" in a compound (so the answer, *corpora commixta*, ¶6), not "unmixed". ABCD read *multitudine* for *multiplicacione* (243.23).

[^13]: **S/T.** Loserth refers to Paulinus, *Vita S. Ambrosii* c. 49, but that chapter tells of an appearance on the day of Ambrose's death, not of his being in two places; the story that fits is Gregory of Tours, *De miraculis S. Martini* I.5 (Ambrose, asleep at the altar in Milan, at Martin's funeral), as at prol-b n22 (LEDGER Kprolb-11, K08c-3). B has *Ambrosio* with *Franconio* written outside as a correction (244.1).

[^14]: **C.** The third argument "from the faith of scripture" is the one set out earlier in the chapter; Wyclif answers its texts in the next part (247.13–28). D reads *articulo de fide*, ABC *scriptura* (244.6).

[^15]: **C.** The dower lets a glorified body penetrate another, not share its dimensions; *divisionem* stands in the print (perhaps for *dimensionem*; not flagged in the apparatus).
