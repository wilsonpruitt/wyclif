# *De potestate pape*, chapter 12, part 1: the church can do without a caesarean pope

## ¶1 · 316.25–317.3

> [p. 316] Adhuc restat dubitandum quomodo posset deesse papa cesareus, cum necesse sit esse unum caput ad causas ecclesiasticas terminandum, ad beneficia ecclesiastica parciendum et ad privilegia concedendum; unde ad tantum cecati sunt seculares valde subtiles in ista argucia, quod [p. 317] putant cum clero infatuante dominos, quod non staret sine ordine huius cleri cesarei dominium seculare.

It still remains to be doubted how a [caesarean pope]{g:papa-cesareus} could be lacking, since there must be one [head]{g:caput} for determining ecclesiastical cases, for distributing [ecclesiastical benefices]{g:beneficium-ecclesiasticum} and for granting [privileges]{g:privilegium}; hence the [seculars]{g:secularis}, very subtle in this [quibble]{g:argucia}, are blinded to such a degree that [317] they think, with the clergy who make fools of the lords, that secular [lordship]{g:dominium} would not stand without the order of this [caesarean clergy]{g:clerus-cesareus}.[^1]

## ¶2 · 317.4–317.16

> [p. 317] Sed ambos istos simul convinco tripliciter, primo in hoc quod perfecte stetit ecclesia cum seculari dominio trecentis annis post ascensionem Christi et amplius, quando non fuit talis ceca et venenosa dotacio. Si igitur tunc crevit utrumque istorum membrorum ecclesie, et post decrevit, eciam quousque utrumque istorum capitum reliquum destruxerit ad non gradum, quomodo non est evidens, quod utramque istarum parcium foret viare plus prospere quoad Deum et homines reducta ad ordinem ecclesie quem Christus instituit, quam sit irreligiose commixta in ritibus ex auctoritate cesarea.

But I refute both of these together in three ways: first, in that the [church]{g:ecclesia} stood perfectly, together with secular lordship, for three hundred years and more after Christ's ascension, when there was no such blind and poisonous [endowment]{g:dotacio}. If, then, each of these [members]{g:membrum} of the church grew at that time, and afterwards declined, even until each of these heads should destroy the other to the point of nothing, how is it not evident that each of these parts would [fare on its way]{g:viator} more prosperously, as regards God and men, brought back to the order of the church which Christ instituted, than it does now, irreligiously mingled in [rites]{g:ritus} by caesarean [authority]{g:auctoritas}.[^2]

## ¶3 · 317.17–317.22

> [p. 317] Quod potest confirmari ex lege Dei, ex lege ecclesie et lege cesarea. Ex lege Dei, quia tunc viveret clerus uterque lege Dei placencius, sibi utilius, Christo ac apostolis suis conformius. Quomodo igitur non haberet Deum exinde magis propicium?

This can be confirmed from the law of God, from the law of the church, and from caesarean law. From the law of God, because then the [clergy]{g:clerus} on either side would live more pleasingly by the law of God, more profitably to itself, more conformably to Christ and his apostles.[^3] How, then, would it not have God more favourable to it on that account?

## ¶4 · 317.23–317.34

> [p. 317] Ex lege ecclesie patet idem cum distinccione XCV: Olim ex decreto Jeronymi docentur quomodo ecclesia regulabatur ante dotacionem, eciam in tempore apostolorum communi consilio sacerdotum, et postmodum instigacione dyaboli facta est divisio constituendo capitaneos secundum dignitatem mundanam, captam fallaciter ex preeminencia apostolorum, a quibus instrumentaliter sunt conversi, ut tangit Apostolus prima Cor. I et III. Quare igitur non foret perfeccius stare in statu primevo, quem Christus instituit quam in statu extraneo qui instinctu dyaboli inolevit?

From the law of the church the same is clear, with distinction 95, *Olim*: from the decree of Jerome men are taught how the church was ruled before the endowment, even in the time of the apostles, by the common counsel of the [priests]{g:sacerdos}, and how afterwards, at the instigation of the devil, a division was made by setting up [captains]{g:capitaneus} according to worldly dignity, taken fallaciously from the [preeminence]{g:preeminencia} of the apostles, by whom, as instruments, they had been converted, as the Apostle touches on in 1 Corinthians 1 and 3.[^4] Why, then, would it not be more perfect to stand in the first [state]{g:status}, which Christ instituted, than in a foreign state which grew up at the devil's prompting?

## ¶5 · 318.1–318.26

> [p. 318] Et quantum ad legem civilem, patet quod imperator sicut et quilibet civilis dominus tenetur defendere suum dominium seculare vel mutare in melius, sed in solucione ordinis cleri Christo deterioratur utraque pars saltem in spiritualibus quoad Deum, igitur repugnat principiis legis humane talis prepositi spiritualis mundana dotacio. Item, prius tempore et natura est secularis dominacio quam talis cleri dotacio, ut docet Crysostomus In Imperfecto Omelia XXXIV, sed omne sic prius potest esse tali posteriori subducto, igitur possibile est ecclesiam viare prospere sine tali capitaneo. Mirum itaque foret, quod ita fuit de facto ex ordinacione divina, et cum modo non potest sic esse propter ordinacionem cesaream, sic quod ordinacionem Christi licet extinguere, sed ordinacio cesaris non potest deficere stante prosperitate ecclesie. Inconsequenter igitur loquuntur qui dicunt quod ecclesia potest prospere procedere sine Romano imperio adiuvante, ut patuit in primitiva ecclesia, et patet hodie de multis christianis, exemtis ab illo imperio infirmato, sed non potest stare sine suo episcopo vel ab eo collato privilegio. Numquid fumus est maioris constancie quam ignis?

And as to the civil law, it is clear that the emperor, like any civil [lord]{g:dominus}, is bound to defend his secular lordship or to change it for the better; now in the loosening of the clergy's order to Christ each part is made worse, at least in spiritual things as regards God; therefore such worldly endowment of a spiritual [superior]{g:prepositi} [is incompatible]{g:repugnare} with the principles of human law.[^5] Again, the secular [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio} is prior in time and nature to such endowment of the clergy, as Chrysostom teaches in the *Opus imperfectum*, homily 34;[^6] now everything so prior can exist with such a posterior thing withdrawn; therefore it is possible for the church to fare prosperously on its way without such a captain. It would therefore be a wonder that it was so in fact by divine [ordinance]{g:ordinacio}, while now it cannot be so because of the caesarean ordinance, so that it is lawful to extinguish Christ's ordinance, but Caesar's ordinance cannot fail while the church's prosperity stands. Those, therefore, speak inconsistently who say that the church can go forward prosperously without the help of the Roman empire, as was clear in the primitive church and is clear today of many Christians exempt from that weakened empire, but cannot stand without its bishop or the privilege conferred by it. Is smoke, then, of greater constancy than fire?[^7]

## ¶6 · 318.27–318.30

> [p. 318] Nec valet ficticia qua dicitur, quod ordinacio pape processit immediate a Christo, ut patet de Petro, ordinacio autem imperatoris processit ab homine.

Nor is the [fiction]{g:ficticia} valid by which it is said that the [pope]{g:papa}'s ordinance proceeded immediately from Christ, as is clear of Peter, but the emperor's ordinance proceeded from man.[^8]

## ¶7 · 318.31–319.34

> [p. 318] In ista autem materia necesse foret utramque partem cognoscere conclusiones ad confutandum cautelas dyaboli. Necesse est secundo distinguere inter papam, vel episcopum cesareum et ministrum [p. 319] simplicem christianum; nam iuxta dicta talis cesareus innititur privilegiis humanis tamquam principiis per se constituentibus servum talem. Minister autem pure christianus irridet hec privilegia ut superflua vel nociva. Et patet tercio defectus argucie, quando sic arguitur: Ordinacio pape immediate processit a Christo, ut patet de Petro, igitur ordinacio pape cesarei sic processit ex ordinacione Christi. Per papam Petrum et suos socios adiutores regebatur ecclesia, ergo per idem ex ordinacione Constantini oportet quod per gradus prelatorum cesareos regatur ecclesia; Christus indubie docens Matth. XX et Luce XXII maioritatem, quam sui debent attendere, negat istam arguciam. Si igitur Christus per se constituit capitaneum tocius militantis ecclesie, nec Petrus vel alius apostolus audebat talem capitaneum constituere vel se ipsum huiusmodi vendicare, sed constituerunt per loca episcopos, quanta irreligiosa presumpcio in cesare statuere, quod generaliter suus episcopus foret caput tocius ecclesie aut quod maioritatem civilem sub pallio privilegii contra decretum Christi in ecclesiam suam induceret? Ordinacio autem regis longe ante hec tempora processit a Deo, ut patet II De Civitate Dei XII, sic quod, licet Christus tempore legis veteris approbavit dominium seculare divinitus, et tempore legis nove approbavit multipliciter imperatorem, regem et dominum secularem humanitus, tamen nedum layci sed professores scripture verecunde proclamant, quando deficiunt argumenta, quod per idem vel forcius deficeret in ecclesia quodcumque dominium seculare.

And in this matter it would be necessary for each party to know the conclusions, in order to confute the devil's [wiles]{g:cautela}. It is necessary, secondly, to distinguish between the pope, or caesarean [bishop]{g:episcopus}, and the [319] simple Christian minister; for, according to what has been said, such a caesarean leans on human privileges as on principles that of themselves constitute such a servant. But the purely Christian minister laughs at these privileges as superfluous or harmful. And, thirdly, the defect of the quibble is clear when it is argued thus: "The pope's ordinance proceeded immediately from Christ, as is clear of Peter; therefore the ordinance of the caesarean pope so proceeded from Christ's ordinance. The church was ruled by Pope Peter and his companions and helpers; therefore, by the same token, by Constantine's ordinance the church must be ruled through the caesarean grades of [prelates]{g:prelatus}." Christ, who undoubtedly teaches in Matthew 20 and Luke 22 the [greatness]{g:majoritas} his own ought to attend to, denies this quibble.[^9] If, then, Christ himself set up the captain of the whole [church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans}, and neither Peter nor any other apostle dared to set up such a captain or to claim himself to be such, but they set up bishops place by place, how great an irreligious presumption [was it] in Caesar to decree that his bishop should be, in general, head of the whole church, or to bring civil greatness into his church under the cloak of privilege against Christ's decree? But the ordinance of a king proceeded from God long before these times, as is clear from *De civitate Dei* II.12,[^10] so that, although Christ in the time of the old law approved secular lordship divinely, and in the time of the new law approved in many ways emperor, king and secular lord humanly, yet not only laymen but professors of scripture shamefacedly proclaim, when arguments fail them, that by the same token or more strongly any secular lordship whatever would fail in the church.[^11]

## ¶8 · 320.1–320.21

> [p. 320] Sed ista deduccio non habet fundamentum, nisi ut ironice loquar, quod autor scripture crebro ponit secularem primatum et sacerdotalem primatum cesareum interdicit. Et ideo dicit Crysostomus ut supra, quod in acceptando primatum ecclesiasticum neque est racio neque utilitas, quia tempore suo saltem in gente sua non fuit adiuncta primatui ecclesiastico, ut modo, pinguedo commodi temporalis. Si igitur signetur quicumque papa, cuius ordinacio immediate processit a Christo, videtur michi quod hoc docto totus populus debet eum venerabiliter amplecti. Sed nimis aliena est ab ista doctrina humana eleccio vel institucio. Ideo oportet quod illud doceatur ex vite et operum sanctitate; non ex aspiracione ad seculi commodum vel honorem. Nec ex fulminacione ficte censure ad ostendendum dominacionem nec ex tonsione gregis subiecti per fraudulentam temporalium subtraccionem, sed per condiciones apostolorum istis oppositas; et talis papa non foret alicui fidelium onerosus sed procurans undique pro lucro anime et profectu.

But this deduction has no foundation, unless, to speak ironically, that the author of scripture often sets down a secular [primacy]{g:primatus} and forbids a caesarean priestly primacy. And so Chrysostom says, as above, that in accepting ecclesiastical primacy there is neither reason nor profit, because in his time, at least in his nation, the fatness of temporal advantage was not joined to ecclesiastical primacy, as it is now.[^12] If, then, any pope whatever be pointed out whose ordinance proceeded immediately from Christ, it seems to me that, this being shown, the whole people ought to embrace him with reverence. Now human election or institution is far too foreign to this [doctrine]{g:doctrina}.[^13] Therefore it must be shown from the holiness of life and works, not from aspiring to the world's advantage or honour; nor from the thundering of a feigned [censure]{g:censura} to display the exercise of lordship, nor from the shearing of the subject flock by a fraudulent withdrawal of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, but by the conditions of the apostles that are opposed to these; and such a pope would be burdensome to none of the faithful, but would make provision everywhere for the gain and progress of the soul.

## Notes

[^1]: **R.** At 316.25 both manuscripts note in the margin: *Papa cesarius si deest ecclesia sancta melius staret*, "if the caesarean pope is lacking, holy church would stand better". **C.** *Cum clero infatuante dominos*: the clergy who make the lords fools, not clergy who are themselves foolish.
[^2]: **C.** *Utrumque istorum membrorum* / *capitum*: the two parts are the clergy and secular lordship, each called a member of the church and then a head. *Ad non gradum*, literally "to no degree", that is, to nothing. *Utramque … foret viare … reducta*: an accusative with *foret* and the infinitive; we take *reducta* and *commixta* with *utramque*, and supply "now" for *sit*.
[^3]: **C.** *Clerus uterque*: "the clergy on either side", taken as the clergy of both kinds the chapter sets against each other (caesarean and purely Christian, 318.31–319.3). The singular *clerus* with *uterque* is odd, and a word may be lost (e.g. *clerus [et populus] uterque*, clergy and people, the two parts of ¶2).
[^4]: **S.** Decretum, D. 95 c. 5 (*Olim*), Jerome on presbyters and bishops; Loserth's reference, cited in modern form by us (LEDGER P12a-1). Jerome's text has *communi presbiterorum consilio*; Wyclif writes *sacerdotum* (to be checked, LEDGER P12a-1). The Apostle: 1 Cor. 1:12 and 3:4, "I indeed am of Paul; and I am of Apollo."
[^5]: **T.** *Legis humane* is Loserth's correction; both manuscripts read *humanis* (318.7). **C.** *In solucione ordinis cleri Christo*: the dissolving of the order that binds the clergy to Christ; *Christo* could also go with *deterioratur* ("each part is made worse for Christ").
[^6]: **S.** Pseudo-Chrysostom, *Opus imperfectum in Matthaeum*, hom. 34; no reference at the page foot, to be checked (LEDGER P12a-2).
[^7]: **C.** *Ab eo collato privilegio*: "conferred by it", the empire; it may also be "by him", the bishop. The figure: the empire is the fire, the privilege drawn from it the smoke, which on the opponents' view outlasts the fire.
[^8]: **R.** A notes in the margin at 318.27: *Argumentum pro papa*, "an argument for the pope". **C.** *Ordinacio pape*: the ordinance by which a pope is set up, not the sacrament of ordination (GLOSSARY *ordinacio*).
[^9]: **S.** Matt. 20:25–26 and Luke 22:25–26: "The kings of the Gentiles lord it over them … But you not so: but he that is the greater among you, let him become as the younger."
[^10]: **S.** Augustine, *De civitate Dei* II.12 as Wyclif gives it; no reference at the page foot, and the book and chapter are to be checked (cf. 324.8, where Loserth corrects a "II" to XX) (LEDGER P12a-3).
[^11]: **T.** B reads *negandum* for *nedum* (319.30), and *et* for *nec* at 319.17; the print is translated in both places. **C.** *Verecunde*: "shamefacedly", to their shame; the professors fall back on this claim when their arguments fail.
[^12]: **S.** "As above": the *Opus imperfectum* (318.11); the saying on primacy is to be checked (LEDGER P12a-4).
[^13]: **C.** *Sed* brings the minor premiss closed by *ideo*: hence "now". *Doctrina* here is the showing just named (*hoc docto*, "this being shown"): human election cannot show that a pope is from Christ.
