# *De eucharistia*, chapter 9, part 6: what the pronoun demonstrates, the blessed bread, and the silver temples of Diana

## ¶1 · 292.15–293.32

> [p. 292] Unde Doctor Communis super illud distinccione XI, questione III, videtur dicere quod mutacio ista differt ab aliis, cum ibi sit corrupcio et nullius substancie generacio vel subiectum mutacionem illam suscipiens, et cum hoc verbum substantivum est dicit quandam mutacionem, quia aliter non inplicaretur sacramentalis confeccio, unde ipse videtur dicere quod per Lyncolniensem hoc demonstratur corpus Christi, ut sub illis accidentibus Doctor Subtilis super distinccione VIII videtur dicere quod demonstratur ens analogum sub quadam contraccione. Glossa decreti super distinccione II, De Consecracione, capitulo Timorem, dicit quod nichil demonstratur, quia proposicio illa sumitur materialiter, cum aliter foret falsa. Sed sanctus Richardus, Armachanus et alii sancti dicunt cum [p. 293] evangelio quod demonstratur panis, cum aliter foret locucio illa inpertinens. Admisso ergo quod panis demonstratur in proposicione illa conversiva: Hoc est corpus meum, manifestum est quod supposita eius veritate sequitur panem remanere post consecracionem; nam post consecracionem est verum quod ille panis est corpus Christi, et si sic ille panis est panis, ideo post consecracionem remanet quod ille panis est panis. Confirmatur ex hoc quod Christus inmediate ante benedixit illi pani quem mandavit ab apostolis comedi, quod non est signum desicionis panis sed pocius melioracionis. Nam si ille panis benedictus secundum se totum destruatur, illa benediccio foret crudelior sive severior quam malediccio qua Christus maledixit ficui, Math. XXIᵒ, quia post malediccionem ficus, substancia eius remansit arida, hic autem dicitur nichil de panis substancia remanere et conversio nichil facit ad bonitatem panis sive sue essencie. Nec dubium quin Christus non mandavit suis manducare ex hoc, nisi post eius consecracionem, et per consequens Veritas que tantum odivit duplicitatem sophisticam voluit hoc remanere post consecracionem et fraccionem ab apostolis comedendum. Illi ergo qui horrent veritatem scripture et illi qui ipsam diligunt tamquam fidem sollicitarentur circa sensum katholicum huius principii. Unde nimis longum a veritate scripture videtur michi concedere ydemptificacionem vel inpanacionem quam transsubstanciacionem predictam et propinquius loyce sanctorum, quos non licet scandalizare quod ignorarunt logicam seminantes verba heretica, cum ipsi loquuntur scripture similius nec sciunt fundare glossam quam illi fingunt.

Hence the [Common Doctor]{g:doctor-communis}, on that [text], distinction 11, question 3,[^1] seems to say that this change differs from the others, since there is corruption there, and generation of no [substance]{g:substancia}, nor a subject receiving that change; and since this substantive verb "is" says a certain change, because otherwise sacramental [consecration]{g:confeccio} would not be implied; hence he seems to say that through Lincoln[^2] "this" demonstrates the body of Christ as under those [accidents]{g:accidens}. The [Subtle Doctor]{g:doctor-subtilis}, on distinction 8, seems to say that there is demonstrated an [analogous]{g:analogum} being under a certain contraction.[^3] The gloss of the decree on distinction 2, *De consecratione*, chapter *Timorem*, says that "nothing is demonstrated, because that proposition is taken materially", since otherwise it would be false.[^4] But Saint Richard [Armachanus]{g:ardmacanus} and other [saints]{g:sancti} say with [293] the gospel that bread is demonstrated, since otherwise that utterance would be irrelevant.[^5] It being admitted, then, that bread is demonstrated in that [converting]{g:conversivus} [proposition]{g:proposicio}, "This is my body", it is manifest that, its truth being supposed, it follows that bread remains after consecration; for after consecration it is true that that bread is the body of Christ, and if so, that bread is bread; so after consecration it remains [true] that that bread is bread. This is confirmed from the fact that Christ, immediately before, blessed that bread which he commanded to be eaten by the apostles, which is a sign not of the [ceasing]{g:desicio} of the bread but rather of its betterment. For if that blessed bread were destroyed according to its whole self, that blessing would be crueller or more severe than the curse by which Christ cursed the fig tree, Matt. 21, since after the cursing of the fig tree its substance remained, withered;[^6] but here it is said that nothing of the bread's substance remains, and the [conversion]{g:conversio} does nothing toward the goodness of the bread or of its [essence]{g:essencia}. Nor is there doubt that Christ did not command his own to eat of this except after its consecration; and consequently the Truth, which so hated sophistical doubleness, willed this to remain after consecration and breaking, to be eaten by the apostles. Those, then, who shudder at the truth of scripture and those who love it as faith should be solicitous about the catholic [meaning]{g:sensus} of this principle. Hence it seems to me [exceedingly]{g:nimis} [less] far from the truth of scripture to grant [identification]{g:ydemptificacio} or [impanation]{g:inpanacio} than the aforesaid [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio},[^7] and nearer to the [logic]{g:loyca} of the saints, whom it is not lawful to defame [by saying] that they were [ignorant of]{g:ignorare} logic, sowing heretical words, since they speak more like scripture; nor can they found the gloss which those [feign]{g:fingere}.[^8]

## ¶2 · 293.33–296.22

> [p. 293] Quoad tercium verbum patet quod adversarii destruunt mendaciter devocionem populi, cum dicunt [p. 294] illud sentitum in manibus sacerdotis non posse esse corpus Christi vel partem eius, cum sit unum ignotum accidens; quod nec sciunt nec audent detegere; nam (ut sophiste arguunt) non potest esse terna dimensio, quia latitudo est alterius speciei infinitum maior quam eukaristie longitudo, et profunditas est alterius speciei quam altera priorum, infinitum maior quam hostie latitudo, maior in profundo calice et minor in minus repleto. Devocio itaque populi foret maior secundum sentenciam veridicam dicentem quod sacer panis remanet habens vere ad omnem punctum sui corpus Domini, ymmo est quodammodo ipsum corpus, quam fingendo quod solum remanet ibi sacrum sed ignotum accidens quod non potest esse corpus Domini vel pars eius. Suspecta quidem foret populo ista ficticia; sed utentes istis clamorosis ficticiis notarent scripturam Act. XIXᵒ, quomodo Demetrius quidam faciens edes argenteas Dyane commovit populum contra Paulum. Unde, inquit, scitis quod de hoc artificio est nobis acquisicio, et videtis quia non solum Ephesi sed pene tocius Asie Paulus hic disuadens avertit multam turbam, dicens quoniam non sunt dii qui manibus fiunt. Non solum autem hoc sed et magne Dyane templum in nichilum reputabitur et destrui incipiet maiestas eius quam tota Asia et orbis colit. Et hiis dictis clamaverunt: Magna Dyana Ephesiorum. Nam non solum in ymagine argentea vel trunco putrido colunt Christum sed fingunt mendaciter edes accidentales quas faciunt illi populum credere esse Christum, et ipsi colunt tanquam Deum, ut creditur propter questum. Et quando [p. 295] quis rimatur veritatem fidei in ista materia commovent populum contra eum. Et illi tantum adorant quod nesciunt nec cognoscunt quod genus accidentis est sacramentum nec quid per verba sacramentalia demonstratur. Non enim est color in glossa Thome super distinccione VIII, questione Iᵃ, IVⁱ Sentenciarum quod demonstratur corpus Christi ut sub illis accidentibus, primo quia pro toto tempore prolacionis pronominis usque in finem foret sensus ille falsus, et sic tota proposicio nunquam foret vera. Secundo quia Christus non cogitavit singulariter de accidentibus panis tui aut mei quod contentum sub illis accidentibus sit corpus suum, quod sic foret sensus proposicionis nimis longus et inexplicabilis Christi discipulis. Et hec creditur racio Scoti quod pronomen significat confuse omne accidens et evidencius omnem substanciam corporalem vel spiritualem. Sed nimis confusa foret huius sensus Christi expressio. Et tercio quia iste sensus fidei scripture latebat maiores sanctos ut Augustinum, Ambrosium, Jeronimum et eis similes eciam per mille annos et amplius; quos hereticare propter sensum huic contrarium foret nimis difficile. Multi enim erant sancti qui complecius laborarunt circa fidem sensus scripture qui nunquam cognoverunt quod Christus intellexit corpus suum sic repetere sub illis accidentibus. Nec dubium quin hereticum sit fingere tante scripture sensum alium quam Spiritus Sanctus flagitat, ut dicit decretum Jeronimi positum XXIV questione III Heresis. Sensum itaque illius scripture scrutarentur doctores antequam cece arguerent [p. 296] quod, si panis remaneat, non diceretur: Hoc est corpus meum, sed: Hic est corpus meum. Coloracius enim emendaretur scriptura sic: Hoc quod continetur sub istis accidentibus est corpus meum; aliud enim posset laycus verificare. Ideo videtur dicendum (ut docet processus evangelii) quod per illam proposicionem demonstratur et significatur principaliter panem quem Christus porrexerat esse corpus suum. Ad quem sensum sacerdos in persona Christi vere asserit: Hoc est corpus meum. Et demonstracionem ac sensum secundarium habet proposicio sacerdotis quod ille panis quem ipse habet in manibus sit corpus Christi. Egena quidem foret scriptura Domini, nisi ebulire posset in talem sensum vicarium. Radix itaque dissensionis illius materie stat in intellectu proposicionis sacramentalis; et videtur quod demonstratur panis (ut dictum est quinto capitulo), quia de pane dixit Christus: Manducate ex hoc omnes. Et animando eos ad fructum huius manducacionis subiunxit pertinenter: Hoc est corpus meum. Aliter enim nimis sine causa dixissent Ambrosius et alii sancti quod panis erit corpus Christi.

As to the third word,[^9] it is clear that the adversaries lyingly destroy the devotion of the people when they say [294] that that thing [perceived]{g:sentire} in the hands of the priest cannot be the body of Christ or a part of it, since it is an unknown accident; which they neither know nor dare to [expose]{g:detegere}; for (as the [sophists]{g:sophista} argue) it cannot be the threefold dimension, since the breadth is of another species, infinitely greater than the length of the [Eucharist]{g:eukaristia}, and the depth is of another species than either of the former, infinitely greater than the breadth of the [host]{g:hostia}, greater in a deep chalice and less in one less filled.[^10] The devotion of the people, therefore, would be greater according to the truthful [teaching]{g:sentencia} that says that the [sacred]{g:sacer} bread remains, having [truly]{g:vere} at every point of itself the [body of the Lord]{g:corpus-domini}, indeed is in a manner the body itself, than by feigning that there remains there only a sacred but unknown accident which cannot be the body of the Lord or a part of it.[^11] This [fiction]{g:ficticia} would indeed be suspect to the people; but those who use these clamorous fictions should mark the scripture, Acts 19, how "a certain Demetrius, who made silver temples for Diana", stirred up the people against Paul. "Hence," he says, "you know that our gain is by this trade; and you see that not only of Ephesus, but almost of all Asia, this Paul, by dissuading, hath drawn away a great multitude, saying: They are not gods which are made by hands. And not only this, but also the temple of great Diana shall be reputed for nothing, and her majesty shall begin to be destroyed, whom all Asia and the world worshippeth. And when these things were said, they cried out: Great is Diana of the Ephesians."[^12] For not only in a silver image or a rotten trunk do they worship Christ, but they lyingly feign accidental temples, which they make the people believe to be Christ, and they themselves worship [them] as God, for gain, as is believed.[^13] And when [295] anyone searches into the truth of the faith in this matter, they stir up the people against him. And they so adore what they know not,[^14] nor do they recognise what genus of accident the sacrament is, nor what is demonstrated by the sacramental words. For there is no [colour]{g:color} in the gloss of Thomas on distinction 8, question 1, of the fourth [book] of the *Sentences*, that the body of Christ is demonstrated as under those accidents:[^15] first, because for the whole time of the uttering of the pronoun, up to the end, that meaning would be false, and so the whole proposition would never be true. Second, because Christ did not think [in a singular way]{g:singulariter} of the accidents of your bread or mine, that what is contained under those accidents is his body;[^16] for so the meaning of the proposition would be exceedingly long and inexplicable to Christ's disciples. And this is believed to be Scotus's reason, that the pronoun signifies [indistinctly]{g:confuse} every accident, and more evidently every substance, bodily or spiritual. But Christ's expression of this meaning would be exceedingly indistinct. And third, because this meaning of the faith of scripture lay hidden from the greater saints, such as Augustine, Ambrose, Jerome and those like them, even for a thousand years and more; and to [pronounce them heretical]{g:hereticare} for a meaning contrary to this would be exceedingly hard. For there were many saints who laboured more completely about the faith of the meaning of scripture who never knew that Christ meant his body to recur so under those accidents.[^17] Nor is there doubt that it is heretical to feign for so great a scripture a meaning other than the Holy Spirit demands, as the decree of Jerome placed in [Causa] 24, question 3, *Heresis*, says.[^18] The doctors, therefore, should search out the meaning of that scripture before they argued blindly [296] that, if bread remained, it would not be said "This (*hoc*) is my body" but "This (*hic*) is my body".[^19] For scripture would be emended with more colour thus: "This which is contained under these accidents is my body"; for a layman could make the other true.[^20] So it seems it must be said (as the [course]{g:processus} of the gospel teaches) that by that proposition is demonstrated and signified [principally]{g:principaliter} that the bread which Christ had held out is his body. In this meaning the priest, in the person of Christ, truly asserts: "This is my body." And the priest's proposition has a secondary demonstration and meaning: that the bread which he himself has in his hands is the body of Christ. Needy indeed would the scripture of the Lord be, unless it could bubble up into such a vicarial meaning. The root, therefore, of the dissension in that matter stands in the understanding of the sacramental proposition; and it seems that bread is demonstrated (as was said in the fifth chapter), because of the bread Christ said: "Eat ye all of this."[^21] And, encouraging them to the fruit of this [eating]{g:manducacio}, he added [fittingly]{g:pertinenter}: "This is my body." For otherwise Ambrose and the other saints would have said exceedingly without cause that the bread will be the body of Christ.

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 292.15: Erroneous opinions of sundry doctors as to what the pronoun (hoc) refers to.
- 293.2: Fitzralph gives the true meaning, viz. bread.
- 293.12: If the bread ceased to be, Christ's blessing would work more destruction than His curse, since the figtree was not annihilated.
- 294.1: Our opponents destroy devotion by saying that what is in the priest's hand is only an accident.
- 294.27: Like the Ephesians they forge accidental shrines for gain's sake.

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** Thomas on *Sent.* IV d. 11 q. 3 as Wyclif numbers it, that is the Parma d. 11 q. 1 a. 3 qc. 1 (this conversion differs from all natural ones: it reaches the matter, has no subject, and its end is not changed by generation), with a. 4 on the substantive verb; Loserth refers to Thomas, *Opera* X, 174 (LEDGER K09f-1). D *questione II*. AB omit *substantivum* (292.19).

[^2]: **C.** *Per Lyncolniensem*: Grosseteste, "the [bishop] of Lincoln"; the phrase does not attach plainly, and is taken as "by way of Lincoln", Thomas's account read through Grosseteste. The pause after *accidentibus* is ours: *ut sub illis accidentibus* ends Thomas's account (so 295.6–7), and the Subtle Doctor begins a new sentence.

[^3]: **S.** Scotus on *Sent.* IV d. 8 q. 2 (Wadding VIII, c. 437–441; tenth and eleventh conclusions: *hoc … demonstrat singulare entis, et non per se singulare alicuius minus universalis quam ens*, though understood *pro singulari … quod est corpus Christi*); Loserth: Duns Scotus, l.c., p. 80 (LEDGER K09f-2). ABC *anologum*.

[^4]: **S.** The ordinary gloss on *De cons.* D. 2 c. *Timorem* (modern form ours); Loserth quotes it: *ad hoc dico quod nichil demonstratur, nam ipsa materialiter ponitur ibidem* (LEDGER K09f-3). The quotation marks follow Loserth's italics; Wyclif's words differ from the gloss's.

[^5]: **C/S.** One man, Richard FitzRalph, archbishop of Armagh (Loserth's side-note: "Fitzralph"); the print's comma after *Richardus* does not make two persons. Wyclif gives no place; the nearest is FitzRalph, *Summa* IX.5 (Paris 1512, ff. 67v–68r), where the reading he calls *probabile* is that Christ *de illo quod dedit et fregit affirmasse videtur discipulis quod erat corpus suum* (LEDGER K09f-4).

[^6]: **S.** Matt. 21:19, "and immediately the fig tree withered away" (*arefacta est*); *remansit arida* is Wyclif's. B omits *arida*.

[^7]: **T.** *Nimis longum … quam … et propinquius*: the *quam* and the comparative *propinquius* need a comparative that the print lacks. We supply [less] (our emendation; a *minus* may be lost at 293.30–31). As printed, the sentence would say that granting identification or impanation is exceedingly far from scripture, which the comparison with transubstantiation will not bear.

[^8]: **C.** *Scandalizare*: "defame". The subject of *nec sciunt* is taken as the adversaries, whom *illi fingunt* names, though the Latin does not mark the change; on the other reading the saints "do not know how to found the gloss which those feign".

[^9]: **C.** The third head of the objection at 287.13–15, the devotion of the people; the first two were answered at 289 and 291.24.

[^10]: **C.** The sophists' argument as Wyclif reports it: the three dimensions of the host are of different species and incommensurable, so the "unknown accident" cannot be its threefold dimension.

[^11]: **C.** *Sentencia*: "teaching" (CONTEXTUAL row), the position taught. *Ymmo est quodammodo ipsum corpus*: the subject is still the bread.

[^12]: **S.** Acts 19:24–28 (Loserth: Acts 19:24–28), Douay wording where Wyclif's Latin agrees. He departs from the Vulgate: he omits *nomine, argentarius* (19:24); *Unde, inquit* for *Viri* (19:25); he omits *et auditis* and reads *disuadens* "by dissuading" for *suadens* "by persuasion" (19:26); he cuts *haec periclitabitur nobis pars in redargutionem venire* and the second *sed* (19:27); *hiis dictis clamaverunt* for *His auditis, repleti sunt ira, et exclamaverunt dicentes* (19:28).

[^13]: **C.** *Edes accidentales*: the *aedes* of Demetrius (Douay "temples") turned on the host taken as an accident; Loserth's side-note gives "shrines".

[^14]: **S/C.** John 4:22, "You adore that which you know not" (*adoratis quod nescitis*). *Tantum* taken as "so much"; or "only" ("they adore only what they know not"). DE *quod nec cognoscunt*.

[^15]: **S/G.** Thomas on *Sent.* IV d. 8 q. 1 as Wyclif numbers it, that is the Parma d. 8 q. 2 a. 1 qc. 4 ad 1: *hoc contentum sub his speciebus est corpus meum*; Loserth: l.c., p. 118 (LEDGER K09f-5). *Color*: a plausible ground, not a pretext. D *probacionis* for *prolacionis* (295.8).

[^16]: **C.** *Singulariter*: CONTEXTUAL row, here "in a singular way": Christ did not have in mind the accidents of each particular host.

[^17]: **C.** *Intellexit corpus suum sic repetere*: "meant his body thus to recur", that is, meant "body" to be taken again as "what is contained under those accidents"; *repetere* is hard, and *reperiri* "be found" is possible. ABC *intellexerunt* (295.24–25); ABC *dixit*, C *intellexit* in the margin (295.25).

[^18]: **S.** *Decretum* C. 24 q. 3 c. 27 *Heresis* (Loserth's foot; modern form ours), there given to Jerome (LEDGER K09f-6).

[^19]: **C.** *Hoc* is neuter, *hic* masculine; *panis* is masculine, so the adversaries argued that a pronoun pointing to the bread would have to be *hic*.

[^20]: **C.** *Verificare*: "make true", the active beside *verificari* "be true of"; *aliud* taken as the gospel's own form of words, which needs no emending.

[^21]: **S.** *Manducate ex hoc omnes* is not the wording of Matt. 26:26 (*Accipite et comedite*); it is the canon of the mass, *Accipite et manducate ex hoc omnes, hoc est enim corpus meum*, as Rabanus quotes it, *De sacris ordinibus* c. 19 (PL 112, 1192); 1 Cor. 11:24 has *Accipite, et manducate* without *ex hoc omnes* (LEDGER K09f-7). "The fifth chapter" is Wyclif's own ch. 5.
