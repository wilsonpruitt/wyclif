## The volume

*Tractatus de eucharistia* and *Tractatus de penitencia sive de confessione*, ed. Johann Loserth (Wyclif
Society, 1892), from the plates of archive.org `latinworks13wycl` (**leaf = page + 71**; leaf images in
`text/eucharistia/raw/leaves/`). Three texts:

Chunk ids: `prol-a … prol-c` the compendium, `01-a … 09-n` the nine chapters, `pen01 … pen06` *De
penitencia*, `quest` the *Questio*.

- **pp. 1–10, the compendium** (*Sentencia tractatus de Eukaristia in compendio*, under "Prologus"): a
  medieval summary of the nine chapters that came down with the work. Translate it like the text.
- **pp. 11–326, the *Tractatus de eucharistia* (maior)**, nine long chapters. Written in Wyclif's last
  years, before 1382–83 (Loserth, Introd. §4: it shares passages word for word with *Sermones* III, and
  it avoids the words *substancia* and *materialis* of the bread, which Wyclif says in *Sermo* III.54 he
  has stopped using). Wyclif's case against the "modern" doctrine: that the consecrated host is in its
  nature bread and wine and sacramentally, figuratively, the body and blood of Christ; that an accident
  without a subject is impossible; that the decree *Ego Berengarius* (*De cons.* D. 2 c. 42) is the
  faith of the ancient Roman church; against the friars, Thomas, Scotus and "the sect of the signs".
  A **treatise**: Wyclif speaks in the first person, states conclusions, raises objections and answers
  them. The tractatus minor (the *Confessio*) is NOT in this volume (Shirley, *Fasc. Ziz.*).
- **pp. 327–348, *De eucharistia et penitencia sive de confessione*** in six chapters (only ch. 1 is on
  the eucharist; the rest is penance and auricular confession), then the short ***Questio ad fratres de
  sacramento altaris*** (pp. 347–348, its first part only: Loserth notes that the rest of the
  manuscript's text is not on the sacrament, and is Hus's).

**Sigla. ⚠ They differ between the two works.** *De eucharistia* (Introd. §5): **A** Vienna 3927;
**B** Vienna 3932; **C** Vienna 1387 ("the best of the three" Vienna MSS, correcting A and B); **D** Prague
Univ. IV D 22 (1404–05, Andreas of Kouřim); **E** Prague Univ. XI E 3 (1460; Loserth: "the best and most
reliable text of all the five"). Loserth's lettering "does not in any way refer to their respective
value". The Vienna MSS are one group, the Prague MSS another. *De penitencia* (Introd. §6): **A** Vienna
3927; **B** Bautzen, Gersdorf Library M. St. 80.7; **C** Vienna 1337; **D** Prague Univ. III G 11; **E**
Vienna 3980; **F** Vienna 4527. Cite variants with the sigla of the work you are in. Marginal notes in the
manuscripts that Loserth prints ("F in marg.", "DB in marg.") are reception: an **R** note where they
show how a reader took the text.

**The page foot** (in your brief as "apparatus", by page) has two blocks, both keyed to the **line**:
the variants (`19. C: ecclesie in marg. alia manu.`) and Loserth's source references. The OCR of it is
rough: read it for sense, not letter by letter, and read the plate foot where a variant matters.

2. **Connectives carry the argument.** ⛔ Never omit one, never turn one into filler.

   | Latin | English | Never |
   |---|---|---|
   | enim, nam | for | "now", "indeed" |
   | ergo, igitur, ideo | therefore, so, then | "now", "and", "indeed" |
   | sed / autem, marking the **minor premiss or next step** | now | (omitted) |
   | sed / autem, adversative | but | (omitted) |
   | autem, continuative | and | (omitted), and **not "now"** |
   | cum (concessive / causal) | although / since | "whenever" |
   | tamen | yet, nevertheless | "therefore" |
   | item | again, also | "now" |
   | vero (continuative) | and; but (adversative) | "now", "truly" |
   | verumtamen | nevertheless | "but truly" |
   | unde | hence, so | "whence" (archaic), "where" |
   | per idem | by the same token | "in the same way" |
   | ymmo | indeed, rather (correcting upward) | (omitted) |
   | patet; constat | it is clear; it is evident | |

   "Now" is reserved for the minor premiss, or the step that brings a new premiss to bear. An *autem*
   that only extends the same step is "and". ⚠ The commonest error in *De ecclesia* (close-out, ~50 of
   69 connective fixes): a *sed* that brings the minor premiss of a syllogism closed by *ergo / ideo /
   igitur* rendered "but". Before writing "but" for a *sed*, look ahead for the *ergo*: if one follows,
   it is "now".
3. **Ordinary words with a medieval sense:** GLOSSARY §D; look up any word whose classical and
   Wyclifite senses may differ, and propose additions.
4. **Syntax:** construe before rendering (main verb, subject, what every relative and genitive hangs
   on). Keep quantifier order where it is meaning. Keep Wyclif's long periods where English tolerates
   them. **A doubtful construal is never chosen silently:** translate the likelier reading and write a
   **C** note giving the alternative.
   ⚠ **Predication is the matter here, as in *De incarnacione*.** *Hoc est corpus meum*, *panis est
   corpus Christi*, *hoc sacramentum est panis*: Wyclif argues about what the pronoun demonstrates and
   in what mode the predicate holds. Keep subject and predicate in his order, keep *est* as "is" (never
   "exists", "becomes" or "represents" unless the Latin says so), and keep the mode adverb where he puts
   it (*est sacramentaliter corpus Christi* "is sacramentally the body of Christ"). ⛔ Never soften "is"
   into "signifies" or harden "figuratively" into "really": the difference is the treatise's matter.
   Demonstrative pronouns (*hoc*, *istud*) under discussion: render "this", and keep their gender
   visible in a C note where the argument turns on it (*hoc* neuter against *hic*, 348).
   **Propositions in indirect speech** whose sense is under discussion: render as the quoted proposition
   ("who say 'this is an accident without a subject'"), not "who say that …", which asserts it.

## Glossary (the 492 rows these chunks use; the full file is GLOSSARY.md)

# GLOSSARY — FROZEN 2026-09-28 (Wilson's pilot review)

*One Latin term, one English rendering, every time, across the whole corpus. A reader must be able
to recognise the same word coming back, because Wyclif's arguments turn on it. A rendering changes
only by a dated decision in CONVENTIONS' decision log, never silently in a chunk. Entries may be
*added* by recorded decision as new terms appear. **Tag keys:** a chunk tags a term as
`[English]{g:latin-form}` (CONVENTIONS §11), and the key must match a Latin form in one of the rows
below. These rows are also the source of the site's hover glossary. Where a term has two senses,
the entry says so and each chunk's note picks one. A term marked **CONTEXTUAL** holds together
senses English splits: its English is chosen by context, under the dikaiosune rule (CONVENTIONS §6).*

**Sources of the decisions.** "Pilot" = settled in `pilots/` this session. "T. p. N" = Mark
Thakkar, "Duces caecorum," *Vivarium* 58 (2020), page N (`reference/`), which documents the
errors of the two most recent translations. His corrections are adopted here unless an entry says
otherwise.

**Orthography.** Headwords are given in the Society editions' medieval spelling (*eciam*,
*racio*, *quiditas*, *ymmo*), because that is what the translator will meet on the page.

# *De eucharistia*, chapter 9, part 13: [TITLE]

## ¶1 · 320.23–321.5

> [p. 320] Tercio vero confirmatur ex factis regum Anglie qui crebrius pro levi delicto acceperunt in manus suas temporalia prelatorum; cum hoc vendicant ut regalia ad quam sine repugnancia episcopi sunt iurati; evidens videtur quod non reputabunt illud hereticum sed plane katholicum, cum aliter foret clerus in Anglia monstruosus dominus capitalis et proditor regis sui. Nec oportet plus timere excommunicacionem vel leges hominum sic agendo quam domini predicti timuerant, cum factum foret plus consonum legi Dei: ideo dicunt [p. 321] experti quod sacerdotes Antichristi non excommunicant ipsos qui eos persecuntur in faciem, cum non cederet eis ad lucrum sed remotos excommunicant cecatos per satrapas suos intrinsecos, quia per hoc putant iuvamine generacionis adultere lucrum eis accrescere.

[ENGLISH]

## ¶2 · 321.6–321.18

> [p. 321] Secundo principaliter arguitur per hoc quod plus pensanda est salus et utilitas communioris ecclesie quam utilitas partis sue; sed clerus Anglicane ecclesie est modica pars respectu ecclesie tocius regni nostri; ergo debet eius utilitas plus pensari; cum ergo per hunc modum salvaretur totum regnum ab hostibus corporalibus (quod aliter faciliter perire poterat), videtur quod naturalis racio ad hoc cogit. Unde considerantes alcius statum regni dicunt quod regnum nostrum posset plene defendi ab intra per ministracionem temporalium que sunt in manu mortua; et insuper hoc foret medium multiplicandi homines qui in ista causa certare possent legitime.

[ENGLISH]

## ¶3 · 321.19–322.7

> [p. 321] Nec valet fingere quod accresceret regno peccatum sacrilegii ex hoc facto quod necessitaret ad ruinam ecclesie nostre, quia ut dicit decretum beati Ambrosii XII, questione IIᵃ, Aurum habet ecclesia, pro alendis pauperibus, pro redimendis captivis et pro edificiis necessariis locis sanctis licet eciam vasa sacra confringere sine peccato. Cum ergo hoc sit maius quam omnia ista, simul nec oportet vasa sacra ad hoc vendere nec sustentacionem cleri vel eius religiositatem propterea minuere sed augere, videtur quod hoc undique sit salubre. Non enim videtur finis thesauri sic cumulati in clero qui debet mundum deserere, nisi quod ydolatrando congreget et parando [p. 322] conservet thesaurum regni forinsecis inimicis. Sic vero beatus Ambrosius dicit aurum collectum in templo Jerusalem esse causam quare Nabuchodonosor ipsam destruxerat. Et hec racio quare Anglia et multa alia regna sepius sunt destructa. Non ergo credi debet cecatis, dimissa fide scripture, quousque regnum nostrum irremediabiliter sit destructum.

[ENGLISH]

## ¶4 · 322.8–324.3

> [p. 322] Item, (ut omnis fidelis fatetur) multiplicacio peccatorum est causa destruccionis regnorum, sed illa inter christianos potissime originatur a clero (ut docet Gregorius in Registro suo libro III, cap. XXIX), ergo causa destruccionis regnorum originatur ex peccato cleri. Cum ergo peccatum cleri potissimum sit avaricia que est ydolorum servitus (ut dictum est tractatu De Simonia), videtur quod remedium potissimum ad tollendum destrucciones regnorum foret tollere occasionem simonie in clero; que per hoc tolleretur potissime quod clero ut angelis vel Christo et aliis statum innocencie servantibus servarentur vite necessaria sine pluri; aliter enim inficiunt ministerium, consecrant eukaristiam ad sui iudicium et reddunt se utcumque dampnabiles per consensum. Ideo minus malum foret undique tale ministerium non adesse sacerdotes itaque hoc ministerium, quia debent in paupertate habendo omnia in communi propinquius sequi Christum. Quod si murmuraverimus contra dictam sentenciam que secundum legem Dei foret ad utilitatem ecclesie, indignificamus nos, etsi panem sacramentalem conficimus, refici pane vite. Deus enim [p. 323] communia bona preponderat, sicut ordinat in prosperitate communis populi stare valetudinem regni nostri. Quomodo ergo permitteremus avaris spoliacionem pauperis populi de elemosinis quibus vivimus, et habendo bona eius incastrata non ipsos superflue quoad Deum et homines foveremus. Colligendo ergo radices errorum in clero, patet quod tria sunt in quibus sophisticatur ecclesia, scilicet cleri dominacio, tradicionum suarum magnificacio et sanctitatis sue simulacio; et ex illis procedunt infinita sophismata regis superbie, ut excommunicacionis terrificacio, observacionis legis sue preponderacio et signorum in sacramentis ac sacramentalibus nimia attencio. Remedium contra primum est fides de Christi pauperie quam totus clerus debet sequi propinquius et consequenter exemplo suo ad hoc trahere populares. Remedium contra secundum est quod leges humane precise de tanto sunt valide, de quanto in lege Domini sunt fundate. Remedium contra tercium est credere vite et operibus conformibus legi Dei; nam res cui credimus est insensibilis; cui non pertinet sensum sensibile nisi de quanto fidem excitat ad credito secundum caritatis regulas adherendum. Et in isto tercio est magis velatum periculum, ut patet de sacramentorum sophisticacione supra fidem scripture et specialiter in illis que lucrum sapiunt, ut patet de eukaristia, penitencia et sepultura. Ideo debet fidelis theologus contra simulaciones istas constanter invehere. Finis itaque gracia cuius hec dixerim est ut tollendo errorem in signis fidelis memoretur meritorius signatorum. Nec credo esse aliquod genus hominum, quod memoratur Christum falsius tam speculative quam practice sequendo Scarioth quam facit communitas sacerdotum, quia in quantum sic [p. 324] celebramus memoriam Christi, debemus ipsum sequendo similius pacem et veritatem diligere; et nullus magis exorbitat in utriusque contrarium.

[ENGLISH]

## ¶5 · 324.4–324.28

# *De eucharistia*, De eucharistia et penitencia, chapter 1: [TITLE]

## ¶1 · 329.2–330.18

> [p. 329] Duo sunt sacramenta precipua in quibus ecclesia est illusa, scilicet sacramentum eukaristie et sacramentum penitencie. In sacramento eukaristie Antichristus paulative tantum seduxit ecclesiam quod fecit eam quiditatem huius sacramenti sensibilis ignorantem. Superaddit eciam quod ignorat quod quilibet sacerdos quantumcunque indignus fuerit conficiendo facit regulariter ultra hoc quod fecerunt patres veteris testamenti vel apostoli miracula infinita. Sed miraculum istud est imperceptibile atque inutile. Imperceptibile quidem, quia post consecracionem sensus ut prius renunciant quod est panis; et inutile est, quia per confeccionem suam (ut false inquiunt) panis destruitur, ymmo materia prima que est fundamentum nature secundum se totam desinit esse, ac si Deus in mundi principio materiam crearet superfluam vel aliter pro sacerdotibus Antichristi discipulis materiam primam creavit in mundi principio, ut ex ea blaspheme et irregulariter consecrent corpus Christi et postmodum sine causa miraculose ipsam adnichilent. Talia autem ridicula infinita que fideles evacuant, dicendo quod hoc [p. 330] sacramentum naturaliter est panis et sacramentaliter corpus Christi. Non enim vident causam quare Deus hoc fundamentum nature destrueret, sicut nec vident quod corpus Christi in natura sua est materialius, densius aut maius quam prius fuerat; nec vident in quid ista materia convertatur nisi secundum fantasiam suam erroneam in nichilum convertatur, quia utendo terminis de conversione (ut prius usa fuit ecclesia) illa materia non converteretur in materiam corporis Christi vel in corpus Christi aut eius partem nisi fieret id conversum, sicut nos dicimus quod panis convertitur in corpus Christi et prius iniustus in filium Dei est conversus; quod omnis talis conversio dicit aliquam faccionem, sic quod natura conversi sit accidentaliter de novo quod vel cuiusmodi prius non fuerat. Materiam autem istam non possunt fingere sic converti, ideo necessitantur dicere quod virtute sui miraculi est annichilata. Sed procul a fidelibus talis vesania.

[ENGLISH]

## ¶2 · 330.19–331.22

> [p. 330] In inpossibiles autem errores incidunt Antichristi filii de penitencia, cum nesciunt fundare istam penitenciam quam papa de novo instituit; evidenciam autem que fidem faceret vel scripture nescit; nescit eciam si penitens sit contritus vel peccatum suum deletum a Domino, sicut nescit quantitatem vel qualitatem penitencie iniungende. Communiter tamen limitatur quiditas huius penitencie quod est peccati ostensio sacerdoti cum contricione precedente et satisfaccione penitencie consequente; et sic (ut quidam inquiunt) sacramentum penitencie est in diversis generibus; nec est possibile tres eius partes, scilicet cordis contricionem, oris confessionem et operis satisfaccionem temporaliter [p. 331] esse simul. Spreta ista irregulari descripcione penitencie videtur in ipsam ulterius procedendum, et primo notanda est sentencia sepedicta quod non est simpliciter de necessitate salutis quod quilibet beatificandus eciam dum discrecionem attigerit confiteatur omnia peccata sua que commisserat proprio sacerdoti, quia antequam ista lex fuit edita multi sancti fuerant salvati tam de lege antiqua quam de lege gracie. Et ista confessio est alibi multipliciter impugnata.
