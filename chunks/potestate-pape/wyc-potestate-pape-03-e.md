# *De potestate pape*, chapter 3, part 5: Peter's humility and charity

## ¶1 · 58.8–60.9

> [p. 58] Quantum ad secundam virtutem que est humilitas, patet quod necesse est cementem fidei coniungi lapidi ad construccionem celestis edificii, unde Math. XVIII. 1, 2, quando mota est questio, quis maior est in regno celorum magister decreverat, quod parvulus in mente humilior; ideo cum Petrus sit post Christum fundamentum ecclesie, necesse est quod sit quodammodo fratribus suis humilior. Et istam leccionem didicerunt apostoli a magistro Math. XI, 29: Discite, inquit, a me, quia mitis sum et humilis corde, quod Petrus quia eminenter didicerat constitutus est fundamentum primum inter apostolos; unde Augustinus in De Verbis Domini Sermone X: Discite a me non mundum fabricare, non creare, non miracula facere, nec mortuos suscitare, sed quoniam mitis sum et humilis corde. Quamvis autem istam leccionem convenit et oportet quemlibet salvandum advertere, oportet tamen Petrum, si debet esse fundamentum substratum aliis, profundius illam cognoscere, oportet enim fundamentum ecclesie esse humillimum, quia aliter tanta fabrica cuius fundus in terra capitur et cacumen usque ad summum celum erigitur non stabit solidum; fabrica igitur tam gravis in moribus requirit fundacionem [p. 59] in summo gradu humilitatis possibili. Et hec racio quare Christus noluit fundare suam ecclesiam in mundi diviciis tamquam stercoribus vel aquis labilibus nec in mundi gloria vel fastu seculi tamquam ventis, sed in stabili paupertate, que securius, brevius et statui innocencie conformius facit humiles regnum celorum acquirere. Unde, ut declarat Lincolniensis in quodam sermone de humilitate, quod vere humilem iste octo condiciones inseparabiliter consequuntur: Discutiat, inquit, unusquisque se ipsum diligenter, an vere humilitatis signa ipsum sequuntur. Vere namque humilis hiis signis dinoscitur: Si correptus a minori non dedignatur, si non insolescat honoribus, si non elevetur vel delectetur in suis laudibus, si non reddat malum pro malo, nec maledictum pro maledicto sed e contrario benedicit. Si inter equales primus est in ope et ultimus in ordine, primus in labore et ultimus in requie, primus in ministrando et ultimus in recumbendo. Si gracias agit divine benevolencie, si tam libenter obedit quam precipit, si in potestate constitutus prius se noscit subiectum quam prepositum, sicut fecit centurio, de quo Luce VII. Et inter omnes scolas pertinentes preposito et Petri vicario non est alia pertinencior, sicut enim Petrus acute correptus a Paulo, ut patet Gal. II, humiliter toleravit, quando eciam missum est pro illo ad confirmandum centurionem Act. X. non petiit constipacionem comitive elegantis equestrium sed humiliter resedit in Yoppen pedester et requisitus in domo Simonis coriarii iuxta mare venit sibi humilius et instruxit, non expectans ab eo retribucionem temporalium vel [p. 60] donorum, et quando crebro se exposuit ante alios apostolos, non sic fecit gracia mundani honoris vel commodi, sed humiliter, obedienter et serviliter pro supportando onere Christi. Et sic quicquid de ipso legimus sonans in eius maioritatem super alios apostolos dicit maioritatem sue humilitatis atque servicii, quia iuxta hoc debet in eis maioritas attendi, ut patet Math. XVII et Luce XXII ex diffinicione magistri.

As to the second [virtue]{g:virtus}, which is humility, it is clear that the cement of faith must be joined to the stone for the construction of the heavenly building; hence in Matthew 18:1–2, when the question was raised who "is the greater in the kingdom of heaven", the master had decided that [it is] the "little child", humbler in mind; therefore, since Peter is, after Christ, the foundation of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, he must be in a certain way humbler than his brethren.[^1] And the apostles learned this lesson from the master, Matthew 11:29: "Learn," he says, "of me, because I am meek, and humble of heart"; and because Peter had learned it eminently, he was appointed the first foundation among the apostles; hence Augustine, *De verbis Domini*, sermon 10: "Learn of me, not to fabricate the world, not to create, not to do miracles, nor to raise the dead, but that I am meek and humble of heart."[^2] And although it is fitting and needful for everyone who is to be saved to heed this lesson, yet Peter, if he is to be the foundation laid beneath the others, must know it more deeply; for the foundation of the church must be the humblest, since otherwise so great a fabric, whose base is taken in the earth and whose summit is raised up to the highest heaven, will not stand firm; therefore a fabric so weighty in morals requires a foundation [59] in the highest degree of humility possible. And this is the reason why Christ would not found his church on the riches of the world, as on dung or slipping waters, nor on the glory of the world or the pomp of the age, as on winds, but on stable poverty, which makes the humble acquire the [kingdom]{g:regnum} of heaven more securely, more quickly, and more in conformity with the state of innocence.[^3] Hence, as Lincoln declares in a certain sermon on humility, these eight conditions follow the truly humble man inseparably: "Let each one," he says, "examine himself diligently, whether the signs of true humility follow him. For the truly humble man is discerned by these signs: if, rebuked by a lesser man, he does not disdain it; if he does not grow insolent with honours; if he is not lifted up or delighted in his own praises; if he does not render evil for evil, nor railing for railing, but contrariwise blesses; if among equals he is first in help and last in rank, first in labour and last in rest, first in serving and last in reclining at table; if he gives thanks to the divine benevolence; if he obeys as willingly as he commands; if, set in [power]{g:potestas}, he knows himself a subject before [he knows himself] a [superior]{g:prepositi}, as the centurion did, of whom Luke 7 speaks."[^4] And among all the schools pertinent to a superior and [vicar of Peter]{g:vicarius-petri} there is none more pertinent. For as Peter, sharply rebuked by Paul, as is clear from Galatians 2, humbly bore it, [so] also when he was sent for to confirm the centurion, Acts 10, he did not seek the press of an elegant mounted company, but humbly stayed on foot at Joppa, and, sought out in the house of Simon the tanner by the sea, he came to him the more humbly and instructed him, not expecting from him any return of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} or [60] gifts; and when he often put himself forward before the other apostles, he did not do so for the sake of worldly honour or advantage, but humbly, obediently and as a servant, to bear the burden of Christ.[^5] And so whatever we read of him that sounds toward his [greatness]{g:majoritas} over the other apostles speaks of the greatness of his humility and [service]{g:servicium}, since it is according to this that greatness ought to be regarded among them, as is clear from Matthew 17 and Luke 22, from the master's [definition]{g:diffinicio}.[^6]

## ¶2 · 60.10–60.22

> [p. 60] Quantum ad terciam virtutem, scilicet caritatem, patet quod Petrus socios suos precesserat, patet ex fervore operum, quod oportet correspondenter ex maiori dileccione procedere; et confirmatur ex hoc quod aliter Petrus foret nimis ingratus nisi magistrum tam singulariter eum diligentem a tanta blasphemia abstergentem et super oves suas generose preficientem correspondenter magis dilexerit. Et confirmatur tercio ex hoc; quod aliter magister Joh. ultimo inpertinenter quereret: Symon Johannis, diligis me plus hiis, et statim ex maioritate dileccionis committit sibi suam ecclesiam ad pascendum.

As to the third virtue, namely [charity]{g:caritas}, it is clear that Peter had surpassed his companions; it is clear from the fervour of his works, which must proceed correspondingly from a greater love; and it is confirmed from this, that otherwise Peter would be too [ungrateful]{g:ingratus} unless he had loved correspondingly more the master who loved him so singularly, wiped him clean of so great a blasphemy, and generously set him over his sheep.[^7] And it is confirmed thirdly from this, that otherwise the master, in the last chapter of John, would ask beside the point: "Simon, son of John, lovest thou me more than these?"; and at once, from the greatness of his love, he commits his church to him to feed.[^8]

## ¶3 · 60.23–61.12

> [p. 60] Sed notandum (ut supra tetigi) quod multiplex est racio amandi: quidam enim plus amant Christum secundum racionem divinitatis, ut creditur de Evangelista, quidam vero plus amant eum secundum racionem sue humanitatis, ut creditur de Philippo, et quidam secundum racionem dileccionis sui corporis, quod est ecclesia; et sic de infinitis aliis racionibus et istorum subdivisionibus, pro quibus de quolibet sancto dicunt illud Ecclesiastici XLIV, 20 verificari: Non est [p. 61] inventus similis illi. Et sic quilibet humilior est caritativior, et sic de aliis virtutibus; et quoad aliquam earum eminenciam. Videtur tamen Augustinum super Joh. Omeliam CXXIV difficultare istam materiam in hoc, quod inpertinenter tam crebro diceret Johannes de se ipso quod ipsum diligebat Ihesus, nisi eum ultra alios preeminenter diligeret. Et certum est quod proporcionaliter ut Deus quicquam prius diligit est ipsum melius et e contra, et ex alio latere videtur quod si Petrus plus diligit Ihesum suum est ipse melior, et per consequens plus amatur.

But it is to be noted (as I touched on above) that the ground of loving is manifold: for some love Christ more under the aspect of his [divinity]{g:divinitas}, as is believed of the Evangelist; some love him more under the aspect of his humanity, as is believed of Philip; and some under the aspect of love of his body, which is the church; and so of infinitely many other aspects and their subdivisions, on account of which they say of every saint that the word of Ecclesiasticus 44:20 is verified: "There was not [61] found the like to him."[^9] And so anyone who is humbler is more charitable, and so of the other virtues; and [this] as regards some eminence among them.[^10] Yet Augustine, on John, homily 124, seems to make this matter difficult in this, that John would say of himself so often, beside the point, that Jesus loved him, unless he loved him preeminently beyond the others. And it is certain that in proportion as God loves anything sooner, it is the better, and conversely; and on the other side it seems that if Peter loves his Jesus more, he is himself better, and consequently is loved more.[^11]

## ¶4 · 61.13–62.8

> [p. 61] In ista materia videtur sanctum sic dicere, quod isti duo apostoli signant duas partes ecclesie, Petrus activam et Johannes contemplativam, quarum prima imperfecta finitur in via; et secunda perfeccior consumatur in patria, et sic ad sensum anagogicum Christus plus diligit Johannem dum plus amat vitam in patria, sed Petrus plus amat Christum, quia senciendo miseriam nostram plus diligimus Dei misericordiam, veritatem autem contemplacionis, quia nondum apparet nobis, minus diligimus. Et ad istum sensum Christus plus amavit Johannem, quia minus, inquit Augustinus, amat nos Christus miseros quam beatos, sed quam personam de illis plus diligit in celo non innotescit. Ex isto dicto videtur tamen quod in vita activa plus amoris Christi presbiter hic indicat in effectu, et sic Deus eternaliter plus amat membrum suum esse in beatitudine quam in via; et tamen quidquid amaverit eternaliter eque amat; et sic non est nostrum modo cognoscere utrum Deus simpliciter plus amat Petrum [p. 62] in beatitudine quam Johannem. Non, inquit Augustinus, quantum ab alio quolibet Christus diligitur scire poterat, qui cor alterius videre non poterat, et ideo prudenter non dicit Petrus: Domine, Tu scis, quia amo te plus hiis sed asseruit: Domine, inquit, Tu scis, quia amo te; caritatem itaque et alias virtutes Petrus habuit in via secundum aliquam racionem ceteris apostolis plus perfectas.

In this matter the saint seems to say thus: that these two apostles signify two parts of the church, Peter the active and John the contemplative, of which the first, imperfect, is ended in the [way]{g:via}, and the second, more perfect, is consummated in the [homeland]{g:patria}; and so in the anagogical sense Christ loves John more, inasmuch as he loves more the life in the homeland, but Peter loves Christ more, because, feeling our misery, we love God's mercy more, and the truth of contemplation, because it does not yet appear to us, we love less. And in this sense Christ loved John more, "because", says Augustine, "Christ loves us less wretched than blessed"; but which person of those he loves more in heaven is not made known.[^12] From this saying, however, it seems that in the active life the [presbyter]{g:presbiter} here shows in effect more of the love of Christ; and so God eternally loves more that his member be in blessedness than in the way; and yet whatever he has loved, he loves eternally and equally; and so it is not ours now to know whether God [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} loves Peter [62] more in blessedness than John.[^13] "For he could not know," says Augustine, "how much Christ is loved by any other, since he could not see another's heart; and therefore Peter prudently does not say: Lord, thou knowest that I love thee more than these, but asserted: Lord," he says, "thou knowest that I love thee." Peter, therefore, had charity and the other virtues in the way, in some respect, more perfect than the other apostles.[^14]

Matt. 18:1–4 (DR "Who, thinkest thou, is the greater in the kingdom of heaven?"; "as this little child"). B reads *fit* for *sit* (58.14).

[^2]: **S/R.** Matt. 11:29 (DR). A in marg.: *Fundamentum ecclesie quomodo dicitur Petrus* (58.18). Augustine, *De verbis Domini* sermon 10 (now *Sermo* 69); Loserth compares *Opera* V, 1688 (58.21); the words are *Sermo* 69.2, PL 38, 441 (*discite a me: non mundum fabricare … sed quoniam mitis sum et humilis corde*) (LEDGER P03e-1). B omits *in* (58.21).

[^3]: **C.** *Fundus in terra capitur*: "whose base is taken in the earth", i.e. laid in it. Riches as dung and waters, glory as winds: the house on sand, Matt. 7:26–27, is perhaps in view.

[^4]: **S/R.** Robert Grosseteste, bishop of Lincoln, a sermon on humility, which Wyclif names elsewhere as the sermon *Beati pauperes spiritu* (*Opus evangelicum* I, p. 17; the same signs are quoted in *De civili dominio* III, p. 40) (the manuscript reads *Lincon.*, 59.8); Loserth compares Wyclif's *Trialogus* p. 166 and *Sermones* II, 149 (LEDGER P03e-2, editor; Grosseteste's own text not consulted). A in marg.: *Humilis vere habet octo condiciones*; B: *Octo signa vere humilitatis* (59.13). 1 Pet. 3:9 (DR "Not rendering evil for evil, nor railing for railing, but contrariwise, blessing"); Luke 7:8 (DR "I also am a man subject to authority").

[^5]: **S/C.** Gal. 2:11–14; Acts 10:5–33 (Cornelius at Caesarea; Peter lodged with Simon the tanner at Joppa, 10:6). *Sicut enim … toleravit, quando eciam*: the correlative is not expressed; we supply "[so]". *Constipacionem comitive elegantis equestrium*, "the press of an elegant mounted company".

[^6]: **S/R.** "Matthew 17" as printed; the dispute over greatness is Matt. 18:1–4 (or 20:26); Luke 22:24–26 (DR "he that is the greater among you, let him become as the younger"). A in marg.: *Petro quidquid ascribitur super alios apostolos dicit maioritatem sue humilitatis et servicii* (60.4).

[^7]: **T/R.** AB in marg.: *Caritas* (60.10). The blasphemy is Peter's denial (Matt. 26:69–75); B omits *blasphemia* (60.16). Set over his sheep: John 21:15–17. *Dileccio* and *diligere* are "love"; *caritas* is "charity".

[^8]: **S.** John 21:15 (DR). The "second" confirmation is the one introduced by *et confirmatur ex hoc*; the text counts it implicitly.

[^9]: **S/R.** B in marg.: *Racio amandi multiplex* (60.23). *Racio* is the ground or aspect under which one loves; untagged, since the GLOSSARY's "aspect" row is the technical sense against *substancia*. John the Evangelist and the divinity, Philip and the humanity (cf. John 14:8–9): no source is given. Ecclus. 44:20 (DR "there was not found the like to him in glory"), said there of Abraham, and applied in the liturgy to each confessor.

[^10]: **T/C.** The clause *et quoad aliquam earum eminenciam* (61.2–3) does not construe as it stands and may have lost a word. Alternative construal: each saint is humbler and more charitable [than another] in some respect, in which he has an eminence among the virtues. B reads *eorum* for *earum* (61.3).

[^11]: **S/C.** Augustine, *Tractatus in Iohannem* 124.4–5 (PL 35, 1971–75: John's repeated *quem diligebat Iesus* is the question of 124.4, the two lives the answer of 124.5; Loserth: *Opera* III, 2, 820, 61.4; LEDGER P03e-3). *Prius diligit* is the print (read on the plate); *plus*, "more", would suit the argument, but "sooner" is translated. *Ihesum suum*, "his Jesus", is the print.

[^12]: **S.** Augustine, *Tractatus in Iohannem* 124.5–6 (PL 35, 1973–75; *minus nos amat Christus miseros quam beatos* is 124.6) (LEDGER P03e-4): Peter loves more, John is loved more; Christ loves us less in our misery than he will in blessedness.

[^13]: **C.** *Presbiter hic*: Peter, figure of the active life (cf. 1 Pet. 5:1, where Peter calls himself a fellow *senior*); the referent is not certain. *Eque amat*: what God loves he loves eternally, and with an equal love.

[^14]: **S.** Augustine, *Tractatus in Iohannem* 124.4 (PL 35, 1972: *Non enim quantum ab alio quolibet diligeretur scire poterat, qui cor alterius videre non poterat*); Loserth: "loosely quoted" (62.1; LEDGER P03e-5). John 21:15 (DR "Yea, Lord, thou knowest that I love thee"). *Plus perfectas* agrees with *virtutes*: the virtues, not Peter, are "more perfect" than [those of] the other apostles.
