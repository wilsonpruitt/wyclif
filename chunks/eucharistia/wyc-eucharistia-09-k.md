# *De eucharistia*, chapter 9, part 11: the glossators' new faith, the members of Christ, and the seventh doubt on adoring the host

## ¶1 · 312.1–312.17

> [p. 312] Unde sicut fratres palliant vitam cesaream consensu multiplici, sic sophisticant in materia de accidentibus eukaristie; sed sicut sunt in signis, professione et opere contradictorii sibi ipsis, sic alternatim contradicunt in materia de accidentibus sacramenti; ideo non est conveniens in hoc testimonium eorum; et cum evidenter creditur quod facta generali sinodo plures voces haberet in utrolibet istorum articulorum pars Antichristi quam pars Christi et sic amplius seduceretur ecclesia, ideo debemus pulsare Veritatem obnixius, ut in utroque istorum, et specialiter in primo quod plus spectat ad mores reducat fideles, quia quoad paginas contra scripturam non est aliud nisi adducere corruptos satrapas qui sciant exponere veritatem scripture per eius oppositum. Et ista sentencia erit diffuse katholica et contraria proscripta tanquam heretica.

Hence, just as the [friars]{g:frater} [cloak]{g:palliare} the caesarean life by manifold consent, so they [play the sophist]{g:sophisticari} in the matter of the [accidents]{g:accidens} of [the Eucharist]{g:eukaristia};[^1] but just as they are contradictory to themselves in signs, profession and work, so by turns they contradict [themselves] in the matter of the accidents of the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}; therefore their testimony on this is not fitting. And since it is evidently believed that, were a general synod held, the part of Antichrist would have more votes than the part of Christ on either of these articles, and so the [church]{g:ecclesia} would be led further astray,[^2] therefore we must knock at Truth the more earnestly, that in both of these, and especially in the first, which bears more on morals, he may bring the faithful back; for as to the [pages]{g:pagina} against scripture, there is nothing else for it but to bring in corrupt [satraps]{g:satrapa} who know how to expound the truth of scripture by its opposite.[^3] And this [teaching]{g:sentencia} will be catholic far and wide, and the contrary proscribed as heretical.[^4]

## ¶2 · 312.18–313.17

> [p. 312] Sed fideles in veritate confidunt quod, sicut in tempore Christi inposuerunt Christo et suis gravissimum genus heresis quod est blasphemia, et post hec tiranni cruciantes martires Christi inposuerunt eis heresim ac infidelitatem non colendo deos eorum, sic necesse est quod succedat in ecclesia pestis gravior, inponens fideli heresim, quia defendit veritatem scripture. In omnibus enim istis docetur commune proverbium quod viciosus cicius impetet adversarium suum ex crimine in quo timendo redargucionem se noverit magis reum. Et in omnibus istis est fides cum karitate et paciencia optima medicina. Scimus quidem quod ante tribunal Veritatis omnes astabimus, ubi non clamor vel multitudo testimonii sed ipsa veritas facti ante oculos iudicis accusabit. Nam si vel [p. 313] propter magnificenciam nostram vel pecuniam a populo cumulandam ipsos errare facimus, peccata illorum comedimus et nos ipsos pro duplici crimine ipsorum et nostra cecitate temeraria obligamus, dicente Gregorio Omelia XVII: Debemus, inquit, sine cessacione meminisse quod de quibusdam Osee dictum est: Peccata populi mei comedunt et sunt hii qui delinquencium peccata fovent ne temporalia stipendia vel honores amittant. Sed et nos qui ex obligacione fidelium vivimus quod illi pro peccatis suis nobis conferunt, si comedimus et tacemus, procul dubio eorum peccata comedimus. Quid enim oportet infundabiliter in magnificencia sacramenti fingere nostram excellenciam sine fructu? Unde frequenter mirando movebam quid movit primos glossatores sic dicere, et cum nec racio nec testimonium, solum superest revelacio vel scriptura.

But the faithful trust in the truth that, just as in the time of Christ they imputed to Christ and his own the gravest kind of heresy, which is blasphemy, and after this the tyrants who tortured Christ's martyrs imputed to them heresy and [unbelief]{g:infidelitas} for not worshipping their gods, so it must be that a graver plague succeeds in the church, imputing heresy to the faithful man because he defends the truth of scripture. For in all these the common proverb is taught, that the vicious man is quicker to attack his adversary for the crime of which, fearing [refutation]{g:redargucio}, he knows himself the more guilty. And in all these, faith with [charity]{g:caritas} and patience is the best medicine. We know indeed that we shall all stand before the tribunal of Truth, where not clamour or the multitude of testimony but the very truth of the deed before the judge's eyes will accuse. For if, either [313] for our magnificence or for money to be heaped up from the people, we make them err, we eat their sins and bind ourselves for a twofold crime, theirs and our own rash blindness,[^5] as Gregory says in Homily 17: "We must," he says, "remember without ceasing what was said by Osee of certain men: *They eat the sins of my people*; and these are they who foster the sins of offenders lest they lose temporal stipends or honours. But we too, who live by the obligation of the faithful, what they confer on us for their sins, if we eat and are silent, without doubt eat their sins."[^6] For what need is there to [feign]{g:fingere}, [without foundation]{g:infundabiliter}, our excellence in the magnificence of the sacrament, without fruit? Hence I have often, wondering, raised the question what moved the first [glossators]{g:glossator} to speak so; and since [they had] neither reason nor testimony, there remains only revelation or scripture.

## ¶3 · 313.18–314.3

> [p. 313] Quantum ad revelacionem, in diebus eorum fuit sermo Domini preciosus; scripturam autem cum sensu suo nesciunt allegare; et cum scriptura tam crebro vocat hostiam panem, periculosum videtur credere vel docere quod Spiritus Sanctus intellexit per panem illum non panem sed accidens aliene nature, cum sancti et racio contradicant. Non enim est racio quin panis sacratus et vinum simpliciter sint sacramentum corporis dominici, sicut aqua benedicta sit sacramentum baptismi et sic de aliis sacramentis; nec est racio quod Deus deteriorat universitatem suam, destruendo signi substanciam sine causa; nec est racio quod Veritas ordinet sacramentum tam spirituale, ut sit [p. 314] falsum tanquam insolubile puerorum, sic quod illa accidencia sensibilia significent panem vel subiectum suum ad esse, dum totaliter ibi desit.

As to revelation, in their days "the word of the Lord was precious";[^7] and scripture with its [meaning]{g:sensus} they do not know how to [allege]{g:allegare}. And since scripture so often calls the [host]{g:hostia} [bread]{g:panis}, it seems dangerous to believe or teach that the Holy Spirit understood by that bread not bread but an accident of an alien nature, since the [saints]{g:sancti} and reason contradict [it]. For there is no reason why the [hallowed]{g:sacrare} bread and [wine]{g:vinum} should not be [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} the sacrament of [the Lord's body]{g:corpus-dominicum}, as blessed water is the sacrament of baptism, and so of the other sacraments; nor is there reason that God should make [his universe]{g:universitas-sua} worse by destroying the [substance]{g:substancia} of the sign without cause; nor is there reason that Truth should ordain a sacrament so spiritual that it [314] would be false like a boys' [insoluble]{g:insolubilia}: so that those [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} accidents should signify bread, or their subject, as to [being]{g:esse}, while it is wholly absent there.[^8]

## ¶4 · 314.4–316.9

> [p. 314] Sed sicut false memoramur vitam Christi conversando contrarie, sic Deus permittit nos false signa concipere. Concedo tamen quod inconveniens est ecclesiam tam diu errasse in speculabilibus, sed maius inconveniens est ipsam diucius sic errasse in operabilibus et (ut credo) superiores ac inferiores huius secte discredunt quod referunt, cum nunquam vel raro celebrant, et tamen longe plus et fervencius laborant pro faccione nedum temporalium ut auri modici sed pro possessione vel habicione eorum (que est respectus debilissimus) acquirenda. Quomodo ergo credam ex opere quod talis convertit panem suum in regnum celorum, quia personaliter in corpus Christi, quod exinde credit perpetuo se habere; ymmo si credit plene evangelio, laboraret pro cognacione Christi habenda, qui Matth. XIIᵒ, 5 dicit verissime: Quicunque fecerit voluntatem patris mei qui in celis est, ipse meus frater, soror et mater est. Ista quidem cognacio que ex tanta auctoritate procedit cum verbo substandi foret longe melior quam conficere, tractare vel (ut ipsi inquiunt) facere corpus Christi. Cultum autem rectum non reprobo sed desidero et procurarem quantum sufficio, sed cultum infidelem et duplicem detestor quo adoratur sacramentum tamquam ydemptice corpus Christi. Pocius autem concederem cum Augustino, Lincolniensi et ceteris quod omne membrum Christi sit realiter et vere sed participacione quadam dominus Jesus Christus; nam quodlibet tale [p. 315] est membrum vivum Christi, ipso Christo in se loquente et faciente omnia eius viva opera tanquam quidditate intrinsecus informante, quod deficit a sacramento altaris; ideo evidencius ex racione et scriptura Christus faceret se esse quodlibet membrum suum. Aliter enim, supposita fide scripture Math. XXVᵒ, pascendo vel opus misericordie inpendendo uni in nomine Christi, sic faceret homo duobus hominibus, et sic de indigenciis eorum et toto facto foret falsum Christum tunc fuisse esurientem, sicientem aut nudum nisi fuerit eius membrum, quia posito quod scimus post ascensionem quando Christus esuriit et sic patitur, patet quod non in persona propria sic modo patitur. Et illud considerarent fingentes accidencia esse sine subiecto propter hoc quod Christus vere sed figurative dicit panem esse corpus suum. Utrumque enim dicunt simpliciter; nec convincetur ex scriptura, racione vel sanctorum testimonio plus unum quam aliud preter glossatores recentes qui ficte fabricant novam fidem; et (ut tactum est) non est maior color: Si Innocencius IIIᵘˢ sic determinat, ergo verum; quod si ipse cum complicibus determinat quod rex Anglie solvere debeat sibi et sedi sue in perpetuum nongentas marcas annuatim in partim pecunie pro commissis, tunc hoc fuit consonum racioni; utrobique enim est tanta auctoritas. Sed Anglici et cuncti fideles dicerent quod illud fuit irreligiosa cupiditas; ideo suspecta est eius assercio nisi de quanto est fundabilis in scriptura. Revera si per inpossibile Jesus noster sic sibi et suis apostolis accumulasset lucrum pro crimine, tota evangelisacio sua suspecta fuisset fidelibus, ideo sicut docemur in practica noscere istam [p. 316] fallaciam: Robertus Gibbonensis asserit sic cum suo clero, ergo verum; et secta sua tanta probabilitate negat consequenciam istam: Urbanus noster cum suis cardinalibus sic determinat, ergo verum. Sic docemur noscere has fallacias in quocunque Petri vicario et specialiter post dotacionem ecclesie. Sic enim negamus tanquam plenam fallaciam: si Johannes archipresbiter successor sancti Thome in India sic asserit, ergo verum.

But just as we falsely remember the life of Christ by [living]{g:conversari} contrary [to it], so God permits us to conceive the signs falsely. I grant, nevertheless, that it is an [absurdity]{g:inconveniens} that the church should have erred so long in speculative matters; but it is a greater absurdity that it should have erred so for longer in practical matters.[^9] And (as I believe) the superiors and inferiors of this [sect]{g:secta} disbelieve what they report, since they never or rarely celebrate, and yet they labour far more and more fervently for the [making]{g:faccio} not only of temporal goods, such as a little gold, but for the acquiring of the possession or [holding]{g:habicio} of them (which is a most feeble [relation]{g:respectus}). How then shall I believe from his work that such a man converts his bread into the [kingdom]{g:regnum} of heaven, because [he converts it] personally into the body of Christ, which he believes he will thereafter have forever?[^10] Indeed, if he believed the gospel fully, he would labour to have [kinship]{g:cognacio} with Christ, who in Matt. 12:50 says most truly: "Whosoever shall do the will of my Father that is in heaven, he is my brother, sister and mother."[^11] This kinship indeed, which proceeds from so great an [authority]{g:auctoritas} with the verb of being, would be far better than to [consecrate]{g:conficere}, handle, or (as they say) make the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}.[^12] And right [worship]{g:cultus} I do not reprove, but desire, and would procure as far as I am able; but the unbelieving and double worship I detest, by which the sacrament is adored as [identically]{g:ydemptice} the body of Christ. But I would rather grant with Augustine, [Lincoln]{g:lincolniensis} and the rest that every [member]{g:membrum} of Christ is [really]{g:realiter} and [truly]{g:vere}, but by a certain participation, the Lord Jesus Christ;[^13] for each such [member] [315] is a living member of Christ, Christ himself speaking in it and doing all its living works, as a quiddity [informing]{g:informare} it from within, which is lacking to the [sacrament of the altar]{g:sacramentum-altaris}; therefore Christ would more evidently, from reason and scripture, make himself to be each of his members. For otherwise, supposing the faith of scripture in Matt. 25, a man feeding one in the name of Christ, or bestowing a work of mercy on him, would so do it to two men; and so, as to their needs and the whole deed, it would be false that Christ was then hungry, thirsty or naked, unless [the man] were his member; for, supposing that we know [a time] after the ascension when Christ hungered and so suffers, it is clear that he does not now so suffer in his own [person]{g:persona}.[^14] And this those should consider who [feign]{g:fingere} "accidents are without a subject" because Christ truly but [figuratively]{g:figurative} says that bread is his body. For they say both [without qualification]{g:simpliciter};[^15] nor will the one be [proved conclusively]{g:convincere} more than the other from scripture, reason or the testimony of the saints, apart from the recent glossators who fictitiously fabricate a new faith. And (as has been touched on) there is no greater [colour]{g:color} [in]: "If Innocent III so determines, therefore [it is] true," than [in]: if he with his accomplices determines that the king of England must pay to him and his see in perpetuity nine hundred marks yearly in part of money for his offences, then this was consonant with reason; for [on both sides]{g:utrobique} there is as much authority.[^16] Now the English and all the faithful would say that that was irreligious cupidity; therefore his assertion is suspect except insofar as it can be founded in scripture. [In truth]{g:revera}, if per impossibile our Jesus had so heaped up gain for himself and his apostles in return for crime, his whole preaching of the gospel would have been suspect to the faithful. Therefore, just as we are taught in practice to recognise this [316] [fallacy]{g:fallacia}: "Robert of Geneva asserts so with his clergy, therefore [it is] true," and his sect with as much probability denies this [inference]{g:consequencia}: "Our Urban with his cardinals so determines, therefore [it is] true,"[^17] so we are taught to recognise these fallacies in any [vicar of Peter]{g:vicarius-petri} whatever, and especially after the [endowment]{g:dotacio} of the church. For so we deny as a full fallacy: "If John the archpriest, successor of St Thomas in India, so asserts, therefore [it is] true."[^18]

## ¶5 · 316.10–316.18

> [p. 316] Septimo dubitatur: Si eukaristia sit latria vel dulia adoranda, et videtur quod latria, quia non est adoranda nisi ut ymago Dei, nec absolute ut ymago, sed ut figurative et vicarie ipse Deus, sed ut sic est adoranda latria, ergo conclusio: Adoramus enim crucem Domini non sub racione qua truncus putridus sed sub racione qua Deus in ipsa passus fuit pro nobis. Sic enim Alexander Magnus adoravit sacerdotem Jerusalem, ut dicit magister in historia Machabeorum.

Seventh, it is doubted whether the Eucharist is to be adored with [latria]{g:latria} or [dulia]{g:dulia}.[^19] And it seems [with] latria, because it is to be adored only as the image of God, and not absolutely as an image, but as figuratively and vicariously God himself; now as such it is to be adored with latria; therefore the [conclusion]{g:conclusio} [holds]. For we adore the Lord's cross not under the aspect in which it is a rotten trunk but under the aspect in which God suffered on it for us. For so Alexander the Great adored the [priest]{g:sacerdos} of Jerusalem, as the Master says in the history of the Maccabees.[^20]

## ¶6 · 316.19–316.22

> [p. 316] Sed contra illud videtur quod Christus quoad humanitatem adorandus est yperdulia (ut hic supponitur), ergo hostia et ymagines adorande sunt adoracione inferiori.

But against that it seems that Christ as to [the humanity]{g:humanitas} is to be adored with [hyperdulia]{g:yperdulia} (as is supposed here); therefore the host and images are to be adored with a lower adoration.[^21]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 312.1: Friars support falsehood on that point as on the Eucharist.
- 312.18: The faithful are charged with heresy, as were Christ and His apostles.
- 313.18: The makers of the gloss had no revelation, and Scripture is against them.
- 314.4: The prevalence of false belief is God's punishment for our evil life.
- 315.1: Identification might be asserted of the members of Christ as well as of the sacramental bread.
- 315.21: If we are to accept all Pope Innocent says, we must pay tribute as he appointed.
- 316.9: What kind of worship is to be rendered to the host.

## Notes

[^1]: **G.** *Sophisticant* is the active form used without an object, so the sense of *sophisticari* "play the sophist" is taken, not the transitive *sophisticare* "counterfeit". The "caesarean life" is the endowed life of the clergy.
[^2]: **C.** "These articles" are the two matters in hand: the caesarean life of the endowed church and the accidents of the sacrament; "the first", which "bears more on morals", is the endowment.
[^3]: **C/T.** *Paginas* "pages" (of decrees and glosses against scripture) is the print; D reads *paganos* "pagans". Truth (*Veritas*) is Christ, hence "he".
[^4]: **C.** *Sentencia* is CONTEXTUAL; here the position taught, "teaching".
[^5]: **C.** *Pro duplici crimine ipsorum et nostra cecitate temeraria*: the twofold crime is taken as their sin and our blindness; alternatively "for their twofold crime and for our rash blindness".
[^6]: **S/T.** Gregory, *Hom. in Evang.* I.17 (Loserth). Wyclif quotes Hos. 4:8 as *comedunt* "they eat", where the Vulgate and DR have "They shall eat the sins of my people". Loserth prints Gregory's text as *ex oblacionibus fidelium vivimus, quas illi pro peccatis suis obtulerunt* "live by the oblations of the faithful, which they have offered for their sins"; the print's *obligacione* (all witnesses) is translated. PL 76, 1499–1500 confirms Gregory's *ex oblationibus fidelium vivimus* and his *comedent*. (LEDGER K09k-1)
[^7]: **S.** 1 Kings (1 Sam.) 3:1, "the word of the Lord was precious in those days" (DR); Wyclif turns *in diebus illis* into *in diebus eorum*, "in their days".
[^8]: **C.** *Tam spirituale* (ABCD *speciale* "so special") and *ad esse* are translated as printed: the accidents would signify bread "as to being", that is as there, while no bread is there. The boys' insoluble is the schoolboy's self-falsifying proposition.
[^9]: **C.** *Inconveniens* is CONTEXTUAL; here an absurdity in argument. *Speculabilia / operabilia* are matters of speculation and of practice (the accidents against the endowment).
[^10]: **C.** The construal of *quia personaliter in corpus Christi* is open: taken as "because [he converts it] personally into the body of Christ", the priest thinking he thereby holds heaven; it may also be read as Wyclif's gloss, "that is, personally into the body".
[^11]: **S/T.** Matt. 12:50, DR wording for Wyclif's Latin, which lacks *enim* "for" and the second and third "and"; the print's verse "5" is taken as 50.
[^12]: **C.** *Cum verbo substandi*: the substantive verb *est* in "he is my brother", against "make the body of Christ"; alternatively "with the word of [one who] stands under [it]".
[^13]: **S.** Augustine on the members of Christ: Loserth refers in general to the Maurist *Opera* (V 1346, 1412, 1413; IV 1083, 1394 *et alibi*). Lincoln is Robert Grosseteste, bishop of Lincoln; no work is named. Neither is checked: Loserth's Augustine places are general, and no work of Grosseteste is named by Wyclif or Loserth. (LEDGER K09k-2; K09k-3)
[^14]: **C.** Cf. Matt. 25:35–40 (DR): "as long as you did it to one of these my least brethren, you did it to me." *Sic faceret homo duobus hominibus* is taken as: unless the poor man is Christ's member, the one act would be done to two men (the poor man and Christ), and Christ himself would not have hungered.
[^15]: **C.** "Both" are the two propositions just named: that accidents are without a subject, and that Christ says bread is his body.
[^16]: **C/S.** *Color* is a plausible ground (CONTEXTUAL row). *Quod si* is translated as "than [in]: if"; a *quam* may be lost or replaced (Latin doubt). The tribute is that of King John's surrender to Innocent III (1213); the nine hundred marks are Higden's 700 for England and 200 for Ireland (*Polychronicon* VII.33, Rolls Series VIII, 190–192; LEDGER K09b-4). *In partim pecunie* is printed so. (LEDGER K09k-4)
[^17]: **S/T.** Robert of Geneva, elected at Fondi in 1378 as Clement VII, against Urban VI. ABCE read *Gillianensis*, D *Gibonensis*. *Negat* is printed for the MSS' *negant*. Wyclif's *Robertus* is always the Avignon pope (LEDGER K05d-1, K05k-4). (LEDGER K09k-5)
[^18]: **S.** Prester John; Loserth refers to Wyclif, *Sermones* III, 77. (LEDGER K09k-6)
[^19]: **G.** *Latria* is the worship due to God alone; *dulia* the honour given to the saints; *hyperdulia* (316.20) the higher honour of the humanity of Christ and his mother. ABC open *utrum eukaristia*; E *si adoracio eukaristie*.
[^20]: **S.** The Master is Peter Comestor, *Historia scholastica*; Loserth refers to Josephus, *Ant.* XI.8.5, for Alexander's homage to the high priest. Comestor, PL 198, 1497A (*De Alexandro*, leading into the Maccabees history): *nomen Dei adoravit, et pontificem veneratus est*; Josephus has the same. (LEDGER K09k-7)
[^21]: **C.** The objection argues from the lower to the lower: if Christ's humanity receives only hyperdulia, the host and images receive less. ABCE read *adoranda*.
