# *De ecclesia*, chapter 22, part 5: conditional prayer, prayer for the damned in name only, and Gregory and Trajan answered

## ¶1 · 532.21–533.23

> [p. 532] voluntatem divinam, ponendo in eius voluntate quid et qualiter decreverit succurrendum, et talis debet esse oracio viatorum; vel secundo quoad capacitatem subiecti pro quo orare intenditur, et sic supposita eius incapacitate ipsum subiectum non est persona pro qua sic oratur, sed vel ipse orans vel alia capax persona ecclesie. Quod autem vulgariter dicitur orari pro tali intento suffragari, licet sit inhabilis, equivocatur in nominetenus oracione, sicut forte oracio indigni non est aput Deum univoce oracio pro quocunque; licet ergo orare pro viatore prescito, cum sit capax meriti ut micius puniatur, sicut est de prescitis qui ad oracionem alterius faciunt nedum [p. 533] multa bona de genere, sed meritoria secundum presentem iusticiam, racione cuius micius dampnabuntur et interim ecclesie merebuntur. Sed cum certum sit quod dampnati sunt, amplius orare pro illis foret omnino inutile, ymmo oracio catholici non est pro illis, cum impossibile sit ipsam illis proficere; contendens vero quod ipse orat pro tali mortuo potest mortificare oracionem propriam contendendo. Nec contendo contra volentem defendere quod licet particulariter orare pro dampnato, dum tamen orator hoc ignoraverit, quia talis oracio nonnisi equivoce est pro illo, sed intendens quod sit pro illo faciliter meretur veniam; nec valet iuramentum oratoris, quo obligat se ad orandum annaliter temporaliter pro defuncto, sed amici defuncti debent appetere quod generaliter oretur pro mortuis in purgatorio, et talis oracio tantum vel plus sibi valet sicut oracio specialis, supposito quod sit ibi; et nulla oracio valet sibi dummodo sit dampnatus. Ideo utrimque non mediocriter peccant qui errando postponunt melius et eligunt minus bonum. Quando ergo dicitur quod oratur pro dampnato, intelligitur quod pretenditur orare pro eo, quod fit nunc licite et nunc male.

the divine will, placing in his will what he has decreed to succour and how; and such ought to be the prayer of [wayfarers]{g:viator}; or secondly as regards the capacity of the [subject]{g:subiectum} for whom one intends to pray; and so, his incapacity supposed, that subject is not the person for whom prayer is so made, but either the one praying himself or another capable person of the [church]{g:ecclesia}.[^1] And as to what is commonly said, that one prays for such a one, intending [to aid him by intercession]{g:suffragari}, although he is [unqualified]{g:inhabilis}, there is equivocation on a prayer in name only; as perhaps the prayer of an unworthy man is not before God [univocally]{g:univoce} a prayer for anyone.[^2] It is lawful, therefore, to pray for a [foreknown]{g:prescitus} wayfarer, since he is capable of merit, so that he may be punished more mildly; as is the case with the foreknown who at another's prayer do not only many things [533] good in kind, but meritorious [according to present justice]{g:secundum presentem iusticiam}, by reason of which they will be damned more mildly and meanwhile will merit for the church.[^3] But since it is certain that they are damned, to pray further for them would be wholly useless; indeed the prayer of a catholic is not for them, since it is impossible that it profit them; and one who contends that he prays for such a dead man can mortify his own prayer by contending.[^4] Nor do I contend against one who wishes to defend that it is lawful to pray particularly for one damned, provided only that the one praying is ignorant of it, because such prayer is only [equivocally]{g:equivoce} for him; but one [maintaining]{g:intendere} that it is for him easily merits pardon. Nor does the oath of the [bedesman]{g:orator} avail, by which he binds himself to pray yearly, for a term, for the departed; but the friends of the departed ought to desire that prayer be made generally for the dead in purgatory, and such prayer avails him as much as, or more than, special prayer, supposing that he is there; and no prayer avails him if he is damned.[^5] Therefore those on both sides sin not moderately who in error put the better after and choose the lesser good. When therefore it is said that prayer is made for one damned, it is understood that one professes to pray for him, which is done now lawfully and now wrongly.[^6]

## ¶2 · 533.24–534.9

> [p. 533] Ad secundum confirmacionem admitto cronicam et dico quod nec beatus Gregorius nec Deus ipse scivit Traianum fuisse dampnatum ad penam perpetuam, quia Deus eternaliter predestinavit Traianum ad gloriam; et sicut eternaliter ordinavit eum ad penam purgatoriam, sic eternaliter ordinavit quod mediante intercessione beati Gregorii salvaretur, quia debemus credere quod nec beatus Gregorius nec beatus in celo potest mutare divinum consilium vel per preces mutare vel flectere suum propositum (ut dicit decretum beati Jeronimi [p. 534] ubi supra). Et patet quod Traianus decessit in gracia predestinacionis, nec audivi suasum quod non decessit in gracia secundum presentem iusticiam. Multi enim extra iudaismum erant catholici (ut patet de Job, de Nabugodonosor et eis similibus), et sic est de multis quos propter solempnitatem ritus nostri decernimus esse foris. Nam corde creditur ad iusticiam, et in tali iusticia decedens est vere ecclesie filius et flamine baptizatus.

To the second confirmation I admit the chronicle, and I say that neither blessed Gregory nor God himself knew Trajan to have been damned to perpetual punishment, because God eternally predestined Trajan to glory; and as he eternally ordained him to the punishment of purgatory, so he eternally ordained that by means of the intercession of blessed Gregory he would be saved; for we ought to believe that neither blessed Gregory nor any blessed one in heaven can change the divine counsel or by prayers change or bend his purpose (as the decree of blessed Jerome says, [534] where above).[^7] And it is clear that Trajan died in the grace of predestination; nor have I heard it made persuasive that he did not die in grace according to present justice. For many outside Judaism were catholics (as is clear of Job, of Nebuchadnezzar and those like them), and so it is with many whom, because of the solemnity of our [rite]{g:ritus}, we judge to be outside. For with the heart we believe unto justice, and one dying in such justice is truly a son of the church and [baptized by the Spirit]{g:baptismus flaminis}.[^8]

## ¶3 · 534.10–535.2

> [p. 534] Nec vidi suasum quod beatus Gregorius peccavit in quantum hoc peciit, cum factum Dei in concedendo ostendit hoc fuisse iustum et Deo beneplacitum. Ideo si sanctus papa peccavit in sic orando, hoc fuit quia defecit in modo, ut puta, affeccione singulari suum studium occupando, suffragia ecclesie magis utilia dimittendo, vel in alia tali circumstancia, propter quod ad sui meritum temporaliter est punitus. Unde sepe contigit hominem mereri in volendo et orando pro illo quod Deus non vult fieri, ut patet de stimulo carnis pro cuius decessu apostolus rogavit ter dominum (ut patet IIª Cor. XII°), et sepe contingit hominem laborare, orare et facere illud quod Deus vult fieri, et tamen peccare graviter propter defectum modi agendi, sicut meminit Augustinus, XXII° De Civitate Dei, capit. II°: Multa, inquit, volunt fieri sancti eius ab illo inspirata sancta voluntate, nec fiunt; sicut orant pro quibusdam pie sancteque, et quod orant non facit, cum ipse in eis hanc orandi voluntatem sancto spiritu suo fecerit. Et videtur equivocando de velle, Deum velle in aliis quod non vult in se ipso. Movet ergo Deus corda hominum ad nunc modeste orandum pro illo quod ipse vult fieri, et nunc pro illo quod ipse non vult fieri; [p. 535] semper tamen talis oracio habet fructum suum et est peticio racionalis ac voluntati divine conformis.

Nor have I seen it made persuasive that blessed Gregory sinned in asking this, since God's deed in granting it showed it to have been just and well-pleasing to God. Therefore if the holy [pope]{g:papa} sinned in praying so, this was because he failed in the manner, for instance by occupying his zeal with a singular affection, by setting aside the more useful [suffrages]{g:suffragia} of the church, or in some other such circumstance; for which he was punished temporally, to his merit.[^9] Hence it has often happened that a man merits in willing and praying for that which God does not will to be done, as is clear of the sting of the flesh, for whose departure the apostle besought the Lord thrice (as is clear in 2 Corinthians 12); and it often happens that a man labours, prays and does that which God wills to be done, and yet sins gravely through a defect in the manner of acting, as Augustine recalls, *City of God* 22, chapter 2:[^10] "Many things," he says, "his saints will to be done, by a holy will inspired by him, and they are not done; as they pray for some [piously]{g:pie} and holily, and what they pray for he does not do, although he himself has made in them, by his Holy Spirit, this will to pray." And it seems, equivocating on "to will", that God wills in others what he does not will in himself.[^11] God therefore moves the hearts of men to pray modestly, now for that which he wills to be done, and now for that which he does not will to be done; [535] yet such prayer always has its fruit, and is a reasonable petition and conformable to the divine will.

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 533.24: As to the example I say that Trajan was predestined to be saved by the intercession of Gregory.

- 534.18: It may be right for a man to ask what God will not grant.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** The answer to the first confirmation (532.20): a conditional prayer is conditional either on God's will (*quoad voluntatem divinam*) or on the capacity of the one prayed for. *Ponendo in eius voluntate*: leaving it to his will. *Quid et qualiter decreverit succurrendum*: what help he has decreed, and how. *Ipsum subiectum non est persona pro qua sic oratur*: if he is incapable, the prayer is not for him, but profits the one praying or another member of the church.

[^2]: **C.** *Vulgariter*: commonly, in ordinary speech. *Orari pro tali intento suffragari*: taken as "that one prays for such a one, intending to aid him"; *intento* may instead be "the person intended", "for such an intended one, to aid him". *Equivocatur in nominetenus oracione*: "prayer" is used equivocally of a prayer in name only (cf. row *nominetenus clerici*). *Aput Deum*: before God. *Oracio indigni*: the prayer of one unworthy, in mortal sin.

[^3]: **C.** *Capax meriti ut micius puniatur*: capable of merit, to the end that he be punished more mildly. *Bona de genere*: good in kind, by the object of the act, as against meritorious. *Secundum presentem iusticiam*: by their present justice, though not predestinate (row). *Ecclesie merebuntur*: they will earn merit for the church.

[^4]: **C.** *Ymmo*: indeed, correcting upward: the prayer is not merely useless, it is not for them at all. *Mortificare oracionem propriam*: make his own prayer dead, without merit (cf. row *mortificacio*, of a privilege).

[^5]: **C.** *Nec contendo contra volentem defendere*: Wyclif leaves the point to one who holds it. *Dum tamen orator hoc ignoraverit*: so long as the one praying (*orator*) does not know that the man is damned. *Intendens quod sit pro illo*: one who maintains (row *intendere*) that it is for him errs pardonably (*faciliter meretur veniam*). *Iuramentum oratoris*: the bedesman's (row *orator*) oath to say a year's or a term's masses for one soul. *Annaliter temporaliter*: yearly, for a term of time; perhaps a doubled adverb. **T.** *Quod sit* is Loserth's correction; the codices *quo sit* (533.12). **R.** Side-note 533.14.

[^6]: **C.** *Utrimque*: on both sides, the friends who want special prayers and the priests who swear to say them. *Non mediocriter*: not moderately, echoing the canon *Non mediocriter* (530.10). *Postponunt melius*: put the better after (cf. row *proponere*). *Pretenditur orare*: one professes to pray.

[^7]: **S.** The decree of Jerome, Decretum C.13 q.2 c.20 *In presenti seculo*, cited at 527.27; the canon says only that before Christ's tribunal no one can pray for another, not that the saints cannot change God's counsel (LEDGER E22e-1). **C.** *Admitto cronicam*: I accept the chronicle's account of Gregory and Trajan. *Secundum confirmacionem*: so printed, for *secundam*. *Predestinavit … ordinavit*: Trajan's salvation through Gregory's intercession was itself eternally ordained, so the prayer changed nothing in God's counsel. **T.** A and B *sic eternaliter* (533.28). **R.** Side-note 533.24; A in the margin at 533.24: *Nota de Traiano quem Deus per oracionem Gregorii de dampnacione liberavit*.

[^8]: **S.** Romans 10:10, Douay–Rheims (Wyclif omits *enim*). **C.** *Suasum*: shown persuasively. *Gracia predestinacionis* and *gracia secundum presentem iusticiam*: final grace and present grace. *Catholici*: members of the true church. *Solempnitas ritus nostri*: the formal observance of our rite (baptism by water). *Flamine baptizatus*: baptized with the baptism of the Spirit (row *baptismus flaminis*). **R.** Side-note 534.4.

[^9]: **S.** The tradition that Gregory, offered the choice, accepted bodily sickness as the penalty of his prayer for Trajan: *Legenda aurea*, c. 46, *De sancto Gregorio* (ed. Graesse, p. 197); John the Deacon mentions no punishment of Gregory (LEDGER E22e-2). **C.** *Factum Dei in concedendo*: God's act in granting the prayer. *Affeccione singulari*: by a private affection for one man. *Ad sui meritum*: to his merit, the punishment itself being meritorious.

[^10]: **S.** 2 Corinthians 12:7–8: "a sting of my flesh … For which thing thrice I besought the Lord, that it might depart from me" (Douay–Rheims). **C.** *Mereri in volendo … pro illo quod Deus non vult fieri*: Paul merited in asking what God did not grant. **T.** B lacks *II* (534.21). **R.** Side-note 534.18; A in the margin at 534.25: *Augustinus*.

[^11]: **S.** Augustine, *De civitate Dei* 22.2 (Loserth, 534.25: Aug. Opp. VII, 656; LEDGER E22e-3). **C.** The quotation is closed at *fecerit*; the next sentence is taken as Wyclif's summary of the chapter, where Augustine says *ut ipsum dicamus velle, qui ut velint isti facit* (LEDGER E22e-4). *Equivocando de velle*: taking "to will" equivocally. *Modeste*: with restraint. **T.** B *non faciunt* (534.28).
