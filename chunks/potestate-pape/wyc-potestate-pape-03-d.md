# *De potestate pape*, chapter 3, part 4: Peter's faith, the first of his three virtues

## ¶1 · 55.27–58.7

> [p. 55] Quantum ad primum, expeditum est in parte, sed pro secundo notandum quod racio quare Petrus ordinatus fuit a Christo post eum caput ecclesie fuit preeminencia virtutum ad regendum [p. 56] ecclesiam; aliter enim sapiencia patris inprovide eum constituisset ecclesie sue episcopum. Et cum omnes virtutes in genere sint connexe, patet quod Petrus habuit quandam preeminenciam in omni genere virtutum. Tres autem erant virtutes in quibus pertinencius Petrus precellerat, scilicet fides, humilitas, caritas; fides, quam oportet esse petram ecclesie, precellebat in Petro, propter quod ordinavit magister Matth. XVI, 16 quod ad questionem, qua quesivit de se ipso, quem dicunt homines esse filium hominis, Petrus pro omnibus responderet: Tu es, inquit; filius Dei vivi. Ubi orthodoxe confitetur humanitatem in Christo, quia signat illum prophetam promissum patribus vel Messiam. Et secunda pars confitetur Christum Dei filium naturalem, quia aliter impertinens foret confessio. Et inter omnes articulos fidei nullus est ad edificandum ecclesiam Christi pertinencior quam secundum beatum Joh. I. Joh. V, 4 Dei filius vincit mundum: Quis est inquit, qui vincit mundum, nisi qui credit quoniam Ihesus est filius Dei. Nam hoc fundamento habito consequenter credendum, quod omnia, que fecit vel docuit; sunt sine calumpnia possibili a tota ecclesia acceptanda. Ideo audivit Petrus a Domino: Beatus es Symon Baryona, quia caro et sanguis non revelavit tibi, sed pater meus, qui est in celis. Et ex ista fide accepit arras prefecture ecclesie. Et ego dico tibi, inquit, quia tu es Petrus, et super hanc petram edificabo ecclesiam meam. De quo August, super Johannem sermone ultimo sic loquitur: Ecclesia quatitur sed non cadit, quando fundatur super petram, unde Petrus nomen accepit, [p. 57] non enim a Petro petra, sed Petrus a petra, petra autem erat Christus. Et propter istas raciones tenentur superiores prepositi predicare fidem ecclesie, unde Luce XXII, 32: Ego autem rogavi pro te, ut non deficiat fides tua, et tu aliquando conversus confirma fratres tuos. Ecce quomodo Christus exauditus pro sua reverencia singulariter rogavit pro fide Petri; et secundo iniunxit sibi primatum officii et per hoc fundamentum operatus est Petrus singulariter supra ceteros apostolos; per hanc; ut supra dixi, fidem extraxit Petrus gladium et percussit servum pontificis auferendo dextram eius auriculam, ut patet Joh. XVIII; per hanc eciam introivit in monumentum Evangelista, qui prius venerat stante foras, per hanc audito ab Evangelista, quia dominus est, misit se in mare, aliis discipulis venientibus navigio ad littus, ubi Christus stabat, ut patet Joh. ultimo, et sic de aliis superexcessivis operibus, que ex vehemencia sue fidei processerunt. Primum autem allegorizatur a sancto Gregorio, ut patet XXIV, q. I, Si Petrus: Primo inquit, docetur quod aurem in specie habere non debent, quam in ministerio non habebant. Sed bonus dominus ipsi refundit auditum demonstrans et ipsos, si convertantur, posse sanari qui in passione Christi vulnerati sunt; tollit eciam gladio spirituali interiorem auriculam male intelligentis de passione Domini, dum vel prescindit errorem quo putant esse dexterum non pati pro Christo, vel quod eius divinitas paciatur. Per secundum allegorizatur quod Petrus, gentes signans, licet tardius venit ad thesaurum fidei quam Johannes ex virginitate [p. 58] sterilis, signans synagogam, tamen prius plenius introivit copiosius ad credendum in Christum. Per tercium vero signatur anagogice quod imitatores Petri et specialiter martyres in sermone fidei citius veniunt ad Christum stantem in littore eternitatis quam alii navigio, qui arte confessorum prudenter certant diu in vita cum hostibus.

As to the first, it has been dealt with in part; but for the second it is to be noted that the reason why Peter was ordained by Christ [head]{g:caput} of the [church]{g:ecclesia} after him was his preeminence in [virtues]{g:virtus} for ruling [56] the church; for otherwise the wisdom of the Father would have improvidently appointed him bishop of his church.[^1] And since all virtues are connected in their kind, it is clear that Peter had a certain preeminence in every kind of virtue. And there were three virtues in which Peter more fittingly excelled, namely faith, humility, [charity]{g:caritas}. Faith, which must be the rock of the church, excelled in Peter; on account of which the master ordained, Matthew 16:16, that to the question he asked about himself, "Whom do men say that the Son of man is?", Peter should answer for all: "Thou art," he says, "[Christ,] the Son of the living God."[^2] Here he confesses, as the orthodox do, the humanity in Christ, since [the name] signifies that prophet promised to the fathers, or the Messiah. And the second part confesses Christ the natural Son of God, since otherwise the confession would be beside the point. And among all the [articles of faith]{g:articulus-fidei} none is more fitting for building the church of Christ than [that] the Son of God overcomes the world, according to blessed John, 1 John 5:4: "Who is he," he says, "that overcometh the world, but he that believeth that Jesus is the Son of God?"[^3] For with this foundation held, it is consequently to be believed that all things which he did or taught are to be accepted by the whole church without possible [challenge]{g:calumpnia}. Therefore Peter heard from the Lord: "Blessed art thou, Simon Bar-Jona: because flesh and blood hath not revealed it to thee, but my Father who is in heaven." And from this faith he received the earnest of charge over the church: "And I say to thee," he says, "That thou art Peter; and upon this rock I will build my church." Of which Augustine, on John, in the last sermon, speaks thus: "The church is shaken but does not fall, since it is founded upon the rock from which Peter received his name; [57] for the rock is not from Peter, but Peter from the rock; and the rock was Christ."[^4] And for these reasons the higher [superiors]{g:prepositi} are bound to preach the faith of the church; hence Luke 22:32: "But I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not: and thou, being once converted, confirm thy brethren." Behold how Christ, "heard for his reverence", prayed singularly for Peter's faith; and secondly enjoined on him the [primacy]{g:primatus} of office; and by this foundation Peter worked singularly above the other apostles.[^5] By this faith, as I said above, Peter drew the sword and struck the servant of the high priest, cutting off his right ear, as is clear from John 18; by this also he went into the sepulchre, while the Evangelist, who had come first, stood outside; by this, having heard from the Evangelist "that it is the Lord", he cast himself into the sea, while the other disciples came in the ship to the shore where Christ stood, as is clear from the last chapter of John; and so of the other surpassing works which proceeded from the vehemence of his faith.[^6] And the first is allegorized by St Gregory, as is clear from *Causa* 24, question 1, *Si Petrus*: "First," he says, "it is taught that they ought not to have in outward form the ear which they did not have in ministry. But the good Lord restores to him his hearing, showing that they too can be healed, if they are converted, who were wounded in the passion of Christ." He also takes away with the spiritual sword the inner ear of one who understands the Lord's passion wrongly, when he cuts off either the error by which they think it the right thing not to suffer for Christ, or [the error] that his [divinity]{g:divinitas} suffers.[^7] By the second it is allegorized that Peter, signifying the gentiles, although he came later to the treasure of faith than John, barren from [58] virginity, signifying the synagogue, yet went in first, more fully and more abundantly, to believe in Christ. And by the third it is signified anagogically that the imitators of Peter, and especially the martyrs in the word of faith, come more quickly to Christ standing on the shore of eternity than the others in the ship, who by the art of confessors contend prudently and long in life with the enemies.[^8]

## Notes

[^1]: **R/C.** A and B in marg.: *Petrus caput ecclesie quare nominatur*, "why Peter is named head of the church" (55.27). "The first" and "the second" are the first two of the three purposes just stated (55.21–26): the authorities of scripture, and the sense of the church. "Head of the church after him" is said of Peter under Christ, as the chapter's argument has it.

[^2]: **T/S.** Matt. 16:13, 16 (DR). Loserth's print has *Tu es, inquit, filius Dei vivi* without *Christus* (56.14), but the next sentence expounds a first part that names the Messiah, which presupposes the Vulgate's *Tu es Christus*: we supply "[Christ,]" (our emendation). *Pre cellebat* is one word, *precellebat*.

[^3]: **T/S.** 1 John 5:5 (DR); Loserth gives 5:4; the manuscripts read *I. Joh. IV* and *quia quam* (56.19). The construal of *quam secundum beatum Joh.* is ours: the article meant is "the Son of God overcomes the world". B in marg.: *Excellencia Petri in tribus virtutibus* (56.20).

[^4]: **S.** Matt. 16:17–18 (DR). *Arras prefecture*, "the earnest of charge": a pledge of the office to come. Augustine on John, "the last sermon": *Tractatus in Iohannem* 124.5; Loserth gives *Opera* IV, 1207 (LEDGER P03d-1, editor; to be checked). 1 Cor. 10:4 (DR "and the rock was Christ").

[^5]: **T/S.** Luke 22:32 (DR); B reads *unde* for *ut* (57.5). Heb. 5:7 (DR "was heard for his reverence"). B omits *Petri* (57.8).

[^6]: **T/S/C.** John 18:10 (the reference *Joh.* is Loserth's; the manuscripts omit it, 57.13); John 20:4–6; John 21:7–8 (DR "It is the Lord … cast himself into the sea"). *Evangelista … stante* is read as an ablative absolute: John stood outside the tomb.

[^7]: **T/S/C.** *Decretum* C. 24 q. 1 c. 17 (*Si Petrus*), under Gregory's name; Loserth gives "Cap. XVII" (57.22; LEDGER P03d-2, editor; modern form ours; to be checked). A reads *allegorizant* (57.21). *Sanari*: the manuscripts read *saciari* (57.26); Loserth's print is translated. Where the quotation ends is Loserth's: the italics stop at *habebant*. *Dexterum*, "the right thing", plays on Malchus's right ear. The subject of *tollit* is Peter as preacher.

[^8]: **S.** The allegory of John 20:4–6 follows Gregory, *Homilia in evangelia* 22 (LEDGER P03d-3, to-verify): John, who came first, the synagogue; Peter, who entered first, the gentiles. John 21:4–8: Christ on the shore, the others in the boat. *Martyres in sermone fidei* may also be construed "come in the word of faith".
