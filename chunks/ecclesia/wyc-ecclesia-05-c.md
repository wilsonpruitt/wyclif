# *De ecclesia*, chapter 5, part 3: squaring the circle; the body of the devil

## ¶1 · 100.13–102.6

> [p. 100] Dicitur tercio quod nisi christianus sciverit quadrare circulum, non est de membris ecclesie. Quadratura quidem circuli est scibilis, ut patet in Predicamentis, sed fuit in tempore Aristotelis nondum scita. Constat quidem quod figura tripunctalis ad instar trinitatis habet tria puncta supposita quorum nullum excedit reliquum sicut nec latera vel anguli, sed habent figuram et angulos utrobique. Correspondenter mistice tota trinitas personarum est parilis et nocio personarum ut latera nec non et proprietates earum ut anguli, et communem habent essenciam ubique plenariam ut figuram. Et sicut omnes illi tres anguli valent duos rectos, sic omnes tres persone divine valent ydemptice commune principium et commune principiatum. Si ergo in medio trium punctorum sit punctus positus, gerens similitudinem essencie que secundum racionem communicabilitatis distinguitur a personis, tunc figurat illud simul existens quadratum et circulus Deum nostrum qui secundum unum de viginti quatuor prophetis est circulus intelligibilis cuius centrum est ubique et circumferencia [p. 101] nusquam; iste autem simul circulus intelligibilis et quadratum secundum multiplicem racionem miserendi ecclesie constituit quadratum sensibile, quando in plenitudine temporis compaginavit quatuor elementa in quattuor humores ac quattuor complexiones et assumpsit plenum hominem quem sic fecit. Tunc enim sensibile quadratum humanitatis et circulus intelligibilis divinitatis factum est utrumque natura idem suppositum verbi Dei. Et istam sentenciam inculcat sanctus et devotus doctor magister Johannes de Howdan, alloquens amorem qui incarnavit filium in hec verba: Artem nosti, quadrantem circulum quem quadrasti, carnis quadrangulum spere Dei dans per miraculum sauciatum sanando seculum. Isti circulo oportet hominem inniti et secundum quadraturam quatuor virtutum cardinalium incorporari, si debeat esse membrum ecclesie dicente veritate Johannis XV° 4: Sicut palmes non potest ferre fructum nisi manserit in vite, sic nec vos nisi in me manseritis. Oportet enim christianum esse Deo insorptum per amorem, sic quod tota terrestritas instar ferri vel carbonis candentis pro zelo honoris Dei, honore seculi postposito, sit ignita; in cuius signum apostoli ex doctrina Christi Luce XXII° vocarunt se in suarum epistolarum iniciis servos Dei et domini Jesu Christi, ut per hoc reprimatur in eis fastus vel dignitatis apparencia, sed in dominum suum convertatur tota dignitatis tendencia. Sed hodie prepostere de dignitate, primatu et reverencia servi fit tota intencio, de honore vero Christi vel suo contemptu [p. 102] qui stat in legis suo observancia vel despectu non fit scrutinium; quod est signum evidentissimum quod tales superbi prepositi sunt extra Christum et suam ecclesiam, membra regis superbie que suam quadraturam ostentant in figura mundi, figuram autem substancie divine postponendo occultant.

The answer is, thirdly, that unless a Christian knows how to square the circle, he is not of the
members of the [church]{g:ecclesia}. The squaring of the circle is indeed knowable, as is clear in
the *Categories*, but in the time of Aristotle it was not yet known.[^1] It is evident indeed that a
figure of three points, after the likeness of the Trinity, has three points set under it, of which
none exceeds the rest, as neither do the sides or the angles; but they have the figure and the angles
alike on each side. Correspondingly, in a mystical sense, the whole trinity of persons is equal, and
the notion of the persons [is] as the sides, and their properties too as the angles; and they have a
common essence, everywhere full, as the figure. And just as all those three angles are equal to two
right angles, so all three divine persons are, identically, equal to a common principle and a common
[term that is] from a principle.[^2] If, then, a point is set in the middle of the three points,
bearing the likeness of the essence, which by the account of communicability is distinguished from
the persons, then that [figure], existing at once as square and circle, figures our God, who
according to one of the twenty-four prophets is an intelligible circle whose centre is everywhere and
whose circumference [101] nowhere.[^3] And he, at once intelligible circle and square, according to
the manifold reason of having mercy on the church, constituted a [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} square
when, in the fulness of time, he joined the four elements into the four humours and the four
complexions, and assumed a full man, whom he thus made. For then the perceptible square of the
humanity and the intelligible circle of the [divinity]{g:divinitas} became, each [being] a nature,
the same suppositum of the Word of God.[^4] And this [teaching]{g:sentencia} the holy and devout
doctor, master John of Howden, drives home, addressing the love that incarnated the Son, in these
words: "You know the art, the squaring circle which you have squared, giving by a miracle the
quadrangle of flesh to the sphere of God, healing the wounded world."[^5] On this circle a man must
lean, and be incorporated according to the squaring of the four cardinal virtues, if he is to be a
member of the church, as the Truth says in John 15:4: "As the branch cannot bear fruit, unless it
abide in the vine, so neither can you, unless you abide in me."[^6] For a Christian must be absorbed
into God by love, so that his whole earthiness, like iron or a glowing coal, be set on fire for zeal
for the honour of God, the honour of the world being put after; in sign of which the apostles, from
the [doctrine]{g:doctrina} of Christ in Luke 22, called themselves at the beginnings of their
epistles servants of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ, so that by this pride or the show of dignity
might be repressed in them, but the whole tendency of dignity be turned towards their lord.[^7] But
today, preposterously, the whole concern is with the dignity, primacy and reverence of the servant,
while into the honour of Christ, or the contempt of him, [102] which consists in the observance or
the despising of his law, no inquiry is made; which is a most evident sign that such proud
[superiors]{g:prepositi} are outside Christ and his church, members of the king of pride, who display
their squareness in the figure of the world, but hide the figure of the divine substance by putting
it after.[^8]

## ¶2 · 102.7–102.12

> [p. 102] Tercio arguitur contra dicta de corpore ecclesie per hoc quod pari evidencia qua illa foret unum corpus, et congregacio prescitorum constitueret unum corpus, consequens falsum, quia nec corpus naturale nec misticum, eo quod non foret forma uniens, nisi foret peccatum, illud autem non continuat sed seiungit.

Thirdly, [it is argued, by appeal to]{g:arguitur-per} this, against what has been said about the body
of the church: that by the same [evidence]{g:evidencia} by which it would be one body, the
congregation of the [foreknown]{g:prescitus} too would constitute one body; the
[consequent]{g:consequens} is false, since [it would be] neither a natural nor a mystical body,
because there would be no uniting form unless it were sin; now that does not join but
separates.[^9]

## ¶3 · 102.13–104.17

> [p. 102] Hic dicitur ex fide scripture quod omnes presciti constituunt unum corpus. Christus enim dicit Joh. VIII° 44, Judeis et principaliter sacerdotibus et phariseis qui dicebantur esse capitales ecclesie: Vos ex patre diabolo estis et desideria patris vestri vultis facere. Ex quo patet quod oportet esse unam generacionem que fuit mala in vulgaribus, peior in secularibus prepositis, sed pessima in prelatis, sicut generacio iustorum secundum tres gradus oppositos eis proporcionaliter correspondet. Si ergo sit una generacio perversorum, oportet quod sit unus iniquus homo habens partes que sunt membra diaboli, et cum non potest esse membrum vel caput nisi in comparacione ad totum corpus integrum, manifestum est quod est unum corpus diaboli, sicut est unum corpus Christi. Ubi autem corpus Christi vocatur corpus misticum propter misterium celestis coniugii inter Christum et ecclesiam Christi, corpus diaboli non est sic misticum sed obscurum, cum sic coniungi diabolo [p. 103] non sonat directe in misterium sed flagellum, et sic corpus diaboli habet esse naturale, cum secundum Augustinum in libro De Natura Boni... omne malum oportet fundari in bono: ergo omne malum moris fundatur in bono nature. Nec est inconveniens sed consonum modo loquendi sanctorum et vulgi vocare quamcunque creatam substanciam ex partibus quantitativis sive materialibus sive formalibus compositam verum corpus, sic enim vocatur aggregatum ex spiritualibus et indivisibilibus legibus corpus iuris. Tale ergo esse naturale habet quecunque aggregata substancia, unita in suo communi ut specie vel genere, et preter esse naturale habet racionalis creatura esse beatitudinis, virtutis vel vicii, ut est de membris Christi atque diaboli. Et loquendo de tali unione vel esse corporis negat Augustinus IIIª De Doctrina Christiana, XXXII° cap., quod ex membris Christi atque diaboli sit unum corpus compositum. Et ulterius quando tangitur de forma uniente corpus diaboli, dicitur quod est dare formam extrinsecam et deformitatem intrinsecam. Forma autem extrinseca est presciencia Dei eterna qua scit et ordinat omnes tales constringi ad penam perpetuam, deformitas autem intrinseca est finalis inobediencia vel superbia, contumacia quam sancti vocant culpam finalis impenitencie vel peccatum in spiritum sanctum. Et sic idem peccatum continuat et seiungit. Continuat enim membra diaboli, constringens eos in tartaris in sua malicia, et segregat eos a consorcio beatorum, sicut calor dissolvens mixtum congregat omogena, cum facit quodlibet elementum [p. 104] mixti dissoluti petere locum suum, sed segregat ethrogena, cum dissolvit compleccionem armonicam, resolvendo partes mixti in suas naturas simplices separatas. Oportet enim in die iudicii ex contrarietate frigoris corporis diaboli et caloris caritatis corporis Christi secundum legem forme ultime corpus bipartitum dissolvi, quando partes leves advolabunt cum capite suo quod est ignis consumens ad mansiones naturales in superis. Partes autem plumbee affeccione terrestri descendunt ad inferos, et sic duo hec nedum sunt duo corpora, sed due persone (ut tetigi cap. XLIII° tercii libri); de quo satis est adducere unum testimonium beati Gregorii, IV° Moralium, cap. XI°. Sicut, inquit, redemptor noster una persona est cum congregacione bonorum (ipse namque caput est corporis et nos huius capitis corpus), ita antiquus hostis una persona est cum cuncta colleccione reproborum.

To this the answer is, [from the faith of scripture]{g:ex-fide}, that all the foreknown constitute
one body. For Christ says in John 8:44, to the Jews, and principally to the
[priests]{g:sacerdos} and Pharisees, who were said to be the chiefs of the church: "You are of your
father the devil: and the desires of your father you will do." From which it is clear that there must
be one generation, which was bad in [the common people]{g:vulgares}, worse in the secular superiors,
but worst in the [prelates]{g:prelatus}, just as the generation of the just corresponds to them
proportionally according to three opposite degrees.[^10] If, then, there is one generation of the
perverse, there must be one wicked man having parts that are members of the devil; and since there
cannot be a member or a [head]{g:caput} except in relation to a whole, integral body, it is manifest
that there is one body of the devil, just as there is one body of Christ. But whereas the body of
Christ is called the [mystical body]{g:corpus-christi-misticum} on account of the mystery of the
heavenly marriage between Christ and the church of Christ, the body of the devil is not mystical in
this way but obscure, since to be so joined to the devil [103] does not sound directly of mystery but
of the scourge;[^11] and so the body of the devil has natural [being]{g:esse}, since according to
Augustine in the book *On the Nature of the Good* … every evil must be founded in a good: therefore
every evil of conduct is founded in a good of nature.[^12] Nor is it an
[absurdity]{g:inconveniens}, but in harmony with the manner of speaking of the saints and of the
common folk, to call any created substance composed of quantitative parts, whether material or
formal, a true body; for thus the aggregate of spiritual and indivisible laws is called the body of
[law]{g:ius}. Such natural being, therefore, any aggregate substance has that is united in what it
has in common, as in a species or genus; and besides natural being the rational creature has the
being of blessedness, of virtue or of vice, as is the case with the members of Christ and of the
devil.[^13] And speaking of such union, or being of a body, Augustine denies in *On Christian
Doctrine* III, chapter 32, that one body is composed of the members of Christ and of the devil.[^14]
And further, when the form uniting the body of the devil is touched on, the answer is that there is
to be given an extrinsic form and an intrinsic deformity. And the extrinsic form is God's eternal
[foreknowledge]{g:presciencia}, by which he knows and ordains that all such are bound to perpetual
punishment; and the intrinsic deformity is final disobedience, or pride, a contumacy which the saints
call the guilt of final impenitence, or the sin against the Holy Spirit.[^15] And so the same sin
joins and separates. For it joins the members of the devil, binding them in hell in their malice, and
segregates them from the fellowship of the blessed; just as heat, dissolving a mixed body, gathers
together things of one kind, since it makes each element [104] of the dissolved mixture seek its own
place, but segregates things of different kinds, since it dissolves the harmonious complexion,
resolving the parts of the mixture into their simple, separated natures.[^16] For on the day of
judgement, from the contrariety of the cold of the body of the devil and the heat of charity of the
body of Christ, the bipartite body must, according to the law of the final form, be dissolved, when
the light parts will fly up with their head, which is a consuming fire, to their natural mansions
above. But the leaden parts, by earthly affection, go down to the lower regions; and so these two are
not only two bodies, but two persons (as I touched on in chapter 43 of the third book);[^17] on which
it is enough to adduce one testimony of blessed Gregory, *Moralia* IV, chapter 11: "Just as," he
says, "our Redeemer is one person with the congregation of the good (for he is the head of the body,
and we the body of this head), so the old enemy is one person with the whole collection of the
reprobate."[^18]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 100.13: A Christian must know how to square the circle.
- 100.27: God is said to be a circle with its centre everywhere and its circumference nowhere.
- 101.3: He has combined the four elements on man and has assumed the square thus made.
- 101.20: The Christian should lose himself in God by love.
- 102.7: 3rd objection: If the predestinate form one body, so must the foreknown.
- 102.13: Answer: The foreknown do form one body
- 102.24: of which the devil is the head.
- 103.6: A body may be composed either of material or formal parts.
- 103.19: The body of the devil is united by an outward form (God's foreknowledge) and an inward deformity (the sin of final impenitence).
- 104.4: At the day of judgment the parts of these two bodies (of Christ and the devil) will be finally separated.

## Notes

[^1]: **S/C/R.** The third answer to the second objection (96.20), after "first" (98.23) and
    "secondly" (99.1). A margin *Nota de quadratura circuli*, B margin *Quadratura circuli* (100.13).
    Aristotle, *Categories* 7, says that the squaring of the circle, if it is knowable, is not yet known:
    the identification is from memory and to be checked (LEDGER E05c-1); Loserth gives no reference here
    (his note on *Predicamentis* belongs to 104.27).

[^2]: **C/T.** *Figura tripunctalis*: the triangle of three points; *utrobique*, "on each side",
    i.e. the figure and the angles belong alike to all three. *Nocio personarum*: the notions
    (paternity, filiation and the rest) by which the persons are known apart. *Principiatum*: what is
    from a principle; the persons together are one principle and one term from a principle. Loserth's
    foot has "17. In codd.: quod figuratur", which we cannot place with certainty; the print is
    translated (see Latin doubts).

[^3]: **S/T.** "One of the twenty-four prophets": the *Book of the Twenty-Four Philosophers*, whose
    second proposition defines God as an infinite sphere whose centre is everywhere and circumference
    nowhere; the identification and wording are from memory and to be checked (LEDGER E05c-2). Loserth:
    "This passage is not to be found in the prophets." A A₁ lack *cuius — intelligibilis* (100.32–101.1),
    an eye-skip between the two *circulus intelligibilis*.

[^4]: **T/C.** A A₁ *racionum miserendi ecclesie*, "the manifold reasons"; B *miterendi*; B *que sic
    fecit* (101.2–6). *In plenitudine temporis*: Gal. 4:4, DR "when the fulness of the time was come".
    The four humours and complexions answer to the four elements: the man assumed is the "perceptible
    square". *Utrumque natura*: each, being a nature, became one suppositum (person) of the Word; A
    *utramque* (101.8). B margin *Circulus quadratus*.

[^5]: **S/T/C.** Codd. *magister J. de Howdan*, expanded by Loserth; A margin *J. Howdan* (101.10).
    Loserth: John of Howden (d. 1275), chaplain of Eleanor, mother of Edward I (LEDGER E05c-3). The poem
    the lines come from is to be checked (same row). *Quadrantem*: we take it with *circulum*, "the
    squaring circle"; it can also be read "you know the art, squaring the circle you have squared". *Spere
    Dei*: the sphere of God, the divinity as circle.

[^6]: **S/T.** "The Truth": Christ (John 14:6). Codd. *facere fructum*; Loserth corrected to *ferre*
    from the Vulgate (101.19), and the print is translated. Wyclif lacks the Vulgate's *a semetipso*
    (DR "of itself"). B margin *In ecclesia esse etc.* (101.17).

[^7]: **S/T/C.** *Insorptum*: absorbed (*insorbere*); so the print on the plate. B *quod nos*
    (101.21), place uncertain. Luke 22:26, DR "he that is the greater among you, let him become as the
    younger". The epistle openings: e.g. Jas. 1:1, DR "James, the servant of God and of our Lord Jesus
    Christ".

[^8]: **S/T.** "The king of pride": Job 41:25, DR "he is king over all the children of pride",
    alluded to. *Figura mundi*: cf. 1 Cor. 7:31, DR "the fashion of this world passeth away"; the play
    is on *figura* and *quadratura* above. A *figuram figuram* (102.5).

[^9]: **R/T/C.** A margin (102.7): *Malorum congregacio est unum corpus naturale sicut bonorum est
    unum corpus misticum*. A *continat* (102.12). *Continuare*: to make continuous, "join", the
    opposite of *seiungere*. The foreknown are those God foreknows will be damned (GLOSSARY).

[^10]: **T/R.** A margin *Responsio* (102.13); B margin, later hand, *Ecclesia malignancium per
    consequens est unum corpus* (102.14; cf. Ps. 25:5); A margin *Triplex generacio mala*. *Capitales*:
    the chief men, echoing *caput*. A A₁ *correspondent* (102.22).

[^11]: **S/T.** B lacks *corpus* (102.26) and *Christi* (102.30); B *cum coniungi* (102.31). The
    "mystery" of the marriage: Eph. 5:32, DR "This is a great sacrament: but I speak in Christ and in
    the church" (Vulgate *sacramentum*, Wyclif *misterium*).

[^12]: **S/T.** Loserth: a lacuna in the codd. after *boni* (103.3), and the sentence is not extant in

[^13]: **C/T/R.** *Inconveniens* here in its argumentative sense (GLOSSARY CONTEXTUAL). *Corpus iuris*:

    uncertain. A margin *Quid est corpus*; B margin, later hand: *Ex esse virtutis (vel) vicii unum

[^14]: **S.** Augustine, *De doctrina christiana* III.32, on the second rule of Tyconius, "on the

[^15]: **C/R.** A margin *Nota de forma uniente malos in corpus unum* (103.19). The foreknowledge is

[^17]: **T/S/C.** *Caritatis* (104.5): the print (plate); the OCR had *cantatis*. For *caloris*: A *calore*, A₁ *calce*, B

[^18]: **S/T.** Gregory, *Moralia* IV, chapter 11 (codd. *cap. IX*; PL 75:647B–C, cap. XI [Vet. XIV]; Loserth: *Opp.
    Greg.* I, p. 112): Wyclif lacks *isdem* and adds *inquit* (LEDGER E05c-8); A margin *Gregorius* (104.13); B *est cunta* (*sic*, 104.17). *Reproborum*: the
