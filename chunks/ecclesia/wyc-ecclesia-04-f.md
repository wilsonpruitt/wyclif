# *De ecclesia*, chapter 4, part 6: the gloss's three replies; knowing our mother by faith

## ¶1 · 88.31–90.8

> [p. 88] Unde glosa obicit in contrarium per duo capitula, videlicet XXIIIª, q. IV, cap. Forte et cap. Hec [p. 89] autem vita, ubi vere dicitur quod quamdiu sumus in hoc mundo, non deficiet nobis ruga venialis. Et solvit tripliciter: primo quod ecclesia quandoque large sumitur, ut granum et paleam complectatur, ut in allegatis capitulis, quandoque stricte pro puro grano, ut XXXIIª q. IV: Recurrat, ubi sic scribitur (ut supra tetigi): Itaque, inquit Augustinus, sive intus versari videantur sive aperte foris sint, quod caro est caro est, quod palea est palea est. Et semper ab illius ecclesie que sine macula est vel ruga unitate decisus. Vult iste sanctus dicere (ut patet ex dictis) quod impossibile est prescitum esse membrum sancte matris ecclesie vel predestinatum a maternitate illa deficere, quomodocunque secundum presentem iusticium varientur. Secunda responsio glose est quod aliud est esse de ecclesia et aliud esse in ecclesia, ut patet XXIIIª q. IV: Vasis ire et supra exposui. Tercia responsio distinguit de quadruplici habitudine viantis ad sanctam matrem ecclesiam. Quidam autem sunt in ecclesia nomine et re ut obedientes catholici, quidam nec re nec nomine ut pagani, quidam nomine tantum ut presciti ypocrite, et quidam re licet videantur nomine esse foris ut predestinati, quos satrape videntur in facie ecclesie condempnare. Ecce decretorum concordia sentencie supradicte. Unde videtur nimis blasphema heresis papam vel collegium suum credere quod in quantum eliguntur in illo officio tenent primatum tocius ecclesie. Oportet enim quod sint filii imitacionis in moribus parentum quorum [p. 90] pretendunt se gerere vicariam potestatem. Aliter enim sunt de generacione pessima meretricis diaboli. Generacio enim sunt Christi, neque constat ex sanguinibus ut in propagacione naturali neque ex voluntate carnis ut in electis nude ex affeccione carnali neque in electis pro pompa seculi que nascitur ex voluntate viri, sed ex Deo nati sunt tamquam filii imitacionis per graciam adoptantem et tunc ex condicione generis patrizabunt.

Hence the [gloss]{g:glosa} [objects, by appeal to]{g:obicitur per} two chapters, to the contrary:
namely 23 q. 4, the chapter *Forte* and the chapter *Hec [89] autem vita*, where it is truly said
that as long as we are in this world the wrinkle of venial [sin] will not be lacking to us.[^1] And
it resolves [the objection] in three ways. First, that the [church]{g:ecclesia} is sometimes taken
broadly, so as to embrace the grain and the chaff, as in the chapters cited; sometimes strictly, for
the pure grain, as at 32 q. 4, *Recurrat*, where it is written thus (as I touched on above):
"Therefore," says Augustine, "whether they seem to dwell within or are openly outside, what is flesh
is flesh, what is chaff is chaff. And [he is] always cut off from the unity of that church which is
without spot or wrinkle." This saint means (as is clear from what has been said) that it is
impossible for one [foreknown]{g:prescitus} to be a member of holy mother church, or for one
[predestinate]{g:predestinatus} to fall away from that motherhood, however they may vary according
to present justice.[^2] The second reply of the gloss is that it is one thing to be of the church and
another to be in the church, as is clear at 23 q. 4, *Vasis ire*, and as I expounded above.[^3] The
third reply distinguishes a fourfold [condition]{g:habitudo} of [one on the road]{g:viantes} in
relation to holy mother church. And some are in the church in name and in fact, as obedient
catholics; some neither in fact nor in name, as pagans; some in name only, as foreknown hypocrites;
and some in fact, although in name they seem to be outside, as the predestinate whom
[satraps]{g:satrapa} seem to condemn in the face of the church.[^4] Behold the agreement of the
decrees with the [teaching]{g:sentencia} stated above. Hence it seems a too blasphemous heresy for
the [pope]{g:papa} or his college to believe that, inasmuch as they are elected to that office, they
hold the primacy of the whole church. For they must be sons of imitation in the manners of the
parents whose vicarious power they [90] claim to bear. For otherwise they are of the most wicked
generation of the devil's harlot.[^5] For they are the generation of Christ: it is neither of blood,
as in natural propagation, nor of the will of the flesh, as in those elected merely out of carnal
affection, nor, in those elected for the pomp of the world, of that which is born of the will of
man; but they are born of God, as sons of imitation, through adopting grace, and then by the
condition of their kind they will take after their father.[^6]

## ¶2 · 90.9–92.5

> [p. 90] Tercio dicitur ad obiectum de noticia matris nostre quod satis cognoscitur a fideli: est enim dare noticiam distinctam et confusam et utraque potest subdividi, quoad personam vel circumstanciam, quoad sensum vel intellectum, quoad fidem vel racionem. Sic enim cognosco caput ecclesie et tamen ipso ostenso michi nude in specie humana nescirem ipsum ab alio discernere. Unde, cum fide ambulamus et vivimus, consonum est quod nos quasi modo geniti infantes confuse in velamento fidei habeamus noticiam matris nostre: sic enim nec prescitos reprehendimus nec predestinatos extollimus viciose, sed correspondenter ad noticiam tam membra matris nostre quam infideles sibi et nobis inimicos diligimus; illos autem quos videmus vivere secundum legem Dei reputamus esse filios regni, et sic de aliis habemus maiorem evidenciam, de aliis minorem. De illis tamen nunc credimus quod sunt membra matris nostre vel meretricis opposite plus quam fide. Unde, quesito a me de quacunque persona vel populo conversante nobiscum utrum sit membrum matris nostre, sic quod videndo ipsum videamus parcialiter matrem nostram, dico quod ego ignoro sed reputo vel suppono ex modo conversandi sic vel sic, nec est inconveniens sed consonum statui vie quod ignoremus [p. 91] et nesciamus in particulari illud quod cognoscimus in communi; et illa scimus demonstracione intellectus limitati per fidem quod hec est mater nostra, licet demonstracio sensualis hoc non certificet. Unde neminem conversantem nobiscum debemus absolute contempnere sed vicia, quia ignoramus si in hoc despicimus matrem nostram et omnino sponsum ecclesie. Et ita certi sumus quod quidquid bene agimus secundum legem sponsi, in hoc honoramus et proficimus matri nostre. Nec est difficile scire voluntatem sponsi, quia ex fide cognoscimus omne factum naturaliter vel meritorie fieri secundum beneplacitum voluntatis divine. Et sic possumus facillime voluntatem divinam cognoscere in effectu, et ita cum omnes debemus esse domestici fidei et sic de familia huius coniugii, debemus regulariter honorem sponsi et profectum sponse utrobique primo intendere, sicut exemplificat, Hugo De Sacramentis, libro II°, parte IIª, secundo capitulo, ex textu apostoli Iª. Cor. XII°. Quamvis enim visio subiectatur in oculo, ipse tamen videt prius toti corpori quam sibi ipsi, et ita de quolibet membro humani corporis in tantum quod naturaliter membrum ignobilius ut brachium exponitur ad mutilacionem pro membro nobiliori ut capite, et quelibet pars naturalis integri intendit principaliter et finaliter conservacionem et bene esse suo toti. Et ita debet quilibet christianus quoad sanctam matrem ecclesiam, nullum membrum contrariando alteri sed iuvando; unde intendens prepostere proprietati commodi aspirat implicite secundum luciferinam superbiam ad honorem Deo proprium qui constituit universitates creaturarum que gracia sui finaliter ordinantur, quelibet autem pars unius tocius [p. 92] creati debet plus esse gracia sui tocius quam gracia sui ipsius. Et sic omnes creature et singule sunt finaliter propter Deum: et hec est racio, quare religio christiana ordinatur amare communi vivendo mentaliter in communi. Unde Christus non potuit civiliter dominare.

Thirdly, the answer to the [objection]{g:objectum} about the knowledge of [our mother]{g:mater nostra}
is that she is sufficiently known by the faithful: for there is distinct and
[indistinct]{g:confusus} knowledge, and each can be subdivided: as to the person or the
circumstance, as to sense or understanding, as to faith or reason. For thus I know the
[head]{g:caput} of the church, and yet, were he shown to me barely in human form, I should not know
how to tell him from another.[^7] Hence, since we walk and live by faith, it is fitting that we, "as
newborn babes", should have the knowledge of our mother indistinctly, under the veil of faith: for
so we neither rebuke the foreknown nor extol the predestinate viciously, but, in correspondence with
our knowledge, we love both the members of our mother and the [unbelievers]{g:infidelis} who are
enemies to her and to us; and those whom we see live according to the law of God we reckon to be
"the children of the [kingdom]{g:regnum}", and so of some we have greater [evidence]{g:evidencia},
of others less. Yet of them we now believe that they are members of our mother, or of the opposed
harlot, more than by faith.[^8] Hence, when I am asked of any person or people whatever living among
us whether it is a member of our mother, such that in seeing it we partly see our mother, I say that
I am ignorant, but I reckon or suppose, from the manner of living, this way or that; nor is it an
[absurdity]{g:inconveniens}, but in keeping with the state of the [way]{g:via}, that we should [be
ignorant of]{g:ignorare} [91] and not know in particular that which we know in general; and we know
these things, by a demonstration of the understanding limited by faith, that this is our mother,
although a demonstration of sense does not make it certain.[^9] Hence we ought not absolutely to
despise anyone living among us, but [their] vices, since we are ignorant whether in this we despise
our mother, and altogether the [bridegroom]{g:sponsus} of the church. And so we are certain that
whatever we do well according to the law of the bridegroom, in this we honour our mother and profit
her. Nor is it difficult to know the will of the bridegroom, since we know from faith that
everything done comes about, naturally or meritoriously, according to the good pleasure of the
divine will. And so we can very easily know the divine will in its effect; and so, since we all ought
to be "of the household of the faith", and so of the [household]{g:familia} of this marriage, we
ought as a rule to aim first, in both respects, at the honour of the bridegroom and the profit of the
bride, as Hugh exemplifies, *On the Sacraments*, book 11 [read 2], part 2, the second chapter, from
the text of the apostle, 1 Corinthians 12.[^10] For although vision has its subject in the eye, the
eye nevertheless sees for the whole body before [it sees] for itself; and so of every member of the
human body, so far that by nature a less noble member, such as the arm, is exposed to mutilation for
a nobler member, such as the head; and every part of a natural whole aims principally and finally at
the preservation and well-being of its whole. And so ought every Christian [to stand] toward holy
mother church, no member opposing another but helping; hence one who aims preposterously at the
ownness of advantage aspires implicitly, after Luciferian pride, to the honour proper to God, who
established the universes of creatures, which are ordered finally for his sake; and every part of
one [92] created whole ought to be more for the sake of its whole than for its own sake.[^11] And so
all creatures, and each one, are finally for the sake of God; and this is the reason why the
Christian religion is ordained to love in common, living in mind in common. Hence Christ could not
[exercise]{g:dominari} civil lordship.[^12]

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** The gloss objects to the canon of 88.21 (*De pen.* D. 1 c. 70; 04-e n. 17) from two
    canons: Decretum C. 23 q. 4 c. 11 (*Forte*) and c. 15 (*Hec autem vita*) (Loserth: *causa XXIII,
    q. IV, capp. XI and XV*; the modern form is ours) (LEDGER E04f-1). *Ruga venialis*: the wrinkle of
    venial sin, as in the gloss at 88.25.

[^2]: **S/T/G.** Decretum C. 32 q. 4 c. 2, *Recurrat* (Loserth: *ibid., XXXII, q. IV, cap. II*; the
    modern form is ours) (LEDGER E04f-2); it opens with Augustine, *In Ioh. tr.* 11.8, but the sentence
    *Itaque sive intus … caro est caro est … palea est* is his *De baptismo* I.17.26 (PL 43), which
    Gratian reuses (*divisus*; Wyclif *decisus*) (LEDGER E04f-3). A margin *Triplex solucio* (89.3).  B in the margin *sint quod caro est,
    quod palea est* (89.5). *Decisus*: the excerpt breaks off, and the singular participle has no
    subject; "[he is]" is supplied. *Ut supra tetigi*: earlier in this work. *Prescitus*: one God
    foreknows will be damned (GLOSSARY §A). *Iusticium* (89.14–15, *iusti-|cium*) is printed so (plate),
    for *iusticiam* (so 88.30); translated "justice".

[^3]: **S/R.** Decretum C. 23 q. 4 c. 23, *Vasis ire* (Loserth; the modern form is ours) (LEDGER
    E04f-4). A *Vasis enim* (89.17). A margin: *Ecclesia Christi sponsa. Nota* (89.14); *De ecclesia
    esse, in ecclesia esse, quid est* (89.16). *Supra exposui*: earlier in this work.

[^4]: **C/T.** *Habitudo*: here the condition of a person as it relates him to the church (GLOSSARY
    §B). *Viantis*: the participle, "one on the road" (GLOSSARY §A, *viantes*). *Autem* opens the
    division ("And"). B lacks *et — nomine* (89.20). *Satrape*: church-court officials wielding
    delegated power (GLOSSARY §B); *in facie ecclesie*, publicly, before the church.

[^5]: **R/T/C.** A margin (89.26): *Conclusio. Eleccio pape vel cardinalium non facit eos primatum
    tenere in ecclesia sed imitacio sancta*, "The election of the pope or of the cardinals does not make
    them hold the primacy in the church, but holy imitation." *Filii imitacionis*: sons by imitation,
    not by election; kept literal because 90.7 returns to it. *Aliterenimp* (90.1) stands so, an OCR
    scar for *Aliter enim*; the stray *p* is not translated. *Meretrix diaboli*: the harlot set against
    the bride (cf. 90.26, *meretricis opposite*).

[^6]: **S/C/T.** John 1:13, DR "Who are born, not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the
    will of man, but of God", whose clauses Wyclif glosses one by one. The *electi nude ex affeccione
    carnali* are those raised [to office] merely for kinship, the *electi pro pompa seculi* those raised
    for worldly show. *Generacio enim sunt Christi*: the subject is not expressed; we take it as those
    who truly are Christ's, the "sons of imitation", not the pope and college as such. B lacks *sunt*
    (90.2); A margin *Generacio Dei* (90.2); A *patrissabunt* (90.8). *Patrizare*: to take after one's
    father.

[^7]: **T/C.** A margin *De noticia* (90.9). *Si cenim* (about 90.12) stands so, an OCR scar; we read
    *Sic enim*, "For thus". The other repair, *Si enim*, "For if", leaves the sentence without a main
    clause. The *not* after *discernere* (about 90.15) is a fragment of the English side-note at 90.16
    ("Knowledge not going …"), not Wyclif's, and is not translated. *Noticia distincta / confusa*:
    knowledge of a thing in its parts / in the whole (GLOSSARY §C). The example: Christ the head is
    known by faith, not by sight.

[^8]: **S/T.** 2 Cor. 5:7, DR "For we walk by faith", alluded to. 1 Pet. 2:2: Wyclif *quasi modo geniti
    infantes*, the Vulgate *sicut modo geniti*; DR "As newborn babes". Matt. 13:38, DR "the children of
    the kingdom". A margin (90.19): *Noticiam qualem habemus de ecclesia et de partibus eius hic in
    via*. *De illis tamen nunc credimus … plus quam fide* (90.25–26): Loserth's foot, *Hic locus
    corruptus esse videtur; fortasse legendum: tamen non credimus*. The print construes and is
    translated: what we believe of particular persons goes beyond what faith gives, as reckoning (cf.
    *reputo vel suppono*, 90.29). On Loserth's conjecture: "yet we do not believe of them … more than by
    faith". B *iidem* (90.26).

[^9]: **C/T.** A margin *Questio* (90.27), *Responsio* (90.31); B *Unde queso* (90.27).
    *Inconveniens*: here "absurdity", the logical sense (GLOSSARY §C, CONTEXTUAL): nothing absurd
    follows from our ignorance. *Conversante, conversandi*: "living among us", "manner of living" (cf.
    *conversacio*, GLOSSARY §D). *Demonstracio*: here the pointing out of a thing as "this" (*hec est
    mater nostra*), not a proof; *illa scimus … quod* anticipates the *quod*-clause.

[^10]: **S/T.** A margin *Conclusio* (91.11), *Hugo* (91.17). *Ex fide cognoscimus*: the verb governs
    *ex*, so "from faith", not the formula (GLOSSARY §C). Gal. 6:10, DR "those who are of the household
    of the faith". Hugh of St Victor, *De sacramentis*, as Wyclif cites it; *libro II°* (91.17; book 2, as at 86.6). Loserth: "I have not been able to find this passage
    in Hugo de S. Victore l. c. but comp. lib. I, pars X, cap. VI" (LEDGER E04f-5); the figure of the eye that sees for the whole
    body is in II.2.2 (*Soli enim oculi vident … toti corpori*), the rest of the wording is not found
    there. The apostle's text is presumably 1 Cor. 12, the body and its members. *Utrobique*: in both, the bridegroom's honour and the bride's
    profit.

[^11]: **T/C.** B lacks *prius* (91.20); AA₁ lack *ut brachium* (91.22–23); A margin *Conclusio*
    (91.29). *Subiectatur*: has its subject, the eye being the subject in which sight inheres (GLOSSARY
    §C, *subiectum*); *ipse* is the eye. *Intendens … proprietati commodi*: "aiming at the ownness of
    advantage", advantage as one's own rather than common. *Gracia sui* in the relative clause: for
    God's sake (cf. 92.2, *propter Deum*).

[^12]: **T/R.** *Amare communi* (92.4) is printed so (plate); *amore communi*, "by common love", would run
    more easily, but the print construes ("to love in common"). *Dominare* (92.5): so AA₁, which add
    *In vigilia Pentecostes*, "on the vigil of Pentecost" (Loserth's foot). Loserth lists Hus's reuse of
    chapter 4 (cf. Hus, *De eccl.*, several folios) (LEDGER E04f-6).
