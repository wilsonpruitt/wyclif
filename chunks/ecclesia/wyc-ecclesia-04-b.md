# *De ecclesia*, chapter 4, part 2: membership by predestination cannot be lost; the prelates who trust in the temple

## ¶1 · 72.11–75.15

> [p. 72] Conceditur tamen quod ordinacione humana clerici constituuntur in officiis ecclesiasticis tam predestinati quam presciti, sed ordinantes eis talia officia non habent potestatem ordinandi quod sint membra vel partes vere ecclesie. Ulterius conceditur quod predestinatus quantumcunque fuerit in mortali non desinit esse membrum vere ecclesie, licet excommunicacione pretensa ponatur nominetenus extra temporalem ac corporalem communicacionem pretense ecclesie. Unde Ricardus de Sancto Victore in quodam tractatu De Potestate Clavium qui sic incipit: Quodcunque ligaveris Membrum, inquit, Christi potest dici homo secundum geminam consideracionem, aut secundum predestinacionem aut secundum habitudinem. Secundum predestinacionem potest quis membrum Christi esse et necdum caritatem habere. Juxta namque domini testimonium: Refrigescet caritas multorum. Secundum ergo hanc accepcionem contingit hominem esse membrum Christi et adhuc vinculo debite dampnacionis teneri; secundum habitudinem autem membrum Christi dicitur, quando in se habet hoc, unde hoc ipsum efficitur. Sed numquid membrum Christi [p. 73] caritas illa facit que ad tempus inardescit et in momento refrigescit? Absque dubio qui perseverantem caritatem nunquam accipiunt, nunquam de veris illis Christi membris fuerunt qui corporis eius integritatem perficiunt. Secundum quandam accepcionem tamen possumus et solemus caritatem transitoriam habentes membra Christi dicere propter membrorum ipsius conformitatem quam ad tempus videntur habere. Sed ut a pie querentibus omnem scrupulum amoveamus, dicimus quod triplici modo membrum Christi dici solemus: predestinacione, preparacione, concorporacione. Predestinacione, quando ad vitam divinitus preordinatur; preparacione, quando paganus vel falsus christianus veraciter compungitur et ad veritatem ac caritatem inbuitur; concorporacione, quando ille per corporis ablucionem, iste per sacerdotis absolucionem ecclesie Christi conformatur. Primum agitur proposito divine voluntatis, secundum proposito proprie deliberacionis, tercium vero officio sacerdotis. Ex corporali advertere possumus quid de spirituali edificacione sentire debeamus. Ecce de silva lignum eligitur; ut in edificio ponatur, postea dolatur, et admodum formam congruam dolando preparatur, ad ultimum edificio connectitur et ipsi debite in loco coaptatur. Ecce quomodo unum denique lignum fit edificii membrum: primo sola eleccione, secundo preparacione et tercio compaginacione. Sic sane multi iam sunt membra Christi predestinacione, necdum tamen preparacione; multi vero preparacione, sed necdum tamen concorporacione. Augustinus cum adhuc infidelis esset, secundum predestinacionem [p. 74] membrum Christi fuit, conformiter preparacione membrum Christi extitit, cum corde credens et devocione fervens ad baptismum festinavit. Baptizatus vero et ecclesie consociatus membris Christi concorporatus fuit. Et istam sentenciam docet Augustinus, tercio De Doctrina Christiana, cap. XXXII°: Non enim re vera corpus domini est quod cum illo non erit in eternum. Ypocrite inquit, non solum in eternum, verum eciam nunc non cum illo esse dicendi sunt, quamvis in eius esse videantur ecclesia. Et idem dicit Chrisostomus In Imperfecto, Omelia IXª: Qui Dei fuerunt perire non potuerunt, quia de Deo nemo rapere potest. Et eadem est sentencia sanctorum doctorum qui se ipsos intelligunt in ista materia; nam aliter fides scripture foret falsa, cum Joh. X°, 27, 28, dicit Christus: Oves mee vocem meam audiunt, et ego cognosco eas, et sequuntur me, et ego vitam eternam do eis, et non peribunt in eternum, et non rapiet eos quisquam de manu mea. Et post probat ex immensitate doni patris (quod est spiritus sanctus) quod nemo potest hoc facere, quia pater suus est omnipotens de cuius manu nemo potest quidquam eripere. Sed Christus et pater suus unum sunt cum spiritu sancto qui est donum suum; quo sibi copulatur ecclesia (ut patet cap. X). Ergo nemo potest oves suas de manu eius eripere. Ipse enim eternaliter elegit quodlibet membrum ecclesie in sponsam; ergo sicut nemo potest desinere esse predestinatus, sic non est possibile quod aliquod membrum sponse sue deserat vel expellat, quia (ut dixi superius) tunc persona eligens foret improvida vel ingrata. [p. 75] Secundo confirmatur idem ex fide scripture 1° Joh. IIª 19, ubi sanctus evangelista loquitur de prescitis qui ad tempus manserunt in gracia. Si, inquit, ex nobis fuissent, permansissent utique nobiscum. Non enim sapit quod ista condicionalis tam alti evangeliste sit impossibilis et heretica. Tercio confirmatur ex dicto apostoli Rom. VIII°, 35, ubi loquens de se et aliis membris ecclesie palam asserit quod nulla creatura poterit eos separare a caritate que est numerus fundatus in Christo Jesu colligans membra sua. Ideo dicit eodem capitulo, 9: Vos autem in carne non estis, sed in spiritu, si tamen spiritus Dei habitat in vobis. Si quis autem spiritum Christi non habet, hic non est eius. Et intelligit quod ille non est pars sui corporis.

Yet it is granted that by human [ordinance]{g:ordinacio} [clerics]{g:clericus}, the
[predestinate]{g:predestinatus} as well as the [foreknown]{g:prescitus}, are constituted in
ecclesiastical offices; but those who ordain such offices for them do not have the power to ordain that
they be [members]{g:membrum} or parts of the true [church]{g:ecclesia}.[^1] Further, it is granted that a
predestinate [man], however far he may be in mortal [sin], does not cease to be a member of the true
church, although by a pretended excommunication he is put, in name, outside the temporal and bodily
communion of the pretended church. Hence Richard of St Victor, in a certain treatise *On the Power of
the Keys*, which begins thus, "Whatsoever thou shalt bind": "A man," he says, "can be called a member
of Christ according to a twofold consideration: either according to predestination or according to
[condition]{g:habitudo}. According to predestination someone can be a member of Christ and not yet have
[charity]{g:caritas}; for according to the Lord's testimony, 'the charity of many shall grow cold.'
According to this acceptation, therefore, it happens that a man is a member of Christ and is still held
by the bond of due damnation; but according to condition he is called a member of Christ when he has
in himself that whereby he is made this very thing.[^2] But does that charity make a member of Christ
[73] which for a time burns and in a moment grows cold? Without doubt those who never receive
persevering charity were never of those true members of Christ who complete the integrity of his body.
Yet according to a certain acceptation we can, and are accustomed to, call those who have transitory
charity members of Christ, because of the conformity with his members which for a time they seem to
have. But, to remove every scruple from those who inquire piously, we say that we are accustomed to be
called members of Christ in three ways: by predestination, by preparation, by [concorporation]{g:concorporacio}.[^3] By
predestination, when one is divinely preordained to life; by preparation, when a pagan or a false
Christian is truly pricked to compunction and imbued with truth and charity; by concorporation, when
the former, through the washing of the body, the latter, through the absolution of the
[priest]{g:sacerdos}, is conformed to the church of Christ. The first is done by the purpose of the
divine will, the second by the purpose of one's own deliberation, but the third by the office of the
priest. From the bodily [building] we can observe what we ought to think of the spiritual building.
See: a timber is chosen from the forest; that it may be set in a building, it is afterwards hewn, and
by hewing is prepared to a fitting shape; at last it is joined to the building and duly fitted to it in
its place. See how one timber at length becomes a member of the building: first by choice alone,
second by preparation, and third by joining.[^4] So indeed many are already members of Christ by
predestination, yet not yet by preparation; and many by preparation, but yet not yet by
concorporation. Augustine, while he was still an unbeliever, was by predestination [74] a member of
Christ; correspondingly, he became a member of Christ by preparation when, believing in heart and
fervent in devotion, he hastened to baptism. But baptized and joined in fellowship with the church, he
was concorporated with the members of Christ."[^5] And this [teaching]{g:sentencia}[^6] Augustine teaches,
*On Christian Doctrine* 3, chapter 32: "For that is not in truth the body of the Lord which will not be
with him for ever. Hypocrites," he says, "are to be said not to be with him, not only for ever, but even
now, although they seem to be in his church."[^7] And Chrysostom says the same in the *Incomplete*
[*Work*], homily 9: "Those who were God's could not perish, because no one can snatch from God." And
the same is the teaching of the holy doctors who understand themselves in this matter; for otherwise
the faith of scripture would be false, since, John 10:27–28, Christ says: "My sheep hear my voice. And I
know them: and they follow me. And I give them life everlasting: and they shall not perish for ever.
And no man shall pluck them out of my hand."[^8] And afterwards he proves from the immensity of the
Father's gift (which is the Holy Spirit) that no one can do this, because his Father is omnipotent, out
of whose hand no one can snatch anything. Now Christ and his Father are one with the Holy Spirit, who
is their gift, by whom the church is joined to him (as is clear in chapter 10). Therefore no one can
snatch his sheep out of his hand.[^9] For he eternally chose every member of the church as his
[bride]{g:sponsa}; therefore, just as no one can cease to be predestinate, so it is not possible that he
should desert or expel any member of his bride, since (as I said above) the person choosing would then
be improvident or ungracious.[^10] [75] Second, the same is confirmed [from the faith of
scripture]{g:ex-fide}, 1 John 2:19, where the holy evangelist speaks of the foreknown who for a time
remained in grace: "If," he says, "they had been of us, they would no doubt have remained with us." For
it does not make sense that this conditional of so lofty an evangelist should be impossible and
heretical. Third, it is confirmed from the saying of the apostle, Romans 8:35, where, speaking of
himself and the other members of the church, he openly asserts that no creature shall be able to
separate them from the charity which is a number founded in Christ Jesus, binding together his
members.[^11] Therefore he says in the same chapter, [verse] 9: "But you are not in the flesh, but in the
spirit, if so be that the Spirit of God dwell in you. Now if any man have not the Spirit of Christ, he
is none of his." And he means that that man is not a part of his body.

## ¶2 · 75.16–75.30

> [p. 75] Et si obicitur per hoc quod prescitus existens in caritate habet hoc vinculum, et per consequens, unionem, sicut predestinatus existens in crimine caret hoc vinculo, et per consequens, unione: patet quod, sicut in humano corpore est dare humidum fluens et humidum radicale, sic in corpore Christi mistico est dare graciam secundum presentem iusticiam et graciam consummatam. Unde sicut apostemata per humidum fluens sunt adnata, non continuata propter disparitatem nature: sic est de membris diaboli, licet pro tempore secundum presentem iusticiam sint adnati. Predestinati autem licet ad tempus priventur fluente gracia, habent tamen radicalem graciam a qua non possunt excidere, et sic habentes duplicem graciam duplici vinculo sunt ligati. Ligamentum tamen primum est insolubile.

And if it is [objected, by appeal to]{g:obicitur-per} this: that a foreknown [man] who is in charity
has this bond, and consequently union, just as a predestinate [man] who is in crime lacks this bond, and
consequently union: it is clear that, just as in the human body one must posit a [flowing moisture and
a radical moisture]{g:humidum-radicale}, so in the [mystical body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi-misticum} one must posit grace
according to present justice and consummated grace.[^12] Hence, just as [abscesses]{g:apostema} are
grown on through the flowing moisture, not continuous [with the body] because of the disparity of
nature, so it is with the members of the devil, although for a time, according to present justice,
they are grown on. But the predestinate, although for a time they are deprived of flowing grace, yet
have the radical grace, from which they cannot fall away; and so, having a twofold grace, they are
bound by a twofold bond. Yet the first ligament is indissoluble.[^13]

## ¶3 · 76.1–76.24

> [p. 76] Sic ergo videtur mihi quod sentencia sanctorum de membris vere ecclesie sit pernecessaria hodie, sicut fuit tempore sinagoge. Sicut enim tunc sacerdotes pretensi dixerunt repugnare promissioni divine quod ipsi vel possessio eorum pereat, sicut recitat Jeremias VII°, cap. 4: Nolite confidere in verbis mendacii, dicentes templum domini, templum domini, templum domini est, sic et modo videtur quosdam prepositos particularis ecclesie qui pretendunt se ex fide convincere quod ipsi capitaliter regant Petri naviculam, sic quod ipsa potest fluctibus seculi concuti sed non mergi. Et sic implicant se ex predestinacione divina non posse dampnari nec temporalia posse a sua ecclesia spoliari, sed de necessitate salutis oportet omnes alios christianos eis subici et tamquam capitales in dominacione seculari ac spirituali omnium bonorum ecclesie dominari. Unde, sicut sacerdotes Aaronite dixerunt quod non peribit lex a sacerdote neque consilium a sapiente neque sermo a propheta (ut dicitur Jeremie XVIII°, 18), sic videntur dicere hodie quod pretensa et particularis ecclesia sit tripliciter stabilita, scilicet Christi promissione, fulcimento legis ecclesie que equivalet fidei christiane, et multorum sapientum constipacione.

So, then, it seems to me that the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of the saints on the members of the true
church is most necessary today, as it was in the time of the synagogue. For just as then the pretended
[priests]{g:sacerdos} said that it was repugnant to the divine promise that they or their possession
should perish, as Jeremiah recounts, chapter 7[:4]: "Trust not in lying words, saying: The temple of
the Lord, the temple of the Lord, it is the temple of the Lord," so too now it seems [so with] certain
[superiors]{g:prepositi} of a particular church, who claim to prove from faith that they rule Peter's
little ship [as its head]{g:capitaliter}, so that it can be shaken by the waves of the world but not sunk.[^14] And so
they imply that, by divine predestination, they cannot be damned, nor can [temporal
goods]{g:temporalia} be stripped from their church; but that, of necessity of salvation, all other
Christians must be subject to them, and that they, as [chiefs]{g:capitales}, in secular and spiritual
[exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio}, [lord it]{g:dominari} over all the goods of the church.[^15] Hence,
just as the Aaronite priests said that "the law shall not perish from the priest, nor counsel from the
wise, nor the word from the prophet" (as is said in Jeremiah 18:18), so they seem to say today that the
pretended and particular church is established in three ways: namely, by Christ's promise, by the prop
of the church's law, which is equivalent to the Christian faith, and by the close ranks of many wise
men.[^16]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 72.11: The foreknown as well as the predestinate may hold ecclesiastical office.
- 73.10: Three ways of being a member of Christ; by predestination, by preparation, by concorporation.
- 74.6: That only is a part of the Lord's body which will remain with Him for ever.
- 74.27: The predestinate cannot cease to be such.
- 75.2: The foreknown cannot continue in grace.
- 75.7: St. Paul says that nothing can separate us from the love of God.
- 75.16: It is objected that this applies to the foreknown who is for the time in charity;
- 75.22: but the foreknown are not parts of Christ's body, but excrescences.
- 76.8: There are prelates who say that the safety of the Church is due to them.

[^1]: **T/C.** The answer to the third objection
    (71.20–28) continues: human ordinance makes officers, not members. AB margin: *Conclusio* (72.15).

[^2]: **S/C.** Richard of St Victor, *De potestate ligandi et solvendi*, capp. 19–20 (PL 196:1172A–1173A;
    Loserth: col. 1172) (LEDGER E04b-1); Wyclif omits *et e converso … non esse* and has *in momento* for
    *in modico*; its incipit is Matt. 16:19, *Quodcumque ligaveris*. AB margin:
    *Ricardus* (72.20). Matt. 24:12, DR. *Habitudo*: the present condition (state) of a man, against
    God's predestination; B's later hand: *Membrum scilicet secundum habitudinem eius* (73.11).

[^3]: **T.** *Qui corporis* (73.4) is Loserth's; the MSS *que corporis*. *Concorporacio*: being made one
    body with the members, Loserth's "concorporation". AB margin (later hand): *Membrum Christi
    predestinacione, preparacione, concorporacione* (B adds *ecclesie tripliciter*); *Triplex membrum*
    (73.11).

[^4]: **C/T.** *Ille … iste*: the former (the pagan), by baptism; the latter (the false Christian), by
    absolution. *Edificacio*: the spiritual "building", the figure of the timber that follows. A *lignum
    sit* for *fit* (73.26). AB margin: *Exemplum* (73.20, 73.29).

[^5]: **T/C.** *Conformiter* (74.1) is Loserth's correction (*Correxi*); the MSS *conformitatis*, which is
    Richard's word (*conformitatis praeparatione*, PL 196:1172D); we translate the print. The quotation
    ends with *concorporatus fuit*, where Richard goes on *Si quis ergo* (LEDGER E04b-1). *Infidelis*: an unbeliever.

[^6]: **C.** *Sentencia* (CONTEXTUAL): the position taught, "teaching", not a judicial sentence.

[^7]: **S/T.** Augustine, *De doctrina christiana* 3.32.45, on Tyconius' second rule, the Lord's
    bipartite body (Loserth: *Opp.* III/1, 58) (LEDGER E04b-2). The second sentence (*Ypocrite …*) is
    Augustine's own (PL 34:82), without his *quia* and with *inquit* added. AB margin: *Augustinus* (74.5).

[^8]: **S.** *Opus imperfectum in Matthaeum*, hom. 9 (Loserth: *Chrysost. Opp.* VI, lxi E; "Imperfecto
    sc. opere") (LEDGER E04b-3). John 10:27–28, DR; Wyclif *eos* for *eas* (no change in English). A
    margin: *Predestinatum membrum Christi non potest cessare tale membrum* (74.11).

[^9]: **S/C.** John 10:29–30. The Vulgate *quod dedit mihi, maius omnibus est* (DR "That which my Father
    hath given me is greater than all") is read of the gift, the Holy Spirit. *Sed* brings the minor
    premiss ("Now"). *Cap. X* (74.24): John 10 (*Ego et Pater unum sumus*, 10:30) or chapter 10 of this
    book (the church joined to Christ by the Spirit); the reference is unchecked (LEDGER E04b-4).

[^10]: **G/C.** *Ingrata*: rendered "ungracious", of God choosing, where GLOSSARY §D's "displeasing (to
    God)" cannot apply and "ungrateful" has no object; proposed. *Ut dixi superius*: not identified
    (LEDGER E04b-6).

[^11]: **T/S.** 1 John 2:19, DR; AA₁ *per tempus* for *ad tempus* (75.3). Rom. 8:35–39 paraphrased: DR
    "the love of Christ" (8:35), "the love of God which is in Christ Jesus" (8:39), for *caritas*
    (GLOSSARY §A: "charity"); Wyclif cites 8:35. *Numerus fundatus* (75.10), as printed: B *minus
    fundatus*, which may point to *munus*, "a gift" (cf. *donum*, 74.22) or *nexus*, "a bond"; a doubt,
    reported. B lacks *eos* (75.9). Rom. 8:9, DR.

[^12]: **C/R.** AB margin: *Obieccio* (75.16); B: *Solucio … Predestinati quomodo sunt in ecclesia et
    quomodo presciti qui sunt ad tempus in gracia* (75.19). *Humidum radicale*: the radical (innate)
    moisture of medieval physiology, against the flowing (nutritive) moisture. *Est dare*: "one must
    posit". *Graciam secundum presentem iusticiam*: the grace of present righteousness, which the
    foreknown can have for a time.

[^13]: **T/C/R.** *Adnata … non continuata*: grown onto the body, not continuous with it (Loserth's
    "excrescences"). *Predestinati autem* (75.26) is B's reading; A₁ *vero*, as also in Hus's reuse
    (Loserth: Hus, *Opp.* I, 204b; cf. Hus, *De eccl.*). B *Ligamentum cum* (75.30). *Ligamentum …
    primum*: taken as the bond of radical grace, the only indissoluble one, though it is named second.

[^14]: **S/C.** Jer. 7:4, DR (Wyclif *VII°, cap. 4*, the verse). The MSS *repungnare* (76.4). *Videtur
    quosdam prepositos*: the accusative has no infinitive; supply "[so with]" or "[that] certain
    superiors [say]". *Ex fide convincere*: "to prove from faith" (a verb governs *ex*, so not the
    formula, GLOSSARY §C). *Petri naviculam*: the bark of Peter (Matt. 14:24–32). A margin: *Nota de
    papa* (76.10).

[^15]: **C.** *Capitaliter … capitales*: as head, as chiefs, the claim Wyclif denies them (cf.
    *capitaneus*, GLOSSARY §A). *De necessitate salutis*: the formula of *Unam sanctam* (*Extrav.
    comm.* 1.8.1), cited by Loserth earlier in the work (LEDGER E04b-5). *Dominari*: "lord it", the
    pejorative sense.

[^16]: **S/C.** Jer. 18:18, DR; Wyclif *neque … neque*. *Aaronite*: the priests of Aaron's line.
    *Constipacio*: a crowding together, the "close ranks" of their learned supporters.
