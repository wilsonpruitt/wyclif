# sed/autem extract, wave 7: chunks 03-g 03-h 04-a

### 03-g ¶2 · 78.9–78.26  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 78] Secundo sequitur quod omnis sensus philosophorum vel naturales eorum non sufficiunt convincere de aliqua substancia aliquam quatuor questionum, **sed** in istis necessitati sunt ad fidem recurrere quoad diffinicionem, distinccionem et perfeccionem specificam. In omnibus istis sunt compares idiotis. Nam iuxta dicta nullus sensus vel intellectus convincit esse quamcunque materialem substanciam, cum stante omni noticia sensitiva vel experiencia apparet compossibile et consonum quod tota universitas creata sit globus accidencium; ideo ponens talia necesse habet recurrere ad fidem scripture et supponere eius sensum; et deficiente naturali noticia questionis si est de quacunque materiali substancia, necesse est quod deficiat questio quid est et questio cuiusmodi est et questio propter quid est de quolibet assignando, quia omnes dicte tres questiones presupponunt questionem si est, que non potest naturaliter stabiliri.

ENGLISH: Second, it follows that all the sense of the philosophers, or [of] the natural [philosophers] among them,[^4] do not suffice to [prove conclusively]{g:convincere}, of any [substance]{g:substancia}, any of the four questions; but in these they are compelled to have recourse to faith as to the [definition]{g:diffinicio}, distinction and specific perfection.[^5] In all these they are on a par with [unlearned men]{g:idiota}. For according to what has been said, no sense or intellect proves conclusively that any material substance exists, since, all sensitive knowledge or experience standing, it appears [compossible]{g:compossibilis} and consonant that [the whole created totality]{g:universitas-creata} be a mass of accidents;[^6] therefore one who posits such things must have recourse to the faith of scripture and suppose its sense. And if natural knowledge of the question whether it is fails for any material substance, then the question what it is, and the question of what sort it is, and the question why it is, must fail for anything to be assigned; since all the said three questions presuppose the question whether it is, which cannot be naturally established.

### 03-g ¶3 · 78.27–79.9  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 78] **Sed** contra istam perfidiam arguitur quod nedum tollit omnem naturalem scienciam **sed** eciam omnem fidem; nam sic devians nedum tenetur sentire cum antiquis errantibus quod nullam affirmacionem cognoscunt [p. 79] **sed** negaciones ut quod nichil sciunt et eis similia **sed** tenentur habere conscienciam de asserendo vel iurando aliquid contingens praxim hominum, et periret quelibet policia vel religio christiana; nam nemo debet mentiri pro toto mundo, **sed** generaliter pars securior est tenenda; cum ergo nulla noticia quam habemus de materiali substancia fundatur infallibiliter in principio infallibili cognoscendo, videtur quod irregulariter debemus nullam talem asserere.

ENGLISH: But against this faithlessness it is argued that it takes away [not only]{g:nedum} all natural knowledge but also all faith. For one who strays so is bound not only to [hold]{g:sentire} with the ancient erring [philosophers] that they know no affirmation [79] but [only] negations, as that they know nothing, and things like these; but they are bound to [be troubled in conscience over]{g:habere-conscienciam-super} asserting or swearing anything touching the practice of men;[^7] and every [polity]{g:policia} or Christian religion would perish. For no one ought to lie for the whole world; now in general the safer part is to be held;[^8] since therefore no knowledge that we have of material substance is founded infallibly on an infallible principle of knowing, it seems that, [irregularly]{g:irregulariter}, we ought to assert no such [substance].[^9]

### 03-g ¶5 · 79.24–80.10  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 79] Item, periret fides scripture, cum posset negari alicui persone hominis revelacionem vel noticiam huiusmodi esse datam, licet nature ex spiritu et accidente que servat equivoce idem nomen, quod nos inponimus nostris substanciis; et illa natura non foret inperfeccior propter carenciam inperfecte materialis substancie, **sed** quantum ad omnem operacionem [p. 80] eque perfectam; et periret omnis differencia specifica et sic species alleganda et sic scala per quam fidelis ascenderet ad Deum, cum non sit Dei boni noticie humane instabili sic sine regulari fundamento deficere vel omnem stabilitatem sensacionis tollere, et specialiter in sacramento quod est Deo proprium, in quo minime foret sensus illusio circa materialem substanciam. Ideo videtur quod non est credendum istis maniacis in hiis que sine fundamento sompniant, cum publicant se non credere sibi ipsis.

ENGLISH: Again, the faith of scripture would perish, since it could be denied that a revelation or knowledge of this kind was given to any [person]{g:persona} of a man, although [it was given] to a [nature]{g:natura} [made] of spirit and accident, which keeps [equivocally]{g:equivoce} the same name that we impose on our substances;[^11] and that nature would not be less perfect for lacking imperfect material substance, but, as to every operation, [80] equally perfect;[^12] and every specific [difference]{g:differencia} would perish, and so the species to be alleged, and so the ladder by which the faithful man would ascend to God; since it is not [the part] of a good God thus to fail human knowledge, unstable [as it is], without a regular foundation, or to take away all stability of [sensation]{g:sensacio}; and especially in the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} which is proper to God, in which there would least of all be [illusion]{g:illusio} of sense about material substance.[^13] Therefore it seems that these madmen are not to be believed in the things they dream without foundation, since they publish that they do not believe themselves.

### 03-h ¶1 · 80.11–82.8  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 80] Tercio sequitur de istis possibili, quod nedum dubitanda est quidditas cuiuscunque substancie, **sed** concedi debet predicacio mutua cuiuslibet de quocunque, ut posito quod Deus communicet Petro sacerdoti potenciam transsubstanciandi hostiam in corpus dominicum, habeatque dyaconus suus potenciam transsubstanciandi eam in ecclesiam, et habeat subdiaconus suus potestatem transsubstanciandi ipsam in totum mundum et quamlibet eius partem, et posito quod isti tres simul incipiant et simul finiant super eadem hostia verba sua sacramentalia, et cum Deus non subtrahit potestatem conficiendi propter peccatum ministri, posito quod Deus concurrat cum quolibet istorum usque in finem perficiendo correspondenter ad potenciam illis datam; quo posito videtur quod in fine corpus Christi erit ecclesia et mundus cum qualibet sui parte, quia quodlibet istorum est res eiusdem sacramenti; et iterum cum tam ecclesia quam mundus manet secundum quamlibet sui partem, videtur quod mundus sit secundum se totum in qualibet sui parte. Et iterum videtur quod quelibet pars mundi [p. 81] potest perpetuo servari in hostia, licet corrumpatur in sui natura. Sic enim foret de corpore Christi sacerdote conficiente in sancto triduo, ipso servato in pixide. Et videtur possibilitas casus quoad Deum ex isto patescere quod eque facile foret Deo convertere panem in totum mundum sicut in corpus suum, cum tamen repugnat infinite parvitati hostie converti in corpus Christi septipedale sicut converti in totum mundum; nec plus obstat quod sint multe transsubstanciaciones sub uno hostie sacramento quam quod quelibet pars Christi tam secundum corpus quam secundum animam sit sub qualibet particula hostie consecrate; videtur enim quod panis transsubstanciatur nedum in carnem Christi et sanguinem **sed** in corpus et animam ac quamlibet eius partem; non enim est conversio unionis **sed** transitoria (ut dicit Hugo); et ad illam videtur sufficere quod succedat sub eisdem accidentibus convertendum. Ex quo videtur quod quelibet pars vini vel panis convertitur in quamlibet partem Christi, ymmo in quodlibet accidens eius absolutum, cum quodlibet tale accidens transit sub accidentibus panis et vini, et sicut concomitanter sunt anima et quelibet pars corporis Christi ad omnem punctum hostie consecrate, ita videntur concomitanter converti sicut et accidens; et pars principalis videtur habere principalem racionem conversionis, et ita videtur esse multiplex transaccidentacio sicut est multiplex transsubstanciacio. Et hoc graviter ferunt novelli baptiste signorum sentenciam dictam superius de transsubstanciacione et conversione [p. 82] Hugonis. Videtur enim quod oportet transsubstanciatum mutari; cuius mutacionis terminum oportet esse substanciam, quia aliter foret transinferioracio vel transsubieccio, **sed** iuxta viam predictam nec oportet panem perdere vel acquirere aliquam qualitatem **sed** solum respectum ad quem non est motus secundum philosophos, dum panis fit hostia consecrata.

ENGLISH: Third, it follows, as possible on these [men's premisses],[^1] that [not only]{g:nedum} is the [quiddity]{g:quiditas} of every [substance]{g:substancia} to be doubted, but the mutual [predication]{g:predicacio} of anything of anything must be granted. As: supposing that God communicates to Peter the priest the [power]{g:potencia} of transubstantiating the [host]{g:hostia} into the Lord's body, and his deacon has the power of transubstantiating it into the [church]{g:ecclesia}, and his subdeacon has the [power]{g:potestas} of transubstantiating it into the whole world and every part of it;[^2] and supposing that these three begin together and finish together their sacramental words over the same host; and, since God does not withdraw the power of [consecrating]{g:conficere} on account of the minister's sin, supposing that God concurs with each of them to the end, accomplishing correspondingly to the power given them: which supposed, it seems that at the end [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} will be the church and the world with every part of it, since each of these is the [reality]{g:res-sacramenti} of the same sacrament; and again, since both the church and the world remain according to every part of themselves, it seems that the world is, according to its whole self, in every part of itself. And again it seems that any part of the world [81] can be perpetually preserved in the host, although it be corrupted in its own nature. For so it would be with the body of Christ, if a priest consecrated in the holy [three days]{g:triduum}, it being preserved in the pyx.[^3] And the possibility of the case as regards God seems to come to light from this, that it would be as easy for God to convert [bread]{g:panis} into the whole world as into his body; while yet it is as [incompatible]{g:repugnare} with the infinite smallness of the host to be converted into the seven-foot body of Christ as to be converted into the whole world. Nor is it more of an obstacle that there be many [transubstantiations]{g:transsubstanciacio} under one [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} of the host than that every part of Christ, both as to body and as to soul, be under every particle of the consecrated host. For it seems that the bread is transubstantiated not only into the flesh and blood of Christ but into the body and soul and every part of him; for it is not a [conversion]{g:conversio} of union but a transitory [one] (as Hugh says); and for that it seems to suffice that what it is to be converted into succeed under the same [accidents]{g:accidens}.[^4] From which it seems that every part of the [wine]{g:vinum} or bread is converted into every part of Christ, indeed into every [absolute accident]{g:accidens-absolutum} of his, since every such accident passes under the accidents of the bread and wine; and as the soul and every part of the body of Christ are [concomitantly]{g:concomitanter} at every point of the consecrated host, so they seem to be concomitantly converted, as the accident also [is]; and the principal part seems to have the principal account of the conversion; and so there seems to be a manifold [transaccidentation]{g:transaccidentacio}, as there is a manifold transubstantiation.[^5] And at this the [newer]{g:novelli} [baptizers of the signs]{g:baptiste-signorum} take hard the [teaching]{g:sentencia} stated above on the transubstantiation and conversion [82] of Hugh.[^6] For it seems that what is transubstantiated must be changed, and the terminus of that change must be a substance, since otherwise it would be a [transinferioration]{g:transinferioracio} or a transsubjection; but according to the way stated, the bread need neither lose nor acquire any [quality]{g:qualitas}, but only a [relation]{g:respectus}, to which there is no motion according to the philosophers, while the bread becomes a consecrated host.[^7]

### 03-h ¶2 · 82.9–82.21  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 82] Hic videtur quod terminus conversionis ex sinistro intellectu convertitur in venenum; potest ergo dici quod panis et vinum convertuntur principaliter in corpus Christi et sanguinem **sed** concomitanter et secundarie totam humanitatem Christi et quamlibet eius partem; nec panis aut vinum deterioratur **sed** melioratur, ad quam melioracionem est motus extense loquendo, manet namque utraque natura; **sed** sacramentum melioratur, cum fit sacrum, et corpus Domini est illud cui est toto animo attendendum; et hoc signatur per conversionem et transsubstanciacionem; et videtur quod in quamlibet partem humanitatis Christi potest concedi facta conversio.

ENGLISH: Here it seems that the term "conversion", from a perverse understanding, is converted into poison.[^8] It can therefore be said that the bread and wine are converted [principally]{g:principaliter} into the body and blood of Christ, but concomitantly and secondarily [into] the whole [humanity]{g:humanitas} of Christ and every part of it;[^9] nor is the bread or wine made worse, but bettered, and to that bettering there is motion, [speaking in an extended sense]{g:extense-loquendo}; for each nature remains. But the sacrament is bettered, since it becomes [sacred]{g:sacer}; and [the body of the Lord]{g:corpus-domini} is that to which one must attend with the whole mind; and this is signified by "conversion" and "transubstantiation". And it seems that the conversion can be granted to have been made into every part of the humanity of Christ.

### 03-h ¶3 · 82.22–83.9  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 82] **Sed** quantum ad verba consecracionis et intencionem colentis attinet, fit quadam principalitate conversio in corpus Christi et sanguinem et in alia concomitanter et accessorie vel confuse; et potest terminus ultra hoc quod philosophi noverunt extendi ad motum, cuius motus terminus a quo est panis nudus et terminus extrinsecus ad quem est corpus dominicum, **sed** terminus intrinsecus ad quem est sacramentalis sanctitas iuxta quod concedi potest extense loquendo de motu quod panis et vinum insensibiliter alterantur. Et sic ille motus dicit aggregative motus multiplices et [p. 83] multas motus materias, ut bonitatem signi, efficaciam signati et multas alias habitudines relativas; et quando dicimus quod corpus Christi non exinde movetur, loquimur equivoce et striccius tunc de motu, sicut non dicimus quod homo movetur ex hoc quod species sua diffusius multiplicatur per medium nec est michi vis de dictis institucionibus terminorum (ut patet posterius) etc.

ENGLISH: But as far as concerns the words of consecration and the intention of the worshipper, the conversion is made, by a certain [principality]{g:principalitas}, into the body and blood of Christ, and into the other things concomitantly and accessorily, or [indistinctly]{g:confuse}; and the term can be extended, beyond what the philosophers knew, to motion: of which motion the terminus from which is bare bread, and the extrinsic terminus to which is the Lord's body, but the intrinsic terminus to which is sacramental holiness;[^10] according to which it can be granted, speaking of motion in an extended sense, that the bread and wine are imperceptibly altered. And so that motion [signifies]{g:dicere} [aggregatively]{g:aggregative} manifold motions and [83] many matters of motion, as the goodness of the [sign]{g:signum}, the efficacy of the [thing signified]{g:signatum}, and many other [relative respects]{g:habitudo-relativa}. And when we say that the body of Christ is not moved thereby, we then speak [equivocally]{g:equivoce} and more strictly of motion; as we do not say that a man is moved by the fact that his species is multiplied more widely through the medium.[^11] Nor do I care about the said institutions of terms (as will appear later), etc.[^12]

### 04-a ¶1 · 83.10–83.13  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 83] **Sed** restat videndum ulterius quam efficaciam habent verba sacramentalia et quomodo corpus Christi habet non esse dimensionale **sed** sacramentale in signis illis absconditum.

ENGLISH: But it remains to see further what efficacy the sacramental words have, and how the body of Christ has not dimensional but [sacramental being]{g:esse-sacramentale}, hidden in those [signs]{g:signum}.

### 04-a ¶2 · 83.14–84.6  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 83] Quantum ad verba sacramentalia, videtur quod Christus in illis loquitur figurative vel tropice, sicut prius allegatum est legem novam sub habitudine ad veterem sepius loqui. Sic enim dicit Christus quod Johannes est Helias Math. XIº, 14. Sic enim dicit Math. XIIIº, 37—40: Qui seminat bonum semen est filius hominis; ager **autem** est mundus; bonum vero semen sunt filii regni, zizania **autem** sunt filii nequam; inimicus **autem** qui seminavit ea est diabolus, messis vero est consummacio seculi, messores **autem** angeli sunt. Sicut igitur Veritas loquitur in istis parabolice ut dicit evangelium, sic videtur loqui figurative in quatuor evangeliis, scilicet Math. XXVIº, 26, Marci XIVº, 22, Luce XXIIº, 19, 20, Iᵃ Cor. XIº, 24, quando dicit panem et vinum esse corpus suum et sanguinem, sicut docet Apostolus prima Cor. XIº, 24 (ut patet [p. 84] posterius). Differencia tamen est in modis loquendi, cum unus sit tropologicus vel moralis et alius allegoricus vel sacramentalis; habet enim efficaciam faciendi corpus Christi et sanguinem esse de facto sub sacramentalibus speciebus, a quo defecerunt alie figure tam nove legis quam veteris.

ENGLISH: As to the sacramental words, it seems that Christ speaks in them [figuratively]{g:figurative} or [tropically]{g:tropice}, as it was alleged before that the new law, in its [respect]{g:habitudo} to the old, often speaks [so].[^1] For so Christ says that John is Elias, Matt. 11:14. For so he says, Matt. 13:37–40: "He that soweth the good seed is the Son of man. And the field is the world. And the good seed are the children of the kingdom. And the [cockle]{g:zizania} are the children of the wicked one. And the enemy that sowed them, is the devil. But the harvest is the end of the world. And the reapers are the angels."[^2] As, therefore, the Truth speaks in these in parables, as the gospel says, so he seems to speak figuratively in the four gospels, namely Matt. 26:26, Mark 14:22, Luke 22:19–20, 1 Cor. 11:24, when he says that the [bread]{g:panis} and [wine]{g:vinum} are his body and blood, as the Apostle teaches in 1 Cor. 11:24 (as will appear [84] later).[^3] Yet there is a difference in the manners of speaking, since one is tropological or moral and the other allegorical or sacramental; for it has the efficacy of making [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} and blood to be in fact under the sacramental [species]{g:species}, which the other [figures]{g:figura}, both of the new law and of the old, fell short of.[^4]

### 04-a ¶3 · 84.7–86.20  (14 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 84] **Sed** ulterius notandum quod sicut rex habet triplex esse in regno, scilicet situale, intencionale et potenciale, correspondenter videtur Christum habere humanitus in mundo modum essendi triplicem; habet enim in celo ubi sedet ad dextram Dei modum essendi situalem, cum ibi situatur loco per quem diffunditur coequatus. Modum **autem** essendi potencialem videtur habere eciam humanitus, cum sit rex regum et dominus dominancium ad quamlibet partem mundi; verumptamen (ut dicunt multorum sanctorum testimonia) non habet humanitus esse situale vel dimensionale per totum mundum, **sed** limitate in celo ubi ponitur, **sed** per totum mundum habet esse essenciale divinitus; quoad medium **autem** esse sunt in Christo ultra alios homines divisiones multiplices; habet **autem** per tantum celi per quantum multiplicatur species sui corporis esse presens intencionale quo (quantum ad corpus suum) pascuntur oculi beatorum; et ista specierum multiplicacio corporis Christi secundum naturales est striccior et secundum magicos et perspectivos diffusior; limites **autem** istius diffusionis expedit nobis viantibus ignorare. **Sed** illud credimus quod beati in celo post diem iudicii ex elongacione a corpore Christi non pacientur angustias, **sed** beaciores non obstante maioritate [p. 85] distancie videbunt corpus Christi oculo corporali beacius; stat enim sensibile in maiori distancia videri clarius, quanto magis in beatitudine, ubi sensualis noticia ultra presenciam sensibilis secundum aliam legem emanabit ab anima. Unde pie credimus ordinem posicionis beatorum quoad corpus Christi esse regulatissimam in qua omnes beati mutuo gaudio quietantur. **Sed** preter istum modum essendi intencionalem quoad humanitatem Christi videtur quod secundo alii probabiliter sunt ponendi, scilicet modus essendi supernaturalis et modus essendi sacramentalis. Primo modo potest intelligi illud Math. ultimo 20: Ecce ego vobiscum sum omnibus diebus usque ad consumacionem seculi. Cum enim secundum fidem scripture Hebr. IIº, 17 Christus sit frater noster, videtur quod spectat caritati sue quod sit saltem in mente cum fratribus iuxta illud Math. XVIIIº, 20: Ubi duo vel tres congregati sunt in nomine meo, ibi sum ego in medio eorum. Si enim secundum evangelium Apostoli conversacio apostolorum in terra viancium est in celis (ut dicitur Phil. IIIº, 20, et a sanctis exponitur), evidencius anima Christi est cum fratribus quibus afficitur. Sic enim legitur IV. Reg. Vº, 26 de Elizeo multum distante a Gezi et Naaman: Nonne cor meum in presenti erat, quando reversus est homo de curru suo in occursum tui? Talia sunt multa dicta et argumenta ex quibus tam fideles quam infideles convincunt quod anima hominis est nedum ubi afficitur, **sed** eciam ubi a Deo ponitur ad aliquid singulariter cognoscendum. Modus **autem** essendi sacramentalis licet varietur in Christo secundum [p. 86] distinccionem sacramentorum, tamen specialiter est in hostia consecrata, quia omnes quatuor evangeliste Matthaeus, Marcus, Lucas et Paulus commemorant quomodo Christus dixit panem et vinum esse corpus suum et sanguinem; que locucio ex solempnitate loquentis et circumstancie captate ac testimonio sanctorum oportet quod habeat aliquam insolitam efficaciam ultra alia signa novi vel veteris testamenti; nam crux muta, benediccio laycalis et alia signa que facimus, **sed** specialiter in die Parasceues, significant atque commemorant corpus Christi et sanguinem. **Sed** magister optimus et Cesar semper Augustus in cena ultima qua prandebat vians cum suis apostolis, post esum pure figuralem agni paschalis introduxit sollempniter hoc sacramentum; ex quo patet, cum oportet Christum meliorando procedere, quod hoc sacramentum sit efficacius et rei sacramenti propinquius quam sacramenta antiqua. Aliter enim non foret racio quare illa cessarent novo non excedente superflue introducta.

ENGLISH: But further it is to be noted that, as a king has a threefold [being]{g:esse} in a kingdom, namely [situal]{g:esse-situale}, intentional and potential, correspondingly it seems that Christ has, [humanly]{g:humanitus}, a threefold mode of being in the world.[^5] For in heaven, where he sits at the right hand of God, he has a situal mode of being, since there he is situated in a place through which he is spread out, made equal [to it]. And he seems to have a potential mode of being, also humanly, since he is "King of kings and Lord of lords" to every part of the world.[^6] Nevertheless (as the testimonies of many [saints]{g:sancti} say) he does not have, humanly, situal or dimensional being throughout the whole world, but limitedly in heaven, where he is placed; but throughout the whole world he has essential being, [divinely]{g:divinitus}.[^7] And as to the middle being, there are in Christ, beyond other men, manifold divisions. And he has, through as much of heaven as the species of his body is multiplied through, a present intentional being, by which (as to his body) the eyes of the blessed are fed; and this multiplication of the species of the body of Christ is narrower according to the natural philosophers, and more diffuse according to the magicians and [the perspectivists]{g:perspectivi}.[^8] But the [limits]{g:limites} of this diffusion it is expedient for us, [who are on the road]{g:viantes}, [to be ignorant of]{g:ignorare}. But this we believe: that the blessed in heaven after the day of judgement will not suffer distress from their distance from the body of Christ, but the more blessed, notwithstanding the [greatness]{g:majoritas} [85] of the distance, will see the body of Christ more blessedly with the bodily eye. For it can stand that a [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} thing is seen more clearly at a greater distance; how much more in blessedness, where sensual knowledge, beyond the presence of the perceptible thing, will flow from the soul by another law. Hence we [piously]{g:pie} believe the order of the position of the blessed with respect to the body of Christ to be most regulated, in which all the blessed are set at rest in mutual joy.[^9] But besides this intentional mode of being, as to the [humanity]{g:humanitas} of Christ, it seems that secondly others are to be posited [with probability]{g:probabiliter}, namely a supernatural mode of being and a sacramental mode of being. In the first way that [word] of Matthew, the last [chapter], 20, can be understood: "Behold I am with you all days, even to the consummation of the world." For since, according to the faith of scripture, Heb. 2:17, Christ is our brother, it seems that it belongs to his [charity]{g:caritas} that he be at least in mind with his brothers, according to that of Matt. 18:20: "Where there are two or three gathered together in my name, there am I in the midst of them."[^10] For if, according to the Apostle's gospel, the "[manner of life]{g:conversacio}" of the apostles, while on the road on earth, "is in heaven" (as is said in Phil. 3:20, and expounded by the saints), more evidently is the soul of Christ with the brothers toward whom he is moved in affection.[^11] For so it is read in 2 Kings 5:26 of Eliseus, far distant from Giezi and Naaman: "Was not my heart present, when the man turned back, from his chariot, to meet thee?"[^12] Such are many sayings and arguments from which both believers and [unbelievers]{g:infidelis} prove conclusively that the soul of a man is not only where it is moved in affection, but also where it is placed by God to know something [in a singular way]{g:singulariter}.[^13] And the sacramental mode of being, although it varies in Christ according to the [86] distinction of the [sacraments]{g:sacramentum}, yet is especially in the consecrated [host]{g:hostia}; for all four evangelists, Matthew, Mark, Luke and Paul, record how Christ said that the bread and wine are his body and blood; and this speech, from the solemnity of the speaker and of the circumstance chosen, and from the testimony of the saints, must have some unwonted efficacy beyond the other signs of the new or the old testament. For the dumb cross, the lay blessing, and the other signs that we make, but especially on the day of Parasceve, signify and commemorate the body of Christ and his blood.[^14] But the best master, and Caesar ever August, at the last supper, at which he dined [as a wayfarer]{g:vians} with his apostles, after the purely figural [eating]{g:esus} of the paschal lamb, solemnly introduced this sacrament;[^15] from which it is clear, since Christ must proceed by bettering, that this sacrament is more efficacious and nearer to the [reality of the sacrament]{g:res-sacramenti} than the ancient sacraments. For otherwise there would be no reason why those should cease, the new, introduced superfluously, not exceeding [them].[^16]

### 04-a ¶4 · 86.21–87.28  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 86] Nec dubium fideli quin Christus reliquit istud ut sacramentum sacerdotibus celebrandum, quia Luce XXIIº, 19 mandat generaliter episcopis scole sue: Hoc facite in meam commemoracionem. Cum ergo figure corporis Christi in lege veteri profuerunt suis servantibus ad salutem (ut patet prima Cor. Xº), videtur quod oportet figuram novam huiusmodi corporis ipsas excedere; quod foret efficax et consonum scripture ponere in presencia corporis et sanguinis figurati, cum humanitas Christi sit quodammodo in aliis sacramentis, ergo multo magis in isto, cum verba huiusmodi sacramenti efficaciter illud vocant; et hoc confirmant facta discipulorum Christi memoriam eius in [p. 87] isto sacramento celebrancium, sentencie sanctorum doctorum de fundacione istius sacramenti ex verbis scripture scribencium et multiplices leges ecclesie. Unde dicitur communiter quod hoc sacramentum excedit alia in hiis tribus: Primo quod, ubi alia sacramenta multum distant a re sacramenti tam loco quam tempore, hoc sacramentum habet rem suam tam loco quam tempore concomitantem. Nam agnus paschalis legis veteris (de quo Exodi XIIº) multum distitit loco et tempore a corpore Christi; hoc **autem** sacramentum habet totam humanitatem Christi necessario comitantem. Secundo ubi alie figure veteris testamenti defecerunt ab efficacia conficiendi concomitanciam rei sacramenti, figura huius rei habet efficaciam efficienter causandi non corpus Christi **sed** quandam eius presenciam sacramentum sensibile concomitantem; unde non solum verba **sed** panis et vinum cum suis accidentibus dicuntur coniunctim et divisim sacramentum corporis Christi, et sic quamvis omnia ista sint unum sacramentum, tamen sunt valde multa sacramenta, que omnia reducuntur, ut spiritus aqua et sanguis, ad unum in effectu. Unde hoc sacramentum quoad eius permanenciam cum comitancia rei sue excedit sacramenta alia legis nove. Et tercio videtur quod hoc sacramentum excedit alia nedum quoad graciam unionis membrorum ecclesie cum Christo, **sed** rite celebratum prodest ministranti super alia sacramenta.

ENGLISH: Nor is it doubtful to a faithful man that Christ left this as a sacrament to be celebrated by priests, since in Luke 22:19 he commands the [bishops]{g:episcopus} of his school generally: "Do this for a commemoration of me." Since, therefore, the figures of the body of Christ in the old law profited those who kept them unto salvation (as is clear from 1 Cor. 10), it seems that the new figure of this body must exceed them; and it would be effective and consonant with scripture to place it in the presence of the body and blood [figured]{g:figuratum}; since the humanity of Christ is in some manner in the other sacraments, therefore much more in this one, since the words of this sacrament efficaciously call it [there].[^17] And this is confirmed by the deeds of the disciples of Christ, celebrating his memory in [87] this sacrament, by the [teachings]{g:sentencia} of the holy doctors, writing of the founding of this sacrament from the words of scripture, and by the manifold [laws of the church]{g:leges-ecclesie}.[^18] Hence it is commonly said that this sacrament exceeds the others in these three [respects]. First, that whereas the other sacraments are far distant from the reality of the sacrament both in place and in time, this sacrament has its reality concomitant with it both in place and in time. For the paschal lamb of the old law (of which Exod. 12) was far distant in place and time from the body of Christ; but this sacrament has the whole humanity of Christ necessarily accompanying it. Second, whereas the other figures of the old testament fell short of the efficacy of making the concomitance of the reality of the sacrament, the figure of this reality has the efficacy of efficiently causing, not the body of Christ, but a certain presence of it accompanying the perceptible sacrament;[^19] hence not only the words but the bread and wine with their accidents are called, jointly and severally, the sacrament of the body of Christ; and so, although all these are one sacrament, yet they are very many sacraments, which are all reduced, as the spirit, the water and the blood, to one [in actual fact]{g:in-effectu}.[^20] Hence this sacrament, as to its permanence together with the accompaniment of its reality, exceeds the other sacraments of the new law. And third, it seems that this sacrament exceeds the others not only as to [the grace of union]{g:gracia-unionis} of the [members]{g:membrum} of the [church]{g:ecclesia} with Christ, but, rightly celebrated, it profits the one ministering above the other sacraments.[^21]
