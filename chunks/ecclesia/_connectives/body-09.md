### 22-e ¶1 · 532.21–533.23  (6 sed/autem)

Nec contendo contra volentem defendere quod licet particulariter orare pro dampnato, dum tamen orator hoc ignoraverit, quia talis oracio nonnisi equivoce est pro illo, **sed** intendens quod sit pro illo faciliter meretur veniam; nec valet iuramentum oratoris, quo obligat se ad orandum annaliter temporaliter pro defuncto, **sed** amici defuncti debent appetere quod generaliter oretur pro mortuis in purgatorio, et talis oracio tantum vel plus sibi valet sicut oracio specialis, supposito quod sit ibi; et nulla oracio valet sibi dummodo sit dampnatus. Ideo utrimque non mediocriter peccant qui errando postponunt melius et eligunt minus bonum. Quando ergo dicitur quod oratur pro dampnato, intelligitur quod pretenditur orare pro eo, quod fit nunc licite et nunc male.

ENGLISH: the divine will, placing in his will what he has decreed to succour and how; and such ought to be the prayer of [wayfarers]{g:viator}; or secondly as regards the capacity of the [subject]{g:subiectum} for whom one intends to pray; and so, his incapacity supposed, that subject is not the person for whom prayer is so made, but either the one praying himself or another capable person of the [church]{g:ecclesia}.[^1] And as to what is commonly said, that one prays for such a one, intending [to aid him by intercession]{g:suffragari}, although he is [unqualified]{g:inhabilis}, there is equivocation on a prayer in name only; as perhaps the prayer of an unworthy man is not before God [univocally]{g:univoce} a prayer for anyone.[^2] It is lawful, therefore, to pray for a [foreknown]{g:prescitus} wayfarer, since he is capable of merit, so that he may be punished more mildly; as is the case with the foreknown who at another's prayer do not only many things [533] good in kind, but meritorious [according to present justice]{g:secundum presentem iusticiam}, by reason of which they will be damned more mildly and meanwhile will merit for the church.[^3] But since it is certain that they are damned, to pray further for them would be wholly useless; indeed the prayer of a catholic is not for them, since it is impossible that it profit them; and one who contends that he prays for such a dead man can mortify his own prayer by contending.[^4] Nor do I contend against one who wishes to defend that it is lawful to pray particularly for one damned, provided only that the one praying is ignorant of it, because such prayer is only [equivocally]{g:equivoce} for him; but one [maintaining]{g:intendere} that it is for him easily merits pardon. Nor does the oath of the [bedesman]{g:orator} avail, by which he binds himself to pray yearly, for a term, for the departed; but the friends of the departed ought to desire that prayer be made generally for the dead in purgatory, and such prayer avails him as much as, or more than, special prayer, supposing that he is there; and no prayer avails him if he is damned.[^5] Therefore those on both sides sin not moderately who in error put the better after and choose the lesser good. When therefore it is said that prayer is made for one damned, it is understood that one professes to pray for him, which is done now lawfully and now wrongly.[^6]

Indeed, if prayer is made for everything bettered by occasion of it, then the prayers of the saints for the coming of Christ in the flesh would be made for the whole world: not only for all the beasts and the damned, but indeed for all inanimate things and every single creature.[^1] For all these receive perfection and betterment, indeed even sin itself does, by virtue of the passion of Christ; and yet theologians do not speak so broadly, as neither does holy scripture, as to say that one must pray for such things as receiving the intended fruit of prayer; although it could be said that men pray for such things as following by accident upon the fruit intended of itself. Hence Augustine, *City of God* 21, chapter 25, says that it would be very contrary to sound [doctrine]{g:doctrina} that heresiarchs [be freed] who received in the church baptism, the eucharist and other gifts of grace, but afterwards ungratefully attack the church more sharply than other [unbelievers]{g:infidelis}, since these are not so ungrateful. "Worse," he says, "is the deserter of the faith, and one made from a deserter into its assailant, than he who has not deserted what he never held."[^7] From these another sense can be gathered of the sayings of blessed Augustine, *Enchiridion* 110, which are set in the Decretum, C.13 q.2, *Tempus*.[^8] For he lays down first that in this middle space of time between a man's death and the day of judgement it is possible to merit or to help the spirits of the departed. He lays down secondly that suffrages profit those who, while they lived, merited that these might afterwards be able to profit them. Thirdly he says that, of the four kinds of the dead, the merits of the survivors are, for the very good, thanksgivings; for the moderately good, propitiations; for the very bad they are consolations of the living; but for the moderately bad they make their damnations more tolerable: so that on every side pious prayers intended for the dead avail; for to the blessed they are turned into thanksgivings, to those to be purged into propitiations; and to the damned, although they are not helps, yet they profit the church militant; and for the damned who, [538] while they survived, merited by good works, they are the cause why they are punished more mildly (as I touched on in the last chapter of book 5).[^9]

### 22-g ¶2 · 540.17–542.18  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 540] In isto dicto videtur innuere triplicem racionem: Prima ex hoc quod nemo debet orare sibi ipsi contrarie, **sed** oramus Deum: Fiat voluntas tua undique, ideo non debemus velle huic contrarie. Secunda racio est ex hoc quod oportet salvandum meritorie et per consequens voluntarie pati mortem. Tercia racio stat in isto quod diligens se vel alterum debet gaudenter acceptare accessum eius proprium, sicut est mors cuilibet salvando, et prescito eciam est ad bonum, cum continue plus nocet ecclesie et amplius demeretur. In oppositum sunt consuetudo et leges ecclesie de cura agenda pro mortuis et ecclesiastica sepultura, unde oblaciones Jude Machabei laudat ecclesia, Machabeorum XII°, et sepultura mortuorum computatur inter opera misericordie, pro [p. 541] quo laudatur Tobias Tobie. Unde decretum recitat Augustinus De Cura agenda pro mortuis sub hiis verbis: Quamvis cure pro mortuis non pro omnibus pro quibus fiunt prosint, **sed** hiis tantum pro quibus dum viverent ut prosint meruerant; **sed** quia non discernimus qui sint illi, oportet illa pro regeneratis omnibus facere. Et assignat causam eius triplicem: Prima est profectus mortuorum capacium, secunda est excitacio affectus superstitum, et tercia est naturalis instinctus et meritorius, dum fuerit caritate formatus curam talem agencium. Pro prima sic scribit: Melius enim supererunt ista hiis quibus nec obsunt nec prosunt, quam eis deerunt quibus prosunt. Vult dicere quod oportet intendere orare generaliter pro cunctis fidelibus, subintelligendo et Deo limitante suffragium capacibus. Pro secunda causa patet quod ex hoc excitatur devocio superstitum et provocatur occupacio circa actum meritorium. Unde Augustinus: Diligencius facit hoc quisque pro amicis suis, ut pro illo fiat similiter. Et quoad terciam causam patet, cum necesse sit evacuare cadavera bestiarum, multo magis cadavera hominum, magis carorum et plus inficiencium. Unde Augustinus: Humanitatis, inquit, hoc est officium secundum affectum quo nemo unquam carnem suam odio habet. Unde oportet ut quam potest pro carne proximi curam gerat, cum spiritus inde recesserit qui hoc gerebat. Et si hoc faciunt qui carnis resurreccionem non credunt, quanto magis [p. 542] debent facere qui credunt ut corpori resurrecturo et eternaliter mansuro impensum officium sit quodammodo evidens eiusdem fidei testimonium. Et patet cum talis operacio non sit per se virtuosa, **sed** valde per accidens quod contingit eam nunc bene nunc male fieri. Cavere **autem** debemus de malicia ne fastui seculi principaliter intendamus, nec magis necessarium omittamus; nichil enim est magis necessarium obsequium agendum pro mortuis quam vivere virtuose. Ille ergo inordinate agit curam corporalem pro suo mortuo qui plus attendit ad illam quam ad superhabundanciam virtutis in animo. Vis ergo affectuose proficere anime defunctorum, exercita te ipsum in scola virtutum; quod quanto plus feceris, tanto cum paribus facis mortuis plus profectum; oportet ergo iuxta caritatis regulas quod superstes misereatur anime proprie placens Deo, et ad illud consequitur efficacissime suffragium mortuorum.

The first, from this: that no one ought to pray contrary to himself; now we pray God, "Thy will be done" everywhere; therefore we ought not to will contrary to this. The second reason is from this: that one who is to be saved must suffer death meritoriously, and consequently willingly. The third reason consists in this: that one who loves himself or another ought joyfully to accept his own advancement, as death is to everyone who is to be saved; and for the [foreknown]{g:prescitus} also it is for good, since he continually harms the [church]{g:ecclesia} more and demerits further.[^9] On the opposite side are the [custom]{g:consuetudo} and laws of the church about the care to be had for the dead and about ecclesiastical burial: hence the church praises the offerings of Judas Maccabeus, Maccabees 12, and the burial of the dead is counted among the works of mercy, for [541] which Tobias is praised in Tobias.[^10] Hence the decree cites Augustine, *On the Care to Be Had for the Dead*, in these words: "Although the cares taken for the dead do not profit all for whom they are made, but only those who, while they lived, had merited that they should profit them; yet because we do not discern who these are, it is fitting to do them for all the regenerate."[^11] And he assigns a threefold cause of it: the first is the profit of the dead who are capable; the second is the stirring of the affection of the survivors; and the third is the natural instinct, meritorious when it has been formed by [charity]{g:caritas}, of those who take such care. For the first he writes thus: "For it is better that these things be superfluous to those whom they neither harm nor profit, than that they be lacking to those whom they profit." He means that one must intend to pray generally for all the faithful, [understanding]{g:subintelligere} the condition as implied, and with God [assigning]{g:limitare} the suffrage to those who are capable.[^12] For the second cause, it is clear that by this the devotion of the survivors is stirred and their occupation with a meritorious act is called forth. Hence Augustine: "Each one does this more diligently for his friends, so that it may be done likewise for him." And as to the third cause it is clear, since it is necessary to clear away the carcasses of beasts, much more the corpses of men, who are dearer and infect more. Hence Augustine: "This," he says, "is an office of humanity, according to the affection by which no one ever hated his own flesh. Hence it is fitting that one take what care one can for a neighbour's flesh, when the spirit that took this care has departed from it. And if those do this who do not believe the resurrection of the flesh, how much more [542] ought those to do it who believe, so that the office spent on a body that will rise again and remain eternally may be in a way a manifest testimony of that same faith."[^13] And it is clear, since such a work is not virtuous of itself but very much by accident, that it happens to be done now well, now badly. And we must beware of malice, lest we aim principally at the pomp of the world, and lest we omit what is more necessary; for there is no more necessary duty to be done for the dead than to live virtuously. He therefore takes bodily care for his dead inordinately who attends more to that than to the superabundance of virtue in the soul.[^14] Do you wish, then, with affection to profit the soul of the departed? Exercise yourself in the school of the virtues; and the more you do this, the more profit, other things being equal, you make for the dead. It is needful, therefore, according to the rules of charity, that the survivor "have pity on his own soul, pleasing God"; and on that follows most effectually the suffrage of the dead.[^15]

Inferiores **autem** ut abbates et presbiteri non possunt indulgencias tales concedere publice ex auctoritate iurisdicionis, **sed** private in foro penitencie (ut patet in De Penitenciis et Remissionibus, capitulo Indulgencie); super vero conceduntur per Innocentem et Archidiaconum: licet, inquit, restricta sit potestas de indulgenciis que fiunt publice, non tamen in foro penitenciali in quo sacerdos potest indulgere annos et dies dummodo discrete faciat et penitenciam iniunctam per superiorem non remittat.

ENGLISH: It remains next to treat of [indulgences]{g:indulgencie} and of absolutions from punishment and guilt, in which there seems to be error among simple people who are ignorant of this matter.[^1] First, then, I suppose the signification of this sign *indulgencia*, [what the name means]{g:quid nominis}. For every indulgence is an act of one who indulges, so that indulgence is nothing other than one who has the power for it indulging; and to indulge is to grant freely, to give one's effort, or to remit; and it is compounded of *inde* and *algere* (which is to be cold or to be mortified), as is the case with offences handed over to oblivion as regards the seeking of vengeance.[^2] And so scripture speaks in Judith 8:14: "Let us with tears beg the Lord's indulgence"; and so it is taken in Isaiah 26, 61 and 63.[^3] And to indulge sinners in this sense belongs to God alone, as also to forgive sins (as is clear in the last chapter of the fifth book);[^4] but the term has been drawn out of these senses and baptized into another sense, partly by the doctors of theology and partly by the canonists. For sometimes, according to them, it connotes remission of punishment, as in the second question, third chapter, *Indulgencie*, as the Archdeacon notes: that sometimes it signifies actively a grant, as "the lord pope indulged a subject this or that", that is, granted; and sometimes it signifies a dispensation, sometimes a right, as John notes on C.33 q.2 *Interfectores*; and sometimes it is taken for "to remit" or "to pardon", in *De penitenciis* [550] *et remissionibus*, chapter *Indulgencie*, at the beginning, by the Archdeacon.[^5] Hence they draw all these indulgences in and [limit]{g:limitare} them to the [prelates]{g:prelatus} of the [church]{g:ecclesia}. Hence they lay down for themselves as a rule that the [pope]{g:papa} alone can give indulgences general as to place and time, but bishops have a limited power of making indulgences. For when they dedicate churches their indulgence does not extend beyond a year, even if the dedication is made by several bishops; and in other cases a bishop cannot indulge beyond the number of forty days. Nevertheless a bishop confirmed before he is a priest, as also legates who are not priests, make indulgences, because this power is theirs from jurisdiction rather than from order.[^6] And inferiors, such as abbots and presbyters, cannot grant such indulgences publicly by authority of jurisdiction, but privately in the court of penance (as is clear in *De penitenciis et remissionibus*, chapter *Indulgencie*); and on the word *conceduntur*, by Innocent and the Archdeacon: "Although," he says, "the power is restricted concerning indulgences which are made publicly, yet not in the penitential court, in which a priest can indulge years and days, provided he does it discreetly and does not remit a penance enjoined by a superior."[^7]

And this gloss is more pertinent to the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of the decretists and more conformable to their sayings, (as is clear in *De penitenciis et remissionibus*, chapter *Quod autem*) at the words "But you will ask what it means".[^1] And the foundation of this teaching is taken from this fantastic imagining. For it is supposed, first, that there are supererogated merits of the saints, and especially of Christ, in heaven, infinite in number and value, beyond what is required for their blessedness; and all these are called the spiritual treasury of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, laid up in its [head]{g:caput}.[^2] It is supposed, secondly, that the lord [pope]{g:papa}, since he is [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius Christi} on earth, has full power of distributing that treasury to the faithful, but as much as he pleases, more or less; and it is not lawful to doubt that the power of imparting these spiritual [suffrages]{g:suffragia} remains continually unexhausted, since otherwise Christ would have failed in giving the power of the keys to his vicar here on earth.[^3] Thirdly, it is said that when the pope or his bishop dispenses to subjects from this treasury, [exchanging]{g:commutacio} the merit of a blessed one in place of the merit by which the sinner would merit remission of sin, then he grants indulgence, more or less. And on this foundation these are said to sell their sins and those to buy them, even for money; and the people has been seduced by an impossible and groundless barter, for the sake of the pomp and the gain to be acquired by the priest.[^4] And Saint Thomas seems to assent to this teaching, on the fourth book of the *Sentences*, distinction 20, question [552] 3: "It is granted by all," he says, "that indulgences avail something, since it would be impious to say that the church does anything in vain. But this [opinion]{g:opinio} does not seem true: first, because it is expressly against the [privilege]{g:privilegium} given to Peter, that what he remitted on earth should be remitted in heaven; hence the remission which is made as to the court of the church avails also as to the court of God. Again, the church making an indulgence of this kind would harm more than help, since it would remit them to heavier punishments, namely those of purgatory, by absolving from enjoined penances."[^5] And the deduction is manifestly clear, since according to the opinion an indulgence does nothing toward remission in purgatory; but with the indulgence withdrawn, one would work in the fullness of penance toward remission in purgatory, which would be infinitely more excellent than any remission of the penance of the way. Therefore on this view every indulgence would hinder the fruit of penance and be harmful; therefore the canonists prudently say that an enjoined penance [553] for sin is not to be dropped on account of indulgences; nor would indulgences of many thousands of years have [colour]{g:color} as to the bare mitigation of penance here on the way, since we are certain that so great a penance would not be undergone even by Methuselah here on the way.[^6] "Therefore," says the doctor, "it must be said otherwise: that indulgences avail both as to the court of the church and as to the judgment of God, and as to the remission of the punishment remaining after contrition, confession and absolution, whether it was enjoined or not. Now the reason why they can avail is the unity of the [mystical body]{g:corpus Christi misticum}, in which many in works of penance have paid out beyond the measure of their debts, and have also patiently borne many tribulations unjustly, by which a multitude of punishments could be expiated, if they were owed to them. Of which merits so great is the plenty that they exceed all punishment owed to those now living, and especially on account of the merit of Christ; which, though it works in the sacraments, yet its efficacy is not shut up in the sacraments, but by its infinity it exceeds the efficacy of the sacraments.[^7] Therefore, since one can make satisfaction for another, and the saints in whom a superabundance of works of satisfaction is found did such works not determinately for this man who needs remission (for otherwise he would obtain remission without any indulgence), or they were done in common for the whole church, as the apostle says that he fills up those things that are wanting to the passion of Christ in his body for the church, to which he writes, Corinthians 1: so the aforesaid merits, though they are common to the whole church," yet the pope distributes them unequally, as those "that are common to some multitude are distributed to the individuals of the multitude according to the judgment [554] of him who presides over the multitude. Hence, if anyone should obtain remission of punishment as though another had made satisfaction for him, so that the satisfaction of another is distributed to him by one who is able"; so it is to be judged of indulgences.[^8]

### 23-c ¶4 · 556.9–556.21  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 556] Quantum ad primum sic arguo: Deus nedum potest de sua absoluta potestate quecunque peccata eciam sine satisfaccione remittere, **sed** unus potest de communi cursu pro alio satisfacere, et per consequens, Deus potest committere suo vicario potenciam contra quecunque peccata taliter dispensandi. ENGLISH: As to the first, I argue thus: not only can God, of his [absolute power]{g:potencia absoluta}, remit any sins whatever even without satisfaction, but one can, in the common course, make satisfaction for another; and consequently God can commit to his vicar the power of dispensing in this way against any sins whatever. ### 23-c ¶7 · 557.3–557.32  (1 sed/autem)

ENGLISH: The [assumption]{g:assumptum} of the first argument, then, is shown thus. Again, it is possible for one to bring condemnation on another, as is clear of the first parents and of many others, on account of their heads and associates who were accused. ### 23-c ¶8 · 557.33–558.21  (2 sed/autem)

Exemplum secundi est quomodo nedum Christus **sed** alia membra iuvant ad plenam satisfaccionem confratrum; et tercio modo unus satisfacit pro debito particulari alterius particulari creditori. In utraque primarum satisfaccionum oportet debitorem concurrere, ut quamvis Christus fecit se naturam humanam que debuit Deo satisfaccionem pro peccato primi hominis, tamen illa satisfaccio non potuit membris proficere, nisi ex adiutorio capitis se disponentibus active ad suscipiendum medicinam vel saltem ad consolvendum ministracionem debitam, in tantum quod infans unius diei decedens in gracia primo baptizatur flamine, secundo servit Deo meritorie et sic tercio participat fructum celestis medicine.

ENGLISH: First, then, this [conclusion]{g:conclusio} is to be granted: that it is possible for one to make satisfaction for another. But it must be noted [558] that this can be understood in three ways: first participatively, by making satisfaction for one's own share; secondly helpingly, by co-causing the whole satisfaction in one wholly other; and thirdly of oneself, without the concurrence of the other, by making satisfaction for the whole debt.[^12] An example of the first is in the assumption of human nature by Christ in an individual, and the completion of the first-fruits of the whole debt of the human race for sin. An example of the second is how not only Christ but other members help toward the full satisfaction of their brethren; and in the third way one makes satisfaction for the particular debt of another to a particular creditor.[^13] In each of the first two satisfactions the debtor must concur; so that, although Christ made himself the human nature which owed God satisfaction for the sin of the first man, yet that satisfaction could not profit the members unless, by the help of the head, they disposed themselves actively to receive the medicine, or at least to pay together the service owed; so much so that an infant of one day, dying in grace, is first [baptized by the Spirit]{g:baptismus flaminis}, secondly serves God meritoriously, and so thirdly shares in the fruit of the heavenly medicine.[^14]

### 23-d ¶2 · 559.14–560.5  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 559] Ad secundum argumentum patet quod conclusio est concedenda, **sed** nunquam potest unus dampnare alium quoad Deum, nisi ipse superaddat proprium delictum, ut patet in materia de originali peccato, et ideo canit fidelis ecclesia quod nulla ei nocebit adversitas, si nulla dominetur iniquitas. Et patet quod licet brachium se exponat pro capite, tamen oportet caput quodammodo se iuvare. Ideo quamvis quodlibet membrum iuvet aliud ad agendum, tamen nullum membrum supplet omnino alterius membri officium, **sed** iuvando satisfacit; et sic est de corpore Christi mistico, quia aliter posset Deus omnino eximere hominem a debito servitutis quoad Deum, quod notum est claudere repugnanciam formalem. Ideo sicut Deus in opere creacionis non potest habere naturam coadiuvantem [p. 560] propter solitudinem sue supereminencie, sic creatura non potest opus quodcunque perficere nisi cum iuvamine alterius nature propter per se subieccionem servitutis et essencialem dependenciam a suo principio. Et per hec patet responsio.

ENGLISH: To the second argument it is clear that the [conclusion]{g:conclusio} is to be granted; but one can never damn another as regards God unless the other adds his own offence besides, as is clear in the matter of original sin; and therefore the faithful [church]{g:ecclesia} sings that "no adversity shall harm her, if no iniquity [exercise lordship]{g:dominari} over her." How, I ask, could God damn one not guilty, and consequently not damnable?[^5] And the goodness of God is clear in this: that he saves no one except one whom he goes before by helping, nor damns anyone except one from whom, going before him, he takes occasion by sin.[^6] And it is clear that although the arm exposes itself for the [head]{g:caput}, yet the head must in some way help itself. Therefore, although each [member]{g:membrum} helps another to act, yet no member wholly supplies the office of another member, but makes satisfaction by helping; and so it is with the [mystical body of Christ]{g:corpus Christi misticum}, because otherwise God could wholly exempt a man from the debt of service as regards God, which is known to imply a formal repugnance.[^7] Therefore, just as God in the work of creation cannot have a nature helping together with him, [560] because of the solitude of his supereminence, so a creature cannot perfect any work whatever except with the help of another nature, because of the subjection of service that belongs to it of itself and its essential dependence on its principle. And by these things the reply is clear.[^8]

### 23-d ¶3 · 560.6–561.18  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 560] Ad tercium dicitur negando minorem, nam oportet Deum in omni satisfaccione creature sue concurrere et est in racione debiti hominis ad hominem ac in racione debiti hominis ad Deum neganda differencia. Homo enim potest omnino solvi a corporali debito sui ad hominem, quia non est essencialiter debitor sui pro omni bono quod possidet, quia tunc alius foret Deus suus, **sed** nemo potest omnino eximi a debito Dei sui, quia tunc non teneretur Deo pro esse suo, **sed** foret Deus simpliciter par cum illo. Ideo ista est condicio obligacionis hominis ad Deum, quod de quanto copiosius et complecius solvit sibi debitum de tanto est obligacior quoad eum, quia de tanto plus boni ab eo recipit. Et hec racio quare homo potest recipere satisfaccionem debitoris sui in vita vel morte eius per procuratorem ipso non cooperante ad hoc. Deus **autem** non potest recipere a servo suo satisfaccionem pro debito nisi ipse in persona propria satisfaciat adiutus per Deum, et hoc testatur immensitatem libertatis Dei et excellenciam sui dominii, ut non oportet, si homo potest libere ac meritorie exuere se a dominio servi sui subiciendo se sue servituti, quod deitas sic potest quoad servum simpliciter; homo potest relinquere domini superioris vindictam offense non vindicando in persona propria, ergo Deus sic potest; homo potest dimittere culpam non expetendo sibi emendam satisfactoriam, ergo Deus sic potest, et sic de infinitis exemplis attestantibus infirmitatem humani dominii et invincibilitatem dominii divini. Et patet [p. 561] quod ista sentencia est pia fidelibus sese consolantibus in domino et non blaspheme exigentibus honorem debitum soli Deo, unde qui putat peccatum commutari vel vendi pro nudo numismate implicat quod Deus vendit a se dominium parificans sibi creaturam a qua non exigit servitutem, et per consequens Deus venderet se ipsum, quia insuperabilem iusticiam, pro vili precio proprio, ut puta, pro minuta, cum pro qualibet parte precii venderet indivisibilem iusticiam que est Deus. **Sed** quid rogo blasfemius? Ideo si quis peccat in Deum, oportet quod ipse in persona propria expectat satisfaccionem vel satispassionem ab eadem persona, qui in eum peccaverit. Et patet quod primum assumptum est falsum, videlicet quod Deus potest peccata sine satisfaccione dimittere vel communicare vicario suo potenciam vel peccata taliter remittendi vel contra peccata taliter dispensandi. Hoc enim est de regalia propria deitati.

ENGLISH: To the third the answer is by denying the minor; for God must concur in every satisfaction of his creature, and in the notion of the debt of man to man and in the notion of the debt of man to God a difference is [not] to be denied.[^9] For a man can be wholly released from his bodily debt to a man, because he is not essentially that man's debtor for every good that he possesses, for then the other would be his God; but no one can be wholly exempted from the debt to his God, because then he would not be bound to God for his being, but God would be, [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}, on a par with him. Therefore this is the condition of man's obligation to God: that by as much as he pays him the debt more copiously and more completely, by so much is he the more obliged as regards him, because by so much the more good does he receive from him.[^10] And this is the reason why a man can receive the satisfaction of his debtor, in his life or at his death, through a proctor, the debtor himself not cooperating in it. But God cannot receive from his servant satisfaction for a debt unless the servant makes satisfaction in his own person, helped by God; and this attests the immensity of God's freedom and the excellence of his [lordship]{g:dominium}; so that it need not be, if a man can freely and meritoriously strip himself of lordship over his servant by subjecting himself to that servant's service, that the [Godhead]{g:deitas} can do so as regards one who is a servant without qualification. A man can leave to a superior lord the avenging of an offence, by not avenging it in his own person: therefore God can do so? A man can forgive a fault by not demanding of the offender amends by way of satisfaction: therefore God can do so? And so with infinite examples that attest the infirmity of human lordship and the invincibility of divine lordship.[^11] And it is clear [561] that this [teaching]{g:sentencia} is pious to the faithful who console themselves in the Lord, and not to those who blasphemously demand the honour due to God alone; hence whoever thinks that sin is [exchanged]{g:commutacio} or sold for bare coin implies that God sells away from himself his lordship, making a creature equal to himself, from which he exacts no service; and consequently God would sell himself, because [he would sell] unsurpassable justice for a vile price of his own, as, say, for a mite, since for any part of the price he would sell the indivisible justice which is God.[^12] But what, I ask, is more blasphemous? Therefore, if anyone sins against God, God himself in his own person must look for satisfaction or [satispassion]{g:satispassio} from the same person who has sinned against him. And it is clear that the first [assumption]{g:assumptum} is false, namely that God can remit sins without satisfaction, or communicate to his vicar the power either of remitting sins in this way or of dispensing against sins in this way. For this belongs to the [regality]{g:regalia} proper to the Godhead.[^13]

### 23-e ¶1 · 561.19–563.6  (6 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 561] Et per hec patet responsio ad primum argumentum quo suadetur dominum papam de facto sic facere; non enim pretendit vicarius Christi aut Petri vel sic se posse facere, nisi forte blasphemet et postea resipiscat. Unde signum infallibile foret homini quod sit extra regulam quam Christus suis instituit, nec sit de numero illorum pro quibus Christus tunc oravit, si potestatem talem sibi usurpaverit, nec sollicitor circa facta talia particularia dominum papam vel confratres suos temere accusando, **sed** scio quod si finaliter consummat in ista sentencia, nec fuit Christi vicarius nec eciam christianus, quia non intravit [p. 562] humiliter per ostium Christum ad tale officium, **sed** ascendit aliunde violente per tractum regis superbie, et per consequens est, si factum suum sic se habuerit, fur et latro. Ad secundum argumentum captum ex dicto Christi Matthei XVI° patet ex sepe dictis quod quicunque sic extollitur, vel ad eum de potestate data Petro et eius vicariis, ideo necesse est papas addiscere scolam Petri specialiter in humilitate et ministerio spirituali, quia aliter non est ille qui sic pretenditur, ut patet quadragesima distinccione. Oportet ergo dominum papam circumspicere ut teneat se in limitibus sancti Petri, et specialiter quod notet verba scripture sacre, non usurpando quod ligat et solvit in purgatorio subter terram nec ascendit in celum imperciendo premia beatorum, cum sint insolubiliter limitata, **sed** oportet quod teneat se virtuose in medio super terram. Et patet quod minor argumenti est falsa, nam unus apostata potest pretendere se sic solvere vel ligare, **sed** non exhinc sequitur quod sic facit. Ideo (ut sepe dixi) oportet dominum papam cavere de modis sophisticis. Ad tercium argumentum dicitur quod minor est falsa, cum deitas Christi, scriptura sacra et beatus Petrus relicti sunt in ecclesia militante ad ipsam regulandum et eius dubia decidendum, que tria secundum singulum non sunt vicarii sancti Petri. Christus enim in ascensione sua dereliquit humanitus deitatem suam in terris, **sed** non divisit, cum copulatus [p. 563] est sibi ypostatice, ubicunque fuerit humanitus, et ipsa deitas est caput ecclesie. Christus **autem** humanitus reliquit testamentum suum in terris per quod voluit suam ecclesiam regulari, nec voluit quod crederetur alicui Petri vicario, nisi de quanto vivit et docet conformiter illi legi.

ENGLISH: And by these things the reply to the first argument is clear, by which it is urged that the lord [pope]{g:papa} in fact does so; for the [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius Christi}, or of Peter, does not claim even that he can do so, unless perhaps he blasphemes and afterwards comes to his senses.[^1] Hence it would be an infallible sign to a man that he is outside the rule which Christ instituted for his own, and not of the number of those for whom Christ then prayed, if he has usurped such power for himself. Nor am I anxious about such particular deeds, rashly accusing the lord pope or his brethren; but I know that if he finally ends in this [teaching]{g:sentencia}, he was neither Christ's vicar nor even a Christian, because he did not enter [562] humbly by the door, Christ, into such an office, but climbed up another way, violently, drawn by the king of pride; and consequently, if his deed has been so, he is "a thief and a robber."[^2] To the second argument, taken from the saying of Christ in Matthew 16, it is clear from what has often been said that whoever is so exalted, or to him concerning the power given to Peter and his vicars […]; therefore it is necessary for popes to learn in the school of Peter, especially in humility and spiritual ministry, because otherwise he is not the one he is claimed to be, as is clear in the fortieth distinction.[^3] The lord pope must therefore look about him, to keep himself within the [limits]{g:limites} of St Peter, and especially to note the words of holy scripture: not usurping [the claim] that he binds and looses in purgatory beneath the earth, nor ascending into heaven by imparting the rewards of the blessed, since these are [assigned]{g:limitare} beyond loosing; but he must keep himself virtuously in the middle, upon the earth.[^4] And it is clear that the minor of the argument is false; for an apostate can claim that he looses or binds in this way, but it does not follow from this that he does so. Therefore (as I have often said) the lord pope must beware of sophistical ways.[^5] To the third argument the answer is that the minor is false, since the Godhead of Christ, holy scripture and blessed Peter have been left in the [church militant]{g:ecclesia militans} to rule it and to decide its doubts, which three, taken singly, are not vicars of St Peter.[^6] For Christ in his ascension, as man, left his [Godhead]{g:deitas} on earth, but did not divide [it from himself], since it is [563] joined to him hypostatically wherever he is as man; and that Godhead is [head]{g:caput} of the [church]{g:ecclesia}. And Christ as man left his testament on earth, by which he willed his church to be ruled; nor did he will that any vicar of Peter be believed, except so far as he lives and teaches in conformity with that law.[^7]

### 23-e ¶2 · 563.7–564.6  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 563] Unde quicunque sacerdos ecclesie post mortem Petri vivens et docens testamento illi conformiter potest racionabiliter dici Petri vicarius, **sed** specialiter ille qui vivit Petro conformius et ad istam doctrinam testamenti Christi similius; et ille supponitur esse Romanus episcopus, **sed** in synodis pro dubiis decidendis non est credendum cuicunque vianti, nisi de quanto dictum suum fundaverit in scriptura. Unde non est credendum quod fides catholica vel in veritate que creditur vel in habitu ecclesie catholice dependet ab ista persona, cum sit errabilis et de possibili non membrum ecclesie. Ego **autem** non sollicitor quid papa facit vel loquitur, cum scio quod nec sibi nec alicui istorum doctorum credendum est nisi de quanto se in scriptura fundaverit, cum in ipsa sit omnis veritas proporcionaliter, ut est necessarium ipsam nosci (ut patet secundo De Doctrina Christiana in fine). Unde sepe miratus sum quomodo aliquis istorum trium doctorum fundaret supradictam sentenciam in scriptura. Scimus **autem** quod papa cum cetu cardinalium eciam in materia fidei sepe errat, **sed** supponendum est quod scriptores bullarum papalium vel examinatores aut falsi suggestores sepe falsificant bullas suas, sicut noverunt auctores scripture de multis indulgenciis, de multis collacionibus [p. 564] prefecturarum et de multis privilegiis et terroribus censurarum; ideo tales apud Deum incurrunt penam falsarii et sunt apud potentes, scientes et ordinate volentes notabiliter renotandi. Caput ergo ecclesie Christus cum scriptura sua et sanctis doctoribus debet esse regula ecclesie in dubiis decidendis.

ENGLISH: Hence any priest of the church, living after Peter's death, who lives and teaches in conformity with that testament, can reasonably be called Peter's vicar; but especially he who lives more conformably to Peter and more like to this [doctrine]{g:doctrina} of Christ's testament; and he is supposed to be the Roman bishop. But in synods, for deciding doubts, no [one on the road]{g:viantes} whatever is to be believed, except so far as he has grounded his saying in scripture.[^8] Hence it is not to be believed that the catholic faith, either in the truth which is believed or in the habit of the catholic church, depends on this person, since he is liable to err and possibly not a [member]{g:membrum} of the church. But I am not anxious about what the pope does or says, since I know that neither he nor any of these doctors is to be believed except so far as he has grounded himself in scripture, since in it is all truth, in proportion as it is necessary for it to be known (as is clear from the second book of *On Christian Doctrine*, at the end).[^9] Hence I have often wondered how any of these three doctors would ground the above-said teaching in scripture. And we know that the pope with the company of cardinals often errs, even in matter of faith; but it is to be supposed that the writers of papal bulls, or the examiners, or false suggesters often falsify his bulls, as the authors of the writing have known, concerning many [indulgences]{g:indulgencie}, many [conferrals]{g:collacio} [564] of prefectures, and many [privileges]{g:privilegium} and terrors of [censures]{g:censura}; therefore such men incur before God the penalty of a [falsifier]{g:falsarius}, and among the powerful, the knowing and those of rightly ordered will they are to be notably marked out.[^10] Christ, therefore, the head of the church, with his scripture and the holy doctors, ought to be the rule of the church in deciding doubts.[^11]

### 23-f ¶1 · 564.32–566.25  (8 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 564] Item, omnibus beatis diffinitum est a Deo premium beatitudinis correspondenter ad omnia merita sua, [p. 565] que adhuc manent in fructu, **sed** aliqua merita, requisita ad beatitudinem patriarcharum, non subiacent potestati huius Gregorii sic distribuere racione premii ex eis limitati; ergo nec alia. Assumptum patet, tum quia nihil est ociosum in meritis beatorum, tum quia beati habent gaudium et sic premium accidentale de quocunque bono quod fecerint. Minor **autem** sic ostenditur: In facta distribucione meriti stricte requisiti ad beatitudinem essencialem diminueretur de illa beatitudine correspondenter, quam iniuriam non potest papa facere, sicut nec Christus potest concives suos sic depauperare. **Sed** dicitur quod meritum beatorum, cum sit spirituale, non excommunicacione tali minuitur **sed** pocius perficitur. **Sed** (rogo) ad quid limitantur tunc merita supererogata ultra mensuram debitorum suorum vel superhabundanciam meritorum, cum merita stricte ad beatitudinem requisita eque sufficerent ad distribucionem huius faciendam, aut quomodo est necessarium ponere nunc tantam copiam talium meritorum? Videtur multis fidelibus quod non est modo numerus talium meritorum, vel aliter omnia illa et longe plura si fierent non sufficerent ad aliquem gradum beatitudinis promerentis, nisi quod Deus ex infinitate gracie sue acceptat merita illa de congruo. Item, iuxta istam sentenciam sequitur quod potestas iudiciaria cuiuscunque pape se extendit ad omnes animas in purgatorio et omnes beatos in celo; consequens impossibile, et consequencia sic probatur: papa eciam prescitus habet potestatem ad imperciendum merita bonorum cuicunque et quandocunque voluerit, ut dicit posicio; **sed** longe minor potestas sufficit ad fundandum iudiciariam potestatem, ergo papa habet iudiciariam potestatem super beatos eciam super Christum. Potest enim facere quod beatus [p. 566] mereatur cuicunque affecto suo quantumcunque voluerit, quia potest dare sibi meritum alterius, quod est idem. Ille enim qui habet potestatem super tam secretum thesaurum anime habet potestatem super personam quoad inferius corporale; in cuius signum Deus dedit dyabolo potestatem primo super substanciam beati Job, secundo super corpus. Verumptamen, inquit, animam eius serva, Job II°, 6. Et multo evidencius sequeretur quod papa preest cuicunque spiritui in purgatorio vel dampnato, et hoc videtur doctorem intendere, cum dicit quod bona communia multitudinis distribuuntur singulis de illa multitudine secundum arbitrium eius qui illi multitudini preest, et multo evidencius sequitur quod papa preest dampnatis, cum preest personis dignioribus, ut puta, purgandis spiritibus et beatis; qui enim habet potestatem cassandi opus dyaboli et tollendi ab eo predam quam tantum appetit, habet potestatem in eum, **sed** papa habet potestatem ad dandum quantamlibet indulgenciam captivo dyaboli, ne sufficiat amplius valere in eum, ut dicitur. Dicitur et Luce IX° quod Christus dedit discipulis potestatem super omnia demonia, **sed** nimis ampla foret pape iurisdiccio, qui instar Christi habet potestatem super multitudinem celestium, terrestrium et infernorum.

ENGLISH: Again, for all the blessed a reward of blessedness has been defined by God, corresponding to all their merits, [565] which still remain in their fruit; but some merits, required for the blessedness of the patriarchs, are not subject to the power of this Gregory to distribute in this way, by reason of the reward [assigned]{g:limitare} from them; therefore neither are the others.[^1] The [assumption]{g:assumptum} is clear, both because nothing is idle in the merits of the blessed, and because the blessed have joy, and so accidental reward, from whatever good they have done. Now the minor is shown thus: if a distribution were made of merit strictly required for [essential blessedness]{g:beatitudo essencialis}, that blessedness would be diminished correspondingly; which injury the [pope]{g:papa} cannot do, just as Christ cannot so impoverish his fellow citizens.[^2] But it is said that the merit of the blessed, since it is spiritual, is not diminished by such communication but rather perfected. But (I ask) to what end, then, are supererogated merits assigned beyond the measure of their debts, or a superabundance of merits, since the merits strictly required for blessedness would equally suffice for making this distribution? Or how is it necessary to posit now so great a [plenty]{g:copia} of such merits?[^3] It seems to many of the faithful that there is now no number of such merits; or else all those, and far more if they were done, would not suffice for any degree of blessedness for the one meriting, except that God, of the infinity of his grace, accepts those merits [by congruity]{g:de congruo}.[^4] Again, according to this [teaching]{g:sentencia} it follows that the judicial power of any pope whatever extends to all the souls in purgatory and all the blessed in heaven; the [consequent]{g:consequens} is impossible, and the [inference]{g:consequencia} is proved thus: a pope, even a [foreknown]{g:prescitus} one, has power to impart the merits of the good to whomever and whenever he wishes, as the position says; but a far lesser power suffices to ground judicial power; therefore the pope has judicial power over the blessed, even over Christ.[^5] For he can bring it about that a blessed one [566] merits for anyone he favours as much as he wishes, because he can give him another's merit, which is the same thing. For he who has power over so secret a treasure of the soul has power over the person as regards the lower, bodily part; in token of which God gave the devil power first over the substance of blessed Job, secondly over his body: "But yet," he says, "save his life," Job 2:6.[^6] And much more evidently it would follow that the pope is set over every spirit in purgatory or damned; and this the doctor seems to [maintain]{g:intendere}, when he says that the common goods of a multitude are distributed to the single members of that multitude according to the judgment of him who is set over that multitude; and much more evidently it follows that the pope is set over the damned, since he is set over worthier persons, as, say, the spirits to be purged and the blessed.[^7] For he who has power to quash the work of the devil and to take from him the prey which he so desires has power over him; but the pope has power to give an indulgence as great as he pleases to the devil's captive, so that the devil may no longer prevail against him, as it is said. And it is said in Luke 9 that Christ gave the disciples power over all devils; but the pope's jurisdiction would be far too ample, who, like Christ, has power over the multitude of those in heaven, on earth and under the earth.[^8]

### 23-f ¶4 · 567.14–568.4  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 567] Assumptum patet ex hoc quod tunc posset facere opus suum esse quantumcunque meritorium sibi placuerit, quia limitare beatis quod sibi assisterent quantumcunque voluerit promerendo. Ideo daret suo vicario potestatem, ut sibi retribuat tantum bonum. **Sed** Hostiensis (ut recitavi superius) horrebat istam ficticiam, quia certum videtur quod, si haberet tantam potestatem super demonia et super cunctos subditos suos quoad corpus et animam, haberet eciam potestatem super eorum substanciam, et tunc multi pape (ut creditur) fecissent miracula, mandando spiritibus afferre sibi thesauros absconditos et acquisivissent sibi dominia adeo affectata. **Sed** (ut innuitur Act. III, 6), quia Petrus non habuit possessionem auri vel argenti, ideo habuit potestatem sanandi [p. 568] infirmum, **sed** Simon, quia affectabat temporalia, caruit apostolica potestate (ut patet Act. VIII°). Apostoli enim denudati ab affeccione temporalium habuerunt super eis spiritualem potestatem atque dominium.

ENGLISH: The assumption is clear from this: that then he could make his own work as meritorious as it pleased him, because [he could] assign to the blessed that they should assist him by meriting as much as he wished. For he would be a fool who, for pride or gain, did more good to another, less worthy, than he would do even to himself; and if it is said that the power is not given that he may so do good to himself, it is clear that God gives power only for the good of his [predestinate]{g:predestinatus}. Therefore he would give his vicar power that he might repay himself so much good.[^12] But Hostiensis (as I recounted above) shuddered at this [fiction]{g:ficticia}, because it seems certain that, if he had so great a power over devils and over all his subjects as regards body and soul, he would also have power over their substance; and then many popes (as is believed) would have done miracles, commanding the spirits to bring them hidden treasures, and would have acquired for themselves the lordships they so [desired]{g:affectare}.[^13] But (as is hinted in Acts 3:6), because Peter had no possession of gold or silver, therefore he had power to heal [568] the sick man; but Simon, because he desired [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, lacked apostolic power (as is clear in Acts 8). For the apostles, stripped of affection for temporal goods, had spiritual power and [lordship]{g:dominium} over them.[^14]

### 23-f ¶5 · 568.5–568.24  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 568] Item, secundum doctorem beati non fecerunt determinate huiusmodi superhabundancia opera satisfactoria pro isto vel isto qui remissione indiget, ideo restat ut Deus derelinquat vicario suo in terris distributivam potestatem illius thesauri egenis ecclesie; **sed** illud videtur multipliciter palliatum, ergo posicioni deficit fundamentum. [p. 585] Dico octavo quod sacerdotes Christi, licet habeant potestatem ad absolvendum subditos a pena et culpa, non tamen debent absolvere sub hac forma nec subiecti illud expetere, nisi hoc eis specialiter fuerit revelatum. Prima pars patet ex hoc quod sacerdos potest sacramentaliter ostendere sibi confitentem taliter absolutum, qui ad tantum conteritur quod statim decedens sine pena purgatorii advolaret, et hoc est sacerdotem absolvere. Nec est potestas alicuius sacerdotis in casu ultime necessitatis sic ligata quin, quantum Deus revelans permiserit, possit absolvere; foret **autem** nimia presumpcio aliquem Christi vicarium absolucionem talem pretendere, nisi Deus hoc sibi revelaverit faciendum, ne forte incurrat blasphemum mendacium. Quid ergo valet subiectos inoportune absolucionem talem expetere, cum certe debent credere quod correspondenter ad sua merita vel demerita taxabuntur? Licet **autem** sufficiat apud Christum ubique presentem contricio, tamen sacramentum penitencie est valde necessarium, licet non proderit sine illa. Ideo foret stulticia sacerdotem cui non fit ad hoc revelacio diffinire quod penitencia vel aliud sacramentum suo suscipienti proderit ad salutem. Sic enim vetaret ut pro absoluto suo a pena et culpa in mortis articulo fieret suffragium ecclesie, sicut non facimus pro quocunque mortuo quem constat nobis iam esse beatum. Istam sentenciam de suffragiis et indulgenciis dixerim ad instruccionem inferiorum ecclesie, et exposicionem correccioni, supplecioni et approbacioni superiorum secundum regulas scripturarum et leges humanas ac decreta potentatuum precise ad tantum apprecior. **Sed** ad materiam de peccati purgacione quam tetigi in proximo argumento videtur mihi probabile quod quilibet purgandus tam diu habet peccatum [p. 586] in purgatorio, quam diu inibi subit penam. Nam sequitur: Hec est pena peccati, ergo est aliquid peccati, cum omnis pena sit res iusta per quam peccatum bonificatur, et per consequens illud peccatum est. Similiter, si quilibet in purgatorio purgatus est omnino sine peccato, tunc est ita mundus sicut beatus in celo, cum solum peccatum causat talem inmundiciam, et per consequens foret tam amabilis in purgatorio ab ecclesia triumphante, sicut ipso beato, quia tam pulcher. Similiter, quilibet existens in purgatorio propter peccatum suum preteritum deficit a plena dileccione Dei et ecclesie, ymmo a pleno profectu sibi et aliis quem debuit tunc habere. Ille ergo defectus purgandus est peccatum, ideo videtur penam purgare illum defectum et satisfacere pro defectu preterito, et quando lux amoris primo inducitur, tunc primo obscuritas peccati educitur.

ENGLISH: I say seventh that the [prelates]{g:prelatus} of the [church]{g:ecclesia} ought to instruct their subjects in this catholic truth, lest the laity, faithlessly busied about less useful things, attend upon the pride and avarice of priests.[^1] This is clear by a like case from the law about questors, for whom it is [assigned]{g:limitare} that they say nothing to the people except what has been assigned to them in the bishops' letters, as is clear in the seventh book, *Of Penances and Remissions*, chapter *Abusionibus*. Much more, therefore, ought the [vicars of Christ]{g:vicarius-christi} to teach the people according to the [limits]{g:limites} of the letters of the Holy Spirit, which he has delivered to them to teach.[^2] [585] I say eighth that the [priests]{g:sacerdos} of Christ, although they have power to absolve their subjects from punishment and guilt, yet ought not to absolve under this form, nor ought the subjects to seek it, unless this has been specially revealed to them. The first part is clear from this: that a priest can sacramentally show the one who confesses to him to be so absolved, being contrite to such a degree that, dying at once, he would fly off without the punishment of purgatory; and this is for the priest to absolve. Nor is the power of any priest, in a case of last necessity, so bound that he cannot absolve, as far as God, revealing it, permits; but it would be excessive presumption for any vicar of Christ to put forward such an absolution unless God had revealed to him that it was to be done, lest perhaps he fall into a blasphemous lie.[^3] What does it avail, then, for subjects to seek such an absolution unseasonably, since they surely ought to believe that they will be [assessed]{g:taxare} correspondingly to their merits or demerits? And although contrition suffices with Christ, who is everywhere present, yet the sacrament of penance is very necessary, though it will not profit without that [contrition].[^4] So it would be folly for a priest to whom no revelation is made for this to define that penance, or any other sacrament, will profit its receiver to salvation. For so he would forbid that the [suffrage]{g:suffragia} of the church be made for the one he has absolved from punishment and guilt at the point of death, as we do not make it for any dead man whom we know to be already blessed.[^5] This [teaching]{g:sentencia} on suffrages and indulgences I would have spoken for the instruction of the lower [members] of the church; and the exposition [I submit] to the correction, supplementing and approval of my superiors according to the rules of the scriptures; and human laws and the decrees of the powerful I value precisely so far [as they agree with these].[^6] But as to the matter of the purging of sin, which I touched on in the last argument, it seems probable to me that anyone who is to be purged has sin [586] in purgatory for as long as he undergoes punishment there.[^7] For [it follows]{g:sequitur}: this is the punishment of sin, therefore it is something of sin; since every punishment is a just thing through which sin is made good, and consequently that sin exists.[^8] Likewise, if anyone in purgatory, being purged, is wholly without sin, then he is as clean as one blessed in heaven, since sin alone causes such uncleanness; and consequently he would be as lovable to the [church triumphant]{g:ecclesia-triumphans}, while in purgatory, as the blessed one himself, since he is as beautiful.[^9] Likewise, anyone who is in purgatory falls short, on account of his past sin, of the full love of God and of the church, indeed of the full profit to himself and to others that he ought then to have. That defect, then, which is to be purged, is sin; so it seems that punishment purges that defect and [makes satisfaction]{g:satisfacere} for the past defect; and when the light of love is first brought in, then first is the darkness of sin brought out.[^10]

### 23-l ¶2 · 586.18–586.30  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 586] Videtur ulterius quod pro quolibet peccato in quo decedit predestinatus Deus statuit certam penam, cum quo non poterit illo peccato supposito dispensare. Nam per idem posset dispensare cum quolibet peccato in purgatorio vel in inferno, et dimittere homini peccatum sine hoc quod voluerit converti ad dominum, sine amore faciendo ipsum beatum, cum nulla pena essencialiter consequeretur peccatum **sed** foret tantum per accidens arbitraria quoad Deum. Sicut ergo pena essencialis est per se iusta, sic est tanta iusticia in pena accidentali, **sed** meritum ecclesie facit esse aliud peccatum totale in purgatorio quam foret merito illo dimisso.

ENGLISH: It seems, further, that for every sin in which a [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} [person] dies God has established a certain punishment, with which, that sin being supposed, he will not be able to dispense. For by the same token he could dispense with any sin in purgatory or in hell, and remit sin to a man without his having willed to be converted to the Lord, making him blessed without love; since no punishment would follow sin essentially, but it would be only by accident, arbitrary as regards God.[^11] As, therefore, essential punishment is just of itself, so there is as much justice in accidental punishment; but the merit of the church makes the total sin in purgatory other than it would be were that merit set aside.[^12]

### 23-l ¶3 · 586.31–587.15  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 586] Videtur tercio quod sicut beati in celo merentur sibi continue usque ad diem iudicii premium accidentale, aliis **autem** utrumque, **sed** existentes in [p. 587] purgatorio merentur sibi premium utrumque continue, sic dampnati usque in finem continue demerentur. Prima pars videtur: Eo quod beati, sicut continue merentur aliis in via, sic merentur ut post habeant consorcium eorum in patria; purgati vero merentur sibi nedum remissionem peccaminum, **sed** beatitudinem tam essencialem quam accidentalem futuram, voluntarie gracia huius ex Dei gracia paciendo; licet **autem** peccent continue, quia tunc sunt in gracia sine mortali, non video quomodo proprie demerentur, sicut nec libere contradictorie tunc merentur. Dampnati vero cum sint peccato mortali in malivolencia sua continue peiorati, continue demerentur, licet prosint per accidens. De isto alibi. Explicit tractatus de sancta matre ecclesia.

ENGLISH: It seems, thirdly, that as the blessed in heaven merit for themselves continually, until the day of judgement, [accidental reward]{g:premium-accidentale}, but both [rewards] for others, but those who are in [587] purgatory merit both rewards for themselves continually, so the damned continually demerit until the end.[^13] The first part is evident thus: because the blessed, as they continually merit for others on the [way]{g:via}, so they merit that afterwards they may have their fellowship in the [homeland]{g:patria}; and those being purged merit for themselves not only remission of sins but future blessedness, both [essential and accidental]{g:beatitudo-essencialis}, suffering willingly for its sake out of God's grace; and although they sin continually, since they are then in grace without mortal [sin], I do not see how they properly demerit, as neither do they then merit with the freedom of contradiction.[^14] But the damned, since in mortal sin they are continually made worse in their malevolence, continually demerit, although they profit [others] by accident. Of this elsewhere. Here ends the treatise on holy mother church.[^15]
