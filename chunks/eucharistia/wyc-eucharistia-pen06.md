# *De eucharistia*, De eucharistia et penitencia, chapter 6: the arguments from the ten lepers and from Lazarus

## ¶1 · 340.29–342.10

> [p. 340] Sed adhuc replicant quidam per exiles argucias que non forent digne memoria, nisi stulticia per diabolum fuit introducta; ideo ut ecclesia caucius caveat de illa intoxicante stulticia; videtur racionabile pro [p. 341] imprudentibus quod iste argucie per ordinem salutentur. Arguitur enim per illud evangelium dictum de decem leprosis: Ite et ostendite vos sacerdotibus. Cum ergo Christus omnia talia dicta dixerat in figura, videtur quod ex hoc dicto figuravit quod leprosi sunt specialiter a sacerdotibus confitendi. Et sic introducitur sacramentalis confessio usitata ex auctoritate evangelii. Sed pro solucione istius oportet supponere in sacramento quomodo est sensus quadruplex scripture, scilicet literalis, allegoricus, tropologicus et anagoycus. Literalis autem secundum principia Augustini habet summam auctoritatem, alii autem sensus nisi de quanto fundantur in sensu isto autentico vel evidencia racionis, quia aliter quilibet posset moralisare vel trahere auctoritatem scripture ad sensum quem vellet habere, sicut fertur quendam fratrem asseruisse quod veritas evangelica sit quecunque veritas per quam communitas salubriter est hortanda; et sic cum quelibet veritas est huiusmodi, quelibet veritas est veritas evangelica. Sic tenendo scripturam in suis limitibus, videtur quod striccius sit loquendum. Concedenda est ergo historia evangelica supradicta et dicendum quod ex ista non infertur fideliter quod homines debent in tempore gracie taliter confiteri. Sed hic videtur probabiliter concludi quod sicut in lege veteri leprosi debuerant se sacerdotibus ostendere, ut patet Luc. XVII, sic Christus autor utriusque legis approbat pro suo tempore veterem istam legem et per anterioracionem sanacionis quod ista ostensio sacerdotibus cessare debuit pro tempore legis nove. Et istud figurat reditus Samaritani qui ultra novem iudeos sanatos taliter [p. 342] est regressus. Potest eciam figurare quod spiritualiter leprosi debent currere ad sacerdotes sub lege gracie, et illi debent per verba evangelica ad salubrem cordis contricionem et peccati detestacionem inmundos spiritualiter inclinare et antea saltem naturaliter sunt ex Dei gracia expurgandi. Sicut enim non infertur ex hoc evangelio quod confitendus ferat in manu sua denarium presbitero offerendum, ita non sequitur ex ista historia quod fidelis debeat istam tradicionem hominum confiteri.

But some still [rejoin]{g:replicatur} with slender [quibbles]{g:argucie} which would not be worthy of remembrance, had not folly been brought in by the devil; therefore, so that the [church]{g:ecclesia} may beware the more cautiously of that poisoning folly, it seems reasonable, for the sake of [341] the imprudent, that these quibbles be greeted in order.[^1] For [it is argued by appeal to]{g:arguitur-per} that gospel spoken of the ten lepers: "Go, shew yourselves to the priests."[^2] Since, therefore, Christ had said all such sayings in [figure]{g:figura}, it seems that by this saying he [figured]{g:figurare} that lepers are specially to be confessed by priests.[^3] And so the customary sacramental [confession]{g:confessio} is introduced from the [authority]{g:auctoritas} of the gospel. But for the [resolution]{g:solucio} of this one must suppose, in the sacrament, how the sense of scripture is fourfold, namely literal, allegorical, tropological and anagogical.[^4] Now the literal, according to the principles of Augustine, has the highest authority, but the other senses [have it] only in so far as they are founded on this [authentic]{g:autenticus} sense or on the [evidence]{g:evidencia} of reason,[^5] because otherwise anyone could moralize, or draw the authority of scripture to whatever sense he wished to have, as a certain [friar]{g:frater} is reported to have asserted that gospel truth is any truth by which the community is [wholesomely]{g:salubriter} to be exhorted; and so, since every truth is of this sort, every truth is gospel truth.[^6] Holding scripture thus within its [limits]{g:limites}, it seems that one must speak more strictly. The gospel history above is therefore to be granted, and it is to be said that from it it is not faithfully inferred that men ought, in the time of grace, to confess in this way.[^7] But here it seems to be [inferred]{g:concludere} [with probability]{g:probabiliter} that, as in the old law lepers had to show themselves to the priests, as is clear from Luke 17, so Christ, author of both laws, approves that old law for its time; and, by the healing's being put first, that this showing to the priests had to cease for the time of the new law.[^8] And this is figured by the return of the Samaritan, who, beyond the nine Jews who were healed, [342] came back in this way.[^9] It can also figure that the spiritually leprous ought to run to the priests under [the law of grace]{g:lex-gracie}, and that these ought, by the words of the gospel, to incline the [spiritually]{g:spiritualiter} unclean to wholesome [contrition]{g:contricio} of heart and detestation of sin; and before that they are, at least [naturally]{g:naturaliter}, to be cleansed by the grace of God.[^10] For just as it is not inferred from this gospel that the one to be confessed should carry in his hand a [penny]{g:denarius} to be offered to the [presbyter]{g:presbiter}, so it does not [follow]{g:sequitur} from this history that the faithful man ought to confess this tradition of men.[^11]

## ¶2 · 342.11–342.18

> [p. 342] Secundo arguitur per istud Joh. XI de suscitacione Lazari, ubi postquam ligatus est institis et postquam suscitatus fuerat de sepulcro, Christus precepit suis apostolis ipsum solvere de huiusmodi ligamentis. Ex quo videtur quod Cristus docuit figuraliter quod sacerdotes sui habent potestatem solvendi spiritualiter mortuos secundum formam quam approbat usus noster.

Secondly, it is argued by appeal to this, John 11, on the raising of Lazarus, where, after he was bound with bands and after he had been raised from the sepulchre, Christ commanded his apostles to [loose]{g:solvere} him from these bindings.[^12] From which it seems that Christ taught figurally that his priests have the [power]{g:potestas} of loosing the spiritually dead according to the form which our [use]{g:usus} approves.[^13]

## ¶3 · 342.19–343.5

> [p. 342] Sed sic arguentes ab imprudenti similitudine debent primo attendere quando Jesus prius vivificavit Lazarum et fecit eum surgere de sepulcro fetido atque stare. Et tunc replicans figuram ad propositum debet concedere quod Jesus spiritualiter prius vivificat peccato sic mortuos et eos suscitans facit stare. Et postmodum fideles presbiteri debent solvere ligamenta consuetudinum peccandi sancta predicacione, quibus post delecionem peccati per Deum postea sint ligati. Sed ista consuetudinis diminucio sive solucio est presbyteris hodie pretermissa, cum nec salubribus exhortacionibus nec sanctis exemplacionibus docent populum, quando illa consuetudo peccandi est propter periculum limitanda. Et istam fidelem sentenciam debent viri apostolici noticie populi declarare et non se ipsos [p. 343] illis institis tamquam quibusdam apparenciis ypocrisis insolubiliter vinculare. Nunc autem ligant se ipsos et indisponunt ad proficiendum populo, cum obligant se diabolo propter apparenciam potestatis et lucri et non reverenter dant gloriam Deo suo.

But those who argue thus from an imprudent likeness ought first to attend to when Jesus first quickened Lazarus and made him rise from the fetid sepulchre and stand. And then, turning the figure back upon the point, one ought to grant that Jesus first spiritually quickens those thus dead in sin and, raising them, makes them stand. And afterwards faithful presbyters ought, by holy preaching, to loose the bands of the [customs]{g:consuetudo} of sinning by which they may still be bound after the [blotting out]{g:delecio} of the sin by God.[^14] But this diminishing or loosing of custom is passed over by presbyters today, since they teach the people neither by wholesome exhortations nor by holy examples when that custom of sinning is to be [limited]{g:limitare} because of the danger.[^15] And this faithful [teaching]{g:sentencia} apostolic men ought to [make clear]{g:declarare} to the people's knowledge, and not bind themselves [343] [inextricably]{g:insolubiliter} with those bands, as with certain appearances of hypocrisy.[^16] But now they bind themselves and make themselves [unfit]{g:indispositus} to profit the people, since they bind themselves over to the devil for the appearance of power and gain, and do not reverently give glory to their God.

## ¶4 · 343.6–343.13

> [p. 343] Et per ista dicta breviter possunt solvi similes argucie per diabolum introducte. Scimus quidem quod consuetudo istorum ypocritarum novorum ordinum remurmurat contra istam sentenciam. Sed super omnia vincit veritas racionis. Non sumus autem sic desponsati cum ista sentencia quam adhuc credimus esse catholicam quin docto contrario velimus ipsam humiliter dimittere et contrarium constancius confiteri.

And by these sayings like quibbles brought in by the devil can be briefly resolved. We know indeed that the custom of these hypocrites of the new orders murmurs back against this teaching. But above all the truth of reason "conquers".[^17] But we are not so wedded to this teaching, which we still believe to be catholic, that, were the contrary taught us, we would not humbly lay it aside and confess the contrary more steadfastly.[^18]

## Notes

[^1]: **C/T.** *Salutentur*, "be greeted": the quibbles are to be saluted one by one as they come, an ironic courtesy; we keep the verb. D reads *intoxicatem* for *intoxicante*. A, C and D lack the chapter heading.
[^2]: **S.** Luke 17:14, Vulgate *Ite, ostendite vos sacerdotibus*; Wyclif adds *et* ("Go and shew"), which the Douay wording leaves out.
[^3]: **C.** *A sacerdotibus confitendi*: the passive gerundive, "to be confessed by priests", i.e. to have their confession heard by them. D lacks *dicta*; B reads *quod Christus*.
[^4]: **C/T.** *Supponere in sacramento* is odd; we take it as "in [the matter of] the sacrament" of penance under discussion, and flag a possible corruption. D's margin: *Scripture sensus est quadruplex*; one manuscript (siglum lost) reads *vocalis* for *literalis*.
[^5]: **C/S/R.** The clause on the other senses lacks its verb; *habent auctoritatem* is understood from the first clause. The appeal is to Augustine's principle that argument stands on the literal sense, to be checked (LEDGER Kpen06-1). B's margin: *Literalis sensus quo extencione (sic) alios*, as Loserth prints it.
[^6]: **S.** The friar's saying is reported (*fertur*), without a name or a source (LEDGER Kpen06-2). B lacks *scripture* at 341.15.
[^7]: **T.** A, C, D, E and F read *dicitur* for *dicendum*, "and it is said"; A reads *ex illa*. The sense is the same.
[^8]: **C.** The second *quod* clause depends, like the first, on *videtur concludi*: it is inferred that Christ approves the old law for its time, and, from the healing's coming before the showing (Luke 17:14, "as they went, they were made clean"), that the showing had to cease. *Anterioracio* is a rare abstract, "putting before". A, D and E lack *Luce XVII*; D reads *cessare* (the lemma is not given).
[^9]: **C/S.** *Reditus* is the Samaritan's "return" (Luke 17:15–18; cf. 17:17, "where are the nine?"), not the row's "revenue(s)". He came back to Christ, not to the priests, which is the figure.
[^10]: **C/T.** *Naturaliter* "naturally", in the order of nature: God's cleansing comes before the priest's exhortation. A and C lack *figurare*.
[^11]: **C.** *Istam tradicionem hominum confiteri*: literally "confess this tradition of men", i.e. make the confession that men's tradition requires. The penny is the offering brought to the confessor; the gospel says nothing of it.
[^12]: **S.** John 11:44 has *ligatus pedes, et manus institis* and *Dixit eis Jesus: Solvite eum*; the gospel says "to them", the bystanders, where Wyclif's objector has "his apostles". F has a marginal mark (the OCR gives "20") at 342.11.
[^13]: **C/T.** *Figuraliter*, "figurally", is kept apart from *figurative* "figuratively". D reads *finaliter* at 342.15 (lemma not given; probably for *spiritualiter*). A and C read *Jesus noster* at 342.17–18 (lemma not given).
[^14]: **T/R.** B reads *primum* for *primo*; B, C, E and F read *vivificat* (present) for *vivificavit*. D and B have in their margins *Instite Lazari significant ligamenta consuetudinum peccandi* (so Loserth, as far as the OCR shows).
[^15]: **C.** *Limitanda* in the restricting sense, "to be limited" (the row's other sense), not "assigned" as at 339.6.
[^16]: **C/T.** *Sentencia* here is the teaching Wyclif has just set out (CONTEXTUAL row: "teaching", not "sentence"). B reads *nunquam quibusdam* (343.1) and *adsiciendum* (343.3).
[^17]: **S.** *Super omnia vincit veritas* echoes 3 Esdras 3:12 (Vulgate appendix), to be checked (LEDGER Kpen06-3); Loserth's italics mark *vincit veritas*.
[^18]: **C/T.** *Confiteri* closes the treatise on confession by playing on its word: "confess" the contrary. The manuscripts' explicits vary: D, E, F *Explicit tractatus de confessionibus* (A, D, E add *editus a magistro Johanne*; A *Anglico*); C *Finis huius*, in red ink.
