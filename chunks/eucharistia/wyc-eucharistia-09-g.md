# *De eucharistia*, chapter 9, part 7: our lesser trope, and the fifth argument: the accidents co-multiplied with the body

## ¶1 · 296.23–298.4

> [p. 296] Et minus tropicat nostra sentencia illud dictum quam sentencia contraria que intelligit quod accidencia panis erunt figura corporis Christi, quia illud infundabiliter tropicat utrumque extremum; sed nos tantum alterum sicut facit Spiritus Sanctus in Paulo in eodem sacramento, quando dicit prima Cor. XI, 25: Hic calix novum testamentum est in meo sanguine, ubi patet quod Paulus tropat predicatum ultra sacramentum [p. 297] et rem et addit in signum quod non demonstrat naturam sanguinis sed naturam vini, quia ponit tantum in meo sanguine in obliquo. Nec credo quod adversarii invenient tantum fundamentum ad tam compendiose tropandum dicta sua. Scio quidem quod adversarius posset infideliter tropare totam historiam scripture, ut omnia gesta Christi posset intelligere tropice, quod non Christus in persona propria sed per se accidens illa fecit, ut in assimili tangit Augustinus in libello suo De Heresibus. Scio quidem quod nos qui irreligiose contendimus circa temporalia et signa adinvencionis nostre in penam peccati cecati sumus in sensu quem habuit ecclesia primitiva iuxta prophetiam senis Symeonis, Luc. IIᵒ, 34, positus est hic in signum cui contradicetur. Sicut enim in conversacione contradicimus gestis Christi, sic in speculacione contradicimus realibus verbis Christi, ideo in istis paratus sum stare decreto sancte matris ecclesie; horreo autem inducere hunc tantum extraneum accidens vel quantitas quod Spiritus Sanctus per panem aut vinum in verbis sacramentalibus ipsum intenderat, sicut beatus Jeronimus detestatur nomen ypostasis, ne sub melle sanctitatis venenum quod horruerunt pure per hec sit absconsum. Et sic donemus ipsis iniuriam qui nituntur extinguendo falsam devocionem nostram destruere in nobis ydolatriam, quia revera nimis malum foret tales ydolatras domi decorticare canabem quam sic ydolatriam geminare; ideo Christus contra excusaciones universales ydolatrarum precipit suis discipulis quod habeant prudenciam [p. 298] serpentinam; nam Paulus committendo blasphemiam credidit se prestare obsequium meritorium Deo suo. Veritas itaque fidei est instancia necessaria cuilibet viatori.

And our [teaching]{g:sentencia} takes that saying less tropically than the contrary teaching, which understands that the [accidents]{g:accidens} of the [bread]{g:panis} will be a [figure]{g:figura} of the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}; for that [teaching] tropes both extremes [without foundation]{g:infundabiliter}, but we only the one, as the Holy Spirit does in Paul in the same [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}, when he says, 1 Cor. 11:25: "This [chalice]{g:calix} is the new testament in my blood";[^1] where it is clear that Paul tropes the predicate beyond the sacrament [297] and the reality, and adds, as a sign that [the pronoun] [demonstrates]{g:demonstrare} not the nature of blood but the nature of [wine]{g:vinum}, that he puts "in my blood" only [in an oblique case]{g:in-obliquo}.[^2] Nor do I believe that the adversaries will find so great a foundation for troping their sayings so economically. I know indeed that an adversary could faithlessly trope the whole history of scripture, so that he could understand all the deeds of Christ [tropically]{g:tropice}: that not Christ in his own person but an accident [by itself]{g:per-se} did them, as Augustine touches on in a like case in his little book *On Heresies*.[^3] I know indeed that we who irreligiously contend about [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} and signs of our own invention have been blinded, in punishment of sin, in the sense which the primitive [church]{g:ecclesia} had, according to the prophecy of the old man Simeon, Luke 2:34: "this [child] is set for a sign which shall be contradicted".[^4] For as in our [manner of life]{g:conversacio} we contradict the deeds of Christ, so in speculation we contradict the real words of Christ; therefore in these matters I am ready to stand by the [decree]{g:decretum} of holy mother church. But I shudder to bring in this so foreign [sense]: that the Holy Spirit by "bread" or "wine" in the sacramental words had meant an accident or quantity,[^5] just as blessed Jerome detests the name [hypostasis]{g:ypostasis}, lest under the honey of holiness the poison which they abhorred, purely, be hidden by these [words].[^6] And so let us forgive the wrong to those who strive, by extinguishing our false devotion, to destroy idolatry in us; for [in truth]{g:revera} it would be less bad for such idolaters to strip hemp at home than thus to double idolatry.[^7] Therefore Christ, against the universal excuses of idolaters, commands his disciples that they "have the prudence [298] of serpents"; for Paul, in committing blasphemy, believed that he was offering a meritorious [duty]{g:obsequium} to his God.[^8] The truth of faith, therefore, is a necessary [objection]{g:instancia} for every [wayfarer]{g:viator}.[^9]

## ¶2 · 298.5–298.10

> [p. 298] Quinto principaliter arguitur contra dicta per hoc quod, si corpus Christi multiplicatur ad omnem punctum hostie, tunc foret ibi non quantum, et cum hoc foret ibi corpus panis quia in signo, ergo foret simul et semel quantum et non quantum, et sic de aliis denominacionibus accidentis.

Fifthly, [as a main head]{g:principaliter}, [it is argued by appeal to]{g:arguitur-per} this against what has been said: that if the body of Christ is multiplied to every point of the [host]{g:hostia}, then it would be there not [quantified]{g:quantus}; and with this the body of the bread would be there, because [it is there] in sign; therefore it would be at once and together quantified and not quantified; and so of the other [namings]{g:denominacio} of an accident.[^10]

## ¶3 · 298.11–298.21

> [p. 298] Hic dicitur communiter quod omnia accidencia absoluta corporis Christi que sunt in celo commultiplicantur cum ipso corpore in ipsa hostia concomitanter; et ita nedum tota quantitas et qualitas, ymmo totum corpus Christi et anima et sic tota eius humanitas et deitas indubie est ubicunque fuerit aliqua pars Christi; sed de septem accidentibus respectivis non oportet. Et ita posicio que est differencia quantitatis continue permanentis sed non posicio que est penultimum predicamentum commultiplicatur concomitanter ad corpus Christi in omni puncto hostie.

Here [the answer]{g:dicitur} commonly given is that all the [absolute accidents]{g:accidens-absolutum} of the body of Christ which are in heaven are co-multiplied with the body itself in the host itself [concomitantly]{g:concomitanter}; and so [not only]{g:nedum} the whole [quantity]{g:quantitas} and [quality]{g:qualitas}, indeed the whole body of Christ and the soul, and so his whole [humanity]{g:humanitas} and [Godhead]{g:deitas}, is without doubt wherever any part of Christ is; but of the seven [relational]{g:respectivus} accidents it need not be so. And so [position]{g:posicio} which is a [difference]{g:differencia} of continuous permanent quantity, but not position which is the penultimate [category]{g:predicamentum}, is co-multiplied concomitantly with the body of Christ at every point of the host.[^11]

## ¶4 · 298.22–299.5

> [p. 298] Contra illud arguitur primo per hoc quod Deus posset multiplicare corpus non commultiplicando accidens, cum hoc foret minus quam servare accidens sine subiecto; nec fides scripture nec racio docet oppositum de inesse, ergo hoc non est articulus fidei publicandus. Patet consequencia, tum quia timendus esset de tam manifesto mendacio, tum quia religio christiana non est oneranda fidei confuso dispendio quam Spiritus Sanctus voluit esse in isto liberam, [p. 299] brevem et levem. Nec dubium isti vie de antecedente, cum Deus qui potest annichilare substanciam servato accidente inmoto potest multiplicare ipsam et movere quorsumcunque libuerit sine comitancia tam aliene nature.

Against that it is argued, first, by appeal to this: that God could multiply the body without co-multiplying the accident, since this would be less than to preserve an [accident without a subject]{g:accidens-sine-subiecto}; and neither the faith of scripture nor reason teaches the opposite [as a matter of fact]{g:de-inesse}; therefore this is not an [article of faith]{g:articulus-fidei} to be published. The [inference]{g:consequencia} is clear: both because one would have to fear so manifest a lie,[^12] and because the christian religion is not to be burdened with a confused waste of faith, [the religion] which the Holy Spirit willed to be, in this, free, [299] brief and light. Nor is there doubt for this way about the [antecedent]{g:antecedens}, since God, who can annihilate the [substance]{g:substancia} while the accident is preserved unmoved, can multiply it and move it whithersoever he pleases without the accompaniment of so alien a [nature]{g:natura}.

## ¶5 · 299.6–299.20

> [p. 299] Item, non sunt fingenda miracula sine patencia racionis, sed foret summum miraculum intrudere tantam molem, sicut est tota corporeitas corporis Christi in infinitum modico loco; ergo cum non subsit racio isti miraculo, videtur ipsum non esse fingendum. Quis inquam non diceret quod esset inopinatum miraculum ponere totam mundi machinam non minoratam in nucleo in parvo loculo, sic quod idem corpus potest deperdere de loco usque ad non quantum et ita nullibi locum acquirere cum hoc quod non minoraretur sed in infinitum maioraretur? et sic pedale infinitum magnum locum posset successive occupare, cum hoc quod non diminuatur ad non quantum, et periret certitudo motus localis (ut dictum est de multiplicacione).

Again, miracles are not to be [feigned]{g:fingere} without the plainness of a reason; now it would be the highest miracle to thrust so great a mass, such as is the whole corporeity of the body of Christ, into an infinitely small place; therefore, since no reason underlies this miracle, it seems that it is not to be feigned. Who, I say, would not say that it would be an unheard-of miracle to put the whole [frame]{g:machina} of the world, not lessened, into a kernel, in a little space, so that the same body can lose place down to [being] not quantified, and so acquire place nowhere, together with this, that it would not be lessened but infinitely enlarged? And so a foot-long [body] could successively occupy an infinitely great place, together with this, that it is not diminished to not quantified; and the certainty of local motion would perish (as has been said of multiplication).

## ¶6 · 299.21–300.5

> [p. 299] Item, sequitur quod pars quantitativa quantitatis potest parificari et infinitum extendere suum totum, quia ponatur quod tota quantitas mundi multiplicetur per A cubum et maneat cum hoc A quantitas extensa et continuetur cum residuo quantitatis mundi; et videtur quod A parificatur quantitati tocius mundi, quia coextenduntur adequate per eundem locum. Et possibilitas casus patet isti vie, cum Deus potest multiplicare unam medietatem quantitatis reliqua non multiplicata, ymmo ad omnem punctum hostie est continuacio cuiuslibet partis quantitatis Christi eciam in celo; et eadem posicio parcium que est in celo, [p. 300] quia ex integro eadem quantitas in numero, que non potest maiorari vel minorari nec mutari de specie in speciem. Ex quibus colligitur quod omnis figura quantitatis Christi in celo sit ubique in hostia et per consequens omnis rectitudo vel extensio linearum.

Again, [it follows]{g:sequitur} that a [quantitative part]{g:pars-quantitativa} of a quantity can be made equal [to its whole] and extend its whole infinitely: for let it be posited that the whole quantity of the world is multiplied through the cube A, and that A, with this, remains an extended quantity and is [made continuous]{g:continuare} with the remainder of the world's quantity; and it seems that A is made equal to the quantity of the whole world, since they are coextended adequately through the same place. And the possibility of the case is clear to this way, since God can multiply one half of a quantity, the other not being multiplied; indeed, at every point of the host there is the [continuity]{g:continuacio} of every part of the quantity of Christ, even in heaven; and the same position of the parts which is in heaven, [300] since [it is] wholly the same quantity in number, which cannot be enlarged or lessened or changed from species to species. From which it is gathered that every figure of the quantity of Christ in heaven is everywhere in the host, and consequently every straightness or extension of lines.[^13]

## Notes

[^1]: **C/S.** *Tropicare* and *tropare* (both "trope", "take tropically") are the verbs of tropical predication; the two "extremes" are the subject and predicate of *Hoc est corpus meum*, of which Wyclif's teaching takes only the predicate tropically. *Sentencia* here is the position taught ("teaching"), not a sentence. 1 Cor. 11:25 agrees with the Vulgate (DR). The chapter numbers are Wyclif's, the verse numbers Loserth's. ABCE read *primo* for *prima*; ABC *ut fecit* for *sicut facit*.

[^2]: **C.** *Ultra sacramentum et rem*, "beyond the sacrament and the reality", is construed as the print stands: Paul tropes the predicate ("new testament") over and above the sign and the reality it signifies. Because "my blood" stands in the ablative (*in meo sanguine*), the subject "this chalice" is shown to point to the wine, not to the nature of blood.

[^3]: **S/T.** Augustine, *De haeresibus*; Loserth (297.10) gives *Opp.* VIII, 8, with a query; no heresy is named; the likeliest are the Cerdonians, who held that Christ had no flesh and feigned the passion (*De haer.* 21, PL 42, 29: *nec vere mortuum vel quidquam passum, sed simulasse passionem*), or the Manichees (*De haer.* 46, PL 42, 37: *simulatam speciem carnis*) (LEDGER K09g-1). *Per se accidens* is Loserth's correction (D *quod per se*; C *persone*). *Per se* here has the sense "by itself", an accident with no subject, as in the spatial sense of the row.

[^4]: **S.** Luke 2:34, abridged: the Vulgate reads *Ecce positus est hic in ruinam et in resurrectionem multorum in Israël, et in signum cui contradicetur* (DR "Behold this child is set for the fall and for the resurrection of many in Israel and for a sign which shall be contradicted"). Wyclif plays the "sign which shall be contradicted" against the "signs of our own invention".

[^5]: **T.** *Hunc tantum extraneum* has no noun for the masculine *hunc*; "[sense]" is supplied (our emendation in the English only; the Latin stands as printed). *Accidens vel quantitas* are nominative, as quoted terms. A and B read *et vinum* (A *aut* above the line).

[^6]: **S/T.** Jerome refused the formula "three hypostases" lest a poison lie hid under the honey (Jerome, *Ep.* 15.4 to Damasus, PL 22, 357: *Taceantur tres hypostases … venenum sub melle latet*; LEDGER K09g-2). The codices spell *ypostatis*; C reads *horruerit* in the text, *horruerunt* in the margin. *Pure per hec* is rendered as printed; its construal is uncertain ("purely", "by these [words]").

[^7]: **T/C.** Print *nimis malum … quam* does not construe with the comparative *quam*; A and C read *minus*, which is translated. *Donemus ipsis iniuriam* is taken in the sense "forgive a wrong" (*iniuriam donare*): the wrong that those who extinguish false devotion seem to do us. C reads *canobum* for *canabem* ("hemp").

[^8]: **S/C.** Christ's command echoes Matt. 10:16 (DR "Be ye therefore wise as serpents"); Wyclif's wording is his own. Paul's blasphemy: 1 Tim. 1:13 (DR "Who before was a blasphemer … because I did it ignorantly in unbelief"). *Prestare obsequium Deo* is the phrase of John 16:2 (DR "will think that he doth a service to God"). *Obsequium* is rendered "duty", the row's sense for service owed, not "obeisance" paid to a lord.

[^9]: **C.** *Instancia* is "objection" (the counter-instance that refutes an excuse): the truth of faith is what tells against a wayfarer's good intention, as with Paul. The row's other sense, "urgency", would give "a necessary urgency". ACD read *primo necessaria*.

[^10]: **C.** *Foret ibi corpus panis quia in signo* is taken as "the body of the bread would be there", i.e. a quantified body, there as the sign. Another construal is "the body would be there [as] bread, because in sign". D reads *non panis*; the codices have *si in* (in C *si* is erased).

[^11]: **C/S.** Two senses of *posicio*: the "position" of a quantity whose parts have position (line, surface, body: a difference within continuous permanent quantity, Aristotle, *Categories* 6), and the category position (*situs*), the ninth of the ten, "penultimate". The first is *Cat.* 6 (4b20–5a37: line, surface, solid and place have parts with position; time does not, since its parts do not abide); the second is penultimate in the school order of the *Liber sex principiorum* (action, passion, when, where, position, having), though seventh in Aristotle’s list at *Cat.* 4 (LEDGER K09g-3). *Posicio* (twice) is Loserth's reading: ABCD *ita pono*, "an error of the reader, as it seems"; the codices *pono*. *Et ita* is Loserth's correction (D *et illa*, C *et ista*).

[^12]: **C.** *Timendus esset de tam manifesto mendacio* has a masculine gerundive with no expressed subject; taken impersonally ("one would have to fear"). It may refer to the [article] just named: "it would be suspect of so manifest a lie".

[^13]: **G.** *Continuare* here is the making of one quantity continuous with another (the continuation of bodies), not "carrying on"; *continuacio* at 299.29 is the resulting continuity. *Figura* at 300.4 is geometric shape (with "straightness or extension of lines"), not the sacramental figure of 296.25; the English keeps the one word.
