### 01-b ¶1 · 7.13–8.23  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 7] Nec dubium quin ex principiis beati Augustini plane sequitur Christum esse creaturam. Quia iuxta suam infringibilem sentenciam Christus nedum est completa humanitas assumpta, **sed** tam corpus quam anima. Et quis dubitat de ista sequela? Christus est corpus creatum sicut et⁸ spiritus creatus. Ergo Christus est creatura et secundum illam creatus. Nec aliter esset univoce homo nobiscum:⁹ cum omnis alius homo sit duarum naturarum utraque. Nec credo sine [alta]¹⁰ illa philosophia De Anima nos posse recte concipere incarnacionem Verbi. Ideo Augustinus¹¹ Epistula 3ᵃ ad Volusianum sic loquitur: ‘Nunc vero inter Deum et homines mediator apparuit ut in unitate¹² persone¹³ copulans utramque naturam et solita¹³ sublimaret insolitis et insolita solitis temperaret.... Ille¹⁴ ergo sine seminibus operatus est hominem, qui in rerum natura sine seminibus operatus est semina.¹⁴ Ille in suo corpore numeros temporum mensurasque servavit etatum qui sine ulla¹⁵ mutabilitate mutando ordinem contexuit seculorum.¹⁶ Hoc¹⁷ enim crevit in¹⁸ [p. 8] tempore quod cepit ex tempore: Verbum **autem** in principio, per quod facta sunt omnia tempora, tempus elegit quo susciperet carnem, non tempori¹ cessit ut verteretur in carnem: homo quippe Deo accessit, non Deus a se recessit.’ Et sequitur ad propositum. ‘Sic **autem** quidam² reddi sibi racionem flagitant, quomodo Deus homini permixtus sit, ut una fieret persona Christi, cum hoc³ semel fieri oporteat. **Sed** quam racionem reddant ipsi de re quae cotidie⁴ fit, quomodo anima misceatur corpori ut una persona fiat hominis? Nam sicut in unitate⁵ persone anima unitur corpori ut homo sit, ita in unitate⁵ persone Deus unitur⁶ homini ut Christus⁷ sit. In illa ergo persona mixtura est anime et corporis, in hac persona⁸ mixtura est Dei et hominis. Si tamen recedat, auditor⁹ a consuetudine corporum qua⁹ solent duo liquores ita misceri ut neutrum servet suam integritatem.’ Et sequitur. ‘Quia ergo non oportebat ut in plenitudine temporis novum faceret¹⁰ mundum, nova¹¹ fecit in mundo. Homo enim de virgine procreatus et a mortuis in eternam vitam resuscitatus et super celos exaltatus potencius¹² fortasse opus¹³ est quam mundus.’ Quotlibet¹⁴ sunt talia dicta hujus boni hominis, ex quibus aperiri¹⁵ potest sensus scripture loquentis varie de ipso¹⁶ et controversia doctorum sequencium concordari.¹⁷

ENGLISH: Nor is there any doubt that from the principles of blessed Augustine it plainly [follows]{g:sequitur} that Christ is a [creature]{g:creatura}. For according to his unbreakable [teaching]{g:sentencia} Christ is not only the complete [humanity]{g:humanitas} [assumed]{g:assumere}, but both body and soul. And who doubts of this, [what follows]{g:sequela}? Christ is a created body, as he is also a created spirit. Therefore Christ is a creature, and created as to it.[^1] Nor otherwise would he be [univocally]{g:univoce} man with us, since every other man is both of two [natures]{g:natura}. Nor do I believe that without that [lofty][^2] philosophy of the *De anima* we can rightly conceive the incarnation of the [Word]{g:verbum}. So Augustine, in the third letter to Volusian, speaks thus: "But now a mediator between God and men has appeared, so that, joining both natures in the unity of [person]{g:persona}, he might both raise up the accustomed by the unaccustomed and temper the unaccustomed by the accustomed. ... He therefore wrought man without seeds, who in the nature of things wrought seeds without seeds. He kept in his body the numbers of the times and the measures of the ages, who without any changeableness, by changing, wove together the order of the ages. For that grew in [8] time which began from time; but the Word in the beginning, through whom all times were made, chose the time in which to take up flesh; he did not yield to time so as to be turned into flesh: for man drew near to God, God did not withdraw from himself."[^3] And it goes on, to the purpose: "And some demand thus that an account be rendered them, how God was mingled with man so that the one person of Christ came to be, since this had to happen once. But what account do they themselves render of a thing that happens every day, how the soul is mingled with the body so that the one person of a man comes to be? For as in the unity of person the soul is united to the body that there may be a man, so in the unity of person God is united to man that there may be Christ. In that person, therefore, there is a mixture of soul and body; in this person a mixture of God and man. Provided, however, that the hearer withdraws from the habit of bodies, by which two liquids are wont to be so mixed that neither keeps its integrity."[^4] And it goes on: "Since, therefore, it was not fitting that in the fullness of time he should make a new world, he made new things in the world. For a man procreated of a virgin, and raised from the dead into eternal life, and exalted above the heavens, is perhaps a mightier work than the world." There are any number of such sayings of this good man, from which the sense of scripture, which speaks variously of him, can be opened, and the controversy of the doctors who followed can be brought into agreement.

### 01-b ¶2 · 8.24–8.30  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 8] Et patet quod in simbolo Athanasii quando dicitur ‘Filius a Patre solo non est factus nec creatus **sed** genitus’ loquitur de eo pure secundum deitatem.¹⁸ Aliter enim contradiceret sibi ipsi, cum secundum humanitatem conceptus est a tota Trinitate ex substancia matris: ut dicit idem simbolum quando post introducit Christi incarnacionem secundum quam dicit verissime quod Christus est minor patre.

ENGLISH: And it is clear that in the [creed]{g:symbolum} of Athanasius, when it is said "The Son, of the Father alone, is not made nor created, but begotten", it speaks of him purely [as to the Godhead]{g:secundum-deitatem}.[^5] For otherwise it would contradict itself, since [as to the humanity]{g:secundum-humanitatem} he was conceived by the whole Trinity from the substance of his mother; as the same creed says when it afterwards brings in the incarnation of Christ, according to which it says most truly that Christ is less than the Father.[^6]

### 01-b ¶3 · 8.31–9.12  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 8] Et idem patet in alio simbolo ecclesie; ubi primo loquens [p. 9] de eo secundum quod Deus est vere dicit ‘genitum non factum, consubstancialem Patri per quem omnia facta sunt.’ **Sed** post loquendo de Christo ut homine dicit quod ‘incarnatus est de Spiritu Sancto ex Maria virgine et homo factus est.’ Ideo in talibus attendendum est ad regulam beati Augustini ‘quid et secundum quam naturam de Christo dicitur:’ et facile videtur quotquot auctoritates occurrerint¹ concordare. Cum enim sit Christus multorum singulum, sicut essencia divina, species humana, vel aliud universale; patet quia, sicut non repugnat Deum esse genitum et eundem Deum esse ingenitum, sic non repugnat eundem Christum esse creatorem et tamen ex tempore esse creatum.

ENGLISH: And the same is clear in the other creed of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, where, speaking first [9] of him [insofar as]{g:secundum-quod} he is God, it says truly "begotten not made, consubstantial with the Father, by whom all things were made." But afterwards, speaking of Christ as man, it says that he "was incarnate of the Holy Spirit from the virgin Mary, and was made man." Therefore in such [statements] attention must be paid to the rule of blessed Augustine, "what, and according to which nature, is said of Christ"; and it is easily seen that however many authorities occur agree.[^7] For since Christ is a single [thing] of many, as the divine essence, the human species, or another [universal]{g:universale} [is],[^8] it is clear that, just as it is not [incompatible]{g:repugnare} that God is begotten and that the same God is unbegotten, so it is not incompatible that the same Christ is [creator]{g:creator} and yet is created from time.

### 01-b ¶4 · 9.13–9.23  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 9] **Sed** oportet in istis diversas² naturas subintelligere³ cum ydemptitate persone. Unde in primitiva ecclesia quando Christi divinitas fuit minus cognita,⁴ ordinaverunt apostoli quod fideles baptizarentur in nomine domini Iesu Christi, ut patet Actuum 8. ut vel sic Christiani elevarentur in animo⁵ ad credendum Christum esse supremam naturam possibilem⁶ scilicet deitatem. **Sed** postquam sancti doctores ecclesie ut Augustinus, Ieronimus et ceteri cultores assidui scripturarum intellexerunt quod Christus fuit gigas gemine substancie, scilicet⁷ tam divina substancia quam humana, concesserunt copulative Christum esse tam creatorem quam⁸ creaturam.

ENGLISH: But in these [statements] one must [understand as implied]{g:subintelligere} diverse natures together with identity of person. Hence in the primitive church, when the [divinity]{g:divinitas} of Christ was less known, the apostles ordained that the faithful be baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ, as is clear from Acts 8, so that at least in this way Christians might be raised in mind to believing that Christ is the supreme possible nature, namely [the Godhead]{g:deitas}. But after the holy doctors of the church, such as Augustine, Jerome and the other assiduous cultivators of the scriptures, understood that Christ was "a giant of twin substance", namely both divine substance and human,[^9] they granted conjunctively "Christ is both creator and creature".

### 01-b ¶6 · 10.3–10.16  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 10] Nec credo quod sanctus iste intellexit Christum esse creaturam sub racione qua eternaliter genitus; propter tria. Primo quia tunc concederet³ Christum eciam secundum deitatem esse creaturam; et cum creatura dicitur abstractive, sicut substancia vel essencia, tunc Christus esset alia substancia vel essencia quam Pater; quod est heresis Arriana. Et ad tantum non licet catholico laxare terminos, cum omnis creatura secundum Augustinum in Dialogo ad Felicianum et in De Questionibus⁴ Veteris et Nove Legis 122, sit de nichilo facta essencia. **Sed** constat quod Christus secundum deitatem non est factus ex nichilo; ergo nec secundum deitatem est creatura. Non est ergo de intencione huius sancti et profundi doctoris baptizare et⁵ tam extraneare⁶ a scriptura significacionem huius nominis ‘creatura.’

ENGLISH: Nor do I believe that this saint understood "Christ is a creature" under the aspect by which he is eternally begotten; for three [reasons]. First, because then he would grant "Christ, even as to the Godhead, is a creature"; and since "creature" is said abstractively, like "substance" or "essence", then Christ would be another substance or [essence]{g:essencia} than the Father; which is the Arian heresy. And it is not permitted to a catholic to [loose]{g:laxare} the terms so far, since every creature, according to Augustine in the *Dialogue to Felician* and in the *Questions of the Old and New Law* 122, is an essence made from nothing.[^11] Now it is evident that Christ as to the Godhead is not made from nothing; therefore neither as to the Godhead is he a creature. It is not, therefore, the intention of this holy and profound doctor to baptize, and so far to estrange from scripture, the signification of this name "creature".[^12]

### 01-b ¶7 · 10.17–11.1  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 10] Secundo movet me causa quam beatus Ieronimus innuit. Videtur enim loqui de fidelibus sui temporis negantibus adverbia⁷ scripturarum loquencium⁷ de Christo secundum humanitatem. **Sed** quis queso fidelium trepidaret concedere⁸ Christum esse genitum⁹ eternaliter de Patre? cum puri philosophi diu ante incarnacionem hoc professi sunt, ut declaravi¹⁰ in tractatu De Trinitate.¹¹ Non ergo timebat concedere Christum esse¹² genitum vel genitum¹² eternaliter adintra; **sed** non secundum hominem assumptum et¹³ racione cuius concedimus de Christo predicata et gesta hominum ut sanctus doctor exemplificat et clarebit posterius.¹⁴ Unde evidencia Ieronimi est quod concedendum est Christum esse creaturam eo quod concedimus ipsum verum¹⁵ esse [p. 11] hominem et cetera huiusmodi que humanitatem concernunt.

ENGLISH: Second, the cause that blessed Jerome hints at moves me. For he seems to speak of the faithful of his time who denied the adverbs of the scriptures that speak of Christ as to the humanity.[^13] Now who of the faithful, I ask, would tremble to grant "Christ is begotten eternally of the Father"? since mere philosophers professed this long before the incarnation, as I have [made clear]{g:declarare} in the treatise *On the Trinity*. He did not, therefore, fear to grant "Christ is begotten, or begotten eternally, within";[^14] but not as to [the assumed man]{g:homo-assumptus}, by reason of whom we grant of Christ the predicates and deeds of men,[^15] as the holy doctor gives instances, and as will be clear later. Hence Jerome's [evidence]{g:evidencia} is that "Christ is a creature" is to be granted because we grant "he is true [11] man", and other things of this kind that concern the humanity.[^16]

### 01-c ¶1 · 11.21–12.12  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 11] Et istum sensum⁷ declarat Ecclesiasticus 24 cap. loquens de eadem sapiencia: primo sic: ‘ego ex ore altissimi prodii primogenita ante omnem creaturam,’ et sic⁸ de multis que conveniunt divinitati. **Sed** post prosequitur quoad eius humanitatem: ‘tunc precepit et dixit mihi creator omnium, et qui creavit me requievit in tabernaculo⁹ meo:’ dixit, inquam, in plenitudine temporis quando misit me incarnandam; iuxta¹⁰ illud Psalmi 31 iuxta exposicionem Apostoli ad Hebre. 10. ‘holocaustum et propeccato non postulasti; tunc dixi, ecce venio’ scilicet incarnandus; et sic deitas creavit me secundum essenciam corpoream ante omnia secula: que quidem essencia transivit honorata¹¹ a patribus veteris testamenti nunc in gentibus et nunc in patribus, ut patet [p. 12] alibi. **Sed** in plenitudine temporis quando erectum est tabernaculum ex carne assumpta de beata virgine et forma humanitatis copulata ypostatice, tunc requievit tota Trinitas in assumpto corpore iuxta illud Colos. 2.¹ ‘In ipso, inquit Apostolus, habitat omnis plenitudo divinitatis corporaliter.’ Nam tota Trinitas est eadem divina essencia que est Christus existens illud corpus. Unde et illud tabernaculum figuratum est per quelibet tabernacula veteris testamenti. Et ad istum sensum² loquitur apostolorum capitaneus de corpore quod est ipse: ‘iustum, inquit, arbitror quamdiu sum³ in hoc tabernaculo suscitare vos in commonicione, certus quod velox est deposicio tabernaculi mei.’ 2 Petri 1° capitulo.

ENGLISH: And Ecclesiasticus, chapter 24, [makes clear]{g:declarare} this sense, speaking of the same wisdom: first thus: "I came out of the mouth of the most High, the firstborn before all [creatures]{g:creatura}", and so with many things that befit the [divinity]{g:divinitas}. But afterwards it goes on with regard to its [humanity]{g:humanitas}: "Then the creator of all things commanded, and said to me: and he that made me, rested in my tabernacle":[^1] he said, I say, in the fullness of time, when he sent me to be incarnate; according to that [text] of Psalm 31, according to the exposition of the Apostle in Hebrews 10: "Burnt offering and sin offering thou didst not require: then said I, Behold I come", that is, to be incarnate.[^2] And so [the Godhead]{g:deitas} created me as to the bodily essence before all ages; which essence indeed passed on, honoured by the fathers of the old testament, now among the gentiles and now among the fathers, as is clear [12] elsewhere.[^3] But in the fullness of time, when the tabernacle was set up from the flesh [assumed]{g:assumere} from the blessed virgin and the form of the humanity joined hypostatically, then the whole Trinity rested in the assumed body, according to that [text] of Colossians 2: "In him," says the Apostle, "dwelleth all the fulness of the Godhead corporeally."[^4] For the whole Trinity is the same divine [essence]{g:essencia} which is Christ, existing [as] that body. Hence that tabernacle too was prefigured by every tabernacle of the old testament. And to this sense the [captain]{g:capitaneus} of the apostles speaks of the body that is himself:[^5] "I think it meet," he says, "as long as I am in this tabernacle, to stir you up by putting you in remembrance, being assured that the laying away of this my tabernacle is at hand." 2 Peter, chapter 1.

### 01-c ¶3 · 12.20–12.27  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 12] Tercio **autem**, declinante scola ab antiqua logica¹⁰ de universalibus et a recta metaphisica¹¹ de formis substancialibus, negarunt doctores moderniores concorditer quod homo est anima, vel Christus humanitas, et¹² consequenter quod Christus est creatura, intelligentes autonomatice¹³ Christum solum secundum excellentissimum quod est Christus; et sic licet verum concipiant, multum tamen degenerant a logica scripturarum.

ENGLISH: And third, as the school declined from the old logic of [universals]{g:universale} and from the right [metaphysics]{g:metaphisica} of substantial forms, [the more modern doctors]{g:moderniores-doctores} denied with one accord "man is the soul", or "Christ is the humanity", and consequently "Christ is a creature", understanding "Christ" [by antonomasia]{g:autonomatice} only according to the most excellent [thing] that Christ is; and so, although they conceive something true, yet they greatly degenerate from the logic of the scriptures.

### 01-c ¶4 · 12.28–13.2  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 12] Ego **autem** finaliter¹⁴ concordo cum quocunque catholice loquente in illa materia, concedens cum fidelibus primitive ecclesie quod Christus est purissimus homo possibilis: verumtamen non pure¹⁵ vel tantum est homo, cum sit assumpta [p. 13] humanitas; et cum plene sit divinitas, patet quod est aliud **sed** non alius quam illa humanitas.

ENGLISH: But I, finally, agree with whoever speaks in a catholic way on that matter, granting with the faithful of the primitive [church]{g:ecclesia} that Christ is the purest man possible: nevertheless he is not purely or only man, since he is the assumed [13] humanity; and since he is fully the divinity, it is clear that he is something other, but not someone other, than that humanity.[^8]

### 01-c ¶5 · 13.3–13.6  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 13] Concordo 2° cum approbatis doctoribus ut Augustino Ieronimo et ceteris concedens quod Christus nedum est creator et essencia que nullo¹ modo creari poterit vel moveri, **sed** eciam² creatura.

ENGLISH: Second, I agree with the approved doctors, such as Augustine, Jerome and the rest, granting that Christ is not only [creator]{g:creator} and an essence that can in no way be created or moved, but also a creature.[^9]

### 02-a ¶3 · 14.8–14.17  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 14] Quando⁷ ergo illa⁸ opinio videtur Arrio in hoc consentire bonum videtur differenciam⁹ declarare. Nam propterea videtur Magistro et ipsum sequentibus potissime quod non debet concedi simpliciter Christum esse creaturam vel factum **sed** cum determinacione¹⁰ quod fuit factus secundum hominem. Primo ergo notandum quod nunquam fuit hereticus quin in aliquo dixit verum: omnes enim concesserunt Deum esse: et ideo valde inconsonum est doctori imponere alteri¹¹ heresim Arrianam, nisi prius diligenter notaverit in quibus et quomodo talis heresis est fundata.

ENGLISH: Since, therefore, that opinion seems to agree with Arius in this, it seems good to make clear the [difference]{g:differencia}.[^4] For it is above all for this reason that it seems to the Master and those who follow him that "Christ is a creature" or "made" ought not to be granted [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}, but with the determination that he was made as to the man. First, then, it must be noted that there was never a heretic who did not say something true in some point: for all granted "God is"; and so it is very discordant for a doctor to impute the Arian heresy to another unless he has first diligently noted in what and how such a heresy is founded.

### 02-a ¶4 · 14.18–14.22  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 14] Stetit ergo heresis Arriana in his tribus precipue. Primo in hoc quod negavit Christum esse Deum deorum et magnum Deum, cum posuit eum¹² esse non eiusdem substancie cum Patre, **sed** ipsos differre ab invicem eciam secundum deitatem substancialiter sicut¹³ differunt personaliter.

ENGLISH: The Arian heresy, then, stood chiefly in these three [points]. First, in this: that [Arius] denied "Christ is the God of gods and a great God", since he held that he is not of the same substance with the Father, but that they differ from one another even [as to the Godhead]{g:secundum-deitatem} substantially, as they differ personally.

### 02-a ¶5 · 14.23–15.31  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 14] Unde Augustinus Epa. 33 ad Bonifacium, 'Ut, inquit, breviter¹⁴ insinuem dilectioni tue quid inter Arrianorum¹⁵ et Donatistarum intersit errorem: Arriani Patris et Filii et Spiritus Sancti diversas esse substancias dicunt; Donatiste **autem** non hoc¹⁶ dicunt, **sed** unam Trinitatis substanciam confitentur.' Dixit itaque¹⁷ Arrius quod summa essencia que est Christus sit citra essenciam Dei Patris, ut est essencia angeli vel hominis quos scriptura sacra vocat deos pluraliter, ut allegat Salvator Iudeis Ioh. 10. 'Nonne scriptum est in lege vestra, 'Ego dixi dii estis?' Si igitur eos vocat deos, [p. 15] ad quos sermo Dei factus est, et non potest solvi scriptura; quem Pater sanctificavit et misit in mundum, quomodo et vos dicitis 'blasfemas,'¹ quia dixi 'Filius Dei sum¹?' Et forte ex isto dicto capta est heresis Arriana. Unde dicit Augustinus Ome 48.² quod Iudei audientes illud Ioh. 10. 'Ego et Pater unum sumus,' et intelligentes Christum intendere quod sit eadem substancia cum Patre, 'Iudei, inquit, intellexerunt quod Arriani non intellexerunt; quoniam senserunt non posse dici ''ego et Pater unum sumus,'' nisi ubi equalitas est Patris et Filii. **Sed** Dominus videns Judeos pre ira non ferre splendorem veritatis, eum temperavit in verbis eis allegans illud Psⁱ· 81.³ ''Ego dixi dii estis etc.''' Nec est putandum quod Veritas illuserit⁴ Iudeis⁵ sophistice in hoc dicto, **sed** dulciter manuduxit mitigando eorum rancorem ex⁶ scriptura patriarche David plus eis autentica⁷ inter omnes. Cum⁸ enim non fuit in Christo EST et NON, et dixit⁸ eis superius Ioh. 10. quod ipse est omnipotens et eadem substancia cum Patre: patet quod illam sentenciam postmodum non retractat. Minor patet notato textu illius capⁱ. Nam ibi dicitur: 'oves mee vocem meam audiunt et ego vitam eternam do eis, et non rapiet eas⁹ quisquam de manu mea' et cetera que secuntur. Probatur illa trimembris conclusio ex ibi positis. Nam ibi¹⁰ dicitur, 'Pater meus quod dedit michi maius omnibus est:' ergo Pater meus¹¹ est omnipotens, et per consequens 'nemo potest rapere de manu Patris mei,' et ultra sequitur¹² 'nemo potest rapere de manu Patris mei;¹³ et 'Ego et Pater unum sumus;' ergo 'nemo potest rapere oves de manu mea.' Istam itaque connexionem subtilissimam Iudei conceperant quam ignorabant Arriani. Ex quo patet quod in deduccione sequenti eandem conclusionem intenderat **sed** medio plus occulto.

ENGLISH: Hence Augustine, letter 33, to Boniface: "That I may briefly," he says, "convey to your Love what difference there is between the error of the Arians and of the Donatists: the Arians say that the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit are diverse substances; but the Donatists do not say this, but confess one substance of the Trinity."[^5] So Arius said that the highest [essence]{g:essencia} which is Christ falls short of the essence of God the Father, as is the essence of an angel or of a man, whom holy scripture calls gods in the plural, as the Saviour [alleges]{g:allegare} to the Jews, John 10: "Is it not written in your law: I said, you are gods? If therefore he calls them gods, [15] to whom the word of God was spoken, and the scripture cannot be broken; how do you also say of him whom the Father hath sanctified and sent into the world: Thou blasphemest; because I said: I am the Son of God?"[^6] And perhaps the Arian heresy was taken from this saying. Hence Augustine says, Homily 48, that the Jews, hearing that [text] of John 10, "I and the Father are one", and understanding Christ to [maintain]{g:intendere} that he is the same substance with the Father: "the Jews," he says, "understood what the Arians did not understand; for they perceived that 'I and the Father are one' could not be said except where there is equality of the Father and the Son. But the Lord, seeing that the Jews for anger did not bear the splendour of the truth, tempered it in words, alleging to them that [text] of Psalm 81, 'I have said: You are gods, etc.'"[^7] Nor is it to be thought that the Truth mocked the Jews sophistically in this saying; but he gently led them by the hand, mitigating their rancour from the scripture of the patriarch David, the most [authentic]{g:autenticus} to them of all. For since there was not in Christ "It is" and "It is not", and he said to them above, in John 10, that he is omnipotent and the same substance with the Father, it is clear that he does not afterwards retract that teaching.[^8] The minor is clear when the text of that chapter is noted. For there it is said: "My sheep hear my voice; and I give them life everlasting, and no man shall pluck them out of my hand", and the rest that follows. That three-membered [conclusion]{g:conclusio} is proved from what is laid down there. For there it is said, "That which my Father hath given me is greater than all": therefore my Father is omnipotent, and consequently "no one can snatch them out of the hand of my Father"; and further it [follows]{g:sequitur}: "no one can snatch them out of the hand of my Father; and 'I and the Father are one'; therefore 'no one can snatch the sheep out of my hand.'" So the Jews had grasped this most subtle [conditional]{g:connexio}, of which the Arians [were ignorant]{g:ignorare}.[^9] From which it is clear that in the deduction that follows he maintained the same conclusion, but by a more hidden [middle term]{g:media}.

### 02-a ¶6 · 16.1–16.33  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 16] Pro quo notandum quod sicut Judei multum appreciati¹ sunt prophetam² David, sic³ ex eius textu cognoverant quod Messias venturus ex eis foret tam Deus quam homo. Hoc videtur evidens ex argumento Salvatoris facto Phariseis, Mt 22. ubi sic congregatis Phariseis interrogat ab eis⁴ Jesus dicens,⁵ 'Quid vobis videtur de Christo? cuius filius est? dicunt ei, 'David:'⁶ ait illis, 'quomodo ergo David⁷ in spiritu vocat eum dominum dicens: 'dixit Dominus Domino meo, 'sede a dextris, donec ponam inimicos tuos scabellum pedum tuorum.' Si ergo David vocat eum Dominum quomodo filius eius est? Et nemo poterat ei respondere verbum, nec ausus est quisquam ex illa die eum amplius interrogare.' Non enim contendebant⁶ illi legis periti in ampliacione⁸ verbi de presenti scientes ex sensu scripture quod apud Deum sunt cuncta⁹ presencia. Ideo sciverunt Christum vel Messiam esse filium David pro quodam tempore. Sciverunt 2^do illud Psalmi 109 quod Veritas allegavit dictum esse de Christo. Sciverunt 3° quod propheta tam eximius non vocaret filium suum tam pauperem et abiectum Dominum suum secundum naturam ab¹⁰ eo assumptam **sed** secundum naturam¹⁰ superiorem. Ideo non affuit eis vel ullum¹¹ coloratum subterfugium evadendi. Unde pro 2° argumento Salvatoris supponitur quod Verbum vel Sermo Dei quod idem est, sit eternum idem substancialiter Deo Patri. Patet hoc per illud Psalmi 32¹² 'Verbo Domini celi firmati sunt.' 2^do supponitur quod dictum verbum fuit incarnandum. Patet hoc per illud Psalmi 106¹³ 'misit verbum suum et sanavit eos.' Unde et dictum Verbum vocaverunt prophete concorditer Messiam vel Christum eis promissum. 3° supponitur quod dicti Verbi participacione sit multitudo hominum in¹⁴ Christum directe¹⁴ credencium deificanda participative, sicut carbo ex ignis participacione est ignitus. Patet hoc per illud Ps. 81.¹⁵ 'Ego dixi dii estis.'

ENGLISH: On which it must be noted that, as the Jews greatly prized the prophet David, so from his text they had come to know that the Messiah who was to come from them would be both God and man. This seems evident from the Saviour's argument made to the Pharisees, Matthew 22, where, the Pharisees being thus gathered together, Jesus asks of them, saying: "What think you of Christ? Whose son is he? They say to him: David's. He saith to them: How then doth David in spirit call him Lord, saying: The Lord said to my Lord: Sit on the right hand, until I make thy enemies thy footstool? If David then call him Lord, how is he his son? And no man was able to answer him a word: neither durst any man from that day forth ask him any more questions." For those learned in the law did not dispute over the [ampliation]{g:ampliacio} of the verb in the present [tense], knowing from the sense of scripture that with God all things are present.[^10] Therefore they knew that Christ or the Messiah is the son of David for a certain time. Second, they knew that [the text] of Psalm 109 which the Truth alleged was said of Christ. Third, they knew that so eminent a prophet would not call his son, so poor and abject, his Lord as to the [nature]{g:natura} [assumed]{g:assumere} from him, but as to a higher nature. Therefore there was not available to them even any subterfuge [given colour]{g:colorare} for escaping.[^11] Hence for the Saviour's second argument it is supposed that the [Word]{g:verbum} or Speech of God, which is the same, is eternal, substantially the same as God the Father.[^12] This is clear from that [text] of Psalm 32: "By the word of the Lord the heavens were established." Second, it is supposed that the said Word was to be incarnate. This is clear from that [text] of Psalm 106: "He sent his word, and healed them." Hence also the prophets with one accord called the said Word the Messiah or Christ promised to them. Third, it is supposed that by participation in the said Word a multitude of men who believe directly in Christ is to be deified [participatively]{g:participative}, as a coal is set on fire by participation in fire. This is clear from that [text] of Psalm 81: "I have said: You are gods."

### 02-a ¶7 · 17.1–17.19  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 17] Istis suppositis formetur racio sic ex sentencia Salvatoris: Ille Sermo vel Verbum, in quo concordant omnes prophete de operibus miraculosis, de tempore et aliis circumstanciis, et illis quibus mittitur salvandis et deificandis, est verus Messias, et per consequens Deus et homo iuxta textus propheticos: **sed** in me omnia ista conveniunt: ergo sum Deus¹ et homo: et per consequens non solum¹ participacione deificatus.² Ideo hic³ dicit Augustinus; 'Si Sermone⁴ Dei facto ad⁵ homines fiunt ipsi dii, tunc⁶ participando fiunt ipsi dii⁶; et per consequens Sermo ille, quo participant, erit Deus. Si lumine. luminati dii sunt, lumen quod illuminat non est Deus? si calefacti igne salutari⁷ dii efficiuntur, ignis unde calefiunt non est Deus?' Oportet ergo quod⁸ Sermo, quo⁸ fontaliter deificantur homines ex unitate substancie cum suo eternaliter adintra dicente, sit eadem deitas cum dicente; et per consequens non blasfemat⁹ dicendo se naturalem filium Dei esse. Et ex ignorancia huius sensus literalis scripture exciderant¹⁰ Arriani, sicut et Sabelliani, ut recitat Augustinus Ome. 47.

ENGLISH: These being supposed, let the argument be formed thus from the Saviour's teaching: That Speech or Word, in whom all the prophets agree concerning the miraculous works, concerning the time and the other circumstances, and concerning those to whom he is sent, who are to be saved and deified, is the true Messiah, and consequently God and man according to the prophetic texts; now in me all these things come together; therefore I am God and man; and consequently not deified only by participation.[^13] So Augustine says here: "If by the Speech of God made to men they themselves become gods, then by participating they themselves become gods; and consequently that Speech in which they participate will be God. If those lit by light are gods, is the light that lights them not God? If those warmed by the saving fire are made gods, is the fire by which they are warmed not God?"[^14] It must be, therefore, that the Speech by which men are fontally deified, from unity of substance with the one who speaks him eternally within, is the same Godhead as the speaker;[^15] and consequently he does not blaspheme in saying that he is the natural son of God. And from ignorance of this literal sense of scripture the Arians had fallen away, as also the Sabellians, as Augustine recounts, Homily 47.[^16]
