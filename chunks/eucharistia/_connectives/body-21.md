### 08-b ¶2 · 237.3–237.9  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 237] **Sed** contra istud replicatur primo per hoc quod tale corpus est solum semel per datum locum, cum postquam semel intravit illum nunquam exivit, ut suppono. Sicut ergo non quociens comedisti, tociens fuisti homo (ut dicunt loyci), eo quod semel es homo continue, ita videtur dicendum quod solum semel es in isto loco.

ENGLISH: But against this [it is rejoined]{g:replicatur}, first, by this: that such a body is only once through a given place, since after it once entered it, it never went out, as I suppose. Therefore, just as you were not a man as many times as you have eaten (as the logicians say), because you are a man once, continuously, so it seems it must be said that you are only once in this place.[^2]

### 08-b ¶4 · 237.17–237.21  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 237] Loquamur **autem** de aliquo modo, non ut dicit vicissitudinem temporis, **sed** ut dicit multitudinem ordinis naturalis; et tunc oportet dicere quod Deus non potest ponere creaturam alicubi nisi ponat infinicies eam ibi.

ENGLISH: And let us speak of some mode [of "so many times"],[^4] not as it [signifies]{g:dicere} the alternation of time but as it signifies a multitude of natural order; and then one must say: "God cannot put a [creature]{g:creatura} anywhere unless he puts it there infinitely many times."

### 08-b ¶7 · 238.1–239.14  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 238] Item, videtur quod omne corpus sit infinitum in quacumque denominacione sua corporea; nam corpus potest esse infinitum sine acquisicione alicuius quantitatis preter illam quantam habet et quantumcunque magnum potest esse cum illis paribus, ita magnum est modo: ergo omne corpus est infinitum. Consequencia patet ex hoc quod omnem quantitatem quam potest habere cum illis paribus habet modo. Et assumptum probatur capiendo A cubum pedalem tunc multiplicato eo per sex loca in quibus tangat se secundum sex superficies varias et fiat continuacio, et patet quod A equivalet sextipedali quadrangulo sine hoc quod aliqua quantitas generetur secundum se totam; pono iterum quod quelibet secunda pars proporcionalis istius A cum toto residuo multiplicetur per duodecim loca et fiat continuacio secundum superficies dispares, et patet quod A equivalebit uni corpori duplo ad sextipedale, et sic in infinitum secundum omnem dimensionem, petito hoc principio: si sint duo quanta et unum superpositum reliquo, nec excedit nec exceditur ab eodem, tunc illa sunt equalia. A enim est equale loco vel situi per quem sic extenditur. Nec valet dicere quod generatur nova quantitas, quia nulla generatur secundum se totam, sicut non generatur in A superficies, punctus vel linea. Cum ergo penes multiplicacionem punctualium attenditur maioritas quantitatis, patet quod A non maioratur in casu posito, et per idem non minoraretur ipso iterum demultiplicato, ymmo coextensa una medietate A cum sua quantitate cum reliqua, et sic in infinitum usque ad situm punctale, videtur quod tam [p. 239] A quam sua quantitas manebit continue eque magna, quia nullam quantitatem deperdet **sed** aliquam per accidens acquiret. Ex quo videtur quod nullam magnitudinem acquiret vel perdet, et sic omne quantum foret infinitum magnum et infinitum parvum, ymmo tantus mundus ut est iste et infinitum maior posset constitui per punctale. Quis ergo scit utrum sit ita de facto, cum brevius cum punctali materia posset fieri quoad beatitudinem vel aliquem per se finem; nec racio vel experiencia convincit oppositum. Quomodo ergo poneretur a philosopho cui non sit revelacio tanta multitudo rerum superflua per verbum Dei abbreviatum quod conpendiosissime ordinat media ad fines quos intendit?

ENGLISH: Again, it seems that every body is infinite in whatever bodily [naming]{g:denominacio} of it;[^7] for a body can be infinite without the acquisition of any [quantity]{g:quantitas} beyond that which it has, and however great it can be, other things being equal, so great is it now: therefore every body is infinite. The [inference]{g:consequencia} is clear from this, that every quantity it can have, other things being equal, it has now. And [the assumption]{g:assumptum} is proved by taking A, a [foot-long]{g:pedalitas} cube: then, it being multiplied through six places in which it touches itself along six different surfaces, and [continuation]{g:continuacio} being made, it is clear that A is equivalent to a six-foot quadrangle without any quantity being generated as a whole;[^8] I suppose again that each second [proportional part]{g:partes-proporcionales} of this A, with the whole residue, is multiplied through twelve places and continuation is made along unequal surfaces, and it is clear that A will be equivalent to one body double the six-foot one; and so to infinity in every dimension, this principle being asked for:[^9] "if there are two [quantified]{g:quantus} things and one is laid upon the other, and it neither exceeds nor is exceeded by it, then they are equal." For A is equal to the place or site through which it is so extended. Nor does it avail to say that a new quantity is generated, for none is generated as a whole, just as no surface, point or line is generated in A. Since, therefore, the [greatness]{g:majoritas} of a quantity is reckoned by the multiplication of points,[^10] it is clear that A is not made greater in the case posited; and by the same token it would not be made less when it is again [demultiplied]{g:demultiplicare}; indeed, one half of A with its quantity being coextended with the other, and so to infinity down to a point-site, it seems that both [239] A and its quantity will remain continuously equally great, for it will lose no quantity but will acquire some [by accident]{g:per-accidens}. From which it seems that it will acquire or lose no magnitude, and so every quantified thing would be infinitely great and infinitely small; indeed, a world as great as this one is, and infinitely greater, could be constituted through a point. Who then knows whether it is so in fact, since it could be done more briefly with point-sized matter as regards blessedness or any end [of itself]{g:per-se}? Nor does reason or experience [conclusively prove]{g:convincere} the opposite. How then would so great a multitude of things, superfluous by the abbreviated word of God, which orders means most compendiously to the ends it intends,[^11] be posited by a philosopher to whom there is no revelation?

### 08-b ¶11 · 240.12–240.30  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 240] Quantum ad differencias figurarum patet quod ille presupponunt differenciam situs que tollitur; nam signato A triangulo qui sit medietas quadrati et multiplicato ipso per duo loca immediata sic quod applicetur unum extremum basis A ad aliud extremum eiusdem basis, ita quod fiat ad omnem eius punctum continuacio, patet quod annichilata reliqua medietate fieret unum quadratum, et sic quantumcunque magna figura et cuiuscunque speciei potest esse eciam per solum punctum multiplicatum; nam multiplicato A puncto per omnem situm punctalem qui iam est in mundo, et annichilato toto residuo, patet quod nullibi deficiet situs vel figura. Et patet inanitas responsionis qua dicitur quod non constituitur figura nisi per quantitatem secundum se totam non multiplicatam, quia si eadem persona potest multiplicari, replendo situs eque sicut tot homines, per idem eadem quantitas et figura, quia aliter non foret multiplicacio **sed** persone per unum situm posicio.

ENGLISH: As to the differences of figures, it is clear that they presuppose the difference of site, which is taken away; for, A being designated a triangle that is half of a square, and it being multiplied through two [immediate]{g:immediatus} places[^15] so that one end of A's base is applied to the other end of the same base, so that continuation is made at its every point, it is clear that, the other half being annihilated, one square would come to be; and so a figure however great and of whatever species can be even through a single point multiplied; for, A, a point, being multiplied through every point-site that now is in the world, and the whole residue annihilated, it is clear that nowhere will site or figure be lacking. And the emptiness is clear of the response by which it is said that a figure is constituted only through quantity as a whole not multiplied; for if the same [person]{g:persona} can be multiplied, filling sites equally as so many men [would], by the same token the same quantity and figure [can], for otherwise it would not be multiplication but the position of the person through one site.

### 08-c ¶2 · 241.23–243.7  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 241] Et conformiter fantasiatur ponendo quod Deus cum quolibet instanti ponat infinita instancia et cum quolibet tempore infinita tempora equalia non communicancia; et tunc videtur quod omne durans per tempus infinitum diu durat, quia per infinitum magnum tempus, eo quod per infinitum multa tempora equalia non communicancia, quod equivalet. Ymmo videtur quod Deus ex duobus coextensis potest constituere [p. 242] unum tempus, sicut ex duabus superficiebus, sitibus vel actibus eiusdem speciei parcialiter componibilibus potest constituere tercium compositum, et sic sicut nemo sciret utrum infinitum diu durabit, sic nesciret utrum velociter movebitur vel immediate post hoc erit dies iudicii propter multiplicacionem temporis et propter tempora et loca abscondita; et periret in toto mundo noticia sensibilium et per consequens omnis sciencia, cum omnis homo tamquam vertiginosus nesciret discernere de quocunque sensibili. Et omnium istorum errorum origo est mendacium fictum de sacramento altaris. Non enim finget cultor accidentis quod potencia Dei terminatur ad tantum mirabile quod potest facere et non ultra, vel quod sic est de facto quoad mundum et alia sensibilia et non modo infinitum magis mirabili nobis abscondito, et sic redirent opiniones antique, ut loquitur Aristoteles primo Ethicorum et specialiter quod omnis homo nichil scit **sed** solummodo opinatur; et cum vacuum non foret nisi qualitas carens materiali substancia, quilibet cultor accidencium ignorat si per totum mundum sit vacuum eciam infinitum, et sic rectificando in infinitum corticem interceptam inter duas lineas girativas in corulo columpnari deveniretur demum ad partem proporcionalem ultimam coruli et ad ultimum pedale linee girative; et oporteret dare ubi est extremum alterum, cum dato situato dandus est situs, sicut et dandus foret, ubi sit sor qui infigatur continue in extremo corticis moto. Sic enim dicitur quod servando ligulam secundum eius extrema in manibus distantibus fixis continue et rarefacta illa in infinitum atque correspondenter girata super corpus columpnare interceptum inter manus quod circumgiretur proporcionaliter [p. 243] ut ligula exigit se circumvolvi; quo facto foret ligula infinita et tamen secundum eius ambo extrema in manibus tuis tenta; mille sunt casus quibus perturbatur ecclesia, qui omnes dependent sive fundantur super fantasia maxime inpossibili atque heretica occasione materie de sacramento eukaristie introducta.

ENGLISH: And in conformity it is [fancied]{g:fantasiare}, by positing that God with any instant whatever posits infinitely many instants, and with any time whatever infinitely many equal times [not communicating]{g:non-communicans};[^4] and then it seems that everything that lasts through a time lasts infinitely long, for [it lasts] through an infinitely great time, because through infinitely many equal times not communicating, which is equivalent. Indeed, it seems that God can constitute out of two coextended [times][^5] [242] one time, just as out of two surfaces, sites or acts of the same species, partially composable, he can constitute a third composite; and so, just as nobody would know whether he will last infinitely long, so he would not know whether he will be moved swiftly, or whether [without intermediary]{g:immediate} after this will be the day of judgement, on account of the multiplication of time and on account of hidden times and places; and in the whole world the knowledge of [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} things would perish, and consequently all science, since every man, like one dizzy, would not know how to discern about any perceptible thing whatever. And the origin of all these errors is the lie feigned about the [sacrament of the altar]{g:sacramentum-altaris}. For the [worshipper]{g:cultor} of the [accident]{g:accidens} will not [feign]{g:fingere} that the power of God is terminated at a marvel so great as it can do and not beyond, or that it is so in fact as to the world and other perceptible things and not now in an infinitely more marvellous [way] hidden from us;[^6] and so the ancient [opinions]{g:opinio} would return, as Aristotle speaks in the first [book] of the *Ethics*, and especially that every man knows nothing but only opines (LEDGER K08c-2); and since a vacuum would be nothing but a [quality]{g:qualitas} lacking material [substance]{g:substancia}, every one of the [worshippers of accidents]{g:cultores-accidencium} is ignorant whether through the whole world there is a vacuum, even an infinite one; and so, by straightening to infinity the bark intercepted between two winding lines on a [columnar hazel-rod]{g:corulus-columpnaris}, one would come at last to the last [proportional part]{g:partes-proporcionales} of the hazel-rod and to the last [foot-length]{g:pedalitas} of the winding line;[^7] and one would have to assign where the other end is, since, a situated thing being given, a site must be given, just as it would have to be given where Sor is, who is fixed continuously in the end of the moved bark.[^8] For so it is said: that, keeping a strap by its ends in distant hands held continuously fixed, and it being rarefied to infinity and correspondingly wound upon a columnar body intercepted between the hands, which is turned round proportionally [243] as the strap requires to be wound round, this done, the strap would be infinite and yet held by both its ends in your hands.[^9] A thousand are the cases by which the [church]{g:ecclesia} is disturbed, which all depend or are founded upon an [imagination]{g:fantasia} supremely impossible and heretical, introduced on occasion of the matter of the sacrament of [the Eucharist]{g:eukaristia}.

### 08-c ¶4 · 243.23–243.31  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 243] **Sed** pro multiplicacione communiter sic arguitur: Non magis repugnat idem corpus in numero multiplicari simul per diversa loca quam repugnat diversa corpora situari simul per eundem locum eis adequatum, cum tanta sit utrobique distancia atque diversitas **sed** secundum est satis possibile, ut patet de corporibus glorificatis secundum dotem subtilitatis coextensis et de elementis immixtis: ergo et primum est per idem possibile.

ENGLISH: But for multiplication it is commonly argued thus: "It is no more [incompatible]{g:repugnare} for the same body in number to be multiplied at once through different places than it is incompatible for different bodies to be situated at once through the same place adequate to them, since there is as great a distance and diversity [on both sides]{g:utrobique}; now the second is quite possible, as is clear of glorified bodies coextended by the [dower of subtlety]{g:dos-subtilitatis},[^11] and of elements mingled together: therefore the first too is by the same token possible."[^12]

### 08-d ¶1 · 244.17–245.23  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 244] Et idem est iudicium de quantitate et qualitate coextensis cum materiali essencia. Si enim quantitas, qualitas, forma et materia haberent per se dimensiones, sicut habet materialis substancia (ut fantasiatur error predictus de eukaristia), tunc forent quatuor quantitates preter quantitatem communem coextense per quinque loca coextensa, et quantitas cum suo loco esset prius quoad consequenciam quam alia loca vel alie quantitates quatuor sequentes, quia (ut fantasiatur) quantitas cum suo loco posset esse vacuum sine aliqua aliorum quatuor, non econtra, et (ut videtur michi) tunc foret locus mundi diminutus, cum deficerent quatuor communitatis multitudines individualium; aliud enim foret unam istarum quinque rerum per se situari et aliud aliam sic situari, [p. 245] cum Deus posset annichilare posterius (ut inquiunt) servato priori; vel enim oportet dare materialem substanciam sine tali quantitate limitata vel si hoc non potest contingere, tunc quantitas talis est longe prior materiali substancia. Deus enim (ut inquiunt) potest miraculose servare quantitatem sine materiali substancia, non econtra, et sic videtur quod ad omnem punctum mundi relictum foret infinitum multum de vacuo, cum Deus (ut inquiunt) posset ponere in situ istius mundi infinita corpora et alia que poterunt per se esse infinitum disparium specierum et cuilibet illorum oporteret dare locum proprium. Non enim est locus prius natura quam corporalis substancia **sed** econtra, ideo non est possibile eundem locum in numero suscipere vicissim quotlibet species materialis substancie, formarum substancialium et accidentalium, quia tunc foret locus vacuum; quod Deus non posset movere precedens omnia ista. **Sed** quid stulcius aut infidelius. Oportet ergo omne situatum per se possibile habere situm proprium qui est illam essenciam situari (ut nunc supponitur). Et patet quod secunda pars maioris assumpte sit falsa nec per minorem probata.

ENGLISH: And the judgement is the same of [quantity]{g:quantitas} and [quality]{g:qualitas} coextended with material [essence]{g:essencia}. For if quantity, quality, form and matter had dimensions [by themselves]{g:per-se},[^1] as material [substance]{g:substancia} has (as the aforesaid error about [the Eucharist]{g:eukaristia} [fancies]{g:fantasiare}), then there would be four quantities besides the common quantity, coextended through five coextended places; and quantity with its place would be prior as to [inference]{g:consequencia} to the other places or the four other quantities that follow, for (as it fancies) quantity with its place could be a vacuum without any of the other four, not conversely; and (as it seems to me) the place of the world would then be diminished, since four multitudes of individual [sites] of the common [place] would be lacking;[^2] for it would be one thing for one of these five things to be situated by itself and another for another to be so situated, [245] since God could annihilate the posterior (as they say) while keeping the prior; for either one must assign material substance without such a limited quantity,[^3] or, if this cannot happen, then such quantity is far prior to material substance. For God (as they say) can miraculously keep quantity without material substance, not conversely; and so it seems that at every point of the world left there would be infinitely much of vacuum, since God (as they say) could put in the site of this world infinitely many bodies, and other things that could be by themselves, of infinitely many disparate species, and to each of them one would have to assign a proper place. For place is not prior by nature to bodily substance, but conversely; therefore it is not possible for the same place in number to receive in turn any number of species of material substance, of [substantial forms]{g:forma-substancialis} and accidental forms, for then place would be a vacuum; which God could not move, [it] preceding all these.[^4] But what is more foolish or more faithless? Therefore every situated thing possible by itself must have a proper site, which is that essence's being situated (as is now supposed). And it is clear that the second part of the [assumed]{g:assumere} major is false,[^5] and not proved by the minor.

### 08-d ¶3 · 246.6–246.14  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 246] **Sed** da quod per idem tempus sine interpolacione sit sanctus per illa loca distancia, adhuc non oporteret concedere illud propositum, cum stat sensus illusio propter similitudinem de ydemptificacione corporis; possunt enim spiritui sancti Ambrosii aptari corpora similia et eandem personam secundum illa corpora diversa ministrare simul tempore per diversa loca, ideo illud miraculum supposita eius veritate est nimis nudum ad inferendum propositum.

ENGLISH: But grant that through the same time, without [interruption]{g:interpolacio}, the saint is through those distant places: still one would not have to grant that point proposed, since an [illusion]{g:illusio} of sense can stand,[^7] on account of the likeness, about the [identification]{g:ydemptificacio} of the body; for like bodies can be fitted to the spirit of Saint Ambrose, and the same [person]{g:persona}, according to those different bodies, can minister at once in time through different places; therefore that miracle, its truth supposed, is [exceedingly]{g:nimis} bare for inferring the point proposed.

### 08-d ¶4 · 246.15–247.11  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 246] Et quantum ad illud dictum, patet quod non iuvat propositum, cum idem corpus numero potest vicissim esse per diversa loca, sicut idem corpus potest esse per diversa instancia. Unde si applicaretur ad propositum, concederetur quod stat idem corpus esse simul per diversa instancia, sic intenditur quod sit simul per diversa loca, nam tam irracionale et tam inpossibile foret unum sicut reliquum; ideo sicut non potest esse quod idem corpus sit simul per diversa instancia, sic non potest esse quod ipsum sit simul per diversa loca, loquendo de esse dimensionali; faciat ergo sic opinans vel probet quod sint simul tempore diversa eius instancia, et tunc est pro opinione evidencia; sicut enim non possunt simul distendi tempora sic nec loca; loquimur enim de situ inmobili equali linea lineari superficiali vel profundali substancie locate in qua est locus subiective; iste (inquam) sensus de loco est pertinencior quam sensus de concavitate [p. 247] corporis continentis. Et patet quod patencius est nulla instancia posse esse simul tempore quam loca posse esse simul situaliter; immediata (inquam) esse possunt **sed** simul esse non possunt, quia si A locus sit simul cum B loco sibi equali, tunc ex equali continencia uterque locat reliquum, et sic uterque superfluit componens cum reliquo intensive, et conformiter si multa instancia sunt simul; non enim loquimur de simultate inmediacionis **sed** de simultate adequacionis, sic quod eadem res de genere loci vel temporis quodlibet sit simul cum alio contineat adequate.

ENGLISH: And as to that saying,[^8] it is clear that it does not help the point proposed, since the same body in number can be in turn through different places, just as the same body can be through different instants. Hence, if it were applied to the point proposed, it would be granted that it can stand that the same body is at once through different instants, as it is [maintained]{g:intendere} that it is at once through different places; for the one would be as irrational and as impossible as the other. Therefore, just as it cannot be that the same body is at once through different instants, so it cannot be that it is at once through different places, speaking of dimensional [being]{g:esse}. Let him then who so opines make, or prove, that its different instants are at once in time, and then there is [evidence]{g:evidencia} for the [opinion]{g:opinio}; for just as times cannot be stretched apart at once, so neither can places. For we speak of the immovable site equal, line by line,[^9] linear, superficial or in depth, to the located substance in which place is [subjectively]{g:subiective}; this sense (I say) of place is [more fitting]{g:pertinencior} than the sense of the concavity [247] of the containing body. And it is clear that it is more evident that no instants can be at once in time than that places can be at once [situally]{g:situaliter}; they can (I say) be immediate,[^10] but they cannot be at once, for if place A is at once with place B equal to it, then by equal containing each locates the other, and so each is superfluous, composing with the other intensively; and in conformity if many instants are at once. For we do not speak of the simultaneity of immediacy but of the [simultaneity of adequacy]{g:simultas-adequacionis}, so that the same thing of the genus of place or of time, whichever is at once with another, contains it adequately.[^11]
