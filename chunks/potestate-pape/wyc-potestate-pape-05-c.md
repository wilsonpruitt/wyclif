# *De potestate pape*, chapter 5, part 3: objections from the papal dignity and from the saints who approved endowment

## ¶1 · 86.18–89.20

> [p. 86] Sed contra illud arguitur, primo de papa quomodo non sufficeret dignitati sui officii nisi haberet regna et copiosa dominia pro se et suis officiariis limitata. Et idem est iudicium de nostris episcopis atque abbatibus. Sed ad istud sepe dictum est quod officiariorum superfluitas et apparatuum sumptuositas est in causa. Unde correspondenter ad istorum crementum decrescit religiositas et edificatoria officiositas. Unde Bernhardus libro IV ad Eugenium: Inter hec tu pastor procedis deauratus, tam multa circumdatus varietate. Oves quid capiunt? Si auderem dicere, demonum magis quam ovium pascua hec sunt. Non sic factitabat Petrus vel Paulus ludebat. Et sequitur: Pastorem te populo aut nega aut exhibe; non negabis, ne cuius sedem tenes te neges heredem; Petrus hic est, qui nescitur processisse [p. 87] vel gemmis ornatus vel sericis, non tectus auro vel vectus equo albo nec stipatus milite nec circumstrepentibus ministris; absque hiis tamen credidit satis posse impleri salutare mandatum. Si amas me, pasce oves meas. In hiis successisti non Petro sed Constantino. Inconsequens igitur foret quod sequitur quod in prelatis mundi gloria sit sepulta vita edificatoria instar Christi et apostolorum in ipso abscondita et undique conversacio consona ac lucrifactiva in clero et laycis copulata. Sed donemus autori isti iniuriam. Non querat igitur prelatus mundanam excellenciam sed iuxta regulam Christi inferioritatem ministerii et pacienciam contemptus mundani et iniurie, si contingat propter fervorem anhelandi celestia et totum reputatum mundo inconveniens erit dulce; et loco momenti mundane glorie accrescet nomen vite et operum sanctitate et quod est maius gaudium eterna inscripcio libro vite. Unde racio quare non insequimur istam sentenciam est infidelitas, qua non credimus scriptis sanctorum et iudicio sapientum. Ad tantum enim pulvis mundi et suorum sensibilium nos cecat et diutina delectacio innaturalis in illis sopita palatum animi discrasiavit, quod ad magnum Ve nobis iuxta vaticinium Ysaie V, 20 dicimus bonum malum; et sic de illorum contrariis perversum est nostrum iudicium maniace in contrarium iudicium racionis, et tam multi ac magni inciderunt in istam rabiem, quod maior pars mundi arguet docentes et servantes istam sentenciam ut insanos, sic quod generalior, accusacior et perseverancior est persecucio in paucos docentes licet remisse [p. 88] istam sentenciam quam olim fuerat in prophetas. Cuius causa est quia superiores ecclesie qui videntur esse aliquid perseveranter dogmatizant oppositum, cum summe odiunt recedere a mundi deliciis, quibus ex declinacione a Christi vestigiis tam diu subdole sunt sopiti. Quomodo igitur non duceretur currus in precipicium cuius veredus et auriga ducit tam fervide ad deorsum? Secundo obicitur per hoc quod multi sancti dotacionem huiusmodi approbarunt; cum igitur in secularibus sit licita, foret in clericis magis sancta; quamvis igitur quibusdam expediat vivere exproprietarie, tamen decorat totum corpus cleri quod docetur ut regina celi sit circumdata varietate. Ad illud sepe dictum est quod tales argucie non procedunt, ideo cum sanctus sanctorum docet verbo et opere quod clerus suus debet omnem secularem modum habendi relinquere, patet quod sibi debemus in isto plene credere. Nam iuxta dicta non debemus credere vel imitari alium sanctum ecclesie, nisi de quanto in ipso sua sentencia est fundata. Specialiter autem debemus credere et exemplum capere ab illis sanctis, qui primicias spiritus habuerunt, modo autem ordine nimis prepostero fundamus nos in sanctis infimis et postremis, qui magis declinarunt ad seculum, supponentes infideliter si ipsi sic fecerant igitur bene, nam apostoli multa culpabiliter fecerunt de quibus posterius sunt purgati; et multo evidencius posteriores confessores commiserunt multa crimina, de quibus finaliter penitebant. Magna itaque cecitas in nostris arguere: ipsi sic fecerant, igitur bene. Quis igitur color, si isti per magnam partem vite sue appropriaverint [p. 89] dotacionem ecclesie, tunc non fuit illicita. Secunda stulticia est; qua plus credimus portentis quomodocumque factis, ut imponimus virtuti corporum mortuorum, quam signis vivacibus sanctorum evangelizancium, peccatores plurimos convertendo, in tantum quod si portentum tale sancto nostro ascribimus, cece arguimus quod quicquid fecerat non pecavit. Sed tercia stulticia est qua arguimus, si illi meritorie susceperunt elemosinas donatas ecclesie sub condicione, quam oportet intelligi, quod tunc nec licet ipsas auferre nostris, nec phas est negare, quin licet nobis ipsas expendere quomodolibet consumendo. Notaremus igitur formam, condicionem et usum sanctorum et equaremus ista in nobis, antequam concluderemus per locum a simili. Nec est color, si cerimonia sit in laycis meritoria, quod foret in clericis magis sancta. Sicut patet de homicidiis, de connubiis et multiplici meritorio ritu in laycis, qui foret in clericis venenosus.

But against this it is argued, first concerning the [pope]{g:papa}: how he would not suffice for the dignity of his office unless he had kingdoms and abundant [lordships]{g:dominium} [assigned]{g:limitare} to himself and his officers. And the judgement is the same of our [bishops]{g:episcopus} and abbots. But to this it has often been said that the superfluity of officers and the costliness of trappings is the cause. Hence, in step with their increase, religiousness and edifying diligence decrease. So Bernard, *On Consideration*, book IV, to Eugenius: "Amid these things you, the shepherd, go forth gilded, surrounded with so much variety. What do the sheep get? If I dared say it, these are pastures of demons rather than of sheep. Not so did Peter act, nor Paul play." And there follows: "Either deny that you are shepherd to the people, or show it; you will not deny it, lest he whose seat you hold deny that you are his heir. This is Peter, who is not known to have gone forth [87] adorned either with gems or with silks, not covered with gold, nor carried on a white horse, nor attended by soldiery, nor by ministers clamouring round him; yet without these he believed that the saving commandment could well enough be fulfilled: 'If thou lovest me, feed my sheep.' In these you have succeeded not Peter but Constantine."[^1] It would therefore be an inconsequence [to say] what follows: that in prelates the glory of the world is buried, an edifying life after the pattern of Christ and the apostles hidden in him, and everywhere a harmonious and gainful [manner of life]{g:conversacio} joined together in clergy and laity.[^2] But let us forgive this author the wrong [he does].[^3] Let the [prelate]{g:prelatus}, then, not seek worldly excellence, but, according to the rule of Christ, lowliness of ministry and patience under the world's contempt and injury, if it should come; through the fervour of longing for heavenly things, all that the world reckons [troubles]{g:inconveniens} will be sweet; and in place of the moment of worldly glory there will grow up a name for holiness of life and works, and, what is a greater joy, an eternal inscription in the book of life.[^4] Hence the reason why we do not follow this [teaching]{g:sentencia} is [unbelief]{g:infidelitas}, by which we do not believe the writings of the saints and the judgement of the wise. For the dust of the world and of its [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} things blinds us so far, and long unnatural delight, lulled asleep in them, has so distempered the palate of the mind, that, to our great Woe, according to the prophecy of Isaiah 5:20, we call "good evil"; and so concerning their contraries our judgement is maniacally perverted into the contrary of reason's judgement; and so many and such great men have fallen into this madness that the greater part of the world will accuse those who teach and keep this teaching as madmen, so that the persecution against the few who teach, though slackly, [88] this teaching is more general, more accusing and more persevering than it once was against the prophets.[^5] The cause of this is that the superiors of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, who "seemed to be some thing", persistently dogmatize the opposite, since they supremely hate to withdraw from the world's delights, in which, by turning aside from Christ's footsteps, they have so long been craftily lulled asleep.[^6] How then would the chariot not be driven over the precipice whose horse and charioteer drive so hotly downward?

Second, [it is objected, by appeal to]{g:obicitur-per} this, that many saints approved [endowment]{g:dotacio} of this kind; since therefore it is lawful in seculars, it would be holier in clerics; although therefore it is expedient for some to live [without property]{g:exproprietarie}, yet it adorns the whole body of the [clergy]{g:clerus}, as is taught, that the queen of heaven should be "surrounded with variety".[^7] To this it has often been said that such [quibbles]{g:argucie} do not hold; and so, since the holy of holies teaches by word and deed that his clergy ought to leave every secular manner of having, it is clear that we ought to believe him fully in this. For according to what has been said, we ought not to believe or imitate any other saint of the church except in so far as his teaching is founded in him. And especially ought we to believe and take example from those saints who had "the firstfruits of the Spirit"; but as it is, in an order too preposterous, we found ourselves on the lowest and last saints, who turned aside more to the world, faithlessly supposing: if they did so, then [it was] well. For the apostles did many things culpably, of which they were afterwards purged; and much more evidently later confessors committed many crimes, of which they repented at the end.[^8] So it is great blindness in our [people] to argue: they did so, therefore [it was] well. What [colour]{g:color}, then, [is there for saying that], if these men [appropriated]{g:appropriare} [89] the church's endowment for a great part of their life, then it was not unlawful?[^9] The second folly is that by which we believe portents, however they were done, as we ascribe them to the [power]{g:virtus} of dead bodies, more than the living signs of saints preaching the gospel by converting very many sinners, so far that, if we ascribe such a portent to our saint, we blindly argue that whatever he had done, he did not sin.[^10] But the third folly is that by which we argue, if they meritoriously received [alms]{g:elemosina} given to the church under a condition (which must be understood), that then it is neither lawful to take them away from our [people], nor right to deny that it is lawful for us to spend them, consuming them in any way whatever.[^11] We should therefore note the form, condition and use of the saints and match these in ourselves, before we [infer]{g:concludere} by the topic from likeness. Nor is there colour, if a ceremony is meritorious in the laity, [for saying] that it would be holier in clerics; as is clear of killings, of marriages and of many a meritorious [rite]{g:ritus} in the laity which would be poisonous in clerics.[^12]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 87.11: Let prelates devote themselves to Christ's service and welcome the contempt of the world.
- 87.20: We are so blinded that we persecute those who teach Christ's doctrine.
- 88.9: It is urged that many saints have approved of endowment.
- 88.22: All saints are not to be followed in all things; even the apostles committed faults.
- 89.1: Another folly is the weight laid on portents as a proof of saintliness.
- 89.8: A third folly is to argue that if alms were rightly received they must not be withdrawn, even though the conditions of the gift are disregarded.

## Notes

[^1]: **S/T/R.** Bernard, *De consideratione* IV.3.6 (PL 182, 776A: *Si amas me, pasce oves meas. In his successisti, non Petro, sed Constantino*; Loserth here "cap. II", but "Cap. III" at 92.30; Migne, Opp. I, p. 776) (LEDGER P05c-1). *Si amas me, pasce oves meas* is Bernard's compression of John 21:15–17 (Loserth: John 21:15; DR 21:17 "lovest thou me? … Feed my sheep"). One manuscript has *implere* for *impleri* (87.4). A in the margin (86.17): *Papa ornatus seculariter*, "The pope adorned in secular fashion". Loserth: cf. Hus, Opp. 212b (LEDGER P05c-2, editor).

[^2]: **C.** A doubtful construal. *Inconsequens igitur foret quod sequitur quod …*: taken as Wyclif's comment that, prelates having succeeded Constantine, it would not follow that worldly glory is buried in them; *quod sequitur* may instead mean "what follows [in Bernard]". *in ipso abscondita*: cf. Colossians 3:3, "your life is hid with Christ in God".

[^3]: **C.** *donemus autori isti iniuriam*: construed "let us forgive this author the wrong", i.e. let Bernard's harshness pass; alternatively "let us grant that this author does [us] wrong". Either way the next sentence draws its rule from him.

[^4]: **C/G.** *inconveniens*: here what the world reckons "troubles" (GLOSSARY, the sense in affairs). *si contingat* refers to the contempt and injury. *inscripcio libro vite*: cf. Philippians 4:3.

[^5]: **S.** Isaiah 5:20, DR "Woe to you that call evil good, and good evil"; Wyclif quotes *bonum malum* (Loserth's italics) inside his own sentence. *sentenciam* here and in *docentes … istam sentenciam* is the teaching that the clergy should live without civil lordship.

[^6]: **S.** *qui videntur esse aliquid*: Galatians 2:6, DR "them who seemed to be some thing"; not italicised by Loserth, and Wyclif has the present *videntur*.

[^7]: **S/R.** Psalm 44:10 (Vulgate numbering), DR "surrounded with variety"; Bernard's *circumdatus varietate* (86.28) answers it. A in the margin (88.9): *Secunda obieccio pro dotacione*; B lacks *-modi* in *huiusmodi* (88.10).

[^8]: **S/C.** *sanctus sanctorum*: Christ. *primicias spiritus*: Romans 8:23, DR "the firstfruits of the Spirit". *in ipso*: in Christ, the foundation of any saint's teaching.

[^9]: **T/G.** *appropriaverint* (88.34): so the print; Loserth queries *approbaverint*, "approved", which matches the objection (88.9); translated as printed. *color*: a plausible ground (GLOSSARY), as again at 89.17.

[^10]: **T.** *virtuti* (89.3): so the print; the manuscripts read *virtute*, without change of sense. *virtus*: power, the faculty (GLOSSARY).

[^11]: **C.** *sub condicione, quam oportet intelligi*: the condition on which alms are given (that they serve the poor and the clerical office), set out at 89.21–27. *nostris*: "from our [people]", the clergy.

[^12]: **C.** *locus a simili*: the dialectical topic from likeness. *homicidiis*: lawful killings by laymen (in war or in judgement), which would be poisonous in clerics.
