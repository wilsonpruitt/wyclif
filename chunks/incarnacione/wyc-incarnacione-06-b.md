# *De benedicta incarnacione*, chapter 6, part 2: that Christ is man univocally: the gloss "insofar as man" fails

## ¶1 · 89.11–90.5

> [p. 89] 2°. per hoc quod Christus non foret mobilis, sensibilis, passibilis, subditus patri vel parentibus secundum quod homo: que tamen glosa est maximum refugium negantibus de virtute sermonis scripturam sacram de Christo enunciantem⁴ talia. Et consequens⁴ sic probatur. Christus non⁵ secundum quod homo est univoce aliquid, nec aliqualiter accidentatus cum aliqua creatura: ergo non secundum quod homo est huiusmodi, ut predicitur. Patet consequencia⁶ ex hoc quod non est color concedendi, quod Christus secundum quod homo est sic formatus, nisi vel quia homo est sic denominatus, vel quia illa humanitas est sic formata. Primum non valet, cum non⁷ sit possibile Christum sic informari ut dicunt. Nec⁸ secundum prodest,⁸ cum illam humanitatem ponunt esse⁹ non suppositum, cui soli debetur accio. Et sic nec illam ponunt concipi, nasci, conversari, pati, ascendere, vel quascumque tales predicaciones personales suscipere. Cum ergo non sit predicacio synecdochica, nisi¹⁰ pars primo et principaliter recipit predicacionem,¹¹ que exinde secundario toti tribuitur; sequitur quod hec non est construccio synecdochica—Christus¹⁰ secundum humanitatem fatigatur, sicut tristatur aut moritur —cum repugnat illam humanitatem sic denominari; ut [p. 90] homo non est albus secundum faciem nisi facies sit alba. Et illa est acuta obex contra negantes scripturas cum glosa seipsam inficit¹ plus quam textus. Non ergo Christus secundum quod homo est alicuiusmodi,² nisi racio hominis univoce sibi conveniat; quia aliter, quid sibi et homini?³

Second, by this: that Christ would not be [movable]{g:mobilis}, [perceptible]{g:sensibilis}, [passible]{g:passibilis}, subject to his father or parents [insofar as]{g:secundum-quod} man. Yet this [gloss]{g:glosa} is the greatest refuge for those who deny that holy scripture, [in the proper sense of the words]{g:de-virtute-sermonis}, states such things of Christ. And the [consequent]{g:consequens} is proved thus.[^1] Christ is not, insofar as man, anything [univocally]{g:univoce}, nor in any way [subject to accidents]{g:accidentatus} along with any [creature]{g:creatura}: therefore he is not, insofar as man, of this kind, as is said before. The [inference]{g:consequencia} is clear from this, that there is no [colour]{g:color}[^2] for granting "Christ insofar as man is so formed", except either because the man is so [named]{g:denominare}, or because that [humanity]{g:humanitas} is so formed. The first does not hold, since it is not possible, as they say, for Christ to be so informed. Nor does the second avail, since they posit that humanity to be not a [supposit]{g:suppositum}, to which alone action is due. And so they do not posit that it is conceived, is born, lives its life among men, suffers, ascends, or takes up any such personal [predications]{g:predicacio}. Since, then, there is no [synecdochic predication]{g:predicacio-synecdochica} unless the part first and principally receives the predication, which is thereupon attributed secondarily to the whole, [it follows]{g:sequitur} that this is not a [synecdochic construction]{g:construccio-synecdochica}: "Christ [as to the humanity]{g:secundum-humanitatem} is wearied", as also "is saddened" or "dies", since it [is incompatible]{g:repugnare} for that humanity to be so named;[^3] as [90] a man is not white as to his face unless the face is white. And that is a sharp bar against those who deny the scriptures, since the gloss taints itself more than the text does. Christ, therefore, is not of any kind insofar as man, unless the account of man belongs to him univocally; for otherwise, what has he to do with man?

## ¶2 · 90.6–90.32

> [p. 90] 3°. confirmatur sic. Ad hoc quod satisfaciat⁴ pro peccato nature vel generis,⁵ oportet quod natura, que peccaverat, emerendo⁶ restituat:⁶ sed genus humanum peccavit in Adam et suis posteris: ergo oportet idem genus secundum aliquod eius suppositum ut satisfaciat⁷ pro peccato; sed non potuit secundum pure⁸ hominem: ergo relinquitur quod suppositum impeccabile satisfaciat assumendo eandem naturam in aliqua essencia singulari: quod et factum est per Verbum,⁹ ut superius dixerat Augustinus. Et illam¹⁰ racionem tangit Anselmus 2°· Cur Deus Homo ca°. 8. que, licet non sapiat negantibus universalia, saperet tamen cuicunque pio theologo.¹¹ Aliter enim non conveniencius assumendo humanitatem redemisset Deus peccatum hominis, quam peccatum angeli; cum plus convenit Christus post incarnacionem¹² secundum humanitatem angelo quam homini.¹² Nam ‘dii’ solum participative non plus in natura cum Deo conveniunt quam ante participacionem: sed tam equivoce dicitur ‘homo’ de Deo et de angelis¹³ quam equivoce ‘deus’ de creatura et natura divina dicitur: ergo tam equivoce disconveniunt Verbum et homo post incarnacionem sicut ante: sed ante plus conveniebat angelo quam homini: ergo¹⁴ modo. Quid ergo pertinuit¹⁵ Christi¹⁵ meritum humano generi a quo tante distitit? Non ergo satisfecit illud genus hominum quod peccavit; et per consequens manet in pleno debito sicut ante; quod est contra Apostolum ad Colos. 2. dicentem quod ‘in Christo Deus delevit cirographum decreti, quod erat nobis contrarium.’

Third, this is confirmed thus. In order that [one] make satisfaction for the sin of a [nature]{g:natura} or of a kind, the nature that had sinned must restore by making amends:[^4] now the human kind sinned in Adam and his posterity: therefore the same kind must, as to some supposit of it, make satisfaction for the sin; now it could not as to a mere man: therefore it remains that a supposit unable to sin make satisfaction by [assuming]{g:assumere} the same nature in some singular essence; which was also done by the [Word]{g:verbum}, as Augustine had said above. And Anselm touches on that argument, *Cur Deus homo* II, chapter 8;[^5] which, although it has no savour for those who deny [universals]{g:universale}, would yet have savour for any pious theologian. For otherwise God would not more fittingly have redeemed the sin of man by assuming the humanity than the sin of an angel; since Christ after the incarnation agrees, as to the humanity, more with an angel than with a man.[^6] For those who are "gods" only [participatively]{g:participative} agree with God in nature no more than before the participation: now "man" is said as [equivocally]{g:equivoce} of God and of the others as "god" is said equivocally of a creature and of the divine nature:[^7] therefore the Word and man disagree as equivocally after the incarnation as before: now before, he agreed more with an angel than with a man: therefore [so he does] now. How, then, did Christ's merit pertain to the human kind, from which he stood so far apart?[^8] Therefore that kind of men which sinned did not make satisfaction; and consequently it remains in full debt as before; which is against the Apostle, Colossians 2, saying that "in Christ God blotted out the handwriting of the decree, which was contrary to us."[^9]

## ¶3 · 91.1–91.11

> [p. 91] Confirmatur. Vel est conveniencia maxima, qua Christiani cum Christo conveniunt tantum ex parte signorum, vel tantum ex parte naturarum¹ conveniencium, vel mixtim. Primum dictum est nimium² puerile, specialiter cum hoc signum ‘homo’ summe equivoce significat Christum et alios. Si ex parte signatorum, vel oportet dare eis naturam communem, vel aliter dicere quod in nullo conveniunt, nisi in quo differunt; et³ sic, ut plus conveniunt, plus differunt³; et cum tanta sit conveniencia quanta differencia et e contra; si⁴ igitur in infinitum differunt, in infinitum conveniunt, et e contra.⁴

[91] This is confirmed. Either the greatest agreement, by which Christians agree with Christ, is only on the side of the signs, or only on the side of the natures that agree, or mixed.[^10] The first saying is too childish, especially since this sign "man" signifies Christ and the others most equivocally. If on the side of the things signified, either one must posit a common nature for them, or else say that they agree in nothing except in that in which they differ; and so, the more they agree, the more they differ; and since there is as much agreement as [difference]{g:differencia}, and conversely: if therefore they differ infinitely, they agree infinitely, and conversely.[^11]

## ¶4 · 91.12–92.16

> [p. 91] Item, si Christus non sit univoce homo cum aliis, tunc nullum accidens consequens eius humanitatem foret univoce accidens cum accidentibus correspondentibus in suis fratribus. Consequens interimeret totam fidem, spem, et caritatem de Christi incarnacione, et falsificaret scripturas eque nequiter velut Eutichiani et alii heretici supra dicti. Et consequencia patet ex hoc, quod omne accidens individuatur, specificatur, et habet totam essenciam suam a subiecto. Ergo, si subiecta in nullo univoco conveniunt, a maiori vel pari nec accidencia que secuntur; ut, si angelus non sit eiusdem speciei secundum copulatum corpus cum nostro corpore, tunc nec⁵ accidencia, que appropriate⁶ consecuntur nostra corpora, conveniunt eis nisi equivoce; ut, angelus non dormit, comedit, sentit, gignit, et sic de accidentibus competentibus toti homini: aliter enim essent procreati ab incubis et succubis filii diaboli naturales, et non filii hominum nec proximorum parentum; quod nemo dicit. Si ergo locucio animati⁷ psittaci⁸ vel⁸ inanimati organi non est univoce⁹ locucio cum humana, saltem secundum speciem specialissimam¹⁰ propter diversitatem specificam¹¹ subiectorum; multo magis nullum accidens Christum consequens foret eiusdem [p. 92] racionis cum accidente create substancie; cum subiecta nec specie, nec genere, nec in aliquo univoco convenirent. Non enim plus convenit Christus nobiscum quam deitas, ut sic opinantes concorditer confitentur; et per consequens, cum accidencia non sint nisi modi create substancie, sequitur quod nulla accidencia de genere¹ Iesu nostro² conveniant; ut non pocius conceptus est, vel natus humanitus, ambulavit, predicavit, paciebatur sitim, esuriem, opprobria vel flagella, quam deitas ipsa; et sic fuit obiectum maxime illusivum, cum nec fuit quantus, nec qualis, nec aliquod³ aliorum⁴ encium, cum non sit mobilis aut sensibilis eo quod omne tale accidens univocum cum aliis⁵ requirit primum subiectum univocum quod informat. Sed Christus in nullo substanciali est nobis similis, ut dicitur; ergo nec in aliquo accidentali: non enim tantum conveniret sicut elementum, minera, bufo, bestia, vel quelibet⁶ creatura.

Again, if Christ is not man univocally with the others, then no accident consequent on his humanity would be an accident univocally with the corresponding accidents in his brethren. The consequent would destroy the whole faith, hope, and [charity]{g:caritas} concerning Christ's incarnation, and would falsify the scriptures as wickedly as the Eutychians and the other heretics spoken of above. And the inference is clear from this, that every accident is individuated, specified, and has its whole essence from its [subject]{g:subiectum}. Therefore, if the subjects agree in nothing univocal, then, by the argument from the greater or from the equal, neither do the accidents that follow them; as, if an angel is not of the same species with our body as to the body joined [to him], then neither do the accidents which follow our bodies as proper to them belong to them except equivocally; as, an angel does not sleep, eat, feel, beget, and so of the accidents that belong to the whole man: for otherwise there would be natural sons of the devil procreated by incubi and succubi, and not sons of men nor of their nearest parents; which no one says. If, then, the speech of an animate parrot or of an inanimate organ is not speech univocally with human [speech], at least as to the [most special species]{g:species-specialissima}, because of the specific diversity of the subjects,[^12] much more would no accident consequent on Christ be [of the same kind]{g:eiusdem-racionis} [92] with an accident of a created substance; since the subjects would agree neither in species, nor in genus, nor in anything univocal. For Christ agrees with us no more than the [Godhead]{g:deitas} does, as those who so opine confess with one accord; and consequently, since accidents are nothing but modes of created substance, it follows that no accidents in kind belong to our Jesus;[^13] so that he was no more conceived, or born [humanly]{g:humanitus}, walked, preached, suffered thirst, hunger, reproaches or scourges, than the Godhead itself; and so he was a most [deceptive]{g:illusivus} object, since he was neither [quantified]{g:quantus}, nor qualified, nor any of the other [beings]{g:ens}, since he is not movable or perceptible, because every such accident univocal with others requires a first univocal subject which it informs. Now Christ is like us in nothing substantial, as is said; therefore not in anything accidental either: for he would not agree [with us] as much as an element, a mineral, a toad, a beast, or any creature whatever.

## Notes

[^1]: **T.** A B C read *enunciante talia et consequencia* (89.14–15): "stating such things; and the inference [is proved thus]". The print, with O, has the participle in the accusative and *consequens*, so that what is proved is the consequent ("Christ is not movable … insofar as man"), whose inference is then shown at 89.18.

[^2]: **C.** *Color*: a plausible ground for granting the proposition, the period sense ("colour of title"), not a pretext.

[^3]: **T.** The rule of synecdochic predication and the words "it follows that this is not a synecdochic construction" (89.28–30, *nisi … synecdochica*) are lacking in A B C; the print has them from O. Without them the sentence has no main clause, and the examples "is wearied … dies" are left hanging.

[^4]: **T.** *Emendando* "by making amends" (90.8), with A B C (*emendando resipiscat*) and O (*emêdo restituat*, read as the abbreviation of *emendando*), against the print's *emerendo* "by meriting", which no manuscript reported at the foot gives; *restituat* is O's and the print's against A B C *resipiscat* "come to its senses".

[^5]: **S.** Anselm, *Cur Deus homo* II.8: satisfaction must be made by one of Adam's race (LEDGER I06b-1, to be checked).

[^6]: **T.** A B C read *post incarnacionem cum angelo quam cum homine* (90.19–20), without *secundum humanitatem*; the print has the qualification from O. The argument is the opponents' own consequence: even as to the humanity, their Christ would agree more with an angel.

[^7]: **T.** *De aliis* "of the others" (90.23), with all the manuscripts (*aliis codd. omn.*), against the print's *de angelis* "of the angels", Harris's emendation. The comparison is between Christ and the other men, as the conclusion "the Word and man" shows.

[^8]: **T.** A B C read *participavit Christus* (90.27) for *pertinuit Christi*: "how then did Christ share [his] merit with the human kind". The print follows O.

[^9]: **S.** Col. 2:14, freely: Vulgate *delens quod adversus nos erat chirographum decreti, quod erat contrarium nobis* (DR "Blotting out the handwriting of the decree that was against us, which was contrary to us"); "in Christ God" comes from the context (2:13).

[^10]: **T.** A B C read *signatorum* "of the things signified" (91.3) for *naturarum* "of the natures"; the next sentence takes up this member as *ex parte signatorum*, which agrees with A B C. The print follows O; the sense of the division is the same.

[^11]: **T.** The words "and so, the more they agree, the more they differ" (91.8) are lacking in A B C, and "if therefore they differ infinitely … and conversely" (91.10–11) in O; the print joins the two families.

[^12]: **T.** *Psittaci* "of a parrot" (91.29) is the print's spelling of O's *sithaci*; A B C have *siccati*, *secati*, *securati* (A, margin, *secati*). For *speciem specialissimam* and *specificam* (91.30–31) A B C have *sensitivam* and *factam*; the print follows O.

[^13]: **C.** *Accidencia de genere*: accidents in kind, that is, belonging to a category of accident, as *esse accidentale in genere* (95.19); O adds a further *de* (92.6).
