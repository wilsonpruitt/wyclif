# *De potestate pape*, chapter 12, part 3: Augustine on Antichrist; four degrees of belief

## ¶1 · 324.8–326.3

> [p. 324] Secundo notandum quod Augustinus II De Civitate Dei quasi per totum exponens scripturam de Antichristo, specialiter Apokalipsim Iohannis et propheciam Danielis inter cetera capitulo II exponit textum predictum apostoli de Antichristo. Textus autem Apostoli, quem Augustinus dicit nullomodo pretereundum, in nostris codicibus ita iacet: Non terreamini, quasi instet dies Domini, quoniam, inquit, nisi venerit dissensio primum, et revelatus fuerit homo peccati filius perdicionis, qui adversatur et extollitur super omne quod dicitur Deus, aut quod colitur, ita ut in templo Dei sedeat, ostendens se tamquam ipse sit Deus, supple nisi talis Antichristus prius venerit, non veniet dies iudicii; nulli dubium est, inquit Augustinus, eum de Antichristo isto dixisse, unde Apostolus videns abhominacionem desolacionis, pro dolore loquitur interrupte, sicut Christus videns peccatum Jerusalem flevit dicens, quia si cognovisses et tu; supple peccatum tuum et penam exinde tibi infligendam; tu fleres, ut ego te defleo. Unde Apostolus consequenter declarat Antichristi versucias in hec verba: Non retinetis, quod cum apud vos essem, hec dicebam vobis, et nunc quid detineat [p. 325] scitis, ut reveletur in suo tempore; nam misterium iam operatur iniquitatis, tantum ut qui tenet nunc teneat, donec de medio fiat. Et tunc revelabitur ille iniquus, quem dominus Ihesus interficiet spiritu oris sui et destruet illustracione adventus sui eum, cuius est adventus secundum operacionem sathane in omni virtute et signis et prodigiis mendacibus et in omni seduccione iniquitatis hiis, qui pereunt, eo quod caritatem veritatis non receperunt, ut salvi fierent; ideo mittet illis Deus operacionem erroris, ut credant mendacio, ut iudicentur omnes, qui non crediderunt veritati, sed consenserunt iniquitati. Istum autem textum exposuit Augustinus secundum partem ex certa sciencia et secundum aliam partem tamquam sensum probabilem; certus est enim quod Apostolus hic loquens de Antichristo vocat eum hominem peccati et filium perdicionis, qui extollitur super omne, quod dicitur Deus. Est iterum Augustinus sicut et pius theologus de hoc certus, quod cuicumque viatori iste condiciones conveniunt hic est Antichristus. Et est tercio iste sanctus certus quod Christus non veniet ad diem finalis iudicii, quem Apostolus vocat diem Domini, iudicaturus vivos et mortuos nisi prius venerit ad seducendum in anima mortuos eius adversarius Antichristus. Primo quod iste sit sensus textus Apostoli, quod Antichristus, quem vocat refugam, sedebit in templo Dei, hoc est in ecclesia, non, inquit, templum alicuius ydoli vel demonis templum Dei vocat apostolus intelligens per Antichristum non solum ipsum principem, sed multitudinem impiorum hominum qui sunt quodammodo corpus eius. Unde in greco dicitur, tamquam in templum [p. 326] Dei sedeat, tamquam ipse sit templum Dei, quod est ecclesia. Sicut dicimus Petrum sedere in amicum id est velut amicus.

Secondly, it is to be noted that Augustine, in *De civitate Dei* II, expounding scripture about Antichrist almost throughout, especially the Apocalypse of John and the prophecy of Daniel, among other things expounds in chapter 2 the aforesaid text of the Apostle about Antichrist.[^1] And the Apostle's text, which Augustine says is by no means to be passed over, runs thus in our books: "Be not terrified, as if the day of the Lord were at hand," for, he says, "unless there come a dissension first, and the man of sin be revealed, the son of perdition, who opposeth and is lifted up above all that is called God or that is worshipped, so that he sitteth in the temple of God, shewing himself as if he were God";[^2] supply: unless such an Antichrist come first, the day of judgment will not come. "No one doubts," says Augustine, "that he said this of Antichrist"; hence the Apostle, seeing the abomination of desolation, speaks brokenly for grief, as Christ, seeing the sin of Jerusalem, wept, saying: "If thou also hadst known" (supply: your sin and the punishment to be inflicted on you for it), you would weep, as I weep over you.[^3] Hence the Apostle next declares Antichrist's cunning in these words: "Remember you not that, when I was with you, I told you these things? And now [325] you know what withholdeth, that he may be revealed in his time. For the mystery of iniquity already worketh: only that he who now holdeth do hold, until he be taken out of the way. And then that wicked one shall be revealed: whom the Lord Jesus shall kill with the spirit of his mouth and shall destroy with the brightness of his coming: him whose coming is according to the working of Satan, in all power and signs and lying wonders: and in all seduction of iniquity to them that perish: because they receive not the [charity]{g:caritas} of the truth, that they might be saved. Therefore God shall send them the operation of error, to believe lying: that all may be judged who have not believed the truth but have consented to iniquity."[^4] And Augustine expounded this text in part from certain knowledge and in another part as a probable sense; for he is certain that the Apostle, speaking here of Antichrist, calls him "the man of sin" and "the son of perdition", "who is lifted up above all that is called God". Again, Augustine, like any pious theologian, is certain of this: that whatever [wayfarer]{g:viator} these conditions fit, he is Antichrist. And, thirdly, this saint is certain that Christ will not come to the day of final judgment, which the Apostle calls the day of the Lord, to judge the living and the dead, unless his adversary Antichrist has first come to lead astray those dead in soul. First, that this is the sense of the Apostle's text: that Antichrist, whom he calls "the deserter", will sit in the temple of God, that is, in the [church]{g:ecclesia}; "for the apostle does not," he says, "call the temple of some idol or demon the temple of God", understanding by Antichrist not only the prince himself but the multitude of impious men who are in a way his body.[^5] Hence in the Greek it is said, "as if he sits into the temple [326] of God", as if he himself were the temple of God, which is the church; just as we say that Peter "sits into a friend", that is, as a friend.[^6]

## ¶2 · 326.4–326.27

> [p. 326] Secundo vero exponit sequencia: Sed nunc quid detineat scitis, ne adhuc veniat ille magnus Antichristus, quia oportet ecclesiam parum profundari in fide, nec ad coronam eius Antichristus reveletur in suo tempore. Iam enim misterium iniquitatis operatur Antichristus tacite in suis membris, ut dicit evangelista, ut fidelis christianus habens iudicia sui exercitus, qui tenet fidem ecclesie, nunc teneat in principio illam fidem, donec fiat revelacio de medio sue malicie. Victoria enim in belli inicio confundit adversarios et animat expugnantes; in medio enim illius concertacionis veniet unus capitalis malignus, licet post et antea erunt mali eciam usque ad diem iudicii, ut patet Daniel IX. Unde quia sensus Apostoli est multum absconditus, non confunditur iste sanctus dicere: Ego prorsus quid dixerit me fateor ignorare. Et recitando tres opiniones dicit prima, quod Neronem vocavit Apostolus tacite Antichristum, secunda quam Augustinus approbat, dicit hunc esse sensum Apostoli, qui tenet imperium, teneat donec tollatur de medio, quia post dotacionem ecclesie revelabitur Antichristus. Et tercia sentencia est supradicta.

And secondly he expounds what follows: "But now you know what withholdeth", that is, that that great Antichrist may not yet come, because the church must be somewhat deepened in faith, nor Antichrist be revealed, to its crown, in his time.[^7] "For already" Antichrist "worketh the mystery of iniquity" silently in his [members]{g:membrum}, as the evangelist says, so that the faithful Christian, having the judgments of his army, he who holds the faith of the church, should now hold that faith in the beginning, until the revelation of his malice comes out of the midst.[^8] For victory at the beginning of a war confounds the adversaries and gives heart to those who attack; for in the midst of that contest one chief evil one will come, although after and before there will be evil men, even up to the day of judgment, as is clear in Daniel 9. Hence, because the Apostle's sense is much hidden, this saint is not ashamed to say: "I confess that I am [wholly ignorant]{g:ignorare} of what he said." And, reciting three [opinions]{g:opinio}, he says: the first, that the Apostle tacitly called Nero Antichrist; the second, which Augustine approves, says that this is the Apostle's sense: "he who holds the empire, let him hold until he be taken out of the midst", because after the [endowment]{g:dotacio} of the church Antichrist will be revealed.[^9] And the third [teaching]{g:sentencia} is the one stated above.

## ¶3 · 326.28–327.15

> [p. 326] Tercio vero prosequitur, quomodo Antichristus seducet virtute dyaboli tam in causa temporali, quam spirituali, in qua pretendet se habere virtutem in signis et prodigiis mendacibus, per que quidam intelligunt bullas atque epistolas de plenitudine potestatis papalis, plenas mendacio, ut verificant de indulgenciis et prefeccione inhabilium, quos menciuntur per eos esse habiles, [p. 327] cum divinum iudicium sit in multis contrarium. In temporalibus vero fingunt se super omnes seculares dominos dominari civiliter, et sic notato dicto Augustini, quod oportet Antichristum esse de Romano imperio, quod extinguet, ut dicit XXIV cap. et dicto beati Johannis quod Antichristi prenuncii magni Antichristi prodierunt ex apostolis et per consequens de clero, cum factis paparum Christo contrariis eliciunt quidam probabiliter quod ipsi perversi sunt pessimi Antichristi; Deus autem secundum Apostolum mittet tales contra suos apostolos vel seductos in operacionem erroris, ut credant mendacio in penam peccati. Quod ego credo ydolatriam in colendo signa ut Deum, sicut specialiter patet in ostia consecrata.

And thirdly he goes on [to show] how Antichrist will lead astray by the devil's [power]{g:virtus}, in a temporal cause as well as in a spiritual one, in which he will claim to have power "in signs and lying wonders"; by these some understand bulls and letters on the [plenitude of]{g:plenitudo-potestatis} papal [power]{g:potestas}, full of lying, as they show true of [indulgences]{g:indulgencie} and of the promotion of the [unqualified]{g:inhabilis}, whom they falsely say are [qualified]{g:habilis} through them, [327] although God's judgment is in many cases the contrary.[^10] And in temporal things they feign that they [exercise lordship]{g:dominari} civilly over all [secular lords]{g:seculares-domini}; and so, noting Augustine's saying that Antichrist must be of the Roman empire, which he will extinguish, as he says in chapter 24,[^11] and blessed John's saying that the antichrists, forerunners of the great Antichrist, went out from the apostles, and consequently from the clergy, some infer probably from the deeds of the popes contrary to Christ that these perverse men are the worst antichrists.[^12] And God, according to the Apostle, will send such men against his apostles, or [will send] the seduced into the operation of error, "to believe lying", as the punishment of sin.[^13] This I believe to be idolatry, in worshipping signs as God, as is especially clear in the consecrated host.

## ¶4 · 327.16–327.25

> [p. 327] Recolligendo igitur dicta superius, videtur michi quod aliqua sunt ex fide simpliciter concedenda, alia vero ut omnia scripture sacre contraria simpliciter sunt neganda. In tercio vero gradu sunt quedam probabiliter asserenda ut illa que probabiliter creduntur esse in scriptura sacra implicita. In quarto vero gradu sunt aliqua credenda probabiliter esse falsa ut illa de quibus habemus probabiliter evidenciam quod sunt scripture sacre contraria.

Gathering up, then, what was said above, it seems to me that some things are to be granted [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} [as faith teaches]{g:ex-fide}, and others, such as everything contrary to holy scripture, are to be denied without qualification. And in the third degree are some things to be asserted probably, such as those which are probably believed to be implicit in holy scripture. And in the fourth degree are some things to be believed probably to be false, such as those of which we have probable [evidence]{g:evidencia} that they are contrary to holy scripture.[^14]

## ¶5 · 327.26–328.6

> [p. 327] In primis vero et secundo contrariis iacet fides expellens formidinem. In mediis vero cadit formido cum prestanciori via ad credendum, ut in gradu tercio aut cum prestanciori evidencia ad discredendum ut in gradu ultimo. Et ista quatuor apud theologum sunt notanda; tunc dico me credere in primo gradu quod Antichristus est in predictis scripturis descriptus. [p. 328] Secundo quod licet omnes presciti sint unus magnus Antichristus, oportet tamen esse unum principalem et maximum Antichristum. Tercio vero sive papa sive cesar sive quicumque alius fuerit maximus adversarius legis Christi, ipse est potissimus Antichristus.

And in the first and the second, which are contraries, lies faith that expels [misgiving]{g:formido}. But in the middle ones misgiving falls in, with the stronger way toward believing, as in the third degree, or with the stronger evidence toward disbelieving, as in the last degree. And these four are to be noted by the theologian; then I say that I believe in the first degree that Antichrist is described in the scriptures cited. [328] Secondly, that although all [the foreknown]{g:prescitus} are one great Antichrist, yet there must be one principal and greatest Antichrist. And thirdly, that whether pope or Caesar or anyone else be the greatest adversary of the law of Christ, he is the chief Antichrist.

## ¶6 · 328.7–328.13

> [p. 328] Quarto credo quod dictus Antichristus cum omnibus suis complicibus damnabitur ex mendacio in die iudicii fugatis peccatorum tenebris et illucescente lumine veritatis. Ulterius dico tamquam michi probabile in gradu tercio quod papa sit ille Antichristus qui describitur in scriptura.

Fourthly, I believe that the said Antichrist with all his accomplices will be damned for lying on the day of judgment, when the darkness of sins has been put to flight and the light of truth dawns. Further, I say, as probable to me in the third degree, that the [pope]{g:papa} is that Antichrist who is described in scripture.[^15]

## ¶7 · 328.14–328.20

> [p. 328] In istis autem mediis paratus sum cuicumque parti ad quam concepero quod evidencia est pocior consentire. Et sic dividitur diccio mea in fidem et opinionem. In fidem, concedendo vel negando, in opinionem, remisse credendo vel discredendo illa, quorum pura credulitas est mihi preternecessaria ad salutem.

And in these middle [degrees] I am ready to consent to whichever side I perceive the evidence to be stronger for. And so my speech is divided into faith and opinion: into faith, by granting or denying; into opinion, by believing or disbelieving with slackened [assent] those things the bare [credence]{g:credulitas} of which is [beyond]{g:preternecessarius} what is necessary to me for salvation.[^16]

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** Augustine, *De civitate Dei*: Loserth notes, rightly, that the book is not II but XX (*Opp.* VII, 687; PL 41, 685–87). The chapter Wyclif gives as "2" is printed so; Augustine's discussion of 2 Thess. 2 is XX.19. **T.** The manuscripts lack *Dei* at 324.9.
[^2]: **S.** 2 Thess. 2:2–4. Wyclif's text runs together the end of v. 2 and v. 3, and reads *dissensio* ("dissension") where the Vulgate has *discessio* (DR "a revolt"); Augustine's own text has *refuga* (cf. 325.27, "the deserter"), hence "in our books". **R.** A notes in the margin at 324.15: *Antichristus quomodo ostenditur*, "how Antichrist is shown".
[^3]: **S.** Luke 19:42 (with 19:41, "he wept over it"). Augustine's words are given in the print as a quotation; *nulli dubium est … dixisse* is *De civitate Dei* XX.19.2 ("There is no doubt that he said these things of Antichrist"; Wyclif *isto* for *ista*).
[^4]: **S.** 2 Thess. 2:5–11 (DR numbers the last sentence as v. 10). Wyclif's text lacks *adhuc* ("yet") in v. 5. *Caritatem* is kept as "charity" (GLOSSARY), where DR has "the love of the truth".
[^5]: **T.** The manuscripts place *primo quod* after *apostoli* (325.27); Loserth moves it, and the print is translated. **S.** Augustine on the temple of God as the church, and Antichrist as the prince with his body: XX.19.2 (PL 41, 685–86), where Augustine reports this as the view of "some" and leaves open which temple is meant; Loserth's "p. 597" at 325.29 is presumably a misprint for 687.
[^6]: **C.** *In templum … sedeat*: Augustine's rendering of the Greek *eis ton naon*, "into the temple", read as "for the temple", that is, as the temple; so *sedere in amicum*, "to sit as a friend". The English keeps the literal "into" to show the construction.
[^7]: **C.** The sentence does not construe cleanly. *Nec ad coronam eius Antichristus reveletur*: we take it as a second purpose with *ne*, "nor [that] Antichrist be revealed, to its [the church's] crown, [before] his time". An *et* for *nec* would give "and that Antichrist may be revealed in his time, to its crown". **S.** 2 Thess. 2:6, where the Vulgate has *et nunc*, not *sed nunc*.
[^8]: **C.** We take *Antichristus* as the subject of *operatur* and *misterium* as its object (2 Thess. 2:7). *Ut dicit evangelista*: John, presumably 1 John 2:18. *Habens iudicia sui exercitus* is obscure and is translated as printed; *sue malicie* refers to Antichrist.
[^9]: **S.** Augustine (XX.19.3, PL 41, 686) reports the Nero opinion, which he finds a great presumption, and says that *qui tenet* is "not absurdly" taken of the Roman empire; Wyclif's "approves" overstates this. The clause on the endowment of the church is Wyclif's application, not Augustine's words. **T.** B reads *apostolicum* at 326.24; the word it replaces is not legible in the apparatus.
[^10]: **C.** *Virtute dyaboli*, "by the devil's power": *virtus* as faculty, as in *in omni virtute*, 2 Thess. 2:9. *Ut verificant de*: "as they show [the text] true of". *Quos menciuntur per eos esse habiles*: *per eos* most likely refers to the bulls and letters.
[^11]: **S.** Augustine, *De civitate Dei* XX, "ch. 24" as Wyclif gives it; no reference at the page foot. Not found in Augustine: XX.24 is on the Psalms, and XX.19 says only that *qui tenet* may be the Roman empire; cf. Jerome, *Ep.* 121.11 ("Antichrist will not come unless the Roman empire is first destroyed") and the Pseudo-Augustinian *De Antichristo* of Adso (LEDGER P12c-5).
[^12]: **S.** 1 John 2:18–19: "even now there are become many Antichrists … They went out from us." **C.** *Cum factis* is taken as "from the deeds", the ground of the inference (*eliciunt*).
[^13]: **S.** 2 Thess. 2:11 (DR 2:10), "God shall send them the operation of error, to believe lying". **C.** *Mittet tales contra suos apostolos vel seductos in operacionem erroris*: we take *seductos* as a second object ("the seduced into the operation of error"); *vel* may also join *apostolos* and *seductos* as both objects of *contra*.
[^14]: **R.** The manuscripts note in the margin *Concedenda simpliciter, neganda simpliciter* (327.16), *Asserenda probabiliter* (327.20, as we read the apparatus), and *Antichristus quis est*, "who Antichrist is" (327.32).
[^15]: **C.** *Papa* has no article in Latin: "the pope" (the office, or the present holder) or "a pope". Only the third degree is claimed: probable, not of faith.
[^16]: **C.** *Remisse*, "with slackened [assent]": the weaker assent of opinion, against the firm granting or denying of faith (¶4–5).
