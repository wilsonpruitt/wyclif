# *De potestate pape*, chapter 3, part 1: Peter's primacy: the sifting, the faith that fails not, the order of the names

## ¶1 · 43.11–45.12

> [p. 43] Habito generali sermone de potestate membrorum ecclesie cum suis subditis et eorum singulis superest videre de potestate summi sacerdotis, quem alii vocant papam et alii Romanum pontificem. Et quia tota moderna ecclesia exemplata est ab ecclesia peregrinante pro tempore Christi, ideo restat videre in isto explicari, quis apostolorum post ascensionem Christi primatum tenuit; et arguit dominus Ardmacanus VII libro De Questionibus Armenorum viginti racionibus quod Petrus inter omnes habuit preeminenciam capitalem, primo per illud Math. XX, quando facta est contencio inter discipulos Jesu, quis illorum videtur esse maior, in determinando questionem docet ut qui maior est eorum fiat ut minor; quod dictum implicat aliquem esse inter eos maiorem ceteris. Unde postquam ostenderit quod maioritas, quam ipse definitive diligit [p. 44] attenter penes maioritatem mitis et humilis ministerii vertitur verbum ad Symonem: Ecce, inquit, sathanas expetivit vos, ut cribraret sicut triticum, ego autem rogavi pro te, ut non deficiat fides tua, et tu aliquando conversus confirma fratres tuos. Ecce post generalem solucionem facta est particularis applicacio preminencie ad Petrum, cum non aliter pertinenter magister optimus decideret questionem sedando brigam et instruendo eos in regulis religionis sue, quas in posterum observaverunt; unde relinquens exemplum suis; ne superbiant de prepositura ecclesiastica, sed pocius timeant propter annexum periculum, sub quodam velamine includente periculum adiutorium atque officium insinuat Petro primatum. Per cribrum intelligunt doctores ad sensum misticum naturam lapsam corpoream. Ipsa enim est quasi caminus temptacionis, in qua spiritus humanus colliditur, sicut in cribro triticum ventilatur. Curvitas ligni signat declinacionem hominis a rectitudine originalis iusticie per peccatum. Tegule dicto ligno infixe que se intersecant ex adverso sunt versucie diaboli et fraus mundi, quibus sensualitati infixis spiritus temptacionibus periculosis concutitur, et eo timorosius quo gradus prepositure est alcior; foramina vero spatulis intercepta sunt genera virtutum, quibus temptatus cognoscit humiliter vitam suam esse ventum aut vacuum. Per istas siquidem rimas patet temptati subterfugium, ut ex gracia et gravedine humilitatis evadat collisionis hostilis periculum, si fuerit horreandus in terra vivencium, quia aliter ventus superbie exsufflat [p. 45] ipsum ab area virtutum comburendum ut paleam vel bestiis civitatis dyaboli devorandum. Expetivit igitur sathanas ut post percussionem pastoris posset eos temptando concutere vento superbie, perplexitate diffidencie et aliis collusionibus spiritualibus, non ad expurgacionem predestinatorum, licet hoc sciverit, sed ad dampnificandum prescitos relictos ut palea, quod obtinuit in Juda. Et sic licencia concessa a Deo sathane ad temptandum apostolos preter eius propositum sicut in beato Job cessit eis ad commodum, cum erant cribrati sicut triticum.

Having had a general discourse on the [power]{g:potestas} of the [members]{g:membrum} of the [church]{g:ecclesia} with their subjects, and of each of them, it remains to see about the power of the highest priest, whom some call [pope]{g:papa} and others [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex}. And since the whole modern church is patterned on the church as it was a pilgrim in the time of Christ, it therefore remains to see set out in this [place] which of the apostles held the primacy after Christ's ascension; and [the lord of Armagh]{g:ardmacanus} argues in book 7 of the *De questionibus Armenorum*, by twenty reasons, that Peter had among all a preeminence as head:[^1] first by that of Matthew 20, when a contention arose among the disciples of Jesus which of them seemed to be the greater, in settling the question he teaches "that he who is the greater among them should become as the lesser"; which saying implies that someone among them was greater than the rest.[^2] Hence, after he had shown that the [greatness]{g:majoritas} which he definitively loves [44] [is weighed] attentively by the greatness of a meek and humble ministry, the word is turned to Simon: "Behold," he says, "Satan hath desired to have you, that he may sift you as wheat. But I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not: and thou, being once converted, confirm thy brethren."[^3] Behold, after the general solution a particular application of preeminence to Peter was made, since otherwise the best master would not have decided the question fittingly, settling the quarrel and instructing them in the rules of his religion, which they observed afterwards; hence, leaving an example to his own, that they should not grow proud of an ecclesiastical [office of superior]{g:prepositura} but rather fear on account of the peril annexed to it, under a certain veil that includes peril, help and office he intimates the primacy to Peter. By the sieve the doctors understand, in the mystical sense, fallen bodily nature. For it is as it were a furnace of temptation, in which the human spirit is dashed about, as wheat is tossed in a sieve. The curve of the wood signifies man's turning aside from the rectitude of original justice through sin. The laths fixed in the said wood, which cross one another from opposite sides, are the wiles of the devil and the fraud of the world; with these fixed in sensuality the spirit is shaken by perilous temptations, and the more fearfully the higher the degree of the office of superior; and the holes caught between the slats are the kinds of virtues, by which the tempted man knows humbly that his life is wind or emptiness.[^4] Through these chinks, indeed, a way of escape lies open to the tempted, so that by grace and the weight of humility he may escape the peril of the enemy's collision, if he is to be gathered into the barn in the land of the living, since otherwise the wind of pride blows [45] him off the threshing floor of virtues, to be burned like chaff or devoured by the beasts of the devil's city.[^5] Satan therefore desired that after the striking of the shepherd he might shake them, by tempting, with the wind of pride, the [perplexity]{g:perplexitas} of distrust and other spiritual collusions, not for the purging of [the predestinate]{g:predestinatus}, though he knew this [would follow], but to harm [the foreknown]{g:prescitus} left behind like chaff, which he obtained in Judas.[^6] And so the licence granted by God to Satan to tempt the apostles turned, beyond his purpose, to their profit, as in blessed Job, since they were sifted as wheat.[^7]

## ¶2 · 45.13–47.8

> [p. 45] Sed secundo, ut cognoscat ecclesiam non a se hoc habere, sed a fide et gracia domini Jesu Christi, ut vel sic postposita superbia celebret eius laudem, statim subiungit: Ego autem rogavi pro te, ut non deficiat fides tua in quo videtur innuere primatum Petri in hoc quod nomine suo exprimit totam ecclesiam militantem; semper enim manet fides ecclesie in una persona vel in alia, sicut in sancto sabbato, quando defecit in Petro; servata fuerat in Maria. Ista igitur negacio accomoda, verificans dictum Christi, docet quod nomine suo tamquam capitanei expressit ecclesiam. Sed tercio (ut exposui in capitulo V huius) limitat prelacie officium, cum subinfert: Et tu aliquando conversus confirma fratres tuos quod officium post Joh. XXI, 17 exponit expressius, cum sibi singulariter iniungit tribus vicibus pascere oves suas; unde Joh. XX, 1 9 dicitur quod apostoli audito a Magdalena sublatum esse corpus Domini Petrus et Johannes, in quibus fides remissius est extincta, cucurrerunt, ut haberent experienciam [p. 46] rei gestae. Et quamvis Johannes, virgo et sterilis, synagogam singnificans, precucurrit citius Petro, tamen Petrus ex fervore amoris in effectu posito introivit et ab experiencia cum inspiracione interna edoctus resurreccionem credidit, et sic Johannem qui fuit post eum precipuus confirmavit. Unde sequitur in textu: Tunc inquit, introivit et ille discipulus, qui venerat primus ad monumentum, et vidit et credidit; nondum enim sciebant scripturam, quia oportuit eum a mortuis resurgere. Quamvis enim Johannes quoad devocionem amoris contemplantis fervencius Petro Christum dilexerit, tamen Petrus quantum ad amorem practicum plus ceteris apostolis diligebat. In cuius signum Johannes, tamquam speculativus ad percipiendum archana Dei magis dispositus; de Jesu stante in littore ad signandum cursum mundane viacionis sibi transactum, prius percepit quam Petrus, quia dominus est, ut dicitur Joh. ultimo, verumtamen Petrus, audito quia dominus est, misit se fervencius in mare. Et propter magnitudinem istius amoris practici et experienciam fragilitatis humane, cum ipsemet ter negavit Christum Math. XXVI, fuit racioni consonum, ut ipse preficeretur caput ecclesie, Johannes autem virgo et contemplativus a mundo abstractus habuit custodiam matris et virginis que fuit locale sponsi preciosissimum. Et sic habuit signum preeminencie amoris in hoc, quod de tam prope quietus factus est celestis coniugii paranymphus, et preter hec habuit cum ceteris apostolis generalem custodiam matris ecclesie; sed quadam prerogativa Petrus. Et sic secundum unam racionem amandi videtur catholicum quod Johannes plus Petro dilexit celeste coniugium [p. 47] et sine huiusmodi repugnancia quoad aliam racionem amandi Petrus plus hiis diligens plus dilexit Christum atque ecclesiam quam Johannes; et proporcionaliter erant a sponso ecclesie readamati; racione cuius Johannes tam crebro dicit, non quod Jesus eum maxime diligebat; ut patet Joh. XIX, XX et XXI, sed quod eum quodam amoris privilegio precipue diligebat.

But secondly, that he may know that the church has this not of itself but from the faith and grace of the Lord Jesus Christ, so that thus at least, setting pride aside, he may celebrate his praise, he immediately adds: "But I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not"; in which he seems to hint at Peter's primacy in this, that under his name he expresses the whole [church militant]{g:ecclesia-militans}; for the faith of the church always remains in one person or another, as on Holy Saturday, when it failed in Peter, it had been kept in Mary.[^8] This fitting negation, therefore, verifying Christ's saying, teaches that he expressed the church under his name as under a [captain]{g:capitaneus}'s.[^9] But thirdly (as I set out in chapter 5 of this [work]) he [assigns]{g:limitare} the office of prelacy, when he adds: "And thou, being once converted, confirm thy brethren"; which office he sets out more expressly afterwards, John 21:17, when he enjoins on him singly, three times, "to feed his sheep";[^10] hence in John 20:1–9 it is said that, when the apostles had heard from Magdalene that "the body of the Lord" had been "taken away", Peter and John, in whom faith was less fully extinguished, ran, that they might have experience [46] of what had been done.[^11] And although John, virgin and barren, signifying the synagogue, "did outrun" Peter "more swiftly", yet Peter, from the fervour of love put into effect, went in, and, taught by experience together with inward inspiration, believed the resurrection, and so confirmed John, who was the chief after him. Hence it follows in the text: "Then," he says, "that disciple also went in, who came first to the sepulchre: and he saw and believed. For as yet they knew not the scripture, that he must rise again from the dead." For although John, as to the devotion of contemplative love, loved Christ more fervently than Peter, yet Peter, as to practical love, loved more than the other apostles. In sign of which John, as one speculative and more disposed to perceive the secrets of God, perceived sooner than Peter, of "Jesus standing on the shore" (to signify that the course of worldly [wayfaring]{g:viacio} was over for him), that it is the Lord, as is said in the last chapter of John; nevertheless Peter, having heard that it is the Lord, cast himself more fervently into the sea.[^12] And on account of the greatness of this practical love and his experience of human frailty, since he himself thrice denied Christ (Matthew 26), it was consonant with reason that he should be set over [it] as [head]{g:caput} of the church; and John, virgin and contemplative, withdrawn from the world, had the keeping of the mother and virgin, who was the bridegroom's most precious dwelling. And so he had a sign of preeminence of love in this, that, from resting so near, he was made groomsman of the heavenly marriage; and besides these things he had, with the other apostles, the general keeping of mother church; but Peter [had it] by a certain prerogative.[^13] And so, according to one way of loving, it seems catholic that John loved the heavenly marriage more than Peter, [47] and, without any such conflict, as to another way of loving, Peter, loving "more than these", loved Christ and the church more than John; and in proportion they were loved in return by the bridegroom of the church; by reason of which John says so often, not that Jesus loved him most, as is clear from John 19, 20 and 21, but that he loved him especially by a certain privilege of love.[^14]

## ¶3 · 47.9–47.26

> [p. 47] Ex ista ranga potest pius theologus tamquam fundamento colligere quod Petrus habuit quendam primatum apostolice dignitatis; secundo confirmatur idem ex serie verborum sacre scripture et prerogativa pre ceteris data Petro. Nam Math. X, 2 sic dicitur: Duodecim apostolorum nomina sunt hec Primus Symon, qui dicitur Petrus, et Andreas frater eius. Cum autem nec ordo verborum nec sillaba in scriptura ponatur, quin sit omissa sentencia, quid pertinencius daretur intelligi; quod Petrus dicitur primus apostolorum, nisi quod ipse singulariter tenet primatum apostolice dignitatis; cum vocacione Andreas Petrum precesserit, ut patet Joh. I. Non igitur ex indifferencia preposuit Christus Petrum in vocacione; tercio; ut patet Math. X et Luce VI, ymo communiter connumerando apostolos Petrus preponitur ab eo, qui dixit et facta sunt.

From this ranking a pious theologian can gather, as from a foundation, that Peter had a certain primacy of apostolic dignity; secondly the same is confirmed from the order of the words of holy scripture and the prerogative given to Peter before the rest.[^15] For in Matthew 10:2 it is said thus: "The names of the twelve apostles are these: The first, Simon who is called Peter, and Andrew his brother." Now since neither an order of words nor a syllable is placed in scripture without some teaching being put forth in it, what more fitting could be given to be understood by Peter's being called the first of the apostles, than that he singly holds the primacy of apostolic dignity, since in the calling Andrew preceded Peter, as is clear from John 1?[^16] Not without distinction, therefore, did Christ put Peter first in the naming; thirdly, as is clear from Matthew 10 and Luke 6, indeed in commonly enumerating the apostles Peter is put first by him who "spoke, and they were made".[^17]

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** Richard FitzRalph, *Summa in quaestionibus Armenorum* VII; Loserth gives no chapter (LEDGER P03a-1, to be checked). *Primatus* is rendered "primacy" throughout; *preeminenciam capitalem*, "a preeminence as head" (cf. *capitaliter*). A in marg.: *Petrus si ut caput ecclesie primatum tenuit* (43.21).

[^2]: **S.** Wyclif cites Matt. 20 (the request for the sons of Zebedee, 20:20–28), but the contention and the answer are Luke 22:24, 26 (DR "which of them should seem to be the greater"; "he that is the greater among you, let him become as the younger"). His *fiat ut minor* is translated.

[^3]: **T/S.** The period does not construe as printed at the turn of pp. 43–44: *maioritas … attenter penes maioritatem … ministerii* lacks a verb; we supply "[is weighed]" (perhaps *attenditur* lies behind *attenter*). The plate confirms *attenter*. Luke 22:31–32 (DR); Wyclif omits *Simon, Simon*.

[^4]: **C/S.** The sieve is described as a framed instrument: a bent wooden rim (*curvitas ligni*), crossing strips (*tegule*, literally "tiles"; here laths) and the holes between the slats (*spatule*). Job 7:7 (DR "my life is but wind"). A in marg.: *Cribrum mistice quid signet?*; B in marg.: *Per cribrum intelligitur* (44.16).

[^5]: **T/C.** B reads *ruinas* for *rimas*, "chinks" (44.30). *Horreandus*, a non-classical gerundive from *horreum*, "barn": "to be gathered into the barn". "The land of the living": Ps. 26:13.

[^6]: **S/G.** The striking of the shepherd: Matt. 26:31 (DR "I will strike the shepherd"). *Presciti*, "the foreknown": those whom God foreknows will be damned; not "the reprobate".

[^7]: **S.** Job 1:12, the licence given to Satan.

[^8]: **C.** The subject of *cognoscat* and *celebret* is taken to be Peter (*ecclesiam … habere* is the accusative and infinitive); it may be any hearer. The faith kept in Mary alone on Holy Saturday is a medieval commonplace.

[^9]: **T.** The manuscripts read *negacio accomoda aconda* (sic); Loserth omits *aconda* (45.22–23). *Negacio*: the negative "fail not". A reads *vel alia* (45.20–21); B *deiicit* for *defecit* (45.21).

[^10]: **C/S.** *Ut exposui in capitulo V huius*: chapter 5 of this treatise comes later; the reference is translated as printed (LEDGER P03a-2, to be checked). John 21:17 (DR "Feed my sheep"); Wyclif's indirect *pascere oves suas* is quoted, as the italics have it. B omits *tu* (45.26).

[^11]: **T/S.** "John 20:1–9": the print has *Joh. XX, 1 9*. The quoted words are not the Vulgate's: John 20:2 has *Tulerunt Dominum de monumento* (DR "They have taken away the Lord out of the sepulchre").

[^12]: **S.** John 20:4 (DR "that other disciple did outrun Peter"), 20:8–9; John 21:4, 7 (DR "Jesus stood on the shore"; "It is the Lord … cast himself into the sea"); Loserth gives John 21:4 (46.17).

[^13]: **S/C.** Matt. 26:69–75 (the denials); John 19:26–27 (the mother given to John); John 13:23 (DR "leaning on Jesus' bosom"), which *de tam prope quietus* recalls. *Paranymphus*: the friend of the bridegroom, groomsman; *locale*: a place to dwell in.

[^14]: **S/C.** John 21:15 (DR "lovest thou me more than these?"); "the disciple whom Jesus loved", John 19:26, 20:2, 21:7, 21:20. *Racio amandi*: "way of loving".

[^15]: **T.** *Ranga*: Loserth compares *De veritate sacrae scripturae* p. 124 l. 4 and takes the word, with Matthew, from English "range" or "rank" (47.9).

[^16]: **T/S.** *Quin sit omissa sentencia* (47.18; the plate confirms *omissa*) gives the opposite of the needed sense ("without a teaching being omitted"); we translate *emissa*, "put forth" (our emendation). Matt. 10:2 (DR, without the Vulgate's *autem*); John 1:40–42 (Andrew called before Peter).

[^17]: **C/T/S.** *In vocacione*: here the naming in the list, against the calling (*vocacione*) in which Andrew came first. *Tercio*: the manuscript reads *tercia* (47.24). Luke 6:14; Ps. 148:5 (DR "he spoke, and they were made").
