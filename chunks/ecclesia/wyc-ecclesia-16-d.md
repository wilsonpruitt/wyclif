# *De ecclesia*, chapter 16, part 4: Leonard against Silvester; the fifth argument: perpetual alms and the colleges

## ¶1 · 368.29–370.14

> [p. 368] Si, inquam, cum talibus exhortacionibus evangelicis securius procurasset Silvester pro futura ecclesia, tunc verisimilius plus profuisset utrique parti, sicut beatus Leonardus plus profuit regi Francie retrahendo ipsum ab affeccione mundana per hoc quod renuit officium [p. 369] presulatus, quam fecisset in retinendo illud officium procurando a rege dimidium regni sui. Ideo fecit conformius ad evangelium et (sicut credo) utilius utrique parti ecclesie, quam fecit beatus Silvester recipiendo illam cartam a cesare, sicut innuit cronica de voce angeli dicentis quod in die illius dotacionis diffusum est venenum in ecclesia sancta Dei. Nec est carta cesaris magis credibilis quam ista cronica, ymmo evidencius est ex fide scripture et lege ecclesie quod Nabuchodonosor qui destruxit synagogam et sacerdotes Jerusalem sit salvatus, quam quod Constantinus salvabitur, quia primum asserit decretum cum Augustino ex testimonio scripture, sed secundum relinquitur insuasum. Sed arguit doctor quod Silvester tenebatur de necessitate salutis recipere dotacionem predictam a cesare eo quod spolians christianos bonis suis tenebatur eis restituere. Sed si evidencia ista procederet, moveret primo credere quod Constantinus propter defectum plene satisfaccionis fuit dampnabilis, secundo cum beatus Silvester debuit sub pena mortalis restitucionem illam recipere, sequitur quod totum imperium principaliter debebatur Romano pontifici, et per idem, extincto quolibet seculari capitali domino cuiuslibet regni, episcopus debet esse eius civilis dominus capitalis, ut deductum est et improbatum priori capitulo. Tercio sequitur quod Christo et cuilibet eius vicario debetur principaliter dominacio secularis, cum aliter posset cesar renunciasse toti civilitati et distribuisse bona pauperibus, sicut fecerunt sanctus Thomas apostolus et sanctus Laurencius; nam dato opposito non oportuit quod cesar resignasset Silvestro imperium, et per consequens non oportuit sub pena mortalis beatum Silvestrum illam dotacionem a cesare recepisse. [p. 370] Unde ego credo quod meritorius fuisset utrique parti quod distributa fuissent bona pauperibus, et ordinatum per laycos ministerium continuum in subsidium egenorum; tunc enim posset clerus stare in suo gradu vivendo exproprietarie sicut primo, et ablata confederacione, assecuracione et occupacione cum mundo cresceret undique fructus cleri. Nec est credendum quod ad satisfaccionem tyranni requiritur ut det clero dominacionem civilem, sed oportet quod satisfaciens capitali domino iuvet suam ecclesiam, aliis ut egentibus conferendo, et superhabundantibus vel abutentibus subtrahendo. Sic enim dicitur Nabuchodonosor ex maiori evidencia quam nostrum cesarem meruisse.

If, I say, with such evangelical exhortations Silvester had provided more securely for the future [church]{g:ecclesia}, then in all likelihood he would have profited both parties more, as blessed Leonard profited the king of France more by drawing him back from worldly affection through refusing the office [369] of prelacy than he would have done by retaining that office and procuring from the king half of his [kingdom]{g:regnum}.[^1] Therefore he acted more in conformity with the gospel and (as I believe) more usefully for both parts of the church than blessed Silvester acted in receiving that charter from Caesar, as the chronicle hints concerning the voice of the angel saying that on the day of that [endowment]{g:dotacio} poison was poured into the holy church of God.[^2] Nor is Caesar's charter more credible than that chronicle; indeed it is more evident, [from the faith of scripture]{g:ex-fide} and the law of the church, that Nabuchodonosor, who destroyed the synagogue and the priests of Jerusalem, was saved, than that Constantine will be saved; because the decree, with Augustine, asserts the first from the testimony of scripture, but the second is left without any to urge it.[^3] But the doctor argues that Silvester was bound, of necessity of salvation, to receive the aforesaid endowment from Caesar, on the ground that [Caesar], having despoiled Christians of their goods, was bound to restore [them] to them.[^4] But if that [evidence]{g:evidencia} held, it would move [one], first, to believe that Constantine, through defect of full satisfaction, was damnable; second, since blessed Silvester ought under pain of mortal [sin] to receive that restitution, it follows that the whole empire was owed principally to the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex}, and by the same token, when any secular [chief lord]{g:capitalis-dominus} of any kingdom is extinct, the [bishop]{g:episcopus} ought to be its civil chief lord, as was deduced and disproved in the previous chapter. Third, it follows that secular [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio} is owed principally to Christ and to each vicar of his, since otherwise Caesar could have renounced all [civil standing]{g:civilitas} and distributed his goods to the poor, as Saint Thomas the apostle and Saint Laurence did; for, the opposite being granted, it was not necessary that Caesar should resign the empire to Silvester, and consequently it was not necessary under pain of mortal [sin] that blessed Silvester should have received that endowment from Caesar.[^5] [370] Hence I believe that it would have been more meritorious for both parties that the goods had been distributed to the poor, and a continual ministry ordained by lay people in aid of the needy; for then the [clergy]{g:clerus} could stand in its degree, living [without property]{g:exproprietarie} as at first, and, with alliance, assurance and occupation with the world taken away, the fruit of the clergy would grow on every side. Nor is it to be believed that, for the satisfaction of a tyrant, it is required that he give the clergy civil exercise of lordship; but it is needful that one making satisfaction to the chief lord should help his church, by conferring on others as being needy, and by withdrawing from those who have superabundance or abuse [it]. For so Nabuchodonosor is said, on greater evidence than our Caesar, to have merited.[^6]

## ¶2 · 370.15–370.29

> [p. 370] Quinto arguitur per deducens ad familiare inconveniens scilicet: Si beatus Silvester peccavit in recipiendo dotacionem ecclesie in perpetuum, sequitur a pari quod collegia nostre universitatis verisimiliter peccarent in recipiendo temporalia pro sustentacione perpetua pauperum clericorum; et ita sequitur quod tam clerici domini Wyntoniensis quam alii collegiati tenentur non perpetuari, et per consequens monere patronos ad dissolvendum privilegia perpetua, ut est de privilegiis perpetuis concessis universitati nostre a rege, et sic de cantariis et aliis elemosinis perpetuis. Revocetur, inquit, ista heresis, cum extingueret devocionem populi, elemosinas perpetuas clericorum, et per consequens cederet ad detrimentum maximum pauperibus in futurum.

Fifth, [it is argued by appeal to]{g:arguitur-per} [an argument] leading to a familiar [absurdity]{g:inconveniens}, namely: if blessed Silvester sinned in receiving the endowment of the church in perpetuity, it follows by parity that the colleges of our university would in all likelihood sin in receiving [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} for the perpetual sustenance of poor [clerics]{g:clericus}; and so it follows that both the clerics of the lord of Winchester and other collegiates are bound not to be perpetuated, and consequently [are bound] to urge the [patrons]{g:patronus} to dissolve perpetual [privileges]{g:privilegium}, as is the case with the perpetual privileges granted to our university by the king; and so with chantries and other perpetual [alms]{g:elemosina}.[^7] "Let this heresy be revoked," he says, "since it would extinguish the devotion of the people, the perpetual alms of the clerics, and consequently would turn to the greatest detriment of the poor in future."[^8]

## ¶3 · 370.30–372.1

> [p. 370] Hic dico primo quod consequencia non procedit, cum homo potest facere nedum bonum de genere, sed bene moraliter et tamen cum hoc et in hoc peccare venialiter, ut ista pars habet dicere in familiariori [p. 371] exemplo. Nam dominus Symon Hyslep archiepiscopus Cantuariensis fundavit unum collegium in Oxonia plus pia intencione, ut evidencius creditur quam de fundacione cuiuscunque abbathie in Anglia, et ordinavit quod in ea sub forma laudabili studeant ad utilitatem ecclesie pure clerici seculares, quod et factum est. Et cum ipso mortuo symoniace cum commentis mendacii eversum est tam pii patroni propositum et illis expulsis pauci alii non egentes sed diviciis affluentes irregulariter introducti contra decretum captum ex dictis beati Jeronimi, positum XIIª q. II: Gloria episcopi est pauperum opibus providere, ignominia sacerdotis est propriis studere diviciis. Et cum pretextu illius fuci sophistici episcopus et suum capitulum sunt una persona a qua non licet alienare bona illius ecclesie, ista persona vendicat bona illius collegii proprietarie possidere. Unde consulendum videtur domino Wyntoniensi ut caveat hanc cautelam. Credo autem quod dictus Symon peccavit fundando dictum collegium, sed non tantum quantum Antisymon qui ipsum dissolverat, sed ut credo nunquam fuit ecclesia appropriata in Anglia vel possessio in perpetuam elemosinam mortificata quin appropriacio sapuit peccatum altrinsecus. Ulterius pro materia argumenti affectarem, si Deus decreverit quod non foret in regno nostro talis ecclesiarum appropriacio vel reddituum temporalium mortificacio, sed quod totus clerus vivendo pure exproprietarie de decimis, oblacionibus et privatis elemosinis sit contentus; quamvis enim illud non dixerim per modum conclusionis, sed per viam condicionis et [p. 372] imprecacionis, tamen eius racionabilitas suaderi potest

To this I say, first, that the [inference]{g:consequencia} does not hold, since a man can do not only what is good in its kind but what is morally good, and yet with this and in this sin venially, as this party has to say in a more familiar [371] example.[^9] For lord Simon Islep, archbishop of Canterbury, founded a college in Oxford with a more pious intention (as is more evidently believed) than [is believed] of the foundation of any abbey in England, and he ordained that in it purely secular clerics should study, under a laudable form, for the utility of the church; which was also done. And when he was dead, the purpose of so pious a patron was overthrown simoniacally with the devices of a lie, and, those [clerics] being expelled, a few others, not needy but abounding in riches, [were] irregularly brought in, against the decree taken from the sayings of blessed Jerome, placed in [Causa] 12, q. 2: "It is the glory of a bishop to provide for the wealth of the poor; it is the shame of a [priest]{g:sacerdos} to study his own riches."[^10] And since, under pretext of that sophistical paint, the bishop and his chapter are one person, from whom it is not lawful to alienate the goods of that church, that person claims to possess the goods of that college as property. Hence it seems that the lord of Winchester should be counselled to beware of this [wile]{g:cautela}.[^11] Now I believe that the said Simon sinned in founding the said college, but not so much as the Anti-Simon who dissolved it; but, as I believe, never was a church [appropriated]{g:appropriare} in England, or a possession amortized in perpetual alms, but that the [appropriation]{g:appropriacio} savoured of sin on both sides. Further, for the matter of the argument, [I would desire]{g:affectare} that God should decree that there should not be in our kingdom such appropriation of churches or [amortization]{g:mortificacio} of temporal [revenues]{g:reditus}, but that the whole clergy, living purely without property, should be content with [tithes]{g:decime}, offerings and private alms; for although I would not say this by way of [conclusion]{g:conclusio}, but by way of condition and [372] prayer, yet its reasonableness can be urged[^12]

## Notes

[^1]: **R.** A in the margin at 368.29: *Nota defectus beati Silvestri*. **C.** *Inquam* resumes the counterfactual of the preceding part: Silvester should have taught Constantine from the gospel instead of taking his charter. **S.** St Leonard of Noblac, who in his legend refused a bishopric offered by the Frankish king; the offer of half the kingdom is Wyclif's form of it (to be checked, LEDGER E16d-1).

[^2]: **C.** The subject of *Ideo fecit* is Leonard. **S.** The angel's voice at the donation (*hodie diffusum est venenum in ecclesia Dei*) is a medieval commonplace Wyclif cites often, not traced to a single chronicle; Loserth gives no source here. Early Dante commentators give it in the same form (Jacopo della Lana: *diffusum*) (LEDGER E16d-2).

[^3]: **S.** Nebuchadnezzar's repentance: Daniel 4:31–34 (DR 4:34 "I, Nabuchodonosor, do now praise, and magnify, and glorify the King of heaven"). The decree that, with Augustine, asserts his salvation is probably Gratian, *Decretum* C.23 q.4 c.22, from Augustine: *Nabuchodonosor penitenciam meruit fructuosam* (LEDGER E16d-3); Loserth does not identify it. A in the margin at 369.10: *Salvacio Nabuchodonosor*. **C.** *Insuasum*: "not urged", i.e. no authority recommends it; *evidencius est* "it is more evident" is not the glossary's noun *evidencia*.

[^4]: **C.** *Doctor*: the opponent whose arguments this chapter answers, unnamed here. *Spolians christianos*: Constantine, as persecutor before his conversion; "[Caesar]" and "[them]" are supplied.

[^5]: **C.** The second and third consequences show the cost of the opponent's premiss: if Silvester was bound to take the empire as restitution, temporal lordship belongs of right to the pope and to every bishop, which chapter 15 disproved. *Sub pena mortalis*: "[sin]" supplied, as throughout. **S.** Thomas the apostle giving the king's money to the poor is told in the *Acts of Thomas*; Laurence distributing the treasure of the Roman church to the poor in Ambrose, *De officiis* 2.28 (both to be checked, LEDGER E16d-4, E16d-5).

[^6]: **T.** At 370.3 B lacks *continuum*; at 370.6 B reads *et assecuracione*; at 370.11–12 B reads *ab utentibus trahendo*. **C.** *Meritorius* is the neuter comparative, "more meritorious". *Capitali domino*: God as chief lord, to whom the tyrant owes satisfaction (so Nebuchadnezzar). *Aliis ut egentibus*: *ut* is taken as "as being"; the construal is uncertain.

[^7]: **R.** A in the margin at 370.15: *Quinta racio*. **C.** *Arguitur per deducens*: the noun is not expressed; "[an argument]" is supplied. *Inconveniens* (CONTEXTUAL) is here the absurd consequence of a premiss. **R.** The lord of Winchester is William of Wykeham, bishop of Winchester, whose college at Oxford (New College) dates from 1379 (to be checked, LEDGER E16d-6). **T.** At 370.22 B reads *tenentur velle non perpetuari*, "are bound to will not to be perpetuated"; at 370.24–25 B reads *nostri a rege*.

[^8]: **C.** *Elemosinas perpetuas clericorum* stands in apposition to *devocionem populi*: the devotion is its perpetual alms; the print has nothing between them. **T.** Loserth reports *extigueret* in the manuscript at 370.26 and prints *extingueret*.

[^9]: **R.** A in the margin at 370.30: *Responsio*. **C.** *Bonum de genere*: an act good in its kind, as against one done well morally. *Ista pars*: taken as "this party", the objector's side, who must grant the example; it could be "this part [of the answer]".

[^10]: **R.** Simon Islip, archbishop of Canterbury (d. 1366), founded Canterbury Hall, Oxford; his successor Simon Langham (the "Anti-Simon" below) expelled its secular clerks and brought in monks; Wyclif had been its warden. Loserth refers to Lechler, *Johann von Wiclif* I, 294, 303 (to be checked, LEDGER E16d-7). **C.** *Et cum ipso mortuo* is read as "and when he was dead", with *eversum est* and *introducti [sunt]* as main verbs; taken as a causal clause the sentence would lack its main clause. **T.** At 371.10 B reads *irregulari introducti*. **S.** Gratian, *Decretum* C.12 q.2 c.71 (Loserth: "Decreti II pars, causa XII, q. II, cap. LXXI"; the modern form is ours), from Jerome (LEDGER E16d-8).

[^11]: **C.** *Fucus*: paint, cosmetic. The "one person" is the corporation sole of bishop and chapter, whose goods cannot be alienated; the college, once annexed, is claimed under it.

[^12]: **C.** *Antisymon* plays on Islip's name and on *symoniace* above. *Affectarem, si Deus decreverit*: a wish, "I would desire that God should decree"; *imprecacio* is prayer, not curse. The sentence runs on into the next part. **R.** A in the margin at 371.25: *Conclusio. Contra dotacionem*.
