# sed/autem extract, wave 27: chunks 09-j 09-k 09-l

### 09-j ¶1 · 307.24–309.16  (9 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 307] Quantum ad mocionem corporis Christi videtur homines in ista materia loqui dupliciter vel tropice ad sensum quo concedunt illa que contingunt sacramentum corporis Christi contingere (ut loquitur decretum Ego Beringarius) vel sine figura ydemptice, modo quo dicitur Deum esse ubique et Petrum solum esse ubi est diffinitive; et sic videtur corpus Christi post ascensionem nedum non moveri in terris nec esse, **sed** virtus sua est per totum mundum, cum perficit [p. 308] humanitus quamlibet creaturam. **Sed** propter innovacionem vel incepcionem virtutis non oportet corpus Christi correspondenter moveri, sicut non oportet regem moveri correspondenter ad motum baiuli suas litteras deferentis. Quamvis enim corpus Christi sit ubique, eciam per totum mundum sublunarem, tamen est quadam singularitate in sacramento altaris; **sed** sicut non alteratur vel movetur in quantitate ad motum hostie, ita non movetur localiter correspondenter ut ipsa movetur localiter, quia non acquirit sibi ex motu hostie quantitatem aut qualitatem, sicut nec ex delacione hostie sibi acquirit novum, quod sicut corpus Christi non conburitur conbusta pixide, sic nec elevatur nec submittitur presbitero hostiam sic movente. Et patet quod corpus Christi non evomitur vel screatur. Et quoad manducacionem patet ex dictis dupliciter potest intelligi, vel tropice vel ydemptice; ydemptice non manducatur corporaliter **sed** spiritualiter a fideli, dum mens sua pascitur ex memoria corporis Christi; et sic non potest manducari a bestia nec manducatur sic a simpliciter fideli, **sed** indignus manducat sacramentum et accipit corpus Christi. Unde aliud est capere, aliud manducare; nam multi accipiunt Spiritum Sanctum qui non est mobilis nec corporaliter manducabilis; aer eciam secundum sic loquentes accipit corpus Christi, sicut os hominis; intestina eciam bestiarum in tantum quod nedum corpus Christi est ad omnem punctum mundi **sed** sacramentaliter est in interioribus bestiarum, cum posset ab illis forma panis extrahi; et quamdiu remanet forma panis aut vini consecrata, dicunt adversarii corpus Christi eciam quantumcunque modica [p. 309] particula sacramentaliter remanere. Et isto modo videtur Thomam dicere super distinccione IX, questione IIᵃ, quod creatura irracionalis sumit corpus Christi. Wilelmus **autem** in De Sacramento Altaris videtur dicere quod quinque modis accipitur corpus Christi et sic mus (ut inquit) comedit corpus Christi realiter **sed** non sacramentaliter neque spiritualiter. **Sed** absit, licet omnes isti (eciam papa) dixerint, quod bestia et omne manducans hostiam manducat propterea corpus Christi, sicut exemplificatum est de anima, ymmo loyci dicunt quod, sicut fons, lacus vel bestia accipit aquam que de facto est sacramentum baptismi, et tamen non baptizatur vel accipit baptismum, sic multa accipiunt illum panem vel illa accidencia, que de facto sunt sacramentum, et tamen non accipiunt sacramentum quia non sub racione sacramenti.

ENGLISH: As to the [motion]{g:mocio} of the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}, it seems that men speak in this matter in two ways: either [tropically]{g:tropice}, in the sense in which they grant that the things which befall the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} of the body of Christ befall [the body] (as the [decree]{g:decretum} *Ego Berengarius* speaks),[^1] or without figure, [identically]{g:ydemptice}, in the way in which God is said to be everywhere and Peter to be only where he is [definitively]{g:diffinitive}. And so it seems that the body of Christ after the ascension [not only]{g:nedum} is not moved on earth, nor is [there], but its power is through the whole world, since it perfects [308] [humanly]{g:humanitus} every [creature]{g:creatura}. But because of a renewal or [beginning]{g:incepcio} of the power the body of Christ need not be moved correspondingly, just as a king need not be moved correspondingly to the motion of the porter who carries his letters. For although the body of Christ is everywhere, even through the whole sublunar world, yet it is with a certain singularity in the [sacrament of the altar]{g:sacramentum-altaris}; but just as it is not altered or moved in [quantity]{g:quantitas} at the motion of the [host]{g:hostia}, so it is not moved [locally]{g:localiter} correspondingly as the host itself is moved locally, because it does not acquire for itself from the motion of the host quantity or [quality]{g:qualitas}, just as it acquires nothing new for itself from the carrying of the host either, in that, just as the body of Christ is not burned when the pyx is burned, so neither is it raised or lowered when the [presbyter]{g:presbiter} so moves the host. And it is clear that the body of Christ is not vomited or spat out. And as to the [eating]{g:manducacio}, it is clear from what has been said [that] it can be understood in two ways, either tropically or identically.[^2] Identically, it is not eaten [bodily]{g:corporaliter} but [spiritually]{g:spiritualiter} by the faithful man, while his mind is fed from the memory of the body of Christ; and so it cannot be eaten by a beast, nor is it so eaten by one who is faithful [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}, but the unworthy man eats the sacrament and receives the body of Christ.[^3] Hence it is one thing to take, another to eat;[^4] for many receive the Holy Spirit, who is not [movable]{g:mobilis} nor bodily eatable. The air too, according to those who speak so, receives the body of Christ, as does a man's mouth; the intestines of beasts too, so far that not only is the body of Christ at every point of the world, but it is [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} in the inward parts of beasts, since the form of [bread]{g:panis} could be drawn out of them; and as long as the consecrated form of bread or [wine]{g:vinum} remains, the adversaries say that the body of Christ remains sacramentally even in however tiny a [309] particle. And in this way Thomas seems to say, on distinction 9, question 2, that "an irrational creature receives the body of Christ."[^5] And William, in *On the Sacrament of the Altar*, seems to say that the body of Christ is received in five ways, and so "a mouse" (as he says) "eats the body of Christ [really]{g:realiter} but not sacramentally nor spiritually."[^6] But far be it, although all these (even the [pope]{g:papa}) have said it, that a beast and everything that eats the host therefore eats the body of Christ, as was illustrated of the soul;[^7] indeed the logicians say that, just as a fountain, a lake or a beast receives water which is in fact the sacrament of baptism, and yet is not baptized or receives baptism, so many things receive that bread or those [accidents]{g:accidens} which are in fact the sacrament, and yet do not receive the sacrament, because [they do] not [receive it] under the aspect of the sacrament.

### 09-j ¶2 · 309.17–311.15  (6 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 309] **Sed** dimissa ista altercacione loycis concedo cum glossa communi super distinccione secunda, capitulo Non iste, quod corpus Christi non descendit in stomachum, et usque ad quem locum ducitur per gulam nescio. Unde textus vere dicit: Non iste panis est qui vadit in corpus. Tripliciter enim accipitur panis ad propositum in scriptura sacra et sanctis doctoribus, scilicet pro pane usuali quem vulgus comedit, pro pane consecrato qui est sacramentum; quem Augustinus vocat panem celestem, et pro pane ultimo figurato qui est Christus Deus noster. Et istam equivocacionem sicut equivocam istorum predicacionem oportet theologum notare. Si enim panis qui est corpus Christi [p. 310] gloriosum in celo foret in sua persona nobiscum, ut sentimus de hostia, nullus vivens hanc vitam sufficeret suffere gloriam et extasim quam inferret; nam Petrus in transfiguracione cadebat ut mortuus. **Sed** in pane et vino figuratur nobis corpus Christi gloriosum, ut allegavi superius Augustinum et Rabanum et Damascenum. Scribit enim iste Grecus IIIᵒ Sentenciarum suarum, capitulo VIIIᵛᵒ: Quemadmodum consuetudo est homini aqua lavari et oleo ungi quo coniuncta gracia Spiritus Sancti facit lavacrum regeneracionis, sic quia consuetudo est hominibus panem comedere, vinum et aquam bibere, coniugavit Deus ipsis sui ipsius deitatem et fecit ipsa corpus et sanguinem sui ipsius. Nec repugnat **sed** consonat quod dicitur eodem capitulo quod panis et vinum transit et transsubstanciatur in corpus Christi et sanguinem. Per hoc enim quod dicit Deum fecisse ea corpus suum et sanguinem, patet quod intelligit ipsa esse tropice corpus Christi et sanguinem ad modum loquendi scripture; nam ut dicit Scotus: Eodem spiritu exponende sunt scripture quo condite erant; et sic ecclesia katholica ad illum sensum exposuit ad quem docuit. [p. 311] **Sed** absit fidelem ex illo concludere quod si Innocencius IIIᵘˢ cum suis satrapis sic diffinit, tunc ecclesia katholica ita sensit; verumptamen (ut dixi) presumpcio est quod katholice sensierunt de transsubstancione et accidentibus modo quo supra exposui, et non contrariabantur sanctis qui dicunt quantitatem et qualitatem non posse per se esse; **sed** generaliter modo scripture vocant ea panem et vinum, et sic intelligunt ipsi bene per sua accidencia, quia XXV, questione prima, Sunt quidam, dicit Urbanus papa katholice: ubi vero aperte Dominus vel eius apostoli et eos sequentes sancti patres sentencialiter aliquit diffinierunt, ibi non novam legem Romanus dare potest **sed** pocius quod predicatum est usque ad animam et sanguinem confirmare, quia aliter nimium oberraret.

ENGLISH: But, leaving this wrangling to the logicians, I grant with the common [gloss]{g:glossa} on distinction 2, chapter *Non iste*, that "the body of Christ does not descend into the stomach," and "up to what place it is led through the gullet I do not know."[^8] Hence the text says [truly]{g:vere}: "This is not the bread which goes into the body." For bread is taken in three ways for the purpose in holy scripture and the holy doctors: namely for the common bread which the people eat; for the consecrated bread which is the sacrament, which Augustine calls "heavenly bread";[^9] and for the bread [figured]{g:figuratum} last of all, which is Christ our God. And this equivocation, like the equivocal predication of these, the theologian must note. For if the bread which is the body of Christ, [310] glorious in heaven, were with us in its own person, as we [perceive]{g:sentire} of the host, no one living this life would be able to bear the glory and ecstasy it would bring; for Peter at the transfiguration fell as dead.[^10] But in the bread and wine the glorious body of Christ is figured to us, as I [alleged]{g:allegare} Augustine and Rabanus and Damascene above. For this Greek writes in [book] 3 of his *Sentences*, chapter 8: "As it is the custom for man to be washed with water and anointed with oil, with which the grace of the Holy Spirit, joined, makes the laver of regeneration, so, because it is the custom for men to eat bread, to drink wine and water, God conjoined to them the [Godhead]{g:deitas} of himself and made them the body and blood of himself."[^11] Nor is it [incompatible]{g:repugnare}, but it accords, that it is said in the same chapter that "the bread and wine pass over and are transubstantiated into the body and blood of Christ." For by his saying that God made them his body and blood, it is clear that he understands them to be tropically the body and blood of Christ, after scripture's manner of speaking; for, as Scotus says, "the scriptures are to be expounded in the same spirit in which they were composed";[^12] and so the catholic [church]{g:ecclesia} expounded [them] to that [meaning]{g:sensus} to which it taught [them]. [311] But far be it from a faithful man to [infer]{g:concludere} from that, that if Innocent III with his [satraps]{g:satrapa} so defines, then the catholic church so [held]{g:sentire}; nevertheless (as I said) the presumption is that they held in catholic fashion about [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio} and the accidents in the way I expounded above, and did not contradict the [saints]{g:sancti}, who say that quantity and quality cannot be [of themselves]{g:per-se}; but generally, after the manner of scripture, they call them bread and wine, and so they themselves understand [them] well by "its accidents".[^13] For in [Causa] 25, question 1, *Sunt quidam*, Pope Urban says in catholic fashion: "But where the Lord or his apostles, and the holy fathers following them, have openly defined something by way of teaching, there the Roman [pontiff] cannot give a new law, but rather confirm, even unto soul and blood, what has been preached; because otherwise he would go [exceedingly]{g:nimis} astray."[^14]

### 09-k ¶1 · 312.1–312.17  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 312] Unde sicut fratres palliant vitam cesaream consensu multiplici, sic sophisticant in materia de accidentibus eukaristie; **sed** sicut sunt in signis, professione et opere contradictorii sibi ipsis, sic alternatim contradicunt in materia de accidentibus sacramenti; ideo non est conveniens in hoc testimonium eorum; et cum evidenter creditur quod facta generali sinodo plures voces haberet in utrolibet istorum articulorum pars Antichristi quam pars Christi et sic amplius seduceretur ecclesia, ideo debemus pulsare Veritatem obnixius, ut in utroque istorum, et specialiter in primo quod plus spectat ad mores reducat fideles, quia quoad paginas contra scripturam non est aliud nisi adducere corruptos satrapas qui sciant exponere veritatem scripture per eius oppositum. Et ista sentencia erit diffuse katholica et contraria proscripta tanquam heretica.

ENGLISH: Hence, just as the [friars]{g:frater} [cloak]{g:palliare} the caesarean life by manifold consent, so they [play the sophist]{g:sophisticari} in the matter of the [accidents]{g:accidens} of [the Eucharist]{g:eukaristia};[^1] but just as they are contradictory to themselves in signs, profession and work, so by turns they contradict [themselves] in the matter of the accidents of the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}; therefore their testimony on this is not fitting. And since it is evidently believed that, were a general synod held, the part of Antichrist would have more votes than the part of Christ on either of these articles, and so the [church]{g:ecclesia} would be led further astray,[^2] therefore we must knock at Truth the more earnestly, that in both of these, and especially in the first, which bears more on morals, he may bring the faithful back; for as to the [pages]{g:pagina} against scripture, there is nothing else for it but to bring in corrupt [satraps]{g:satrapa} who know how to expound the truth of scripture by its opposite.[^3] And this [teaching]{g:sentencia} will be catholic far and wide, and the contrary proscribed as heretical.[^4]

### 09-k ¶2 · 312.18–313.17  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 312] **Sed** fideles in veritate confidunt quod, sicut in tempore Christi inposuerunt Christo et suis gravissimum genus heresis quod est blasphemia, et post hec tiranni cruciantes martires Christi inposuerunt eis heresim ac infidelitatem non colendo deos eorum, sic necesse est quod succedat in ecclesia pestis gravior, inponens fideli heresim, quia defendit veritatem scripture. In omnibus enim istis docetur commune proverbium quod viciosus cicius impetet adversarium suum ex crimine in quo timendo redargucionem se noverit magis reum. Et in omnibus istis est fides cum karitate et paciencia optima medicina. Scimus quidem quod ante tribunal Veritatis omnes astabimus, ubi non clamor vel multitudo testimonii **sed** ipsa veritas facti ante oculos iudicis accusabit. Nam si vel [p. 313] propter magnificenciam nostram vel pecuniam a populo cumulandam ipsos errare facimus, peccata illorum comedimus et nos ipsos pro duplici crimine ipsorum et nostra cecitate temeraria obligamus, dicente Gregorio Omelia XVII: Debemus, inquit, sine cessacione meminisse quod de quibusdam Osee dictum est: Peccata populi mei comedunt et sunt hii qui delinquencium peccata fovent ne temporalia stipendia vel honores amittant. **Sed** et nos qui ex obligacione fidelium vivimus quod illi pro peccatis suis nobis conferunt, si comedimus et tacemus, procul dubio eorum peccata comedimus. Quid enim oportet infundabiliter in magnificencia sacramenti fingere nostram excellenciam sine fructu? Unde frequenter mirando movebam quid movit primos glossatores sic dicere, et cum nec racio nec testimonium, solum superest revelacio vel scriptura.

ENGLISH: But the faithful trust in the truth that, just as in the time of Christ they imputed to Christ and his own the gravest kind of heresy, which is blasphemy, and after this the tyrants who tortured Christ's martyrs imputed to them heresy and [unbelief]{g:infidelitas} for not worshipping their gods, so it must be that a graver plague succeeds in the church, imputing heresy to the faithful man because he defends the truth of scripture. For in all these the common proverb is taught, that the vicious man is quicker to attack his adversary for the crime of which, fearing [refutation]{g:redargucio}, he knows himself the more guilty. And in all these, faith with [charity]{g:caritas} and patience is the best medicine. We know indeed that we shall all stand before the tribunal of Truth, where not clamour or the multitude of testimony but the very truth of the deed before the judge's eyes will accuse. For if, either [313] for our magnificence or for money to be heaped up from the people, we make them err, we eat their sins and bind ourselves for a twofold crime, theirs and our own rash blindness,[^5] as Gregory says in Homily 17: "We must," he says, "remember without ceasing what was said by Osee of certain men: *They eat the sins of my people*; and these are they who foster the sins of offenders lest they lose temporal stipends or honours. But we too, who live by the obligation of the faithful, what they confer on us for their sins, if we eat and are silent, without doubt eat their sins."[^6] For what need is there to [feign]{g:fingere}, [without foundation]{g:infundabiliter}, our excellence in the magnificence of the sacrament, without fruit? Hence I have often, wondering, raised the question what moved the first [glossators]{g:glossator} to speak so; and since [they had] neither reason nor testimony, there remains only revelation or scripture.

### 09-k ¶3 · 313.18–314.3  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 313] Quantum ad revelacionem, in diebus eorum fuit sermo Domini preciosus; scripturam **autem** cum sensu suo nesciunt allegare; et cum scriptura tam crebro vocat hostiam panem, periculosum videtur credere vel docere quod Spiritus Sanctus intellexit per panem illum non panem **sed** accidens aliene nature, cum sancti et racio contradicant. Non enim est racio quin panis sacratus et vinum simpliciter sint sacramentum corporis dominici, sicut aqua benedicta sit sacramentum baptismi et sic de aliis sacramentis; nec est racio quod Deus deteriorat universitatem suam, destruendo signi substanciam sine causa; nec est racio quod Veritas ordinet sacramentum tam spirituale, ut sit [p. 314] falsum tanquam insolubile puerorum, sic quod illa accidencia sensibilia significent panem vel subiectum suum ad esse, dum totaliter ibi desit.

ENGLISH: As to revelation, in their days "the word of the Lord was precious";[^7] and scripture with its [meaning]{g:sensus} they do not know how to [allege]{g:allegare}. And since scripture so often calls the [host]{g:hostia} [bread]{g:panis}, it seems dangerous to believe or teach that the Holy Spirit understood by that bread not bread but an accident of an alien nature, since the [saints]{g:sancti} and reason contradict [it]. For there is no reason why the [hallowed]{g:sacrare} bread and [wine]{g:vinum} should not be [without qualification]{g:simpliciter} the sacrament of [the Lord's body]{g:corpus-dominicum}, as blessed water is the sacrament of baptism, and so of the other sacraments; nor is there reason that God should make [his universe]{g:universitas-sua} worse by destroying the [substance]{g:substancia} of the sign without cause; nor is there reason that Truth should ordain a sacrament so spiritual that it [314] would be false like a boys' [insoluble]{g:insolubilia}: so that those [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} accidents should signify bread, or their subject, as to [being]{g:esse}, while it is wholly absent there.[^8]

### 09-k ¶4 · 314.4–316.9  (10 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 314] **Sed** sicut false memoramur vitam Christi conversando contrarie, sic Deus permittit nos false signa concipere. Concedo tamen quod inconveniens est ecclesiam tam diu errasse in speculabilibus, **sed** maius inconveniens est ipsam diucius sic errasse in operabilibus et (ut credo) superiores ac inferiores huius secte discredunt quod referunt, cum nunquam vel raro celebrant, et tamen longe plus et fervencius laborant pro faccione nedum temporalium ut auri modici **sed** pro possessione vel habicione eorum (que est respectus debilissimus) acquirenda. Quomodo ergo credam ex opere quod talis convertit panem suum in regnum celorum, quia personaliter in corpus Christi, quod exinde credit perpetuo se habere; ymmo si credit plene evangelio, laboraret pro cognacione Christi habenda, qui Matth. XIIᵒ, 5 dicit verissime: Quicunque fecerit voluntatem patris mei qui in celis est, ipse meus frater, soror et mater est. Ista quidem cognacio que ex tanta auctoritate procedit cum verbo substandi foret longe melior quam conficere, tractare vel (ut ipsi inquiunt) facere corpus Christi. Cultum **autem** rectum non reprobo **sed** desidero et procurarem quantum sufficio, **sed** cultum infidelem et duplicem detestor quo adoratur sacramentum tamquam ydemptice corpus Christi. Pocius **autem** concederem cum Augustino, Lincolniensi et ceteris quod omne membrum Christi sit realiter et vere **sed** participacione quadam dominus Jesus Christus; nam quodlibet tale [p. 315] est membrum vivum Christi, ipso Christo in se loquente et faciente omnia eius viva opera tanquam quidditate intrinsecus informante, quod deficit a sacramento altaris; ideo evidencius ex racione et scriptura Christus faceret se esse quodlibet membrum suum. Aliter enim, supposita fide scripture Math. XXVᵒ, pascendo vel opus misericordie inpendendo uni in nomine Christi, sic faceret homo duobus hominibus, et sic de indigenciis eorum et toto facto foret falsum Christum tunc fuisse esurientem, sicientem aut nudum nisi fuerit eius membrum, quia posito quod scimus post ascensionem quando Christus esuriit et sic patitur, patet quod non in persona propria sic modo patitur. Et illud considerarent fingentes accidencia esse sine subiecto propter hoc quod Christus vere **sed** figurative dicit panem esse corpus suum. Utrumque enim dicunt simpliciter; nec convincetur ex scriptura, racione vel sanctorum testimonio plus unum quam aliud preter glossatores recentes qui ficte fabricant novam fidem; et (ut tactum est) non est maior color: Si Innocencius IIIᵘˢ sic determinat, ergo verum; quod si ipse cum complicibus determinat quod rex Anglie solvere debeat sibi et sedi sue in perpetuum nongentas marcas annuatim in partim pecunie pro commissis, tunc hoc fuit consonum racioni; utrobique enim est tanta auctoritas. **Sed** Anglici et cuncti fideles dicerent quod illud fuit irreligiosa cupiditas; ideo suspecta est eius assercio nisi de quanto est fundabilis in scriptura. Revera si per inpossibile Jesus noster sic sibi et suis apostolis accumulasset lucrum pro crimine, tota evangelisacio sua suspecta fuisset fidelibus, ideo sicut docemur in practica noscere istam [p. 316] fallaciam: Robertus Gibbonensis asserit sic cum suo clero, ergo verum; et secta sua tanta probabilitate negat consequenciam istam: Urbanus noster cum suis cardinalibus sic determinat, ergo verum. Sic docemur noscere has fallacias in quocunque Petri vicario et specialiter post dotacionem ecclesie. Sic enim negamus tanquam plenam fallaciam: si Johannes archipresbiter successor sancti Thome in India sic asserit, ergo verum.

ENGLISH: But just as we falsely remember the life of Christ by [living]{g:conversari} contrary [to it], so God permits us to conceive the signs falsely. I grant, nevertheless, that it is an [absurdity]{g:inconveniens} that the church should have erred so long in speculative matters; but it is a greater absurdity that it should have erred so for longer in practical matters.[^9] And (as I believe) the superiors and inferiors of this [sect]{g:secta} disbelieve what they report, since they never or rarely celebrate, and yet they labour far more and more fervently for the [making]{g:faccio} not only of temporal goods, such as a little gold, but for the acquiring of the possession or [holding]{g:habicio} of them (which is a most feeble [relation]{g:respectus}). How then shall I believe from his work that such a man converts his bread into the [kingdom]{g:regnum} of heaven, because [he converts it] personally into the body of Christ, which he believes he will thereafter have forever?[^10] Indeed, if he believed the gospel fully, he would labour to have [kinship]{g:cognacio} with Christ, who in Matt. 12:50 says most truly: "Whosoever shall do the will of my Father that is in heaven, he is my brother, sister and mother."[^11] This kinship indeed, which proceeds from so great an [authority]{g:auctoritas} with the verb of being, would be far better than to [consecrate]{g:conficere}, handle, or (as they say) make the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}.[^12] And right [worship]{g:cultus} I do not reprove, but desire, and would procure as far as I am able; but the unbelieving and double worship I detest, by which the sacrament is adored as [identically]{g:ydemptice} the body of Christ. But I would rather grant with Augustine, [Lincoln]{g:lincolniensis} and the rest that every [member]{g:membrum} of Christ is [really]{g:realiter} and [truly]{g:vere}, but by a certain participation, the Lord Jesus Christ;[^13] for each such [member] [315] is a living member of Christ, Christ himself speaking in it and doing all its living works, as a quiddity [informing]{g:informare} it from within, which is lacking to the [sacrament of the altar]{g:sacramentum-altaris}; therefore Christ would more evidently, from reason and scripture, make himself to be each of his members. For otherwise, supposing the faith of scripture in Matt. 25, a man feeding one in the name of Christ, or bestowing a work of mercy on him, would so do it to two men; and so, as to their needs and the whole deed, it would be false that Christ was then hungry, thirsty or naked, unless [the man] were his member; for, supposing that we know [a time] after the ascension when Christ hungered and so suffers, it is clear that he does not now so suffer in his own [person]{g:persona}.[^14] And this those should consider who [feign]{g:fingere} "accidents are without a subject" because Christ truly but [figuratively]{g:figurative} says that bread is his body. For they say both [without qualification]{g:simpliciter};[^15] nor will the one be [proved conclusively]{g:convincere} more than the other from scripture, reason or the testimony of the saints, apart from the recent glossators who fictitiously fabricate a new faith. And (as has been touched on) there is no greater [colour]{g:color} [in]: "If Innocent III so determines, therefore [it is] true," than [in]: if he with his accomplices determines that the king of England must pay to him and his see in perpetuity nine hundred marks yearly in part of money for his offences, then this was consonant with reason; for [on both sides]{g:utrobique} there is as much authority.[^16] Now the English and all the faithful would say that that was irreligious cupidity; therefore his assertion is suspect except insofar as it can be founded in scripture. [In truth]{g:revera}, if per impossibile our Jesus had so heaped up gain for himself and his apostles in return for crime, his whole preaching of the gospel would have been suspect to the faithful. Therefore, just as we are taught in practice to recognise this [316] [fallacy]{g:fallacia}: "Robert of Geneva asserts so with his clergy, therefore [it is] true," and his sect with as much probability denies this [inference]{g:consequencia}: "Our Urban with his cardinals so determines, therefore [it is] true,"[^17] so we are taught to recognise these fallacies in any [vicar of Peter]{g:vicarius-petri} whatever, and especially after the [endowment]{g:dotacio} of the church. For so we deny as a full fallacy: "If John the archpriest, successor of St Thomas in India, so asserts, therefore [it is] true."[^18]

### 09-k ¶5 · 316.10–316.18  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 316] Septimo dubitatur: Si eukaristia sit latria vel dulia adoranda, et videtur quod latria, quia non est adoranda nisi ut ymago Dei, nec absolute ut ymago, **sed** ut figurative et vicarie ipse Deus, **sed** ut sic est adoranda latria, ergo conclusio: Adoramus enim crucem Domini non sub racione qua truncus putridus **sed** sub racione qua Deus in ipsa passus fuit pro nobis. Sic enim Alexander Magnus adoravit sacerdotem Jerusalem, ut dicit magister in historia Machabeorum.

ENGLISH: Seventh, it is doubted whether the Eucharist is to be adored with [latria]{g:latria} or [dulia]{g:dulia}.[^19] And it seems [with] latria, because it is to be adored only as the image of God, and not absolutely as an image, but as figuratively and vicariously God himself; now as such it is to be adored with latria; therefore the [conclusion]{g:conclusio} [holds]. For we adore the Lord's cross not under the aspect in which it is a rotten trunk but under the aspect in which God suffered on it for us. For so Alexander the Great adored the [priest]{g:sacerdos} of Jerusalem, as the Master says in the history of the Maccabees.[^20]

### 09-k ¶6 · 316.19–316.22  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 316] **Sed** contra illud videtur quod Christus quoad humanitatem adorandus est yperdulia (ut hic supponitur), ergo hostia et ymagines adorande sunt adoracione inferiori.

ENGLISH: But against that it seems that Christ as to [the humanity]{g:humanitas} is to be adored with [hyperdulia]{g:yperdulia} (as is supposed here); therefore the host and images are to be adored with a lower adoration.[^21]

### 09-l ¶3 · 317.9–317.29  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 317] Secundo notandum quod multiplex est racio adorandi naturam racionalem, vel pro excellencia quam ex se habet qualiter solus Deus est adorandus, vel pro excellencia quam a Deo participat, sive fuerit angelus sive homo secundum excellenciam secularem sive excellenciam sacerdotalem. Unde secundum istam racionem mediam Jacob adoravit Esau fratrem suum Genes. XXXIIIᵒ. **Sed** tercio modo adoramus ymagines nude ut signa, Deum **autem** debemus quantum sufficimus adorare. **Sed** in adoracione creature consistit nimia religionis perturbacio ut ydolatria et personarum accepcio; quandocunque enim per se adoratur ymago creata ut Deus vel inordinate aut moroso amore committitur ydolatria. Et isto modo dicit Apostolus Ephes. Vᵒ, 5 quod avarus est ydolorum servitus. Et in isto peccato gentes quas vocamus infideles laborant, adorantes bestias vel simulacra muta tanquam ymagines Dei quem adorant et credunt finaliter. Et in ista ydolatria nos vocati christiani peccamus sepe plus barbaris adorando sanctorum ymagines, reliquias et sacramentum altaris.

ENGLISH: Second, it is to be noted that the ground of adoring rational nature is manifold: either for the excellence it has of itself, in which way God alone is to be adored; or for the excellence it participates from God, whether it be an angel or a man, according to secular excellence or priestly excellence. Hence according to this middle ground Jacob adored Esau his brother, Gen. 33.[^4] But in the third way we adore images barely as signs; but God we ought to adore as far as we are able. But in the adoration of a [creature]{g:creatura} there lies an [exceeding]{g:nimis} disturbance of religion, such as [idolatry]{g:ydolatria} and [respect of persons]{g:accepcio-personarum}; for whenever a created image is adored [of itself]{g:per-se} as God, or inordinately, or with lingering love, idolatry is committed.[^5] And in this way the Apostle says, Eph. 5:5, that "the covetous man is the [servitude of idols]{g:servitus-ydolorum}."[^6] And in this sin the gentiles whom we call [unbelievers]{g:infidelis} labour, adoring beasts or dumb idols as images of the God whom they adore and believe in as their end.[^7] And in this idolatry we who are called Christians often sin more than the barbarians, by adoring the images of saints, relics and the [sacrament of the altar]{g:sacramentum-altaris}.

### 09-l ¶4 · 317.30–318.11  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 317] Unde penes infideles fiunt plura vocata miracula, ut decipiant infideles. Religio **autem** christiana primeva non habuit tales ymagines nec sanctos canonisatos [p. 318] **sed** vixerunt perfeccius nobis in fide filii Dei, cum omnia talia signa non adiuvant nisi de quanto iuvant et excitant ad Christum sponsum ecclesie diligendum. Et hinc creditur Deum ordinasse corpus matris Domini non adorari in loco habitabilis nostre, ut nec corpus Moysi in lege veteri nec coli corpora Apostolorum tam sumptuose et splendide, sicut coluntur corpora sanctorum in Anglia. Et hinc creditur Apostolum tam crebro vocare sacramentum altaris panem. Et omnia ista sicut prohibicio ymaginum erant propter ydolatriam precavendam.

ENGLISH: Hence among unbelievers many [so-called]{g:vocatus} miracles happen, so as to deceive the unbelievers. But the primitive Christian religion did not have such images nor [canonized]{g:canonizare} saints, [318] but they lived more perfectly than we in the faith of the Son of God, since all such signs do not help except insofar as they help and stir [us] to love Christ, the [bridegroom]{g:sponsus} of the church.[^8] And hence it is believed that God ordained that the body of the mother of the Lord should not be adored in any place of our habitable [world], as neither the body of Moses in the old law, nor that the bodies of the Apostles should be worshipped so sumptuously and splendidly as the bodies of the saints are worshipped in England.[^9] And hence it is believed that the Apostle so often calls the sacrament of the altar [bread]{g:panis}. And all these, like the prohibition of images, were for guarding against idolatry.

### 09-l ¶5 · 318.12–318.29  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 318] Conceditur ergo quod reliquie, ymagines et sacramenta sunt cum prudencia adoranda. Ille ergo qui sciret suspendere consideracionem signi sensibilis et appendere totam intencionem et affeccionem in creatore ymaginis, modo quo clericus suspendit scripturas concipiendo sentenciam; ille inquam adoraret latria Deum in illo signo. **Sed** nimis multi sunt layci et bestiales sensibilibus nimium intendentes et multi, ut loquitur Apostolus Iᵃ Cor. XIIᵒ, 2, cum adorando hostiam tanquam gentes ad simulacra muta prout ducebantur captivi euntes ad ydolatrandum sicut brutum ad iugum servilis operis. Qui **autem** adorat humanitatem Christi ut talem in hostia, adorat in ipsa Christum yperdulia, et nemo rite adorat ipsam sub racione propria, et sic vere concluditur quod homo sit multiplicius honorandus quam hostia et adorandus tanquam ymago Dei, vas virtutum et sic Christus, verius quam hostia consecrata.

ENGLISH: It is granted, therefore, that relics, images and sacraments are to be adored with prudence. He therefore who could [suspend]{g:suspendere} the consideration of the [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} sign and hang his whole intention and affection on the creator of the image, in the way a cleric suspends the letters in conceiving the [sense]{g:sentencia-verborum}, he, I say, would adore God with [latria]{g:latria} in that sign.[^10] But exceedingly many are lay and beastly men, attending too much to perceptible things; and many, as the Apostle speaks, 1 Cor. 12:2, with adoring the host "as the heathens to dumb idols, according as they were led" captive, going to [idolatrize]{g:ydolatrare} like a brute to the [yoke]{g:iugum} of servile work.[^11] But he who adores the [humanity]{g:humanitas} of Christ as such in the host adores in it Christ with [hyperdulia]{g:yperdulia}, and no one rightly adores the host under its own aspect; and so it is [truly]{g:vere} [inferred]{g:concludere} that a man is to be honoured in more ways than the host, and to be adored as the image of God, a vessel of virtues and so Christ, more truly than the consecrated host.[^12]

### 09-l ¶6 · 318.30–320.14  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 318] Et sic creditur quod error circa cultum hostie dimittit caritatem in homine. Tres enim sunt cause profectus et fructus huius sacramenti scilicet ut pax et caritas in hominibus augeatur, ut memoria gratitudinis [p. 319] Christi recencius habeatur et ut vigilancius in modo conversandi misse particeps Domini exequatur; et omnium horum trium opposita contingunt populo et specialiter sacerdotibus misse qui ex officio pro istis tribus magis sollicite laborarent. Nemo enim plus perturbat pacem Christi, extinguit caritatem, minus fructuose memoratur vitam Christi, ut sequatur ipsum in moribus quam faciunt sacerdotes, et specialiter prelati qui ad hoc ex nomine plus tenentur. Debemus enim instar Christi minus intendere mundo cum suis ritibus, plus aufugere secularia desideria cum suis contencionibus, cum ipsa plus extinguunt pacem et karitatem ecclesie. Nemo minus studet, ut vivat conformiter vite Christi, ymmo nullus Antichristus magis ab ea degenerat, cum non studemus circa vitam parcam penalem et exproprietariam, quam indubie Christus vixit in terris, **sed** circa vitam largam, lautam, proprietariam, pompaticam et maxime secularem. Cum ergo cultus hostie maxime consistit in complexu istorum trium et in aufugio horum: ita non in preciosis vel sumptuosis ornamentis nec in ritibus inclinacionum, genuflexionum vel decapuciacionum **sed** in mente pura et sanctitate vite conversacioni Christi similis. Et hinc videtur quod sacerdotes quibus singulariter commissum est hoc ministerium debent in paupere vita sequi Christum et sine offensione ecclesie vel cura de propriis habere omnia in communi, ut dicit Apostolus Iᵃ Cor. Xᵒ, 17: Unus panis et unum corpus multi sumus. Unde solebam pro tempore illius perturbacionis et invasionis regni nostri asserere quod, cum bona regni in manibus religiosorum sint bona pauperum, quorum bonorum ipsi religiosi sunt procuratores [p. 320] non domini, expediret communitati regni in parliamentis constanter instare quod de bonis illis pauperum magis vacantibus regnum intrinsecus ab hostibus defendatur, servatis bonis vivis ad usus pauperum et proviso religiosis sic alleviatis de necessariis vite et ministerio eorum; quod fieret si pensata gravitate, quantitate, qualitate ipsorum limitata sit eis porcio bonorum de quibus debent racionabiliter contentari. Tota ista sentencia patet ex dictis alibi; et repetitur hic triplex sentencia, nam Wilhelmus Conquestor cepit nedum temporalia **sed** possessiones et vitam licite ab Anglicana ecclesia in Deum et ipsum leviter delinquente: ergo multo magis licet regi et regno micius recipere elemosinas a sua ecclesia gravius delinquente.

ENGLISH: And so it is believed that error about the [worship]{g:cultus} of the host lets [charity]{g:caritas} go in a man. For there are three causes of the profit and fruit of this sacrament: namely, that peace and charity among men be increased; that the memory of the [kindness]{g:gratitudo} [319] of Christ be had [more freshly]{g:recencius}; and that the partaker of the Lord's mass may carry [it] out more watchfully in his manner of [living]{g:conversari}.[^13] And the opposites of all these three befall the people, and especially the [priests]{g:sacerdos} of the mass, who by office should labour the more solicitously for these three. For no one disturbs the peace of Christ more, extinguishes charity, remembers the life of Christ less fruitfully so as to follow him in conduct, than priests do, and especially [prelates]{g:prelatus}, who by their name are more bound to this. For we ought, after the pattern of Christ, to attend less to the world with its [rites]{g:ritus}, to flee more the secular desires with their contentions, since these most extinguish the peace and charity of the church. No one studies less to live in conformity with the life of Christ; indeed no Antichrist degenerates more from it, since we do not study for the sparing, [hard]{g:penalis} and [propertyless]{g:exproprietarii} life which without doubt Christ lived on earth, but for a life large, luxurious, [proprietary]{g:proprietarius}, boastful and supremely secular. Since, therefore, the worship of the host consists chiefly in the embrace of those three and in the flight from these, so [it consists] not in precious or sumptuous ornaments, nor in rites of bowings, genuflexions or doffings of hoods, but in a pure mind and holiness of life like the [manner of life]{g:conversacio} of Christ.[^14] And hence it seems that priests, to whom [alone]{g:singulariter} this ministry is committed, ought to follow Christ in a poor life and, without offence to the church or care for their own things, have all things in common, as the Apostle says, 1 Cor. 10:17: "We, being many, are one bread, one body."[^15] Hence at the time of that disturbance and invasion of our [kingdom]{g:regnum} I used to assert that, since the goods of the kingdom in the hands of the [religious]{g:religiosi} are the goods of the poor, of which goods the religious themselves are [proctors]{g:procurator}, [320] not [lords]{g:dominus}, it would be expedient for the community of the kingdom to press constantly in parliaments that from those goods of the poor which lie more idle the kingdom be defended within from its enemies, the living goods being kept for the uses of the poor, and provision being made for the religious, so relieved, of the necessaries of life and of their ministry; which would be done if, their gravity, quantity and quality being weighed, a portion of goods were [assigned]{g:limitare} to them with which they ought reasonably to be content.[^16] This whole [teaching]{g:sentencia} is clear from what is said elsewhere; and a threefold teaching is repeated here. For William the Conqueror took, lawfully, not only [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} but possessions and life from the English church, which offended lightly against God and him: therefore much more is it lawful for the king and kingdom more mildly to take back [alms]{g:elemosina} from their own church, which offends more gravely.[^17]
