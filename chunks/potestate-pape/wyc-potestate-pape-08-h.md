# *De potestate pape*, chapter 8, part 8: redeeming the time

## ¶1 · 192.10–194.6

> [p. 192] Et ne pallietur huius negacionis negacio, que potissime consistit in facto, attendamus quomodo superiores satrape, quibus regeretur ecclesia, honorificum reputant legem Christi ignorando contempnere eos qui pie volunt vivere, et pericula eorum, sicut debent ex Ezechielis XXXIV detegere, nituntur omnimode tribulare, ut ostendit Augustinus in De Verbis Domini Sermone LXXXIX: Quisquis, inquit, me audis, incipe in Christo vivere, et probabis quod dico, nam cetera pericula, de quibus Apostolus, possunt quiescere, pericula autem in falsis fratribus usque ad finem seculi quiescere non noverunt. Et intelligit modo Apostoli per falsos fratres generaliter quoscumque falsos nomine christianos. Redimamus itaque tempus, quantum dies mali sunt; hoc est, inquit, tempus redimere, quando aliquis infert tibi litem perdere substanciam, ut Deo vaces, non litibus. Quia, inquam, superiores ecclesie factis negant hanc legem, quam Chrisus in vase suo loquitur, ideo statim multitudine persequuntur eum, qui nititur dare fidem in populo, quod ipsi dicte legi apostoli precipue sunt astricti; et sic ipsi et multi per ipsos perdunt tempus propter lucrum [p. 193] turpissimum, vocatum a ceteris commodum temporale. Nec sunt audiendi discoli, qui fingunt quod omnis res durans sit tempus, et sic clerus emens ecclesiastica beneficia vel secularia dominia emit tempus, quod est hodie necessarium clero, cum layci clericis oppido sunt infesti; antichristiana deliramenta sunt ista et sensus Apostoli subversiva. Intelligit enim Apostolus tempus proprie pro duracione successiva tocius mundi, que quamvis omni homini et omni corpori sit communis, tamen quando homo in illa secundum legem Dei progreditur et allocatur in die novissimo de expensione impensi temporis sibi ipsi proficit ad salutem tunc propter illum proprium et utilem modum habendi redimit sibi tempus. Redimit dico; quia Christus emit tempus toti ecclesie, cum aliter pure naturaliter numerasset tempus hominum. Sed merito Christi factum est tempus acceptabile, quia in brevi tempore homo ex accepcione divina potest beatitudinem promereri. Sed cum ille qui creavit te sine te, non iustificat te sine te, necesse habemus doctrinam Apostoli Ephes. V, 15—16 attendere: Videte, inquit, quomodo caute ambuletis, redimentes tempus, quoniam dies mali sunt. Oportet igitur preter redempcionem generalem temporis per Christum nobis tempus redimere, quod tunc secundum Augustinum facimus, quando temporalium affluenciam et vitam voluptuosam pretermittimus vel mutamus, ut Christo vivamus accepcius et nobis utilius, tunc enim novo modo habendi acquirimus nobis tempus. Et secundum Augustinum iudicium illius episcopi, ymo iudicium Christi, in ipso sufficeret finem imponere omni liti. Ideo illud iudicium debet [p. 194] dux christiani exercitus, scilicet vicarius Christi, magis attendere, cum filii huius seculi sunt prudentes et callidi; ut ex stultis commerciis habeant hic dies prosperos quoad mundum, sed mali sunt et fatui cum ex tali negociacione dies una perditur valens mille.

And lest the denial of this denial be [cloaked]{g:palliare}, a denial which consists chiefly in deed,[^1] let us attend to how the higher [satraps]{g:satrapa}, by whom the church should be ruled, reckon it honourable, [being ignorant]{g:ignorare} of the law of Christ, to despise those who will live [piously]{g:pie}; and whereas they ought, from Ezekiel 34, to [expose]{g:detegere} their dangers, they strive in every way to trouble them,[^2] as Augustine shows in *On the Words of the Lord*, Sermon 89: "Whoever of you hears me," he says, "begin to live in Christ, and you will prove what I say; for the other perils of which the Apostle [speaks] can come to rest, but the perils from false brethren know not how to rest until the end of the world."[^3] And he understands by false brethren, in the Apostle's manner, generally any who are false, Christians in name. "Let us therefore redeem the time, inasmuch as the days are evil."[^4] "This is," he says, "to redeem the time: when someone brings a lawsuit against you, to lose your substance, that you may be free for God, not for lawsuits." Because, I say, the superiors of the church deny by their deeds this law, which Christ speaks in his vessel,[^5] therefore they at once persecute in a crowd the one who strives to make the people believe that they themselves are especially bound to the said law of the Apostle; and so they, and many through them, lose time for the sake of [193] most filthy lucre, which others call temporal advantage. Nor are the [froward]{g:discoli} to be heard, who imagine that every enduring thing is time, and so that the [clergy]{g:clerus}, buying [ecclesiastical benefices]{g:beneficium-ecclesiasticum} or secular lordships, buys time, which is needful to the clergy today, since the laity are exceedingly hostile to clerics. These are antichristian [ravings]{g:deliramenta}, and subversive of the Apostle's sense.[^6] For the Apostle understands time properly as the successive duration of the whole world; and although it is common to every man and every body, yet when a man advances in it according to the law of God, and is allowed on the last day for the spending of the time laid out, he profits himself to salvation; then, by reason of that proper and useful mode of having, he redeems time for himself.[^7] "Redeems", I say, because Christ bought time for the whole church, since otherwise he would have numbered the time of men purely naturally. But by Christ's merit the time has been made "acceptable", for in a short time a man, by divine acceptance, can merit blessedness.[^8] But since he who created you without you does not justify you without you,[^9] we must needs attend to the [doctrine]{g:doctrina} of the Apostle, Ephesians 5:15–16: "See," he says, "how you walk circumspectly, redeeming the time, because the days are evil."[^10] We must therefore, besides the general redemption of time through Christ, redeem time for ourselves; which, according to Augustine, we do when we forgo or change an affluence of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} and a voluptuous life, that we may live more acceptably to Christ and more usefully to ourselves; for then by a new mode of having we acquire time for ourselves. And according to Augustine the judgement of that [bishop]{g:episcopus}, nay rather the judgement of Christ in him, would suffice to put an end to every lawsuit.[^11] Therefore the leader [194] of the Christian army, namely the [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius-christi}, ought to attend the more to that judgement, since "the children of this world" are prudent and crafty, so as to have by foolish [trading]{g:commercium} prosperous days here as regards the world;[^12] but they are evil and foolish, since by such dealing one day is lost that is worth a thousand.[^13]

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Huius negacionis negacio*: construal doubtful. Taken as the denial of Christ just described (Augustine's *negamus*, 192.6–8), here said to lie chiefly in deed; "this denial" may be the genitive of definition, "the denial that this is".
[^2]: **S/C.** Ezekiel 34, against the shepherds who do not tend the flock. *Legem Christi ignorando* is taken with *contempnere*: they despise the pious in ignorance of Christ's law. *Pericula eorum*: the dangers that beset the pious.
[^3]: **S/R.** Augustine, *De verbis Domini* sermo 89 in Wyclif's numbering; Loserth: Opp. tom. V, 803/4; now Sermo 167.2–3 (PL 38, 910) (LEDGER P08h-1). The Apostle's perils: 2 Corinthians 11:26 ("in perils from false brethren"). A and B note: "the peril of false brethren".
[^4]: **S/C/R.** Ephesians 5:16, in Wyclif's (or Augustine's) form, with *quantum* for the Vulgate's *quoniam*; taken as "inasmuch as". The quotation bounds follow the italics only from *hoc est*. A and B note: "What it is to redeem the time."
[^5]: **S/T.** "His vessel": Paul, the "vessel of election" (Acts 9:15). The print has *Chrisus* (192.27, so the plate), a misprint for *Christus*.
[^6]: **T.** The apparatus at 193.11 records a manuscript reading *sensum legem* (OCR rough); Loserth's *sensus* is translated.
[^7]: **T/C.** *Allocatur*: Loserth's emendation; the codex reads *allocaciter* (apparatus 193.12). An accounting term: the man is "allowed" (credited) in his account on the last day for the time he has spent. *Sibi ipsi proficit* stands without a connective; the sentence is construed as two clauses.
[^8]: **S.** "Acceptable time": 2 Corinthians 6:2 ("now is the acceptable time").
[^9]: **S.** Augustine's saying, *qui creavit te sine te, non iustificat te sine te* (Sermo 169.13, PL 38, 923: *Qui ergo fecit te sine te, non te justificat sine te*; Wyclif has *creavit*; LEDGER P08h-2).
[^10]: **S.** Ephesians 5:15–16, shortened (DR "See therefore, brethren, how you walk circumspectly: not as unwise, But as wise: redeeming the time, because the days are evil").
[^11]: **C.** *Iudicium illius episcopi*: the bishop's arbitration of disputes, to which Augustine's sermon points the litigant; the bishop is Augustine himself, who in Sermo 167.4 speaks of the cases that come before him for judgment (*quando ad nos judicanda veniunt … quia episcopum audivit*; PL 38, 910–11) (LEDGER P08h-3).
[^12]: **S/C.** Luke 16:8 ("the children of this world are wiser … than the children of light"). *Ut ex stultis commerciis* is taken as the purpose of their craft.
[^13]: **S.** Psalm 83:11 ("better is one day in thy courts above thousands"). *Negociacio* "dealing" keeps it beside *negociatores* "dealers" (GLOSSARY).
