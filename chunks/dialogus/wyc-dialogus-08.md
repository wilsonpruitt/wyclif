# *Dialogus*, chapter 8: Falsehood's reply: poverty for a time

## Falsehood · 15.2–15.5

> [p. 15] MENDACIUM. Nimis patenter reseras conversacionem, quam increpas prelatorum; sed audi partem alteram in responsionibus et repplicacionibus, et tunc potes magis evidenter procedere.

FALSEHOOD. Too openly you lay bare the manner of life of the [prelates]{g:prelatus}, which you
rebuke; but hear the other side in answers and rejoinders, and then you can proceed more clearly.

## Falsehood · 15.6–15.18

> [p. 15] Concedo quidem tibi quod Christus vixit et docuit vitam pauperem sed noluit ipsam esse perpetuam, cum dicat in apostolo allegato quod Christus sic egenus factus est ut illis inopia vos divites essetis. Superbia autem et cupiditas sacerdotum legis veteris qui Christum occiderant requirebant Christum sic vivere et docere. Sed postquam repressa sunt ista peccata dignitas sacerdotum exigit quod clerus ad priorem gloriam revertatur; in cujus signum predia quibus dotatur ecclesia vocantur patrimonium crucifixi. Christus enim virtute passionis sue et meriti adquisivit sue ecclesie omnia ista dominia, et, si, justicia posset procedere, ampliora; et cum ista sentencia concordant concessiones dominorum temporalium et vite papales patule atque leges.

I grant you indeed that Christ lived and taught a poor life, but he did not will it to be
perpetual, since he says, in the apostle already cited, that Christ was made needy in such a way
"that through his poverty you might be rich".[^1] And the pride and greed of the
[priests]{g:sacerdos} of the old law, who had killed Christ, required Christ to live and teach so.
But after these sins were repressed, the dignity of priests demands that the [clergy]{g:clerus}
return to its former glory; in token of which the estates with which the [church]{g:ecclesia} is
endowed are called "the patrimony of the crucified".[^2] For Christ, by virtue of his passion and
merit, acquired for his church all these [lordships]{g:dominium}, and, if justice could take its
course, greater ones; and with this view agree the grants of the temporal [lords]{g:dominus} and
the manifest lives and laws of the popes.[^3]

## Truth · 15.19–16.31

> [p. 15] VERITAS. Video quod fraudulenter misceas quedam vera cum falsis. Concipis quidem veritatem fidei quod Christus tam in vita quam doctrina docuit pauperiem observari. Sed quod consequenter addis, hanc Christi sentenciam durare ad tempus modicum et postea clerum suum redire ad seculum amplius quam vixerunt legis veteris sacerdotes, infideliter garrulas et contrarie racioni. Si enim Christus tam diu et [p. 16] tam dure reprobavit hoc peccatum in sacerdotibus legis veteris, quomodo sacerdotem legis gracie nove movet redire ad dictam avariciam sicut canis redit ad vomitum? Jam enim plus ditantur et gloriantur in seculo clerici et precipui sacerdotes quam ditabantur vel gloriabantur in seculo scribe pharisei vel principes sacerdotum. Ideo, cum tanta sit racio perpetuacionis hujus legis Christi ut patet de statu innocencie et statu glorie, patet quod ista lex Christi debet precipue in novissimis temporibus observari; tunc enim diabolus plus temptat homines ad avariciam. Ideo est magis necessarium in clero, qui debet esse speculum populi, apponere medicinam. Que ergo racio, cum hodie tantum dominatur cupiditas quare Christi pauperies tantum deprimitur et antichristi rabies tantum extollitur? Numquid racio concordat quod hec lex Christi in racione exemplari eterna sit per trecentos annos tantummodo observata et lex antichristiana contraria in temporibus tam periculosis sit perpetuata. Idem enim est sic dicere et extollere diabolum supra Christum, ac si infidelis annueret quod lex Christi tam necessaria et salubris durabit ad horam sub colore divini beneplaciti, sed lex antichristi plena veneno atque mortifera duret perpetuo ut excitet homines ad peccandum. Sed quis Christianus talem blasfemam responsiunculam tolleraret? Ulterius novit catholicus quomodo sunt due diviciarum maneries, scilicet divicie temporales et divicie celestes et spirituales. Et prime divicie vix cum virtutibus moderate ducunt ad celestes divicias, que sunt vere, de quibus loquitur apostolus Christi supra. Ideo haberes conscienciam super ista loquela heretica, quam innuis super fide scripture apostoli. Oportet enim per penalitatem et pauperiem hic in via mereri celestes divicias et beatitudinem in patria.

TRUTH. I see that you craftily mix certain true things with false. You do indeed grasp the truth
of faith, that Christ, as much in life as in teaching, taught that poverty was to be observed. But
what you add next, that this judgement of Christ lasts for a short time, and that afterwards his
clergy returns to the world more fully than the priests of the old law lived [in it], you prattle
in unbelief and contrary to reason. For if Christ so long and [16] so harshly reproved this sin in
the priests of the old law, how does he move a priest of the new [law of grace]{g:lex-gracie} to
return to the said avarice, as a dog returns to its vomit?[^4] For [clerics]{g:clericus}, and
the leading priests, are now more enriched and glorified in the world than the scribes, Pharisees
or chief priests were enriched or glorified in the world. Therefore, since the reason for the
perpetuation of this [law of Christ]{g:lex-christi} is so great, as is clear from the state of
innocence and the state of glory, it is clear that this law of Christ ought to be observed
especially in the last times; for then the devil tempts men more to avarice. Therefore it is more
necessary to apply the medicine in the clergy, which ought to be the mirror of the people. What
reason is there, then, since greed so [lords it]{g:dominari} today, why Christ's poverty is so
much put down and Antichrist's rage so much exalted? Does reason agree that this law of Christ,
eternal in its exemplar reason, should have been observed for three hundred years only, and the
contrary antichristian law be perpetuated in times so perilous?[^5] For to say this is the same as
to exalt the devil above Christ, as if an unbeliever were to grant that the law of Christ, so
necessary and wholesome, will last for a time, under colour of the divine good pleasure, but that
the law of Antichrist, full of poison and deadly, should last for ever, to stir men up to
sin.[^6] But what Christian would tolerate such a blasphemous little answer? Further, the catholic
knows that there are two kinds of riches, namely temporal riches, and heavenly and spiritual
riches. And the first riches, even held with the virtues in moderation, scarcely lead to the
heavenly riches, which are the true ones, of which Christ's apostle speaks above.[^7] Therefore you
ought to be troubled in conscience over this heretical talk, which you hint at on the faith of the
apostle's scripture. For it is by hardship and poverty here on [the way]{g:via} that one must merit
heavenly riches, and blessedness in [the homeland]{g:patria}.

## Pollard's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 15.2: Mend. objects:
- 15.6: Christ preached poverty but not as a permanent law.
- 15.11: The need for it is passed, and the glory of the clergy should be restored.
- 15.14: Their domains are held in the name of Christ crucified and should be greater.
- 15.19: Ver. answers:
- 15.23: Can Christ who reproved avarice in priests of the old law permit it in those of the new?
- 16.3: Priests are more avaricious now than ever.
- 16.8: There is thus the greatest need that the law of poverty should be observed.
- 16.15: Is Christ's law to last three centuries, Antichrist's for ever?
- 16.17: To maintain this is to put the Devil above Christ.
- 16.24: There are two sorts of riches, earthly and heavenly, which sort ill together.
- 16.29: By contemning the earthly, men should win the heavenly.

## Notes

[^1]: **S/T.** 2 Cor. 8:9, cited in the previous chapter. The Vulgate reads *propter vos egenus
    factus est … ut illius inopia vos divites essetis* ("he became poor for your sakes: that
    through his poverty you might be rich"). Pollard prints *illis inopia*, which does not construe,
    and the apparatus records no variant; we translate *illius*, the Vulgate's word (our
    emendation). B, B¹, C read *nos … essemus*, "that we might be rich". *Cum dicat* has no
    expressed subject: Christ speaking in the apostle is the likelier reading, Paul himself the
    alternative.

[^2]: **R.** *Patrimonium crucifixi*: "the patrimony of the crucified", a current name for the goods
    of the church, on which the clergy were held to live as stewards of the poor (LEDGER D08-1).
    At 15.12 a later hand in the margin of B, and C, read *patrimonii* for (or beside) *priorem*:
    "to the glory of the patrimony".

[^3]: **C/T.** *Et, si, justicia posset procedere, ampliora*: "and greater ones, if justice could
    take its course", i.e. by right the church should have more. B¹, C read *ut sic* for *et si*,
    B, B¹, C *in sciencia* for *justicia*, and B, C *ampliori*; none gives a clearer sense, and the
    print is kept. *Vite papales patule atque leges*: we take *papales* with both nouns and
    *patule* ("open, manifest") with *vite*: "the manifest lives and laws of the popes".

[^4]: **C/S.** *Quomodo sacerdotem … movet*: we take Christ as subject: how can the Christ who
    condemned this avarice move a priest of the new law back to it? B, B¹, C read *sacerdotes …
    moveret*, with the same construal. The dog is proverbial: Prov. 26:11 (*sicut canis qui
    revertitur ad vomitum suum*) and 2 Pet. 2:22 (*canis reversus ad suum vomitum*); Wyclif's
    *sicut canis redit ad vomitum* follows neither exactly.

[^5]: **C/R.** *In racione exemplari eterna*: "eternal in its exemplar reason", eternal as the idea
    of it in the divine mind. The "three hundred years" run from Christ to the endowment of the
    church under Constantine and Pope Sylvester, which Wyclif elsewhere treats as the entry of
    the poison into the church (LEDGER D08-2).

[^6]: **T/S.** At 16.18 B, B¹, C read *fidelis*: "as if a believer were to grant". *Plena veneno
    atque mortifera* echoes Jas. 3:8, of the tongue: *plena veneno mortifero*, "full of deadly
    poison". *Ad horam*: "for a time", as in the Vulgate's *ad horam* (e.g. John 5:35).

[^7]: **C.** *Vix cum virtutibus moderate ducunt*: we take *moderate* as the adverb, "held in
    moderation" together with the virtues; the alternative joins it to *ducunt*: "scarcely lead,
    and then only moderately". *Supra*: 2 Cor. 8:9 again, where Falsehood read "that you might be
    rich" as licensing later endowment; Truth answers that the riches Paul promises are the
    heavenly ones. At 16.24 B, C read *sunt enim* for *scilicet*.
