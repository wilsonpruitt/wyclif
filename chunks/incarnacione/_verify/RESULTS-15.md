## For the coordinator to decide

1. **172.24 (I10d-5).** Augustine reads ***formam servi*** (*De Trin.* I.7.14), not *formam hominis*. No manuscript is reported with *servi*, since O only abbreviates *forma*. The likeliest course is to keep the print as Wyclif's own wording and let the note record Augustine's *servi*, which the replacement does. Augustine also has *utrumque … utrumque*, which matches B's *utrumque* (172.25) against the print's *utraque*.
2. **174.11 (I10d-7).** Campanus has no *singulariter*. Both the 1482 and the 1516 editions read ***similiter se habere***. *Singulariter* is probably a misreading of an abbreviated *similiter*, and O omits it. The English "stand as they do singly" in n12 should probably become "stand in the same way" (the aggregate stands to the aggregate as one to its counterpart). n12's sentence *O omits *singulariter*, and the word is doubtful* could then add "Campanus reads *similiter*". I have not changed this.
3. **174.8.** Campanus has *aliarum* where Wyclif has *aliquarum*, and he does not have *ut eque maiores aut eque minores*, which looks like Wyclif's own expansion. No change is needed.
4. **I10d-3.** n5 previously gave the locus as PL 158 c. V. Migne actually prints the sentence at the head of c. VI (278A). Schmitt ends c. 10 with it, so Wyclif's "6 at the end" holds on the Schmitt division. The replacement says this.
5. **I10d-1, I10d-8, I10f-4** stay untraced. Harris gives no loci for Augustine at 174 or 181. I10d-1 may be a conflation that went through the *Summa sententiarum*/*Contra Felicianum* tradition.
