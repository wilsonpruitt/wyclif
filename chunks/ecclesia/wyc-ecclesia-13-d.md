# *De ecclesia*, chapter 13, part 4: the measure of alms; the third objection, from security; the primitive church and the schisms

## ¶1 · 287.21–288.8

> [p. 287] Quoad quantitatem muneris, quoad libertatem donacionis et quoad diuturnitatem temporis patet quod viventes exproprietarie religione perpetua faciunt elemosinas largissimas et ordinatissimas, quia primo miserentur anime sue placentes Deo, et secundo facientes spiritualia opera misericordie toti mundo ex vi communionis sanctorum plus ad honorem Dei prosunt ecclesie quam secularis accumulans quotlibet temporalia domui private secundum religionem preter evangelicam adinvente. Unde absit fidelem credere quod proporcionaliter ad quantitatem molis vel valoris donati in foro seculi attenditur quantitas elemosine donatoris, cum infinitum maiorem elemosinam contingit quantumlibet minus valere in foro seculi. [p. 288] Tunc enim apostoli fecissent elemosinas nimis parcas, et mundiales extollerentur supra precipuos religiosos in merito, dareturque secularibus occasio ad inordinate querendum et ad inordinate retinendum bona fortune, ut in fine condendo grande testamentum dent grandem elemosinam abbathie vel clero, quia certum est quod proporcionaliter ad elemosinam vel opus misericordie viatoris est beatificandus in patria.

As to the quantity of the gift, as to the freedom of the giving, and as to the long duration of the time, it is clear that those who live [without property]{g:exproprietarie} in a perpetual religion give the most generous and best-ordered [alms]{g:elemosina}; for, first, they "have pity on their own soul, pleasing God", and, second, doing spiritual works of mercy to the whole world by force of the communion of saints, they profit the [church]{g:ecclesia} more, to the honour of God, than a [secular]{g:secularis} who heaps up any amount of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} for a private house devised according to a religion other than the gospel's.[^1] Hence God forbid that a believer should believe that the quantity of the giver's alms is reckoned in proportion to the quantity of the bulk or the value of what is given in the world's market, since it can be that an infinitely greater alms is worth however much less in the world's market.[^2] [288] For then the apostles would have made too sparing alms, and worldly men would be exalted above the leading [religious]{g:religiosi} in merit, and occasion would be given to seculars to seek and to keep the goods of fortune inordinately, so that at the end, by making a great testament, they might give a great alms to an abbey or to the [clergy]{g:clerus}; for it is certain that a [wayfarer]{g:viator} is to be beatified in the [homeland]{g:patria} in proportion to his alms or work of mercy.[^3]

## ¶2 · 288.9–289.15

> [p. 288] Unde logici cognoscunt quomodo data elemosina est alia maior; que alia est priori quantumlibet maior elemosina. Unde contingit quod potus aque frigide sit maior elemosina danti ex simpliciori oculo et maiori fervore caritatis, quam fuit dotacio qua Constantinus dotavit ecclesiam, vel elemosina qua rex terrenus fundavit grandissimam abbathiam. Nam nisi spirituale opus misericordie prevaleat corporali, stante isto cursu ecclesie, domini seculares forent nimis exaltati in celo et viri apostolici nimis mediocriter premiati. Et patet ad questionem qua queritur, cui seculares darent elemosinas nisi clero; nam illis tenentur dare prudenter de quanto sustinerent eorum privilegia data a Christo, et continuarent eorum disposicionem ad rependendum elemosinas spirituales. Alias autem elemosinas darent corporaliter magis egentibus. Unde si ex ceca pietate et falsa misericordia nimis onerant clerum cum possessione civili, contingit eos ex hinc incidere in excommunicacionem et heresim racione ablacionis primevi privilegii concessi a Christo suis discipulis, ut meminit Canon XXIIª d. Omnes. Et ita concedo quod dotacio ecclesie fuit altrinsecus meritoria, sed dotacio ac status in quo Christus ipsam [p. 289] instituit fuit perfeccior. Unde verisimile est quod multi ex defectu serpentine prudencie in ista elemosina ecclesie altrinsecus sunt dampnati; diuturnior autem elemosina est breviori prestancior, sed nemo dicit quod diuturnitas elemosine penes formam diuturnitatis expresse in cartis regulariter attendatur, cum stat elemosinam horalem de correpcione fraterna manere perpetuo in fructu, ubi elemosina cartalis sub nomine perpetuitatis expressa extinguitur infra annum tam in substancia elemosine quam in fructu. Sic ergo dans prudenter, ferventer et paulative elemosinas continue plus crescit et superaddit elemosinas quam ceteris paribus faceret sub signo elemosine perpetue dando simul, quia fervor caritatis et vigil custodia rectificande elemosine forent meritorius conservata.

Hence logicians know how, any alms being given, there is another greater; and that other is an alms greater than the former by any amount whatever. Hence it can be that a drink of cold water is a greater alms, for one who gives it with a simpler eye and with greater fervour of [charity]{g:caritas}, than was the [endowment]{g:dotacio} with which Constantine endowed the church, or the alms with which an earthly king founded a very great abbey.[^4] For unless the spiritual work of mercy outweighed the bodily, as this course of the church stands, secular lords would be too much exalted in heaven and apostolic men too moderately rewarded. And [the answer] is clear to the question that asks to whom seculars should give alms, if not to the clergy: for they are bound to give to them prudently as much as would sustain their [privileges]{g:privilegium} given by Christ and continue their disposition to repay spiritual alms. And other alms they should give in bodily form to those more needy.[^5] Hence if, out of blind [piety]{g:pietas} and false mercy, they overload the clergy with civil possession, it happens that they thereby fall into excommunication and heresy, by reason of the taking away of the primeval privilege granted by Christ to his disciples, as the canon *Omnes* in distinction 22 recalls.[^6] And so I grant that the endowment of the church was meritorious on either side, but the endowment and state in which Christ [289] instituted it was more perfect. Hence it is likely that many, from lack of the serpent's prudence, have been damned on either side in that alms of the church; and a longer-lasting alms is more excellent than a briefer one, but no one says that the long duration of an alms is to be reckoned, as a rule, by the form of duration expressed in charters, since it can be that an hour's alms of fraternal correction remains perpetually in its fruit, where a charter alms expressed under the name of perpetuity is extinguished within a year, both in the substance of the alms and in its fruit.[^7] So, then, one who gives alms prudently, fervently and little by little grows continually more, and adds on alms more, than, other things being equal, he would by giving all at once under the sign of perpetual alms; because the fervour of charity and the watchful keeping of the alms that is to be set right would be meritoriously preserved.[^8]

## ¶3 · 289.16–289.22

> [p. 289] Tercio principaliter arguitur per hoc quod elemosina debet sic dari, ut generi secularium elemosinanti accresceret maior utilitas et generi cleri elemosinandi accresceret plus excitativa securitas ad vigilanter perficiendum officium clericale; sed quis dubitat quin hoc contingeret sub forma dotacionis perpetue? Ergo illa est dotacione temporali prestancior.

Third, principally, [it is argued by appeal to]{g:arguitur per} this, that alms ought to be given in such a way that a greater utility would accrue to the line of seculars giving the alms, and a security more stirring to the vigilant performance of the clerical office would accrue to the line of clergy receiving the alms; now who doubts that this would come about under the form of perpetual endowment? Therefore that is more excellent than temporary endowment.[^9]

## ¶4 · 289.23–290.10

> [p. 289] Hic dicitur quod verum assumitur, unde pro minori quilibet christianus debet credere quod in temporalibus dotacio temporalis excluso signo perpetuitatis humane elemosine foret undiquaque perfeccior: et patet defectus consequencie. Unde pro declaracione prime partis maioris assumpte patet eius veritas ex dictis, cum talis elemosinacio foret factis Christi et apostolorum conformior, et per consequens elemosinanti utilior. Unde Act. III° legitur quomodo possessores agrorum vendebant et ponebant ante pedes apostolorum, non ut hereditarie vel perpetue possiderent, sed ut divideretur singulis prout cuique opus [p. 290] erat. Nec solum servata est illa forma elemosinandi in Judea, ubi Deus scivit apostolos non perpetuo permansuros, sed in Asia, in India, in Ethiopia et ubicunque terrarum christiani conversi sunt et presbiteri constituti; sed indubie illi ex maiori fervore caritatis, ex maiori prudencia ac simplicitate quam nostri succedentes qui sub forma dotacionis perpetue dabant sua et magis quieta, ac in pace Christi plus firmata est ecclesia illa primitiva conversa quam fuit nostra ecclesia cesarea sic dotata.

To this the answer is that what is assumed [in the major] is true; hence, as to the minor, every Christian ought to believe that in temporal goods a temporary endowment, with the sign of the perpetuity of human alms excluded, would be in every respect more perfect: and the defect of the [inference]{g:consequencia} is clear.[^10] Hence, for the declaration of the first part of the major [premiss] assumed, its truth is clear from what has been said, since such almsgiving would be more conformed to the deeds of Christ and of the apostles, and consequently more useful to the [almsgiver]{g:elemosinans}. Hence in Acts 4:34–35 we read how the owners of lands sold and laid [the price] before the feet of the apostles, not that they should possess hereditarily or perpetually, but that distribution should be made to every one according as he had [290] need.[^11] Nor was that form of giving alms kept only in Judaea, where God knew that the apostles would not remain forever, but in Asia, in India, in Ethiopia, and wherever in the world Christians were converted and [presbyters]{g:presbiter} appointed; but without doubt those [gave theirs] with greater fervour of charity, with greater prudence and simplicity, than our successors, who gave theirs under the form of perpetual endowment; and that primitive converted church was quieter and more firmly established in the peace of Christ than was our caesarean church so endowed.[^12]

## ¶5 · 290.11–290.19

> [p. 290] Unde illud magnum scisma factum per Sergium monachum sicut et illud scisma quod hodie pullulat per duos pseudomonachos cum aliis particularibus divisionibus in ecclesia causata sunt ex insaciabili affeccione cleri ad temporalia, quam nisi occasione dotacionis cesaree verisimiliter non haberent. Si enim non esset ista dotacio, non sic contenderent pro primatu, cum circa preeminenciam servilis humilitatis non fit contencio.

Hence that great schism made by Sergius the monk, as also that schism which is sprouting today through two false monks, together with other particular divisions in the church, were caused by the clergy's insatiable affection for temporal goods, which very likely they would not have except on the occasion of the caesarean endowment. For if that endowment did not exist, they would not so contend for the primacy, since there is no contention about pre-eminence in servile humility.[^13]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 287.21: Those who live without property give alms best.
- 287.29: The worth of alms does not depend on their amount.
- 288.4: or the miser would be justified by a large bequest.
- 288.20: b) We ought to give to the clergy what enables them to minister spiritually;
- 288.27: all beyond should be given to the poor.
- 289.16: 3. Objection: Perpetual alms profit most, because they make the clergy secure to perform their duty.
- 289.23: Answer: This is a mere assumption, temporary alms would enable them much better to do their duty.
- 290.1: In the primitive Church there were no foundations, but immediate distribution.

## Notes

[^1]: **C/S.** Side-note 287.21. The answer to the second objection continues, taking its three heads (quantity, freedom, duration: 285.20–21) in turn. *Religione perpetua*: in a perpetual, vowed religious life. *Miserentur anime sue placentes Deo*: Ecclesiasticus 30:24 ("Have pity on thy own soul, pleasing God", DR). *Domui private … adinvente*: a house of a private religion, devised by a rule other than the gospel (cf. GLOSSARY §B, *religio privata*).

[^2]: **C.** Side-note 287.29. *In foro seculi*: in the world's market, by the world's reckoning of value. *Molis vel valoris*: of bulk or of value. *Contingit*: it is possible.

[^3]: **C/T.** Side-note 288.4; A in the margin *Conclusio* (288.7). *Mundiales*: worldly men. *Precipuos religiosos*: the leading religious, those of the most perfect life. *Condendo grande testamentum*: by a large bequest at death.

[^4]: **C/T/S.** A in the margin *Nota*. *Data elemosina est alia maior*: for any alms given there is a greater, the logicians' quantity with no maximum; for *maior* in the second clause B reads *purior quantumlibet* (288.10), "purer by any amount", and the construal of the second clause is ours. *Potus aque frigide*: cf. Matthew 10:42. *Simpliciori oculo*: cf. Matthew 6:22.

[^5]: **T/C.** B reads *prevalet* (288.16). Side-notes 288.20, 288.27; A in the margin *Conclusio* and B in the margin *Ecclesie dotacio fuit meritoria* (288.20). The question is the objection's, 285.19–20. *Rependendum*: to repay; the clergy return spiritual alms for bodily ones.

[^6]: **S.** The canon is Gratian, D.22 c.1 (*Omnes*), per Loserth (*Decr. I pars, d. XXII, c. I*; the modern form is ours). Its § 1 makes a heretic of whoever *Romanae ecclesiae priuilegium ab ipso summo omnium ecclesiarum capite traditum auferre conatur* (Friedberg I, 73–74): the privilege is the Roman church's, given by Christ; Wyclif applies it to Christ's privilege of poverty (LEDGER E13d-1).

[^7]: **C/S.** *Altrinsecus*: on either side, for givers and receivers (as 274.30). *Serpentine prudencie*: Matthew 10:16 ("wise as serpents", DR). *Cum stat*: since it is consistent that, it can be that. *Horalem*: of an hour. *Elemosina cartalis*: alms granted by charter.

[^8]: **C.** *Paulative*: little by little. *Simul*: all at once. *Rectificande elemosine*: the alms that is to be set right as occasion shows. *Meritorius* stands where an adverb is needed; we translate "meritoriously".

[^9]: **C.** Side-note 289.16; A in the margin *Contra. Tercia obieccio*. *Generi secularium elemosinanti … generi cleri elemosinandi*: the line (lineage, succession) of lay givers and of clerical receivers. *Excitativa securitas*: a security that stirs them to their duty.

[^10]: **C/T.** Side-note 289.23; A in the margin *Responsio*. *Verum assumitur*: we take it of the major, which Wyclif grants (its first part is declared next); the minor, that perpetual endowment best serves both, is denied. Loserth's side-note reads the phrase as "a mere assumption". *Defectus consequencie*: the inference fails.

[^11]: **S/T.** The passage is Acts 4:34–35 (cf. Acts 2:45), paraphrased; the print's *Act. III°* is either Wyclif's slip or the text's. The last clause follows the Douay–Rheims of 4:35. B reads *quomodo possessiones* (289.31). Side-note 290.1.

[^12]: **C.** *Sed indubie illi …*: the clause has no verb of its own; we supply it from *dabant sua* in the relative clause. *Nostri succedentes*: those who came after among us, the later givers. *Ecclesia cesarea*: the caesarean church, the church as endowed by Caesar (cf. GLOSSARY §B, *clerus cesareus*).

[^13]: **R.** A in the margin *Nota* (290.11). Sergius the monk: the renegade monk who instructed Muhammad (n. 15-b n5); Wyclif lays the Muslim schism to the endowed church's avarice also in *Sermones* I, 178 (*maior pars ecclesie per Sergium monachum venenatur*). Loserth's Pope Sergius III (897–904; Gregorovius III, 242) does not fit *monachum*, and he later grouped this page with 333 and 517 (*De pot. pape* 374 foot) (LEDGER E13d-2). *Hodie*: Loserth dates it 1378 and takes the *duos pseudomonachos*, "two false monks", to be Urban VI and Clement VII (LEDGER E13d-3). *Dotacionis cesaree*: Constantine's endowment.
