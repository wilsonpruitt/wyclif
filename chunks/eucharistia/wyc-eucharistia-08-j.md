# *De eucharistia*, chapter 8, part 10: God above time, multiplication, and how the body of Christ is in the host

## ¶1 · 267.23–268.9

> [p. 267] Ulterius notandum quod non esset fuisse vel fore nisi racione temporis et per consequens subducto omni tempore Deus non fuit vel erit sed est absolute necessario. Et sic in eternitate non est formaliter hec differencia, et per consequens cum Deus et sua eternitas, angelus et sua evitas fuerunt et erunt, oportet quod capiant illas denominaciones eis accidentales a tempore et sic habent quando; quod non presupponit in eis naturalem periodum sicut creature naturaliter [p. 268] corruptibiles, et ita Deus equivoce fuit et erit in comparacione ad naturas corruptibiles, sicut alia respectiva accidencia equivoce sibi insunt. Idem ergo tempus in numero mensurat eternitatem Dei, evitatem angeli et sic de aliis supra tempus quoad denominacionem respectivam qua tempore mensurantur. Et ita videtur quod non propterea tempus aliud sed alia quandalitas sine mutacione essencie est ponenda. Ista autem hic sufficiunt.

It is further to be noted that there would be no having-been or going-to-be except by reason of time;[^1] and consequently, all time being taken away, God was not, nor will be, but is, absolutely, necessarily. And so in eternity this [difference]{g:differencia} is not formally; and consequently, since God and his eternity, an angel and its [aeveity]{g:evitas}, were and will be, they must take those [namings]{g:denominacio}, accidental to them, from time, and so they have a when;[^2] which does not presuppose in them a natural period, as [it does in] [creatures]{g:creatura} naturally [268] corruptible; and so God was and will be [equivocally]{g:equivoce} in comparison with corruptible natures, as other [relational]{g:respectivus} [accidents]{g:accidens} are in him equivocally. The same time in number, then, measures the eternity of God, the aeveity of an angel, and so of the other things above time, as to the [relational naming]{g:denominacio-respectiva} by which they are measured by time. And so it seems that not, on this account, another time, but another [whenness]{g:quandalitas},[^3] without change of essence, is to be posited. And these things suffice here.

## ¶2 · 268.10–271.24

> [p. 268] Sed redeundo post digressionem ad difficultatem tactam de multiplicacione videtur quod secunda sentencia sit tenenda; cum enim inpossibile sit idem corpus multiplicari situaliter vel dimensionaliter per diversa loca et tam fides de eukaristia quam eciam communis philosophia docet esse multiplicacionem substancie, patet veritas secunde sentencie. Vocatur autem multiplicacio, quando eadem res creata est simul in tempore per multa loca secundum se totam, ut anima, universalia et instancia; tempus autem non sic multiplicatur, cum omni loco pro quocunque instanti partes sue defuerint ut partes preterite et future; potest tamen extense loquendo concedi multiplicari, quia secundum multas partes suas est per diversa loca et sic commune lumen, communis species, vel similitudo rei multiplicatur, quia per unum magnum situm est et per quamlibet eius partem secundum aliquod eius suppositum, sicut universale et ubicunque habuerit aliquod singulare. Si autem quodlibet tale singulare habuerit esse per quamlibet partem dati situs, tunc et illud singulare multiplicatur per situm eundem, sicut ponitur de speciebus visibilis. [p. 269] Deus autem non potest multiplicari, quia necessario est secundum se totum ubique; nec potest aliquo loco vel tempore diffinire vel secundum motum sui alicubi esse; ideo ex parte sui non contingit multiplicacio vel secundum tempus diffinicio. Nam secundum sanctos non est in eo formaliter multitudo, alia autem licet sint ubique scilicet mundus, tempus et universalia quorum individua sunt, ubique tamen dispariter a Deo; nam mundus non secundum se totum est ubique, quia non quelibet eius pars quantitativa ubique distenditur, nec quelibet pars subiectiva generis corporis vel universalis huius est ubique, nec tempus habet omnes partes suas simul pro eodem instanti, sed quadam dependencia ex motu aliter est in celo et aliter in terra. Deus autem secundum immensitatem suam primo et per se replet omnem locum et secundum eternitatem suam primo et per se est per omne tempus; non potest contingere variacio ex parte sui in essendo per locum vel tempus sed oportet quod sit variacio in substancia creata; cuius variacio vocatur accidens, ut Deus per se efficienter replet omnem locum et corpus, vel eius principium per se formaliter replet locum, sic quod punctale materialis essencie cum non possit maiorari vel minui est per se punctale, cuius punctalitas est punctus; et sic de quantitate corporis que consequitur ad suum primum subiectum, sicut risibilitas ad hominem et secundum racionem qua Deus immobilis ponit substanciam materialem secundum racionem immobilem, cuius situacio vel locacio est situs vel locus quem Aristoteles vocat inmobile primum. Deus enim non posset movere illam racionem secundum primum motum qui est motus localis. Et hec est prima racio immobilitatis sensibilis qua ascendimus ad cognoscendum inmobilitatem [p. 270] ultimam Dei nostri; et cum videmus rem moveri de loco ad locum ex ordinacione Dei, vocamus illam vicissitudinem motus tempus vel duracionem, et individuamus locum et tempus a genere substancie vel motus cum aliis circumstanciis individuantibus quas Deus ad hoc ordinat. Et sic videmus dominum nostrum in omni vicissitudine creature. Et sicut Deus est primo magnus et immensus per se efficaciter et finaliter replens locum, ita est primo per se durans et eternus, per se finaliter mensurans omnium temporalium vices, et sic inmensitas et eternitas Dei non sunt accidencia vel divisibiles (ut hic supponitur); et sicut cum Deus non potest esse alius Deus, ita per eundem situm punctalem non potest esse simul alius punctus vel materialis essencia, cum tota capacitas sua repletur, quia non est aliud nisi illam materialem essenciam situari vel saltem aliam tantam aut tantam speciem quantitatis. Cum autem forma substancialis aut accidentalis non sit nisi talem essenciam informari, patet quod non exinde multiplicatur situs replecio. Loquor autem de situ vel loco (ut supra), ut dicit materialem essenciam situari. Et patet logicis quod aliud est Petrum esse in loco sibi adequato et aliud est ipsum esse per locum sibi adequatum. Cum enim sint mille loca inter ipsum et celum sibi equalia, quia ut sunt laciora sunt magis tenua, et omnia ipsum undique circumstancia, patet quod est in totidem locis sibi equalibus et per consequens adeo sibi adequatis, et tamen per nullum illorum locorum est, cum per nullam partem talis loci sit aliqua pars Petri cum a quolibet tali distat, ideo longe aliud est Petrum esse in multis locis sibi adequatis et aliud est ipsum [p. 271] esse per multa loca sibi adequata. Est enim per locum, quando ad aliquam partem illius loci signati est aliqua pars sui, sicut omnis materialis substancia per locum diffunditur, qualiter repugnat idem corpus in numero per distancia loca simul distendi; potest tamen distendi in uno loco et habere aliud esse spirituale in alio tamquam in signo aut virtute, sicut dictum est de rege. Et sic patet de corpore Christi, quod est dimensionaliter in celo et virtualiter in hostia ut in signo. Sed in isto est magna dissensio de modo essendi corporis Christi in hostia: Quidam enim dicunt quod corpus Christi est tantum secundum essenciam suam ibi presens; quod tantum diffinitur loco hostie quod posset esse illo modo ibidem et alibi nusquam esse, sed alii admittunt hoc, quia foret Deo longe facilius quam servare accidens sine subiecto, ideo super illos iacet difficultas, quomodo multiplicatur cum corpore Christi quantitas et alia accidencia committantur. Super nos autem non iacet difficultas ista: Esto quod corpus Christi sit in sacramento panis, aut virtus et non corpus Christi quod in celo extenditur, sic quod corpus Christi non est non corpus, sed est non corpus Christi ibi; et est illud quod ibi extenditur sed non ibi extenditur.

But, returning after the digression to the difficulty touched on concerning [multiplication]{g:multiplicacio-corporis}, it seems that the second [teaching]{g:sentencia}[^4] is to be held. For since it is impossible for the same body to be multiplied [situally]{g:situaliter} or [dimensionally]{g:dimensionaliter} through diverse places, and both the faith about [the Eucharist]{g:eukaristia} and also common philosophy teach that there is a multiplication of [substance]{g:substancia},[^5] the truth of the second teaching is clear. And it is called multiplication when the same created thing is at once in time through many places as a whole, as the soul, [universals]{g:universale} and instants; but time is not so multiplied, since for any instant whatever its parts are lacking to every place, as the past and future parts. Yet, [speaking in an extended sense]{g:extense-loquendo}, it can be granted to be multiplied, because according to its many parts it is through diverse places; and so a common light, a common species,[^6] or likeness of a thing is multiplied, because it is through one great site and through every part of it according to some [supposit]{g:suppositum} of it, as a universal is also wherever it has some singular. And if each such singular has being through every part of a given site, then that singular too is multiplied through the same site, as is posited of the species of a visible thing. [269] But God cannot be multiplied, because he is necessarily everywhere as a whole; nor can he be defined[^7] by any place or time, or be somewhere according to his own motion; so on his side there occurs no multiplication, nor definition according to time. For according to [the saints]{g:sancti} there is not formally [multitude]{g:multitudo} in him; and other things, although they are everywhere (namely the world, time, and the universals whose individuals are [everywhere]), are yet everywhere otherwise than God. For the world is not everywhere as a whole, since not every [quantitative part]{g:pars-quantitativa} of it is spread out everywhere; nor is every [subjective part]{g:partes-subiective} of the genus body, or of such a universal, everywhere; nor does time have all its parts at once for the same instant, but by a certain dependence on motion it is one way in heaven and another way on earth. But God, according to his immensity, first and [of himself]{g:per-se}[^8] fills every place, and according to his eternity is first and of himself through all time. No variation can occur on his side in being through place or time, but the variation must be in the created substance, whose variation is called an accident: as God of himself efficiently fills every place and body, or its principle of itself formally fills the place, so that the [punctual]{g:punctale} of a material essence, since it cannot be made greater or less, is of itself punctual, and its [punctuality]{g:punctalitas} is the point; and so of the [quantity]{g:quantitas} of a body, which follows on its first [subject]{g:subiectum} as [risibility]{g:risibilitas} on man; and according to the aspect by which God, [immovable]{g:immobilis}, posits material substance according to an immovable aspect, whose situation or location is the site or place which Aristotle calls the first immovable.[^9] For God could not move that aspect with the first motion, which is local motion. And this is the first ground of perceptible immovability, by which we ascend to knowing the ultimate immovability [270] of our God. And when we see a thing moved from place to place by God's [ordinance]{g:ordinacio}, we call that [alternation]{g:vicissitudo} of motion time or duration, and we individuate place and time from the genus of substance or of motion, with the other individuating circumstances which God ordains for this. "And so we see our Lord in every alternation of the creature."[^10] And as God is first great and immense, of himself efficaciously and finally filling place, so he is first of himself lasting and eternal, of himself finally measuring the turns of all temporal things; and so the immensity and eternity of God are not accidents, nor divisible (as is supposed here). And as God cannot be another God, so through the same punctual site there cannot be at once another point or material essence, since its whole capacity is filled; for [its being filled] is nothing other than for that material essence to be sited, or at least another as great, or so great a species of quantity.[^11] And since a [substantial form]{g:forma-substancialis} or accidental form is nothing but such an essence's being [informed]{g:informare}, it is clear that the filling of the site is not multiplied thereby. And I speak[^12] of site or place (as above) as it [signifies]{g:dicere} a material essence's being sited. And it is clear to logicians that it is one thing for Peter to be in a place adequate to him and another for him to be through a place adequate to him. For since there are a thousand places between him and the heaven equal to him (since, as they are wider, they are thinner), and all surrounding him on every side, it is clear that he is in as many places equal to him, and consequently so far adequate to him; and yet he is through none of those places, since through no part of such a place is there any part of Peter, since he is distant from every such [place]. So it is a far different thing for Peter to be in many places adequate to him and for him [271] to be through many places adequate to him. For a thing is through a place when at some part of that designated place there is some part of it, as every material substance is spread out through place; in which way it [is incompatible]{g:repugnare} for the same body in number to be extended at once through distant places. Yet it can be extended in one place and have another, spiritual, being in another, as in a sign or in power, as was said of the king.[^13] And so it is clear of [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} that it is [dimensionally]{g:dimensionaliter} in heaven and [virtually]{g:virtualiter} in the [host]{g:hostia} as in a sign. But on this there is great disagreement about the mode of being of the body of Christ in the host. For some say that the body of Christ is present there only according to its essence, which is so far defined by the place of the host that it could be there in that mode and be nowhere else; but others admit this, because it would be far easier for God than to preserve an [accident without a subject]{g:accidens-sine-subiecto};[^14] so on them lies the difficulty how quantity is multiplied with the body of Christ, and the other accidents accompany it.[^15] But this difficulty does not lie on us. Grant that the body of Christ is in the sacrament of bread, or [its] power, and not the body of Christ which is extended in heaven; so that the body of Christ is not not-a-body, but is not the body of Christ there; and it is that which is extended there, but it is not extended there.[^16]

## ¶3 · 271.25–272.11

> [p. 271] Et iste est tercius punctus in quo vario a modernis, verumptamen (ut sepe protestatus sum) volo parate obedire docto quod quantum ad veritatem illius materie fuero sive superfluus, sive parcus. Miratur autem mundus quomodo post dotacionem ecclesie vel variaretur sacramentum vel innotesceret nostris plus a Christo extraneis vel racio vel fieret revelacio plus quam Paulo et sanctis prioribus. Sic enim tanquam domini leges Christi possent extraneare totam fidem [p. 272] catholicam. Nec dubium quin veritas catholica fuit per auctores scripture sufficienter edocta, licet officium ecclesie peregrinantis cui Deus dedit scienciam sit detegere fidem in scriptura implicita, quando pullulant errores circa eius sensum. Et nunquam est per illos aliquod novum fidei catholice fabricandum; et hinc Deus semper ordinat aliquos qui intendant loyce et sensui scripture, cum tamen debet credi cuicunque pontifici in istis, de quanto validius adduxerit testimonium scripturarum; et notum est eos post dotacionem ecclesie in isto sicut in moribus defecisse.

And this is the third point in which I differ from [the moderns]{g:moderni};[^17] nevertheless (as I have often [solemnly declared]{g:protestari}) I am willing readily to obey a learned man [who shows] that, as regards the truth of this matter, I have been either excessive or sparing.[^18] And the world wonders how, after the [endowment]{g:dotacio} of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, either the sacrament should be varied, or either a reason should become known or a revelation be made to our men, more estranged from Christ, more than to Paul and the earlier saints. For so, as lords, they could [estrange]{g:extraneare} the laws of Christ, the whole catholic [272] faith.[^19] Nor is there doubt that the catholic truth was sufficiently taught by the authors of scripture, although it is the office of the church [on pilgrimage]{g:peregrinari}, to which God has given knowledge, to [expose]{g:detegere} the faith implicit in scripture[^20] when errors sprout about its [meaning]{g:sensus}. And never is anything new of the catholic faith to be fabricated by them; and hence God always ordains some who attend to the logic and the meaning of scripture, since yet any [pontiff]{g:pontifex} whatever is to be believed in these things in the measure that he has adduced the testimony of the scriptures more strongly; and it is known that they, after the endowment of the church, have failed in this as in morals.

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 267.23: Past and future are founded in time. God neither was nor will be, but is eternally.
- 268.10: Multiplication is a thing's being as a whole in many places at once.
- 269.1: It cannot be predicated of God, who is necessarily every where.
- 271.8: How Christ is present in the host.

## Notes

[^1]: **T.** 267.23: AB lack *non*; ABC read *esse* for *esset*; AB *fuisse* (C corrected). The print is translated.

[^2]: **C.** *Quando*, the category "when", is the denomination God and the angels take from time: they "were" and "will be" only by relation to it, not by any change in themselves.

[^3]: **G.** *Quandalitas*, "whenness": being in a when (cf. 267.15–16, where ABC read the same word). Proposed for the glossary.

[^4]: **C.** *Sentencia* (CONTEXTUAL): "teaching", the second of the positions on the multiplication of a body set out earlier in this chapter.

[^5]: **T/R.** 268.16: A has *substancie* struck out in the text and *sentencie* in the margin ("teach that there is a multiplication of the teaching"); the print is translated. 268.13: C *situaliter* in marg. alia manu, D *signanter*.

[^6]: **C.** *Species* here is the likeness a thing sends through the medium (the species of light and of the visible), not the sacramental species and not the logical species. *Speciebus visibilis* at the end of the sentence: "of the visible [thing]", the genitive singular as printed.

[^7]: **T/C.** 269.3: *diffinire* is active in the print; it is read in the passive sense (*diffiniri*, "be defined", of definitive presence in a place), which the next clause's *diffinicio* supports. The print may have lost a letter.

[^8]: **C.** *Per se* (CONTEXTUAL) is "of himself", God's filling place by his own immensity, not the spatial "apart".

[^9]: **S.** Aristotle, *Physics* IV.4 (212a20–21): place as "the first immovable limit of the container" (LEDGER K08j-1).

[^10]: **S.** Loserth sets the sentence in italic, as a quotation; his page foot gives no source, and none was found in the Patrologia Latina or on the web (LEDGER K08j-2).

[^11]: **T/C.** 270.17: *aliam tantam aut tantam speciem quantitatis*; E reads *vel tantam*. The repeated *tantam* may be a doubling; read as "another [essence] as great, or so great a species of quantity". *Quia non est aliud nisi …*: the subject, the site's being filled, is supplied from *repletur*.

[^12]: **T.** 270.21: ABCD *Loquitur* ("he speaks"); Loserth prints E's *Loquor*, which is translated. 270.22: ABCE *loycis*.

[^13]: **C.** The king's spiritual being in a place where his body is not, as in his sign or his power, was set out in ch. 4 (84.7–19), where the king has a threefold being in his realm, *situale, intencionale et potenciale* (LEDGER K08j-3).

[^14]: **C.** Wyclif names neither party. *Alii admittunt hoc* is read as granting the first party's mode (present by essence, defined to the host's place), since it would be easier for God than an accident without a subject; *illos* are these second, who then owe an account of the accidents.

[^15]: **T.** 271.19: *committantur* is taken for *comitantur*, "accompany"; C reads *committanter*.

[^16]: **C.** 271.19–22: Wyclif's own position, in compressed predication. *Non est non corpus, sed est non corpus Christi ibi*: the negations are kept as he orders them; what is extended there (the bread) "is" the body sacramentally, while the body is not extended there. ABC read *aut non* (for *aut virtus*?), E lacks *autem*, DE lack *Esto* and read *quia* and *est*; the clause is hard to construe and the print is translated as it stands.

[^17]: **C.** "The third point" in which Wyclif differs from the moderns in this chapter; the first two are not restated here.

[^18]: **C.** The submission formula (*protestacio*). *Docto* is read as "a learned man" with the verb of showing understood ("[who shows]"); it could also be "to one taught [by God]". 271.27–28: E *materie sim*.

[^19]: **C.** *Leges Christi* and *totam fidem catholicam* are taken as two objects in apposition; *domini* may also be read with *leges* ("as lords of the law of Christ"). 271.32: E *tamquam diu*. The subject is *nostri*, "our men", the moderns.

[^20]: **C.** *In scriptura implicita* agrees in form with *scriptura*; it is read as "the faith implicit in scripture", the faith wrapped in scripture's text. 272.2: ABC *satis edocta*; 272.4: E *protegere* for *detegere*; 272.9–10: E *in testimonium*.
