# *Dialogus*, chapter 30: perpetual alms, and the clergy as almoners

## Truth · 63.13–66.17

> [p. 63] VERITAS. Item suppono quod clerus propter cupidinem horum temporalium velit dotacionem dimittere et sub forma elemosine perpetue bona ista accipere et sic redit idem clericis quo ad fructus. Istud autem est multis inevidens cum tunc caderet a titulo quem silvester a cesare acceptavit, nec veritas perpetue elemosine in anglia salvaretur cum defendere non posset quin propter multa et magna peccata que perpetraverat sepius cadebat a titulo elemosine, cum non sit elemosina taliter apostatas enutrire. Immo non docetur vel evidenter ostenditur quod talis donacio unquam fuit elemosina voluntati divine conformata, quomodo ergo fingerent quod sit pura et perpetua elemosina? Numquid credimus quod sit in potestate istorum hominum perpetuare [p. 64] talem elemosinam cleri sui ac si dicerent contra Christum, ego volo quod sit elemosina quomodocunque vixerint et fuerint contrarie legi tue, vel ego volo quod vivat ad edificacionem ecclesie Christi utiliter, quia indubie sic vult deus, ergo mea volicio est impleta. Stephanus quidem Act. 7° negavit hanc consequenciam cum dixit Judeos specialiter religiosos atque episcopos sepe voluntati divine restitisse, quis ergo color quod sic ditantes ecclesiam benefecerant cum habuerunt huiusmodi voluntatem, deus enim non obstante voluntate huiusmodi inculpabili auffert collata istis clericis cum sic delinquunt, quare ergo non debent sic domini se dei bene placito conformantes, cum in hoc non iacet evidens periculum sed conformitas legi dei, quia non dubium quin clerus in apostolis et Chrysti discipulis vixit securius atque perfeccius carendo talibus diutinis elemosinis quam vivit vel vixerit clerus noster. Quis ergo fructus spiritualis vel vita miraculosa daret evidenciam ad continuandum tales elemosinas sic inceptas? Et patet defectus argucie consequentis, quando arguitur quod sic servari possent elemosine perpetue a progenitoribus concesse per generacionem ex dei ordinancia succedentem. Nec enim docetur quod iste sunt elemosine perpetue fundate a progenitoribus, nec quod illi exinde a dampnacione perpetua sunt exempti, nec quod esset meritorium generacioni illorum dictam elemosinam servare pro morula in hac forma, nec video per hoc evidenciam, nisi quod isti domini in hoc excedunt et declinant a regula domini Jesu Christi. Cum ergo in talibus pars securior sit tenenda, videtur quod foret securius talem ad ewangelicam paupertatem restringere et non excedere limites Jesu Christi. Et concedi posset quod seculares domini conservent et colligant ad usus cleri omnes redditus istos sive fructus et [p. 65] expendant residuum suorum redituum circa colleccionem istam servilem. Sed non sequitur quod tunc bene, quia talis servitus istis clericis non est in lege domini exemplata, et ipsimet clerici propter zelum quem habent ad dominacionem secularem non tale servicium secularium acceptarent. Sed evidenter infertur quod episcopi vel ewangelisantes fideliter dyaconos limitarent ad moderatam elemosinam ministrandam. Sed quid hoc ad perpetuacionem sive dominium, cum secundum legem domini insecuritas in tali elemosina sit meritoria et ad non confidendum in temporalibus sed in vita et spe ad beatitudinem inductiva? Numquid credimus quod tales per castra sua caynitica possunt subvertere legem dei et facere hic manentem civitatem et prosperitatem mundanam premium laboris et mercedem ultimam viatoris? Sed arguitur quod hoc officium pertineat sacerdoti quia Math. 6° et 8° legitur quo modo Christus fecit suos apostolos de panibus et piscibus quos miraculose multiplicaverat populo ministrare; ergo licet nostris episcopis habere ultra vite necessaria ad pauperibus parciendum. Sed hic concedi debet conclusio cum episcopi debent esse hospitales secundum apostolum. Sed iste locus simealis a simili videtur pocius concludere quod ipsi debent in persona propria ministrare egentibus regis elemosinas eis datas. Sed ex isto non sequitur quod thesaurisare debent vel perdere regis elemosinas pauperibus parciendas. Nec sequitur ex isto quod possunt per diem vel septimanam et multo magis per centum annos elemosinas totidem domini conservare. Et claret in facto quod nec manibus propriis ministrant egenis fideliter ista bona, nec per ministros ipsa faciunt fideliter ministrare, quia deus scit si voluntati sue eterne sit contrarium ut sic fiat. Ideo argucia ista est similis illi qua concluditur, quod episcopi debent pati Christum vel papam lavare sibi pedes, quia apostoli cum domino sic fecerunt. Si [p. 66] enim ipsi debent sic facere, tunc vel papa peccat non veniendo sic facere hoc ministerium vel episcopi peccant non veniendo ad ipsum ut hoc debitum ministerium compleatur. Et sic concedi debet quod stat servari dominium in manu principalis domini secularis et genus sacerdotum per quantumcunque tempus racionabile percipere puros fructus, quia hoc verificaretur ipsis percipientibus fructus quantumcunque modicos vel per horam. Sed quod debent per totidem annos fructus percipere, ut iam de facto percipiunt, non est evidencia asinina. Et sic concedi debet conclusio illata ulterius, scilicet quod episcopi possunt vivere continue in paupertate ewangelica et pauperibus distribuere fideliter quod superest de elemosinis sibi datis, quia a probabili sic fecerunt apostoli, qui erant episcopi, et multi alii episcopi in illo centenario in quo vixerunt exproprietarie ante dotacionem ecclesie. Sed quid ista conclusio ad iustificandum dotacionem vel elemosinam cleri a tempore Silvestri continue decurrentem?

TRUTH. Again, I suppose that the [clergy]{g:clerus}, out of desire for these [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, should be willing to give up the [endowment]{g:dotacio} and to receive these goods under the form of perpetual [alms]{g:elemosina}, and so the same thing returns to the [clerics]{g:clericus} as regards the fruits. But this is not evident to many, since then [the clergy] would fall from the [title]{g:titulus} that Silvester accepted from Caesar;[^1] nor would the truth of perpetual alms be preserved in England, since [the clergy] could not maintain that it had not often fallen from the title of alms because of the many and great sins it had committed, since it is not alms to nourish apostates in such a way.[^2] Indeed, it is not taught or evidently shown that such a donation was ever alms conformed to the divine will; how then would they pretend that it is pure and perpetual alms? Do we believe that it is in the power of these men to perpetuate [64] such alms of their clergy, as if they said against Christ, "I will that it be alms however they have lived and have been contrary to your law"; or, "I will that it live profitably to the edification of Christ's [church]{g:ecclesia}, because undoubtedly God so wills; therefore my volition is fulfilled"? Stephen indeed, in Acts 7 [:51], denied this [inference]{g:consequencia}, when he said that the Jews, especially the [religious]{g:religiosi} and the [bishops]{g:episcopus}, often resisted the divine will. What colour, then, [is there for saying] that those who so [enriched]{g:ditare} the church had done well, since they had such a will?[^3] For God, notwithstanding a blameless will of this kind, takes away what was conferred on these clerics when they so offend; why then ought not the [lords]{g:dominus} to [do] so, conforming themselves to God's good pleasure,[^4] since in this lies no evident danger but conformity to [God's law]{g:lex-dei}? For there is no doubt that the clergy, in the apostles and Christ's disciples, lived more securely and more perfectly, lacking such lasting alms, than our clergy lives or has lived. What spiritual fruit, then, or miraculous life would give [evidence]{g:evidencia} for continuing such alms begun in this way? And the defect of the following [quibble]{g:argucie} is clear, when it is argued that perpetual alms granted by forebears could be kept up in this way through the generation that succeeds by God's [ordinance]{g:ordinancia}. For it is not taught that these are perpetual alms founded by the forebears, nor that they are thereby exempt from perpetual damnation, nor that it would be meritorious for their generation to keep the said alms for a little while in this form; nor do I see evidence by this, except that these lords in this exceed and turn aside from the rule of the Lord Jesus Christ. Since, then, in such matters the safer side is to be held, it seems that it would be safer to restrain such a [clergy] to gospel poverty and not to exceed the [limits]{g:limites} of Jesus Christ.[^5] And it could be granted that [secular]{g:secularis} lords should keep and gather for the uses of the clergy all these [revenues]{g:reditus} or fruits, and [65] spend the rest of their own revenues on this servile collecting. But [it does not follow]{g:sequitur} that then [all is] well, because such servitude is not exemplified for these clerics in the Lord's law, and the clerics themselves, for the zeal they have for secular [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio}, would not accept such [service]{g:servicium} from seculars. But it is evidently [inferred]{g:concludere} that bishops, or those who faithfully preach the gospel, would [assign]{g:limitare} deacons to minister moderate alms. But what has this to do with perpetuation or [lordship]{g:dominium}, since according to the Lord's law insecurity in such alms is meritorious, and leads [a man] to trust not in temporal goods but in the life and hope that lead to blessedness? Do we believe that such men, by their Caim's castles, can overturn God's law and make an abiding city here, and worldly prosperity the reward of labour and the final wage of the [wayfarer]{g:viator}?[^6] But it is argued that this office belongs to the [priest]{g:sacerdos}, because in Matthew 6 and 8 it is read how Christ made his apostles minister to the people from the loaves and fishes that he had miraculously multiplied; therefore it is lawful for our bishops to have beyond the necessaries of life for sharing out to the poor.[^7] But here the conclusion must be granted, since bishops ought to be given to hospitality, according to the apostle [1 Tim. 3:2]. But this [topic from likeness]{g:locus-a-simili} seems rather to infer that they ought in their own person to minister to the needy the king's alms given to them.[^8] But from this it does not follow that they ought to treasure up or waste the king's alms that are to be shared out to the poor. Nor does it follow from this that they may keep so many of the lord's alms for a day or a week, and much less for a hundred years.[^9] And it is plain in fact that they neither minister these goods faithfully to the needy with their own hands, nor have them faithfully ministered through servants; for God knows whether it is contrary to his eternal will that it be done so. So this quibble is like the one by which it is inferred that bishops ought to let Christ or the [pope]{g:papa} wash their feet, because the apostles did so with the Lord. [66] For [if] they ought so to do, then either the pope sins by not coming to do this ministry so, or the bishops sin by not coming to him that this due ministry may be fulfilled.[^10] And so it must be granted that it holds that the lordship be kept in the hand of the principal secular lord and that the priestly kind receive the bare fruits for any reasonable length of time whatever, because this would be made true by their receiving fruits however small, or for an hour. But that they ought to receive the fruits for so many years, as they now in fact receive them, is not asinine evidence.[^11] And so the conclusion drawn further must be granted, namely that bishops may live continually in gospel poverty and faithfully distribute to the poor what is left over from the alms given them; because in all probability the apostles, who were bishops, did so, and many other bishops in that century in which they lived [as the propertyless]{g:exproprietarii} before the endowment of the church.[^12] But what has this conclusion to do with justifying the endowment or alms of the clergy running on continually from the time of Silvester?

## Truth · 66.18–69.18

> [p. 66] Sed quia quidam qui videntur esse aliquid multum ponderant hanc evidenciam sicut sepe verum innuit, ideo aliqui necessitantur particulatim ipsum dissolvere seriose. Assumitur ergo in evidencia cum clerici possent vivere continue in paupertate ewangelica et pauperibus fideliter distribuere quod superest de elemosinis sibi datis, quare non licet dominis sic dare suas elemosinas, exonerando se et suos posteros, et ex parte altera licet clericis prudenter distribuere has elemosinas sicut apostoli et dyaconi perfecerunt ut patet Actuum vi°? Hic dicitur quod hoc ideo quia clerus debet peccandi occasionem tollere et dare evidenciam aliis ad peccata mundialia fugiendum et omnino capere exemplum a Christo et suis apostolis ad ipsos in vita et moribus et pauperie imitandum. Ille enim fuit finis, quare Christus vixit vitam tam pauperem [p. 67] et penalem; iuxta hanc fidem prima Petri secundo Christus passus est pro nobis, nobis relinquens exemplum ut sequamur vestigia eius. Si ergo tam patens evidencia et salubris evidet clero ut sequatur vestigia antichristi, potest Christum deserere et huius dyaboli vestigia imitari. Immo iuxta istam stulticiam clerus qui vovit virginitatem potest cottidie dormire cum femina et cum hoc ab incontinencia per dei graciam preservari, quare ergo non licet clero sic facere ad meritum augmentandum. Ulterius dicitur quod superiores clerici non debent continue cum distribucionibus huiusmodi occupari ut patet ex eleccione apostolorum, qui deserentes istud officium elegerunt septem dyaconos Actuum vi°. Et Stephanus propter eius inperfeccionem officium deseruit. Que ergo religio antichristi obligaret papam, episcopos et superiores ecclesie ad tales corporales elemosinas parciendum cum spiritualis elemosina infinitum melior, scilicet ewangelii predicacio, sit a Christo et suis apostolis exemplata? Domini ergo superhabundantes diviciis possunt per fidelem subministracionem sibi subserviencium subtiliare redditus suorum pauperum tenencium, vel alios fideles laycos ad istud officium limitare, licet non onerent ad istud abiectum officium sacerdotes, cum patet experiencia satis certa, quod ipsi sacerdotes infra tempus satis modicum peccabiliter et infideliter ministrarent. Quis ergo episcopus monachus vel alius dotatus clericus staret in isto officio cum superbia cupidine vel gulosa libidine propter purum premium pauperibus ministrandi? Et sic nostri clerici renuerunt indubie formam dyaconorum satis similem in hac parte. Nec pertinet superioribus sacerdotibus officium istud tam abiectum facere et taliter cum ipso se et suos posteros desponsare. Et quantum ad consequentem arguciam quod apostoli collegerunt fragmenta ut dicitur Math. xiiii. et xv. quare ergo non licet episcopis [p. 68] nostris sic facere, evidencia ista plena est dolositate, quia hoc factum apostolorum non arguit quod papa vel episcopi debent taliter dominari, sed quod possunt in facto laudabili fragmenta ad pascendum pauperes humiliter congregare, nec scio ex ewangelio docere quod apostoli distribuebant fragmenta ista pauperibus, sed potuerunt, si Christus voluerit, post istam collectionem limitare egenis ut ipsa tollerent, et apostoli intenderunt interim ministerio digniori. Domini ergo seculares debent exonerare clericos ab isto vili officio et ipsos ad ewangelium, oracionem vel exercicium aliud salubrius secundum formam ewangelii limitare, et subtilient, ut supra dictum est, redditus in suis pauperibus per ministros, et non sic extorqueant peccuniam de suis egenis tenentibus ad conventum dyaboli sic ditandum. Lapsus ergo infidelis cleri in hoc ministerio daret occasionem Christianis dominis ad hos redditus confiscandum. Cum rex Anglie cum cleri repugnancia dicitur confiscasse bona episcopi quia episcopus ille noluit secundum formam ewangelii suum curatum officium regis dimittere et in sua ecclesia residere. Et re vera videtur multis quod ista vecors taciturnitas in tantum periculum regis et regni foret causa sufficiens ad confiscandum omnes redditus huiusmodi clericorum. Rex vero debet dominanter secundum formam ewangelii imitacionem domini practisare, et clerus dotatus potest, sicud sciverit, istud opus regis ex racione vel ewangelio inpugnare. Videat ergo fidelis theologus, quod clerus cupidus non deficiat in argumentis ex forma ewangelii fabricandis, et fundet se in completa similitudine, capiendo ut fidem quod Christus ab instanti sue concepcionis usque ad horam sue mortis fuit homo pauperrimus ad pauperiem suis ovibus exemplandam, et ista exemplacio debet esse preciosior quam decreta omnium paparum, eciam Iohannis 22ⁱ, nisi in fide ewangelii sint fundata. Et pauperies in [p. 69] vita apostolica est plus a fidelibus attendenda quam omnes vite episcoporum divitum vel cleri alterius, licet sompniati fuerint nunc in beatitudine residere: fides enim docet, quod apostoli pauperes sunt beati, sed non docet illud de istis divitibus, sed pocius docet practice quod canonisaciones istorum care pro pecunia erant empte. Iudas enim, quem Christus vocat filium perdicionis, adhuc in fine vite sue proiecit argenteos et sic, licet desperavit, abiit satis pauper; fratres autem videntur superare Scarioth, eo quod incollunt manentem civitatem, ac si de celesti Ierusalem desperarent, et sic vocatas elemosinas statim inpossibilitant ut iuvent pauperes vel quantumcunque fratres derelinquerant ab elemosinantibus revocentur, cum preter exemplacionem Christi faciunt sibi basilicas sumptuosas et procurant ypocritice quod maiores domini et domine in eorum basilica sint humati, ut vel sic perpetuacio habitacionis loci sui in terris per istorum sepultorum progeniem defendantur. Nec dubium quin melius foret hiis mortuis in campo inter bestias sepeliri.

But because certain men who seem to be something [Gal. 2:6] weigh this evidence heavily, as often hinting at what is true, some are therefore compelled to dissolve it piece by piece, in order.[^13] It is [assumed]{g:assumptum}, then, in the evidence: since clerics could live continually in gospel poverty and faithfully distribute to the poor what is left over from the alms given them, why is it not lawful for lords to give their alms in this way, unburdening themselves and their posterity, and, on the other side, lawful for clerics prudently to distribute these alms, as the apostles and deacons carried it out, as is clear from Acts 6 [:1–6]? Here it is said that this is so because the clergy ought to take away the occasion of sinning, and give others evidence for fleeing worldly sins, and altogether take example from Christ and his apostles for imitating them in life and manners and poverty. For that was the end for which Christ lived so poor [67] and hard a life; according to this faith, 1 Peter 2 [:21]: "Christ suffered for us, leaving us an example that we should follow his steps."[^14] If, then, evidence so plain and wholesome makes it evident to the clergy that it should follow the steps of antichrist, it may desert Christ and imitate the steps of this devil.[^15] Indeed, by this folly a clergy that has vowed virginity may sleep daily with a woman and with this be preserved from incontinence by God's grace; why then is it not lawful for the clergy to do so, to increase its merit? Further, it is said that the higher clerics ought not to be continually occupied with distributions of this kind, as is clear from the choosing by the apostles, who, leaving this office, chose seven deacons, Acts 6 [:2–5]. And Stephen left the office because of its imperfection.[^16] What religion of antichrist, then, would bind the pope, bishops and higher men of the church to share out such bodily alms, since a spiritual alms infinitely better, namely the preaching of the gospel, is exemplified by Christ and his apostles?[^17] Lords, then, abounding in riches, may through the faithful ministration of those who serve them lighten the revenues of their poor tenants,[^18] or assign other faithful laymen to this office, provided they do not burden priests with this abject office, since fairly certain experience makes it clear that the priests themselves would within a fairly short time minister sinfully and unfaithfully. What bishop, then, or monk, or other endowed cleric would stand in this office, with his pride, covetousness or gluttonous lust, for the mere reward of ministering to the poor? And so our clerics have undoubtedly refused the form of the deacons, fairly like it in this respect.[^19] Nor does it belong to the higher priests to do this so abject office, and in such a way to wed themselves and their posterity to it. And as to the following quibble, that the apostles gathered the fragments, as is said in Matthew 14 [:20] and 15 [:37], "why then is it not lawful for our [68] bishops to do so?", this evidence is full of guile; because this deed of the apostles does not argue that the pope or bishops ought [to lord]{g:dominari} it in such a way, but that they may, in a praiseworthy deed, humbly gather fragments to feed the poor. Nor can I teach from the gospel that the apostles distributed these fragments to the poor; but they could, if Christ willed, after this collecting, have assigned [them] to the needy for them to carry off, and the apostles meanwhile attended to a worthier ministry. Secular lords, then, ought to relieve the clerics of this vile office and assign them to the gospel, to prayer, or to some other more wholesome exercise according to the form of the gospel; and let them lighten, as was said above, the revenues among their poor through servants, and not so extort money from their needy tenants in order thus to enrich the convent of the devil. The lapse, then, of a faithless clergy in this ministry would give Christian lords occasion to confiscate these revenues. Since a king of England, against the clergy's resistance, is said to have confiscated the goods of a bishop, because that bishop would not, according to the form of the gospel, give up his office of the king, being a [curate]{g:curatus}, and reside in his church.[^20] And in truth it seems to many that this [faint-hearted]{g:vecors} silence, to such peril of the king and [kingdom]{g:regnum}, would be sufficient cause for confiscating all the revenues of clerics of this kind. But the king ought, as a lord, according to the form of the gospel, to put into practice the imitation of the Lord; and the endowed clergy may, as far as it knows how, attack this work of the king from reason or the gospel. Let the faithful theologian see to it, then, that the covetous clergy does not fall short in arguments to be fashioned from the form of the gospel,[^21] and let him ground himself in the complete likeness, taking as of faith that Christ, from the instant of his conception to the hour of his death, was a most poor man, to exemplify poverty for his sheep; and this exemplifying ought to be more precious than the decrees of all the popes, even of John XXII, unless they are founded in the faith of the gospel.[^22] And poverty in [69] the apostolic life is more to be heeded by the faithful than all the lives of rich bishops or of other clergy, although they have been dreamed now to dwell in blessedness: for faith teaches that the poor apostles are blessed, but it does not teach that of these rich men; but rather it teaches in practice that the [canonizations]{g:canonizare} of these men were dearly bought for money. For Judas, whom Christ calls the son of perdition [John 17:12], still at the end of his life cast down the pieces of silver [Matt. 27:5], and so, although he despaired, went off poor enough; but the [friars]{g:frater} seem to surpass Iscariot, in that they inhabit an abiding city, as if they despaired of the heavenly Jerusalem; and the alms so called they at once make impossible to help the poor, or to be recalled by [those who gave them]{g:elemosinare}, however far the friars have forsaken [their rule],[^23] since beyond the example of Christ they make themselves sumptuous basilicas and hypocritically contrive that the greater lords and ladies be buried in their basilica, so that at least in this way the perpetuation of their place's dwelling on earth may be defended by the progeny of those buried.[^24] Nor is there doubt that it would be better for these dead to be buried in the field among the beasts.

## Pollard's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 63.13: Supposing the clergy ready to exchange endowments for a perpetual alms, they would lose their title, and yet the alms would be no true alms.
- 63.24: Have men the power of investing any donation they please with the character of alms?
- 64.7: Despite the good intention of the donor, God takes away alms from priests when they sin, and secular lords should conform to His decree.
- 64.17: The argument that perpetual alms can be maintained by renewals from successive heirs is bad, for (i) these are not perpetual alms, (ii) they have not the merit ascribed to such alms, (iii) the heirs would do no good work in renewing them.
- 64.30: Granting that temporal lords would be willing to act as rent-gatherers for the clergy, they would be acting unscripturally, and the clergy would refuse their services.
- 65.8: The essence of dependence on alms is that it should be precarious.
- 65.12: Are we to believe that by their 'Caim's Castles' they can set up an abiding city here?
- 65.16: It is argued that as the Apostles distributed Christ's loaves and fishes, so Bishops should have superfluities to give to the poor.
- 65.20: But by this text bishops should personally distribute the king's alms, not hoard or squander them. It certainly does not sanction the retention of alms for centuries.
- 65.28: As a fact they minister to the needy neither themselves nor by others.
- 65.31: Compare the argument that Bishops should have their feet washed by the Pope.
- 66.1: Which convicts either the Pope or Bishops of sin in disobedience.
- 66.4: We must allow that the clergy may receive mere fruits, if given in moderation, and for the needs of the moment.
- 66.8: But not continuously, as at present.
- 66.10: We allow further that bishops living in poverty may distribute of their superfluity.
- 66.15: But this does not justify endowments held since the time of Silvester.
- 66.22: It is argued, why may not lords compound for their own and their descendants' alms, and why may not the clergy distribute, as did the apostles and deacons?
- 66.28: The answer is that the clergy should avoid all occasion of sin, and follow Christ.
- 67.5: If their risking temptation is meritorious, why should they not do so as to purity?
- 67.11: The apostles left the distribution of alms to the deacons.
- 67.14: Why then should the heads of the Church distribute temporal alms instead of spiritual?
- 67.18: Lords should find their own almoners, not burden the clergy and tempt them to sin.
- 68.2: That the apostles gathered up the fragments cannot justify clerical lordship: I do not even know that they themselves distributed the fragments.
- 68.9: Let lords leave clergy to their proper work, and not rack tenants to endow a convent of the devil.
- 68.14: The unfaithful ministry of the clergy would justify the confiscation of their revenues, according to the precedent of an English king.
- 68.22: The king should obey Christ, and confiscate their goods, and the clergy show reason against it, if they can.
- 68.28: The example of Christ's poverty must outweigh the decrees of all the popes, even of John XXII.
- 69.1: It outweighs, too, the example of saints, whose canonisations were probably purchased.
- 69.11: The friars squander the alms given them for the poor on sumptuous buildings, and get great lords to be buried in them, so as to gain the protection of their descendants.

## Notes

[^1]: **T/R.** At *elemosine* (63.20) Pollard notes that everything from here to the end of the chapter is wholly omitted in his base codex and supplied from B¹ (Vienna 1387, a hand of about 1400; Pollard p. xxii). The title Silvester received from Caesar is the endowment of the church by the Donation of Constantine.

[^2]: **C/T.** The subject of *caderet*, *posset* and *perpetraverat* is taken as the clergy (*clerus*, 63.13). *Quin ... cadebat*: "could not maintain that it had not fallen", i.e. could not deny that it had. B, C omit *propter* (63.19; B¹ has it added in the margin by another hand). B, B¹, C omit *autem* (63.16).

[^3]: **C/T.** *Color*: a plausible pretext. B, C read *dum* for *cum sic* (64.10), and C *semper* for *sepe* (64.7).

[^4]: **C/T.** *Quare ergo non debent sic domini* has no infinitive: "[do] so", i.e. take away what God takes away. In B¹ and B *non* (64.11) is added in the margin by another hand, and B has *aufferre* supplied there: "why then ought not lords so to take away".

[^5]: **C/T.** *Talem* (64.28) has no expressed noun; taken as *clerum*. *Elemosinam* (64.24) restored from the plate; the extraction read *elemosmam*. B, C omit *domini* before *Jesu Christi* (64.26).

[^6]: **S/R.** *Hic manentem civitatem* recalls Heb. 13:14, "we have not here a lasting city"; Cain built the first city (Gen. 4:17). *Castra caynitica* ("Caim's Castles", Pollard's side-note) is Wyclif's name for the friars' houses, CAIM being read as the initials of the four mendicant orders (Carmelites, Augustinians, Jacobins, Minorites) .

[^7]: **S.** *Math. 6° et 8°*: the two feedings stand in Mark 6 and 8 (Mark 6:41, 8:6: "he ... gave to his disciples to set before them"); Matthew has them in chapters 14 and 15, as Wyclif cites them at 67.32. The printed reference is left as it stands. B, C omit *vite* (65.19).

[^8]: **T/C.** *Locus simealis a simili*: *simealis* is printed so on the plate and has no variant; it is left untranslated, the phrase being taken as the dialectical topic *a simili*, "from likeness". B reads *quod episcopi pocius debent*, C *quod ipsi debent pocius* (65.22).

[^9]: **C.** *Elemosinas totidem domini*: taken as "so many of the lord's alms", with *domini* the king or donor. *Multo magis* is rendered "much less" because the clause is under *nec sequitur*: the longer the keeping, the less it follows.

[^10]: **T.** The plate prints *Si* at the end of 65.33 (*sic fecerunt. Si*); the extraction dropped it at the page turn; restored (OVERRIDE). B reads *episcopi* for *ipsi* (66.1).

[^11]: **C.** Literally "is not asinine evidence". The sense seems to be that the claim has no evidence, not even an ass's; possibly a *nisi* ("is no evidence but an asinine one") has fallen out. Pollard's side-note: "But not continuously, as at present." No variant is recorded.

[^12]: **T.** B, C read *tercentenario*, "in those three hundred years", for *centenario* (66.14), and *tantum dotacioni* for *ante dotacionem* (66.15).

[^13]: **S/C.** *Qui videntur esse aliquid*: Gal. 2:6, "them who seemed to be some thing". *Sicut sepe verum innuit* is taken with *hanc evidenciam*: the argument often hints at a truth. B¹, C read *ponderat* (66.19). *Ipsum* (masc.) has no expressed antecedent; taken as the argument. *Seriose*, "in order", point after point.

[^14]: **S.** 1 Pet. 2:21: Wyclif has *nobis ... ut sequamur*, "leaving us ... that we should follow", for the Vulgate's *vobis ... ut sequamini* (DR "leaving you an example that you should follow his steps").

[^15]: **T/C.** *Evidet* is printed so on the plate, with no variant; taken as formed from *evidens*, "makes evident". The sentence is ironic: Falsehood's "evidence" would license following antichrist.

[^16]: **C/R.** Acts does not say Stephen left the office of deacon; Wyclif infers it from his preaching and disputing (Acts 6:8–10). B¹ reads *pauperiores* for *superiores* (67.9); B, C read *ut patet Actuum* (67.12).

[^17]: **T.** *Superiores* (67.14–15) is divided *su-|periores* on the plate; the extraction's stray mark is removed (OVERRIDE). *Religio antichristi* plays on *religio privata*, the orders.

[^18]: **C.** *Subtiliare redditus*: "to make the revenues thin", i.e. lighten what poor tenants pay, as the side-note's "not rack tenants" and *non sic extorqueant* (68.12) show. At 68.11 (*subtilient redditus in suis pauperibus*) the same verb may rather mean "dispense finely among their poor"; "lighten" is kept for both.

[^19]: **C/T.** *Cum superbia cupidine vel gulosa libidine*: the vices the clerics would bring, i.e. none would stay in the office without them, for its reward alone. *Satis similem*: the deacons' form, fairly like the office now proposed. B reads *induere*, "put on", for *indubie* (67.27), and *dispensare* for *desponsare* (67.30).

[^20]: **C/R.** The *cum*-clause has no main verb; it is rendered as it stands. *Suum curatum officium regis*: literally "his curate['s] office of the king", i.e. a curate holding royal office who would not return to his cure. The king and bishop are not identified .

[^21]: **C.** *Videat ... quod clerus cupidus non deficiat*: taken as "see to it that [the argument against] the covetous clergy does not lack gospel arguments"; literally the clergy is the subject. C reads *igitur* for *ergo* (68.25).

[^22]: **R.** John XXII, in *Cum inter nonnullos* (1323), condemned as heretical the assertion that Christ and the apostles had nothing, individually or in common . B omits *sue* before *concepcionis* (68.29).

[^23]: **T/C.** *Inpossibilitant ut iuvent ... vel ... revocentur*: "make impossible that they help ... or be recalled". C reads *viverent* for *iuvent*, and B has *iuvent* corrected from *viverent* (69.11). *Derelinquerant* has no object; "[their rule]" is supplied; B reads *deliquerant*, "however much the friars have offended" (69.12). B, C read *incolunt hic* for *incollunt* (69.9).

[^24]: **T.** The extraction read a stray *is* at 69.14 (*maiores is domini*); the plate has *maiores | domini*, and the *is* is removed (OVERRIDE). *Defendantur* is plural against *perpetuacio*; B, C read *defendatur* (69.17). B reads *sua* for *eorum* (69.15).
