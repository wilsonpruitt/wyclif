## Doubtful, left as they are

- **09-d ¶2, p. 286**: *Sed pro distinccione doctorum in ista materia notandum*, rendered "But". Either (b), turning from the "cannot be investigated" excuse to Wyclif's own account, or (a), the next step (the binding and loosing of Satan as the ground for preferring the earlier doctors). "But" is defensible as the turn.
- **09-d ¶2, p. 287**: *Nunc autem conturbant reciproce sentencias suas; ideo infidelis foret*, rendered "Now in fact". This is (a), the minor premiss (the recent doctors must agree with the earlier; *nunc autem* they confound one another; *ideo*). "Now" carries *autem*, "in fact" carries *nunc*: correct, noted only because *nunc autem* also reads "but as it is".
- **09-e ¶3, p. 291**: *Ista autem sentencia clare docet omnes decretales …*, rendered "And". Either (c), Wyclif's comment continuing the Gratian quotation, or (a), the step that brings the quoted teaching to bear on his case (superfluous and unjust). "And" is defensible.
- **09-e ¶5, p. 291**: *Quod autem demonstratur panis aut vinum, sonat quadruplex evangelium*, rendered "And". Either (c), or (a), the proof of the assumption (*panis demonstratur*) on which the "tropical meaning" conclusion rests (cf. *Minor patet*). "And" is defensible; the following *Illud autem esset stultissimum* is correctly "Now".
- **09-f ¶1, p. 292**: *Sed sanctus Richardus, Armachanus et alii sancti dicunt*, rendered "But". An *ergo* follows (*Admisso ergo*), but it draws on Richard's opinion, not on a syllogism; the *sed* sets Richard against Thomas, Scotus and the Gloss. (b) is right.
