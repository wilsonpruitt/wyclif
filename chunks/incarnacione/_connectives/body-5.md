### 05-a ¶3 · 65.21–68.4  (8 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 65] Probatur tripliciter. Primo sic. Deus non potest dampnificare naturam aliquam sine suo demerito¹² precedente: **sed** [p. 66] si dimitteret naturam assumptam, eam sic dampnificaret: ergo et cetera.¹ Maior patet ex hoc, quod dampnificacio est creature racionali² mali illacio inutilis,² que non potest sibi inexistere nisi oriatur principaliter a se ipsa, iuxta sentenciam beati Iohannis Chrisostomi et aliorum doctorum communiter. Confirmatur. Si Deus aufert creature racionali bonum aliquod, iustum et pulcrum est illud auferri et per consequens non sonat³ in malum inutile: et sic⁴ servata immunitate peccati non est dampnum. Nam si Deus dampnificat hominem, quia facit eum iuste carere bono possibili; tunc infinitum multum dampnificaret quamlibet beatificabilem creaturam; et, ut communiter deducitur, infinitum impediret quodlibet opus suum. 2°. Confirmatur⁵ ex hoc, quod infliccio⁶ pene perpetue pro peccato non dampnificat **sed** meliorat dampnatum in tantum quod, si esset in eleccione⁷ sua sic dampnari post peccatum vel vacare⁸ impunem,⁹ eligeret racionabiliter sic dampnari: ergo a maiori vel pari, si Deus aufert ab aliquo¹⁰ sine suo demerito¹⁰ quodvis bonum, cum sit pulcrum et bonum quod ita faciat nedum toti universitati¹¹ **sed** persone, que aliter frustratorie¹² occuparet; sequitur quod talis ablacio non sit dampnificacio; cum nec pro illa ablacione nec pro eius causa racionabiliter¹³ sit dolendum. Ex quo¹⁴ videtur 3°. quod innocens hoc videns eligeret¹⁵ racionabiliter sic privari: et cum nemo¹⁶ potest racionabiliter eligere¹⁶ dampnificari, sequitur quod Deus non dampnificat creaturam, **sed** solum ipsamet¹⁷ seipsam¹⁸ vel aliud alliciens ad peccandum. Ymo si bene consideretur, omnis dampnificacio sapit iniuriacionem, que non potest per se bono et iusto competere, quin pocius bonificare et corrigere. Minor **autem** argumenti sic [p. 67] probatur. Humanitas assumpta a Verbo non potest demereri previe suam dimissionem: ergo, illa dimissa, hoc sibi contingeret sine suo demerito. Antecedens patet per hoc, quod non potest demereri stante unione, quia tunc Verbum peccaret: et si pro instanti dissolucionis vel post peccaret, patet quod demeritum [dimissionem] sequetur;¹ et per consequens non dampnificaretur ex suo demerito precedente. Quod **autem** dimissio² foret dampnosa humanitati Christi ex hoc evidet, quod deperdicio gracie unionis foret deperdicio maximi boni possibilis intrinseci creature:³ et per consequens tum⁴ loco illius non succederet tantum bonum: sequitur quod humanitas ipsam perdens foret notabiliter peiorata, et per consequens dampnificata. Valorem **autem** ypostatice unionis suppono⁵ ex dictis et dicendis: per illam enim fuit homo assumptus idem personaliter Verbo Dei. Magna ergo peioracio sua foret quod dimitteretur sine suo demerito tanquam extranea. Non enim pertinet ad dictum Cesarem semper Augustum⁶ sic peiorare sine suo demerito carissimam⁷ creaturam: quia indubie creatura dimissa pro instanti dimissionis posset racionabiliter conqueri de ingratitudine Dei sui, cum continue ante fecit quidquid debuit, et finaliter reciperet pro ‘retribucione⁸ mercedis’ tantum dampnum.⁸ Non ergo foret spes⁹ in tali Deo. Et hinc nimirum¹⁰ dicit Augustinus Ome. 47: ‘Absit¹¹ quod Christus pro triduo animam suam dimiserit:¹² ipsam enim inseparabiliter habuit:’ unde indubie, si post peccaret, dimissio gracie unionis preservantis ne peccet foret causa principalis peccati: quia si affirmacio sit causa affirmacionis, negacio est causa negacionis, ut absencia naute est causa periclitacionis navis ex 2° Physicorum. Illud ergo peccatum, ex dimissione originatum, esset Deo principaliter imputandum, cum non ex parte creature, **sed** Dei, causatur talis absencia: talis¹³ ergo [p. 68] Deus excederet in malicia deum Manicheorum,¹ qui sine² demerito servi sui absentaret se pro temptacionis tempore non iuvando. Unde et illam racionem tangit sanctus Thomas De Christo,³ articulo 70.⁴

ENGLISH: It is proved in three ways. First thus. God cannot [damage]{g:dampnificare} any nature without its own preceding demerit: now, [66] if he were to lay aside the assumed nature, he would thus damage it: therefore etc.[^1] The major is clear from this, that [damaging]{g:dampnificacio} is, for a rational [creature]{g:creatura}, the useless [bringing-on]{g:illacio} of evil, which cannot be present in it unless it arises principally from itself, according to the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of blessed John Chrysostom and of the other doctors commonly.[^2] This is confirmed. If God takes away some good from a rational creature, it is just and beautiful that it be taken away, and consequently it does not amount to useless evil: and so, immunity from sin being preserved, it is no loss. For if God damages a man because he makes him justly lack a possible good, then he would damage every beatifiable creature infinitely much; and, as is commonly deduced, he would infinitely hinder each of his works. Second, this is confirmed from this, that the infliction of perpetual punishment for sin does not damage but betters the damned man, to such a degree that, if it were in his choice to be thus damned after sin or to remain unpunished, he would reasonably choose to be thus damned: therefore, from the greater or from the equal, if God takes away any good whatever from anyone without his demerit, since it is beautiful and good that he so act, [not only]{g:nedum} for the whole universe but for the person, who otherwise would hold [it] to no purpose, [it follows]{g:sequitur} that such a [taking-away]{g:ablacio} is not damaging, since neither for that taking-away nor for its cause is one reasonably to grieve.[^3] From which, third, it seems that an innocent seeing this would reasonably choose to be thus deprived: and since no one can reasonably choose to be damaged, it follows that God does not damage a creature, but only [the creature] itself [damages] itself, or another enticing [it] to sin.[^4] Indeed, if it is well considered, all damaging savours of wronging, which cannot [of itself]{g:per-se} belong to one who is good and just, but rather to [make good]{g:bonificare} and to correct. Now the minor of the argument is [67] proved thus. The humanity assumed by the Word cannot [demerit]{g:demereri} its [laying-aside]{g:dimissio} beforehand: therefore, were it laid aside, this would befall it without its demerit. The [antecedent]{g:antecedens} is clear by this, that it cannot demerit while the [union]{g:unio} stands, for then the Word would sin: and if it sinned at the instant of the dissolution or after, it is clear that the demerit will follow [the laying-aside]; and consequently it would not be damaged from its own preceding demerit.[^5] And that the laying-aside would be damaging to the humanity of Christ is evident from this, that the loss of [the grace of union]{g:gracia-unionis} would be the loss of the greatest possible good intrinsic to a creature: and consequently no so great a good would then succeed in its place: it follows that the humanity losing it would be notably made worse, and consequently damaged.[^6] And the worth of the hypostatic union I suppose from what has been said and is to be said: for through it [the assumed man]{g:homo-assumptus} was personally the same as the Word of God. Great, therefore, would be its [worsening]{g:peioracio}, that it should be laid aside without its demerit as a stranger. For it does not belong to the said Caesar ever August thus to make worse, without its demerit, his dearest creature: for undoubtedly the creature laid aside could, at the instant of the laying-aside, reasonably complain of the [ingratitude]{g:ingratitudo} of its God, since continually before it did whatever it ought, and at the last would receive, for "recompense of reward", so great a loss.[^7] There would therefore be no beauty in such a God.[^8] And hence, no wonder, Augustine says, Homily 47: "Far be it that Christ for the three days laid aside his soul: for he had it inseparably":[^9] hence undoubtedly, if it sinned afterward, the laying-aside of the grace of union, which preserves [it] from sinning, would be the principal cause of the sin: for if an affirmation is the cause of an affirmation, a negation is the cause of a negation, as the absence of the pilot is the cause of the ship's peril, from the second [book] of the *Physics*.[^10] That sin, therefore, originating from the laying-aside, would be principally imputable to God, since such an absence is caused not on the creature's side but on God's: such a [68] God, therefore, would exceed in malice the god of the Manichees, who without the demerit of his servant would absent himself for the time of temptation, not helping. Hence St Thomas also touches that argument, *On Christ*, article 50.[^11]

### 05-b ¶1 · 68.5–70.3  (6 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 68] Item quilibet beatus in celo est tam insolubiliter Deo connexus quod repugnat illi statui miseriam peccati in anima succedere vel⁵ coesse: **sed** Christus ab instanti unionis fuit perfeccius beatus secundum animam quam alius, cum fuerit sanctus sanctorum: ergo multo magis repugnat anime sue peccatum succedere vel⁶ coesse: **sed** si⁶ dimissio foret possibilis,⁷ hoc potuit contigisse:⁸ ergo illa⁹ dimissio non fuit possibilis. Maior probatur ex hoc. Omnes beati sunt immortales et impeccabiles, sicut omnes rectiloqui¹⁰ confitentur: non quia non possunt peccare, quia multi prius tempore suo peccant; **sed** sunt in gracia confirmacionis et glorie, cui repugnat peccatum vel miseriam coexistere, vel post illam succedere: patet ergo quod beatitudo est qualitas implicans securitatem perpetuitatis pleni gaudii et exclusionis miserie. [Eciam¹¹¹² securitas perpetuitatis pleni gaudii¹² et exclusionis miserie est per seipsum securitas talis, cum sit deveniendum ad ultimum formale, quo formaliter et non alio est quis securus de perpetuitate pleni gaudii et exclusionis miserie. **Sed** contradiccionem implicat tali coexistere in subiecto suo vel post illam in ipso subiecto suo miseriam succedere: igitur etcetera.]¹¹ Unde omnes theologi concorditer confitentur, quod¹³ contradiccionem claudit aliquem esse¹³ vel fuisse beatum cum hoc quod fiat postmodum non beatus. Nec valet dicere quod beatitudo per accidens sit beatitudo, quia ipsa est ultima forma abstracta, qua quis est beatus formaliter. Ideo, si illa non sit per se beatitudo, non superest quid aliud per se foret. [p. 69] Sicut ergo iusticia non potest esse non iusticia et de formis similibus;¹ sic status beatitudinis non potest esse non huiusmodi² status. Unde notum est methaphisicis quod tales forme abstracte non suscipiunt sic³ predicacionem per accidens ut concreta: si enim beatitudo sit per accidens beatitudo—cum non sit processus in infinitum; **sed**, cum omne per accidens sit reducibile ad aliquid⁴ per se, est devenire ad ultimum formale, quo quis est beatus—et illud per se et non per accidens est beatitudo: et de tali est michi sermo. Confirmatur. Si A qualitas⁵ sit nunc beatitudo et potest manere et⁶ desinere esse beatitudo; per idem post esse potest reincipere esse beatitudo. Quo concesso, patet quod preter A, quod non sufficit denominare hominem beatum, requiritur forma superaddita; et sic non A, **sed** forma sibi adveniens,⁷ foret beatitudo. Patet ergo, quod cum beatitudo et confirmacio non possunt esse, nisi sint perpetue; nec possunt esse nisi huiusmodi quod, eo ipso quod insunt, reponunt hominem in statu cui repugnat miseriam succedere vel coesse; nec dependet beatitudo pocius a futuro, quam qualitates alie absolute;⁸ tunc enim non forent beati de sua beatitudine securati. Minor ergo argumenti patet generaliter omni professori fidei incarnacionis: cum anima Christi ab instanti sue creacionis fuit plena omni genere carismatum, habens clariorem⁹ visionem divine essencie quam alia creatura, et per consequens plenam fruicionem ad tantam noticiam naturaliter¹⁰ consequentem. Cum ergo ‘vita eterna sit¹¹ sic cognoscere Deum Patrem ac Dominum Iesum Christum,’ ut testatur Veritas Ioh. 17; patet quod anima Christi ab instanti sue creacionis perpetue est beata.¹² Nec audivi hoc negatum ab aliquo, cum palam sequitur ex incarnacione. Si enim homo solum¹³ intelligit quod intelligit mens¹³ [p. 70] ypostatice copulata; per locum a sufficienti similitudine spiritus Iesu cognoscit quidquid cognoverat¹ ipse Iesus specialiter cum sit personaliter Verbum Dei.

ENGLISH: Again, every blessed one in heaven is so [inextricably]{g:insolubiliter} joined to God that it [is incompatible]{g:repugnare} with that [state]{g:status} that the misery of sin should succeed or coexist in the soul: now Christ from the instant of the [union]{g:unio} was more perfectly blessed as to the soul than any other, since he was the holy of holies: therefore much more is it incompatible with his soul that sin should succeed or coexist: now if the [laying-aside]{g:dimissio} were possible, this could have happened: therefore that laying-aside was not possible.[^1] The major is proved from this. All the blessed are immortal and impeccable, as all [who speak correctly]{g:rectiloqui} confess: not because they cannot sin, for many sin earlier, in their own time; but they are in the grace of confirmation and of glory, with which it is incompatible that sin or misery should coexist, or succeed after it: it is clear, therefore, that blessedness is a quality implying security of the perpetuity of full joy and of the exclusion of misery.[^2] Hence all theologians confess in concord that "someone is or was blessed, together with his afterwards being made not blessed" [implies a contradiction]{g:claudit-contradictionem}. Nor does it avail to say that blessedness is blessedness [by accident]{g:per-accidens}, for it is the ultimate abstract form by which someone is formally blessed. So, if it is not blessedness [of itself]{g:per-se}, nothing else remains that would be [so] of itself.[^3] [69] As, therefore, justice cannot be not-justice, and so of similar forms, so the state of blessedness cannot be not such a state. Hence it is known to metaphysicians that such abstract forms do not admit [predication]{g:predicacio} by accident in the way that concretes do: for if blessedness is blessedness by accident (since there is no process to infinity, but, since everything by accident is reducible to something of itself, one must come to an ultimate formal [principle] by which someone is blessed), that too is blessedness of itself and not by accident: and it is of such that I am speaking.[^4] This is confirmed. If the quality A is now blessedness, and can remain and cease to be blessedness, by the same token, after its being [so], it can begin again to be blessedness.[^5] Which being granted, it is clear that besides A, which does not suffice to [name]{g:denominare} a man blessed, a superadded form is required; and so not A, but a form coming to it, would be blessedness. It is clear, therefore, that since blessedness and confirmation cannot be unless they are perpetual, neither can they be except of such a kind that, by the very fact that they are present, they set a man in a state with which it is incompatible that misery should succeed or coexist; nor does blessedness depend on the future any more than other absolute qualities do; for then the blessed would not be made secure of their blessedness.[^6] The minor of the argument, therefore, is clear generally to every professor of the faith of the incarnation: since the soul of Christ from the instant of its creation was full of every kind of [charisms]{g:carismata}, having a clearer vision of the divine [essence]{g:essencia} than any other [creature]{g:creatura}, and consequently full enjoyment following naturally on so great a knowledge.[^7] Since, therefore, "eternal life is thus to know God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ", as the Truth testifies, John 17, it is clear that the soul of Christ from the instant of its creation is perpetually blessed.[^8] Nor have I heard this denied by anyone, since it follows openly from the incarnation. For if a man understands only what the mind [70] hypostatically joined understands, by the topic from sufficient likeness the spirit of Jesus knows whatever Jesus himself had known, especially since he is personally the Word of God.[^9]

### 05-b ¶2 · 70.4–70.32  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 70] Ex istis colligitur talis racio. Tam determinatum et necessarium est ipsum hominem fuisse assumptum, quam necessarium est mundum vel aliquid aliud prefuisse: **sed** non potuit fuisse assumptus nisi fuisset exinde beatus: ergo tam necessarium est ipsum fuisse beatum quam necessarium est aliquid prefuisse: **sed** pretericiones huiusmodi non possunt solvi: ergo nec beatitudo vel assumpcio Domini nostri Iesu Christi. 2°. confirmatur ex hoc; quod anima Christi actu elicito vidit clare in Verbo omnia preterita vel futura; et per consequens clare vidit perpetuitatem sue beatitudinis; et ultra, cum illa visio² non potuit defuisse vel verti in ignoranciam aut³ aliud genus entis; sequitur quod illa anima non potuit desinere esse beata vel Verbo ypostatice copulata. 3°. confirmatur ex testimonio Leonis Pape in quodam sermone dicentis: ‘tanta fuit unicio⁴ Dei et hominis ut nec supplicio posset⁵ dirimi nec morte distingui.’ Nec obest isti sentencie divina dispensacio, qua voluit Verbum suum ad tempus esse corpus, non plene **sed** quasi arraliter tunc⁶ beatum, et per consequens esse animam secundum actuacionem corporis et racionem, qua⁷ ipsam respicit, quantumlibet dolorosam.⁸ Nam ille homo est multarum naturarum quelibet,⁹ secundum quarum unam¹⁰ fuit ad tempus abiectus viator, et simul secundum aliam perfectissimus comprehensor: et sic simul summe gaudens et summe tristis secundum vires et raciones dispares, ut¹¹ ostenditur consequenter: et dico¹¹ quod arraliter habuit in via dotes corporis, cum dote subtilitatis exivit sponsus de clauso utero,¹² dote agilitatis ambulavit super aquas, dote incorruptibilitatis et claritatis in transfiguracione emicuit.¹³

ENGLISH: From these such an argument is gathered. It is as determined and necessary that "this man has been assumed" as it is necessary that "the world, or something else, has been before": now he could not have been assumed unless he had thereupon been blessed: therefore it is as necessary that "he has been blessed" as it is necessary that "something has been before": now pasts of this kind cannot be loosed: therefore neither [can] the blessedness or the [assumption]{g:assumpcio} of our Lord Jesus Christ.[^10] Second, this is confirmed from this, that the soul of Christ by an elicited act saw clearly in the Word all things past or future; and consequently saw clearly the perpetuity of its blessedness; and further, since that vision could not have been lacking, or be turned into ignorance or another genus of being, it follows that that soul could not cease to be blessed or [hypostatically]{g:ypostatice} joined to the Word. Third, this is confirmed from the testimony of Pope Leo in a certain sermon, saying: "So great was the uniting of God and man that it could neither be severed by punishment nor divided by death."[^11] Nor does the divine [dispensation]{g:dispensacio} stand against this [teaching]{g:sentencia}, by which he willed his Word for a time to be a body not fully but, as it were, [by way of earnest]{g:arraliter} then blessed, and consequently to be a soul, as to the actuation of the body and the aspect under which it regards it, however sorrowful.[^12] For that man is each of many natures, as to one of which he was for a time a cast-down [wayfarer]{g:viator}, and at the same time as to another a most perfect [comprehensor]{g:comprehensor}: and so at the same time supremely rejoicing and supremely sorrowful as to diverse powers and aspects, as is shown in what follows: and I say that he had by way of earnest on [the way]{g:via} the [dowries]{g:dotes} of the body: with the dowry of subtlety the bridegroom went out from the closed womb, with the dowry of agility he walked upon the waters, with the dowry of incorruptibility and of brightness he shone out in the transfiguration.[^13]

### 05-b ¶3 · 70.33–71.15  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 70] 3°. per deducens ad impossibile patet idem.¹⁴ Nam si [p. 71] humanitas ypostatice copulata posset dimitti et sic dampnari pro peccato, et tam caro quam anima est personaliter ipsum Verbum; sequitur quod Verbum Dei peccare¹ poterit et dampnari; quod nephas² est dicere. Patet consequencia supposito fundamento, cum arguitur ab inferiori ad suum superius personale, cui per se competit accio personalis. Sequitur ergo—hec humanitas potest peccare: hec humanitas est persona Verbi: ergo persona Verbi potest peccare—. Nam persona Verbi nedum est hec³ humanitas, **sed** aliquid aliud³ quia divinitas; ideo est superior⁴ vel communior quam⁴ est hec⁵ humanitas. Unde, sicut sequitur quod Filius Dei fuit natus humanitus, conversatus,⁶ passus, mortuus, et sepultus, et ascendit, eo quod humanitas, que fuit dictus Filius, sic se habuit; ita eciam sequeretur quod Verbum peccaret, si humanitas, que fuit dictum Verbum, peccaret.

ENGLISH: Third, the same is clear through [one] leading to the impossible.[^14] For if [71] the [humanity]{g:humanitas} hypostatically joined could be [laid aside]{g:dimittere} and so be damned for sin, and both the flesh and the soul are personally the Word himself, [it follows]{g:sequitur} that the Word of God could sin and be damned; which it is impious to say. The [inference]{g:consequencia} is clear, the foundation being supposed, since it is argued from an inferior to its personal superior, to which personal action belongs of itself. It follows, therefore: "This humanity can sin: this humanity is the [person]{g:persona} of the Word: therefore the person of the Word can sin." For the person of the Word is [not only]{g:nedum} this humanity but something else, since [it is] the [divinity]{g:divinitas}; so it is superior to, or more common than, this humanity.[^15] Hence, as it follows that the Son of God was born [humanly]{g:humanitus}, lived among [men], suffered, died, and was buried, and ascended, because the humanity, which was the said Son, was so, so also it would follow that the Word sinned, if the humanity, which was the said Word, sinned.

### 05-b ¶4 · 71.16–72.18  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 71] Confirmatur ex testimonio moderniorum⁷ dicencium quod hec humanitas non posset⁸ ypostatice copulari peccando nisi Verbum exinde peccaret;⁹ et, quod plus est,¹⁰ racione unionis prioris Verbum denominatum est secundum moderniores jacuisse pro triduo in sepulcro, et ad sensum consimilem peccasse in casu, quo¹¹ natura dimissa pro tunc peccaverit. Et conformi racione nec iacuit sepultus nec descendit ad inferos, nisi conformi racione denominaciones predicte in casu⁸ posito sibi insint,⁸ vel aliter dicatur sentencia supradicta, quod mansit pro dicto triduo unio ypostatica ad utrumque.

ENGLISH: This is confirmed from the testimony of [the more modern]{g:moderniores-doctores} [doctors], who say that this humanity could not be hypostatically joined while sinning unless the Word thereupon sinned; and, what is more, by reason of the prior union the Word is named, according to the more modern, as having lain for [the three days]{g:triduum} in the tomb, and in a quite similar sense as having sinned, in the case in which the [nature]{g:natura} laid aside had sinned at that time.[^16] What, I ask, is the cause of the difference, that the sense of the Holy Spirit in this [article of faith]{g:articulus-fidei}, "Christ descended into hell, was dead and buried", is "not that person, but a part of him, for the time in which it was not a part or hypostatically joined, was so", and that one would not grant, in a most similar sense, that the nature laid aside and afterwards reassumed, if it sinned in the meantime, would name the Son as so sinning?[^17] Let it be posited, then, that on the day of the Parasceve the soul of Christ completed the freeing of the fathers from [limbo]{g:limbus}, and at that time the body is driven by a demon, battering many men, and that on [72] the holy sabbath the soul is reunited to the body, the hypostatic union being suspended, and sins before none by adultery, homicide, blasphemy, and every kind of sin; and after none repents, and begets lawfully, and justly condemns many to death, the humanity being [assumed]{g:assumere} on the first [day] of the week into the unity of the [supposit]{g:suppositum} of the Word of God:[^18] a case which [the modern]{g:moderni-doctores} [doctors] admit, and more marvellous ones, as necessarily possible; in which case it seems, according to the [gloss]{g:glosa} they give to the articles of faith, that all the said operations in the three days would redound upon Christ, or else one would be feigning, from the prompting of the Holy Spirit, an impossible and unaccustomed sense.[^19] It is certain, therefore, that Christ did not stifle these men, lose the [aureole]{g:laureola} of virginity by the begetting of seed, incur [irregularity]{g:irregularitas}, or that traces of sins remained in him. And by a conformable reason he neither lay buried nor descended into hell, unless by a conformable reason the aforesaid [namings]{g:denominacio} in the case posited belong to him; or else the teaching stated above must be said: that the hypostatic union remained for the said three days to both.[^20]

### 05-c ¶1 · 72.19–73.3  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 72] Ex istis colligitur quod, si beatus alius tantum adheret Deo quod non potest post beatitudinem dissolvi unio, qua est unus spiritus cum eo;⁹ multo magis natura Christi, que ultra hoc ypostatice est unita: ut si homo, qui potest peccare, habet per graciam quod sit impeccabilis, immortalis, et sic de aliis que peioracionem sapiunt: quanto magis natura humana in fine bonitatis possibilis exaltata, que omnino peccare non poterit, ab illa gracia dimitti non poterit. Unde, quamvis Mt. 27 secundum naturam completam creatam clamet Christus; ‘Deus meus, Deus meus, utquid me dereliquisti?’ non tamen dimisit ypostaticam unionem, **sed** dereliquit illud ‘infirmum Dei,’ quod est suppositum ‘forcius’ ad tempus, inconservatum a¹⁰ persecucionibus et flagellacionibus Iudeorum; sed¹⁰ nunquam [p. 73] dimisit vel simpliciter dereliquit, quia dimissio dicit existenciam dimissi et separacionem ypostaticam ab eodem, quod non fuit in triduo.

ENGLISH: From these it is gathered that, if another blessed one so cleaves to God that after blessedness the [union]{g:unio} by which he is one spirit with him cannot be dissolved, much more [so] the [nature]{g:natura} of Christ, which beyond this is [hypostatically]{g:ypostatice} united: as, if a man, who can sin, has by grace that he is impeccable, immortal, and so of the other things that savour of [worsening]{g:peioracio}, how much more the human nature, exalted to the limit of possible goodness, which can in no way sin, cannot be [laid aside]{g:dimittere} from that grace.[^1] Hence, although in Matthew 27, as to the complete [created]{g:creatus} nature, Christ cries: "My God, My God, why hast thou forsaken me?", yet he did not lay aside the hypostatic union, but forsook that "weakness of God", which is the "stronger" [supposit]{g:suppositum}, for a time unprotected from the persecutions and scourgings of the Jews;[^2] but he never [73] laid [it] aside or forsook [it] [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}, because laying-aside [signifies]{g:dicere} the existence of what is laid aside and its hypostatic separation from the same, which was not [so] in [the three days]{g:triduum}.

### 05-c ¶2 · 73.4–75.5  (5 sed/autem)

Et ad illum sensum exponit Augustitinus Ome. 44.³ ‘Forte, inquit, aliquis dicit⁴: Si pater eum sanctificavit, ergo⁵ aliquando non erat sanctus; **sed** si sic eum sanctificavit,⁵ quomodo genuit? ut enim sanctus esset, gignendo ei⁶ dedit, quia sanctum eum genuit.’ Et respondet posterius ad obiectum. ‘Si, inquit, quod sanctificatur aliquando, non erat sanctum; quomodo dicimus Deo Patri —“sanctificetur nomen tuum”—?’ Ille ergo, qui est absolute necessario in se sanctus, fit sanctus⁷ in nobis per prevenientem graciam, noticiam, et operacionem, et confessionem meritoriam.⁸ Unde Spiritus Sanctus ad relinquendum nobis exemplar, quod loquitur de scriptura vitali, subdit in genere masculino— ‘quem Pater sanctificavit’— innuendo nobis relacionem faciendam ad Filium. Et isti modo¹¹ loquendi alludit Apostolus ad Gall. 3°; ‘non, inquit,¹² dicit seminibus quasi in multis, **sed** semini tuo, qui est Christus.’ Indubie, sicut sanctus Apostolus notavit¹³ numerum, sic et genus. ENGLISH: And if anyone, for [evidence]{g:evidencia} of this truth, once famous, awaits the testimony of scripture, behold, the Truth, John 10, addresses the Pharisees: "the scripture," he says, "cannot be broken, whom the Father hath sanctified and sent into the world."[^3] For the understanding of this, three things are to be noted in order. First, that the Truth does not speak of our artificial scripture, put together out of the shapings of ink and the skins of the dead; both because such scripture can daily be loosed by craftsmen, and also because sanctification and sending into the world do not belong to it: but that scripture is the "book of life", in which all things are written, since it is the inward [Speech]{g:sermo} of God.[^4] And in that sense Augustine expounds, Homily 48: "Perhaps," he says, "someone says: If the Father sanctified him, then at some time he was not holy; but if he so sanctified him, how did he beget him? For that he should be holy, he gave it him in begetting, because he begot him holy." And he answers the [objection]{g:objectum} afterwards: "If," he says, "what is sanctified was at some time not holy, how do we say to God the Father, 'hallowed be thy name'?"[^5] He, therefore, who is absolutely necessarily holy in himself becomes holy in us through [prevenient grace]{g:gracia-preveniens}, knowledge, and working, and meritorious confession. Hence the Holy Spirit, to leave us a pattern [showing] that he speaks of the living scripture, adds in the masculine gender, "whom the Father hath sanctified", intimating to us that the reference is to be made to the Son. For it is the custom of scripture and of Augustine to conceive persons through masculine adjectives, and natures through neuters.[^6] And to this manner of speaking the Apostle alludes, Galatians 3: "He saith not," he says, "to seeds, as of many: but to thy seed, which is Christ."[^7] Undoubtedly, as the holy Apostle noted the number, so also the gender. [74] Second, it is supposed that the Truth does not speak in this saying of the [loosing]{g:solucio} of the mind from the body, or of the Father from the Son: not of the first, because above, John 2, the Truth says to the Jews prophetically that they would [loose]{g:solvere} the temple of his body from the soul, and he in three days would raise it up. Since, therefore, "there was not in him, It is and It is not", it is clear that here he did not mean "the body cannot be loosed from the soul".[^8] Nor is the other sense pertinent; both because binding and loosing is of two natures to one another, and not of persons of the same supremely indivisible [essence]{g:essencia}; and also because that sense would have been [irrelevant]{g:impertinens} to those addressed, not a matter of doubt to them.[^9] So it remains, third, by the topic from sufficient division, that he meant the [inextricability]{g:insolubilitas} of [the Godhead]{g:deitas} from [the humanity]{g:humanitas}, which he expressed by the name "scripture", so that at least thus they might search the scriptures, which give testimony of these two natures thus hypostatically joined. Hence in John 5 too it is said: "Search the scriptures, in which you think to have life everlasting. And the same are they that give testimony of me."[^10] For they would not have had evidence to say "he blasphemes in saying he is the natural Son of God", had a sufficient searching of the scriptures been applied. For if it is written in the law of the Messiah or Christ to come in two natures and one [hypostasis]{g:ypostasis} (which bond, because of the hypostatic union to an infinite [person]{g:persona}, cannot be closed by an end of time, as can the union of those [participatively]{g:participative} deified through the Speech of God made to them and not joined to them personally), what [colour]{g:color} is there for [inferring]{g:concludere} blasphemy from the assertion of natural sonship?[^11] And the middle [term] through which this invective holds is the truth that this "scripture" "cannot be broken", in the sense expounded. For from it [it follows]{g:sequitur} that there is an uncreated nature in the supposit of [the Word]{g:verbum}, and together with this a created nature joined in time but [inextricably]{g:insolubiliter}. Hence most pointedly the Truth speaks the negative, "the scripture cannot be broken"; because, although [the saints]{g:sancti} in [the homeland]{g:patria} are inseparably joined to God, yet most contingently they can be loosed, since for [75] their time they are [those on the road]{g:viantes}, [able to sin]{g:peccabilis}. But [the man assumed by the Word]{g:homo-assumptus}, the Lord Jesus Christ, could at no time sin. And the [conclusion]{g:conclusio} is clear.[^12] Nor otherwise would the holy doctors have evidence for invoking anathema on those who grant the [laying-aside]{g:dimissio} or loosing of such natures.

### 05-c ¶4 · 75.11–75.22  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 75] Confirmatur tripliciter. Primo ex hoc, quod idem est iudicium de una creatura et qualibet, cum tanta sit utrobique divina libertas: **sed** aliquam creaturam Deus potest adnichilare: ergo⁵ quamlibet. 2°. per hoc quod aliter Deus esset necessitatus ad conservandam dictam unionem, et sic non libere contradictorie eam servat. 3°. ex hoc quod Deus non esset omnipotens, nisi posset tam contingentem et fragilem⁶ nedum dissolvere,⁷ **sed** et adnichilare.⁷ Tales⁸ sunt multe⁸ instancie, que omnes currunt sub uno circulo quod Deus non esset omnipotens nisi posset in talia. ENGLISH: This is confirmed in three ways. First from this, that the judgement is the same of one [creature]{g:creatura} and of any, since the divine freedom is as great on both sides: now God can [annihilate]{g:adnichilare} some creature: therefore any. Second, by this, that otherwise God would be necessitated to conserve the said union, and so he does not keep it freely, as between contradictories. Third, from this, that God would not be omnipotent unless he could not only dissolve [a union] so contingent and frail, but also annihilate [it].[^14] Such are many [objections]{g:instancia}, which all run within one circle: that God would not be omnipotent unless he had power over such things. Or thus they argue by the topic from likeness: If God can create, and on both sides the distance is as great; therefore by the same token he can annihilate.[^15]

### 05-d ¶1 · 76.32–77.27  (3 sed/autem)

ENGLISH: Would that we schoolmen, who do not know how to prove such cases even [sophistically]{g:sophistice}[^1] by a lively spark of [evidence]{g:evidencia}, would apply ourselves to the knowledge and uncovering of the underlying necessary truth, which is today very much hidden from us on account of idle occupation with impossible conclusions that follow from impossible principles: as is clear especially of the cases in which it is posited that God [annihilates]{g:adnichilare} his [creature]{g:creatura}, or that [the Word]{g:verbum} [lays aside]{g:dimittere} natures earlier [assumed]{g:assumere} and, by assuming, communicates other persons in the same natures.[^2] It is granted, therefore, that just as he assumed a man [freely, as between contradictories]{g:libere-contradictorie}, and consequently most contingently, so he can, absolutely, necessarily, now and always, make or cause that he assumed no one, and that there is not, was not, or will not be any creature; indeed, that he himself was not, or will no longer be, the Word of God.[^3] This is clear on the supposition that there is no time. For God necessitates himself by eternal volitions to everything that he effects, and yet he cannot effect anything except in the highest degree freely [and] contingently, since that necessity is not one of coercion by a superior cause that limits [him]; and consequently not of purely natural inclination either, but [necessity of supposition]{g:necessitas-supposicionis}, as I have set out at length elsewhere. On the first side were the Ebionites, in the time of blessed John the evangelist, saying "Christ is a mere man and not God"; and this is heresy 10, which Augustine recounts in his book *On Heresies*.[^1] After them came the heretics the Theodotians,[^2] who in like manner denied the [divinity]{g:divinitas} of Christ; and this is heresy 33. ### 05-e ¶2 · 81.27–82.30  (3 sed/autem)

Nor is there [colour]{g:color}[^7] for this: "If a person, who is the common humanity, suffers, then that [humanity] does too"; since the common nature, abstracted, does not formally receive personal predications of this kind. And much more evidently it is not [82] necessary that the Father suffer, even if the Son, who is another person and the same essence, suffers. Nor would it be possible for the Word to suffer, the Father not suffering, unless the Word were both something that the Father is not and a person other than the Father is.[^8] Hence, since the Word is the [assumed]{g:assumere} humanity, more distant from the Father than the body from the soul, it is clear that there is no colour for [inferring]{g:concludere}: "If the soul suffers with the body, since each is the same person of the man, then by the same token the Father suffers with the Son"; since between them there is a personal distinction. Hence such heretical paralogisms please both young and grown men more today than the solid arguments of the scriptures. Hence to those ignorant of the nature of universals this sophistical deduction seems an insoluble argument: "If Peter laughs, Paul eats, and so of other personal acts, and every man is [the nature of the species]{g:natura-specifica}, then that nature of the species so laughs, or otherwise acts personally." And such [quibbles]{g:argucie}, occupying place and time, rank themselves, pleasing very many, among probable [evidences]{g:evidencia},[^9] but undoubtedly as Thersites [does].[^10] And I have [made clear]{g:declarare} elsewhere, in the matter *On Universals*, that the common humanity does not laugh or eat, although it is each person of man, who does so.[^11] Correspondingly the common Godhead does not beget nor is begotten, nor does it rejoice [humanly]{g:humanitus} nor grieve; although it is the Father who begets, [and] the Word who is begotten and, as to [the assumed man]{g:homo-assumptus}, suffers and grieves above other mortals. It befits theologians to know such [sophistical entanglements]{g:intricaciones-sophistice} in order to avoid them, since they are superficial colours [cloaking]{g:palliare} poisonous heresies and leading those unwary in logic into very many errors. And this is the reason why Augustine so diligently recounts the heresies about Christ with their evidences: that they may be the more cautiously guarded against.

### 05-e ¶3 · 82.31–84.9  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 82] **Sed** inter omnes hereses Nestoriana et Eutichiana sunt hodie eo diligencius precavende, quo theologi eciam ‘qui videntur aliquid’ sunt iam ad sapiendum predictas hereses [p. 83] proniores. Scribit enim Augustinus de Heresi 89 et 90 in fine libri sui De Heresibus, quod Nestorius episcopus contra fidem catholicam dogmatizare ausus est, Christum Dominum Deum nostrum hominem esse tantum; nec illud, quod ‘mediator Dei et hominum’ effectus est, in utero virginis de Spiritu Sancto fuisse conceptum; **sed** postea Deum homini¹ fuisse permixtum, nec Deum-hominem fuisse passum sepultumque² dicebat; evacuare³ contendens omne nostrum remedium, quo Verbum Dei hominem sic suscipere dignatus est in utero virginis, ut una persona fieret Dei et hominis; propter quod sic signanter natus, passus, mortuus, et pro nostris peccatis resurgens, ascendit in celum. Vidit enim iste⁴ Nestorius quod humanitas assumpta non fuit divinitas,⁵ et hinc paralogisatus credidit⁶ quod non fuit Verbum Dei, concedens cum heresi 73 quod Verbum non patitur nisi divinitas⁵ paciatur; et sic, cum illam⁷ esse personam sit suppositum,⁷ dixit quod in Christo fuerunt due persone; et per consequens Christus vel⁸ esset aggregatum ex his, vel persona Verbi non passionata humanitus, ut nec passa, mortua, nec sepulta; quia non vidit quomodo due tam distantes nature possent⁹ esse utraque eadem persona. Quidquid **autem** moderni dixerunt, illo modo intelligunt eum Augustinus, Boecius, Anselmus, et alii capitales doctores concorditer ante Petrum Lombardum.¹⁰ Aliter enim non tam crebro dicerent,¹¹ quod utraque natura tam corpus quam anima est eadem persona hominis; et quod in Christo tam divinitas⁵ quam humanitas est eadem persona Verbi¹²; et quod aliquid,¹³ quod nos sumus, recenter factum est¹⁴ Verbum; ut dicit Augustinus super Ps. 80. Euticiani autem¹⁵ volentes declinare a Nestorianis cum Appollinaristis coinciderant in caribdim, negantes naturam [p. 84] nostram cum proprietatibus et accidentibus ipsam consequentibus per veram humanitatis assumpcionem fuisse in Christo, et per consequens omnes acciones vel denominaciones humanas negant¹ esse in Christo¹ formaliter, nescientes exinde consequi quod² toti corpori Christi mistico auferri oporteat que capiti defuissent; ut, si Christus non patitur vel meretur, multo evidencius nullum³ membrum; cum in capite exemplari oporteat quidquid perfeccionis fuerit⁴ esse in posteriores derivatum.⁴

ENGLISH: But among all the heresies the Nestorian and the Eutychian are today to be guarded against the more diligently, as theologians too "who seem [to be] something"[^12] are now the more prone to savour the aforesaid heresies. [83] For Augustine writes of heresies 89 and 90, at the end of his book *On Heresies*,[^13] that Nestorius, a bishop, dared to teach as dogma, against the catholic faith, "Christ, our Lord God, is only a man"; nor [did he say] that that which was made "mediator of God and men" was conceived in the womb of the virgin from the Holy Spirit, but that afterwards God was mingled with the man; nor did he say that the God-man suffered and was buried: striving to empty out all our remedy, by which the Word of God deigned so to take up a man in the womb of the virgin that one person should come to be of God and of man; on account of which, so signally born, having suffered, died, and risen for our sins, he ascended into heaven. For this Nestorius saw that the assumed humanity was not the divinity,[^14] and hence, misled by a paralogism, believed that it was not the Word of God, granting with heresy 73 "the Word does not suffer unless the divinity suffers"; and so, since it had been supposed that it is a person,[^15] he said that in Christ there were two persons; and consequently Christ would either be an aggregate of these, or the person of the Word not humanly [subject to passion]{g:passionatus}, so as neither to have suffered, died, nor been buried: because he did not see how two natures so distant could each be the same person. But whatever the moderns have said, Augustine, Boethius, Anselm, and the other chief doctors before Peter Lombard with one accord understand him in that way. For otherwise they would not so often say that each nature, both body and soul, is the same person of the man; and that in Christ both the divinity and the humanity are the same person of the Word; and that something which we are has newly been made the Word,[^16] as Augustine says on Psalm 80, Homily 48, *On the Visitation of the Sick* 2, and elsewhere.[^17] And the Eutychians, wishing to turn aside from the Nestorians, had fallen with the Apollinarists into Charybdis, denying that our [84] nature, with the properties and accidents that follow on it, was in Christ through a true assumption of the humanity; and consequently they deny that all human actions or [namings]{g:denominacio} are formally in Christ, not knowing that from this it follows that whatever had been lacking to the head must be taken away from the whole [mystical body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi-misticum}: so that, if Christ does not suffer or merit, much more evidently no [member]{g:membrum} [does]; since in the exemplary head whatever there was of perfection must be derived into its posterity.[^18]

### 05-e ¶4 · 84.10–84.15  (3 sed/autem)

### 06-a ¶3 · 85.14–86.15  (4 sed/autem)

ENGLISH: This is confirmed, first, from the fact that neither the body nor the soul, since [each] is a [qualitative part]{g:pars-qualitativa} of the [supposit]{g:suppositum}, is [of itself]{g:per-se} in a species, but the supposit itself, to whose species parts of this kind are reduced. Since, therefore, Christ [as to the Godhead]{g:secundum-deitatem} is not in a species, and his humanity is not of itself in a species, as those say who deny "it is a supposit", it remains that Christ is a supposit of itself in a species. But in which, I ask, if not in the human? Nor will a catholic dare to say that the body and soul of Christ were only [imaginary]{g:fantasticus}, not of a like species with the parts of other men. But when one converses [86] with men who posit "all universals are signs", one must speak otherwise: for instance, supposing that every human nature were [assumed]{g:assumere} by [the Word]{g:verbum}, and he uttered such spoken and written words as "man is a rational animal", and correspondingly elicited concepts as we do; then, just as the human species would not on that account fail on the side of the thing, so such univocal signs would be true as before, supposing that those concepts remain after the assumptions, signifying naturally and univocally as before. For otherwise, with Christ alone existing of the human species, he would truly know, in the former univocal sense, [not only]{g:nedum} "no man is God" but "no man is an animal";[^2] since the species would not be, when any supposit of it whatever was lacking. Which seems to me mad to say, since Christ would most of all perfect his genus; and consequently, if every man be God, the species of man is not on that account taken away.

### 06-a ¶4 · 86.16–86.29  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 86] 2°. confirmatur ex hoc quod Christus est formaliter homo humanitate consimilis racionis cum humanitate alterius: ergo est univoce homo cum alio. Patet consequencia eo quod, quantumcunque⁹ subiecta variantur, dummodo¹⁰ forme, a quibus recipiunt predicaciones huiusmodi, sunt consimilis racionis, predicaciones sunt univoce; ut, quantumcunque subiecta¹¹ corpora¹¹ variantur, dummodo habent per totum albedines consimilis racionis, sunt alba univoce.¹² Quod **autem** humanitates¹³ Christi et aliorum essencialiter conveniunt exinde evidet, quod dimissa humanitate foret illa consimilis racionis cum alia; ergo cum unio sit sibi accidentalis, eo quod potest¹⁴ manere eadem essencia denudata;¹⁵ sequitur quod stante unione erit eiusdem racionis substancialis¹⁶ cum alia.

ENGLISH: Second, this is confirmed from the fact that Christ is formally a man by a humanity of like kind with the humanity of another; therefore he is univocally a man with another. The [inference]{g:consequencia} is clear, because, however much the [subjects]{g:subiectum} vary, provided the forms from which they receive predications of this kind are of like kind, the [predications]{g:predicacio} are univocal: as, however much the subject bodies vary, provided they have whitenesses of like kind throughout, they are white univocally. Now that the humanities of Christ and of others agree essentially is evident from this, that, the humanity [being laid aside]{g:dimittere}, it would be of like kind with another; therefore, since the [union]{g:unio} is accidental to it, because it can remain the same [essence]{g:essencia} stripped [of it], it follows that while the union stands it will be of the same substantial kind with another.[^3]

### 06-a ¶6 · 87.14–88.2  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 87] Item omne suppositum speciei humane⁹ est homo univoce; Christus est suppositum speciei humane,⁹ cum sit filius hominis; ergo est homo univoce. Maior patet ex hoc, quod omne tale suppositum est illa species, et per consequens quiditas vel racio speciei. Et sic eadem est communis racio illi individuo et cuilibet¹⁰ eidem sibi¹⁰ in specie. Nam secundum Apostolum ed Hebre. 2. ‘nusquam angelos apprehendit, **sed** semen Abrahe ut fieret per omnia fratribus consimilis¹²;’ quia tam secundum naturam corpoream quam¹³ incorpoream,¹³ et illa sunt omnia. ENGLISH: Again, every supposit of the human species is univocally man; Christ is a supposit of the human species, since he is the son of man; therefore he is univocally man. The major is clear from this, that every such supposit is that species, and consequently the [quiddity]{g:quiditas} or account of the species. And so the account common to that individual and to each that is the same as it in species is the same. Since, therefore, those things are by definition said to be univocally predicated to which the same account of the predicate belongs,[^5] it is clear that there cannot be an [individual]{g:individuum} of the human species unless it is univocally man; for otherwise the individual would be man, and not the [definition]{g:diffinicio} or quiddity of man; and this would be to posit "man is not man". And the minor of the argument is clear [from faith]{g:ex-fide}. For otherwise he would not be "mediator [88] of God and men, the man Christ",[^7] as the Apostle says, 1 Timothy 2.
