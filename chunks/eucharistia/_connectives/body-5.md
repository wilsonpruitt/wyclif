# sed/autem extract, wave 5: chunks 03-a 03-b 03-c

### 03-a ¶1 · 53.26–54.4  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 53] Restat superaddere alia argumenta contra doctores novellos pro antique ecclesie sentencia et primo per hoc quod non est Dei destruere suam fabricam in auctorisando ecclesiastica sacramenta; **sed** sic faceret iuxta sentenciam istam noviter adinventam, ergo ipsa [p. 54] est irracionabilis. Minor patet ex hoc quod iuxta hanc viam quantum de pane et vino in toto mundo est transsubstanciatum, tantum desinit atque destruitur tam quoad materiam quam quoad formam.

ENGLISH: It remains to add further arguments against the [newer]{g:novelli} doctors for the [teaching]{g:sentencia}[^1] of the [ancient church]{g:antiqua-ecclesia}; and first by this, that it is not God's part to destroy his own fabric in authorising the church's [sacraments]{g:sacramentum}; now he would do so according to this newly invented teaching; therefore it [54] is unreasonable. The minor is clear from this: that on this way, as much of [bread]{g:panis} and [wine]{g:vinum} in the whole world as is [transubstantiated]{g:transsubstanciacio}, so much ceases and is destroyed, as to the matter as well as to the form.

### 03-a ¶3 · 54.9–54.23  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 54] Item, cum nec remanet materia nec forma transsubstanciati, ne corpus Christi foret nimis gravidatum novella materia, videtur iuxta concedentes possibilitatem anichilacionis quod simpliciter anichiletur natura transsubstanciata; nam possibile est secundum illos quod anichiletur tam panis quam vinum et succedant corpus Christi et sanguis sacramentaliter sub istis accidentibus, ut modo videmus, **sed** quomodocunque foret tunc ex parte panis aut vini est materia; ergo nunc est irrevocabiliter anichilata in consecracione. Assumptum est famosum aput illos, cum substancia potest anichilari servato accidente et econtra, ut inquiunt, nec obest quin Christus potest pro eodem instanti multiplicare corpus suum sub illis accidentibus, cum sint motus omnimode separati.

ENGLISH: Again, since neither the matter nor the form of the transubstantiated thing remains, lest the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} be [exceedingly]{g:nimis} burdened[^2] with new matter, it seems, according to those who grant the possibility of [annihilation]{g:anichilacio}, that the transubstantiated [nature]{g:natura} is annihilated [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}; for it is possible according to them that both the bread and the wine be annihilated and the body of Christ and the blood succeed [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} under these [accidents]{g:accidens}, as we now see; now, however it would then be on the part of the bread or the wine, so it is with the matter;[^3] therefore it is now irrevocably annihilated in the consecration. The [assumption]{g:assumptum} is well known among them, since a substance can be annihilated while the accident is preserved, and conversely, as they say; nor does it stand in the way that Christ can at the same instant multiply his body under those accidents, since the motions are separate in every way.

### 03-a ¶7 · 56.26–57.27  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 56] Item, secundum Augustinum super Joh., Omelia XXVIᵃ, super isto Joh. VIᵒ, 56: Caro mea vere est cibus et sanguis meus vere est potus: In illis, inquit, rebus Deus commendavit nobis hoc sacramentum, [p. 57] que ad unum aliquid reducuntur ex multis. Namque ex multis granis panis conficitur, et ex multis racemis vinum confluit; nunc secundum adversarios istud foret hereticum, cum plane implicat panem et vinum post consecracionem hostie remanere; ideo non in illis rebus **sed** in accidentibus que ignorant dicunt quod accipimus corpus Christi. Cum ergo Deus decrevit nobis dare donum tam magnum, videtur convenire sue veritati magnifice quod dedit nobis ipsum in velamento honorifico illusionem hominis excludente. Omnis enim talis decepcio est mala, cum homo naturaliter innititur cognoscere veritatem, ideo cum sciencia veritatis sit bona et utilis, ignorancia veritatis est mala, beatitudini repugnans, ideo dicimus accidencia remanere ut sensus a decepcione immunes reddantur; sensus **autem** interiores iudicant de substanciis materialibus, cum sint sensibilia per accidens; ideo magis malum foret in illis decipi. Cum ergo sensus hominis tam exteriores quam interiores iudicant illud remanens esse panem et vinum rei non consecrate simillimum, videtur quod non convenit domino veritatis tantam illusionem inducere in graciose communicando donum tam dignum; non enim punit nisi citra condignum propter culpam que meruit sic puniri. Unde ergo puniret decepcione tam ignobili eciam beatum qui sacramentaliter assumeret corpus suum?

ENGLISH: Again, according to Augustine on John, Homily 26, on this text of John 6[:56], "My flesh is [truly]{g:vere} meat and my blood is truly drink":[^10] "In those things," he says, "God has commended this sacrament to us, [57] which are brought back to some one thing out of many. For out of many grains bread is made, and out of many grapes wine flows together."[^11] Now according to the adversaries this would be heretical, since it plainly implies that the bread and the wine remain after the consecration of the [host]{g:hostia}; so they say that we receive the body of Christ not in those things but in accidents of which they are ignorant. Since therefore God decreed to give us a gift so great, it seems to befit his magnificent truth that he gave it to us in an honourable veil that excludes man's [illusion]{g:illusio}. For every such deception is evil, since man naturally strives to know the truth; so, since knowledge of the truth is good and useful, ignorance of the truth is evil, [incompatible]{g:repugnare} with blessedness; so we say that the accidents remain, so that the senses may be rendered immune from deception. Now the inner senses judge of material substances, since these are perceptible [by accident]{g:per-accidens};[^12] so it would be a greater evil to be deceived in them. Since therefore the senses of man, outer as well as inner, judge that remaining thing to be bread and wine, most like an unconsecrated thing, it seems that it does not befit the Lord of truth to bring in so great an illusion in graciously communicating a gift so worthy; for he does not punish except short of the condign, on account of a fault that has deserved to be so punished. On what ground, then, would he punish with so ignoble a deception even a blessed man who sacramentally took his body?

### 03-b ¶2 · 58.8–59.3  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 58] Unde sanctus Thomas super IVᵒ, distinccione XIᵃ, articulo IIᵒ glosat beatum Ambrosium in libro suo de sacramentis et ponitur in De Consecracione, distinccione IIᵃ, Panis est in altari: Hoc est, dicit Thomas, quod est sub speciebus panis, primo fuit panis et postmodum corpus Christi. **Sed** Magister IVᵒ Sentenciarum, distinccione Xᵃ, capitulo IIIᵒ glosat melius talia dicta sanctorum quod utuntur tropo quodam, quo solent signa suorum signatorum sortiri vocabula. Nam ante consecracionem non fuit panis ille sic corpus Christi, **sed** virtute verborum Christi fit signum sic efficax; et sic intelligitur quod panis convertitur in corpus Christi, transsubstanciatur vel quocunque termino mutacionis exprimatur. Aliter tamen intelligunt moderni doctores **sed** non docent, ymmo se ipsos intricantes sibimet contradicunt. In omnibus enim istis angustiantur de subiecto mutacionis, cum repugnat panem et vinum expectare motum talem, ut inquiunt. Angustiantur eciam in declaracione triplicis termini supradicti et quid ponit illa conversio ultra hoc quod poneret panis anichilacio [p. 59] et corporis Christi sub illis accidentibus sacramentaliter successio. Ad tantum enim peioratur universitas corporum sensibilium utrobique.

ENGLISH: Hence Saint Thomas, on [Book] IV, distinction 11, article 2, [glosses]{g:glosare} blessed Ambrose in his book *On the Sacraments*, and it is placed in *De consecratione*, distinction 2, *Panis est in altari*: "That is," says Thomas, "what is under the [species]{g:species} of bread was first bread and afterwards the body of Christ."[^3] But the [Master]{g:magister}, [Book] IV of the *Sentences*, distinction 10, chapter 3, glosses such sayings of the [saints]{g:sancti} better: that they use a certain trope, by which [signs]{g:signum} are wont to take the names of the things they signify.[^4] For before the consecration that bread was not in this way the body of Christ, but by the [power]{g:virtus} of Christ's words it becomes a sign thus efficacious; and in this way it is understood that bread is converted into the body of Christ, is transubstantiated, or by whatever term of change it be expressed. Yet the modern doctors understand otherwise, but they do not teach [it]; indeed, entangling themselves, they contradict themselves. For in all these they are in straits about the [subject]{g:subiectum} of the change, since it is [incompatible]{g:repugnare} that bread and [wine]{g:vinum} should await such a motion, as they say.[^5] They are in straits also in [making clear]{g:declarare} the threefold term mentioned above, and what that conversion posits beyond what the [annihilation]{g:anichilacio} of the bread [59] and the succession of the body of Christ [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} under those [accidents]{g:accidens} would posit. For the totality of [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} bodies is [made worse]{g:peiorare} to the same extent on both sides.[^6]

### 03-b ¶3 · 59.4–60.14  (5 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 59] Item, manifeste videtur secundum istos doctores quod in conversione hostie est dare duos motus natura priores, scilicet desicionem panis secundum se totam et presencie corporis Christi inchoacionem secundum se totam; et illa conversio vel est illa duo vel ad illa formaliter consequens ut passio ad subiectum; cum ergo deficit subiectum illius conversionis nec illa sit de numero accidencium que potuerunt per se esse **sed** naturam corruptam mutari, videtur quod illa conversio sit aggregatum ex illis duobus motibus; cum ergo prior illorum sit idem motus qui foret, si de possibili careret concomitancia aliorum, videtur quod sicut tunc panis anichilaretur sic et modo. Conversio enim cum sit mocio positiva, requirit existenciam essencie subiecti, quia aliter ipsum non plus converteretur quam anichilata essencia, cui succederet corpus novum. Sic **autem** (ut inquiunt) posset esse de Dei potencia absoluta, nec sompnium nec racio docet adversarios oppositum, quare ergo sic non ponunt? et cum anichilacio non sit per accidens anichilacio, videtur quod talis desicio foret anichilacio quolibet motu alio comitante; nam conversio vel presencie corporis Christi acquisicio, cum foret accidentalis illi desicioni, videtur quod non tollit sibi speciem aut quidditatem; et hoc videntur sic opinantes qui negant composicionem continui ex non quantis admittere de possibili illam desicionem panis pro uno instanti et illam conversionem immediate post succedere, quia aliter videtur quod pro [p. 60] instanti quo foret uterque motus remaneret panis nondum conversus, et per consequens conversio foret futura; **sed** hoc implicat duo instancia esse inmediata, sicut via que dicit quod est dare primum instans in quo angeli ceciderunt et ultimum instans in quo steterunt, **sed** maior pars difficultatis foret intelligibiliter declarare quod panis transit, convertitur vel cedit corpori Christi, cum iuxta hoc concedentes possibile est substanciam panis anichilari sive extra sive intra dicta accidencia; ideo preter desicionem panis et incepcionem presencie corporis Christi ponunt conversionem omnimode separatam; **sed** illam nesciunt omnes loquentes exponere; nec docetur in fide scripture vel simbolo ecclesie.

ENGLISH: Again, it plainly seems according to these doctors that in the conversion of the [host]{g:hostia} [one may posit]{g:est-dare} two motions prior by nature, namely the [ceasing]{g:desicio} of the bread according to its whole self and the beginning of the presence of the body of Christ according to its whole self; and that conversion either is those two or is formally consequent on them, as an [attribute]{g:passio} on its subject. Since therefore the subject of that conversion fails, and it is not among the number of accidents that could exist [of themselves]{g:per-se} but [rather] be changed with the nature corrupted,[^7] it seems that that conversion is an aggregate of those two motions; since therefore the prior of them is the same motion that it would be if, as is possible, the concomitance of the others were lacking, it seems that, just as the bread would then be annihilated, so too now. For conversion, since it is a positive motion, requires the existence of the essence of the subject, because otherwise this would no more be converted than an annihilated essence to which a new body succeeded. And thus (as they say) it could be by God's [absolute power]{g:potencia-absoluta}, nor does dream or reason teach the adversaries the opposite: why therefore do they not posit it so? And since annihilation is not annihilation [by accident]{g:per-accidens}, it seems that such a ceasing would be annihilation whatever other motion accompanied it; for conversion, or the acquiring of the presence of the body of Christ, since it would be accidental to that ceasing, seems not to take from it its species or quiddity. And those who opine so and deny the composition of the continuum from non-[quantified]{g:quantus} [parts] seem to admit this: that it is possible for that ceasing of the bread to be at one instant and that conversion to succeed [without intermediary]{g:immediate} after,[^8] because otherwise it seems that at the [60] instant at which both motions would be, the bread would remain not yet converted, and consequently the conversion would be future; but this implies that two instants are without intermediary [to each other], as [does] the way that says one may posit a first instant in which the angels fell and a last instant in which they stood. But the greater part of the difficulty would be to make clear intelligibly that bread passes, is converted or gives way to the body of Christ, since according to those who grant this it is possible for the [substance]{g:substancia} of bread to be annihilated either outside or inside the said accidents; so, besides the ceasing of the bread and the [beginning]{g:incepcio} of the presence of the body of Christ, they posit a conversion separate in every way; but this all the speakers do not know how to expound;[^9] nor is it taught in the faith of scripture or in the creed of the [church]{g:ecclesia}.

### 03-b ¶4 · 60.15–61.2  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 60] In natura **autem**, quando unum corpus convertitur in aliud corpus in quod essencia prioris corporis convertitur, manet essencialiter corpus conversum; ideo corpus sic desinens non convertitur in sua accidencia nec accidencia noviter creata, quia sic posset omnis substancia creata converti in punctum sine anichilacione, cum conversio non dicit melioritatem conversi, et tunc illo puncto anichilato totus mundus converteretur in nichilum et tamen nec ante priorem conversionem nec post; ymmo subtiliores doctores istius secte asserunt quod foret in ista transsubstanciacione anichilacio, nisi panis aliquitas remaneret in carne Christi in quam fit conversio. In cuius testimonium dicunt quod Deus potest de hostia corpus suum sacramentaliter subtrahere et ipsam in naturam eiusdem panis revertere; **sed** certum est quod non remaneret sic panis aliquitas, nisi remaneret quod [p. 61] panis est aliquid et sic panis, cui accidit quod corpus Christi est sacramentaliter ibi presens.

ENGLISH: Now in [nature]{g:natura}, when one body is converted into another body, into which the essence of the prior body is converted, the converted body remains essentially; so a body ceasing in this way is not converted into its accidents nor into newly created accidents, because thus every created substance could be converted into a point without annihilation, since conversion does not imply the bettering of the thing converted; and then, that point being annihilated, the whole world would be converted into nothing, and yet [it would be annihilated] neither before the prior conversion nor after.[^10] Indeed, the subtler doctors of this sect assert that there would be annihilation in this transubstantiation, unless some [somethingness]{g:aliquitas} of the bread remained in the flesh of Christ into which the conversion is made. In witness of this they say that God can sacramentally withdraw his body from the host and turn it back into the nature of the same bread; but it is certain that a somethingness of the bread would not remain in this way unless "[61] bread is something" remained [true], and so bread, to which it [befalls]{g:accidens} that the body of Christ is sacramentally present there.[^11]

### 03-b ¶5 · 61.3–61.13  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 61] Alii **autem** stulte ponunt quod omne desinens convertitur in omne remanens; et sic affirmacio posset converti in negacionem et tolleretur possibilitas anichilacionis; quod negant, sicut et illi qui dicunt quod omne anichilatum convertitur in Deum secundum suum esse supremum, et omne corruptum convertitur in non esse panis aut convertitur in non esse secundum se totum, ut dicitur; nec docetur quod convertitur in corpus Christi, ex hoc quod corpus Christi succedit sub illis accidentibus ibi presens; nec verba sacramentalia illud docent, ut patebit inferius.

ENGLISH: But others foolishly posit "everything ceasing is converted into everything remaining"; and so an affirmation could be converted into a negation, and the possibility of annihilation would be taken away; which they deny,[^12] as do those also who say "everything annihilated is converted into God according to its supreme [being]{g:esse}," and "everything corrupted is converted into the not-being of bread, or is converted into not-being according to its whole self," as is said; nor is it taught that it is converted into the body of Christ from the fact that the body of Christ succeeds under those accidents, present there; nor do the sacramental words teach that, as will be clear below.

### 03-c ¶1 · 61.14–62.10  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 61] Item, secundum adversarium foret possibile quod corpus Christi maneret sacramentaliter sub pane, sicut iam manet sub nudis accidentibus et nec scriptura sacra nec racio nec revelacio docet aliquem quod sit talis transsubstanciacio; ergo stultum foret talem sensum inducere. Cum enim secundum Augustinum IIIᵒ De Doctrina Christiana in fine, omnis veritas sit in scriptura sacra, pertineret prepositis perscrutari fundamentum suum ibidem, ne ex falsitate illudant et onerent ecclesiam plus quam debent; et istud movet multos, cum nemo debet credere eciam pape in materia fidei, nisi de quanto se fundaverit in scriptura. **Sed** nec scriptura nec racione nec revelacione potest ecclesia Avinonica fundare predictam transsubstanciacionem, ergo non pocius tenemur ipsam credere quam credidit ecclesia primitiva; sic eciam [p. 62] posset ecclesia onerari mille fallaciis tanquam fide, et posset processu temporis induci lex Antichristi; creditur, inquam, quod in edicione ultima illius decretalis De Fide catholica, capitulo Firmiter in fine, non concurrerunt papa et CXIIIᶜⁱᵐ episcopi, ut contigit in sinodo qua decretum est quod panis et vinum remanent; ideo (ut dixi superius) non est racio quare debemus eis credere plus quam illis, potissime cum fabricatores mendacii de novo subrepserant, quos creditur istam ficticiam invenisse.

ENGLISH: Again, according to the adversary it would be possible for the [body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} to remain [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} under [bread]{g:panis}, just as it now remains under bare [accidents]{g:accidens}; and neither sacred scripture nor reason nor revelation teaches anyone that there is such a [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio}; therefore it would be foolish to bring in such a sense. For since, according to Augustine, *On Christian Doctrine* III at the end, all truth is in sacred scripture,[^1] it would belong to [superiors]{g:prepositi} to search out their foundation there, lest out of falsehood they deceive and burden the [church]{g:ecclesia} more than they ought; and this moves many, since no one ought to believe even the [pope]{g:papa} in a matter of faith except insofar as he has grounded himself in scripture. Now neither by scripture nor by reason nor by revelation can the church of Avignon ground the aforesaid transubstantiation; therefore we are no more bound to believe it than the primitive church believed [it]. In this way, too, [62] the church could be burdened with a thousand [fallacies]{g:fallacia} as though with faith, and in process of time the law of Antichrist could be brought in. It is believed, I say, that in the last edition of that decretal *De fide catholica*, chapter *Firmiter*, at the end, the pope and the hundred and thirteen bishops did not concur, as happened in the synod in which it was decreed that the bread and the [wine]{g:vinum} remain;[^2] so (as I said above) there is no reason why we should believe these more than those,[^3] especially since fabricators of falsehood had newly crept in, who are believed to have invented this [fiction]{g:ficticia}.

### 03-c ¶2 · 62.11–63.22  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 62] Cum ergo subtiliores doctores moderni vident et asserunt quod nichil cogit hoc credere nisi predicta decretalis et ipsa non daret per se fidem fidelibus, videtur quod in isto deficit huius fidei fundamentum. Oportet enim legistas fundantes tam sollempniter novum festum baptismo dimisso aliquid novum atque mirabile inculcare. Unde omnes legiste et eorum auctores nesciunt describere transsubstanciacionem et quomodo ab anichilacione distinguitur cum annexis difficultatibus, quibus exinde ecclesia est involuta. Conceditur tamen quod accidencia illa que quidam vocant panem et vinum sunt honoranda ut signum corporis Christi et sanguinis. **Sed** quantum patres legis veteris cavebant colere ymagines tanquam Deum, ut patet in epistola Jeremie missa Babiloniam per Baruch, tantum debet christianus cavere ne colat illud quod moderni vocant accidens et prior ecclesia [p. 63] vocavit panem et vinum tanquam verum corpus et sanguinem Jesu Christi, quia sicut ille ymagines non poterant se iuvare vel suos cultores contra hostes, latrones vel aves, ut docet Jeremias Baruch ultimo, revera sic nec dictum accidens. Ideo illo dimisso signatum suum quod est corpus Christi et sanguis, ymmo tota Christi humanitas, quantum univoce sufficimus est colenda; et licet illud signum habeat maiorem efficaciam quam signa legis veteris et nostre ymagines (ut patebit posterius), tamen nimis magna infidelitas foret credere illud tam abiectum in natura esse Deum; ymmo sic immolans infideliter facit abhominacionem et despectum Deo sicut alii infideles ydolatre; et illud predicandum foret laycis, ne stulta pietate precipitarentur infideliter in ydolatriam. Nec videtur quod aliqua pars panis aut vini convertitur in aliquam partem corporis Christi nisi per idem in quamlibet et sic in sanguinem et animam et quelibet accidencia corporis dominici absoluta; nam racione istius conversionis sunt omnia de novo ibi presencia; et hoc sufficit ad conversionem huiusmodi, ut videtur.

ENGLISH: Since therefore the subtler [modern doctors]{g:moderni-doctores} see and assert that nothing compels [one] to believe this except the aforesaid decretal,[^4] and this would not of itself give faith to the faithful, it seems that in this the foundation of this faith fails. For the [legists]{g:legiste}, founding so solemnly a new feast, with baptism set aside, must inculcate something new and wonderful.[^5] Hence all the legists and their authors do not know how to [define]{g:describere} transubstantiation, and how it is distinguished from [annihilation]{g:anichilacio}, with the annexed difficulties in which the church has since been entangled. It is granted, nevertheless, that those accidents which some call bread and wine are to be honoured as a [sign]{g:signum} of the body of Christ and of [his] blood. But as much as the fathers of the old law took care not to worship images as God, as is clear in the letter of Jeremiah sent to Babylon by Baruch, so much ought a Christian to take care not to worship that which the [moderns]{g:moderni} call an accident and the prior church [63] called bread and wine as the true body and blood of Jesus Christ; because, just as those images could not help themselves or their worshippers against enemies, robbers or birds, as Jeremiah teaches, Baruch, the last [chapter],[^6] so [in truth]{g:revera} neither [can] the said accident. Therefore, that being set aside, its [thing signified]{g:signatum}, which is the body of Christ and the blood, indeed the whole [humanity]{g:humanitas} of Christ, is to be worshipped as far as we [univocally]{g:univoce} suffice;[^7] and although that sign has greater efficacy than the signs of the old law and our images (as will be clear later), yet it would be [exceedingly]{g:nimis} great [unbelief]{g:infidelitas} to believe that thing, so abject in nature, to be God; indeed, one who sacrifices so commits, unbelievingly, an abomination and contempt toward God, like other unbelieving idolaters; and that ought to be preached to lay people, lest by foolish [piety]{g:pietas} they be cast headlong, unbelievingly, into idolatry. Nor does it seem that any part of the bread or wine is [converted]{g:conversio} into any part of the body of Christ unless, by the same token, into every [part], and so into the blood and the soul and all the [absolute accidents]{g:accidens-absolutum} of the Lord's body; for by reason of that conversion they are all newly present there; and this suffices for a conversion of this kind, as it seems.[^8]

### 03-c ¶3 · 63.23–63.29  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 63] Secundo principaliter arguitur de racione accidentis: nam omne accidens formaliter inherens substancie non est nisi veritas que est substanciam esse accidentaliter alicuius modi, ut hic supponitur, **sed** nulla talis veritas potest esse sine substancia, sicut nulla creatura potest esse sine Deo, ergo non est cumulus talium accidencium sine subiecto que sit hostia consecrata.

ENGLISH: Secondly, [as a main head]{g:principaliter}, it is argued from the account of accident:[^9] for every accident formally inhering in a [substance]{g:substancia} is nothing but a truth, which is "the substance is accidentally of some mode," as is supposed here; now no such truth can be without substance, as no [creature]{g:creatura} can be without God; therefore there is no heap of such [accidents without a subject]{g:accidens-sine-subiecto} that is the consecrated [host]{g:hostia}.

### 03-c ¶4 · 64.1–64.11  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 64] Item, omne accidens predicamentale est univoce accidens cum quolibet, **sed** aliquod accidens, ut omnis respectus in aliquo septem generum, non potest esse nisi informet subiectum suum, ergo nec aliquod; assumptum videtur ex hoc quod, sicut racio unica essendi sit et accidendi, quia aliter non cognosceretur a nobis analoga descripcio vel distinccio accidentis (nam substancia potest inesse et alias separari), ideo videtur quod analoga racio accidentis sit inesse substancie formaliter, non constituendo ipsam quid nec habendo potenciam per se esse.

ENGLISH: Again, every accident of the [categories]{g:predicamentum} is univocally an accident with any other; now some accident, as every [relation]{g:respectus} in any of the seven genera, cannot be unless it informs its [subject]{g:subiectum}; therefore neither [can] any.[^10] The [assumption]{g:assumptum} seems [clear] from this: that, just as there is a single account of being and of being-accidental (because otherwise the analogous [definition]{g:descripcio} or distinction of accident would not be known by us; for a substance can be in [something] and otherwise be separated),[^11] so it seems that the analogous account of accident is to be in substance formally, not constituting it as a what, nor having a [potency]{g:potencia}[^12] to exist [of itself]{g:per-se}.

### 03-c ¶5 · 64.12–64.29  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 64] Item, ponendo quantitatem et qualitatem linitas substancie adhuc oportet ponere informacionem earum qua immediate et formaliter substancia sit quanta et qualis; et illa non potest per se esse, ne sit processus in infinitum, ergo per idem nec aliquod accidens absolutum; assumptum patet eo quod informacio istorum accidencium potest suspendi ipsis manentibus (ut inquiunt), et per consequens oportet dare informacionem que sit vinculum quo substancia et tale accidens colligantur, quia aliter indubie non probabitur quantitas vel qualitas distincta a substancia, **sed** sufficerent partes quantitative et quidditas substancie; per hoc ergo quod substancia esset non tanta nec talis, alias **autem** est contrarie se habens, concludimus utrumque accidens. Et consimili argumento oportet dare informacionem distinctam que sit quantitas et qualitas, non **autem** ficta accidencia sic linita.

ENGLISH: Again, positing [quantity]{g:quantitas} and [quality]{g:qualitas} [smeared]{g:linire} on the substance,[^13] one must still posit their [informing]{g:informacio}, by which [without intermediary]{g:immediate} and formally the substance is [quantified]{g:quantus} and qualified; and that cannot exist of itself, lest there be a process to infinity; therefore by the same token neither [can] any absolute accident. The assumption is clear from this, that the informing of these accidents can be [suspended]{g:suspendere} while they remain (as they say), and consequently one must posit an informing that is the bond by which the substance and such an accident are tied together; because otherwise, without doubt, quantity or quality distinct from substance will not be proved, but the [quantitative parts]{g:pars-quantitativa} and the quiddity of the substance would suffice. From this, therefore, that a substance would be not so great nor such, and at another time is disposed contrariwise, we [infer]{g:concludere} each accident. And by a like argument one must posit a distinct informing which is quantity and quality, and not feigned accidents smeared on in this way.

### 03-c ¶6 · 64.30–65.17  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 64] Item, cum illa accidencia non superfluunt **sed** subiacent tantum divine potencie, poterunt coextendi [p. 65] punctus, linea, superficies; et multo magis forma substancialis poterit per se esse; ex quo generantur mille difficultates, ut puta quod tam quantitas quam qualitas componitur ex suis partibus intensivis, quia coextensis illis et continuatis ad omnem punctum non restat quomodo componerentur, nisi intensive fuit integrum; et de quantitate ac tempore, de materia et forma suasum est alibi multipliciter quod obviant veritati. Videtur eciam contra adversarios quod continuum componitur ex suis partibus indivisibilibus, cum Deo auferente a celo extimam superficiem sine pluri foret residuum mundi finitum et habens superficiem extimam eadem racione qua prius, sicut Deus posset linire unum tale cum alio et continuare partes ad unum punctum; quibus compositis forent duo indivisibilia inmediata in eodem continuo.

ENGLISH: Again, since those accidents do not overflow but lie subject only to the divine power,[^14] point, line [and] surface could be [65] coextended; and much more could [substantial form]{g:forma-substancialis} exist of itself. From this a thousand difficulties are generated: for instance, that quantity as well as quality is composed of its intensive parts, because, these being coextended and [made continuous]{g:continuare}[^15] at every point, there remains no way in which they would be composed, unless it was intensively whole; and about quantity and time, about matter and form, it has been urged elsewhere in many ways that they go against the truth. It seems also, against the adversaries, that the continuum is composed of its indivisible parts, since, if God took away from the heaven its outermost surface and nothing more, the rest of the world would be finite and would have an outermost surface by the same reasoning as before; just as God could smear one such [world] together with another and make the parts continuous at one point; and these being so put together, there would be two indivisibles without intermediary in the same continuum.[^16]
