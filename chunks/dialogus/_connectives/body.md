> [p. 5] VERITAS. Quia autem vellem quod ista lex et cleri specialiter esset recencius memorata, feci illam scribi sepius in duplici testamento. Et sicut nemo potest excusari quin noscat in gradum qui sibi pertineat legem dei, sic nemo potest excusari quin noscat quodammodo matrem suam. Sicut enim oportet servire deo qui est caput ecclesie, sic oportet honorare matrem ecclesiam, que est principaliter conjux sua. Sed sicut habemus de Christo tam secundum divinitatem suam quam humanitatem hic parvam noticiam, sic habemus hic de ecclesia parvam noticiam et confusam. Unde ignorancia [p. 6] legis Christi et partis ecclesie que hic vivit facit communiter membra diaboli et eciam filios ecclesie contra ipsam culpabiliter rebellare. Cum tamen ex primo mandato secunde tabule omnis fidelis debet ad sanacionem hujus matris modo quo deus voluerit laborare, nam numerorum 18 scribitur Dixit Dominus ad aaron in terra eorum nichil possidebitis nec habetis partem inter eos; ego pars et hereditas tua in medio filiorum israel. Filiis autem levi dedi omnes decimas israelis in possessionem pro ministerio quo serviunt mihi in tabernaculo federis et sequitur legitimum sempiternum erit in generacionibus vestris. Nichil aliud possidebunt, decimarum oblacione contenti quas in usus eorum et necessaria separavi. Ex quibus verbis notaret fidelis quod hec non dicit imperator vel papa sed hec dicit dominus tanquam fidem. Nec dubium quin verba ista dominus dirigat ad primam partem ecclesie, scilicet clerum suum, nec est color dicere quod hec verba directa fuerunt clero legis veteris et non nobis; cum debemus nunc vivere spiritualius sicut Christus. Quare ergo virtute istius legis vendicamus nunc decimas, et primam partem negativam hujus legis deserimus. Revera hoc testatur contra nos quod degeneramus a clero Christi in lege sua duplici terrenis possessionibus ocupati. Si ergo illi sunt maledicti qui declinant a mandatis domini manifestum videtur quod clerus qui tam pertinaciter in temporali [p. 7] dominio obviat legi dei est hereticus maledictus. Et sic creditur quod, si consencientes et agentes sunt pariter heretici maledicti, magna pars militancium est divino judicio hereticus maledictus. Et ex illo convincunt ulterius, cum talis apud deum sit indispositus ad convincendum alios super heretica pravitate juxta illa jo 8 qui sine peccato est vestrum mittat in illam lapidem, curia romana est nimis indisposita ad convincendum alios super heretica pravitate. Istud tamen presumit temere, quia est ad sensum misticum castellum quod est contra Christi apostolos ut dicitur M. 21°.

TRUTH. And because I wished that this law, and the clergy's especially, should be remembered the
more freshly, I had it written more than once in the twofold testament. And as no one can be excused
from knowing God's law in the degree that pertains to him, so no one can be excused from
knowing, in some manner, his mother. For as one must serve God, who is the head of the
church, so one must honour mother church, who is chiefly his spouse. But as we have here
little knowledge of Christ, as much according to his divinity as according to his
humanity, so we have here a little and confused knowledge of the church. Hence ignorance [6] of the
law of Christ and of the part of the church that lives here commonly makes members of
the devil, and even sons of the church, rebel culpably against her. Whereas, nevertheless, by the
first commandment of the second table every believer ought to labour for the healing of this mother,
in the way God wills; for in Numbers 18 it is written: "And the Lord said to Aaron: You shall
possess nothing in their land, neither have you a portion among them: I am thy portion and
inheritance in the midst of the children of Israel. And I have given to the sons of Levi all the
tithes of Israel for a possession, for the ministry wherewith they serve me in the
tabernacle of the covenant"; and it follows: "It shall be an everlasting ordinance in your
generations. They shall not possess any other thing, but be content with the oblation or tithes,
which I have separated for their uses and necessities." From these words the believer should note
that it is not the emperor or the pope who says this, but the Lord says it, as [a matter of] faith.
Nor is there any doubt that the Lord directs these words to the first part of the church, namely his
clergy; nor is there any colour for saying that these words were directed to the clergy of
the old law and not to us, since we ought now to live more spiritually, like Christ. Why then do we
now claim the tithes by virtue of this law, and abandon the first, negative part of this law? In
truth this testifies against us that we have degenerated from the clergy of Christ in his twofold
law, being occupied with earthly possessions. If then they are cursed who decline from the
commandments of the Lord, it seems manifest that the clergy which so obstinately, in temporal [7]
lordship, withstands God's law is an accursed heretic. And so it is believed that, if
those who consent and those who act are alike accursed heretics, a great part of the [church]
militant is, by divine judgement, an accursed heretic. And from this they prove further: since
such a one is, before God, unfit to convict others of heretical depravity, according to that in John
8, "He that is without sin among you, let him cast a stone at her," the Roman curia is all too unfit
to convict others of heretical depravity. Yet it presumes this rashly, because in the mystical
sense it is the village that is over against Christ's apostles, as is said in Matt. 21.

> [p. 9] VERITAS. Tercia autem lex veteris testamenti sequitur Ezekiel 44° cap° in hiis verbis. Ait dominus deus, non erit sacerdotibus hereditas; ego hereditas eorum. Et possessionem non dabitis eis in israel; ego enim possessio eorum. Victimam et pro peccato et pro delicto ipsi comedent et omne votum in israel ipsorum erit, et primitiva omnium pecorum progenitorum et omnia libamenta ex omnibus que offeruntur sacerdotum erunt et primitiva ciborum vestrorum dabitis sacerdoti, ut reponat benediccionem domui sue. Omne morticinum et captum a bestia de avibus et de pecoribus non comedent sacerdotes. Talia sunt multa mandata domini, ut patet illi qui in hiis tribus voluerit fideliter quietari. In istis autem tanguntur multe ceremonie, sed omnes ipse sunt cum basali prevaricancia ad sensum alium duplicate. Et cum Christus ex fide sit optimus legis interpres, [p. 10] patet quod sicut ipse qui non venit legem solvere sed implere docuit ipsam debere servari tam opere quam sermone, sic servanda est a sacerdotibus legis gracie, nec capit dictum istud excusacionem, nisi istam nephariam, quod sacerdotes isti quos vides in mandatum istud dei offendere non sunt sacerdotes Christi vel pars ejus; ideo ipsis non attinent iste leges, qui pocius sunt sacerdotes baal vel belial; ideo debent sequi antichristum et per consequens in vita adversari domino jhu Christo. Et sic, si lex domini debet credi, patet quod tales prevaricatores cum suis fautoribus sunt heretici manifesti. Nec credimus [quod] antichristus dispensavit cum istis legibus vel suspendit ipsas quamdiu sibi libuerat. Patet quod ista verba sunt frivola et in die finalis judicii condempnanda. Cum enim tanta sit racio vel major observancie harum legum, modo invalescente cupidine sicut fuit tempore veteris testamenti, patet quod necesse foret sacerdotes modo servare has leges contra avariciam tam sui quam populi, sicut fuit tempore quo non tantum candebat cupiditas. Et videtur peccatum istud invalescere ex cautela speciali diaboli invidentis Christo, qui in temptacione tercia renuit taliter dominari. Ex hoc enim symonia forcius est intrata, parsimonia est suspensa, et evangelizacio cum aliis ministeriis injunctis a Christo est a diabolo istis sacerdotibus interdicta. Per hoc autem inhabiles aspirant ad superiores gradus sacerdocii et impediunt verbum dei predicari in populo; et anelantes ad illud tanquam hereticos persecuntur, et ista persecucio est heresis sue manifestum indicium; [p. 11] nec sufficiunt pauperes et pauci fideles sacerdotes resistere, nisi deus per seculare brachium vel aliunde cicius manus apposuerit adjutrices.

TRUTH. Now the third law of the Old Testament follows, in Ezekiel, chapter 44, in these words:
"Saith the Lord God: The priests shall have no inheritance; I am their inheritance.
Neither shall you give them any possession in Israel, for I am their possession. They shall eat the
victim both for sin and for trespass: and every vowed thing in Israel shall be theirs. And the
firstfruits of all the firstborn of the cattle, and all the libations of all things that are
offered, shall be the priests': and you shall give the firstfruits of your meats to the priest, that
he may return a blessing upon his house. The priests shall not eat of any thing that is dead of
itself or caught by a beast, whether it be fowl or cattle." There are many such commandments of
the Lord, as is clear to him who will rest faithfully in these three. Now in these many ceremonies
are touched on; but all of them, their base being passed beyond, are doubled into another sense.
And since Christ, as faith teaches, is the best interpreter of the law, [10] it is clear that, as he
who came not to destroy the law but to fulfil it taught that it ought to be kept as much in deed as
in word, so it is to be kept by the priests of the law of grace; nor does this saying admit any
excuse but this wicked one: that these priests whom you see offend against this commandment of God
are not priests of Christ or part of him; therefore these laws do not concern them, since they are
rather priests of Baal or Belial; therefore they are bound to follow Antichrist, and consequently to
oppose the Lord Jesus Christ in their life. And so, if the Lord's law is to be believed, it is
clear that such transgressors, with their abettors, are manifest heretics. Nor do we believe that
Antichrist has dispensed from these laws, or suspended them for as long as it pleased him. It is
clear that these words are frivolous, and to be condemned on the day of the final judgement. For
since there is as great a reason, or a greater, for the observance of these laws now, while greed
grows strong, as there was in the time of the Old Testament, it is clear that it would be necessary
for priests now to keep these laws against the avarice both of themselves and of the people, as it
was in the time when greed did not burn so hot. And this sin seems to grow strong from a special
wile of the devil, who envies Christ, who in the third temptation refused to exercise
lordship in this way. For by this simony has entered the more strongly, frugality is
suspended, and the preaching of the gospel, with the other ministries enjoined by Christ, is
forbidden to these priests by the devil. And by this the unqualified aspire to the higher grades of
the priesthood and hinder the word of God from being preached among the people; and those who long
for it they persecute as heretics, and this persecution is a manifest token of their own heresy;
[11] nor are the poor and few faithful priests sufficient to resist, unless God quickly lays helping
hands to it, through the secular arm or from elsewhere.

> [p. 11] VERITAS. Ex multiplici testimonio legis gracie patet quomodo Christus prima veritas autorizavit istam sentenciam tam opere quam sermone. Et cum ex fide ipse sit autor primus, quia deus et homo, patet quomodo ista sententia sit fides elaborata vivacius. Et sic omnis catholicus debet credere, predicare et defendere istam fidem. Ex hoc enim Christus ipsam sic docuit ut foret exemplar suis sacerdotibus, ut patet de apostolis, ad vivendum; ymmo cunctis fidelibus ad mundanam superbiam deserendum. Et hec racio quare fides evangelica tam crebro in ecclesia est edocta; quia totum evangelium redundat in istam sentenciam tanquam finem. Pauca autem testimonia sufficit hic adducere, particulariter ad istam fidem vigilancius memorandum. Nam M. 10 postquam jacobus et johannes aspiraverant ad excellenciam super alios, dicit Christus. Scitis quia hii qui videntur principari gentibus dominantur eis et principes eorum potestatem habent ipsorum. Non est autem ita in vobis, sed quicunque voluerit fieri major erit minister vester, et quicunque primus voluerit esse vobis erit omnium servus. Nam et filius hominis non venit ut ministraretur [p. 12] ei, sed ut ministraret et daret animam suam redempcionem pro multis Ex ista fide patet luce clarius quomodo Christus tam opere quam sermone docuit apostolos et suos vicarios prioritatem atque majoritatem mundanam relinquere. Patet 2° quomodo Christus magister optimus distinxit inter has duas prioritates, scilicet mundanam et evangelicam, et ipsam primam a suis sacerdotibus separavit. Non est, inquit, ita in vobis. Et patet tercio penes quid prioritas sive majoritas evangelica debeat mensurari. Ille autem qui est humilior servitivior et paciencior est prior vel major coram domino jhu Christo. Talis enim fuit ipse coram deo et propterea fuit primus. Et istam fidem notarent prelati ecclesie et papa precipue; quia pervertentes illam ad sensum contrarium et affectantes majoritatem mundanam more gencium, forent prelati heretici in isto perverso dogmate principales. Cum ergo Christus docet tam expresse in verbis istam sentenciam, et tam laboriose atque diutine exemplat in opere, nullus prelatus est antichristus pocior quam ille qui huic fidei est contrarius opere et sermone. Nec solum isti prelati antichristiani sed mundani domini consencientes isti facinori sunt, ut sunt, capitales inimici domini jhu Christi quia faciunt partem perfidam contra Christum.

TRUTH. From the manifold testimony of the law of grace it is clear how Christ, the first
truth, authorised this teaching as much in deed as in word. And since, as faith teaches, he is
the first author, because God and man, it is clear how this teaching is faith worked out the more
vigorously. And so every catholic ought to believe, preach and defend this faith. For on this
account Christ so taught it, that it should be an exemplar to his priests, as is clear of
the apostles, for living; indeed, to all the faithful, for forsaking worldly pride. And this is the
reason why the gospel faith is so often taught in the church: because the whole gospel
flows back into this teaching as into its end. And it suffices to bring forward a few testimonies
here, in particular so that this faith may be remembered the more watchfully. For in Mark 10, after
James and John had aspired to pre-eminence over the others, Christ says: "You know that they who
seem to rule over the Gentiles lord it over them: and their princes have power over them.
But it is not so among you: but whosoever will be greater shall be your minister, and whosoever
will be first among you shall be the servant of all. For the Son of man also is not come to be
ministered [12] unto: but to minister and to give his life a redemption for many." From this
faith it is clear, clearer than light, how Christ, as much in deed as in word, taught the apostles
and his vicars to relinquish worldly priority and greatness. It is clear[, secondly,] how Christ,
the best master, distinguished between these two priorities, namely the worldly and the
evangelical, and separated the first from his priests: "It is not so," he says, "among you."
And it is clear, thirdly, by what evangelical priority or greatness ought to be measured. And he who
is humbler, more serviceable and more patient is prior or greater before the Lord Jesus Christ.
For such was he himself before God, and therefore he was first. And the prelates of the
church, and the pope especially, should mark this faith; because, if they perverted it to the
contrary sense and coveted worldly greatness after the manner of the Gentiles, they would be
heretical prelates, the principal ones in this perverse dogma. Since, therefore, Christ teaches
this teaching so expressly in words, and so laboriously and for so long exemplifies it in deed, no
prelate is a greater antichrist than he who is contrary to this faith in deed and word. And not
only these antichristian prelates, but the worldly lords consenting to this crime are, as
indeed they are, chief enemies of the Lord Jesus Christ, because they make a faithless party
against Christ.

> [p. 13] VERITAS. Iterum Christus docet in verbis suam pauperiem Mat. 8° cuidam fingenti se velle sequi Christum, dum tamen terrenorum cupiditas fuit causa. Vulpes, inquit, foveas habent, et volucres celi nidos, filius autem hominis non habet ubi capud suum reclinet; quasi diceret: noli sequi me propter spem lucri terreni, cum sim pauperrimus homo mundi, sicut dicit in suo apostolo 2 Cor. 8° Scitis enim graciam domini nostri jhu Christi qui propter vos egenus factus est, ut illius inopia vos divites essetis. Et ad idem sonat evangelium totum concorditer a nativitate Christi que facta est in diversorio usque ad mortem penalem et pauperem que facta est in cruce. Ubi ergo major infidelitas quam prelatos et sacerdotes alios tam cece deserere sanctam fidem. Nec solum dotati presbiteri ipsam deserunt, sed exproprietarii imponentes Christo blasfeme quod ab ipso edocti sunt taliter mendicare. Christus enim fuit homo pauperrimus, cum pauperies sua sonuit in perfeccionem virtutum, et in qualibet perfeccione hujusmodi fuit summus; et tamen citra istam mendicacionem tenebat se in medio, juxta illud quod docet sapienciam salomonis prov. 30 mendicitatem et divicias ne dederis michi, tribue tantum victui meo necessaria. Et hoc movebat apostolum pᵃ thymo 6° tradentem [p. 14] generaliter sacerdotibus pro regula istam fidem: Nichil, inquit intulimus in hunc mundum, haut dubium nec aliquid inde auferre possumus. Habentes igitur alimenta et quibus tegamur hiis contenti simus. Et hic videtur fidelibus si auderent depromere, quod papa et tota secta sua dotata temporali dominio sit hereticus induratus, quia scripture que non potest solvi pertinaciter contrarius; sed quis est hereticus nisi talis? Unde petrus pᵃ petri 5° precipit: pascite qui in vobis est gregem dei, providentes non coacte sed spontanee secundum deum, neque turpis lucri gracia sed voluntarie. Neque ut dominantes in clero, sed forma facti gregis ex animo, ut cum apparuerit princeps pastorum percipiatis immarcessibilem glorie coronam. Sed sicut vita pape et cleri sui cesarii soporavit istam petri sentenciam, sic decretales epistole soporaverant istam fidem. Ideo videtur evidens quod prelati ignorantes istam fidem tam opere quam sermone sunt perfidi antichristi. Et cum ex fide claret fidelibus quod in isto unico verbo petri sit salubrior sentencia quam in omnibus epistolis decretalibus vel bullis papalibus, patet quod stulte clerici deserunt discere hanc fidem salubrem et student ista apocrifa que seducunt.

TRUTH. Again, Christ teaches his poverty in words, in Matthew 8, to a certain man who feigned that he
wished to follow Christ, while yet greed for earthly things was the cause: "The foxes," he says,
"have holes, and the birds of the air nests; but the Son of man hath not where to lay his head"; as
if he said: Do not follow me in hope of earthly gain, since I am the poorest man in the world; as he
says in his apostle, 2 Cor. 8: "For you know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, who for your sakes
became poor, that through his poverty you might be rich." And the whole gospel sounds in
agreement to the same effect, from the birth of Christ, which took place in an inn, to his penal and
poor death, which took place on the cross. Where, then, is greater unbelief than
for prelates and other priests so blindly to desert the holy faith? Nor
do the endowed presbyters alone desert it, but also the propertyless, blasphemously
imputing to Christ that they were taught by him to beg in this way. For Christ was the poorest
of men, since his poverty resounded in the perfection of the virtues, and in every perfection of
this kind he was highest; and yet, short of this begging, he kept himself in the mean, according to
what the Wisdom of Solomon teaches, Prov. 30: "Give me neither beggary, nor riches: give me only the
necessaries of life." And this moved the apostle, 1 Tim. 6, delivering [14] generally to priests as a
rule this faith: "We brought nothing," he says, "into this world: and certainly we can carry nothing
out of it. Having therefore food and wherewith to be covered, with these we are content." And
here it seems to the faithful, if they dared to bring it out, that the pope and his whole
sect, endowed with temporal lordship, is a hardened heretic, because
stubbornly contrary to scripture, which cannot be broken; now who is a heretic but such a one?
Hence Peter, in 1 Peter 5, commands: "Feed the flock of God which is among you, taking care of it,
not by constraint but willingly, according to God: not for filthy lucre's sake but voluntarily:
neither as lording it over the clergy but being made a pattern of the flock
from the heart; that when the prince of pastors shall appear, you may receive a never fading crown
of glory." But as the life of the pope and of his caesarean clergy has
lulled this teaching of Peter to sleep, so the decretal letters have lulled this faith to sleep.
Therefore it seems evident that prelates who are ignorant of this faith, as much in
deed as in word, are faithless antichrists. And since, as faith teaches, it is
plain to the faithful that in this single word of Peter there is a more wholesome teaching than in
all the decretal letters or papal bulls, it is clear that clerics foolishly leave off
learning this wholesome faith and study these apocrypha, which lead astray.

> [p. 15] MENDACIUM. Nimis patenter reseras conversacionem, quam increpas prelatorum; sed audi partem alteram in responsionibus et repplicacionibus, et tunc potes magis evidenter procedere.

FALSEHOOD. Too openly you lay bare the manner of life of the prelates, which you
rebuke; but hear the other side in answers and rejoinders, and then you can proceed more clearly.

> [p. 15] Concedo quidem tibi quod Christus vixit et docuit vitam pauperem sed noluit ipsam esse perpetuam, cum dicat in apostolo allegato quod Christus sic egenus factus est ut illis inopia vos divites essetis. Superbia autem et cupiditas sacerdotum legis veteris qui Christum occiderant requirebant Christum sic vivere et docere. Sed postquam repressa sunt ista peccata dignitas sacerdotum exigit quod clerus ad priorem gloriam revertatur; in cujus signum predia quibus dotatur ecclesia vocantur patrimonium crucifixi. Christus enim virtute passionis sue et meriti adquisivit sue ecclesie omnia ista dominia, et, si, justicia posset procedere, ampliora; et cum ista sentencia concordant concessiones dominorum temporalium et vite papales patule atque leges.

I grant you indeed that Christ lived and taught a poor life, but he did not will it to be
perpetual, since he says, in the apostle already cited, that Christ was made needy in such a way
"that through his poverty you might be rich". And the pride and greed of the
priests of the old law, who had killed Christ, required Christ to live and teach so.
But after these sins were repressed, the dignity of priests demands that the clergy
return to its former glory; in token of which the estates with which the church is
endowed are called "the patrimony of the crucified". For Christ, by virtue of his passion and
merit, acquired for his church all these lordships, and, if justice could take its
course, greater ones; and with this teaching agree the grants of the temporal lords and
the manifest lives and laws of the popes.

> [p. 15] VERITAS. Video quod fraudulenter misceas quedam vera cum falsis. Concipis quidem veritatem fidei quod Christus tam in vita quam doctrina docuit pauperiem observari. Sed quod consequenter addis, hanc Christi sentenciam durare ad tempus modicum et postea clerum suum redire ad seculum amplius quam vixerunt legis veteris sacerdotes, infideliter garrulas et contrarie racioni. Si enim Christus tam diu et [p. 16] tam dure reprobavit hoc peccatum in sacerdotibus legis veteris, quomodo sacerdotem legis gracie nove movet redire ad dictam avariciam sicut canis redit ad vomitum? Jam enim plus ditantur et gloriantur in seculo clerici et precipui sacerdotes quam ditabantur vel gloriabantur in seculo scribe pharisei vel principes sacerdotum. Ideo, cum tanta sit racio perpetuacionis hujus legis Christi ut patet de statu innocencie et statu glorie, patet quod ista lex Christi debet precipue in novissimis temporibus observari; tunc enim diabolus plus temptat homines ad avariciam. Ideo est magis necessarium in clero, qui debet esse speculum populi, apponere medicinam. Que ergo racio, cum hodie tantum dominatur cupiditas quare Christi pauperies tantum deprimitur et antichristi rabies tantum extollitur? Numquid racio concordat quod hec lex Christi in racione exemplari eterna sit per trecentos annos tantummodo observata et lex antichristiana contraria in temporibus tam periculosis sit perpetuata. Idem enim est sic dicere et extollere diabolum supra Christum, ac si infidelis annueret quod lex Christi tam necessaria et salubris durabit ad horam sub colore divini beneplaciti, sed lex antichristi plena veneno atque mortifera duret perpetuo ut excitet homines ad peccandum. Sed quis Christianus talem blasfemam responsiunculam tolleraret? Ulterius novit catholicus quomodo sunt due diviciarum maneries, scilicet divicie temporales et divicie celestes et spirituales. Et prime divicie vix cum virtutibus moderate ducunt ad celestes divicias, que sunt vere, de quibus loquitur apostolus Christi supra. Ideo haberes conscienciam super ista loquela heretica, quam innuis super fide scripture apostoli. Oportet enim per penalitatem et pauperiem hic in via mereri celestes divicias et beatitudinem in patria.

TRUTH. I see that you craftily mix certain true things with false. You do indeed grasp the truth
of faith, that Christ, as much in life as in doctrine, taught that poverty was to be observed. But
what you add next, that this judgement of Christ lasts for a short time, and that afterwards his
clergy returns to the world more fully than the priests of the old law lived [in it], you prattle
in unbelief and contrary to reason. For if Christ so long and [16] so harshly reproved this sin in
the priests of the old law, how does he move a priest of the new law of grace to
return to the said avarice, as a dog returns to its vomit? For clerics, and
the leading priests, are now more enriched and glorified in the world than the scribes, Pharisees
or chief priests were enriched or glorified in the world. Therefore, since the reason for the
perpetuation of this law of Christ is so great, as is clear from the state of
innocence and the state of glory, it is clear that this law of Christ ought to be observed
especially in the last times; for then the devil tempts men more to avarice. Therefore it is more
necessary to apply the medicine in the clergy, which ought to be the mirror of the people. What
reason is there, then, since greed so lords it today, why Christ's poverty is so
much put down and Antichrist's rage so much exalted? Does reason agree that this law of Christ,
eternal in its exemplar reason, should have been observed for three hundred years only, and the
contrary antichristian law be perpetuated in times so perilous? For to say this is the same as
to exalt the devil above Christ, as if an unbeliever were to grant that the law of Christ, so
necessary and wholesome, will last for a time, under colour of the divine good pleasure, but that
the law of Antichrist, full of poison and deadly, should last for ever, to stir men up to
sin. But what Christian would tolerate such a blasphemous little answer? Further, the catholic

TRUTH. From what has been said there is a middle term that leads on to answering the other points brought in afterwards. For we ought not to receive papal bulls, or in general the sayings of that curia, as faith; since they are wayfarers able to sin and not in general inspired, and the experience of the eyes teaches that they are often deceived and often err against the rule of truth. And so we ought to believe, on sufficient evidence, the companions with whom we commonly live; but we are not bound, as faith teaches, to believe every spirit that we hear, nor ought we to believe in indulgences, any more than the pope or his curia [ought], since he does not know whether the person to whom he grants the said indulgence is to be damned, just as he does not know whether it is of God's will to grant what he himself thus assents to in bulls. Therefore no great discretion or wisdom shines out in these indulgences, especially if they are not founded on Christ's deeds or words, nor on the wisdom of any pope before Satan was loosed. What evidence, then, moves these satraps thus to set themselves against the will of the Lord? Or what covetousness would urge this prelate thus to write and seal what is doubtful to himself, where it is likely that it is a presumptuous lie? Therefore it is evident to many that this presumption is a sin; for with equal evidence he could condemn a predestinate son of God to Tartarus. Therefore some wayfarers say that on account of such a rash judgement [a man is] neither more nor less [blessed], but on account of merit or demerit with respect to God. And the same is probably to be thought of many episcopal letters granting forty days of pardon. And as for [26] letters of fraternity: since it seems certain that they themselves do not know whether they are a reprobate band of the foreknown, what then does it avail them to sell a share in their suffrages, since they do not know whether any merits remain to them for attaining blessedness? Nor is it inferred from these things that anyone of the church militant should despair; but that he should not trust in these apocryphal and uncertain things; indeed rather, that he should gather his whole hope in the grace of Christ. And this would do away with the final despair of many who have been deceived in these suffrages. And as for human contracts or civil dealings, it is clear that they are commonly made with sin, since [they are made] with unlawful oaths and testimonies. Therefore forgive the apostolic man the wrong, who because of the danger avoids this. And as for inheritances descending from given fathers, priests ought not to be anxious about such things; just as none ought to presume to charge a given heir that he is not descended from those parents, unless he has been moved by the Holy Spirit to impugn such descent. And so it is rightly inferred that there are many truths in human acts, present as well as past, about which wayfarers ought not to be anxious. Therefore it would be more prudent for a man to refuse for himself what is neutral or irrelevant to his blessedness, a by-way in which many sin, speculatively as well as practically; as is clear from the art of obligation and from contentions about temporal goods to be possessed by inheritance and in a temporal manner.

> [p. 26] MENDACIUM. Et sic quelibet talis oracio implicat tacite condicionem, si et quatenus placet deo per talis sancti meritum nobis miseris suffragari. Et ut videtur multis nulla oracio porrecta specialiter sancto Christi est laudabilis, nisi de quanto acuit devocionem in Christum, sic quod si omnes intenciones et oraciones uniri possent in Christum, sincerius directe unite in Christum, esset utilius Christiano omnes oraciones specialiter Christo porrigere. Ideo dicunt quidam quod porrecciones oracionum ad istos sanctos sepe [p. 28] sunt superflue vel nocive. Conceditur ergo quod non est articulus fidei appropriare cuilibet sancto de quo dicimus specialem articulum fidei sed beatitudinem illorum supponimus secundum majorem evidenciam vel minorem. Unde absit quod omnes legende sanctorum in ecclesiam introducte sint fides catholica, cum in festo corporis Christi de sancto sanctorum scribitur quod accidencia sunt in eucaristia sine subjecto, quod non fuit fides tempore augustini. Ideo dicit in de sermone Domini in monte quod multa vera, licet in legendis talibus inserantur non sunt fides catholica; sicut exemplificat de ministro convivii cujus unum brachium a cane rapido est abstractum. Sufficit ergo ad tales oraciones secundarias habere probabilem supposicionem, et de Christo qui fundamentaliter oratur habere cum aliis virtutibus firmam fidem; et patet solucio. In ista tamen materia dicunt scolastici quod aliqua est fides explicita vel expressa et aliqua est fides implicita sive tacita. Fidem autem implicitam dicunt esse quamcunque veritatem juxta illud apostoli prima cor. 13° Caritas omnia credit, et tamen infinite sunt veritates de quibus non oportet nos hic habere fidem explicitam; cum sufficit fideli quod credat quamcunque veritatem secundum gradum et formam qua deus vult ipsum hanc credere. Ut carnis resurreccionis est articulus fidei cuilibet Christiano, et tamen non oportet de qualibet persona hominis habere expressum articulum fidei quod resurget. Sed deus vult quod per istam fidem generalem sua militans ecclesia excusetur. Non enim vult quod sua ecclesia cum veritatibus inutiliter oneretur. Communis autem carnis resurreccio erit tempore sempiterno adhuc futura, et postmodum tempore suo presens; sed post diem judicii est elapsa.

FALSEHOOD. Again, you plainly hint that it is not, as a matter of faith, [27] to be believed that those are saints who are inserted in masses, in litanies, and in the other services of the church. To what purpose, then, should we speak of them, unless we believed that they are blessed in the homeland? since the hope of merit in believers must be founded on stable faith.

TRUTH. It has been said here more than once that of the saints whom the church canonizes we suppose, but do not believe as faith, that they are blessed in the homeland. Yet the eleven apostles, with Paul, Magdalene and the others whom the faith of scripture implies to be saints, are to be believed as faith to be blessed; and the others, however greatly they excel in holiness in heaven, we believe to be in blessedness, but short of faith. And therefore the church has ordained that any prayer whatever offered to a saint be directed principally to God. And so every such prayer tacitly implies a condition: if and in so far as it pleases God, through the merit of such a saint, to aid us wretches. And, as it seems to many, no prayer offered specially to a saint of Christ is praiseworthy except in so far as it sharpens devotion towards Christ; so that if all intentions and prayers could be united in Christ, more sincerely united directly in Christ, it would be more profitable for a Christian to offer all prayers specially to Christ. Nor does it avail to believe falsely that Christ, because of the height of his lordship, cannot easily be entreated by us wretches, [and] that therefore it is necessary to have other persons mediating; for Christ, being come a high priest, flows immediately into the wayfarer, and with this is immediately joined to the Godhead. Therefore no saint is more merciful, or more ready to hear a wretch, than Christ is. Nor do the prayers of other saints avail, except in so far as Christ first, of his grace, accepts them. Therefore some say that the offerings of prayers to these saints are often [28] superfluous or harmful.

Therefore it is granted that it is not an article of faith to appropriate to each saint of whom we speak a special article of faith; but we suppose their blessedness according to greater or lesser evidence. Hence far be it that all the legends of the saints brought into the church should be the catholic faith, since on the feast of Corpus Christi it is written of the holy of holies that the accidents are in the eucharist without a subject, which was not the faith in Augustine's time. Therefore he says in *On the Lord's Sermon on the Mount* that many true things, although they are inserted in such legends, are not the catholic faith; as he illustrates by the servant at the banquet, one of whose arms was torn off by a ravening dog. It suffices, therefore, for such secondary prayers to have a probable supposition, and, of Christ, who is prayed to fundamentally, to have firm faith together with the other virtues; and the solution is clear. Yet in this matter the schoolmen say that some faith is explicit or expressed and some faith is implicit or tacit. And they say that implicit faith is any truth whatever, according to that saying of the apostle, 1 Cor. 13, "Charity believeth all things"; and yet infinite are the truths of which we need not have explicit faith here, since it suffices for the faithful man that he believe any truth according to the degree and form in which God wills him to believe it. As, there is an article of faith of the resurrection of the flesh for every Christian, and yet one need not have, concerning each human person, an express article of faith that he will rise again. But God wills that by this general faith his church militant be excused. For he does not will that his church be burdened with truths unprofitably. And the common resurrection of the flesh will be, in everlasting time, still future, and afterwards in its own time present; but after the day of judgement it is past.

Who then would presume so to act against the heavenly marriage which one is bound by the fourth commandment of the decalogue to venerate?

> [p. 33] VERITAS. Licet diabolus et membra ejus multiplicaverunt mendacia contra Christicolas, ille tamen foret nimis neophitus qui propter talia mendacia dimitteret defendere veritatem, cum petrus, johannes et alii in verbis asperioribus persteterunt. Scimus quidem quod non sic loquimur propter amorem aut questum temporalium, sed propter zelum quo afficimur evangelice veritati. Quod si quandoque temptacione diaboli surrepit superbia condolemus. Intendimus ergo purgacionem et perfeccionem cleri, quam scimus non stare in multitudine personarum, sed in observancia status quem Christus instituit; et sicut toto corpore hominis verso in oculos foret indisposicius quam est modo, sic clero facto irregulari quoad statum domini, quamlibet augmentato, et in se imperficitur et nocet matri ecclesie militanti. Et patet quod assumptum est a patre mendacii mendicatum: volo enim totum clerum sicut et me ipsum stare in statu quem Christus instituit, quia scio ex fide quod Christus ex deleccione cleri taliter ordinavit. Scio insuper ex eadem fide quod Christus ordinavit clerum suum expertem dominii secularis. Et scio 3° quod, licet clerus posset in temporalibus pati penuriam, hoc [p. 34] tamen est securius quam habere temporalium affluenciam; cum Christus ordinavit suos apostolos ita pati, et sic debeo niti pro profectu anime proprie et ad hoc sequi regulam domini jhu Christi. Et sic licet videar stultus in isto agens contra profectum proprium, scio tamen sapienciam dei ad hoc movere, et mihi credo ad perpetuam salutem proficere si in isto proposito perseverem. Et sic, diminuta multitudine cleri cesarii et augmentata sua virtute, vel pocius conversa in clerum Christi, ecclesia foret ab apostematibus superfluis expurgata et partes ejus infirme secundum regulam et formam sanacionis voluntati domini conformate. Sed felix talis imitacio, cum ipocrite qui simulant sanctitatem sunt apostemata magis superflua et matri ecclesie magis nocent. Cum evidens sit multis racionibus quod capiens simulatorie statum cleri propter honorem mundanum, dignitatem secularem, vel corporis voluptatem, tanquam dei proditor symoniacus et hereticus in peccatorum voraginem se involvit. Et sic incipiendo a papa usque ad religiosum infimum sunt heretici spissim in ecclesia seminati. Et sic supponis mendaciter quod eo ipso quo quis quo ad seculum habuerit nomen cleri, est clericus de suprema parte ecclesie militantis, sed principes sacerdotum, pharisei et scarioth contradicunt. Et sicut olym isti de secta antichristi practizaverant contra Christum, sic hodie sunt eorum semina derelicta que operantur copiosius contra Christum. Isti enim sunt meretrices que adulterinis signis nituntur celeste conjugium deturpare.

TRUTH. Although the devil and his members have multiplied lies against the worshippers of Christ, yet he would be too much a novice who for such lies would leave off defending the truth, since Peter, John and others persisted in harsher words. We know indeed that we do not speak thus for love or gain of temporal goods, but for the zeal with which we are moved toward the gospel truth. And if at any time, by the devil's temptation, pride creeps in, we grieve over it. We intend, therefore, the purging and perfecting of the clergy, which we know stands not in the multitude of persons but in the observance of the state which Christ instituted; and as, were the whole body of a man turned into eyes, it would be more unfit than it is now, so the clergy, once made irregular with respect to the Lord's state, however much increased, is both made imperfect in itself and harms mother church militant. And it is clear that the assumption was begged from the father of lies: for I wish the whole clergy, as also myself, to stand in the state which Christ instituted, because I know as faith teaches that Christ ordained it so out of love for the clergy. I know besides, from the same faith, that Christ ordained his clergy to have no part in secular lordship. And I know, thirdly, that although the clergy might suffer penury in temporal goods, this [34] is nevertheless safer than to have an abundance of temporal goods; since Christ ordained his apostles so to suffer, and so I ought to strive for the profit of my own soul, and to this end follow the rule of the Lord Jesus Christ. And so, although I may seem foolish in this, acting against my own profit, yet I know that the wisdom of God moves [me] to it, and I believe it profits me toward perpetual salvation if I persevere in this purpose. And so, the multitude of the caesarean clergy being diminished and its virtue increased, or rather [it being] converted into the clergy of Christ, the church would be purged of superfluous abscesses, and its sick parts conformed to the Lord's will according to the rule and form of healing. But happy such an imitation! since hypocrites who feign holiness are abscesses more superfluous, and harm mother church more; since it is evident by many reasons that he who feignedly takes up the state of the clergy for worldly honour, secular dignity, or bodily pleasure, as a traitor to God, a simoniac and a heretic, wraps himself in the whirlpool of sins. And so, beginning from the pope down to the lowest religious, heretics are sown thickly in the church. And so you suppose lyingly that by the very fact that anyone has, as far as the world goes, the name of cleric, he is a cleric of the supreme part of the church militant; but the chief priests, the Pharisees and Iscariot contradict [it]. And as once these of the sect of antichrist had practised against Christ, so today their seeds are left behind, which work more abundantly against Christ. For these are the harlots who strive by adulterous signs to defile the heavenly marriage.

######## CHUNK 18  (2 sed/autem)

> [p. 34] MENDACIUM. Quomodo potes subterfugere quin moliaris destruere matrem nostram, quia nulli dubium isti prelati [p. 35] majores cum suo dominio et confederacione magnorum de suo sanguine extinguunt multa peccata et hereses, faciunt elemosinas multas et magnas et tenent venerabilem domum et magnam familiam que regnis est ad fortitudinem et honorem. Quis ergo intenderet tales prelatos nobiles mendicare? Videtur quod hoc machinans intendit regnum destruere et laxare abenas hominibus ad peccandum.

FALSEHOOD. How can you escape [the charge] that you are striving to destroy our mother? For it is doubtful to no one that these greater prelates, [35] with their lordship and the alliance of great men of their own blood, extinguish many sins and heresies, give alms many and great, and keep a venerable house and a great household, which serves the kingdoms for strength and honour. Who, then, would intend such noble prelates to beg? It seems that whoever contrives this intends to destroy the kingdom and to slacken the reins for men to sin.

> [p. 35] VERITAS. Scimus quod nostra intencio est catholica quia fides. Intendimus enim profectum trium parcium ecclesie secundum armoniam et formam quam dederat jhus Christus. Sed quis infidelis posset inficere hoc sanctum propositum? Christus enim fuit nobilissimus homo mundi et tamen postquam acceperat hunc statum cleri tam in se quam in suis cognatis servavit ad regulam paupertatem humilitatem et dei obedienciam. Et fecit alios quos elegerat in apostolos, quorum quidam erant sui cognati virtute nobiles, istam regulam observare. Quomodo ergo non erubesceret quis gratis accipiens istum statum, et post acceptum degenerans ab eodem, nam in hoc faceret se servum diaboli et peccati. Et ista est servitus maxima, servitute seculi plus horrenda. Et sic magnates, nitentes cognatos suos in dignitatibus magnis preficere, propter honores mundanos et lucrum seculi in seipsis, primo degenerant a domino jhu Christo, quia videntur esse socii furum et fautores hereticorum; quod est contra facta et verba domini, ut patet Mat. 20 de filiis zebedei. Conceditur ergo quod nobilitas generis in prelatis potest esse tam in ipsis quam ecclesia occasio multi boni, sicut et potest esse occasio multi mali, specialiter si virtutum nobilitas non jungatur. Ideo Christus prudens medicus eligendo suos [p. 36] cognatos in apostolos ipsos a mundana superbia, a vita voluptuosa et avaricia coartavit, et istam regulam Christianis posteris reservavit, quia jo 13 dicit ipse: Mandatum novum do vobis ut diligatis invicem; sicut et ego dilexi vos ut et vos invicem diligatis. Et patet quod operacio prelatorum hujusmodi, sicut et operacio luciferi, potest occasionaliter multa peccata extinguere; sicut et potest vocari elemosina et honor regnorum quod est bonorum pauperum spoliacio et flagicium contra simplices ecclesie militantis; et talis conversacio non foret honor regnorum sive utilitas, sed diaboli machinacio introducta. Sic ergo prelatorum divicie atque nobilitas possunt esse occasio tam prelatis quam aliis ad peccandum superbia, avaricia, atque gula, et possunt esse occasio ad tam sibi quam ecclesie promerendum: et nisi assint gracia et virtus primus effectus contigit communius quam secundus. Ideo videamus disposicionem quam Christus adhibuit ad consequendum graciam et virtutes; et vel oportet concedere quod Christus fuit fatuus, vel quod pauperies quam ipse disposuit plus disponit episcopos ad virtutes. Nec sequitur ex isto quod debeant mendicare ab homine, cum Christus, exemplar nostrum, tenebat se in medio et utrumque extremum tanquam culpabile reprobavit. Ideo ista evidencia est similis stulticie cecorum qui baculum suum proiciunt ad astra ut ipsa in sua substancia stulte ledant. Et patet quod istud sentenciare non esset regnum destruere, nec dare hominibus licenciam ad peccandum. Nisi forte infidelis dicat cum sacerdotibus Christum accusantibus, quod ipse subvertit totum regnum judee et imperatoris imperium perturbavit. Ideo sic judicantes secundum faciem sunt communiter tam verbis quam opere infideles.

TRUTH. We know that our intention is catholic, because [it is] faith. For we intend the progress of the three parts of the church, according to the harmony and form which Jesus Christ had given. But what unbeliever could taint this holy purpose? For Christ was the noblest man in the world, and yet, after he had taken up this state of the clergy, he kept as a rule, both in himself and in his kinsmen, poverty, humility, and obedience to God. And he made the others whom he had chosen as apostles, some of whom were his kinsmen, noble in virtue, observe this rule. How then would anyone not blush who, receiving this state freely, after receiving it degenerates from it? For in this he would make himself a servant of the devil and of sin. And this is the greatest servitude, more horrible than the servitude of the world. And so magnates, striving to set their kinsmen over [others] in great dignities for the sake of worldly honours and the world's gain in themselves, in the first place degenerate from the Lord Jesus Christ, since they seem to be "companions of thieves" and abettors of heretics; which is against the deeds and words of the Lord, as is clear in Matthew 20 concerning the sons of Zebedee. It is granted, then, that nobility of birth in prelates can be the occasion of much good, both in themselves and [in] the church, just as it can also be the occasion of much evil, especially if nobility of virtues is not joined [to it]. Therefore Christ, a prudent physician, in choosing his [36] kinsmen as apostles, restrained them from worldly pride, from a voluptuous life and from avarice, and reserved this rule for Christians after him; for in John 13 he himself says: "A new commandment I give unto you, that you love one another; as I also have loved you, that you also love one another." And it is clear that the working of such prelates, like the working of Lucifer, can by occasion extinguish many sins; just as what is the despoiling of the goods of the poor, and an outrage against the simple of the church militant, can also be called alms and the honour of kingdoms; and such a manner of life would not be the honour or the benefit of kingdoms, but a contrivance of the devil brought in. So then the riches and nobility of prelates can be an occasion, both to prelates and to others, of sinning by pride, avarice, and gluttony, and they can be an occasion of meriting both for themselves and for the church; and unless grace and virtue are present, the first effect comes about more commonly than the second. Therefore let us see the disposition which Christ applied for attaining grace and virtues; and either one must grant that Christ was a fool, or that the poverty which he himself disposed disposes bishops more to virtues. Nor does it follow from this that they ought to beg from man, since Christ, our exemplar, kept himself in the mean and reproved each extreme as culpable. Therefore this evidence is like the folly of the blind, who throw their staff at the stars so as, foolishly, to hurt them in their substance. And it is clear that to pronounce this would not be to destroy the kingdom, nor to give men licence to sin. Unless perhaps an unbeliever should say, with the priests accusing Christ, that he subverted the whole kingdom of Judea and disturbed the emperor's empire. Therefore those who judge thus "according to the appearance" are commonly unbelievers, as much in words as in deed.

> [p. 37] MENDACIUM. Videtur quod innuitive accusas in isto omnes quatuor sectas cleri; sed quis auderet hoc curie romane imponere, et specialiter sectis fratrum, cum tunc regulando dominos, dominas atque regna forent proditores eorum et heretici ex consensu. Sed quis auderet sic accusare sapienciam hujus cleri? Si enim lex Christi in scriptura sacra sic diceret, verisimile est quod tot et tanti sancti de fratribus istud dominis, episcopis et abbatibus intimarent. Cum ergo in hoc tacent, ymmo impugnant istud tanquam hereticum, videtur quod nedum consenciunt, sed defendunt contrarium ut autores. Et si istud concernit salutem anime ac prosperitatem regnorum, evidens videtur quod sic similando vel asserendo forent heretici et proditores dei, hominum et regnorum.

FALSEHOOD. It seems that by implication you accuse in this all four sects of the clergy;
but who would dare to lay this on the Roman curia, and especially on the sects of

be their traitors, and heretics by consent? But who would dare so to accuse the wisdom of this

asserting they would be heretics and traitors of God, of men and of kingdoms.

> [p. 37] VERITAS. In ista materia respondendum est a sectis et partibus quas impugnas; ego enim nescio in isto romanam curiam excusare, nec fratres aut sectas alias, sed adhuc in fide vivo domini Jesu Christi quod ipse sic vivere docuit clerum suum. Verum tamen si per impossibile fuero doctus contrarium, volo veritati catholice humiliter consentire, et omnino si doctus fuero a papa vel superioribus ecclesie ut prelatis. Sed scio quod conversacio et factum eorum non est tenendum a catholicis tanquam fides cum ipsi possunt macula avaricie infici ut mundani. Cupiditas enim infecit primum apostatam, genus humanum et alios multos lapsos. Et quantum ad quatuor sectas fratrum, ipse etatem habent, pro se ipsis respondeant, quia in hoc ipsos nescio excusare, et specialiter cum eligunt ex autoritati Christi vitam exproprietariam [p. 38] ut ceteris plus perfectam. Maledicta autem sit talis secta que ipsos necessitat ad falsum tam proditorie nutriendum. Ideo, ut quidam asserunt, fideles non communicarent cum illis, nec ave eis dicerent, antequam de isto cleri dominio palam proderint fidem suam. Si autem approbarent istud cleri dominium, tunc forent stulti sine autoritate Christi taliter mendicantes, cum foret perfeccio eis habere seculare dominium quod a Christo fuit multipliciter approbatum, et nunquam taliter mendicare, et specialiter a viris tam validis et tanto numero glomeratis, pro tantis edificiis construendis, et aliis sumptibus contra evangelium Christi fictis. Si autem ex autoritate scripture reprobant istud cleri dominium et tam multipliciter tacent ipsum hominibus quibus ex consciencia pupplicarent, quis dubitat quin sunt vir duplex animo, inconstanter tenens cum falsitate quam credit superare ad tempus modicum ut arundo? Et cum factum plus docet quam verba videtur ex professione sui ordinis quod plus est credendum suis operibus quod dominacio ista sit illicita, quam verbis suis duplicibus ad que pro temporali precio conducuntur. Nam plus est credendum medico de salubritate herbarum ab homine sicitato dicenti quod a herba est saluberrima et b herba mortifera, si a herbam aufugeret ut venenum et b herbam avide gustaverit, tanquam prandium salutare; plus, inquam, credendum est tali operi quam hiis verbis. Et sic videtur de fratribus. Sed unum scio quod fides scripture propter duplicitatem eorum non titubat. Sicut nec ordo eorum aliquam perfeccionem secte Christiane continet, nisi que ex evangelio est fundata; quod si essent usque ad illud a superfluis [p. 39] expurgate, non remaneret eis nisi clara et meritoria secta Christi.

TRUTH. In this matter answer must be made by the sects and parties that you attack; for I do not
know how to excuse the Roman curia in this, nor the friars or the other sects, but I still live in
the faith of the Lord Jesus Christ that he taught his clergy to live so. Yet if, per impossibile, I
should be taught the contrary, I am willing humbly to consent to catholic truth, and above all if I
should be taught by the pope or by the superiors of the church, such as prelates. But I
know that their manner of life and deed is not to be held by catholics as faith,
since they can be infected with the stain of avarice, as worldly men are. For covetousness infected
the first apostate, the human race, and many others who have fallen. And as for the four sects of
friars, they are of age: let them answer for themselves, because in this I do not know how to
excuse them, and especially since they choose, on Christ's authority, the
propertyless life [38] as more perfect than the rest. Nor is there doubt that
in words and deed they consent to the endowment of the clergy, since they so
faint-heartedly keep silent from telling the truth on this in their preaching, in confessions and in
other dealings. And cursed be such a sect as compels them to nourish falsehood so traitorously.
Therefore, as some assert, the faithful should not have dealings with them, nor say "God speed" to
them, until they have openly disclosed their faith concerning this lordship of the
clergy. Now if they approved this lordship of the clergy, then they would be fools, [to go]
begging in this way without Christ's authority, since it would be perfection for them
to have secular lordship, which was approved by Christ in many ways, and never to beg in this way;
and especially by men so able-bodied and massed in such numbers, for building such great edifices
and for other expenses contrived against the gospel of Christ. But if on the authority of
scripture they reject this lordship of the clergy, and in so many ways keep it silent from men to
whom in conscience they should publish it, who doubts that they are "a double minded man",
inconstantly holding with the falsehood which he believes will prevail for a little while, like a
reed? And since the deed teaches more than words, it seems from the profession of their order
that their works, [teaching] that this exercise of lordship is unlawful, are more to be believed than
their double words, to which they are hired for a temporal price. For a fevered man is more to
believe a doctor, about the wholesomeness of herbs, who tells him that herb a is most wholesome and
herb b deadly, if he fled herb a as poison and eagerly tasted herb b as a wholesome meal: more, I say,
is such a deed to be believed than these words. And so it seems with the friars. But one thing I
know: that the faith of scripture does not totter because of their doubleness. Just as their order
contains no perfection of the Christian sect except what is founded on the gospel; and if they were
[39] purged of superfluities down to that, nothing would remain to them but the clear and
meritorious sect of Christ.

######## CHUNK 20  (5 sed/autem)

> [p. 39] MENDACIUM. Adhuc innuis quod omnes canonici regulares, nigri et albi, et omnes religiosi post ipsos prudenter in ecclesiam introducti, ut fratres hospitales, monachi cartusienses, fratres boni homines, sanctimoniales et sororisse cum anacoritis et ceteris inclusis deficerent et puram sectam Christi de novo induerent. Sed que major heresis plus religionis ecclesie turbativa?

FALSEHOOD. You still imply that all the canons regular, black and white, and all the religious prudently brought into the church after them, such as the Hospitaller brothers, the Carthusian monks, the brothers Bonshommes, the nuns and the sisters, with the anchorites and the other recluses, should come to an end and put on the pure sect of Christ anew. But what greater heresy, more disruptive of the church's religion?

> [p. 39] VERITAS. In parte verum concludis quod omnes iste religiones private cessarent, et puram ac nudam religionem Christi libere ut prius induerent. Verumtamen non est aliqua privata religio nunc cum prudencia moderata, quin in pura religione Christi homines possent penalitatem ejus prudentis subire, spiritu domini instigante. Et hoc foret liberius et meritorius quam est istius religionis private perpetuacio, et tanquam religionis domini stulta concatenacio. Unde ista divisio a religione Christi divisionem in moribus attestatur, ut divisiones in thematibus fratrum et aliorum hodie predicantium, dimissa integritate vivacis sensus scripture et fabulis introductis, attestantur causaliter divisionem a caritate Christi sicut recessum a sua ordinacione; et sic fiunt omnes divisiones hujusmodi in peccatum. Nam omnes tales novitates rituum in nullo addunt ad meritum, cum omnia opera meritoria illorum, si sunt aliqua, possent esse sine tali cerimonia magis facilia, et plus deo placencia. Cui ergo dubium quin [p. 40] a patre generacionis adultere sunt talia superflue introducta, quod pateret evidencius discurrendo particularius per istas 8 species et vicia per que occasione earum in criminibus tam latentibus quam pupplicis involvuntur. Ideo manifestum videtur quod hii qui sine autoritate domini hec autorizant, defendunt, vel eis consenciunt in gravi culpa contra dominum se involvunt. Ideo de isto peccato introducende consuetudinis sine autoritate domini, homines et specialiter majores in ecclesia peccaverunt. Nam videtur quod introducendo a proposito talem consuetudinem tanquam perpetuam, homo incidit in blasfemiam contra deum; quia deo est proprium autorizare talem consuetudinem perpetuam, quis ergo mortalis et momentaneus presumeret talem consuetudinem introducere supra vires? Fundamentum autem ad stabiliendum totam istam sententiam stat in isto, quod lex et ordinacio Christi est optima sine defectu possibili, quia ut patet jo 8° nemo potest ipsum arguere de peccato. Cum ergo Christus ordinavit sectam unicam militantem et ipsam habere tres partes sine hiis novis ritibus, tanquam difficultans ac illibertans perpetuum introductis, manifestum videtur quod ordinacionem istam Christi in laxacione sue libertatis dimittere, et aliam novam sine autoritate admittere est peccatum grave et stulticia manifesta; specialiter cum Christus non innuit approbative quod talis consuetudo perpetua sit catholice admittenda. Quis, inquam, dubitat quin talem consuetudinem malam admittere et consuetudinem bonam contempnere sit peccatum grave? Ordinacio autem Christi, sive pura sua religio, excellit omnes alias in hiis tribus; in autoritate, in facilitate, et in securitate. in auctoritate] quia Christus deus et homo ex omnisciencia sua ipsam instituit. In [p. 41] facilitate excellit quia eximit omnes ocupaciones frivolas sive superfluas circa signa, ideo, tota ocupacione circa noticiam istorum signorum superfluam expensa circa salubrem noticiam legis dei, posset Christianus currere compendiosius ad patriam quam nunc faciunt religiosi nostri, qui in istis ceremoniis retardantur. In securitate autem excedit hec religio Christi quia potest sine dispensacione hominis quantum racio exigit variare, et substantia illius regule, cum sit evangelium, non potest deficere. Et patet quod stulte, quia imprudenter, fuerunt iste secte in ecclesiam introducte. Et sic sentencia ista non est heresis sed doctrina catholica Christi ecclesie juvativa, licet sit synagoge sathane turbativa.

TRUTH. In part you infer truly that all these private religions should cease, and [their members] freely put on the pure and naked religion of Christ as before. Nevertheless there is no private religion, now tempered with prudence, but that men could undergo its prudent hardship within the pure religion of Christ, at the prompting of the Spirit of the Lord. And this would be more free and more meritorious than is the perpetuation of this private religion, and as it were a foolish chaining-up of the Lord's religion. Hence this division from the religion of Christ attests a division in morals; just as the divisions in the themes of the friars and of others preaching today, with the integrity of the living sense of scripture abandoned and fables brought in, attest causally a division from the charity of Christ, as a withdrawal from his ordinance; and so all divisions of this kind turn into sin. For all such novelties of rites add nothing whatever to merit, since all their meritorious works, if there are any, could be easier without such ceremony, and more pleasing to God. To whom, then, is it doubtful that [40] such things were superfluously brought in by the father of an adulterous generation, as would appear more evidently by running through in more detail these eight kinds and the vices by which, on their occasion, they are entangled in crimes both hidden and public? Therefore it seems manifest that those who without the Lord's authority authorize these things, defend them, or consent to them, involve themselves in grave guilt against the Lord. Therefore men, and especially the greater ones in the church, have sinned in this sin of introducing a custom without the Lord's authority. For it seems that by deliberately introducing such a custom as perpetual, a man falls into blasphemy against God; for since it is proper to God to authorize such a perpetual custom, who then, mortal and momentary, would presume to introduce such a custom beyond his powers? Now the foundation for establishing this whole teaching rests on this, that the law and ordinance of Christ is the best, without any possible defect, because, as is clear from John 8, no one can convince him of sin. Since, then, Christ ordained a single sect militant, and that it should have three parts, without these new rites, introduced as making it perpetually difficult and unfree, it seems manifest that to abandon this ordinance of Christ in the loosening of its liberty, and to admit another, new one without authority, is a grave sin and manifest folly; especially since Christ did not imply by way of approval that such a perpetual custom is to be admitted as catholic. Who, I say, doubts that to admit such a bad custom and to despise a good custom is a grave sin? Now the ordinance of Christ, or his pure religion, excels all others in these three: in authority, in ease, and in security. [In authority,] because Christ, God and man, instituted it out of his omniscience. Et quantum ad indulgencias, privilegia, et regulacionem ecclesie in lege nova, patet quod foret sibi expediens et toti ecclesie viatorum quod omnes novitates hujusmodi sint sopite. Sic enim fuit tempore Christi antequam antichristus sic regnaverat, et erit post diem judicii vel tempore anteriore quo placet domino jhu Christo. Omnia enim talia videntur esse fantasmata non fundata nec racionibus nec scriptura. Sicut ergo illis deficit fundacio racionis, sic profectus meriti beatitudinis, et sicut in declinacione a veritate claudicant, sic in profectu ad beatitudinem promerendam, ita quod omnes operaciones concernentes sacerdotes cesarios, in quantum tales, non proficiunt directe ad beatitudinem sed impediunt vel retardant. Omnia enim ista sonant in cupidinem vel lucri temporalium vel honoris. Et sic non oportet propter tales cupidines Christum de celo descendere nec in terris ipsum talem vicarium reservare. Licet ergo antichristus videatur ad tempus supra fideles Christi tyrannice dominari, tamen fideles supponunt quod ante diem judicii pars Christi exaltabitur et pars contraria deprimetur; quia certum videtur quod vecordia potencium et timor servilis paciencium est in causa quare sic pars diaboli hodie in angulo habitabilis exaltatur.

FALSEHOOD. If "he who exalts himself shall be humbled", it seems that one who so openly attacks the pope and the lower prelates of the church must necessarily be suppressed; for if they were to lack secular lordship, their estate and dignity would collapse. But who then would grant indulgences and privileges, consecrate bishops and other priests, and settle other doubtful matters that fall to them according to the laws of the church? Do we believe that Christ must always descend from heaven whenever his church is newly to be consulted? However you may speak here in corners, you would not dare to defend these things or say them before the pope. TRUTH. Trusting in the faith, I dare to say these things before God. And if I still fear temporal death, as Peter did, I reckon that I ought to defend this teaching before the pope. And so, as regards the gospel exaltation that you allege, I ought [49] to speak this teaching in a spirit of humility, and to hope that I shall thereby be humbled the more in virtue. I speak this teaching, therefore, for the good of the pope and of the church; and if slaying or other penalty should come of it, I ask my God to give me the virtue to suffer steadfastly and humbly. And as for the ruin of the estate and dignity of the caesarean prelate, you could not accuse them more plainly in such matters, for you imply that, contrary to Christ and his apostles, they depend on secular lordship. But far from the faithful be such a dignity of a priest! For it seems that that [dignity] qualifies for a prelacy in hell which is founded, on the way, upon earthly goods or worldly honours; for it would be against God's law that this worldly elevation should directly exalt anyone in blessedness. Therefore, as a sinner, inasmuch as he is such, is contrary to himself, so, inasmuch as you strive to speak thus for the pope and the caesarean prelates, you speak against them. And so, since this name "pope" is a term outside the faith of scripture, it seems that it was invented at the endowment of the church presumed upon by Caesar. And so, if it connotes this ordinance, as you imply, it would be wholesome for the church that there should be no pope or any cardinals; for the bishop of souls, the Lord Jesus Christ, with his faithful servants, would rule the church militant far better without such a pope and other prelates. For so Peter and other poor priests did after the Lord's ascension, before the church was endowed: why then should they not do so today? — since those estates are a seedbed of dissension and of strife over secular lordship. Therefore, if you should grieve over such a fall, the chief matter for grief seems to be the humbling of Antichrist. For the blasphemous grants of indulgences in the homeland, the wearisome burdenings of the faithful here on the way, together with the traditions and persecutions of Antichrist, would cease in the church; but a happy cessation! And so it is with all the works [50] that are done thus loftily by such prelates; and so the church would prosper, with the person who is more excellent before God following the footsteps of Christ in conduct and the footsteps of St Peter. Nor is there any doubt that then no pope, bishop, or any of the clerics would exercise lordship in a secular way, for it is manifest from the faith of the scripture of the twofold testament that so the Lord Jesus Christ lived and taught. And as for indulgences, privileges, and the regulation of the church under the new law, it is clear that it would be expedient for him, and for the whole church of wayfarers, that all such novelties be laid to rest. For so it was in Christ's time, before Antichrist had so reigned, and so it will be after the day of judgement, or at an earlier time, when it pleases the Lord Jesus Christ. For all such things seem to be phantasms, founded neither on reasons nor on scripture. As, therefore, they lack a foundation in reason, so [they lack] advance in the merit of blessedness; and as they limp in their swerving from the truth, so [they limp] in progress towards meriting blessedness; so that all operations concerning caesarean priests, inasmuch as they are such, do not advance directly towards blessedness but hinder or retard [it]. And so it is with the three dignities or offices that the bishop reserves to himself, which are the confirmation of the young, the ordination of clerics, and the consecration of places. For all these ring of desire, either of gain in temporal things or of honour. And so there is no need for Christ to descend from heaven for the sake of such desires, nor to keep such a vicar for himself on earth. Although, therefore, Antichrist seems for a time to lord it tyrannically over Christ's faithful, yet the faithful suppose that before the day of judgement Christ's party will be exalted and the contrary party pressed down; for it seems certain that the faint-heartedness of the powerful and the servile fear of those who suffer is the cause why the devil's party is thus exalted today in a corner of the habitable [world].

TRUTH. God be my witness, I desire the spiritual prosperity and advantage of priests; yet I would not that they sell their prayers, nor that they be worldly merchants, nor occupied in usuries or other unlawful business. But I would that they apply themselves to the preaching of the gospel, or to mechanical labour, such as writing, or to other bodily [labour], provided that they keep themselves from avarice and other manifest crimes. Indeed, as it seems to me, they could lawfully agree with seculars, or with others who hire them, how much they will receive in pay yearly, provided that, within the limits of the apostle in 1 Timothy 6, "having food and wherewith to be covered", they are with prudence content. And [I would] above all that they beware of the greater sins and, idleness set aside, apply themselves to priestly works; and especially that through their covetousness they be not burdensome to the church, that is, that they be not incontinent or covetous, or occupied in worldly offices or sports. And, as it seems to me, it would be more wholesome for priests, with these [conditions], to apply themselves to manual exercise, such as writing, teaching grammar, or other work to which [52] God may move his servants, so that they keep themselves within the limits aforesaid. But which of us is worthier than this Paul? The doing of such bodily labour indeed does not taint a priest, or make him unfit for the consecration of the Lord's body; but idleness does, proud occupation, or being a burden to the church, or anything else that taints his mind. Let the priest therefore trust in the Lord and fight manfully against the devil, the world and the flesh, and not despair but that God will provide him the temporal things necessary to him, considering that passage of Matthew 6, how Christ tells his faithful that they are not to be solicitous about these things, admonishing them by the example of the birds and the lilies how God does not fail them in necessaries: how much more [will he] not [fail] his faithful servants, who are worthier. Let the faithful priest therefore put himself in the Lord's hand, and not set an obstacle against himself through sin, and there is no doubt that whatever befalls him will turn to the saving profit of his soul. Let him therefore suffer hunger, thirst and nakedness of body, yet not so much as the apostle, and in all these things give thanks to God. And let him stand constant in faith that God cannot desert his faithful servants who faithfully persevere in their faith and life. But there are priests called from these four sects, and especially from the friars, who by sinning set an obstacle to themselves, as these, by deserting the liberty of the Lord's law, put on newly invented orders, and, beyond what God counsels, mass themselves together in superfluous friars, in sumptuous houses and other useless rites. And such men must at times be perplexed by reason of the prior sin, in which, deserting the liberty of the Lord's law, they foolishly entangle themselves; and such men are said to have [53] this custom, that they take a yearly pension from many men at once, as though they were the particular bedesmen of each of them, and so they defraud these men and themselves; since, if they kept themselves in the liberty of the Lord's law, they could live more easily and more fittingly and be less burdensome to the faithful. For one sin leads into another, as those who wallow in a pool are by that very thing sunk deeper. Nor do I see but that the foundation of this teaching is to be observed with greater diligence by the higher priests, such as are prelates and curates, taking care always that they be not idle in the church nor burdensome to their subjects. Yet it seems to me reasonable that a curate taken up, by God's ordinance, with bodily infirmity ought to be sustained by the parish by title of alms, as before, with moderation.

Since this teaching has been confirmed in so many ways, that the clergy ought to keep evangelical poverty after the pattern of Christ and the apostles, as is clear from the faith of the twofold testament, and on the contrary side lies so great a peril, both future, in the soul, and temporal and perceptible for the present, each of the three parts of the church, but especially the temporal lords, ought to avoid this peril, and first, in common talk, to say to these caesarean prelates that they do not wish to be damned for love of them, indeed rather for hatred of them. And it has been told them by catholics that if they continue in this crime, enriching the clergy thus against Christ's rule and not repenting fruitfully of this folly, then they are to be condemned beyond excuse; nor does experience or human knowledge of prosperities or other worldly things teach the opposite. Therefore they wish to relieve the clergy of these lordships, as the clerics pretend to have relieved the secular arm for a time. They do not say for a thousand years, but for seven, or for so much time as it takes them to show clearly that this life of the clergy is founded on the law of Christ and does not result, for the secular arm, in the peril of souls. And it seems that this is reasonable, since both the clergy and the secular arm ought to call to mind with care that which so greatly concerns the salvation of his soul; and both the works of Christ and his doctrine seem to them expressly to sound in favour of this teaching. But the clergy, murmuring against it, in this signals itself guilty. How, I ask, should the temporal lords have continued this work of mercy for a thousand years, and the clerics have accepted it for so long a time, and yet the clerics murmur that [85] for seven years the temporal lords should relieve them, a time in which they could live more easily and more in conformity with the law of Christ! Does it not accord more with reason and with the faith of scripture that the temporal lords should take up this burden than that Christ's clergy should be hindered by it from Christ's ministry? The friars, on this point at least, by their profession and rule, are willing to bear witness to the reasonableness of the said temporal lords. And this exchange seems able to profit each of the three parts of the church militant. For it would profit the clergy, since it would put it in the number and measure that God disposed, and, other secular offices and pompous lives being shaken off, would assign the clergy purely and wholly to the office appointed by Christ. Secondly, it would profit the secular arm. But I set aside the perceptible worldly profit and consider another, spiritual profit: namely, that the temporal lords would not then be driven to make themselves debtors to the third part of the church, nor would endowed cloisterers and others have to be burdensome to their tenants, as is said now. For when an unnatural lordship is brought in by the devil's working, it vexes subjects more than a lordship assigned by the Lord. Therefore it is believed that the temporal lords would be more merciful and more gracious to their tenants than clerics, who are endowed against Christ's admonitions. And so the third part of the church would prosper both in bodily things and in spiritual. And the whole body of the church would be tempered according to the law of the Lord. Hence it seems to some that he is faithless to the Lord, and to every part of the church, as a traitor and heretic, who contradicts this teaching. I pray God that he move all three parts of the church to consent fruitfully to the truth in this matter. Here ends the Mirror of the Church Militant.
