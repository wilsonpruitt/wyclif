# *De potestate pape*, chapter 8, part 5: rival popes from the chronicle of Ralph de Diceto

## ¶1 · 180.33–181.15

> [p. 180] Abusio autem termini infundabilis videtur michi vocare quemcumque pseudopapam nudo nomine [p. 181] papam vel caput ecclesie. Unde gentilitas et primatus affeccio docuerunt quod quicumque Romanus pontifex censeatur papa et gerat primatum huiusmodi super singulos alios sacerdotes; unde patet quod Deus non ordinasset apostolorum martyrium Rome propter hunc finem, nisi verus papatus regulariter sequeretur. Sed frequenter post dotacionem ecclesie separatus est papatus a Romano pontifice, igitur Deus non intenderat per illud martyrium illum finem; tunc enim Deus frustratus a suo proposito improvide ordinasset et negligenter omisisset in suo testamento tam necessarium primatum ad regimen sue ecclesie. Ideo certum est quod invencio humana fabricavit institucionem istam de papa.

And it seems to me an ungroundable abuse of the term to call any pseudo-pope, by the bare name, [181] [pope]{g:papa} or [head]{g:caput} of the [church]{g:ecclesia}.[^1] Hence it was [paganism]{g:gentilitas} and the affection for [primacy]{g:primatus} that taught that whoever is [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex} is reckoned pope and holds a primacy of this kind over every other [priest]{g:sacerdos};[^2] hence it is clear that God would not have ordained the martyrdom of the apostles at Rome for this end unless true papacy followed [as a rule]{g:regulariter}. Now often, after the [endowment]{g:dotacio} of the church, the papacy has been separated from the Roman pontiff; therefore God had not intended that end by that martyrdom; for then God, frustrated of his purpose, would have ordained improvidently, and negligently omitted from his testament a primacy so necessary to the rule of his church. Therefore it is certain that human invention fabricated this institution of the pope.

## ¶2 · 181.16–181.32

> [p. 181] In cronicis autem Cestrensis narravi alias quomodo multi Romani pontifices de papatu defecerant, et repeto illa que scripta sunt in cronica aurea Radulphi de Dyceto decani sancti Pauli, cui Anglici darent fidem. Narrat enim libro I cap. II, quomodo anno domini CCCCXX presidebat in Roma Boni facius papa et contra hunc ordinato Eulalio, ac pro hoc dissidente ecclesia ambo iussu Honorii Augusti urbe egrediuntur, et sic reprobato Eulalio iussu Augusti Bonifacius, quia prior ordinatus fuerat, sedi apostolice restituitur. Nondum enim tunc tantum sopitum fuit dominium Romani imperii nec ad tantum invaluit usurpacio Romani pontificis quod fuit reputatum hereticum, quod domini temporales possunt auferre temporalia, ymo dignitates atque officia ab ecclesia delinquente.

And in the chronicles of the Chester [chronicler] I have related elsewhere how many Roman pontiffs had fallen away from the papacy, and I repeat what is written in the golden chronicle of Ralph de Diceto, dean of St Paul's, to whom Englishmen would give credence.[^3] For he relates in book 1, chapter 2, how "in the year of the Lord 420 Pope Boniface presided in Rome, and when Eulalius had been ordained against him, and the church was divided over this, both left the city by order of Honorius Augustus; and so, Eulalius being rejected, Boniface, by order of Augustus, because he had been ordained first, is restored to the apostolic see."[^4] For not yet then had the [lordship]{g:dominium} of the Roman empire been so lulled to sleep, nor had the usurpation of the Roman pontiff grown so strong, that it was reputed heretical that [temporal lords]{g:temporales-domini} can take away [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, indeed dignities and offices, from a church that is delinquent.[^5]

## ¶3 · 182.1–182.2

> [p. 182] Secundo, anno domini CCCCXCIII contra Symachum papam ordinatur Laurencius per contencionem.

"Second, in the year of the Lord 493, Laurence is ordained by contention against Pope Symmachus."[^6]

## ¶4 · 182.4–182.7

> [p. 182] Tercio, anno domini DCCLXVIII Constantino papa scismatico oculis privato Stephanus papa constituitur; hic synodum Rome congregat et ordinatos a Constantino scismatico reordinat.

"Third, in the year of the Lord 768, the schismatic Pope Constantine having been deprived of his eyes, Stephen is made pope; he gathers a synod at Rome and reordains those ordained by the schismatic Constantine."

## ¶5 · 182.8–182.9

> [p. 182] Quarto, anno domini DCCCLIII adversus Benedictum papam Anastasius presulatum invadit.

"Fourth, in the year of the Lord 853, Anastasius invades the bishopric against Pope Benedict."[^7]

## ¶6 · 182.10–182.11

> [p. 182] Quinto, anno domini DCCCCVII presidet Leo papa, contra quem Chrystoforus surgit.

"Fifth, in the year of the Lord 907, Pope Leo presides, against whom Christopher rises."

## ¶7 · 182.12–182.30

> [p. 182] Sexto, anno domini DCCCCLXVIII collecta a tota Ytalia episcoporum synodo Johannes papa de nephariis causis infamatur, qui dum se excusaturum venire cunctatur, Leo adhuc laycus eleccione omnium et consensu imperatoris papa substituitur. Sic Leo fecit ordinaciones et alia, que erant apostolica. Nec longum Romani alterata fide apud imperatorem papam recipiunt Johannem. Ille autem synodo collecta Leonem deposuit et eius gesta cassavit. Statutumque est synodum a Leone non nominandam synodum sed prostibulum favens adulteris. Quicumque igitur eo ordinante erant dampnati iussi suam ipsorum proscripsionem presentare in cartha hec continente: Pater meus nichil sibi habuit, nichil michi dedit, et sic depositi manserunt in illis gradibus, quos habuerant nondum a Leone ordinati. Iste Johannes papa cum uxore cuiusdam oblectans a dyabolo in tempore coitus percutitur et sine viatico dominico obiit.

"Sixth, in the year of the Lord 968, a synod of bishops having been gathered from all Italy, Pope John is defamed for nefarious causes; and while he delays to come to excuse himself, Leo, still a layman, is put in his place as pope by the election of all and the consent of the emperor. So Leo made [ordinations]{g:ordinacio} and other acts which were apostolic. Not long after, the Romans, having broken faith with the emperor, receive John as pope. And he, gathering a synod, deposed Leo and annulled his acts. And it was decreed that the synod [held] by Leo should be named not a synod but a brothel favouring adulterers. All, therefore, who stood condemned [as ordained] by him were ordered to present their own proscription in a charter containing this: 'My father had nothing for himself, gave nothing to me'; and so, deposed, they remained in those grades which they had held when not yet ordained by Leo. This Pope John, dallying with a certain man's wife, is struck by the devil in the act of intercourse, and died without the Lord's viaticum."[^8]

## ¶8 · 183.1–183.7

> [p. 183] Septimo contigit Romanos contra iuramentum, quod imperatori fecerant, scilicet quod nunquam eligerent papam sine eius et sine filii eius Othonis consensu, Benedictum papam constituunt, sed imperator Roma obsessa adeo Romanos affflixit, ut Leonem papam se recepturos promitterent et sic Benedictus renuitur.

"Seventh, it happened that the Romans, against the oath which they had made to the emperor, namely that they would never elect a pope without his consent and that of his son Otto, make Benedict pope; but the emperor, besieging Rome, so afflicted the Romans that they promised to receive Leo as pope, and so Benedict is rejected."[^9]

## ¶9 · 183.8–183.13

> [p. 183] Octavo, anno domini MXLV Benedictus, qui symoniace papatum invaserat, cum esset rudis literarum, alterum ad vices ecclesiastici officii exequendas secum papam Silvestrum consecrari fecit; quod cum multis non placeret, tercius superinducitur Gregorius, qui solus vices duorum impleret.

"Eighth, in the year of the Lord 1045, Benedict, who had invaded the papacy simoniacally, since he was unlettered, had another, Silvester, consecrated pope with him to discharge the duties of the ecclesiastical office; and since this displeased many, a third, Gregory, is brought in over them, to fill alone the duties of the two."[^10]

## ¶10 · 183.14–184.4

> [p. 183] Nono, anno domini MXLVI Rome uno contra duos et duobus contra unum de papatu altercantibus rex Henricus contra eos Romam vadit et eis canonica et imperiali censura depositis, Clemens presidet, et ab eo rex Henricus in imperatorem benedicitur, iurantibus Romanis se sine eius consensu nunquam papam electuros. Tunc enim practizata fuit lex sepe tacta, que ponitur III. Reg. II, 35 quod instar Salomonis rex debet in casu deponere summum pontificem. Tunc eciam accepit rex ab eo humiliter benediccionem, quod post infidelitatem vocatum est necessarium sacramentum et confirmacio sine qua non fit aliquis imperator. Sed ista ficta mendacia extra fidem scripture ex gestis et factis preteritis cum racionibus professorum scripture relicta sunt a domino convincenda. Tercio patet quod antequam introducta est novella et infundabilis secta cardinalium; papa fuit electus a populo Romanorum, [p. 184] Decimo, anno domini MLXVIII Rome duobus de papatu contendentibus Alexander cum suam purgasset innocenciam de symonia recipitur, Cadalus Parmensis episcopus reprobatur, unde dictum est

"Ninth, in the year of the Lord 1046, at Rome, with one disputing against two and two against one over the papacy, King Henry goes to Rome against them, and, they having been deposed by canonical and imperial [censure]{g:censura}, Clement presides, and by him King Henry is blessed as emperor, the Romans swearing that they would never elect a pope without his consent."[^11] For then the law often touched on was [put into practice]{g:practizare}, which is set down in 3 Kings 2:35, that after the manner of Solomon a king ought, in a [given] case, to depose the [highest pontiff]{g:summi-pontifices}.[^12] Then also the king humbly received the blessing from him, which after unbelief [set in] was called a necessary sacrament and a confirmation without which no one becomes emperor.[^13] But these feigned lies, outside the faith of scripture, are left by the Lord to be refuted from past deeds and acts, together with the reasons of the professors of scripture. Third, it is clear that before the novel and ungroundable [sect]{g:secta} of the cardinals was introduced, the pope was elected by the people of the Romans.[^14] [184] "Tenth, in the year of the Lord 1068, at Rome, two contending over the papacy, Alexander, when he had purged his innocence of simony, is received; Cadalus, bishop of Parma, is rejected; whence it was said:"[^15]

## ¶11 · 184.5–184.5

> [p. 184] Cadalus in Parma Parme fuit arcus et arma.

"Cadalus at Parma was Parma's bow and arms;"

## ¶12 · 184.6–184.6

> [p. 184] Cadalus occubuit, Parma ruina fuit.

"Cadalus has fallen, Parma was [his] ruin."[^16]

## ¶13 · 184.7–184.10

> [p. 184] Undecimo, anno domini MLXXXIII Henricus urbem Rome infregit et Wybertum in sede apostolica constituit, Hildebrandus vero Beneventum adiit, ubi usque ad obitum suum deguit.

"Eleventh, in the year of the Lord 1083, Henry broke into the city of Rome and set Wibert in the apostolic see; and Hildebrand went to Benevento, where he lived until his death."

## ¶14 · 184.11–184.13

> [p. 184] Duodecimo, anno domini MLXXXVII Desiderius, qui et Victor, papa contra Clementem est factus.

"Twelfth, in the year of the Lord 1087, Desiderius, also called Victor, was made pope against Clement."

## ¶15 · 184.14–184.23

> [p. 184] Decimo tercio, anno domini MXCI fertur fuisse duos, qui dicebantur Romani pontifices a se invicem discordantes et ecclesiam Dei inter se divisam trahentes, Urbanus scilicet, qui primum fuerat episcopus Ostiensis, et Clemens qui Wybertus archiepiscopus Ravenensis. Que res per plures annos ecclesiam Anglie in tantum occupavit, quod ex quo Gregorius, qui et Hildebrandus, defunctus fuit, nulli loco pape usque ad hoc tempus subdi et obedire voluerit.

"Thirteenth, in the year of the Lord 1091, there are said to have been two who were called Roman pontiffs, at odds with each other and dragging the church of God, divided, between them: Urban, namely, who had first been bishop of Ostia, and Clement, who [was] Wibert, archbishop of Ravenna. This matter so occupied the church of England for many years that, from the time that Gregory, also called Hildebrand, died, it would not be subject to or obey anyone in the place of pope up to this time."[^17]

## ¶16 · 184.24–184.28

> [p. 184] Decimo quarto, anno domini MCXXX Innocencio papa regente intrusus est Petrus Leonis et dictus est Anacletus, qui regem Rogerum Sycilie consecravit, sed Innocencius papa transivit in Franciam, unde dicitur:

"Fourteenth, in the year of the Lord 1130, while Pope Innocent was ruling, Peter Leonis was intruded and was called Anacletus, who consecrated Roger king of Sicily; but Pope Innocent crossed into France, whence it is said:"

## ¶17 · 184.29–184.29

> [p. 184] Romam Petrus habet, totum Gregorius orbem.

"Peter holds Rome, Gregory the whole world."[^18]

## ¶18 · 184.30–184.33

> [p. 184] Decimo quinto, anno domini MCLIX rexit Alexander papa, contra quem Octavianus intruditur, sed in scismate moritur, et sic Guido Cremensis sed Johannes intrusus reconciliatur.

"Fifteenth, in the year of the Lord 1159, Pope Alexander ruled, against whom Octavian is intruded, but he dies in schism, and so Guido of Crema; but John, the intruder, is reconciled."[^19]

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Abusio … termini infundabilis*: *infundabilis* may agree with *abusio* ("an ungroundable abuse of the term") or with *termini* ("abuse of an ungroundable term"); the first is taken. **G.** *pseudopapa*, "pseudo-pope", formed like *pseudochristus*.

[^2]: **T.** *huiusmodi*: the codices lack *-modi* (apparatus 181.4), reading *huius*; the print is translated. B reads *gerit* for *gerat* (181.3).

[^3]: **S.** Ralph de Diceto, dean of St Paul's, London (d. 1202 or 1203), *Abbreviationes chronicorum*; Loserth cites the edition of Stubbs, vol. 1, p. 83 (LEDGER P08e-1). Where Wyclif "related elsewhere" the Chester chronicle's account is not identified here; to be checked (LEDGER P08e-2). **R.** Loserth notes Hus's reuse, *Opera* fol. 230a. AB in the margin (181.20): *Deposicio paparum*.

[^4]: **C.** The quotation bounds follow Loserth's italics, which run from the year to *restituitur*. **T.** *quia prior*: the codices read *qui* (apparatus 181.26); Loserth's *quia* is translated.

[^5]: **C.** The point Wyclif draws: in 420 the emperor settled a disputed election, and no one yet called it heresy that temporal lords may take temporal goods, and even dignities and offices, from a delinquent church.

[^6]: **S.** Diceto, ed. Stubbs, vol. 1, p. 89; the following entries at pp. 124, 137, 142 and 151 (Loserth) (LEDGER P08e-3). The list of fifteen schisms is Diceto's, quoted in turn; Loserth's italics cover all of it, so each entry is in quotation marks.

[^7]: **T.** Loserth corrects the year to 855 (*Recte: 855*); for the fifth entry to 903, and for the sixth to 963. The years of the print are translated.

[^8]: **T.** *excusaturum*: Loserth notes *Recte: excusatum*; either way "to excuse himself". *nominandam synodum*: the codices read *nominanda synodus* (182.21–22), corrected by Loserth. *meus*: the codices read *nimis* (182.25). *manserunt*: Loserth gives the codices' reading as *mansuerunt*, correct *remanserunt*. B reads *cessavit* for *cassavit*. **C.** *Quicumque … eo ordinante erant dampnati*: as printed, "whoever stood condemned through his ordaining"; the sense requires those whom Leo had ordained, and a word such as *ordinati* may have fallen out; "[as ordained]" supplies it. **R.** Hus, *Opera* fol. 230a (Loserth).

[^9]: **C.** *contigit Romanos … constituunt*: the infinitive construction after *contigit* gives way to a finite verb; translated by the sense. Diceto's page for this entry is unreadable in the apparatus OCR (LEDGER P08e-4).

[^10]: **S.** Diceto, ed. Stubbs, vol. 1, p. 187 (Loserth) (LEDGER P08e-5).

[^11]: **S.** Diceto, as before (Loserth, *Ibid.*, at 183.14) (LEDGER P08e-7).

[^12]: **S.** 3 Kings 2:35 (DR), "Sadoc, the priest, he put in the place of Abiathar": Solomon deposed the priest Abiathar (2:27) and put Sadoc in his place. **R.** AB in the margin (183.14): *Juramentum imperatoris dicitur necessarium sacramentum*, "the emperor's oath is called a necessary sacrament".

[^13]: **C.** *post infidelitatem*: "after unbelief [set in]", that is, once the church had fallen from the faith; the phrase may also mean "after the [king's] unbelief". The relative *quod* refers loosely to the blessing.

[^14]: **C.** *Tercio patet*: no first or second point precedes in this passage, and the sentence ends with a comma at the foot of p. 183, where Diceto's list resumes. It reads like a remark set into the list, perhaps from the margin.

[^15]: **S.** Diceto, ed. Stubbs, vol. 1, p. 200; the entries that follow at pp. 211, 216, 246 and 303 (Loserth) (LEDGER P08e-6). **R.** Hus, *Opera* fol. 230b (Loserth).

[^16]: **C.** *Parma ruina fuit*: "Parma was ruin", or "Parma's was the ruin"; with the first line, the couplet plays on Parma as Cadalus's strength and his fall.

[^17]: **C.** *nulli loco pape*: "to no one in the place of pope". **T.** *per plures annos*: one codex reads *populares annos* (apparatus 184.19); the print is translated.

[^18]: **C.** The verse sets Peter Leonis (Anacletus II) against Innocent II, whose name before election was Gregory.

[^19]: **C.** The entry is compressed: Octavian (Victor IV) died in schism, Guido of Crema (Paschal III) followed him, and John (Calixtus III) was later reconciled. *et sic Guido Cremensis* has no verb; *intruditur* is understood from the clause before.
