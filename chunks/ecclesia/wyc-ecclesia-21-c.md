# *De ecclesia*, chapter 21, part 3: the lying witnesses: Bonaventure on the character of every order

## ¶1 · 498.30–500.2

> [p. 498] Et post respondet ad quintuplex argumentum per quod probaretur ficta sentencia, et patet primum commentum mendacii et repugnancia testium adductorum. [p. 499] Si (inquam) liceret de testibus mendacibus colare commentum in quo erraverint, patet quod sentencia summe heretica posset colligi nedum ex sentencia dyaboli et membrorum suorum, sed ex sentencia doctorum catholicorum. Dyabolus enim ex arte mendacii inmiscet sepe cum mendaciis veritatem, igitur quando rite capitur unus doctor in testem, supponi debet quod non sit contrarius illi in eodem libro et eadem materia cuius sentenciam testaretur, etsi arguatur quod ego sim in eadem dampnacione, cum allego pro una supposicione quam supposui totum mundum, et constat quod mundus est mendax et proditor. Talia autem argumenta non sunt digna ut remittantur in vicum scolarum examinanda sophistis, sicut conclusio supradicta que ponit quod eadem ecclesia in numero que ante mortem Christi fuit synagoga incepit post mortem Christi, sed brasiatricibus Oxoniensibus mittenda est ista argucia examini concludens ex particularibus particulare eis impertinens, sicut replicacio qua dictum est quod non valet fingere hoc vel illud mitti debet grammaticis. Ego, inquit, valeo hoc fingere (nam sicut sunt multi mundi, sic valet) est equivocum iuxta grammaticos sic loquentes: Qui valet ille potest, et qui valet hic validus est; impersonale nunc personale notatur. Quid ergo totum genus hominum iudicare potest de istis arguciis? Secundum commentum mendacii est de caractere ordinis quod solum sacerdotibus imprimatur, et allegatur in testimonium huius ficticie Bonaventura super dist. XXIVª quarti Sentenciarum; et per illud mendacium dicit se tollere supposicionem quam supposui [p. 500] describendo clericum. Ostenditur autem illud mendacium ex veritate scripture doctorum.

And afterwards he answers the fivefold argument by which the feigned [teaching]{g:sentencia} would be proved; and so the first fabrication of the lie, and the conflict of the witnesses brought forward, is clear.[^1] [499] If (I say) it were permitted to strain out of lying witnesses the fabrication in which they have erred, it is clear that a most heretical teaching could be gathered not only from the teaching of the devil and his [members]{g:membrum}, but from the teaching of catholic doctors. For the devil, by the art of lying, often mingles truth with lies; therefore, when one doctor is duly taken as a witness, it ought to be supposed that he is not contrary, in the same book and the same matter, to that [teaching] to which he would be bearing witness;[^2] even if it should be argued that I am under the same condemnation, since for one supposition that I made I allege the whole world, and it is evident that the world is a liar and a traitor.[^3] And such arguments are not worthy to be sent back to the street of the schools to be examined by sophists, as [neither is] the [conclusion]{g:conclusio} stated above, which holds that the [church]{g:ecclesia} which before Christ's death was the synagogue, the same in number, began after Christ's death; but this [quibble]{g:argucia}, [inferring]{g:concludere} from particulars a particular irrelevant to them, is to be sent to the Oxford alewives for examination;[^4] just as the rejoinder about what was said, that "it does not avail to feign this or that", ought to be sent to the grammarians. "I," he says, "am able to feign this" (for just as there are many worlds, so it avails): it is equivocal, according to the grammarians who speak thus: "He who *valet* is able, and he who *valet* is strong; now the impersonal is marked, now the personal."[^5] What then can the whole race of men judge of these quibbles? The second fabrication of the lie is about the [character]{g:caracter} of order, that it is imprinted only on [priests]{g:sacerdos}; and Bonaventure on distinction 24 of the fourth [book] of the *Sentences* is [alleged]{g:allegare} in testimony of this [fiction]{g:ficticia}; and by that lie he says he [disposes of]{g:tollere} the supposition that I supposed [500] in defining the cleric.[^6] And that lie is shown [to be one] from the truth of the doctors' writing.

## ¶2 · 500.3–500.25

> [p. 500] Querit autem primo utrum in omnibus ordinibus caracter imprimitur et, probando quod sic, per quadruplex argumentum recitat duas opiniones quas reprobat; quarum prima dicit quod caracter in solo ordine sacerdotali imprimitur, in ordinibus autem inferioribus solum fit disposicio ad sacerdotalem caracterem, sed hoc dicit esse falsum. Cum in ordinibus sacris inferioribus fiat segregacio perpetua clericorum a laycis, ideo manifestum est quod limitacio in illis ordinibus est vel requirit clericalem caracterem. Secunda opinio quam reprobat est quod caracter imprimitur in omnibus ordinibus sacris, sed non in inferioribus, quia isti et non alii possunt laicis commisceri; sed cum nullus ordo sit iterabilis et quilibet habeat aliquid de racione sacramenti, videtur quod in quolibet sit aliquid quod habeat racionem rei et signi perpetui; hoc autem caracterem dicimus. Ideo est tercia opinio probabilior quod in omnibus ordinibus caracter imprimitur sicut raciones ostendunt expresse. Cum enim caracter sit signaculum expressum, distinctivum et perpetuum et Christo configurativum, et hoc est in omnibus ordinibus, non dubitandum est in omnibus caracterem imprimi.

And he asks first whether a character is imprinted in all orders and, proving that it is by a fourfold argument, he recites two opinions, which he rejects. The first of them says that a character is imprinted in the priestly order alone, while in the lower orders there is made only a disposition to the priestly character; but this he says is false. Since in the lower sacred orders there is made a perpetual separation of the clergy from the laity, it is therefore manifest that the [assignment]{g:limitacio} in those orders is, or requires, a clerical character.[^7] The second opinion, which he rejects, is that a character is imprinted in all the sacred orders but not in the lower ones, since these and not the others can mingle with lay people; but since no order is repeatable and each has something of the nature of a sacrament, it seems that in each there is something that has the nature of a thing and of a perpetual sign; and this we call a character.[^8] Therefore the third opinion is more probable, that a character is imprinted in all orders, as the reasons show expressly. For since a character is a seal that is express, distinctive and perpetual and configuring to Christ, and this is so in all orders, it is not to be doubted that a character is imprinted in all.[^9]

## ¶3 · 500.26–502.9

> [p. 500] Et consequenter respondet ad obiectum quadruplicem. Ad primum quo sic arguitur: Caracter est signaculum spirituale, ergo in illis solis imprimitur in quibus datur aliqua potestas nova et spiritualis, sed potestas omnium ordinum inferior est corporalis, ut aperire ostium vel custodire, prophetias dicere, candelabra [p. 501] deferre, ergo in istis nullo modo imprimitur caracter. Dicendum, inquit, quod omnibus ordinibus datur potestas vel spiritualis de se vel ad spiritualem ordinans. Unde potestas hostiarii est ad admittendum homines ad divinum sacrificium, potestas lectoris non solum ad excitandum auditorem exteriorem, verum eciam interiorem, et sic de aliis. Secundo sic arguitur: Caracter est signaculum quod respicit vel habet imprimi in ymagine ad configurandum ipsam Deo; sed actus quorundam ordinum solum sunt circa ministeria inferiora, ergo cum talia non spectant ad configuracionem huius, videtur quod in illis nullus caracter imprimitur. Dicendum, inquit, quod caracter ordinis ut conservativus animam configurat Deo secundum ymaginem quasi assimilatam Deo, quod potissime fit in ordine supremo, vel dicendum quod magis proprie configuratur tale signaculum Christo. Unde cum Christus triplex habuerit officium, scilicet ministri in agendo, pugilis in paciendo, redemptoris in moriendo, triplex fuit sacramentum secundum quod homo configuratur sic Christo tripliciter; unde tantum est triplex sacramentum in quo fit caracteris impressio. Nam baptismus configuratur morienti et redimenti, unde sepelitur homo in mortem. Confirmacio vero configuratur pacienti et pugnanti, ideo signum crucis fit in fronte. Sed ordo configuratur ministranti et operanti, sicut magister ostendit in litera, et quia in omnibus ordinibus configuratur clericus Christo secundum plus et minus, ideo in omnibus illis caracter imprimitur. Tercio sic [p. 502] arguitur: Caracter est signum indelebile; ergo si collocat hominem in aliquo statu, impossibile est ab illo statu recedere, quin fiat contumelia sacramento, sed in minoribus ordinibus constituti possunt licite redire ad laycum, ergo in eis caracter non imprimitur. Dicendum, inquit, quod caracter omnium ordinum manet in anima sive revertentis ad laicatum sive morientis ut signaculum distinctivum et hoc vel ad gloriam si bene usus est, vel ad ignominiam si male.

And consequently he answers the fourfold [objection]{g:objectum}. To the first, by which it is argued thus: A character is a spiritual seal; therefore it is imprinted only in those in which some new and spiritual power is given; but the power of all the lower orders is bodily, as to open a door or guard it, to speak prophecies, to [501] carry candlesticks; therefore in these a character is in no way imprinted. "It must be said," he says, "that in all orders a power is given that is either spiritual in itself or ordering to the spiritual. Hence the power of the porter is for admitting men to the divine sacrifice; the power of the lector is not only for rousing the outward hearer but also the inward; and so of the others."[^10] Secondly it is argued thus: A character is a seal that regards, or is to be imprinted in, the image, to configure it to God; but the acts of some orders concern only lower ministries; therefore, since such things do not pertain to the configuring of this [image], it seems that in them no character is imprinted. "It must be said," he says, "that the character of order, as preservative, configures the soul to God according to the image, as [a soul] made like to God, which is done most of all in the highest order; or it must be said that such a seal is more properly configured to Christ.[^11] Hence, since Christ had a threefold office, namely of minister in acting, of champion in suffering, of redeemer in dying, there was a threefold sacrament according to which a man is thus configured to Christ in three ways; hence there is only a threefold sacrament in which the impression of a character is made. For baptism is configured to the one dying and redeeming; hence a man is buried into death. And confirmation is configured to the one suffering and fighting; therefore the sign of the cross is made on the forehead. But order is configured to the one ministering and working, as the Master shows in the text;[^12] and since in all orders a cleric is configured to Christ more or less, therefore in all of them a character is imprinted." Thirdly [502] it is argued thus: A character is an indelible sign; therefore if it places a man in some state, it is impossible to withdraw from that state without an insult being done to the sacrament; but those placed in the minor orders can lawfully return to the lay [state]; therefore in them a character is not imprinted. "It must be said," he says, "that the character of all orders remains in the soul, whether of one returning to the lay state or of one dying, as a distinctive seal, and this either to glory, if he has used it well, or to ignominy, if badly."[^13]

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Quintuplex argumentum*: the five arguments for mitigation, which Bonaventure answers (495.20–497.20, chunk 21-b). *Primum commentum mendacii*: the first of the opponent's fabrications, that Bonaventure taught prayer for the damned; the second follows at 499.27. *Repugnancia testium*: the witness alleged contradicts the one who alleges him. *Sentencia*: "teaching", here the opponent's.

[^2]: **C.** *Colare commentum*: to strain out of a witness the one point on which he erred, and cite him for it. *Contrarius illi … cuius sentenciam testaretur*: *illi* is taken as the teaching for which the doctor is cited. *Membrorum suorum*: the devil's members, as the predestinate are Christ's.

[^3]: **S.** *In eadem dampnacione*: the Thief's words in Luke 23:40 (cited at 496.3), turned on himself. **C.** Wyclif grants that he too might be said to cite an unreliable witness, since for his supposition about the cleric (cap. 19, p. 441) he appealed to the usage of the whole world. **T.** The apparatus at 499.21 reads "In codd.: in quia"; it does not show which printed words this replaces.

[^4]: **C.** *In vicum scolarum*: the street of the schools, Oxford's *vicus scolarum* (LEDGER E21c-1). *Brasiatricibus*: alewives, the women who brewed ale. *Conclusio supradicta*: the opponent's thesis that the church began after Christ's death (493.15–17, chunk 21-a). *Ex particularibus particulare*: from particular premisses alone nothing follows, still less a conclusion irrelevant to them. **T.** The codices read *Oxon.* (499.18); *Oxoniensibus* is Loserth's expansion. **R.** Side-note 499.12.

[^5]: **C.** Wyclif had written *Nec valet fingere* (492.7–8), impersonal, "it avails"; the opponent answered *Ego valeo hoc fingere*, personal, "I am able". *Replicacio qua dictum est*: literally "the rejoinder by which it was said"; taken as the rejoinder made to that saying. *Nam sicut sunt multi mundi, sic valet*: a sarcastic aside, as if it "avails" in the way there are many worlds. The verse (*Qui valet ille potest, et qui valet hic validus est; impersonale nunc personale notatur*) is a grammarians' mnemonic for the senses of *valet* (LEDGER E21c-2); *valet* is left in Latin because the pun does not pass into English. **T.** AA₁ *validus sit*; B *validus fit* (499.24).

[^6]: **S.** Bonaventure, *In IV Sent.* d. 24, p. 2, a. 1, q. 1 (Opp. IV, p. 650; Loserth, 499.30; LEDGER E21c-3). Wyclif's supposition defining the cleric: cap. 19, p. 441 (Loserth, 499.27; LEDGER E21c-4). **C.** *Caracter ordinis*: the character of the sacrament of order. *Ex veritate scripture doctorum*: from the truth of what the doctors wrote, here Bonaventure's own text, not holy scripture. **R.** Side-note 499.27; A in the margin at 499.27: *Secundum commentum*.

[^7]: **S.** Bonaventure, ibid. (Loserth, 500.6: what follows is drawn from Bonaventure on book IV of the *Sentences*; LEDGER E21c-3). **C.** *Limitacio*: "assignment", the deputing of the cleric to an office by the lower sacred orders. **R.** Side-note 500.3; A in the margin at 500.5, 500.6, 500.9: *Duplex opinio*; *De caractere ordinis*; *Bonaventura*.

[^8]: **C.** *Isti … possunt laicis commisceri*: those in the lower orders can mix with the laity. *Iterabilis*: repeatable. *Racionem rei et signi perpetui*: the nature of the scholastic *res et sacramentum*, at once a reality and a lasting sign.

[^9]: **T.** The codices read *distinctum … configuratum* (500.22); Loserth prints *distinctivum … configurativum*, apparently after Bonaventure, and the print is translated. **C.** *Signaculum*: "seal", the mark impressed. **R.** Side-note 500.19.

[^10]: **C.** *Obiectum quadruplicem*: the genders do not agree (*obiectum* neuter, *quadruplicem* not); taken as "the fourfold objection". *Potestas omnium ordinum inferior*: taken as the power of the lower orders. The offices named: porter (the door), lector (the prophecies), acolyte (the candles). **R.** Side-note 500.26; A and B in the margin at 500.26: *Quadruplex argumentum*; A at 501.2: *Responsio*.

[^11]: **T.** Bonaventure reads *ut est conservantis animam* (Loserth, 501.14). **C.** *Respicit vel habet imprimi in ymagine*: the image of God in the soul. *Configuracionem huius*: of this [image]. *Quasi assimilatam*: taken with *animam*. **R.** A in the margin at 501.15: *Responsio*.

[^12]: **S.** *Sepelitur homo in mortem*: Romans 6:4 ("buried together with him by baptism into death"). *Magister … in litera*: Peter Lombard, in the text of the *Sentences* (IV d. 24; LEDGER E21c-5). **T.** The codices read *habuit* (501.18), Loserth *habuerit*; Bonaventure has *sicut recte ostendit* for *sicut magister ostendit* (501.27–28). **R.** A in the margin at 501.22: *Tantum in tribus sacramentis caracter imprimitur*.

[^13]: **T.** AA₁ *signaculum destitutum*, B *distinctum* (502.8); the print's *distinctivum* is translated. **C.** *Laycum, laicatum*: the lay state. **R.** A in the margin at 501.28: *Utrum in singulis ordinibus caracter imprimitur*; at 502.6: *Responsio*.
