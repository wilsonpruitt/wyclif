# *De potestate pape*, chapter 12, part 19: the pope and heresy; the first six of the twelve abuses

## ¶1 · 385.32–386.26

> [p. 385] Ulterius quantum ad decisiones heresum, pro quibus dicitur esse necessarium esse papam, patet quod a tempore dotacionis cesaree alii fuerant [p. 386] apciores ad discernendum veritatem catholicam quam papa cesareus. Et si dicitur quod auctoritate sui debent vocati decidere, cum solus papa potest synodum congregare, picta et ficta tradicio balbutitur; nam auctoritate illius debet christianus ad edificacionem ecclesie proferre sentenciam a quo capit scienciam; quod est pape impertinens, cum singulariter fit a Deo; debet igitur fieri synodus ex mocione divina consensu unanimi. Ad quem consensum debet papa precipue laborare et in defectu sui alius fidelis ecclesie, ut cesar vel alius magis ydoneus. Unde credo quod facta synodo ad discernendum quid est naturaliter hostia consecrata Robertus et multi prelati cesarei proferrent satis nudam sentenciam nec excederent in diccione procuratoria cesarem vel simplicem ydiotam. Alii autem dicunt quod est quantitas, alii vero quod est qualitas, et tercii quod est panis et sic de aliis variis sentenciis, quas non probant. Ideo sanum videtur fideles se tenere in limitibus quos lex Christi edocet et in aliis curiositatibus non musitare vel plus sapere quam oportet; sufficit enim fides scripture certificare quoad omnia agibilia vel credibilia, que necesse est viantes cognoscere. De toto isto patet diffuse alibi et cetera.

Further, as to the deciding of heresies, for which it is said to be necessary that there be a [pope]{g:papa}, it is clear that from the time of the caesarean [endowment]{g:dotacio} others have been [386] more apt to discern catholic truth than the [caesarean pope]{g:papa-cesareus}.[^1] And if it is said that those called [to a synod] ought to decide by his [authority]{g:auctoritas}, since the pope alone can gather a synod, a painted and feigned tradition is being stammered out; for a Christian ought to put forth a [teaching]{g:sentencia} for the edifying of the [church]{g:ecclesia} by the authority of him from whom he takes his knowledge; and this is [irrelevant]{g:impertinens} to the pope, since it comes uniquely from God. A synod therefore ought to be held by divine motion, with unanimous consent. For that consent the pope ought chiefly to labour, and in his default another of the church's faithful, such as the emperor or another more fit. Hence I believe that, were a synod held to discern what the consecrated host is in its nature, Robert and many [caesarean prelates]{g:prelatus-cesarius} would put forth a teaching bare enough, and would not surpass the emperor or a plain [unlettered man]{g:ydiota} in proctorial pronouncement.[^2] And some say that it is quantity, and others that it is quality, and a third party that it is bread, and so of other various teachings, which they do not prove. Therefore it seems sound for the faithful to keep themselves within the [limits]{g:limites} that the [law of Christ]{g:lex-christi} teaches, and not to mutter about other curiosities, or "to be more wise than it behoveth"; for the faith of scripture suffices to make certain all things to be done or believed that [those on the road]{g:viantes} must know. Of all this it is clear at length elsewhere, etc.[^3]

## ¶2 · 386.27–386.35

> [p. 386] Possunt autem abusiones Romani pontificis reduci ad numerum duodecim, ut alie abusiones communiter reducuntur. Prima quod institutis ad hoc cardinalibus dimissis ministeriis que Christus instituit, eligant unum caput, quem ex institucione cesarea papam vocant. Hoc nedum caret auctoritate scripture, sed videtur societati apostolorum et eleccioni Mathie singulariter facte per Dominum repugnare.

And the abuses of the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex} can be reduced to the number twelve, as other abuses are commonly reduced.[^4] The first: that cardinals, instituted for this, setting aside the ministries that Christ instituted, elect one [head]{g:caput}, whom by caesarean institution they call pope. This not only lacks the authority of scripture, but seems [to be incompatible]{g:repugnare} with the fellowship of the apostles and with the election of Matthias, made uniquely by the Lord.[^5]

## ¶3 · 387.1–387.21

> [p. 387] Secunda quod mundanos honores contra Christum et apostolos tantum affectant. Debet enim recolere, quia ex Matth. XX debet esse omnibus fratribus suis servus, unde Gregorius II. Pastoral. VI: Petrus, inquit, autore Deo sancte ecclesie principatum tenens a bene agente Cornelio, et sese ei humiliter prosternente, inmoderacius venerari recusavit seque illi similem recognovit dicens: Surge, ne feceris, et ego ipse homo sum, sicut et tu. Patet historia Act. X. 26. Et sequitur: Paulus bene agentibus fratribus prelatum se esse nesciebat, cum diceret: Non quia dominamur fidei vestre sed adiutores sumus gaudii vestri. Atque illico adiunxit: In fide enim statis, ac si id quod pretulerat, aperiret dicens: Ideo non dominamur fidei vestre, quia fide statis; equales enim vobis sumus, in quo vos stare cognoscimus; quasi prelatum se esse fratribus nesciebat, cum diceret: Facti sumus sicut parvuli in medio vestrum, prima Tess. II, 7. Et rursum, nos autem servos vestros per Christum. Patet . . . .

The second: that they [desire]{g:affectare} worldly honours so much, against Christ and the apostles. For he ought to recall that by Matthew 20 he ought to be the servant of all his brethren; hence Gregory, *Pastoral [Rule]* II.6:[^6] "Peter," he says, "holding by God's authority the chief rule of the holy church, refused to be venerated beyond measure by Cornelius, who was doing well and humbly prostrating himself before him, and acknowledged himself like him, saying: Arise, do not do it; I myself also am a man, as thou art." The story is plain in Acts 10:26. And it follows: "Paul did not know himself to be [prelate]{g:prelatus} over brethren who did well, when he said: Not because we exercise dominion over your faith: but we are helpers of your joy. And at once he added: For in faith you stand; as if he were opening what he had put forward, saying: Therefore we exercise no dominion over your faith, because you stand by faith; for we are equal to you in that in which we know you to stand. As if he did not know himself to be prelate over the brethren, when he said: We became as little ones in the midst of you," 1 Thessalonians 2:7. "And again: and ourselves your servants through Christ." It is clear . . . .[^7]

## ¶4 · 387.22–388.2

> [p. 387] Tercia est quod papa innitatur seculari dominio. Unde Gregorius ubi supra: Ne presidentis animus ad elacionem potestatis sue delectacione rapiatur. Recte Ecclesiastici XXXII. 1 dicitur: Ducem te posuerunt, noli extolli sed esto in illis quasi unus ex illis. Hinc eciam Petrus ait: Non dominantes in clero sed forma facti gregis. Hinc eciam Veritas per semetipsam dicit: Scitis quia principes gencium dominantur eorum, non sic erit inter vos. [p. 388] Ideo secundum Bernhardum: Nihil intoxicat ani- mum pape plus quam dominandi libido.

The third is that the pope relies on secular [lordship]{g:dominium}. Hence Gregory in the same place: "Lest the mind of him who presides be carried off into pride by delight in his own [power]{g:potestas}, it is rightly said in Ecclesiasticus 32:1: They have made thee leader: be not lifted up, but be among them as one of them. Hence also Peter says: Not lording it over the clergy, but being made a pattern of the flock. Hence also the Truth in his own person says: You know that the princes of the Gentiles lord it over them; it shall not be so among you."[^8] [388] Therefore, according to Bernard: "Nothing poisons the mind of the pope more than the lust of lording it."[^9]

## ¶5 · 388.3–388.12

> [p. 388] Quarta quod papa sit dux loquens grandia, magnificando suam potenciam et faciens modicum m effectu. Ex hinc itaque docet beatus Gregorius, ut patet XCIX distinccione: Quod papa non debet vocari universalis episcopus. Hec enim est condicio Antichristi, cum Christus cepit facere et docere, celans suam potenciam, nisi de quanto est necessarium ipsam detegere et credere ad salutem. Mendax autem potestatis ostensio inducit perfidiam Antichristi.

The fourth: that the pope is a leader speaking great things, magnifying his [power]{g:potencia} and doing little in effect. Hence, accordingly, blessed Gregory teaches, as is clear in Distinction 99: "That the pope ought not to be called universal [bishop]{g:episcopus}." For this is the condition of Antichrist, since Christ began to do and to teach, hiding his power, except so far as it is necessary to [expose]{g:detegere} it and [for it] to be believed unto salvation. But a lying display of power brings in the perfidy of Antichrist.[^10]

## ¶6 · 388.13–388.30

> [p. 388] Quinta quod papa, dimissa arte paciendi iniurias et facta evangelica inducendi timorem filialem subiectis, innitatur crudeliter pugnis corporalibus et vindictis non solum in causa Christi ad vindicandum suas iniurias, sed precipue in causa propria ad magnificandum suas potencias, et ad hoc allegat Gregorius II Pastor ubi supra, illud Matth. XXIV, 48: Quod si dixerit malus ille servus in corde suo: Moram facit Dominus meus venire, et ceperit percutere conservos suos, Dominus suus inopinate veniet et ponet partem eius cum ypocritis. Inter ypocritas, inquit, iure deputatur qui ex simulacione discipline ministerium regiminis vertit in usum dominacionis. Deficiente enim spirituali armatura et specialiter gladio spiritus, necesse est quod papa cesareus innitatur refrigescentibus armis timorem servilem inducentibus prepostere contra Christum.

The fifth: that the pope, setting aside the art of suffering injuries and the gospel deeds of inducing filial fear in subjects, relies cruelly on bodily fights and vengeances, not only in Christ's cause, to avenge his injuries, but chiefly in his own cause, to magnify his powers; and on this Gregory, *Pastoral* II, in the same place, [alleges]{g:allegare} that of Matthew 24:48: "But if that evil servant shall say in his heart: My lord is long a coming: and shall begin to strike his fellow servants, his lord shall come unexpectedly and shall appoint his portion with the hypocrites. Among the hypocrites," he says, "is he rightly reckoned who under pretence of discipline turns the ministry of rule to the uses of the [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio}."[^11] For when the spiritual armour fails, and especially the sword of the spirit, it is necessary that the caesarean pope rely on arms that grow cold, inducing servile fear, preposterously, against Christ.[^12]

## ¶7 · 388.31–389.27

> [p. 388] Sexta quod papa appetat dominari nedum pecunia et prediis sed suis fratribus, non secundum racionem, qua similantur bestiis ad brutalitatem [p. 389] in honorem Domini expurgandam, sed, dimissa illa sollicitudine vicaria pro Christo, nitatur modo Luciferi secundum omnem racionem que sapit excellenciam suam fratribus suis et specialiter in testinacionem veritatis precellentibus dominari. Istam materiam declarat Gregorius II Pastor. VI notans primo quod culpa ex insufficiencia proprii regiminis induxit huiusmodi prelaciam, secundo quod prelatus debet esse humilis socius bonis subditis, et tercio quod regimen suum debet exercere cum debita severitate in feras egentes regimine, ut puta contra eos in quibus Ihesus contempnitur. Et adducit ad hoc illud Genesis XLVI, 34: Pastores ovium sumus. Secundo Genes. IX, 1 dicit Deus terror vester ac tremor sit super cuncta animalia terre; in quo esse super homines prohibetur. In hoc enim, quod metum sibi a perverse viventibus exigunt, quasi non hominibus sed animalibus dominantur. Ex istis elicit sanctus tercio multas demonstrabiles veritates, prima quod contra naturam est et superbia Luciferi velle ab equalibus in natura et moribus dominari, secunda quod curatus ut prosit debet appetere illud officium, non ut presit, et tercia quod nemo assumeret pastoris officium nisi evidenter haberet artem spiritualis medici ad prodessendum anime subditorum.

The sixth: that the pope seeks [to lord it]{g:dominari} not only over money and estates but over his brethren; not according to the respect in which they are likened to beasts, [389] for purging brutishness to the honour of the Lord, but, setting aside that vicarial solicitude for Christ, he strives after the manner of Lucifer, according to every respect that savours of his own excellence, to lord it over his brethren, and especially over those who excel in [witness] to the truth.[^13] This matter Gregory sets out, *Pastoral* II.6, noting first that it was fault, from the insufficiency of one's own rule, that brought in prelacy of this kind; second, that a prelate ought to be a humble fellow to good subjects; and third, that he ought to exercise his rule with due severity upon the wild beasts that need rule, as, say, against those in whom Jesus is despised. And he brings to this that of Genesis 46:34: "We are shepherds of sheep." Second, in Genesis 9:1 God says: "Let the fear and dread of you be upon all the beasts of the earth"; in which to be over men is forbidden. "For in this, that they require fear of themselves from those who live perversely, they lord it, as it were, not over men but over animals."[^14] From these the saint draws, third, many demonstrable truths: first, that it is against nature, and the pride of Lucifer, to wish to lord it over one's equals in nature and morals; second, that a [curate]{g:curatus} ought to desire that office in order to profit, not in order to preside; and third, that no one should take up the office of shepherd unless he evidently had the art of the spiritual physician for profiting the souls of his subjects.[^15]

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Dotacio cesarea*: the endowment of the church by Constantine, from which Wyclif dates the decline of the papacy. The manuscripts' margin at 385.32: *Hereses decidere ad quem spectat*.
[^2]: **R/C.** *Robertus*: presumably Robert of Geneva, elected in September 1378 as Clement VII against Urban VI (to be checked, LEDGER P12s-1). *In diccione procuratoria*: we take *diccio* as "pronouncement", the speech of those who claim to act as Christ's proctors; *dicio*, "authority" ("in proctorial authority"), is the alternative.
[^3]: **C/S.** The three positions concern what the consecrated host is in its nature: quantity, quality, or bread (Wyclif's own view). He names no authors. *Plus sapere quam oportet*: Rom. 12:3 (DR "not to be more wise than it behoveth to be wise"). The manuscripts read *de aliis de variis* (386.19).
[^4]: **S/R.** The count of twelve abuses recalls the pseudo-Cyprianic *De duodecim abusivis saeculi* (to be checked, LEDGER P12s-2). The manuscripts' margin: *Abusiones pape*, and at 386.29 the numeral *1*.
[^5]: **S.** Acts 1:26 (DR "the lot fell upon Matthias"): *singulariter … per Dominum*, the lot leaving the choice to God alone.
[^6]: **S.** Matt. 20:27 (DR "he that will be first among you shall be your servant"). Gregory, *Regula pastoralis* II.6 (Loserth; the manuscripts read *II Past. XVII*, which he marks as an error; LEDGER P12s-3). The manuscripts' margin numbers the abuse *2*.
[^7]: **T/S.** Loserth prints *a bene agente* and *bene agentibus*; the manuscripts read *benedicente*, *benedicentibus* (387.6, 11). We translate the print. Acts 10:26 (DR), with Gregory's *ne feceris*, "do not do it", added. 2 Cor. 1:23 (DR); 1 Thess. 2:7, where the manuscripts read *vestri* and the Vulgate has no *sicut*; 2 Cor. 4:5, with *per Christum* for the Vulgate *per Iesum*. *Patet . . . .* is the print's gap; Loserth would add *historia II Cor. IV, 5*.
[^8]: **S/T.** Gregory, *Reg. past.* II.6 continued. Ecclus. 32:1: the Vulgate reads *Rectorem te posuerunt?* (DR "Have they made thee ruler? be not lifted up: be among them as one of them"); Gregory's text has *Ducem*, "leader". The print's margin gives "Eccl. XXXI". 1 Pet. 5:3 (DR "lording it over the clergy … a pattern of the flock"); Matt. 20:25–26 (DR). B lacks *rapiatur*, which A adds in the margin (387.24). The manuscripts' margin numbers the abuse *3*.
[^9]: **S.** Bernard, *De consideratione* III.1 (Loserth's reference; the wording to be checked, LEDGER P12s-4).
[^10]: **S.** *Loquens grandia*: Dan. 7:20 (DR "a mouth speaking great things"), of the little horn. Decretum D.99 c.3 *Primae sedis* (Loserth; the form is ours; LEDGER P12s-5). *Cepit facere et docere*: Acts 1:1 (DR "began to do and to teach"). The manuscripts number the abuse *IV*.
[^11]: **S.** Matt. 24:48–51, through Gregory (*Reg. past.* II.6): *facit* for the Vulgate *fecit*; v. 49 is cut short and v. 50 is given as *inopinate veniet*, "shall come unexpectedly". The manuscripts' margin numbers the abuse *5*.
[^12]: **C/S.** *Gladius spiritus*: Eph. 6:17. *Refrigescentibus armis*: "arms that grow cold", which we take as the arms of those whose charity has grown cold (cf. Matt. 24:12); the sense is doubtful. *Prepostere*: in reversed order, the servile fear put where filial fear should be.
[^13]: **T/C.** *Testinacionem* is so in the manuscripts (Loserth: *ita codd.*) and is no Latin word; "[witness]" guesses at *testificacionem*. *Racio* here is "respect": a ruler may be over men only so far as they are like beasts, in their vices, which is Gregory's point below. The manuscripts read *Cap. XVII* for *VI* (389.6) and number the abuse *6*.
[^14]: **S.** Gregory, *Reg. past.* II.6 (Loserth at 389.19: "St. Gregorius l. c."). *Pastores ovium sumus* is Gen. 47:3 (DR "We, thy servants, are shepherds"); Gen. 46:34 reads *Viri pastores*. The words given as Gen. 9:1 are Gen. 9:2 (DR).
[^15]: **C.** *Curatus* stands for Gregory's ruler of souls; *ut prosit … non ut presit*, "to profit, not to preside", is the old play on *prodesse* and *praeesse* (cf. Augustine, *De civitate Dei* 19.19; to be checked, LEDGER P12s-6). *Prodessendum* is the print's form of the gerund.
