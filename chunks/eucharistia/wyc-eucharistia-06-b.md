# *De eucharistia*, chapter 6, part 2: the baked bread, the three virtues misplaced, and the paschal lamb

## ¶1 · 161.14–163.12

> [p. 161] Et oportet quarto quod persona salvanda recipiat formam panis que imposita furno tribulacionis recipiat ex fervore caritatis quam habet ad Deum duriciem gracie consumate, et sic per ignem caritatis incorporabitur finaliter in circulo pani vivo et verificabitur illud Apostoli 1. Cor. Xº, 17: Quoniam unus panis et unum corpus multi sumus. Nam omnes de uno pane et de uno calice participamus. Et sic intelligunt de triplici gracia, scilicet preveniente quantum ad meritoriam passionem; nam pati et recipere precedit in nobis naturaliter meritoriam accionem, ad quam requiritur gracia cooperans. Et tercio post graciam predestinacionis requiritur ad generanciam et continuacionem gracia consumans. Oportet enim vitam veram dare nobis graciam calidam confuse informantem; unde signum infallibile est quod manducamus hunc panem indigne quod inter omnes gentes pro temporalibus magis contendimus et refrigescente caritate multorum ex superhabundante frigiditate temporalium preoccupancium mentem egeni in spiritualibus [p. 162] sequelam Christi magis contempnimus, dum tercio tempus et studium circa avidam conquisicionem, improvidam dispensacionem et avaram retencionem temporalium occupamus, sic quod ubi nobis sacerdotibus secundum regulam Christi omnia debent esse communia, unus manducans hunc panem esurit et alter est ebrius. Et inter omnes sacerdotes illi superiores qui efficacius imitarentur Christi vestigia in faciendo istud officium magis contemptibiliter quia plus Christo dissimiliter celebrant vel audiunt missas suas, in tantum quod ut periuri ad sui dispendium memorantur et nominant nomen Dei, sic et isti memorantur in sacramento eukaristie vite Christi; ideo tollerabilius et minus nocivum foret eis et populo quod non facerent talem falsam memoriam Salvatoris. Et sic ubi domini temporales care conducunt huiusmodi, ut eos habeant dignos et assiduos oratores, habent eos precipuos proditores. Nec mirum, quia contra legem Christi et racionem sacerdotalis ministerii dissolvendo statum primevum, onerant eos ac inhabilitant ad implendum suum ministerium. Ideo oportet quod racio militet contra utrumque membrum ecclesie sic protervum. Hoc enim sacramentum significaret quoscunque qui ipsum digne suscipiunt paratos ut pro Christo et causa legis Christi moriantur cum gaudio sed signata obmittimus. Ad signa autem que ritum et fastum sapiunt tamquam generacio adultera nimis attendimus; in tantum quod superiores persone ecclesie signis istis sensibilibus sic cecantur quod Samaritanis et paganis dampnabilius adorant (ut Christus dicit Joh. IVº capitulo) quod ignorant. Numquid credimus [p. 163] quod congregatis omnibus prelatis cesariis omnes sciunt quid est hostia consecrata? et errante intellectu necesse est quod erret affectus, cum cecitas oculi precipitat ambulantem. Cum igitur accipimus hoc sacramentum gracia gratitudinis Domini memorande, patet quod ad iudicium nobis manducamus et bibimus, quia remanemus post tam sollempnem memoriam Domino tam ingrati. Unde decretum Augustini positum in de Consecracione distinccione II sic loquitur: Quia morte Christi liberati sumus, huius rei memores in edendo carnem et potando sanguinem eius, que pro nobis oblata sunt nos obligaciores signamus.

And fourthly the person to be saved must receive the form of [bread]{g:panis}, which, placed in the oven of tribulation, receives from the fervour of [charity]{g:caritas} which he has towards God the hardness of consummate grace; and so through the fire of charity he will finally be incorporated, [in a round]{g:in-circulo}, into the living bread,[^1] and that word of the Apostle [will be true]{g:verificari}, 1 Cor. 10:17: "For we, being many, are one bread, one body: for all partake of one bread and of one [chalice]{g:calix}."[^2] And so they understand [it] of a threefold grace, namely [prevenient]{g:gracia-preveniens} as to meritorious [being-acted-on]{g:passio};[^3] for to be acted on and to receive precedes in us by nature meritorious action, for which cooperating grace is required. And thirdly, after the grace of predestination, [consummating grace]{g:gracia-consumans} is required for [begetting]{g:generancia} and [continuity]{g:continuacio}.[^4] For the true life must give us warm grace, [informing]{g:informare} [us] [indistinctly]{g:confuse};[^5] hence it is an infallible sign that we eat this bread unworthily, that among all peoples we contend most for [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, and, the charity of many growing cold from the superabounding coldness of temporal goods preoccupying the mind, we, needy in spiritual things, [162] most despise the [following]{g:sequela} of Christ, while, thirdly, we occupy time and study about the greedy acquisition, improvident [dispensation]{g:dispensacio} and avaricious retention of temporal goods;[^6] so that where, for us priests, according to the rule of Christ all things ought to be common, "one" eating this bread "is hungry and another is drunk".[^7] And among all [priests]{g:sacerdos} those superiors who would more effectively imitate the footsteps of Christ in performing this office celebrate or hear their masses more contemptibly, because more unlike Christ,[^8] so much so that, as perjurers make mention of and name the name of God to their own loss, so these too make mention, in the sacrament of the Eucharist, of the life of Christ; therefore it would be more tolerable and less harmful for them and for the people that they did not make such a false memorial of the Saviour. And so, where [temporal lords]{g:temporales-domini} hire such men dearly, to have them as worthy and assiduous [bedesmen]{g:oratores}, they have them as chief [traitors]{g:proditor}.[^9] Nor is it a wonder, for, against the law of Christ and the nature of the priestly ministry, dissolving the primitive [state]{g:status}, they burden them and [disqualify]{g:inhabilitare} them from fulfilling their ministry. Therefore reason must fight against both [members]{g:membrum} of the [church]{g:ecclesia} so [obstinate]{g:protervus}.[^10] For this [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} would signify that whoever receive it worthily are ready to die with joy for Christ and the cause of the law of Christ; but we omit the [things signified]{g:signatum}. And to the [signs]{g:signum} that savour of rite and pomp we attend [too much]{g:nimis}, as an adulterous generation;[^11] so much so that the superior persons of the church are so blinded by these [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} signs that, more damnably than Samaritans and pagans, they adore (as Christ says, John 4) what they know not.[^12] Do we believe [163] that, were all the [caesarean prelates]{g:prelatus} gathered, all of them know what the consecrated [host]{g:hostia} is? And when the understanding errs, the affection must err, since blindness of the eye throws the walker headlong. Since, therefore, we receive this sacrament for the sake of the [kindness]{g:gratitudo} of the Lord, which is to be remembered, it is clear that we eat and drink to our judgement, because after so solemn a memorial we remain so [ungrateful]{g:ingratus} to the Lord.[^13] Hence the [decree]{g:decretum} of Augustine placed in *De consecratione*, distinction 2, speaks thus: "Because we have been freed by the death of Christ, mindful of this thing in eating the flesh and drinking the blood, which were offered for us, we signify ourselves the more bound."[^14]

## ¶2 · 163.13–164.3

> [p. 163] Et patet quomodo fides, spes, caritas sunt necessaria celebranti; sed hodie est nobis nimis sinistra perversio, dum fides infigitur in signo, spes in mundo et caritas in infimo. Ponimus enim plus quam generacio phariseorum adultera fidem huius sacramenti in signo et in perfidis circumstanciis signorum, quia circa illorum observancias est nostra sollicitudo, nostra disputacio et nostra religio. Quoad spem patet quod ipsam ponimus in temporalium possessione, in amicorum confederacione et in prosperitatis mundane perseveracione. Et quoad caritatem patet quod ipsa sequitur in transformacione duas sorores priores, dum illos fratres quos exaltamus ad fimum mundi et honorem seculi, fingimus nos caritative diligere; illum sexum quem appetimus illecebris carnis confundere fingimus nos amare. Et quoad caritatem Dei ille fingitur plus [p. 164] Deum diligere qui superius aspirat vivere, quia (ut inquiunt) Deo similius. Et certum est quod quodlibet istorum est odium.

And it is clear how faith, hope and charity are necessary to the one who celebrates; but today there is among us an exceedingly sinister perversion, while faith is fixed in the sign, hope in the world and charity in the lowest.[^15] For, more than the adulterous generation of the Pharisees, we place the faith of this sacrament in the sign and in the faithless circumstances of the signs, for about their observances is our solicitude, our disputation and our religion. As to hope, it is clear that we place it in the possession of temporal goods, in alliance with friends and in the continuance of worldly prosperity. And as to charity, it is clear that it follows its two prior sisters in transformation, while we pretend that we love charitably those brethren whom we exalt to the dung of the world and the honour of the age;[^16] we pretend that we love that sex which we desire to confound with the allurements of the flesh. And as to the charity of God, he is feigned to love [164] God more who aspires to live higher, because (as they say) more like God. And it is certain that each of these is hatred.

## ¶3 · 164.4–165.16

> [p. 164] Quantum ad secundum, scilicet conversacionem virtuosam unitatoriam Jesu Christi patet ex dictis quod gracia huius constituitur hoc sacramentum. Unde inaniter celebrant qui pretereunt hoc figuratum. Figuratur autem nobis hec imitacio conversandi Exod XIIº, 7, ubi precipitur quod in esu agni paschalis ista 14ᶜⁱᵐ observentur, ut exponit Gregorius Super Evangelia Omelia XII: Quis, inquit, sit sanguis agni, non iam audiendo sed bibendo didicistis, qui sanguis super utrumque postem ponitur, quando non solum ore corporis sed eciam ore cordis accipitur aut hauritur. In utroque etenim poste sanguis agni positus est, quando sacramentum passionis illius cum ore ad redempcionem sumitur, ad imitacionem quoque intenta mente cogitatur. Nam qui sic redemptoris sui sanguinem accipit, ut imitari passionem illius necdum velit, in uno poste sanguinem posuit, qui eciam in superliminaribus domorum ponendus est. Quid enim spiritualiter domos nisi mentes nostras accipimus, in quibus per cogitacionem habitamus? Cuius domus superliminare est ipsa intencio que preeminet accioni. Qui igitur intencionem cogitacionis sue ad imitacionem dominice passionis dirigit in superliminari domus agni sanguinem ponit. Vel certe domus nostre sunt ipsa corda in quibus quousque vivimus, habitamus et in superliminari domus agni sanguinem ponimus, quia crucem passionis illius in fronte portamus. De quo [p. 165] adhuc agno subditur: Et edent carnes nocte illa assas igni. In nocte quippe agnum comedimus, quia in sacramento modo corpus dominicum accipimus, quando adhuc adinvicem consciencias nostras non videmus. Que tamen carnes igne assande sunt, quia nimirum dissolvit carnes quas aqua coxerit, quas vero ignis sine aqua excoquit roborat. Carnes itaque agni nostri coxit ignis, quia eum ipsa vis passionis illius ad resurreccionem valenciorem reddidit atque incorrupcionem roboravit. Qui enim ex morte convaluerit, videlicet carnes illius ab igne duruerunt. Unde per Psalmistam eciam dicit: Exaruit velud testa virtus mea quid namque est testa ante ignem; nisi molle lutum? Sed ei ex igne agitur ut solidetur, virtus igitur humanitatis eius velut testa exaruit, quia ab igne passionis ad virtutem incorrupcionis crevit.

As to the second, namely the virtuous [manner of life]{g:conversacio} imitating Jesus Christ,[^17] it is clear from what has been said that this sacrament is constituted for the sake of this. Hence they celebrate emptily who pass over this [thing figured]{g:figuratum}.[^18] And this imitation in [living one's life]{g:conversari} is figured for us in Exod. 12:7, where it is commanded that in the [eating]{g:esus} of the paschal lamb these fourteen [things] be observed,[^19] as Gregory expounds, *On the Gospels*, Homily 12: "What the blood of the lamb is," he says, "you have learned not now by hearing but by drinking; which blood is put upon both posts when it is received or drawn not only with the mouth of the body but also with the mouth of the heart. For the blood of the lamb is put on both posts when the sacrament of his passion is taken with the mouth for redemption and is also thought on with an attentive mind for imitation. For he who so receives the blood of his Redeemer that he does not yet wish to imitate his passion has put the blood on one post, which is also to be put on the lintels of the houses. For what do we take the houses to be, [spiritually]{g:spiritualiter}, but our minds, in which we dwell by thought? The lintel of this house is the intention itself, which stands above action. He therefore who directs the intention of his thought to the imitation of the Lord's passion puts the blood of the lamb on the lintel of the house. Or certainly our houses are the hearts themselves, in which we dwell as long as we live, and we put the blood of the lamb on the lintel of the house because we bear the cross of his passion on the forehead. Of which [165] lamb it is further added: 'And they shall eat the flesh that night roasted at the fire.' For we eat the lamb at night, because in the sacrament we now receive [the Lord's body]{g:corpus-dominicum} while we do not yet see one another's consciences.[^20] Yet this flesh is to be roasted with fire, because the flesh which water has boiled it indeed dissolves, but what fire cooks without water it hardens.[^21] Fire therefore cooked the flesh of our lamb, because the very force of his passion rendered him stronger for the resurrection and hardened him to incorruption. For he who grew strong out of death: that is, his flesh was hardened by the fire.[^22] Hence through the Psalmist he also says: 'My strength is dried up like a potsherd.' For what is a potsherd before the fire but soft clay? Now it is acted on by fire so that it is made solid. The strength of his [humanity]{g:humanitas} therefore dried up like a potsherd, because by the fire of the passion it grew to the strength of incorruption."[^23]

## Notes

[^1]: **C/T.** The fourth stage of the bread (after the grain, the grinding and the kneading of 06-a): the baking. *In circulo*: the round form of the host, into which the saved are made one with the living bread; the construal is ours. For *furno* (161.15) ABDE read *summo* (perhaps *fumo*).
[^2]: **S.** 1 Cor. 10:17, DR "For we, being many, are one bread, one body: all that partake of one bread"; Wyclif writes *Nam omnes* and adds *et de uno calice*, which are translated from his Latin.
[^3]: **G/C.** *Passio* set against *accio* is the category "being-acted-on", not suffering: grace is first received (*pati et recipere*) before it acts. The subject of *intelligunt* is unnamed interpreters. ABD lack *de* (161.21).
[^4]: **T/C.** For *post graciam* ABCE read *per graciam* ("through the grace of predestination", 161.25). *Continuacio* here is the continuing of grace, kept to the row "continuity".
[^5]: **T/C.** For *vitam veram* ABD read *vitem* ("the true vine", cf. John 15:1); C corrects (161.27). The print construes and is translated. *Confuse informantem*: grace informs the soul as a whole, without distinction of parts.
[^6]: **S/T.** *Refrigescente caritate multorum*: Matt. 24:12, DR "the charity of many shall grow cold". D reads *egent* for *egeni* (161.31). *Tercio* numbers the third mark of unworthy eating.
[^7]: **S.** 1 Cor. 11:21 (Loserth's reference), DR "And one indeed is hungry and another is drunk". D reads *nisi nobis* (162.4).
[^8]: **C/T.** *Qui efficacius imitarentur*: those who, as superiors, ought most effectively to imitate Christ. AB read *essent superioris*; A has *essent* in the margin in another hand; in C *essent* is struck out (162.7). D reads *et faciendo* (162.8).
[^9]: **C/T.** *Proditores*: the row for Judas, "the traitor"; the hired priests betray as he did. E reads *magis care*; ABCD lack *-modi* of *huiusmodi* (162.16).
[^10]: **C.** *Utrumque membrum*: the temporal lords who hire and the priests hired. ABE read *nec periuri* (162.11), D *nocuum* (162.14).
[^11]: **S/T.** *Generacio adultera*: Matt. 12:39 (DR "An evil and adulterous generation seeketh a sign"). D reads *signaret* (162.23), *cum gaudio Christi moriantur* (162.25); ABCD *genera* (162.27).
[^12]: **S/T.** John 4:22, DR "You adore that which you know not"; Wyclif's *adorant quod ignorant* is translated. E lacks *capitulo* (162.31).
[^13]: **S/G.** *Gracia* with the genitive is "for the sake of" (cf. *gracia concomitancie*). *Ad iudicium … manducamus et bibimus*: 1 Cor. 11:29 (Loserth's reference). Here persons are ungrateful to the Lord, so *ingrati* is "ungrateful". C has *omnibus* in the margin (163.1); E *necesse est ut* (163.3); D *ergo* (163.4); C *nobis* in the margin (163.6).
[^14]: **S/T.** Gratian, *De cons.* D. 2 c. 50 (Loserth's foot, *cap. L*, read on the plate n234; the form is ours). Loserth notes that the passage is not Augustine's but "Ambrose" on 1 Cor. 11, and that the Decretum lacks *nobis obligaciores* (LEDGER K06b-1). E lacks *positum* (163.8–9); ABCE read *nobis* for *nos* (163.12).
[^15]: **T/G.** *Nimis* "exceedingly" (the row; excess is not the point here, as it is at *nimis attendimus* above, "too much"). C reads *figitur*, *in* erased (163.15); D *genera* (163.16).
[^16]: **T.** *Fimum* "dung" is the print; AB read *ad summum*, CDE *finium* (163.25); C has *quos* in the margin. CD read *consequitur* for *sequitur*, C *duas* in the margin (163.24); AB *illecebras*, C corrects (163.27); D *et amicorum* (163.21–22).
[^17]: **T/C.** The print reads *unitatoriam* (plate n235, no variant in the apparatus); we translate *imitatoriam* "imitating" (our emendation): at 158.23, where the same two heads are set out, the print has *imitatoriam*, and the next sentences speak of *imitacio*. *Unitatorius* is not otherwise attested. Loserth's side-note (164.4) follows his print. C has *vivere* in the margin in another hand (164.1).
[^18]: **T.** For *figuratum* D reads *signatum*, ABC *figurativum* (164.7).
[^19]: **C/S.** *Ista 14ᶜⁱᵐ*: "these fourteen [things]", as printed (*quatuordecim*); the precepts of Exod. 12 are not listed. The verse cited, Exod. 12:7, is the blood on the posts. CD read *observantur* (164.10).
[^20]: **S/T.** Gregory, *Homiliae in Evangelia* II, hom. 22 (Wyclif's "XII"), Loserth's reference *Opp.* I pp. 1533–1535 (LEDGER K06b-2). The scripture is Exod. 12:7–8 (DR "both the side posts, and on the upper door posts"; "And they shall eat the flesh that night roasted at the fire"). C has *quis* over an erasure (164.12) and *quando — hauritur* in the margin (164.13–14); D *et hauritur*; E *enim* (164.15); AB *quoque cum*, D *quasi*, E *quam intenta* (164.17). For *agno* (165.1) B reads *ligno*, ACDE *signo*, C corrects in the margin to *agno*. For *modo* (165.3) the manuscripts read *nostro*; E *assumimus accipimus*; the manuscripts *abinvicem* (165.4).
[^21]: **C.** The print places *aqua* in the relative clause, so the subject of *dissolvit* is left to be supplied (water); the sense is that boiling softens and roasting hardens. D reads *quod tamen*, C *in igne* (165.5).
[^22]: **T/C.** *Videlicet* is Loserth's correction of the manuscripts' *videt* (165.11); the manuscripts read *dirruerunt* for *duruerunt*, E *alias exaruerunt*. The relative *qui … convaluerit* has no main clause; it is translated as it stands. For *eum ipsa* E reads *ipsum*, ABC *cum ipsa* (165.8); for *incorrupcionem* E reads *ad incorrupcionem* (165.10).
[^23]: **S.** Ps. 21:16 (Loserth's reference; DR "My strength is dried up like a potsherd"); Gregory's *Exaruit velud* for the Vulgate's *Aruit tamquam* gives the same English. E reads *aruit* (165.12).
