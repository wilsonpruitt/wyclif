# *De potestate pape*, chapter 12, part 5: the wicked prelate worse than the devil; two heads, two clergies

## ¶1 · 330.13–333.5

> [p. 330] Similiter, Augustinus cap. IX De Gradibus Abusionum: Sciat, inquit, rex sicut in regno hominum primus constitutus est, sic et in penis, si iusticiam non fecerit, primatum habiturus est. Omnes namque quoscumque peccatores sub se habuerit in presenti, supra se modo implacabili in pena futura habebit. Et idem est evidencius de papa, ut docet Crysostomus Omelia LI Operis Imperfecti et patet distinccione XL: Si papa. Quomodo sacerdos, inquit, super omnem populum glorificatur, si bene fecerit, non solum propter suam iusticiam sed propter omnium quia ipse causa est iusticie universorum, ita et peccans super omnes punitur non solum propter sua peccata, sed propter omnium, quia ipse causa fit omnibus ad peccandum. Unde efficax est testimonium Crysostomi ubi supra: Qui, inquit, fingunt se esse christianos, cum sint Antichristi, videntur esse in ecclesia et agunt ypocritice contra ecclesiam, ymo Omelia XLIV [p. 331] dicit sanctus iste, quod homo malus est peior dyabolo, ymo malum pessimum, hoc est habet racionem supereminentis malicie super dyabolum, super feras et quamlibet aliam creaturam malam. Dyabolus, inquit, fugatur ex iusticia hominis et virtute, homo autem perversus crudelius talem persequitur; dyabolus non superat hominem nisi per hominem, Antichristus autem superat hominem per se ipsum. Ideo ubi non valet Meander dyaboli, ipse supplet temptacionem per hominem, a quo non prodest benedicere, qua arte fugatur dyabolus. Si igitur homo per censuras ecclesiasticas illudit populo veniens in nomine Christi, quis est pocior Antichristus? Tercio Lincolniensis in sermone ad papam declarat quod, sicut opus divinissimum viatoris est assimilare Deo animas peccato ab eo lapsas, sic opus summum sathane, Antichristi atque heretici est huic arti divine contrarie, quod faciunt presidentes huic curie. Si precipiunt aliquid mandatis Christi contrarium, si propter lucrum ordinant aliquid quod distrahat pastoris officium, aut si potestatem huius curie suspendunt, debilitant vel retardant; tunc, inquit, in ianuis est revelacio filii perdicionis. Si inquam papa ista perficiat et in eis constanter et finaliter perseverat, ipse est indubie Antichristus; ut, sicut sacerdotibus legis veteris successit Christus superaddendo interpretacionem laudabilem legi sue, sic episcopis cesareis succedet Antichristus, superaddendo obscuracionem culpabilem legi Christi. Et utrobique servabitur idem genus; [p. 332] quod fit cece et male preficiendo ineptum prepositum, suspendendo boni prelati officium, et omnino negligendo se movere, prohibens exercicium Christi ad expugnandum dyabolum. Et declarat quod curia est causa multorum talium; quod potest discerni per hoc, quod discordat a triplici descripcione pape superius recitata. Sed quantum ad primum punctum de capite causas ecclesiasticas terminante patet quod Christus est caput sufficientissimum, sicut probavit per trecentos annos et amplius, quando prosperata fuit ecclesia, et lex sua est ad hoc efficacissima, cum Deus ipsam edidit ad hunc finem; sed forte necesse est post dotacionem ecclesie esse legislatorem extraneum, ut puta Antichristum, sed hoc est ad desolacionem ecclesie, ideo idem foret asserere quod oportet esse tale caput ecclesie ad causas ecclesiasticas terminandum et dicere quod oportet Christum et legem suam deserere et Antichristum cum legibus suis atque complicibus amplexari; indignum namque fuit Christi apostolos in talibus taliter occupari. Ideo oportet considerare sectam cleri duplicem, scilicet clerum Christi et clerum Antichristi priori contrarium; clerus Christi quietatur in ipso capite ac suis legibus, clerus vero Antichristi vel totaliter vel preponderanter innititur legibus Antichristi et tamen palliatur esse clerus Christi atque ecclesie, ut populus simulacius seducatur. Et ista tam contraria oportet inniti duobus contrariis capitibus cum suis legibus. Et evidencia facti docet descripciones membrorum. [p. 333] Constat quidem quod clerus ecclesie claudicat in duas partes divisa, scilicet in partem Christi et partem cesaream. Ideo necesse est laycos supportatos per ipsos in opinione et vita tam dispares titubare.

Likewise Augustine, *On the Degrees of Abuses*, chapter 9:[^1] "Let the king know," he says, "that as he has been set first in the kingdom of men, so also in punishments, if he has not done justice, he will have the [primacy]{g:primatus}. For all the sinners whatsoever whom he has had under him in the present he will have above him, implacably, in the punishment to come."[^2] And the same holds more evidently of the [pope]{g:papa}, as Chrysostom teaches in Homily 51 of the *Opus imperfectum*, and as is clear in Distinction 40, *Si papa*:[^3] "As a priest," he says, "is glorified above all the people if he has done well, not only for his own justice but for that of all, because he is the cause of the justice of all, so also, sinning, he is punished above all, not only for his own sins but for those of all, because he becomes the cause of sinning to all." Hence the testimony of Chrysostom in the same place is effective: "Those," he says, "who feign themselves to be Christians, though they are Antichrists, seem to be in the [church]{g:ecclesia} and act hypocritically against the church"; indeed, in Homily 44 [331] this saint says that an evil man is "worse than the devil, indeed the worst evil", that is, he has the character of a malice surpassing the devil, the beasts and any other evil creature. "The devil," he says, "is put to flight by a man's justice and [virtue]{g:virtus}, but a perverse man persecutes such a one more cruelly; the devil does not overcome a man except through a man, but Antichrist overcomes a man by himself." Therefore, where the devil's meander does not avail, he supplies the temptation through a man, against whom it profits nothing to bless oneself, the art by which the devil is put to flight.[^4] If, then, a man mocks the people by ecclesiastical [censures]{g:censura}, coming in the name of Christ, who is a greater Antichrist?[^5] Thirdly, [Lincoln]{g:lincolniensis}, in his sermon to the pope, declares that, as the most divine work of a [wayfarer]{g:viator} is to make like to God souls fallen from him by sin, so the highest work of Satan, of Antichrist and of a heretic runs contrary to this divine art, and this those who preside over this curia do.[^6] If they command anything contrary to the commandments of Christ, if for gain they ordain anything that draws away the office of the shepherd, or if they [suspend]{g:suspendere}, weaken or retard the [power]{g:potestas} of this curia, then, he says, "the revelation of the son of perdition is at the doors".[^7] If, I say, the pope accomplishes these things and perseveres in them constantly and finally, he is without doubt Antichrist; so that, as Christ succeeded the priests of the old law, adding to his law a praiseworthy interpretation, so Antichrist will succeed the caesarean bishops, adding to the law of Christ a culpable obscuring.[^8] And on both sides the same kind will be kept; [332] which is done blindly and wickedly by setting over [the people] an unfit [superior]{g:prepositi}, by suspending the office of a good [prelate]{g:prelatus}, and by wholly neglecting to stir oneself, forbidding the exercise of Christ for overcoming the devil.[^9] And he declares that the curia is the cause of many such things; which can be discerned by this, that it disagrees with the threefold [definition]{g:descripcio} of the pope recited above. But as to the first point, concerning a [head]{g:caput} that terminates ecclesiastical causes, it is clear that Christ is a most sufficient head, as he proved for three hundred years and more, when the church prospered; and his law is most effective for this, since God issued it to this end. But perhaps after the [endowment]{g:dotacio} of the church there must be a foreign lawgiver, say Antichrist; now this is to the desolation of the church; therefore it would be the same to assert that there must be such a head of the church for terminating ecclesiastical causes, and to say that one must desert Christ and his law and embrace Antichrist with his laws and accomplices; for it was unworthy that Christ's apostles should be occupied in such things in such a way. Therefore one must consider a twofold [sect]{g:secta} of the [clergy]{g:clerus}, namely the clergy of Christ and the clergy of Antichrist, contrary to the former:[^10] the clergy of Christ rests in that head and his laws, but the clergy of Antichrist leans either wholly or preponderantly on the laws of Antichrist, and yet is [cloaked]{g:palliare} as being the clergy of Christ and of the church, so that the people may be the more deceitfully led astray. And these, being so contrary, must lean on two contrary heads with their laws. And the evidence of the fact teaches the definitions of the [members]{g:membrum}. [333] It is evident, indeed, that the clergy of the church limps, divided into two parts, namely into the part of Christ and the caesarean part. Therefore the laity, supported by men so unlike in opinion and life, must needs stagger.[^11]

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** *De duodecim abusivis saeculi*, the ninth abuse (*rex iniquus*), transmitted under the names of Augustine and Cyprian; Loserth: Augustine, *Opera* (Maurist) VI, Appendix, p. 215 (LEDGER P12e-1). The modern ascription to an Irish author of the seventh century is to be checked (LEDGER P12e-2).
[^2]: **T.** *Modo implacabili*: so Loserth, from the source; the manuscripts read *modo plagabili* (330.18), which does not construe as well. We translate the print.
[^3]: **S.** Pseudo-Chrysostom, *Opus imperfectum in Matthaeum*, hom. 51; Loserth: Chrysostom, *Opera* VI, pp. 925–928 (LEDGER P12e-3). Decretum D.40 c.6 *Si papa* (Loserth "Cap. VI"; the modern form is ours) (LEDGER P12e-4). The second extract ("Those who feign …") Loserth places at *Op. imp.* p. 928 (LEDGER P12e-5); "Homily 44" (331.1) he does not identify, to be checked (LEDGER P12e-6).
[^4]: **C.** *Meander*: the winding of the river Maeander, a common figure for tortuous wiles. *Benedicere*: to bless oneself (with the sign of the cross), the art that puts the devil to flight, but which avails nothing against a man.
[^5]: **S.** "Coming in the name of Christ" alludes to Matthew 24:5 ("many will come in my name"). *Pocior* is the comparative of *potissimus* (329.28).
[^6]: **S.** Robert Grosseteste. Loserth, hesitantly: "seems to be" his letter to Innocent IV, *Epistolae*, no. 128, p. 432 (LEDGER P12e-7). Wyclif's "sermon to the pope" may rather be the memorandum read before Innocent IV at Lyons in 1250; to be checked (LEDGER P12e-8). **C.** *Contrarie* is taken as the adverb ("runs contrary").
[^7]: **S.** 2 Thessalonians 2:3 ("the son of perdition") and Matthew 24:33 ("at the doors"), joined. **C.** *Potestatem huius curie*: the curia's own (pastoral) power, which its presidents hamper; Grosseteste's context suggests *cure*, the pastoral charge, a reading the apparatus does not give.
[^8]: **C.** *Legi sue*: "his law", the old law as Christ's own; *sue* could refer to the priests ("their law").
[^9]: **C.** *Prohibens*: a nominative participle left without a subject; it describes the one who sets over and suspends.
[^10]: **R.** Loserth: Hus takes over the whole passage on the twofold clergy (*Opera*, p. 226) (LEDGER P12e-9). Both manuscripts note in the margin *Clerus duplex Christi et Antichristi*.
[^11]: **S.** "Limps … into two parts" echoes 1 Kings 18:21 (DR 3 Kings 18:21), "How long do you halt between two sides?" **C.** *Tam dispares* is taken with *ipsos*, the two parts of the clergy; it could go with *laycos*, "the laity, so unlike in opinion and life".
