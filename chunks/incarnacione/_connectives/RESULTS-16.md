## Doubtful, left as they are

- **11-a, p. 188**: *Sed ulterius pro materia argumenti dicitur*, rendered "But further". Either (b), the reply turning against the argument, or (a), the next step of the answer ("Now further"). No *ergo* follows to settle it; "But" kept.
- **11-b, p. 190**: *Illa autem non fit corpus Christi sed fit signum*, rendered "And it does not become". Either (c), extending the account of the remaining essence, or (b), set against "is the bread" ("But it does not become"). "And" is defensible.
- **11-c, p. 197** (×2): *Paulus autem per idem tempus in infinitum intendetur* and *Paulus autem per idem tempus assumptus*, both rendered "and Paul". Peter and Paul are set in contrast, so (b) "but Paul" is possible; but the *autem* only adds the second limb of a parallel case, (c). Kept.
- **11-d, p. 201**: *Infinitatem autem inconveniencium … videtur michi stultum deducere*, rendered "But to deduce". Either (b), breaking off the list against its continuation, or (c), the closing remark of the paragraph. "But" kept.
- **12-a, p. 202** (unbolded in the extract): *Veritas enim obiective movet …: sed ad exprimendum falsitatem deficit obiectum huiusmodi in natura. Ideo …*, rendered "now". The truth/falsehood contrast would allow (b) "but"; the *Ideo* that follows makes it the minor premiss, (a). "Now" kept per the card's look-ahead rule.
