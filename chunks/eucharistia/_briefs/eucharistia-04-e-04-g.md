## The volume

*Tractatus de eucharistia* and *Tractatus de penitencia sive de confessione*, ed. Johann Loserth (Wyclif
Society, 1892), from the plates of archive.org `latinworks13wycl` (**leaf = page + 71**; leaf images in
`text/eucharistia/raw/leaves/`). Three texts:

Chunk ids: `prol-a … prol-c` the compendium, `01-a … 09-n` the nine chapters, `pen01 … pen06` *De
penitencia*, `quest` the *Questio*.

- **pp. 1–10, the compendium** (*Sentencia tractatus de Eukaristia in compendio*, under "Prologus"): a
  medieval summary of the nine chapters that came down with the work. Translate it like the text.
- **pp. 11–326, the *Tractatus de eucharistia* (maior)**, nine long chapters. Written in Wyclif's last
  years, before 1382–83 (Loserth, Introd. §4: it shares passages word for word with *Sermones* III, and
  it avoids the words *substancia* and *materialis* of the bread, which Wyclif says in *Sermo* III.54 he
  has stopped using). Wyclif's case against the "modern" doctrine: that the consecrated host is in its
  nature bread and wine and sacramentally, figuratively, the body and blood of Christ; that an accident
  without a subject is impossible; that the decree *Ego Berengarius* (*De cons.* D. 2 c. 42) is the
  faith of the ancient Roman church; against the friars, Thomas, Scotus and "the sect of the signs".
  A **treatise**: Wyclif speaks in the first person, states conclusions, raises objections and answers
  them. The tractatus minor (the *Confessio*) is NOT in this volume (Shirley, *Fasc. Ziz.*).
- **pp. 327–348, *De eucharistia et penitencia sive de confessione*** in six chapters (only ch. 1 is on
  the eucharist; the rest is penance and auricular confession), then the short ***Questio ad fratres de
  sacramento altaris*** (pp. 347–348, its first part only: Loserth notes that the rest of the
  manuscript's text is not on the sacrament, and is Hus's).

**Sigla. ⚠ They differ between the two works.** *De eucharistia* (Introd. §5): **A** Vienna 3927;
**B** Vienna 3932; **C** Vienna 1387 ("the best of the three" Vienna MSS, correcting A and B); **D** Prague
Univ. IV D 22 (1404–05, Andreas of Kouřim); **E** Prague Univ. XI E 3 (1460; Loserth: "the best and most
reliable text of all the five"). Loserth's lettering "does not in any way refer to their respective
value". The Vienna MSS are one group, the Prague MSS another. *De penitencia* (Introd. §6): **A** Vienna
3927; **B** Bautzen, Gersdorf Library M. St. 80.7; **C** Vienna 1337; **D** Prague Univ. III G 11; **E**
Vienna 3980; **F** Vienna 4527. Cite variants with the sigla of the work you are in. Marginal notes in the
manuscripts that Loserth prints ("F in marg.", "DB in marg.") are reception: an **R** note where they
show how a reader took the text.

**The page foot** (in your brief as "apparatus", by page) has two blocks, both keyed to the **line**:
the variants (`19. C: ecclesie in marg. alia manu.`) and Loserth's source references. The OCR of it is
rough: read it for sense, not letter by letter, and read the plate foot where a variant matters.

   *hostia* host · *panis / vinum* bread / wine · *calix* chalice · *corpus Christi / Domini* kept apart ·

   *secta signorum* · *novella / antiqua ecclesia* · *predicacio ydemptica / tropica* identical / tropical
   predication. **The mode adverbs, one word each, never merged, each tagged at first occurrence:**
   *sacramentaliter* sacramentally · *realiter* really (never "actually") · *vere* truly (never "really") ·
   *spiritualiter* spiritually · *virtualiter* virtually · *figurative* figuratively · *tropice* tropically ·
   *ydemptice* identically · *invisibiliter* invisibly · *sensibiliter* perceptibly · *corporaliter* bodily ·
   *substancialiter* substantially · *dimensionaliter* dimensionally. A term still missing a row (*ydemptificacio*,
   *conversio*, *fractio*, *confessio auricularis*, *absolucio*, *claves* …): render it sensibly, untagged, and
   propose it. *Ego Berengarius* stays as the decree's title (Wyclif's spelling *Beringarius* in the Latin;

   row: the compendium's *de rebus huius sacramenti* (1.3) is the three-membered distinction the chapter sets
   out, so keep its *res* consistent with that row.
2. **Connectives carry the argument.** ⛔ Never omit one, never turn one into filler.

   | Latin | English | Never |
   |---|---|---|
   | enim, nam | for | "now", "indeed" |
   | ergo, igitur, ideo | therefore, so, then | "now", "and", "indeed" |
   | sed / autem, marking the **minor premiss or next step** | now | (omitted) |
   | sed / autem, adversative | but | (omitted) |
   | autem, continuative | and | (omitted), and **not "now"** |
   | cum (concessive / causal) | although / since | "whenever" |
   | tamen | yet, nevertheless | "therefore" |
   | item | again, also | "now" |
   | vero (continuative) | and; but (adversative) | "now", "truly" |
   | verumtamen | nevertheless | "but truly" |
   | unde | hence, so | "whence" (archaic), "where" |
   | per idem | by the same token | "in the same way" |
   | ymmo | indeed, rather (correcting upward) | (omitted) |
   | patet; constat | it is clear; it is evident | |

   "Now" is reserved for the minor premiss, or the step that brings a new premiss to bear. An *autem*
   that only extends the same step is "and". ⚠ The commonest error in *De ecclesia* (close-out, ~50 of
   69 connective fixes): a *sed* that brings the minor premiss of a syllogism closed by *ergo / ideo /
   igitur* rendered "but". Before writing "but" for a *sed*, look ahead for the *ergo*: if one follows,
   it is "now".
3. **Ordinary words with a medieval sense:** GLOSSARY §D; look up any word whose classical and
   Wyclifite senses may differ, and propose additions.
4. **Syntax:** construe before rendering (main verb, subject, what every relative and genitive hangs
   on). Keep quantifier order where it is meaning. Keep Wyclif's long periods where English tolerates
   them. **A doubtful construal is never chosen silently:** translate the likelier reading and write a
   **C** note giving the alternative.
   ⚠ **Predication is the matter here, as in *De incarnacione*.** *Hoc est corpus meum*, *panis est
   corpus Christi*, *hoc sacramentum est panis*: Wyclif argues about what the pronoun demonstrates and
   in what mode the predicate holds. Keep subject and predicate in his order, keep *est* as "is" (never
   "exists", "becomes" or "represents" unless the Latin says so), and keep the mode adverb where he puts
   it (*est sacramentaliter corpus Christi* "is sacramentally the body of Christ"). ⛔ Never soften "is"
   into "signifies" or harden "figuratively" into "really": the difference is the treatise's matter.
   Demonstrative pronouns (*hoc*, *istud*) under discussion: render "this", and keep their gender
   visible in a C note where the argument turns on it (*hoc* neuter against *hic*, 348).
   **Propositions in indirect speech** whose sense is under discussion: render as the quoted proposition
   ("who say 'this is an accident without a subject'"), not "who say that …", which asserts it.

## Glossary (the 481 rows these chunks use; the full file is GLOSSARY.md)

# GLOSSARY — FROZEN 2026-09-28 (Wilson's pilot review)

*One Latin term, one English rendering, every time, across the whole corpus. A reader must be able
to recognise the same word coming back, because Wyclif's arguments turn on it. A rendering changes
only by a dated decision in CONVENTIONS' decision log, never silently in a chunk. Entries may be
*added* by recorded decision as new terms appear. **Tag keys:** a chunk tags a term as
`[English]{g:latin-form}` (CONVENTIONS §11), and the key must match a Latin form in one of the rows
below. These rows are also the source of the site's hover glossary. Where a term has two senses,
the entry says so and each chunk's note picks one. A term marked **CONTEXTUAL** holds together
senses English splits: its English is chosen by context, under the dikaiosune rule (CONVENTIONS §6).*

**Sources of the decisions.** "Pilot" = settled in `pilots/` this session. "T. p. N" = Mark
Thakkar, "Duces caecorum," *Vivarium* 58 (2020), page N (`reference/`), which documents the
errors of the two most recent translations. His corrections are adopted here unless an entry says
otherwise.

**Orthography.** Headwords are given in the Society editions' medieval spelling (*eciam*,
*racio*, *quiditas*, *ymmo*), because that is what the translator will meet on the page.

# *De eucharistia*, chapter 4, part 5: [TITLE]

## ¶1 · 98.15–99.22

> [p. 98] Sed tercio obicitur per hoc quod nedum laycus potest conficere, sed sacramentum posset confici sola mente, vel non est confeccio et per consequens eque in fermento ut azimis, et sic eque in aliis liquoribus ut in vino; ymmo cum omne vinum nostrum sit mixtum aqua, non oportuit inmiscere aquam sed simul benedicere aquam et vinum. Et videtur ulterius quod corpus Christi movetur de celo ad loca volita secundum arbitrium sacerdotis. Quantum ad istud, patet quod aliud est querere de posse quod se extendit ad potenciam Dei absolutam et aliud est secundum testimonium scripture querere de licere. Nullus (inquam) fidelis dubitat quin Deus posset dare layco potenciam conficiendi, sicut laycus cum possit esse sacerdos (ut dicunt loyci) possit conficere. Ymmo videtur iuxta testimonium Augustini, Crisostomi et aliorum sanctorum quod omnis predestinatus laycus est sacerdos, et multo magis devotus laycus conficiens, [p. 99] cum daret ecclesie sacrum ministerium, haberet racionem sacerdotis. Verumptamen sicut ecclesia racionabiliter variavit in verbis confeccionis ab omnibus hiis quatuor evangelistis servando sentenciam, quod patet ex fide scripture debere fieri, sic racionabiliter ordinavit quod soli sacerdotes propter religiositatem et dignitatem in moribus hoc sacramentum conficerent. Levis autem fundacio valet ad hoc quod Christus dedit singulariter illud officium suis apostolis, nec occurrit mihi efficax testimonium quod apostoli gerebant in hoc typum universalis ecclesie sed singulariter sacerdotum; nec oportet propter necessitatem ministerium accidencium huius hostie plus extendi, cum stat fidelem laycum laborando qui nunquam vidit hostiam accipere presbitero efficacius corpus Christi, cum secundum Augustinum super Johannem Omelia XXV accipere corpus Christi sit pie in ipsum credere: Ut quid, inquit, paras dentes et ventrem? crede et manducasti. Oportet enim quod sit spiritualis manducacio, ut dictum est, que consistit in pia consideracione et grata, quomodo Christus passus est pro humano genere.

[ENGLISH]

## ¶2 · 99.23–100.10

> [p. 99] Sed graviter ferunt plurimi de nimis levi efficacia verborum sacramenti, cum illis nihil conficitur vel mutatur, quod est contra decreta sanctorum et specialiter beati Ambrosii in libro suo De Officiis et ponitur in De Consecracione, distinccione II, Forte dicis, et in libro suo De Sacramentis, ut consequenter ponitur eadem distinccione, capitulo Panis est, ubi dicit quod de pane fit caro Christi; quod probat per [p. 100] hoc quod verbum Christi suffecit mutare aliquid a puro non esse ad existenciam naturalem, ut patet de creacione Genes. I°; ergo per locum a maiori illud verbum potest facere, ut quod erat panis ante consecracionem sit corpus Christi per consecracionem; et idem confirmat doctor per exempla scripture, ut patet eadem distinccione, capitulo sequente Revera, ubi probat conversionem illam esse possibilem per locum a maiori per illa que fiebant a prophetis in veteri testamento.

[ENGLISH]

## ¶3 · 100.11–101.7

> [p. 100] Sed quantum ad omnia ista, patet quod iste sanctus non intendit dicere quod panis iste fiet ydemptice corpus Christi, sed quod fiet eius figura efficax modo dicto; et ad hoc vadunt tam argumenta quam sentencia. Et sic conversio illa non destruit naturam panis nec mutat naturam corporis, inducendo in materiam aliam quidditatem, sed facit presenciam corporis Christi et tollit principalitatem panis, ut in corpore Christi colligatur tota intencio adorantis; et istam conversionem probat sanctus per locum a maiori possibilem, et de ista mirabili et miraculosa conversione debet presbiter contentari. Et patet quod falsum assumitur, cum panis mutetur insensibiliter, ut figuret efficaciter corpus Christi; quod sancti intelligunt per hoc quod panis fit corpus Christi et quod post consecracionem panis est corpus Christi. Et qui ignorat istam predicacionem tropicam vel figurativam nedum male intelliget sanctos doctores et decreta ecclesie sed fidem scripture. Nam Christus dicit Joh. XV°, 5: Ego sum vitis vera, et Joh. XI°, 14, [p. 101] ac XIV°, 6: Ego sum pastor bonus. Ego sum via, etc., sicut eciam Luc. XXI°, 33 dicit quod celum et terra transibunt, sed verba sua non transibunt; ubi non dubium intelligit per verba que sunt signa inconstantissima signatam sentenciam; et isto tropo sepe utitur, sicut post eum utuntur sancti doctores et leges ecclesie.

[ENGLISH]

## ¶4 · 101.8–101.19

> [p. 101] In isto autem tropo sunt gradus multiplices, quorum mirabilis et plus miraculosus est quod loco panis succedit corpus Christi sacramentaliter, ut probat sanctus Ambrosius Deo esse possibile et credibile, non quod panis destruitur, sed quod significat corpus Domini ibi presens in sacramento, cui oportet panis consideracione dimissa plene attendere. Unde hec est porta lapsus multorum qui ignorantes voces proprias baptizant terminos: aliqui ponentes transsubstanciacionem ad sensum expositum, alii ponentes ydemptificacionem, et alii ponentes inpanacionem, ut patet inferius.

[ENGLISH]

## ¶5 · 101.20–102.14

> [p. 101] Sed redeundo conceditur quod Deus ex omnipotencia sua posset concedere fideli potenciam conficiendi eukaristiam pia mente, subducta voce. Sed de hoc non queritur, ymmo quid Deus ordinavit in lege scripture sue debere fieri; et patet quod ipse docendo verba confeccionis predicta docuit ut nos faciamus similiter. Et sic videtur domino Armachano probabile libro IX°, capitulo V°, De Questionibus Armenorum, quod Christus non illis verbis sed ante illorum prolacionem confecit in sacramento presenciam sui corporis et sanguinis. Sic enim ultimate instituit [p. 102] originaliter alia sacramenta, et ita levi evidencia sed probabili credimus quod sacerdos ex virtute Dei conficit illis verbis; et sic licet sacerdos posset habere virtutem conficiendi panem et non vinum, panem triticeum et non ordeaceum vel alterius speciei et econtra et sic de aliis variacionibus tam in pane quam in vino, ymmo verba quantumcunque varia possent esse pars huius sacramenti et sacramentum posset esse nude sacra hostia sine verbis, tamen formam quam observamus credimus a Domino institutam, sicut credimus profunditatem ordinacionis divine habere racionem, propter quam oportuit eum taliter ordinare, sed vias istas nobis investigabiles oportet transcurrere.

[ENGLISH]

# *De eucharistia*, chapter 4, part 6: [TITLE]

## ¶1 · 102.29–104.12

> [p. 102] Quoad ritum magis pertinenter conficimus et sic pertinenter inmiscemus aquam, cum sanguis et aqua figurati per hoc sacramentum fluxerunt de latere [p. 103] crucifixi Joh. XX°, 20. Et ideo (ut dicit Alexander papa) sic pertinenter quoad utrumque signatum recolitur memoria passionis. Nec oportet nos sollicitari de necessitate fidei ad distincte discernendum differenciam inter vinum et acetum, quia nullus nostrum in confinio conversionis sufficit illud facere; ymmo in liquore reputato et vere existente vinum secundum maiorem partem sui quecunque pars notabilis est acetum; nam aqua, terra etc. tam elementa quam mixta, licet sepe nobis insensibilia, sunt partes vini, et ideo mussitat decretalis libro III De Celebracione missarum capitulo Cum Marthe: Si aqua in vino confecto convertitur in partem Christi vel cedit in nihilum et recitando multas opiniones stolidas non diffinit. Prima sentencia est quod licet oportet aquam misceri vino in calice, tamen non debet fieri verbis sacramenti confeccio, antequam aqua assimiletur vino et sic aqua convertitur in aquam que est in Christo et vinum in sanguinem ut in hoc sacramento plene sit veritas et figura. Secunda est quod sit assimilacio aque in vinum et post totum transsubstanciatur in sanguinem, sic quod quelibet pars quantitativa consecrandi huiusmodi sit vinum, cum nulla pars quantitativa mixti sit elementum, licet phisici aquam a vino asserunt posse per artificium separari. Sed tercia est innaturalis sentencia cui videtur decretalis annuere: Potest, inquit, dici, quod aqua non transit in sanguinem sed remanet prioris vini accidentibus circumfusa ita quod vini saporem assumit; quod inde cognosci potest quod si post calicis consecracionem aliud vinum [p. 104] inmittatur in calicem, illud non transit in sanguinem, nec sanguini commiscetur, sed accidentibus prioris vini commixtum corpori quod sub eis latet undique circumfunditur non madidans circumfusum, ipsa tamen accidencia vinum appositum videntur afficere, quia si aqua fuerit apposita, vini saporem assumit. Contingit igitur accidencia mutare subiectum, sicut contingit subiectum accidencia permutare: cedit quippe natura miraculo et virtus supra consuetudinem operatur. Et finaliter videtur decretali illam sentenciam esse magis probabilem que asserit tam aquam quam vinum in Christi sanguinem transmutari.

[ENGLISH]

## ¶2 · 104.13–104.28

> [p. 104] Quantum ad istam sentenciam patet philosophis et theologis quod ista est sentencia indigesta, nam quoad primam sentenciam patet philosophis quod sacerdos nescit quando aqua assimilatur vino, ergo sepe contingit quod aqua maneat seorsum per se quousque sacerdos insorbeat sacramentum, sicut sensualiter patet in hieme ubi aqua per se congelatur, et vinum ex calore intrinseco manet seorsum fluxibile, sicut primo, nec est color quod aqua solum vertatur in aquam lateris Christi et vinum in sanguinem, quia per idem quodlibet elementum in vino converteretur appropriate in elementum in Christo, cum tamen totus Christus sit in hoc sacramento ad quemlibet eius punctum, quia aliter non foret conversio aque in aquam sed oportet aquam infusam aque in Christi latere coequari vel ydemptificari aque alii.

[ENGLISH]

## ¶3 · 104.29–105.16

> [p. 104] Quoad secundam sentenciam patet ut supra, quod non oportet aquam ante consecracionem assimilari vino nec facta assimilacione oportet quod sit ad omnem [p. 105] punctum commixtio, ut noverunt philosophi qui considerant quomodo elementa sunt in mixtis. Si enim post mixtionem sit vinum ad omnem punctum sine aliquo elemento, videtur pro tunc illud consecrandum esse alienum a figurato corporis Christi et sanguinis, ymmo superinfusio aque in vinum olim facta per laycum foret sufficiens. Non enim est sacramentum benediccionis aque a sacerdote qui debet celebrare tam necessarium, aquam tamen benedicimus et non vinum, vel quia primordialis materia ante benediccionem Dei vocata est aqua, ut patet Genes. primo, vel quia vinum expressius et proprius figurat illum sacrum sanguinem quem actuat anima Christi et non sic aqua, ideo eget benediccione; vel tercio quia baptismus figuratus per aquam est in re non sic sacra ut generis humani redempcio.

[ENGLISH]

[ENGLISH]

## ¶5 · 105.24–106.20

> [p. 105] Ex quo videtur quod illa aqua sit sacramentum, quia forma eius est sacramentum, et sic coleret homo illam aquam ut sanguinem, ydolatrando (ut adversarii probant) quod panis non remanet ob hanc causam; ymmo videtur, cum accidens potest esse ad omnem punctum corporis cum hoc quod non informet, sine evidencia ponitur accidens taliter informare, ymmo inconsequenter dicitur quod illud sacramentum est ponderositas et non ponderosum, cum quantitas et qualitas alterius speciei possunt recipere predicaciones [p. 106] huiusmodi in concreto. Nec videtur ex evidenciis quod accidencia prioris vini circumfunduntur vino post consecracionem iniecto, quia eque vinum infusum utribus vel alii vasi recipit saporem ab eis quod non potest fingi tingi a sapore per se latente in utribus, sed corpora subtilia latencia consumat, odorem et saporem, ex quorum commixtione liquor infusus intingitur; et forte sic intelligunt hii accidencia que per se existunt in hostia esse corpora subtilia modo quo pictores vocant corpora quibus liniendo pingunt colores pictorum; nam (ut fatentur) sacramentum calicis potest misceri cum vino, et sic totum aggregatum foret sacramentum sine ydolatria, et tam quantitas quam qualitas rarefacta composita ex suis partibus intensivis, posito quod unum aliud sacramentum, tercium et quartum commisceantur in calice que constituant unum sacramentum, et sic nec quantitas nec qualitas foret sacramentum eucharistie, sed vel vinum vel ex vino et sanctitate aut alio aggregatum. Veritas itaque irridet tales vanas ficticias.

[ENGLISH]
