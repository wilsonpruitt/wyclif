# *De ecclesia*, chapter 6, part 3: the first difficulty answered: Christ's three weddings, and the temple as figure of the church

## ¶1 · 123.28–125.32

> [p. 123] Ad illud autem videtur dicendum iuxta sepe dicta quod Christus in primo instanti mundi fuit sponsus ecclesie predestinacione, in confirmacione autem angelorum dedit dotem parti sponse sue et sic in confirmacione [p. 124] Abel iusti et aliorum sanctorum usque ad incarnacionem, manente continue eadem desponsacione, cum Cesar semper Augustus continue auget dotem. In incarnacione vero fecit secundas nupcias, creando quandam reginam partem tocius ecclesie que quadam proprietate dicitur ecclesia christiana. Tunc enim dux et legifer noster familiarius alloquens sponsam suam (ut dicit apostolus Hebr. 1°) per assumpcionem humanitatis inducit arma nostra et ut gigas superavit hostes ecclesie, redimens partem sponse incarceratam et docens aliam quomodo sequeretur eius vestigia. Unde tota doctrina christiana stat in ista oracione ecclesie qua rogamus sponsum per adventum eius in carnem, ut doceat nos terrena despicere et amare celestia. Despicere, id est, in affeccione postponere. Ille enim despicit temporalia qui a summa sede anime ipsa abicit et facit ipsa intencioni celestium abiectissime ministrare. Et bene foret sibi qui perfecte didicerit istam regulam religionis, tunc enim foret in recta via sequendi sponsum et assumptam iuvenculam in regnum celorum. Tercio vero in ultimo adventu faciet agnus nupcias consummatas, coronando et confirmando reginam integram que est una columba et universalis ecclesia, non pars alterius ecclesie (ut patet Apoc. XXI°). Et super ista consideracione dependet magna pars sensus scripture et servicii ecclesie. Nam ex fide erit una totalis ecclesia glorificata post diem iudicii, et illa secundum magnam partem est modo sponsa in celo, in purgatorio et in mundo. Ideo illa est continue una universalis et sancta mater ecclesia, et illa est quam [p. 125] debent leges intelligere et omnis christianus reverenter intendere; illa, inquam, figurata est in Salomone et templo, ut canit ecclesia. Frustra namque ordinaretur vel legeretur quod Salomon haberet tot reginas, tot concubinas et tot adolescentulas, nisi ut figurando Christum significet nobis suum coniugium cum spiritibus celestibus, tam angelis quam animabus, quam cum adolescentibus viantibus; ex quibus omnibus componitur una columba que est universalis ecclesia, eadem que coronabitur in patria, quia aliter foret continue alia et alia universalis ecclesia. Frustra eciam ordinaretur et legeretur construccio templi Salomonis (de quo III° Reg. VI° cap.) ad figurandum Christum et ecclesiam, que est templum suum (ut dicit apostolus Iª Cor. III° 17). Sicut enim fuerunt tres partes templi: prima in quam totum vulgus et alienigene intraverunt, quam sacerdotes in tempore Christi negociacionibus occuparunt (ut patet Matthei XXI°, 12). Et illa signat militantem ecclesiam in qua sunt eciam alienigene ut presciti. Secunda vero fuit corpus ecclesie populo Israhelitico deputata, signans perfecciores pure Israhelitas a rugis in purgatorio depurandos. Tercia vero fuit sancta sanctorum in quam soli sacerdotes intrarunt solempniter semel in anno. Et illa signat triumphantes, ut exponit apostolus Hebr. IX°, 11: Christus assistens pontifex etc. Sic, inquam, tota fabrica templi signat universalem ecclesiam iuxta fidem. Unde istum articulum fidei tenentur episcopi docere inferiores sacerdotes et populum, quia aliter ignorarent usum servicii ecclesie ad cuius noticiam obligantur, et specialiter illum articulum fidei ecclesiam sanctam catholicam quem omnis catholicus debet scire.

Now to that, it seems, the answer must be, according to what has often been said, that Christ at the
first instant of the world was the [bridegroom]{g:sponsus} of the [church]{g:ecclesia} by
predestination; and at the confirmation of the angels he gave a dowry to part of his
[bride]{g:sponsa}, and so at the confirmation of [124] Abel the just and of the other saints up to the
incarnation, the same espousal remaining continuously, since Caesar ever Augustus continually
increases the dowry.[^1] And at the incarnation he held a second wedding, creating a certain part of
the whole church a queen, which by a certain propriety is called the christian church.[^2] For then
our leader and lawgiver, speaking more familiarly to his bride (as the apostle says, Hebrews 1),
through the assumption of humanity puts on our armour, and as a giant overcame the enemies of the
church, redeeming the imprisoned part of the bride and teaching the other how it should follow his
footsteps.[^3] Hence the whole christian [doctrine]{g:doctrina} stands in this prayer of the church,
in which we ask the bridegroom, by his coming into the flesh, to teach us to despise earthly things
and to love heavenly. To despise, that is, to put after in affection. For he despises [temporal
goods]{g:temporalia} who casts them away from the highest seat of the soul and makes them serve, most
abjectly, the intention of heavenly things.[^4] And it would be well with him who had perfectly
learned this rule of religion; for then he would be on the right way of following the bridegroom and
the [young girl]{g:iuvencula} he has taken to himself into the [kingdom]{g:regnum} of heaven.[^5] And
thirdly, at the last coming the Lamb will make the nuptials consummate, crowning and confirming the
queen entire, which is one dove and the universal church, not part of another church (as is clear,
Apocalypse 21).[^6] And on this consideration depends a great part of the sense of scripture and of
the church's service. For [as faith teaches]{g:ex fide} there will be one total church glorified
after the day of judgment, and that [church] is now, in great part, the bride in heaven, in purgatory
and in the world. Therefore she is continuously one universal and holy mother church, and she it is
whom [125] the laws ought to understand and every christian reverently to attend to; she, I say, was
figured in Solomon and the temple, as the church sings.[^7] For in vain would it be ordained or read
that Solomon had so many queens, so many concubines and so many [young maidens]{g:adolescentula},
unless it were that, by figuring Christ, he signifies to us his marriage with the heavenly spirits,
angels as well as souls, as well as with the young ones who are [on the road]{g:viantes}; of all of
whom is composed one dove, which is the universal church, the same that will be crowned in the
[homeland]{g:patria}; for otherwise there would continually be one universal church after
another.[^8] In vain, too, would the building of Solomon's temple (of which 3 Kings, chapter 6) be
ordained and read, [were it not] to figure Christ and the church, which is his temple (as the apostle
says, 1 Corinthians 3:17).[^9] For just as there were three parts of the temple: the first, into
which the whole common people and the foreigners entered, which the priests in Christ's time occupied
with their trafficking (as is clear, Matthew 21:12). And that signifies the [church
militant]{g:ecclesia militans}, in which there are foreigners too, as the [foreknown]{g:prescitus}.[^10]
And the second was the body of the church, assigned to the people of Israel, signifying the more
perfect, purely Israelites, who are to be cleansed of their wrinkles in purgatory. And the third was
the holy of holies, into which the priests alone entered solemnly once a year. And that signifies the
triumphant, as the apostle expounds, Hebrews 9:11: "Christ, being come an high priest" etc.[^11] So, I
say, the whole fabric of the temple signifies the universal church, according to faith. Hence the
[bishops]{g:episcopus} are bound to teach this [article of faith]{g:articulus fidei} to the lower
[priests]{g:sacerdos} and to the people, since otherwise they would [be ignorant of]{g:ignorare} the
use of the church's service, to the knowledge of which they are obliged; and especially that article
of faith, "the holy catholic church", which every catholic ought to know.[^12]

## ¶2 · 126.1–126.13

> [p. 126] Et patet quod Christus semper est caput ecclesie. Unde ego credo quod, quicunque episcopus vere presidet in particulari ecclesia, remanet post mortem suam caput vel patronus eiusdem ecclesie. Quamvis enim moriatur in corpore, procurat tamen in spiritu pro subditis eciam fervencius quam in via, et post diem iudicii habebit quandam preeminenciam et illuminacionem, sicut in angelis longe alterius racionis quam prepositura viancium. Ideo rogamus, ut sanctos episcopos semper habeamus patronos in celis. Sed illi non contendebant circa primatus, adulterantes scripturam sacram, ut populus astringatur in via credere quod ipsi sunt capita sancte matris ecclesie.

And it is clear that Christ is always [head]{g:caput} of the church. Hence I believe that whatever
bishop truly presides in a particular church remains after his death head or [patron]{g:patronus} of
that same church.[^13] For although he dies in the body, yet in spirit he acts for his subjects even
more fervently than on [the way]{g:via}, and after the day of judgment he will have a certain
pre-eminence and illumination, as among the angels, of a far other kind than the [office of
superior]{g:prepositura} of those on the road. Therefore we pray that we may always have holy bishops
as patrons in heaven. But they did not contend about primacies, adulterating holy scripture, so that
the people on the way should be bound to believe that they themselves are heads of holy mother
church.[^14]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 123.29: but he was always head by predestination.
- 124.4: In the incarnation he made second nuptials, raising a part of the Church to be Queen.
- 124.22: At the last advent the Lamb will make marriage consummate and the whole Church will be crowned as Queen.
- 125.4: Solomon's queens, concubines and virgins figure the three states that compose the Church.
- 126.1: A good bishop remains after death patron of his (local) Church.

## Notes

[^1]: **R/S/C.** A margin *Christus in primo instanti mundi fuit sponsus ecclesie* (123.30); Loserth
    (123.30): cf. Hus, *De ecclesia*, *Opp.* I, 198b (LEDGER E06c-1). *Ad illud*: the first
    difficulty, whether Christ was before the incarnation (123.24–27, 06-b). *Cesar semper Augustus*:
    the imperial style "ever Augustus", taken from *augere*: he who ever increases (*auget*) the dowry.
    Loserth (124.3): cf. Wyclif, *De benedicta incarnacione* c. 5, p. 67 (LEDGER E06c-2).

[^2]: **R/C.** A margin *Christus in incarnacione originavit ecclesiam christianam* (124.4), *Nupcie
    Christi per incarnacionem* (124.8). *Vero* here and at *Tercio vero* marks the stages of a series:
    "and". *Quadam proprietate*: by a certain propriety of name; the part of the church since the
    incarnation is "christian" in a proper sense.

[^3]: **S/C/R.** Heb. 1:1–2: God, who "spoke in times past to the fathers by the prophets, last of all,
    in these days, hath spoken to us by his Son" (DR); hence *familiarius*. *Ut gigas*: Ps. 18:6, the
    bridegroom "hath rejoiced as a giant to run the way" (DR). *Inducit*: "puts on" (of armour or
    clothing), a present beside the perfect *superavit*, as printed. *Partem … incarceratam*: the
    fathers held below until Christ's descent. *Dux et legifer noster*: perhaps after the Advent
    antiphon *O Emmanuel, rex et legifer noster* (to be checked; LEDGER E06c-3).

[^4]: **R/S/T.** B margin *Tota doctrina christiana stat in una oracione* (124.12). The petition *ut
    … terrena despicere et amare celestia* is in a postcommunion of Advent (to be checked; LEDGER
    E06c-4). B *preponere* for *postponere* (124.15), which reverses the sense; the print is
    translated. *Abicit … abiectissime*: Wyclif's play, "casts away … most abjectly".

[^5]: **C.** *Regula religionis*: a rule of life, with a glance at the rules of the orders.
    *Assumptam iuvenculam*: the church militant, taken as bride at the incarnation (*assumere*, as in
    *assumpcio humanitatis*). We take *in regnum celorum* with *sequendi*: following into the kingdom.

[^6]: **T/S/R.** The token *ad-* before *Tercio* (124.22) is not Wyclif's and is not translated (Latin
    doubts). A margin *Nupcie consumacionis*; B *nupcias consumptas* (124.22). *Una columba*: Cant.
    6:8, "One is my dove" (DR). Apoc. 21:2, 9: the city "prepared as a bride adorned for her husband",
    "the bride, the wife of the Lamb" (DR). The page foot gives "B: Moralium XXI" (124.25), apparently
    in place of *Apoc. XXI°*; the print is translated.

[^7]: **C/S.** *Secundum magnam partem*: the greater part of that church is already the bride, in its
    three states (triumphant, sleeping, militant). *Servicium ecclesie*: the liturgy (untagged,
    GLOSSARY *servicium*). *Intendere*: to attend to, not the thesis sense "maintain". *Ut canit
    ecclesia*: perhaps the office of the dedication of a church (to be checked; LEDGER E06c-5).
    Loserth (125.3): cf. above, 97.5.

[^8]: **S/T/C.** Cant. 6:7, "There are threescore queens, and fourscore concubines, and young maidens
    without number" (DR), with Solomon taken as the Song's speaker. A *regninas*; A margin *Concubine,
    regine, adolescentule Salomonis* (125.4). *Tam angelis quam animabus, quam cum adolescentibus
    viantibus*: three classes, answering the three of the Song; *adolescentibus viantibus*, "the young
    ones on the road", picks up *adolescentulas*.

[^9]: **S/T/C.** 3 Kings 6 (1 Kings 6 in modern numbering); A *III Reg. VI cap.* (125.13). 1 Cor. 3:17,
    "For the temple of God is holy, which you are" (DR).
    *Frustra … ad figurandum*: the parallel with *nisi ut figurando* above gives "in vain, unless to
    figure"; hence our "[were it not]". A margin *Distinccio templi Salomonis ad ecclesiam* (125.15).

[^10]: **T/C/S.** A *primam in*, *aligenigene* (125.16); B margin (later hand) *Templi partes tres*.
    *Sicut enim* has no answering *sic* until *Sic, inquam* below; the period is left open as printed.
    Matt. 21:12, the sellers cast out of the temple, is cited, not quoted. *Ut presciti*: the
    foreknown are such foreigners in the church militant.

[^11]: **C/T/S.** *Corpus ecclesie*: the nave, the church-building's word given to the temple's holy
    place. *Pure Israhelitas*: Israelites without admixture; *a rugis … depurandos*: the church
    sleeping, still to be cleansed of the "wrinkle" of Eph. 5:27. The page foot gives "Codd.: qua"
    (125.23, cut short), apparently against the print's *quam*, which we follow. Heb. 9:7 has the high
    priest alone (*solus pontifex*) enter once a year; *soli sacerdotes* is Wyclif's. Heb. 9:11 agrees
    with the Vulgate less *autem*.

[^12]: **C.** *Iuxta fidem*: according to faith, not the formula *ex fide*. *Usum servicii ecclesie*:
    the meaning of the church's rites, as of the temple-figure above. *Ecclesiam sanctam catholicam*:
    the creed's article, quoted in the accusative it has after *credo*.

[^13]: **R/T/C.** A margin *Conclusio* (126.1). At 126.4 the page foot reads "A: est (in red ink) suam
    est caput": A apparently has *post mortem suam est caput*, with *est* for *remanet*; the OCR is
    unclear. *Patronus*: the bishop as patron in heaven of his church, not the patron of a benefice;
    *caput vel patronus* grants him "headship" of a particular church only in that sense.

[^14]: **C/T.** *Procurat … pro subditis*: acts on his subjects' behalf, as a proctor would. B
    *quandam primiciam* (126.7), apparently for *quandam preeminenciam*. *Alterius racionis*: of
    another kind. *Illi*: the holy bishops of old, against present prelates who claim to be *capita*;
    *in via* goes with *populus*.
