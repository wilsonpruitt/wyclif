### 07-j ¶1 · 226.1–226.16  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 226] O quam detestanda glossa que tociens exponit textum per suum contradictorium; et in eadem proposicione mutat sensum eiusdem termini contra regulam propriam. Recoleret, inquam, de textu Augustini, ubi dicit de omnibus modis contendere approbandum quod caro est carnis et sanguis est sanguinis sacramentum; et illam carnem vocat iste sanctus celestem panem qui vere est corpus Christi suo modo; tunc enim verecundaretur de glossa quam hic addicit, hoc est: inproprie dicitur corpus Christi suo modo, **sed** non rei veritate. Beatus **autem** Augustinus exponit se ipsum quod ille panis sacratus videtur esse corpus Christi modo signi, cum tropice et sacramentaliter vere significat corpus Christi. Nec dubium quin Augustinus intendit per panem naturam panis, non accidens, quod tam expresse asserit non posse per se esse.

ENGLISH: O how detestable a gloss, which so often expounds the text by its contradictory, and in the same proposition changes the [meaning]{g:sensus} of the same term against its own rule![^1] Let him recall, I say, the text of Augustine, where he speaks of contending in every way to prove that "the flesh is the [sacrament]{g:sacramentum} of the flesh and the blood is the sacrament of the blood"; and that flesh this saint calls "the heavenly [bread]{g:panis} which [truly]{g:vere} is [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} in its own manner";[^2] for then he would be ashamed of the gloss which he adds here, that is: "it is improperly called the body of Christ in its own manner, but not in the truth of the thing." But blessed Augustine expounds himself: that that [hallowed]{g:sacrare} bread appears to be the body of Christ in the manner of a [sign]{g:signum}, since [tropically]{g:tropice} and [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} it truly signifies the body of Christ.[^3] Nor is there doubt that Augustine means by bread the [nature]{g:natura} of bread, not an [accident]{g:accidens}, which he so expressly asserts "cannot be [of itself]{g:per-se}".[^4]

### 07-j ¶2 · 226.17–227.4  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 226] Et conformiter intelligit beatus Ambrosius illud decretum: Panis est in altari; non enim est bona glossa, beato Ambrosio probante et asserente quod panis potest esse corpus Christi; quam glossator sibi addicit dicens: Inpossibile est quod panis sit corpus Christi, **sed** talia sunt ad sanum sensum intelligenda. Nec dubium quin glossator iste non tantum insanivisset, nisi fuisset seductus per glossatores theologos. Unde signum prolixitatis et dubitacionis sue in ista materia est quod in principio illius distinccionis recitat tanquam opinionem probabilem quod panis et vinum remanent post consecracionem; quod probat ex illo decreto Ego Berengarius et non reprobat. [p. 227] Foret **autem** immemor sensus proprii, nisi appareret sibi sensum illius facere pro hac via. Oportet ergo intelligere sanctos cum scriptura quod figurative et vere vocant signum nomine signati.

ENGLISH: And in conformity [with this] blessed Ambrose understands that [decree]{g:decretum}, "Bread is on the altar";[^5] for it is not a good gloss (blessed Ambrose proving and asserting that "bread can be the body of Christ") which the [glossator]{g:glossa} adds to it, saying: "It is impossible that bread should be the body of Christ, but such things are to be understood in a sound meaning."[^6] Nor is there doubt that this glossator would not have raved so much, had he not been led astray by the theologian glossators. Hence a sign of his prolixity and doubting in this matter is that at the beginning of that distinction he recites as a probable [opinion]{g:opinio} that the bread and the [wine]{g:vinum} remain after [consecration]{g:consecracio}; which he proves from that decree *Ego Berengarius*, and does not reprove.[^7] [227] And he would be unmindful of his own meaning, unless the meaning of that [decree] appeared to him to make for this way.[^8] We must therefore understand [the saints]{g:sancti}, together with scripture, [as saying] that [figuratively]{g:figurative} and truly they call the sign by the name of the [thing signified]{g:signatum}.

### 07-j ¶3 · 227.5–227.25  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 227] Habito ergo sensu decreti magni Augustini et aliorum sanctorum inveho contra istam sentenciam, primo per hoc quod iuxta sanctorum sentenciam panis materialis per benediccionem fit corpus Christi; quod non foret verum iuxta hanc viam: ergo illa repugnat sanctorum sentenciis. Assumptum licet sit contra regulas modernorum doctorum, patet per beatum Augustinum, Sermone LXIᵒ pro feria IIᵃ Pasche, et ponitur in De consecracione, distinccione IIᵃ: Mementote, inquit, carissimi quemadmodum dominus Jesus ab eis quorum oculi tenebantur ne illum agnoscerent in fraccione panis voluit se agnosci. Non, inquit, omnis panis **sed** accipiens benediccionem Christi fit corpus Christi. Nec dubium quin loquitur de pane materiali, ut IVᵗᵒ de Trinitate: Corpus et sanguinem Christi dicimus illud quod ex terre fructibus acceptum et prece mistica consecratum recte sumimus ad salutem in memoriam dominice passionis. Nulli dubium quin non intelligit ignotum accidens acceptum ex terre fructibus et sacramentaliter benedictum **sed** (ut verba sonant) verum panem usualem taliter consecratum.

ENGLISH: Having therefore the meaning of the decree of the great Augustine and of the other saints, I inveigh against this [teaching]{g:sentencia},[^9] first by this: that according to the saints' teaching material bread by blessing becomes the body of Christ; which would not be true according to this way: therefore it [is incompatible with]{g:repugnare} the saints' teachings. [The assumption]{g:assumptum}, although it is against the rules of [the modern doctors]{g:moderni-doctores}, is clear from blessed Augustine, Sermon 61, for the second [day of the week]{g:feria} of Easter, and it is placed in *De consecracione*, distinction 2: "Remember," he says, "dearly beloved, how the Lord Jesus willed to be known in the [breaking]{g:fractio} of bread by those whose eyes were held that they should not know him." "Not every bread," he says, "but [the bread] receiving the blessing of Christ becomes the body of Christ."[^10] Nor is there doubt that he speaks of material bread, as in [*On the Trinity*] 4: "We call the body and blood of Christ that which, taken from the fruits of the earth and consecrated by mystical prayer, we rightly receive for salvation in memory of the Lord's passion."[^11] To no one is there doubt that he does not mean an unknown accident taken from the fruits of the earth and sacramentally blessed, but (as the words sound) true ordinary bread so consecrated.

### 07-j ¶5 · 228.24–230.3  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 228] Item (ut sepe asserui), non est sompniandum mendacium in ista materia, ne ydolatra false immolet patri mendacii. Apostolus I. Cor. Xᵒ, 14 incipiens tractare de hoc sacramento hortatur sibi carissimos fugere ab ydolorum cultura. Cum ergo ista inpanacio non habet evidenciam ex scriptura, ex racione vel ex testimonio dante fidem, videtur quod katholicus pro evitando periculo non debet inpanacionem talem asserere. Et ista regula usus sum contra transsubstanciacionem [p. 229] ecclesie Avinonice et contra omnem novitatem preter scripturam sacram de ista materia sompniatam. Notum est (inquam) istam ecclesiam eciam in materia fidei sepius deviasse. Cum ergo lex scripture tradidit nobis de eukaristia sufficiens ad credendum, videtur quod sit presumptuosa stulticia preter eius fundacionem superaddere novitates; iuxtaponamus itaque exposiciones sanctorum et videamus ubi inpanacio sit fundata; ymmo non videbimus per locum a simili, ubi eius possibilitas fundaretur. Verbum enim infinite potencie assumpsit personaliter humanitatem redimendam; **sed** quid hoc ut caro Christi et sanguis assumant sibi panem et vinum? Et iterum, illa humanitas non fuit, antequam fiebat Deus, quia aliter foret ydemptificacio, nec potest amitti; quorum utriusque oppositum contingit in sacramento altaris. Et iterum, idem suppositum est persona veri hominis et Dei, quod si foret evidenter simile in inpanacione, idem suppositum foret panis in inpanacione qui prefuit et vera caro Christi. Quod negatur, quia tunc foret ydemptificacio et non mutacio; quod negant leges ecclesie cum scriptura; ymmo sicut Christus assumendo multas humanitates foret multi homines, sic caro Christi et sanguinis forent multi panes et vina quantumcunque varie accidentata; et sic Christus moveretur eodem motu quo movetur aliud tale corpus; et reciperet mediante tali pane predicaciones multas superfluas et inhonestas, ut tactum est in materia de incarnacione, ubi inprobatur ista via. Sequitur enim panis quem fecit Petrus pistor est Deus, et alius panis quem pinsuit Paulus pistor est idem Deus, ergo multe substancie create forent Deus, et sic Deus foret facillime factibilis a pistore et inhonestissime ac crudelissime [p. 230] tractabilis nedum ab homine **sed** a bestia, sicut tractabatur secundum humanitatem suam in via. **Sed** nichil plus hereticum, impium et insane.

ENGLISH: Again (as I have often asserted), a lie is not to be dreamed up in this matter, lest the idolater falsely immolate to the father of lies. The Apostle, 1 Cor. 10:14, beginning to treat of this sacrament, exhorts "his dearly beloved" to "fly from the service of idols".[^15] Since therefore this impanation has no [evidence]{g:evidencia} from scripture, from reason, or from testimony giving faith, it seems that a catholic, to avoid the danger, ought not to assert such impanation. And this rule I have used against the transubstantiation [229] of the Avignon [church]{g:ecclesia}, and against every novelty dreamed up beyond holy scripture concerning this matter. It is known (I say) that this church has quite often gone astray even in a matter of faith. Since therefore the law of scripture has handed down to us concerning [the Eucharist]{g:eukaristia} what suffices for believing, it seems that it is presumptuous folly to add novelties over and above, beyond its foundation; let us therefore set side by side the expositions of the saints and see where impanation is founded; indeed we shall not see, by the topic [from likeness]{g:a-simili}, where its possibility would be founded. For [the Word]{g:verbum} of infinite power [assumed]{g:assumere} personally [the humanity]{g:humanitas} that was to be redeemed; but what is this to the flesh and blood of Christ assuming bread and wine to themselves? And again, that humanity was not before it became God, because otherwise it would be identification; nor can it be lost; the opposite of each of which happens in [the sacrament of the altar]{g:sacramentum-altaris}.[^16] And again, the same supposit is the [person]{g:persona} of true man and of God; and if this were evidently alike in impanation, the same supposit would be, in impanation, the bread which was there before and the true flesh of Christ. This is denied, because then it would be identification and not change; which [the laws of the church]{g:leges-ecclesie} deny together with scripture. Indeed, just as Christ by assuming many humanities would be many men, so the flesh and blood of Christ would be many breads and wines, however variously [subject to accidents]{g:accidentatus};[^17] and so Christ would be moved with the same motion with which another such body is moved; and he would receive, by means of such bread, many superfluous and unseemly [predications]{g:predicacio}, as was touched on in the matter of the incarnation, where this way is disproved.[^18] For [it follows]{g:sequitur}: "the bread which Peter the baker made is God, and another bread which Paul the baker kneaded is the same God"; therefore many [created]{g:creatus} substances would be God; and so God would be most easily makeable by a baker, and most unseemly and most cruelly [230] handleable, [not only]{g:nedum} by a man but by a beast, as he was handled [as to his humanity]{g:secundum-humanitatem} in [the way]{g:via}. But nothing [is] more heretical, impious and insane.

### 07-j ¶6 · 230.4–230.18  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 230] Et sic intelligi debet confessio Berengarii qui primo negavit predicacionem tropicam, quomodo referri debet ad signum quod teritur et non Christus. Notandum quod multiplex est variacio loyce fidelium in ista materia, ut aliqui fideliter concedunt proposiciones Christi et tocius scripture esse verissimas et tamen negant quod panis quem Christus cepit et sacravit est vel fuit corpus suum, et a pari nullus panis sacerdotum sequencium; nec proposicio scripture sacre illud significat **sed** significat quod panis sacramentaliter fiat corpus Christi. Proposicionibus **autem** nostris non debemus dare talem sensum tropicum, cum Christus concessit hoc: Baptista est Helias, et Baptista negavit ipsum esse Heliam, reservando tropum magistro.

ENGLISH: And so the confession of Berengar must be understood, who first denied the [tropical predication]{g:predicacio-tropica}: how it must be referred to the sign which is ground, and not [to] Christ.[^19] It is to be noted that the variation of the logic of the faithful in this matter is manifold: so that some faithfully grant the propositions of Christ and of all scripture to be most true, and yet deny "the bread which Christ took and hallowed is or was his body", and by parity [say] that no bread of the priests who follow [is]; nor does the [proposition]{g:proposicio} of holy scripture signify that, but it signifies "the bread sacramentally becomes the body of Christ".[^20] But to our propositions we ought not to give such a [tropical meaning]{g:sensus-tropicus}, since Christ granted this, "the Baptist is Elias", and the Baptist denied himself to be Elias, reserving the [trope]{g:tropus} to the master.[^21]

### 07-j ¶7 · 230.19–230.31  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 230] Secunda via concedit generaliter quod panis sacramentaliter consecratus est corpus Christi, quia sacramentaliter ipsum figurat et erit ac fuit et per consequens potest esse corpus Christi; **sed** non concedit plus cum aliis verbis adiectivis, ut quod homo videt, terit vel sentit corpus Christi oculo corporali. Et pro utraque parte istius loyce sunt multa dicta sanctorum et determinaciones ecclesie; et nisi sic diceretur, oporteret negare dicta sanctorum et ecclesie tanquam heretica, et non esset michi efficax testimonium vie mee. Et quantum ad sensum eorum haberem mille glossatores michi contrarios, et negando dicta sanctorum tanquam heretica deficeret michi efficacia replicandi.

ENGLISH: The second way grants generally that "the bread sacramentally consecrated is the body of Christ", because it sacramentally [figures]{g:figurare} it, and will be and was, and consequently can be, the body of Christ; but it does not grant more with other [adjectival verbs]{g:verba-adiectiva}, as that a man sees, grinds or [perceives]{g:sentire} the body of Christ with the bodily eye.[^22] And for each part of this logic there are many sayings of the saints and [determinations of the church]{g:determinaciones-ecclesie}; and unless it were so said, one would have to deny the sayings of the saints and of the church as heretical, and the testimony of my way would not be effective for me. And as to their meaning I should have a thousand glossators contrary to me, and by denying the sayings of the saints as heretical I should lack the effectiveness of [rejoining]{g:replicatur}.

### 07-k ¶1 · 230.32–231.9  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 230] Tercia via laxius loquitur, ut quod panis sacratus nedum est, fit vel erit corpus Christi ad sensum [p. 231] expositum, **sed** corpus Christi movetur corporaliter, conficitur, sanctificatur, et sic de aliis predicacionibus quas panis sacratus suscipit. Nec repugnat corpori Domini ut sit signum; unde decretum Jeronimi positum in De Consecracione, distinccione IIᵃ, distinguit de duplici corpore Christi. Et sic intelligitur caro Christi et sanguis, vel spiritualis illa de qua ait: Caro mea vere est cibus, vel caro illa que crucifixa est; et sic de sanguine.

ENGLISH: The third way speaks more loosely: so that the hallowed bread [not only]{g:nedum} is, becomes or will be [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi} in the [meaning]{g:sensus} [231] expounded, but the body of Christ is moved [bodily]{g:corporaliter}, [is consecrated]{g:conficere}, is sanctified, and so with the other [predications]{g:predicacio} that the [hallowed]{g:sacrare} [bread]{g:panis} receives.[^1] Nor is it [incompatible with]{g:repugnare} [the body of the Lord]{g:corpus-domini} that it be a [sign]{g:signum}; hence the [decree]{g:decretum} of Jerome placed in *De consecracione*, distinction 2, distinguishes concerning the twofold body of Christ.[^2] And thus the flesh and blood of Christ is understood: either that spiritual [flesh] of which he says, "My flesh is meat indeed", or that flesh which was crucified; and so of the blood.[^3]

### 07-k ¶2 · 231.10–231.24  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 231] Nec contendo circa istam variacionem loyce cum hoc quod sane intelligatur quod panis non sit essencialiter **sed** tropice corpus Christi, **sed** quod natura panis remanet post consecracionem subiectans talia accidencia, licet consideracio eius ex presencia nobilioris corporis sit sopita. Nec contendo quod ad sensus equivocos concedatur quod panis est corpus Christi et ille panis non est corpus Christi, cum Christus et Baptista propter equivocacionem non fuerunt contrarii. Aliquando **autem** pro instruccione populi expedit nunc uno modo loqui nunc alio. Unde quia in tercia via iacet periculum, sicut patet ex paulativa decepcione ecclesie, ideo expedit cavere in illa declinando ad viam primam nec alteram duarum viarum sequencium exprimere nisi sensu exposito.

ENGLISH: Nor do I contend over this variation of logic, provided that it is soundly understood that the bread is not [essentially]{g:essencialiter} but [tropically]{g:tropice} the body of Christ, but that the [nature]{g:natura} of bread remains after [consecration]{g:consecracio}, [being the subject of]{g:subiectare} such [accidents]{g:accidens}, although the consideration of it is lulled to sleep by the presence of the nobler body. Nor do I dispute its being granted, in equivocal meanings, that "the bread is the body of Christ" and "that bread is not the body of Christ", since Christ and the Baptist were not contrary, because of [equivocation]{g:equivocacio}.[^4] And sometimes, for the instruction of the people, it is expedient to speak now in one way, now in another. Hence, because danger lies in the third way, as is clear from the gradual deceiving of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, it is therefore expedient to beware in it, by turning to the first way, and not to express either of the two ways that follow except with the meaning expounded.

### 08-a ¶1 · 232.5–232.17  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 232] Restat ulterius videre de corporis multiplicacione. Non enim queritur hic quomodo species vel commune multiplicatur in suis suppositis, cum sit singula eius singularia essencialiter et numeraliter condistincta. **Sed** quomodo idem suppositum in numero potest multiplicari per distincta loca et distancia, sicut dicimus animam eandem hominis secundum se totam esse in quolibet membro suo, oportet eciam fidelem concedere quod tam anima Christi quam totum corpus eius sit simul tempore per diversa loca in multis ecclesiis atque altaribus, et illud vocamus multiplicacionem corporis, sicut dicitur totum corpus esse ad omnem punctum hostie consecrate.

ENGLISH: It remains further to see about the [multiplication of a body]{g:multiplicacio-corporis}. For it is not asked here how a species or a common [thing] is multiplied in its [supposits]{g:suppositum}, since its singulars are severally distinct from one another, [essentially]{g:essencialiter} and numerically.[^1] But how the same supposit in number can be multiplied through distinct and distant places: just as we say that the same soul of a man is, according to its whole self, in each [member]{g:membrum} of him, the faithful man must also grant that both the soul of Christ and his whole body are at the same time through diverse places, in many [churches]{g:ecclesia} and altars; and this we call the multiplication of a body, as the whole body is said to be at every point of the [consecrated host]{g:hostia-consecrata}.[^2]

### 08-a ¶2 · 232.18–233.8  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 232] In ista **autem** materia sunt due vie: prima que ascribitur Doctori Subtili et suis sequacibus, ponens quod stat idem corpus in numero multiplicari dimensionaliter simul tempore per quotlibet loca non communicancia. Et secunda que ascribitur Doctori Communi cum suis sequacibus quod idem corpus potest simul multiplicari per quotlibet loca, sic quod per unicum sit dimensionaliter et per alia incommunicancia virtualiter vel sacramentaliter sicut est de corpore Christi, quod dimensionaliter est in celo et sacramentaliter in quotlibet locis aliis distantibus, [p. 233] quia ubicunque est hostia consecrata; non tamen est dimensionaliter in aliqua earum, quia tunc extenderetur per locum hostie coequatum eidem, sic quod quelibet pars loci eiusdem hostie haberet appropriate partem corporis Christi singulariter occupatum, sicut est de aliis corporibus que per loca eis equalia diffunduntur; tunc enim foret omnino equale loco suo totali et omnino conformiter figuratum.

ENGLISH: And in this matter there are two ways. The first, which is ascribed to [the Subtle Doctor]{g:doctor-subtilis} and his followers, posits that it is consistent for the same body in number to be multiplied [dimensionally]{g:dimensionaliter} at the same time through any number of places that do not communicate.[^3] And the second, which is ascribed to [the Common Doctor]{g:doctor-communis} with his followers, [holds] that the same body can be multiplied at the same time through any number of places, so that through one only it is dimensionally, and through the others, [that do not communicate]{g:incommunicans}, [virtually]{g:virtualiter} or [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter}: as is the case with [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}, which is dimensionally in heaven and sacramentally in any number of other distant places, [233] because [it is] wherever the consecrated host is.[^4] Yet it is not dimensionally in any of them, because then it would be extended through the place of the host, made equal to it, so that each part of the place of that host would have appropriated to it a part of the body of Christ, occupied by it alone, as is the case with other bodies that are spread through places equal to them; for then it would be altogether equal to its total place and altogether conformably [figured]{g:figurare}.[^5]

### 08-a ¶3 · 233.9–233.30  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 233] Contra primam arguitur, primo per hoc quod omne corpus sit potencie infinite; nam Petrus foret eiusdem potencie cuius foret multiplicatus dimensionaliter per mille miliaria aut quotlibet loca; **sed** tunc foret correspondenter ad multiplicacionem maioris potencie: ergo modo est tante potencie. Assumptum patet ex hoc quod potencia est una qualitas absoluta que est locacioni inpertinens, quia aliter progrediens continue variaret potenciam. Et patet possibilitas assumpti cuius veritas supponatur; et minor probatur ex hoc quod in quocunque loco est Petrus tante potencie sufficit cum paribus tantum efficere et per consequens penes mensuram penes quam attendi debet magnitudo potencie est proporcionaliter maioris potencie; si enim sufficit ex potencia quam nunc habet facere tantum quantum mille homines, quare non est nunc tante potencie quante illi? Et assumptum patet, posito quod Petrus fortissimus multiplicaretur dimensionaliter per mille loca, foret tam potens sicut mille homines eque fortes, cum ubicunque fuerit manet tanta potencia non suspensa. Sufficeret ergo Petrus sic multiplicatus pugnare cum mille hominibus et sic in infinitum.

ENGLISH: Against the first it is argued, first by this: that every body would be of infinite [power]{g:potencia}; for Peter would be of the same power as he would be of, multiplied dimensionally through a thousand miles or any number of places; now then he would be, correspondingly to the multiplication, of greater power: therefore he is now of so great power.[^6] [The assumption]{g:assumptum} is clear from this, that power is one absolute [quality]{g:qualitas}, which has no bearing on location, because otherwise one walking forward would continually vary his power. And the possibility of the assumption is clear, whose truth let be supposed; and the minor is proved from this, that in whatever place Peter is, of so great power, he suffices with his equals to effect so much, and consequently, by the measure by which the magnitude of power ought to be gauged, he is of proportionally greater power; for if from the power that he now has he suffices to do as much as a thousand men, why is he not now of as great power as they?[^7] And the assumption is clear: supposing that Peter, being very strong, were multiplied dimensionally through a thousand places, he would be as powerful as a thousand men equally strong, since wherever he were, so great a power remains, not [suspended]{g:suspendere}. Peter so multiplied would therefore suffice to fight with a thousand men; and so to infinity.

### 08-a ¶5 · 234.27–235.24  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 234] Hoc ergo habito queritur in secunda questione utrum Petrus mereretur in casu paciendo mortem a se ipso ipsam iniuste et irracionabiliter inferente; et quare non cessat, cum utrobique sentit dolorem et remanet compos mentis, nam actus sensibiles individuantur a loco et tempore et per consequens propter diversitatem eorum forent actus illi diversi. Et Deus, [p. 235] qui ex omnipotencia sua potest sic concomitanter multiplicari miraculose, potest demultiplicare, coartare sive restringere, ut accidens spirituale in uno loco multiplicetur et alibi supernaturaliter impediatur a multitudine; **sed** superinducatur eius contrarium, cum contraria non repugnant nisi pro simul et semel; ymmo omnia contraria Deus potest ponere simul; nec lex nature potest inpedire Deum quin potest facere quod informent; quod si Deus potest facere maius, potest facere et minus, ut communiter assumitur in ista materia. Forma ergo humanitatis et forma deitatis forent secundum istos magis contraria et plus distancia quam sunt ista, et ita virtus et vicium, meritum et demeritum possent simul tempore **sed** in locis diversis inesse eidem persone simplici, que foret et predestinata et prescita; quomodo ergo Petrus beatus videns in verbo suam dampnacionem non compateretur sibi ipsi quem tantum caritative diligit? nam habet quidquid vult et nichil mali vult; nec denominaciones contrarie destruerentur, ut sancti ipsas describunt, ymmo consequenter loquendo mille forent forme contrarie in natura; Deus enim posset coextendere quodcunque calidum cum quocunque frigido et facere ut forme simul informent.
