# Connective check: *sed* / *autem*, De potestate pape, extract 11 (chunks 11-e – 11-g)

Classes: (a) minor premiss / next step, "now" · (b) adversative, "but" · (c) continuative *autem*, "and";
*non … sed* (incl. a negative verb or *ne … nec* answered by *sed*), "but".

## Changes

| chunk | page | Latin phrase | was | now | reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

None. Every *sed* / *autem* in this extract is rendered, and rendered per its class. The passage is the
vestments exposition (largely Grosseteste / Hugh-type allegory): no syllogism closes with *ergo / igitur*
after any *sed*, so no class (a) "now" is owed anywhere.

## Doubtful, left as they are

- **11-g ¶1, p. 287**: *Sed quia surrepit ommissionis negligencia … ideo quarto ponitur … manipula*, rendered "But since negligence of omission creeps in, … therefore, fourth". The *sed* moves to the next vestment in the list; the *ideo* closes its own *quia* clause, not a syllogism, so the card's look-ahead rule does not force "now". A light contrast (however devoted we are, negligence creeps in) makes "But" defensible; "Now since" would also serve.
- **11-g ¶2, p. 288**: *Sed sexto induit casulam*, rendered "But, sixth". Enumerative *sed* opening the next item of the list; neither adversative nor a premiss. "But" kept as the nearer fit (the chasuble set against the narrower old vestments); "And, sixth" or "Now, sixth" possible.
- **11-g ¶1, p. 286**: *Notandum autem quod*, rendered "And it is to be noted". (c), but could be heard as (a) bringing the next point ("Now it is to be noted"). "And" is the card's default for a continuative *autem*.
- **11-g ¶2, p. 288**: *nec apostoli nec patres … poterant …, sed quibusdam circulis … fuerant colligata*, rendered "but they had been bound". Either *non … sed* (answering *nec … nec*) or plain adversative; "but" under both.
