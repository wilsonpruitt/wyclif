# *De potestate pape*, chapter 12, part 10: provisions, elections and the stipends of failing superiors

## ¶1 · 349.27–350.22

> [p. 349] Ex isto morali principio sequitur, primo quod papa debet sollicitudinem talium provisionum dimittere et occupari circa edificacionem ecclesie, sicut Petrus fuerat occupatus. Hoc enim foret ecclesie et sibi utilius. Nichil enim acquirit sibi per huiusmodi provisiones et alias dominativas preeminencias infundabiles, si non pecuniam, favorem et famam seculi. Nam postquam distribuit [p. 350] sic bona pauperum vocatus est pater beatissimus, inpeccabilis et Deus in terris, quod si sitit triplicitatem istam per hoc medium, tunc est principium symonie patri mendacii, et in terris fortissimus Antichristus; et subtraccione talis officii rediret ecclesia ad statum primevum, quem Christus instituit. Sed donemus sibi istam iniuriam. Secundo sequitur quod omnis elector spiritualis prepositi sed papa precipue debet positus in adopcione multorum omni obstante postposito ipsum eligere, quem reputat meliorem, hoc est plus ad honorem Dei et utilitatem ecclesie; hoc enim foret sibi, utilius, quia fidelitati sui ministerii, et per consequens remuneracioni Dei plus congruum. Et patet quod qui propter pecuniam, propter sanguinem vel favorem seculi preponit illum quem credit vel debet credere minus idoneum peccat mortaliter, quia infideliter in amore preponderat temporale vanissimum Deo suo, quia illud, ob cuius amorem facit quod Deo displicet; quod non faceret propter omnia bona mundi.

From this moral principle it [follows]{g:sequitur}, first, that the [pope]{g:papa} ought to give up his concern with such provisions and be occupied about the building up of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, as Peter had been occupied.[^1] For this would be more useful to the church and to himself. For through provisions of this kind and other lordly [preeminences]{g:preeminencia} [without foundation]{g:infundabilis} he acquires nothing for himself, if not money, favour and the fame of the world. For after he has [350] thus distributed the goods of the poor he is called most blessed father, sinless, and God on earth; so that if he thirsts for this threefold [thing] by this means, then he is the principle of simony to the father of lies, and on earth the strongest Antichrist;[^2] and by the withdrawal of such an office the church would return to the primitive [state]{g:status} which Christ instituted. But let us forgive him this wrong.[^3] Secondly, it follows that every elector of a spiritual [superior]{g:prepositi}, but the pope above all, when he is placed in the choice among many, ought, every obstacle set aside, to elect the one whom he reckons the better, that is, [the one] more for the honour of God and the profit of the church;[^4] for this would be more useful to himself, because more congruous with the fidelity of his ministry, and consequently with the reward of God. And it is clear that he who, for money, for blood or for the favour of the world, prefers one whom he believes, or ought to believe, less fit sins mortally, because in his love he unfaithfully makes the vainest temporal thing outweigh his God, because [he so weighs] that for love of which he does what displeases God; which he would not do for all the goods of the world.[^5]

## ¶2 · 350.23–350.31

> [p. 350] Et tercio sequitur ex dictis quod papa non debet propter peticionem aut instanciam secularium dominorum preficere clericum vel carum regis in episcopum vel prelatum ecclesie, dum reputat vel reputare debeat alium magis aptum; nam cicius desereret omnem favorem principum seculi, omnem dotacionem ecclesie et omnem gloriam, quam ipse vel fratres sui in suo officio habent de seculo.

And, third, it follows from what has been said that the pope ought not, at the petition or [urging]{g:instancia} of [secular lords]{g:seculares-domini}, to set a [cleric]{g:clericus} or one dear to the king over [the church] as [bishop]{g:episcopus} or [prelate]{g:prelatus} of the church, while he reckons, or ought to reckon, another more apt;[^6] for he should sooner give up all the favour of the princes of the world, all the [endowment]{g:dotacio} of the church and all the glory which he or his brethren have from the world in their office.

## ¶3 · 350.32–351.8

> [p. 350] Ad reputandum autem talem aptitudinem oportet fidem scripture de bonitate pastoris cognoscere et non cecari in isto porizmate: Plus lucri, plus favoris vel laudis humane reportarem ego cum [p. 351] meis complicibus, ideo hoc est ecclesie Christi utilius. Unde miratur mundus quomodo papa preficit idiotas artis regiminis spiritualis ignaros, dum vel reputat vel reputare debeat quod sunt in eadem provincia multi plus habiles eciam sacre pagine professores, sed divicie quibus liniuntur ecclesie sunt occasio unde franguntur fides et christiana religio.

And to reckon such aptness one must know the faith of scripture about the goodness of a shepherd, and not be blinded in this [porism]{g:porisma}: "More gain, more favour or human praise would I bring back with [351] my accomplices; therefore this is more useful to the church of Christ." Hence the world marvels how the pope sets over [others] [unlearned men]{g:idiota} ignorant of the art of spiritual rule, while he either reckons, or ought to reckon, that there are in the same province many more [qualified]{g:habilis} [men], even professors of the sacred page; but the riches with which the churches are smeared are the occasion by which faith and the Christian religion are broken.[^7]

## ¶4 · 351.9–351.19

> [p. 351] Et patet cum predictis condicio Antichristi; ipse enim est dux capitaneorum qui procuratores pro parte dyaboli dissipant legem Christi, et ideo omnes fideles debent expugnare ducem huiusmodi, cum ille qui debet esse servus cunctis aliis illibertat eos cogens eos recipere cocum mentis et medicum, qui vel non curat vel ex inercia Dei et arte dyaboli dat venenum. Deus enim non potest procurare se esse episcopum populi, nisi qui eligendo Dominum vult sibi gratis subici, sed blasfemia Antichristi facit contrarium.

And with what has been said the condition of Antichrist is clear; for he is the leader of the [captains]{g:capitaneus} who, as [proctors]{g:procurator} for the devil's side, scatter the law of Christ; and therefore all the faithful ought to fight against a leader of this kind, since he who ought to be the servant of all others robs them of liberty, compelling them to receive a cook and physician of the mind who either does not cure, or, out of sloth towards God and by the devil's art, gives poison.[^8] For God cannot procure that he be bishop of a people except [of one] that, by electing the Lord, wills freely to be subject to him; but the blasphemy of Antichrist does the contrary.[^9]

## ¶5 · 351.21–352.2

> [p. 351] Ex istis videtur sequi quod a quocumque preposito spirituali notorie deficiente in suo officio licet stipendia mundana subtrahere ut decimas, oblaciones et alias elemozinas speciales. Probatur sic: Stipendia non dantur tali nisi ut tali, quia scilicet facit quod incumbit tali officio, sed hec causa deficit in quocumque huiusmodi, igitur est causa quecumque medians ad hunc finem. Illa enim est racio, quare ministrari non debet tali preposito, quia aliter subiectus sibi racionabiliter ministraret. Scrutetur igitur fidelis racionem talis ministerii et videbit quod aliena est a racione talis prepositi, quia ut capitur talis deficit a racione veri prepositi, et dare alteri [p. 352] debitum secundum racionem falsam et simulatam caret colore discipline veritatis.

From these [things] it seems to follow that from any spiritual superior notoriously failing in his office it is lawful to withdraw the worldly stipends, such as [tithes]{g:decime}, offerings and other special [alms]{g:elemosina}. It is proved thus: stipends are not given to such a one except as such, namely because he does what pertains to such an office; now this cause fails in any [superior] of this kind; therefore it is any cause whatever mediating to this end.[^10] For that is the reason why such a superior ought not to be ministered to: because otherwise his subject would reasonably minister to him. Let the faithful man therefore examine the reason for such ministering, and he will see that it is foreign to the reason of such a superior, because, as he is taken, such a one falls short of the reason of a true superior; and to give to another [352] what is owed according to a false and feigned reason lacks the [colour]{g:color} of the discipline of truth.[^11]

## ¶6 · 352.3–352.8

> [p. 352] Item, talis prepositus posset ad tantum indisponi in moribus, quod redderet se indignum stipendium tale recipere. Est igitur dare latitudinem indisposicionis et gradum remississimum dignitatis, sub quibus indigne reciperet tale stipendium.

Again, such a superior could be made so [unfit]{g:indisponere} in morals that he would render himself unworthy to receive such a stipend. One must therefore posit a latitude of [unfitness]{g:indisposicio} and a most remiss degree of worthiness, below which he would receive such a stipend unworthily.[^12]

## ¶7 · 352.9–352.30

> [p. 352] Sed probatur quod quilibet gradus talis latitudinis consequitur ad notorium defectum officii prelacie. Nam omne peccatum mortale facit indignum quoad Deum, ut hic supponitur. Sed datus prepositus est notorie in mortali, ergo est notorie indignus quoad Deum et per consequens apud quemlibet qui in nomine Domini tale debitum sibi daret. Nemo enim iuste dat debitum, nisi quod dat in nomine Dei debenti. Cum Deus secundum Augustinum graciose donat omne debitum, et cuilibet creature quodammodo obligatur. Si enim conductus a ministerio omnino se subtrahit, secundum iustas leges humanas subtrahi debet stipendium, multo magis secundum legem Dei iustissimam, in debito magis stricto. Et illud videtur Apostolum sentire II. Tess. III, 10 Qui non laborat, inquit, non manducet. Item, nemo tenetur fovere discolos et discipulos Antichristi, sed sic faceret quicumque voluntarie ministraret talibus ultra vite necessaria, igitur hoc non debet. Deus enim non fovet tales ut tales, sed permittit eos foveri et sustentat naturam.

Now it is proved that every degree of such a latitude follows upon a notorious failure in the office of prelacy. For every mortal sin makes [a man] unworthy as regards God, as is here supposed. Now the given superior is notoriously in mortal [sin]; therefore he is notoriously unworthy as regards God, and consequently in the eyes of anyone who would give him such a due in the name of the Lord. For no one justly gives what is due except what he gives in the name of God to one to whom it is owed.[^13] Since God, according to Augustine, graciously gives everything owed, and is in a certain way bound to every creature.[^14] For if a hired man withdraws himself altogether from the service, according to just human laws his stipend ought to be withdrawn; much more according to the most just law of God, in a stricter debt. And the Apostle seems to mean this, 2 Thessalonians 3:10: "He that does not work," he says, "let him not eat."[^15] Again, no one is bound to cherish the [froward]{g:discoli} and the disciples of Antichrist; now anyone who voluntarily ministered to such [men] beyond the necessaries of life would do so; therefore he ought not [to do] this. For God does not cherish such [men] as such, but permits them to be cherished, and sustains [their] nature.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** *Ex isto morali principio*: the principle argued at the end of the preceding part (349.7–26), that God wills a man to choose the better of two. *Provisiones* are papal provisions to benefices, the subject of 12-h and 12-i.
[^2]: **C.** *tunc est principium symonie patri mendacii*: the dative hangs loosely; taken as "the source of simony [serving] the father of lies" (the devil, John 8:44). The construction is doubtful and a word may be lost at 350.3. *Triplicitatem istam*: the money, favour and fame just named.
[^3]: **C.** *donemus sibi istam iniuriam*: "let us forgive him this wrong", i.e. let the charge pass; Wyclif turns to the second consequence.
[^4]: **C.** *positus in adopcione multorum*: "placed in the choice among many [candidates]". *Sed papa precipue* intensifies ("but the pope above all"). *Propter sanguinem* in the next sentence: for kinship.
[^5]: **C.** The close (350.20–22) is elliptical: *quia illud, ob cuius amorem facit quod Deo displicet* lacks a verb, and *preponderat* is supplied from the clause before; *quod non faceret propter omnia bona mundi* is taken as what the faithful man would not do (displease God) for all the world's goods. A word may be lost.
[^6]: **C.** *instanciam*: here "urging" (the glossary's second sense of *instancia*), not "objection". *Carum regis*: "one dear to the king", Loserth's "because the king favours him".
[^7]: **C.** *liniuntur*: "are smeared" (as with ointment or grease); the riches with which the churches are greased.
[^8]: **C.** *non curat*: both "does not care" and "does not cure"; the physician figure takes "cure". *Illibertat*, a non-classical verb, "robs of liberty". *Ex inercia Dei*: taken as an objective genitive, "sloth towards God". **R.** The manuscripts' margin at 351.11: *Cocum qui non vult coquere vel venenum procurat quomodo communitas reciperet* ("how could a community receive a cook who will not cook, or who procures poison?").
[^9]: **C.** *nisi qui eligendo Dominum vult sibi gratis subici*: *qui* has no expressed antecedent; taken as "[of a people] that", so that God becomes a people's bishop only by its free choice, against Antichrist's compulsion (*cogens*) just described.
[^10]: **C.** *igitur est causa quecumque medians ad hunc finem* does not give the conclusion the syllogism needs (that the stipend lacks its cause); a negative or a verb may be lost at 351.25–26. Translated as printed.
[^11]: **C.** *color*: "colour", a plausible ground, as in "colour of title". *Racio* runs through the paragraph: the reason or ground of the payment, measured against what a superior is.
[^12]: **C.** *Est … dare*: the schoolmen's "one must posit". *Latitudo* and *gradus remississimus* are the language of the intension and remission of qualities: a range of unfitness with a lowest degree of worthiness, below which the stipend is unworthily taken.
[^13]: **T.** *debenti* is Loserth's print; the manuscripts read *debentis* (352.17), which would agree with *Dei*: "in the name of God, who owes [it]", the sense the next sentence supports. The print is translated; strictly *debens* is "one who owes", and "to one to whom it is owed" renders it by sense.
[^14]: **S.** Augustine: the thought of *Confessions* 1.4.4, *reddis debita nulli debens, donas debita nihil perdens*, PL 32.663, where it follows *supererogatur tibi ut debeas*, the sense of Wyclif's *obligatur*; Wyclif paraphrases. **C.** The sentence is a *cum*-clause without a main verb; it gives the ground of the sentence before.
[^15]: **S.** 2 Thessalonians 3:10. Wyclif's *Qui non laborat … non manducet* departs from the Vulgate *si quis non vult operari, nec manducet* (DR "if any man will not work, neither let him eat"); his Latin is translated.
