# *De potestate pape*, chapter 1, part 4: the divisions of power, and five objections to multiplying them

## ¶1 · 12.1–14.10

> [p. 12] Doc., consul, castiga, solare, remitte, fer, ora, quia (ut dictum est) quotquot receperunt Christum fide formata, dedit eis potestatem sic filios Dei fieri, ut liceat eis dirigere se et fratres suos in via patris sui Christi, eciam corripiendo, sicut patet Matth. XVIII. Unde correspondenter ad potestatem spiritualem potestas secularis est duplex, scilicet civilis et communis. Civilis autoritativa solum domino civili competit, sed civilis vicaria convenit ballivis vel aliis gerentibus vicem secularium dominorum. Potestas autem communis secularis est qua quilibet potest secundum bona nature et fortune dirigere se et suos. Et ita sicut homo non potest esse sine anima et corpore, nec filius Dei adoptivus sine bonis nature et gracie, sic non stat viatorem esse nisi habeat tam secularem quam spiritualem potestatem communem, quamvis in infantibus et morionibus sit ligata. Spiritualis autem potestas est utrobique perfeccior, sed et sacerdotalis potestas spiritualis superaddit spiritualem communem. Quod autem limitatur potestas clericis super bonis suis temporalibus, seclusa potestate secularium, videtur esse presumpcio et avaricia sacerdotum. Et sic sunt quotlibet divisiones magistrales potestatum, quas curiositas ecclesie adinvenit, ut archidiaconus et electus dicuntur habere potestatem iurisdiccionis, eciam ad absolvendum et excommunicandum, licet non habeant ad hec potestatem ordinis, et talem potestatem in foro contencioso dicitur laycos habere ex commissionibus prelatorum. Contra dicta de multiplicitate potestatis humane arguitur. Videtur enim quod Deus non potest [p. 13] ordinare medium multiplicius pro fine qui eque perfecte fieret per medium brevius, cum omnis talis ordinacio foret frustra; sed omnis finis pro quo ordinaretur potestas tam multiplex eque perfecte si non perfeccius fieret per unicam simplicem potestatem, que est liberum arbitrium, sicud per quotlibet potestates superadditas, igitur superfluit ponere tam multas. Minor probatur ex hoc, quod omnis potestas creata est propter opus perfeccionis universi secundum ecclesiam vel aliam eius partem, sed in omne tale opus sufficit liberum arbitrium sine potestate accidentali, nam in statu innocencie foret solum talis potestas, sicut post statum innocencie illi qui vixerunt religiosius caruerunt huiusmodi potestate, quid (queso) sufficit homo ordinare vel facere, quod non sufficit ex puro libero arbitrio? Videtur enim quod palliata potestas superaddita pocius perturbat ecclesiam. Item, eadem potestas in numero potest nunc laxari et nun restringi, ut patet de potestate ordinis pari in quolibet sacerdote, licet in prelatis suscipiat in laxacione et restriccione gradus multiplices; igitur per idem naturalis potestas potest nunc laxari et nunc restringi quantum oportet pro regimine ecclesie, igitur cum in tali laxacione non acquiritur nova potestas, nec in restriccione deperditur, sequitur quod non oportet superaddere novam huiusmodi potestatem. Minor patet, tum quia potestas non componitur ex suis partibus intensivis, tum eciam quia aliter non foret eadem potestas restricta vel laxata sed omnino vel antiqua perdita vel nova genita. Et illud evidencius movet quod non oportet in sacerdote ponere distinccionem specificam inter [p. 14] potestatem ordinis et iurisdiccionis, sed dans potestatem ordinis dat ut sic potestatem iurisdiccionis, quia aliter foret nuda potestas ordinis nimis superflua, quia insufficiens ut sit in materiam quantumcunque exercita. Christus autem quando dedit discipulis potestatem predicandi dixit Math, ultimo generaliter: Euntes igitur docete omnes gentes, baptizantes eos; quando igitur conceditur principale, conceditur accessorium sine quo ipsum non posset proficere.

"Teach, counsel, chastise, comfort, forgive, bear, pray";[^1] for (as has been said) "as many as received" Christ by formed faith, "he gave them power" so "to be made the sons of God" that it is lawful for them to direct themselves and their brothers in the way of their father Christ, even by correcting them, as is clear in Matthew 18.[^2] Hence, corresponding to spiritual [power]{g:potestas}, secular power is twofold, namely civil and common. Civil authoritative [power] belongs only to the civil [lord]{g:dominus}, but civil vicarious [power] befits bailiffs or others acting in place of the secular lords. And common secular power is that by which anyone can direct himself and his own according to the goods of nature and fortune. And so, just as a man cannot be without soul and body, nor an adopted son of God without the goods of nature and of grace, so it does not hold that one is a [wayfarer]{g:viator} unless he has both secular and spiritual common power, although in infants and fools it is bound. And spiritual power is the more perfect in both cases, and priestly spiritual power indeed adds over and above the common spiritual [power]. But that power over their temporal goods is [assigned]{g:limitare} to clerics, the power of seculars being shut out, seems to be the presumption and avarice of priests.[^3] And so there are any number of masters' divisions of powers, which the curiosity of the church has devised, as an archdeacon and a bishop-elect are said to have [power of jurisdiction]{g:potestas-iurisdiccionis}, even for absolving and excommunicating, although they do not have the [power of order]{g:potestas-ordinis} for these; and laymen are said to have such power in the contentious court by commissions of prelates. Against what has been said of the multiplicity of human power it is argued.[^4] For it seems that God cannot [13] ordain a more manifold means for an end that would be done equally perfectly by a shorter means, since every such ordinance would be in vain; now every end for which so manifold a power would be ordained would be done equally perfectly, if not more perfectly, by a single simple power, which is [free choice]{g:liberum-arbitrium}, as by any number of superadded powers; therefore it is superfluous to posit so many. The minor is proved from this, that every created power is for the work of the perfection of the universe according to the church or another part of it; now for every such work free choice suffices without accidental power, for in the state of innocence there would be only such power, just as after the state of innocence those who lived more religiously lacked power of this kind. What (I ask) is man sufficient to ordain or do that he is not sufficient for out of pure free choice? For it seems that a [cloaked]{g:palliare} superadded power rather disturbs the church. Again, the same power in number can at one time be [loosed]{g:laxare} and at another restricted[^5], as is clear of the power of order, equal in every priest, although in prelates it takes on manifold degrees in loosing and restriction; therefore, by the same token, natural power can at one time be loosed and at another restricted as much as is needed for the rule of the church; therefore, since in such loosing no new power is acquired, nor is one lost in restriction, [it follows]{g:sequitur} that it is not necessary to superadd a new power of this kind. The minor is clear, both because power is not composed of its intensive parts, and also because otherwise it would not be the same power restricted or loosed, but either the old one wholly lost or a new one begotten. And this moves more evidently [to the view] that it is not necessary to posit in a priest a specific distinction between [14] power of order and of jurisdiction, but he who gives the power of order gives as such the power of jurisdiction, since otherwise the bare power of order would be too superfluous, being insufficient to pass into its matter however much it were exercised.[^6] Now Christ, when he gave the disciples the power of preaching, said generally, Matthew, the last chapter: "Going therefore, teach ye all nations: baptizing them";[^7] when, therefore, the principal is granted, the accessory is granted, without which it could not profit.

## ¶2 · 14.11–14.22

> [p. 14] Item, nulla pura creatura cooperatur cum Deo in producendo quantitatem mentis indivisibilem omnis huiusmodi potestas est qualitas mentis indivisibilis, ergo nulla pura creatura cooperatur cum Deo in producendo huiusmodi potestatem, et per consequens preter potestatem quam Deus creat in anima non est dare aliam datam ab homine. Assumptum patet ex hoc quod nullum materiale agit in immateriale, ut dicit Augustinus XII Sup. Gen. ad litteram: Omnis potentatus est substancia materialis, igitur non imprimit in animam spiritualem huiusmodi potestatem.

Again, no pure creature cooperates with God in producing an indivisible quantity of the mind; every such power is an indivisible quality of the mind; therefore no pure creature cooperates with God in producing such power, and consequently, besides the power which God creates in the soul, there is no other given by man.[^8] The [assumption]{g:assumptum} is clear from this, that nothing material acts on the immaterial, as Augustine says, *On Genesis according to the Letter* XII: "every potentate is a material substance";[^9] therefore he does not imprint such power on the spiritual soul.

## ¶3 · 14.23–14.29

> [p. 14] Similiter, nulla pure creatura cooperatur cum Deo nisi ut eius organum, sed Deus non requirit creaturam ut organum in produccione subdita huius indivisibilis qualitatis pocius quam in produccione gracie, cum hoc superflueret, igitur creatura non comproducit cum Deo huiusmodi qualitatem.

Likewise, no pure creature cooperates with God except as his instrument; now God does not require a creature as instrument in the sudden production of this indivisible quality any more than in the production of grace, since this would be superfluous; therefore a creature does not co-produce such a quality with God.[^10]

## ¶4 · 14.30–16.21

> [p. 14] Similiter, posito quod dominus papa committat potestatem per bullas in Angliam Petro archiepiscopo ad absolvendum Paulum criminosum in [p. 15] casibus curie reservatis, et pono quod dominus papa ante adventum bullarum in Angliam moriatur, tunc dominus papa nec pro instanti propositi sui conferendi Petro potestatem huiusmodi imprimit in Petro subito huiusmodi potestatem nec pro aliquo posteriori signando. Et idem iudicium de qualibet creatura, que daret alteri potestatem, igitur non est aliqua potestas huiusmodi nisi ficta; non enim potest Christi vicarius ad votum imprimere tam distanter qualitatem in animam, quia tunc foret superfluum auctorizare potestatem huiusmodi per bullas, et continue post foret accio pape impertinens, sicut foret ipso mortuo pro instanti noticie bullarum. Et per idem sequitur, quod non subiacet potestati sue revocare potestatem a Deo datam nec laxare potestatem prius ligatam, sicut non subest potestati sue quod quandocumque pretendit se dare potestatem huiusmodi quod Deus concurrat prebens principaliter potestatem. Et aliter certum est quod non datur potestas talis ab homine; sicut igitur non datur potestas inhabili vel invito, sic nec potentatus dat potestatem alteri si pretendit, aliter enim possent homines in se potestatum commercia experiri, et generalis volicio qua omnis papa vult quod omnes sacerdotes Christi habiles et solum tales habeant potestatem causaret longe aliter quam reputat ecclesia potestatem. In ista materia videtur mihi quod potestas dicit nunc absolute potenciam ad regulandum, et nunc dicit aggregative potenciam talem cum notificacione aut promulgacione autentica, et cum [p. 16] sensus sint eque noti, patet quod non repugnat non esse potestatem nisi a Deo; et tamen aliud a Deo dare potestatem, hoc est promulgacionem autenticam in facie ecclesie, quod persona creata habeat a Deo huiusmodi potestatem; tale quidem aggregatum secundum partem datur ab homine, sed nonnisi Deus principaliter auctorizet, ut patet inferius. Ad primum conceditur consequencia cum assumpto, sed negatur minor, cum post lapsum necesse est habere potestatem secundum timorem servilem promulgatam ab homine ad cohercendum rebelles sicut non oportuit in statu innocencie nec in regimine pie obediencium, quia lex talis propter transgressores posita est, quam non timebit humilis Christianus. Et conceditur quod potentatus male accipiunt occasionem ad perturbandum ecclesiam ex ficticia potestatis; oportet tamen Deum dare potestates spirituales ad ministrandum sacramenta, ad cohercendum seculariter rebelles ecclesie, preter liberum arbitrium, ut patet ex scriptura multiplici.

Likewise, supposing that the lord [pope]{g:papa} commits power by bulls into England to Peter the archbishop, to absolve Paul, a criminal, in [15] cases reserved to the curia, and I suppose that the lord pope dies before the bulls arrive in England,[^11] then the lord pope neither at the instant of his purpose to confer such power on Peter suddenly imprints such power in Peter, nor at any later [instant] that can be designated. And the same judgment [holds] of any creature that would give power to another; therefore there is no such power except a feigned one. For the [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius-christi} cannot at will imprint a quality in the soul at so great a distance, since then it would be superfluous to [authorize]{g:autorizare} such power by bulls, and continuously afterwards the pope's action would be irrelevant, as it would be, he being dead, at the instant the bulls became known. And by the same token it follows that it is not subject to his power to revoke power given by God, nor to loose power previously bound, just as it is not subject to his power that, whenever he claims to give such power, God concurs, principally providing the power. And otherwise it is certain that such power is not given by man; as, therefore, power is not given to the [unqualified]{g:inhabilis} or the unwilling, so neither does a potentate give power to another if he claims to; for otherwise men could try out a [trade]{g:commercium} in powers among themselves, and the general volition by which every pope wills that all the [qualified]{g:habilis} priests of Christ, and only such, have power would cause power far otherwise than the church reckons. In this matter it seems to me that power at one time means absolutely a [potency]{g:potencia} for ruling, and at another means, [aggregatively]{g:aggregative}, such a potency together with [authentic]{g:autenticus} notification or promulgation;[^12] and since [16] the senses are equally known, it is clear that it is not repugnant that there is no power except from God, and yet that something other than God gives power, that is, authentic promulgation in the face of the church that a created person has such power from God; such an aggregate is indeed given in part by man, but only if God principally authorizes it, as is clear below. To the first, the [inference]{g:consequencia} is granted with the assumption, but the minor is denied, since after the fall it is necessary to have power promulgated by man according to servile fear for [coercing]{g:cohercere} rebels, as it was not needed in the state of innocence nor in the rule of the piously obedient, since such a law is set because of transgressors, which the humble Christian will not fear.[^13] And it is granted that potentates wrongly take occasion to disturb the church from the [fiction]{g:ficticia} of power; yet God must give spiritual powers for ministering the sacraments, for coercing the rebels of the church in secular fashion, besides free choice, as is clear from manifold scripture.

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** The verse names the seven spiritual works of mercy, the mnemonic line *Consule, castiga, solare, remitte, fer, ora* with *doce* (LEDGER P01d-1; the line recurs in Hus, *De ecclesia* ch. 10). It completes the sentence left open at 11.32.

[^2]: **S/C.** John 1:12 (Loserth 12.1), DR "as many as received him, he gave them power to be made the sons of God"; the quoted words follow Loserth's italics, and Wyclif adds *Christum fide formata*, "by formed faith" (faith formed by charity). *corripiendo*: Matthew 18:15, "rebuke him between thee and him alone". Hus repeats 12.1–20 (Loserth: "Hussii Opp." 213b) (LEDGER P01d-2, editor).

[^3]: **C.** *limitatur*: "is assigned" (GLOSSARY *limitare*); *seclusa potestate secularium*: the seculars' power being shut out. Wyclif condemns the clergy's claim to sole power over their temporal goods; Loserth's side-note (12.22) reads it of jurisdiction. *sed et*: "and indeed", an addition, not a contrast.

[^4]: **R.** B marks the objections in its margin: *Obieccio* at 12.33, then 2 (13.20), 3 (14.11), 4 (14.23), 5 (14.30); the answers begin at 16.8 (AB *ad 1*) and run into the next part. "It is argued" renders *arguitur*.

[^5]: **T.** *nun* (p. 13): so the print, read on the plate; a misprint for *nunc* (*nunc … nunc*), our emendation.

[^6]: **C.** *quia insufficiens ut sit in materiam quantumcunque exercita*: construed "being insufficient to pass into its matter (to take effect on anything), however much exercised", with *exercita* agreeing with *potestas*. The phrase is compressed and may have lost a word.

[^7]: **S.** Matthew 28:19 (*Math. ultimo*, "the last chapter"), quoted to the end of Loserth's italics.

[^8]: **T.** 14.12–15: the words from *quantitatem* to *producendo* are wanting in A and B; in A another hand adds them in the margin (Loserth). Within them *quantitatem*, "quantity", stands where the minor premiss and 14.24 have *qualitas*, "quality", which the argument needs; translated as printed. The minor premiss has no connective. B in the margin (14.11): 3.

[^9]: **S/C.** Augustine, *De Genesi ad litteram* XII XII.16.33 (PL 34, 467: *nec sane putandum est facere aliquid corpus in spiritu … praestantior est qui facit, ea re de qua aliquid facit*; Loserth's page is lost in the scan) (LEDGER P01d-3). Loserth italicises *Omnis potentatus est substancia materialis*, but the sentence may be Wyclif's minor premiss rather than Augustine's words. *potentatus* here, and at 15.13, 16.17 and 20.28, is the holder of power, "potentate", not the abstract "sovereignty".

[^10]: **T.** *in produccione subdita* (14.25): so the print; A reads *subita*, "sudden", which gives the sense (cf. *subito*, 15.4) and is translated. B in the margin (14.23): 4.

[^11]: **T.** 15.2–3: the words from *ante* to *papa* are wanting in A and B; in A another hand adds them in the margin (Loserth). Translated as printed. B in the margin (14.30): 5.

[^12]: **C/R.** *potenciam ad regulandum*: "potency", the bare capacity Wyclif sets against the aggregate (capacity together with authentic promulgation); *potestas* and *potencia* name one power here. AB in the margin (15.30): *Potestas*; B: *Responsio ad obiectum*. Hus repeats 15.30–16.7 (Loserth: "Hussii Opp." 213b).

[^13]: **S.** *lex talis propter transgressores posita est*: an echo of Galatians 3:19, "it was set because of transgressions" (Vulgate *propter transgressiones posita est*); Wyclif has *transgressores*. AB in the margin (16.8): *ad 1*.
