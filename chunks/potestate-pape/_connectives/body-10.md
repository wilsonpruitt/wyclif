### 10-k ¶1 · 257.33–258.18  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 257] Notandum est tercio quod iste doctor videtur prudenter innuere quod nullus episcopus cesareus [p. 258] assumeret illud papale officium, quia obligat se ut sic quod sit in terris proximus sequax Christi, quod forte statui quem accipit est incompossibile. Ideo dicit Crysostomus Omelia XXVIII Operis Imperfecti: Quicumque, inquit, Domino humilitati studente iactancie studet, non est imago Christi, et qui Domino paupertatem amante diviciarum amator est in hoc seculo, repellit a se Christi similitudinem nec est vere discipulus qui non imitatur magistrum. Studeat igitur Romanus pontifex, si imitatur simillime vias Christi. Ideo dicit Epistola XLIII: Scio, inquit, domini pape et sancte sedis hanc esse potestatem, ut de omnibus beneficiis ecclesiasticis posset libere ordinare, scio quoque quod quisquis abutitur hac potestate edificat ad gehennam. Ideo (ut videtur multis) descripcio quam ponit de papa non convenit apostolis, **sed** solummodo pape summo.

ENGLISH: It is to be noted, thirdly, that this doctor seems prudently to hint that no caesarean [bishop]{g:episcopus} [258] would take up that papal office, because he binds himself, as such, to be on earth the nearest follower of Christ, which is perhaps [incompossible]{g:incompossibilis} with the [state]{g:status} he takes on.[^1] Therefore Chrysostom says in Homily 28 of the *Opus imperfectum*: "Whoever," he says, "is zealous for boasting while the Lord was zealous for humility is not the image of Christ; and he who is a lover of riches in this world while the Lord loved poverty repels from himself the likeness of Christ; nor is he truly a disciple who does not imitate the master."[^2] Let the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex}, therefore, be zealous [to see] whether he imitates the ways of Christ most closely. Therefore he says in Letter 43: "I know," he says, "that this is the [power]{g:potestas} of the lord [pope]{g:papa} and of the holy see, that he could freely dispose of all ecclesiastical benefices; I know also that whoever abuses this power builds toward Gehenna."[^3] Therefore (as it seems to many) the [definition]{g:descripcio} that he gives of the pope does not fit the apostles, but only the highest pope.[^4]

### 10-k ¶2 · 258.19–259.8  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 258] Unde propter talia verba quidam ad tantum insaniunt, quod dicunt neminem posse salvari nisi credat istum articulum fidei, quod quilibet papa sit per se causa tocius profectus spiritualis ecclesie. Unde Lyncolniensis Epistola XXXV: Ex generali debito subieccionis, quo non solum populus christianus **sed** totum humanum genus obligatur et sine cuius solucione nullus salutem consequitur, debitor sum sanctissime paternitati vestre excellentissimeque dominacioni plenitudinis, obediencie et reverencie, honoris et timoris. Et forte ex talibus verbis declamatoriis fuit iste doctor punitus a Domino per eum quem verbis floridis [p. 259] sic palpavit. Si enim iste sit tam necessarius articulus fidei, cum apostoli et omnes christiani ante dotacionem non crediderunt istum articulum, videtur quod absit quod eorum quilibet sit dampnandus. **Sed** dicti beati possunt de sic errantibus dicere irridendo: Novi recentesque articuli fidei per constitucionem imperatoris venerunt quos non coluerunt patres eorum.

ENGLISH: Hence, because of such words, some go so far in madness that they say that no one can be saved unless he believes this [article of faith]{g:articulus-fidei}, that every pope is of himself the cause of all the spiritual profit of the [church]{g:ecclesia}.[^5] Hence Lincoln, Letter 35: "By the general debt of subjection, by which not only the Christian people but the whole human race is bound, and without the payment of which no one attains salvation, I am a debtor to your most holy paternity and most excellent [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio} of fullness of obedience and reverence, of honour and fear."[^6] And perhaps for such declamatory words this doctor was punished by the Lord through him whom he [259] so flattered with flowery words.[^7] For if this is so necessary an article of faith, since the apostles and all Christians before the [endowment]{g:dotacio} did not believe this article, it seems (God forbid) that every one of them is to be damned. But the said blessed ones can say mockingly of those who err so: "New and recent" articles of faith "have come" by the constitution of the emperor, "whom their fathers worshipped not".[^8]

### 10-k ¶3 · 259.9–259.23  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 259] Item (ut patet ex decretis et cronicis tactis VIII capitulo) licencia et auctoritate cesaris factum vel fictum est quod generaliter suus episcopus sit caput ecclesie. **Sed** ille imperator non habet plus potestatem condendi novos articulos fidei quam quilibet alius christianus; sic enim posset condere novam legem Christi et legem conditam abrogare; non enim licet vel angelo de celo onerare sanctam ecclesiam novis articulis vel ritibus extra scripturam sacram a Spiritu Sancto doctam, ut patet in materia De Cessacione Legalium. Magna (inquam) libertas et privilegium fuit facilitas in qua Christus posuit suam ecclesiam, ut patet Gal. II; et hoc privilegium debent cuncti christiani defendere.

ENGLISH: Again (as is clear from the decrees and chronicles touched on in chapter 8), it was by Caesar's [licence]{g:licencia} and [authority]{g:auctoritas} made, or feigned, that his bishop should generally be [head]{g:caput} of the church.[^9] Now that emperor has no more power of making new articles of faith than any other Christian; for so he could make a new law of Christ and abrogate the law already made; for it is not lawful even for an angel from heaven to burden holy church with new articles or rites outside holy scripture, taught by the Holy Spirit, as is clear in the matter *De cessatione legalium*.[^10] A great liberty and [privilege]{g:privilegium} (I say) was the lightness in which Christ set his church, as is clear in Gal. 2; and this privilege all Christians ought to defend.[^11]

### 10-k ¶4 · 259.24–259.32  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 259] Item, non est possibile quod fidei orthodoxe sit falsitas substrata; **sed** sepe contingit quod Romanus pontifex non est papa, ymo posito quod sit; non est tantum necessarium ad salutem cuiuslibet christiani hoc credere, quod sine hoc non posset salvari, nam papa mortuo vel perverso non ex hoc titubat fides in quolibet christiano; et illud testarentur conversi per apostolos nostram nimirum demenciam deridendo.

ENGLISH: Again, it is not possible that falsehood should underlie the orthodox faith; now it often happens that the Roman pontiff is not pope. Indeed, supposing that he is, it is not so necessary to the salvation of every Christian to believe this that without it he could not be saved; for when a pope is dead or perverted, the faith of any Christian does not totter on that account; and those converted by the apostles would testify to this, mocking, no doubt, our madness.[^12]

### 10-k ¶5 · 259.33–260.20  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 259] Assumptum patet ex hoc quod aliter Deus necessitaret hominem ad menciendum et per consequens ad peccandum, et unus alius ymo sic [p. 260] illusus pie crederet eius contradiccionem, sicut christianus credit implicite esse verum; et sic oportet utramque sectam Urbani et Roberti dicere quod reliqua sit heretica, quia articuli fidei non limitantur sic ad loca, ut in ista villa teneantur credi et in proxima villa discredi. Nec valent sophismata quibus putantes subtiliare referunt quod papa potest facere novos articulos fidei et per consequens facere antiquos cessare, ymo facere christianos teneri credere falsitati, quia si quelibet veritas sit articulus fidei, tunc sacramenta potest facere, novos articulos ymo musca; **sed** et illum articulum sollempnizatum, quod iste sit papa, potest nedum imperator facere vel electores, **sed** quodcumque animatum vel inanimatum fuerit ad hoc movens. **Sed** evidencius foret, quod quis rogando Deum et perficiens papale officium, faciat se ipsum et plus timeat quod venenum peccati quam quod venenum corporis corrumpat istum christiane fidei articulum.

ENGLISH: The [assumption]{g:assumptum} is clear from this, that otherwise God would necessitate a man to lie, and consequently to sin; and another man, indeed one so [260] deluded, would piously believe its contradictory, as a Christian implicitly believes it to be true; and so each sect, Urban's and Robert's, must say that the other is heretical, because articles of faith are not so limited to places that they are to be believed in this town and disbelieved in the next town.[^13] Nor do the [sophisms]{g:sophisma} avail by which those who think to refine report that the pope can make new articles of faith, and consequently make the old ones cease, indeed make Christians bound to believe a falsehood; for if every truth is an article of faith, then [one] can make sacraments, new articles, indeed a fly [can].[^14] And indeed that solemnized article, that this man is pope, not only the emperor or the electors can make, but whatever thing, animate or inanimate, has been moving to it. But it would be more evident that someone, by praying to God and carrying out the papal office, should make himself [pope], and should fear more that the poison of sin than that the poison of the body should corrupt this article of the Christian faith.[^15]

### 10-k ¶6 · 260.21–260.32  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 260] In ista materia dixi alias quomodo quedam est credulitas explicita vel distincta, et quedam est implicita vel confusa; et isto modo tenetur omnis christianus credere singulas veritates, quia tenetur credere Deum esse, quod est omnis veritas. Hoc **autem** omnis fidelis tenetur pro aliquo tempore vie distincte credere, et sic quicumque in Deum finaliter crediderit salvabitur, etsi non plus de grano synapis habuerit. Alios **autem** oportet plus et alios minus de fide distincte credere secundum quod plus vel minus de gracia ad hoc obligante habuerit.

ENGLISH: On this matter I have said elsewhere how one [credence]{g:credulitas} is explicit or distinct, and another implicit or [indistinct]{g:confusus}; and in this way every Christian is bound to believe each single truth, because he is bound to believe that God is, which is every truth.[^16] And this every believer is bound to believe distinctly for some time of [the way]{g:via}; and so whoever has finally believed in God will be saved, even if he has had no more than a grain of mustard seed.[^17] And some must believe more and others less of the faith distinctly, according as they have had more or less of the grace that binds them to it.

### 10-k ¶7 · 261.1–261.7  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 261] Unde quidam vocant solum illas veritates catholicas quas oportet viatorem distincte credere articulos fidei, cuiusmodi sunt veritates expresse in symbolo; alii **autem** quamlibet veritatem catholicam articulum fidei, quia partem parvulam totalis fidei. Et illa concedit conclusiones sequentes, que equivocantibus videntur mirabiles.

ENGLISH: Hence some call articles of faith only those catholic truths which the [wayfarer]{g:viator} must believe distinctly, such as are the truths expressed in the [creed]{g:symbolum}; but others [call] any catholic truth an article of faith, because [it is] a small part of the whole faith.[^18] And that [usage] grants the following conclusions, which seem marvellous to those who equivocate.

### 10-l ¶1 · 261.8–263.5  (6 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 261] **Sed** quomodocumque loquatur, fideli nichil insanius quam credere quod omnis fidelis debet credere quod omnis episcopus Romanus sit papa vel quod omnis subditus tenetur facere quicquid preceperit aut quod auctoritate et declaracione illius tenetur omnis christianus credere cuicumque scripture quam ipse limitat; nam mille sunt fideles, qui habent immediate a Deo fidem scripture independenter ab illo, eciam plus quam ipse; verumtamen cum multi pape sunt gloriosi post dotacionem ecclesie, quilibet christianus tenetur saltem confuse credere quod multi Romani episcopi sunt veri pape, et illi qui non fecerunt exaccionem talis fidei Antichristi; **sed** hoc non est generaliter ad laudem Romani pontificis, quia sic tenentur credere quod multi Romani pontifices non sunt pape, quia multi sunt dampnati, non membra sancte ecclesie, et sic de omni veritate tenetur omnis christianus credere quia est, etsi non sonat in pompam pape, quod omnis christianus tenetur ipsum credere esse papam, sicut non sonat in pompam cuiuscumque predestinati quod omnis homo tenetur credere ipsum esse salvandum, sicut omnem prescitum tenetur credere esse dampnandum, cum oportet Deum distincte illud cognoscere; ymo est michi evidens quod tam subtilis logicus atque theologus, sicut fuit Robertus Lyncolniensis [p. 262] non induceret hunc errorem fidei ad onus ecclesie, quod quemlibet Romanum pontificem tenetur credere esse papam, vel quod alius non habens ad hoc revelacionem de se ipso vel quocumque alio conversante secum tenetur expresse credere quod sit predestinatus vel apud Deum acceptus; de se ipso tamen tenetur hoc sperare et de aliis si moveat evidencia operis reputare. Nam ante dotacionem fuit privilegium et libertas ecclesie quod non tenebatur sic credere. Ideo non obstante dotacione debent ecclesie filii defendere pristinam libertatem; et sic intelliguntur multa dicta Roberti Grostest, que dicit condicionaliter aut reputative, quia aliter contradiceret sibi ipsi. Unde Epistola XXXVII. ut recitavi in fine secundi cap. libri V, dicit Lynconiensis quod iniustarum possessionum quas occupat ecclesia abscisio non est ecclesiarum imminucio, **sed** pocius earum pulcrificacio vel plurificacio. Et sic secundum eum et alios sanctos, qui iniuste occupat locum papalem, non sequendo Christum in moribus, non est papa vel clericus, **sed** unus dyabolicus antipapa. Ex istis patet consideranti quantum malum et onus irreligiosum ecclesie induxit dotacio; ad tantum enim insaniunt pape cesarei, quod tenent cum suis satrapis quod papa non debet corripi ab aliquo viatore. Quod probant tripliciter: primo per hoc gentile principium: par in parem non habet imperium, cum igitur papa excedit omnem personam viantem, videtur quod non interest alicuius viantis papam corripere. **Sed** quantum ad illud patet, quod tam deficit [p. 263] in forma quam in materia; in forma, quia necesse est antecedens esse verum, cum necessario pater in Spiritum Sanctum non habet imperium **sed** missionem et tamen necessario sunt pares.

ENGLISH: But however one speaks, to a believer nothing is more insane than to believe that every believer ought to believe that every [Roman bishop]{g:romanus-episcopus} is [pope]{g:papa}, or that every subject is bound to do whatever he has commanded, or that on his [authority]{g:auctoritas} and declaration every Christian is bound to believe whatever scripture he [assigns]{g:limitare};[^1] for there are a thousand believers who have the faith of scripture immediately from God, independently of him, even more than he [has]. Nevertheless, since many popes after the [endowment]{g:dotacio} of the [church]{g:ecclesia} are glorious, every Christian is bound at least [indistinctly]{g:confuse} to believe that many Roman bishops are true popes, and [that] those are who did not make an exaction of such a faith, [the faith] of Antichrist;[^2] but this is not in general to the praise of the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex}, because in the same way they are bound to believe that many Roman pontiffs are not popes, because many are damned, not [members]{g:membrum} of holy church. And so every Christian is bound to believe of every truth that it is [true], although it does not sound to the pope's [boasting]{g:pompa} that every Christian is bound to believe him to be pope, just as it does not sound to the boasting of any [predestinate]{g:predestinatus} [man] that every man is bound to believe him to be saved, as [every man] is bound to believe every [foreknown]{g:prescitus} [man] to be damned, since God must know it distinctly.[^3] Indeed it is evident to me that so subtle a logician and theologian as Robert of Lincoln was [262] would not bring in this error of faith, to the burden of the church, that [a man] is bound to believe every Roman pontiff to be pope, or that another, not having a revelation to that effect about himself or about any other who lives with him, is bound to believe expressly that he is predestinate or accepted with God;[^4] yet of himself he is bound to hope this, and of others, if the [evidence]{g:evidencia} of works moves [him], to esteem it. For before the endowment it was the [privilege]{g:privilegium} and liberty of the church that it was not bound to believe so. Therefore, notwithstanding the endowment, the sons of the church ought to defend their former liberty; and so are to be understood many sayings of Robert Grosseteste, which he says conditionally or [by estimation]{g:reputative}, because otherwise he would contradict himself. Hence in Letter 37, as I recited at the end of the second chapter of book 5, Lincoln says that "the cutting off of the unjust possessions which the church occupies is not a diminishing of the churches, but rather their beautifying" or "multiplying".[^5] And so, according to him and other saints, he who unjustly occupies the papal place, not following Christ in his morals, is not pope or cleric, but a devilish antipope.[^6] From these things it is clear to one who considers how great an evil and irreligious burden the endowment brought upon the church; for the caesarean popes go so far in madness that they hold, with their [satraps]{g:satrapa}, that the pope ought not to be reproved by any [wayfarer]{g:viator}.[^7] This they prove in three ways: first, by this pagan principle, "an equal has no command over an equal"; since, therefore, the pope exceeds every person [on the road]{g:viantes}, it seems that it is not the business of anyone on the road to reprove the pope.[^8] But as to that, it is clear that it fails as much [263] in form as in matter: in form, because the antecedent is necessarily true, since necessarily the Father has no command over the Holy Spirit but a sending, and yet necessarily they are equals.[^9]

### 10-l ¶3 · 263.11–263.16  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 263] Et iterum quoddam est imperium vicarium, cuiusmodi est omne humanum imperium, quomodo loquitur Apostolus prima Thym. VI, 17: Divitibus huius seculi precipe non sublime sapere. Quoddam **autem** imperium est per se vel originaliter autenticum super quamlibet creaturam.

ENGLISH: And again, one command is vicarial, of which kind is every human command, in the way the Apostle speaks in 1 Tim. 6:17: "Charge the rich of this world not to be highminded." But another command is of itself, or originally, [authentic]{g:autenticus} over every creature.[^10]

### 10-l ¶4 · 263.17–264.11  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 263] Et patet quam nuda sit ista argucia quoad materiam et formam. Intelligit enim per hoc principium quod par non habet imperium autoritativum in suum parem, sub racione qua par est taliter imperanti. **Sed** quid obest huic quod excellencior virtute corripiat inferiorem in moribus, licet sit superior quoad statum. Aliter enim periret lex Christi, que ordinat quemcumque christianum prelatum peccabilem, et cum peccaverit, ab alio corrigendum, ut patet Matth. XVIII, in tantum quod si per impossibile Christus peccaverit, foret ab ecclesia corrigendus; cum enim sit frater noster Hebre. II. 12 et lex sua dicit universaliter: si peccaverit in te frater tuus, corripe eum, patet quod omnis viator peccando debet corripi a confratre. Aliter enim deficeret lex [p. 264] Christi ordinando remedium contra maculam sponse sue, cui Paulus contradicit Gal. II. cum pro levi culpa in facie restitit Petro pape; ideo nichil infidelius quam negare quod statu superior et inferior quoad mores potest corripi a subiecto. Ideo secundo arguitur per hoc quod papa gerit in terris vicem domini Ihesu Christi, **sed** ipsum non licuit alicui corripere, ut patet Matth. XVI, 23 ubi propter correpcionem Christi Petrus vocatur sathanas, igitur nec eius vicem gerentem licet corripere.

ENGLISH: And it is clear how bare this [quibble]{g:argucia} is as to matter and form. For by this principle he understands that an equal has no [authoritative]{g:auctoritativus} command over his equal, in the respect in which he is equal to the one so commanding.[^11] But what stands in the way of this, that one more excellent in [virtue]{g:virtus} should reprove one inferior in morals, although he is superior as to [state]{g:status}? For otherwise the law of Christ would perish, which ordains that any Christian [prelate]{g:prelatus}, being able to sin, and when he has sinned, is to be corrected by another, as is clear in Matt. 18:15; so much so that if, per impossibile, Christ had sinned, he would have to be corrected by the church.[^12] For since he is "our brother" (Heb. 2:12), and his law says universally: "If thy brother shall offend against thee, rebuke him," it is clear that every wayfarer, by sinning, ought to be reproved by a fellow brother.[^13] For otherwise the law [264] of Christ would fail in ordaining a remedy against the stain of his [bride]{g:sponsa}; which Paul contradicts in Gal. 2, when for a light fault he "withstood" Peter the pope "to the face";[^14] therefore nothing is more faithless than to deny that one superior in state and inferior as to morals can be reproved by a subject. Therefore, secondly, [it is argued by appeal to]{g:arguitur-per} this, that the pope bears on earth the place of the Lord Jesus Christ; now it was not lawful for anyone to reprove him, as is clear in Matt. 16:23, where, for reproving Christ, Peter is called Satan; therefore neither is it lawful to reprove one who bears his place.[^15]

### 10-l ¶5 · 264.12–264.16  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 264] **Sed** ista argucia nimis claudicat, cum oporteret dicere quod omnis Christi vicarius sit impeccabilis sicut Christus: et sic vere concluditur quod nec papa nec alius debet corripi vel corrigi, in quantum sequitur caput Christum.

ENGLISH: But this quibble limps badly, since one would have to say that every [vicar of Christ]{g:vicarius-christi} is impeccable as Christ is; and so it is truly [inferred]{g:concludere} that neither the pope nor another ought to be reproved or corrected in so far as he follows Christ the [head]{g:caput}.[^16]

### 10-l ¶6 · 264.17–264.21  (3 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 264] **Sed** tercio arguitur ex lege XXI distinccione Nunc **autem** divina, ubi dicitur, quod nullus sanctorum episcoporum ausus est in Marcellinum papam inferre sentenciam, **sed** dixerunt: tuo ore iudica causam tuam, non nostro iudicio subiacebis.

ENGLISH: But, thirdly, it is argued from the law, Distinction 21, *Nunc autem divina*, where it is said that "none of the holy [bishops]{g:episcopus} dared to pass [sentence]{g:sentencia} on Pope Marcellinus, but they said: 'Judge your cause with your own mouth; you will not be subject to our judgement.'"[^17]

### 10-l ¶7 · 264.22–265.5  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 264] **Sed** constat quod illud verbum episcoporum non potest tollere legem Dei, qua in archiepiscopo Paulo corripuit papam Petrum, ut patet Gal. II. Ideo idem foret dicere quod non licet papam corripere et dicere quod est personaliter impeccabilis dominus Ihesus Christus vel ipseLucifer, qui per correpcionem fraternam non potest corrigi. Unde cum papa racione multiplici sit pronior et periculosior in peccando, oportet de correpcione et correccione sua prudencius precavere; et ad hoc ordinantur leges ecclesie, ut patet distinccione [p. 265] XL: Si papa, et distinccione LXXIX: Si quis pecunia; et aliter forent omnia facta in deposicione paparum iniusta et per consequens deponentes forent heretici, sicut et ecclesia approbans eius correpcionem et pape novi intronizacionem.

ENGLISH: But it is evident that that word of the bishops cannot [dispose of]{g:tollere} the law of God, by which, in the archbishop Paul, it reproved Peter the pope, as is clear in Gal. 2.[^18] Therefore it would be the same to say that it is not lawful to reprove the pope and to say that he is personally the impeccable Lord Jesus Christ, or Lucifer himself, who cannot be corrected by [fraternal correction]{g:correpcio-fraterna}. Hence, since the pope for many reasons is more prone and more dangerous in sinning, it is necessary to take precaution more prudently for his reproof and correction; and to this [end] the laws of the church are ordained, as is clear in Distinction [265] 40, *Si papa*, and Distinction 79, *Si quis pecunia*;[^19] and otherwise all the things done in the deposition of popes would be unjust, and consequently those deposing would be heretics, as also the church that approves his reproof and the enthronement of a new pope.

### 10-l ¶8 · 265.7–265.16  (1 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 265] Et quantum ad dictum episcoporum de correpcione Marcellini, dicitur quod valde superfluum foret eos ipsum in tali casu corripere, quia perceperunt ex eius contricione quod fuit plene correptus a Domino, Ideo nimis levis evidencia foret ex isto facto concludere quod nullus papa debet in aliquo casu a fratribus reprehendi, ut patet ex libro beati Bernhardi ad papam Eugenium. Mille **autem** sunt blasfemie cum nonnulla intronizacione pape cesarei introducte.

ENGLISH: And as to the saying of the bishops about the reproof of Marcellinus, [the answer is]{g:dicitur} that it would have been quite superfluous for them to reprove him in such a case, because they perceived from his contrition that he had been fully reproved by the Lord. Therefore it would be too light evidence to infer from this fact that no pope ought in any case to be rebuked by his brothers, as is clear from the book of blessed Bernard to Pope Eugenius.[^20] And there are a thousand blasphemies brought in with many an enthronement of a caesarean pope.[^21]

### 11-a ¶1 · 265.18–266.3  (2 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 265] Contra dicta arguitur per hoc quod Deus posuit quamlibet universitatem suam in mensura, numero et pondere, ut dicitur Sapiens XI, 31 et per consequens oportet esse ordinem in sacerdotali potestate sacramenta et sacramentalia ministrandi. Sic enim in lege veteri fuit unicus summus sacerdos, cui erant excellenciora officia limitata, in cuius signum Deus ordinavit unicam vestem sacerdotalem pro Aaron, ut patet Exod. XXVIII, 4; unde et consequenter dicitur: Vestem **autem** sanctam qua utitur Aaron habebunt filii eius post eum, ut ungantur in ea et consecrentur manus eorum. Summo **autem** sacerdoti appropriata sunt multa officia, ut patet Exod. XXIX, [p. 266] XXX et Levitici VIII, multo magis igitur in novo testamento, ubi debet esse in perfecciori ordine maior distinccio.

LATIN: [p. 273] Item, in novo testamento debet ecclesia magis dimittere figuras et tamquam etate proveccior evacuando que erant parvuli, plus simulari ecclesie triumphanti; **sed** in beatis cessat omnis talis figura atque exaccio, cum sint ad ultimum unicordes; igitur in ecclesia apostolica debent ultra legem veterem ad illum statum accedere. Et hec racio, quare apostoli docuerunt in facto, verbo et [p. 274] scripto, quod cessarunt huiusmodi legalia; nec inducerentur nova ad onus ecclesie, ut patet Act. XV, ymo apostoli fuissent nimis immemores future ecclesie pretermittendo exempla in facto, verbo et scripto, quomodo successores sui episcopi militarent. Unde pro ulteriori sensu notandum quod omnis summus sacerdos legis veteris figuravit Christum in omnibus factis suis legalibus. Ideo autonomastice et singulariter ipse dicitur summus sacerdos et episcopus animarum et hinc illa multitudo sacerdotum et suorum officiorum fuit in Christo unico consumata, ut pulchre docet Apostolus Heb. IX, et Heb. VII dicitur: plures facti sunt sacerdotes secundum legem etc. Et hec racio quare apostoli non vocarunt se papas sanctissimos, capita universalis ecclesie vel universales pontifices, **sed** habendo secum summum pontificem usque ad consumacionem seculi, vocarunt se servos Christi in tribulacione, socios et ministros ecclesie; unde ista religio servata fuit tunc tempore beati Gregorii, et ex decreto suo incorporata legi ecclesie distinccione XCIX Prime sedis et cap. Ecce. Quare igitur non magis periculoso tempore plus indigni sine divisione vel phariseica expectacione inniterentur unite pontifici. Revera videtur iuxta distinccionem predictam et veritatem facti paparum sequi quod vel sunt personaliter Ihesus noster, vel quod sunt infidelitate peiores sacerdotibus legis veteris figuraliter expectantibus Ihesum Christum, cum propinquissime preparant viam Antichristo. Non igitur sequitur hec dominativa precellencia ex lege veteri.

ENGLISH: Again, in the new testament the church ought rather to put away figures and, as one more advanced in age, putting away the things of a child, be made more like the [church triumphant]{g:ecclesia-triumphans};[^9] now in the blessed all such figure and exaction ceases, since at the last they are of one heart; therefore in the apostolic church they ought to come nearer that state, beyond the old law. And this is the reason why the apostles taught in deed, word and [274] writing that such legal precepts had ceased; and new ones were not to be brought in to burden the church, as is clear from Acts 15;[^10] indeed, the apostles would have been too unmindful of the future church had they left out examples, in deed, word and writing, of how their successors the bishops should serve as soldiers. Hence, for the further sense, it is to be noted that every high priest of the old law figured Christ in all his legal acts. Therefore [by antonomasia]{g:autonomatice} and singularly he is called the high priest and "bishop of souls"; and hence that multitude of priests and of their offices was consummated in the one Christ, as the Apostle finely teaches, Heb. 9; and in Heb. 7 it is said: "Many were made priests according to the law", etc.[^11] And this is the reason why the apostles did not call themselves most holy popes, heads of the universal church or universal pontiffs, but, having with them the highest pontiff "even to the consummation of the world", called themselves servants of Christ, partners in tribulation, and ministers of the church.[^12] Hence this observance was kept then, in the time of blessed Gregory, and by his decree incorporated into the law of the church, Distinction 99, *Prime sedis* and the chapter *Ecce*.[^13] Why then should not the more unworthy, in a more perilous time, rest in unity on the pontiff, without division or pharisaic expectation?[^14] In truth it seems to follow, according to the aforesaid distinction and the truth of the popes' deeds, that either they are personally our Jesus, or they are worse in [unbelief]{g:infidelitas} than the priests of the old law who awaited Jesus Christ in figure, since they prepare the way for Antichrist most nearly. This lordly pre-excellence, therefore, does not follow from the old law.[^15]

### 11-d ¶1 · 275.1–276.23  (4 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 275] Quantum ad secundum scribit dominus Ardmacanus in De Questionibus Armenorum libro XI in fine cap. V de questione Armenorum: Ex isto capite sequi michi videtur, quod conclusio ad potestatem ligandi et solvendi sive remittendi peccata sive retinendi, sive baptisandi aut consecrandi aut benedicendi, et breviter quoad quantumcumque potestatem actus sacramentalis expressam a Christo in evangelio, quiscumque sacerdos in ordinacione sua totam accipit aut nichil ipsius, quoniam singulis in simul et indivise sunt date a Christo, et Matth. XIV, 6 ac Mar. X, 9 mandat ipse, quod Deus coniunxit, homo non separet. Unde videtur racionabiliter posse concludi, quod hec potestates sacramentales in omnibus sacerdotibus sunt equales. Hec doctor. **Sed** notandum quod crescente multitudine presbiterorum in sciencia et potencia variata, potest racionabiliter limitari sacerdotibus habilioribus singulariter officium, attestans in eis preeminenciam dignitatis, salva tamen potestate sacerdotibus inferioribus tempore necessitatis vel indisposicionis superioris presbiteri sacramenta ista et sacramentalia ministrandi; nam in lege veteri, ubi magis attenderant ad tales ritus sensibiles, propter indisposicionem summi pontificis non solum licuit sacerdoti inferiori, **sed** debuit supplere ministerium summi pontificis: aliter enim quando summus sacerdos foret infirmus vel sompno nocturno vel alia causa pollutus, sequeretur inconveniens legis Dei; aliquando enim polluti sunt ad vesperum, ut dicitur Levitici XV, 5 et Deuteronomio XXIII, et aliquando usque ad septimum diem, ut dicitur Ezech. XLV. Cum igitur debuerunt cottidie offerre super altare incensi [p. 276] vespere et mane, ut patet Exod. XXX, patet, quod inferior sacerdos potestatem habuit defectus summi pontificis exsupplere. Quare igitur non in lege nova, ubi ritus talis minus attenditur? Hoc enim officium pertinebat singulariter cum paribus ad summum pontificem, ut patet Exod. XXX et XXXIX et Levitici VIII, oportet tamen tam ex parte superiorum quam inferiorum quam eciam tocius populi cavere pericula. Ex parte superiorum, ne exinde superbiant, putantes hoc eis contingere pure ex institucione divina, secundo ne ad illum statum sumantur inhabiles, et tercio omnino ne ex cupiditate eorum tale ministerium sit venale. Sacerdotem **autem** inferiorem oportet cavere ab istis et omnino, ne ex presumpcione temeraria det scandalum fratri suo. Et omnem fidelem oportet attendere ne talibus nimis ponderante precio innitatur. Aliqui enim plus appreciantur hos ritus bene **sed** humanitus adinventos quam evangelium vel mandata decalogi. Periculosum est, inquam, iudaice hos ritus preternecessarios nimis attendere; Deus enim supplet defectus eorum ex aliis operibus virtuosis.

ENGLISH: As to the second, [the lord of Armagh]{g:ardmacanus} writes in *On the Questions of the Armenians*, book 11, at the end of chapter 5, on the question of the Armenians: "From this chapter it seems to me to follow, as a conclusion, that as regards the [power]{g:potestas} of binding and loosing, or of remitting sins or retaining them, or of baptizing or consecrating or blessing, and in short as regards any power whatever of a sacramental act expressed by Christ in the gospel, every priest in his [ordination]{g:ordinacio} receives the whole of it or nothing of it, since they were given by Christ to each together and undividedly; and in Matt. 19:6 and Mark 10:9 he commands: What God hath joined together, let no man put asunder. Hence it seems that it can reasonably [be inferred]{g:concludere} that these sacramental powers are equal in all priests." So the doctor.[^1] But it is to be noted that, as the multitude of presbyters grew, varied in knowledge and [power]{g:potencia}, an office may reasonably [be assigned]{g:limitare} singularly to the more [qualified]{g:habilis} priests, attesting in them a [preeminence]{g:preeminencia} of dignity; saving, however, to the lower priests the power of ministering these sacraments and [sacramentals]{g:sacramentalia} in time of need or of the [unfitness]{g:indisposicio} of the superior presbyter.[^2] For in the old law, where they attended more to such [perceptible]{g:sensibilis} rites, because of the unfitness of the [high priest]{g:summi-pontifices} it was not only lawful for a lower priest, but he was bound, to supply the ministry of the high priest: for otherwise, when the [high priest]{g:summus-sacerdos} was sick, or polluted by sleep at night or by another cause, an [ill consequence]{g:inconveniens} for God's law would follow;[^3] for sometimes they are polluted "until the evening", as is said in Leviticus 15:5 and Deuteronomy 23, and sometimes until the seventh day, as is said in Ezek. 45.[^4] Since therefore they had to offer daily upon the altar of incense [276] evening and morning, as is clear from Exod. 30, it is clear that the lower priest had power to make up the defects of the high priest. Why then not in the new law, where such a rite is less attended to? For this office belonged singularly, with its like, to the high priest, as is clear from Exod. 30 and 39 and Lev. 8;[^5] yet dangers must be guarded against, on the part of the superiors as well as of the inferiors, and also of the whole people. On the part of the superiors: lest they grow proud from it, thinking that this befalls them purely by divine institution; second, lest the [unqualified]{g:inhabilis} be taken up into that [state]{g:status}; and third, above all, lest through their covetousness such a ministry be for sale. And the lower priest must beware of these things, and above all lest from rash presumption he give scandal to his brother. And every believer must take heed lest he rest on such things, setting too heavy a price on them.[^6] For some prize these [rites]{g:ritus}, well but humanly devised, more than the gospel or the commandments of the decalogue. It is dangerous, I say, to attend too much, in Jewish fashion, to these rites, which are beyond necessity; for God supplies their defects from other virtuous works.[^7]

### 11-d ¶3 · 277.11–279.15  (8 sed/autem)

LATIN: [p. 277] Quantum ad istud, patet ex dictis quomodo licite possunt fieri et licite pretermitti. Ideo requiritur magna prudencia ad acceptandum ista in suo ordine. Quidam enim putant sine istorum sacramentorum et sacerdotum ordine non posse recte viare ecclesiam, unde tantum ponderant has cerimonias, quod offendentem plus puniunt quam qui infideliter in caritate deficiat. Conceditur igitur cum beato Dyonisio quod sunt tales tres ordines sacerdotum, cum iuxta decretum Crysostomi positum distinccione XL Multi sunt sacerdotes et pauci sunt sacerdotes. **Sed** non oportet, quod sacerdos ex gracia Dei factus superior accipiat exinde novum ecclesiasticum sacramentum, dum ego omnino omitterem habere episcopalem consecracionem, antequam accipere presumerem hoc episcopale ministerium; est enim preternecessarium et predicacione, oracione ac multis aliis cultibus Dei inferius, nec non ad multos cultus Dei non tam necesse sicut creditur requisitum. Conceditur ergo, quod benediccio, consecracio et alia ministracio episcopalis est laudabilis rite facta. **Sed** grave est quod ecclesia [p. 278] oneretur tam multiplici cerimonia, gravius quod tantam vim ponat in illa, et gravissimum quod nemo potest salvari a Deo, ut dicunt, nisi credat et accipiat secundum formam iam currentem huiusmodi sacramenta. De istis **autem** in tractatu De Sacramentis est sermo lacior. Et sic conceditur quod vestes sacerdotales et ornamenta episcopalia possunt introduci pie et religiose, licet mendaciter et impie communius sint accepte, ut tractantes ritus humanos et statuta hominum, notant quinque vestimenta sacerdotalia, quibus communiter induitur celebrando, et variantur in parte ab ornamentis sacerdotum veteris testamenti, ut declarat Hugo libro secundo De Sacramentis parte IV. Singularis, inquit, habitus sacerdotibus Christi in ministerio traditur, ut per id quod foris apparet quales intus esse debeant ostendatur, sacrarum **autem** vestium ritus partim a veteri testamento assumptus est et partim a patribus novi testamenti adiectus. Legitur enim Exod. XXVIII, XXIX, XXX et XXXIX quomodo Moyses indueret Aaron fratrem suum, primo tunica bissina, que grece poderis id est talaris dicitur, que a collo usque ad talos extenditur, que in novo sacerdocio non solum de bisso **sed** de lino contexitur; ipsa **autem** modificata membris corporis et aptata nichil superfluum aut dissolutum in vita sacerdotis debere esse ostendit. **Sed** in novo testamento laxatur post dotacionem ecclesie, cum multa temporalia preter necessaria officio sacerdotis sibi monstruoso adiaceant. Hec **autem** vestis ob spem candoris nomen Albe sortitur, que signat spiritualem mundiciam ministrorum, iuxta [p. 279] illud Apok XIX, 8: Datum est sponse ut operiat se bissino splendenti candido. Et sequitur exposicio: Bissinum enim iustificaciones sunt sanctorum. Caro quidem hominis mundiciam quam ex natura non habet, studio bono aquirit per graciam, sicut bissus vel linum candorem suum, quem natura non habuit, per studium et industriam artificis aquisivit, dum multis tunsionibus ac locionibus illustracione solis siccantis quasi quadam violencia attritum vertitur in alborem. Oportet quidem crebro castigare corpus et in servitutem redigere, sicut dicit Apostolus prima Cor. IX se fecisse; et sic positus in aqua tribulacionis et tunsus tortoribus ac interim sole iusticie consolatus a tribulacionibus siccatus et calore veritatis accensus.

ENGLISH: As to this, it is clear from what has been said how they can lawfully be done and lawfully be left undone. Therefore great prudence is required to accept these things in their order. For some think that without the order of these sacraments and priests the church cannot rightly [be a wayfarer]{g:viator}; hence they weigh these ceremonies so heavily that they punish one who offends [against them] more than one who faithlessly fails in [charity]{g:caritas}.[^9] It is granted, therefore, with blessed Dionysius that there are such three orders of priests, since, according to the decree of Chrysostom placed in Distinction 40, "Many are priests and few are priests".[^10] But it is not necessary that a priest made superior by the grace of God should receive thereby a new ecclesiastical sacrament; for my part I would altogether forgo having episcopal consecration sooner than presume to receive this episcopal ministry.[^11] For it is beyond necessity, and lower than preaching, prayer and many other acts of the worship of God, and moreover is not so necessarily required for many acts of worship of God as is believed. It is granted, therefore, that blessing, consecration and the other episcopal ministration is praiseworthy when duly done. But it is grievous that the church [278] should be burdened with so manifold a ceremony, more grievous that it should set such force in it, and most grievous that no one can be saved by God, as they say, unless he believes and receives such sacraments according to the form now current. And of these there is fuller discussion in the treatise *On the Sacraments*.[^12] And so it is granted that priestly vestments and episcopal ornaments can be introduced [piously]{g:pietas} and religiously, although more commonly they have been received lyingly and impiously; as those who treat of human rites and the statutes of men note five priestly vestments, with which [the priest] is commonly clothed in celebrating, and these vary in part from the ornaments of the priests of the old testament, as Hugh declares in the second book of *On the Sacraments*, part 4: "A singular habit," he says, "is handed down to the priests of Christ in the ministry, so that by what appears without it may be shown what they ought to be within; and the rite of the sacred vestments was in part taken from the old testament and in part added by the fathers of the new testament.[^13] For one reads in Exod. 28, 29, 30 and 39 how Moses clothed Aaron his brother, first with a tunic of fine linen, which in Greek is called *poderis*, that is, ankle-length, which reaches from the neck to the ankles, and which in the new priesthood is woven not only of fine linen but of linen; and it, measured and fitted to the members of the body, shows that there ought to be nothing superfluous or loose in the life of the priest." But in the new testament it is loosened after the [endowment]{g:dotacio} of the church, since many [temporal goods]{g:temporalia} beyond what the priest's office needs lie about him, made monstrous.[^14] "And this vestment, for the hope of its whiteness, takes the name Alb, which signifies the spiritual cleanness of the ministers, according to [279] that of Apoc. 19:8: It is granted to the bride that she should clothe herself with fine linen, glittering and white."[^15] And the exposition follows: "For the fine linen are the justifications of saints. The flesh of man indeed acquires by grace, through good effort, the cleanness which it does not have by nature, as fine linen or linen acquired its whiteness, which it did not have by nature, through the effort and industry of the craftsman, when, worn down by many beatings and washings, by the shining of the drying sun, as by a kind of violence, it is turned to whiteness." It is needful indeed often to chastise the body and bring it into subjection, as the Apostle says, 1 Cor. 9, that he did; and so, placed in the water of tribulation and beaten by tormentors, and meanwhile consoled by the sun of justice, dried from tribulations and kindled by the heat of truth.[^16]
