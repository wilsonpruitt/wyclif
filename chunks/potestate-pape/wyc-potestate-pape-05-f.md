# *De potestate pape*, chapter 5, part 6: the keys given for the church, Paul's licence from God, and three conclusions

## ¶1 · 97.13–98.26

> [p. 97] Quantum ad illud, patet quod turpiter petitur quod probaretur, quia, ut patet in parte ex dictis, propter spiritualem primatum, quo ceteris fuit humilior, servicior et ecclesie Christi amancior, et non propter aliquam dignitatem mundanam Deus dedit sibi primatum ecclesie, et sic fuit ad laborem et sollicitudinem suam, quod habuit illam preeminenciam, et hoc fuit obligare obedire minoribus. Et hinc sumitur racio, quare sic meruit paciendo humiliter reprobacionem Apostoli, de qua Gal. II, ut declarat Augustinus Epistola IX. Quilibet igitur apostolus habuit regimen quodammodo universale viantis ecclesie, sed Paulus Petro extensius. Et quantum ad illud dictum Petro Mar. XVI Tibi dabo claves regni celorum, patet quod fuit dictum in persona ecclesie sacerdotum, quod aliter nos non gauderemus hoc privilegio virtute horum verborum Domini; unde miror quod aliquis habens fidem scripture retorqueret ex isto verbo singulare privilegium Romani pontificis in solvendo. Hoc enim fuit singulariter [p. 98] dictum Petro tamquam generali procuratori ecclesie sacerdotum et non tamquam singulari recipienti pro se solum hoc privilegium, sed limitatum et diffinitum fuit a Christo preter ordinacionem humanam quomodo sacerdotes sibi habiles privilegio isti participent. Et ista est sentencia Augustini super X, Omelia CXXIV: Petrus, inquit, apostolus propter apostolatus sui primatum gerebat figurata generalitate personam ecclesie; quod enim ad ipsum proprie pertinet, natura unus homo erat gracia christianus, habundanciori gracia unus idemque primus apostolus. Sed quando ei dictum est: Tibi dabo claves regni celorum, et quodcumque ligaveris super terram, erit ligatum et in celis, et quodcumque solveris super terram, erit solutum et in celis, universam signat ecclesiam, que in hoc seculo diversis temptacionibus ut ymbribus et tempestatibus quatitur, et non cadit, quoniam fundatur super petram; unde Petrus nomen accepit, que fundatur in Christo. Horrenda itaque et infidelis seduccio simplicium laycorum foret ex isto textu evangelii episcopum irracionabiliter reservare sibi potenciam ad ligandum et cecare populum cum laxacione potestatis proprie et suspensione potestatis alterius, ut patet in materia de sacramento penitencie.

As to that, it is clear that what was to be proved is shamefully begged; for, as is clear in part from what has been said, it was because of a spiritual [primacy]{g:primatus}, by which he was humbler than the others, more serviceable and more loving of the [church]{g:ecclesia} of Christ, and not because of any worldly dignity, that God gave him the primacy of the church; and so it was for his labour and solicitude that he had that preeminence, and this was to bind [him] to obey lesser men.[^1] And hence is taken the reason why he so merited, by humbly suffering the reproof of the Apostle, of which Galatians 2 [speaks], as Augustine declares in Letter 9.[^2] Every apostle, therefore, had a government in a way universal over the church [on the road]{g:viantes}, but Paul more extensively than Peter.[^3] And as to that saying to Peter, Mark 16, "I will give to thee the keys of the kingdom of heaven", it is clear that it was said in the person of the church of priests, since otherwise we should not enjoy this [privilege]{g:privilegium} by virtue of these words of the Lord; hence I marvel that anyone having the faith of scripture should twist out of this word a singular privilege of the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex} in loosing.[^4] For this was said singularly [98] to Peter as to the general [proctor]{g:procurator} of the church of priests, and not as to a singular recipient of this privilege for himself alone; but it was [assigned]{g:limitare} and defined by Christ, apart from human [ordinance]{g:ordinacio}, how [priests]{g:sacerdos} [qualified]{g:habilis} for it should share in this privilege.[^5] And this is the [teaching]{g:sentencia} of Augustine on [John], Homily 124: "Peter the apostle," he says, "because of the primacy of his apostleship, bore in a figurative generality the person of the church; for as to what belongs to him properly, by nature he was one man, by grace a Christian, by more abundant grace one and the same first apostle. But when it was said to him, 'I will give to thee the keys of the kingdom of heaven. And whatsoever thou shalt bind upon earth, it shall be bound also in heaven: and whatsoever thou shalt loose on earth, it shall be loosed also in heaven', he signified the whole church, which in this world is shaken by divers temptations as by rains and storms, and does not fall, because it is founded upon the rock, whence Peter received his name"; which is founded in Christ.[^6] It would therefore be a horrible and [faithless]{g:infidelis} seduction of simple laymen, from this text of the gospel, for a [bishop]{g:episcopus} unreasonably to reserve to himself the [power]{g:potencia} of binding, and to blind the people by relaxing his own [power]{g:potestas} and [suspending]{g:suspendere} the power of another, as is clear in the matter on the sacrament of penance.[^7]

## ¶2 · 98.28–99.34

> [p. 98] Sed tercio nimis leviter sollempnisatur quod Paulus accepit autoritatem et licenciam a Petro ad predicandum in gentibus ex textu Gal. II, 9: Cum cognovissent, inquit, graciam que data est [p. 99] michi, Jacobus et Cephas et Johannes, qui videbantur esse columpne, dexteras dederunt michi et Barnabe societatis, ut nos in gentes, ipsi vero in circumcisionem. Sed notet rogo arguens ex isto textu, quomodo Paulus triennio ante illud tempus sine eorum auctoritate predicavit in gentibus, de quo non cepit penitenciam vel reprobacionem a Petro, quia Paulus cicius illud insereret quam reprehensionem Petri. Notet secundo quomodo communi consilio et auctoritate Christi, ut dicit Jeronimus, locuti sunt apostoli in ecclesia primitiva. Petrus autem ex decreto cetus apostolici fuit paracior ad laborem, ut patet Act. VIII, 14 ubi dicitur, quod apostoli, qui erant Jerusolimis, miserunt Petrum et Johannem in Samariam ad confirmandum populum, qui prius receperat verbum Dei; ubi non notatur Petro humana dignitas, quod apostoli eum miserunt ad illud officium, sicut papa non acceptaret sibi dignitatem et honorem, quod cardinales mitterent eum et unum episcopum socium ad convertendum unam gentem. Et patet quod minus inevidenter eliceretur ex isto textu quod Petrus et Johannes cepissent autoritatem ab apostolis qui residebant Jerusolimis; non enim est magna auctoritas cursorem mitti ad operam. Sed tercio notet falsarius verba textus Apostoli et videat si sequatur: Jacobus, Petrus et Johannes dederunt Paulo et Barnabe dextram societatis, igitur dederunt eis autoritatem sive licenciam; dederunt, inquam, eis bonam societatem, gaudentes quod dedit eis Deus graciam predicandi gentibus, sicut Petro Judeis, ymmo precepit separare sibi Paulum et Barnabam ad illud officium.

But thirdly, it is too lightly made a solemn matter that Paul received [authority]{g:auctoritas} and [licence]{g:licencia} from Peter to preach among the gentiles, from the text of Galatians 2:9: "When they had known," he says, "the grace that was given [99] to me, James and Cephas and John, who seemed to be pillars, gave to me and Barnabas the right hands of fellowship: that we should go unto the Gentiles, and they unto the circumcision."[^8] But let him who argues from this text note, I pray, how Paul for three years before that time preached among the gentiles without their authority, for which he received no penance or reproof from Peter; for Paul would sooner have inserted that than the rebuke of Peter.[^9] Let him note secondly how, by common counsel and the authority of Christ, as Jerome says, the apostles spoke in the primitive church.[^10] And Peter, by decree of the apostolic company, was the readier for labour, as is clear from Acts 8:14, where it is said that "the apostles, who were in Jerusalem", "sent Peter and John" into Samaria to confirm the people, who had earlier received the word of God; where no human dignity is marked in Peter in that the apostles sent him to that office, just as the [pope]{g:papa} would not take it as dignity and honour to himself that the cardinals should send him, with one bishop as companion, to convert one nation. And it is clear that it would be drawn from this text with less want of evidence that Peter and John had received authority from the apostles who stayed in Jerusalem; for it is no great authority for a runner to be sent to a task. But thirdly let the [falsifier]{g:falsarius} note the words of the Apostle's text and see whether [it follows]{g:sequitur}: "James, Peter and John gave Paul and Barnabas the right hand of fellowship; therefore they gave them authority or licence". They gave them, I say, good fellowship, rejoicing that God had given them the grace of preaching to the gentiles, as to Peter [that of preaching] to the Jews; indeed, he commanded that Paul and Barnabas be separated to him for that office.[^11]

## ¶3 · 100.1–100.22

> [p. 100] Unde nimis magna stulticia foret apostolos presumere dare illis vasis licenciam, postquam cognoverunt quod Deus prius dedit ad hoc licenciam et preceptum. Maniaca, inquam, foret stulticia, nos dare licenciam angelis ad exequendum suum officium, quod Deus preceperit. Sufficit igitur Christi auctoritas sive licencia, qua cognita licet rogare et hortari ad illam sedule exequendum. Unde oportet notare pro intellectu istius historie apostolice benedicte, quomodo Apostolus incipit calculare a conversione sua; de quaAct...: tunc enim segregatus fuit ex utero matris sue synagoge, pro cuius defensione infideliter laboravit; et hinc vocat se ipsum abortivum prima Cor. XV, 8: Novissime, inquit, tamquam abortivo Christus visus est michi, quia de matre sua synagoga prodiit ipsa ocasione illius exitus pereunte, quia, ut dicitur Act. IX, 10: Mansit cum discipulis diebus aliquot; quos absit credere fuisse apostolos, et continue invalescebat confundens Judeos propter amorem indubie, quo dilexerat matrem suam.

Hence it would be too great a folly for the apostles to presume to give those vessels licence, after they knew that God had earlier given them licence and command for this.[^12] It would be, I say, a maniacal folly for us to give licence to the angels to carry out their office, which God had commanded. Christ's authority or licence therefore suffices; once it is known, it is lawful to ask and exhort [them] to carry it out diligently. Hence, for the understanding of this blessed apostolic history, it must be noted how the Apostle begins to reckon from his conversion, of which Acts [speaks]: for then he was separated from the womb of his mother the synagogue, for whose defence he had laboured [faithlessly]{g:infidelis}; and hence he calls himself one born out of due time, 1 Corinthians 15:8: "Last," he says, "as to one born out of due time, Christ was seen by me"; because he came forth from his mother the synagogue, she perishing on the occasion of that going forth.[^13] For, as is said in Acts 9:10, "He stayed with the disciples some days", whom God forbid we should believe to have been apostles; and he continually grew stronger, confounding the Jews, without doubt because of the love with which he had loved his mother.[^14]

## ¶4 · 100.23–101.12

> [p. 100] Ex istis colligitur, quod sicut omnes apostoli preter Scarioth erant socii sibi obedientes, honore invicem prevenientes et superiores invicem arbitrantes, qui inmediate a Deo potestatem et licenciam ad evangelizandum acceperunt; sic debet esse de sacerdotibus et episcopis ecclesie iam instantis, ita quod disparitas ditacionis societatem et amiciciam evangelicam non dissolvet, quia per idem Christus per possibile reconversans nobiscum, observans scolam suam ex integro sicut olim, non haberet papam vel dotatos episcopos socios vel amicos sed dominos, quibus solveret tributum sicut olim fecit cesari. Non enim consonat [p. 101] scole sapientis habere familiam tam discordem a capite quod monstruose et contra policiam humanam servicium ab eis exigeret, et in propria familia doctrine capitis repugnaret, quod si veniens in mundum illum non invenerit episcopos in conversacione sibi consentaneos et amicos, quomodo laudaret eos ut fideles vicarios, nec ut fideles negociatores in die iudicii beatitudine premiandos; a multis creditur quod nullos vel paucos inveniet, in quibus scintilla sue conversacionis sit propter antichristianam declinacionem ad seculum exemplata.

From these things it is gathered that, just as all the apostles except Iscariot were companions obedient to one another, "with honour preventing one another" and esteeming one another superiors, who received power and licence to evangelize immediately from God, so it ought to be with the priests and bishops of the church now present, so that disparity of enrichment shall not dissolve fellowship and evangelical friendship.[^15] For by the same token Christ, if, as is possible, he were living among us again and keeping his school entire as of old, would have the pope or the endowed bishops not as companions or friends but as [lords]{g:dominus}, to whom he would pay tribute as once he did to Caesar.[^16] For it does not befit [101] the school of a wise man to have a [household]{g:familia} so discordant from its [head]{g:caput} that, monstrously and against human polity, it should exact service from them, and in his own household be [incompatible]{g:repugnare} with the [doctrine]{g:doctrina} of the head; so that if, coming into the world, he does not find bishops agreeing with him in [manner of life]{g:conversacio}, and friends, how would he praise them as faithful vicars, or as faithful [dealers]{g:negociatores} to be rewarded with blessedness on the day of judgement? It is believed by many that he will find none or few in whom a spark of his manner of life is copied, because of the antichristian decline towards the world.[^17]

## ¶5 · 101.13–101.22

> [p. 101] Si autem queritur quid nos sacerdotes debemus facere, dicitur indubie quod debemus in conversacione sequi ipsum quantum sufficimus. Sic quod primo abraderemus quantum sufficimus omnem occupacionem mentis aut corporis, que fuerit inpertinens aut contraria officio sacerdotis. Secundo vero poneremus omnem sacerdotalem potestatem quantum sufficeremus efficaciter in effectum, et omnium istorum exemplar et specula debet esse dominus Jhesus Christus.

And if it is asked what we priests ought to do, [the answer is]{g:dicitur}, without doubt, that we ought to follow him in manner of life as far as we are able: so that, first, we should shave away, as far as we are able, every occupation of mind or body that is irrelevant or contrary to the office of priest; and secondly, we should put every priestly power, as far as we are able, effectually into effect; and of all these the exemplar and mirror must be the Lord Jesus Christ.[^18]

## ¶6 · 101.23–102.24

> [p. 101] Ex istis sequerentur tres conclusiones graciose ecclesie, primo quod papa non intromitteret se iudicando de negocio seculari, quia est alienum a sacerdotali officio iuxta illud II Thim. II, 4: Nemo militans Deo implicat se negociis secularibus, Secundo quod renueret omnem dotacionem cesaream, cum ex facto Christi et Petri patet quod foret impertinens papali officio; et per consequens omnes alii episcopi ut sit conformitas ad caput, et revera necessarior foret conformitas paparum sequencium in ista pauperie ad Christum, sicut [p. 102] Petrus et alii sub quibus crevit ecclesia se Domino conformarunt. Tercio quod papa et omnes alii episcopi debent evangelizacione, oracione et sancte vite exemplacione assidue pascere oves Christi; patet ex officio ter limitato Petro Johannis ultimo. Unde Augustinus Epistola CXLI ad Paulinum notat, quomodo in textu Apostoli Ephe. IV, ii officium pastoris et doctoris iunguntur: Pastores, inquit, et doctores quos maxime discernere voluisti eosdem puto esse et non alios pastores et alios doctores intelligamus; et ideo cum predixisset pastores, subiunxit doctores, ut pastores intelligerent ad officium suum pertinere doctrinam. Ideo vero non ait: quosdam vero pastores et quosdam doctores, ut superiori locucionis genere, sed quosdam pastores et doctores, tamquam unum aliquid duobus nominibus amplexus est, quosdam, inquit, pastores et doctores. Et sic spoliatis superfluis, pateret quis episcopus remaneret habilis ex thesauro Domini et quis remaneret vacuus prescisso organo et officio laycali; et forte reperirentur plures pastores cesarei quam fideles vicarii domini Jhesu Christi. Et utinam talis discernendi monstracio foret facta.

From these would follow three [conclusions]{g:conclusio} gracious to the church.[^19] First, that the pope would not meddle in judging a secular business, for it is alien to the priestly office, according to 2 Timothy 2:4: "No man, being a soldier to God, entangleth himself with secular businesses." Secondly, that he would renounce every caesarean [endowment]{g:dotacio}, since from the deed of Christ and of Peter it is clear that it would be irrelevant to the papal office; and in consequence all the other bishops [would], that there may be conformity to the head; and in truth the conformity of the popes who follow to Christ in this poverty would be more necessary, as [102] Peter and the others under whom the church grew conformed themselves to the Lord.[^20] Thirdly, that the pope and all the other bishops ought to feed the sheep of Christ assiduously by evangelizing, by prayer, and by the example of a holy life; this is clear from the office thrice assigned to Peter in the last [chapter] of John.[^21] Hence Augustine, Letter 141 to Paulinus, notes how in the Apostle's text, Ephesians 4:11, the office of pastor and of doctor are joined: "Pastors," he says, "and doctors, whom you most wished me to distinguish, I think are the same, and let us not understand some to be pastors and others doctors; and therefore, when he had spoken of pastors, he added doctors, that pastors might understand that doctrine belongs to their office. And therefore he does not say: 'and some pastors, and some doctors', as in the earlier kind of expression, but 'some pastors and doctors', as though he embraced one thing under two names: 'some', he says, 'pastors and doctors'."[^22] And so, the superfluities being stripped away, it would be clear which bishop remained qualified from the treasury of the Lord, and which remained empty, the instrument and the lay office being cut off; and perhaps more caesarean pastors would be found than faithful vicars of the Lord Jesus Christ. And would that such a [muster]{g:monstracio} for discerning had been made.[^23]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 97.13: Such primacy was given only because he was more loving and humble than the rest.
- 97.26: The power of the keys was given to him as representing the priesthood and confers no privilege on the Roman pontiff.
- 98.28: Paul did not accept authority or licence from Peter.
- 99.4: He had preached three years before he met Peter.
- 99.15: Peter was sent to Samaria by the apostles.
- 100.1: The apostles would not have given licence to one who had already received it of God.
- 100.23: The apostles consorted on terms of mutual service and honour.
- 100.30: So should our priests and bishops do.
- 101.1: If Christ were to revisit the earth, he would find few faithful servants.
- 101.13: We priests ought to devote ourselves entirely to priestly duties.
- 101.23: The pope should not judge in secular business.
- 101.29: He should give up all cesarean endowments.

## Notes

[^1]: **C/T.** *turpiter petitur quod probaretur*: the fallacy of begging the question. *hoc fuit obligare obedire minoribus*: the preeminence was itself a bond to obey those beneath him. Loserth reports a corrupt A reading at 97.15–16 (*fiunt humilior*); translated as printed.

[^2]: **S/R.** Augustine on Peter's humility under Paul's rebuke; Loserth: "St. Augustini Opp. tom. II, 85"; Wyclif's *Epistola IX* stands as printed (LEDGER P05f-1, editor; the modern letter number to be checked). A in the margin (97.23): *Petrus habuit primatum super apostolos*, "Peter had primacy over the apostles".

[^3]: **G.** *viantis ecclesie*: the church of those on the road (GLOSSARY *viantes*), the church in this life.

[^4]: **S/T.** The words are Matthew 16:19, DR; *Mar. XVI* stands as printed. *in persona ecclesie*: as representing the church. *in solvendo*: in the power of loosing.

[^5]: **T/G.** B reads *diffinitum fuerit modo preter* (98.4). *limitatum*: "assigned", laid down (GLOSSARY *limitare*), not restricted. *habiles*: qualified (GLOSSARY *habilis*).

[^6]: **S/T/C.** Augustine, *In Iohannis evangelium tractatus* 124.5; Loserth refers instead to *Enarr. in Ps.* 108 (LEDGER P05f-2, to be checked). The print's *super X* and the manuscripts' *super 10* (98.7) both seem to stand for *super Jo[hannem]*; we supply "John" (our emendation). B lacks *apostolus* (98.12). Matthew 16:19, DR. *que fundatur in Christo*: the feminine relative after the quotation takes *petram*, the rock founded in Christ; B's *qui* (98.19) makes it Peter. Loserth at 98.20: cf. Hus, Opp. 210 (LEDGER P05f-3, editor).

[^7]: **C/S.** *potenciam … potestatis*: both "power" here (GLOSSARY *potencia*, CONTEXTUAL; Wyclif uses the two words interchangeably). The reserved cases of penance: the bishop binds by withholding his own power and suspending the priest's. *in materia de sacramento penitencie*: Wyclif's cross-reference (LEDGER P05f-4, to be checked). B lacks *populum* (98.24).

[^8]: **S/T.** Galatians 2:9, DR. The print's stray parenthesis after *cognovissent* is not carried.

[^9]: **S.** Galatians 1:17–18: Paul went to see Peter "after three years".

[^10]: **S.** Jerome on the common counsel of the apostles and priests: presumably the *Olim* of Decretum D.95 c.5, cited at 96.27 (LEDGER P05f-5, to be checked).

[^11]: **S/C/G.** Acts 13:2, DR "Separate me Saul and Barnabas". The subject of *precepit* is God (the Holy Ghost in Acts). *ymmo*: "indeed", correcting upward. *falsarius*: the opponent's epithet.

[^12]: **S.** *illis vasis*: Paul and Barnabas; Paul as "a vessel of election", Acts 9:15, DR.

[^13]: **S/C.** Galatians 1:15, DR "who separated me from my mother's womb"; Wyclif makes the womb the synagogue's. The print has *de qua Act...* with the reference wanting. 1 Corinthians 15:8: Wyclif departs from the Vulgate (*novissime autem omnium tamquam abortivo visus est et mihi*, DR "And last of all, he was seen also by me, as by one born out of due time"); his Latin is translated. *ipsa … pereunte*: the mother dying in the untimely birth.

[^14]: **S.** Acts 9:19 (Wyclif "IX, 10"): Vulgate *fuit autem cum discipulis qui erant Damasci per dies aliquot*; Wyclif's *mansit … diebus aliquot* recalls Galatians 1:18 (*mansi apud eum diebus quindecim*). *invalescebat confundens Judeos*: Acts 9:22 (DR "Saul increased much more in strength and confounded the Jews"), not quoted.

[^15]: **S/C.** Romans 12:10, DR; *superiores invicem arbitrantes* recalls Philippians 2:3. *ditacionis*: enrichment, the endowing of some above others.

[^16]: **C/S.** *per possibile*: so the print, "as is possible"; the usual formula for such a supposition is *per impossibile*, "if, per impossibile". *per idem*: "by the same token". The tribute: Matthew 17:26; 22:21.

[^17]: **C.** The subject of *exigeret* and *repugnaret* is taken to be the household, which would exact service of them (the head and his own), against human polity; if the subject is the head, the sense is that Christ would demand service of the endowed. *negociatores*: the servants who traded with the talents (Matthew 25; Luke 19:13).

[^18]: **R/C.** B in the margin (101.13): *Sacerdotes quid debent facere*, "What priests ought to do". *abraderemus*: "shave away".

[^19]: **T/R.** *graciose*: Loserth's reading; the manuscripts read *gloriose*, "glorious" (101.23). A in the margin: *prima conclusio*; B in the margin at 101.28: *Secundo*; at 102.2: *3*.

[^20]: **S/T/C.** 2 Timothy 2:4, DR. B lacks *II* before *Tim.* (101.26). *dotacionem cesaream*: the endowment from Caesar (Constantine), cf. *clerus cesareus*. *necessarior … sicut*: the comparison is loose: as necessary for the popes now as it was for Peter and the others.

[^21]: **S/G.** John 21:15–17 (DR 21:17 "Feed my sheep"). *limitato*: "assigned" (GLOSSARY *limitare*).

[^22]: **S/T/R.** Augustine, Ep. 149 to Paulinus (Loserth: "Ep. CXLIX. Opp. tom. II, pag. 508"; Wyclif's *CXLI* stands as printed; LEDGER P05f-6, editor; the section to be checked). [^23]: **C/T.** *prescisso organo et officio laycali*: the worldly means and lay office cut away, the bishop is left empty. B lacks *facta* (102.26). A in the margin (102.25): *C. VI.*, the next chapter.
