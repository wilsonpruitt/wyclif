# *De potestate pape*, chapter 5, part 5: the saints' venial fault, and the powers of order and of jurisdiction

## ¶1 · 93.31–96.20

> [p. 93] Et quantum ad beatos Silvestrem, Gregorium et similes dicitur quod cum pena habuerunt tantum tumultum temporalium exproprietarie per [p. 94] medias personas distribuendo bona ecclesie, ipsi autem servabant vitam humilem et pauperem in se ipsis, quod si scivissent peccatores sequentes sic obtinuisse illas divicias, firmiter credo quod ipsas pro se et posteris reliquissent. Et stultus est qui sompniat et defendit quod non in hoc venialiter peccaverunt. Non enim repugnat quod remisse in gracia occuparunt illud officium sicut meritorie, sed defectu veniali occuparunt temporalia et in posteris propter profundiorem maliciam in occupacionem mortalem et simpliciter Deo odibilem est perversa. Periculum igitur foret trahere in exemplum vel consequenciam modum agendi venialiter culpabilem, quia sic ex pietate doceremus cum Petro gentes iudaizare, propter quod fuit a Paulo, ut patet capitulo II ad Gal., reprehensus. Et credo quod longe maius peccatum fuit in dotatis episcopis ex hoc quod dotacionem huiusmodi admiserunt, Caveamus igitur exempla capere sanctorum ab operibus eorum mixtim laudabilibus et venialiter culpabilibus secundum disparem racionem, quia nescimus si in nobis sit predestinacionis gracia vel excusandi racio a mortali. Ideo debemus sequi secure sanctum sanctorum et virtutes in membris suis, nec non in operibus sic mixtis debemus colare racionem laudabilem et illi inniti racione alia separata. Et credo quod ignorancia huius sentencie fecit multos a via Domini declinare. Noverunt sophiste quomodo eadem res est simul laudabilis et culpabilis secundum disparem racionem, ut Petrus et operacio sua est laudabilis, quia sub aliquo gradu bona moraliter, et est cum hoc culpabilis et reprobabilis propter defectum habundancie [p. 95] virtutis et fervorum amoris. Sic enim sumus omnes in vita continue increpandi; et talis tepiditas, ut dicitur Apocalipsis III, 16 causat vomitum, quia indubie inducit plurimos in mortale. Sed tercio arguitur contra dicta per hoc quod iuxta sepe et publice dicta a me alia est potestas iurisdicionis et alia ordinis; sicut declarat dominus Ardmacanus libro VII De Questionibus Armenorum VI capitulo, ubi dicitur, quod potestates ordinis in omnibus sacerdotibus sunt equales. Sed potestates regiminis variantur, cum Petro et suis successoribus qui soli habent universalem potestatem regiminis data est sic potestas super universalem ecclesiam; et consonat factum ecclesie cum multis legibus de regimine vel iurisdicione loquentibus; oportet igitur docere quod officium Petro conveniat et quid sit ab eo vetitum. Quantum ad illud non discucio, quid fiat de facto, sed quid auctoritate scripture vel racione debet fieri. Unde Deum contestor, nec ex scriptura nec ex sanctis doctoribus fundari video istius iurisdicionis extensionem tam vanam, sed ex ritu gentilium sicut institucionem pape et cardinalium introductam. Concedo igitur distinccionem predictam. Pro quo notandum quod multi sacerdotes et episcopi habent dispares potestates. Sed potestas ordinis, que est par in quolibet sacerdote, est potestas quam clericus habet in quantum est Christi sacerdos, quam oportet esse communem et quodlibet eius iudicium est potestas sic remissa, qua sacerdos non potest carere manente suo sacerdocio; et illa potestas non potest maius et [p. 96] minus suscipere. Et preter illam est dare aliam maneriem potestatis iurisdicionis sive regiminis, que fuit dispar in diversis apostolis et continue est dispar in diversis episcopis. Sed illa non attenditur penes institucionem humanam, sed penes hoc quod Deus ordinavit episcopum intensius vel remissius debere regere maiorem partem ecclesie vel minorem. Et non attenditur in isto ad humanum primatum sed ad quantitatem potestatis quam Deus attribuit ad edificandum suam ecclesiam, et (ut dictum est) omnes habuerunt commixtam potenciam super eodem populo sine repugnancia, Petrus primatum non propter amplitudinem iurisdiccionis sed propter fervorem dileccionis Christi et sue ecclesie. Nam ut credo ceteri extensius laborarunt, ita quod turpissime et inevidentissime arguitur quod, si Petrus habuit primatum inter apostolos, tunc ille habuit iurisdiccionem universalem tocius ecclesie, et alii particularem a Petro et eius vicariis limitatam.

And as to the blessed Silvester, Gregory and the like, the answer is that, since they had with pain so great a turmoil of [temporal goods]{g:temporalia}, distributing the goods of the [church]{g:ecclesia} [without property]{g:exproprietarie} through [94] intermediate persons, while they themselves kept a humble and poor life in themselves, [it follows] that, if they had known that the sinners following would so obtain those riches, I firmly believe that they would have left them, for themselves and their successors.[^1] And he is a fool who dreams and maintains that they did not sin venially in this.[^2] For it is not [incompatible]{g:repugnare} that they held that office slackly in grace, as [they did] meritoriously, but held the temporal goods with a venial defect; and in their successors, because of a deeper malice, [the holding] was perverted into a mortal holding, hateful to God [without qualification]{g:simpliciter}.[^3] It would therefore be a danger to draw into an example or a precedent a way of acting that is venially culpable, for so, out of [piety]{g:pietas}, we should teach the gentiles with Peter to judaize, for which he was rebuked by Paul, as is clear in chapter 2 of Galatians.[^4] And I believe that the sin was far greater in the endowed [bishops]{g:episcopus} in that they admitted an [endowment]{g:dotacio} of this kind. Let us therefore beware of taking examples of the saints from their works that are, in mixed fashion, praiseworthy and venially culpable under different aspects, for we do not know whether there is in us the grace of predestination or a reason for being excused from mortal [sin]. Therefore we ought to follow securely the holy of holies and the virtues in his [members]{g:membrum}; and in works so mixed we ought to strain out the praiseworthy aspect and lean on it, the other aspect being separated. And I believe that ignorance of this [teaching]{g:sentencia} has made many turn aside from the way of the Lord. The [sophists]{g:sophista} know how the same thing is at once praiseworthy and culpable under different aspects: as Peter and his working is praiseworthy, because morally good in some degree, and is with this culpable and reprovable for want of an [abundance]{g:habundancia} [95] of [virtue]{g:virtus} and of the fervours of love.[^5] For so we are all, in this life, continually to be rebuked; and such lukewarmness, as is said in Revelation 3:16, causes vomiting, for without doubt it leads very many into mortal [sin].[^6] But, thirdly, [it is argued]{g:arguitur-per} against what has been said, by appeal to the fact that, according to what I have often and publicly said, the [power of jurisdiction]{g:potestas-iurisdiccionis} is one thing and that [of order]{g:potestas-ordinis} another; as [the lord of Armagh]{g:dominus-ardmacanus} declares in book 7 of the *Questions of the Armenians*, chapter 6, where it is said "that the powers of order are equal in all priests".[^7] But the powers of government vary, since to Peter and his successors, who alone have universal power of government, power was thus given over the universal church; and the practice of the church agrees with many laws that speak of government or [jurisdiction]{g:iurisdiccio}; it is therefore necessary to teach what office befits Peter and what is forbidden him.[^8] As to that, I do not discuss what is done in fact, but what ought to be done by the [authority]{g:auctoritas} of scripture or by reason. Hence I call God to witness: I see so vain an extension of this jurisdiction grounded neither in scripture nor in the holy doctors, but introduced from the rite of the gentiles, as [was] the institution of the [pope]{g:papa} and the cardinals. I grant, therefore, the aforesaid distinction. For which it is to be noted that many [priests]{g:sacerdos} and bishops have unequal powers. But the power of order, which is equal in every priest, is the power that a cleric has inasmuch as he is a priest of Christ, which must be common, and every judgement of it is a power so remitted, which a priest cannot lack while his priesthood remains; and that power cannot admit more and [96] less.[^9] And besides it there is another [kind]{g:maneries} of power, of jurisdiction or government, which was unequal in different apostles and is continually unequal in different bishops. But that [power] is not reckoned according to human institution, but according to God's having ordained that a bishop should rule, more intensely or more slackly, a greater or a lesser part of the church.[^10] And regard is paid in this not to human [primacy]{g:primatus} but to the quantity of power that God assigned for building up his church; and (as has been said) all had a mingled [power]{g:potencia} over the same people without [incompatibility]{g:repugnancia}, Peter [having] the primacy not because of the breadth of his jurisdiction but because of the fervour of his love of Christ and of his church.[^11] For, as I believe, the others laboured more extensively, so that it is most shamefully and with the least evidence argued that, if Peter had the primacy among the apostles, then he had universal jurisdiction over the whole church, and the others a particular one [assigned]{g:limitare} by Peter and his vicars.

## ¶2 · 96.21–96.24

> [p. 96] Tres tamen evidencias innuit dominus Ardmacanus ad roborandum istam arguciam, primo quod oportet aliquem capitaliter sic regere ex auctoritate domini, sed ille potissime foret Petrus.

Yet the lord of Armagh hints at three pieces of [evidence]{g:evidencia} to strengthen this [quibble]{g:argucia}: first, that someone must so rule [as head]{g:capitaliter} by the authority of the Lord; now that one would above all be Peter.[^12]

## ¶3 · 96.25–97.5

> [p. 96] Ad illud dicitur et in futurum declarabitur quod hodierna gentilis iurisdiccio fuit preter autoritatem Domini ex superbia introducta, quia iuxta decretum beati Jeronimi XCV distinccione: Olim ecclesia regulabatur in principio communi sacerdotum consilio, sicut fuit tempore apostolorum subducta dominacione. Et ad docendum nos istam sentenciam credo Spiritum Sanctum ordinasse, quod in lege Christi preponeretur quandoque [p. 97] ante Petrum alius Christi apostolus, ut Gal. II Paulus preponit Jacobum fratrem Domini et Act. VIII miserunt apostoli Petrum et Johannem Samariam. Et Act. II docetur eleccionem Matthie ex communi apostolorum consilio processisse.

To that the answer is, and it will be declared in future, that today's gentile jurisdiction was introduced out of pride beyond the authority of the Lord; for according to the decree of blessed Jerome in distinction 95, "Formerly the church was ruled in the beginning by the common counsel of the priests", as it was in the time of the apostles, the [exercise of lordship]{g:dominacio} being withdrawn.[^13] And to teach us this teaching I believe the Holy Spirit ordained that in [the law of Christ]{g:lex-christi} another apostle of Christ should sometimes be set [97] before Peter: as in Galatians 2 Paul puts James, the brother of the Lord, first, and in Acts 8 the apostles sent Peter and John to Samaria. And in Acts 2 it is taught that the election of Matthias proceeded from the common counsel of the apostles.[^14]

## ¶4 · 97.6–97.12

> [p. 97] Secundo arguitur per hoc quod Petro singulariter dicta sunt et concessa multa a Domino; oportet igitur quod illa dicant in Petro quandam preeminenciam, sed nullam pertinencius quam regimen universale predictum; et illud multum notatur, quod soli Petro dictum est Math. XVI, 19: Tibi dabo claves regni celorum.

Secondly, it is argued by appeal to the fact that many things were said and granted singularly to Peter by the Lord; therefore they must signify some preeminence in Peter, but none more pertinently than the aforesaid universal government; and this is much noted, that to Peter alone it was said, Matthew 16:19: "I will give to thee the keys of the kingdom of heaven."[^15]

## Notes

[^1]: **C/T.** The period runs *dicitur quod cum … quod si scivissent … firmiter credo quod*: the second *quod* resumes the first after the long *cum*-clause, and *firmiter credo* breaks in, so the construction is left open in the English. *scivissent* (94.3): Loserth's reading; the manuscripts have *scissent*, the same sense. *per medias personas*: through intermediaries, not handling the goods themselves.

[^2]: **R.** A in the margin (94.7): *Sancti suscipiendo dotacionem venialiter peccaverunt*, "The saints sinned venially in accepting the endowment".

[^3]: **C.** *sicut meritorie*: their holding of the office stayed meritorious though slack; the venial defect lay in the holding of temporal goods. The subject of *est perversa* is not expressed; we supply "the holding" from *occuparunt … occupacionem*.

[^4]: **S/G.** Galatians 2:11–14 (DR 2:14 "how dost thou compel the Gentiles to live as do the Jews?"). *trahere in … consequenciam*: the legal idiom "to draw into a precedent"; not the logical *consequencia* "inference" of GLOSSARY.

[^5]: **T/C.** B lacks *et culpabilis* (94.31). *Petrus* is the apostle, whose fault at Antioch is in view; the logicians' sample name would also fit.

[^6]: **T/S.** Loserth notes *3° causat* in the manuscripts (95.3). Revelation 3:16, DR "I will begin to vomit thee out of my mouth"; the words are not quoted.

[^7]: **S/R.** FitzRalph, *Summa in quaestionibus Armenorum* VII.6, as Wyclif cites it; no reference in Loserth (LEDGER P05e-1, to be checked). B spells the name *Archidmanus* (95.9). A in the margin (95.8): *Nota de duplici potestate scilicet iurisdiccionis et ordinis*, "Note on the twofold power, namely of jurisdiction and of order".

[^8]: **C.** Where the objection ends is not marked; we take it to run to *vetitum*. *quod officium* is read as an interrogative, "what office", beside *quid sit … vetitum*.

[^9]: **C.** *quodlibet eius iudicium est potestas sic remissa*: so the print, rendered word for word; the sense is doubtful (perhaps every judgement made by the power of order is power at this lower, equal degree). AB in the margin (95.27): *Potestas dispar in episcopis et sacerdotibus*, "Power unequal in bishops and priests".

[^10]: **T.** B reads *quod Deus est* (96.6) for the print's wording; translated as printed.

[^11]: **C/R.** *potenciam*: "power" (GLOSSARY *potencia*, CONTEXTUAL), here the same as the *potestas* of the sentence; not Aristotle's potency. *Petrus primatum*: the verb *habuit* is understood. AB in the margin (96.13): *Petrus quomodo primatum habuit inter apostolos*, "How Peter had the primacy among the apostles".

[^12]: **C/R.** *sed ille*: the minor premiss of FitzRalph's first argument, so "now". A in the margin (96.21) numbers it *1*; the second follows at 97.6.

[^13]: **S.** Jerome, *Olim*: Decretum D.95 c.5, to which Loserth refers back ("Vide supra pag. 92"; the modern form is ours) (LEDGER P05e-2, editor). Jerome's own words (on Titus 1:5) speak of the common counsel of presbyters; Wyclif writes *sacerdotum*.

[^14]: **S.** Galatians 2:9 names James first among the pillars. Acts 8:14. The election of Matthias is Acts 1:15–26 (DR 1:26); Wyclif's *Act. II* stands as printed.

[^15]: **S/R/T.** Matthew 16:19, DR. A in the margin (97.6): *2*, the second of FitzRalph's arguments. *dicant*: "signify, imply".
