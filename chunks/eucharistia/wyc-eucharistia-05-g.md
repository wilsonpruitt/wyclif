# *De eucharistia*, chapter 5, part 7: the decree and the decretal, and the answer to the charge of unfittingness

## ¶1 · 139.29–140.8

> [p. 139] Unde istud scandalum fabricatum super scripto sancti Thome fuit statim, quia tempore Doctoris Subtilis, fabricatum mendaciter et destructum; ideo absit a fideli credere quod omnes contrarii isti sentencie [p. 140] sancto Thome imposite sunt fedati heretica pravitate, quia tamen receperunt istum ordinem sanctum et pium, quamvis habeant Scarioth inter illos. Suppono sanctum Thomam errasse secundum formam que fingitur et respondeo ad argucias per ordinem, ne devoti et scioli illius ordinis vel alii occasionaliter seducantur; unum tamen scio quod iste sagitte plumbee nunquam processerant de pharetra sanctitatis.

Hence this scandal fabricated upon the writing of saint Thomas was at once (for [it was] in the time of [the Subtle Doctor]{g:doctor-subtilis}) fabricated lyingly and destroyed;[^1] therefore far be it from a faithful man to believe that all who are contrary to this [teaching]{g:sentencia} [140] imposed on saint Thomas are defiled with [heretical depravity]{g:heretica-pravitas}, because they nevertheless received this order as holy and pious, although they have an Iscariot among them.[^2] I suppose that saint Thomas erred according to the form that is feigned, and I reply to the [quibbles]{g:argucie} in order, lest the devout and the [knowing]{g:sciolus} of that order, or others, be led astray [by occasion]{g:occasionaliter}; one thing, nevertheless, I know: that these leaden arrows never came forth from the quiver of holiness.

## ¶2 · 140.9–140.17

> [p. 140] Quantum ad primam solucionem trimembrem patet quod est temeraria, non probata. Est, inquam, temeraria, quia ponit ut articulum fidei quod nec panis nec vinum post consecracionem remaneant; quod est nimis temerarium propter multa; nam in nullo articulo triplicis fidei ecclesie est iste articulus declaratus; quis ergo color quod propter assercionem unius temerariam sit ecclesia novo articulo fidei gravidanda?

As to the first, three-membered [resolution]{g:solucio},[^3] it is clear that it is rash, not proved. It is rash, I say, because it posits as an [article of faith]{g:articulus-fidei} that neither [bread]{g:panis} nor [wine]{g:vinum} remains after [consecration]{g:consecracio}; which is [exceedingly]{g:nimis} rash for many reasons; for in no article of the threefold faith of the [church]{g:ecclesia} is this article [made clear]{g:declarare};[^4] what [colour]{g:color},[^5] then, [is there] that, because of the rash assertion of one man, the church should be made pregnant with a new article of faith?

## ¶3 · 140.18–141.10

> [p. 140] Iterum, scriptura sacra et antiqui sancti doctores ut Ambrosius, Augustinus et alii autores canonum dicunt regulariter quod hostia consecrata est panis et nunquam dicunt quod est accidens. Que ergo foret presumpcio sollempnisare hoc sine auctoritate ut fidem katholicam? sicut decretum Romane ecclesie tempore Nicholai IIⁱ decrevit quod post consecracionem panis et vinum remaneant (ut patet superius) et concordat plene decretum Ambrosii, ibidem, Omnia quecunque, et breviter quotcunque ponit quod panis erit post consecracionem corpus Domini; cum ergo [p. 141] illud decretum fuit diu ante Innocencium IIIᵘᵐ (ut patet per Cestrensem libro VIIᵒ), videtur quod ecclesia diu viavit in floribus fidei sine ista ficticia (non enim est evidens quod papa Nicolaus IIᵘˢ et tota sua ecclesia cum sanctis doctoribus qui nunquam sciverunt vel recoluerunt istam ficticiam sed asseruerunt tamquam per se notum eius contrarium, ut patet per beatum Augustinum superius allegatum de accidentibus) sic dampnata; nimis ceca itaque foret temeritas hereticare in hoc sanctos cum primitiva ecclesia.

Again, holy scripture and the ancient holy doctors, such as Ambrose, Augustine and other authors of the canons, say [as a rule]{g:regulariter} that the [consecrated host]{g:hostia-consecrata} is bread, and never say that it is an [accident]{g:accidens}. What presumption, then, would it be to solemnize this without [authority]{g:auctoritas} as the catholic faith? as the [decree]{g:decretum} of the Roman church in the time of Nicholas II decreed that after consecration the bread and wine remain (as is clear above),[^6] and the decree of Ambrose, in the same place, *Omnia quecunque*, fully agrees, and, in short, all those who posit[^7] that the bread will be [the body of the Lord]{g:corpus-domini} after consecration; since therefore [141] that decree was long before Innocent III (as is clear from [the Chester chronicler]{g:cestrensis}, book 7),[^8] it seems that the church long [journeyed as a wayfarer]{g:viator}[^9] in the flowers of faith without this [fiction]{g:ficticia} (for it is not evident that Pope Nicholas II and his whole church, with the holy doctors who never knew or called to mind this fiction but asserted its contrary as known [of itself]{g:per-se}, as is clear through the blessed Augustine [alleged]{g:allegare} above on accidents, was so damned);[^10] exceedingly blind, therefore, would be the rashness to [pronounce the saints heretical]{g:hereticare} in this, together with the primitive church.

## ¶4 · 141.11–142.21

> [p. 141] Ex quo videtur ulterius probabiliter supponendum quod ecclesia tempore Innocencii tercii loquens de transsubstanciacione locuta est de illa ad sensum famosorum doctorum, quo usa fuit ecclesia primitiva. Videtur enim Hugo loqui de transsubstanciacione panis ad sensum quo utitur Ambrosius de conversione in corpus Christi. Transitus autem ille vel illa conversio non potest salvari nisi ad illum sensum quo concedunt quod panis fit, erit et est corpus Christi, hoc est, post consecracionem figurat sacramentaliter corpus Christi. Hoc enim est maius miraculum, quam foret conversio de qua fit mencio in lege veteri, ymmo quam foret conversio desinente panis substancia, quia utrobique est corpus Christi eque vere et realiter ibi presens; et super hec tolluntur inconveniencia que ponit panis destruccio. Laudabilis ergo foret mutacio que ponit tot et plura bona et evacuat magis mala. Nec scio aliter concordare decretalem Innocencii et decretum. Non enim est racionabile quod decretalis sit immemor decreti prioris aut quod hereticet priorem ecclesiam sine causa. Unde patet [p. 142] quod stat cum isto sensu quod post istam transsubstanciacionem panis transsubstanciatus remaneat, verumptamen diffido quod ecclesia Avinonica cum suis satrapis significacionem istam acciperet. Sed que cura de hoc, cum prior ecclesia hoc approbans fuit gravior, sicut gravior fuit persona Innocencii tercii cum pluribus quam mille prelatis licet non episcopis sed abbatibus ydiotis quam ecclesia Gregorii IXⁱ cum satrapis secte sue? Ab illo namque dicitur quinque libros decretalium capere auctoritatem novam, priori sopita, sicut Machomet dicit legem suam ab ipso capere auctoritatem non a lege nova vel veteri. Sic inquam decretales epistole ex multis antiquis edicionibus decretalium prioris ecclesie sunt collecte dimissis partibus verioribus aut displicentibus et novis sive placentibus acceptatis; ideo est novum opus paris auctoritatis cum aliis epistolis pape de novo editis, quia aliter caperent iste ultime decretales ex multitudine paparum priorum longe maiorem auctoritatem quam evangelium. Nam prima epistola decretalis secundum ipsos est paris auctoritatis cum evangelio.

From which it seems that it is further to be supposed, [with probability]{g:probabiliter}, that the church in the time of Innocent III, speaking of [transubstantiation]{g:transsubstanciacio}, spoke of it in the [meaning]{g:sensus} of the renowned doctors which the primitive church used.[^11] For Hugh seems to speak of the transubstantiation of the bread in the meaning which Ambrose uses of the [conversion]{g:conversio} into [the body of Christ]{g:corpus-christi}.[^12] Now that passing-over, or that conversion, cannot be saved except in that meaning in which they grant that "the bread becomes, will be and is the body of Christ", that is, after consecration it [figures]{g:figurare} [sacramentally]{g:sacramentaliter} the body of Christ. For this is a greater miracle than would be the conversion of which mention is made in the old law, indeed than would be a conversion with the substance of the bread ceasing, because in both cases the body of Christ is equally [truly]{g:vere} and [really]{g:realiter} present there; and besides this the [absurdities]{g:inconveniens}[^13] that the destruction of the bread posits are [disposed of]{g:tollere}. Praiseworthy, then, would be a change that posits so many goods and more, and empties out more evils. Nor do I know any other way to make the [decretal]{g:decretalis} of Innocent and the decree agree. For it is not reasonable that the decretal should be unmindful of the earlier decree, or that it should pronounce the earlier church heretical without cause. Hence it is clear [142] that it is consistent with this meaning that after this transubstantiation the transubstantiated bread remains; nevertheless I doubt[^14] that the church of Avignon with its [satraps]{g:satrapa} would accept this signification. But what care [need we have] about this, since the earlier church approving this was weightier, just as the person of Innocent III, with more than a thousand prelates (though not bishops but [unlettered]{g:ydiota} abbots), was weightier than the church of Gregory IX with the satraps of his sect?[^15] For from him the five books of decretals are said to take a new authority, the earlier being put to sleep, as Mahomet says that his law takes its authority from himself, not from the new law or the old. So, I say, the decretal letters have been collected from many ancient editions of the decretals of the earlier church, the truer or displeasing parts being left out[^16] and new or pleasing ones accepted; therefore it is a new work, of equal authority with the pope's other letters newly issued, because otherwise these latest decretals would take from the multitude of earlier popes a far greater authority than the gospel. For the first decretal letter, according to them, is of equal authority with the gospel.[^17]

## ¶5 · 142.22–142.26

> [p. 142] Ex quo videtur magnum esse peccatum ab ipsa aliquid abrogare. Sic ergo videtur et libros decretalium paris esse auctoritatis cum nudis epistolis pape et cardinalium quorum auctoritatem excedit sinodus facta per Nicholaum II.

From which it seems to be a great sin to abrogate anything from it.[^18] So, therefore, it seems that the books of decretals too are of equal authority with the bare letters of the pope and cardinals, whose authority the synod held by Nicholas II exceeds.

## ¶6 · 142.27–143.3

> [p. 142] Quantum ad illud trimembre argumentum quo suadetur incompetencia huius sentencie, patet ad primum quo dicitur quod hoc posicio impedit veneracionem debitam huic sacramento, patet quod falsum assumitur, sed nititur destruere ydolatriam qua nimis multi colunt corpus mathematicum false [p. 143] fictum per se existere tanquam Deum, et sic nititur cultum erroneum signo exhibitum destruere et totum cultum Christo quem fide ibi videmus colligere.

As to that three-membered argument by which [the case is made]{g:suadere} for the [unfittingness]{g:incompetencia} of this teaching, it is clear as to the first, in which it is said that "this [position]{g:posicio} impedes the veneration due to this [sacrament]{g:sacramentum}", it is clear that a falsehood is assumed;[^19] but it strives to destroy the idolatry by which exceedingly many worship as God a [mathematical body]{g:corpus-mathematicum} falsely [143] feigned to exist of itself; and so it strives to destroy the erroneous worship shown to a [sign]{g:signum}, and to gather the whole worship to Christ, whom by faith we see there.

## ¶7 · 143.4–143.26

> [p. 143] Et quantum ad occasionem ydolatrie, dictum est superius quomodo Doctor Subtilis destruit hanc evidenciam usque ad fundamentum in ea; nam ita pronus est populus ad colendum hoc sacramentum quod ista via ponit inpossibiliter corpus de genere quantitatis, sicut est pronus colere panem illum ut corpus Christi quod foret ex maiori similitudine minus inconveniens in natura. Et preterea per hoc quod nominatur panis foret populus pronior ex naturali ingenio ad cognoscendum quod non est corpus Christi. Adversarii enim infideliter abscondunt naturalem quidditatem sacramenti sic quod populus non cognoscit distinccionem eius a corpore Christi, in tantum quod ista secta verecundatur detegere suam ficticiam. Unde conveniencius fantasiaret ponendo quod sacramentum non est nisi species vel similitudo rei qualis est in speculo vel medio quam ponendo unam talem rem insolidam ut panis sine subiecto, cum magnus doctor Augustinus ponit philosophice quod illa terna dimensio non potest esse sine subiecto. Ex ista sentencia efficaciter tolleretur falsus cultus ydolatrandi, quo signum vel ymago vel accidens peius ymagine colitur tamquam Deus; sed donate istam iniuriam.

And as to the occasion of idolatry, it has been said above how the Subtle Doctor destroys this [evidence]{g:evidencia} down to the foundation in it;[^20] for the people are as prone to worship this sacrament, which this way impossibly posits [to be] a body of the genus of [quantity]{g:quantitas}, as they are prone to worship that bread as the body of Christ, which, from the greater likeness, would be less absurd in nature.[^21] And moreover, by this, that it is named bread, the people would be more prone, from natural wit, to know that it is not the body of Christ. For the adversaries faithlessly hide the natural quiddity of the sacrament, so that the people do not know its distinction from the body of Christ, so much so that this sect is ashamed to [expose]{g:detegere} its fiction. Hence it would [fancy]{g:fantasiare} more fittingly[^22] by positing that the sacrament is nothing but a [species]{g:species} or likeness of a thing, such as is in a mirror or a medium, than by positing one such [unsolid]{g:insolidus} thing as bread without a subject, since the great doctor Augustine posits philosophically that that threefold dimension cannot be without a subject.[^23] By this teaching the false worship of [idolatrizing]{g:ydolatrare} would effectively be disposed of, by which a sign or an image, or an accident worse than an image, is worshipped as God; but pardon this injury.[^24]

## Loserth's side-notes (PD, verbatim; not our translation)

- 140.9: To insist on the cessation of the bread is to add a new article to the creed.
- 140.18: The great saints, Ambrose &c. never call the host an accident; are they to be damned as heretics?
- 141.11: Therefore as to Innocent's decretal, we must suppose that it follows the sense of the primitive church.
- 142.27: 1. We would not lessen devotion to the Sacrament, but would prevent men from worshipping a mathematical figure.
- 143.4: The use of the word 'bread' would teach people not to worship the matter of the Sacrament.

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** The period runs *fuit statim … fabricatum mendaciter et destructum*, with *quia tempore Doctoris Subtilis* set between: the forgery was exposed at once, already in Scotus's time. The scandal is the one just stated (139.12–18), friars corrupting Thomas's writings.
[^2]: **C/T.** *Contrarii isti sentencie*: those opposed to the teaching ascribed to Thomas. *Receperunt*: E reads *reputant*, C has *reputarunt* in the margin ("held this order holy and pious"); the print translated. The order is Thomas's (*illius ordinis*, just after). *Scarioth*: Judas Iscariot. C has *sint* in an erasure; D *heretici heretica*.
[^3]: **C/T.** *Solucionem*: C's correction in the margin; ABCD read *salutacionem*. The "first resolution" is taken as Thomas's threefold verdict (unfitting, impossible, heretical, 137.12–138.8), which Wyclif now answers member by member.
[^4]: **S.** *Triplex fides ecclesie*: presumably the three creeds (Apostles', Nicene, Athanasian); to be checked (LEDGER K05g-1). C has *fidei* in the margin.
[^5]: **C.** *Color*: a plausible ground (the §C row), not a pretext.
[^6]: **S.** The decree is *Ego Berengarius*, of the Roman synod under Nicholas II: Gratian, *De cons.* D. 2 c. 42; *Omnia quecunque*, *De cons.* D. 2 c. 74 (Loserth; the modern form is ours) (LEDGER K05g-2, K05g-3). D reads *Similiter decretum*; E *remanent* and *ibi quecunque*.
[^7]: **T.** The print has *quotcunque ponit*; C, which read *ponit*, was corrected to *ponunt*, and the plural is translated ("all those who posit").
[^8]: **S/T.** *Cestrensis*: Ranulf Higden, *Polychronicon*, book 7; the place to be checked (LEDGER K05g-4). ABC read *Cistrensem*, D *Cestronensem*; ABCE omit the numeral of *Innocencium III*.
[^9]: **C.** *Viavit*: from *viare*, to travel the way; the church as wayfarer (cf. *via / patria*).
[^10]: **C.** Loserth's parenthesis closes before *sic dampnata*, which completes its own clause (*non enim est evidens quod … ecclesia … sic dampnata [sit]*); the English closes the parenthesis after it. ABC read *asserunt*.
[^11]: **S.** The church under Innocent III: the Fourth Lateran Council (1215), c. 1 *Firmiter* (X 1.1.1), with its *transsubstantiatis*; to be checked (LEDGER K05g-5). *Sensus*, the row "meaning". AB omit *de* at 141.13.
[^12]: **S.** Hugh (of St Victor) and Ambrose are named without works; the places to be checked (LEDGER K05g-6). AB read *nec ad illum*; C *concedit*; AB *sit, erit*; E *fiat* for *figurat*.
[^13]: **C.** *Inconveniencia*, the CONTEXTUAL row, here "absurdities" in argument; so also *minus inconveniens* at 143.9.
[^14]: **T.** ABCE read *diffiniendo* for *diffido*; the print translated.
[^15]: **S.** Innocent III's council (Lateran IV, 1215) and Gregory IX's five books of decretals (the *Liber extra*, 1234); the numbers at the council to be checked (LEDGER K05g-7). The description of the prelates is Wyclif's.
[^16]: **T.** Loserth notes that *veterioribus* ("the older parts") would be better than *verioribus*; his print is translated. B reads *atque*.
[^17]: **S.** The canonists' claim that decretal letters have the authority of the canons (Gratian, D. 19 c. 1?); to be checked (LEDGER K05g-8). C reads *secunda epistola*, ABE *secundum epistolam*.
[^18]: **C/T.** *Ab ipsa*: feminine, so the first decretal letter (*epistola*), not the gospel. D reads *aliud*; at 142.26 ABE read *sancta per* for *facta per*.
[^19]: **C/T.** The print repeats *patet* (*patet ad primum … patet quod*), kept in the English; *hoc posicio* has a neuter pronoun with a feminine noun. The first member is Thomas's at 137.14 (Loserth). The subject of *nititur* is the position Wyclif defends, that the bread remains. ABC read *in quo*; B *sicut nititur*.
[^20]: **S.** *Dictum est superius*: the earlier place where Scotus is brought against the argument from idolatry, to be checked (LEDGER K05g-9); Loserth refers to 137.17 (Thomas's argument).
[^21]: **C.** *Ita pronus … sicut est pronus*: "as prone … as". The first *quod* has *sacramentum* for antecedent (the sacrament that this way posits as a body of quantity); the second, worshipping the bread as the body. ABC omit *Christi* at 143.14.
[^22]: **C/G.** *Fantasiaret*: the sect is the subject; the verb of *fantasia* "imagination", rendered "fancy". *Species* here is the likeness in a mirror or medium, the species of optics, not the sacramental species. ABC read *conveniens*; E reads *insolitam* ("unusual") for *insolidam*.
[^23]: **S.** Augustine on the three dimensions of body that cannot be without a subject; the place to be checked (LEDGER K05g-10).
[^24]: **S.** 2 Cor. 12:13, Vulgate *donate mihi hanc iniuriam* (Douay–Rheims "pardon me this injury"); Wyclif's *istam*, without *mihi*, is translated.
