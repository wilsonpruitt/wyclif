# *De potestate pape*, chapter 10, part 4: the donation of Constantine and the caesarean jurisdiction

## ¶1 · 232.14–235.34

> [p. 232] Patet autem ex predictis quomodo Romanus pontifex fuit consocius aliis pontificibus usque ad dotacionem ecclesie et exhinc ex auctoritate cesaris cepit capitaliter dominari, unde decretum XCVI distinccione quod non possumus pro verecundia negare sic loquitur: Constantinus imperator quarto die sui baptismi privilegium Romane ecclesie pontifici contulit, ut in toto orbe pontifices ita hunc caput habeant sicut iudices regem. In quo privilegio ita inter cetera legitur: Tribuimus ei potestatem atque vigorem et honorificenciam imperialem, decernentes ut principatum teneat tam super quatuor sedes: Alexandrinam, Anthiochinam, Jerusolimitanam et Constantinopolitanam; celsior et princeps cunctis sacerdotibus tocius mundi existat. Et specificat posterius iurisdicionem sui episcopi, specificando patrias, Yndiam, Greciam et cetera secundum quatuor partes mundi. Propter hoc quidam vocant hanc potestatem iurisdicionis indulgencias et alia privilegia paparum cesarea. [p. 233] Et hinc nimirum defidant christiani in Yndia, in Grecia et aliis contratis hoc privilegium, scientes quod non est hominis dare potestatem spiritualem, sed solius Dei, ut dicit Lynconiensis, Epistola XXIII, et sic postponunt indulgencias suas et privilegia, scientes quod secundum dignitatem persone habebit a Deo indulgenciam, nec suum est talem concedere, nisi instrumentaliter in quantum fuerit fidelis Domini promulgator; et aliter illudit populo. Quam sentenciam audivi de papa nostro Urbano VI ipsum dixisse cardinalibus Gregorii qui excessit decalogum ac quia increpans eorum limitavit eos ad vitam apostolicam primevam, conspiraverant contra eum, eligendo sibi Robertum Gilbonensem, virum ut dicitur dissolutum, superbum, bellicosum et legis Christi ignarum. Ideo procul ab Anglicis scola sua, cum Luce XVII, 23 de tali divisione dicit Christus: Nolite abire neque sectemini; nolite abire a religione primeva quam ab apostolis didicistis, neque sectemini talem ducem, cum duceret edos suos in precipicium principis tenebrarum. Sequamur autem Urbanum, de quanto precurrat reccius viam Christi et non ulterius; ymo supposito quod dictus Robertus miraculose sic currat, cum eo debemus humiliter sequi ambos, quia ut recitatur a Gregorio, XXIIª distinccione, De Constantinopolitana sedes vel loca non distrahunt, quin debemus in bono indifferenter imitari meliores; cum, inquit, culpa nou exigit omnes secundum racionem humilitatis equales sumus. Licet igitur multi [p. 234] Romani pontifices sint preeminenter boni, de quibus intelliguntur leges et dicta doctorum, multi tamen ignari vite apostolice mundanum dominium siciebant; hoc autem et consensum eius ac aliorum errorum debemus fugere ut venenum, et specialiter errorem de indulgenciis et de ficta potestate iurisdicionis sive regiminis quam dicunt ex auctoritate imperatoris se extendere per totam habitabilem nostram singulariter quoad Romanum pontificem. Et ex ista radice fingunt quod solus ipse potest indiffinite concedere indulgenciam et alia spiritualia dona Dei. Et error huiusmodi, ut estimo, est causa, quare nec imperator nec papa sufficit exercere in Yndia, in Grecia, vel Yudea et aliis patriis, dissencientibus quibusdam suis erroribus et quibusdam Antichristi legibus, iurisdicionis officium. Error enim modicus in principio inducit maximum consequenter. Si igitur solus Romanus pontifex habet iurisdicionem universalem super Yndos, Saracenos et Barbaros, quare non exercet eam in eos, sicut apostoli fecerunt in infideles populos et rebelles? Si tantum habet iurisdiccionem super eos qui volunt obedire suo dominio, tunc est papalis iurisdiccio mirabiliter diminuta et dependens a voluntate populi ab eis capitur. Illa autem est alterius racionis quam potestas regiminis apostolica quam imperator non potest concedere, sed divisa per istam divisionem bipapalem variaretur ut populus sibi consenciens, vel diminueretur finaliter ad non quantum; quod superbia non sinit concedere, ideo dicit quod habet potestatem regiminis super totum mundum. Ymo cum illa potestas sit subiective [p. 235] in spiritu, videtur quod manet perpetuo ut caracter; et sic haberemus plurimos papas simul, et sic pape moderni redderent se nimis dampnabiliores ex ocio potestatis; cum enim, ut inquiunt, habent tantam potestatem ut Petrus et Paulus simul, ymo ut Ihesus habebat humanitus, nec potest fingi defectus ex Deo, sed ex eis, quare non fructificant ut ante dotacionem cum huiusmodi potestate? Videtur quod ex potestatis abusu durissimum iudicium ex confessione propria illis fiet. Petrus enim veniet ad iudicium cum suis conversis et quilibet apostolorum cum suis, ut dicit beatus Gregorius super evangelia Omelia XVII, sed talis antichristus veniet cum suis perversis condempnandus. Nec valet antichristiana ficticia qua dicitur papam habere tantam potestatem secundum legem Christi, sicut apostoli ad regulandum populum et insuper habet potestatem ad regulandum populum secundum leges quas ipse condidit et illi regimini propter variacionem temporis oportet attendere, quia nichil patencius indicaret condiciones filii perdicionis, qui secundum Apostolum extollitur super omne quod dicitur Deus, quia certum est quod Christus non potest tales leges condere, sic regere vel taliter dominari. Sacerdotes itaque Christi habent potestatem ante istam iurisdicionem cesaream edificandi populum, ubicumque terrarum quantum sufficiunt, predicando, sancte conversando vel instar sanctorum doctorum scriptis sentenciam catholicam commendando; talem autem potestatem regiminis independentem ab invicem habuerunt apostoli plus et minus, Paulus ut estimo magis Petro.

And it is clear from what has been said how the [Roman pontiff]{g:romanus-pontifex} was a fellow of the other pontiffs until the [endowment]{g:dotacio} of the [church]{g:ecclesia}, and from then on, by Caesar's authority, began to exercise lordship [as head]{g:capitaliter}; hence the decree in distinction 96, which we cannot for shame deny, speaks thus: "The emperor Constantine on the fourth day after his baptism conferred a [privilege]{g:privilegium} on the pontiff of the Roman church, that throughout the world the pontiffs should have him as [head]{g:caput}, as judges have the king. In which privilege, among other things, it reads thus: 'We grant him [power]{g:potestas} and vigour and imperial honour, decreeing that he hold the principate as well over the four sees, Alexandria, Antioch, Jerusalem and Constantinople; let him be loftier than, and prince over, all the [priests]{g:sacerdos} of the whole world.'"[^1] And afterwards he specifies the jurisdiction of his bishop, specifying the lands, India, Greece and the rest, according to the four parts of the world.[^2] For this reason some call this [power of jurisdiction]{g:potestas-iurisdiccionis}, the [indulgences]{g:indulgencie} and other privileges of the popes, caesarean. [233] And hence it is no wonder that Christians in India, in Greece and other countries distrust this privilege, knowing that "it is not man's to give spiritual power, but God's alone", as Lincoln says, Letter 23,[^3] and so they set aside his indulgences and privileges, knowing that one will have indulgence from God according to the worthiness of the person, and that it is not his to grant it except instrumentally, in so far as he is a faithful proclaimer of the Lord; and otherwise he makes sport of the people. This [teaching]{g:sentencia} I heard that our [pope]{g:papa} Urban VI spoke to the cardinals of Gregory, who transgressed the decalogue; and because, rebuking their [avarice or luxury], he [limited]{g:limitare} them to the first apostolic life, they conspired against him, electing for themselves Robert of Geneva, a man, as is said, dissolute, proud, warlike and ignorant of the [law of Christ]{g:lex-christi}.[^4] Therefore far from the English be his school, since in Luke 17:23 Christ says of such a division: "Go ye not away, nor follow"; do not go away from the first religion that you learned from the apostles, nor follow such a leader, since he would lead his kids into the precipice of the prince of darkness.[^5] But let us follow Urban, in so far as he runs ahead more rightly on the way of Christ, and no further;[^6] indeed, supposing that the said Robert should miraculously run so, we ought humbly to follow both, him together [with Urban], because, as is related from Gregory in distinction 22, *De Constantinopolitana*, sees or places do not draw [us] apart so that we should not imitate the better in good without distinction: "when," he says, "fault does not require [otherwise], we are all equal according to the reckoning of humility."[^7] Although therefore many [234] Roman pontiffs are eminently good, of whom the laws and the sayings of the doctors are understood, yet many, ignorant of the apostolic life, thirsted for worldly [lordship]{g:dominium}; and this, and consent to it and to other errors, we ought to flee like poison,[^8] and especially the error about indulgences and about the feigned power of jurisdiction or rule, which they say extends by the emperor's authority over the whole of our inhabited world uniquely as regards the Roman pontiff. And from this root they feign that he alone can grant without limit indulgence and the other spiritual gifts of God. And an error of this kind, as I judge, is the cause why neither emperor nor pope is able to exercise the office of [jurisdiction]{g:iurisdiccio} in India, in Greece, or Judea and other lands, since certain men dissent from some of his errors and from certain laws of Antichrist. For a small error in the beginning brings on the greatest in consequence.[^9] If therefore the Roman pontiff alone has universal jurisdiction over Indians, Saracens and barbarians, why does he not exercise it upon them, as the apostles did upon [unbelieving]{g:infidelis} and rebellious peoples? If he has jurisdiction only over those who wish to obey his lordship, then papal jurisdiction is wonderfully diminished and, depending on the will of the people, is received from them. And that [jurisdiction] is of another kind than the apostolic [power of rule]{g:potestas-regiminis}, which the emperor cannot grant; but, divided by this two-pope division, it would vary as the people consenting to him [varies], or would at last be diminished to no quantity at all;[^10] which pride does not allow [him] to grant, and so he says that he has power of rule over the whole world. Indeed, since that power is in the spirit as in its subject, [235] it seems that it remains perpetually, like a [character]{g:caracter}; and so we should have very many popes at once,[^11] and so the modern popes would make themselves far more damnable through the idleness of their power. For since, as they say, they have as much power as Peter and Paul together, indeed as Jesus had in his humanity, and no defect can be feigned on God's side but on theirs, why do they not bear fruit with power of this kind as before the endowment? It seems that, from the abuse of power, "a most severe judgment" will be passed on them by their own confession.[^12] For Peter will come to the judgement with his converts, and each of the apostles with his own, as blessed Gregory says on the gospels, Homily 17, but such an antichrist will come with his perverted ones, to be condemned.[^13] Nor does the antichristian [fiction]{g:ficticia} hold by which it is said that the pope has as much power according to the law of Christ as the apostles to regulate the people, and besides has power to regulate the people according to laws that he himself has made, and that because of the change of the times one must attend to that rule;[^14] for nothing would show more plainly the conditions of the "son of perdition", who according to the Apostle "is lifted up above all that is called God", because it is certain that Christ cannot make such laws, rule thus, or [exercise lordship]{g:dominari} in such a way.[^15] The priests of Christ, therefore, have power, prior to this caesarean jurisdiction, to build up the people wherever on earth, as far as they are able, by preaching, by living holily, or, like the holy doctors, by commending the catholic teaching in writings; and the apostles had such power of rule, independent of one another, more and less, Paul, as I judge, more than Peter.

## Notes

[^1]: **S/T.** Gratian, *Decretum* D.96 c.14 *Constantinus*, the Donation of Constantine (Loserth 232.18: "Cap. XIV. Loosely quoted"; the modern form is ours; LEDGER P10d-1). *Tam super quatuor sedes* has no answering *quam*: the Donation goes on "as over all the churches of God in the whole world" (LEDGER P10d-2), which the quotation here leaves out; we translate *tam* "as well". The manuscripts note in the margin (232.19): *Pape institucio a cesare*.
[^2]: **T.** *Yndiam*: Loserth's foot reads "Indiam; recte: Judeam" (232.30): the Donation names Judaea. The print is translated; Wyclif's own argument takes "India" up below (233.1, 234.12).
[^3]: **S.** Grosseteste, *Epistolae*, ed. Luard, p. 90 (Loserth 233.4; LEDGER P10d-3).
[^4]: **T/S/C.** "[avarice or luxury]": Loserth's supplement after *eorum* ("adde: avariciam vel luxuriam", 233.13); the print does not construe without a noun. *Qui excessit* is singular: we take Gregory (XI) as its subject; the manuscripts carry a marginal note here that Loserth's foot gives only in part ("… falsus Gregorius XI", 233.12). *Robertum Gilbonensem*: Robert of Geneva, elected by the rebel cardinals in 1378 as Clement VII (LEDGER P10d-4). *Limitare* in its restricting sense, "limited".
[^5]: **S.** Luke 17:23. Wyclif's *abire* stands for the Vulgate's *ire* (DR "Go ye not after, nor follow them"), and his gloss turns on it ("go away from the first religion"); his Latin is translated. *Edos*: the kids (goats) of Matt. 25:33.
[^6]: **T.** *Precurrat* is Loserth's correction; the manuscripts read *precucurrit* (233.23).
[^7]: **S/T.** Gratian, *Decretum* D.22 c.4 *De Constantinopolitana*, under Gregory's name (LEDGER P10d-5; Loserth's foot at 233.27 reads "Cap. IX" in our OCR, to be checked). The bracketed "[otherwise]" fills the ellipsis of *cum culpa non exigit*.
[^8]: **T.** *Sint* (234.1): Loserth's foot "recte: fuerint" ("have been"); the print is translated. *Consensum* is his correction; the manuscripts read *consensui* (234.4).
[^9]: **S.** The proverb from Aristotle, *De caelo* 1.5, 271b8–13 (LEDGER P10d-6).
[^10]: **T/C.** *Bipapalem*: the division of the schism of 1378, two popes. Loserth reports *Codd.: ut* against a printed *vel* at 234.31 (that is, *ut diminueretur* or *vel populus*); the print is translated. *Ad non quantum*: to nothing, "no quantity".
[^11]: **C.** *Subiective in spiritu*: the power inheres in the soul as in its subject, so (like the character of orders) it cannot be lost: a deposed pope would remain pope, hence "many popes at once".
[^12]: **S.** Wis. 6:6: "a most severe judgment shall be for them that bear rule." Loserth marks no italics here; we quote the scriptural words.
[^13]: **S.** Gregory the Great, *Homiliae in Evangelia* 17 (Loserth: "Gregor. l. c. Opp. (ed. Migne) II, 1148"; LEDGER P10d-7).
[^14]: **C.** The objectors' "fiction" claims a double power: the apostles' power under the law of Christ, and a power to rule by the pope's own laws, required by the change of the times.
[^15]: **S.** 2 Thess. 2:3–4 (Loserth 235.23). Wyclif's *super* is the Vulgate's *supra*; DR is kept.
