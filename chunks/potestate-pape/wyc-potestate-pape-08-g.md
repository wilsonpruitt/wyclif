# *De potestate pape*, chapter 8, part 7: antichrist seeks glory from men; popes struck down

## ¶1 · 188.18–190.15

> [p. 188] Tercium testimonium notificans Antichristi discipulos est quod sint gloriam ab invicem exspectantes et gloriam, que a solo Deo est, non querentes, ut docet Veritas Joh. V; nam in hoc sunt Christo contrarii, qui instanter fecit actus iustissimos, et correspondenter ad illos exspectavit gloriam a solo Deo. Unde in predicto capitulo Johannis de Antichristo: Amen amen dico vobis, non potest filius a se ipso facere quicquam, nisi quod viderit patrem facientem. Ex quo patet quod oportet omnia opera Christi esse iustissima, et sic intelligitur auctoritas Christi sequens: Iudicium meum iustum est, quia non quero voluntatem meam sed voluntatem eius, qui misit me, patris. Et patet veritas connexionis sequentis: Si ego [p. 189] testimonium perhibeo de me ipso, testimonium meum non est verum, sed pater testimonium perhibet. Si eciam Baptista sine Christi exaccione testimonium perhibuit Veritati: Ego, inquit, habeo testimonium maius Johanne, opera enim que dedit michi pater ut perficiam; ipsa opera que ego facio testimonium perhibent de me. Ex istis textibus eliciunt sancti versucias antichristi, ipse enim summe sitit honorem et lucrum mundanum, cum Christus omnino fecit contrarium. Ideo docet Augustinus ubi supra quomodo Antichristus nitetur quod sine eius seculari obediencia non est salus, quod cum non habet testimonium ex scriptura, fingit in multis epistolis testimonium a se ipso: Si boni sumus, inquit Augustinus, facite quod scriptum est: Imitatores mei estote, sicut et ego Christi. Si autem mali sumus, quod Deus magis facit, sic nec deserti estis nec sine Consilio remansistis; audite dicentem: Que dicunt facite, que autem faciunt nolite facere. Sed prelati contra hec: Nisi boni fuerimus, peristis. Sed hic est alius, qui veniet in nomine Christi. Sed hunc Antichristum alloquor, igitur vita mea ex te pendebit et salus mea ex te religabitur: Sed fundamentum aliud nemo potest ponere nisi Christum, veni igitur mecum, Antichriste, si vis esse super petram, et noli velle esse michi pro petra. Et patet ex textu Johannis sequenti, quomodo Christus nititur testimonio iustorum operum et scripture, non autem solis verbis hominum, ut superius dicit Crysostomus esse consuetudinem Antichristi; hec considera et applica ad facta Romanorum pontificum et vide si consonant ritibus antichristi, [p. 190] quod si uterque istorum dicit conformiter ad illam extravagantem decretalem Bonifacii VIII Unam Sanctam quod nisi quis obediat sibi ut capiti tocius ecclesie, non est de numero salvandorum, oportet utrumque relinquere tamquam alterum Antichristum. Multi enim erant tales de Romanis pontificibus, unde narrat predictus cronicans in libro suo de Floribus Historiarum, quomodo anno gracie DCV papa Sabinianus liberalitatem beati Gregorii predecessoris sui derogavit et sub hoc obtentu manum egenis subtraxit, Gregorius eum per visum ter pro culpa tenacie et huius derogacionis increpavit, et quarto horribiliter increpans et comminans in capite percussit, quo dolore vexatus paulo post obiit.

The third testimony, which makes known the disciples of Antichrist, is that they expect glory from one another and do not seek the glory that is from God alone, as the Truth teaches in John 5;[^1] for in this they are contrary to Christ, who earnestly did the most just acts and, in correspondence with them, expected glory from God alone. Hence in the aforesaid chapter of John on Antichrist: "Amen, amen, I say unto you, the Son cannot do any thing of himself, but what he seeth the Father doing." From which it is clear that all the works of Christ must be most just; and so is understood the following [authority]{g:auctoritas} of Christ: "My judgment is just: because I seek not my own will, but the will of him that sent me", the Father.[^2] And the truth of the following conditional is clear: "If I [189] bear witness of myself, my witness is not true"; but the Father bears witness.[^3] Even if the Baptist, without Christ's demanding it, bore witness to the Truth, "I", he says, "have a greater testimony than that of John: for the works which the Father hath given me to perfect, the works themselves which I do, give testimony of me." From these texts the saints draw out the cunning of antichrist; for he thirsts supremely for honour and worldly gain, whereas Christ did entirely the contrary. Therefore Augustine teaches in the place cited how Antichrist will strive [to make out] that without secular obedience to him there is no salvation; and since he has no testimony for this from scripture, he fabricates in many letters a testimony from himself:[^4] "If we are good," says Augustine, "do what is written: 'Be ye followers of me, as I also am of Christ.' But if we are evil, which God rather does, even so you are neither deserted nor left without counsel: hear him who says, 'What they say, do; but what they do, do not do.'[^5] But the [prelates]{g:prelatus} [say] against this: 'Unless we are good, you have perished.' But this is the other who will come in the name of Christ.[^6] But I address this Antichrist: so then my life will hang on you, and my salvation will be bound to you? But 'other foundation no man can lay' but Christ.[^7] Come then with me, Antichrist, if you wish to be upon the rock, and do not wish to be a rock for me." And it is clear from the following text of John how Christ relies on the testimony of just works and of scripture, but not on the bare words of men, which Chrysostom said above is the custom of Antichrist.[^8] Consider these things and apply them to the deeds of the [Roman pontiffs]{g:romanus-pontifex}, and see whether they accord with the rites of antichrist. [190] And if each of them says, in conformity with that extravagant decretal of Boniface VIII, *Unam sanctam*, that unless someone obeys him as [head]{g:caput} of the whole [church]{g:ecclesia} he is not of the number of those to be saved, we must leave each of them as the other Antichrist.[^9] For many of the Roman pontiffs were such. Hence the aforesaid chronicler relates in his book *Flores historiarum* how in the year of grace 605 [Pope]{g:papa} Sabinian disparaged the liberality of blessed Gregory his predecessor and, under this pretext, withdrew his hand from the needy; Gregory three times rebuked him in a vision for the fault of tight-fistedness and of this disparagement, and the fourth time, rebuking and threatening him horribly, struck him on the head; and, afflicted by that pain, he died soon after.[^10]

## ¶2 · 190.16–191.5

> [p. 190] Item, simile narrat Cestrensis libro suo VII MCCVI ad papam Innocencium misit Robertus Lynconiensis epistolam invectivam satis et tonantem, pro eo quod ecclesias Anglie indebitis exaccionibus vexare videretur; hac de causa ad curiam vocatus est, et cum ibi molestaretur, appellavit constanter a curia Innocencii ad tribunal Christi; unde contigit, quod Roberto in Anglia obeunte audita est vox in curia pape: Veni miser ad iudicium; repertusque est in crastino papa exanimis, quasi cuspide baculi in latere percussus. Qui licet perspicacius effulserit miraculis, transferri tamen in sanctorum cathalogo a curia non est permissus. Nec est putandum istos sanctos iregularitatem vel offensam Dei incurrere ex percussione letali summorum pontificum; qui viderunt quod erant precipui [p. 191] Antichristi, persequentes ecclesiam, et receperunt mandatum a Domino sic tractare hostes ecclesie, pro cuius prosperitate secundum religionem et institucionem Christi, dum erant vivi, a Deo zelaverunt.

Again, the Chester [chronicler] relates a like thing in his seventh book: [in] 1206 Robert of Lincoln sent to Pope Innocent a letter invective enough and thundering, because he seemed to vex the churches of England with undue exactions.[^11] For this reason he was summoned to the curia, and when he was harassed there, he appealed steadfastly from the court of Innocent to the tribunal of Christ. Whence it happened that, when Robert died in England, a voice was heard in the pope's curia: "Come, wretch, to judgment"; and on the morrow the pope was found lifeless, as if struck in the side with the point of a staff. And Robert, although he shone forth very conspicuously with miracles, was yet not permitted by the curia to be transferred into the catalogue of the saints. Nor is it to be thought that these saints incurred irregularity, or an offence against God, by the lethal striking of the highest pontiffs;[^12] for they saw that these were chief [191] antichrists, persecuting the church, and they received a mandate from the Lord so to treat the enemies of the church, for whose prosperity, according to the religion and institution of Christ, they were zealous from God while they lived.[^13]

## ¶3 · 191.6–191.28

> [p. 191] Item, notat lex ecclesie, quomodo Marcellinus episcopus urbis Rome, qui foret dux christiani exercitus thurificavit ydolis, ut patet distinccione XXI Nunc autem divina, ubi dicit glossa ordinaria quod consilio congregato Confessus quod fecerat et in se tulit sentenciam deposicionis, et post (ut reor) exutus ab illo officio veniens coram Dyocleciano, confessus est se esse christianum et martyr effectus est; considerans enim suam blasfemam vecordiam in papatu et preparans se per satisfaccionem ad martyrium, pro nichilo habuit huiusmodi dignitatem; diebus autem nostris, ut plus involverunt se seculari dominio sunt peiores, quia Christus non diceret de eis: Omnia quecumque dixerint vobis, facite. Nec illi ut sacerdotes legis veteris putant habere vitam perpetuam in scriptura, cum ex eius ignorancia multi antichristi cadunt in multas blasfemias et ex scripture contemptu multos, qui pie vivere volunt in Christo persequuntur, nituntur enim viis variis facere, ut credantur quod sint caput universalis ecclesie, et ipsa indubie est tunc cum paribus acephala quoad ipsos.

Again, the law of the church notes how Marcellinus, [bishop]{g:episcopus} of the city of Rome, who should have been the leader of the Christian army, offered incense to idols, as is clear in Distinction 21, *Nunc autem divina*, where the ordinary gloss says that, a council having been gathered, he confessed what he had done and pronounced on himself the [sentence]{g:sentencia} of deposition;[^14] and afterwards (as I think), stripped of that office, coming before Diocletian, he confessed that he was a Christian and was made a martyr. For, considering his blasphemous [faint-heartedness]{g:vecordia} in the papacy, and preparing himself by [satisfaction]{g:satisfaccio} for martyrdom, he held such a dignity for nothing. But in our days, the more they have involved themselves in secular [lordship]{g:dominium}, the worse they are; for Christ would not say of them: "All things whatsoever they shall say to you, do."[^15] Nor do they, like the priests of the old law, think to have life everlasting in scripture, since from ignorance of it many antichrists fall into many blasphemies, and from contempt of scripture persecute many who "will live [piously]{g:pie} in Christ";[^16] for they strive by various ways to bring it about that they are believed to be head of the universal church, and then that church is undoubtedly, along with its peers, [acephalous]{g:acephala} as far as they are concerned.[^17]

## ¶4 · 191.29–192.9

> [p. 191] Sed Augustinus in De Verbis Domini ubi supra: Quid, inquit, tibi fecit ecclesia ut eam velis quodammodo decollare? qui, inquit, sic decollare [p. 192] vult et corpus et caput conatur occidere. Cum enim non sit communicacio Christi ad Belial, quomodo Christo tam contrarius in vita et moribus erit ex eius participio caput ecclesie? Erubescunt, inquit, negare Christum, hoc tamen maius est, cum Christum non vidimus sed credimus, verba autem illius nobiscum sunt, in quibus ipsum negamus, comparate itaque vos Iudeis, illi contempserunt pendentem in ligno, vos contempnitis sedentem in celo.

But Augustine, in *On the Words of the Lord*, in the place cited: "What," he says, "has the church done to you, that you wish in a manner to behead her? He who wishes so to behead her," he says, [192] "strives to kill both body and head."[^18] For since there is no fellowship of Christ with Belial, how will one so contrary to Christ in life and morals be, by [participation]{g:participium} in him, head of the church?[^19] "They blush," he says, "to deny Christ; yet this is greater, since we have not seen Christ but believe, and his words are with us, in which we deny him. Compare yourselves therefore with the Jews: they despised him hanging on the tree; you despise him sitting in heaven."

## Notes

[^1]: **S.** John 5:44 in indirect speech (DR "who receive glory one from another: and the glory which is from God alone, you do not seek"); Wyclif writes *exspectantes* for the Vulgate's *accipitis*.
[^2]: **S.** John 5:19 and 5:30 (Loserth's references, 188.25, 188.29). Wyclif adds *patris* to 5:30; the English keeps it outside the quotation. *Auctoritas* here is an authoritative text.
[^3]: **S/C.** John 5:31; *sed pater testimonium perhibet* is Wyclif's summary of 5:32 ("There is another that beareth witness of me"). *Connexio*: the conditional proposition, "if … then". The quotation that follows is John 5:36 (Loserth's reference).
[^4]: **S/C.** Augustine, Sermo 129.8 (Loserth: Opp. tom. V, 636; PL 38, 724; LEDGER P08g-1). *Sine eius seculari obediencia*: "without secular obedience to him", i.e. obedience in temporal matters; *in multis epistolis* is Wyclif's.
[^5]: **S/T.** 1 Corinthians 11:1 (DR) and Matthew 23:3 in Augustine's short form (DR "whatsoever they shall say to you, observe and do: but according to their works do ye not"). *Quod Deus magis facit* hardly construes; Augustine's text has no such clause: *simus iniqui, et hoc magis ipse scit … Si autem mali sumus, nec sic deserti estis* (Sermo 129.8, PL 38, 724), and Wyclif's words look like a garbling of *hoc magis ipse scit*, "[God] knows it better" (LEDGER P08g-2). The print is translated. B omits *fuerimus* in the next sentence (apparatus 189.21).
[^6]: **C.** *In nomine Christi*: John 5:43, which Augustine is applying, has *in nomine suo*, "in his own name"; the print is translated.
[^7]: **C/S.** *Igitur vita mea … religabitur* is read as an indignant question. 1 Corinthians 3:11, shortened (Vulgate *praeter id quod positum est, quod est Christus Jesus*).
[^8]: **S.** Chrysostom was cited above in chapter 7: on 2 Tim. 3:1 (hom. 8), the devil and his members "show nothing in works, but promise great things falsely in words" (148.6–17), and "Antichrist relies on the bare leaves of words" (149.25–27, untraced) (LEDGER P08g-3). "The following text of John" is John 5:36–39.
[^9]: **S/C/R.** Boniface VIII, *Unam sanctam* (1302); Loserth: *Extrav. comm.* 1.8.1 (modern form ours; Friedberg II, 1245–46; LEDGER P08g-4). *Tamquam alterum Antichristum*: "as the other", the *alius* of John 5:43 who comes in his own name. A and B note here: *Obediencia pape nota bene*.
[^10]: **S/T/R.** Loserth refers the story to Higden's *Polychronicon* V, 412 (LEDGER P08g-5), although Wyclif names the *Flores historiarum*. The date: Loserth prints DCV; the manuscripts read LXVII (apparatus 190.9). A and B note: "St Gregory the pope killed his successor."
[^11]: **S/R.** Ranulf Higden of Chester, *Polychronicon* VIII, 240–42 (Loserth; Higden names *Innocentium quartum*; LEDGER P08g-6). Robert Grosseteste, bishop of Lincoln. Loserth: the date 1206 is so in the text, and the pope is Innocent IV, not Innocent III. A and B note: "Lincoln appealed to the tribunal of Christ."
[^12]: **C.** *Irregularitas*: the canonical impediment incurred by shedding blood, which bars from orders and their exercise.
[^13]: **C.** *A Deo zelaverunt*: "were zealous from God", i.e. with a zeal given by God; *pro Deo* may have been meant. The print is translated.
[^14]: **S/C.** Decretum D. 21 c. 7 *Nunc autem* (Loserth "Cap. VII"; modern form ours) with the ordinary gloss, ed. Venice 1595, p. 91 (LEDGER P08g-7). *Consilio* is read as *concilio*, "council". *Sentencia* here is the judicial sentence (CONTEXTUAL). B reads *exustus* for *exutus* (apparatus 191.12).
[^15]: **S/T.** Matthew 23:3 shortened. Loserth prints *seculari dominio*; the manuscripts read *in seculari* (apparatus 191.18).
[^16]: **S.** Echoes John 5:39 ("you think in them to have life everlasting") and 2 Timothy 3:12 ("all that will live godly in Christ Jesus").
[^17]: **C/R.** *Cum paribus*: "along with its peers", probably the church under the other claimant as well; possibly "other things being equal". A and B note *Caput ecclesie* here (apparatus 191.26).
[^18]: **S.** Augustine, Sermo 129.9 (PL 38, 725), the sermon of the *ubi supra*: *Quid tibi fecit Ecclesia, ut eam velis quodam modo decollare? … Qui decollare vult, et caput et corpus conatur occidere. Erubescunt negare Christum … illi contempserunt pendentem in ligno, vos contemnitis sedentem in coelo*; Loserth's "Sermo 130 … Opp. tom. V, 637" and "Sermo XLVI, Opp. tom. V, 230" do not contain it (LEDGER P08g-8). The italics run the second quotation from *vult* to *occidere* and the last from *negare* to *celo*.
[^19]: **S.** 2 Corinthians 6:15 (DR "what concord hath Christ with Belial?"), with *communicacio* for the Vulgate's *conventio*.
