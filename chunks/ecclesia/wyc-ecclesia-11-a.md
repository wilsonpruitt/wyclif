# *De ecclesia*, chapter 11, part 1: how the Westminster privilege is to be read: its interpreter, its ends, and no privilege against the common law

## ¶1 · 227.20–232.33

> [p. 227] Cum elemosina tantorum principum non debet omnino extingui propter sinistram interpretacionem abutencium, sicut nec veritas scripture sacre frustrari debet a suo lumine propter hereticos qui sinistrum sensum occasione male accepta ex ipsa eliciunt, videndum est ad quem sensum dictum privilegium debet concipi, ut remaneat tam re quam nomine privilegium utrobique. Igitur inprimis, cum illius sit interpretari leges ac privilegia cuius est condere (ut patet XXVª q. I, Ideo permittente, et V° Decretalium, De Sentencia [p. 228] Excomunicacionis, Inter alia), rex autem Anglie debet concedere et condere leges tales privatas de suo regali dominio ad edificacionem ecclesie: ideo capiendum est tamquam per se notum quod regis est illas leges privatas ac elemosinas interpretari et in rectitudine sua defendere. Romanus autem pontifex super isto consultus, et sacerdos suus legius requisitus de interpretacione sensus catholici, tenentur de lege evangelii quantum sciunt humiliter declarare; quibus non est parendum nisi de quanto sentenciam suam fundaverint in scriptura sacra. Unde secundo oportet supponere quod sacram theologiam necesse sit omnem talem scienciam principaliter regulare; patet ex hoc quod illa sola principaliter docet viam qua celeste coniugium honoratur, sed iuxta hunc finem regulandum est omne factum viatoris legitimum; ergo conclusio. Et ista est sentencia XXª dist. De quibus, ubi Innocencius papa docet quinque gradus procedendi in dubiis discernendis, primo in quantum recurrendum est ad scripture auctoritatem quam episcopi et specialiter papa usque ad sanguinem tenentur defendere, ut patet XXVª, q. I. Sunt quidam. Secundo si ex scriptura sacra in lingua latina solucio dubii non clare eluceat, recurratur ad scripta greca, ut vel sic divina sentencia habeatur. Tercio si nec sic lux veritatis appareat, ad ecclesiasticas historias catholicas congregatas a sanctis doctoribus, mitte manum. Quarto si nec illis eluceat difficultas que queritur, sanctorum exempla perspicaciter recordare, exempla enim vite sanctorum debent esse nobis speculum conversandi. [p. 229] Quinto si omnibus istis inspectis questionis qualitas non lucescat, per decretum seniorum provincie ad hoc congregatorum mediante Dei assistencia qui non potest suis deficere, decidatur. Tercio suppono: Cum dictum privilegium non fundatur in iure canonico vel civili, sed specialiter in legibus regni nostri et cartis illius ecclesie superius recitatis, quod notetur forma et finis gracia cuius hec privilegia sunt concessa (finis enim ex secundo Phisycorum mensurat omnia media ad eundem), in privilegiis autem regis Edgari triplex finis exprimitur, scilicet reverencia Dei in reliquiis sui apostoli, quies monachorum ibi degencium et honor sanctitatis illius peculiaris basilice in qua degant speciales oratores pro rege et regno de quorum elemosinis ad hoc specialiter sustentantur. Unde in bulla Nicolai III sic habetur: Monachi ibidem nulli omnino persone subdantur nisi regi. Ex quo videtur quod incole illius loci, non solum quia legii homines regis, nec solum quia speciales eius elemosinarii sed ex mandato pape regi suo specialiter sunt subiecti; nam secundum beatum Gregorium multiplicitas beneficiorum auget obligacionem beneficiati ad suum datarium. Ex quo videtur, cum monachi illius loci regi tam singulariter sunt subiecti, nec hospites vel fugitivi sui debent esse plus exempti vel privilegiati quam monachi a quibus emanat privilegium, videtur quod omnes profugi, de quanto sunt in illo loco, regi Anglie quadam preeminencia sunt subiecti ex hoc quod ab eo ibi recipiunt beneficium singulare; nec consonat fidei religionis Christi quod monachus, si per possibile occiderit hominem in dicto loco, erit post liberior et plus percipiens de fructu privilegii, quam quando fuit immunis quoad Deum et [p. 230] hominem magis capax. Nec videtur racionabile quin, supposita capacitate in monacho vel quocunque famulo illius loci, habebit fructum privilegii post delictum eciam pocius quam unus extraneus; aliter enim omnis contumaciter ibi quiescens in crimine ut sic indisponeret se ad habendum privilegium supradictum. Istis premissis videtur mihi (ut alias) quod, licet per totam Angliam indulgeri debet omnimoda remissio, de quanto sonat in misericordiam et non ultra, tamen pro loco Westmonasterii servari debet generalis talis remissio virtute singularis privilegii pie concessi a regibus illi loco. Prima pars patet ex hoc quod privilegia cum (in quantum huiusmodi) sonant per se bonum, sunt racionabiliter amplexanda; nam melius esset quod tota ecclesia haberet privilegium, hoc est, legem ad sui commodum quam quod aliqua pars ecclesie a privilegio vero deficiat, quia de tanto peccat vel punitur de quanto deficit a plenis privilegiis beatorum. Et patet quod nullum est privilegium, nisi de quanto sonat in elemosinam vel opus misericordie, quia non est privilegium, nisi de quanto misericorditer datur a Deo, vergens homini ad profectum. Et secunda pars patet ex hoc quod oportet concessionem regis habere specialem efficaciam ibi diligencius observandam, nam aliter foret frustra. Et ex istis patet quod nullum privilegium est iuri communi contrarium, nam si foret, tunc esset contra iusticiam et per consequens lex iniqua et non privilegium. Unde videtur mihi omnino insipidum dictum quorundam dicencium quod non esset privilegium nisi foret iuri communi contrarium. Nam licita variacio ritus pro loco et tempore non est contrarium veritati et per consequens nulli legi. Ideo oportet privilegiantes omnino attendere, [p. 231] ne subtrahant iusticiam vel inferant iniuriam alicui. Unde XXVª q. II, Quod vero, dicit Gregorius: Absit hoc a me ut statuta maiorum cum sacerdotibus meis in qualibet ecclesia infringam, quia mihi iniuriam facio, si fratrum meorum iura perturbo. Et indubie idem foret iudicium de quolibet viatore, quod si privilegiando precludit cuicunque iusticiam, dampnificat tam se quam privilegiandum; quod non licet vel Deo facere. Ex istis secundo sequitur quod fugitivi privilegiati in Westmonasterio non ex hinc libertantur, ut non satisfaciant debitori vel quod ibidem maneant pro suo perpetuo sine pena correccionis salubris, sed aliquam defensionem singularem habent ibidem que sonat eis in commodum et non in dampnum vel iniuriam alicui. Prima pars patet, tum quia hoc non sequitur ex tenore verborum privilegii, tum eciam quia hoc non foret libertas vel privilegium sed pravissima corruptela. Nam mandatum apostoli et sic Dei est Rom. XIII° 8: Nemini quidquam debeatis nisi ut invicem diligatis. Quomodo ergo licet creature licenciare aliquem, ut remaneat in debito fratris, non satisfaciens, eciam quoad Deum? Tales itaque hereses si pullulaverint eciam per dominos regni nostri, tenentur prelati destruere. Unde XXVª, q. I, Omne quod, sic scribitur: Iniustum iudicium et diffinicio iniusta regio metu vel iussu a iudicibus ordinata non valeat, nequaquam enim quod contra evangelicam vel propheticam vel apostolicam doctrinam constitucionemve eorum sive sanctorum patrum actum fuerit, stabit. Unde istam doctrinam dicitur Romanos [p. 232] pontifices debere usque ad animam et sanguinem confirmare (ut capitulo proximo precedente). Quomodo ergo stant simul: nulli liceat quidquam debere alteri extra caritativam dileccionem, et tamen fugitivus licenciatur in dicto loco perpetuo debere tam debitum corporale quam spirituale sine hoc quod rependat satisfaccionem. Revera in istam blasphemiam usurpatam Deus non potest, cum non licenciat hominem ad aliquid pro quo dampnat. Secunda autem pars conclusionis patet ex hoc quod oportet aliquod privilegium ex concessione regis dari talibus fugitivis ex conclusione proxima. Et ista sentencia notabiliter docetur XXVª, q. I. Ideo permittente domino, ubi dicitur quod quandocunque Romana ecclesia concedit privilegium, exprimit vel subintelligit, nisi aliter decreverit ordinandum. Unde, inquit, in nonnullis capitulis conciliorum, cum aliquid observandum decernitur, statim subinfertur, nisi auctoritas Romane ecclesie aliter fieri imperaverit vel salvo semper in omnibus iure Romane ecclesie vel salva in omnibus apostolica auctoritate. Cum ergo alia privilegia ab apostolico aliquibus conceduntur, et si contra generalem legem aliquid sonare videantur, non tamen contra ipsam aliquid concedere intelliguntur. Privilegia, inquit, dicuntur quasi privata legia; et concludit finaliter: Valet ergo Romana ecclesia extra generalia decreta quedam speciali beneficio indulgere, considerata tamen racionis equitate, ut mater iusticie in nullo ab ea dissentire inveniatur, ut videlicet privilegia que ob religionis vel necessitatis vel exhibiti obsequii graciam conceduntur, neminem relevando ita divitem faciant, ut multorum detrimenta non circumspiciendo in paupertatis miseriam nonnullos deiciant.

Since the [alms]{g:elemosina} of such great princes ought not to be wholly extinguished because of the perverse interpretation of those who abuse it, just as the truth of holy scripture ought not to be cheated of its light because of heretics who, taking occasion amiss, draw a perverse sense out of it, we must see in what sense the said [privilege]{g:privilegium} ought to be conceived, so that it may remain a privilege on both counts, in fact as well as in name.[^1] First, then: since it belongs to him whose it is to make laws and privileges to interpret them (as is clear from C. 25 q. 1, *Ideo permittente*, and from the fifth [book] of the Decretals, *De sentencia [228] excommunicacionis*, *Inter alia*), and [since] the king of England must grant and make such private laws out of his royal [lordship]{g:dominium} for the edification of the [church]{g:ecclesia}: therefore it must be taken as known of itself that it belongs to the king to interpret those private laws and alms and to defend them in their rightness.[^2] And the Roman pontiff, consulted on this, and his liege priest, asked for the interpretation of the catholic sense, are bound by the [gospel law]{g:lex evangelica} to declare humbly as much as they know; and they are not to be obeyed except in so far as they have founded their [teaching]{g:sentencia} in holy scripture.[^3] Hence, second, it must be supposed that holy theology must principally regulate every such science. This is clear from the fact that it alone principally teaches the way by which the heavenly marriage is honoured; now every lawful deed of a [wayfarer]{g:viator} is to be regulated according to this end; therefore the [conclusion]{g:conclusio}. And this is the teaching of distinction 20, *De quibus*, where Pope Innocent teaches five degrees of proceeding in deciding doubts. First, in so far as recourse must be had to the authority of scripture, which bishops, and especially the [pope]{g:papa}, are bound to defend even to [shedding their] blood, as is clear from C. 25 q. 1, *Sunt quidam*. Second, if the solution of the doubt does not shine out clearly from holy scripture in the Latin tongue, let recourse be had to the Greek writings, so that at least in this way the divine teaching may be had. Third, if not even so does the light of truth appear, put your hand to the catholic ecclesiastical histories gathered by the holy doctors. Fourth, if the difficulty that is enquired into does not come clear even by them, call keenly to mind the examples of the saints; for the examples of the saints' life ought to be to us a mirror of how to live. [229] Fifth, if, when all these have been examined, the nature of the question does not grow clear, let it be decided by decree of the elders of the province gathered for this, with the help of God, who cannot fail his own.[^4]

Third, I suppose this. Since the said privilege is not founded in canon or civil law, but specially in the laws of our kingdom and in the charters of that church recited above, note should be taken of the form, and of the end for the sake of which these privileges were granted (for the end, as the second [book] of the *Physics* teaches, measures all the means to it); now in the privileges of King Edgar a threefold end is expressed: namely, reverence for God in the relics of his apostle; the quiet of the monks living there; and the honour of the holiness of that peculiar basilica, in which there live special [bedesmen]{g:oratores} for the king and kingdom, who are maintained specially for this out of their alms.[^5] Hence in the bull of Nicholas III it is contained thus: "Let the monks there be subject to no person at all except the king."[^6] From which it seems that the inhabitants of that place are specially subject to their king, not only because [they are] the king's liege men, nor only because [they are] his special [almsmen]{g:elemosinarius}, but by the pope's command; for according to blessed Gregory the multiplicity of benefits increases the obligation of the one benefited to his giver.[^7] From which it seems (since the monks of that place are so singularly subject to the king, and their guests or fugitives ought not to be more exempt or privileged than the monks from whom the privilege flows), it seems, [I say,] that all refugees, in so far as they are in that place, are subject to the king of England with a certain pre-eminence, from the fact that they receive from him there a singular benefit. Nor does it accord with the faith of the religion of Christ that a monk, if (to suppose what is possible) he should kill a man in the said place, would afterwards be freer and receive more of the fruit of the privilege than when he was guiltless before God and [230] man and more capable [of it]. Nor does it seem reasonable [to deny] that, supposing capacity in a monk or in any servant of that place, he will have the fruit of the privilege after an offence even rather than an outsider; for otherwise everyone resting there obstinately in crime would, as such, make himself [unfit]{g:indispositus} to have the aforesaid privilege.[^8]

These things premised, it seems to me (as elsewhere) that, although throughout all England remission of every kind ought to be granted, in so far as it tends to mercy and no further, yet for the place of Westminster such a general remission ought to be kept, by virtue of the singular privilege piously granted to that place by the kings.[^9] The first part is clear from this, that privileges, since (as such) they tend of themselves to good, are reasonably to be embraced; for it would be better that the whole church should have privilege, that is, law for its own advantage, than that any part of the church should fall short of true privilege, since it sins or is punished by so much as it falls short of the full privileges of the blessed. And it is clear that there is no privilege except in so far as it tends to alms or a work of mercy, since there is no privilege except in so far as it is mercifully given by God, turning to a man's profit. And the second part is clear from this, that the king's [grant]{g:concessio} must have a special efficacy, to be observed there more diligently; for otherwise it would be in vain. And from these it is clear that no privilege is contrary to the common [law]{g:ius}; for if it were, then it would be against justice, and consequently an unjust law and not a privilege. Hence the saying of some, who say that it would not be a privilege unless it were contrary to the common law, seems to me wholly foolish. For a lawful variation of rite for place and time is not contrary to truth, and consequently not to any law.[^10] Therefore those who privilege must take every care [231] not to withdraw justice from anyone or inflict injury on anyone. Hence in C. 25 q. 2, *Quod vero*, Gregory says: "Far be this from me, that I should break the statutes of our forebears with my priests in any church; for I do myself injury if I disturb the rights of my brothers." And without doubt the judgment would be the same of any wayfarer: that if by privileging he shuts anyone off from justice, he harms both himself and the one to be privileged; which it is not lawful even for God to do.[^11]

From these, second, it [follows]{g:sequitur} that the fugitives privileged in Westminster are not thereby set free so as not to satisfy [their creditor], or so as to remain there in perpetuity without the penalty of wholesome correction; but they have there some singular defence, which tends to their advantage and not to the harm or injury of anyone.[^12] The first part is clear, both because this does not follow from the tenor of the words of the privilege, and also because this would not be liberty or privilege but the most depraved corruption. For the command of the apostle, and so of God, is Romans 13:8: "Owe no man any thing, but to love one another." How, then, is it lawful for a creature to license anyone to remain in debt to his brother, not making satisfaction, even as regards God? Such heresies, therefore, if they should sprout even through the lords of our kingdom, the [prelates]{g:prelatus} are bound to destroy.[^13] Hence in C. 25 q. 1, *Omne quod*, it is written thus: "An unjust judgment and an unjust definition, ordained by judges through a king's fear or command, shall not be valid; for in no way shall anything stand that has been done against the evangelical or prophetic or apostolic [doctrine]{g:doctrina}, or their constitution, or [that] of the holy fathers." Hence it is said that the Roman [232] pontiffs ought to confirm this doctrine even to [the giving of] life and blood (as in the chapter immediately preceding).[^14] How, then, do these stand together: that it is lawful for no one to owe anything to another beyond the love of [charity]{g:caritas}, and yet a fugitive is licensed in the said place to owe perpetually both a bodily and a spiritual debt without paying back satisfaction? Truly God cannot [consent] to this usurped blasphemy, since he does not license a man to anything for which he damns [him].[^15] And the second part of the conclusion is clear from this, that some privilege must be given to such fugitives from the king's grant, by the preceding conclusion.

And this teaching is notably taught in C. 25 q. 1, *Ideo permittente domino*, where it is said that whenever the Roman church grants a privilege, it expresses or understands [the clause] "unless it should decree that it be ordered otherwise". "Hence," it says, "in some chapters of the councils, when something is decreed to be observed, there is at once added: 'unless the authority of the Roman church should command it to be done otherwise', or 'saving always in all things the right of the Roman church', or 'saving in all things the apostolic authority'. When, therefore, other privileges are granted to some by the apostolic [pontiff], even if they seem to say something against the general law, yet they are not understood to grant anything against it." "Privileges," it says, "are so called as being private laws (*privata legia*)"; and it concludes finally: "The Roman church, therefore, has power to grant certain things by special favour outside the general decrees, yet with regard had to the equity of reason, so that the mother of justice may be found in nothing to dissent from it: that is, so that privileges, which are granted for the sake of religion, or of necessity, or of service rendered, may not, by relieving someone, make him so rich that, through failing to look to the losses of many, they cast some into the misery of poverty."[^16]

## Notes

[^1]: **C.** A in the margin *Cap. XI* (227.20). *Elemosina tantorum principum*: the royal grants to Westminster discussed in cap. 10, treated as alms. *Occasione male accepta*: occasion taken amiss, as of the heretics' misuse of scripture. *Utrobique*: in both respects, *re* and *nomine*.

[^2]: **S/C.** Side-note 228.2. Loserth (227.28): C. 25 q. 1 c. 16 and X 5.39.31 (our modern form of his references; LEDGER E11a-1); the words Wyclif uses are Gratian's dictum after c. 16, §§1–4 (Friedberg I, 1011–12). B reads *concedere* for *condere* (227.28); the print is translated. *Autem* brings the second premiss (the king makes these laws), hence "[since]". *Leges privatas*: private laws, the etymology of *privilegium* quoted at the end of this paragraph.

[^3]: **C/T.** *Sacerdos suus legius*: the king's liege priest, i.e. a priest of his realm bound to him in allegiance; *suus* refers to the king, not the pope. B omits *suus* (228.7) and reads *humiliter declinare* (228.9); the print is translated. Side-note 228.12.

[^4]: **S.** A in the margin *Nota quomodo est dubium discernendum* (228.17); B in the margin *Ad quem quis debet recurrere pro dubiis discrete decidendis* (228.25). The manuscripts read *dist. XXI*; Loserth prints *XX* (228.17). Loserth: Wyclif draws the five degrees, "with some words changed", from D. 20 c. 3 and C. 25 q. 1 c. 6 (LEDGER E11a-2); the five degrees are the canon's own words as Gratian prints it (ascribed to Innocent I; Friedberg I, 66), Wyclif adding *in lingua latina* (LEDGER E11a-3). B corrects *mitte* to *mittere* (228.27). *Recordare*: imperative.

[^5]: **S/C.** Side-notes 229.5–229.17 (their a), b), c) follow Wyclif's threefold end). A in the margin *Conclusio. Nota quomodo sunt privilegia intelligenda* (229.18). Aristotle, *Physics* II.3 (Loserth, 229.9; LEDGER E11a-4). *Apostoli*: St Peter, Westminster's patron (side-note 229.10). *Peculiaris basilice*: that "peculiar" church, taken as Westminster's standing as a royal foundation exempt from the bishop; it may mean only "special". *De quorum elemosinis*: the alms of the king and realm.

[^6]: **S.** B in the margin *Bulla Nicolai III* (229.16). Loserth gives no reference. Whether Nicholas III is right, or a papal privilege of another pope to Westminster is meant, is to be checked (LEDGER E11a-5).

[^7]: **S.** The saying ascribed to Gregory (gifts increase the account owed for them) is *Hom. in Ev.* 9.1, *Cum enim augentur dona, rationes etiam crescunt donorum* (PL 76:1106), paraphrased; Loserth does not locate it (LEDGER E11a-6). *Datarium*: the giver.

[^8]: **C/T.** *Videtur … videtur*: Wyclif restarts the sentence after the *cum* clause; we keep the repetition. A reads *preminencia* (229.28); B omits *per* in *per possibile* (229.31). *Immunis quoad Deum et hominem*: guiltless before God and man (cf. 239 on *immunitas*). *Nec … quin*: a double negative, "it is reasonable that". *Aliter*: if the privilege did not depend on capacity, i.e. on worthiness. Side-note 229.31.

[^9]: **C.** A in the margin *Prima conclusio* (230.7). The conclusion has two parts (*remissio* in all England only as mercy allows; a general remission kept at Westminster), proved in turn below. *Sonat in*: tends to (as 10-f). *Ut alias*: as elsewhere, perhaps *De civ. dom.*; not identified.

[^10]: **C/T.** A and A₁ read *ex hoc cum privilegia* (230.12), A *sonant* (230.20); the print is translated. A in the margin *Correlarium* (230.19, 230.25); B in the margin *Privilegium non est communi iuri contrarium* (230.26). *Ius commune*: the general law (of the church), not the English common law. The view rejected (a privilege is by definition against the common law) is the canonists'; Gratian (D. 3 c. 3) gives only Isidore's *privilegia sunt leges privatorum*, and the gloss that sets privilege against *ius commune* is not traced (LEDGER E11a-7).

[^11]: **S/C.** Loserth: C. 25 q. 2 c. 10 (231.2; LEDGER E11a-8). *Cum sacerdotibus meis*: Friedberg reads *consacerdotibus meis*, "my fellow priests"; *cum sacerdotibus*, Wyclif's reading, is that of the old editions (Friedberg I, 1014; LEDGER E11a-8). *Vel Deo*: "even for God", since God cannot do injustice.

[^12]: **T/C.** A in the margin *Secunda conclusio* (231.10); side-note 231.10. *Debitori*: the print has "debtor"; the sense requires the one to whom the debt is owed, and we translate "[their creditor]". Whether *debitor* here is Wyclif's slip or a loose use is to be checked. *Pro suo perpetuo*: literally "for their perpetuity", i.e. for life.

[^13]: **S/R.** Romans 13:8, as the Vulgate; DR. The manuscripts read *Rom. XII*; Loserth corrects (231.20). *Eciam quoad Deum*: the debt is owed to God as well as to the brother. B in the margin *Est hic manifeste elicibile quod non licet contra apostolum dispensare* (231.28), an early reader taking the point as a limit on dispensation.

[^14]: **S/C.** Loserth: C. 25 q. 1 c. 8 (231.25; LEDGER E11a-9); *Omne quod inreprehensibile est* is the canon's incipit, and Wyclif quotes its § 1 (Friedberg I, 1009; LEDGER E11a-9). *Eorum*: of the evangelists, prophets and apostles. *Capitulo proximo precedente*: probably the preceding canon of the same *quaestio* (*Sunt quidam*, c. 6, cited at 228.20), though it could mean cap. 10 of this treatise.

[^15]: **T/C.** A and A₁ read *extra caritatem* (232.4); the print is translated. *Deus non potest*: Loserth notes that a verb seems to be wanting (232.8); we supply "[consent]". Side-note 232.3.

[^16]: **S/T.** C. 25 q. 1 c. 16, *Ideo permittente* (LEDGER E11a-1), here quoted at length as Gratian's text, with *inquit* for its author. B omits from *vel salva* to *conceduntur* (232.19); A reads *que vel* (232.30). The manuscripts read *pauperis*; Loserth corrects to *paupertatis* from the Decretum (232.33). *Mater iusticie*: the Roman church; *ab ea*: from equity. *Privata legia*: the etymology of *privilegium*, kept in Latin.
